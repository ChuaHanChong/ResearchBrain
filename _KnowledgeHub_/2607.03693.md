# CoRE-VLA: Towards Scalable and Robust Vision-Language-Action Modeling via Conditional Routing of Experts

> [!summary] Summary
> CoRE-VLA introduces a Vision-Language-Action framework that uses conditional routing of experts to enable robots to perform diverse manipulation tasks robustly across varied sensor configurations and efficiently adapt to different task demands. This approach improves average success rates on benchmarks like RoboCasa GR1 Tabletop to 56.5% and shows increased robustness in real-world tasks, maintaining performance even when auxiliary sensors are absent.

## Problem

- Existing VLA policies struggle with sensor heterogeneity and missing auxiliary sensors, often over-relying on them and becoming brittle when sensors are unavailable.
- Current VLA policies use shared dense action generators, leading to inefficient computation, conflicting gradients, and task interference for diverse and long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- Existing VLA policies with fixed sensor interfaces cannot flexibly extend to new sensing modalities or switch between exploiting and ignoring auxiliary inputs without full retraining of the action backbone.

## Method

- **CoRE-VLA** frames action generation as context-conditioned sparse computation, utilizing a ==Conditional Routing of Experts (CoRE)== mechanism within an ==Action Diffusion Transformer==.
- It employs ==intent-conditioned routing== based on task intent and selective processing of action-side representations to dynamically route computation to general and modality-specialized experts.
- ==Modality dropout== and modality-specialized expert masking are used during training to simulate sensor absence, ensuring robustness while enabling exploitation of auxiliary sensor data when available.

## Results

- Achieved superior performance across LIBERO benchmarks, including a **97.0%** success rate on LIBERO-Long without depth (**97.6%** with depth), demonstrating robustness against sensor absence.
- Attained the highest average success rate of **56.5% ± 0.4** on the RoboCasa GR1 Tabletop benchmark, outperforming Diffusion Policy and GR00T-N1.x baselines.
- Demonstrated robustness in real-world tasks (e.g., Vegetables-Picking: **65.0%** success without auxiliary depth vs. **35.0%** for baseline), and further improved performance when physical depth was available (e.g., Fabric-Folding increased from **70.0%** to **80.0%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly conditioning action generation on sensor availability through expert routing allows robust operation with heterogeneous and potentially failing sensor configurations.
> - Dynamic, task-intent-conditioned routing of experts mitigates task interference and enhances specialization for diverse and long-horizon manipulation tasks by adapting computation.
> - Training with modality dropout and specialized experts prevents over-reliance on auxiliary sensors, enabling flexible policy extension to new modalities without complete retraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of generalist robotic manipulation, specifically concerning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. VLA models aim to enable robots to perform diverse tasks based on visual observations and natural language instructions. Recent advancements in this area, exemplified by systems such as RT-1, RT-2, Octo, OpenVLA, π0, and GR00T N1, demonstrate the scalability of policies trained on large and varied robot data, often leveraging pretrained vision-language models (VLMs).

Despite these developments, the practical deployment of VLA policies encounters two primary challenges that CoRE-VLA seeks to address. First, real-world robotic platforms exhibit heterogeneity in their sensor configurations. Robots may possess auxiliary sensors like depth, tactile, or force feedback, while others may lack them due to hardware design or operational constraints. Furthermore, even when available, these auxiliary sensors can experience unexpected failures. Existing VLA policies typically rely on a fixed observation interface and shared dense computation, which can lead to policies over-relying on auxiliary sensors and becoming brittle when these sensors are absent. The current work aims to introduce robustness to such sensor configuration variability and potential failures.

Second, existing VLA policies often employ a shared dense action generator that applies the same computational path across diverse tasks and long-horizon behaviors. This approach can be inefficient and lead to issues observed in multi-task learning, such as conflicting gradients, task interference, and negative transfer, particularly for manipulation tasks requiring varied forms of action processing (e.g., precise reaching, bimanual coordination, contact-rich interaction). The paper references prior work on conditional computation and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) as mechanisms to increase model capacity and adapt computation sparsely. While some VLA research has explored MoE-style routing to decouple reasoning from control or preserve pretraining, CoRE-VLA distinguishes itself by applying conditional computation specifically to action generation, conditioning it on both task intent and sensor availability. This aims to enable more adaptive and scalable action generation compared to uniformly dense computation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a scalable and robust VLA framework that can effectively operate across diverse robotic platforms with heterogeneous sensor configurations, while also handling the potential absence or failure of auxiliary sensors during deployment. Concurrently, the framework aims to improve the specialization and scalability of VLA policies for diverse and long-horizon manipulation tasks.

The motivation for these objectives stems from two fundamental challenges observed in the deployment of contemporary VLA models.

#### 2.1. Challenge of Sensor Heterogeneity and Missing Auxiliary Sensors

- **Problem:** Real-world robots are equipped with varied sensor suites; some have auxiliary inputs like depth, tactile, or force sensors, while others do not. Even when present, these sensors can fail during operation.
- **Limitation of Existing Policies:** Current VLA policies are typically designed and trained with a fixed set of sensors. Their action generation mechanism is tightly coupled to this fixed sensor set through shared dense computation. This coupling causes two issues:
 - **Brittleness:** If trained with auxiliary sensors, the policy may over-rely on them, leading to performance degradation when those sensors are unavailable (either by design or due to failure) during deployment.
 - **Lack of Adaptability:** Policies cannot gracefully switch between exploiting auxiliary information when available and operating reliably from core inputs (e.g., RGB, proprioception, language) when auxiliary sensors are absent, without requiring retraining.
- **Motivation:** A practical VLA policy is required that can leverage auxiliary perceptual inputs when they are available, yet remain robust and reliable, generating appropriate actions, when these inputs are absent. This adaptability is crucial for real-world generalist robot deployment across varying hardware and operational conditions.

#### 2.2. Challenge of Scalability for Diverse and Long-Horizon Tasks

- **Problem:** Robotic manipulation tasks are heterogeneous, involving a wide range of actions from spatially precise movements to contact-rich interactions or bimanual coordination. Long-horizon tasks further involve sequences of distinct subgoals.
- **Limitation of Existing Policies:** Most VLA policies utilize a shared, dense action generator. This means the same computational path is applied uniformly to all tokens across different tasks and subgoals.
 - **Task Interference:** Prior research in multi-task learning indicates that forcing diverse objectives to share the same parameters can result in conflicting gradients and negative transfer, thereby hindering learning and performance across tasks.
 - **Inefficient Computation:** Applying uniform computation to all tasks and subgoals may not be optimal, as different manipulation behaviors might require specialized processing.
- **Motivation:** Scalable VLA modeling necessitates an action generator that can dynamically adapt its computation. This adaptation should be conditioned on both the current task intent (derived from language instructions and visual context) and the available sensory information, allowing for specialized processing paths that can mitigate interference and improve efficiency across a wide array of manipulation tasks and complex long-horizon behaviors.

To address these motivations, the authors propose CoRE-VLA, which re-conceptualizes action generation as context-conditioned sparse computation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoRE-VLA frames action generation as a context-conditioned sparse computation process, departing from the fixed dense computation paths common in existing VLA policies. The framework utilizes a Conditional Routing of Experts (CoRE) mechanism within its Action Diffusion Transformer (DiT).

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The CoRE-VLA architecture integrates a pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) for encoding RGB observations and language instructions. An auxiliary modality encoder processes supplementary sensory inputs (e.g., depth). The core action generation is handled by an Action Diffusion Transformer, which is augmented with CoRE blocks. This DiT is conditioned on proprioceptive states and noisy action representations.

#### 3.2. Conditional Routing of Experts (CoRE Block)

The CoRE block is central to the framework's adaptability and robustness, employing two forms of context for sparse computation:

- **Intent-conditioned Routing:**
 - **Task Intent Embedding (g):** A compact representation of task intent is derived by average pooling the VLM-encoded text representations (C_text). These text representations are grounded in the visual context through VLM attention. The router is conditioned on this `g` to enable intent-adaptive computation paths for action-side representations.
- **Action-side Representation Selection:**
 - To allocate computation efficiently, CoRE selectively processes action-side representations. For each representation `h_i` in the Action DiT sequence, a selection score `p_i` is computed based on `h_i` and the task-intent embedding `g`.
 - A capacity-limited subset `S` of the most task-relevant representations (e.g., Top-K based on `p_i`) is selected for further cross-attention and expert computation. Representations not in `S` bypass these computations and are merged back later. This mechanism aims to bound activated computation by a selection-capacity ratio `ρ`.
- **General and Modality-Specialized Experts:**
 - The expert set within each CoRE layer is divided into `E_gen` (general experts) and `E_mod` (modality-specialized experts). `E_gen` handles shared manipulation patterns, while `E_mod` is designed for modality-dependent computation.
 - This design facilitates extending an RGB-pretrained VLA policy with auxiliary modalities (and their corresponding specialized experts) through continued training, without requiring a complete redesign or retraining of the action backbone.
- **Modality Dropout and Modality-Specialized Expert Masking:**
 - A binary modality indicator `z_mod` (0 for absent, 1 for present) is introduced to manage auxiliary sensor availability.
 - During training, `z_mod` is randomly sampled with a dropout probability (`p_drop`), simulating both full-modality and RGB-language-proprioception conditions. When `z_mod = 0`, the auxiliary modality branch is disabled, and modality-specialized experts (`E_mod`) are masked.
 - For a selected representation `h_i`, it first attends to the conditional sequence `C`. If `z_mod = 0`, auxiliary modality representations are excluded from `C`. Expert routing logits (`r_i,e`) are then computed from the conditioned representation and `g`, and subsequently masked based on `z_mod`. This ensures that `E_mod` are only considered when the auxiliary modality is active.
 - At inference time, `z_mod` is set according to the actual availability of the auxiliary sensor.
- **Differentiable Routing Path:**
 - While the Top-K selection and hard expert assignment are discrete operations, the routing path remains differentiable. Gradients are propagated through the continuous selection scores (`p_i`) and expert routing probabilities (`π_i`) that gate the computation.

#### 3.3. Training Objective and Inference

- **Flow Matching Objective (L_act):** CoRE-VLA is trained using a flow matching objective over action chunks. Given a ground-truth action chunk `a`, proprioceptive state `s_t`, Gaussian noise `ϵ`, and a continuous flow time `τ`, the model predicts a velocity field `v_hat` (the difference between `a` and `ϵ`). The `v_hat` is a function of the interpolated action `x_τ`, `τ`, `s_t`, the conditional sequence `C`, `g`, and `z_mod`.
- **Regularization:**
 - **Selection Regularizer (L_sel):** Encourages the average representation-selection mass to align with the target capacity ratio `ρ`, preventing degenerate selection.
 - **MoE Load-Balancing Loss (L_moe):** Applied over selected representations and available experts to promote balanced expert utilization and prevent routing collapse, as commonly used in Mixture-of-Experts models.
- **Total Loss:** The final training objective combines these components: `L = L_act + λ_sel L_sel + λ_moe L_moe`, with `λ_sel` and `λ_moe` as weighting coefficients.
- **Inference:** During inference, the CoRE-VLA policy generates an action chunk by integrating the learned velocity field from Gaussian noise to the action space. The modality indicator `z_mod` is dynamically set based on the actual auxiliary sensor availability.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup Details

- The research focuses on depth as a representative auxiliary modality, using Depth Anything V2 (DA-V2) to generate noisy pseudo-depth maps from RGB observations during training.
- The training strategy involves first training a dense Action DiT from scratch on RGB-language-proprioception inputs. This model is then converted to CoRE-VLA by duplicating FFN modules as general experts, adding modality-specialized experts, and an auxiliary sensor encoder, followed by continued training with auxiliary perceptual inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CoRE-VLA was conducted across simulated environments (LIBERO, RoboCasa GR1 Tabletop) and real-world dual-arm manipulation tasks, with a focus on depth as the auxiliary modality.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** CoRE-VLA achieved superior performance across all four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), with a notable improvement in the LIBERO-Long suite.
 - On LIBERO-Long, CoRE-VLA achieved a 97.6% success rate with depth enabled and maintained 97.0% without depth. This contrasts with a naive depth-augmented baseline, which improved to 95.6% with depth but dropped significantly to 91.2% when depth was removed, indicating over-reliance.
 - Ablation studies confirmed that task-intent routing contributed to improved long-horizon execution (94.4% to 95.2% for the RGB-only case), and that modality-specialized experts combined with modality dropout were crucial for exploiting depth while maintaining robustness under depth absence.
 - Analysis of expert routing demonstrated task-dependent expert utilization, modality-dependent expert usage, temporally varying routing during long-horizon rollouts, and sparse action-token activation patterns, indicating adaptive computation rather than uniform expert usage.
- **RoboCasa GR1 Tabletop Benchmark:** CoRE-VLA achieved the highest average success rate of 56.5% ± 0.4 across 24 tabletop manipulation tasks, outperforming Diffusion Policy (40.4%), GR00T-N1.5 (48.0%), and GR00T-N1.6 (47.6%). This outcome supports its multi-task manipulation capability and its ability to mitigate negative transfer through task-dependent expert specialization.

#### 4.2. Real-World Results

- Experiments were conducted on an AgileX ALOHA dual-arm robot for Vegetables-Picking, Clothes-Folding, and Fabric-Folding (zero-shot generalization). Training utilized DA-V2 pseudo-depth.
- **Robustness to Missing Depth:** Even when deployed without an auxiliary physical depth camera, CoRE-VLA consistently outperformed the dense Action DiT baseline. For example, in Vegetables-Picking, CoRE-VLA (without auxiliary depth) achieved a 65.0% success rate compared to the baseline's 35.0%. This result indicates that modality dropout and modality-specialized expert masking effectively prevent over-reliance on depth, ensuring reliable execution from RGB, language, and proprioception alone.
- **Exploitation of Physical Depth:** When a physical depth camera was enabled during inference, CoRE-VLA's performance further improved across all tasks. On Fabric-Folding, the success rate increased from 70.0% (without depth) to 80.0% (with physical depth). This suggests that modality-specialized experts can effectively leverage geometric cues when available.
- **Outperformance of Baseline:** CoRE-VLA with physical depth also surpassed the pretrained π0.5 baseline across all three real-world tasks, despite π0.5 having been initialized from a large-scale pretrained checkpoint. This indicates the effectiveness of the CoRE-VLA design even without extensive policy pretraining.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Analysis (Appendix D)

- CoRE-VLA is designed to improve conditional computation capacity while maintaining sparse activation. The framework decouples the total parameter capacity (which increases with more experts) from the per-input activated computation, which remains proportional to the selected token count.
- The activated computation of a CoRE block is approximately proportional to the selection-capacity ratio `ρ`, plus a small routing overhead, allowing for computational efficiency compared to dense networks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CoRE-VLA framework introduces a method for addressing critical challenges in the deployment and scalability of Vision-Language-Action models for robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Robustness to Sensor Heterogeneity and Failures:** By explicitly formulating sensor availability as a routing condition within the action generator, CoRE-VLA provides a robust solution for operating robots with diverse and potentially unreliable sensor configurations. This addresses a fundamental practical hurdle in deploying generalist robotic policies in real-world environments, where hardware variations and sensor malfunctions are common.
- **Scalable Multi-Task and Long-Horizon Learning:** The task-intent-conditioned routing of action-side representations to specialized experts enables the action generator to adapt its computation based on task demands. This approach is demonstrated to improve performance on complex multi-task and long-horizon manipulation benchmarks, potentially mitigating issues like negative transfer and task interference prevalent in dense, shared-parameter models. This indicates a pathway toward more scalable VLA policies capable of handling broader and more complex skill sets.
- **Flexible Policy Extension:** The framework allows for existing RGB-pretrained VLA policies to be extended with auxiliary perceptual modalities (such as depth) through continued training, without requiring a complete retraining from scratch or leading to brittleness when the auxiliary sensors are unavailable. This modularity can simplify the process of adapting generalist policies to new hardware capabilities.
- **Demonstrated Performance and Generalization:** The consistent strong performance across diverse simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboCasa GR1 Tabletop) and real-world tasks, including zero-shot generalization to unseen scenarios (Fabric-Folding), suggests the practical efficacy of CoRE-VLA. Its ability to exploit auxiliary depth when available and gracefully degrade when it is absent provides empirical evidence for its proposed benefits.
- **Foundational Contribution to VLA Architecture:** The paper claims to be the first to explicitly frame the robust real-world deployment of unified VLA policies under sensor-configuration heterogeneity as a joint architectural and training problem. This suggests a foundational contribution to the design principles for future VLA models, emphasizing adaptive computation within the action generation pipeline.

The implications of CoRE-VLA extend to making generalist robot policies more practical and deployable in varied real-world settings. Future work outlined by the authors, such as scaling CoRE-VLA to larger cross-embodiment robot data and extending it to richer physical modalities like tactile sensing and force feedback, suggests continued exploration of this adaptive computation paradigm for broader robotic capabilities.
