# Kairos: A Native World Model Stack for Physical AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Kairos is a unified world model stack designed for Physical AI that integrates world representation and robotic control to learn from diverse experiences, maintain persistent states, and operate efficiently. It achieves competitive performance across embodied and general world model benchmarks with significantly reduced inference latency and memory usage.

## Problem

- Existing world models struggle to synthesize knowledge from diverse data types (open-world videos, human behavior, robot interaction), leading to incomplete competencies.
- Maintaining consistent world states over long temporal horizons is challenging for current systems, which often degrade due to reliance on purely local continuation heuristics.
- A disconnect exists between world understanding and embodied control, as current models often fail to learn how an embodied agent's actions reliably change future states for effective decision-making.

## Method

- A Native Pre-training Paradigm with a Cross-Embodiment Data Curriculum (CEDC) progressively learns physical laws, behavioral semantics, and embodied grounding from heterogeneous data sources.
- A unified Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture incorporates Hybrid Linear Temporal Attention (Gated Linear, Sliding Window, Dilated Sliding Window) to ensure coherent, long-horizon world state maintenance.
- Deployment-aware system co-design, including timestep distillation and hardware-aware optimizations (e.g., mixed-parallel inference, FP8 quantization), ensures efficient real-time operation under real-world constraints.

## Results

- Kairos-4B achieved a total score of 9.30 on WorldModelBench-robot, outperforming all baselines with high instruction following (2.36) and physics adherence (4.96).
- The model attained a state-of-the-art average score of 90.8 on LIBERO-Plus after fine-tuning, showcasing strong generalization in complex bimanual manipulation tasks.
- It demonstrated high inference efficiency, with significantly lower memory usage (23.5GB) and computational complexity (2.3 PFlops) compared to larger models, resulting in orders-of-magnitude faster inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Purely local temporal models are inherently insufficient for accurate long-horizon prediction, necessitating mechanisms that preserve global state consistency to avoid irreducible excess risk.
> - A hybrid multi-scale temporal attention mechanism, combining gated linear attention for long-term memory with sliding window and dilated attention for short-to-mid-term dynamics, enables robust, consistent long-horizon generation by geometrically damping error accumulation.
> - Systematically integrating knowledge from diverse data sources—general physical laws, human-centric behavior, and robot interactions—through a progressive curriculum significantly enhances world-modeling capabilities and action grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of world models is evolving from systems primarily focused on visual generation to foundational infrastructure for Physical AI. This transition implies a shift in requirements: world models must be capable of acquiring knowledge from diverse experiences, maintaining persistent states over extended periods, and operating efficiently within real-world deployment constraints.

The paper identifies three primary streams of advancement in world models:
1. **Generative Pixel-Level Rendering:** This stream, exemplified by models such as NVIDIA's Cosmos, focuses on producing high-fidelity, temporally coherent visual futures.
2. **Predictive Latent Embedding Learning:** Models like Meta’s JEPA family (e.g., V-JEPA, DINO-world) learn predictive structures within abstract representation spaces, enabling physical understanding, planning, and robot control without explicit pixel rendering.
3. **Interactive Environment Modeling:** This direction, seen in World Labs’ Marble and DeepMind’s Genie, focuses on creating persistent, manipulable simulations where agents can explore and interact, often used as internal simulators for long-horizon behavior optimization.

The Kairos project positions itself within this evolving landscape by proposing a unified world model stack that integrates capabilities from these distinct streams. It moves beyond merely generating visually plausible futures to functioning as an operational substrate for embodied and physical intelligence, emphasizing a cohesive approach to learning, maintaining, and running simulated worlds for future self-evolving agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of Kairos is motivated by several challenges currently facing world models as they transition into operational infrastructure for Physical AI. The paper outlines four major challenges:

1. **Fragmented World Learning Across Heterogeneous Experience Sources:** Knowledge essential for a capable world model is distributed across diverse data types, including open-world videos (broad physical regularities, but lacking action grounding), human behavioral data (structured behavior, but not directly aligned with robot embodiment), and scarce robot interaction data (most relevant for robots, but expensive and narrow). Current systems often optimize for one data type, leading to incomplete competencies. Kairos aims to overcome this by synthesizing knowledge from these disparate sources.

2. **Maintaining Persistent World States Over Long Temporal Horizons:** While short video continuations can rely on local visual smoothness, effective world modeling requires preserving global state consistency over extended durations, including object permanence, delayed physical effects, and latent task progress. Existing systems often degrade over longer horizons due to local continuation heuristics that do not guarantee global state consistency, an issue the paper formally proves to be an unavoidable structural limitation. Kairos seeks to address this by robustly maintaining world states over long horizons.

3. **The Gap Between World Understanding and Embodied Control:** Many world models can predict or generate plausible futures but struggle to learn how an embodied agent's actions reliably change those futures. This disconnect arises because observation, behavior, and control often exist in different representational regimes. Kairos aims to bridge this gap by enabling stable perception-action grounding for downstream embodied decision-making.

4. **Deployment and Closed-Loop Operation Under Real-World Constraints:** Even high-performing offline world models may be impractical for real-world Physical AI if they cannot operate with low latency, low memory overhead, and efficient communication. For self-evolutionary learning, real-time observation-action-feedback loops are critical. Kairos emphasizes deployment-ready operation to ensure models can be embedded into real systems and support continual adaptation.

Kairos is designed to address these interconnected challenges through a native world-action model stack. Its central objective is to function as a cohesive operational foundation for self-evolving physical intelligence by learning from progressive cross-embodiment experience, maintaining world states through consistent temporal attention mechanisms, and ensuring efficient execution through deployment-aware system co-design.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Kairos framework is structured around three core contributions: a native pre-training paradigm, a unified architectural design, and deployment-aware system co-design.

#### 3.1. Native Pre-training Paradigm with Cross-Embodiment Data Curriculum (CEDC)

Kairos introduces a Native Pre-training Paradigm for Physical AI, which integrates general physical laws, behavioral semantics, and embodied grounding from the initial stages of model scaling. This is achieved through a Cross-Embodiment Data Curriculum (CEDC), organizing heterogeneous data into a progressive developmental pathway:

- **Stage I: Physical Pretraining:** This foundational phase leverages massive open-world video datasets (millions of hours) to internalize broad physical and environmental regularities (e.g., gravity, collision mechanics). The model functions as a "spectator," learning implicit laws. Causal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning is integrated to formalize these physical principles within the latent space. This stage uses a progressive training protocol, moving from lower visual resolutions and shorter sequences to high-fidelity, long-horizon video forecasting. It includes image pre-training, image-video mixed pre-training, and continual pre-training, employing a shape-aware exponential timestep shifting for robust handling of varying video lengths and resolutions.

- **Stage II: Embodied Pretraining with Human-centric Data:** The curriculum transitions to internalizing "behavioral experience" from human-centric datasets (over 100,000 hours). This shifts the focus from "what happens" to "how tasks are organized through human intent," learning causal consequences of structured interventions. This stage primarily optimizes the VideoDiT component, learning goal-directed actions and their environmental consequences without binding to a specific robotic action space. It progresses through human-centric pre-training, robot-centric training, target-embodiment fine-tuning, and RL refinement (using DPO to align with human preferences and physical standards).

- **Stage III: Joint World-Action Training:** This final stage grounds physical and behavioral priors into explicit robotic action. It focuses on training the ActionDiT component to unify perception and actuation into a World-Action Model (WAM). Video and action sequences are strictly temporally aligned, optimizing both VideoDiT (initialized from prior stages) and a substantially reduced-scale ActionDiT using flow matching objectives. The training objective combines video and action losses (L = L_video + λL_action), with ActionDiT leveraging a fixed timestamp shift. This joint training aims to eliminate representation misalignment between simulation and execution.

The training objective used across Kairos is Flow Matching, which learns a continuous-time conditional velocity field to transport samples from a Gaussian noise distribution to the data distribution in latent space.

#### 3.2. Native Unified Architecture with Hybrid Linear Temporal Attention

Kairos employs a single endogenous backbone that integrates World Understanding, World Generation, and World Prediction within a unified Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architecture, ensuring shared world state and long-horizon coherence.

- **World Understanding:** Leverages a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically Qwen series, to extract high-level semantic representations from diverse heterogeneous sources (physical law descriptors, multi-modal sensor streams).
- **World Generation:** The generative component, a conditional diffusion model, produces high-fidelity outcomes (e.g., photorealistic environments) conditioned on semantic understanding and interactive instructions. It comprises a high-compression video VAE, a multimodal conditioning encoder, and a temporally scalable DiT backbone.
- **World Prediction:** Formulated as a unified World-Action Model (WAM) with a Mixture-of-Transformer (MoT) architecture, consisting of a Video DiT and an Action DiT. It jointly models environmental dynamics and robot action generation. Input sequences are organized into history video tokens, future video tokens, and future action tokens, with a unified attention masking strategy. This allows for efficient action-only prediction at deployment.

To address the quadratic complexity of standard attention in long video generation, Kairos introduces a **LinearDiT backbone with Hybrid Linear Attention** (Figure 5) designed for efficiency, long-horizon modeling, and expandability. The temporal structure is decomposed hierarchically:
- **Gated Linear Attention (GLA):** (Long-term) Implemented using GatedDeltaNet, GLA provides global temporal reasoning with linear complexity. It uses a Delta Update Rule with a decay gate ($\alpha_t$) and a writing strength ($\beta_t$) to selectively retain and update key-value associations in an associative memory state ($S_t$), enabling persistent memory and causal reasoning over hundreds of frames.
- **Sliding Window Attention (SWA):** (Short-term) Restricts attention to a fixed temporal window to efficiently model local motion patterns and short-term dynamics.
- **Dilated Sliding Window Attention (DSWA):** (Mid-term) Extends the receptive field beyond SWA by introducing a dilation factor along the temporal dimension, capturing mid-range dependencies while maintaining linear complexity.

**Theoretical Analysis:** The paper provides formal theoretical bounds to justify this design.
- **Necessity of Persistent Latent States (Theorem 1, Corollary 1):** It is formally proven that purely local temporal models are fundamentally insufficient for long-horizon prediction if the Bayes-optimal predictor relies on information outside a bounded recent window. Such models incur a strictly positive, irreducible excess risk, formalizing why local continuation heuristics fail to guarantee global state consistency over extended durations.
- **Sufficiency of Hybrid Multi-Scale Memory (Theorem 2, Corollary 2):** The factorization into short-range (SWA), mid-range (DSWA), and gated global (GLA) components is shown to be approximately sufficient. The theory demonstrates that if the Bayes predictor admits such a decomposition and the global memory branch (GLA) is contractive, error accumulation is strictly limited. This geometric damping ensures that one-step perturbations in global memory do not amplify over time, enabling consistent generation over extended horizons.

#### 3.3. Deployment-Aware System Co-Design

Kairos incorporates deployment-aware system co-design, treating real-time edge-side execution as a first-order modeling principle.

- **Timestep Distillation:** A pretrained 480P Embodied World Model is distilled into an efficient 4-step generator to reduce inference latency. This uses a hybrid objective combining **Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD)** to align the student's implicit distribution with the teacher's, and **Consistency Distillation (CM)** to enforce trajectory consistency, preserving fine-grained temporal and structural details.
- **Hardware-aware Inference Optimization:**
 - **Mixed-Parallel Inference Optimization:** Utilizes a customized hybrid parallelism scheme, combining Ulysses sequence parallelism for SWA, basic sequence parallelism for cross-attention, and modified tensor parallelism for GLA, along with VAE decoder segmentation, to manage memory and computation for long input sequences.
 - **DiT-Cache Optimization:** Reuses computation results correlated with time steps to reduce latency and GPU memory.
 - **Compilation and Kernel Fusion Optimization:** Employs `torch.compile` and dedicated fusion operators for graph optimization and performance improvement.
 - **Low-Precision Computing Optimization (FP8):** Applies INT8/INT4 quantization to attention layers and FP8 to PV MatMul, including techniques like Q/K mean subtraction and per-thread/per-warp quantization, to reduce memory and accelerate computation.
 - **Hardware-Aware Memory Optimization:** Implements Tiled Gated DeltaNet with Streaming Access to process extensive sequence dimensions in localized blocks, and Weight-Only INT4 Text Encoder Quantization to compress model weights and mitigate memory overhead on consumer-grade hardware.

#### 3.4. Data and Training Infrastructure

The data collection involves a hybrid strategy of open-source datasets (e.g., Koala-36M, AgiBotWorld-Beta) and large-scale in-house proprietary data (internet-crawled and real-world collected). A hierarchical taxonomy with millions of leaf nodes covers human, robot, general scenes, and physical phenomena. Unified shot segmentation is performed to create standardized clips (5-40 seconds).

A multi-stage data curation pipeline filters clips based on aesthetic, motion, AIGC, NSFW, blurriness, human motion, and OCR scores, followed by CLIP-based deduplication.

Tagging and captioning are performed using a comprehensive system of video attribute tags and video domain tags (Human, Robot, Physics, General). An end-to-end automatic annotation pipeline based on Qwen3-VL-8B generates tags. For captions, a Divide-and-Conquer (DC) strategy, combining structured tag information with visual content, is used, along with a multi-model ensemble annotation for high-quality data. Text enhancement includes "Physics-centric" captions (explaining underlying physical principles) and "Long-horizon Task" captions (decomposing tasks into sub-steps with causal logic).

An efficient data engineering infrastructure optimizes Shot Detection, Frame Filtering, and Captioning operators, achieving significant speedups (7.4x to 34.0x) through computational parallelization, I/O optimization, and task scheduling.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Kairos demonstrates competitive performance across various benchmarks while exhibiting high inference efficiency.

#### 4.1. Embodied World Model Benchmarks

- **WorldModelBench-robot:** Kairos-4B achieved a total score of 9.30, surpassing all baselines, with the highest Instruction Following score (2.36) and high Physics Adherence (4.96), including perfect scores in Newtonian mechanics, fluid dynamics, and gravity. It also demonstrated robust Common Sense reasoning.
- **DreamGen Bench:** Kairos-4B ranked first in Average Physical Adherence (0.538) and overall Average Score (0.618), with competitive Average Instruction Following (0.698), outperforming larger models despite having fewer parameters.
- **PAI-Bench-robot:** Among small-scale models (<10B), Kairos-4B achieved the best Domain Score (88.59) and Overall Score (82.57), matching or exceeding many large-scale (≥10B) models.
- **Human Evaluation:** Subjective evaluations across PAI-Bench, WorldModelBench, and DreamGen showed Kairos-4B achieving win rates of 60.2% against Cosmos-Predict2.5 14B and 49.1% against Lingbot-28B on PAI-Bench, and higher win rates against other baselines on WorldModelBench and DreamGen, indicating strong generation quality, physical plausibility, and instruction following.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Human-Centric Data Scaling:** Incorporating large-scale human-centric data improved Instruction Following (2.10 to 2.33) and total score (9.08 to 9.25) on WorldModelBench-Robot, indicating enhanced world-modeling capabilities and generalization.
- **Understanding Module:** Upgrading the VLM encoder from Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct to Qwen3.5-2B (which has stronger multimodal understanding) further improved instruction following (2.33 to 2.36) and total score (9.25 to 9.30).

#### 4.2. World Action Model Benchmarks

- **RoboTwin-2.0:** Kairos achieved an average success rate of 96.1, ranking among the top performers and demonstrating effective joint modeling of world dynamics and action evolution for complex bimanual manipulation.
- **LIBERO-Plus:** After fine-tuning, Kairos-joint achieved a state-of-the-art average score of 90.8, indicating strong generalization to perturbed evaluation settings and robustness across environmental variations. The non-joint Kairos achieved 89.0.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Embodied Human-centric Pretraining:** Human-centric pretraining yielded a significant 6.0 point gain on LIBERO-Plus, highlighting its effectiveness in transferring action-relevant knowledge.
- **Joint Training of Generation and Prediction:** Jointly optimizing video generation and action prediction improved performance by 23.2 points on LIBERO-Plus compared to training only ActionDiT, emphasizing the importance of world-modeling supervision.
- **Joint Denoising of Generation and Prediction:** Allowing action prediction to actively attend to video generation at inference time (Kairos-joint) further elevated performance from 89.0 to 90.8, suggesting benefits from explicit future imagination.

#### 4.3. General World Model Benchmarks

- **PAI-Bench (full set):** Kairos-4B achieved high performance across multiple sub-domains, particularly in background consistency, demonstrating robust stability in background scenes during video generation.
- **WorldModelBench (full set):** Kairos-4B achieved high scores in both TI2V and T2V settings, demonstrating competitive performance even against larger models, with strong object structural consistency and physically plausible interactions.
- **VideoPhy:** Kairos achieved the highest average score of 45.55, outperforming larger baseline models. This indicates strong physical reasoning capabilities and adherence to real-world physical laws across diverse physical interactions (solid–solid, solid–fluid, fluid–fluid).

#### 4.4. Long-horizon Generation

- **PAI-Bench-15s:** Under a 15-second generation setting, Kairos achieved the best overall score of 79.9, outperforming baselines which showed noticeable degradation. Kairos maintained strong performance in domain-level metrics (autonomous driving, robot, industry, human) and image-to-video consistency, indicating effective preservation of scene consistency and physical interactions over longer durations.

#### 4.5. Inference Efficiency

Kairos-4B demonstrated high inference efficiency.
- On an Nvidia A800, 480P (5s) generation latency was 11.7s on 1 GPU and 3.0s on 4 GPUs.
- Compared to other models (Lingbot-28B, Cosmos-Predict2.5-14B, Wan2.2-5B), Kairos-4B showed significantly lower memory usage (23.5GB vs. 46.1-70.2GB for larger models) and computational complexity (2.3 PFlops vs. 16.6-347.4 PFlops), resulting in much faster inference (9s on 4 GPUs for 720P, 5s video vs. 85s-1436s for baselines).
- Latency comparisons (Figure 3c) showed Kairos-4B consistently achieving lower latency across different resolutions and video durations, with orders-of-magnitude speedup over larger models and 2.5x to 3.7x speedup over similar-parameter competitors. Its scaling with increased workload was linear, in contrast to the exponential growth observed in other models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Kairos represents a step towards developing operational infrastructure for Physical AI by integrating world representation and robotic control into a unified framework. Its native pre-training paradigm, which leverages a Cross-Embodiment Data Curriculum, addresses the challenge of fragmented world knowledge by systematically progressing from open-world observations to human behavior and robot-specific interactions. The Native Understanding-Generation-Prediction Architecture with Hybrid Linear Temporal Attention is designed to maintain consistent world states over long horizons, supported by theoretical bounds on error accumulation in its global memory mechanism.

The Deployment-Aware System Co-Design, including timestep distillation and hardware-aware optimizations, facilitates efficient, low-latency inference on both server and consumer-grade hardware. This efficiency is critical for enabling real-time observation-action-feedback loops necessary for continuous adaptation and self-evolution in physical systems.

The experimental results demonstrate that Kairos achieves competitive performance across embodied world model, world action model, and general world model benchmarks, including state-of-the-art results on several challenging tasks like LIBERO-plus and RoboTwin 2.0, while also showing significant efficiency advantages. These capabilities position Kairos as a robust foundation for future physical agents.

Future work outlined by the authors includes advancing towards autonomous self-evolution through recursive imagination, enabling the model to continually interact with environments, evaluate policy execution, and refine its internal physics simulator. Another area is scaling Kairos to become a generalist embodied substrate, expanding its action space to diverse hardware embodiments (e.g., humanoids, multi-finger manipulators) and leveraging heterogeneous pre-training for zero-shot complex intent recognition and high-success-rate execution in unconstrained physical domains. The development of Kairos aims to transform world models into evolvable operational substrates capable of continuous learning and adaptation in the physical world.
