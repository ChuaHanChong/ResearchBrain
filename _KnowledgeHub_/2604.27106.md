# Reconstruction by Generation: 3D Multi-Object Scene Reconstruction from Sparse Observations

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Amsterdam and Toyota Motor Europe developed RecGen, a generative framework that jointly estimates 3D multi-object and part shapes along with 6-DoF poses from sparse RGB-D observations. This method significantly improves reconstruction quality, pose accuracy, and robustness to occlusions, while also enabling consistent texture generation for symmetric objects.

## Problem

- Existing 3D reconstruction methods often separate shape generation and pose estimation, leading to compounded errors, especially under occlusion and for symmetric objects.
- Many models struggle with robustly completing occluded or unobserved object regions and lack the capability for fine-grained part-level shape and pose estimation for articulated objects.
- Current generative models typically lack effective multimodal (RGB-D) and multi-view conditioning, limiting their robustness to sensor noise and ability to integrate complementary observations.

## Method

- Introduces **RecGen**, a generative framework employing rectified flow models and a ==transformer==-based architecture for joint probabilistic estimation of object and part shapes and their 6-DoF poses.
- Utilizes multimodal (RGB-D) and multi-view conditioning through features derived from DINOv2, camera-invariant pointmaps from depth, and object masks to provide context and structural cues.
- Implements pose-conditioned appearance generation by explicitly encoding the predicted pose and concatenating it with input features, crucial for view-consistent texturing of symmetric objects.

## Results

- RecGen consistently outperformed baselines, achieving an average normalized Chamfer Distance of 0.033 for shape and **92.7%** ADD-SB@0.1 for pose on object-centric benchmarks, with multi-view inputs further enhancing performance.
- Demonstrated superior robustness to occlusions and substantial improvements in part-level reconstruction, reducing CD_norm by half and improving ADD-SB@0.05 by +**38.2** percentage points on the ArtVIP dataset compared to SAM3D.
- Achieved **74%** texture orientation alignment for symmetric objects via pose-conditioned appearance generation (compared to **41%** for SAM3D) and showed improved inference efficiency (1.8 times faster, 1.6 times less GPU memory than SAM3D).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly modeling shape and 6-DoF pose probabilistically in a unified generative framework enhances geometric consistency and robustness to occlusions by directly inferring transformations in the camera frame.
> - Explicitly conditioning appearance generation on the estimated object pose resolves ambiguities for symmetric objects, leading to superior texture alignment and view-consistent appearance.
> - Training on a large-scale synthetic dataset with natural occlusions, realistically noisy depth, and part-level supervision enables robust generalization to real-world scenarios and accurate fine-grained articulated object reconstruction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the challenge of reconstructing complex, full multi-object 3D scenes from sparse observations, which is a fundamental problem in computer vision with implications for robotics and embodied AI systems. The broader landscape is characterized by increasing reliance on high-fidelity digital twins for training and evaluating AI systems. However, creating such digital twins traditionally involves labor-intensive manual scanning and registration.

Existing approaches to 3D reconstruction and pose estimation fall into several categories:
- **Generative 3D models**: These models can reconstruct real-world objects from sparse observations, often from images. However, many treat shape generation, completion, and pose estimation as separate, sequential stages. This modularity can lead to error accumulation and reduced robustness, particularly under occlusions.
- **Model-free pose estimation methods**: These leverage generated 3D shapes to perform pose registration against input images or depth maps.
- **Joint shape-pose reconstruction**: Recent efforts aim to predict both shape and 6-DoF (degrees of freedom) pose simultaneously. These are broadly categorized into:
 - **Modular pipelines**: Combine an image-to-3D reconstructor with a separate pose alignment stage. Examples include GigaPose, Pos3R, and OmniShape. While flexible, they may suffer from decoupled geometry and pose reasoning.
 - **Unified feed-forward methods**: Predict both shape and pose in a single pass, such as CenterSnap and ShAPO. More recent scene-level generative methods like SAM3D also jointly model instance geometry and spatial arrangement.

Current limitations in the field that RecGen aims to address include:
- **Brittle pose registration**: Existing generative models often require post-hoc alignment of predicted shapes, which is challenging in cluttered scenes or for symmetric/weakly textured objects.
- **Incomplete object completion**: Models often struggle to infer occluded or unobserved regions due to training on occlusion-free objects or masked images.
- **Ambiguity with symmetric objects**: 6-DoF pose estimation and texture reconstruction for symmetric objects are inherently ambiguous, leading to inconsistent textures if pose is not explicitly considered.
- **Monolithic object representation**: Most methods reconstruct objects as single meshes, lacking recovery of internal part structures, which is crucial for tasks like articulated object manipulation.
- **RGB-centricity**: Many generative models primarily use RGB input, only leveraging depth in later alignment stages, contributing to multi-step error-prone pipelines. While some, like SAM3D, use depth, they can be sensitive to sensor noise.
- **Lack of multi-view conditioning**: Most current generative models do not inherently support multi-view inputs, despite their practical relevance in real-world scenarios.

RecGen positions itself as a unified generative framework that addresses these limitations by jointly inferring object geometry and 6-DoF pose probabilistically, supporting part-level estimation, and integrating multi-view and multimodal (RGB-D) conditioning. This is particularly relevant for real-to-sim pipelines in robotics, which increasingly demand accurate, physically plausible, and multi-view scene models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a generative framework for robust and scalable 3D multi-object scene reconstruction from sparse observations, specifically single or multiple RGB-D images. This aims to facilitate the creation of high-fidelity digital twins for embodied AI systems, circumventing the need for labor-intensive manual scanning and registration.

The motivation for RecGen stems from several limitations observed in existing 3D reconstruction methods:

- **Joint Estimation of Shape and Pose**: Current approaches often separate shape generation and pose estimation, leading to compounded errors and reduced robustness, especially under occlusion. RecGen aims to overcome this by performing joint probabilistic estimation of shape and pose directly in the camera frame, ensuring geometric consistency.
- **Robust Object Completion under Occlusion**: Many generative models are trained on datasets with fully visible or pre-masked objects, making them perform poorly in real-world scenarios with heavy occlusions. The motivation is to learn priors that support robust shape completion by training on a large-scale synthetic dataset of occluded objects and parts, utilizing object masks as positional signals for context.
- **Handling Object Symmetries and View-Consistent Texturing**: Symmetric objects present inherent ambiguities in 6-DoF pose estimation and texture reconstruction. Texturing networks often produce inconsistent or misaligned textures without explicit pose conditioning. RecGen seeks to address this by explicitly conditioning texture reconstruction on the estimated object pose, enabling view-consistent and semantically aligned texturing.
- **Part-Level Reconstruction**: Existing methods typically reconstruct objects as monolithic meshes, neglecting their internal part structure. This limits their utility for tasks like articulated object manipulation. A key objective is to support part-level shape and pose estimation by unifying scene decomposition into objects and objects into parts within a single framework.
- **Effective Use of Depth Information and Robustness to Sensor Noise**: Many generative models are RGB-centric, using depth only in post-processing or alignment stages. Methods that do use depth can be sensitive to commodity sensor noise. RecGen aims to directly leverage 3D structural cues from depth, making the model robust to imperfect measurements by training on realistically estimated depth rather than perfect rendered depth.
- **Support for Multi-View Conditioning**: Real-world setups often provide multiple cameras, but current generative models largely lack multi-view conditioning. RecGen aims to explicitly support both single-view and multi-view inputs within a unified framework, allowing the model to integrate complementary observations, reduce ambiguities, and improve consistency and accuracy.

In summary, RecGen is motivated by the need for a unified, robust, and generalizable generative framework that can accurately reconstruct complex, occluded, and multi-part scenes from sparse, noisy RGB-D observations, suitable for scalable real-to-sim applications in robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RecGen is a generative framework designed for probabilistic joint estimation of object and part shapes, as well as their pose, from single or multiple RGB-D images. The framework employs rectified flow models and a transformer-based architecture to capture complex data distributions efficiently.

#### 3.1. Reconstruction by Generation Framework

The framework comprises two main stages:

#### 3.2. Object Structure and Pose Generation

- **Joint Generation**: This stage jointly generates a sparse object structure (represented as active voxels from a discrete occupancy grid) and its 6-DoF pose, parameterized by rotation (R), translation (t), and isotropic scale (s), forming a similarity transformation T ∈ Sim(3).
- **Latent Space Encoding**: To manage computational complexity, the dense binary occupancy grid (O ∈ {0, 1}^64×64×64) is encoded into a lower-resolution continuous feature grid (S ∈ R^16×16×16×8) using a 3D convolutional VAE. This provides a smooth latent space suitable for rectified flow training.
- **Flow Transformer (G_SP)**: A transformer-based generator, G_SP, is trained to jointly denoise S and T from noisy inputs. Pose parameters are concatenated with structure features as an additional token. The generator predicts velocity fields for S and T, updated via Euler integration.
- **Conditioning**: Multimodal features condition the transformer via cross-attention. These features are derived from DINOv2 image features, as well as point map and object mask features.
- **Pose Parameterization and Normalization**: Pose representations are chosen to avoid discontinuities (6D continuous representation for rotation). All pose components (rotation, translation, scale) are z-score normalized over the training set to ensure zero mean and unit variance, preventing any single component from dominating the flow matching objective. Denormalization occurs during inference.
- **Dynamic Cropping and Mask Conditioning**: Instead of full or strictly foreground images, dynamic cropping is applied around the object, allowing 20% to 100% padding. The binary object mask is encoded via a convolutional layer and added to image features, providing context for occlusions and scene layout.
- **Pointmap Conditioning**: To directly leverage depth cues, a camera-invariant pointmap (P ∈ R^d×d×3) is created from the depth map and camera intrinsics. This pointmap, filtered by the object mask and normalized (scaling object into^3 space), is encoded through a learnable layer and added to image features, enhancing pose accuracy and shape consistency.

#### 3.3. High-Fidelity Asset Recovery

- **Latent Generation (G_L)**: The second stage generates local latents {z_i} conditioned on the sparse structure and predicted pose from Stage 1. A sparse transformer, G_L, is used, packing latents within 2x2x2 spatial neighborhoods using sparse convolutions for efficiency.
- **Pose Conditioning for Appearance**: The predicted pose T from Stage 1 is encoded and concatenated with image, mask, and pointmap features. This explicit pose conditioning is critical for symmetric objects with view-dependent appearance (e.g., cylindrical containers with labels), ensuring texture consistency with the object's orientation.
- **Decoding Structured Latents**: The resulting structured latents are decoded by a mesh decoder (D_M) using FlexiCubes for geometry extraction and a Gaussian Splatting (GS) decoder (D_GS) for appearance. A textured mesh is obtained by rendering the GS representation from multiple viewpoints and baking the images onto the mesh.

#### 3.4. Training and Losses

- Both G_SP and G_L are trained independently using the Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) objective.
- For G_SP, the total loss combines CFM for structure (S) and pose (T) with a weighting factor (α=0.01) for pose prediction: L_total = L_CFM(S) + α · L_CFM(T̃).
- Synthetic datasets with ground-truth shapes and poses are used for supervision, ensuring consistency across stages.
- Classifier-free guidance (CFG) is employed during training (drop rate of 0.1) and inference (scale of 3.0). Training uses AdamW optimizer for 55K iterations on 64 GPUs.

#### 3.5. Extension to Multiple Views

- RecGen supports multi-view conditioning by taking an optional second RGB image, pointmap, and mask tuple, along with its corresponding pose.
- Per-view conditioning features are concatenated, and a learnable frame token embedding is added to each view's patches to distinguish their origin.
- Each pose output token receives a learnable view ID embedding.
- During training, the second view is dropped with a probability of 0.33 to retain single-view inference capability.

#### 3.6. RecGen Dataset

- The training dataset leverages 198K high-quality 3D assets from 6 public datasets: Objaverse-XL, ABO, HSSD (for objects), and PhysXNet, PartNext, PartNet-Mobility (for parts).
- Compositional scenes are created with object-based datasets, placing distractor objects for natural occlusions. Part-based scenes typically feature single, self-occluded objects.
- Scenes are rendered into 20 images with random camera poses, totaling 3.2M synthetically generated RGB images, depth maps, segmentation masks, GT poses, and stereo depth maps. Training for appearance generation excludes lower-quality texture datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RecGen was evaluated against baselines for model-free pose estimation (Any6D), scene completion (SceneComplete), and 3D reconstruction from single images (SAM3D) on various object-centric (LM-O, HB, HOPE, ReOcS) and part-centric (ArtVIP) datasets.

#### 4.1. Pose and Shape Estimation

- **Overall Performance**: RecGen consistently outperformed all baselines across all metrics and datasets.
 - On object-centric benchmarks, RecGen (1-view) achieved an average normalized Chamfer Distance (CD_norm) of 0.033, compared to 0.039 for SAM3D and 0.076 for Any6D. The 2-view variant further improved shape generation (CD_norm of 0.034 for objects, 0.024 for parts).
 - For pose estimation, RecGen (1-view) reached 92.7% ADD-SB@0.1 and 71.1% ADD-SB@0.05 on average, significantly surpassing SAM3D's average of 84.2% / 38.6%. The 2-view variant improved average performance to 73.6% ADD-SB@0.05.
- **Robustness to Occlusions**: RecGen demonstrated higher robustness to occlusions. The performance gap between RecGen and SAM3D widened with increased occlusion severity. On object-based datasets, the ADD-SB difference grew from 0.044 vs. 0.053 at 0–3% occlusion to 0.073 vs. 0.116 at 40–70% occlusion (a 37% relative improvement for RecGen).
- **Part-Level Reconstruction**: RecGen showed significant improvements in reconstructing and localizing articulated object parts. On the ArtVIP part-level benchmark, RecGen (1-view) reduced CD_norm by half compared to SAM3D (0.056 → 0.026) and improved ADD-SB@0.05 by +38.2 percentage points (45.8% → 84.0%).
- **Diameter Relative Error (DRE)**: RecGen showed substantial improvements in the DRE metric, indicating its ability to accurately predict full object scale even for heavily occluded samples.
- **Per-Object Analysis**: On the HB dataset, RecGen achieved lower ADD-SB on 29 out of 33 objects and lower Chamfer distance on 26 out of 33 objects, indicating consistent improvement across various object instances.

#### 4.2. Posed Appearance Generation

- **Perceptual Quality**: Before ICP alignment, RecGen generally outperformed other baselines in perceptual metrics (LPIPS, SSIM, PSNR). After ICP refinement, RecGen and SAM3D performed comparably.
- **Symmetric Objects**: RecGen's pose-conditioned appearance generation demonstrated superior performance for symmetric objects. A VLM-based evaluation using GPT-5 for texture orientation alignment showed RecGen achieved an overall alignment rate of 74% compared to 41% for SAM3D, attributing this to its pose-conditioned formulation that aligns textures with input views.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Stereo Noise Augmentation**: Removing stereo noise augmentation significantly degraded object-centric metrics (CD_norm and ADD-SB), highlighting its importance for robustness to real-world sensor noise.
- **Pose Normalization**: Disabling pose normalization negatively impacted pose estimation, confirming its role in simplifying the learning task and stabilizing joint optimization.
- **Part-Centric Training**: Training without part-level data maintained performance on object-centric benchmarks but led to a substantial degradation on the ArtVIP part-centric dataset, demonstrating the necessity of part supervision for articulated structures without compromising object-level performance.
- **Pretraining**: Initializing weights from a pretrained network improved all metrics, indicating a strong geometric prior.

#### 4.4. Inference Efficiency

- RecGen demonstrated improved computational efficiency compared to SAM3D. It was measured to be 1.8 times faster and required 1.6 times less GPU memory on a single NVIDIA A100-SXM4-80GB GPU.

#### 4.5. Inference Optimizations

- **Multi-view Pose Selection**: Utilizing pointmap-based pose selection strategies (single-view or cross-view alignment) for the two candidate poses predicted in the multi-view setting significantly improved reconstruction quality over simply using the first pose. Cross-view alignment achieved the best average performance.
- **Multi-sample Generation Evaluation**: Selecting the best reconstruction from multiple stochastic generations using pointmap alignment also improved results, indicating that the model produces diverse and sometimes better hypotheses from partial information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of RecGen represents a step in 3D multi-object scene reconstruction, with significant implications for computer vision and, in particular, for the field of robotics and embodied AI.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Unified Generative Framework**: RecGen provides a unified approach that jointly estimates object shape, part shapes, and 6-DoF poses. This integration addresses the limitations of multi-stage pipelines that often suffer from error propagation and reduced robustness.
- **Robustness and Generalization**: The framework demonstrates robustness to various real-world challenges, including heavy occlusions, symmetric objects, and noisy depth observations from different sensor types. Its ability to generalize to real-world data despite being trained exclusively on synthetic data is also a key achievement.
- **Part-Level Understanding**: By explicitly supporting part-level shape and pose estimation, RecGen moves beyond monolithic object representations. This is crucial for tasks requiring fine-grained interaction, such as articulated object manipulation.
- **Effective Multimodal and Multi-view Integration**: The method effectively leverages both RGB and depth information and extends to multi-view conditioning, which is a practical necessity in many robotic setups where multiple cameras are available. This reduces reconstruction ambiguities and improves geometric consistency.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: RecGen sets a new state of the art, outperforming previous methods like SAM3D significantly in geometric shape quality, texture reconstruction fidelity (especially for symmetric objects due to pose-conditioned appearance generation), and pose estimation accuracy, while using substantially less training data.
- **Efficiency**: The improved inference efficiency compared to existing methods makes RecGen more practical for applications with computational constraints.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Robotics and Embodied AI**: RecGen's capabilities directly contribute to the creation of high-fidelity digital twins of real-world environments. This can profoundly impact robot learning by providing more accurate and scalable simulation assets for training and evaluating embodied AI systems, especially for complex tasks involving articulated objects and cluttered scenes. It can serve as a foundation for advancing real-to-sim reconstruction pipelines, enabling better policy learning and evaluation.
- **Scalable Digital Twin Generation**: By automating the reconstruction of complex scenes from sparse observations, RecGen reduces the manual effort and cost associated with generating detailed 3D models. This scalability can accelerate research and development in areas reliant on virtual environments.
- **Advanced Scene Understanding**: The ability to reconstruct occluded geometry and part structures from limited views enhances scene understanding for various applications beyond robotics, such as augmented reality, virtual reality, and mixed reality, where accurate digital replicas of physical objects are essential.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The framework's design, including its use of rectified flow, transformer architectures, and specific conditioning strategies, provides a robust base model for further extensions. Future work could include generating physical properties (mass, friction), modeling dynamic scenes with object motion trajectories, or integrating end-to-end segmentation and reconstruction for full autonomy. Addressing current limitations like inference speed for real-time applications also presents an avenue for continued development.

In summary, RecGen provides a significant step towards practical and robust 3D scene reconstruction, particularly beneficial for scalable real-to-sim pipelines in robotics, by delivering high-fidelity digital twins with accurate geometry, pose, and appearance at both object and part levels.
