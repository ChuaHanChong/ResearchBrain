# EVE: A Generator-Verifier System for Generative Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> The EVE framework enhances pretrained generative robot policies by integrating zero-shot Vision-Language Model (VLM) based verifiers with a guided diffusion action incorporator. This system improves task success rates by up to 2.4% across diverse manipulation tasks without requiring any additional policy training or finetuning.

## Problem

- Generative robot policies based on architectures like diffusion models degrade under distribution shifts and exhibit limited recovery capabilities when encountering out-of-distribution states.
- Traditional methods to improve policy performance, such as finetuning or retraining, are resource-intensive, time-consuming, and can compromise the generalist capabilities of the policies.
- Collecting extensive real-world robotics data for retraining is costly and labor-intensive, exacerbating the problem of policy robustness.

## Method

- **EVE** introduces a unified framework that augments frozen, pretrained generative policies with modular, ==zero-shot VLM-based verifier agents== at inference time.
- It employs an ==ensemble of verifiers== (both generator-agnostic and generator-conditioned) whose outputs are aggregated and transformed into a common action trajectory representation.
- A novel ==guided diffusion-based action incorporator== fuses the aggregated verifier feedback with the base policy's action predictions, steering actions towards verifier-approved behaviors while maintaining stability.

## Results

- **EVE** consistently improved task success rates, showing gains of up to **+2.40%** for the 'MOVE NEAR' task and **+2.18%** for 'SetTable-OpenFridge' across both diffusion and flow-matching policies.
- Verifier performance scaled positively with VLM model size, with the **72B Qwen-2.5-VL** model outperforming smaller **7B** and **32B** variants.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of guided diffusion for action incorporation and identified optimal intervention thresholds (e.g., **MMD of 0.48**) and guidance ratios (**10.0**) for balancing policy stability and verifier influence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Zero-shot, VLM-based verifiers can effectively provide semantically grounded feedback to improve low-level control policies in embodied AI without requiring specific training.
> - Ensembles of complementary verifiers, such as primitive steerers and pivot steerers, generally yield higher performance gains than individual verifiers, enhancing robustness.
> - Guided diffusion is a crucial mechanism for stably incorporating verifier feedback, as it biases action synthesis towards verifier-consistent behaviors while ensuring actions remain coherent with the base policy's learned distribution, outperforming direct overrides or averaging.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge in visuomotor policies for robotics, particularly those based on generative models such as diffusion and flow-matching architectures. While these policies have demonstrated performance in diverse robot manipulation tasks, they exhibit limitations in robustness, often degrading under distribution shifts and showing restricted recovery capabilities when encountering out-of-distribution states during deployment. Traditional methods to enhance their performance or robustness, such as finetuning or retraining with additional in-domain data, are typically resource-intensive and expensive due to the cost of collecting high-quality real-world robotics data. The effectiveness of such finetuning is also contingent on the scale and quality of the supplementary data.

Concurrently, advancements in language modeling have introduced methods that scale test-time computation to improve the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). These approaches often involve generating multiple candidate solutions and then using foundation models, acting as zero-shot verification modules, to score and refine these candidates. This paradigm has significantly boosted LLM performance without requiring further model training.

The current research proposes to bridge this gap by applying a similar generation-verification framework to the embodied domain. While some prior work has explored leveraging verifiers for embodied tasks, these often necessitate training a verifier module or latent dynamics models from scratch (e.g., Robomonkey, HAVE). Such approaches can limit generalization due to their reliance on in-domain training data. Furthermore, integrating generator-verifier systems in robotics presents unique challenges compared to language modeling, including managing continuous robot action spaces, the non-trivial selection over multiple continuous action trajectories, and effectively combining diverse feedback from various verifiers with base policy action predictions. This work aims to develop a system that leverages zero-shot vision-language models (VLMs) as verifiers to enhance pretrained generative visuomotor policies without requiring additional training, thereby extending the benefits of test-time compute scaling to embodied artificial intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance and robustness of existing *pretrained* generative visuomotor policies at *test time* without incurring additional training or finetuning costs. The authors hypothesize that integrating zero-shot, VLM-based verifiers within a generation-verification framework can provide similar benefits to embodied policies as observed in language modeling, where test-time compute scaling has significantly improved LLM reasoning capabilities.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in deploying generative policies in real-world robotic scenarios:
- **Limited Robustness to Distribution Shifts:** Generative visuomotor policies, despite their capabilities, often struggle with slight deviations in operating conditions (e.g., changes in tabletop height) and lack strong recovery mechanisms when unexpected out-of-distribution states occur during task execution.
- **High Cost of Finetuning:** Improving policy robustness traditionally involves collecting additional in-domain data or recovery sequences, which is a laborious and expensive process. The performance of finetuned policies is also highly dependent on the quality and scale of this costly data.
- **Adaptation of LLM Verification to Robotics:** The success of generation-verification frameworks in LLMs, which use external verifiers to refine candidate solutions, motivates exploring a similar paradigm for robotics. However, direct application is complicated by fundamental differences:
 - **Modality Mismatch and Information Interface:** In language modeling, both generator and verifier typically operate within a shared modality (text). Embodied settings, however, involve continuous robot actions, making it challenging to effectively communicate action information between a continuous action generator and a VLM-based verifier.
 - **Continuous Action Trajectory Selection:** LLM systems often use "best-of-N" sampling to select the most suitable candidate from multiple generated solutions. For continuous robot action trajectories, selecting the "best" from a diverse set, especially when verifiers might provide feedback in different formats (e.g., selecting trajectories vs. suggesting primitives), is non-trivial.
 - **Fusion of Diverse Feedback:** It is unclear how to optimally combine aggregated feedback from multiple verifiers with the base policy's action predictions. Naive methods like direct action averaging or outright verifier action overrides may not be effective or preserve the learned policy prior.

To address these motivations and challenges, the research proposes EVE, a modular system designed to:
1. Introduce a generator-verifier system specifically tailored for embodied policies, where verifiers can operate with distinct input modalities, capabilities, and action spaces.
2. Develop an "action incorporator" module that uses guided diffusion to effectively fuse aggregated verifier outputs with the base policy's action predictions, ensuring coherence with the original policy prior while incorporating corrective signals.
3. Empirically demonstrate that zero-shot EVE ensembles can outperform state-of-the-art embodied verifier baselines that require substantial in-domain training data, across various simulated and real robotic tasks and embodiments.
4. Provide a detailed analysis of EVE's ability to recover failed rollouts, quantify the benefits of verifier ensembles over individual verifiers, and offer practical guidelines for designing effective generator-verifier systems through extensive ablations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, EVE (Embodied Verifier Ensembles), is a unified framework that augments pre-trained generative policies with modular verifier agents. It operates at inference time without requiring any additional training of the base policy or verifiers.

#### 3.1. Base Policy Candidate Generation

At each timestep *t*, given a task instruction (*x*), observation (*o_t*), proprioceptive state (*s_t*), and a frozen base policy (*π_θ*), EVE generates a set of *K* candidate action sequences, *a_gen* = {*a^(k)_t*} from the base policy. For a diffusion policy, these candidates are action sequences denoised from *K* independent noise samples, representing diverse plausible trajectories. For flow-matching policies, actions are generated by integrating a learned velocity field.

#### 3.2. Verifier Agents

EVE utilizes a collection of *J* verifier modules, *V* = {*V_j*}, each designed with a specific capability to provide feedback. Each verifier *V_j* takes an encoding of the input context and candidate actions, *Φ_j*(*o_t*, *s_t*, *a_gen*), and produces a structured message *m_j* in an output space *M_j*. Verifiers are categorized based on their access to generator policy information:

- **Generator-Agnostic Verifiers:** These verifiers operate solely on robot observations (*o_t*, *s_t*) and do not directly use action information from the generator policy (*a_gen* = ∅). They are conditioned on the task instruction and select action sequences from a predefined list of "action primitives" (e.g., "Nudge Left", "Gripper Open"). This category is instantiated by the **Primitive steerer**. For ManiSkill-HAB, it identifies goal locations and suggests primitives. For SimplerEnv, it reasons purely from language instructions.

- **Generator-Conditioned Verifiers:** These verifiers utilize action information from the generator policy (*a_gen*) in addition to robot observations. They take a representation of candidate action sequences as input, typically by visually overlaying prospective trajectories onto the observation image. This category is instantiated by the **Pivot steerer**, which takes *K*=40 candidate trajectories, selects *K_pivot*=5 visually distinct ones (based on cosine similarity of pixel paths), and asks a VLM to select the best trajectory for task completion.

#### 3.3. Verifier Output Aggregation

The outputs *m_j* from all verifiers are transformed into a common action trajectory representation. For generator-agnostic verifiers, selected primitives are mapped to corresponding trajectory sequences. For generator-conditioned verifiers, the selected candidate trajectory is used directly. An aggregation operator *A* then fuses these individual verifier outputs into a single, combined trajectory, *˜m*, through a weighted interpolation process.

#### 3.4. Action Incorporator

The action incorporator (*I*) fuses the base policy's output with the aggregated verifier trajectory *˜m*. Instead of simple averaging or overriding, it leverages a guided denoising process that steers the base policy's action generation:

- **Guided Diffusion (GD) for Diffusion Policies:** For diffusion-based policies, action synthesis is directed by an objective function *ξ*(*τ*, *z*) that quantifies the alignment between the generated trajectory *τ* and the verifier-derived feedback *z* (which is *˜m*). At each reverse diffusion step, a guidance coefficient (*β_k*) controls the influence of the alignment gradient, *∇a_k_t ξ*(*a_k_t*, *z*). This gradient, calculated as the L2-norm discrepancy (*a_k_t* - *z*), biases the denoising process towards verifier-consistent actions while preserving the original policy's learned conditional distribution, *p*(*a_t* | *o_t*, *s_t*).

- **Guided Inference for Flow Policies:** For flow-matching policies, EVE adapts the guided inference scheme from prior work. It computes an estimated terminal action from the current flow state and policy velocity, then defines a guidance objective to minimize the squared error between this estimate and the reference action *˜m*. The velocity prediction is adjusted using a guidance scale, and Euler integration is performed.

#### 3.5. Intervention Detection

To manage computational expense, verifier feedback is not computed at every timestep. Instead, EVE employs an off-the-shelf failure detector based on the **Maximum Mean Discrepancy (MMD)** metric. MMD quantifies the divergence between the distribution of action trajectories predicted at the current timestep and those predicted for the same future window at a later timestep. If the cumulative MMD in the trajectory exceeds a predefined threshold (*τ*), it indicates a potential distribution shift or erratic behavior, triggering an EVE intervention.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

- **VLM Backbone:** Qwen-2.5-VL-72B is used for all verifiers in simulation experiments. Qwen3-VL-8B is used for real robot experiments.
- **Base Policies:** Experiments utilize π0 for SimplerEnv, a standard diffusion policy (DP) for ManiSkill-HAB, and π0.5 for RoboTwin and real-world Franka manipulation.
- **Inference Engine:** vLLM is used for efficient VLM inference, supporting optimized KV cache management and high throughput via batching.
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluations are conducted on SimplerEnv (7 tasks, 2 embodiments), ManiSkill-HAB (long-horizon mobile manipulation), and RoboTwin-2.0 (bimanual arm tasks), as well as on a real-world Franka Emika Panda arm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding EVE's performance and design across diverse robotic tasks and environments.

#### 4.1. Performance on SimplerEnv

EVE demonstrated improved task success rates compared to both the unsteered base policy and existing verifier-based baselines that require extensive training.
- **Overall Performance:** EVE-Ensemble achieved a total average success rate of 72.2%, surpassing the base π0 policy (67.1%), RoboMonkey (68.1%), and V-GPS (27.3%). This performance was observed across both WidowX and Google Robot embodiments, with 76.2% and 66.9% average success rates, respectively.
- **Outperformance of Trained Baselines:** EVE utilized zero-shot, training-free verifiers, contrasting with RoboMonkey, which was finetuned with 20M synthetic action preferences, and V-GPS, trained with 175K demonstrations. Despite this, EVE variants consistently achieved higher average performance. The authors hypothesize that EVE's action chunk-level feedback contributes to this, providing better temporal coherence than per-step scoring used by trained verifiers.
- **Benefit of Ensembling:** Both individual EVE-Pivot (69.8%) and EVE-Primitive (68.0%) configurations improved over the base policy. However, the EVE-Ensemble consistently yielded the strongest performance, indicating complementary feedback from the distinct verifier types.
- **Impact on Task Difficulty:** Significant performance gains were observed in harder, lower-success tasks (e.g., Put Apple in Drawer: 29.9% → 40.3%; Stack Blocks: 56.2% → 63.2%). Improvements were modest on near-saturated tasks, suggesting limited remaining performance headroom.

#### 4.2. Performance on Maniskill-HAB and RoboTwin

EVE demonstrated generalization to new embodiments and longer-horizon, complex manipulation tasks.
- **Maniskill-HAB:** EVE-Ensemble consistently steered a base diffusion policy towards higher success rates on mobile manipulation tasks. The largest improvements were noted in tasks like SetTable-OpenFridge (+2.18%) and SetTable-Place (+1.78%), indicating effectiveness in scenarios requiring recovery from subtle execution degradations.
- **RoboTwin-2.0:** EVE improved performance on bimanual manipulation tasks on the RoboTwin 2.0 benchmark using the π0.5 VLA policy. For example, Beat Block Hammer saw a +3.82% improvement. This finding is notable as other trained verifier baselines were not applicable in a zero-shot manner to these tasks and embodiments.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis: How EVE recovers failed trajectories

Case studies illustrated EVE's recovery capabilities:
- **Complementary Verifier Capabilities:** In a "Place Apple in Drawer" task, the base policy failed to open the drawer sufficiently. EVE's Primitive steerer proposed a "Nudge Left" action to guide the gripper, while the Pivot steerer selected optimal outward-pulling trajectories. This combined feedback enabled full drawer opening and successful apple placement.
- **Robustness via Ensembling:** In a "Move Near" task, the base policy failed due to a faulty grasp. The Primitive steerer proposed a "Nudge Left" correction. Although the Pivot steerer incorrectly selected a sub-optimal trajectory, the aggregated ensemble feedback provided a sufficient leftward correction, leading to a stable grasp and successful task completion. This indicated EVE's robustness to imperfect individual verifier feedback.

#### 4.4. Ablating Individual Components within EVE

Extensive ablations provided insights into EVE's design choices:
- **Verifier Model Scaling:** Larger VLM backbones (e.g., Qwen-2.5-VL-72B) correlated with improved task success rates, aligning with trends in language modeling.
- **Action Incorporator Design:** Guided Diffusion significantly outperformed naive "Verifier Override" (which led to performance reduction) and "Averaged" approaches, suggesting that it effectively integrates corrective feedback while maintaining fidelity to the base policy's action distribution.
- **Guidance Ratio:** The diffusion guidance coefficient showed an optimal value (e.g., 10), with performance dropping at higher values. This indicates that excessive guidance can push denoising too far from the base policy's distribution, causing temporal inconsistencies.
- **Verifier Image Resolution:** Using higher visual resolution images (e.g., 512p) enhanced performance, particularly for contact-rich tasks.
- **Pivot Trajectories:** An optimal number of candidate trajectories for the Pivot steerer (e.g., 5) was found; increasing it further led to performance degradation, possibly due to VLM difficulty in discerning between too many options.
- **Primitive Frames:** Increasing the number of history frames for the Primitive steerer did not significantly impact performance, suggesting that for certain tasks, a single current observation might be sufficient for primitive selection.

#### 4.5. Latency Analysis

- EVE with a smaller, newer VLM backbone (Qwen3-VL-8B) still outperformed RoboMonkey on SimplerEnv, indicating that EVE's benefits scale with VLM strength.
- Despite higher per-step VLM inference latency, EVE achieved comparable average rollout times to RoboMonkey. This is attributed to EVE's MMD-based intervention trigger, which selectively invokes verifiers only when necessary, and its generation of feedback at the action chunk level, reducing intervention frequency.
- System scalability experiments showed a near-linear reduction in total evaluation time when scaling the number of parallel inference servers.

#### 4.6. Real Robot Experiments

EVE was validated on a real Franka Emika Panda arm for block placement tasks.
- EVE improved performance of the π0.5 VLA policy on in-distribution tasks and achieved the best performance across out-of-distribution (OOD-Prompt and OOD-Object) tasks.
- RoboMonkey showed degraded performance in OOD-Prompt settings, indicating limited zero-shot applicability to new scenarios. EVE's semantically-informed VLM-guided corrections proved effective in these generalization tests.

#### 4.7. Further Analysis and Limitations

Sankey plots illustrated that EVE consistently redirected catastrophic failures (e.g., "Place-in-goal failure", "Excessive Collision") towards successful outcomes. However, not all tasks benefited uniformly; for example, PrepareGroceries-Pick saw minor performance drops, potentially because feedback from verifiers was limited for objects inside refrigerators. This suggests future work could involve incorporating additional feedback signals like progress estimation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces EVE, a generator-verifier system that contributes to the field of embodied artificial intelligence by enhancing the robustness and performance of visuomotor policies. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Improving Generative Policy Robustness without Retraining:** EVE directly addresses the critical challenge of generative policies degrading under distribution shifts and lacking recovery capabilities. By demonstrating significant performance improvements at test-time without requiring expensive finetuning or retraining, the work offers a practical and cost-effective solution for deploying such policies in dynamic, unpredictable environments. This reduces the resource overhead associated with data collection and model re-training.
- **Leveraging Zero-Shot Vision-Language Models for Embodied Control:** The successful integration of zero-shot VLM-based verifiers for providing semantically grounded feedback for continuous robot actions represents a notable advancement. It validates the hypothesis that the generation-verification paradigm, effective in LLMs, can be successfully adapted to the embodied domain, unlocking the reasoning capabilities of large foundation models for robotic control.
- **Modular and Scalable Framework:** EVE's modular architecture, allowing for the incorporation of diverse verifier agents with distinct capabilities and input modalities, offers a flexible and extensible platform. This design facilitates future advancements by enabling the integration of new, stronger VLM backbones and specialized verifiers as they become available, potentially leading to further performance gains.
- **Practical Design Guidelines:** Through extensive ablations on components like the action incorporator design, guidance ratios, verifier model scale, and image resolution, the research provides empirical insights and practical guidelines for building effective generator-verifier systems. These insights can inform the development of future robust robotic control systems.
- **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated performance improvements on long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks (ManiSkill-HAB), bimanual manipulation (RoboTwin), and real-world out-of-distribution scenarios highlight EVE's ability to generalize to new embodiments and complex task settings where traditional trained verifiers might fail without extensive in-domain data. This contributes to the development of more versatile and adaptable robots.
- **Reduced Data Dependency and Faster Deployment:** By enabling performance improvements with zero-shot verifiers, EVE significantly reduces the reliance on large-scale, task-specific training data. This accelerates the deployment cycle for robotic systems, as new tasks or environments may not necessitate extensive data collection for robust operation.
- **Bridging AI Disciplines:** This work serves as an interdisciplinary bridge, extending successful principles from the field of large language models to robotics. It underscores the potential for cross-domain fertilization of ideas, where advancements in one area of AI can provide new avenues for solving challenges in another.

In summary, EVE presents a step towards more resilient and capable robotic systems by enabling pretrained generative policies to adapt and recover from errors at inference time, utilizing the power of zero-shot VLM-based verification.
