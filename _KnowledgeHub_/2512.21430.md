# EVE: A Generator-Verifier System for Generative Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> The EVE framework enhances pretrained generative robot policies by integrating zero-shot Vision-Language Model (VLM) based verifiers with a guided diffusion action incorporator. This system improves task success rates by up to 2.4% across diverse manipulation tasks without requiring any additional policy training or finetuning.

## Problem

- Generative robot policies based on architectures like diffusion models degrade under distribution shifts and exhibit limited recovery capabilities when encountering out-of-distribution states.
- Traditional methods to improve policy performance, such as finetuning or retraining, are resource-intensive, time-consuming, and can compromise the generalist capabilities of the policies.
- Collecting extensive real-world robotics data for retraining is costly and labor-intensive, exacerbating the problem of policy robustness.

## Method

- **EVE** introduces a unified framework that augments frozen, pretrained generative policies with modular, ==zero-shot VLM-based verifier agents== at inference time.
- It employs an ==ensemble of verifiers== (both generator-agnostic and generator-conditioned) whose outputs are aggregated and transformed into a common action trajectory representation.
- A novel ==guided diffusion-based action incorporator== fuses the aggregated verifier feedback with the base policy's action predictions, steering actions towards verifier-approved behaviors while maintaining stability.

## Results

- **EVE** consistently improved task success rates, showing gains of up to **+2.40%** for the 'MOVE NEAR' task and **+2.18%** for 'SetTable-OpenFridge' across both diffusion and flow-matching policies.
- Verifier performance scaled positively with VLM model size, with the **72B Qwen-2.5-VL** model outperforming smaller **7B** and **32B** variants.
- Ablation studies confirmed the effectiveness of guided diffusion for action incorporation and identified optimal intervention thresholds (e.g., **MMD of 0.48**) and guidance ratios (**10.0**) for balancing policy stability and verifier influence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Zero-shot, VLM-based verifiers can effectively provide semantically grounded feedback to improve low-level control policies in embodied AI without requiring specific training.
> - Ensembles of complementary verifiers, such as primitive steerers and pivot steerers, generally yield higher performance gains than individual verifiers, enhancing robustness.
> - Guided diffusion is a crucial mechanism for stably incorporating verifier feedback, as it biases action synthesis towards verifier-consistent behaviors while ensuring actions remain coherent with the base policy's learned distribution, outperforming direct overrides or averaging.
