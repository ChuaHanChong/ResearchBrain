# Beyond Transformers: Linear Attention Policy for Open-Vocabulary Object Goal Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named LANav, utilizing Linear Attention (LA) as a policy backbone, addresses Open-Vocabulary Object Goal Navigation by enhancing temporal information integration. The proposed Weighted State-Expansion Linear Attention (WSLA) module achieves a 36.4% success rate on the HM3D-OVON benchmark, outperforming Transformer-based policies by 6.3 percentage points, and exhibits linear computational complexity with improved generalization.

## Problem

- Open-Vocabulary Object Goal Navigation (OVON) requires agents to locate ambiguously described objects in unseen environments, posing challenges for robust temporal information integration.
- Traditional Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) struggle with long-range dependencies in complex navigation, while Transformer-based policies do not consistently improve performance with increased context length in OVON.
- Existing policy backbones lack a computationally efficient and effective mechanism for synthesizing extended historical observations in partially observable embodied navigation.

## Method

- Introduced **LANav**, a framework utilizing ==Linear Attention (LA)== backbones for temporal state integration in OVON, which maintains a ==recurrent state matrix== with linear computational complexity.
- Evaluated multiple LA variants (==Linear Transformer==, ==DeltaNet==, ==Gated DeltaNet==) as policy backbones, comparing their structured state update mechanisms.
- Developed ==Weighted State-Expansion Linear Attention (WSLA)==, a novel LA module that expands attention heads into multiple independent sub-heads and uses learnable weighted aggregation for enhanced state expressivity.

## Results

- The proposed WSLA achieved a **36.4%** average Success Rate (SR) on the HM3D-OVON benchmark, outperforming the best Transformer baseline by **6.3** percentage points.
- WSLA demonstrated superior performance in long-distance navigation, showing gains of **+7.68** SR and **+4.21** SPL for episodes over 15m, and exhibited an **82%** success rate in real-world robot deployment.
- Linear Attention backbones, including WSLA, exhibited linear computational complexity with respect to context length, maintaining significantly lower FLOPs (e.g., **0.048G** FLOPs/action for WSLA vs. **42.53G** for Transformer at L=1000).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - More structured and regulated state update mechanisms within Linear Attention models lead to improved navigation performance compared to simpler attention linearization.
> - Standard Transformer policies struggle to effectively leverage increased context lengths in OVON, sometimes even degrading performance, whereas Linear Attention policies consistently benefit.
> - Linear Attention provides a more robust and scalable mechanism for integrating long historical observations in partially observable navigation due to its state update formulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses Open-Vocabulary Object Goal Navigation (OVON), a challenging task in embodied intelligence where an autonomous agent must locate a specified object in an unseen environment, potentially using novel or semantically ambiguous object descriptions. This extends traditional Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav), which typically operates with a fixed, known set of target categories.

Previous research in embodied navigation has relied on various policy backbones to manage partial observability and integrate temporal information from observation sequences. Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs), such as LSTMs and GRUs, have been widely used but often struggle with preserving long-range dependencies over extended trajectories. More recently, Transformer-based policies, leveraging self-attention over a context window, have been introduced to address these limitations by enabling direct interaction among historical observations. While Transformers have shown improvements over RNNs in certain navigation tasks, including OVON, their effectiveness in integrating extended histories in complex OVON settings remains an area of active investigation.

This paper positions itself by questioning the suitability of Transformer-based self-attention for state integration in OVON. The authors observe that the performance of Transformer-based policies does not consistently improve with increasing context length in their controlled experiments. This finding motivates the exploration of alternative state update mechanisms. Specifically, the paper investigates Linear Attention (LA), a class of models originally developed to reduce the computational complexity of Transformers while maintaining strong scalability in long-sequence tasks like language and decision modeling. The current work re-examines LA from the perspective of its state update mechanism, proposing it as a potentially more suitable approach for history integration in partially observable navigation environments compared to both RNNs (which compress history into a single hidden state) and Transformers (which perform self-attention over a fixed context window).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations guiding this research are:

1. **Address limitations of Transformer-based policies in OVON:** The authors conduct controlled experiments revealing that Transformer-based policies for OVON do not exhibit performance improvements with increasing context window length. This observation indicates that the self-attention mechanism, while powerful, might not be optimally suited for the specific requirements of state integration in navigation, especially under partial observability and with semantically ambiguous goals. This motivates the search for alternative architectural designs for the policy backbone.

2. **Investigate Linear Attention as an alternative state update mechanism:** Given the limitations identified in Transformers, the paper aims to explore Linear Attention (LA) as a policy backbone for OVON. LA offers a distinct approach to temporal information integration by maintaining a structured, recurrent internal state whose update mechanism is independent of the context length, in contrast to the quadratic complexity of Transformer's self-attention. The hypothesis is that LA's state update formulation might be more effective for synthesizing relevant historical information in dynamic, partially observable environments.

3. **Evaluate and compare different LA variants:** The research seeks to comprehensively compare various LA architectures, including Linear Transformer, DeltaNet, and Gated DeltaNet, under identical experimental settings. This objective is driven by the desire to understand how progressively more structured and regulated state update rules within the LA family impact navigation performance, thereby identifying design principles for effective history integration.

4. **Develop an improved Linear Attention variant for OVON:** Based on the insights gained from comparing existing LA variants, the authors aim to design a novel Linear Attention module, called Weighted State-Expansion Linear Attention (WSLA). The motivation behind WSLA is to enhance state expressivity and effectiveness in navigation by expanding the state capacity of attention heads and introducing learnable mechanisms to regulate the contribution of these expanded states during readout. This aims to better filter redundant information and emphasize goal-relevant cues over long trajectories.

5. **Demonstrate the computational efficiency and scalability of LA-based policies:** A secondary motivation is to highlight the computational advantages of LA. While the primary focus is on performance through improved state updates, demonstrating that these gains do not come at the cost of excessive computational resources (especially concerning context length scaling) is an important practical consideration for embodied AI.

6. **Validate real-world applicability and generalization:** The authors seek to confirm the practical feasibility of their proposed LANav framework by evaluating its sim-to-real transfer capabilities on a physical robot and assessing its generalization across different scene distributions (HSSD dataset), beyond the primary HM3D-OVON benchmark. This objective aims to demonstrate the robustness and utility of the proposed method in diverse operational contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose Linear Attention-based Navigation (LANav) as a framework for Open-Vocabulary Object Goal Navigation (OVON), focusing on the design of the policy backbone.

**Problem Formulation:**
The OVON task involves a mobile agent navigating an indoor environment to locate an object specified by a natural language goal. The agent operates under partial observability, receiving only egocentric RGB observations ($I_t$), a language goal ($G$), and its previous action ($a_{t-1}$) at each step $t$. The action space is discrete, including `move-forward`, `turn-left`, `turn-right`, `look-up`, `look-down`, and `stop`. Success is defined as issuing a `stop` action within a predefined distance of a goal object instance. The task is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP), requiring the policy to integrate historical information.

**Overview of LANav Architecture:**
LANav follows a modular design, similar to DAgRL (Yokoyama et al. 2024).
1. **Encoders:**
 - **Visual Encoder:** A frozen SigLIP$_{RGB}$ encoder processes $I_t$ to produce a visual embedding $v_t \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$.
 - **Text Encoder:** A frozen SigLIP$_{text}$ encoder processes $G$ to produce a goal embedding $g \in \mathbb{R}^{768}$. This is computed once per episode.
 - **Action Embedding:** A learnable embedding layer maps the previous action $a_{t-1}$ to an embedding $e_a_t \in \mathbb{R}^{32}$.
2. **Per-Step Embedding:** $v_t$, $g$, and $e_a_t$ are concatenated and linearly projected into a hidden dimension $d=512$, forming $\tilde{o}_t$.
3. **Policy Backbone:** The policy network receives a context window of the $L_t$ most recent $\tilde{o}_t$ embeddings, where $L_t = \min(l, t)$ and $l$ is the maximum context length. This sequence is processed by a four-layer, decoder-only backbone $\Phi(\cdot)$.
4. **Action Prediction:** The backbone's output corresponding to the current time step, $y_t$, is projected by an action head to produce a categorical distribution over the discrete action space using a Softmax function.

**Policy Backbones Compared:**
The core of the methodology lies in comparing different temporal policy backbones:

1. **RNN-based (GRU):** Used as a baseline, representing traditional recurrent architectures that compress history into a single hidden state.
2. **Transformer-based:** A lightweight, decoder-only Transformer with causal self-attention serves as the primary baseline.
 - It projects tokens into queries, keys, and values.
 - Attention is computed over the context window $C_t = \{t - L_t + 1, \dots, t\}$ by comparing the current query with all keys in the window.
 - The output involves a weighted sum of value vectors, with weights derived from query-key similarity and softmax normalization.
 - This formulation has a quadratic computational complexity with respect to context length.
3. **Linear Attention-based (LANav):**
 - Instead of computing attention over a context window at each step, LA backbones maintain a recurrent state matrix $S^{(h)}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{d_k \times d_v}$ for each attention head $h$, whose size is independent of context length.
 - At each step, this state is updated using the current key-value pair, $S^{(h)}_t = \text{Update}(S^{(h)}_{t-1}, k^{(h)}_t, v^{(h)}_t)$.
 - The current query $q^{(h)}_t$ reads from the updated state, $o^{(h)}_t = q^{(h)\top}_t S^{(h)}_t$.
 - This results in linear computational complexity with respect to context length.

 The paper evaluates several LA variants:
 - **Linear Transformer (Schlag, Irie, and Schmidhuber 2021):** Directly accumulates key-value associations.
 - **DeltaNet (Yang et al. 2024b):** Updates the state based on a prediction error and a learned write coefficient.
 - **Gated DeltaNet (Yang, Kautz, and Hatamizadeh 2025):** Extends DeltaNet by adding a data-dependent retention gate to control how much of the previous state is preserved.

4. **Weighted State-Expansion Linear Attention (WSLA):**
 This novel variant builds upon the Gated DeltaNet.
 - **State Expansion:** Each attention head $h$ is expanded into $E$ independent sub-heads. Query $q^{(h)}_t$ and key $k^{(h)}_t$ are linearly projected into $E$ sub-head queries $\tilde{q}^{(h)}_t$ and sub-head keys $\tilde{k}^{(h)}_t$. The value vector $v^{(h)}_t$ is replicated and shared across sub-heads for parameter efficiency.
 - **Independent Gating:** Each sub-head $(h, e)$ maintains its own state $S^{(h,e)}_t$ and predicts independent gating factors ($\beta^{(h,e)}_t$, $\gamma^{(h,e)}_t$) for state retention and update, similar to Gated DeltaNet.
 - **Gated Delta Update:** The state update rule is applied to each sub-head state: $S^{(h,e)}_t = \gamma^{(h,e)}_t S^{(h,e)}_{t-1} + \beta^{(h,e)}_t k^{(h,e)}_t \Delta v^{(h,e)\top}_t$, where $\Delta v^{(h,e)}_t$ is the prediction error.
 - **Weighted Readout:** After each sub-head $(h, e)$ produces its output $o^{(h,e)}_t = q^{(h,e)\top}_t S^{(h,e)}_t$, these $E$ sub-head outputs are combined. A learnable logit vector $\lambda \in \mathbb{R}^E$ (shared across heads and time) is used to compute softmax-normalized weights for aggregating the sub-head outputs.
 - **Output Gating:** The combined head output $u^{(h)}_t$ is then RMS-normalized and modulated by an input-dependent SiLU gate, similar to Gated DeltaNet.
 - In the implementation, $H=4$ heads and $E=4$ sub-heads are used, maintaining comparable parameter counts and FLOPs to other backbones.

**Experimental Settings:**
- **Benchmark:** HM3D-OVON dataset, with three validation splits: VAL SEEN, VAL SEEN SYN (semantically similar unseen goals), and VAL UNSEEN (semantically distinct unseen goals).
- **Metrics:** Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL).
- **Training:** A two-stage pipeline: 150M steps of DAgger (imitation learning) followed by 100M steps of PPO (reinforcement learning fine-tuning).
- **Infrastructure:** 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, 32 parallel environments per GPU, Variable Experience Rollout, Adam optimizer.
- **Generalization:** Evaluated on Habitat Synthetic Scenes Dataset (HSSD) for zero-shot transfer and fine-tuning.
- **Real-world Validation:** Deployment on a Unitree Go2 EDU robot for 50 trials across 5 object categories, using egocentric RGB and language goal inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the performance and characteristics of Linear Attention (LA) backbones in Open-Vocabulary Object Goal Navigation (OVON):

1. **Superior Performance of LA-based Backbones:**
 - Across HM3D-OVON validation splits, LA-based backbones consistently outperformed both RNN (GRU) and Transformer-based baselines under identical architectural and training settings (Table 1).
 - WSLA, the proposed LA variant, achieved the highest average Success Rate (SR) of 36.4%, surpassing the strongest Transformer-based baseline (30.1%) by 6.3 percentage points.
 - The gains were particularly noticeable on the VAL SEEN SYN split, indicating stronger generalization to semantically related but unseen goal names.

2. **Impact of State Update Mechanism Design:**
 - A progressive improvement in performance was observed within the LA family: Linear Transformer < DeltaNet < Gated DeltaNet < WSLA (Table 1).
 - This trend suggests that progressively more structured and regulated state update rules, beyond simply linearizing attention, contribute to better navigation performance. The vanilla Linear Transformer showed only marginal improvement over the standard Transformer, highlighting that effective state update design is crucial.

3. **Context Length Scaling Differences:**
 - Transformer-based policies did not benefit from larger training context lengths (e.g., $l_{train}$=500) and sometimes exhibited performance degradation with increased test-time context length ($l_{test}$) (Figure 2a). This suggests a limited ability to effectively exploit extended histories with self-attention in this setting.
 - In contrast, LA backbones (Linear Transformer, WSLA) showed consistent performance improvements with larger training context lengths and maintained stable performance across varying test-time context lengths (Figures 2b, 2c). This indicates that LA provides a more robust mechanism for integrating historical observations over longer horizons.

4. **Effectiveness of WSLA Design Components:**
 - An ablation study for WSLA (Table 3) confirmed the contributions of its core components:
 - State expansion alone (without weighted aggregation) improved SR from 34.6% (Gated DeltaNet baseline) to 35.2%.
 - Adding learnable weighted aggregation further enhanced SR to 36.4%.
 - These results suggest that increasing intra-head capacity and allowing the model to learn to regulate the contributions of different sub-states are beneficial for navigation decision-making.

5. **Distance-Stratified Performance Gains:**
 - WSLA consistently outperformed the Transformer baseline across all initial geodesic distance bins (0–8m, 8–15m, 15m+) (Table 2).
 - The performance gains in both SR and SPL for WSLA increased with longer distances (e.g., +7.68 SR and +4.21 SPL for 15m+ episodes), indicating its particular effectiveness in scenarios requiring extended exploration and long-horizon navigation.
 - Qualitative trajectory analysis showed WSLA sustaining exploration over longer horizons and successfully reaching targets, whereas the Transformer often exhibited repetitive local exploration or premature termination (Figure 3, Supplementary S2).

6. **Computational Efficiency:**
 - LA backbones, including WSLA, maintained significantly lower online FLOPs per action compared to Transformer-based policies, especially at longer context lengths (Figure 4).
 - While Transformer FLOPs increased quadratically with context length (e.g., 3.52G at $l=100$ to 42.53G at $l=1000$), LA backbones remained nearly constant (WSLA at approximately 0.048G FLOPs/action).
 - WSLA incurred a moderate increase in parameters due to state expansion but still offered a favorable balance between performance and computational cost.

7. **Generalization and Adaptation:**
 - WSLA demonstrated improved generalization on the Habitat Synthetic Scenes Dataset (HSSD). In zero-shot transfer, WSLA achieved a 9.62 percentage point higher SR and 1.31 higher SPL compared to the Transformer (Table 4).
 - After fine-tuning on HSSD, WSLA further improved its lead, with 8.33 higher SR and 10.17 higher SPL, indicating robust adaptation and efficient exploration in new scene distributions.

8. **Real-World Validation:**
 - Deployment of LANav with the WSLA backbone on a Unitree Go2 robot in an indoor environment yielded an 82% success rate over 50 trials across five object-goal categories (Figure 5).
 - This result provides empirical evidence for the sim-to-real transferability and practical feasibility of the LANav approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, LANav, that leverages Linear Attention (LA) as a policy backbone for Open-Vocabulary Object Goal Navigation (OVON), demonstrating its efficacy and efficiency compared to conventional Transformer-based and RNN-based approaches. The findings have several implications for the field of embodied AI and sequential decision-making:

1. **Re-evaluation of Temporal Information Integration for Navigation:** The study challenges the assumption that standard Transformer-based self-attention is the optimal mechanism for integrating long-range historical observations in partially observable navigation tasks. The empirical evidence that Transformer performance does not scale with context length in OVON, unlike LA backbones, suggests that the direct global attention mechanism of Transformers may struggle with redundancy or relevance filtering over extended, egocentric observation streams. This encourages a re-examination of how temporal information is processed and stored in embodied agents.

2. **Establishment of Linear Attention as a Viable Policy Backbone:** By systematically comparing LA variants with established RNN and Transformer baselines under controlled conditions, the work establishes LA as an effective and competitive backbone for sequential navigation decision-making. The consistent outperformance of LA across various metrics and validation splits indicates a promising alternative design direction for future embodied policies, particularly in tasks requiring robust history integration.

3. **Emphasis on Structured State Update Design:** The observation that performance improves with progressively more structured and regulated state update rules within the LA family (from Linear Transformer to DeltaNet, Gated DeltaNet, and WSLA) highlights the critical importance of memory update mechanisms. This suggests that simply accumulating or attending to history is less effective than selectively writing, retaining, and reading information from an organized internal state. This insight could guide the development of more sophisticated memory architectures for agents operating in dynamic, complex environments.

4. **Advancement in Open-Vocabulary Object Goal Navigation:** The proposed Weighted State-Expansion Linear Attention (WSLA) achieves a notable performance gain on the HM3D-OVON benchmark. The improvements are particularly significant in challenging aspects like generalization to unseen goal categories (VAL SEEN SYN) and long-distance navigation episodes. This directly contributes to advancing the state of the art in OVON, enabling agents to operate more effectively in environments with diverse and unknown object vocabularies.

5. **Practical Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The demonstration that LA backbones, including WSLA, maintain linear computational complexity with respect to context length, contrasting with the quadratic scaling of Transformers, has practical implications. This efficiency enables the use of longer context windows without prohibitive computational costs, which is crucial for real-world robotic applications that demand both effectiveness and resource constraints.

6. **Enhanced Generalization and Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The successful zero-shot transfer and fine-tuning results on the HSSD dataset, alongside the real-world deployment on a Unitree Go2 robot with an 82% success rate, confirm the robustness, transferability, and practical feasibility of the LANav framework. This validation against scene distribution shifts and physical deployment bridges the gap between simulation research and real-world robotic capabilities, suggesting that the developed policies are sufficiently robust for tangible applications.

While the authors acknowledge limitations such as the use of shallow backbones and bounded context windows, the work lays a foundation for future research to scale model capacity and extend horizons. Overall, this research significantly contributes to the understanding of effective temporal modeling for embodied agents and offers a computationally efficient and high-performing alternative to Transformer-based architectures for challenging navigation tasks.
