# Goal Reaching with Eikonal-Constrained Hierarchical Quasimetric Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Eikonal-Constrained Quasimetric RL (Eik-QRL) and its hierarchical extension, Eikonal-Hierarchical QRL (Eik-HiQRL), propose a new approach to Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning by incorporating continuous-time PDE constraints for trajectory-free value function learning. Eik-HiQRL demonstrated state-of-the-art performance in offline humanoidmaze navigation, with notable improvements in long-horizon tasks and a reduction in collision rates in simpler environments.

## Problem

- Reward engineering in Reinforcement Learning (RL) is complex and time-consuming, often leading to artificial structures and limited generalization.
- Existing Quasimetric RL (QRL) relies on discrete, trajectory-based local consistency constraints, which limit applicability in data-scarce settings and hinder out-of-distribution generalization.
- Complex, high-dimensional environments with non-isotropic dynamics challenge the learning of accurate value functions and maintaining a high signal-to-noise ratio in long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- ==Eikonal==-Constrained Quasimetric RL (**Eik-QRL**) reformulates local consistency using a continuous-time Eikonal PDE constraint, derived from the HJB equation under isotropic dynamics, enabling trajectory-free learning from i.i.d. state-goal samples.
- Eikonal-Hierarchical QRL (Eik-HiQRL) extends Eik-QRL by applying it at a high level within a lower-dimensional abstract space, combined with Implicit V-Learning for low-level control.
- The approach leverages advancements in Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) to embed PDE constraints directly into neural network training objectives for practical optimization.

## Results

- Eik-QRL and Eik-HiQRL achieved significantly lower collision rates in Pointmaze environments compared to the original QRL, indicating improved value function accuracy under isotropic dynamics.
- Eik-HiQRL established state-of-the-art performance in offline Humanoidmaze navigation tasks, outperforming all baselines with substantial gains, especially in long-horizon `stitch` datasets.
- In complex manipulation tasks involving hybrid and contact-rich dynamics (Antsoccer, robotic cubes/scenes), Eik-HiQRL performed comparably to baselines, highlighting current limitations of PDE constraints in non-smooth environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous-time PDE constraints can replace discrete trajectory-based constraints in QRL, enabling trajectory-free learning and acting as an implicit regularizer for value functions.
> - While Eik-QRL benefits from idealized isotropic dynamics, a hierarchical architecture (Eik-HiQRL) is essential for addressing the method's limitations in complex, high-dimensional, and non-isotropic environments.
> - PDE-based constraints lead to improved value function accuracy, resulting in reduced collision rates and enhanced generalization, particularly in tasks requiring trajectory stitching.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes in various domains, including game playing, robotics, and autonomous driving. However, these applications often rely on reward functions that are manually designed, a process known to be complex and time-consuming. This "reward engineering" can introduce artificial structures, additional hyperparameters, and assumptions about task solutions, potentially undermining the goal of generalized AI systems.

Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) emerged as an approach to mitigate this challenge by reformulating tasks as reaching arbitrary goals rather than maximizing specific reward signals. This framework allows learning from sparse success indicators, enabling more flexible and general-purpose behaviors across multiple objectives. A fundamental insight in GCRL is that the optimal goal-conditioned value function naturally forms a quasimetric, representing the shortest feasible path between a state and a goal.

Building on this, Quasimetric RL (QRL) was introduced, which constrains the value function to be a quasimetric mapping. This reduces the hypothesis space for learning, aligning it with the geometric properties of goal-reaching tasks. QRL enforces local consistency through discrete, trajectory-based constraints, distinguishing it from traditional Temporal-Difference (TD) learning methods.

The current work situates itself within the evolving landscape of GCRL and QRL by exploring a continuous-time reformulation. This connects to continuous-time optimal control theory, which traditionally uses Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) like the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equation. Historically, the computational difficulty of solving such PDEs has limited their direct application in RL. However, recent advancements in Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) have made it possible to embed PDE constraints directly into neural network training objectives, offering a new avenue for RL research.

Furthermore, the paper addresses challenges in offline RL, where policies are trained from fixed datasets without further environmental interaction. Offline GCRL, in particular, combines the difficulties of generalizing to unseen state-goal pairs with learning from static data. The use of PDE-based constraints is explored as a mechanism to improve generalization, especially for out-of-distribution state-goal pairs and scenarios requiring trajectory stitching. The work also integrates hierarchical RL, a long-standing concept that decomposes policies into high- and low-level components to improve exploration and credit assignment in long-horizon tasks, which is particularly effective in GCRL for subgoal decomposition.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a novel formulation for Quasimetric Reinforcement Learning (QRL) that addresses limitations of existing methods by enforcing local consistency in a continuous-time framework.

The core motivations for this work are:

- **Addressing limitations of discrete trajectory-based constraints in QRL**: Current QRL formulations, like that by Wang et al. (2023), rely on discrete, trajectory-based constraints to maintain local consistency in value functions. While effective, this reliance on observed transitions limits their applicability, particularly in scenarios where rich trajectory data is scarce or when strong out-of-distribution generalization is required.
- **Leveraging continuous-time optimal control principles**: The authors argue that a continuous-time perspective, which naturally leads to Partial Differential Equations (PDEs), can offer fundamental structural advantages for value function learning. This perspective is motivated by the observation that for many practical use cases, the benefits of a discrete-time formulation are primarily computational convenience rather than inherent theoretical superiority.
- **Exploiting advancements in Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)**: Recent progress in PINNs has made it feasible to approximate solutions to PDEs using neural networks by embedding PDE constraints directly into the training objective. This technological development opens the door to practical applications of continuous-time optimal control theory in RL.
- **Developing a trajectory-free GCRL method**: A key motivation is to create a GCRL algorithm that does not require short rollouts or transition pairs (s, s'), but instead can learn solely from sampled states and goals (s, g). Such a trajectory-free approach would be highly beneficial in domains like navigation with maps, robotic manipulation within collision-free workspaces, or autonomous driving where waypoints are sampled. This would enhance state-space coverage by allowing each (s, g) pair to contribute a full gradient vector, promoting global consistency.
- **Improving out-of-distribution generalization and learning stability**: The continuous-time PDE formulation is hypothesized to act as an implicit regularizer on the value function. This regularization is expected to improve both the stability of the learning process and the accuracy of value function estimation for out-of-distribution state-goal pairs.
- **Mitigating Eik-QRL's inherent limitations under complex dynamics**: While the Eikonal PDE simplifies the HJB equation under assumptions of isotropic dynamics, these assumptions may not hold in real-world complex environments. The authors are motivated to design a hierarchical extension that can retain the benefits of the Eikonal formulation while robustly handling more complex, non-isotropic dynamics and mitigating issues like low signal-to-noise ratios in long-horizon tasks.
- **Achieving state-of-the-art performance**: Ultimately, the work aims to develop an algorithm that empirically outperforms existing Offline GCRL baselines in navigation and manipulation tasks.

The explicit contributions summarized by the authors are:
1. Introduction of Eikonal-Constrained QRL (Eik-QRL), a novel PDE-constrained formulation derived from the Eikonal PDE, along with theoretical guarantees and identification of its limitations in complex dynamics.
2. Proposal of Eikonal-Hierarchical QRL (Eik-HiQRL), which integrates Eik-QRL into a hierarchical decomposition to address the identified limitations and achieve competitive performance.
3. Minor contributions including additional theoretical analysis supporting the hierarchical design and an experimental protocol that accounts for collision avoidance in addition to goal-reaching success.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research develops a new formulation of QRL called Eikonal-Constrained Quasimetric RL (Eik-QRL), and extends it to a hierarchical architecture, Eik-Hierarchical QRL (Eik-HiQRL).

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The work frames the problem within a finite-horizon discounted Markov Decision Process (MDP) for Goal-Conditioned RL (GCRL), where rewards are sparse (0 for goal, -1 otherwise). The optimal goal-conditioned value function $V^*(s, g)$ is recognized as defining a quasimetric $d^*(s, g) = -V^*(s, g)$. QRL, as previously defined, optimizes a parameterized quasimetric $d_\theta(s, g)$ to maximize global relationships while enforcing local consistency via trajectory-based constraints that ensure $d_\theta(s, s') \approx \text{cost}$ for observed transitions. Continuous-time optimal control problems are typically formulated through the Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) PDE, $0 = \inf_{a \in A} [c(s, a) + \nabla_s d^*(s, g)^\intercal f(s, a)]$.

#### 3.2. Eikonal-Constrained Quasimetric RL (Eik-QRL)

The core of the methodology lies in reformulating the local consistency constraint of QRL using a continuous-time PDE.

- **Assumptions**: The derivation relies on four key assumptions:
 1. **Compact state and action spaces**: Guarantees well-posed minimization over actions.
 2. **Lipschitz dynamics**: Deterministic transition map $T(s, a)$ is Lipschitz in $s$, or in continuous-time limit, $f(s, a)$ is Lipschitz in $s$. This ensures unique and well-defined ODE solutions, making the value function and HJB PDE well-posed.
 3. **Cost regularity**: Continuous, bounded, non-negative running cost $c(s, g)$, specifically $c(s, g)=1$ for $s \neq g$ and $c(g, g)=0$. Ensures finite path costs.
 4. **Value function regularity**: For fixed $g$, $d^*(s, g)$ is finite and locally Lipschitz. Permits Taylor expansions.

- **Derivation from HJB and Eikonal PDE**:
 The discrete QRL constraint $E_{(s,a,s') \sim D}[\max(d_\theta(s, s') - 1, 0)^2] \leq \epsilon^2$ penalizes violations of $d(s, s') \leq 1$. By applying a first-order Taylor expansion to the triangle inequality $d(s, g) \leq d(s, s') + d(s', g)$ and taking the continuous-time limit ($\Delta t \to 0$, $d(s,s') \to c(s,g)\Delta t \approx \Delta t$), the static HJB inequality $0 \leq c(s, g) + \nabla_s d(s, g)^\intercal f(s, a)$ is obtained.
 An intermediate formulation, HJB-QRL (Eq. 7), replaces the discrete constraint with one derived from the HJB residual: $E_{(s,s') \sim D, g \sim P_{goal}} [(\nabla_s d_\theta(s, g)^\intercal (s' - s) + 1)^2] \leq \epsilon^2$. However, this is noted to be difficult to optimize due to ill-conditioned gradients in high-dimensional spaces and still relies on transition tuples $(s, s')$.
 To address this, Eik-QRL (Eq. 8) assumes simplified dynamics: unit-speed, isotropic dynamics $f(s, a) = a$ with $||a|| \leq 1$. Under this assumption, the optimal action $a^*$ becomes $- \nabla_s d(s, g) / ||\nabla_s d(s, g)||$, which reduces the HJB PDE to the Eikonal PDE $|| \nabla_s d^*(s, g) || = 1$. This leads to the Eik-QRL objective:
 $\max_\theta E_{s,g} [\zeta(d_\theta(s, g))] \quad \text{s.t.} \quad E_{s \sim P_{state}, g \sim P_{goal}} [ (|| \nabla_s d_\theta(s, g) || - 1)^2 ] \leq \epsilon^2$.

- **Benefits of Eik-QRL**:
 - **Trajectory-free**: Requires only i.i.d. samples of states and goals $(s, g)$, rather than short rollouts or transition pairs $(s, s')$. This enables learning from static maps or sampled workspaces.
 - **Improved state-space coverage and regularization**: Each $(s, g)$ pair contributes a full gradient vector $\nabla_s d_\theta(s, g)$, coupling all coordinate directions and promoting global consistency.

- **Limitations of Eik-QRL**:
 - The assumption of isotropic dynamics ($f(s, a) = a, ||a|| \leq 1$) is restrictive and may not hold for all MDPs.
 - The method requires strong regularity assumptions (Lipschitz continuity of dynamics and value function) for theoretical guarantees.

- **Theoretical Guarantees**:
 - **Lemma 4.7**: Shows that under unit-speed integrator dynamics and constant running cost, the optimal value function $d^*(s, g)$ is 1-Lipschitz. This implies $|| \nabla_s d^*(s, g) || = 1$, making $d^*$ feasible for the Eik-LRs constraint.
 - **Theorem 4.8**: States that a universal quasimetric approximator trained with Eik-QRL approximately recovers $V^*(s, g)$ with high probability under the conditions of Lemma 4.7. The learned quasimetric $d_\theta^*(s,g)$ is bounded such that $-V^*(s,g) \le d_\theta^*(s,g) \le -(1+\epsilon)V^*(s,g)$.

#### 3.3. Eikonal-Constrained Hierarchical QRL (Eik-HiQRL)

To address the practical limitations of Eik-QRL in complex, high-dimensional spaces where regularity assumptions may be violated, a hierarchical architecture is proposed.

- **Motivation for Hierarchy**:
 - Mitigate low signal-to-noise ratio in value estimates for long-horizon tasks (as in prior hierarchical GCRL work).
 - Overcome the exponential growth of approximation error for quasimetric parameterizations with state-space dimension.
 - Preserve the benefits of Eik-QRL by applying it in a lower-dimensional, abstract space where its assumptions are more likely to hold.

- **Architecture**: Eik-HiQRL decomposes the problem into high-level planning and low-level control.
 - **High-level module**: Operates in a lower-dimensional abstract space $\bar{S}$ (e.g., agent coordinates, or a learned latent space). It consists of:
 - A quasimetric model $d_h$, parameterized as an Interval Quasimetric Embedding (IQE), trained with the Eik-QRL objective (Eq. 17).
 - A high-level policy $\pi_h$ that outputs subgoal embeddings $z_{t+k}$, trained via advantage-weighted regression (Eq. 19).
 - **Low-level module**: Operates in the full state space, guiding the agent to reach short-term subgoals. It consists of:
 - A value function $V_l$ (MLP) and a goal representation network $\phi$ (MLP), trained using Implicit V-Learning (Eq. 18).
 - A low-level policy $\pi_l$ that takes current state and subgoal embedding as input, trained via advantage-weighted regression (Eq. 20).

- **Abstract Space Learning**: For tasks like robotic manipulation, the abstract latent space is learned end-to-end by an MLP $\nu: S \to Z$, with gradients from the Eik-QRL objective backpropagated through $\nu$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of Eik-HiQRL and its components was primarily conducted in the Offline GCRL setting using tasks from OGbench, focusing on goal-reaching success rate (R) and collision rate (κ).

#### 4.1. Empirical Comparison of Quasimetric Formulations (Table 1)

- **Pointmaze Environment (Isotropic Dynamics)**:
 - Eik-QRL, HJB-QRL, and Eik-HiQRL generally achieved similar performance in terms of success rate.
 - These PDE-constrained methods demonstrated superior accuracy in value function estimation, leading to significantly lower collision rates compared to the original QRL. This was particularly evident in `giant` mazes and `stitch` datasets, where QRL policies often relied on collisions with walls. This finding supports the theoretical benefits of PDE-based constraints under dynamics that align with the Eikonal assumptions.
 - HJB-QRL performed comparably to Eik-QRL, suggesting that the simplification to the Eikonal PDE does not incur a significant performance penalty in idealized settings.
 - Eik-HiQRL showed robust performance, combining the benefits of PDE constraints with hierarchy.

- **Antmaze Environment (Complex Dynamics)**:
 - All purely quasimetric algorithms (Eik-QRL, HJB-QRL, QRL) experienced performance degradation, especially in the `giant` variants, reflecting the increased difficulty of learning accurate quasimetrics in high-dimensional state spaces with complex, non-Lipschitz dynamics.
 - Eik-QRL consistently outperformed HJB-QRL, indicating the numerical challenges associated with optimizing the full HJB-QRL formulation (Eq. 7).
 - Eik-QRL performed on par with QRL in Antmaze, suggesting that the Eikonal constraint, while not perfectly aligned with the complex dynamics, did not excessively compromise effectiveness.
 - Eik-HiQRL achieved the strongest performance across Antmaze tasks, particularly in `large` and `giant` settings, demonstrating the effectiveness of its hierarchical design in mitigating the challenges posed by complex environments and higher dimensionality.

#### 4.2. Evaluation in Challenging and Non-Regular Environments (Figure 4, Table 2)

- **Humanoidmaze Navigation Tasks**:
 - Eik-HiQRL consistently outperformed all baselines (HIQL, CRL, QRL, Eik-HIQL), with substantial gains observed in long-horizon tasks (`large` and `giant` settings).
 - The advantage was most pronounced in `large-stitch` and `giant-stitch` datasets, where Eik-HiQRL achieved statistically significant improvements. This highlights the regularizing effect of PDE-based constraints in scenarios requiring trajectory stitching. Eik-HiQRL established state-of-the-art performance on this benchmark.

- **Antsoccer and Robotic Manipulation Tasks (cube, scene)**:
 - In environments involving external object interactions (e.g., dribbling a ball in Antsoccer, or manipulating cubes/scene objects), Eik-HiQRL achieved performance comparable to baselines but did not show the same significant gains observed in navigation tasks.
 - This is attributed to the non-trivial characteristics of these dynamics, which involve contact-rich interactions, categorical variables, and mode-switching events. Such discontinuities and hybrid structures violate the smooth topology implicitly assumed by the PDE-based constraints (Assumption 4.4). The introduction of Eikonal constraints in these regimes was observed to introduce a bias not perfectly matching the true optimal value landscape.

#### 4.3. Additional Experiments (Appendices H.5, H.6)

- **Trajectory-Free Setting (FetchReach)**: Eik-QRL was evaluated in a purely trajectory-free setup where control was derived directly from the value function's gradient. The method achieved an average success rate of approximately 40% after 100k training steps, demonstrating its potential for bootstrapping learning in data-scarce regimes.
- **Online RL Setting (Pointmaze, FetchReach)**: Eik-QRL and Eik-HiQRL were shown to be applicable in online GCRL. In Pointmaze, Eik-HiQRL outperformed QRL and Eik-QRL. In FetchReach, Eik-QRL showed comparable performance to QRL, supporting its applicability in online settings when modeling assumptions are met.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a novel perspective on goal-conditioned reinforcement learning by integrating continuous-time optimal control theory via Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) into the Quasimetric RL framework. This approach offers both theoretical and practical advancements.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Novelty and Contribution

The primary theoretical contribution is the derivation of Eikonal-Constrained QRL (Eik-QRL), which reformulates local consistency constraints using the Eikonal Partial Differential Equation. This provides a direct connection between the geometry of goal-reaching tasks and continuous-time PDE theory, offering a mathematically grounded alternative to discrete, trajectory-based constraints. The theoretical guarantees, formalized in Lemma 4.7 and Theorem 4.8, establish that the optimal value function is 1-Lipschitz under isotropic dynamics and that Eik-QRL can approximately recover this optimal value. This analysis clarifies the conditions under which PDE-based value learning is most effective.

#### 5.2. Methodological Advancements

Eik-QRL introduces the concept of **trajectory-free learning** to GCRL. By relying solely on i.i.d. samples of states and goals, it removes the dependence on explicit transition tuples or short rollouts. This property is particularly significant for applications where collecting sequential interaction data is costly or impractical, enabling learning from static maps, sampled workspaces, or existing pose databases. Furthermore, the PDE formulation acts as an **implicit regularizer** on the value function, improving learning stability and enhancing **out-of-distribution generalization** by promoting global consistency across the state space.

To address the limitations of Eik-QRL under complex, non-isotropic dynamics or in high-dimensional state spaces, the paper proposes **Eikonal-Constrained Hierarchical QRL (Eik-HiQRL)**. This hierarchical architecture leverages Eik-QRL at a high level within a learned, lower-dimensional abstract space where the Eikonal assumptions are more likely to hold. This design reconciles the theoretical benefits of PDE-based quasimetric learning with the practical demands of complex environments, improving signal-to-noise ratios and scalability for long-horizon tasks.

#### 5.3. Empirical Impact

Empirically, Eik-HiQRL achieves state-of-the-art performance in challenging offline goal-conditioned navigation benchmarks, specifically demonstrating significant gains in humanoidmaze tasks, especially for long-horizon `stitch` datasets. It also shows competitive performance in offline manipulation tasks and robustness in various `pointmaze` and `antmaze` scenarios. The consistent reduction in collision rates in simpler navigation tasks further validates the improved value function accuracy offered by PDE constraints. The demonstration of Eik-QRL in trajectory-free and online settings suggests broader applicability.

#### 5.4. Future Directions and Broader Impact

The research outlines several promising avenues for future work:
- **Extending beyond isotropic dynamics**: While the isotropic assumption simplifies the Eikonal PDE, future work could explore more general PDE formulations (e.g., full HJB equations) that accommodate complex, anisotropic dynamics, while retaining numerical advantages. This could bridge model-free and model-based RL by using simplified dynamical models to impose explicit value-learning constraints.
- **PDE-constrained algorithms for hybrid dynamics**: The limitations observed in contact-rich manipulation tasks highlight the need for PDE-constrained algorithms explicitly tailored to hybrid dynamics, which involve sharp discontinuities and mode-switching variables (e.g., in-contact vs. out-of-contact).
- **Representation learning for PDE-based RL**: A significant long-term impact lies in guiding representation design. By explicitly characterizing the geometric and regularity properties beneficial for PDE-based value learning, this work can inform the development of representation-learning mechanisms that generate embeddings satisfying these structural features, thereby enhancing the applicability and performance of PDE-informed RL in real-world deployments. This could lead to more robust and generalizable learned representations.
- **Bootstrapping learning in data-scarce environments**: The trajectory-free property of Eik-QRL suggests its potential for initializing value functions in complex, data-starved scenarios, effectively bootstrapping learning for subsequent control schemes.

In conclusion, this paper contributes to the theoretical understanding and practical implementation of goal-conditioned reinforcement learning by introducing a novel PDE-constrained framework. It demonstrates how continuous-time optimal control concepts can enhance learning stability, generalization, and efficiency, particularly when combined with hierarchical architectures.
