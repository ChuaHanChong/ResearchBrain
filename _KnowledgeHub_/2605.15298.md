# PhysBrain 1.0 Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysBrain 1.0 integrates physical commonsense understanding derived from human egocentric video into embodied AI, enhancing physically grounded reasoning and achieving higher success rates across VLM benchmarks, VLA simulations, and real-world robot tasks. The system demonstrated notable improvements, including a 16.2 percentage point gain in real-world single-object grasping.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models primarily rely on expensive, platform-dependent robot trajectories, limiting the diversity and coverage of physical interactions learned.
- Models trained solely through action imitation often lack a deep understanding of physical regularities, which is crucial for robust performance under variations in viewpoint, scene layout, or object state.
- Effectively transforming raw human first-person video into structured, actionable physical supervision for robots and transferring these priors without catastrophic forgetting has been challenging.

## Method

- **PhysBrain**'s data engine systematically converts human egocentric interaction videos into structured scene meta-information and physically grounded Question-Answer (QA) pairs, emphasizing ==physical explicitness== and ==depth-aware spatial augmentation==.
- A physically informed base Vision-Language Model (==Qwen3-VL==) was trained with this large-scale, human-derived physical commonsense supervision.
- A ==dual-pathway architecture== for VLA adaptation was introduced, utilizing a frozen general pathway from the base VLM and a trainable embodied pathway for continuous action generation, alongside an ==action-conditioned language alignment== objective to preserve capabilities.

## Results

- PhysBrain 8B achieved top scores on VLM benchmarks, including ERQA (**45.5**) and PhysBench (**50.2**), demonstrating enhanced physically grounded reasoning and general multimodal capability.
- In VLA simulation, PhysBrain 1.0 attained the best average success rates across benchmarks: **80.2%** on SimplerEnv-WidowX, **91.33%** on SimplerEnv-GoogleRobot, and **64.5%** on RoboCasa-GR1, showing improved out-of-domain generalization.
- Real-world experiments on a Franka robot showed a **16.2 percentage point** increase in single-object grasping success (**63.3%** vs. **47.1%**) and a **14.0 percentage point** gain in long-horizon tasks (**45.0%** vs. **31.0%**) compared to strong baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Acquiring general physical commonsense from large-scale human egocentric video *before* robot adaptation can significantly enhance embodied AI's robustness and generalization beyond robot-specific data.
> - Structured scene meta-information and depth-aware spatial augmentation derived from human videos can be effectively transformed into explicit, robot-relevant physical supervision, improving a VLM's perception, state inference, and planning.
> - A dual-pathway adaptation architecture with asymmetric layer-wise fusion successfully transfers human-derived physical priors to robot control while preventing catastrophic forgetting of general multimodal capabilities and maintaining instruction sensitivity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models has experienced advancements, primarily driven by the collection and utilization of large-scale robot trajectories. This dominant training paradigm involves fitting action policies to collected robot interaction data and scaling systems by increasing the volume of such data. While this approach has demonstrated progress, it presents several limitations. Robot trajectory collection is characterized by its expense, platform dependency, and restricted coverage of diverse physical interactions. Furthermore, models trained solely on action imitation may not acquire a deep understanding of physical regularities, which is crucial for robust performance under variations in viewpoint, scene layout, object state, or task composition.

PhysBrain 1.0 investigates a complementary strategy that emphasizes acquiring general physical commonsense *before* adapting models to specific embodied control tasks. This approach seeks to move beyond the constraints of robot-specific data by leveraging large-scale human first-person video. Human egocentric video offers an extensive and accessible source of information on physical interaction, including contact mechanics, object reachability, state changes, tool use, spatial constraints, and multi-step task structures. The challenge within this landscape involves effectively transforming raw human video into structured, actionable physical supervision and subsequently transferring these acquired physical priors to downstream embodied control systems without compromising existing vision-language capabilities, a phenomenon known as catastrophic forgetting.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core principle guiding PhysBrain 1.0 is "Understanding first, action next." This project is motivated by the observation that current VLA systems, predominantly trained through robot action imitation, often lack a comprehensive grasp of physical regularities necessary for robust and generalizable embodied intelligence. The existing paradigm of scaling embodied policies solely through robot trajectories is expensive, platform-dependent, and inherently limited in its coverage of diverse physical interactions.

PhysBrain 1.0 proposes an alternative: to first build a general multimodal model with enhanced physical understanding, and then adapt this model for embodied control. This shift necessitates a different data source and processing methodology. The primary objectives are:

- **Systematic Transformation of Human Video into Physical Supervision:** To investigate whether large-scale human first-person interaction video can be systematically converted into structured physical commonsense supervision that is explicitly useful for robot-oriented physical understanding. This involves extracting detailed scene meta-information (scene elements, spatial dynamics, action execution, and depth-aware relations) and rendering this into physically grounded question-answer (QA) supervision for Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The explicit encoding of latent physical factors (objects, arrangements, spatial evolution, action feasibility, task objectives) is a central design choice.

- **Effective Transfer of Physical Priors to Embodied Control:** To determine if the physical priors acquired from human interaction video can be effectively transferred to downstream robot control policies. This objective addresses the challenge of VLM-to-VLA adaptation, aiming to develop an architectural design that preserves general vision-language capabilities and language sensitivity during adaptation, rather than allowing imitation-dominated post-training to erode previously learned knowledge.

- **Development of a Scalable Annotation Pipeline:** To create a pipeline that transforms human first-person interaction video into structured scene meta-information and physically grounded QA, moving beyond generic free-form captions. This structured data is intended to explicitly train perception, state inference, planning, and execution reasoning within the base VLM.

- **Integrated Adaptation Architecture:** To introduce an architecture that facilitates the transfer of these physical priors into robot control while maintaining broad multimodal capabilities and strong language alignment. The architecture aims to assign robot trajectories a focused role, primarily for embodiment-specific adaptation, rather than for initial acquisition of physical understanding.

- **Demonstration of Data-Efficient Embodied Performance:** To demonstrate that robust human-derived physical priors can support strong downstream embodied performance using only limited, benchmark-specific robot adaptation data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysBrain 1.0 employs a multi-stage methodology comprising a data engine for physical commonsense acquisition from human egocentric video, a physically informed base VLM, and a capability-preserving architecture for VLA adaptation.

#### 3.1. PhysBrain 1.0 Data Engine

The data engine is designed to convert human first-person interaction video into explicit, robot-relevant physical supervision, rather than generic captions. Its design principles emphasize physical explicitness and the separation of structured meta-information from final model supervision.

- **Data Sources and Staged Construction:** The training corpus is assembled in stages from egocentric sources such as Ego4D, BuildAI, and EgoDex, with later expansion to EPIC and SEA-Small. Clips are filtered based on visual quality and camera motion scores. The annotation process focuses on organizing clips into objects, physical properties, spatial relations, depth cues, state changes, and action-relevant dynamics. General multimodal data like FineVision are also incorporated to preserve broad vision-language competence.

- **Structured Scene Meta-Information:** This first layer of annotation extracts structured JSON-formatted records from video segments. A multi-model pool (GPT-5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B, etc.) is used for annotation and cross-checking to ensure quality and diversity. The schema includes three top-level fields:
 - `scene_elements`: Captures static or slowly changing aspects, identifying manipulated objects, nearby objects, and environment details. It records material cues, geometry, and physical state (e.g., folded, rigid, transparent).
 - `spatial_dynamics`: Describes the initial scene layout and how actor-object relations change over time (e.g., approach, contact, reorientation).
 - `action_execution`: Provides a `instruction_brief` for task intent and an `execution_detailed` description emphasizing trajectory, velocity profile, and contact physics.

- **Depth-Aware Spatial Augmentation:** To address limitations in 3D reasoning, point-wise depth estimates from Depth Anything v3 (DA3NESTED-GIANT-LARGE-1.1 model) are associated with scene objects. This supports relative depth QA (closer, farther) and absolute metric distance QA, grounding the model in real-world scale and displacement, which is relevant for robot end-effector control.

- **QA Generation:** This stage converts structured scene meta-information into natural-language QA pairs for VLM training. A full multi-model pool (GPT-5, GPT-5 mini, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B, etc.) is utilized to generate diverse questions and answers. The QA space covers various capability families, including spatial relations, distance and depth, size estimation, visual grounding, viewpoint reasoning, next-step prediction, route planning, affordance and safety, long-horizon planning, object state change, action recognition and counting, temporal ordering, causal/counterfactual reasoning, counting, fine-grained attributes, existence checking, scene text, chart analysis, science/technical knowledge, and visual logic. A single interaction clip can generate multiple forms of supervision.

- **Embodied Reasoning Targets:** For physical interaction, planning, or action feasibility tasks, QA answers are formatted according to `[Perception - Environment] → [Perception - Object] → [Spatial Planning] → [Action Execution]`. This structured answer format aims to enforce an internal thought process aligned with embodied action.

- **Quality Control and Noise Suppression:** Quality checks are integrated at the interfaces between annotation stages. Clips are filtered by quality and motion scores before meta-information extraction. Intermediate records are validated for JSON parsing, required fields, and physical content. Depth processing includes checks for file existence, image loading, and valid depth sampling, with failure statuses assigned to invalid records.

#### 3.2. PhysBrain 1.0 Architecture

The architecture is designed to specialize the model for control without discarding the multimodal and physical priors learned earlier.

- **Physically Informed Base Model:** A general multimodal backbone (Qwen3-VL) is trained with the large-scale, physically grounded QA supervision generated by the data engine. This stage focuses on improving first-person physical scene interpretation (object state, spatial layout, depth, interaction feasibility, temporal dynamics, task structure) while preserving broad multimodal competence through mixed training data.

- **Capability Preservation During Embodied Adaptation (Dual-Pathway):** During VLA training, a dual-pathway architecture is used:
 - **Frozen General Pathway:** Initialized from the physically informed base VLM, it processes visual observations and language instructions as a stable semantic reference.
 - **Trainable Embodied Pathway:** Initialized from the same model family, it learns continuous action generation and is optimized on robot demonstrations.
 The pathways communicate via asymmetric layer-wise fusion, where the embodied pathway queries itself but conditions on stop-gradient features from the general pathway, allowing it to leverage semantic features without updating the frozen reference.

- **Action-Conditioned Language Alignment:** To ensure instruction following and prevent policies from relying solely on visual shortcuts, an action-conditioned language alignment objective is introduced. This involves two branches: a prior branch where action queries attend to vision but not language, and a posterior branch where action queries attend to both vision and language. A log-likelihood-ratio style objective encourages the action representation to retain instruction-relevant information.

- **Unified Action Generation:** Continuous robot actions are decoded from the hidden states of language-conditioned action queries using a flow-matching objective. The predicted trajectory is represented in an end-effector-frame (EEF) action space, consistent with the metric depth QA in the data engine.

- **Robot Adaptation Protocol and Data Efficiency:** The final stage involves fine-tuning PhysBrain 1.0 on benchmark-specific robot trajectories (e.g., Bridge data for SimplerEnv-WidowX, Google Robot data for SimplerEnv-GoogleRobot, NVIDIA GR1 teleoperation data for RoboCasa-GR1, and expert demonstrations for LIBERO). This stage maps the human-derived physical priors to a specific embodiment and action space using limited robot data, aiming for data efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PhysBrain 1.0 was evaluated across multimodal VLM benchmarks, VLA simulation benchmarks, and real-world robot experiments.

#### 4.1. VLM Experiments

- PhysBrain 8B, trained from Qwen3-VL, demonstrated strong overall performance, achieving the best scores on ERQA, PhysBench, MME, MMMU, OCRBench, and TextVQA. Notable improvements include an increase from 43.0 to 45.5 on ERQA and 48.5 to 50.2 on PhysBench, indicating enhanced physically grounded reasoning.
- PhysBrain 4B also showed consistent improvements over Qwen3-VL-4B across all benchmarks, with a significant gain on RealWorldQA from 70.5 to 72.7. These results suggest that the physical commonsense supervision enhances both physically grounded reasoning and general multimodal capability across different model scales.

#### 4.2. VLA Simulation Experiments

PhysBrain 1.0 was adapted and evaluated on SimplerEnv-WidowX, SimplerEnv-GoogleRobot, RoboCasa-GR1, and LIBERO, achieving the best average scores in all reported tables.

- **SimplerEnv-WidowX:** Achieved an 80.2% average success rate, surpassing the strongest prior method, Xiaomi-Robotics-0, by 1.0 percentage point. This performance, particularly on out-of-domain simulation tasks after training on BridgeV2 real-robot data, suggests improved out-of-domain generalization.
- **SimplerEnv-GoogleRobot:** Achieved a 91.33% average success rate, outperforming Xiaomi-Robotics-0 by 2.30 percentage points. Specific gains were observed on "Move Near" (88.8% to 94.8%) and "Pick Coke Can" (100.0%).
- **RoboCasa-GR1:** Achieved a 64.5% average success rate across 24 tabletop manipulation tasks, which is 10.7 percentage points above VP-VLA, the second-best method. This demonstrates transferability to bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks with a different robot embodiment.
- **LIBERO:** Achieved a 98.8% average success rate, slightly improving over Xiaomi-Robotics-0 (98.7%) on a benchmark already approaching saturation for many VLA systems. This indicates that PhysBrain 1.0 maintains strong performance on standardized single-arm imitation tasks without trading off gains on more challenging or out-of-domain benchmarks.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

Real-world experiments were conducted on a Franka Research 3 robot arm for tabletop vegetable grasping and long-horizon tasks.

- **Setup and Data:** A Franka Research 3 robot with a Robotiq gripper was used, with two Intel RealSense D435i cameras. 450 demonstration trajectories were collected across 9 vegetable categories (50 per category) using a SpaceMouse.
- **Single-Object Grasping:** PhysBrain 1.0, post-trained on this Franka data, achieved a 63.3% average success rate across nine vegetable grasping tasks, compared to 47.1% for π
 0.5, representing an average gain of 16.2 percentage points. Improvements were observed across all categories, especially for deformable or visually ambiguous objects.
- **Long-Horizon Tasks:** On tasks requiring multi-step reasoning and instruction following (e.g., "pick up all green vegetables"), PhysBrain 1.0 improved from 31.0% to 45.0% success rate compared to π
 0.5, an average gain of 14.0 percentage points.

These real-world results support the claim that human-derived physical priors can enhance downstream robot adaptation even when the final policy is trained with the same real-robot demonstrations as strong VLA baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysBrain 1.0 introduces a training strategy for embodied foundation models that prioritizes physical understanding before action generation. This approach represents a shift from purely relying on robot trajectory collections as the sole source of embodied capability.

- **Enhanced Data Efficiency and Generalization:** By acquiring physical and spatial priors from large-scale human first-person video, PhysBrain 1.0 suggests a pathway to reduce the dependency on expensive, platform-specific robot data. The improved performance across diverse simulated and real-world embodiments, including strong out-of-domain generalization, indicates that these human-derived priors can make downstream action learning more data-efficient and robust.
- **Broader Physical Understanding:** The research highlights that many physical regularities essential for control (e.g., contact, friction, reachability, stability, appropriate manipulation for deformable/fragile objects) are not robot-specific. Learning these priors from human interaction at scale provides a more comprehensive foundation for VLA systems than imitation learning alone.
- **Architectural Contributions to VLA Adaptation:** The dual-pathway architecture and action-conditioned language alignment objective address critical challenges in VLM-to-VLA transfer. By preserving general multimodal capabilities and maintaining instruction sensitivity, the architecture facilitates effective adaptation to robot control interfaces without catastrophic forgetting or reliance on visual shortcuts.
- **Framework for Future Embodied AI:** PhysBrain 1.0 proposes a framework where physical commonsense acquisition and action imitation play complementary roles: the former shapes the model's understanding of the physical world, and the latter teaches how that understanding is expressed through robot actions. This could guide the development of future embodied AI systems toward more intelligent and adaptable agents capable of reasoning about their physical environment.
- **Identified Limitations:** The authors acknowledge limitations including dependence on upstream perception and annotation quality, errors inherited from depth estimation and object grounding, and the inherent differences between human and robot embodiment constraints. These areas are identified as avenues for future research to further strengthen the approach.
