# ReSplat: Learning Recurrent Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> ReSplat proposes a recurrent Gaussian splatting model that iteratively refines 3D scene representations using rendering error as feedback, achieving state-of-the-art novel view synthesis from sparse views with significantly faster reconstruction and rendering speeds compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Optimization-based 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) achieves high quality but is computationally intensive, requiring per-scene optimization and struggling with sparse input views.
- Single-step feed-forward Gaussian models are efficient but have limited expressive capacity for complex scenes, showing reduced performance and poor generalization to unseen data, view counts, or resolutions.
- Many existing feed-forward methods generate a large number of Gaussians (e.g., one per pixel), leading to scalability issues with many input views or high-resolution images, impacting memory and rendering speed.

## Method

- A two-stage pipeline begins with an Initial ==Gaussian Reconstruction== that predicts a compact set of 3D Gaussians (16x fewer than per-pixel methods) from subsampled depth maps, aggregated with ==kNN== and ==global attention==.
- A weight-sharing ==Recurrent Gaussian Update== network iteratively refines the Gaussian parameters and hidden states in a gradient-free manner for typically four steps.
- Rendering error (pixel-space and feature-space differences between rendered and ground-truth images) is used as a ==feedback signal== to guide the recurrent updates, propagated via global attention to individual Gaussians.

## Results

- ReSplat achieved **27.70 dB** PSNR on DL3DV, outperforming optimization-based 3DGS (**23.46** PSNR) by **4.2 dB** and feed-forward DepthSplat (**24.17** PSNR) by **3.5 dB**, while being **100x** faster in reconstruction and **4x** faster in rendering.
- The recurrent refinement provided a **53x** speedup in reconstruction time for similar quality gains compared to per-scene 3DGS optimization and consistently improved performance across different initializations.
- The model demonstrated robust generalization, achieving up to a **5 dB** PSNR gain when generalizing to lower resolutions, and improved performance on unseen datasets (RealEstate10K, ACID) and varying view counts (**8** to **16** views).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative, gradient-free refinement driven by rendering error feedback enables a feed-forward model to achieve high quality and adaptability similar to optimization-based methods, without their computational cost.
> - Decomposing complex 3D Gaussian prediction into smaller, recurrent steps significantly reduces learning difficulty, enhances expressiveness, and improves generalization to diverse unseen scenarios.
> - Starting with a compact initial set of Gaussians in a subsampled 3D space is crucial for computational efficiency during both initial reconstruction and subsequent recurrent updates, allowing for faster rendering.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis has advanced significantly with the introduction of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). This technique facilitates real-time radiance field rendering, enabling high-quality visual outputs. Within this landscape, two primary paradigms have emerged for generating 3D Gaussian representations:

The first paradigm involves **optimization-based methods**, exemplified by the original 3DGS. These approaches typically achieve high reconstruction quality through iterative, gradient-based optimization for each individual scene. However, this process is computationally intensive, requiring thousands of updates per scene, and can struggle to produce satisfactory results from sparse input views due to potential overfitting or lack of sufficient geometric constraints.

The second paradigm consists of **feed-forward Gaussian splatting models**. Recent advancements in this area, such as MVSplat, DepthSplat, GS-LRM, and Long-LRM, aim to directly predict 3D Gaussian parameters from input images in a single forward pass. This eliminates the need for per-scene optimization, offering substantial computational efficiency and generalization capabilities, particularly for sparse-view scenarios. Despite their efficiency, these single-step feed-forward models are inherently limited in their expressive capacity for complex scenes, and often exhibit reduced performance when generalizing to unseen datasets, varying view counts, or different image resolutions. Additionally, many existing feed-forward methods generate a large number of Gaussians (often one per pixel), leading to scalability issues with many input views or high-resolution images, impacting both memory and rendering speed.

ReSplat positions itself at the intersection of these two paradigms. It addresses the limitations of single-step feed-forward models by introducing a recurrent refinement mechanism, aiming to achieve the adaptability and high quality of iterative optimization without incurring its high computational cost. Simultaneously, it tackles the scalability issues of high Gaussian counts by proposing a compact initial reconstruction. The work draws inspiration from the broader machine learning concept of "Learning to Optimize," where iterative updates are learned through a weight-sharing network rather than explicit gradient computation, enabling superior results and better out-of-distribution generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a feed-forward recurrent Gaussian splatting model, named ReSplat, that can iteratively refine 3D Gaussian representations in a gradient-free manner. This model is designed to achieve state-of-the-art performance in novel view synthesis from sparse input views while maintaining computational efficiency and robust generalization across diverse scenarios.

The motivation behind these objectives is multifaceted:

1. **Overcoming Single-Step Feed-Forward Limitations:** Existing feed-forward Gaussian splatting models, while efficient, are fundamentally constrained by their reliance on a single forward pass for inference. This limitation restricts their ability to capture fine details in complex scenes and often leads to performance degradation when encountering unseen data distributions, view counts, or image resolutions. ReSplat aims to surmount this by introducing a recurrent process that allows for progressive improvement.
2. **Balancing Efficiency and Adaptability:** Optimization-based 3DGS models achieve high quality through iterative updates but are computationally expensive. Single-step feed-forward models are efficient but lack adaptability. The motivation for ReSplat is to bridge this gap by offering a method that combines the computational efficiency of feed-forward inference with the adaptable, high-quality results typically associated with iterative optimization.
3. **Leveraging Rendering Error as Feedback:** A core motivation is the insight that the rendering error—the difference between a rendered image from the current 3D Gaussian prediction and the ground-truth input image—provides a rich and readily available feedback signal. This error can guide a recurrent network to learn effective Gaussian updates without requiring explicit gradient computation. This feedback mechanism is anticipated to naturally adapt to unseen data distributions at test time, thereby enhancing generalization capabilities.
4. **Reducing Learning Difficulty and Increasing Expressiveness:** By decomposing the complex task of 3D Gaussian prediction into smaller, incremental recurrent steps, ReSplat aims to reduce the overall learning difficulty for the network. Each recurrent update allows the model to refine its prediction, effectively simulating a deeper, unrolled network and increasing its expressiveness.
5. **Addressing Computational Overhead from Gaussian Count:** Many contemporary feed-forward Gaussian models generate a very large number of Gaussians (e.g., one per pixel), which scales poorly with increasing input views and image resolutions. This leads to significant computational burden during both the initial prediction and any subsequent refinement processes. A key motivation for ReSplat is to reduce this overhead by designing an initial reconstruction model that predicts a compact set of Gaussians in a subsampled 3D space, making subsequent recurrent updates computationally feasible and efficient.

In summary, ReSplat is motivated by the need for a 3D Gaussian splatting approach that is fast, generalizable, and produces high-fidelity results, particularly in challenging sparse-view settings, by integrating learned recurrent refinement with a compact initial representation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ReSplat pipeline is structured into two main stages: an **Initial Gaussian Reconstruction** followed by **Recurrent Gaussian Updates**. The approach emphasizes gradient-free, feed-forward processing and a compact 3D representation.

#### 3.1. Initial Gaussian Reconstruction

The first stage focuses on generating a compact set of initial 3D Gaussians from `N` posed input images `{I_i}`.

1. **Subsampled 3D Space:**
 - The model starts by predicting per-view depth maps, similar to the DepthSplat architecture. However, a key difference is that these depth maps are generated at `1/4` resolution.
 - These `1/4` resolution depth maps are then unprojected into 3D space using camera parameters, yielding a point cloud. This spatial compression results in `16x` fewer 3D points compared to per-pixel representations, with a total of `M = N * (H*W/16)` points.
 - Each 3D point `p_j` is associated with a feature vector `f_j` extracted from the input images.
2. **Aggregating 3D Context:**
 - To compensate for the reduced number of 3D points (and thus potentially lost detail), the model employs six alternating blocks of kNN attention and global attention.
 - These attention mechanisms process the point features `f_j` along with their 3D coordinates `p_j`, enabling communication between different 3D points. This process aggregates both local and global 3D context, producing enhanced, context-aggregated feature vectors `f*_j`.
3. **Decoding to Gaussians:**
 - The 3D point coordinates `p_j` are directly used as the centers (`μ_j`) for the initial 3D Gaussians.
 - The remaining Gaussian parameters—opacity (`α_j`), covariance (`Σ_j`), and spherical harmonics (`sh_j`)—are decoded from the context-aggregated features `f*_j` using a lightweight two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), referred to as the Gaussian head.
 - The concatenated Gaussian parameters form `g_0_j`. The aggregated feature `f*_j` is also used as the initial hidden state `z_0_j` for the subsequent recurrent process. This results in an initial set of Gaussians `G_0 = {(g_0_j, z_0_j)}`.

#### 3.2. Recurrent Gaussian Update

Following the initial compact reconstruction, a weight-sharing recurrent network iteratively refines the Gaussian parameters and hidden states for `T` steps (typically observed to converge around 4 iterations). This refinement is gradient-free.

1. **Computing the Rendering Error:**
 - At each iteration `t`, the current set of Gaussians `G_t` is used to render the `N` input views, producing `{\hat{I}_t_i}`.
 - A feedback signal is generated by measuring the difference between these rendered views and the ground-truth input views `I_i`. This "rendering error" is a combination of pixel-space and feature-space errors:
 - **Pixel-space error:** Computed as `{\hat{I}_t_i - I_i}`. This error is spatially downsampled `4x` using pixel unshuffle and then projected into a feature space using a linear layer and Layer Normalization to match dimensions.
 - **Feature-space error:** Extracted from the first three stages of an ImageNet pre-trained ResNet-18 applied to both rendered and ground-truth input views. These features (at `1/2`, `1/4`, `1/8` resolutions) are bilinearly resized to `1/4` resolution and then concatenated. The difference is computed as `{\hat{F}_t_i - F_i}`.
 - The two error components are combined via element-wise addition to form `{\hat{E}_t}`.
2. **Propagating the Rendering Error to Gaussians:**
 - To ensure that each 3D Gaussian receives comprehensive feedback, global attention is applied across all the `N * (H*W/16)` rendering errors `{\hat{E}_t}`. This allows local point-wise errors to influence other Gaussians globally.
 - The resulting globally aggregated error `e_t_j` for each Gaussian is then concatenated with its current parameters `g_t_j` and hidden state `z_t_j`, forming `{(g_t_j, z_t_j, e_t_j)}`.
3. **Recurrent Gaussian Update Module:**
 - This module, primarily composed of four kNN attention blocks, takes the concatenated input `{(g_t_j, z_t_j, e_t_j)}` and predicts incremental updates `∆g_t_j` (for Gaussian parameters) and `∆z_t_j` (for the hidden state).
 - These predicted increments are then added to the current Gaussian parameters and hidden states: `g_{t+1}_j = g_t_j + ∆g_t_j` and `z_{t+1}_j = z_t_j + ∆z_t_j`. This process repeats for `T` iterations, leveraging weight-sharing across steps.

#### 3.3. Training Loss

The model is trained in two stages:
1. **Stage 1 (Initial Reconstruction):** The loss `L_1st` combines a rendering loss (`L_render`) on rendered target views (L1 + perceptual loss) and a depth smoothness loss (`L_smooth`) on the predicted depth maps of the input views. The depth smoothness loss acts as a regularization term, encouraging depth gradients to align with image gradients.
2. **Stage 2 (Recurrent Model):** The initial reconstruction model is frozen. The recurrent model is trained end-to-end using the rendering loss `L_render` applied to rendered target views from all Gaussian predictions `G_t` across iterations `t=0` to `T-1`. Exponentially increasing weights are applied to later iterations (`γ = 0.9`), emphasizing the refinement process.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Efficient Global Attention:** To handle high-resolution images efficiently, global attention is computed on features that are spatially downsampled `4x` (to `1/16` total area) using pixel unshuffle, then upsampled back.
- **Efficient kNN:** For scalability with large point clouds, an O(N) local kNN implementation is used instead of a global O(N^2) search. It restricts neighbor searches to spatially adjacent pixels within the same view and projected neighbors from nearby cameras, building a small candidate set from which top-k neighbors are selected using 3D Euclidean distance.
- **Coordinate System:** Empirically, aligning the global reference frame to the spatially central input view's camera pose yielded the best performance, as it balances the spatial distribution of 3D Gaussians.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experimental results demonstrating the performance and capabilities of ReSplat across various benchmarks and configurations.

#### 4.1. Performance on DL3DV (8 views, 512×960 resolution)

- **Quantitative Performance:** ReSplat, after 4 iterations, achieved a PSNR of 27.70 dB, SSIM of 0.868, and LPIPS of 0.160 on the DL3DV dataset.
- **Superior Quality:** It outperformed the optimization-based 3DGS (23.46 PSNR at 4000 iterations) by a significant margin of 4.2 dB PSNR. Compared to feed-forward per-pixel models, ReSplat surpassed DepthSplat (24.17 PSNR) by 3.5 dB PSNR.
- **Efficiency:** ReSplat exhibited a reconstruction time 100 times faster than 3DGS. Crucially, it reduced the number of Gaussians by 16 times (246K vs. 3932K for MVSplat/DepthSplat), leading to a 4 times faster rendering speed. The iterative refinement itself adds minimal overhead per step (0.126s per iteration).

#### 4.2. Optimization-Based vs. Feed-Forward Refinement

- When starting from the same ReSplat initialization, the feed-forward recurrent refinement of ReSplat achieved significant PSNR improvements much faster than per-scene optimization using 3DGS (e.g., comparable improvement in 4 iterations vs. 80 iterations).
- ReSplat’s recurrent refinement provided a 53x speedup in reconstruction time compared to 3DGS for similar quality gains, highlighting the efficiency of its gradient-free updates.

#### 4.3. Generalization Capabilities

ReSplat demonstrated robust generalization, despite being trained solely on DL3DV with 8 input views at 512×960 resolution.

- **Cross-Dataset Generalization (to RealEstate10K):** The recurrent model showed more substantial performance improvements compared to its single-step initialization, indicating its adaptability to unseen data distributions via the rendering error feedback.
- **Cross-View Generalization (8 to 16 views):** The recurrent model benefited more effectively from additional input views, exploiting the extra information for further quality enhancements, whereas the initial model’s performance tended to saturate.
- **Cross-Resolution Generalization (e.g., 512×960 to 320×640):** The recurrent model significantly improved robustness to different testing resolutions, achieving up to a 5 dB PSNR gain when generalizing to lower resolutions.

#### 4.4. Comparison with Single-Step vs. Recurrent Models

- ReSplat-Small (77M parameters, 15M for recurrent part) consistently outperformed significantly larger single-step baselines, including ReSplat-Large (559M) and WorldMirror (1263M).
- This indicates that the recurrent error correction is more parameter-efficient than simply increasing the capacity of a single-step network. A single iteration of ReSplat-Small recurrent model already surpassed ReSplat-Large (init) by 0.31 dB PSNR, and this gap widened to 1.21 dB PSNR after four iterations.

#### 4.5. Consistency Across Different Initializations

- The recurrent model consistently improved rendering quality regardless of the initial Gaussian reconstruction source (e.g., MVSplat, ReSplat-Small, ReSplat-Base). Stronger initializations led to higher final quality.
- ReSplat's compact initialization provided a superior starting point while also enabling more efficient recurrent updates due to the reduced Gaussian count.

#### 4.6. Performance on DL3DV (16 views, 540×960 resolution)

- ReSplat achieved 23.51 PSNR (after 2 iterations), outperforming Long-LRM (22.66 PSNR) by 0.85 dB PSNR, despite reconstructing with 4 times fewer Gaussians.

#### 4.7. Performance on RealEstate10K and ACID (2 views, 256×256 resolution)

- For two-view, low-resolution scenarios, ReSplat achieved state-of-the-art results, surpassing feed-forward 3DGS models like DepthSplat, GS-LRM, and Long-LRM by clear margins.
- It was competitive with the 3DGS-free LVSM (decoder-only variant) while providing a 20 times increase in rendering speed due to its explicit 3D Gaussian representation.
- On the unseen ACID dataset, ReSplat demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization, outperforming previous methods.

#### 4.8. Ablation Studies

- **Rendering Error:** The rendering error was identified as crucial, with its removal leading to a 1.9 dB PSNR drop. A combination of pixel-space and feature-space errors yielded the best performance.
- **Coordinate System:** Aligning the global reference frame to the spatially central input view improved performance by 0.9 dB PSNR compared to using the default COLMAP system.
- **Initial Reconstruction Model:** Both kNN attention and global attention layers were found to be essential for maintaining performance when compressing the Gaussian count by 16 times. These components enabled the initial model to outperform DepthSplat despite having significantly fewer Gaussians.
- **Recurrent Model:** The hidden state input and the use of kNN attention and global attention within the recurrent network were critical for its performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of ReSplat represents a notable advancement in the field of 3D reconstruction and novel view synthesis, particularly within the context of Gaussian Splatting. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Bridging the Gap between Efficiency and Quality:** ReSplat effectively combines the computational efficiency of feed-forward models with the high-quality, adaptable reconstruction typically associated with iterative optimization-based approaches. This balance is crucial for applications requiring both real-time performance and high visual fidelity.
2. **Enhanced Generalization Capabilities:** By leveraging rendering error as a feedback signal, ReSplat demonstrates robust generalization to unseen datasets, varying input view counts, and different image resolutions. This addresses a significant limitation of previous single-step feed-forward models, making it more applicable to real-world, diverse scenarios.
3. **Significant Computational Efficiency:** The compact initial Gaussian representation, which produces 16 times fewer Gaussians than per-pixel baselines, combined with the gradient-free recurrent updates, leads to substantial improvements in reconstruction and rendering speeds. This efficiency makes high-quality 3D content generation more accessible and practical for a wider range of applications.
4. **Novel Recurrent Learning Paradigm:** The work introduces and validates a novel approach to recurrent refinement for 3D Gaussian Splatting, demonstrating that a weight-sharing network can effectively learn to optimize parameters using rendering error as a feedback signal, without relying on explicit gradient computation. This "learning to optimize" framework has broader implications for iterative tasks in computer vision.
5. **Parameter Efficiency:** The ablation studies show that the recurrent refinement is more parameter-efficient than simply scaling up single-step models. A relatively compact recurrent network can achieve superior results compared to significantly larger non-recurrent counterparts, which is advantageous for deployment in resource-constrained environments.
6. **Scalability for Complex Scenes:** The proposed efficient implementations for global and kNN attention, alongside the compact initialization, allow ReSplat to scale effectively to scenes with many input views and high image resolutions, which is challenging for many existing methods.

The potential impact of ReSplat extends to various domains:

- **Real-time 3D Content Creation:** Enabling faster generation of high-quality 3D representations from sparse image inputs could accelerate workflows in industries like gaming, visual effects, and virtual/augmented reality.
- **Consumer 3D Photography:** Making it easier and faster for consumer devices to capture and synthesize 3D content from a few photos.
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Improved 3D scene understanding from sparse camera inputs can benefit navigation, mapping, and interaction capabilities.
- **Research in Learned Optimization:** ReSplat provides a concrete example of successful gradient-free learned optimization in a complex vision task, potentially inspiring further research into recurrent models and feedback mechanisms for other computational problems.

While the paper notes limitations such as a fixed Gaussian count during refinement and saturation after a few iterations, the foundational contributions of ReSplat lay groundwork for future advancements in adaptive 3D Gaussian representations and more sophisticated learned feedback mechanisms.
