# ReSplat: Learning Recurrent Gaussian Splatting

> [!summary] Summary
> ReSplat proposes a recurrent Gaussian splatting model that iteratively refines 3D scene representations using rendering error as feedback, achieving state-of-the-art novel view synthesis from sparse views with significantly faster reconstruction and rendering speeds compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Optimization-based 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) achieves high quality but is computationally intensive, requiring per-scene optimization and struggling with sparse input views.
- Single-step feed-forward Gaussian models are efficient but have limited expressive capacity for complex scenes, showing reduced performance and poor generalization to unseen data, view counts, or resolutions.
- Many existing feed-forward methods generate a large number of Gaussians (e.g., one per pixel), leading to scalability issues with many input views or high-resolution images, impacting memory and rendering speed.

## Method

- A two-stage pipeline begins with an Initial ==Gaussian Reconstruction== that predicts a compact set of 3D Gaussians (16x fewer than per-pixel methods) from subsampled depth maps, aggregated with ==kNN== and ==global attention==.
- A weight-sharing ==Recurrent Gaussian Update== network iteratively refines the Gaussian parameters and hidden states in a gradient-free manner for typically four steps.
- Rendering error (pixel-space and feature-space differences between rendered and ground-truth images) is used as a ==feedback signal== to guide the recurrent updates, propagated via global attention to individual Gaussians.

## Results

- ReSplat achieved **27.70 dB** PSNR on DL3DV, outperforming optimization-based 3DGS (**23.46** PSNR) by **4.2 dB** and feed-forward DepthSplat (**24.17** PSNR) by **3.5 dB**, while being **100x** faster in reconstruction and **4x** faster in rendering.
- The recurrent refinement provided a **53x** speedup in reconstruction time for similar quality gains compared to per-scene 3DGS optimization and consistently improved performance across different initializations.
- The model demonstrated robust generalization, achieving up to a **5 dB** PSNR gain when generalizing to lower resolutions, and improved performance on unseen datasets (RealEstate10K, ACID) and varying view counts (**8** to **16** views).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative, gradient-free refinement driven by rendering error feedback enables a feed-forward model to achieve high quality and adaptability similar to optimization-based methods, without their computational cost.
> - Decomposing complex 3D Gaussian prediction into smaller, recurrent steps significantly reduces learning difficulty, enhances expressiveness, and improves generalization to diverse unseen scenarios.
> - Starting with a compact initial set of Gaussians in a subsampled 3D space is crucial for computational efficiency during both initial reconstruction and subsequent recurrent updates, allowing for faster rendering.
