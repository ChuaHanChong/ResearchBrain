# MoE-GRPO: Optimizing Mixture-of-Experts via Reinforcement Learning in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Korea University and KAIST introduce MoE-GRPO, an RL-based framework to optimize expert routing in Mixture-of-Experts Vision-Language Models. This method learns adaptive expert selection policies, achieving average accuracy gains of 1.7% to 2.3% on multi-modal benchmarks and improving generalization by up to 4.1% on out-of-domain datasets compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Scaling Transformer architectures for higher capacity in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) leads to substantial computational and memory demands.
- Standard deterministic top-K routing in Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures limits exploration of diverse expert combinations, causing expert overfitting and suboptimal selections.
- Heuristic stochasticity in expert selection (e.g., Gaussian noise) does not explicitly optimize a learned policy, missing the full potential for adaptive and efficient computation.

## Method

- Reformulate expert selection in ==MoE-based Vision-Language Models== as a ==sequential decision-making problem==, optimized via ==reinforcement learning==.
- Develop **MoE-GRPO**, an extension of ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, with a broadened action space for ==layer-wise expert selection== and dual objectives (==Token-GRPO== for generation, ==Gate-GRPO== for routing).
- Implement a ==modality-aware router guidance== mechanism to quantify expert specialization and reduce the RL search space, enhancing training stability and efficiency.

## Results

- MoE-GRPO consistently outperformed baseline methods on multi-modal benchmarks, showing average accuracy gains of **2.0%** over deterministic fine-tuning and **1.7%** to **2.3%** over stochastic variants.
- The framework demonstrated improved generalization, achieving an average accuracy gain of **3.1%** on cross-dataset benchmarks and **4.1%** on out-of-domain datasets compared to baseline models.
- Analysis revealed MoE-GRPO fostered more diverse expert utilization (routing entropy increased from **1.05** to **1.82**) and enhanced task-level expert specialization (average JSD increased from **0.06** to **0.20**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing expert selection as an explicit reinforcement learning policy (MoE-GRPO) is more effective than deterministic or heuristically stochastic approaches, improving VLM performance.
> - Effective expert routing requires a dual optimization approach, where Token-GRPO guides token generation and Gate-GRPO provides dense, layer-wise supervision for expert assignment.
> - Modality-aware guidance significantly enhances the efficiency and stability of RL training by narrowing the expert search space to those relevant to the input modality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large-scale Transformer architectures, including Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs), has witnessed significant performance advancements through increased model capacity. However, these gains are accompanied by substantial computational and memory costs during both training and inference. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have emerged as a strategy to address this inefficiency by activating only a sparse subset of parameters for each token, thereby maintaining high model capacity while reducing computational overhead. This MoE paradigm has been extended to VLMs, facilitating scalable multi-modal understanding.

A prevalent limitation in current MoE implementations is the reliance on deterministic top-K routing mechanisms, which select experts based on gating scores. This deterministic approach restricts the exploration of diverse expert combinations, potentially leading to suboptimal expert assignments and a phenomenon known as expert overfitting, where a small subset of experts becomes excessively utilized. While some efforts have introduced stochasticity, such as adding Gaussian noise to gating scores, these are often heuristic and do not explicitly optimize the expert selection *policy*.

Concurrently, reinforcement learning (RL) based fine-tuning methods, like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), have demonstrated effectiveness in aligning LLM and VLM outputs with desired preferences or verifiable rewards. These methods typically optimize the sequence generation process itself. This research extends the application of RL from optimizing output sequences to optimizing the internal mechanism of expert selection within MoE architectures. By formulating expert selection as a sequential decision-making problem, this work aims to introduce a policy-based approach to expert routing, departing from heuristic or deterministic methods and thereby enhancing the diversity and optimality of expert utilization in VLMs. This represents a novel application of RL within the MoE landscape for multi-modal models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central problem addressed by this research is the sub-optimality and lack of diversity in expert selection within existing Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, particularly in the context of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Current MoE models commonly employ a deterministic top-K routing strategy, which selects a fixed number of experts based on their gating scores. This approach presents several limitations:
- **Restricted Exploration**: The deterministic nature of top-K routing limits the model's ability to explore a broader range of expert combinations for a given input token. This can lead to missing potentially more optimal or diverse expert selections that might yield better performance or generalization.
- **Expert Overfitting**: The consistent selection of the same few experts can cause them to become overspecialized, while others remain underutilized. This phenomenon, termed expert overfitting, can hinder the model's overall capacity utilization and generalization capabilities across varied tasks or data distributions.
- **Heuristic Stochasticity**: Prior attempts to introduce stochasticity, such as adding noise to gating scores, have been largely heuristic. These methods do not explicitly learn or optimize an expert selection *policy* based on performance feedback, thus offering only partial solutions to the exploration challenge.

Motivated by the success of reinforcement learning (RL) in optimizing sequential decision-making problems, especially Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) in refining LLM and VLM outputs based on verifiable rewards, this paper aims to apply a similar principle to expert routing. The key objectives are:
1. **Formulate Expert Selection as a Sequential Decision-Making Problem**: To reconceptualize the process of choosing experts at each layer and for each token as a sequence of actions amenable to RL optimization.
2. **Develop an RL-based Framework for Expert Routing**: To propose MoE-GRPO, a novel framework that leverages GRPO to learn an adaptive expert routing policy. This policy would guide the model to explore diverse expert assignments and reinforce those that lead to higher task-specific rewards, thereby moving beyond deterministic or simple stochastic routing.
3. **Enhance Training Stability and Efficiency with Modality-Aware Guidance**: To introduce a mechanism that constrains the exploration space during RL training. Specifically, a modality-aware router guidance aims to discourage the selection of experts that are infrequently associated with a given input modality (e.g., visual or textual tokens), thus making the learning process more stable and efficient by focusing exploration on relevant expert subsets.

Ultimately, the goal is to improve the diversity of expert utilization, mitigate expert overfitting, and achieve better task-level expert specialization, leading to enhanced generalization and performance in MoE-based VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MoE-GRPO framework addresses the expert selection problem by formulating it as a sequential decision-making process optimized through a modified Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

- **Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)**: In a Transformer architecture, an MoE layer routes each token's hidden representation `h_t,l` to a subset of `N` experts, `e_k,l`, which are typically Feed-Forward Networks (FFNs). A gating network `g_l` computes gating scores `g_l(h_t,l)` representing assignment probabilities. The standard MoE selects the `top-K` experts and calculates the output `o_t,l` as a weighted sum of their contributions. This deterministic top-K selection is the target for improvement.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**: GRPO is an RL algorithm based on Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It enhances training efficiency by using group-based relative rewards. For an input `x`, `G` rollout sequences `{y_i}` are sampled from an `old` policy (`π_old`). The objective `L_GRPO` optimizes the current policy `π_θ` by reinforcing high-reward actions and discouraging low-reward ones, based on a normalized advantage `ˆA_i` derived from the relative rewards `R_i` within each group.

#### 3.2. MoE-GRPO Framework

The core innovation of MoE-GRPO is the expansion of the action space and the introduction of two complementary optimization objectives:
- **Expanded Action Space**: Unlike standard GRPO where an action is the generation of the next output token, MoE-GRPO defines an action as the selection of `top-K` experts for a given token at a specific layer. This means the policy makes decisions across all tokens and layers (`[o_1,1..., o_T,L]`), offering fine-grained control over the model's internal processing.
- **Token-GRPO (L_Token-GRPO)**: This objective focuses on optimizing the quality of the generated token sequence. Given an input `x`, the gating networks `g_old` sample `G` expert assignment rollouts `E_i`. For each `E_i`, a token sequence `y_i` is generated by `π_old`. `L_Token-GRPO` updates the model's parameters to reinforce expert selection policies (`E_i`) that lead to high-reward token generations (`y_i`), based on relative rewards within the rollout group. This ensures that expert routing decisions are aligned with the ultimate task output.
- **Gate-GRPO (L_Gate-GRPO)**: This objective directly refines the layer-wise expert routing policy. It uses the same advantage values `ˆA_i` from the rollout group but applies them to optimize the gating function `g_l` for each specific expert selection `E_i_t,l` (set of experts chosen for the t-th token at the l-th layer). This provides dense, fine-grained supervision signals to the routing modules at every layer, encouraging gating networks to assign higher probabilities to experts that contributed to high-reward outcomes.
- **Combined Objective**: The final training objective `L_MoE-GRPO` is the sum of `L_Token-GRPO` and `L_Gate-GRPO`, enabling joint optimization of token-level generation and layer-wise expert selection.
- **Reward Function**: An accuracy-based reward is employed, assigning 1 for a correct model prediction and 0 otherwise. This binary signal is transformed into dense supervision for sequential expert selection decisions by propagating the group-computed advantages to the router at each layer and token position.
- **Absence of Reference Policy**: Since gating networks are trained from scratch with MoE-GRPO, no pre-trained routing policy exists, and thus, a KL divergence regularization term (common in conventional GRPO) is not used.

#### 3.3. Modality-Aware Router Guidance

To improve the efficiency and stability of RL training, which often involves exploring a vast search space of expert selections, a modality-aware router guidance mechanism is introduced:
- **Modality Specialization Scores**: For each expert `e_i`, vision-awareness `ˆs_v(e_i)` and text-awareness `ˆs_t(e_i)` scores are computed. These scores are derived from the number of times an expert is selected by vision tokens (`N_v(e_i)`) or text tokens (`N_t(e_i)`) during initial top-K routing. The scores are normalized to reflect the expert's affinity for each modality.
- **Constrained Exploration**: During rollouts, for a given modality (e.g., visual tokens), the bottom `P%` of experts with the lowest modality-awareness scores for that modality are temporarily deactivated by setting their gating scores to `-∞`. This restricts the multinomial sampling process to experts that are more relevant to the current input modality.
- **Benefit**: This guidance reduces unnecessary exploration of irrelevant experts, thereby enhancing training stability, accelerating convergence, and allowing the router to focus on modality-consistent expert combinations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates MoE-GRPO across multi-modal image and video understanding benchmarks, as well as on cross-dataset and domain generalization tasks.

#### 4.1. Performance on Multi-modal Understanding Benchmarks

Using an InternVL3.5-1B based MoE architecture (1.3B activated out of 2.9B total parameters), MoE-GRPO was compared against standard MoE fine-tuning baselines: Deterministic Fine-Tuning (Det-FT), Stochastic Fine-Tuning with Multinomial Sampling (Stoch-FT-Multi), and Stochastic Fine-Tuning with Gaussian Noise (Stoch-FT-Noise).
- MoE-GRPO consistently outperformed all three baselines. For instance, it achieved average accuracy gains of 2.0% over Det-FT, 2.3% over Stoch-FT-Multi, and 1.7% over Stoch-FT-Noise across 7 out of 9 evaluated benchmarks.
- These results suggest that direct optimization of the expert selection policy via RL leads to more robust performance and improved generalization compared to deterministic or heuristic stochastic routing approaches.

#### 4.2. Cross-Dataset and Domain Generalization

To assess generalization, MoE-GRPO was applied to CLIP-MoE (0.7B activated out of 1.2B parameters) and trained on ImageNet.
- **Cross-dataset Generalization**: When evaluated on 10 target datasets, CLIP-MoE + Det-FT showed performance degradation, indicating overfitting to the training data. In contrast, MoE-GRPO surpassed Det-FT on 9 out of 10 datasets, achieving an average accuracy gain of 3.1%.
- **Domain Generalization**: For out-of-domain evaluation on four datasets, Det-FT often resulted in accuracy drops compared to the base CLIP-MoE. MoE-GRPO consistently outperformed both CLIP-MoE and CLIP-MoE + Det-FT, with average gains of 4.1% and 1.5%, respectively.
- These findings indicate that RL-based expert routing helps mitigate overfitting and improves generalization by promoting more diverse expert utilization.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **MoE-GRPO Components**:
 - Using only Token-GRPO resulted in a 1.8% average accuracy drop compared to the full MoE-GRPO, implying that while token-level optimization captures the main reward signal, explicit layer-wise routing regularization is also beneficial.
 - Using only Gate-GRPO led to a substantial 4.8% average accuracy drop, indicating that optimizing expert routing without explicit shaping of token-level generation is insufficient for aligning with task-level rewards. This demonstrates the complementary and necessary nature of both Token-GRPO and Gate-GRPO.
- **Modality-Aware Router Guidance**:
 - Modality-aware router guidance outperformed modality-agnostic exploration baselines (Gaussian noise and multinomial sampling) by 1.5% and 0.9% in average accuracy, respectively.
 - Training curves showed that modality-aware guidance converged more rapidly in terms of mean reward and exhibited lower reward variance, indicating improved stability and efficiency during policy learning by avoiding irrelevant expert exploration.
- **RL Methods**: Comparisons with DAPO and SAPO within the MoE-GRPO framework showed comparable average accuracy, suggesting that the expert selection policy optimization is robust across different GRPO variants.
- **Existing Routing Methods**: MoE-GRPO outperformed Expert Choice routing and Optimal Transport routing by 1.0% and 1.3% in average accuracy, respectively. It was also found to be complementary to the auxiliary load-balancing loss, with their combination yielding an additional 0.9% performance improvement.

#### 4.4. Analysis of Routing Policy

- **Expert Selection Diversity**: MoE-GRPO exhibited a more diverse token-level expert activation pattern compared to Det-FT, increasing the entropy of the routing distribution from 1.05 to 1.82. Det-FT predominantly activated only two experts.
- **Task-Level Specialization**: On the MVBench dataset, MoE-GRPO demonstrated more distinct expert activation patterns across 20 task categories, indicating stronger task-level specialization. The average Jensen-Shannon divergence (JSD) of task-wise expert distributions increased from 0.06 to 0.20 under MoE-GRPO.
- **Qualitative Analysis**: Deeper layers in both Det-FT and MoE-GRPO showed increasing routing confidence. However, MoE-GRPO exhibited greater variability in routing probabilities across layers, suggesting less reliance on specific experts. This adaptive routing enabled MoE-GRPO to produce correct answers in cases where Det-FT failed.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces MoE-GRPO, a novel reinforcement learning (RL)-based framework for optimizing expert routing in Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) based Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Pioneering RL for Expert Routing**: This work is presented as the first to formulate expert selection as a sequential decision-making problem and apply RL for its optimization within MoE-based VLMs. This extends the application of RL beyond merely optimizing model outputs to refining internal architectural mechanisms, opening a new direction for improving sparse models.
- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization**: MoE-GRPO consistently outperforms conventional deterministic and heuristic stochastic routing methods across a variety of multi-modal image and video understanding tasks. Furthermore, it demonstrates improved generalization capabilities in cross-dataset and out-of-domain settings, mitigating the overfitting often observed with deterministic routing.
- **Diverse and Specialized Expert Utilization**: The framework fosters more diverse expert activation at the token level, reducing over-reliance on a small subset of experts and promoting balanced expert utilization. Concurrently, it enhances task-level expert specialization, enabling experts to adapt more effectively to the specific requirements of different tasks. This leads to a more efficient and adaptive use of the MoE model's total capacity.
- **Improved Training Dynamics**: The introduction of modality-aware router guidance significantly enhances training stability and efficiency. By directing exploration towards experts relevant to the current input modality, it reduces unnecessary exploration, allowing for faster convergence and more robust policy learning.
- **Broader Applicability**: While demonstrated on VLMs, the core principles of MoE-GRPO could be transferable to other MoE architectures, including those in Large Language Models (LLMs). This could lead to more efficient, adaptable, and robust sparse models across various domains, contributing to the development of larger and more capable AI systems with reduced computational footprints.

In conclusion, MoE-GRPO offers a principled and effective method for optimizing expert selection in MoE architectures, addressing limitations of prior approaches and demonstrating substantial improvements in performance, generalization, and expert utilization efficiency. This work contributes to the broader understanding and development of sparse neural networks and their application in complex multi-modal contexts.
