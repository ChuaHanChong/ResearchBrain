# MoE-GRPO: Optimizing Mixture-of-Experts via Reinforcement Learning in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Korea University and KAIST introduce MoE-GRPO, an RL-based framework to optimize expert routing in Mixture-of-Experts Vision-Language Models. This method learns adaptive expert selection policies, achieving average accuracy gains of 1.7% to 2.3% on multi-modal benchmarks and improving generalization by up to 4.1% on out-of-domain datasets compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Scaling Transformer architectures for higher capacity in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) leads to substantial computational and memory demands.
- Standard deterministic top-K routing in Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures limits exploration of diverse expert combinations, causing expert overfitting and suboptimal selections.
- Heuristic stochasticity in expert selection (e.g., Gaussian noise) does not explicitly optimize a learned policy, missing the full potential for adaptive and efficient computation.

## Method

- Reformulate expert selection in ==MoE-based Vision-Language Models== as a ==sequential decision-making problem==, optimized via ==reinforcement learning==.
- Develop **MoE-GRPO**, an extension of ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, with a broadened action space for ==layer-wise expert selection== and dual objectives (==Token-GRPO== for generation, ==Gate-GRPO== for routing).
- Implement a ==modality-aware router guidance== mechanism to quantify expert specialization and reduce the RL search space, enhancing training stability and efficiency.

## Results

- MoE-GRPO consistently outperformed baseline methods on multi-modal benchmarks, showing average accuracy gains of **2.0%** over deterministic fine-tuning and **1.7%** to **2.3%** over stochastic variants.
- The framework demonstrated improved generalization, achieving an average accuracy gain of **3.1%** on cross-dataset benchmarks and **4.1%** on out-of-domain datasets compared to baseline models.
- Analysis revealed MoE-GRPO fostered more diverse expert utilization (routing entropy increased from **1.05** to **1.82**) and enhanced task-level expert specialization (average JSD increased from **0.06** to **0.20**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing expert selection as an explicit reinforcement learning policy (MoE-GRPO) is more effective than deterministic or heuristically stochastic approaches, improving VLM performance.
> - Effective expert routing requires a dual optimization approach, where Token-GRPO guides token generation and Gate-GRPO provides dense, layer-wise supervision for expert assignment.
> - Modality-aware guidance significantly enhances the efficiency and stability of RL training by narrowing the expert search space to those relevant to the input modality.
