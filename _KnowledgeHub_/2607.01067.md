# Human-Centric Transferable Tactile Pre-Training for Dexterous Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A new system, TTP, integrates human-centric transferable tactile pre-training into Vision-Language-Action models, leveraging a large-scale human tactile-action dataset (H-Tac). This approach enables dexterous robots to achieve superior performance, such as 96.7% progress in fine-grained tasks, and robust generalization in contact-rich and vision-deficient manipulation, significantly outperforming tactile-free baselines.

## Problem

- Dexterous robotic manipulation tasks requiring precise contact interaction often fail when VLA models rely solely on visual cues, which lack essential tactile feedback.
- Collecting large-scale tactile data directly from robots is resource-intensive and expensive, and existing large-scale human demonstration datasets focus mainly on visual and action modalities, leaving human tactile interaction data untapped.
- Prior methods for integrating tactile sensing into VLA models typically involve post-training or explicit alignment, which can lead to inefficient learning and domain mismatch issues.

## Method

- Developed the Human-Centric Transferable Tactile Pre-Training (**TTP**) system, which extends a ==VLA foundation model== with a ==tactile modality==, a ==dual-expert architecture==, and a ==Tactile-Action Manifold-Preserving Gating (MPG)== module that modulates context enhancement based on reliability.
- Created **H-Tac**, a large-scale ==egocentric human video dataset== (160 hours, 300+ tasks) with dense tactile and action annotations, serving as the pre-training data source.
- Employed unified ==351-taxel tactile== and ==200-dimensional action spaces== to ensure morphological consistency and facilitate seamless human-to-robot knowledge transfer across diverse robot embodiments.

## Results

- TTP achieved an average progress rate of **96.7%** in fine-grained real-robot tasks, significantly outperforming tactile-free baselines (e.g., BeingH-0.5 at **57.3%**, π0.5 at **43.2%**) and demonstrating robust, controlled behaviors.
- The system showed strong generalization capabilities across out-of-distribution scenarios (e.g., different objects, locations, scenes), maintaining high performance even when facing unseen variations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Manifold-Preserving Gating module and the tactile expert are critical, and that scaling pre-training data consistently improved performance and reduced motion prediction errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-training VLA models on large-scale human-centric tactile-action data imbues tactile-grounded priors crucial for fine-grained manipulation, and a unified representation (e.g., UniTacHand) with consistent action spaces preserves this knowledge across human-to-robot embodiment transfer.
> - Explicitly modeling contact dynamics through a dual-expert architecture, which predicts both future actions and future tactile readings, enables precise, stable, and fine-grained robotic manipulation.
> - The Manifold-Preserving Gating mechanism enhances prediction robustness by dynamically adjusting feature corrections based on the reliability of the current context relative to action and tactile manifolds.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly for dexterous and contact-rich tasks, faces challenges in achieving precise physical interaction. While visual perception has advanced significantly in robotics, it often proves insufficient for scenarios involving occlusion, ambiguity, or fine-grained force control, such as assembly, threading, or handling delicate objects. Tactile sensing is recognized as a crucial modality that provides essential force feedback, which cannot be reliably inferred from vision alone.

Historically, the development of tactile-based manipulation has been hindered by limitations in hardware and data collection. Existing datasets incorporating tactility are typically small in scale and cover a narrow range of contact scenarios. Collecting tactile data on real robots is resource-intensive and expensive, partly due to the non-unified nature of tactile sensors across different robotic embodiments, especially dexterous hands, and the labor required for teleoperating robots in contact-rich tasks.

Concurrently, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have emerged as a prominent paradigm for complex and long-horizon robotic tasks, demonstrating capabilities in planning and semantic reasoning. Many VLA models leverage large-scale egocentric human videos, as human data is comparatively easier and more scalable to acquire. However, these models predominantly rely on visual cues and often overlook tactile information, leading to degraded performance in contact-intensive situations. Prior attempts to integrate tactile modality into VLA models have largely focused on post-training or explicit alignment processes, sometimes encountering issues like vision-tactile mismatch or dynamics-agnostic training limitations.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing a scalable approach to tactile pre-training that integrates into the VLA framework. It aims to bridge the disparity between the ease of collecting human demonstration data and the difficulty of acquiring robot tactile data, while endowing VLA models with tactile-grounded priors. The work positions itself as a method to unify large-scale human-centric tactile data collection with architectural advancements in VLA models, enabling robust human-to-robot skill transfer for fine-grained manipulation. Previous works like OpenTouch, EgoPressure, and VTDexManip have provided tactile datasets, while efforts like TactAlign and UniTacHand have explored universal tactile representations for human-to-robot transfer. This paper builds upon these by developing a comprehensive system for tactile pre-training at scale within the VLA paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a human-centric transferable tactile pre-training system, named TTP, for dexterous robotic manipulation. This system aims to enable robots to perform complex, fine-grained tasks that necessitate precise contact interaction, going beyond the capabilities of purely vision-based or dynamics-agnostic approaches.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in current robotic manipulation research:

1. **Necessity of Tactile Sensing for Dexterous Tasks:** For tasks involving intricate contact, such as assembly, manipulating fragile items, or handling occluded objects, tactile sensing is crucial. Visual perception alone is often insufficient to provide the precise force feedback required for robust execution in these scenarios. The paper highlights that current VLA models, which lack tactile sensing, systematically ignore this critical information, leading to performance limitations.

2. **Difficulty and Cost of Robot Tactile Data Collection:** Acquiring large-scale, diverse tactile data directly from real robots is a significant hurdle. This difficulty arises from the non-uniformity of tactile sensor hardware across different robotic embodiments and the labor-intensive nature of teleoperating robots for contact-rich tasks. This limitation restricts the scale and variety of available robot tactile datasets, hindering the training of robust tactile-aware policies.

3. **Untapped Potential of Human Demonstrations:** Human demonstrations are a comparatively easier and more scalable source of data for robot learning. However, existing large-scale human demonstration datasets predominantly focus on visual and action modalities, neglecting tactile information. This creates a gap where valuable human tactile interaction data remains largely unutilized for training advanced robotic skills.

4. **Limitations of Existing VLA Models:** While VLA models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in high-level planning and semantic reasoning, their reliance on visual cues means they often treat physical interactions in a dynamics-agnostic manner. Existing approaches for integrating tactile modality into VLA models have typically involved post-training or direct injection of tactile tokens, which can lead to inefficient learning, domain mismatch between pre-training and post-training, or the burden of learning tactile-vision alignment during the fine-tuning phase.

To address these motivations, the authors propose TTP with the following key objectives:

- **Create a Large-Scale Human-Centric Tactile-Action Dataset:** To overcome the data scarcity issue, a foundational objective is to collect and open-source a large-scale dataset, H-Tac, comprising egocentric human videos with dense tactile and action annotations. This dataset is designed to provide a rich resource for tactile-conditioned pre-training.
- **Develop a Transferable Tactile Pre-Training System:** The core objective is to integrate tactile-based pre-training within the VLA paradigm using the collected human data. This aims to imbue the model with domain-relevant priors and implicitly align tactile signals with vision and language, enabling effective human-to-robot transfer.
- **Bridge the Human-Robot Gap:** To facilitate seamless transfer, a crucial objective is to employ unified tactile and action spaces across human pre-training and robot post-training phases. This consistency is intended to preserve prior knowledge and mitigate distribution mismatch during transfer.
- **Model Contact Dynamics Explicitly:** The system aims to explicitly model fine-grained physical interactions and contact dynamics through a dual-expert architecture, including a tactile expert for future tactile prediction, alongside an action expert for future action generation.
- **Demonstrate Superior Performance and Generalization:** The ultimate objective is to validate that the pre-trained model, after post-training on downstream robotic tasks, achieves superior performance in real-world tactile-relevant tasks, exhibiting robust generalization across various embodiments and fine-grained manipulation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Transferable Tactile Pre-Training (TTP) system is designed as a human-centric framework for learning fine-grained robotic manipulation skills. It consists of three main components: a large-scale tactile-action dataset (H-Tac), a tactile-based pre-training phase, and a post-training phase for downstream robotic tasks.

#### 3.1. H-Tac Dataset Collection for Pre-Training

The H-Tac dataset is a composite of three sub-datasets, totaling 160 hours of egocentric human videos, covering over 300 tasks and 135,000 episodes. This dataset is designed to provide dense tactile and action annotations.

1. **HOI-Tac Dataset:** This dataset is derived from multiple public hand-object interaction datasets (e.g., ARCTIC, DexYCB, H2O). For each video frame, per-vertex binary contact labels are generated on a 778-vertex MANO hand mesh by thresholding distances between hand and object surfaces. These contact signals are then projected onto a 351-taxel UniTacHand UV space to serve as tactile supervision. HOI-Tac contains approximately 11.5 million frames (around 106 hours) across 124.8K sequences, featuring single-hand and bimanual grasps, static and dynamic interactions, and diverse environments.

2. **DeskTask-Tac Dataset:** This dataset focuses on bimanual manipulation in desktop scenarios. A custom data collection system employs three RealSense cameras (two external, one egocentric) to record videos. Each episode's timeline is centered on the first-person video, synchronizing hand geometry, tactile data, task labels, and action targets. Two pipelines are supported for hand reconstruction:
 - **RawHand Pipeline:** Uses image-based hand keypoint detection and multi-view triangulation to recover 3D hand states, requiring only a tactile glove.
 - **AprilTag Pipeline:** Combines scene AprilTags with MANO hand reference structures and tactile information, requiring both tactile and motion capture (MoCap) gloves.
 Post-processing involves temporal sampling/interpolation of multi-view observations, tactile readings, and hand states. The Spatial Tactile representation combines tactile signals, MANO parameters, and wrist pose to estimate 3D coordinates for each tactile unit, which are then mapped to an 891-dimensional UV vertex and validated to 351 dimensions. DeskTask-Tac comprises 37.2 hours of 30 Hz data (947 episodes, 4M frames).

3. **Tactile-Augmented InternData-Tac Dataset:** This augments the InternDataEngine pipeline with a lightweight tactile recorder, saving contact forces and patch details (position, normal, distance, force) as an episodic sidecar alongside original visuo-linguo-motor streams. To ensure cross-embodiment supervision, collected contact patches are projected onto a shared MANO hand surface. Active contact patches are transformed to the MANO frame, dispersed to adjacent vertices via a Gaussian kernel, and converted into a compact 351-D tactile vector representing local vertex pressure. Near-surface zero-force patches contribute distance-decayed pseudo-contact signals for geometric proximity supervision. This dataset totals 17.8 hours of 30 Hz data (9,563 episodes, 1.9M frames) across three robot configurations: Genie1, Lift2, and Split ALOHA.

**Dataset Statistics:** The aggregated H-Tac dataset reveals specific patterns, such as right hands exhibiting higher tactile magnitudes overall, and fingertip tactile readings being more significant than those on palms, suggesting more frequent hand-object contacts at fingertips. Language instructions predominantly start with verbs, with "grasp" being the most frequent.

#### 3.2. Tactile-Based Pre-Training (TTP Model Architecture)

The TTP model builds upon BeingH-0.5, a VLA foundation model, by extending its visual-language model (VLM) component to include tactile modality and introducing a novel dual-expert system.

1. **Problem Formulation:** At each timestep `t`, the policy receives a language instruction `l`, RGB images `I_t`, proprioceptive state `s_t`, and a strided tactile history `O_hist_t`. The model predicts a future action chunk `A_t` and future tactile readings `O+t` over a prediction horizon `K`. The tactile history `O_hist_t` is a sequence of `L` tactile observations `o_t` sampled with a temporal stride `d`.

2. **Unified Action and Tactile Space:** To maintain consistency between human pre-training and robot post-training across diverse embodiments (human hands, dexterous robot hands, grippers), TTP employs unified action and tactile spaces.
 - **Unified Action Space:** A 200-dimensional space semantically organized into slots for end-effector pose, dexterous hand actions, human MANO values, gripper states, and arm/base joints.
 - **Unified Tactile Space:** Based on UniTacHand, this space consists of 351 taxels distributed on the MANO hand model surface. Piezoresistive tactilities from different embodiments are projected onto these taxels, ensuring morphological consistency and enabling cross-embodiment tactile prediction.

3. **Large Scale Tactile Pre-Training:** Supervision is formulated as a unified multimodal sequence in a VQA-style format. The query includes image, language, proprioceptive state, and tactile observation tokens, while the answer contains action and tactile prediction tokens. TTP utilizes a flow matching objective. For each modality (action `act` and tactile `tac`), noisy trajectories `x_m_τ_m` are constructed from clean targets `x_m_1` and Gaussian noise `x_m_0`. The action and tactile experts predict the target velocity `u_m` (difference between clean target and noise), minimizing a flow matching loss `L_m`. The total objective is a weighted sum of action and tactile losses.

4. **Tactile-Action Manifold-Preserving Gating (MPG):** MPG is applied to the flow-time-dependent context `H_t,τ` (suffix token features projected to the VLM hidden space, including proprioceptive state, tactile observation, noisy action, and noisy tactile prediction) before velocity decoding. MPG computes a reliability gate `g` based on the sliced Wasserstein distance (SWD) between the context `H_t,τ` and noise-free action (`Z_nf,act`) and tactile (`Z_nf,tac`) anchors. The gate `g` modulates a residual enhancement to the context, `H̃_t,τ = H_t,τ + λ [W_MPG (g_sg ⊙ E_obs (H_t,τ)) + b_MPG]`. This mechanism ensures that the feature-dependent correction is applied only when the context is reliable (high `g`), thereby improving robustness against context shifts and unstable predictions. The dual-anchor design ensures alignment with both action and tactile manifolds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents findings from visualizations, simulation experiments, and real-robot experiments, along with ablation studies, to evaluate the TTP system.

#### 4.1. Visualizations of Tactile-Aided Pre-Training

Visualizations demonstrated TTP's capability to generate plausible hand motion and tactile predictions. The tactile predictions, rendered as a heatmap on the MANO hand surface, showed reasonable contact patterns. The model also exhibited generalization capabilities by generating motion and tactile predictions for out-of-distribution (OOD) inpainted scenes, including those with empty or robot arms replacing human hands.

#### 4.2. Simulation Environment Experiments

TTP was evaluated on three simulation benchmarks: LIBERO, LIBERO-plus, and RoboCasa. Since these benchmarks do not inherently feature tactile modalities, a "tactile proxy" (padding of `s_t - a_t-1`) was used during post-training to maintain the dual-level optimization objective.

- **Overall Performance:** TTP achieved comparable performance to state-of-the-art baselines like BeingH-0.5, even with the added complexity and computational burden of the tactile modality.
- **Generalization (LIBERO-plus):** For zero-shot tasks in LIBERO-plus, TTP demonstrated improved generalization capabilities, achieving better performance in Language, Light, and Layout categories.
- **RoboCasa:** TTP showed comparable or superior performance across all task types in the RoboCasa benchmark, including Pick & Place, Doors/Drawers, and other manipulation tasks, securing the best overall performance.

#### 4.3. Real Robot Experiments

Experiments on diverse real-robot platforms and embodiments were conducted to assess TTP's fine-grained manipulation capabilities and human-to-robot transfer effectiveness.

- **Hardware Diversity:** Experiments utilized Franka 7-DoF arms, Realman 6-DoF dual arms, Inspire 6-DoF dexterous hands (piezoresistive tactility), DM-Tac parallel grippers (visuo-tactile sensors), and DexBotic 12-DoF dexterous hands (3D tactile sensors).
- **Task Diversity:** Nine tasks were categorized into Fine-Grained (e.g., Peeling, VaseWiping), Contact-Rich & Fragile (e.g., PickPlaceChips, PaperFolding), and Vision Defect (e.g., SoftHard, PlugIn).
- **Performance Against Baselines (Table 4):** TTP substantially outperformed tactile-free state-of-the-art baselines (π0.5, BeingH-0.5) across all task categories.
 - **Fine-grained tasks:** TTP achieved an average progress rate of 96.7%, significantly higher than BeingH-0.5 (57.3%) and π0.5 (43.2%).
 - **Contact-rich & Fragile tasks:** TTP achieved 79.2%, demonstrating a large improvement over BeingH-0.5 (9.2%) and π0.5 (3.3%).
 - **Vision Defect tasks:** TTP achieved 37.8%, compared to BeingH-0.5 (15.6%) and π0.5 (17.8%).
- **Behavioral Observations:** TTP exhibited robust and controlled behavior, such as continuously peeling radish skin for extended lengths, and applying moderate, non-damaging grasping forces for fragile potato chips, whereas baseline methods frequently showed jittery movements or failed due to incorrect force application.
- **Ablation with Pre-training (TTP vs. TTP w/o pre-training):** TTP, with tactile-based pre-training, consistently outperformed its variant without pre-training (TTP w/o pre-training) across all categories. For example, in fine-grained tasks, TTP achieved 96.7% compared to 71.0% for TTP w/o pre-training, validating the benefit of leveraging pre-trained prior knowledge.
- **In-Distribution (ID) and Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Results (Tables 5 & 6):**
 - **ID Results:** TTP showed high success rates and progress in ID scenarios, such as 23.33 cm peeled skin length for Peeling (Inspire) and 100% success for VaseWiping (single hand).
 - **OOD Results:** TTP maintained strong performance in OOD scenarios (e.g., peeling different vegetables, folding various paper-like materials, handling unseen soft/hard objects, plugging into a black socket). For instance, in OOD Peeling (Inspire), TTP achieved 19.12 cm compared to 11.25 cm for TTP w/o pre-training and ~5 cm for baselines. This demonstrates robust generalization across object, location, and scene variations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

1. **Model Design (Table 7):** Ablations on the pre-training phase showed that both the Tactile-Action Manifold-Preserving Gating (MPG) module and the tactile expert for future tactile prediction positively contributed to the model's performance. Excluding either MPG or the tactile expert resulted in higher motion prediction errors (MPJPE, PA-MPJPE, MPJAE, PA-MPJAE) on the validation set, indicating the effectiveness of these design choices.

2. **Scaling (Table 8):** An ablation study on data scaling demonstrated that increasing the amount of pre-training data led to a reduction in motion prediction errors. Performance consistently improved as the training data percentage increased from 10% to 100%, suggesting that the proposed tactile-based pre-training approach is scalable with more data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents TTP, a system for human-centric transferable tactile pre-training, which contributes to the field of dexterous robotic manipulation in several ways.

Firstly, the work addresses a limitation in robotic tactile data availability by introducing **H-Tac**, a large-scale egocentric human tactile-action dataset. This dataset provides 160 hours of diverse human interaction data with dense tactile and action annotations, serving as a resource for future research in tactile-conditioned pre-training.

Secondly, TTP integrates tactile pre-training into the Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model paradigm. By leveraging human-centric demonstrations, the system enables VLA models to acquire **tactile-grounded priors at scale** before robot-specific post-training. This approach contrasts with prior methods that primarily injected tactile tokens during post-training, potentially leading to more efficient learning and better alignment between tactile and other modalities. The unified tactile and action spaces facilitate knowledge preservation during human-to-robot transfer, which has implications for developing more versatile robot policies.

Thirdly, the introduction of a **dual-expert system** within TTP, comprising an action expert and a tactile expert for future tactile prediction, allows the model to explicitly incorporate contact dynamics and physical interactions. This architectural design enables the model to balance high-level semantic reasoning with low-level fine-grained contact control, thereby improving performance in complex manipulation tasks.

The experimental results, particularly on real robots, demonstrate **superior performance and robust generalization** of TTP in fine-grained, contact-rich, and vision-deficient tasks. TTP consistently outperformed existing state-of-the-art tactile-free baselines, exhibiting more stable and precise manipulation behaviors. The strong performance observed in out-of-distribution scenarios, including object, location, and scene generalization, suggests TTP's potential for deployment in varied real-world environments. The ablation studies further validate the contributions of the tactile pre-training, the Tactile-Action Manifold-Preserving Gating (MPG), and the scalability of the approach with larger datasets.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Enhanced Robotic Dexterity:** By enabling robots to effectively utilize tactile feedback, TTP can significantly advance the capabilities of dexterous robotic manipulation, opening possibilities for automating tasks that currently require human-level precision and sensitivity.
- **Scalable Robot Learning:** The framework provides a scalable pathway for tactile pre-training, leveraging the relative ease of collecting human demonstration data to bootstrap robot skills. This could reduce the dependency on expensive and labor-intensive robot-specific data collection.
- **Improved Generalization:** The demonstrated cross-embodiment and OOD generalization capabilities suggest that TTP-trained policies could be more adaptable to new tasks, objects, and environments, making robots more versatile.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The open-sourcing of the H-Tac dataset and the proposed methodologies offer a foundation for further research into multimodal robot learning, tactile sensing integration, and human-to-robot skill transfer.
