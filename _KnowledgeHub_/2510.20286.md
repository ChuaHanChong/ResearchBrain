# UI-Ins: Enhancing GUI Grounding with Multi-Perspective Instruction-as-Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba Group, Renmin University of China, and CUHK introduced UI-INS, a framework that reframes natural language instructions for GUI agents as dynamic reasoning pathways rather than static inputs. This approach, leveraging a meticulously cleaned and augmented multi-perspective instruction dataset, achieves new state-of-the-art GUI grounding performance across five benchmarks and demonstrates enhanced online agent task success.

## Problem

- Prior GUI grounding methods treated natural language instructions as static inputs, overlooking the impact of instruction diversity and quality on performance.
- Existing GUI grounding datasets suffered from low instruction quality (a 23.3% flaw rate in sampled entries) and lacked systematically diverse instructional perspectives.
- Previous attempts at free-form reasoning often degraded GUI grounding performance, and SFT+RL training frameworks for these tasks were prone to policy collapse.

## Method

- A systematic data pipeline was developed to clean noisy annotations and augment datasets with high-quality, multi-perspective instructions covering appearance, functionality, location, and intent.
- The "Instruction-as-Reasoning" framework was introduced, a two-stage training paradigm where the model first generates intermediate reasoning text (**SFT** stage) and then learns to select the optimal reasoning strategy using Group Relative Policy Optimization (RL stage).
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-32B were used as backbone multimodal large language models, demonstrating the scalability of the approach.

## Results

- UI-Ins models achieved new state-of-the-art performance across five GUI grounding benchmarks, with UI-Ins-**32B** scoring **84.9%** on MMBench-GUI L2 and **87.3%** on UI-I2E-Bench.
- UI-Ins-**7B**, when used as a grounding executor under a GPT-5 planner in the AndroidWorld online benchmark, achieved a **74.1%** task success rate, outperforming strong closed-source models.
- Ablation studies confirmed the crucial role of the intermediate reasoning step, and the RL stage yielded a **24.0%** relative performance gain on the ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark due to policy optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Instruction diversity and quality are critical for GUI grounding; diverse perspectives significantly improve zero-shot performance, and flawed instructions severely hinder model efficacy.
> - Structured "Instruction-as-Reasoning" consistently enhances grounding performance, distinguishing itself from unstructured free-form reasoning approaches that often degrade results.
> - The Instruction-as-Reasoning-based SFT acts as a robust exploratory warm-up, preventing policy collapse during the subsequent RL stage and enabling substantial performance gains in SFT+RL frameworks.
> - The UI-Ins models exhibited emergent reasoning capabilities, including strategic selection of perspectives, compositional integration, and the generation of novel analytical angles not explicitly trained.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of automated agents capable of interacting with graphical user interfaces (GUIs) is a critical frontier in the pursuit of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). At the core of these GUI agents lies **GUI grounding**, the task of accurately mapping natural language instructions to corresponding actionable UI elements on a screen. This paper addresses fundamental limitations in current GUI grounding methodologies, proposing a significant paradigm shift.

Historically, prior work in GUI grounding has largely treated natural language instructions as static inputs—mere proxies for user intent. This perspective overlooks two crucial aspects that the authors systematically investigate: the **diversity** and **quality** of these instructions. Existing models are typically trained on a narrow, fixed style of instructions, failing to capture the human-like flexibility in describing UI elements based on appearance, function, location, or high-level intent. Furthermore, the authors identify a pervasive issue of **low instruction quality** within popular GUI grounding datasets, with a staggering 23.3% flaw rate in sampled entries. These flawed instructions introduce considerable noise, significantly hindering model training and performance.

This work directly challenges these existing limitations. It moves beyond simple input augmentation strategies seen in works like Aria-UI and Phi-Ground, which paraphrase instructions but don't deeply analyze their impact or leverage them as dynamic reasoning pathways. Instead, "UI-INS" introduces the **Instruction-as-Reasoning paradigm**, reframing instructions from static inputs to *dynamic analytical pathways*. This conceptual leap enables the model to actively select the most effective reasoning process (i.e., instruction perspective) to infer user intent, mirroring human cognitive flexibility.

The paper also intervenes in the ongoing debate regarding the effectiveness of **reasoning in GUI grounding**. Prior attempts at Free-Form Reasoning (FFR) within reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks have often degraded performance (e.g., GUI-G1, GTA1, UI-R1), leading to skepticism about the utility of explicit reasoning steps. The authors directly confront this by demonstrating that their *structured* Instruction-as-Reasoning approach, unlike unstructured FFR, consistently enhances performance.

Moreover, the research contributes to the **training paradigm for GUI agents**, specifically addressing challenges associated with SFT+RL (Supervised Fine-Tuning + Reinforcement Learning) frameworks. While SFT+RL has shown promise in some visual-language tasks, its application in GUI grounding has been hampered by issues like policy collapse, as noted by Phi-Ground. UI-INS offers a stable and effective SFT+RL framework, leveraging SFT to instill diverse exploratory capabilities before RL optimizes policy selection, thus mitigating policy collapse and establishing a successful blueprint for future work. By meticulously addressing data quality, reconceptualizing instructions, and demonstrating a robust training methodology, this paper significantly advances the theoretical understanding and practical capabilities of GUI grounding within the broader landscape of AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The "UI-INS" paper is motivated by a critical re-evaluation of the role of natural language instructions in GUI grounding, identifying unaddressed challenges that bottleneck the development of robust GUI agents. Its key objectives are strategically designed to overcome these limitations:

1. **Systematic Investigation into GUI Grounding Instructions:**
 - **Motivation:** The authors recognized that despite being a primary input, the impact of instruction diversity and quality on grounding performance has been largely overlooked in prior research. Instructions were treated as static inputs, ignoring their multifaceted nature.
 - **Objective:** To conduct a comprehensive analysis quantifying the prevalence of instruction flaws in existing datasets and demonstrating the untapped potential of leveraging instruction diversity for significant performance gains. This objective laid the groundwork for their subsequent methodological innovations.

2. **Development of a High-Quality, Multi-Perspective Instruction Dataset:**
 - **Motivation:** Their preliminary analysis revealed a notable 23.3% flaw rate in instructions within prominent datasets, introducing noise detrimental to training. Concurrently, they observed that diverse instructional perspectives (e.g., appearance, functionality, location, intent) significantly improve zero-shot performance, but existing datasets lacked this systematic diversity.
 - **Objective:** To create a robust data pipeline that systematically cleans noisy annotations and, crucially, augments existing data with a rich diversity of instruction styles, curated specifically for multi-perspective instruction reasoning. This was a prerequisite for effective model training under their new paradigm.

3. **Introduction of the "Instruction-as-Reasoning" Paradigm:**
 - **Motivation:** Humans instinctively choose the most effective way to describe a UI element, switching between different analytical perspectives. Current models, trained on single-style instructions, lack this flexible adaptability. The authors hypothesized that treating instructions as dynamic reasoning pathways could unlock superior performance.
 - **Objective:** To propose a novel conceptual framework where instructions are not static inputs but dynamic analytical pathways that offer distinct perspectives. This paradigm enables models to actively select and utilize the most effective reasoning process for a given context.

4. **Achieving State-of-the-Art Performance on Diverse Grounding Benchmarks and Demonstrating Agentic Potential:**
 - **Motivation:** Ultimately, the goal is to advance the practical capabilities of GUI agents, pushing towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) in human-computer interaction. Validating the proposed paradigm with strong empirical results is crucial.
 - **Objective:** To develop UI-Ins models (7B and 32B parameters) that establish new state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across multiple challenging grounding benchmarks and demonstrate strong online agent performance in dynamic, real-world environments like AndroidWorld.

5. **Providing Deeper Insights into Reasoning and SFT+RL Training for GUI Grounding:**
 - **Motivation:** Understanding *why* and *how* a method works is as important as its performance. Prior works faced difficulties with free-form reasoning and policy collapse in SFT+RL frameworks.
 - **Objective:** To conduct in-depth analyses to demonstrate how reasoning can be formulated to enhance, rather than hinder, grounding performance, and how their method specifically mitigates the policy collapse issue commonly observed in SFT+RL frameworks. Furthermore, to explore and document any emergent reasoning capabilities.

These objectives collectively aim to fundamentally improve GUI grounding by addressing both data quality and the strategic utilization of instructional semantics, paving the way for more intelligent and adaptable GUI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "UI-INS" methodology is a two-pronged approach that first establishes a high-quality, diverse data foundation and then introduces a novel two-stage training framework, "Instruction-as-Reasoning," to leverage this data.

#### 3.1. Task Definition

GUI Grounding is formally defined as the task of localizing a single UI element given a GUI screenshot (S) and a natural language instruction (I). The model (`f`) predicts a coordinate point `p = (xp, yp)` indicating the target element's location.

#### 3.2. Data Pipeline for Multi-Perspective Reasoning

Recognizing the critical issues of data quality and lack of instruction diversity, the authors developed a systematic data processing pipeline:

- **Pre-processing:**
 - **Objective:** To rectify pervasive annotation noise and ensure each instruction is associated with a reliable spatial anchor.
 - **Approach:** OmniParser V2 (a UI element detection tool) is employed to detect all UI elements on a screenshot. An IoU (Intersection over Union)-based method is then used to refine or filter original ground-truth bounding boxes. This step effectively cleans the foundation data and filters out flawed instructions.

- **Multi-Perspective Instruction Augmentation:**
 - **Objective:** To enrich instruction diversity by generating new instructions from various analytical perspectives.
 - **Approach:** GPT-4.1 (a powerful Large Language Model) is leveraged to generate new instructions. For each data instance, given the screenshot with a highlighted target element, GPT-4.1 creates a set of diverse phrasings across four predefined analytical perspectives: **Appearance**, **Functionality**, **Location**, and **Intent**.
 - **Verification:** To combat LLM hallucinations and ensure strict one-to-one mapping, each generated instruction undergoes a verification step where GPT-4.1 confirms it unambiguously refers *only* to the target element. This ensures the high quality of the augmented, multi-perspective corpus.

#### 3.3. Instruction-as-Reasoning Framework

This core algorithmic contribution is a two-stage training paradigm built upon the meticulously prepared multi-perspective dataset:

- **SFT Stage: Learning to Generate Diverse Reasoning**
 - **Objective:** To explicitly instill in the model the ability to perform Instruction-as-Reasoning—that is, utilizing diverse instruction perspectives as analytical reasoning *before* predicting the grounding coordinate.
 - **Approach:** The model is fine-tuned (Supervised Fine-Tuning, SFT) to first generate an intermediate reasoning text (`Rgt`)—a rewritten instruction from one of the augmented instruction perspectives—which serves as an actionable reasoning pathway. Subsequently, it outputs the final coordinate point (`pgt`).
 - **Training:** The model is trained to maximize the log-likelihood of the target sequence `Ygt = Rgt ⊕ pgt` (reasoning concatenated with the ground-truth point). `Rgt` is randomly sampled from one of the valid augmented instruction perspectives for each instance. This unified objective compels the model to co-optimize two skills: (i) generating contextually relevant reasoning and (ii) predicting the correct coordinate point conditioned on both inputs and its self-generated reasoning.

- **RL Stage: Learning to Select the Optimal Perspective**
 - **Objective:** To optimize the SFT-trained model to discover and select the most effective reasoning strategies (i.e., optimal analytical angles) that maximize grounding accuracy for a given context.
 - **Approach:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed. In this stage, the prompt is modified to encourage open-ended "thinking" without providing explicit predefined perspectives (e.g., "Appearance," "Function"). This encourages the model to explore a wider space of reasoning patterns, potentially synthesizing multiple perspectives or formulating novel ones.
 - **Reward Mechanism:** Rewards are calculated using a simple point-in-box function (1 if the predicted point is within the ground-truth bounding box, 0 otherwise) and then normalized into advantages.
 - **Optimization:** The model is optimized by minimizing a loss function that leverages these advantages to prioritize reasoning pathways consistently leading to correct predictions. This iterative process fine-tunes the model to dynamically select the optimal, context-dependent reasoning strategy.

#### 3.4. Backbone Architectures

The authors employ **Qwen2.5-VL-7B** and **Qwen2.5-VL-32B** as their backbone multimodal large language models, demonstrating the scalability of their approach across different model sizes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, demonstrating both the underlying issues in current GUI grounding and the superior performance of the proposed UI-Ins framework.

#### 4.1. Preliminary Analysis of GUI Grounding Instructions

- **Instruction Diversity Matters:** Experiments on the ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark with Qwen2.5-VL-7B showed that zero-shot performance significantly improved when provided with instructions from diverse perspectives (Appearance, Functionality, Location, Intent) compared to original instructions. Crucially, the "Combined" scenario (where a model could always pick the best perspective) achieved a **76% relative performance improvement**, highlighting the massive untapped potential of instruction diversity.
- **Instruction Quality Issues:** A manual inspection of 1,909 samples from popular datasets (OS-Atlas, AMEX, Widget Captioning) revealed a substantial **23.3% flaw rate** in existing instructions (ambiguity or mismatch). Training models on cleaned data yielded consistent and substantial performance gains, confirming that poor data quality actively harms model efficacy. Their data pipeline later reduced this error rate to below 8%.

#### 4.2. State-of-the-Art Grounding Performance

The UI-Ins models (7B and 32B) achieved new state-of-the-art results across five challenging grounding benchmarks:
- **MMBench-GUI L2:** UI-Ins-32B scored **84.9%**, significantly outperforming baselines. The method showed progressively larger gains on more difficult tasks (e.g., 159.4% relative improvement over Qwen2.5-VL-7B on the 'Advanced' subset).
- **UI-I2E-Bench:** UI-Ins-32B achieved **87.3%**. Similar to MMBench-GUI L2, UI-Ins demonstrated greater gains on 'implicit' instructions (6.6% over GTA1-32B), indicating enhanced robustness for complex reasoning.
- **ScreenSpot-Pro:** UI-Ins-32B achieved **57.0%**.
- **ScreenSpot-V2 and Showdown:** UI-Ins-32B also set new SOTA, performing well across different platform and software domains.
These results consistently showed that UI-Ins-32B established a new SOTA, and UI-Ins-7B outperformed similarly-scaled models, validating the effectiveness of the Instruction-as-Reasoning paradigm.

#### 4.3. Strong Online Agent Performance

- When UI-Ins-7B was used as the grounding executor under a GPT-5 planner in the dynamic AndroidWorld online benchmark, it achieved a **74.1% task success rate**. This surpassed strong closed-source models like Gemini 2.5 Computer Use and UI-TARS-2.
- This performance also represented a **24.1% improvement** over its counterpart, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, under the same GPT-5 planner configuration, demonstrating that enhanced grounding capability directly translates to improved online agent task success in realistic, interactive environments.

#### 4.4. Deeper Insights and Ablation Studies

- **Necessity of Intermediate Reasoning:** An ablation study confirmed that completely removing the intermediate reasoning step from both SFT and RL stages led to a substantial performance drop (over 10% decrease on UI-I2E-Bench), proving its crucial role.
- **Instruction-as-Reasoning vs. Free-Form Reasoning:** While prior works and the authors' own experiments showed that unstructured Free-Form Reasoning (FFR) often degrades or fails to improve performance, their Instruction-as-Reasoning (IR) approach consistently yielded significant accuracy increases across base models. This established IR as an effective reasoning paradigm for GUI grounding.
- **Mitigating Policy Collapse in SFT+RL:** The authors demonstrated that their IR-based SFT acts as a "powerful exploratory warm-up," instilling diverse exploratory capabilities. This prevents policy collapse during the RL stage, which is a common issue in standard SFT+RL pipelines, leading to a significant performance increase (e.g., 24.0% relative gain on ScreenSpot-Pro after RL).
- **Emergent Reasoning Capabilities:** Qualitative analysis revealed that UI-Ins learned beyond simply using the four predefined perspectives:
 - **Strategic Selection:** The model learned to strategically choose different reasoning perspectives based on the scenario.
 - **Compositional Integration:** It frequently combined multiple perspectives into a single, cohesive reasoning process (e.g., combining Appearance and Functionality).
 - **Emergent Perspectives:** Most remarkably, the model developed the ability to generate entirely new analytical angles not seen during training, such as reasoning from "Component Type," "Structural Relationship," "State," "Sequential Position," "Salience," "Accessibility," or "Group Affiliation" (Fig. 8b, Fig. 9).

#### 4.5. Error Analysis

Common failure types included a lack of domain-specific knowledge, insufficient layout understanding, and visual ambiguity/hallucination when similar distractor icons were present.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "UI-INS" paper presents a highly significant advancement in the field of GUI grounding and, by extension, the development of generalist GUI agents. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Redefining GUI Grounding Paradigm:** The introduction of the "Instruction-as-Reasoning" paradigm is a conceptual breakthrough. By treating natural language instructions not as static inputs but as dynamic, learnable analytical pathways, the work fundamentally shifts how models interpret and act upon user commands. This approach enables greater flexibility and adaptability, moving closer to human-like understanding of complex GUI interactions.

2. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The consistent achievement of new SOTA results across five challenging grounding benchmarks, including MMBench-GUI L2, UI-I2E-Bench, and ScreenSpot-Pro, validates the efficacy of the proposed methodology. The particular strength on "advanced" and "implicit" instructions highlights the model's enhanced robustness and deeper semantic understanding, which is crucial for real-world applications where instructions are often ambiguous or high-level.

3. **Advancement of Practical GUI Agents:** The impressive 74.1% success rate on the AndroidWorld online benchmark, using UI-Ins-7B as an executor, demonstrates its strong agentic potential and practical utility. This result, outperforming established closed-source models and other open-source frameworks, signifies a leap towards building more reliable, general-purpose agents that can operate effectively in dynamic, unconstrained environments. This is a crucial step towards the broader goal of AGI in human-computer interaction.

4. **Addressing Foundational Data Challenges:** The systematic investigation into instruction quality, revealing a 23.3% flaw rate in existing datasets, and the subsequent development of a robust data cleaning and multi-perspective augmentation pipeline, offer a critical blueprint for the community. This emphasizes that data quality is not a secondary concern but a prerequisite for meaningful advancements, and provides a method to systematically enrich instructional diversity, thereby benefiting future dataset creation and model training.

5. **Stabilizing and Advancing SFT+RL Frameworks:** The paper provides crucial insights into the notoriously difficult SFT+RL training paradigm, especially its susceptibility to policy collapse in grounding tasks. By demonstrating how instruction-as-reasoning-based SFT can act as a "powerful exploratory warm-up," preventing policy collapse and enabling significant RL gains, the work offers a successful example for implementing stable and effective SFT+RL. This contribution has implications not only for GUI grounding but potentially for other visual-language tasks that leverage such multi-stage training.

6. **Deepening Understanding of Reasoning in AI:** The detailed ablation studies and the analysis of emergent capabilities (strategic selection, compositional integration, and entirely novel reasoning perspectives) significantly deepen the understanding of *how* and *what kind* of reasoning is beneficial for GUI agents. The finding that structured "Instruction-as-Reasoning" is effective, while unstructured "Free-Form Reasoning" often isn't, provides valuable guidance for designing intelligent agents. The ability of the model to synthesize new analytical angles beyond its training data suggests a nascent form of genuine intelligence within this domain.

By publicly releasing their code and model checkpoints, the authors are fostering an open research environment, encouraging further exploration and building upon their foundational work. Overall, "UI-INS" makes a multi-faceted contribution that pushes the boundaries of GUI grounding, offering both a novel theoretical framework and compelling empirical evidence for its effectiveness, with broad implications for the future of generalist AI agents.
