# UI-Ins: Enhancing GUI Grounding with Multi-Perspective Instruction-as-Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Alibaba Group, Renmin University of China, and CUHK introduced UI-INS, a framework that reframes natural language instructions for GUI agents as dynamic reasoning pathways rather than static inputs. This approach, leveraging a meticulously cleaned and augmented multi-perspective instruction dataset, achieves new state-of-the-art GUI grounding performance across five benchmarks and demonstrates enhanced online agent task success.

## Problem

- Prior GUI grounding methods treated natural language instructions as static inputs, overlooking the impact of instruction diversity and quality on performance.
- Existing GUI grounding datasets suffered from low instruction quality (a 23.3% flaw rate in sampled entries) and lacked systematically diverse instructional perspectives.
- Previous attempts at free-form reasoning often degraded GUI grounding performance, and SFT+RL training frameworks for these tasks were prone to policy collapse.

## Method

- A systematic data pipeline was developed to clean noisy annotations and augment datasets with high-quality, multi-perspective instructions covering appearance, functionality, location, and intent.
- The "Instruction-as-Reasoning" framework was introduced, a two-stage training paradigm where the model first generates intermediate reasoning text (**SFT** stage) and then learns to select the optimal reasoning strategy using Group Relative Policy Optimization (RL stage).
- Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-32B were used as backbone multimodal large language models, demonstrating the scalability of the approach.

## Results

- UI-Ins models achieved new state-of-the-art performance across five GUI grounding benchmarks, with UI-Ins-**32B** scoring **84.9%** on MMBench-GUI L2 and **87.3%** on UI-I2E-Bench.
- UI-Ins-**7B**, when used as a grounding executor under a GPT-5 planner in the AndroidWorld online benchmark, achieved a **74.1%** task success rate, outperforming strong closed-source models.
- Ablation studies confirmed the crucial role of the intermediate reasoning step, and the RL stage yielded a **24.0%** relative performance gain on the ScreenSpot-Pro benchmark due to policy optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Instruction diversity and quality are critical for GUI grounding; diverse perspectives significantly improve zero-shot performance, and flawed instructions severely hinder model efficacy.
> - Structured "Instruction-as-Reasoning" consistently enhances grounding performance, distinguishing itself from unstructured free-form reasoning approaches that often degrade results.
> - The Instruction-as-Reasoning-based SFT acts as a robust exploratory warm-up, preventing policy collapse during the subsequent RL stage and enabling substantial performance gains in SFT+RL frameworks.
> - The UI-Ins models exhibited emergent reasoning capabilities, including strategic selection of perspectives, compositional integration, and the generation of novel analytical angles not explicitly trained.
