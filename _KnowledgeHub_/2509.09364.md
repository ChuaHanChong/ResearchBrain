# AGILOped: Agile Open-Source Humanoid Robot for Research

> [!summary] Summary
> An open-source humanoid robot, AGILOped, was developed to provide an affordable and agile research platform. It integrates Quasi Direct Drive actuators and a selectively compliant structure, demonstrating stable walking, 10 cm vertical jumps, and autonomous fall mitigation with self-recovery.

## Problem

- High-performance humanoid robots are often proprietary, closed-source, or prohibitively expensive, limiting access for most research groups.
- Existing open-source humanoid platforms typically lack the dynamic capabilities and agility required for advanced research.
- The extensive resources needed for humanoid development create an exclusivity gap, hindering broader research and reproducibility.

## Method

- Developed **AGILOped**, an open-source, dynamically capable humanoid robot with a base cost of 6,380 USD to bridge the performance-accessibility gap.
- Utilized 10 ==MyActuator RMD X6==-40 Quasi Direct Drive actuators for high torque density and control bandwidth, enabling agile motions.
- Implemented a selectively compliant structural design leveraging 3D-printed parts and aluminum, incorporating active fall mitigation strategies for robustness.

## Results

- AGILOped was successfully constructed (110 cm height, **14.5** kg weight) at a base cost of 6,380 USD, with all design files released as open-source.
- The robot demonstrated stable bipedal walking on compliant surfaces and achieved vertical jumps of approximately 10 cm.
- It exhibited effective fall mitigation by bracing with arms and reducing impedance, followed by an autonomous get-up routine, showcasing resilience and self-recovery.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining affordable, high power-density QDD actuators with a lightweight, open-source design can democratize access to advanced humanoid robotics research.
> - Strategic use of 3D printing and off-the-shelf components significantly reduces manufacturing costs and complexity, making advanced platforms more widely available.
> - Integrating selective compliance in the physical design alongside active control strategies is crucial for robust fall mitigation and autonomous self-recovery in dynamic humanoid robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robotics is characterized by increasing interest and rapid development, driven by the goal of creating versatile automatons capable of performing human-level work. This progress is facilitated by advancements in key technologies such as energy-dense batteries, efficient motor controllers, miniaturized computing for complex online controls, and the integration of Large Language Models for unstructured task performance.

However, the development of sophisticated humanoid platforms is a complex, multidisciplinary endeavor, often requiring substantial resources. This typically limits such development to a small number of well-resourced institutions or commercial entities. As a result, many high-performance humanoid systems remain proprietary, closed-source, or come with prohibitively high acquisition and maintenance costs. Examples of such platforms include the MIT Humanoid, Berkeley Humanoid, HECTOR, and ARTEMIS, which are largely unavailable to external research groups, and commercial offerings like Unitree G1, Fourier-GR1, and Booster T1, which are expensive, dependent on manufacturers, or restrict low-level control. This exclusivity hinders broader research and limits reproducibility.

Conversely, existing open-source and more accessible humanoid platforms, such as NimbRo-OP2X and DARwIn-OP iterations, often have limitations in motor specifications or overall size. These constraints prevent them from achieving the dynamic and agile motions demonstrated by their more expensive and custom-built counterparts.

The AGILOped project directly addresses this identified gap within the humanoid robotics landscape. It aims to bridge the divide between high performance and accessibility by introducing an open-source humanoid robot that is both dynamically capable and affordable. The work leverages modern Quasi Direct Drive (QDD) actuators, which have become a prevalent choice in contemporary legged robotics for their high torque density and control bandwidth. Furthermore, the paper contributes to the less-explored area of robust design and fall mitigation strategies for humanoids, an aspect often overlooked in the pursuit of dynamic capabilities. By offering a platform that combines dynamic agility with open-source design and cost-effectiveness, AGILOped seeks to democratize access to advanced humanoid robotics research and foster wider collaboration and innovation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the development of AGILOped stems from the current state of humanoid robotics research. While there is a significant surge in interest and technological advancements enabling powerful and dynamic humanoid robots, the platforms capable of these feats often remain exclusive. The multidisciplinary nature and substantial resource requirements for developing such robots mean that only a limited number of organizations can afford them, leading to proprietary designs, high costs, and restricted access for the broader academic community. This exclusivity impedes widespread research, limits the reproducibility of results, and slows down the overall progress in the field. Existing open-source platforms, while accessible, typically lack the dynamic capabilities of their more advanced, expensive counterparts, creating a performance-accessibility gap. The authors emphasize that such exclusivity in a potentially universally applicable technology could have significant socio-economic consequences, underscoring the necessity for open and accessible solutions.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives for AGILOped are:

- **To Develop an Accessible and Affordable Research Platform:** The central objective is to create a humanoid robot that significantly lowers the entry barrier to advanced humanoid robotics research. This involves designing a system that can be manufactured at an affordable price, making it attainable for a wider range of research groups, including those with limited budgets.
- **To Ensure Dynamic Capability:** The platform must not compromise on performance, aiming to achieve dynamic and agile motions comparable to modern humanoids. This objective targets addressing the limitation of existing accessible platforms that often lack the necessary motor specifications or size for dynamic tasks.
- **To Promote Openness and Collaboration:** A core goal is to foster an open-source environment by making the robot's design files (CAD), software, and documentation freely available. This aims to reduce exclusivity, enhance reproducibility, and encourage external researchers to contribute to the platform's development and use it for their own innovations.
- **To Facilitate Ease of Manufacturing, Operation, and Maintenance:** The design prioritizes effortless and reliable operation, easy maintenance, and extensive customization possibilities. This is achieved by relying on readily available, off-the-shelf components, standard electronic parts, and manufacturing methods like 3D printing that require only basic tooling.
- **To Demonstrate Viability Through Practical Experiments:** A practical objective is to validate AGILOped's capabilities through experimental demonstrations. These experiments include fundamental locomotion tasks like walking, as well as more dynamic actions such as jumping, and crucial resilience features like impact mitigation during falls and autonomous getting-up procedures. This serves to showcase the robot's potential for diverse research applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of AGILOped was guided by a set of design principles focused on achieving a balance between accessibility, dynamic capability, and robustness.

**Design Principles:**
- **Low Entry Barrier:** The robot's design prioritizes ease of acquisition, operation, maintenance, and customization. It utilizes readily available off-the-shelf components and relies heavily on 3D printing for structural elements, making it manufacturable with commercial 3D printers and basic tools.
- **Dynamic Capability:** To enable agile and dynamic motions, the platform features a lightweight design (110 cm height, 14.5 kg weight) and employs high power density, backdrivable actuators.
- **Modularity and Openness:** The design is modular, allowing for easy modification and integration of new components or functionalities. All design files are open-source to promote collaboration and reproducibility.
- **Selective Compliance:** The structure combines rigid aluminum elements with flexible 3D-printed plastics and polyurethanes of varying hardness to achieve robustness and mitigate impact forces, particularly during falls.
- **Simplicity and Cost-Effectiveness:** With only 10 actuators controlling 12 joints, the design emphasizes mechanical and electrical simplicity, contributing to a lower overall cost (approx. 6,380 USD) and ease of use.

**Hardware Components:**
- **Actuation:** Ten MyActuator RMD X6-40 Quasi Direct Drive (QDD) units are used for all joints. These actuators were selected for their high torque (40 Nm peak), compact form factor, and integrated 16-bit encoders. They operate via CAN bus communication and support position, velocity, torque, and impedance control modes. The choice of the X6-40, despite its smaller diameter, is justified by its 1:36 planetary gearbox, which delivers high torque and angular velocities exceeding 10 rad/s, while maintaining low rotor inertia compared to larger alternatives.
- **Power System:** Two 26.1 V LiPo UAV batteries are connected in series to provide a 52.2 V supply voltage to the actuators, enabling maximum velocity. A hot-swap circuit, implemented with two high-power diodes, ensures continuous operation during battery changes. The system is designed to tolerate a wide voltage input.
- **Structural Design:**
 - **Legs:** Each leg features a hybrid serial-parallel kinematic chain with four actuated joints (three at the hip for yaw, roll, pitch, and one at the knee for pitch). The hip axes intersect at a common point, and the outward hip pitch placement mimics the human bicondylar angle. Double 4-bar parallelograms are integrated into the thigh and shank, actuated at two points to control hip, knee, and ankle angles. This configuration reduces actuator count, minimizes inertia, and achieves a passive ankle joint that keeps the foot horizontal relative to the torso. The legs are primarily 3D-printed (Nylon recommended for durability) and reinforced with modulated thickness, ribbing, and fasteners. Aluminum rods with universal joints form the parallel kinematic chains.
 - **Feet:** The elongated foot design provides static stability. Elastic plates angled and connected to the ankle offer longitudinal stiffness and energy storage, mimicking medial and lateral arches for lateral compliance. The sole is 3D-printed using foaming Thermoplastic Polyurethane (varioShore TPU) for a balance of rigidity and compliance, incorporating a custom tread pattern. The leg design keeps the center of mass close to the hip (0.16 m from origin, 25.8% leg length), reducing rotational inertia.
 - **Upper Body:** The torso comprises an aluminum extrusion cage for rigidity, housing actuators, computing units, and power distribution. 1-DoF arms (shoulder pitch) are included to aid in fall mitigation and getting-up. Shoulder mounts are 3D-printed with TPU for lateral compliance and impact resistance. An elastic battery backpack, made of aluminum housing press-fit into a TPU enclosure, provides further impact mitigation.
- **Computing and Sensing:**
 - **Base Controller:** A Raspberry Pi 3B+ (upgradeable to Pi 5) serves as the Single Board Computer (SBC) for low-level control, running Ubuntu Mate OS with ROS support. A PiCAN2 Duo hat provides two separate CAN buses for actuator communication.
 - **Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU):** A low-cost (30 USD), open-source AHRS solution based on an RP2040 microcontroller and BNO085 modules is used. It provides pre-calibrated, fused orientation feedback at up to 1 kHz via USB (rosserial for ROS integration).
 - **High-Level Computing (Optional):** An NVIDIA Jetson unit can be integrated for more computationally intensive tasks, such as learning-based or model-based control approaches.

**Control Architecture:**
- **Physical Layer:** The Raspberry Pi acts as the interface, managing CAN bus communication with the motors and processing IMU data.
- **Low-Level Control (RosPi Controller):** The Raspberry Pi runs the RosPi controller, utilizing two CAN buses to communicate with five motors each at a baud rate supporting 800 Hz per motor. Motors are controlled as torque sources using impedance controllers: `τ = min(KP(q_set - q) + KD(dq_set - dq) + τ_ff, τ_max)`. Joint positions are calibrated by resting the robot in a defined pose against hip brackets.
- **Safety Mechanisms:** The system includes multiple safety features: motor shutdown if communication is lost for over 100 ms, a shutdown command to motors if higher-layer communication is lost, and continuous monitoring of motor temperatures, voltages, and torques, triggering warnings or shutdowns for protection.
- **Higher-Level Control:** AGILOped leverages an open-source, ROS-based software stack, previously proven on other NimbRo-OP platforms. This modular framework allows for easy integration of custom hardware, gaits, and motion controllers via plug-ins. A built-in trajectory editor enables designing motion sequences using keyframes, and the system seamlessly integrates with simulators like Gazebo and MuJoCo.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented AGILOped as a successfully developed open-source humanoid robot, demonstrating its viability as a research platform through various experiments.

- **Platform Realization and Accessibility:**
 - AGILOped was successfully constructed with a height of 110 cm and a weight of 14.5 kg, matching the dimensions of a typical 6-year-old child.
 - The robot achieved a base cost of 6,380 USD, positioning it as an affordable and lightweight option within its size class, fulfilling a key objective.
 - Its design primarily utilizes 3D-printed parts (PLA/Nylon, TPU) and off-the-shelf components, which contributes to its low manufacturing cost and ease of assembly using basic tools.
 - The open-source release of its design files facilitates access and customization for a broader research community.
 - The leg design achieved low rotational inertia, with the leg's center of mass located only 0.16 m from the hip origin, approximately 25.8% of the leg length, which is beneficial for high-bandwidth control.

- **Bipedal Walking Performance:**
 - AGILOped demonstrated stable forward balanced walking on a compliant artificial grass surface.
 - This was achieved using a feedback-enhanced Central Pattern Generated (CPG) gait, adapted and tuned from the NimbRo-OP2X platform.
 - The control system incorporated lateral plane stability by adjusting gait frequency based on Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP) Center of Mass (CoM) position and velocity.
 - In the sagittal plane, a regulator computed desired CoM velocity from Zero Moment Point (ZMP) and CoM tracking errors to maintain stability.
 - The proprioceptive capabilities of the actuators allowed for real-time estimation of contact forces from joint torques and limb Jacobian, providing crucial feedback on foot-ground interaction during locomotion.

- **Jumping Capability:**
 - The robot successfully performed vertical jumps of approximately 10 cm.
 - This was accomplished by designing a sequence of whole-body position keyframes that efficiently propelled the robot's center of mass upwards.
 - The jumping experiment served to showcase the peak power capabilities of the MyActuator RMD X6-40 actuators.
 - While the motions were described as not fully optimized (resulting in a slight backward jump), the demonstration validated the actuators' dynamic potential even without a dedicated force controller.
 - Contact force estimations, derived from torque measurements and the Jacobian, clearly illustrated the loading, air, and landing phases of the jump.

- **Falling Mitigation and Standing-up Routines:**
 - AGILOped exhibited robustness against impacts through controlled falling and successful self-recovery.
 - When a fall was detected (e.g., from being pushed), the robot rapidly moved its arms to brace for impact.
 - The stiffness of the impedance controllers was significantly reduced, while damping was maintained, effectively softening the fall.
 - The elastic battery backpack absorbed and dispersed impact energy, allowing the robot to gently bounce.
 - Following a fall, AGILOped initiated an immediate get-up routine, retracting its legs and performing a strong push-off to return to a standing position.
 - This demonstrated the effectiveness of the robot's combined actuator compliance and structural design in mitigating fall damage and enabling autonomous recovery.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development and open-sourcing of AGILOped carry several significant implications and potential impacts for the field of humanoid robotics research and beyond:

- **Democratization of Humanoid Robotics Research:** By providing an open-source, affordable (6,380 USD base cost), and easily manufacturable platform, AGILOped substantially lowers the financial and technical barriers to entry. This makes advanced humanoid robotics research accessible to a broader range of academic institutions, smaller research groups, and even individual enthusiasts who previously lacked the resources for custom-built or expensive commercial robots. This increased accessibility can foster a more diverse and inclusive research community.
- **Enhanced Reproducibility and Collaboration:** The open-source nature of AGILOped's hardware design files (CAD) and software stack directly addresses the issue of reproducibility in robotics research. Researchers worldwide can replicate the platform, validate existing results, and build upon the established foundation. This transparency promotes global collaboration, allowing for collective advancements and the sharing of innovations without proprietary restrictions.
- **Acceleration of Dynamic Robotics Research:** Unlike previous accessible platforms that often had limited dynamic capabilities, AGILOped is engineered for agile locomotion and dynamic whole-body motions. Its lightweight design, powerful off-the-shelf actuators, and low rotational inertia enable research into complex behaviors such as running, jumping, and dynamic manipulation, which were previously confined to high-budget, custom platforms. This can accelerate the development of more versatile and robust control algorithms for dynamic tasks.
- **Advancements in Robustness and Resilience:** AGILOped's integrated approach to fall mitigation, involving selective compliance in its structure and active bracing strategies, offers a valuable testbed for studying robot resilience to impacts. The demonstrated ability to survive falls and autonomously recover is critical for robots operating in unstructured or unpredictable human environments, potentially leading to safer and more reliable human-robot interaction. This contributes to a relatively understudied area in humanoid design.
- **Educational and Prototyping Tool:** Its affordability, straightforward assembly process, and open documentation make AGILOped an ideal platform for educational purposes. Students can gain hands-on experience with the entire robotics development pipeline, from hardware assembly to advanced control programming. It also serves as a cost-effective prototyping platform for new robotic concepts and control strategies.
- **Foundation for Future Development:** The authors explicitly invite other researchers to contribute to AGILOped's ecosystem. The robust software stack, which is compatible with modern learning-based and model-based control approaches, positions AGILOped as a strong foundation for future research. This includes potential for advanced agile locomotion, complex whole-body motions like kicking, and further exploration of sensing and intelligence integration, such as more sophisticated visual perception systems.
