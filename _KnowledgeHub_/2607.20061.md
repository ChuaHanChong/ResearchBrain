# ReferTrack: Referring Then Tracking for Embodied Visual Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> ReferTrack introduces a "referring then tracking" paradigm for embodied visual tracking, directly grounding natural language instructions to image-space bounding box detections within a VLA model. This approach achieves state-of-the-art single-view performance on the EVT-Bench benchmark, particularly improving success rates on distracted (+6.8%) and ambiguity tracking (+22.9%) tasks, and demonstrates robust sim-to-real transfer on physical robots.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for embodied visual tracking often suffer from weak visual grounding, relying on abstract spatial representations that struggle with precise target identification in crowded or complex visual environments.
- Robustly identifying and continuously tracking a natural language-specified target using only a single forward-facing camera, especially amidst distractors or ambiguities, remains a challenge for many approaches.
- Many high-performing embodied visual tracking models necessitate multi-camera setups for environmental awareness or rely on computationally expensive reinforcement learning for policy optimization.

## Method

- **ReferTrack** proposes a "referring then tracking" paradigm where the VLA model explicitly selects a target from a catalog of image-space bounding box detections (==Refer-CoT==) before predicting a trajectory.
- ==Temporal-Viewpoint-Bbox Indicator (TVBI)== tokens are introduced to explicitly inject target-specific geometric features (bounding box coordinates) into the visual history, providing a continuous, target-conditioned memory.
- The model is trained using a two-stage ==supervised fine-tuning== strategy on 1.3M expert tracking trajectories and 1.3M custom-built referring samples to enhance both navigation and visual grounding capabilities.

## Results

- ReferTrack achieved state-of-the-art single-view performance on EVT-Bench, with **73.3%** Success Rate (SR) on Distracted Tracking (DT) and **74.1%** SR on Ambiguity Tracking (AT), representing absolute improvements of **+6.8%** SR and **+22.9%** SR respectively over the strongest single-view baseline (TrackVLA++).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the Refer-CoT module and TVBI tokens are crucial, with their removal leading to significant performance drops (e.g., from **73.3%** SR to **55.7%** SR on DT without both).
- The model demonstrated robust sim-to-real transfer, successfully following targets in real-world scenarios on Unitree Go2 quadruped and Unitree G1 humanoid robots, operating at an average perception-and-control frequency of **10.6 Hz**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit image-space target identification, via the Refer-CoT mechanism, is a primary driver for robustness against distractors and ambiguities, and can compensate for limitations of single-camera setups.
> - Target identification, rather than motion planning, is the main bottleneck in complex distracted tracking environments, as evidenced by significant performance gains when provided with ground-truth target bounding boxes.
> - Integrating temporal bounding box geometry into visual history via TVBI tokens effectively provides target-specific memory, which contributes to improved tracking stability and success over time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied visual tracking (EVT) is a subfield of embodied AI where a mobile agent is required to continuously follow a specific target, described in natural language, using only its onboard vision. This task combines two primary challenges: accurately identifying the target from a natural language description and planning a collision-free trajectory to maintain proximity to the target.

Historically, EVT approaches often decoupled these two capabilities, using visual foundation models for identification and separate learning-based planners for navigation. While offering interpretability, this modularity can lead to error propagation, where inaccuracies in perception negatively impact planning.

More recent advancements have focused on unifying target identification and trajectory planning within single Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models typically adopt a next-token prediction paradigm, drawing inspiration from large language models (LLMs). Some VLA models achieve EVT capabilities by co-training alongside a diverse set of general navigation tasks, learning broad navigational priors through extensive data. Other specialized methods concentrate specifically on EVT and construct target-identification-driven question-answering (QA) datasets to optimize performance. For instance, TrackVLA++ introduced chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning via abstract spatial-aware tokens before action prediction. However, a limitation of these abstract reasoning approaches is their potential difficulty with precise visual grounding in visually complex or crowded environments, as their internal representations may not directly align with explicit image-space visual evidence.

ReferTrack positions itself within this landscape by addressing the visual grounding limitations of existing VLA-EVT models. It bridges concepts from Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) and grounding — where objects are localized based on free-form language descriptions — with the end-to-end VLA framework. By explicitly formulating target identification as a selection among image-space bounding box detections, ReferTrack aims to provide a more direct and visually grounded reasoning pathway than previous abstract CoT methods. Furthermore, it enhances structured visual history modeling, a technique seen in Vision-Language Navigation (VLN) models like NavFoM, by incorporating target-specific geometric features. The work explores whether explicit image-space referring can compensate for limitations like single-camera setups and reduce the reliance on computationally intensive methods such as reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient embodied visual tracking (EVT) policy that can reliably follow a natural language-specified target using only a single forward-facing camera, particularly in dynamic and crowded environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the broader EVT research landscape:

1. **Addressing Weak Visual Grounding:** Current VLA policies for EVT, particularly those employing chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, often rely on abstract spatial latent representations for target identification. This abstract reasoning can be difficult to supervise effectively and may exhibit weak alignment with explicit image-space detections. In complex, crowded scenarios, this can lead to imprecise target identification and compromised tracking performance. The authors aimed to introduce a mechanism that grounds the model's reasoning process more directly in visual evidence.
2. **Improving Target Identification Robustness:** EVT success heavily depends on accurately identifying the target among potential distractors and maintaining that identification over time, even with partial observations or environmental ambiguity. Existing methods sometimes struggle with these identification-heavy tasks, especially when using limited sensor input (e.g., a single camera). A key motivation was to enhance the model's ability to disambiguate targets effectively.
3. **Enhancing Temporal Coherence of Target Cues:** As an agent moves, the visual appearance and position of the target change. Preserving and integrating target motion cues over time is crucial for continuous tracking. The authors aimed to devise a method that explicitly incorporates historical target information into the visual stream in a structured manner.
4. **Reducing Resource Dependence:** Many high-performing EVT models either require multi-camera setups for comprehensive environmental awareness or rely on computationally costly reinforcement learning (RL) for policy optimization. The researchers sought to investigate if a more efficient approach, based on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and explicit visual grounding, could achieve comparable or superior performance with a single camera, thereby reducing reliance on extensive hardware or expensive training protocols.
5. **Facilitating Data Scalability:** Leveraging general referring and grounding datasets could provide a scalable way to improve identification capabilities without solely depending on scarce expert tracking trajectories, which are costly to obtain. The researchers aimed to develop an interface compatible with such diverse data sources.
6. **Validating Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A significant challenge in robotics research is the transfer of policies learned in simulation to real-world deployment. Demonstrating robust sim-to-real capabilities on physical robots was a practical objective to validate the model's efficacy.

In summary, the core motivation was to develop an EVT framework that is more visually grounded, robust in identification, temporally aware, resource-efficient, and demonstrably effective in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReferTrack proposes a "referring then tracking" paradigm, formulating embodied visual tracking as a two-stage process within a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. This approach grounds target identification explicitly in image-space bounding box detections before proceeding to trajectory planning.

The ReferTrack architecture extends existing VLA frameworks with a dual-branch design for both navigation and question-answering. The key components and steps are as follows:

1. **Observation Encoding:**
 - Forward-view RGB observations from the agent's single camera are processed by a dual-encoder architecture, combining features from SigLIP and DINOv2.
 - A grid pooling strategy extracts both fine-grained tokens (for the current observation) and coarse tokens (for historical context).
 - A sliding window of the latest `H` frames maintains visual history. These visual tokens are mapped to the LLM latent space via an MLP projector, `P_vision`.

2. **Temporal-Viewpoint-Bbox Indicator (TVBI) Tokens:**
 - ReferTrack introduces TVBI tokens, an extension of NavFoM's Temporal-Viewpoint Indicator (TVI) tokens, to explicitly inject target geometry into the visual history.
 - TVBI tokens are interleaved between visual token groups for historical frames. For each historical frame, if a target bounding box (`b_t`) is available, it is embedded through a shared MLP projector (`P_bbox`) and added to the TVI token: `E_TVBI(t) = E_TVI(t) + P_bbox(b_t)`.
 - If the target is absent in a historical frame, `b_t` is set to `` as a sentinel.
 - Crucially, only historical TVBI tokens receive target bbox injections. The current-frame fine tokens rely solely on TVI-only indicators, forcing the model to infer the current target position from raw visual features and historical TVBI cues.

3. **Candidate Catalog Generation:**
 - At each timestep, an off-the-shelf real-time object detector (YOLO11) is run on the current forward-view image to detect pedestrians.
 - These detections are organized into an indexed catalog `C_T = {⟨ped_1⟩, ..., ⟨ped_K⟩, ⟨NO EXIST⟩}`. `K` denotes the maximum number of pedestrians, and `⟨NO EXIST⟩` is a special token indicating the target is not currently visible.
 - Each catalog entry consists of an identifier token (e.g., `⟨ped_k⟩`) followed by a bbox token derived from the detected bounding box via the `P_bbox` projector.

4. **Refer-CoT and Trajectory Prediction:**
 - **Refer-CoT (Referring Chain-of-Thought):** The natural language instruction `L`, the candidate catalog `C_T`, and the TVBI-organized visual history `E_V_1:T` are all input to the LLM. In the first forward pass, the LLM performs a classification step to predict a single `Refer-CoT` token `E_refer_T`. This token explicitly selects one indexed bounding box from `C_T` (or `⟨NO EXIST⟩`) as the identified target. This makes target identification a constrained multiple-choice problem directly grounded in image space.
 - **Trajectory Prediction:** In a subsequent LLM pass, the `E_refer_T` token serves as a conditioning prefix. The LLM then generates an action token `E_A_T`, which is decoded by a dedicated MLP head into a sequence of `M` waypoints `W_T = {w_1, ..., w_M}` (each `w_i = (x, y, θ)`).

5. **Referred-Target Bbox Queue:**
 - Once the `Refer-CoT` decision identifies the target bbox (`b_T`), this bbox is stored in a first-in-first-out (FIFO) queue `Q` with capacity `H-1`.
 - This queue (`Q_hist_T = {b_T-H, ..., b_T-1}`) is then used to populate the historical TVBI tokens for subsequent timesteps, providing a continuous, target-specific memory of its past positions.
 - During training, ground-truth bboxes populate this queue, with synthetic noise (occasional incorrect indices) added for robustness. During inference, the queue updates autoregressively using the model's own `Refer-CoT` selections.

6. **Training Objective:**
 - The model is optimized using a weighted sum of three loss functions: `L = αL_traj + L_refer + L_text`.
 - `L_traj`: Mean Squared Error (MSE) between predicted and expert waypoints for navigation.
 - `L_refer`: Cross-entropy loss for the `Refer-CoT` token, supervising the model to predict the ground-truth target index.
 - `L_text`: Standard cross-entropy loss for Refer-QA samples, focusing on visual grounding and language alignment without involving the action head.

7. **Training Data and Strategy:**
 - **Navigation Data:** 1.3M expert tracking trajectories from the EVT-Bench training split in Habitat 3.0, providing language instructions, visual observations, candidate catalogs, ground-truth `Refer-CoT` indices, and expert waypoints.
 - **Refer-QA Data:** 1.3M custom-built referring samples synthesized from the SYNTH-PEDES dataset, designed with the same indexed-bbox catalog interface to explicitly enhance visual grounding.
 - **Two-Stage Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Stage 1:** Vision projectors are aligned using general multimodal QA datasets, with the LLM and vision encoders frozen.
 - **Stage 2:** Full fine-tuning on both navigation and Refer-QA tasks (1:1 ratio), updating all parameters except the vision encoders. AdamW optimizer with a cosine learning rate decay and linear warm-up is used.

The LLM backbone used is Qwen3-4B, demonstrating an efficient architectural choice.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ReferTrack on EVT-Bench and through real-world deployments yielded several findings regarding its performance and architectural contributions.

**Quantitative Performance on EVT-Bench:**
ReferTrack was evaluated under the single forward-view setting, comparing its performance across Single-Target Tracking (STT), Distracted Tracking (DT), and Ambiguity Tracking (AT) splits using Success Rate (SR), Tracking Rate (TR), and Collision Rate (CR).

- **Overall Performance:** ReferTrack achieved state-of-the-art performance for single-view EVT, despite utilizing a compact 4B-parameter backbone, a moderate 1.3M navigation samples, and relying solely on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) without reinforcement learning (RL).
 - **Single-Target Tracking (STT):** Achieved 89.4% SR, 92.5% TR, and 1.6% CR.
 - **Distracted Tracking (DT):** Achieved 73.3% SR, 81.8% TR, and 7.6% CR.
 - **Ambiguity Tracking (AT):** Achieved 74.1% SR, 85.7% TR, and 7.7% CR.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **Against Strongest Single-View Baseline (TrackVLA++):** ReferTrack demonstrated notable absolute improvements, particularly on identification-heavy tasks. On DT, it yielded +6.8% SR and +13.0% TR. On AT, it showed substantial gains of +22.9% SR and +22.3% TR.
 - **Against RL-refined Single-View Methods:** ReferTrack surpassed methods like VLingNav on DT, despite not using RL.
 - **Against Multi-Camera Baselines:** ReferTrack's single-camera results matched or, in some cases, exceeded reported performance of several multi-camera baselines on the DT and AT splits. This suggests that explicit image-space referring can effectively compensate for reduced camera coverage, especially when target disambiguation is critical.
- **STT Performance:** The model also attained the best single-view SR/TR/CR on the STT split, indicating that the referring interface does not compromise standard tracking stability.

**Ablation Study (on Distracted Tracking split):**
An ablation study was conducted to assess the contribution of key architectural components:

- **Target Identification as a Bottleneck:** A variant, "TVBI w/ GT bbox," where TVBI tokens were constructed directly from ground-truth target bounding boxes (bypassing the Refer-CoT module for perfect identification), achieved 81.5% SR and 84.7% TR. This performance was substantially higher than the full ReferTrack model (73.3% SR and 81.8% TR). This result indicates that target identification, rather than motion planning, constitutes the primary bottleneck in distracted environments. It also validates TVBI as an effective mechanism for incorporating discrete bounding box geometry.
- **Contribution of TVBI:** Removing the TVBI component (while retaining the referring pipeline) led to a reduction in SR/TR from 73.3/81.8 to 70.4/80.8. This suggests that temporal bounding box geometry, integrated via TVBI, contributes to improved target-following success and stability over time.
- **Importance of Refer-CoT:** When both Refer-CoT and TVBI were removed, performance dropped significantly to 55.7% SR and 71.4% TR. This finding indicates that explicit image-space target selection (via Refer-CoT) is a primary driver of robustness against distractors, with TVBI providing further stabilization for the identified target across timesteps.

**Real-World Qualitative Evaluation:**
ReferTrack was deployed on two distinct robotic platforms: a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot and a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, both equipped with a single forward-facing camera.

- **Operational Performance:** The complete perception-and-control loop operated at an average frequency of 10.6 Hz, with target detection requiring approximately 12 ms per step.
- **Robustness:** The Unitree Go2 demonstrated the ability to follow a target through cluttered obstacles and maintain tracking when the pedestrian detoured, even with a narrow camera field-of-view capturing only the target's lower body. The Unitree G1 successfully referred and followed the correct target amidst multi-person interference.
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** These qualitative evaluations validate the model's robust sim-to-real transfer capabilities and highlight the practical benefits of the referring-then-tracking design in complex physical environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ReferTrack presents a notable contribution to the field of embodied visual tracking (EVT), with several implications for future research and practical applications:

1. **Enhanced Visual Grounding for VLA Models:** The core "referring then tracking" paradigm, which explicitly grounds natural language instructions to image-space bounding box detections, provides a more interpretable and robust reasoning pathway compared to previous abstract spatial latent methods. This direct alignment between language, vision, and action decisions can lead to more reliable performance in visually complex scenes.
2. **Achieving SOTA with Efficiency:** ReferTrack demonstrates that state-of-the-art performance in single-view EVT can be achieved with a relatively compact 4B-parameter model and supervised fine-tuning (SFT) alone. This challenges the notion that superior performance necessarily requires multi-camera setups, larger models, or computationally expensive reinforcement learning (RL) training. This efficiency can lower the entry barrier for EVT research and facilitate deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.
3. **Compensating for Limited Sensor Input:** The finding that explicit image-space referring can enable a single-camera system to match or surpass multi-camera baselines on identification-heavy tasks suggests a crucial insight: well-grounded reasoning about "who to follow" can be more impactful than broader sensor coverage when target disambiguation is the primary challenge.
4. **Effective Integration of Temporal Cues:** The introduction of Temporal-Viewpoint-Bbox Indicator (TVBI) tokens, which inject target-specific geometric features into the visual history, effectively provides a target-conditioned memory. This mechanism stabilizes tracking when visual evidence is partial or transient, contributing to overall robustness.
5. **Scalable Data Utilization:** By formulating target identification as a catalog-based referring task, ReferTrack opens avenues for leveraging large-scale, readily available referring and grounding datasets (beyond costly closed-loop navigation data) to enhance target identification capabilities. This scalability in data acquisition is valuable for advancing EVT.
6. **Validated Sim-to-Real Transfer:** Successful real-world deployments on both legged (Unitree Go2) and humanoid (Unitree G1) robots, operating at practical frequencies, validate the robust generalization capabilities of the policy. This demonstrates its potential for practical application in diverse robotic systems and environments.
7. **Future Research Directions:** The work suggests that future VLA models could benefit from incorporating more explicit, image-grounded reasoning steps. The public availability of the code base (ReferTrack) is intended to facilitate further research in language-guided EVT, enabling the development of more grounded and efficient VLA architectures.
