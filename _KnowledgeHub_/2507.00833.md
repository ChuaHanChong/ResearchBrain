# HumanoidGen: Data Generation for Bimanual Dexterous Manipulation via LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> HumanoidGen offers an automated, LLM-driven framework to generate diverse bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks and collect high-quality demonstrations for humanoid robots within simulation. The framework achieved an average success rate exceeding 50% on 20 complex tasks and demonstrated effective data for training robotic policies.

## Problem

- Scarcity of high-quality, diverse, and scalable training data for bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks on humanoid robots.
- Existing real-world data collection methods are expensive, time-consuming, and difficult to scale, while current simulation benchmarks lack task diversity and support for dexterous hands in bimanual contexts.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) used for robotic planning have largely overlooked the complexities of bimanual dexterous manipulation.

## Method

- An automated framework leveraging LLMs to generate detailed code-form planning based on spatial annotations for bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks.
- Integration of Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) with a Segment-Truncate-Combine-Resume (STCR) mechanism to enhance the LLM's reasoning for long-horizon and complex tasks, enabling error recovery and exploration.
- A pipeline for scene generation, demonstration collection in simulation for humanoid robots with dexterous hands, and scene scaling for data generalization.

## Results

- The HumanoidGen framework achieved an average success rate exceeding 50% across 20 bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks in simulation, demonstrating superior performance over baselines in handling complex, long-horizon scenarios.
- Integrating MCTS improved the LLM's reasoning success rate by up to 55% for complex tasks, increased plan diversity, and proved cost-effective in terms of token consumption.
- Diffusion policies trained on the generated HGen-Bench data exhibited few-shot learning capabilities and continuous performance improvement with increased data, supporting the data's quality and scalability for policy training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can generate detailed, executable code-form planning that specifies precise spatial and relational constraints for low-level bimanual dexterous movements, rather than only high-level task sequences.
> - Augmenting LLM planning with Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) significantly enhances reasoning ability, task success rates, and plan diversity, particularly for complex and long-horizon tasks, by incorporating simulation feedback and error recovery.
> - Automated spatial annotation and dynamic collision management are crucial for enabling LLMs to plan physically plausible and collision-free bimanual dexterous manipulations in simulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied manipulation aims to equip robots with human-like dexterity and intelligence to interact with the world. A significant bottleneck in achieving this goal, especially for advanced robotic platforms, is the scarcity of high-quality, diverse, and scalable training data. Humanoid robots, with their anthropomorphic morphology, offer a universal platform for complex bimanual and dexterous manipulation. However, existing datasets and simulation benchmarks primarily cater to simpler robot-arm configurations, often lacking the complexity required for dual-arm coordination and intricate hand operations.

Current approaches to data generation for humanoid robots face several limitations:
- **Real-world teleoperation (VR, exoskeletons):** While providing realistic data, these methods are expensive, time-consuming, demand highly skilled human operators, and are difficult to scale for diverse tasks and environments. Consequently, prominent real-world datasets like Open-X predominantly feature single-arm manipulation.
- **Simulation-based benchmarks (SAPIEN, IsaacSim, MuJoCo):** These environments facilitate data acquisition but often fall short in task diversity and, crucially, typically do not involve dexterous hands. Benchmarks like Aloha, PerAct2, and RoboTwin focus on dual-arm or wheeled robots but largely omit fine-grained dexterous manipulation. Similarly, HumanoidBench and BiGym utilize humanoid platforms for loco-manipulation but rely on costly VR-based teleoperation or reinforcement learning policies for data collection.
- **LLM-driven planning in robotics:** Recent advancements have shown the promise of Large Language Models (LLMs) in generating high-level plans or code for robotic tasks (e.g., RoboGen, Gensim, Gensim2). However, these methods have predominantly focused on single-arm robots, overlooking the unique complexities of bimanual dexterous manipulation. Other works like Rekep, OmniManip, and RoboTwin incorporate spatial reasoning but still have limitations in handling the full spectrum of bimanual dexterous tasks.

HumanoidGen strategically positions itself to address these critical gaps. It introduces a novel, fully autonomous framework that leverages LLM reasoning to generate diverse bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks and collect scalable, high-quality demonstrations *in simulation*. By focusing on humanoid robots equipped with dual arms and dexterous hands, and by generating low-level, code-form planning based on intricate spatial constraints, HumanoidGen pushes the boundaries of autonomous data generation. It effectively bridges the gap between high-level LLM planning and low-level robot control for complex multi-contact manipulation, which is essential for realizing the full potential of humanoid robots. This work is a direct response to the urgent need for efficient, scalable data to train generalist policies for highly articulated robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching long-term objective that motivates this research is to achieve human-like manipulation capabilities in embodied robots across a wide array of scenes and tasks. Humanoid robots, by virtue of their human-like morphology, are identified as a universal and promising platform for realizing this goal, particularly through their potential for bimanual coordination and dexterous hand manipulation, enabling tasks far more complex than those achievable by conventional robot arms.

However, the authors pinpoint a critical bottleneck: the inherent intricacy of bimanual dexterous manipulation. This complexity arises from the simultaneous requirement for coordinated arm movements and fine-grained hand operations, which in turn makes the collection of high-quality demonstration datasets exceptionally challenging. Existing data generation pipelines, relying heavily on teleoperation or reinforcement learning, are either resource-intensive, lack diversity, or neglect the specifics of dexterous hands in bimanual contexts.

Given these challenges, the key objectives and motivations behind HumanoidGen are:
- **Automated Task Creation:** To develop a system that can autonomously create diverse manipulation tasks tailored for humanoid robots with dual arms and dexterous hands, moving beyond the limited task diversity of existing benchmarks.
- **Scalable Demonstration Collection:** To establish a fully autonomous paradigm for collecting high-quality demonstrations in simulation, circumventing the costs and limitations of real-world teleoperation or manual data generation. The goal is to produce data that can scale efficiently to cover a vast range of scenarios.
- **LLM-driven Code-form Planning:** To leverage the advanced reasoning capabilities of LLMs not just for high-level task planning, but to generate detailed, executable code-form planning that defines precise spatial and relational constraints for low-level arm and hand movements. This aims to ensure that the generated demonstrations are physically plausible and accurately reflect dexterous manipulation.
- **Enhanced Reasoning for Complexity:** To integrate mechanisms like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to improve the LLM's reasoning abilities, particularly for long-horizon tasks, those requiring complex sequential steps, or situations with insufficient environmental annotations.
- **Benchmark Development:** To construct a comprehensive benchmark, HGen-Bench, specifically designed for bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks on humanoid robots, enabling standardized evaluation of data quality and policy learning.

In essence, the motivation is to unlock the full potential of humanoid robots for complex manipulation by systematically addressing the data scarcity and generation challenges through an intelligent, automated, and scalable framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HumanoidGen presents an end-to-end automated framework designed for scene generation, demonstration collection, and data generalization for bimanual dexterous manipulation. The methodology is structured around several key components:

#### 3.1. Spatial Annotation and Scene Generation

- **Spatial Annotation Preparation:** A fundamental step involves meticulously annotating assets and dexterous hands with spatial information (key points and key axes). These annotations are categorized into:
 - **Hand Atomic Operations:** Defines how dexterous hands interact (e.g., pinch, grasp, press), specifying approach, attach, and parallel axes for proper alignment and manipulation.
 - **Asset Inherent Information:** Describes an asset's inherent functionality (e.g., pouring point/axis for a cup).
 - **Asset Operation Annotations:** Specifies points and axes on assets where atomic operations can be applied (e.g., multiple grasp points on a drawer handle). The paper notes that Stable Diffusion can be integrated for automatic annotation of similar assets, reducing manual effort (Appendix A.1).
 This abstraction of spatial information is crucial for the LLM's understanding of scene geometry and task relations.
- **Scene Generation:** An LLM planner, prompted with asset library information, scene details, and task descriptions, generates code for environment setup. This includes determining asset categories, quantities, placement poses, and attributes. The LLM also defines task-specific success criteria to guide execution.

#### 3.2. LLM-based Task Planning

This core component focuses on generating executable scripts of constraint chains for demonstration collection via trajectory optimizers, departing from traditional LLM high-level planning.
- **Task Decomposition:** The LLM planner decomposes long-horizon tasks into a sequence of atomic operations (`A_i`) and arm movements (`M_i`) for each hand. This reduces reasoning complexity.
- **Relational Action Constraints:** Unlike conventional grippers, dexterous hands require sophisticated motion constraints. The method introduces dynamic coordinate frames (`F_act`) for each hand, evolving with manipulation state. Constraints (`C_goal`, `C_path`) specify geometric relations (coincidence, parallelism, orthogonality) between elements of `F_act` and global frames (`F_all = F_obj ∪ F_hand ∪ F_world`). These relations are translated into a constrained optimization problem solved by a trajectory optimizer (Appendix A.2).
- **Collision Avoidance:** Two strategies ensure feasible atomic operations:
 - **Active Collision Avoidance:** The LLM proactively plans collision-free movements, especially for bimanual coordination (e.g., one hand retracting to make space).
 - **Dynamic Collision Management:** The LLM dynamically adjusts an "object-ignoring list" to permit in-contact manipulation when appropriate (e.g., ignoring drawer contact during pulling) and re-enabling checks for free-space motion.

#### 3.3. Enhancing Reasoning with MCTS

To overcome LLM limitations in long-horizon tasks or with insufficient annotations, a variant of Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) is employed.
- **STCR (Segment-Truncate-Combine-Resume) Mechanism:** This mechanism abstracts executable code into a search tree. Planning code is *Segmented* into steps. Upon error, the process is *Truncated*, invalid steps are discarded, and valid segments are *Combined* into execution units. For newly created nodes, relevant information is *Resumed* for subsequent exploration.
- **Interactive Task Planning via MCTS:** MCTS's Selection, Expansion, and Backpropagation steps are used. The Expansion step incorporates simulation results to evaluate nodes. An intrinsic exploration value `R_intrinsic` based on "valuable moments" (e.g., successfully grasping an object) incentivizes exploration (Appendix A.4).

#### 3.4. Data Collection and Generalization

- **Demonstration Collection:** Trajectories are collected using a Unitree H1-2 humanoid robot with Inspire hands in the SAPIEN simulator. The system generates diverse scenarios by randomizing initial object positions, poses, and joint angles.
- **Scene Scaling:** To enhance data diversity, room-level scene scaling is performed. Demonstration scripts from tabletop scenes are adapted to diverse room-level environments by computing transformation matrices between coordinate systems (Appendix A.3). This allows generalization across numerous scenes from platforms like RoboCasa.
- **HGen-Bench Benchmark:** The framework culminates in HGen-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark comprising 20 bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks with varying difficulty levels, specifically designed for humanoid robots. It provides multi-modal observation data (RGB, depth, point clouds) and joint states (Appendix B).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of HumanoidGen focused on three main areas: the framework's data generation and execution capabilities, the effectiveness of MCTS, and the validity of the collected data for policy learning.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Data Generation and Execution

- **Superior Performance:** HumanoidGen demonstrated superior performance compared to a modified RobotTwin baseline (equipped with dexterous hands and annotations) across 20 bimanual dexterous manipulation tasks. HumanoidGen achieved an average success rate exceeding 50%, with most tasks reaching over 75%, except for some highly complex bimanual long-horizon scenarios.
- **Handling Complexity:** HumanoidGen significantly outperformed RobotTwin in tasks involving long horizons and complex collision scenarios (e.g., "close & open box" and "blocks stack easy & hard"). This was attributed to HumanoidGen's dynamic collision management, which flexibly ignores collisions for in-contact operations (e.g., pulling a drawer) but accounts for them during free-space motion.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness Evaluation of MCTS

- **Improved Reasoning Ability:** MCTS dramatically improved the LLM's reasoning ability, particularly in complex tasks with insufficient object annotations and minimal guidance (e.g., "blocks stack hard," "pyramid stack"). For instance, a 26% increase in token consumption for "block stack single" led to a 55% improvement in reasoning success rate. MCTS enabled the LLM to learn from past errors and infer correct task steps more effectively.
- **Cost-Effective Exploration:** The MCTS exploration strategy was found to be highly cost-effective, reducing average token consumption per successful execution method despite increasing the number of trials.
- **Enhanced Plan Diversity:** MCTS significantly enhanced the diversity of generated plans, yielding a greater number of distinct successful strategies compared to non-MCTS approaches, demonstrating its superior exploratory capabilities.

#### 4.3. Validity Evaluation of Collected Data

- **Policy Scaling:** Experiments with 2D (DP) and 3D (DP3) diffusion policies trained on 20, 50, and 100 trajectories from HGen-Bench validated the quality and scalability of the generated data.
- **Few-shot Learning:** DP3 exhibited few-shot learning capabilities for relatively easy tasks, achieving a 67% success rate with only 20 demonstrations (e.g., "cup pour easy").
- **Continuous Improvement:** Policy performance generally improved with increasing data, demonstrating that HumanoidGen's data scaling approach leads to continuous policy improvement (e.g., DP for "open box hard" improved from 11.1% to 100% success with more data).
- **Challenges in Complex Tasks:** Both DP and DP3 showed performance declines on extremely challenging, long-horizon tasks (e.g., "blocks stack easy," "handover and storage"), especially with limited data, highlighting the need for more advanced policies capable of learning complex long-horizon behaviors.
- **Non-stationary Behaviors:** Some DP policies exhibited "non-stationary characteristics" where initial high success rates were due to unstable, random movements rather than learned strategies, which later decreased as more data enabled more deliberate but still imperfect actions.

#### 4.4. Additional Experiments (Appendix)

- **Real-world Transfer:** Real-world experiments demonstrated high data collection success rates (>85%) and low collection times (~14 seconds per trajectory). Policies trained with a mix of simulated and a few real-world trajectories achieved comparable or better performance than those trained with more real-world data alone, supporting the sim2real potential.
- **Automatic Annotation Efficiency:** The automatic asset annotation method significantly reduced annotation time (0.7s vs. 4-6s manually) with high success rates (90-100%).
- **Challenging Tasks:** The framework successfully handled five additional, more challenging tasks involving complex bimanual coordination and contact-rich operations, achieving high success rates (73-95%), further confirming its scalability.
- **Resource Analysis:** HumanoidGen proved efficient, with reusable scripts for randomization and task variations, minimizing manual intervention.
- **Framework Comparison:** HumanoidGen stands out as the first framework providing a unified, fully automated pipeline supporting bimanual dexterous hands, tabletop scene generation, room-level synthesis, *and* dynamic collision management.
- **LLM Compatibility:** The framework demonstrated compatibility with various LLMs (DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek-Chat-v3, GPT-4o language-only and multimodal), all showing strong performance in generating valid plans.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HumanoidGen represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for the challenging domain of bimanual dexterous tasks performed by humanoid robots. Its core contribution lies in pioneering a fully automated, LLM-driven framework for generating diverse simulation tasks and collecting high-quality demonstrations, thereby directly addressing the critical bottleneck of data scarcity.

The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

- **Accelerated Research in Humanoid Robotics:** By automating the creation of tasks and the collection of demonstrations, HumanoidGen drastically reduces the time, cost, and human effort traditionally associated with acquiring data for complex humanoid systems. This will accelerate research into developing generalist manipulation policies for these highly articulated platforms.
- **Democratization of Advanced Robotics:** The framework lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners working on bimanual dexterous manipulation. Instead of relying on expensive real-world setups or extensive manual programming, they can leverage an automated simulation pipeline to generate data at scale.
- **Enhanced Dexterity and Generalization:** HumanoidGen's meticulous spatial annotations, relational action constraints, and dynamic collision management enable the generation of demonstrations that capture the nuances of dexterous manipulation. The ability to scale scenes from tabletop to room-level, combined with varied object configurations, promotes the training of more generalizable and robust robotic policies.
- **Bridging LLMs and Low-level Control:** The novel approach of using LLMs to generate code-form planning for low-level spatial constraints, augmented by MCTS for enhanced reasoning, showcases a powerful paradigm for integrating high-level AI reasoning with precise robotic control. This is a crucial step towards truly intelligent and autonomous robots.
- **Creation of a Valuable Benchmark (HGen-Bench):** The introduction of HGen-Bench provides the community with a standardized and diverse benchmark for evaluating bimanual dexterous manipulation skills on humanoid robots. This facilitates rigorous comparisons of new algorithms and policies, fostering further innovation.
- **Potential for Real-world Applications:** The demonstrated capability for sim2real transfer suggests that policies trained on HumanoidGen's synthetic data can be effectively deployed on real humanoid robots. This has profound implications for various applications, including:
 - **Assistive Robotics:** Enabling robots to perform delicate tasks like opening bottles, preparing food, or handling personal items for individuals with disabilities.
 - **Industrial Automation:** Automating complex assembly, handling, and packaging tasks that require fine motor skills and bimanual coordination.
 - **Disaster Response:** Equipping robots to manipulate tools, clear debris, or rescue individuals in hazardous and unstructured environments.

While the work acknowledges limitations such as the need for human annotation for entirely novel asset categories and current physics engine constraints, it lays a robust foundation. Future integration with learning-based annotation methods, support for diverse physics engines, and more sophisticated goal-conditioned planning will further amplify its impact, pushing us closer to the vision of human-like intelligence and dexterity in humanoid robots.
