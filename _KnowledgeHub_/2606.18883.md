# ZiMPedance: Impedance-Aware ZMP Modeling and Control for Payload Carrying with Quadruped Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at IIT's Dynamic Legged Systems Lab developed an impedance-aware Zero Moment Point (ZMP) formulation and an associated Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for quadruped robots carrying payloads with passive spring-damper interfaces. This framework reduced ZMP-margin violations by up to 10x and horizontal ground reaction force effort by 15%, enabling stable locomotion and end-effector tracking under disturbances.

## Problem

- Passive, spring-based payload interfaces on legged robots can introduce oscillatory forces that degrade locomotion stability, especially in unstructured environments.
- Existing control methods for payload carrying often don't explicitly model how passive interface dynamics affect locomotion stability margins, particularly for low-speed quasi-static gaits.
- There was a lack of explicit derivation linking passive interface parameters (stiffness, damping, mass) directly to ZMP-based stability criteria.

## Method

- Derived an extended Zero Moment Point (ZMP) formulation that explicitly incorporates passive spring-damper payload interface dynamics.
- Conducted a frequency-domain analysis to identify potential resonance between robot locomotion harmonics and the passive arm's natural frequency.
- Implemented an Impedance-Augmented ==Model Predictive Control== (ARMPC) that integrates passive subsystem dynamics into a Single Rigid Body Dynamics (SRBD) model to manage oscillatory forces.

## Results

- The impedance-augmented MPC (ARMPC) reduced ZMP-margin violations by up to 10-fold and lowered horizontal ground reaction force effort by up to **15%** in simulations.
- In hardware pull-release tests with a 2 kg payload, ARMPC maintained stable locomotion and rejected multi-axis disturbances, while a nominal controller without impedance awareness failed.
- ARMPC enabled end-effector tracking of sinusoidal and circular motions with RMS errors as low as **0.01** m in simulation, utilizing only passive arm dynamics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Underdamped passive arm configurations can exhibit pronounced resonance with locomotion harmonics (e.g., 1 Hz during crawl gaits), significantly amplifying ZMP oscillations and reducing stability; critically damped configurations mitigate this.
> - Explicitly modeling passive interface dynamics within an MPC allows for proactive anticipation and management of oscillatory forces, leading to improved stability and locomotion efficiency.
> - A passive arm, when integrated with an impedance-aware controller, can enable end-effector tracking tasks, leveraging its inherent dynamics without needing active actuation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Heavy load transportation is a physically demanding operation in various sectors, including logistics, industry, and search-and-rescue, often exposing human operators to risks. Robotic systems can assist in these tasks, with legged robots offering advantages over wheeled platforms on uneven terrain and over aerial platforms in terms of payload capacity. This positions legged robots as suitable candidates for carrying tasks in unstructured, human-centered environments.

A common challenge in robotic payload carrying involves the interface between the robot and the load. While fully actuated manipulators offer dexterity, they introduce cost, weight, complexity, and high power consumption. Passive, spring-based arms present a lightweight alternative, providing mechanical compliance and allowing the payload to move relative to the robot, thereby reducing direct transmission of interaction forces. This compliance is analogous to that rendered by impedance control in active manipulators. However, a significant challenge with passive interfaces is the potential for their spring-damper dynamics to store and release energy, generating oscillatory forces that can degrade locomotion stability.

Previous research in payload transport and loco-manipulation with legged robots has explored various approaches, including cable-based towing, ball-joint inter-robot connections, actuated arms, and learning-based methods. These works have demonstrated effective payload interaction and task execution. For instance, some studies have focused on human-humanoid or robot-robot collaborative carrying, incorporating interaction forces into walking pattern generators, whole-body control, or optimal control problems using extended Zero Moment Point (ZMP) formulations. Learning-based methods have shown promise for scalability, where stability and interaction behavior are shaped through training objectives.

However, a specific gap identified by this paper is the lack of explicit derivation of how passive interface stiffness, damping, and carried mass directly affect the locomotion stability margin. Existing models for Model Predictive Control (MPC) in quadruped robotics, while applied to collaborative loco-manipulation, primarily consider active interfaces or dynamic trotting behaviors. There is a need for a framework that explicitly models passive interface dynamics, relates them to locomotion stability, and integrates this understanding into a predictive control scheme for low-speed quasi-static gaits like crawl gait. This paper aims to address this gap by developing an impedance-aware ZMP formulation and incorporating it into an MPC framework, specifically for quadruped robots utilizing passive spring-damper payload interfaces. This distinguishes the current work from prior research by focusing on the internal oscillatory dynamics of passive interfaces and their direct impact on ZMP-based stability criteria.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that the dynamics of passive spring-based arms, while offering benefits in terms of weight and complexity for payload carrying on quadruped robots, can introduce oscillatory forces. These forces have the potential to degrade locomotion stability, particularly when the passive system's natural frequency aligns with locomotion harmonics. Existing work has not fully analyzed this interaction from a stability perspective.

Based on this motivation, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Derive an extended ZMP formulation:** To formulate a Zero Moment Point (ZMP) model that explicitly includes the dynamics of a passive spring-damper payload interface. This objective aims to establish a theoretical relationship between the interface's stiffness, damping, and the carried payload's mass, and how these parameters influence the overall locomotion stability margin of the robot-payload system.
2. **Conduct a frequency-domain analysis:** To analyze the derived ZMP formulation in the frequency domain. This objective seeks to identify potential resonance phenomena where underdamped passive interface configurations might align with and be excited by the harmonic components of locomotion gaits, thereby amplifying oscillations and reducing stability.
3. **Augment a Single Rigid Body Dynamics (SRBD) model for Model Predictive Control (MPC):** To integrate the understanding of passive subsystem dynamics into a predictive control framework. This objective involves augmenting a standard SRBD model with the dynamics of the passive spring-damper interface and embedding this impedance-aware model into an MPC to enable the controller to explicitly account for and manage the oscillatory forces from the payload.
4. **Validate the proposed impedance-aware controller:** To evaluate the performance of the augmented MPC controller through both simulation and hardware experiments. This objective includes demonstrating the controller's ability to reduce stability violations, improve locomotion efficiency, and enable end-effector tracking through passive arm dynamics, even under disturbances, in real-world scenarios.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to provide a more comprehensive understanding of payload carrying with passive interfaces on legged robots and to develop a control strategy that leverages this understanding to enhance stability and performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted methodology that combines theoretical modeling, frequency-domain analysis, and a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework, validated through both simulation and hardware experiments.

**A. ZMP Formulation with Impedance Elements:**
The foundation of the approach involves extending the Zero Moment Point (ZMP) formulation to account for passive payload-interface dynamics.
1. **Passive Arm Modeling:** The passive arm is approximated as an equivalent mass-spring-damper system, capturing the dominant horizontal interaction forces between the payload and the robot base. This model uses joint-level stiffness ($k_i$) and damping ($c_i$) coefficients that map to an equivalent Cartesian spring-damper interaction.
2. **Extended ZMP Derivation:** Starting from the planar ZMP equations, the formulation is extended to include contributions from the payload and its interface. The ZMP along an axis (e.g., x-axis) is expressed as depending on the robot's Center of Mass (CoM) contribution and a payload-interface contribution (Eq. 7). For a local analysis, neglecting base acceleration and treating the CoM contribution as an offset, the ZMP displacement is directly related to payload mass, position, and the stiffness and damping of the interface (Eq. 8).
3. **Frequency-Domain Representation:** To analyze impedance effects in the frequency domain, the system is expressed in Laplace form. A transfer function $G(s)$ is derived (Eq. 11), which relates an external force acting on the impedance subsystem to the resulting ZMP displacement.
4. **Model Validation:** The analytical transfer function model is validated against simulation data obtained from MuJoCo, where a chirp force input is applied to the passive arm end-effector. The validation assesses how well the analytical model captures the frequency response of the full system.

**B. Frequency Response Analysis:**
1. **Damping Condition Study:** The validated analytical model is used to compare the ZMP response under different damping conditions: underdamped (UD), critically damped (CD), and overdamped (OD). Bode magnitude plots of $G(s)$ are generated to identify how these conditions affect resonance peaks and ZMP amplification.
2. **Locomotion Excitation Analysis:** The periodic base accelerations generated during locomotion are analyzed using Fast Fourier Transform (FFT). Two crawl gait patterns (back-diagonal and circular) with a step frequency of 0.5 Hz and duty factor of 0.7 are considered to identify dominant harmonic components of robot motion that could excite the passive arm.
3. **Resonance Identification:** The natural frequency of the passive arm-load subsystem (derived from the impedance parameters) is compared with the locomotion harmonics to identify potential resonance conditions where amplified ZMP oscillations could occur.

**C. Impedance-Augmented Model Predictive Control (ARMPC):**
1. **Augmented SRBD Model:** The core of the control strategy is an MPC that augments the standard Single Rigid Body Dynamics (SRBD) model. The system dynamics are decomposed into floating-base dynamics of the robot and passive dynamics of the arm. The augmented state includes the floating-base pose and spatial velocity, as well as the passive subsystem's coordinates and velocities (Eq. 12). This allows the MPC to explicitly account for spring-damper forces from the payload interface when optimizing ground reaction forces.
2. **Control Inputs and Constraints:** The control inputs remain the ground reaction forces. The Optimal Control Problem (OCP) includes ZMP stability constraints, requiring the predicted ZMP to remain within the support polygon with a specified margin (0.04 m). These constraints are softened with slack variables.
3. **Cost Function:** The cost function includes regularization terms for the arm's joint position and velocity, and a tracking term for desired end-effector positions.
4. **End-Effector Wrench Estimation:** For hardware implementation, the external wrench at the passive arm end-effector is estimated online using a momentum observer, leveraging measured arm joint states and known spring-damper parameters, without requiring a force/torque sensor.

**D. Experimental Validation:**
1. **Simulation Setup:** Simulations are conducted in MuJoCo using a 23 kg Unitree Aliengo robot equipped with a 1.5 kg passive arm. A back-diagonal crawl gait with 0.1 m/s forward velocity is used. The proposed ARMPC is compared against a "Nominal" MPC (from prior work) that treats passive arm effects as external disturbances. Scenarios include nominal locomotion, impulsive perturbations at the end-effector, and sinusoidal end-effector force excitation. Performance metrics include ZMP-margin violation percentage, RMS base pitch and roll, and horizontal ground reaction force (GRF) effort.
2. **Hardware Setup:** The ARMPC is validated on a real Unitree Aliengo. The floating-base state is estimated using a multi-sensor state estimator (MUSE), and passive arm joint positions are measured by encoders.
3. **Hardware Experiments:** A key hardware experiment is the pull-release test with a 2 kg payload, where the arm is manually pulled and released during a forward crawl gait. This test evaluates disturbance rejection and robustness, comparing ARMPC's stability against the Nominal controller. End-effector tracking tasks are also performed in simulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning the interaction between passive payload interfaces and robot locomotion stability, and demonstrated the efficacy of the proposed control approach.

**A. Impedance and Stability Analysis:**
- **Extended ZMP Formulation:** The derived ZMP formulation successfully embeds passive spring-damper payload-interface dynamics, explicitly relating interface stiffness ($k$), damping ($c$), and payload mass ($m_{load}$) to the ZMP displacement. The analytical model's frequency response showed an approximate 75% magnitude match with full MuJoCo simulation data, supporting its utility for analyzing dominant effects.
- **Frequency-Domain Resonance:** Frequency-domain analysis revealed that underdamped (UD) configurations of the passive arm-load subsystem exhibit a pronounced resonance peak around 1 Hz. Concurrently, Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) analysis of robot base accelerations during crawl gaits (both back-diagonal and circular) indicated significant energy at 1 Hz, corresponding to the second harmonic of the 0.5 Hz locomotion frequency. This alignment highlights a condition where locomotion-induced excitation can strongly resonate with and amplify oscillations in an underdamped passive arm, potentially degrading ZMP stability. Critically damped (CD) configurations were found to mitigate this resonant amplification without incurring the sluggish response of overdamped (OD) systems.

**B. Simulation Analysis of Damping Conditions (using Nominal Controller):**
- **Impact on ZMP Margin:** Simulations using a nominal MPC (which does not explicitly model passive arm dynamics) demonstrated the effect of different damping conditions on ZMP stability. With increasing payload (5 kg and 8 kg), underdamped arm configurations resulted in wider ZMP margin distributions and longer lower tails, indicating more frequent and severe reductions in the ZMP margin, especially on rough terrain. In contrast, critically damped arms yielded more compact ZMP margin distributions, predominantly above the desired stability threshold, signifying more consistent stability. Overdamped arms also reduced oscillations but exhibited more dispersed margins at higher payloads compared to critically damped arms. These results align with the theoretical frequency-domain analysis.

**C. Impedance-Augmented MPC (ARMPC) Performance in Simulation:**
- **Reduced Stability Violations:** The proposed ARMPC significantly reduced ZMP-margin violations across all tested scenarios compared to the Nominal controller. For instance, in the sinusoidal excitation scenario, ZMP-margin violations decreased by a factor of 10, from 7.0% to 0.7%.
- **Improved Locomotion Efficiency:** ARMPC consistently lowered the horizontal ground reaction force (GRF) effort by up to 15%, indicating more efficient and less strenuous locomotion for the robot.
- **Reduced Base Oscillations:** Base attitude variations, quantified by RMS pitch and roll, were generally reduced by ARMPC, although not uniformly across all components.

**D. End-Effector Task Capabilities (Simulation):**
- ARMPC demonstrated the ability to exploit the passive arm dynamics for end-effector tracking without direct arm actuation. With a 2 kg payload, the controller successfully tracked sinusoidal and circular end-effector reference motions, achieving RMS errors of 0.01 m for lateral (y-axis) sinusoidal tracking, and 0.01 m (y-axis) and 0.04 m (z-axis) for circular tracking.

**E. Hardware Validation:**
- **Enhanced Robustness:** In pull-release experiments on a physical quadruped robot with a 2 kg payload, the Nominal controller failed to maintain stability and lost balance during the release phase. In contrast, ARMPC maintained stable locomotion throughout all trials, successfully rejecting the large, multi-axis disturbances.
- **Quantitative Hardware Comparison:** While the average ZMP margin was comparable (0.08 m for ARMPC vs. 0.075 m for Nominal before failure), ARMPC kept the margin above the 0.04 m threshold for a larger portion of the experiment (82% vs. 70%) and reduced the peak horizontal GRF effort by 15%, demonstrating its superior robustness in real-world scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of legged robotics and payload manipulation by providing a comprehensive approach to managing the stability challenges associated with passive payload interfaces.

**Fundamental Understanding:** The paper offers a novel theoretical contribution through its extended ZMP formulation. By explicitly embedding passive spring-damper payload-interface dynamics, it establishes a direct analytical link between interface parameters (stiffness, damping, and payload mass) and the robot's locomotion stability margin. This foundational understanding is crucial for designing and controlling robots that interact dynamically with their environment and carried loads.

**Design and Control Implications:** The frequency-domain analysis, which reveals the potential for resonance between locomotion harmonics and underdamped passive interfaces, has direct implications for the mechanical design of passive arms and the selection of gait strategies. This insight suggests that arm impedance and gait parameters should be considered in conjunction to avoid stability-degrading resonant amplifications. This can guide engineers in selecting appropriate stiffness and damping coefficients for compliant interfaces.

**Advanced Control Capabilities:** The introduction of an impedance-augmented SRBD-MPC represents an advancement in legged robot control. By integrating passive subsystem dynamics into the predictive model, the controller gains the ability to anticipate and account for oscillatory forces from the payload, rather than treating them as unmodeled disturbances. This leads to improved stability, as evidenced by reduced ZMP stability violations, and enhanced locomotion efficiency through lower horizontal ground reaction force effort.

**Expanded Robotic Applications:** The demonstrated capability of ARMPC to maintain stable locomotion under significant external disturbances and in conditions where a nominal controller fails, particularly with payloads representing a substantial fraction of the robot's mass, expands the practical utility of quadruped robots. This makes them more reliable for tasks in logistics, industrial settings, and search-and-rescue operations where carrying heavy or dynamic loads is required, especially in unstructured or human-proximate environments.

**New Functional Paradigms:** Beyond stability, the ability of ARMPC to enable end-effector tracking by exploiting the passive arm dynamics opens avenues for new functional paradigms. This suggests that passive interfaces, traditionally viewed as simple load bearers, can also be utilized for active task execution like loco-manipulation without the need for additional complex and heavy active manipulators, reducing system cost and complexity.

**Future Research Directions:** The work lays the groundwork for several future research avenues, including the development of online adaptation methods for passive-interface parameters, the integration with richer whole-body models through advanced solvers, and extensions to multi-robot or human-robot collaborative carrying scenarios using distributed optimization techniques. These directions could further enhance the adaptability, performance, and collaborative capabilities of legged robots in complex payload handling tasks.
