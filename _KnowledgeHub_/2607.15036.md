# Learning Agile Navigation in Crowded Environments for Quadruped Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Wu et al. developed VOP-Nav, an end-to-end deep reinforcement learning framework that enables quadruped robots to navigate crowded, dynamic environments safely and agilely. The system integrates Velocity Obstacle (VO) principles through a "Velocity Obstacle Perception Network" (VOP-Net) trained to implicitly predict safe velocity regions from raw sensor data, achieving superior success rates and reduced collisions in simulation and robust zero-shot transfer to real-world robots.

## Problem

- Model-based navigation methods struggle in dense crowds due to their reliance on precise and continuous obstacle state estimations, making them fragile to sensor noise and occlusions.
- End-to-end learning approaches often lack explicit mechanisms for dynamic obstacle prediction, leading to either overly conservative behavior or reactive avoidance strategies that can result in collisions.
- Existing navigation paradigms typically present a trade-off between safety and agility, hindering a robot's ability to navigate crowded environments both quickly and without collisions.

## Method

- A three-stage ==end-to-end deep reinforcement learning== pipeline (**VOP-Nav**) is trained to map multi-modal sensor data (depth camera, LiDAR, proprioception) directly to joint-level commands for agile quadruped locomotion.
- A "Velocity Obstacle Perception Network" (**VOP-Net**) is introduced, trained on collected trajectories to implicitly predict a ==safe velocity region== from multi-frame LiDAR data, bypassing explicit obstacle detection and tracking.
- VOP-Net's predictions serve as both a supplementary input to the navigation policy during inference and a specific ==velocity constraint reward== signal during reinforcement learning training, fostering safe and agile behaviors.

## Results

- VOP-Nav demonstrated superior navigation performance in unseen simulated crowded environments, achieving success rates up to **83.67%** (e.g., in a challenging Square environment) and significantly reducing collision rates (e.g., **15.67%** vs. **44.19%** for ABS).
- The system showed robust zero-shot transferability to a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot, achieving a **100%** success rate without collisions in **15** real-world indoor trials and **12** successful outdoor trials out of **15**, using only onboard sensors.
- VOP-Nav maintained high agility, reaching peak speeds above **3 m/s** in real-world scenarios, while also demonstrating capabilities for emergency stops and stable operation under continuous, aggressive human interference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Implicitly integrating geometric safety principles, like Velocity Obstacles, into an end-to-end deep reinforcement learning framework can effectively bridge the gap between safety and agility in crowded navigation without relying on explicit perception pipelines.
> - Using temporal and spatial gradients of raw LiDAR data with an attention mechanism allows a neural network (VOP-Net) to effectively infer dynamic obstacle motion and accurately predict safe velocity regions.
> - Leveraging the implicitly predicted safe velocity region from a perception network as both a policy input and a training reward signal provides a synergistic effect, enabling robust and proactive collision avoidance while maintaining high-speed capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic navigation in dynamic and crowded environments, such as urban areas, shopping centers, and industrial facilities, presents challenges including sensor occlusion, unpredictable human motion, and the need to balance safety with agility. Current navigation paradigms typically involve a trade-off between model-based methods and end-to-end learning approaches.

Model-based methods, such as the Dynamic Window Approach (DWA) and Velocity Obstacle (VO) theories, offer theoretical safety guarantees by imposing explicit collision-avoidance constraints, often by reasoning in the velocity domain. However, these methods traditionally depend on accurate estimations of obstacle positions and velocities, as well as reliable environmental maps. In dense and dynamic crowds, sensor noise and frequent occlusions frequently lead to unreliable state estimation, degrading the performance of these methods. Some studies have attempted to relax these assumptions by integrating onboard detection and tracking, but these systems still struggle with occlusions and generalizing to diverse or unseen obstacle types.

In contrast, end-to-end learning approaches, often utilizing reinforcement learning or differentiable physics, directly map raw sensory observations to control actions. This bypasses the need for explicit perception and planning modules, potentially reducing error accumulation and latency. For quadruped robots, end-to-end systems can also facilitate agile, joint-level control, which is beneficial for maneuvering through transient free spaces in crowded settings. Despite these advantages, end-to-end methods often demonstrate limitations in dense, dynamic environments. This is partly due to the difficulty in designing effective reward functions that encourage foresight in collision avoidance and the challenge for implicit representations to robustly capture time-varying collision constraints from raw sensor data.

The current work by Wu et al. positions itself at the intersection of these two paradigms. It addresses the limitations of existing approaches by developing a system that aims to combine the geometric safety principles of Velocity Obstacle methods with the agile adaptability of end-to-end learning. A key distinction is its attempt to circumvent the requirement for explicit obstacle detection and tracking, which is a common fragility in crowded scenes for both traditional VO-based and some learning-based methods. Instead, this research focuses on implicitly encoding dynamic environmental constraints directly from raw local sensor observations, thereby offering a potentially more robust and generalized solution for complex, crowded navigation. Previous hybrid methods have often relied on explicit obstacle state estimation, which this work seeks to avoid.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable quadruped robots to navigate complex, dynamic, and crowded environments with both safety and agility. This objective is driven by the identified limitations of existing navigation systems when applied to such challenging scenarios.

The motivations for this work stem from several observed deficiencies in current robotic navigation:
- **Fragility of Model-Based Methods:** Traditional model-based approaches, like those based on Velocity Obstacles, struggle in dense crowds because they require precise and continuous knowledge of all obstacles' positions and velocities. In environments with high pedestrian density, occlusions are frequent, and sensor noise is significant, making accurate and reliable state estimation difficult or impossible. This dependency on perfect information renders these methods less robust in real-world crowded settings.
- **Limitations of End-to-End Learning:** While end-to-end learning offers robustness to sensor noise and can generate agile movements, it typically lacks an explicit mechanism for dynamic obstacle prediction. This can lead to two main problems: either the robot exhibits overly conservative behavior, slowing down excessively or freezing to avoid potential collisions, or it engages in reactive avoidance strategies that may result in collisions due to a lack of foresight regarding future states of the environment. The design of reward functions to encourage proactive, rather than merely reactive, collision avoidance in dense dynamic scenes remains a significant challenge, and implicit representations learned from raw sensors may not reliably capture the time-varying constraints imposed by moving obstacles.
- **Need for Combined Strengths:** The authors observe a need for an end-to-end navigation framework that can explicitly account for obstacle motion, thus leveraging the safety advantages of VO principles, while simultaneously retaining the robustness and agility afforded by direct sensor-to-action learning. This combination is intended to allow robots to exploit transient clear spaces (gaps) in crowded environments, which might be missed by overly conservative methods, without compromising safety.
- **Elimination of Explicit Perception Pipelines:** A specific motivation is to remove the reliance on explicit obstacle detection and tracking pipelines. These pipelines are susceptible to failure in cluttered environments, especially with occlusions or when encountering unknown object types. By implicitly encoding dynamic constraints, the system aims to overcome this perception bottleneck.

Therefore, the key objectives are to:
1. Develop an end-to-end system that maps onboard sensor data (depth camera and LiDAR) directly to joint-level commands for a quadruped robot.
2. Integrate Velocity Obstacle (VO) concepts into the learning framework to provide explicit safety awareness, without requiring explicit obstacle detection and tracking.
3. Design a "Velocity Obstacle Perception Network" (VOP-Net) capable of implicitly predicting a safe velocity region from multi-frame LiDAR data.
4. Utilize these VOP-Net predictions both as a supplementary input to the navigation policy during inference and as a reward signal during reinforcement learning training to foster safe and agile behaviors.
5. Evaluate the system's performance in simulation and demonstrate its zero-shot transferability and robustness in real-world dynamic environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system, VOP-Nav, integrates Velocity Obstacle principles with deep reinforcement learning in an end-to-end framework. It is structured around a three-stage training pipeline and relies on a specialized perception network, VOP-Net.

**Problem Formulation:** The navigation task is formulated as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), acknowledging that the robot's onboard sensors provide only a partial and occluded view of the environment. The goal is to learn a policy that maximizes expected discounted return using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).

**System Framework (Three-Stage Training):**
1. **Stage 1: Basic Policy Training:**
 - **Objective:** To train a foundational policy for high-speed locomotion and fundamental collision avoidance in a dynamic simulation environment (Isaac Gym). This policy also generates data for the subsequent stage.
 - **Observations:** A 970-dimensional vector comprising proprioceptive data (e.g., foot contact forces, angular velocities, projected gravity, goal distance, joint states), exteroceptive data from a 22x40 depth camera, and a 40-dimensional 360-degree LiDAR scan.
 - **Actions:** 12-dimensional target joint positions for a low-level PD controller.
 - **Reward Function:** Adapted from the ABS framework, it includes terms for collision avoidance, goal-tracking, regularization, and an agility reward (r_agile) that encourages high forward velocities (up to 4.5 m/s) when heading errors are within a specified range.
 - **Architecture & Training:** A hybrid encoder architecture processes heterogeneous sensor data (lightweight CNN for depth, MLPs for LiDAR, combined with proprioceptive states). Optimized using PPO in an Actor-Critic framework across 1,024 parallel Isaac Gym environments, using ray casting for sensor simulation.

2. **Stage 2: VOP-Net Training:**
 - **Objective:** To train the VOP-Net to predict a safe velocity region from raw sensor data.
 - **Data Collection:** Trajectory data is collected from the Basic policy (trained in Stage 1) navigating in dynamic simulation. This dataset includes robot observations and *privileged* ground-truth kinematics for all obstacles (positions and velocities), enabling the computation of the supervision signal.
 - **Safe Velocity Region (Supervision Signal):**
 - Computed based on Velocity Obstacle (VO) theory, similar to ORCA. Both the robot and obstacles are simplified as circles with augmented radii for collision calculation.
 - An individual VO for each obstacle represents the set of robot velocities that would lead to a collision with that obstacle within a specified time horizon (τ).
 - The overall "safe velocity region" (V_safe) is defined as the intersection of the robot's maximum feasible velocity set and the complement of the union of all individual VO regions.
 - V_safe is discretized into 360 directional safe velocity intervals. For directions with multiple safe segments, the lower-speed feasible interval is selected for conservative safety. This transforms a potentially complex geometric region into a structured and network-learnable target.
 - **VOP-Net Architecture (Fig. 5):**
 - **Input:** A sequence of five consecutive LiDAR scans.
 - **Preprocessing:** The raw LiDAR data is converted into a three-channel representation: raw distance, tangential differences (spatial gradients), and radial differences (temporal gradients) to explicitly encode dynamic cues.
 - **Feature Extraction:** MobileNet blocks are used as a lightweight backbone.
 - **Attention Mechanism:** An orthogonal attention block decomposes feature aggregation into temporal and spatial angular directions, allowing the network to focus on critical moments and hazardous sectors.
 - **Output:** The attended features are aggregated and passed through an MLP decoder to regress a 360x2-dimensional vector representing the minimum and maximum safe speeds for each of the 360 angular directions.
 - **Loss Function:** A hybrid loss combining Intersection-over-Union (L_IoU) to capture geometric overlap of predicted and ground-truth intervals, and Mean Squared Error (L_MSE) to ensure numerical precision of interval boundaries (L = 0.2L_IoU + 0.8L_MSE).

3. **Stage 3: Navigation Policy (VOP-Nav) Training:**
 - **Objective:** To train the final VOP-Nav policy, integrating the VOP-Net predictions for safe and agile navigation. This policy is trained from scratch.
 - **Observation Space:** The observation space of the Basic policy is augmented with the robot's linear velocity and the 720-dimensional flattened output of the VOP-Net (360 min/max safe speeds). This increases the total input dimensions to 1693.
 - **Reward Function:** The reward function from Stage 1 is enhanced with a specific velocity constraint reward (r_VO). This term penalizes the robot if its current planar velocity magnitude deviates from the safe velocity range predicted by VOP-Net for its current direction of motion. An inverse-distance weighting interpolation is used to map the discrete VOP-Net predictions to the robot's continuous velocity direction, ensuring a smooth reward signal.
 - **Architecture & Training:** The VOP-Nav policy utilizes a hybrid backbone similar to the Basic policy, with an additional MLP encoder for the VOP-Net predictions. The training also uses PPO, with an adjusted weighting coefficient for the agility reward (r_agile) to balance safety with high-speed performance.

**Simulation Environments:** Training and evaluation are conducted in Isaac Gym. The training environment is a 10x5 m² arena with dynamic and static obstacles. A curriculum learning strategy adaptively increases the complexity by modulating obstacle density, velocity magnitude, stochastic behavior of dynamic obstacles, and terrain roughness based on agent performance. For evaluation, four unseen test environments (Forest, Office, Square, Slow) are designed, featuring different layouts, static obstacle types, and using Reciprocal Velocity Obstacle (RVO) dynamics for dynamic obstacles to introduce a domain shift and test generalization.

**Real-world Deployment:** The trained VOP-Nav policy is deployed zero-shot on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot. The robot is equipped with a Livox Mid-360 LiDAR and an Intel RealSense D435i depth camera. Localization for indoor experiments uses a motion capture system, while outdoor experiments rely on Fast-LIO2, a LiDAR-inertial odometry algorithm. VOP-Net and the VOP-Nav policy run at 50 Hz, with a low-level PD controller operating at 200 Hz.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides comprehensive evaluations in both simulated and real-world environments to assess the VOP-Nav system's performance, the efficacy of VOP-Net, and the system's robustness.

**Evaluation in Unseen Simulated Environments:**
- **Superior Navigation Performance:** VOP-Nav demonstrated the highest success rates across all five evaluated environments (the training environment and four unseen test environments: Forest, Office, Square, Slow). For instance, in the challenging Square environment (high dynamic crowd density), VOP-Nav achieved an 83.67% success rate compared to 78.79% for ORCA(2T) and 53.11% for ABS.
- **Balanced Agility and Safety:** VOP-Nav maintained competitive peak locomotion speeds, comparable to or sometimes exceeding the agile baseline ABS. Its mean velocity was generally lower than ABS, indicating an adaptive strategy of slowing down in hazardous interactions while utilizing high speeds when clear paths were available. This balance resulted in lower collision rates compared to agile end-to-end methods like ABS and REASAN (e.g., 15.67% collision rate in Square for VOP-Nav vs. 44.19% for ABS and 50.60% for REASAN).
- **Comparison with Baselines:**
 - **Model-based (ORCA, NavRL):** These methods achieved lower collision rates than purely learning-based agile methods, highlighting the benefit of VO reasoning. However, they frequently exhibited high timeout rates and lower success rates than VOP-Nav, attributed to conservative velocity selection and sensitivity to dense, non-cooperative crowds or locomotion tracking delays in hierarchical architectures. Even with doubled episode durations, their performance did not consistently surpass VOP-Nav.
 - **Learning-based (HEIGHT, ABS, REASAN):** HEIGHT showed low success rates and high timeouts due to conservative speed limits. ABS, while agile, suffered from high collision rates in dense dynamic settings. REASAN also exhibited high collision rates.
- **Trajectory Analysis:** Qualitative analysis of trajectories (Fig. 8) illustrated VOP-Nav's ability to proactively decelerate and navigate into open spaces rather than entering crowded regions, then accelerate through feasible gaps, which enabled it to reach the goal successfully while other methods collided or froze.
- **Training Stability:** Multi-seed training of VOP-Nav confirmed the consistency of its performance, with small standard deviations across most environments, suggesting robustness in the learning process. The Office environment presented more variability due to increased static occlusion.

**Analysis of Velocity Obstacle Perception (VOP-Net):**
- **Prediction Quality:** VOP-Net achieved reasonable online prediction quality (IoU and MSE) across environments. The Square environment was identified as the most challenging, showing the lowest IoU and highest false-safe rate (over-optimistic predictions) in dense interactions. This suggested that errors in dense settings tended to be safety-critical, while errors in less crowded settings were more conservative.
- **Impact of Prediction Errors:** A comparison with GT-VOP-Nav (using ground-truth safe velocity regions) showed that VOP-Net's prediction errors primarily affected collision avoidance rather than overall task completion. GT-VOP-Nav generally improved success rates and substantially reduced collision rates while achieving higher velocities. This indicated that VOP-Net effectively retained most of the navigation performance achievable with perfect information, with remaining errors largely affecting safety in perception-challenging scenes like Office.
- **Data Efficiency:** Halving the VOP-Net training data (50%d-VOP-Net) did not significantly reduce prediction accuracy or average navigation success rates, though it did reduce policy stability across seeds and led to slightly more conservative velocity estimates.
- **Qualitative Verification:** Case studies (Fig. 9, 10) demonstrated VOP-Net's capability to infer dynamic motion, accurately distinguish between approaching threats and receding targets, and capture VO boundaries, providing reliable guidance for the navigation policy.
- **Ablation Study:** This study confirmed the synergistic effect of integrating VOP-Net predictions as both policy input and a reward signal. The "Only Reward" configuration often led to overly conservative behavior and higher timeouts, while "Only Input" provided agility but lacked comparable safety. VOP-Nav, by combining both, demonstrated the strongest overall balance between progress and safety, reducing collision rates while maintaining high success rates.

**Real-world Experiments:**
- **Zero-shot Deployment:** VOP-Nav was successfully deployed zero-shot on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot using only onboard sensors, without specific fine-tuning for the real world.
- **Robust Performance:** In 15 indoor trials with static and dynamic human obstacles, VOP-Nav achieved a 100% success rate without collisions. In 15 outdoor trials (grove and narrow path environments), 12 were successful, with 2 failures attributed to localization uncertainty and 1 timeout in an extremely narrow passage where the robot prioritized safety by waiting.
- **Sim-to-Real Analysis:** The real-world average traversal speeds were lower than in simulation, a gap attributed to factors such as scene and agent behavior mismatch (real humans versus simulated RVO), onboard sensing mismatch (LiDAR preprocessing, depth image quality, irregular real-world geometry), localization uncertainty (odometry drift in outdoor settings), and execution mismatch (latency, ground friction). Despite these factors, the robot maintained agile locomotion capabilities, reaching peak speeds above 3 m/s in open spaces.
- **Extreme Performance and Robustness Tests:** Qualitative stress tests demonstrated VOP-Nav's capabilities: achieving a peak speed of 3.014 m/s in open space, successfully performing emergency stops when obstacles appeared suddenly at 1m distance, and maintaining stable operation without collisions or falls under continuous, aggressive interference from two operators.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VOP-Nav system represents an advancement in robotic navigation for quadruped robots in complex, crowded, and dynamic environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging a Critical Gap:** The work successfully addresses the trade-off between the safety guarantees of model-based methods (like VO) and the agile adaptability of end-to-end learning. By integrating geometric priors (VO concepts) into a reinforcement learning framework in a novel way, VOP-Nav enables robots to achieve both high-speed locomotion and robust collision avoidance, a combination often difficult to attain simultaneously.
- **Enhanced Perception for Dynamic Environments:** The introduction of VOP-Net is a notable contribution. By implicitly encoding dynamic environmental constraints from multi-frame LiDAR data, it circumvents the fragility of explicit obstacle detection and tracking pipelines. This makes the system more robust to sensor occlusions, noise, and the diversity of unknown obstacles common in real-world crowded scenes. The direct prediction of safe velocity regions provides a structured and learnable representation of dynamic safety.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** VOP-Nav's ability to generalize from simulation (trained with simplified linear obstacles) to unseen environments with complex RVO-controlled crowd dynamics is significant. Furthermore, its zero-shot deployment on a physical quadruped robot in real-world indoor and outdoor settings, without any fine-tuning, validates its practical applicability and robustness in diverse, uncontrolled scenarios. This level of sim-to-real transfer is a key indicator of its potential.
- **Practical Applicability:** The demonstrated balance of agility and safety makes VOP-Nav particularly suitable for real-world applications where both factors are crucial. This includes time-critical tasks like medical emergency response or disaster relief, where robots need to navigate quickly through congested areas, and industrial settings involving interactions with human workers or moving machinery.
- **Modular Integration:** The design of VOP-Nav allows it to function either as an independent local navigation module or as a component within a broader navigation stack, where it can process sub-waypoints provided by a higher-level planner. This flexibility enhances its utility in different robotic architectures.
- **Future Research Direction:** The findings suggest that embedding geometric or physics-based priors into deep learning architectures is a promising direction for creating intelligent and reliable robotic behaviors. The authors identify future work in extending this methodology to 3D volumetric spaces to better handle complex traversability challenges (e.g., overhanging structures) and improving localization robustness in outdoor environments.

In conclusion, VOP-Nav contributes a comprehensive and effective solution for agile and safe navigation of quadruped robots in crowded dynamic environments, overcoming key limitations of prior approaches and demonstrating practical viability through real-world deployment.
