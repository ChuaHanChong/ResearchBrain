# A Minimalist Retargeting-Guided Reinforcement Learning Recipe for Dexterous Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> REGRIND, a minimalist retargeting-guided reinforcement learning pipeline, leverages a single human demonstration and an interaction-preserving retargeting method to generate robot reference trajectories for dexterous manipulation. This system achieved zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and robust generalization on contact-rich tasks, demonstrating 9/10 success on LEAP-Scissors and 10/10 on LEAP-Screwdriver in real hardware experiments.

## Problem

- Learning dexterous manipulation skills often relies on expensive, difficult-to-scale teleoperated demonstrations or faces significant sim-to-real challenges due to physics discrepancies in contact-rich tasks.
- Existing humanoid-style retargeting methods for dexterous hands frequently generate physically implausible robot trajectories for contact-rich tasks, failing to preserve crucial hand-object interactions.
- Achieving robust sim-to-real transfer for complex dexterous manipulation from simulation remains challenging, particularly for tasks involving intricate contact dynamics.

## Method

- **REGRIND**, a minimalist ==real-to-sim-to-real== pipeline, uses a novel ==interaction-aware motion retargeting== approach to convert a single human demonstration into a physically plausible robot reference trajectory.
- A ==residual reinforcement learning== policy is trained in simulation, guided by the retargeted trajectory and an ==asymmetric actor-critic== architecture with a dense, ==keypoint-tracking reward== function.
- The policy incorporates ==dynamic data augmentation==, ==domain randomization==, and ==curriculum learning== to enhance robustness and generalization, enabling zero-shot transfer to real multi-fingered robot hands.

## Results

- REGRIND achieved high success rates in simulation and demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on **three out of four** contact-rich tasks, including **9/10** success for LEAP-Scissors and **10/10** for LEAP-Screwdriver on real hardware.
- The interaction-preserving retargeting produced human-like grasps without robot-object penetrations, unlike baseline methods that often led to physically implausible or unstable reference trajectories.
- Learned policies showed robust generalization to diverse initial object configurations in the real world, achieving comparable success rates (**8/10** to **10/10**) to the original demonstrated configurations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preserving hand-object interaction semantics during motion retargeting is critical for generating physically plausible robot reference trajectories, significantly improving downstream reinforcement learning for contact-rich dexterous manipulation.
> - A minimalist approach, leveraging a single human demonstration combined with robust training techniques like dynamic data augmentation and comprehensive domain randomization, can achieve effective and generalizable dexterous manipulation policies.
> - Successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer in contact-rich tasks requires careful consideration of observation/action space design, system identification, and a strong regularization provided by an interaction-preserving reference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly dexterous manipulation, is characterized by two primary approaches for skill acquisition: learning from teleoperated demonstrations on real robots and learning through reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation followed by sim-to-real transfer. While the former avoids the sim-to-real gap, it is costly and difficult to scale, especially for high-degree-of-freedom dexterous hands. Sim-to-real transfer offers scalability but faces challenges due to discrepancies between simulated and real-world physics, which are particularly pronounced in contact-rich dexterous manipulation tasks.

Recent advancements in humanoid whole-body control have shown success with a pipeline involving retargeting human motion to robot kinematic references, training policies to track these references in simulation via RL, and then deploying them on hardware. This approach leverages the abundance of human motion data. However, applying this "recipe" directly to dexterous manipulation presents difficulties. Dexterous manipulation often involves intricate, sustained contact interactions, requiring precise regulation of contact modes and forces. Simple kinematic retargeting, while matching human poses, frequently generates physically implausible trajectories for robot hands that can be poor references for downstream RL, especially when considering the crucial hand-object interactions. Previous attempts to use humanoid-style retargeting for dexterous hands have largely been confined to simulation or open-loop replays, with limited success in real-world deployment.

This research addresses a specific gap by investigating whether preserving hand-object interactions during the retargeting process improves the efficacy of RL for contact-rich dexterous manipulation and facilitates robust sim-to-real transfer. It aims to develop a minimalist, data-efficient pipeline that bridges human demonstration with real-world robot execution for complex, contact-rich tasks, departing from methods that rely on extensive robot-specific data collection or simplified interaction models. The work positions itself as advancing the understanding and practical application of retargeting-guided RL for dexterous manipulation, specifically focusing on the challenges posed by real-world contact dynamics and the sim-to-real gap.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and underlying motivations of this research are:

- **Develop a Minimalist Retargeting-Guided RL Pipeline for Dexterous Manipulation:** The central objective is to create a streamlined, effective pipeline, named REGRIND, capable of learning dexterous manipulation skills. This directly addresses the complexity and data intensity often associated with training robots for dexterous tasks. The term "minimalist" implies an emphasis on simplicity and efficiency in data requirements and methodology.

- **Leverage Single Human Demonstrations:** A key motivation is to overcome the scarcity of multi-fingered dexterous manipulation data, which is expensive and challenging to collect directly on robots. By developing a pipeline that can learn from a *single* human demonstration, the authors aim to significantly reduce the data collection bottleneck and broaden the applicability of learning-based approaches.

- **Investigate the Impact of Interaction-Preserving Retargeting:** The core hypothesis driving the work is that the success of retargeting human motion for dexterous manipulation hinges on accurately preserving the physical interactions between the hand and the object. Unlike whole-body control where kinematic matching might suffice, dexterous manipulation inherently involves complex, contact-rich dynamics. The motivation here is to ascertain if a retargeting method that maintains hand-object spatial and contact relationships yields more physically plausible and useful robot reference trajectories compared to purely kinematic methods, thereby improving downstream RL training and real-world performance.

- **Enable Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A significant motivation is to train robust policies entirely in simulation and deploy them directly on real hardware without further real-world training. This leverages the scalability of simulation for efficient policy learning. The challenge, particularly for contact-rich tasks, is the notorious sim-to-real gap. The work aims to identify the factors necessary for successful zero-shot transfer in this domain.

- **Generalize Policies to Diverse Initial Configurations:** Learning from a single demonstration inherently limits the coverage of the task space. The authors are motivated to enable the learned policies to generalize beyond the exact demonstrated configuration. This involves developing strategies, such as dynamic data augmentation, to expose the RL policy to a broader range of initial conditions during training, thus enhancing robustness and practical utility.

- **Identify Key Factors for Sim-to-Real Success in Contact-Rich Settings:** Beyond simply achieving task success, a deeper motivation is to systematically analyze and understand the critical elements that govern sim-to-real transfer in dexterous manipulation. This includes evaluating the roles of observation/action space design, domain randomization, curriculum learning, and system identification, with the goal of providing practical guidance for future research in this challenging area.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to make learning dexterous robot manipulation more accessible, efficient, and robust by effectively bridging human motion data with robot capabilities through an interaction-aware, simulation-to-reality pipeline.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The REGRIND pipeline operates on a real-to-sim-to-real paradigm, comprising four main stages: human data collection, interaction-preserving retargeting, RL training with augmentation, and hardware deployment.

#### 3.1. Human Data Collection

Human demonstrations are acquired using motion capture systems. For the scissors task, data from the ARCTIC dataset is utilized. For the screwdriver task, custom data is collected. The demonstrations consist of MANO hand keypoints and 6D object poses, which may include additional joint positions for articulated objects. Hand keypoints are obtained via the HaMeR hand pose estimation model, which triangulates 3D keypoints from multi-view RGB images. Object poses are directly captured by the motion capture system.

#### 3.2. Interaction-Aware Motion Retargeting

The core of REGRIND's retargeting is to convert the human demonstration into a robot trajectory that preserves the hand-object interaction semantics. This is formulated as an optimization problem to find the robot's configuration (wrist pose and joint positions) over time, denoted as $q_{0:T-1}$.

- **Interaction Meshes:** Semantic keypoints are defined on both the human hand (MANO keypoints) and the object. Corresponding robot keypoints are defined on the robot hand. These hand and object keypoints form "source" (human) and "target" (robot) interaction meshes using Delaunay tetrahedralization.
- **Optimization Objective:** The optimization minimizes the deformation energy between the source and target meshes, quantified by the change in Laplacian coordinates of corresponding vertices. This term encourages the robot's hand-object relationship to mirror the human's. A temporal smoothness term is also included to regularize the robot trajectory.
- **Constraints:** The optimization is subject to the robot's kinematic constraints, including joint limits, velocity bounds, and non-penetration constraints between robot parts and the environment/object.
- **Solver:** A Sequential Quadratic Programming (SQP)-style solver is used, solving sequentially for each timestep, warm-started from the previous timestep's solution.

#### 3.3. Reference Motion-Guided Policy Learning

The retargeted robot trajectory serves as a reference for training a residual reinforcement learning policy in simulation.

- **Action Space:** The policy learns to output a residual action, which is added to the nominal robot configuration from the retargeted trajectory. This combined target is then sent to a low-level PD controller, allowing the policy to refine the reference motion.
- **Observation Space:** An asymmetric actor-critic architecture is employed. The actor receives observations that are reliably obtainable in the real world: object configuration (position, Rot6D orientation, articulated joint positions), robot proprioception (current and previous joint positions), the last action, and a phase variable indicating progress through the motion. The critic has access to additional information, such as robot fingertip positions and joint velocities, which are not directly used by the actor to mitigate sim-to-real transfer challenges.
- **Reward Function:** A dense, generalizable reward function is designed around keypoint tracking:
 - **Primary Reward:** An exponential kernel-shaped reward for minimizing the average L2 distance between the current and target object keypoint positions. This metric is applicable to both rigid and articulated objects.
 - **Auxiliary Rewards:** Additional terms penalize discrepancies in object linear/angular velocities, wrist position/orientation, and large action magnitudes or rates to encourage smooth, efficient motion. A penalty for early episode termination is also included. The hand-object interaction prior is implicitly encoded in the reference trajectory.
- **Reference State Initialization (RSI) and Early Termination:** During RL training, episodes are initialized by sampling states from the retargeted trajectory. The robot and object are initialized to these corresponding states with small perturbations to encourage exploration around the reference. Episodes are terminated early if the object deviates excessively from the reference trajectory.
- **Data Augmentation:** To improve policy robustness and generalization from a single demonstration, dynamic data augmentation is performed. Initial object configurations are perturbed (e.g., ±5 cm for position, ±30 degrees for orientation). A time-varying rigid transformation is then applied to the entire reference trajectory, blending from the perturbed initial state back to the original goal state by the end of the trajectory. Critically, this transformation is applied directly to the object and robot root poses without re-running the full retargeting optimization, thereby preserving the relative hand-object relationships established by the original retargeting.
- **Domain Randomization and Curriculum Learning:**
 - **Domain Randomization (DR):** Physics parameters (e.g., friction, motor gains, time delays), object center of mass offset, object geometry scale, robot/object mass scale, joint stiffness/damping, and finger default-joint offsets are randomized across parallel simulation environments.
 - **Curriculum:** Gravity is gradually increased from zero to full value over training steps, aiding in tasks involving lifting objects. Random pushes are applied to the object and robot, with their strength increasing over time.
 - **Observation Noise:** Additive uniform noise and continuous time lags are applied to observations to model real-world sensor imperfections and delays.
- **RL Algorithm:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), implemented with RSL-RL, is used for policy optimization in a large-scale parallel simulation environment (IsaacSim).

#### 3.4. Hardware Deployment

Policies trained in simulation are transferred zero-shot to real robot hardware.
- **Robot Systems:** Experiments are conducted on two multi-fingered robot hands: a 16-DoF LEAP hand and a 20-DoF WUJI hand, each mounted on a 7-DoF UR5e arm.
- **Perception:** Object poses for policy deployment are provided by a motion capture system, bypassing perception errors as a confounding factor to focus on sim-to-real dynamics gaps.
- **System Identification:** Careful system identification is performed to align the real robot's dynamics with the simulation, particularly identifying and accounting for control delays (e.g., 1-2 timesteps delay).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to address four key questions regarding retargeting fidelity, simulation performance, sim-to-real transfer, and generalization to different initial states.

#### 4.1. Retargeting Quality (Q1)

Qualitative analysis of retargeted configurations demonstrated that REGRIND produced human-like grasps that maintained hand-object interaction semantics. In contrast, baselines employing simple inverse kinematics (IK-based), such as Mink IK + RL and DexMachina-style IK, resulted in significant robot-object penetrations, which could lead to instability when used to initialize a simulator for RL training. Although DexMachina included a simulation-based post-processing step to resolve penetrations, this often displaced fingers to suboptimal positions without preserving the intended interaction, which was deemed detrimental for guiding RL. This finding supports the utility of interaction-preserving retargeting for generating meaningful robot reference motions.

#### 4.2. Simulation Performance Comparison (Q2)

REGRIND consistently achieved low object keypoint tracking errors and high success rates (approaching 100%) across all four evaluated tasks: LEAP-Scissors, LEAP-Screwdriver, WUJI-Scissors, and WUJI-Screwdriver. DexMachina also demonstrated high success rates on the screwdriver tasks, but its performance declined substantially on the more complex scissors tasks, which involve intricate object geometry and hand-object interactions. Both Mink IK + RL and SPIDER exhibited poor performance, with SPIDER yielding particularly high tracking errors and 0% success rates, indicating that its open-loop retargeted trajectories were not suitable as initializations for residual RL in these contact-rich scenarios. This suggests that interaction-preserving retargeting within the REGRIND framework provides a more robust foundation for RL training.

#### 4.3. Sim-to-Real Transfer (Q3)

REGRIND policies transferred reliably to real hardware on three of the four tasks: LEAP-Scissors (9/10 success), LEAP-Screwdriver (10/10 success), and WUJI-Screwdriver (9/10 success), achieving low object keypoint tracking errors. Notably, DexMachina policies, despite strong simulation performance on screwdriver tasks, transferred poorly to the real world (2/10 and 5/10 success rates respectively). The authors hypothesize that DexMachina's policies might have exploited simulation artifacts without the strong regularization provided by interaction-preserving retargeting, leading to unstable grasps or overly aggressive motions on the real robot. Mink IK + RL showed no successful transfers. All methods, including REGRIND, failed to achieve successful real-world transfer for the WUJI-Scissors task, which was attributed to a larger sim-to-real gap arising from the non-backdrivable motors of the WUJI hand and potential inaccuracies in the real scissors' mesh model. This finding indicates the importance of carefully designed retargeting and training for real-world robustness.

#### 4.4. Generalization to Different Initial Configurations (Q4)

REGRIND policies demonstrated robust generalization when evaluated with randomized initial object configurations in the real world (position perturbations of ±5 cm along x/y axes, orientation perturbations of ±30 degrees around the z-axis). On the LEAP-Scissors, LEAP-Screwdriver, and WUJI-Screwdriver tasks, the policies achieved tracking errors and success rates (8/10 to 10/10) comparable to those on the original demonstrated configurations. This result highlights the effectiveness of the dynamic data augmentation strategy implemented during RL training in improving the learned policy's robustness and generalizability to varying start states.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of dexterous robotic manipulation by presenting REGRIND, a retargeting-guided reinforcement learning pipeline. The work demonstrates the feasibility of learning complex, contact-rich manipulation skills from a single human demonstration and transferring these policies zero-shot to real hardware.

The primary significance lies in the empirical evidence that **interaction-preserving motion retargeting** is a critical component for successful sim-to-real transfer in dexterous manipulation. By focusing on maintaining the spatial and contact relationships between the hand and the object during retargeting, REGRIND generates physically plausible robot trajectories that serve as effective reference motions for downstream RL. This contrasts with simpler kinematic retargeting methods that often yield unstable or implausible robot behaviors, hindering policy learning and real-world deployment. The distinction is particularly relevant for tasks requiring delicate regulation of contact forces and modes, such as tool use.

The development of REGRIND offers a **minimalist and efficient approach** to data acquisition, leveraging a single human demonstration rather than extensive robot-specific data collection or teleoperation. This addresses a significant bottleneck in scaling dexterous manipulation capabilities across diverse tasks. Furthermore, the demonstrated ability of REGRIND policies to **generalize to randomized initial configurations** using a simple dynamic data augmentation strategy enhances their practical utility and robustness.

Through systematic hardware experiments, the research provides **practical guidance for achieving robust sim-to-real transfer** in contact-rich settings. It identifies that successful transfer requires not only appropriate retargeting but also thoughtful observation and action space design, comprehensive domain randomization, curriculum learning, and careful system identification (e.g., accounting for real-world delays). This comprehensive approach contributes to a better understanding of the factors that govern the sim-to-real gap in dexterous manipulation.

The potential impact of this work includes:
- **Accelerating Skill Acquisition:** By enabling learning from readily available human motion data, REGRIND could accelerate the development of a broader range of dexterous manipulation skills for robots.
- **Expanding Robot Capabilities:** The pipeline's effectiveness on contact-rich tool-use tasks (scissors, screwdriver) suggests its applicability to other complex manipulation scenarios, potentially leading to more versatile and capable robotic systems.
- **Informing Future Research:** The insights regarding interaction-preserving retargeting and the factors influencing sim-to-real transfer provide a foundation for subsequent research, particularly in addressing the remaining challenges such as vision-based policies and adaptive test-time learning to further reduce reliance on explicit state information and careful system identification. The released code and data are intended to facilitate community reproduction and further development.
