# Contact-Aware Neural Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC San Diego and Amazon FAR developed a contact-aware neural dynamics model that integrates explicit contact information from tactile sensors to enhance sim-to-real transfer for robotic manipulation. The model achieves superior long-horizon prediction accuracy in object motion forecasting and improves manipulation policy robustness by leveraging real-world contact data.

## Problem

- Bridging the sim-to-real gap for contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks due to discrepancies between simulated and real-world dynamics.
- Limitations of existing sim-to-real methods (e.g., explicit system identification, domain randomization, contact-agnostic neural dynamics) in capturing high-dimensional, non-smooth contact interactions.
- The inefficiency and cost of collecting sufficient real-world data for training robust manipulation policies.

## Method

- A ==two-stage neural dynamics model== that explicitly predicts future contact events using a ==contact predictor== and subsequently generates object pose trajectories using a ==diffusion pose predictor==, conditioned on these predicted contacts.
- Utilizes a ==binary, hand-level contact signal== derived from force-based tactile sensors in the real world and collision detection in simulation to ground dynamics modeling in physical interactions.
- An ==implicit sim-to-real alignment== framework that pre-trains the model on large-scale simulated data with ==domain randomization==, followed by fine-tuning with a small amount of real-world interaction data containing tactile feedback.

## Results

- The 'Diffusion-UNet w/ Contact' model consistently outperformed baselines, achieving an MSE of **0.0082** and an ADD-S of **88.23%** for single-object tasks after real-world fine-tuning.
- The model demonstrated high fidelity in capturing intricate object motions and abrupt contact transitions, leading to superior long-horizon prediction accuracy compared to contact-agnostic methods.
- Enhanced robustness of downstream manipulation policies, with the contact-aware model achieving task success rates of **73.7%** for single-object and **64.7%** for multi-object tasks, outperforming real-only trained models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating contact information from tactile sensing into neural dynamics models significantly improves the accuracy and physical plausibility of long-horizon object motion predictions, especially for non-smooth contact-rich tasks.
> - A diffusion-based pose predictor, conditioned on future contact sequences, can effectively adapt to varying motion regimes and generate stable, physically consistent trajectories.
> - Combining a robust prior learned from extensive simulation data with targeted fine-tuning using limited real-world tactile data provides an efficient and effective strategy for bridging the sim-to-real gap.
