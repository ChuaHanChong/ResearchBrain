# Contact-Aware Neural Dynamics

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC San Diego and Amazon FAR developed a contact-aware neural dynamics model that integrates explicit contact information from tactile sensors to enhance sim-to-real transfer for robotic manipulation. The model achieves superior long-horizon prediction accuracy in object motion forecasting and improves manipulation policy robustness by leveraging real-world contact data.

## Problem

- Bridging the sim-to-real gap for contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks due to discrepancies between simulated and real-world dynamics.
- Limitations of existing sim-to-real methods (e.g., explicit system identification, domain randomization, contact-agnostic neural dynamics) in capturing high-dimensional, non-smooth contact interactions.
- The inefficiency and cost of collecting sufficient real-world data for training robust manipulation policies.

## Method

- A ==two-stage neural dynamics model== that explicitly predicts future contact events using a ==contact predictor== and subsequently generates object pose trajectories using a ==diffusion pose predictor==, conditioned on these predicted contacts.
- Utilizes a ==binary, hand-level contact signal== derived from force-based tactile sensors in the real world and collision detection in simulation to ground dynamics modeling in physical interactions.
- An ==implicit sim-to-real alignment== framework that pre-trains the model on large-scale simulated data with ==domain randomization==, followed by fine-tuning with a small amount of real-world interaction data containing tactile feedback.

## Results

- The 'Diffusion-UNet w/ Contact' model consistently outperformed baselines, achieving an MSE of **0.0082** and an ADD-S of **88.23%** for single-object tasks after real-world fine-tuning.
- The model demonstrated high fidelity in capturing intricate object motions and abrupt contact transitions, leading to superior long-horizon prediction accuracy compared to contact-agnostic methods.
- Enhanced robustness of downstream manipulation policies, with the contact-aware model achieving task success rates of **73.7%** for single-object and **64.7%** for multi-object tasks, outperforming real-only trained models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly incorporating contact information from tactile sensing into neural dynamics models significantly improves the accuracy and physical plausibility of long-horizon object motion predictions, especially for non-smooth contact-rich tasks.
> - A diffusion-based pose predictor, conditioned on future contact sequences, can effectively adapt to varying motion regimes and generate stable, physically consistent trajectories.
> - Combining a robust prior learned from extensive simulation data with targeted fine-tuning using limited real-world tactile data provides an efficient and effective strategy for bridging the sim-to-real gap.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The core challenge addressed by this paper is the "sim-to-real gap" in robotic learning, particularly for tasks involving complex, dynamic, and discontinuous physical interactions, such as contact-rich manipulation. Accurate physics simulation is crucial for scalable robotic learning, as it allows for the generation of vast amounts of training data without the cost and dangers of real-world experimentation. However, policies trained purely in simulation often fail to transfer effectively to real robots due to discrepancies between simulated and real-world dynamics.

The broader research landscape has explored several avenues to bridge this gap:
- **Explicit System Identification:** This approach attempts to fine-tune explicit simulator parameters (e.g., geometric properties, friction, mass) so that simulated rollouts better match real trajectories. However, the paper argues that this is often insufficient for contact-rich manipulation because many error sources are high-dimensional, state-dependent, or arise from the discrete integration of the simulation, which cannot be captured by a few parametric corrections.
- **Domain Randomization:** This technique involves randomizing simulator parameters during training to encourage policies to be robust to variations. While effective for some tasks and helpful for bridging the "perception gap" (e.g., through aggressive rendering randomization), it often trades accuracy for robustness and still struggles to capture the non-smooth transitions inherent in contact dynamics.
- **Vision-Based Sim-to-Real:** These methods primarily focus on closing the perception gap by using techniques like photorealistic rendering to make simulated visual inputs resemble real-world ones. While enabling robust visual policies, they leave the underlying contact dynamics model largely unaddressed.
- **Implicit Alignment via Learning (Neural Dynamics/World Models):** More recent work has explored learning-based methods, such as neural residual models atop analytical dynamics, differentiable simulators with learned components, or pure neural dynamics models. These approaches often treat the simulator as a prior and use neural networks to model the residual errors. However, a significant limitation highlighted by the authors is that many of these methods are "contact-agnostic," treating discontinuities as noise, or relying solely on kinematic and proprioceptive signals. Models trained purely on simulation data often lack transferability, while those relying only on real-world data are inefficient to collect.

This paper positions itself by addressing the shortcomings of contact-agnostic implicit alignment methods. It argues that contact information itself, especially from high-bandwidth tactile sensing, is a rich and underutilized signal for guiding the dynamics modeling process. By explicitly incorporating contact awareness into a neural dynamics model, the authors aim to achieve a more physically grounded and accurate sim-to-real transfer for dexterous, contact-rich manipulation, leveraging the scalability of simulation while inheriting fidelity from real-world data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an implicit sim-to-real alignment framework that effectively models complex, contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks. The authors aim to overcome the persistent sim-to-real gap by directly aligning the simulator’s dynamics with real-world observations, specifically by leveraging contact information.

The key objectives can be broken down into the following points:
- **Develop a Contact-Aware Neural Dynamics Model:** The primary goal is to learn a neural dynamics model that is explicitly aware of physical contacts. This involves training the model to predict both future contact events and subsequent object pose changes, conditioned on historical states, actions, and contact information.
- **Utilize Tactile Information for Sim-to-Real Alignment:** The work seeks to demonstrate that tactile contact information from robotic hands can effectively model the non-smooth discontinuities inherent in contact-rich tasks. This is crucial for grounding the neural dynamics model in real-world physics.
- **Propose an Implicit Sim-to-Real Alignment Framework:** Instead of explicit parameter tuning, the framework treats an off-the-shelf simulator as a base prior and refines simulated states using a neural network trained with real-world observations.
- **Improve State Prediction Accuracy:** A core objective is to achieve higher fidelity in long-horizon forward predictions of object motion compared to existing methods, particularly in scenarios involving frequent and complex contacts.
- **Enable Better Policy Performance and Refinement:** The ultimate goal is for the learned forward dynamics model to be effectively used to predict the performance of manipulation policies and to refine policies initially trained purely in standard simulators, thereby enhancing their transferability and robustness to real-world deployment.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several critical challenges in robotics:
- **Scalability vs. Fidelity Trade-off:** While simulation offers unparalleled scalability for data generation, its fidelity often falls short for contact-rich tasks, making purely sim-trained policies unreliable.
- **Limitations of Existing Sim-to-Real Methods:** Current approaches, whether explicit system identification or contact-agnostic neural models, are inadequate for capturing the intricate, high-dimensional, state-dependent, and non-smooth dynamics introduced by physical contacts.
- **Underutilization of Rich Contact Signals:** High-bandwidth tactile sensing provides valuable, responsive signals about physical interaction that are often overlooked or treated as noise in dynamics modeling, leading to less physically grounded predictions.
- **Need for Generalizable Manipulation:** Developing generalizable manipulation policies requires accurate physics models that can handle diverse objects and interaction patterns, a need that current simulators struggle to meet for contact-rich scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology formulates sim-to-real alignment as a conditional dynamics prediction problem, where the model learns to forecast contact-dependent object motion using multimodal observations from both simulation and reality.

**Problem Formulation:**
At each time step *t*, the system state is defined by:
- **Object Pose:** `s_t ∈ SE(3)` (translation and rotation).
- **Robot Hand Configuration:** `q_t ∈ R^dq` (joint values).
- **Robot Action:** `a_t ∈ R^dq`.
- **Binary Contact Information:** `c_t ∈ {0, 1}` (1 if any fingertip is in contact, 0 otherwise).
- **Object Geometry:** `P ∈ R^(N×3)` (point cloud from mesh).

The model takes a fixed-length **history window** `H_t = {s_t-K:t, a_t-K:t, q_t-K:t, c_t-K:t, P}` as input and aims to predict future contacts and state trajectories over a horizon *H*: `c-hat_t+1:t+H = f_phi(H_t)` and `delta-s-hat_t+1:t+H = g_theta(H_t, c-hat_t+1:t+H)`.

**Model Framework and Architecture (Two-Stage):**
The architecture comprises two coupled modules:
1. **Stage I: Contact Predictor:**
 - **Input:** A shared latent feature `z_t` derived from multimodal encoders.
 - **Output:** An H-step contact probability sequence `c-hat_t+1:t+H` predicted by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) followed by a sigmoid activation.
 - **Supervision:** Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss `L_cnt` against ground-truth contacts.
 - **Function:** This module explicitly forecasts future contact events, which are crucial for conditioning the subsequent pose dynamics. The predicted sequence is projected into a compact contact feature `f_c` (`R^64`).

2. **Stage II: Diffusion Pose Predictor:**
 - **Input:** A dynamics condition vector `h_t` formed by concatenating the shared latent feature `z_t` and the predicted contact feature `f_c`.
 - **Output:** A sequence of H pose increments `x_0 = delta-s_t+1:t+H`. Each increment is represented in a 6D minimal form (3D translation delta, 3D rotation increment mapped via exponential map).
 - **Mechanism:** A conditional denoising diffusion model, implemented as a 1D U-Net, predicts the noise `epsilon_theta` in a noisy sample `x_t` given the time step `t` and the condition `h_t`.
 - **Supervision:** L2 loss `L_diff` between the predicted noise and the actual noise.
 - **Function:** This module generates physically consistent pose trajectories conditioned on the historical context and the *predicted future contact sequence*, allowing it to adapt to non-smooth dynamics.

**Multimodal Encoders and Fusion:**
- **Temporal Encoder:** Processes sequences of object poses (`s`), robot actions (`a`), and joint configurations (`q`).
- **Contact Encoder:** Processes binary contact sequences (`c`).
- **PointNet Encoder:** Processes the static object point cloud (`P`) to extract geometry embeddings (`f_P`).
- All modality embeddings are concatenated and fused through a lightweight MLP to produce the shared latent feature `z_t ∈ R^512`.

**Contact Modeling:**
To ensure robustness and align sim-to-real transfer, the paper adopts a binary, hand-level contact signal (`c_t`):
- **Simulation:** Binary contact is derived from fingertip and object mesh collision detection in MuJoCo.
- **Real World:** The XHand's force-based tactile sensors detect contact when the aggregated 3D contact force (`|F_x| + |F_y| + |F_z|`) for any fingertip exceeds a calibrated threshold (0.3N). This thresholding provides a consistent and robust binary signal across domains.

**Implicit Sim-to-Real Alignment Process:**
The training process consists of two stages:
1. **Base Model Training (Simulation Only):** The entire model architecture is first trained using large-scale simulated trajectories. This includes extensive domain randomization (adding Gaussian noise to control commands, perturbing object/hand poses) to expose the model to diverse motion patterns and build a robust prior. For example, 8,000 trajectories for single-object and 15,000 for multi-object settings are used.
2. **Fine-tuning (Co-training with Real Data):** A crucial step involves fine-tuning the pre-trained simulation model with a *small amount* of real-world interaction data, which includes tactile sensor readings. This co-training process implicitly aligns the simulated and real-world dynamics by refining the model's contact-conditioned latent representation, allowing it to better capture real contact patterns and dynamics.

**Experiment Setup:**
- **Hardware:** XArm7 robotic arm equipped with an XHand (5 fingertip modules, each with an array of tri-axial tactile sensors, 0.05 N resolution, 80-85 Hz communication).
- **Objects:** Everyday objects from the YCB dataset.
- **Pose Estimation:** FoundationPose is used for real-world object pose estimation, with small random perturbations applied during training to mitigate noise and drift.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that explicitly incorporating contact information into a neural dynamics model significantly enhances long-horizon prediction accuracy and improves sim-to-real transfer for contact-rich manipulation.

**Qualitative Results:**
- **High Fidelity:** The contact-aware neural dynamics model produces simulated rollouts that exhibit high fidelity to real-world observations. It successfully captures intricate object motions and, critically, abrupt contact transitions that standard rigid-body simulators often miss due to their simplified dynamics.
- **Physical Plausibility:** Even under significant variations in contact geometry, the model maintains structural integrity and generates physically plausible states, showing robustness against domain randomization and parameter mismatches.
- **Adaptive Motion Regimes:** The model demonstrates the ability to adaptively switch motion regimes, akin to a simulator with collision detection. For instance, in slippage scenarios, it accurately detects contact loss and adjusts the trajectory accordingly, leading to stable and physically consistent predictions.
- **Improved Sim-to-Real Transfer:** When fine-tuned with a small amount of real-world data, the model shows improved temporal stability and contact consistency, leading to stronger sim-to-real transfer.

**Quantitative Results (Table 1 analysis):**
The quantitative evaluations use Mean Squared Error (MSE↓) for prediction accuracy and the area under the curve (AUC) of ADD-S (%↑) for spatial accuracy over 3D trajectories. The comparisons are made across different training regimes (simulation only, real data for evaluation, and real-world fine-tuning) and tasks (single-object and multi-object manipulation).

- **Superior Performance:** The proposed "Diffusion-UNet w/ Contact" consistently achieves the best performance across all evaluated metrics and regimes. It yields lower MSE (better accuracy) and higher ADD-S (better spatial consistency) compared to neural dynamics baselines (e.g.,) and other ablation models (MLP, UNet, Diffusion-UNet without contact).
- **Impact of Real-World Fine-tuning:** The most significant gains are observed when the model is fine-tuned with real-world data. For single-object tasks, "Diffusion-UNet w/ Contact" achieves an MSE of 0.0082 and an ADD-S of 88.23% after real-world fine-tuning, outperforming its simulation-only counterpart (0.015 MSE, 68.12% ADD-S) and all other baselines.
- **Generalization to Multi-Object Tasks:** The model maintains strong performance in the more complex multi-object setting, achieving 0.0058 MSE and 79.12% ADD-S with fine-tuning, highlighting the generalization capabilities of the contact-aware representation.

**Application: Task Success Rate Evaluation (Table 2 analysis):**
The model's practical utility is demonstrated by evaluating its ability to assess and filter manipulation policies. Task success is defined as the percentage of rollouts where the final predicted object position deviates from the real trajectory endpoint by less than 5 cm.

- **Enhanced Policy Robustness:** The "Sim+Real w/ Contact" model significantly outperforms a "Real-only" trained model. It achieves 73.7% success for single-object tasks (vs. 52.6% for Real-only) and 64.7% for multi-object tasks (vs. 47.1% for Real-only).
- **Reduced Drift:** This result underscores that incorporating structured priors from simulation and refining them with real contact supervision effectively bridges the sim-to-real gap, leading to substantially higher robustness and success in downstream manipulation tasks by reducing accumulated prediction drift over long horizons.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Contact-Aware Neural Dynamics" paper makes several significant contributions to the field of robotic learning, with substantial potential impact:

- **Novelty in Sim-to-Real Alignment:** The work introduces a novel implicit sim-to-real alignment framework that critically leverages *explicit contact information* from tactile sensors. Unlike previous neural dynamics models that often treat contacts indirectly or as noise, this method directly models the non-smooth discontinuities inherent in physical interactions. This direct integration of contact awareness is a key differentiator and a significant step towards more physically grounded learned dynamics.
- **Improved Physical Realism and Prediction Fidelity:** By explicitly predicting contact events and conditioning pose dynamics on these predictions, the model achieves superior accuracy in long-horizon object motion forecasting. This increased fidelity translates into more realistic simulated rollouts that closely match real-world observations, especially for complex, contact-rich scenarios where traditional simulators fall short.
- **Data Efficiency for Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The framework effectively combines the scalability of large-scale simulation data for initial training (providing a robust prior) with the fidelity derived from fine-tuning on only a small amount of real-world interaction data. This hybrid approach offers a practical solution to the persistent challenge of expensive and time-consuming real-world data collection in robotics.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The quantitative results demonstrate significant gains in robustness and generalization across diverse objects and contact configurations, even in complex multi-object settings. This suggests that the contact-aware representation effectively captures underlying physical principles applicable across varying scenarios.
- **Direct Application for Policy Learning and Refinement:** A major impact lies in the model's direct applicability to improving robot policies. The learned forward dynamics model can be used to:
 - **Predict Policy Performance:** Evaluate how well a policy trained in simulation might perform in the real world *before* actual deployment.
 - **Refine Policies:** Fine-tune policies trained purely in standard simulators, allowing them to better adapt to real-world friction, compliance, and contact patterns. This is particularly valuable for large-scale dexterous manipulation policies.
 - **Model-Based Planning:** Serve as a precise and differentiable physical model within model-predictive control or other planning frameworks, enabling more effective and reliable control in challenging manipulation tasks.
- **Advancement of Embodied AI:** By providing a flexible and unified foundation for simulation, inference, and control grounded in real-world contact physics, this work contributes significantly to the development of more capable and generalizable embodied AI systems for physical interaction.

Despite these strengths, the authors acknowledge limitations such as reliance on visual pose estimation accuracy, the simplified binary contact representation (which may not capture all real-world attributes like slip direction), the need for large datasets for broad generalization, and compounding errors in very long-horizon predictions. Future work could address these to further enhance the model's capabilities. Nonetheless, this research represents a crucial step forward in bridging the sim-to-real gap for dexterous manipulation, demonstrating the power of integrating explicit contact awareness into neural dynamics models.
