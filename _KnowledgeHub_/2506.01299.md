# Scalable In-Context Q-Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Scalable In-Context Q-Learning (S-ICQL) introduces a framework for in-context reinforcement learning that combines world modeling for precise prompt construction with dynamic programming-based Q-learning. This approach enables agents to learn optimal policies from suboptimal data and generalize effectively to novel tasks, consistently outperforming existing ICRL baselines across various continuous and discrete environments.

## Problem

- Existing in-context reinforcement learning (ICRL) methods struggle to learn optimal policies when relying on suboptimal datasets, often limited to imitating collected data.
- Current ICRL prompts, frequently composed of verbose and redundant raw transitions, can hinder efficient and precise in-context task inference.
- Integrating robust reinforcement learning principles like dynamic programming into ICRL frameworks while maintaining scalability and training stability is challenging.

## Method

- A ==multi-head transformer== architecture is employed to simultaneously predict in-context policies, state value functions, and action-value functions, leveraging parameter sharing.
- A generalized world model is pretrained to infer compact, task-relevant representations from experience, which are then used to construct lightweight and precise prompts.
- In-context Q-learning integrates dynamic programming through expectile regression for state values and advantage-weighted regression for policies, enabling learning from suboptimal data.

## Results

- S-ICQL consistently achieved superior data efficiency and higher asymptotic performance compared to six ICRL baselines across diverse MuJoCo, DarkRoom, and Meta-World environments.
- The framework demonstrated a genuine "stitching" capability, learning policies that surpassed the best returns observed within suboptimal offline datasets.
- S-ICQL showed robust generalization to out-of-distribution tasks and maintained superior performance even with suboptimal ("Medium" quality) datasets, attributed to its world modeling and Q-learning components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating dynamic programming principles, specifically Q-learning, into an in-context learning framework allows agents to "stitch" optimal behaviors from suboptimal trajectories, learning beyond the quality of the offline data.
> - Abstracting environment dynamics into compact, semantically rich task representations via a pretrained world model provides efficient and precise contextual cues for in-context adaptation.
> - A unified multi-head transformer architecture can jointly optimize in-context policies and value functions in a scalable and stable manner, leveraging the benefits of supervised pretraining and value-based updates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents are increasingly applied to complex tasks, with a persistent objective being to develop agents capable of learning from diverse experiences and generalizing efficiently to novel situations, dynamics, or objectives beyond their training environments. In-context learning (ICL), a paradigm that has enabled large language models to adapt to new tasks via prompt conditioning without model updates, has inspired research into in-context RL (ICRL).

Existing ICRL approaches typically fall into two categories: algorithm distillation (AD) and decision-pretrained transformers (DPT). These methods generally rely on supervised pretraining, using cross-episode transitions as few-shot prompts to train transformer-based policies. While various improvements have been proposed, such as model-based planning, hierarchical decomposition, and importance weighting, these methods encounter challenges when confronted with the complexities of RL, which involve intricate dynamics and temporal correlations.

Specifically, current ICRL techniques often struggle to learn effectively from suboptimal data, as their supervised pretraining paradigm can limit them to imitating collected data rather than transcending it. AD may require extensive contextual information and can perpetuate suboptimal behaviors due to its gradual update mechanism. DPT often necessitates an oracle for optimal action labeling, which is frequently impractical. Furthermore, many ICRL approaches use raw transitions as prompts, which can be verbose, redundant, and potentially bias task inference due to entanglement with behavior policies, hindering precise capture of task-relevant information.

This paper positions its work as an effort to address these limitations by integrating principles from classical RL—dynamic programming and world modeling—into ICRL frameworks. The goal is to facilitate efficient reward maximization and task generalization, particularly when learning from suboptimal datasets, while maintaining the scalability and stability characteristic of supervised pretraining.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Scalable In-Context Q-Learning (S-ICQL) are:

- **Addressing Limitations in Learning from Suboptimal Data:** Existing ICRL methods, predominantly based on supervised pretraining, often replicate the behaviors present in their training datasets. This characteristic can prevent the learning of optimal policies, especially when only suboptimal data is available. The authors are motivated to enable ICRL agents to learn beyond the quality of the collected data, a capability inherent in classical RL algorithms that utilize dynamic programming to "stitch" together optimal parts of suboptimal trajectories.
- **Enhancing Precision and Efficiency of In-Context Inference:** Current ICRL prompts, often consisting of raw interaction transitions, can be lengthy, redundant, and susceptible to biases from the behavior policies that generated the data. This can impede the precise encoding of task-relevant information. The motivation is to develop a lightweight and semantically rich prompt structure that can accurately convey the essence of a decision task, thereby facilitating faster and more precise in-context adaptation. This is inspired by the observation that environment dynamics (state transitions and reward functions), which characterize a decision task, are invariant to specific behavior policies.
- **Maintaining Scalability and Stability:** While seeking to improve ICRL's learning capabilities, the authors aim to retain the practical advantages of supervised pretraining, namely its scalability and stability during training. The objective is to integrate advanced RL principles without sacrificing the computational efficiency and robustness that transformer-based supervised learning models offer.
- **Unifying Policy Learning and Value Updating:** The paper aims to unify the learning of in-context policies and value functions within a single, scalable transformer architecture. This integrated approach is intended to allow for simultaneous optimization, leveraging the benefits of both policy iteration (through dynamic programming) and efficient, large-scale model training.

In essence, the central motivation is to bridge the gap between the generalization promise of in-context learning, as demonstrated in other domains, and the unique challenges of reinforcement learning, by designing a framework that can learn optimal policies from imperfect data using efficient, precise contextual cues, all within a scalable and stable architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Scalable In-Context Q-Learning (S-ICQL) framework integrates dynamic programming and world modeling within a prompt-based multi-head transformer architecture. The methodology involves three main stages: world model pretraining for prompt construction, in-context Q-learning, and overall optimization.

#### 3.1. Scalable Model Architecture

S-ICQL employs a multi-head transformer that simultaneously predicts optimal policies, state value functions, and action-value functions using separate heads. This design aims for parameter efficiency by adding minimal additional heads to a foundational transformer backbone and for model scalability by leveraging the end-to-end causal transformer architecture.

#### 3.2. World Model Pretraining for Prompt Construction

To construct lightweight and precise prompts, S-ICQL pretrains a generalized world model that captures task-relevant information.
- **Task Representation:** The environment dynamics, characterized by state transition and reward functions, are shared across a task distribution, with individual tasks differing in specific reward or transition functions. The world model approximates the dynamics of a task $M_i$ as $P(r_t, s_{t+1}|s_t, a_t; z_i^t)$, where $z_i^t$ is a task representation inferred from the agent's recent $k$-step experience $\eta_i^t = (s_{t-k}, a_{t-k}, r_{t-k}..., s_t, a_t)_i$.
- **Context Encoder and Dynamics Decoder:** A context encoder $E_\phi$ processes $\eta_i^t$ to produce the task representation $z_i^t = E_\phi(\eta_i^t)$. A dynamics decoder $D_\varphi$ then predicts the instant reward $\hat{r}_t$ and next state $\hat{s}_{t+1}$ conditioned on the current state-action pair and the task representation: $[\hat{r}_t, \hat{s}_{t+1}] = D_\varphi(s_t, a_t; z_i^t)$.
- **Pretraining Objective:** The context encoder and dynamics decoder are jointly trained by minimizing the mean squared error between predicted and actual rewards and next states across all tasks in the multi-task offline dataset: $L(\phi, \varphi) = E_{\eta_i^t \sim M_i} \left[ \left\|[r_t, s_{t+1}] - D_\varphi(s_t, a_t; z_i^t)\right\|^2_2 \right]$.
- **Prompt Construction:** After pretraining and freezing the world model, a compact prompt $\beta_i$ for an in-context policy is constructed by transforming a short $h$-step trajectory from dataset $D_i$ into a sequence of task representations: $\beta_i := [z_i^1, z_i^2..., z_i^h] = [E_\phi(\eta_i^1), E_\phi(\eta_i^2)..., E_\phi(\eta_i^h)]$. This lightweight prompt serves as input to the main transformer for fast and precise in-context inference.

#### 3.3. In-Context Q-Learning

S-ICQL incorporates dynamic programming to enable reward maximization and learning from suboptimal trajectories.
- **In-Context Value Functions:** The transformer model, parameterized by $\theta$, outputs a policy $\pi_\theta(a|s; \beta)$, a state value function $V_\theta(s; \beta)$, and an action value function $Q_\theta(s, a; \beta)$ using separate heads.
 - **Q-function Training:** The Q-function is trained by minimizing the Bellman error using sampled transitions: $L_Q(\theta) = E_{(s_i^t, a_i^t, s_i^{t+1})\sim D_i} \left[ \left(r(s_i^t, a_i^t) + \gamma V_\theta(s_i^{t+1}; \beta_i) - Q_\theta(s_i^t, a_i^t; \beta_i)\right)^2 \right]$.
 - **V-function Training (Expectile Regression):** The state value function $V_\theta$ is trained to fit an upper-expectile of the Q-function, using an expectile regression loss: $L_V(\theta) = E_{(s_i^t, a_i^t)\sim D_i} \left[ L_{\omega}^2 \left( \hat{Q}_\theta(s_i^t, a_i^t; \beta_i) - V_\theta(s_i^t; \beta_i) \right) \right]$, where $L_{\omega}^2(u) = |\omega - \mathbf{1}(u < 0)| \cdot u^2$ is an asymmetric loss with an expectile parameter $\omega \in (0.5, 1)$. This encourages $V_\theta$ to approximate the maximum Q-value achievable within the dataset.
- **In-Context Policy Extraction (Advantage-Weighted Regression):** The learned in-context value functions are distilled into a policy using advantage-weighted regression. The policy $\pi_\theta$ is trained with a maximum likelihood loss function weighted by an exponential advantage term: $L_\pi(\theta) = -E_{(s_i^t, a_i^t)\sim D_i} \left[ \exp\left( \frac{1}{\lambda} \left( \hat{Q}_\theta(s_i^t, a_i^t; \beta_i) - V_\theta(s_i^t; \beta_i) \right) \right) \cdot \log \pi_\theta(a_i^t | s_i^t; \beta_i) \right]$. This objective prioritizes actions with higher Q-values relative to the state value, facilitating learning optimal actions from suboptimal data while maintaining a supervised learning style.

#### 3.4. Overall Optimization

The entire multi-head transformer, including the backbone and its three heads, is jointly optimized. The overall loss function combines the three components: $L(\theta) = c_1 L_\pi(\theta) + c_2 L_Q(\theta) + c_3 L_V(\theta)$. The coefficients $(c_1, c_2, c_3)$ are typically set to $(1:1:1)$ for balanced training. This unified optimization framework allows S-ICQL to learn both in-context policies and value functions, integrating supervised training with dynamic programming updates.

During online testing, the prompt is initially empty and dynamically constructed from recent interaction history using the pretrained context encoder, allowing the policy to adapt to new, unseen tasks without further model parameter updates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of S-ICQL spanned various discrete and continuous environments, including DarkRoom, MuJoCo (Cheetah-Vel, Walker-Param, Ant-Dir, Humanoid-Dir), and Meta-World (Reach, Push, PickPlace), using both "Mixed" and "Medium" quality datasets.

- **Overall Performance against Baselines:** S-ICQL consistently demonstrated superior data efficiency and achieved higher asymptotic performance compared to six competitive ICRL approaches (IC-IQL, DIT, DICP, IDT, AD, DPT) and one offline meta-RL method (UNICORN) across a range of environments, particularly with "Mixed" datasets (Table 1, Figure 2). The performance gains were more pronounced in complex environments like Ant-Dir and HalfCheetah-Vel. S-ICQL also exhibited lower variance during the testing phase, indicating improved stability. Offline evaluations confirmed S-ICQL's strong performance even when interaction was restricted (Table 2).
- **Ablation Study:** Ablation experiments highlighted the individual contributions of world modeling and Q-learning.
 - Removing the world modeling component (`w/o_c`) resulted in a noticeable performance drop, underscoring its role in precise in-context inference.
 - Removing the Q-learning component (`w/o_q`) also reduced performance, emphasizing its function in refining policies using suboptimal data and enhancing the "stitching" ability for reward maximization.
 - Removing both (`w/o_cq`), reducing the model to a simple DPT-like behavioral cloning, led to the largest performance decline, indicating its failure to generalize effectively in complex tasks (Table 3, Figure 3).
- **Generalization to Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Tasks:** S-ICQL consistently outperformed baselines on OOD tasks, where training and testing tasks came from different target value ranges (e.g., target velocities for Cheetah-Vel, target directions for Ant-Dir). This suggests that the world model's ability to extrapolate meta-level knowledge across tasks, leveraging inherent environmental structures, contributes to robust generalization beyond the training distribution (Table 4, Figure 4).
- **Stitching Capability:** S-ICQL demonstrated a genuine stitching capability by achieving performance on training tasks that exceeded the best returns found within the offline datasets. This contrasts with purely supervised methods like DPT and the `w/o_q` ablation, which remained limited by the data's best performance. This indicates S-ICQL's ability to compose suboptimal trajectory segments into globally superior policies (Figure 5).
- **Robustness to Offline Dataset Quality:** Experiments on "Medium" quality datasets, containing only suboptimal data, showed that S-ICQL maintained its superior performance over baselines. Many baselines converged to suboptimal policies, whereas S-ICQL's use of Q-learning allowed it to derive better policies even from imperfect data, confirming its robustness to varying data quality (Table 5, Figure 6).
- **Visualization Insights:** t-SNE visualizations of prompt representations for Cheetah-Vel and Ant-Dir tasks showed that the pretrained world model transformed entangled raw transition data into clearly separated clusters in the prompt space. These clusters logically aligned with task parameters (e.g., rectilinear distribution for Cheetah-Vel velocities, cyclic spectrum for Ant-Dir directions), indicating the world model's effectiveness in distilling meaningful, task-specific information into precise prompts (Figure 8).
- **Performance on Complex Environments:** S-ICQL also exhibited strong performance on more complex and diverse environments like the sparse-reward PickPlace and high-dimensional Humanoid-Dir tasks. The performance gap between S-ICQL and baselines widened in these challenging settings, further underscoring its applicability to large-scale problems (Table 6, Figure 7).
- **Hyperparameter Analysis:** Analysis of the temperature parameter $\lambda$ in advantage-weighted regression revealed that smaller $\lambda$ values, which emphasize exploitation of Q-learning, significantly improved performance. A moderate experience step $k=4$ for task representation inference was found to be optimal, balancing sufficient information capture with redundancy. The chosen equal loss weighting (1:1:1) was found to be a stable and satisfactory configuration (Tables 11, 12, 13, Figures 9, 10).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The S-ICQL framework presents a notable advancement in the field of in-context reinforcement learning by addressing several core limitations of existing approaches. Its significance and potential impact stem from:

- **Enhanced Learning from Suboptimal Data:** By integrating dynamic programming principles, specifically through in-context Q-learning and expectile regression, S-ICQL demonstrates the capacity to learn policies that surpass the quality of the offline training data. This "stitching" capability is crucial for real-world applications where collecting optimal or near-optimal data is often impractical or costly. The ability to extract superior policies from suboptimal experiences could broaden the applicability of offline RL to diverse and noisy datasets.
- **Improved Efficiency and Precision of Contextual Information:** The novel world modeling component, used to construct lightweight and precise prompts, allows for accurate task inference. This departure from using raw, potentially redundant transitions as prompts can lead to faster adaptation and more reliable performance in varied tasks. The visualization insights confirm that the world model can extract semantically rich representations that align with the underlying physics and objectives of the environment, suggesting a robust mechanism for meta-level knowledge acquisition.
- **Scalability and Stability of In-Context RL:** S-ICQL retains the benefits of supervised pretraining within a transformer architecture, which is known for its scalability. The end-to-end joint optimization of policy and value functions within a multi-head transformer provides a unified and stable learning pipeline, making it suitable for larger models and more complex environments without sacrificing training stability.
- **Robustness to Distributional Shifts and Data Quality:** The consistent performance of S-ICQL on out-of-distribution tasks and with suboptimal datasets indicates a robust generalization ability. This suggests that the world model's capacity to extrapolate meta-level knowledge helps the agent adapt to unseen task variations, which is a key challenge in developing generally intelligent agents.
- **Foundation for Future ICRL Research:** S-ICQL introduces a methodology that can serve as a foundation for further research in ICRL. The demonstrated benefits of integrating model-based learning and value-based methods into transformer-based ICRL frameworks open avenues for exploring more compact task representations, leveraging natural language for higher-level prompts, and utilizing dynamics models for adaptive learning and distributional shift detection at test time.

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations regarding prompt length for long-horizon problems and the scope for more compact task representations. Future work could focus on these areas, alongside exploring natural language prompts and adaptive learning mechanisms, to further expand the capabilities and applicability of in-context reinforcement learning.
