# MLM: Learning Multi-task Loco-Manipulation Whole-Body Control for Quadruped Robot with Arm

> [!summary] Summary
> MLM introduces a multi-task reinforcement learning framework that enables a quadruped robot with an arm to perform diverse whole-body loco-manipulation tasks using a single policy. The framework integrates a Trajectory-Velocity Prediction network and adaptive curriculum sampling to achieve robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and effective operation under both teleoperation and autonomous execution.

## Problem

- Developing a single, unified control policy for mobile manipulators to perform a diverse range of loco-manipulation tasks, moving beyond task-specific designs.
- Ensuring balanced performance across multiple tasks during reinforcement learning, preventing the policy from favoring easier tasks and neglecting challenging ones.
- Enabling robust deployment of policies in real-world scenarios like teleoperation where crucial future trajectory information for whole-body coordination is often unobservable.

## Method

- A unified reinforcement learning framework (**MLM**) with an ==asymmetric actor-critic== architecture, trained in Isaac Gym using a comprehensive reward function including a style reward from an adversarial motion prior.
- A novel ==Trajectory-Velocity Prediction== (TVP) policy network, incorporating a Normalized Autoencoder (NAE) to predict future end-effector trajectories and a Memory Encoder to predict linear velocity from historical observations.
- An adaptive, curriculum-based sampling mechanism for real-world manipulation trajectories combined with dynamic updates of task-specific reward factors to ensure balanced learning and performance across diverse tasks.

## Results

- The TVP network achieved faster reward growth and higher convergence levels in simulation, resulting in smoother torque control and less joint jitter compared to a single Multi-Layer Perceptron baseline.
- MLM consistently demonstrated the lowest mean 6D tracking errors across seven diverse loco-manipulation tasks in simulation, outperforming previous baselines like sMLP and DeepWBC.
- A unified policy, trained exclusively in simulation, achieved successful zero-shot transfer to a real quadruped robot, performing complex teleoperated tasks and autonomous tasks (e.g., unplugging a charger, opening a container) with up to **85%** success rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Foresight into future end-effector trajectories, accurately predicted by the NAE, is critical for achieving stable and effective whole-body loco-manipulation, especially when directly unobservable during deployment.
> - Adaptive curriculum learning, which dynamically prioritizes challenging tasks through adjusted sampling probabilities and reward factors, is essential for a single policy to balance performance across diverse multi-task loco-manipulation scenarios.
> - Training with real-world manipulation trajectories, extensive domain randomization, and a multi-component reward structure facilitates robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and high fidelity in real-world robotic execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics has seen remarkable advancements in quadrupedal locomotion, largely driven by reinforcement learning (RL). Robots like ANYmal have demonstrated impressive agility on challenging terrains. Concurrently, the integration of robotic arms with quadruped platforms has opened new frontiers, enabling "loco-manipulation" – the coordinated use of mobility and manipulation capabilities to perform complex tasks. This integration, however, introduces significant control challenges.

Historically, loco-manipulation has been approached through model-based methods, often relying on hierarchical optimization for footstep planning and whole-body control. While effective for specific, predefined tasks, these approaches typically suffer from high computational overhead and lack generalizability across multiple tasks, often requiring task-specific designs.

More recently, RL has emerged as a powerful paradigm for end-to-end control of legged robots with arms. Previous RL-based methods have explored various aspects: some model arm motion as external disturbances for locomotion policies to compensate, while others attempt whole-body control with online adaptation. However, these often face limitations such as the arm's inability to fully leverage the quadruped's mobility, instability due to arm-base-defined manipulation spaces, lack of autonomous task execution, or susceptibility to locomotion interference. Crucially, many existing RL approaches for loco-manipulation struggle with the twin challenges of integrating multiple, diverse tasks into a single control policy and maintaining performance balance across these tasks. For instance, vision-driven, trajectory-based methods like UMI-on-legs advanced the field but did not fully address multi-task integration or the suitability for teleoperation scenarios where future trajectory information is unavailable.

MLM directly addresses these unresolved issues. It extends the state-of-the-art by proposing a unified RL framework capable of learning multi-task whole-body loco-manipulation with a *single policy*. This is a significant departure from approaches that require task-specific policies or extensive manual design. The framework tackles the problem of balancing performance across diverse tasks by introducing an adaptive, curriculum-based sampling mechanism for real-world trajectories. Furthermore, it explicitly addresses the challenge of deploying policies in scenarios with only historical observations (e.g., teleoperation) by integrating a Trajectory-Velocity Prediction policy network. By combining real-world data (via UMI-like approaches) with extensive simulation training, and focusing on zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, MLM positions itself at the forefront of robust and versatile loco-manipulation for mobile manipulators, aiming to bridge the gap between impressive simulated behaviors and reliable real-world deployment for a wide array of tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching goal of this research is to develop a robust and versatile control framework for quadruped robots equipped with robotic arms, enabling them to perform whole-body loco-manipulation across multiple tasks autonomously or under human teleoperation. The authors identify several key challenges and motivations driving this objective:

1. **Multi-task Control Complexity:** Traditional methods for loco-manipulation often require task-specific designs, leading to complex control architectures. The primary motivation is to develop a *single, unified policy* that can execute a variety of loco-manipulation tasks, thereby reducing control complexity and increasing the robot's versatility.
2. **Balancing Multiple Tasks during Learning:** When learning multiple tasks, a single policy might gravitate towards easier tasks, leading to poor performance on more challenging ones. The authors aim to introduce an adaptive mechanism that ensures balanced performance across tasks and prevents the policy from getting trapped in local optima.
3. **Leveraging Real-world Data Efficiently:** Simulation-driven paradigms, while powerful for data generation, often suffer from the "sim-to-real visual gap" for manipulation tasks. The motivation is to effectively integrate real-world manipulation trajectories into the training process to provide realistic dynamics and spatial distributions, thereby enhancing generalization and sim-to-real transfer without relying on visual data of task objects.
4. **Deployment with Limited Observation:** In practical deployment scenarios, such as teleoperation, future trajectory information might not be observable, yet it is crucial for stable and effective whole-body execution. A key objective is to develop a mechanism that can predict unobservable future trajectories and velocities, enabling robust control even with partial information.
5. **Enhancing Performance Across Spatial Ranges:** Loco-manipulation tasks can involve both long-range movements and precise local operations. The policy needs to effectively handle these different spatial demands. The prediction of linear velocity is motivated by the need to distinguish and accurately execute these varied tasks.
6. **Achieving Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To make the trained policies practically useful, they must transfer seamlessly from simulation to the real world without further fine-tuning. This necessitates robust training techniques like domain randomization and reward shaping.

By addressing these motivations, the authors aim to push the boundaries of quadrupedal mobile manipulation, enabling robots to robustly perform complex, multi-functional tasks in diverse environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MLM employs a sophisticated reinforcement learning framework driven by both real-world and extensive simulation data to achieve multi-task whole-body loco-manipulation.

**A. Whole-body Loco-Manipulation Policy (RL Framework):**
The problem is modeled as a partially observable Markov decision process (POMDP), leveraging an asymmetric actor-critic framework for efficient training without complex two-stage processes.
- **State Space (Actor Input):** The actor receives a five-frame history of quadruped proprioception (angular velocity, gravity vector, leg joint positions/velocities), robotic arm proprioception (arm joint positions/velocities), the last action, and a crucial end-effector pose trajectory. This trajectory includes both past and *subsequent* (future) TCP poses relative to time `t`, encompassing 3D position and orientation.
- **Privileged State (Critic Input):** The critic, for training stability and efficiency, also accesses privileged information such as quadruped linear velocity, contact forces, external forces, and terrain elevation data, which are unavailable to the actor during inference.
- **Action Space:** The policy outputs 12 leg joint position offsets and 6 arm joint position offsets, which are then used in proportional-derivative (PD) control.
- **Reward Function:** A composite reward system guides learning:
 - `r_g`: Primary tracking reward for the robotic arm's TCP position and orientation, with adaptive, curriculum-based scaling factors (`σ_p, σ_o`) for different tasks.
 - `r_l`: Constraint rewards for regulating reasonable actions, energy consumption, and hardware limits (e.g., torque, acceleration, joint velocity limits, collision penalties).
 - `r_s`: A style reward, derived from an adversarial motion prior (AMP), to encourage natural and agile quadruped gaits.
- **Terrain Curriculum:** To enhance robustness in diverse environments, a terrain curriculum progressively introduces complex terrains (undulations, slopes, obstacles, stairs). Advancement to harder terrains is based on achieving a threshold tracking reward, ensuring stability alongside performance.
- **Domain Randomization:** Critical for sim-to-real transfer, various parameters are randomized during training, including PD gains, link/payload masses, ground friction, motor strength, and observation delays.

**B. Network Architecture (Trajectory-Velocity Prediction Policy Network - TVP):**
The TVP network is a core innovation, designed to handle partially observable future information in deployment. It comprises:
- **Normalized Autoencoder (NAE):** An encoder-decoder structure that processes historical end-effector pose sequences to predict *future* trajectory sequences. The `tanh` activation function is applied to embeddings for normalization, enhancing training stability. The NAE is trained concurrently with other networks using a reconstruction loss (MSE) against ground truth future trajectories.
- **Memory Encoder (ME) and Estimator:** The ME processes historical proprioceptive states, and its embeddings are fed into a supervised estimator to predict the robot's base linear velocity. This prediction is crucial for distinguishing between long-range locomotion and short-range manipulation tasks.
- **Low-level Network:** This final network takes the NAE embeddings, predicted future poses, predicted linear velocity, ME embeddings, current proprioceptive state, and the last action to output the joint action commands.
- **Critic Network:** Comprises trajectory and height encoders, combined with proprioceptive and privileged information, to compute the state value function.

**C. Trajectory Generation and Sampling:**
- **Trajectory Library (Training):** A library of real-world TCP poses from multiple manipulation tasks (e.g., 'clean table', 'pour coke', 'open container', 'pick bear', 'pick cup', 'unplug charger', 'pushing') is used. These trajectories are collected using UMI-like approaches, specifically Fast-UMI, which provides lightweight 6D end-effector manipulation trajectories.
- **Adaptive Trajectory Sampling and Reward Factor Update:** This mechanism addresses the multi-task balancing problem. Initially, tasks are sampled uniformly. As training progresses, task sampling probabilities are adaptively adjusted based on average tracking rewards, prioritizing more challenging tasks to ensure balanced learning. Simultaneously, task-specific reward factors (`σ_p, σ_o`) are updated based on average pose errors, progressively increasing tracking requirements for each task.
- **Deployment Modes:**
 - **Teleoperation:** A handheld gripper (RealSense T265) records 6D poses, which are fed into the policy as target trajectories. A GoPro-based marker estimation triggers gripper commands. The TVP network's NAE is crucial here to predict future trajectories from the historical data available.
 - **Automated Execution (Diffusion Policy - DP):** A pre-trained Diffusion Policy, module (using a ViT-based CLIP visual encoder and fisheye images) generates target trajectories. The RL whole-body policy then executes these trajectories.

**System Setup:**
Training is performed in Isaac Gym with 4,096 parallel agents on an NVIDIA RTX 3090Ti. Real-world deployment is on a Uniree Go2 quadruped robot integrated with a 6-DoF Airbot Play robotic arm and a single-DoF gripper. An NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX handles onboard policy inference (50 Hz), while a host PC runs DP or teleoperation. A Livox Mid-360 LiDAR with odometry establishes the world frame for pose tracking.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling evidence from both simulation and real-world experiments, demonstrating the necessity and effectiveness of the proposed MLM framework.

**A. Simulation Studies:**
- **Necessity of TVP Policy Network:** Comparative ablation studies showed that the proposed TVP network significantly outperformed a single Multi-Layer Perceptron (sMLP) baseline. TVP exhibited faster reward growth and achieved higher convergence levels during training. Quantitatively, TVP resulted in smoother torque control (lower Mean Squared Derivative of arm joint torques) and less joint jitter compared to sMLP, indicating improved stability and control quality.
- **Effectiveness of NAE for Future Trajectory Prediction:** An ablation study removing the Normalized Autoencoder (NAE) from the TVP network highlighted its critical role. Without NAE, the policy's tracking performance degraded considerably, demonstrating that foresight into future trajectories is essential for effective guidance. The NAE successfully predicted future trajectories with minimal reconstruction errors, as visualized by MSE heatmaps of position and orientation, confirming its ability to provide crucial predictive information.
- **Superiority over Baselines:** Across seven diverse loco-manipulation tasks (e.g., 'pushing', 'unplug charger', 'pick bear', 'open container'), TVP (Ours) consistently achieved the lowest mean 6D tracking errors (both position and orientation) compared to TVP wo NAE, sMLP, and DeepWBC. DeepWBC, which uses a single target point and body-frame defined manipulation space, performed significantly worse, underscoring the benefits of continuous trajectory targets and world-frame objectives in MLM. MLM also exhibited lower variance in tracking errors, indicating greater reliability.
- **Necessity of Adaptive Trajectory Sampling:** Experiments comparing adaptive sampling with uniform sampling revealed that uniform sampling led to imbalanced task performance, with challenging tasks receiving insufficient attention and converging to lower rewards. Adaptive sampling effectively balanced performance across tasks, maintaining similar reward levels by dynamically prioritizing harder trajectories.
- **Robustness to Prediction Errors:** The policy demonstrated remarkable robustness to injected Gaussian noise in the NAE's predicted trajectories (up to 10 cm position error and 0.5 rad orientation error). While tracking errors increased with higher noise, the tasks remained successfully completable. This robustness is attributed to the policy's reliance on both predicted and historical ground-truth trajectories.

**B. Real-world Experiments:**
- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A unified whole-body policy, trained entirely in simulation, was successfully deployed on a real Uniree Go2 robot with an Airbot arm without any real-world fine-tuning, achieving impressive zero-shot transfer.
- **Handheld Gripper Teleoperation:** The robot successfully performed a variety of complex real-world tasks through human teleoperation, including 'cleaning papers', 'picking up trash', 'storing a toy', 'unplugging a charger', and 'removing a bolt to open a door'. The robot exhibited sophisticated whole-body coordination, such as leaning downward for ground-level tasks, lifting its body for high targets, and repositioning itself using locomotion when the target was outside its immediate workspace. Real-world tracking performance closely matched simulation results, demonstrating high fidelity.
- **Automated Execution via Diffusion Policy (DP):** MLM seamlessly integrated with a pre-trained Diffusion Policy, allowing the robot to autonomously execute tasks like 'unplugging a charger' and 'opening a container'. The performance was largely limited by the DP's inference capabilities, yet it showcased MLM's ability to act as a robust low-level controller for higher-level autonomous planning. Success rates for these tasks were 80% and 85% respectively, with low end-effector position and rotation errors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MLM framework represents a substantial advance in the field of mobile manipulation, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Unified and Versatile Control:** MLM's primary significance lies in its ability to enable a single reinforcement learning policy to perform a diverse range of complex whole-body loco-manipulation tasks. This moves beyond task-specific controllers, drastically reducing development complexity and increasing the versatility of quadruped robots with arms. This unified approach can lead to more general-purpose robotic platforms.

2. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The combination of a novel Trajectory-Velocity Prediction policy network (TVP), adaptive curriculum-based sampling, comprehensive domain randomization, and a robust reward structure significantly enhances the policy's ability to generalize to unseen trajectories, terrains, and even tolerate prediction errors. This robustness is critical for deploying robots in unpredictable real-world environments.

3. **Improved Teleoperation and Autonomy:** The TVP network's capability to predict future trajectories from historical observations is a game-changer for teleoperation, allowing for stable and intuitive human control in scenarios where foresight is crucial but directly unobservable. Concurrently, its seamless integration with higher-level planning modules like Diffusion Policies empowers autonomous task execution, bridging the gap between perception and action for complex manipulation tasks.

4. **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstrated zero-shot transfer from simulation to a real quadruped robot is a testament to the framework's effectiveness in minimizing the sim-to-real gap. This capability is paramount for accelerating robotic research and development, as it allows for rapid iteration and training in simulation without costly and time-consuming real-world data collection and fine-tuning.

5. **Foundational for Future Robotics Applications:** MLM provides a strong foundation for future advancements in mobile manipulation. Its architecture is amenable to integration with emerging technologies such as vision-language models for higher-level decision-making, enabling robots to understand and execute tasks from natural language commands or complex visual cues. This could unlock more sophisticated applications in fields like logistics, service robotics, inspection in hazardous environments, and disaster response, where agile, versatile manipulation with mobility is essential.

6. **Addressing Key Challenges in RL for Robotics:** By specifically tackling the challenges of multi-task balancing, partially observable future states, and effective real-world data integration within an RL framework, MLM offers a blueprint for overcoming common hurdles in developing intelligent robotic behaviors. The adaptive sampling mechanism, in particular, offers a generalizable strategy for curriculum learning across diverse tasks.

In conclusion, MLM pushes the boundaries of what integrated quadruped-arm systems can achieve, paving the way for a new generation of highly capable, versatile, and robust mobile manipulators that can interact with and operate in complex human environments.
