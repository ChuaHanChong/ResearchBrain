# H2R: A Human-to-Robot Data Augmentation for Robot Pre-training from Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University and the University of Washington developed H2R, a data augmentation pipeline that converts egocentric human-hand videos into robot-centric visual data to bridge the visual domain gap. This approach consistently improved robot policy learning, showing average success rate gains of 1.3% to 10.2% in simulation and 3.3% to 23.3% in real-world manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Large-scale pre-training for robotic manipulation requires extensive, expensive robot-specific demonstrations.
- Abundant egocentric human videos exist but present a visual discrepancy between human hands and diverse robot embodiments.
- This visual gap hinders the direct transferability of learned representations from human videos to robot-centric observations, limiting robot policy generalization.

## Method

- **H2R**, a human-to-robot data augmentation pipeline, is introduced to convert human-centric videos into robot-centric visual data.
- The pipeline first estimates 3D human hand poses from egocentric videos using HaMeR and then retargets these movements to a simulated robot arm, calculating joint angles and aligning camera poses.
- Finally, human hands and arms are removed from the original frames via SAM segmentation and LaMa inpainting, and the rendered robot arm is composited onto the clean background.

## Results

- H2R-augmented data achieved an average CLIP similarity score of **29.83** with robot-centric action descriptions, indicating semantic preservation during visual transformation.
- The framework improved average success rates by **1.3%** to **10.2%** across four simulation benchmarks (e.g., **10.2%** on Robomimic for MAE) and by **3.3%** to **23.3%** in real-world manipulation tasks (e.g., **20.0%** on Leaphand tasks for MAE and R3M under Diffusion Policy).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the precise robot arm overlay and accurate camera-hand retargeting are critical components of H2R's performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly converting human-centric visual features to robot-centric ones during pre-training enhances the effectiveness of robot learning by mitigating the visual domain gap.
> - A systematic pipeline integrating 3D hand reconstruction, precise robot movement retargeting, and image manipulation (segmentation, inpainting) can effectively perform cross-domain visual alignment.
> - Models can learn task semantics from human actions with visual representations directly relevant to robots when pre-trained on visually transformed human data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has increasingly leveraged large-scale pre-training techniques to develop generalizable visual representations. Historically, such pre-training has relied on vast datasets of robot demonstrations, which are costly and labor-intensive to acquire. This has led researchers to explore alternative data sources, particularly readily available egocentric human videos (e.g., Ego4D, Something-Something V2, EPIC-Kitchens), which capture diverse human-object interactions. These human-centric datasets have demonstrated potential for learning transferable visual representations for robotics.

However, a key challenge in utilizing human videos for robot learning is the inherent visual discrepancy between human hands and robot embodiments. This "visual gap" limits the direct transferability of representations learned from human videos to robot-centric observations during deployment. Existing research has explored various methods to bridge this domain gap, including matching task structures, manipulating appearance through segmentation or image processing, and normalizing views.

This work addresses the visual domain gap by proposing a data augmentation pipeline that explicitly converts human-centric visual data into robot-centric data. Instead of relying on raw human videos or solely on expensive robot demonstrations, H2R aims to modify existing human video datasets to make them more visually aligned with robot perceptions. This approach contributes to the broader effort of enabling more efficient and robust robot learning by mitigating visual discrepancies, thereby reducing the reliance on costly robot-specific data collection and enhancing the generalization capabilities of robotic policies. It builds upon advancements in 3D hand reconstruction, image segmentation, and inpainting models, integrating them into a coherent pipeline for cross-domain visual alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to reduce the visual discrepancy between egocentric human-hand videos and robot-centric observations used in downstream manipulation tasks. This is addressed by developing a human-to-robot data augmentation pipeline, H2R, that converts human-hand videos into visually augmented robot-centric data.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several key observations in contemporary robot learning:
- **Effectiveness of Large-Scale Pre-training:** Large-scale pre-training on visual data has proven effective for learning generalizable visual representations, which can subsequently improve robotic policy learning.
- **Data Scarcity in Robotics:** Collecting large-scale robot demonstrations is resource-intensive, requiring significant time, infrastructure, and human effort. This limits the scalability and diversity of robot-specific datasets.
- **Availability of Human Video Data:** In contrast, vast repositories of egocentric human videos are readily available (e.g., Ego4D, SSv2). These datasets contain rich information about human-object interactions and task semantics.
- **Visual Domain Gap:** Despite the potential of human videos, a significant visual gap exists between human hands and the diverse range of robotic embodiments (e.g., grippers, dexterous hands). Models pre-trained directly on human videos may learn representations that are suboptimal for robot learning because they are not explicitly exposed to robot-specific visual features during the pre-training phase. This visual mismatch hinders the effective transfer of learned representations from the human domain to the robot domain, limiting the generalization ability of robotic policies.

To overcome these limitations, the authors aim to develop a method that systematically transforms human-hand videos into an appearance more congruent with robot observations. This explicit bridging of the visual gap during pre-training is hypothesized to lead to more robust and transferable visual encoders, thereby improving downstream robotic manipulation performance in both simulated and real-world environments. The goal is to allow models to learn task-specific actions demonstrated by humans, but with visual representations that are directly relevant to robotic systems, ultimately reducing the reliance on extensive robot-specific data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The H2R (Human-to-Robot) framework is a data augmentation pipeline designed to convert egocentric human-hand videos into robot-centric visual data. The pipeline consists of three main steps: Hand Pose Estimation, Retargeting, and Removal and Inpainting. An additional component involves a CLIP-based metric for evaluating the quality of the augmented data.

**A. H2R Data Augmentation Pipeline**

1. **Hand Pose Estimation:**
 - The initial step involves accurately detecting and reconstructing the 3D pose of human hands in egocentric video frames.
 - The authors utilize HaMeR, a state-of-the-art 3D hand detection and reconstruction model.
 - This process yields the 3D position of the human hand, detailed keypoints, and the intrinsic and extrinsic parameters of the camera capturing the scene. These outputs are crucial for subsequent steps.

2. **Retargeting:**
 - This step focuses on simulating a robot arm to mirror the human hand's movements identified in Step 1.
 - **Robot Joint Angle Calculation:** For dexterous hands, joint angles are estimated from the hand keypoints (three consecutive keypoints along each finger). For grippers, the open/closed state is determined by the Euclidean distance between corresponding fingertips. For parts of the arm not directly involved in hand movements, reasonable default joint values are manually set to complete a plausible robot configuration.
 - **Camera Pose Alignment:** The camera pose in the simulator is adjusted to match the perspective of the original egocentric human video. This is achieved by defining two coordinate systems: C_H (human hand aligned) and C_S (simulator robot arm aligned). The camera position in the human-aligned world frame (W_cam_real) is transformed to the aligned simulator position (W_cam_sim) using transformation matrices derived from the human hand and robot simulator coordinate systems. This ensures the rendered robot arm is viewed from the same perspective as the original human hand.

3. **Removal and Inpainting:**
 - After the robot pose is determined, the human hand and arm regions are removed from the original image.
 - The Segment Anything Model (SAM) is employed to segment out the human hand and arm based on the position and keypoints from Step 1.
 - LaMa, an inpainting model, then fills the removed regions, generating a clean background without human limbs.
 - Finally, the rendered robot arm (from Step 2) is composited onto this inpainted background. Pixel-level alignment is achieved by matching the pixel coordinates of the robot end-effector links (projected through the aligned simulator camera) with the corresponding human hand keypoints from Step 1, ensuring the robot arm accurately reflects the original hand's position and orientation.

**B. Data Quality Evaluation**
To quantitatively assess the visual plausibility and semantic consistency of the H2R-augmented data, a CLIP-based image-text similarity metric is introduced.
- For each original human frame and its H2R-augmented counterpart, action descriptions are generated using Qwen-2.5-VL.
- Two textual prompts are constructed: a human-centric prompt ("A human is [action]") and a robot-centric prompt ("A robotic arm is [action]").
- CLIP ViT-B/32 is used to compute the cosine similarity between each image and its corresponding text prompt.
- The average CLIP similarity scores are reported for 1,000 randomly sampled image pairs, comparing original images with human-centric prompts and H2R-augmented images with robot-centric prompts. This metric helps verify that the augmentation preserves the action semantics while transforming the visual embodiment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of H2R spanned both quantitative data quality assessment and extensive empirical studies in simulation and real-world robotic tasks.

**A. Data Quality Evaluation**
The CLIP-based semantic similarity metric indicated that H2R-augmented images achieved an average similarity score of 29.83 with robot-centric action descriptions, compared to 28.01 for original human images with human-centric prompts. This result suggests that H2R effectively preserves the semantic content of the actions while improving visual alignment with robot-centric descriptions.

**B. Simulation Results**
1. **Performance on Simulation Benchmarks:**
 - H2R consistently improved downstream success rates across four benchmark suites (Robomimic, RLBench, PushT, CortexBench) for both MAE and R3M visual encoders.
 - Average success rate gains ranged from 1.3% to 10.2%. For instance, MAE saw a 10.2% increase in average success rate on Robomimic, including a 25.5% gain on the MoveCan task.
2. **Generalization Across Pre-training Datasets:**
 - The benefits of H2R were not limited to SSv2; augmentation of Ego4D pre-training data also led to improvements. For example, on RLBench, MAE's average success rate increased from 1.7% to 5.0%, and R3M's from 6.7% to 11.7%.
3. **Pretraining on Robotic Datasets:**
 - R3M models pre-trained on H2R-augmented SSv2 (R3M-H2R) achieved stronger overall performance on Robomimic benchmarks (average success rate 61.3%) compared to R3M pre-trained on SSv2 (55.0%) and even R3M pre-trained on the DROID robotic dataset (56.7%). This suggests H2R offers an effective alternative to direct robotic data pre-training.
4. **Effects of Demonstration Density:**
 - H2R consistently yielded performance gains in both sparse and dense demonstration regimes on RLBench tasks, indicating robustness across different data densities. For example, with dense data, MAE's average success rate increased from 45.0% to 55.0% with H2R.

**C. Real-world Results**
1. **Performance on Real-world Manipulation Tasks:**
 - H2R consistently improved real-world success rates across gripper-based, dexterous (Leaphand), and bimanual (Franka) manipulation tasks using Diffusion Policy (DP) and ACT.
 - Gains ranged from 3.3% to 23.3%. The most pronounced improvements were on Leaphand tasks, where average performance increased by 20.0% (for both MAE and R3M under DP) and 13.3% (MAE) / 23.3% (R3M) under ACT.
2. **Generalization Across Pre-training Datasets (Ego4D):**
 - Similar to simulation, H2R benefits extended to real-world Leaphand tasks when visual encoders were pre-trained on Ego4D. MAE's average success rate increased from 28.3% to 43.3%, and R3M's from 21.7% to 28.3%.
3. **Cross-embodiment Generalization:**
 - H2R improved performance even when the augmentation embodiment differed from the downstream robot (e.g., UR5-based augmentation for Leaphand tasks). UR5-based H2R augmentation yielded stronger gains (e.g., 20.0% for MAE-DP) than Franka-based augmentation, but both showed benefits, demonstrating a degree of robustness to embodiment variation.
4. **Synergy with Vision-Language-Action Models:**
 - Fine-tuning the VAE encoder/decoder of the Unified Video Action Model (UVA) with H2R-augmented data led to improved performance on dual-arm UR5e tasks (average success rate of 0.35 with H2R vs. 0.15 with original SSv2 and 0.20 for base UVA).
5. **Ablation Study:**
 - Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both the robot arm overlay and precise camera-hand retargeting. Removing the overlay ("H2R w/o Overlay") resulted in significant performance drops (e.g., -30.0% for MAE-DP average success rate on Leaphand), as did random pasting without precise alignment ("H2R w/o Retarget") (e.g., -16.7% for MAE-DP).
6. **Robustness to Lighting Variations:**
 - H2R combined with lighting perturbations during training ("H2R + LightAug") significantly improved generalization to real-world lighting disturbances during evaluation, increasing MAE's average success rate by 16.6% and R3M's by 8.3% on Leaphand tasks compared to H2R alone, which showed minimal gain without specific lighting augmentation.
7. **Comparison to CutMix Baseline (Appendix):**
 - A comparison to a CutMix baseline, where fixed robot arm images were simply overlaid, showed that H2R generally achieved superior performance. While CutMix offered some improvements in Robomimic tasks, its performance on PushT was worse than training on original data. H2R consistently improved performance across tasks, indicating the value of its detailed hand construction and pose matching.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The H2R framework demonstrates a method for bridging the visual domain gap between human egocentric videos and robot-centric observations in manipulation tasks. This work's significance lies in its potential to enhance the efficiency and effectiveness of robot learning by leveraging readily available human video data.

Key impacts include:
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** By explicitly converting human-centric visual features into robot-centric ones, H2R enables visual encoders to learn representations that are more suitable for robotic systems. This leads to improved generalization abilities of robotic policies, as evidenced by consistent performance gains across diverse simulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks, including gripper-based, dexterous, and bimanual operations.
- **Reduced Reliance on Costly Robot-Specific Data:** H2R offers an effective alternative to collecting extensive and expensive robot demonstrations. It allows for the transfer of task knowledge from human demonstrations by making existing large-scale human video datasets directly applicable for robot pre-training, thereby lowering the barrier to entry for developing capable robotic systems.
- **Compatibility with Existing Pre-training Frameworks:** The framework's compatibility with established visual pre-training methods (MAE, R3M) and policy learning frameworks (Behavioral Cloning, Diffusion Policy, ACT), as well as its synergy with Vision-Language-Action models (UVA), suggests its broad applicability and ease of integration into current robot learning pipelines.
- **Cross-embodiment Transfer:** The demonstration of H2R's effectiveness even when the augmentation embodiment differs from the downstream robot indicates a degree of robustness to variations in robot hardware, suggesting potential for more generalized robot learning from diverse human data.
- **Enhanced Robustness to Environmental Variations:** The finding that H2R, especially when combined with lighting augmentation, improves robustness to real-world lighting changes indicates its utility in creating more adaptable robotic policies for deployment in varied environments.

Overall, H2R contributes to advancing robot learning by providing a systematic data augmentation approach that mitigates a fundamental challenge in cross-domain transfer. This could accelerate the development of more capable and autonomous robots by making more efficient use of abundant human interaction data.
