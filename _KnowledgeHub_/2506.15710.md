# RAST: Reasoning Activation in LLMs via Small-model Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> RAST introduces a method to activate latent reasoning capabilities in large language models by transferring logit-level adjustments from smaller, reinforcement learning-tuned models at inference time. This approach significantly enhances reasoning performance on mathematical and coding benchmarks while reducing GPU memory requirements by over 50% compared to direct RL training.

## Problem

- Enhancing Large Language Models (LLMs) with advanced reasoning capabilities, particularly through reinforcement learning (RL), is computationally prohibitive due to high GPU memory and compute requirements.
- Current RL methods for LLMs often demand multiple model copies (e.g., policy, critic, reward models), making them resource-intensive and inaccessible for many researchers.
- Existing decoding-time strategies for LLMs have not explicitly focused on transferring the specific reasoning signals induced by costly RL training.

## Method

- **RAST** (==Reasoning Activation== in LLMs via Small-model Transfer) is an inference-time method that applies reasoning correction signals to a large base model.
- It computes these signals by taking the difference in logit distributions between a smaller RL-tuned model (SRLS_{RL}SRL​) and its corresponding small base model (SbaseS_{base}Sbase​).
- This 'delta logit' adjustment is added to the large base model's logits before the softmax operation, selectively amplifying probabilities of reasoning-relevant tokens without retraining the large model.

## Results

- RAST consistently improves reasoning performance across six mathematical and three code reasoning benchmarks, sometimes matching or surpassing the accuracy of larger, fully RL-trained models.
- The method significantly reduces GPU memory requirements by over **50%** for **32B** models compared to direct RL training (e.g., **160GB** vs. **350GB**), leading to substantial cost savings.
- RAST successfully generalizes across different model families (Qwen-2.5, Llama-3.1) and tasks, demonstrating enhanced reasoning diversity (pass@k performance) by increasing the frequency of reasoning-related tokens.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning (RL) primarily activates latent reasoning capabilities within large language models by selectively adjusting the probabilities of a small, localized set of reasoning-critical tokens.
> - The distributional shifts induced by RL, particularly for reasoning behaviors, are largely model-size invariant, enabling their transfer across different model scales.
> - Injecting logit-level adjustments from small RL-tuned models into larger base models at inference time can effectively enhance complex reasoning without requiring full RL fine-tuning on the large model.
