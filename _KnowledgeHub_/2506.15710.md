# RAST: Reasoning Activation in LLMs via Small-model Transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> RAST introduces a method to activate latent reasoning capabilities in large language models by transferring logit-level adjustments from smaller, reinforcement learning-tuned models at inference time. This approach significantly enhances reasoning performance on mathematical and coding benchmarks while reducing GPU memory requirements by over 50% compared to direct RL training.

## Problem

- Enhancing Large Language Models (LLMs) with advanced reasoning capabilities, particularly through reinforcement learning (RL), is computationally prohibitive due to high GPU memory and compute requirements.
- Current RL methods for LLMs often demand multiple model copies (e.g., policy, critic, reward models), making them resource-intensive and inaccessible for many researchers.
- Existing decoding-time strategies for LLMs have not explicitly focused on transferring the specific reasoning signals induced by costly RL training.

## Method

- **RAST** (==Reasoning Activation== in LLMs via Small-model Transfer) is an inference-time method that applies reasoning correction signals to a large base model.
- It computes these signals by taking the difference in logit distributions between a smaller RL-tuned model (SRLS_{RL}SRL) and its corresponding small base model (SbaseS_{base}Sbase).
- This 'delta logit' adjustment is added to the large base model's logits before the softmax operation, selectively amplifying probabilities of reasoning-relevant tokens without retraining the large model.

## Results

- RAST consistently improves reasoning performance across six mathematical and three code reasoning benchmarks, sometimes matching or surpassing the accuracy of larger, fully RL-trained models.
- The method significantly reduces GPU memory requirements by over **50%** for **32B** models compared to direct RL training (e.g., **160GB** vs. **350GB**), leading to substantial cost savings.
- RAST successfully generalizes across different model families (Qwen-2.5, Llama-3.1) and tasks, demonstrating enhanced reasoning diversity (pass@k performance) by increasing the frequency of reasoning-related tokens.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning (RL) primarily activates latent reasoning capabilities within large language models by selectively adjusting the probabilities of a small, localized set of reasoning-critical tokens.
> - The distributional shifts induced by RL, particularly for reasoning behaviors, are largely model-size invariant, enabling their transfer across different model scales.
> - Injecting logit-level adjustments from small RL-tuned models into larger base models at inference time can effectively enhance complex reasoning without requiring full RL fine-tuning on the large model.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of large language models (LLMs): the prohibitive computational cost associated with enhancing their reasoning capabilities, particularly through reinforcement learning (RL).

**Current Landscape of LLM Reasoning Enhancement:**
Reinforcement Learning has emerged as a dominant paradigm for post-training LLMs, significantly improving their alignment with human preferences and their ability to perform complex reasoning tasks. Landmark successes like OpenAI's o1 and Deepseek-R1 models have showcased the power of RL algorithms such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). These methods typically involve extensive training, requiring multiple model copies (policy, critic, reference, reward models) and substantial GPU memory and compute, making them resource-intensive and often inaccessible for many researchers and practitioners.

**The "RL Activates Latent Reasoning" Hypothesis:**
A burgeoning line of research challenges the notion that RL endows LLMs with fundamentally *new knowledge*. Instead, several studies suggest that RL primarily *activates and amplifies reasoning capabilities already latent* within the base models. This means RL acts as a "behavior shaper," fine-tuning the model's output distribution to better elicit existing reasoning processes—such as branching out for alternative solution paths, backtracking from errors, or self-verification—rather than imparting entirely novel conceptual understanding. This paper builds directly on this crucial insight.

**Intersection with Decoding-Time Strategies:**
The work also significantly intersects with the field of decoding-time strategies for LLMs. Prior research has explored manipulating logit distributions at inference time to steer text generation towards desired attributes like improved factuality, diversity, or style (e.g., contrastive decoding, DExperts). However, these methods have largely focused on general text generation or task-specific adaptations. RAST extends this idea by explicitly targeting the *transfer of RL-induced reasoning signals*. It leverages the observed differences in logit distributions between an RL-trained model and its base counterpart, applying these differences to larger models without requiring full RL fine-tuning. This is a novel application of decoding-time manipulation, specifically tailored to the complex domain of mathematical and code reasoning, and across different model scales.

**Addressing the Efficiency Gap:**
By proposing RAST, the authors aim to bridge the significant efficiency gap in RL-driven LLM development. While "zero-RL" paradigms (applying RL directly to base LLMs without supervised fine-tuning) have shown promise in improving reasoning, they still demand immense computational resources. RAST offers a more accessible and resource-friendly alternative, allowing the benefits of costly RL training (performed on smaller models) to be effectively transferred to larger, more capable base models. This pragmatic approach could democratize access to advanced LLM reasoning capabilities and accelerate research in this area.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind RAST stems from the high computational and financial costs associated with applying Reinforcement Learning directly to large language models for enhancing their reasoning abilities. Current state-of-the-art methods, such as those used for OpenAI's o1 and Deepseek-R1, involve complex RL pipelines that require loading multiple copies of models and extensive GPU workloads, rendering them impractical for many researchers and organizations.

Building upon recent insights that suggest RL primarily *activates latent reasoning capabilities* already present in base models rather than endowing them with fundamentally new knowledge, the authors formulate a central hypothesis:

**Hypothesis:** The changes in output probabilities induced by RL, particularly those related to key reasoning behaviors, are largely model-size invariant. This implies that the "how-to-reason" adjustments learned through RL might not strongly depend on the specific scale or capacity of the model, allowing for transferability.

Motivated by this hypothesis, the **key objectives** of this research are:

1. **Validate the Hypothesis:** Empirically investigate whether RL-induced distributional shifts are indeed localized and model-size invariant, specifically by analyzing token-level decoding trajectories between base and RL-trained models.
2. **Propose an Efficient Transfer Method:** Develop a simple yet effective method, RAST (Reasoning Activation in LLMs via Small-model Transfer), that can transfer reasoning behaviors by injecting RL-induced probability adjustments from a small, RL-trained model into larger base models at inference time.
3. **Demonstrate Performance and Efficiency:** Evaluate RAST extensively across multiple reasoning benchmarks (mathematical and coding) and various model scales, showcasing its ability to substantially enhance reasoning capabilities while significantly reducing GPU memory requirements compared to direct RL training.
4. **Provide Insights into RL-driven Reasoning:** Conduct detailed analyses to understand *why* RAST works, including examining the similarity of transferred signals and the token-level behavioral shifts induced, thereby offering new insights into the nature of RL's effect on LLMs.
5. **Promote Practical Scalability:** Offer a practical strategy for scaling the benefits of RL-driven reasoning enhancements without incurring the full computational cost, making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible.

In essence, the paper aims to provide a "shortcut" to strong reasoning performance in large LLMs by exploiting the nature of how RL improves models, bypassing the need for costly large-scale RL fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of RAST is structured around two main pillars: a preliminary study to validate the core hypothesis, followed by the design and implementation of the RAST mechanism itself.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Study: Validating the Hypothesis

Before proposing RAST, the authors conducted an empirical study to verify their central hypothesis: that RL primarily adjusts the probabilities of a *small subset of key tokens* related to reasoning behaviors, rather than globally altering the entire output distribution.

- **Approach:** They compared the token-level decoding paths between a base model ($M_{base}$) and its RL-trained counterpart ($M_{RL}$) of varying sizes. The decoding path of $M_{RL}$ was fed token-by-token into $M_{base}$, and the prediction of $M_{base}$ for the next token was checked against $M_{RL}$'s next token. This was done under greedy decoding to minimize randomness.
- **Metric:** They defined **Path Coverage Rate (PCR)** as the proportion of tokens in $M_{RL}$'s decoding path for which $M_{base}$ exactly matched $M_{RL}$'s output.
- **Observations:** As illustrated in Figure 1(a), PCR remained remarkably high (>95%) across all model scales (e.g., Qwen-2.5-32B). This high alignment indicated that $M_{base}$ could largely predict the tokens generated by $M_{RL}$, with deviations occurring only for a small, localized set of tokens. Crucially, these shifts were found to be concentrated on "reasoning-critical tokens" (e.g., those triggering self-verification, branching out, or backtracking), suggesting that RL's primary effect is to modulate these specific behavioral cues.

These findings strongly supported the hypothesis, paving the way for a method that leverages these subtle, targeted probability adjustments.

#### 3.2. RAST: Reasoning Activation in LLMs via Small-model Transfer

Building on the preliminary study, RAST is proposed as a simple yet effective decoding-time method designed to activate latent reasoning capabilities in large base models by transferring logit-level adjustments from smaller, RL-tuned models.

- **Core Principle:** RAST aims to transform a large base model ($M_{base}$) into a stronger reasoner at inference time by applying "reasoning correction signals" derived from the difference in logit distributions between a smaller base model ($S_{base}$) and its RL-tuned version ($S_{RL}$).
- **Mechanism (Logit Adjustment):** At each decoding time step `t`, given the previous input `x_<t`, the final probability distribution over tokens for the "enhanced" model ($\tilde{M}$) is computed using the following formula:

 $P_{\tilde{M}}(X_t | x_{<t}) = \text{softmax} [M_{base}(X_t | x_{<t}) + \lambda(S_{RL}(X_t | x_{<t}) - S_{base}(X_t | x_{<t}))]$

 Where:
 - $M_{base}(X_t | x_{<t})$ are the logit scores for the next token predicted by the large base model.
 - $S_{RL}(X_t | x_{<t})$ are the logit scores from the small RL-trained model.
 - $S_{base}(X_t | x_{<t})$ are the logit scores from the small base model (the base version of $S_{RL}$).
 - The term $\Delta R = (S_{RL}(X_t | x_{<t}) - S_{base}(X_t | x_{<t}))$ represents the "adjustment term" or "delta logit," which encodes the reasoning-oriented shifts learned by the small RL-trained model.
 - $\lambda$ is a scalar hyperparameter that controls the strength of this $\Delta R$ adjustment.

- **Inference-Time Operation:** The method operates entirely at inference time. It does not require any retraining or fine-tuning of the large base model. The delta logits ($\Delta R$) are simply added to the base model's logits before the softmax operation, selectively amplifying probabilities of reasoning-relevant tokens (e.g., "instead" for branching out) while maintaining base predictions for non-reasoning tokens (e.g., "of").
- **Technical Implementation:** To accelerate inference, the authors implemented RAST using a revised `vLLM` framework. Experiments were conducted using a temperature of 1.0 and nucleus sampling with a top-p of 0.95 for mathematical reasoning, and greedy decoding for code reasoning tasks. A maximum generation length of 16,384 tokens was used, and $\lambda$ was set to 1.0 for most experiments.

This approach offers a lightweight and flexible way to transfer complex reasoning behaviors across model scales by focusing on modifying the output distribution, a departure from traditional parameter-level fine-tuning or distillation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across various benchmarks and model families demonstrate RAST's effectiveness, efficiency, and generalizability in enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.1. Consistent and Scalable Reasoning Gains

- **Substantial Improvements:** RAST consistently delivers significant improvements in reasoning capabilities over base models across six mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH500, AIME24, AMC, Minerva, OlympiadBench, GSM8K) using the Qwen-2.5 model family (1.5B, 7B, 14B, 32B).
- **Matching/Surpassing RL Counterparts:** Surprisingly, RAST can sometimes approach or even surpass the performance of the full RL-trained counterparts of the base models. For instance, Qwen-2.5-32B augmented with $\Delta R$ from a 14B RL model (`+ΔR_14B`) achieved comparable or better results on MATH500, Minerva, and GSM8K compared to the `SimpleRL-32B` model, despite requiring significantly less computation.

#### 4.2. Impact of $\Delta R$ Source Strength

- **Stronger Experts Yield Greater Gains:** The effectiveness of RAST is positively correlated with the strength (size/capability) of the smaller RL-trained model ($S_{RL}$) from which $\Delta R$ is derived. For a given base model, using $\Delta R$ from larger RL-tuned experts (e.g., $\Delta R_{14B}$ vs. $\Delta R_{1.5B}$) consistently led to greater performance improvements. This indicates that richer reasoning signals are encoded in larger RL-tuned models.

#### 4.3. Trade-off between Base Model Capacity and $\Delta R$ Source

- **Receptivity of Stronger Base Models:** Stronger base models ($M_{base}$) generally exhibit higher "recovery rates" (how much of the performance gap between $M_{base}$ and $M_{RL}$ is recovered by RAST), suggesting greater receptivity to the transferred reasoning signals. For example, Qwen-2.5-32B showed higher recovery rates than 14B or 7B base models.
- **Optimal Match:** However, an excessively large capability gap between $M_{base}$ and the $\Delta R$ source ($S_{RL}$) can hinder effective transfer. This highlights a nuanced trade-off, where an optimal match between base model capacity and the source of delta logits might exist.

#### 4.4. Boosting Reasoning Diversity (Pass@k Performance)

- **Enhanced Diversity:** RAST improves `pass@k` accuracy (proportion of problems where at least one correct solution is found among `k` samples), with `pass@k` consistently increasing for larger `k`. This indicates that RAST promotes solution diversity, enabling a broader exploration of the answer space.
- **Outperforming RL-trained Models:** Remarkably, RAST's `pass@k` accuracy often equals or exceeds that of the `M_RL` (RL-trained) model, contrasting with previous findings that RL training can sometimes limit diversity. This suggests that RAST might implicitly leverage an "ensemble effect" of knowledge, leading to a richer and more diverse search space for correct answers.

#### 4.5. Generalizability Across Models and Tasks

- **Llama-3.1 Family:** RAST successfully generalizes to the Llama-3.1 model family (8B, 70B), demonstrating consistent gains on all six mathematical reasoning datasets when using $\Delta R$ from a smaller Llama-3.1-8B RL-tuned model.
- **Code Reasoning:** The method also proves effective for code reasoning tasks (HumanEval+, MBPP+, LiveCodeBench), showing consistent improvements when applying $\Delta R$ from a 7B code-specific RL model to a 14B Qwen-2.5-1M base model. This confirms RAST's broad applicability beyond mathematical reasoning.

#### 4.6. Understanding Reasoning Activation

- **Similarity of $\Delta R$ as a Signal:** A positive correlation was found between the cosine similarity of $\Delta R$ (across different source model scales) and the recovery rate. Higher cosine similarity in delta logits implies better alignment of reasoning signals, leading to more effective transferability.
- **Token-Level Behavior Shift:** RAST demonstrably increases the frequency of "reasoning-related tokens" (e.g., for self-verification, branching out, backtracking) in the generated outputs, bringing them closer to the behavior of RL-trained models. This was qualitatively shown in a case study (Figure 5) with high KL divergence for reasoning-critical tokens like "check" and quantitatively via normalized token frequencies (Figure 10).
- **Response Length:** RAST generally leads to increased response lengths compared to base models, mirroring the verbosity often seen in RL-trained models that articulate more step-by-step reasoning. Interestingly, the best-performing RAST configurations often correspond to shorter, more efficient reasoning paths.

#### 4.7. Efficiency Analysis

- **Significant GPU Memory Reduction:** RAST drastically reduces GPU memory requirements compared to conventional RL training (e.g., GRPO). For a 32B model, RAST with $\Delta R$ from a 14B model required approximately 160GB, compared to 350GB for direct RL training—a reduction of over 50%. This translates to fewer GPU cards and substantial cost savings.

#### 4.8. Robustness to Hyperparameters

- **Stable Performance:** RAST showed robustness to moderate fluctuations in decoding-time hyperparameters such as sampling temperature ($\tau$) and the $\lambda$ parameter, maintaining consistently high accuracy within reasonable ranges.

In summary, RAST is a highly effective, efficient, and generalizable method that leverages insights into RL's effect on LLMs to boost their reasoning capabilities without incurring the full computational burden of direct RL fine-tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RAST carries significant implications for both the practical deployment and theoretical understanding of large language models.

#### 5.1. Practical Significance and Efficiency

- **Democratizing RL-driven Reasoning:** RAST offers a pragmatic solution to the notoriously resource-intensive nature of RL fine-tuning for LLMs. By demonstrating that high-quality reasoning enhancements can be transferred from smaller, more affordable RL-trained models to larger base models at inference time, RAST effectively democratizes access to advanced LLM reasoning capabilities. This is particularly impactful for organizations and researchers with limited computational resources who previously couldn't afford direct RL training on large models.
- **Cost Reduction in Development and Deployment:** The substantial reduction in GPU memory and hardware requirements (up to 50% in some configurations) directly translates to lower operational costs for developing, fine-tuning, and deploying LLMs with enhanced reasoning. This efficiency gain can accelerate research cycles and enable broader adoption of complex LLM applications in various industries, including healthcare (as suggested by the GE HealthCare co-author).
- **Faster Iteration:** Decoupling the costly RL training from the application to the largest models allows for faster iteration and experimentation. Researchers can quickly test different reasoning strategies by training smaller RL models and applying their learned deltas to various base models.

#### 5.2. Scientific Insights into RL and LLM Behavior

- **Validation of the "Latent Reasoning" Hypothesis:** The preliminary study and subsequent analyses provide strong empirical evidence for the hypothesis that RL primarily *activates latent reasoning capabilities* by selectively adjusting probabilities of a small subset of tokens. This deepens the scientific understanding of *how* RL shapes LLM behavior, moving beyond a black-box view. It reinforces the idea that base models already possess a wealth of underlying knowledge and reasoning potential that merely needs to be "unlocked" or "activated."
- **Nature of RL-Induced Shifts:** The token-level analysis reveals that RL's impact is highly localized to "reasoning-critical tokens," providing concrete linguistic evidence for behavioral shaping. This insight can guide future research into more targeted and interpretable RL mechanisms.
- **Enhanced Diversity through Logit Manipulation:** The observation that RAST can sometimes surpass the `pass@k` performance of even fully RL-trained models suggests that this logit-level transfer method introduces a unique form of diversity in the search space, potentially acting as an implicit ensemble. This challenges previous notions about RL's potential to reduce output diversity and opens new avenues for exploring the benefits of combining knowledge sources.

#### 5.3. Future Research Avenues

- **Ensemble Methods:** RAST paves the way for exploring ensemble strategies involving multiple small expert models. While initial simple ensembling showed limitations, understanding how to identify and combine diverse reasoning behaviors from different small models could lead to more robust and generalized reasoning.
- **Parameter-Space Transfer (LoRA):** The concept of transferring output-space corrections (delta logits) could be extended to distill lightweight parameter-level adaptations, such as LoRA modules. This could lead to the development of "reasoning-centric LoRA hubs" that allow for flexible and controllable combinations of reasoning patterns.
- **Beyond Reasoning:** The core principle of "reasoning activation via small-model transfer" is not limited to mathematical or code reasoning. It could be applied to other complex capabilities like factual grounding, instruction following, or creative writing, exploring whether RL's influence on these domains can similarly be transferred efficiently.
- **Theoretical Foundations:** While empirically strong, RAST prompts a need for deeper theoretical understanding of why logit-level adjustments from small models effectively transfer across scales. Establishing formal guarantees and identifying failure modes could lead to more robust method designs.
- **Addressing Limitations:** Future work can also focus on addressing RAST's limitations, such as its relatively modest gains on extremely difficult benchmarks like AIME, which might require more profound conceptual shifts, or its dependency on the availability of a high-quality small RL-trained expert model.

In conclusion, RAST represents a significant contribution by offering both a practical, efficient solution for scaling LLM reasoning and a deeper understanding of the mechanisms through which reinforcement learning enhances these complex capabilities. It provides a new paradigm for LLM development that prioritizes efficiency and accessibility while pushing the boundaries of what's possible with large language models.
