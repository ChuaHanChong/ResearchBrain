# Robot Utility Models: General Policies for Zero-Shot Deployment in New Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Robot Utility Models (RUMs) present a framework for enabling robots to perform common household tasks in new environments without additional training. The system achieved a 90% average success rate across 25 novel real-world settings by combining diverse data collection, multi-modal policy learning, and mLLM-based self-verification.

## Problem

- Current robot policies typically require fine-tuning for each new environment, significantly limiting their practical deployment.
- Collecting sufficient diverse, high-quality robot data across varied environments is expensive and time-consuming.
- Robots struggle to represent the multi-modal nature of human demonstrations and to adapt to diverse hardware configurations.

## Method

- Developed a specialized handheld data collection tool, 'Stick-v2,' to efficiently gather large-scale, diverse robot demonstrations across numerous environments.
- Trained multi-modal behavior generation models (VQ-BeT and ==Diffusion Policy==) capable of representing various valid solutions for the same task.
- Implemented a self-verifying deployment strategy utilizing multimodal large language models (mLLMs) for real-time error detection and autonomous task retries.

## Results

- Achieved a **90%** average success rate across five common household tasks (door opening, drawer opening, object reorientation, tissue pickup, bag pickup) in 25 novel, real-world environments.
- mLLM-guided self-verification improved the overall success rate by an average of **15.6%**, increasing from **74.4%** without retries to **90%** with retries.
- Demonstrated successful zero-shot transferability to different robot arms (Hello Robot Stretch, UFactory xArm 7) and camera sensors (iPhone Pro, Intel RealSense) with comparable performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Data quantity and environmental diversity are more crucial than model architecture sophistication for achieving zero-shot generalization in robot policies.
> - Collecting diverse data from many environments outperforms acquiring more demonstrations from fewer environments for generalization.
> - Multimodal large language models (mLLMs) can effectively detect task failures and enable autonomous retries, significantly improving overall success rates in novel environments.
