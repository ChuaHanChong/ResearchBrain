# Language-Guided Object-Centric Diffusion Policy for Generalizable and Collision-Aware Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Lan-o3dp introduces a robotic manipulation framework integrating object-centric 3D representations, natural language guidance via LLMs, and a novel cost-guided diffusion policy for generalizable and collision-aware task execution. The framework achieved an average success rate of 68.7% across 21 RLBench tasks, surpassing prior diffusion-based methods, and enabled effective zero-shot collision avoidance against novel obstacles in real-world robot experiments.

## Problem

- Robotic imitation learning policies often struggle to generalize to unseen scenarios, such as cluttered scenes, changing camera views, or when multiple similar target objects are present.
- Existing methods typically require extensive demonstration data for robust skill acquisition, limiting their applicability in data-scarce real-world settings.
- Integrating effective and training-free collision avoidance into manipulation policies for novel obstacles remains a challenge for safe and adaptable robot operation.

## Method

- ==The Lan==-o3dp framework combines object-centric 3D point cloud perception with a diffusion policy, leveraging open-vocabulary segmentation to filter task-relevant information.
- A large language model interprets user instructions to identify target objects and potential obstacles in natural language, enabling open-vocabulary manipulation and collision avoidance.
- A novel cost-guided generation mechanism in the diffusion process, utilizing estimated clean trajectories, provides training-free collision avoidance for specified obstacles.

## Results

- The framework attained an average success rate of **68.7%** across 21 RLBench tasks, substantially outperforming existing Diffusion Policy (**39.3%**) and 3D Diffusion Policy (**43.6%**) baselines.
- Real-world experiments validated robust generalization to unseen conditions, including open-set objects, multiple similar items, cluttered environments, and varied camera viewpoints.
- Achieved effective zero-shot collision avoidance against novel, language-specified obstacles in real-world robotic tasks, with demonstrated high success rates and computational efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-centric 3D representations, derived from open-vocabulary segmentation, provide more robust and generalizable visual observations for manipulation policies compared to full scene or 2D inputs.
> - Leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) allows for natural language-guided open-vocabulary manipulation and zero-shot collision avoidance with user-defined obstacles.
> - Applying cost-based guidance to the *estimated clean trajectory* within a diffusion model's denoising process is crucial for effective and training-free collision avoidance, unlike guiding noisy intermediate steps.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the fields of robotic manipulation, imitation learning, diffusion models, object-centric representation learning, and language models in robotics.

Traditional imitation learning offers a method for robots to acquire skills, but policies often exhibit limited generalization beyond the training data. While some efforts involve training large models for robots to enhance skill generalization, these typically necessitate extensive datasets. A key challenge in robotics research is to achieve robust skill generalization to novel, unseen situations using a reduced amount of data, simultaneously addressing obstacle avoidance and safety.

Diffusion models have emerged as a generative modeling paradigm with applications in robotic manipulation. In the context of imitation learning, diffusion-based methods have demonstrated the ability to learn complex manipulation tasks, offering advantages such as stable training, high-dimensional output spaces, and the capacity to capture multi-modal action distributions. However, their performance in generalization is often constrained when test environments vary significantly from training scenes, for instance, due to background alterations, camera viewpoint shifts, the presence of multiple similar target objects, or the introduction of obstacles.

Object-centric representation learning is a strategy to process visual observations in a modular manner. Prior approaches have utilized 6D poses, bounding boxes, or segmented masks to represent objects. These methods commonly rely on known object categories or instances. Recent advancements in open-world visual recognition, particularly in object detection and segmentation, have enabled more flexible object identification. While some work, such as Groot, has employed segmented 3D objects for policy learning, its method for identifying target objects may struggle with scenarios involving multiple similar objects.

The increasing capabilities of large language models (LLMs) in comprehension and common-sense reasoning have opened avenues for their application in robotics. LLMs can interpret human instructions and facilitate high-level task planning, for example, by generating robot policy code or selecting skills from pre-trained libraries. When combined with pre-trained open-vocabulary vision-language models, LLMs enable robots to ground user instructions in the real world, supporting the accomplishment of varied and complex tasks.

Collision avoidance and safe navigation are critical aspects of robotic operation. Many guided sampling techniques in diffusion models rely on reward models, which can be challenging to formulate and obtain in robotic scenarios. The use of cost functions provides an alternative. Existing guided sampling methods for motion planning often compute gradients with respect to noisy intermediate states in the diffusion process, which may not always provide effective guidance when the cost function is time-independent.

This work aims to integrate these concepts to develop a robotic manipulation framework that addresses the generalization and safety limitations of current imitation learning approaches, specifically by combining object-centric 3D representations, language guidance, and a novel cost-guided diffusion mechanism.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robotic manipulation framework that can adapt to complex and dynamic environments, ensuring safe and efficient task completion with minimal training data. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives:

- **Enhance Generalization Beyond Training Data:** A central motivation is to overcome the limitation of imitation learning where policies often fail to generalize to unseen scenarios. This includes adapting to:
 - **Cluttered scenes:** Environments with extraneous objects that might confuse a policy trained on simpler scenes.
 - **Shifting camera views:** Variations in camera position or perspective that alter visual inputs.
 - **Ambiguous similar objects:** Scenarios where multiple visually similar objects are present, requiring specific identification of the target.
 - **Novel obstacles:** The introduction of unexpected objects that necessitate collision avoidance without prior training for those specific obstacles.
- **Achieve Data Efficiency:** The research aims to enable effective skill generalization with a comparatively small number of demonstrations, departing from data-intensive approaches that demand extensive training data.
- **Integrate Training-Free Collision Avoidance:** A key motivation is to incorporate inherent collision awareness into the policy without requiring explicit training on collision avoidance scenarios. This aims to improve the safety and robustness of robotic operations in dynamic environments.
- **Leverage Object-Centric 3D Representations:** The motivation behind using object-centric 3D point clouds is to provide the policy with highly relevant visual information while filtering out task-irrelevant background clutter. This is hypothesized to lead to more efficient policy learning and improved generalization by making the policy less sensitive to environmental changes outside the target object.
- **Enable Language-Guided Manipulation:** The motivation for integrating natural language understanding is to allow users to specify tasks, target objects, and obstacles in an intuitive manner. This aims to enable open-vocabulary manipulation, where the robot can interact with objects not seen during training, and open-vocabulary collision avoidance for novel obstacles.
- **Develop an Effective Guided Generation Mechanism for Diffusion Policies:** Current diffusion models, while powerful, face challenges in effectively incorporating external guidance (e.g., cost functions) when these costs are time-independent or when applied to noisy intermediate trajectories. The motivation is to devise a novel guidance mechanism that uses estimated clean trajectories to more effectively guide the diffusion process towards collision-free paths.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for robotic manipulation systems that are simultaneously generalizable, data-efficient, inherently safe, and user-friendly through natural language interaction, addressing critical gaps in current imitation learning and diffusion-based robotics approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Lan-o3dp, is a language-guided object-centric diffusion policy designed for generalizable and collision-aware manipulation. It integrates object-centric 3D perception, a diffusion policy, and a novel cost-guided generation mechanism, all orchestrated by a large language model.

**A. Problem Formulation and Preliminaries**
The work frames the problem as learning a visuomotor policy that generalizes and provides training-free collision awareness. It utilizes a diffusion policy to model the action sequence `P(A_t | O_t)`, where `A_t` is a sequence of end-effector poses over a prediction horizon `n`, and `O_t` comprises visual observation `V_t` and robot states `S_t`. Unlike prior work that uses 2D images or full 3D scene point clouds, Lan-o3dp uses segmented 3D point clouds of task-relevant objects as `V_t`.
For guided sampling, the diffusion model's reverse process, `A_k-1 = μ_k + σ_k * z`, is augmented with a gradient term `ρ * g_k` from a guidance function, where `g_k = ∇_Ak D` (D being a distance or cost). The proposed method introduces a specific way to calculate this gradient term.

**B. Approach - Training Stage**
1. **Object-Centric 3D Representation Acquisition:** During the training phase, the system acquires 3D point clouds of target objects. This is achieved by:
 - Leveraging open-vocabulary segmentation, which combines a Vision Language Model (VLM), the Segment Anything Model (SAM), and a video object segmentation model (Cutie).
 - Given the names of target objects, the VLM/SAM/Cutie pipeline generates real-time segmentation masks.
 - These 2D masks are then projected onto 3D point clouds obtained from a calibrated depth camera.
 - The raw point clouds are downsampled to a fixed number of points (e.g., 256 points) to maintain consistency and efficiency.
2. **Policy Training:** A diffusion model (based on DDPM) is trained on demonstrations.
 - The demonstrations consist of:
 - Object-centric 3D point clouds (from step 1).
 - Robot states (e.g., end-effector poses, gripper states).
 - Corresponding end-effector trajectories.
 - The policy network is conditioned on these fused features, utilizing techniques like FiLM.
 - The objective is to train the diffusion model to predict the noise added at each diffusion timestep (`ϵ(O, A_k, k)`), allowing it to denoise random Gaussian noise into a coherent action sequence. The method's ablation studies indicate that an MLP encoder with a residual connection and "sample" prediction objective performs effectively for point cloud processing.

**C. Approach - Language-Guided Deployment Stage**
1. **User Instruction Interpretation:** When a user provides natural language instructions (e.g., "pour the water from the red bowl into the blue pan, but avoid the bottle"), a large language model (LLM, such as GPT-4) is employed.
 - The LLM decomposes the user's command into:
 - The specific manipulation skill or policy to be executed (from a set of pre-trained policies).
 - The target objects (e.g., "red bowl," "blue pan").
 - Potential obstacles (e.g., "bottle").
 - The LLM also generates code to invoke the necessary detection, segmentation, and tracking modules.
2. **Dynamic Scene Perception:** For each frame during deployment, open-vocabulary segmentation is used again to obtain the 3D point clouds of both the specified target objects and any identified obstacles.
 - GroundingDINO is used for predicting bounding boxes.
 - Segment Anything Model (SAM) generates segmentation masks.
 - Cutie tracks these masks over time.
 - These masks are then converted into 3D point clouds.
3. **Policy Execution with Cost-Guided Generation:**
 - The segmented 3D point cloud of the target objects is fed as the visual observation into the trained diffusion policy.
 - The segmented 3D point cloud of obstacles is used to construct a cost function for collision avoidance.
 - **Cost Function Formulation:** The cost function is based on the Euclidean distance between each waypoint `a_i` in the generated action sequence `A_k` and the closest point `C_ob` on any obstacle. A non-zero gradient is applied if the distance `D(a_i, C_ob)` falls below a predefined safety critical distance `Q*` (determined by the grasped object's size). In real-world experiments, only the x and y coordinates are considered for distance calculation to simplify.
 - **Novel Guidance Mechanism:** Instead of calculating the gradient of the cost with respect to the noisy intermediate trajectory `A_k`, the method calculates the cost based on the *estimated clean trajectory* `A_0|k` at each denoising step.
 - `A_0|k:= E[A_0 | A_k] = (A_k - √(1-α_k) * ϵ_θ(A_k)) / √α_k`
 - The gradient `∇_Ak D(A_0|k, C_ob)` is computed and then incorporated into the reverse diffusion process.
 - **Denoising Step Modification:** The standard reverse diffusion step `A_k-1 = μ_k + σ_k * z` is modified to `A_k-1 = μ_k - ρ * ∇_Ak D(A_0|k, C_ob) + σ_k * z`, where `ρ` is a scaling factor for guidance strength.
 - **Efficiency:** The closest point on the obstacles (`C_ob`) is updated once before each diffusion generation in the closed loop, rather than at every intermediate denoising step, to minimize computational overhead. This allows the guidance to operate without significantly slowing down the diffusion process.
 - The algorithms for cost-guided DDPM and DDIM sampling are formally presented as Algorithm 1 and Algorithm 2, respectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented extensive evaluations in both simulation (RLBench) and real-world scenarios to demonstrate the effectiveness of Lan-o3dp.

**A. Simulation Experiments (RLBench)**
- **Setup:** Experiments were conducted on 21 challenging RLBench tasks (including manipulation, pick-and-place, single and multiple object interactions). A single front camera was used, and 40 demonstrations were collected per task to align with real-world constraints.
- **Baselines:** The proposed method was compared against Diffusion Policy (using 2D RGB images) and 3D Diffusion Policy (using 3D point clouds of the entire scene).
- **Overall Success Rate:** Lan-o3dp, utilizing object-centric 3D representations, achieved an average success rate of 68.7% across the 21 tasks. In contrast, Diffusion Policy attained 39.3%, and 3D Diffusion Policy achieved 43.6%. This indicates a substantial improvement in performance.
- **Success Rate Distribution:** The distribution of success rates showed that Lan-o3dp had a higher number of tasks within the high success rate range (60-100%), with fewer tasks exhibiting low success rates compared to the baselines.
- **Ablation Study:** An ablation study on 7 RLBench tasks investigated design choices for point cloud encoding and learning objectives.
 - **Point Cloud Encoder:** An MLP encoder with a residual connection achieved the highest success rate (68.8%), outperforming a plain MLP (64.1%) and PointNet (14.9%).
 - **Prediction Type:** The "sample" prediction objective slightly outperformed the "epsilon" prediction objective (68.8% vs. 65.7% with the MLP+Residual encoder).

**B. Real-World Experiments**
- **Setup:** Experiments involved a Diana 7 robot arm and a RealSense D415 camera across four tasks: Pouring, Bottle Upright, Tape to Drawer, and Brushing. 40 demonstrations were collected per task, incorporating initial object position variations. GPT-4 was used at deployment for instruction parsing.
- **Generalization Evaluation:** Lan-o3dp demonstrated effective generalization in various unseen situations compared to the Diffusion Policy baseline, which showed limited performance due to fewer demonstrations and reliance on RGB inputs.
 - **Instance Changes:** The framework successfully performed open-set manipulation with objects of similar geometry (e.g., different types of bottles or bowls than those in training).
 - **Multiple Similar Objects:** The policy could be effectively executed by specifying a particular target object using natural language, leveraging open-set segmentation and the LLM.
 - **Cluttered Scenes:** Object-centric representation showed robustness unless the target object was fully occluded. Flexible camera views were noted to assist in such cases.
 - **Camera View Shift:** The method maintained performance when the camera viewpoint was significantly altered from its training position to an unseen test position. In contrast, the RGB-based Diffusion Policy failed entirely under these conditions.
- **Obstacle Avoidance Evaluation:** Zero-shot open-set obstacle avoidance was tested in pouring, brushing, and tape to drawer tasks with various novel obstacles (e.g., bottles, box, laptop, kettle, phone).
 - **Quantitative Results:** With 5 trials per obstacle, Lan-o3dp demonstrated notable success rates for collision avoidance (e.g., 100% for bottles in pouring, 100% for phone in brushing). Without the proposed guidance, all trials resulted in 0% success.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** The guidance mechanism maintained the system's operational frequency (e.g., 100 steps at approximately 1 Hz, 16 steps at around 5 Hz on an RTX A6000 Ada), indicating that it does not introduce prohibitive computational overhead.
 - **Gradient Scale Effect:** The gradient scale `ρ` was found to influence the trade-off between trajectory smoothness and the effectiveness of obstacle avoidance.
 - **Effectiveness of Cost on `A_0|k`:** An ablation showed that the proposed guidance based on the estimated clean trajectory `A_0|k` was more effective. It enabled obstacle avoidance within 20 DDIM steps. In contrast, guidance applied to the noisy intermediate trajectory `A_k` (as in some prior methods) was found to be less influential, contributing mostly to the final denoising step and thus behaving more like a post-processing step rather than effective guidance throughout the diffusion process. When guidance on the final denoising step was removed, `A_k` guidance resulted in 0% success, while `A_0|k` guidance maintained significant success rates (e.g., 100% for bottles in pouring).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Lan-o3dp framework presents several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the field of robotic manipulation:

- **Enhanced Generalization and Data Efficiency:** The work demonstrates a notable advancement in addressing the generalization limitations of imitation learning. By utilizing object-centric 3D representations, the robot's policies become more robust to variations in background, camera viewpoints, and scene clutter, even with a limited number of demonstrations (40 per task). This is crucial for deploying robots in varied real-world environments where extensive data collection for every possible scenario is impractical.
- **Training-Free Collision Avoidance:** A key impact is the integration of training-free, cost-guided collision avoidance. This eliminates the need for explicit training data on collision avoidance scenarios, simplifying the policy learning process and enhancing the inherent safety of robotic operations. The ability to avoid novel, language-specified obstacles in a zero-shot manner significantly increases the adaptability and reliability of the robot.
- **Open-Vocabulary Manipulation and Collision Avoidance:** The framework's integration of large language models (LLMs) and open-set segmentation models allows for intuitive, natural language interaction. This enables the robot to perform open-vocabulary manipulation on objects with similar geometries not seen during training and to avoid novel obstacles specified by a user, fostering more flexible and user-friendly human-robot collaboration.
- **Robustness to Visual Ambiguity:** By focusing on task-relevant 3D object information, the policy gains robustness against visual ambiguities common in cluttered scenes or when multiple similar objects are present. This selective perception makes the system less prone to distraction by irrelevant visual noise.
- **Novel Guided Diffusion Mechanism:** The introduction of a guidance mechanism that leverages estimated clean trajectories (`A_0|k`) for incorporating time-independent costs is a methodological innovation. This approach has been shown to provide more effective guidance during the diffusion process compared to applying gradients to noisy intermediate steps, potentially inspiring similar advancements in other diffusion-based control or generative tasks.
- **Demonstrated Real-World Applicability:** Extensive evaluation in real-world settings validates the practical viability and effectiveness of the proposed methods. The consistent performance in diverse unseen situations, including camera shifts and novel obstacles, moves the field closer to deployable general-purpose robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Work:** The paper identifies limitations, such as reliance on current VLM performance and restricted end-effector-only collision avoidance. This provides clear directions for future research, including incorporating more advanced VLMs, LLM-based task planning, and developing cost functions for whole-body collision avoidance, thereby contributing to the ongoing development of more sophisticated and capable robotic systems.
