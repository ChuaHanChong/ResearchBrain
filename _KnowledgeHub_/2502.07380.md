# Wheeled Lab: Modern Sim2Real for Low-cost, Open-source Wheeled Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> Wheeled Lab, developed by researchers at the University of Washington, provides an open-source framework that integrates low-cost wheeled robots with the high-fidelity Isaac Lab simulator. This platform enables zero-shot Sim2Real transfer of advanced reinforcement learning policies, demonstrated by successfully training agents for controlled drifting, elevation traversal, and visual navigation on affordable hardware.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Sim2Real and robot learning methodologies are largely restricted to expensive, high-maintenance robotic platforms, creating a significant barrier to entry for the broader robotics community.
- Low-cost, open-source wheeled robots, despite their inherent potential, lack access to modern, high-fidelity simulation ecosystems and advanced Sim2Real techniques.
- Existing robotics education and research often struggle to incorporate cutting-edge hardware deployment and advanced robot learning skills due to the inaccessibility of current tools.

## Method

- Developed "**Wheeled Lab**," an open-source, modular ecosystem that integrates popular low-cost wheeled robots (HOUND, MuSHR, F1Tenth) with ==Isaac Lab==, a high-fidelity simulation framework.
- Implemented modern Sim2Real techniques, including massive parallelization via ==PPO==, aggressive ==domain randomization== of physical parameters, and high-fidelity sensor simulation (e.g., RGB-D cameras, elevation maps).
- Created three state-of-the-art zero-shot policies: controlled drifting (π_drift), elevation traversal (π_elev), and visual navigation (π_vis) to demonstrate the framework's capabilities on real hardware.

## Results

- Achieved the first documented zero-shot Sim2Real transfer for controlled drifting on a low-cost HOUND robot, successfully completing full laps with a maximum controlled slip angle of **58 degrees** and an average speed of **1.6 m/s**.
- Demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer of an elevation traversal policy on a MuSHR robot, enabling safe and controlled navigation over ramps and obstacles, showcasing geometric reasoning capabilities.
- Obtained a **3/5** real-world success rate for visual navigation on a MuSHR robot using an MLP policy trained with image augmentations, highlighting the importance of augmentations for visual Sim2Real generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aggressive domain randomization, coupled with improved physical modeling of vehicle articulations and friction, is crucial for achieving robust zero-shot Sim2Real transfer on low-cost, hobby-grade wheeled hardware.
> - End-to-end learned policies, trained with high-fidelity elevation maps and careful reward design, can enable complex geometric reasoning for traversing uneven 3D terrain on accessible robots.
> - For visual navigation tasks on low-cost platforms, comprehensive image augmentations during simulation training are essential for bridging the visual reality gap and ensuring Sim2Real generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The broader robotics research landscape has seen remarkable progress in recent years, largely driven by advances in reinforcement learning (RL) combined with robust simulation-to-real-world (Sim2Real) transfer techniques. Pioneering achievements, such as quadrupeds performing parkour, autonomous drones racing at champion levels, and robot hands solving Rubik’s Cubes, all share the common paradigm of policies trained in simulation and then directly deployed in the real world. These successes leverage sophisticated techniques like massive parallelization, extensive domain randomization, and high-fidelity sensor simulation to bridge the reality gap.

However, these state-of-the-art Sim2Real innovations have predominantly focused on high-cost, high-maintenance, and often space-intensive platforms, including advanced quadrupeds, humanoids, racing drones, and dexterous manipulators. This reliance on expensive and complex hardware creates a significant barrier to entry, limiting access to these cutting-edge methodologies for a broader audience, including educational institutions, hobbyists, and researchers with limited resources. Consequently, there is a growing disconnect between the scientific community pushing the boundaries of robot learning and the broader robotics community, which often works with more accessible, low-cost platforms.

This paper identifies and addresses a critical gap: the lack of modern Sim2Real ecosystems for low-cost, open-source wheeled robots. Platforms like F1Tenth, MuSHR, and HOUND offer excellent entry points into robotics, providing sufficient onboard compute and mobility for complex algorithms at a fraction of the cost of their larger counterparts (typically $900 - $3000). Despite their algorithmic potential, existing simulation environments for these wheeled platforms are often isolated, outdated, or limited in their fidelity and features (as illustrated in Table 1 of the paper). This restricts practitioners to less sophisticated model-based or low-fidelity model-free methods, preventing them from engaging with the latest advancements in Sim2Real.

"Wheeled Lab" directly targets this disparity by integrating these accessible wheeled platforms with Isaac Lab, an open-source, high-fidelity simulation framework widely adopted in state-of-the-art robot learning research. By doing so, this work democratizes access to advanced Sim2Real tools—such as massive parallelization, realistic sensor simulation, and aggressive domain randomization—for a segment of the robotics community previously underserved by these innovations. It aims to elevate the capabilities of low-cost platforms, enabling them to tackle complex tasks previously exclusive to expensive hardware, thereby fostering a more inclusive and skilled robotics community.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the "Wheeled Lab" project is to **bridge the gap between cutting-edge robot learning research and the broader robotics community** by making modern Sim2Real techniques accessible and deployable on low-cost, open-source wheeled platforms. This is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Inaccessibility of State-of-the-Art Robotics:** Current advancements in reinforcement learning and Sim2Real predominantly rely on expensive, complex, and often proprietary hardware and simulation environments. This creates a significant barrier, limiting access to invaluable tools and skills for students, educators, and researchers in resource-constrained settings.
2. **Enhancing Robotics Education and Skill Development:** The prevailing inaccessibility means that educational curricula are poorly equipped to teach indispensable modern robotics skills, such as hardware deployment, iterative development, and the application of advanced Sim2Real techniques. Wheeled Lab seeks to provide a practical ecosystem for learning these skills.
3. **Unlocking the Potential of Low-Cost Platforms:** Small-scale, open-source wheeled robots (e.g., HOUND, MuSHR, F1Tenth) are cost-effective and performative platforms. They possess sufficient onboard capabilities and mobility to execute complex dynamics and environment interactions (like drifting or off-roading), demonstrating high algorithmic potential. However, their full potential remains untapped due to outdated or isolated simulation ecosystems.
4. **Democratizing High-Fidelity Simulation:** With the advent of powerful, open-source simulators like Isaac Lab, the limitations of existing low-fidelity simulation tools for wheeled platforms are no longer acceptable. Wheeled Lab aims to integrate these platforms with such a state-of-the-art framework to provide high-fidelity, research-grounded simulation capabilities to a wider audience.

To achieve this, the paper outlines several specific objectives:

- **Contribute Wheeled Lab:** Develop an open-source ecosystem that integrates popular low-cost wheeled robots with Isaac Lab, thereby creating a coherent framework for modern robot learning.
- **Demonstrate Zero-Shot Policy Transfer:** Showcase the efficacy of Wheeled Lab by developing and deploying three distinct, state-of-the-art zero-shot policies for small-scale RC cars:
 - **Controlled Drifting (π_drift):** To tackle tasks with inherently unstable dynamics, demonstrating the power of extensive domain randomization and massive parallelization for direct Sim2Real transfer.
 - **Elevation Traversal (π_elev):** To exhibit end-to-end training for tasks requiring spatial reasoning and tight coupling of perception and control, particularly over three-dimensional terrain.
 - **Visual Navigation (π_vis):** To illustrate the integration of cameras—a critical sensor modality for semantic understanding—into a low-cost Sim2Real pipeline, addressing challenges in data diversity and transferability.
- **Provide a Low-Cost and Open-Source Stack:** Ensure that the entire hardware and software stack, from the robotic platforms to the simulation and training frameworks, is accessible, affordable, and open-source.
- **Implement and Evaluate Modern Methods:** Thoroughly integrate and demonstrate the effectiveness of modern Sim2Real techniques, including massive parallelization, comprehensive domain randomization, advanced sensor simulation, and end-to-end learning, within the Wheeled Lab framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of Wheeled Lab revolves around creating a **modular, open-source Sim2Real framework** that integrates low-cost wheeled platforms with the high-fidelity, research-backed Isaac Lab simulation ecosystem. This integration is designed to be accessible and reproducible, supporting both research and educational applications.

**Framework Design and Integration with Isaac Lab:**
Wheeled Lab extends Isaac Lab by introducing specialized infrastructure and assets tailored for wheeled robots. This includes designing abstractions such as "Run," "Agent," and "Environment," along with sub-components for "Observation" and "Reward." This structured design facilitates reproducible experimentation by clearly defining run parameters, network architectures, and hyperparameters alongside environment configurations. Crucially, the implementation considers practical accessibility factors inherent to low-cost settings, such as limited onboard compute and sensing, the need for visual diversity in simulation, feasibility of system identification, access to evaluation terrain, and reliance on open-source packages and training resources.

**Reinforcement Learning and Training Paradigm:**
The work utilizes **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)**, implemented via the RSL RL library, as the primary reinforcement learning algorithm for policy training. Training is performed in simulation, leveraging Isaac Lab's capability for **massive parallelization** (up to 1024 environments on a single NVIDIA RTX 3080 GPU, with cloud-based options for broader access). This allows for rapid data collection and exploration, crucial for learning complex behaviors.
Key Sim2Real techniques implemented and evaluated include:
- **Domain Randomization:** Aggressively randomizing physical parameters (e.g., friction, actuator gains, mechanical tolerances, articulations) in simulation to ensure policies generalize to the inherent variability and inaccuracies of real-world low-cost hardware.
- **Sensor Simulation:** High-fidelity simulation of various sensors, including 3D depth, RGB cameras, and LiDAR, enabling end-to-end learning from raw sensor data.
- **Perturbation and Corruption:** Introducing noise and perturbations during simulation to mimic real-world sensor noise and unmodeled dynamics.
- **End-to-End Learning:** Directly mapping sensory inputs to control actions, circumventing traditional modular pipelines that might struggle with tight coupling between perception and control.

**Demonstration Policies and Task-Specific Approaches:**
The paper demonstrates the framework's capabilities through three distinct state-of-the-art policies:

1. **Drifting Policy (π_drift):**
 - **Motivation:** Drifting is a highly unstable maneuver requiring precise control, making it an excellent testbed for robust Sim2Real.
 - **Hardware:** HOUND platform (approx. $3000), modified for rear-wheel drive and reduced tire friction (tape-wrapped wheels).
 - **State:** VIO for state estimation, supplemented by motion capture for evaluation.
 - **Sim2Real Gap Closure:** Focused heavily on aggressive domain randomization for actuator parameters (extracted from spec sheets, then randomized within a narrow range via Sim2Sim cycles) and friction coefficients (estimated with a spring scale). Articulations were improved (e.g., accurate steering linkages, damped suspension) to better match real-world dynamics.
 - **Rewards:** A multi-component reward system including cross-track distance, velocity, stable side-slip, progress, "turn-energy," and a "turn-left-go-right" shaping term to encourage discovery of drifting.

2. **Elevation Policy (π_elev):**
 - **Motivation:** Traversing uneven terrain requires sophisticated geometric reasoning and is typically challenging for low-cost wheeled robots.
 - **Hardware:** MuSHR platform (approx. $930).
 - **State:** Goal-relative position/orientation, and a local (2.5x2.5m) body-centric elevation map (sampled from a global map).
 - **Sim2Real Gap Closure:** Scene augmentation with varied slopes and heights to improve generalization. Suspension articulation (4cm travel) was critical for stable climbing and momentum maintenance.
 - **Rewards:** Designed to incentivize active traversal of risky terrain: goal velocity, Z-position (rewarding upward movement), falling penalty, and penalty for excessive roll on elevation features.

3. **Visual Policy (π_vis):**
 - **Motivation:** Visual navigation is crucial for semantic understanding but presents challenges in data diversity and Sim2Real transfer for cameras.
 - **Hardware:** MuSHR platform.
 - **State:** Grayscale observation (40x60 resolution), linear/angular velocity, and last action.
 - **Sim2Real Gap Closure:** "Simplifying" visual information by using grayscale images. Extensive **image augmentations** (color jittering, Gaussian blur) applied during training to improve generalization to real-world visual variations (illumination, etc.). Randomly generated "figure-8" path environments with black/white foam tiles, allowing for configurable difficulty (number of walkers for path generation).
 - **Architectures:** Explored both Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) policies.
 - **Rewards:** Velocity reward and a traversability reward/penalty for staying on white regions and avoiding black.

**Baselines for Comparison:**
For each policy, baselines (e.g., π_drift_) were established to reflect the limited capabilities of existing low-cost wheeled platform ecosystems, often lacking aggressive domain randomization, perturbation simulation, and sufficient parallelization. This comparison highlights the significant improvements offered by Wheeled Lab's approach.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using Wheeled Lab consistently demonstrated the substantial benefits of integrating modern Sim2Real techniques with low-cost, open-source wheeled platforms, effectively bridging the performance gap observed in existing ecosystems.

**General Observation:**
The paper concludes that there is an "alarming gap" in capabilities between the scientific community's access to modern RL/Sim2Real methods (like massive parallelization, corruption, elevation maps) and what is available to the broader community for wheeled platforms. Wheeled Lab successfully addresses this by providing a unified, state-of-the-art framework.

#### 4.1. Drifting Policy (π_drift)

- **Baseline (π_drift_):** The baseline policy consistently failed to complete stable turns or full laps in real-world experiments (Figure 5). It often spun out uncontrollably or crashed. The underlying issues were attributed to motor cogging, noisy state estimates, constant slipping, and steering biases, leading to significant covariate shift between simulation and reality. The baseline training runs also failed to discover the complex control mode required for drifting.
- **Wheeled Lab (π_drift):** In contrast, the policy trained with Wheeled Lab achieved **zero-shot direct Sim2Real transfer** for controlled drifting, successfully completing full laps (Figure 4). It demonstrated remarkable robustness; even when the platform spun out, the policy would often maintain angular momentum for a full spin (or more) before regaining control and returning to the track. The policy achieved a maximum controlled slip angle of 58 degrees and an average speed of 1.6 m/s. This success was attributed to the aggressive domain randomization of actuator gains and friction, improved vehicle articulations (steering linkages, suspension), and massive parallelization during training. This represents the first documented zero-shot transfer for drifting without real-world fine-tuning.

#### 4.2. Elevation Policy (π_elev)

- **Baseline (π_elev_):** The baseline policy in real-world experiments primarily exhibited obstacle avoidance behavior, deviating significantly from any elevation features to reach the goal (Figure 6a, bottom). When it occasionally attempted to ascend a ramp, it quickly fell off the sides, demonstrating an inability to safely traverse three-dimensional terrain.
- **Wheeled Lab (π_elev):** The policy developed using Wheeled Lab successfully performed geometric reasoning, safely traversing ramps and navigating subsequent obstacles to reach the goal (Figure 6a, top). This was achieved by incentivizing the agent to actively seek out and climb risky terrain, demonstrating stable and controlled ascent and descent. The integration of suspension joints in the simulation and careful reward design played a crucial role in maintaining stability and momentum during ramp traversal.

#### 4.3. Visual Policy (π_vis)

- **Generalization and Augmentations:** The experiments clearly showed that **image augmentations are essential for Sim2Real generalization** in visual navigation tasks (Table 3). Policies trained without augmentations failed in all five real-world trials, unable to generalize from simulated visual data to the physical environment.
- **Architecture Comparison:**
 - **MLP with Augmentation:** Achieved a 3/5 success rate, demonstrating better generalization and producing smoother, more precise actions in the real world.
 - **CNN with Augmentation:** Achieved a lower success rate of 1/5. Despite CNNs generally being more capable of learning complex visual features, they tended to overfit to the simulated data, resulting in noisy, "jerky" actions and audible motor cogging in the real world. The authors noted that CNN training often collapsed to trivial solutions in simulation, highlighting the need for more careful reward and loss function design for stable training with visual inputs.
- **Environment Generation:** The method of generating randomized black and white tile environments (Figure 8) proved effective for creating diverse training scenarios, and the use of grayscale images helped in bridging the Sim2Real gap despite the inherent visual complexity.

In summary, Wheeled Lab demonstrated zero-shot Sim2Real transfer across three diverse and challenging tasks, showcasing the efficacy of modern robot learning techniques when applied to accessible hardware. The results highlight the power of comprehensive domain randomization, parallelized training, improved physical modeling, and appropriate sensor processing/augmentations in achieving robust real-world performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Wheeled Lab" project marks a significant contribution to the field of robotics by addressing a critical accessibility gap between state-of-the-art robot learning research and the broader community. Its significance and potential impact can be categorized as follows:

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Democratization of Advanced Robotics:** The primary significance of Wheeled Lab lies in its success in making cutting-edge Sim2Real methodologies—including massive parallelization, extensive domain randomization, high-fidelity sensor simulation, and end-to-end learning—accessible to low-cost, open-source wheeled platforms. This radically lowers the barrier to entry for engaging with modern robot learning.
- **Unified, Research-Grounded Ecosystem:** By integrating with Isaac Lab, Wheeled Lab provides a modular, self-contained, and open-source framework that is directly linked to a widely adopted and actively developed research simulation ecosystem. This ensures that practitioners can leverage industrial and academic support, fostering reproducible and scalable experimentation.
- **Pioneering Zero-Shot Transfers:** The paper demonstrates several notable "firsts" for low-cost wheeled platforms:
 - The first **zero-shot Sim2Real transfer of a controlled drifting policy** without any online fine-tuning, showcasing robust control over inherently unstable dynamics through aggressive simulation techniques.
 - The first **accessible elevation-based Sim2Real pipeline** with massive parallelization, enabling geometric reasoning for uneven terrain traversal on low-cost hardware.
 - A compelling demonstration of **lower-cost visual Sim2Real**, highlighting effective strategies for visual navigation with cameras on resource-constrained platforms.
- **Practical Validation of Sim2Real Techniques:** The work empirically validates the effectiveness of applying specific Sim2Real techniques (like carefully configured domain randomization for friction and actuators, improved articulations, image augmentations) to mitigate challenges posed by hobby-grade components and real-world variability.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Revolutionizing Robotics Education:** Wheeled Lab has the potential to transform robotics education by providing educators with the tools to teach indispensable modern robotics skills, such as hardware deployment, iterative development, and advanced robot learning concepts, using affordable and widely available platforms. Students can gain hands-on experience with techniques previously limited to advanced research labs. The authors envision developing an open curriculum around Wheeled Lab, similar to F1Tenth, to further aid in teaching complex topics.
- **Empowering Research on Accessible Platforms:** For researchers in constrained environments, Wheeled Lab opens new avenues. It allows them to apply state-of-the-art Sim2Real methods to a diverse range of tasks on low-cost platforms, fostering innovation without requiring substantial hardware investments. This could lead to new discoveries in areas like robust adaptation for varied terrains, resource-constrained perception, and decentralized multi-robot systems.
- **Fostering Community and Collaboration:** By providing a fully open-source and integrated stack, Wheeled Lab encourages contributions and feedback from a diverse community, including academic researchers, hobbyists, and educators. This alignment between scientific and broader communities can accelerate the development and dissemination of robot learning knowledge.
- **Driving Innovation in Low-Cost Robotics:** The ability to perform complex tasks like controlled drifting and elevation traversal with low-cost robots demonstrates that performance is not solely dictated by hardware price. This could spur further innovation in designing more capable and versatile low-cost robotic platforms and algorithms.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge important limitations and outline future work:

- **Pedagogical Evaluation:** While intended for education, the comprehensibility of the pipeline has not yet been formally studied. Future work includes conducting such studies and developing open curricula.
- **"Just Works" vs. Iteration:** Current policies still require patience and iterative development, particularly given the mechanical hysteresis and tolerances of hobby-grade components. The vision is to make platforms "just work" for beginner roboticists.
- **Robust Adaptation:** The work highlights the frontier of robust adaptation for low-cost platforms, suggesting future research into policies that can adapt to varying real-world conditions (e.g., changes in surface friction) to further lower the barrier to entry.

In conclusion, Wheeled Lab is a pivotal step towards a more inclusive future for robot learning. By making advanced Sim2Real tools accessible, it promises to significantly impact both robotics education and research, empowering a broader generation of roboticists to engage with the cutting edge of the field.
