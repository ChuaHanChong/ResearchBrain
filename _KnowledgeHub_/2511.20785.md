# LongVT: Incentivizing "Thinking with Long Videos" via Native Tool Calling

> [!summary] Summary
> LongVT, an end-to-end agentic framework, enables Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to perform human-like, interactive reasoning over long videos by integrating native tool calling for dynamic visual inspection and self-correction. The framework achieves state-of-the-art open-source performance, significantly reducing the gap with proprietary models, supported by a new VideoSIAH dataset for fine-grained evidence retrieval.

## Problem

- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) struggle with reliable reasoning over long videos, often generating hallucinations due to language-centric approaches and uniform frame sampling that misses sparse, critical visual evidence.
- Current LMMs lack mechanisms to mimic human 'global-to-local' reasoning strategies, where users initially skim global content and then 'zoom in' for detailed examination of relevant segments.
- There is a scarcity of high-quality training and evaluation data specifically designed for fine-grained queries that require localizing sparse, causally decisive moments within hours-long video content (the "Video Segment-In-A-Haystack" problem).

## Method

- Introduction of **LongVT**, an end-to-end agentic framework that utilizes interleaved ==Multimodal Chain==-of-Tool-Thought (iMCoTT) to enable dynamic 'global-to-local' video reasoning through a native `crop_video` tool for iterative inspection.
- Curation of the VideoSIAH (Video Segment-In-A-Haystack) data suite, a large-scale, human-in-the-loop verified dataset for both training and evaluating fine-grained, evidence-sparse long-video reasoning.
- A specialized three-stage training strategy comprising Cold-Start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Agentic Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a joint answer-temporal grounding reward, and Agentic Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT) to develop robust agentic behaviors.

## Results

- LongVT-**7B**-RFT achieved state-of-the-art performance among open-source LMMs, scoring an average of 47.7 on various benchmarks, significantly narrowing the gap with proprietary models like GPT-4o (51.5) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (55.2).
- On the challenging VideoSIAH-Eval benchmark, LongVT-**7B**-RFT scored 42.0, a 6-point improvement over the second-best model, demonstrating enhanced long-video reasoning and emergent native tool invocation for temporal localization.
- The IoU-rewarded training significantly outperformed Recall-rewarded training for temporal grounding, achieving a mIoU of 27.2 on Charades-STA compared to 21.6, promoting more precise boundary agreement and disciplined tool usage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly empowering LMMs with a native video cropping tool and an iterative 'hypothesis-verification' cycle within an iMCoTT framework is effective for enhancing temporal localization and self-correction in long-video reasoning.
> - Creating dedicated, fine-grained, and evidence-sparse datasets, like VideoSIAH, is critical for overcoming data scarcity and enabling LMMs to learn robust temporal grounding and reasoning capabilities for long videos.
> - A progressive, multi-stage training pipeline (SFT, RL with IoU reward, RFT) is essential for building and stabilizing complex agentic behaviors in LMMs, from foundational tool invocation to optimized, reliable long-video understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses the challenges of long-form video understanding within the field of multimodal intelligence. Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) have demonstrated capabilities in reasoning over short video clips, often leveraging textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) alongside Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) techniques, such as the R1-style paradigm. However, these conventional approaches often treat videos as a sequence of uniformly sampled frames, which can be computationally intensive for long videos (exceeding 15 minutes) and lead to several limitations.

Specifically, current LMMs tend to be language-centric, limiting their ability to perform fine-grained visual reasoning. This can result in hallucinations, particularly when processing long videos where crucial visual evidence may be sparse and temporally dispersed across thousands of frames. The uniform sampling method typically employed by these models often fails to adaptively identify and focus on key visual evidence, potentially missing decisive moments.

The human cognitive process for comprehending long videos typically involves an initial global skim to identify areas of interest, followed by a detailed examination of relevant shorter clips. This "global-to-local" reasoning strategy is difficult for existing LMMs to replicate effectively due to context length limitations and a lack of mechanisms for dynamic, on-demand visual inspection.

Previous research in RL-based multimodal reasoning has extended GRPO-style RL to various visual and temporal tasks, including image question answering (QA), grounding, and video QA. Concurrently, tool-augmented agentic LMMs have emerged, incorporating external tools to enhance capabilities like pixel-level operations for images or video tools for specific tasks. For instance, VITAL demonstrated improvements in video QA and temporal grounding with tool-augmented RL. However, the existing open-source landscape largely lacks training and evaluation data specifically designed for fine-grained queries that require identifying sparse evidence within long videos—a scenario the authors term "Video Segment-In-A-Haystack." Most public datasets prioritize general, high-level questions, and current tool-augmented LMMs are often trained on coarse-grained, clip-level data. This mismatch leaves models without the necessary supervision to learn how to form, verify, and revise temporal hypotheses. This work aims to bridge these gaps by introducing a new framework and a specialized dataset designed to incentivize human-like, interactive video reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable LMMs to reliably reason over long videos by integrating human-like visual operations into their reasoning processes. This objective is driven by several motivations stemming from the limitations of existing LMMs in long-video understanding:

1. **Addressing Hallucinations in Long Videos:** Current LMMs exhibit vulnerability to hallucinations, particularly when analyzing long-form videos. In such scenarios, relevant visual evidence can be sparse and widely distributed across extended temporal spans. The models, lacking effective mechanisms to pinpoint and verify specific visual cues, may generate inaccurate or unsupported information. The motivation is to reduce these hallucinations by grounding answers directly in retrieved visual evidence.

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Language-Centric Reasoning:** Existing LMM pipelines are predominantly language-centric, relying heavily on textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. This approach often falls short in tasks demanding fine-grained visual comprehension or precise temporal localization within videos. The motivation is to shift towards a more visually grounded reasoning paradigm that enables LMMs to perform operations akin to human visual inspection.

3. **Mimicking Human Cognitive Strategies:** Humans typically approach long video comprehension by first performing a coarse global skim to identify potentially relevant segments, and then "zooming in" on these segments for detailed examination. This global-to-local reasoning strategy is efficient and effective. The research is motivated by the observation that state-of-the-art proprietary LMMs also propose similar multi-stage strategies. The goal is to equip open-source LMMs with this ability to process extremely long videos effectively, despite their inherent context length limitations.

4. **Enabling Adaptive Evidence Gathering:** Uniform frame sampling, a common practice in many LMMs, is inefficient for long videos and often fails to adaptively capture critical visual evidence. The research aims to empower LMMs with the capability for "on-demand temporal retrieval," allowing them to dynamically select and re-inspect specific video segments.

5. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Fine-Grained Reasoning:** A significant challenge in developing agentic LMMs for long-video reasoning is the scarcity of high-quality training and evaluation data, particularly for tasks requiring the localization of sparse, fine-grained, and causally decisive moments within hours-long content (the "Video Segment-In-A-Haystack" setting). Most existing datasets focus on general or high-level questions. This work is motivated by the need to construct a dedicated data suite and benchmark to facilitate both training and rigorous evaluation of such capabilities.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to introduce LongVT, an end-to-end agentic framework incorporating Interleaved Multimodal Chain-of-Tool-Thought (iMCoTT), curate the VideoSIAH data suite, and develop a specialized three-stage training strategy to elicit robust long-video reasoning behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LongVT framework is an end-to-end agentic system designed to facilitate "Thinking with Long Videos" through **interleaved Multimodal Chain-of-Tool-Thought (iMCoTT)**. This approach mimics human comprehension by integrating a dynamic global-to-local reasoning loop.

#### 3.1. The iMCoTT Framework

LongVT utilizes the LMM's inherent temporal grounding ability as a native video cropping tool. The process involves:
1. **Global Preview:** The model first performs a coarse skim of global frames to form an initial hypothesis regarding the temporal location of relevant events.
2. **Tool Invocation:** Based on this hypothesis, the model calls a `crop_video(start_time, end_time)` tool, specifying a time window.
3. **Evidence Retrieval:** The tool fetches the corresponding short video clip and resamples finer-grained frames from it.
4. **Rethinking and Self-Correction:** The LMM then re-evaluates its hypothesis based on the new, detailed visual evidence. It can either refine its proposed time window and repeat the tool-calling process (multi-turn interaction) or directly provide an answer if the evidence is sufficient. This iterative "hypothesis-verification" cycle allows for self-reflection and correction, reducing hallucinations and enhancing temporal localization.

#### 3.2. VideoSIAH Data Suite Curation

Given the lack of suitable data for fine-grained, evidence-sparse long-video reasoning, the authors curated **VideoSIAH (Video Segment-In-A-Haystack)**, a large-scale data suite for both training and evaluation.

- **Data Pipeline:** A semi-automatic, human-in-the-loop pipeline was designed:
 1. **Long Video Collection:** Start with diverse long videos.
 2. **Scene Detection & Segmentation:** Apply pixel-level scene detection and merge short consecutive segments (<10s) to create semantically stable units.
 3. **Video Clip Captioning:** Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct generates detailed descriptions for each segment, capturing objects, relations, and events.
 4. **QA Pair Generation:** Initial QA pairs are generated from these captions, covering various aspects like temporal events, spatial layouts, and object attributes.
 5. **Filtering:**
 - **Text-based QA Filtering:** Linguistic heuristics and model agreement (e.g., OpenAI o3, Gemini 2.5) remove low-quality or ill-posed QAs.
 - **Multimodal QA Filtering:** GLM-4.5V verifies answer consistency against video segments to eliminate hallucinated or visually unsupported claims.
 - Human annotators provide feedback to refine prompting rules for generation and filtering.
 6. **iMCoTT Generation:** Tool-augmented reasoning traces are generated, especially for multi-round tool calling, where longer videos receive proportionally more rounds.

- **Dataset Statistics:**
 - **SFT Data:** 247.9K samples, combining existing video reasoning data (LongVideo-ReasonCoT, Video-R1 CoT), image reasoning data (LLaVA-CoT, OpenVLThinker, We-Math 2.0), and 19.1K tool-augmented iMCoTT samples (distilled from Gemini 2.5 Flash for QA and Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct for temporal grounding).
 - **RL Data:** 1.6K samples of filtered segment-in-a-haystack QA pairs. A length-balanced and difficulty-aware subset is selected, retaining only questions with mixed outcomes from multiple policy rollouts.
 - **RFT Data:** 15.4K samples. These are high-quality trajectories filtered from early RL runs, requiring both correct final answers and a temporal IoU of at least 0.3 with the ground-truth window.
 - **VideoSIAH-Eval:** A dedicated evaluation benchmark of 652 carefully curated, open-ended QA pairs from 244 videos (average duration ~1,688 seconds). This benchmark underwent human-in-the-loop validation and is designed for evidence-sparse long-video reasoning.

#### 3.3. Three-Stage Training Strategy

1. **Cold-Start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):**
 - **Purpose:** To provide a foundational competence for the base LMM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) in tool invocation, evidence integration, and self-correction. Initial experiments showed that without this stage, the model struggled to localize temporal windows, integrate tool outputs, and collapsed during direct RL.
 - **Method:** The model is trained to minimize the negative log-likelihood of target tokens. Online stream packing and dynamic batching are used to optimize training throughput and memory.

2. **Agentic Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Purpose:** To optimize the model's behavior as a tool-using agent, learning when and how to inspect, crop, and integrate retrieved evidence.
 - **Method:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed. A **joint answer-temporal grounding reward function** is introduced, combining three components:
 - **Answer Accuracy (R_acc):** Evaluated by an LLM-as-a-Judge, categorizing answers as fully consistent (1), partially consistent (0.5), or inconsistent (0).
 - **Format Compliance (R_format):** A binary reward (1 or 0) for adhering to the required output schema.
 - **Temporal Overlap (R_time):** Standard temporal Intersection over Union (IoU) between predicted and ground-truth spans. This directly encourages precise temporal localization.
 - The total reward is the sum of these three components. Ablation studies indicated that an explicit tool-calling reward was not necessary after the SFT stage established basic tool semantics.

3. **Agentic Reinforcement Fine-tuning (RFT):**
 - **Purpose:** To stabilize agentic behaviors and consolidate multimodal reasoning learned during RL.
 - **Method:** High-quality trajectories (correct answer and IoU ≥ 0.3) from early RL runs are distilled back into a supervised training corpus. Training on this self-generated, well-grounded subset provides high-precision in-distribution supervision, which helps internalize robust grounding and tool-calling patterns, extending beyond what SFT alone can achieve.

This multi-stage approach aims to progressively build the model's capabilities, from basic tool understanding to optimized, stable, and accurate long-video reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of LongVT across various long-video understanding and reasoning benchmarks yielded several findings:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance in Open-Source LMMs:** LongVT consistently outperformed existing open-source video-centric LMMs. Under sparse frame sampling (64 frames), LongVT-7B-RL demonstrated a slight improvement over the best existing open-source baselines, achieving an average score of 43.7. With dense frame sampling (512 or 768 frames), LongVT-7B-RL and LongVT-7B-RFT exhibited more substantial gains, with average scores of 46.6 and 47.7, respectively, surpassing other methods by a notable margin.

- **Superiority on VideoSIAH-Eval:** On the challenging, fine-grained VideoSIAH-Eval benchmark, which requires evidence retrieval from hours-long videos for open-ended QA, LongVT-7B-RFT achieved a score of 42.0. This represented a 6-point improvement over the second-best model, indicating LongVT's enhanced long-video reasoning capabilities and its emergent ability to invoke native tools for temporal localization.

- **Narrowing Gap with Proprietary LMMs:** The performance of LongVT's best checkpoint (LongVT-7B-RFT) on average benchmark scores (47.7) significantly reduced the gap with proprietary LMMs such as GPT-4o (51.5) and Gemini 1.5 Pro (55.2). This suggests a considerable advancement in long-video reasoning capabilities within open-source models.

- **Importance of Fine-Grained Training Data:** Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the self-curated VideoSIAH data. Removing self-curated iMCoTTs during the SFT stage led to a performance drop (from 44.1 to 24.8 average score). Similarly, excluding self-curated QAs during RL training resulted in diminished performance on VideoSIAH-Eval, characterized by lower answer accuracy, weaker temporal localization, and less systematic tool usage.

- **Effectiveness of IoU Reward:** Comparing reward functions for temporal grounding, IoU-rewarded training significantly outperformed Recall-rewarded training. IoU-based optimization led to a mIoU of 27.2 on the Charades-STA benchmark, compared to 21.6 for Recall-based training. This indicates that IoU, by penalizing span inflation through its union term, encourages more precise boundary agreement and disciplined tool usage.

- **Role of Training Stages:** The three-stage training pipeline (SFT, RL, RFT) was found to be crucial for achieving robust performance.
 - **SFT (cold-start):** Established fundamental competence in proposing temporal windows, reasoning over frames, and self-correcting. Without SFT, the RL-only variant performed poorly (41.9 average score) and exhibited inconsistent behaviors.
 - **RL:** Optimized the model's decision-making as a tool-using agent, pushing performance beyond the SFT ceiling (LongVT-7B-RL: 46.6 average).
 - **RFT:** Stabilized and consolidated the agentic behaviors, leading to the overall best performance (LongVT-7B-RFT: 47.7 average). This stage was essential for fully realizing the benefits of temporal-grounding feedback.

- **Inference Efficiency:** Despite incorporating multi-turn tool interactions, LongVT-7B-RFT demonstrated competitive, and in some cases, lower inference latency compared to single-turn baselines. This efficiency is attributed to LongVT's precise reasoning, which reduces the generation of verbose, hallucination-driven text by proactively seeking and grounding answers in retrieved evidence.

- **Evolution of Reflection Trajectory:** Analysis of reflection token proportions revealed a three-phase evolution: verbose self-correction (initial stages), efficiency optimization (mid-training), and internalized proficiency (later stages), where reflection became concise and semantically grounded, used only when ambiguity resolution was necessary.

- **Data Contamination in Existing Benchmarks:** A contamination study in the supplementary material revealed significant data leakage and option bias in several existing long-video benchmarks (VideoMME, VideoMMMU). VideoSIAH-Eval, in contrast, showed zero leakage (0.00 blind score) and immunity to option bias, validating its design for robust evaluation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of multimodal AI by introducing LongVT, an end-to-end agentic framework that enables Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to engage in "Thinking with Long Videos." The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Advancement in Long-Video Understanding:** LongVT shifts the paradigm of long-video comprehension from passive frame consumption to active, evidence-seeking reasoning. By allowing LMMs to dynamically inspect and re-examine video segments via a native tool-calling mechanism, it provides a more robust and human-aligned approach to processing extensive video content. This capability is particularly relevant for applications requiring detailed analysis of long-form media, such as sports analytics, educational content, or surveillance.

- **Mitigation of Hallucinations and Enhanced Reliability:** A primary contribution is the reduction of hallucinations in LMMs during long-video reasoning. The interleaved Multimodal Chain-of-Tool-Thought (iMCoTT) with its self-correction loop ensures that answers are grounded in retrieved visual evidence, rather than fabricated information. This directly enhances the factual accuracy and reliability of LMM outputs, which is critical for trustworthy AI deployments in high-stakes domains.

- **Novel Data Suite and Benchmark:** The curation and release of the VideoSIAH data suite and the VideoSIAH-Eval benchmark address a critical gap in the open-source community. This fine-grained, evidence-sparse dataset, specifically designed for "Video Segment-In-A-Haystack" scenarios, facilitates both the training and rigorous evaluation of advanced long-video reasoning capabilities, free from common issues like data contamination and option bias found in other benchmarks.

- **Insights into Agentic Training Strategies:** The meticulously designed three-stage training pipeline (cold-start SFT, agentic RL with joint answer-temporal grounding reward, and agentic RFT) provides valuable insights into effectively developing complex agentic behaviors in LMMs. The systematic validation of each stage's contribution and the efficacy of the decoupled IoU reward function establish a state-of-the-art baseline and offer a clear roadmap for future research in training tool-augmented LMMs.

- **Improved Performance and Accessibility:** LongVT achieves state-of-the-art performance among open-source video-centric LMMs, significantly narrowing the performance gap with proprietary models. This makes advanced long-video reasoning more accessible to the broader research community and promotes further innovation in this area. The demonstration that multi-turn agentic reasoning can be as, or more, efficient than single-turn baselines, by generating more concise, evidence-based outputs, challenges the intuition that agentic frameworks inherently incur higher inference latency.

- **Ethical Considerations and Responsible AI:** The framework inherently supports transparency and interpretability by making the reasoning process observable through explicit steps of global skimming, tool invocation, evidence retrieval, and self-reflection. This transparency can facilitate auditing and debugging, enhancing user trust. The authors explicitly acknowledge ethical considerations, advocating for responsible data use and strict adherence to privacy standards, which are crucial as video-based AI systems become more prevalent in diverse real-world applications.
