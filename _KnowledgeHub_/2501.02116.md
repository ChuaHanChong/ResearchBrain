# Humanoid Locomotion and Manipulation: Current Progress and Challenges in Control, Planning, and Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Georgia Institute of Technology, Harbin Institute of Technology, Google DeepMind, and others compiled a comprehensive survey of humanoid locomotion and manipulation. It integrates traditional model-based methods with learning-based techniques and explores the emerging role of foundation models, highlighting the critical importance of whole-body tactile feedback.

## Problem

- Achieving robust, unified locomotion and manipulation simultaneously on complex, high-degree-of-freedom humanoid robots remains a grand challenge.
- Traditional robotics research often studied locomotion and manipulation separately, hindering the development of integrated, whole-body capabilities.
- The rapid advancements in diverse methodologies (model-based, learning-based, foundation models) necessitated a comprehensive and up-to-date synthesis for the field.

## Method

- The authors conducted a structured review spanning three decades of research, categorizing and comparing ==model-based control and planning== (MPC, WBC) with emerging ==learning-based paradigms== (RL, IL).
- The survey specifically highlighted the nascent field of ==foundation models==, assessing their potential for generalist humanoid agents and open-world reasoning.
- It emphasized ==whole-body tactile sensing== as a crucial, yet under-explored, modality for contact-rich interactions in both locomotion and manipulation.

## Results

- The survey found that hybrid methods, combining learning-based (RL/IL) with model-based (MPC/TO) approaches, offer superior efficiency, versatility, and overall performance for complex loco-manipulation tasks.
- A significant gap was identified in the availability of large-scale, systematic benchmarks and high-quality, multimodal robot datasets for humanoid loco-manipulation, hindering systematic evaluation and progress.
- The analysis highlighted that challenges in computational efficiency, sim-to-real transfer, and fundamental hardware limitations (e.g., actuation, battery capacity) are critical barriers to deploying capable humanoids in real-world environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The field of humanoid loco-manipulation is increasingly converging towards hybrid approaches, leveraging both model-based precision and learning-based adaptability for enhanced performance.
> - Whole-body tactile sensing is identified as a critical, often underutilized, modality essential for robust and safe contact-rich interactions in complex humanoid tasks.
> - Foundation models represent a transformative opportunity to imbue humanoids with common sense reasoning and high-level cognitive capabilities, advancing towards generalist robot agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical juncture in the field of humanoid robotics. Humanoid robots are increasingly seen as key enablers for performing human-level tasks in environments designed for humans, ranging from manufacturing and logistics to social assistance and disaster response. However, achieving unified, robust, and versatile locomotion and manipulation (HLM) simultaneously on these complex, high-degree-of-freedom (DOF) platforms remains a grand challenge.

Historically, locomotion and manipulation have often been studied and developed separately within robotics. This survey explicitly aims to bridge this gap by reviewing methods that integrate these capabilities, terming it "loco-manipulation" and, in its most advanced form, "whole-body loco-manipulation." The work is situated against a backdrop of rapid advancements driven by:
- **Machine Learning (ML) and Deep Learning:** The abstract points to ML's role in enhancing robustness and versatility, particularly through reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL). This reflects a broader trend in robotics where data-driven approaches are complementing, and sometimes outperforming, traditional model-based methods.
- **Traditional Model-Based Approaches:** Despite the rise of learning, the paper acknowledges and extensively reviews the enduring importance of model-based control and planning (e.g., Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Whole-Body Control (WBC)), which have formed the backbone of humanoid robotics for decades, providing theoretical guarantees and precision.
- **Emergence of Foundation Models (FMs):** A significant aspect of this survey is its forward-looking perspective on integrating large, pre-trained FMs (like Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models) with humanoid embodiments. This positions the work within the cutting-edge of AI, exploring how these powerful generalist models can enable higher-level cognitive capabilities, open-world reasoning, and semantic understanding for complex robotic tasks, moving towards "generalist humanoid agents."
- **Advanced Sensing Modalities:** The paper highlights the increasing importance of tactile sensing, particularly whole-body tactile feedback, recognizing its crucial role in contact-rich interactions, a feature often overlooked or given limited attention in previous surveys.

Compared to existing surveys on humanoid robotics, this paper distinguishes itself by its up-to-date coverage of learning-based methods, especially FMs, and its comprehensive focus on the *unified* problem of loco-manipulation. It acts as a guide for researchers, synthesizing three decades of model-based work with the past two decades of learning-based advancements, and peering into the future with FMs. Its emphasis on comparing and integrating these paradigms reflects the current hybrid approach gaining traction in the robotics community, where the strengths of both model-based precision and learning-based adaptability are leveraged.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey paper is to provide a detailed and comprehensive overview of the state-of-the-art in humanoid locomotion and manipulation (HLM). The authors are motivated by the rapid advancements in the field, driven by both traditional model-based approaches and emerging machine learning techniques, which have often progressed in isolation.

The key motivations and objectives can be broken down as follows:

- **Comprehensive Synthesis:** To offer a unified review of HLM, encompassing control, planning, and learning methods, which have traditionally been treated separately. This aims to consolidate knowledge and identify interconnections across these domains.
- **Highlighting Foundational Model-Based Methods:** To review the enduring importance of model-based methods (contact planning, motion planning, whole-body control) that have been critical for humanoid robotics, discussing their strengths and trade-offs.
- **Examining Emerging Learning-Based Paradigms:** To focus on the significant recent progress in learning-based methods, particularly reinforcement learning and imitation learning, and their contributions to enhancing robustness and versatility in loco-manipulation.
- **Assessing the Potential of Foundation Models:** To explore the nascent but highly promising role of integrating foundation models with humanoid robots to develop generalist agents capable of open-world reasoning and long-term task planning, a topic often underrepresented in prior robotics surveys.
- **Emphasizing Tactile Sensing:** To specifically highlight whole-body tactile feedback as a crucial, yet relatively underexplored, modality for managing contact-rich interactions essential for both locomotion and manipulation.
- **Comparative Analysis and Future Outlook:** To compare the strengths and limitations of model-based and learning-based methods from multiple perspectives (robustness, computational efficiency, versatility, generalizability) and to suggest potential solutions, future research directions, and emerging opportunities.
- **Resource for Researchers:** To serve as a valuable resource for both newcomers (graduate students and new researchers) and experts in academia and industry, providing an accessible overview of technical methods and insights into the latest advancements.
- **Advancing Understanding:** To clearly define the challenges and outline promising solutions for HLM in bipedal humanoid robots, thereby contributing to a deeper understanding of the field.

In essence, the paper aims to provide a structured roadmap through the complex landscape of humanoid robotics, fostering a holistic understanding of how diverse methodologies can be integrated to realize the full potential of anthropomorphic robots in real-world, human-centric environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a structured and comprehensive review methodology, designed to span three decades of research while incorporating the latest advancements. Their approach is articulated in the "Survey Roadmap" and "Survey Contributions" sections.

The methodological steps and organizational approach are as follows:

1. **Defining Scope and Terminology:** The survey begins by clearly defining what constitutes a humanoid robot (anthropomorphic, bipedal, emphasizing whole-body motion) and key capabilities like bipedal locomotion, navigation, whole-body manipulation, and the combined "loco-manipulation." This establishes a common vocabulary and focus for the subsequent discussions.

2. **Categorization of Methods:** The core of the survey systematically categorizes existing approaches into two main paradigms:
 - **Traditional Planning and Control Approaches (Model-Based):** This includes multi-contact planning (search-based, optimization-based, learning-based planning), model predictive control (MPC) with its various model fidelities, and whole-body control (WBC) using closed-form and optimization-based strategies.
 - **Emerging Learning-Based Approaches:** This section delves into skill learning (reinforcement learning from scratch, imitation learning from robot experience, imitation learning from human data, and hybrid methods) and the application of foundation models (FMs) for humanoid robots (applying LLMs/VLMs and building dedicated humanoid FMs).

3. **Highlighting a Crucial Modality:** The authors dedicate a significant section to **Tactile Sensing**, recognizing its underrepresentation and critical importance for contact-rich loco-manipulation. This modality is discussed across hand, foot, and whole-body applications.

4. **Selection Criteria for Cited Papers:** The authors explicitly state their criteria for selecting the vast body of literature:
 - **Relevance:** Prioritizing topics directly related to humanoid loco-manipulation capabilities.
 - **Foundational Studies:** Including seminal works that established key principles.
 - **State-of-the-Art:** Covering recent papers representing the latest advancements.
 - **Survey Papers:** Suggesting further reading for each topic, indicating a meta-analysis approach.
 - **Temporal Scope:** Collecting model-based papers since 1990 and learning-based methods predominantly spanning the past 20 years.

5. **Structured Comparison and Analysis:** Throughout the paper, and explicitly in Section IX.A ("Model-based Methods Versus Learning-based Methods"), the authors provide a comparative analysis of the different paradigms. This includes evaluating performance measures (e.g., multi-modal sensor incorporation, robustness, accuracy, real-time feasibility, versatility, generalizability) and achieved skill levels (locomotion, loco-manipulation, whole-body multi-contact). This structured comparison is a key contribution for guiding research decisions.

6. **Identification of Challenges and Opportunities:** Each major section concludes with a discussion of current challenges and potential future research directions. This forward-looking perspective, combined with a dedicated "Additional Discussions" and "Conclusion" section, helps readers understand the unsolved problems and emerging opportunities.

7. **Visual Aids:** The paper effectively uses figures (e.g., Fig. 1 for tasks, Fig. 2 for survey organization, Fig. 4 for tactile sensing, Fig. 5 for planning, Fig. 7 for MPC, Fig. 9 for WBC, Fig. 10 for skill learning, Fig. 11 for teleoperation, Fig. 12 for human data, Fig. 14 for hybrid methods, Fig. 15 for skill representations, Fig. 16 for learning examples, and Fig. 17 for foundation models) and tables (e.g., Table I for taxonomy, Table II for tactile roles/challenges, Table III for MPC approaches, Table IV for NMPC speed-up, Table VI for method comparison) to illustrate concepts, summarize information, and enhance readability.

This rigorous, multi-faceted approach allows the authors to provide a comprehensive, well-organized, and insightful review that serves as both an educational resource and a strategic guide for future research in humanoid locomotion and manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey comprehensively synthesizes advancements and challenges across several key domains of humanoid locomotion and manipulation (HLM). The main findings and results are categorized by the paper's primary sections:

#### 4.1. Tactile Sensing (Sec. III)

- **Crucial but Underexplored:** Tactile sensing is identified as a critical, yet relatively underutilized, modality for HLM, especially for contact-rich interactions where vision and proprioception are insufficient or occluded.
- **Applications Across Body Regions:** The paper reviews applications of tactile sensing on hands (for grasping force regulation, slip detection, object property estimation), feet (for Ground Reaction Force (GRF) estimation, terrain classification, obstacle recognition), and whole-body (for compliance, safe human-robot interaction, and large object manipulation).
- **Challenges:** Key challenges include high-dimensional data, the complexity of multi-contact dynamics, difficulties in tactile physics simulation, and the sim-to-real gap.
- **Solutions & Future:** Dimensionality reduction, tactile simulators, and deeper integration with control/planning frameworks are proposed, emphasizing the need for real-time contact reasoning and adaptive contact scheduling.

#### 4.2. Multi-Contact Planning (Sec. IV)

- **Complexity:** Multi-contact planning for HLM involves simultaneously deciding contact positions, modes, forces, and whole-body trajectories, posing significant computational and combinatorial challenges.
- **Approaches:**
 - **Search-based:** Employs state expansion and heuristics for contact sequence generation, often paired with pose optimization for dynamic feasibility. Offers global exploration but can be computationally expensive.
 - **Optimization-based:** Formulates the problem as Contact-Implicit Trajectory Optimization (CITO), integrating dynamics for simultaneous planning. Offers dynamic feasibility but struggles with high computational burden, local minima, and sensitivity to initialization for high-DOF humanoids.
 - **Learning-based:** Demonstrates potential for computational efficiency and assisting contact prediction, often integrated hierarchically with model-based components.
- **Challenges & Future:** Computational efficiency (especially for CITO), realistic patch contact modeling (beyond point contacts), and robust handling of unpredictable contact events in real-time are major hurdles. Hybrid approaches combining the strengths of different paradigms are a promising direction.

#### 4.3. Model Predictive Control (MPC) (Sec. V)

- **Flexibility and Rigor:** MPC is a popular approach due to its flexibility in defining objectives, rigorous mathematical formulation, and ability to plan over a finite horizon.
- **Dynamics Models:**
 - **Simplified Models (ROMs):** (e.g., SRBM, LIPM) offer high-frequency online planning with lightweight computation but sacrifice accuracy and whole-body capability.
 - **Whole-body Models (WBD):** More accurate for versatile motions and interactions but lead to high computational burdens for Nonlinear MPC (NMPC).
 - **Mixed-fidelity Models:** Combine different abstraction levels across horizons (cascaded or overlapped) to balance accuracy and efficiency, suitable for loco-manipulation.
- **NMPC Speed-up Strategies:** Includes exploiting problem structure (e.g., DDP, iLQR, sparsity), successive linearization (SQP, ReLU-QP), warm-starting (gait libraries, memory of motion), and sampling-based methods (MPPI, parallelization on GPUs).
- **Interaction Models:** Differentiates interaction with static environments (multi-contact planning) from dynamic objects (external wrenches vs. unified robot-object models) and dynamic/deformable environments (largely unexplored due to complexity).
- **Challenges & Future:** Effective formulation of interactive dynamics, managing computational load for complex robot-object systems, and dealing with real-world uncertainties are ongoing challenges.

#### 4.4. Whole-Body Control (WBC) (Sec. VI)

- **Purpose:** WBC generates joint torques, constraint forces, and accelerations to track desired trajectories and tasks, often compensating for reduced-order model inaccuracies or environmental disturbances.
- **Dynamics Model:** Typically employs full-order Euler-Lagrangian dynamics, formulated as a linear problem for real-time computation.
- **Approaches:**
 - **Closed-Form:** Inverse dynamics and Operational-Space Control (OSC) offer computational efficiency and direct implementation but struggle with inequality constraints and strict task hierarchies.
 - **Optimization-Based (QP):** Formulates WBC as a Quadratic Program, allowing modular task definition, inequality constraints, and resolution of task conflicts (strict hierarchy via cascaded QP or soft weighting via weighted QP).
- **Loco-Manipulation WBC:**
 - **External Wrench:** Treats object interaction as an external wrench, focusing on robot balance. Strategies involve simultaneous optimization, pre-optimization (contact wrench distribution), or post-optimization (fixed-base system calculation).
 - **Unified Robot-Object Model:** Integrates object dynamics directly into the robot's model for more dynamic behaviors, but requires accurate object state knowledge.
- **Challenges in Numerical Optimization (cross-cutting for Planning/Control):** Non-convexity, numerical robustness, real-time performance, the curse of dimensionality, local optimality, requirement for predefined contact mode sequences (for contact-explicit methods), and handling non-smooth contact complementarity conditions (for contact-implicit methods) are significant issues.

#### 4.5. Learning Loco-Manipulation Skills (Sec. VII)

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) from Scratch:** Learns novel behaviors without explicit demonstrations. Challenges include sample inefficiency, meticulous reward design, and the sim-to-real gap. Domain randomization, system identification, and domain adaptation are common strategies for sim-to-real transfer.
- **Imitation Learning (IL) from Robot Experience:** Leverages robot-generated data (policy execution, teleoperation) for efficient skill acquisition. Teleoperation provides diverse, multimodal data but faces limitations in full-body capture and retargeting.
- **Imitation Learning (IL) from Human Data:** Utilizes abundant human motion data (motion capture, internet videos) to acquire diverse skills, despite the embodiment gap and lack of rich sensory data in human sources. Retargeting strategies are crucial.
- **Hybrid Methods:** Combine learning-based (RL/IL) with model-based (MPC/TO) approaches, often through teacher-student paradigms, learning to track reference trajectories, or augmenting reference trajectories with residuals. These offer superior efficiency, versatility, and performance.
- **Versatile Skill Representation & Composition:** Explores implicit skill representations (motion, goal, state transition/world models) to enable a single policy to master multiple tasks and ensure smooth transitions.
- **Challenges:** Lack of large-scale, systematic benchmarks for HLM (both sim and real), data scarcity (especially high-quality, multimodal robot data), and addressing the sim-to-real gap for complex humanoid dynamics. The need for "purposive learning" (understanding *how* and *why* human actions are performed, not just *what*) is highlighted.

#### 4.6. Foundation Models (FMs) for Humanoid Robots (Sec. VIII)

- **Emerging Paradigm:** FMs (LLMs, VLMs) are a nascent but promising area for humanoid robotics, offering common sense reasoning and open-world understanding.
- **Approaches:**
 - **Applying LLMs/VLMs:** Leveraging pre-trained FMs for high-level task planning (selecting low-level skills) or authoring low-level skills (generating code, reward functions, poses, contacts). These models lack embodied knowledge but provide semantic understanding.
 - **Building Humanoid Foundation Models (RFMs/VLA Models):** Training large models directly on Internet-scale robotic datasets, processing multimodal inputs (vision, language, robot state) and outputting actions. This includes hierarchical vision-language-conditioned policies and end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.
- **Opportunities:** Humanoids may leverage FMs more effectively due to smaller embodiment gaps with human-centric training data, enabling natural human-robot interaction.
- **Challenges:** High inference cost, computational demands (especially for onboard deployment), resource-intensive training, design of effective input/output representations (e.g., incorporating force/tactile feedback), safety constraints, ethical biases, and the lack of a "scaling law" for robotics FMs.

#### 4.7. Hardware Limitations (Sec. IX.B)

- **Actuation:** High-power-density, backdrivable actuators (QDD) are crucial for dynamic motion but face challenges with current, heat, and power electronics.
- **Inertial Properties:** Design strategies (e.g., placing mass away from end-effectors) reduce rotational inertia but may compromise joint range of motion or require complex mechanisms.
- **Battery Technology:** Limited capacity restricts operational time and untethered high-power movements, though solid-state batteries and integrated designs offer potential improvements.

In summary, the paper reveals a field rapidly converging hybrid approaches, integrating model-based precision with learning-based adaptability, all while anticipating the transformative impact of foundation models and the necessity of advanced sensing like whole-body tactile feedback. The challenges are significant, spanning computational efficiency, data scarcity, sim-to-real gaps, and hardware limitations, but the future promises highly capable, autonomous humanoids.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper is highly significant for several reasons and is poised to have a substantial impact on the future trajectory of humanoid robotics research and development:

1. **Comprehensive and Timely Synthesis:** The paper provides an unparalleled, up-to-date synthesis of a complex and rapidly evolving field. By integrating foundational model-based approaches with cutting-edge learning-based methods and the nascent field of foundation models, it offers a holistic view that is essential for both new and experienced researchers. Its recent publication date (April 2025 on arXiv) ensures its relevance to the very latest advancements.

2. **Bridging Disciplinary Gaps:** A major contribution is its explicit effort to bridge the traditional silos between control theory, motion planning, machine learning, and sensor development. By discussing how these fields interact and complement each other, especially in the context of "loco-manipulation," the survey encourages interdisciplinary collaboration and the development of integrated solutions.

3. **Guiding Future Research:** The detailed identification of challenges and suggestions for future research directions within each section are invaluable. For example, the emphasis on dynamic contact reasoning, realistic patch contact modeling, the development of robust benchmarks, the need for "purposive learning" from human data, and the design of effective input/output representations for humanoid foundation models provides a clear roadmap for researchers. This guidance can prevent redundant efforts and direct resources toward the most pressing and promising avenues.

4. **Promoting Hybrid Approaches:** The paper's strong argument for hybrid methods—combining the robustness and theoretical guarantees of model-based control with the versatility and adaptability of learning-based techniques—reflects a pragmatic and increasingly successful trend in robotics. This will likely encourage more researchers to explore such integrated frameworks, potentially leading to more robust and deployable humanoid systems.

5. **Highlighting Under-explored Areas:** The dedicated focus on whole-body tactile sensing brings much-needed attention to a critical modality that is often overshadowed by vision and proprioception. Emphasizing its role in contact-rich interactions can stimulate further innovation in sensor design, data processing, and integration with control schemes.

6. **Pioneering Foundation Models in Humanoids:** By systematically discussing the application and construction of foundation models for humanoid robots, the paper positions itself at the forefront of embodied AI. It anticipates a future where humanoids are not just motorically capable but also possess sophisticated cognitive abilities for open-world understanding and human-like reasoning, thereby unlocking truly general-purpose robotic agents. This vision can inspire significant investments and research efforts.

7. **Addressing Practical Considerations:** The discussion of hardware limitations (actuators, inertia, batteries) and the cross-cutting challenges in numerical optimization underscores the practical realities of deploying humanoid robots. This grounding in physical constraints and computational demands ensures that the proposed directions are not purely theoretical but are also relevant to real-world implementation.

8. **Societal Impact:** The concluding section broadly outlines the immense potential of highly autonomous and capable humanoid robots in diverse sectors like emergency response, manufacturing, and human-centered services (caregiving, domestic tasks). By highlighting the societal benefits alongside the technical, economic, and ethical challenges, the paper contributes to a broader discourse on the responsible development and integration of humanoids into society.

In conclusion, "Humanoid Locomotion and Manipulation" is more than just a review; it is a strategic document that maps the current state, illuminates the most critical challenges, and articulates a compelling vision for the future of humanoid robotics. Its impact will be felt in academic curricula, research funding priorities, and industrial development, accelerating the journey towards realizing truly intelligent and capable humanoid robots in the coming decade.
