# TORL-VLA: Tactile Guided Online Reinforcement Learning for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Meituan developed TORL-VLA, a framework that integrates tactile-derived wrench feedback with online reinforcement learning to enable real-time adaptation for contact-rich robotic manipulation. This approach significantly improved full-task success rates from 12/30 to 28/30 on real-robot tasks by addressing credit-assignment bias with an intervention-censored critic.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often struggle with contact-rich manipulation tasks where subtle physical interactions (e.g., sufficient force, object slippage) are not reliably inferred from visual information alone.
- Existing tactile-aware VLA models are typically deployed as static offline policies, lacking the ability to adapt in real-time to contact conditions that deviate from their training distribution.
- Online reinforcement learning (RL) on real robots, especially in contact-rich tasks requiring human intervention for safety, suffers from credit-assignment bias, where task success after human takeover can be erroneously attributed to preceding suboptimal policy actions.

## Method

![[2606.09337-method.png]]
*Overview of the TORL-VLA framework, which combines a tactile-aware reference model with an online reinforcement learning module for real-time action refinement.*

- A three-stage framework (**TORL-VLA**) was developed, integrating ==tactile-derived wrench== feedback into a VLA model to generate action and future wrench sequence references.
- A lightweight online reinforcement learning module refines these reference actions in real-time, conditioned on stage-specific wrench context (measured feedback and predicted cues).
- An intervention-censored critic was introduced to mitigate credit-assignment bias by applying an intervention cost and removing bootstrap terms for action chunks preceding human interventions, preventing misattribution of subsequent success.

## Results

- TORL-VLA improved full-task completion on a real-robot latch-box setup from **12**/30 (base VLA) to **28**/30, demonstrating enhanced robustness in long-horizon contact-rich manipulation.
- The framework achieved near-perfect subtask success rates (**30**/30 for Coffee Cup and Egg, **29**/30 for Latch), outperforming strong baselines like TA-VLA and ForceVLA.
- TORL-VLA showed superior time-bounded execution efficiency with an average full-task completion time of **165.45** seconds, indicating more reliable and faster task execution compared to other methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating tactile-derived wrench feedback with online reinforcement learning is crucial for transforming contact-aware VLA policies into truly contact-adaptive policies for real-time task execution.
> - An intervention-censored critic effectively addresses the value-learning bias in human-assisted online RL, leading to more stable and data-efficient policy improvement in contact-rich, safety-critical tasks.
> - The use of a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module and a zero-initialized physical bypass in the wrench-aware reference model efficiently fuses tactile features with visual-language representations while preserving fine-grained contact information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which integrate visual observations, language instructions, and large-scale robot trajectory data to perform a wide array of tasks. These models have become a prominent paradigm for general robot control.

However, many real-world manipulation tasks, such as latching, precise placement, or handling fragile objects, fundamentally rely on fine-grained physical interactions and contact states. Traditional VLA models, primarily driven by visual and linguistic inputs, often struggle with these "contact-rich" tasks because critical physical information (e.g., appropriate contact force, slippage, subtle engagement) is often weakly visible in RGB images. The visual scene might appear adequate even when the underlying contact state is problematic.

Recent research has begun to address this by incorporating tactile or force feedback into VLA models, creating "tactile-aware VLAs." These methods integrate physical signals as additional inputs, fusion features, or auxiliary prediction targets during supervised or offline imitation learning. While this enhances the robot's "contact awareness," enabling it to sense subtle physical states, a significant limitation persists: most of these models operate as static, offline policies. They lack a mechanism for real-time adaptation when unforeseen contact conditions deviate from the training distribution, for instance, due to minor misalignments, friction variations, or accumulated execution errors. This absence of online adaptation leads to issues such as inappropriate contact forces, inefficient retries, and overall reduced robustness in dynamic, contact-rich scenarios.

Another relevant area is online reinforcement learning (RL) for VLA policies, which aims to improve performance beyond the ceiling of offline demonstrations. Approaches like learning residual corrections or fine-tuning policies with on-policy data and human interventions have shown promise. However, deploying pure RL-based exploration in real-world contact-rich tasks is often prohibitive due to the high cost of data collection, potential for unsafe actions, and slow learning in sparse-reward environments. Consequently, human intervention is frequently required to guide the robot, prevent failures, or ensure safety. This reliance on human assistance introduces a "value-learning bias" in standard RL frameworks: if a poor policy-generated action leads to human intervention, and the task is subsequently completed, a naive value function might erroneously credit the preceding policy action for the eventual success, leading to sub-optimal policy learning.

TORL-VLA positions itself at the intersection of these challenges. It aims to bridge the gap between "contact awareness" and "contact adaptation" by introducing an online reinforcement learning framework that specifically leverages tactile feedback to refine VLA actions in real-time. Furthermore, it addresses the critical credit assignment problem in human-intervened online learning through a novel intervention-censored critic, making the learning process more robust and efficient in real-world deployment settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robotic manipulation framework, TORL-VLA, capable of online adaptation to tactile feedback for complex, contact-rich tasks. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing the limitations of offline VLA policies in contact-rich manipulation:** While VLA models have demonstrated proficiency in various manipulation tasks, their performance is limited in scenarios requiring precise physical interaction. Tasks such as inserting a cup into a tight holder, manipulating a mechanical latch, or gently grasping a fragile egg involve subtle contact conditions that are difficult to discern from visual cues alone. An object might appear visually aligned, but underlying contact forces could be insufficient, excessive, or misdirected, leading to task failure. Existing tactile-aware VLAs improve the robot's perception of these contact states during training. However, because they operate as static, pre-trained policies, they cannot dynamically adjust their actions when real-world contact conditions deviate from the training data, for example, due to slight positional inaccuracies or variations in object properties. This lack of online adaptability prevents them from correcting errors in real-time.

2. **Enabling real-time contact adaptation at physical bottlenecks:** The critical observation is that contact-rich manipulation demands policies that can not only sense physical interaction states but also adapt their behavior online. Specifically, at key physical bottlenecks where precise contact is essential, the robot needs to refine its actions based on immediate tactile feedback. This necessitates a mechanism that can take a VLA's "reference action" and modulate it in response to the current and anticipated physical interaction, effectively transforming a "contact-aware" policy into a "contact-adaptive" one.

3. **Overcoming challenges in online reinforcement learning with human intervention:** Implementing online reinforcement learning directly on real robots for contact-rich tasks presents significant practical difficulties. Pure policy-driven exploration can be highly data-inefficient, time-consuming, and potentially unsafe, especially when physical interaction could damage objects or the robot. Consequently, human intervention is often indispensable in real-world data collection, allowing operators to correct robot errors, prevent failures, and guide the learning process. However, this common practice introduces a significant challenge for standard RL algorithms: a "value-learning bias." If a robot executes a sub-optimal action that leads to a human taking over control, and the task is subsequently completed successfully by the human, a conventional RL critic might incorrectly attribute the final success to the preceding, flawed robot action. This misattribution can lead the policy to learn undesirable behaviors, hindering effective and stable learning from mixed policy-generated and human-intervention data.

To address these motivations, TORL-VLA aims to achieve the following specific objectives:
- **Develop a tactile-derived wrench-aware VLA model:** This model should not only predict reference actions but also anticipate future wrench sequences, providing both motion priors and expected contact guidance.
- **Design a lightweight online RL module for action refinement:** This module should be capable of refining the VLA's reference actions in real-time, conditioned on current measured tactile feedback and the VLA's predicted future wrench cues.
- **Introduce an intervention-censored critic:** This novel component should mitigate the value-learning bias in human-intervened rollouts by preventing post-intervention success from being incorrectly credited to policy-generated actions that preceded the intervention.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The TORL-VLA framework integrates a tactile-derived wrench-aware VLA as a reference model with a lightweight stage-specific online reinforcement learning (RL) module for action refinement. The methodology is structured in two primary stages:

**Stage I: Tactile-Aware VLA as an Action-and-Wrench Reference Model**

This initial stage focuses on training a robust Vision-Language-Action model that incorporates tactile-derived wrench feedback to predict both future actions and expected wrench sequences.

- **Tactile-Derived Wrench Representation**: The robot gripper is equipped with two piezoelectric tactile pads. Each pad's raw 6x8 tactile array output is mapped to a compact 6D wrench vector (3-axis force and 3-axis torque) via a calibrated sensor interface. The full tactile observation `w_t` is a concatenation of the left and right fingertip wrenches, resulting in a 12D vector. This abstracted representation simplifies the physical interface for the policy.

- **Temporal Wrench Encoding**: To capture short-term contact dynamics, a recent sequence of `J=10` wrench samples (`W_t`) is encoded into a fixed-size wrench token `z_tac_t` using a lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) encoder. Before encoding, the wrench samples are transformed to represent relative changes around the current wrench, emphasizing dynamics rather than absolute values.

- **Wrench-Conditioned Reference Generation**:
 - The VLM (Vision-Language Model) encodes visual inputs from multiple cameras (global, fisheye, wrist), language instructions, and robot proprioceptive states into visual-language (V-L) hidden states (`P_t`).
 - A late fusion strategy is employed where the `z_tac_t` token is appended to `P_t` *after* V-L encoding. A self-attention layer is then applied to the concatenated sequence `[P_t; z_tac_t]` to produce a fused multimodal representation (`H_t`). This preserves pre-trained V-L semantics while allowing interaction with tactile feedback.
 - Following `H_t`, a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) module is used for further feature fusion. A soft router network computes expert weights (`ρ_t`) from `H_t`, and the fused feature (`F_t`) is a weighted sum of outputs from multiple experts (`E_m`). This mechanism allows the model to dynamically route features based on the current contact phase.
 - To prevent the loss of fine-grained physical information during MoE transformations, a zero-initialized wrench-token bypass is introduced. The routed feature `F_t` and the original wrench token `z_tac_t` are projected to the action horizon and combined to form `G_t`. A learnable scalar `α`, initialized to zero, adaptively controls the bypass contribution.
 - This wrench-conditioned guidance `G_t` is added as a residual to the action-side hidden states within a flow-matching action expert, resulting in `˜S_t,s`.

- **Joint Action-and-Wrench Prediction**: The output head of the reference model jointly predicts an `H`-step action chunk (`ˆA_t:t+H−1`, the motion reference) and an `H`-step future wrench sequence (`ˆW_t:t+H−1`, the expected contact guidance). This joint prediction provides comprehensive priors for subsequent online refinement. The model is trained using a flow-matching objective, predicting velocity fields for both action and wrench dimensions.

**Stage II: Stage-Specific Online Reference Adaptation**

This stage involves a lightweight online reinforcement learning module that refines the actions generated by the frozen wrench-aware VLA, adapting to real-time contact conditions.

- **Stage Estimator Routing**: At each policy query, the frozen wrench-aware VLA provides its predictions. A lightweight stage estimator, which reuses features from the VLA query (e.g., visual-semantic tokens `z_vla_t`, robot proprioception `q_t`, current measured wrench `w_t`, recent wrench history `W_t−L:t−1`, predicted future wrench `ˆW_ref_t`), predicts the current contact stage `g_t`. This estimator routes the execution to one of `G` predefined contact stages. Critical stages trigger the corresponding stage-specific actor-critic refiner, while less critical stages or inter-stage transport default to executing the VLA reference directly. A confidence threshold and temporal stability gate prevent unstable switching between stages.

- **Wrench-Conditioned Action Refinement**:
 - From the VLA's `H`-step predictions, the first `K=10` steps are designated as the executable VLA action reference (`A_ref_t`) and the aligned predicted wrench cue (`ˆW_ref_t`).
 - The online actor (`π_ϕ_g_t`) for the selected stage `g_t` generates a refined executable action chunk (`A_ϕ_t`). This refinement is conditioned on a comprehensive context `c_t`, which includes compact VLA visual-semantic tokens, current robot proprioception, current measured wrench, recent measured wrench history, the predicted future wrench cue, and the VLA reference action itself. The actor does not explicitly predict a residual but directly outputs the refined action chunk.

- **Intervention-Censored Actor-Critic Learning**: To address the value-learning bias from human interventions:
 - **Dual Critics**: Two critics are employed:
 - **Task Critic (`Q_task`)**: This is a standard Q-function estimating the expected discounted task return of an action chunk. Its target (`y_task`) is computed using standard Bellman updates.
 - **Intervention-Censored Critic (`Q_ic`)**: This critic is designed to explicitly handle human interventions. For each executed `K`-step action chunk (`A_data_t`), replay stores per-step control source labels (`σ_t,j ∈ {policy, human}`). An intervention-boundary mask (`m_int_t`) is set to `1` if a mostly policy-generated chunk is immediately followed by an intervention-dominated chunk (exceeding a threshold `ρ_int`).
 - The target for `Q_ic` (`y_ic`) incorporates this mask: if `m_int_t = 1`, the bootstrap term from the next state is removed, and a fixed intervention cost (`c_int`) is applied to the current return. This prevents post-intervention success from being incorrectly attributed to the policy actions preceding the intervention.

 - **Actor Objective (`L_π`)**: The actor is optimized using a combined objective:
 - **Behavior Regularization (`λ_bc L_bc`)**: This term anchors policy-generated actions to the VLA reference and encourages imitation of human-intervention steps.
 - **Task-Return Improvement (`-λ_task E[Q_task(c_t, A_ϕ_t)]`)**: Encourages the actor to generate actions with higher expected task returns.
 - **Intervention-Censored Value Penalty (`+λ_ic E[∆Q_ic]`)**: This term penalizes refinements that yield a lower intervention-censored value than the VLA reference action (`∆Q_ic = [min_i Q^(i)_ic(c_t, A_ref_t) - min_i Q^(i)_ic(c_t, A_ϕ_t)]_+`). It discourages the actor from taking actions that are likely to lead to human intervention.

Online data is stored at the `K`-step chunk level, including control source labels, enabling the intervention-censored critic to function. The system maintains real-time performance, with the online refinement adding minimal latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates TORL-VLA on real-robot long-horizon contact-rich manipulation tasks, comparing its performance against several baselines and conducting extensive ablation studies.

**Experimental Setup:**
The evaluation was performed on a real robot platform consisting of a Piper robotic arm with a Pika gripper, featuring multi-view cameras (global, fisheye, wrist) and dual-fingertip piezoelectric tactile sensors (EnsuringTech Tacta), which provide 6D wrench measurements for each finger. Three contact-rich subtasks within a latch-box manipulation benchmark were used: "Coffee Cup" (tight insertion), "Latch" (mechanical engagement including gripping, flipping, and locking), and "Egg" (fragile object grasping and placement).
Evaluation metrics included subtask success rate, full-task success rate (over 30 autonomous trials without human intervention), and 60-min full-task throughput to assess time-bounded execution efficiency.
Baselines included:
- `π_0.5`: A base VLA policy without tactile feedback.
- TA-VLA and ForceVLA: Tactile/force-aware VLAs re-implemented on the `π_0.5` backbone, but deployed as offline policies.
- TORL-VLA w/o RL: Directly executes the learned wrench-aware reference model (Stage I only).
- RLT: Reference-guided online refinement, but without tactile/wrench input during refinement and without the intervention-censored critic.

**Performance on Contact-Rich Tasks:**
- The `π_0.5` baseline exhibited limited success (12/30 full-task), struggling with contact-dependent states that are visually ambiguous.
- Tactile-aware offline policies (TA-VLA, ForceVLA) showed marginal improvements (12/30 and 15/30 full-task, respectively) over `π_0.5`, indicating that contact awareness alone, without adaptation, is insufficient for complex contact-rich tasks.
- TORL-VLA w/o RL, which uses the complete wrench-aware reference model but no online refinement, achieved a higher full-task success rate (21/30), demonstrating the effectiveness of the tactile-derived wrench-aware reference generation. However, it still faced challenges with real-world contact shifts.
- RLT, an online refinement baseline without wrench context or the intervention-censored critic, further improved performance (23/30 full-task), but still had limitations, such as failures in the egg task due to overly aggressive grasps.
- **TORL-VLA consistently outperformed all baselines**, achieving near-perfect subtask success rates (30/30 for Coffee Cup and Egg, 29/30 for Latch) and a significantly higher full-task success rate (28/30).
- In terms of efficiency, TORL-VLA also demonstrated the best 60-minute full-task throughput and the lowest average full-task completion time (165.45 seconds), suggesting more reliable and efficient long-horizon execution under contact deviations.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Wrench-Aware Reference Model Ablation:**
 - The Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) fusion module was identified as critical, with its removal (`w/o MoE`) causing the largest performance degradation across subtasks. This highlights the importance of dynamically routing wrench features with visual-language representations.
 - The zero-initialized physical bypass (`w/o Bypass`) also contributed to performance, particularly for Latch and Egg, suggesting it effectively preserves fine-grained contact information.
 - The wrench history token (WHT) and future wrench prediction objective (FWP) provided complementary benefits, with their removal having smaller, though noticeable, effects.
- **Online Adaptation Ablation:**
 - Removing wrench context from the online refiner (`w/o Wrench Context`) reduced success rates across all subtasks, confirming that current measured wrench feedback and predicted wrench cues are valuable for contact-aware action refinement.
 - Removing the intervention-censored critic (`w/o IC Critic`) degraded final performance, especially in the Latch subtask, where frequent human interventions make proper credit assignment crucial.
 - The adaptation curves for the Latch task indicated that the intervention-censored critic improved early data efficiency, leading to faster gains in both success rate and throughput during online training.
 - Overall, the full TORL-VLA model achieved the best final performance and fastest online adaptation, supporting the necessity of both wrench-context conditioning and intervention-censored value learning.

**Additional Diagnostics:**
- **Stage Estimator Performance:** The stage estimator achieved a high absolute semantic accuracy of 95.5% in routing, with minor discrepancies occurring at continuous phase boundaries, which are managed by confidence and temporal stability gates.
- **Wrench and Contact-State Analysis:** Detailed analysis of measured wrench signals during latching and cup insertion demonstrated continuous, intricate contact evolution, affirming the importance of high-fidelity physical feedback. The reference model's future-wrench prediction capability was observed to anticipate contact consequences (e.g., predicting increasing wrench before contact), indicating that it infers dynamics from visual state and action context, not merely extrapolating history.
- **Intervention-Censored Critic Diagnostics:**
 - Policy-to-human boundary contexts were frequently observed in online training, emphasizing the real-world challenge of credit assignment.
 - The intervention-censored critic (`Q_ic`) effectively distinguished these boundary contexts from clean policy-generated chunks, exhibiting a high AUC score (0.999), significantly better than the task critic (`Q_task`). This suggests `Q_ic` successfully models intervention risk.
 - The actor, over training, learned to move away from actions associated with lower intervention-censored values, reducing the number of risky situations.
 - During late online training, the full TORL-VLA model exhibited lower human intervention reliance and a higher percentage of intervention-free episodes compared to the variant without the IC critic.
- **Deployment Efficiency:** TORL-VLA introduced minimal latency overhead. The end-to-end policy query with actor refinement added only 8.3 ms (an 8.8% increase) compared to the base `π_0.5` policy, maintaining real-time operability at 103.0 ms total mean latency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TORL-VLA framework represents a notable advancement in robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring precise physical interaction. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging Contact Awareness and Adaptation:** A primary contribution is the successful transformation of "contact-aware" VLA policies into truly "contact-adaptive" ones. Existing tactile-aware VLAs enhance perception of physical states but remain static. TORL-VLA enables robots to actively and intelligently adjust their actions in real-time based on live tactile feedback and predicted contact cues, a critical capability for robustness in dynamic, unconstrained environments.

- **Enhanced Robustness and Success Rates in Contact-Rich Tasks:** The experimental results demonstrate a significant improvement in success rates for long-horizon, contact-rich manipulation tasks. By allowing online refinement, TORL-VLA mitigates issues arising from local pose errors, friction variations, and accumulated execution errors that typically plague offline policies. This increased reliability is crucial for deploying robots in practical applications.

- **Improved Efficiency and Throughput:** Beyond merely increasing success, TORL-VLA also enhances the time-bounded execution efficiency and throughput. This indicates that the robot not only succeeds more often but also performs tasks more smoothly and quickly, which has direct implications for productivity in industrial or service robotics.

- **Robust Online Learning from Human Intervention:** The introduction of the intervention-censored critic addresses a fundamental challenge in real-world reinforcement learning: safely and effectively leveraging human intervention data. By preventing the misattribution of post-intervention success to preceding policy failures, the critic allows the policy to learn from human corrections without internalizing undesirable behaviors. This mechanism can accelerate data-efficient learning on real robots, reducing the need for costly and risky pure exploration.

- **Generalizability to Other Physical Interaction Tasks:** While validated on a specific set of tasks, the core principles—integrating multi-modal feedback with online RL and robust credit assignment—could be extensible to a broader range of robotic manipulation problems involving complex physical interaction, such as assembly, dexterous manipulation, or human-robot collaboration.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations, which also suggest avenues for future research:
- **Scope of Evaluation:** The current evaluation is limited to specific latch-box manipulation tasks and a narrow range of contact modes. Broader validation across more diverse objects, complex contact geometries, varied environments, and different robot embodiments is necessary to ascertain the generalizability of TORL-VLA.
- **Reliance on Tactile Sensor Reliability:** The framework heavily relies on accurate and reliable fingertip wrench measurements. Sensor noise, calibration errors, coordinate-frame misalignment, or hardware drift could degrade both measured feedback and the quality of predicted wrench references, potentially impacting performance. Investigating robustness to sensor imperfections or alternative tactile sensing modalities could be valuable.
- **Assumptions about Human Intervention:** The intervention-based learning paradigm assumes that human interventions reliably indicate impending failure or sub-optimal robot behavior. In practice, human interventions might be inconsistent, suboptimal, or reflect operator preferences rather than imminent task failure. Research into more nuanced human feedback mechanisms or learning from heterogeneous human corrections could enhance the system.

Overall, TORL-VLA represents a significant step towards more autonomous, robust, and adaptable robotic manipulation systems capable of operating effectively in contact-rich real-world scenarios.
