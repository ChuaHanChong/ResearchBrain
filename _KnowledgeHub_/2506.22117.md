# Safe Multi-Agent Navigation via Constrained HJB-Informed Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the National University of Singapore developed HJB-GNN, a physics-informed learning framework for safe multi-agent navigation in complex, unknown environments. The system integrates constrained Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations with Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to adaptively balance collision-avoidance and goal-reaching, achieving superior safety and scalability in dense simulated scenarios and on real drone swarms.

## Problem

- Existing multi-agent safety-filtering methods often lead to conservative behaviors, deadlocks, or collisions in dense environments due to myopic (short-horizon) decision-making and reliance on fixed nominal controllers.
- Current constrained optimal control and reinforcement learning approaches struggle with a principled, adaptive trade-off between safety and task objectives, often relying on heuristic penalty weights.
- Many solutions lack scalability to large agent numbers or generalizability to unseen, dense, and dynamic environments with varying interaction topologies.

## Method

- The proposed **HJB-GNN** framework leverages constrained ==Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations== within an infinite-horizon optimal control formulation.
- It derives graph-dependent Lagrange multipliers from ==Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) optimality conditions==, enabling a principled and adaptive trade-off between collision-avoidance and goal-reaching.
- An end-to-end learning algorithm jointly synthesizes a value function, a graph ==Control Barrier Function (CBF)==, and a distributed navigation policy using ==Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)==, eliminating the need for short-horizon QP solvers or predefined nominal controllers.

## Results

- HJB-GNN significantly reduced deadlocks and collisions in dense, cluttered simulated environments, demonstrating superior safety and goal-reaching rates compared to state-of-the-art QP-GCBF+.
- The framework showcased strong scalability and generalizability, maintaining high safety and safe-reaching rates for up to **512 agents** and outperforming baselines in unseen, higher-density environments.
- Successfully deployed the learned policies on physical Crazyflie drone swarms, achieving safe navigation in scenarios with dense agents, static non-convex obstacles, and dynamic obstacles while requiring minimal memory and inference time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An infinite-horizon optimal control formulation, when informed by graph CBFs, can overcome the myopic limitations of short-horizon safety filters, leading to less conservative and more effective navigation policies.
> - KKT conditions provide a foundational mechanism for deriving state- and graph-dependent Lagrange multipliers, enabling a theoretically sound and adaptive balancing of competing control objectives (safety versus goal-reaching).
> - Physics-informed learning with GNNs, approximating key components of the constrained HJB equations, offers a scalable and generalizable approach for safe control in multi-agent systems within complex and unknown environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-agent navigation in complex, dynamic, and unknown environments represents a core challenge for autonomous robotic systems, with applications spanning automated warehouses, drone swarms for search and rescue, and coordinated unmanned surface vehicles. The broad landscape of multi-agent systems (MAS) often necessitates distributed control strategies due to constraints on onboard computation, sensing range, and communication bandwidth.

Existing approaches to multi-agent navigation typically confront a fundamental competition between ensuring collision-avoidance and achieving goal-reaching objectives. A significant line of research has focused on **safety filtering mechanisms**, often employing Quadratic Programming (QP)-based safety filters alongside Control Barrier Functions (CBFs). These methods project a nominal goal-reaching controller onto CBF-derived safety constraints. While effective in known environments where CBFs can be specified analytically, their applicability diminishes in unknown settings. Recent advancements in this area include learning CBFs directly from data, such as Graph Neural Networks (GNNs)-parameterized CBFs (GCBFs), which adapt to dynamic interaction graphs. However, these QP-based methods frequently rely on a predefined, fixed nominal controller, and often provide only one-step-ahead safety guarantees. This can lead to myopic control policies, causing agents to operate excessively close to obstacles or other agents, resulting in conservative behaviors, deadlocks, or collisions in dense multi-agent scenarios. Extensions incorporating Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) alleviate the need for explicit nominal controllers but still exhibit short-horizon limitations.

Another category of recent work frames MAS navigation as a **constrained infinite-horizon optimal control problem**. Approaches in this domain, such as constrained reinforcement learning using proximal policy optimization, attempt to balance safety and task objectives through loss function design, often involving gradient projection mechanisms. However, the trade-off in these methods is typically governed by heuristic penalty weights, which can also induce overly conservative behaviors in dense environments. Furthermore, some methods use epigraph reformulations to indirectly coordinate collision-avoidance and goal-reaching, which may limit responsiveness to rapidly changing interactions and lead to deadlocks or collisions.

This paper positions its proposed framework, HJB-GNN, as addressing the limitations observed in both safety filtering and existing infinite-horizon optimal control approaches. It integrates the analytical rigor of Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations with the adaptability of GNNs. By deriving graph-dependent Lagrange multipliers from KKT optimality conditions within an HJB framework, the work aims to enable a principled and adaptive trade-off between competing objectives, thereby reducing conservative behaviors and improving performance in dense, unseen environments, while supporting scalable, distributed deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and scalable solution for safe multi-agent navigation in unknown and cluttered environments, specifically targeting the inherent conflict between collision-avoidance and goal-reaching objectives.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the current research landscape:

1. **Overly Conservative Behaviors and Failures in Dense Environments:** Existing safety-filtering methods, particularly those based on short-horizon Quadratic Programming (QP) solvers and predefined nominal controllers, are prone to myopic decision-making. This often results in agents adopting excessively conservative behaviors, encountering deadlocks, or even experiencing collisions when operating in dense multi-agent or obstacle-rich environments. The reliance on fixed controllers and one-step-ahead safety guarantees restricts the adaptability required for complex, reactive interactions.

2. **Lack of Principled Adaptive Trade-off:** Many current approaches manage the trade-off between safety and task performance using heuristic loss function designs, manually tuned static penalty weights, or indirect coordination mechanisms (e.g., auxiliary variables). These methods often fail to adapt effectively to dynamically changing interaction graphs and environmental conditions, leading to suboptimal performance or safety compromises. A motivation is to establish a more principled and adaptive mechanism for this trade-off.

3. **Challenges with Scalability and Generalizability:** Solutions relying on fixed-input neural network architectures or those that assume a fixed number of neighbors struggle to scale efficiently to large-scale multi-agent systems (MAS) or generalize to previously unseen, dense environments with varying interaction topologies.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out with the following key objectives:

- **Establish an Infinite-Horizon Safe Optimal Control Framework:** Develop a framework for MAS navigation that leverages graph Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) within an infinite-horizon optimal control formulation.
- **Derive an Adaptive Trade-off Mechanism:** Introduce a principled, state- and graph-dependent adaptive trade-off mechanism between collision-avoidance and goal-reaching. This mechanism should be derived from fundamental optimality conditions, specifically Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions, to yield graph-dependent Lagrange multipliers that adjust dynamically to the interaction topology.
- **Develop an End-to-End Learning Algorithm (HJB-GNN):** Create a novel physics-informed learning algorithm that jointly synthesizes a value function, a graph CBF, and a distributed navigation policy. This algorithm should utilize GNNs to leverage the analytical structure of constrained HJB equations, thereby eliminating the need for short-horizon QP solvers or predefined nominal controllers.
- **Support Centralized Training and Distributed Deployment:** Design the learning framework to allow for centralized training while enabling efficient distributed deployment of the learned policy, promoting scalability.
- **Demonstrate Superior Performance:** Validate the proposed HJB-GNN framework through extensive simulations and real-world experiments, showcasing its effectiveness in terms of safety, goal-reaching performance, scalability, and generalizability in dense, cluttered, and unseen environments, particularly in comparison to existing state-of-the-art methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper involves a multi-faceted approach that integrates optimal control theory, physics-informed learning, and graph neural networks.

**A. System Dynamics and Safety Definition**
The multi-agent system (MAS) consists of $N$ agents, each governed by general nonlinear dynamics $\dot{x}_i = f(x_i) + g(x_i)u_i$. Agents have limited sensing radii ($R_a$), observing neighbors and obstacles only within this range. Interactions are modeled by a dynamic graph $G = (V, E)$, where $V$ includes agents and obstacles, and $E$ represents observable connections.
Safety is defined by a set $S$, which mandates collision-avoidance between agents and between agents and obstacles. This is achieved by ensuring minimum separation distances. To manage dynamically changing neighborhood information in unknown environments, the paper employs a $C^1$ **graph Control Barrier Function (CBF)**, $h(\bar{x}_{\hat{N}_i})$, which takes as input an augmented neighborhood state $\bar{x}_{\hat{N}_i}$ (including the agent and its $M$ closest neighbors/obstacles). The CBF constraint, $\dot{h}(\bar{x}_{\hat{N}_i}) \geq -\alpha(h(\bar{x}_{\hat{N}_i}))$, ensures that if an agent starts in a safe region ($h \geq 0$), it remains safe.

**B. Safe Optimal Control Formulation**
The core of the approach is an **infinite-horizon constrained optimal control problem**. For each agent $i$, the objective is to minimize an integral of an immediate cost $r_i(e_i, u_i) = e_i^T Q e_i + u_i^T R u_i$, where $e_i$ is the goal-reaching error. This minimization is subject to the agent's dynamics and the graph CBF constraint. This formulation unifies goal-reaching and collision-avoidance.

**C. Safe Optimal Controllers Synthesis via Constrained HJB**
The problem is reformulated through the lens of **Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations**. The optimal value function $V_i^*(e_i)$ satisfies an HJB equation, which requires minimizing the Hamiltonian $H_i$ subject to the CBF constraint.
To solve this constrained optimization problem pointwise, **Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) optimality conditions** are applied. This yields an analytical form for the safe optimal controller $u_i^*$. Crucially, the KKT conditions introduce a **graph-dependent Lagrange multiplier $\lambda_i^*$**. This multiplier adaptively balances the goal-reaching component (derived from $\nabla_{e_i} V_i^*$) and the safety component (derived from $\nabla_{x_i} h$) of the control input. The value of $\lambda_i^*$ is determined by whether the goal-reaching control alone threatens safety (i.e., if $\Lambda_i < 0$, where $\Lambda_i$ characterizes the safety margin under goal-reaching control; otherwise, $\lambda_i^* = 0$). This mechanism dynamically increases repulsive effects for safety when agents are close and relaxes safety constraints to prioritize goal-reaching when the interaction density decreases. This approach aims to avoid the conservative trade-offs associated with fixed parameters or heuristic designs.
Theorem 1 formally certifies that if such a $C^1$ optimal value function and graph CBF exist, the derived controllers guarantee both system safety (state remains in the safe set) and asymptotic goal-reaching.

**D. HJB-GNN: Physics-Informed Safe Learning with GNNs**
To implement the analytical controller, which requires knowledge of the optimal value function $V_i^*$ and a valid graph CBF $h$, a learning framework called **HJB-GNN** is developed.

1. **GNN-based Approximation:**
 - **Graph CBF ($h_\vartheta$):** Parameterized by a GNN to handle variable-sized neighbor sets and ensure generalizability. The GNN uses an attention mechanism to aggregate neighbor features, encoding node types (agent, goal, obstacle) and relative positions.
 - **Distributed Safe Policy ($\pi_\varphi$):** Also parameterized by a GNN, making it distributed and adaptable to arbitrary swarm scales.
 - **Value Function ($V_\theta$):** Parameterized by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), as it primarily depends on the goal-reaching error and not the graph topology.

2. **Training Process:** The learning algorithm (Algorithm 1) employs three distinct loss functions for end-to-end joint synthesis of the networks:
 - **Value Function Loss ($L_v$):** Comprises a candidate Lyapunov function loss, $L_{cv_i}(\theta)$, which enforces positive definiteness and radial unboundedness of $V_\theta$, and a squared Bellman error loss, $L_{hv_i}(\theta, \varphi)$, which encourages satisfaction of the HJB optimality condition and ensures the time derivative of $V_\theta$ is negative definite along the trajectory.
 - **CBF Loss ($L_h$):** Enforces the satisfaction of the safety constraint $h \geq 0$ and $\dot{h} + \alpha(h) \geq 0$. It utilizes two datasets ($D_C$ for safe states, $D_A$ for unsafe states) generated from policy rollouts to promote the properties of the graph CBF.
 - **Controller Loss ($L_u$):** This loss guides the policy $\pi_\varphi$ to emulate the analytically derived safe optimal controller. It includes a term $L_{\pi_i}(\theta, \vartheta, \varphi)$ that minimizes the deviation between $\pi_\varphi$ and an approximated KKT-based controller $\hat{u}_i$ (which uses the current learned $V_\theta$ and $h_\vartheta$). It also incorporates the CBF loss ($L_h$) and Bellman error loss ($L_{hv_i}$) to further promote safety and stability.

3. **Two-Phase Iterative Training:** The algorithm alternates between two phases:
 - **Phase 1:** Updates $V_\theta$ and $h_\vartheta$ with a fixed policy $\pi_\varphi$, evaluating the current controller's performance. It terminates when the empirical Bellman error is reduced.
 - **Phase 2:** Updates $\pi_\varphi$ and refines $h_\vartheta$ with a fixed $V_\theta$, improving the controller to be stabilizing and safe. It terminates when the empirical expectation of the Hamiltonian is non-positive.
 This alternating process progressively yields a distributed, safe, and optimal policy.

**E. Centralized Training and Distributed Deployment**
The HJB-GNN framework supports centralized training, leveraging the computational power of a single machine, while enabling the learned policy to be deployed in a distributed manner on individual agents. The computational efficiency of the trained policy is noted to be minimal, allowing for execution on embedded platforms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides both simulation and hardware experimental results to substantiate the capabilities of the HJB-GNN framework. The evaluation focuses on safety rate (proportion of agents avoiding all collisions) and safe-reaching rate (proportion of agents remaining collision-free and reaching goals).

**A. Reduced Deadlocks and Collisions (Simulations)**
- **Comparison with QP-GCBF+:** HJB-GNN demonstrated improvements over the state-of-the-art QP-GCBF+ method in dense, cluttered environments. QP-GCBF+ exhibited deadlocks (e.g., Agent 1 in Fig. 4a, Agent 2 in Fig. 4c) or collisions (Agent 3 in Fig. 4e), while HJB-GNN ensured all agents safely reached their goals in the corresponding scenarios (Fig. 4b, d, f). This improvement is attributed to the infinite-horizon optimal formulation and adaptive trade-off mechanism, which provide more forward-looking decision-making compared to the myopic nature of short-horizon QP solvers and fixed nominal controllers.
- **Single-agent Deadlock Reduction:** In a single-agent scenario with static obstacles, HJB-GNN successfully navigated to the goal without collision, whereas QP-GCBF+ experienced a deadlock near an obstacle (Fig. 3).

**B. Scalability and Generalizability (Simulations)**
- **Increasing Obstacle Density:** HJB-GNN consistently outperformed QP-GCBF+ in terms of both safety and safe-reaching rates when increasing the number of obstacles for fixed numbers of agents (256 and 1024 agents). The performance advantage of HJB-GNN became more pronounced as obstacle density increased, contrasting with the rapid degradation of QP-GCBF+ (Figs. 5, 6). This highlights HJB-GNN's enhanced adaptability to complex, dense, and previously unseen environments.
- **Increasing Agent Density (Obstacle-Free):** In obstacle-free environments, HJB-GNN maintained high safety and safe-reaching rates for up to 512 agents, demonstrating strong scalability. Even at 1024 agents, it maintained a safety rate near 80% and a safe-reaching rate around 50%, showcasing robustness under extreme scalability challenges (Fig. 8 a-b).
- **Comparison with Other Baselines:** Additional comparisons with DGPPO and Def-MARL showed HJB-GNN's superior generalizability to higher agent densities (up to 16x higher than training density). HJB-GNN maintained 89.59% safe-reaching rate at 320 agents, compared to 74.45% for QP-GCBF+, 0.52% for DGPPO, and 0.03% for Def-MARL (Table II). This suggests physics-informed methods offer stronger generalizability than purely data-driven reinforcement learning approaches in scenarios involving distribution shifts.
- **Ablation Study:** The ablation study on loss weights indicated that HJB-GNN remains robust to substantial variations (16x range) in key hyperparameters, with interpretable effects on safety-reaching trade-offs. This suggests the architecture's success primarily derives from its principled HJB-informed structure rather than fine-tuned parameters.

**C. Hardware Experiment Validation (Crazyflie Drones)**
- **Physical Deployment:** HJB-GNN was successfully deployed on Crazyflie 2.1 miniature drone swarms, demonstrating practical applicability and robustness. The policy requires approximately 1.5 MB memory per agent and a few milliseconds for inference, making it suitable for embedded platforms.
- **Dense Agent Environment:** Eight Crazyflie drones successfully performed antipodal position swapping in an obstacle-free environment, reaching goals without collisions (Fig. 7a-e, Fig. 10a, Fig. 11a).
- **Static Obstacles:** Experiments with static large non-convex obstacles (a long static obstacle and multiple complex non-convex obstacles) demonstrated the policy's ability to avoid deadlocks and collisions in challenging cluttered environments (Fig. 7f-o, Fig. 10b,c, Fig. 11b,c).
- **Dynamic Obstacles:** An experiment involving a large, randomly moving dynamic obstacle, unknown to the agents (as obstacle velocities were not used in training), also showed successful collision avoidance and goal reaching by the drones (Fig. 1b-e, Fig. 10d, Fig. 11d). This indicates the generalizability of the approach to unforeseen dynamic elements.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for multi-agent navigation that addresses several persistent challenges in the field, contributing to advancements in autonomous systems.

**Significance:**

- **Principled Adaptive Trade-off:** The work introduces an analytically derived, graph-dependent Lagrange multiplier mechanism that provides a principled method for adaptively balancing collision-avoidance and goal-reaching objectives. This departs from heuristic or manually tuned trade-off strategies common in previous literature, potentially leading to more optimal and less conservative behaviors in dynamic environments.
- **Joint, End-to-End Learning:** HJB-GNN enables the simultaneous learning of a value function, a graph CBF, and a distributed control policy. This end-to-end synthesis removes the reliance on external components like short-horizon QP solvers or predefined nominal controllers, which often introduce limitations (e.g., myopic decision-making, deadlocks).
- **Enhanced Scalability and Generalizability:** The framework demonstrates notable improvements in handling large numbers of agents and obstacles, as well as operating in unseen, dense, and cluttered environments. This addresses a critical need for deploying MAS in real-world scenarios, where environmental conditions and interaction complexities can vary significantly.
- **Physics-Informed Learning:** By integrating the analytical structure of constrained HJB equations, the learning process is guided by fundamental principles of optimal control, contributing to the robustness and theoretical soundness of the learned policies compared to purely data-driven methods.
- **Practical Validation:** The successful deployment on physical Crazyflie drone platforms in challenging scenarios (including dense agents, static non-convex obstacles, and dynamic obstacles) provides empirical evidence of the method's robustness and applicability beyond simulations.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advancements in Autonomous Robotics:** The improved ability of multi-agent systems to navigate safely and efficiently in complex, unknown, and dense environments could enable more reliable deployment of autonomous robots in various domains.
- **Real-world Applications:** This research has implications for applications such as:
 - **Automated Warehouses:** Enabling dense populations of mobile robots to efficiently move goods without collisions.
 - **Search and Rescue Operations:** Improving the coordination and safety of drone swarms in dynamic and cluttered disaster zones.
 - **Coordinated Surveillance and Patrol:** Enhancing the autonomy and safety of unmanned surface vehicles or aerial vehicles in complex operational areas.
 - **Logistics and Delivery:** Facilitating safer and more efficient drone or ground robot deliveries in urban environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework provides a foundation for further research into integrating advanced optimal control theory with deep learning for safety-critical autonomous systems, potentially inspiring new hybrid approaches for complex decision-making under constraints. The demonstration of centralized training for distributed deployment also offers a scalable paradigm for designing and implementing control policies for large-scale robotic teams.
