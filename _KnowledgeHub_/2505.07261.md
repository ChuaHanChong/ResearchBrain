# CHD: Coupled Hierarchical Diffusion for Long-Horizon Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Coupled Hierarchical Diffusion (CHD) from the National University of Singapore introduces a framework that jointly models high-level subgoals and low-level trajectories within a unified diffusion process, enabling bidirectional feedback and asynchronous parallel generation for long-horizon robot tasks. The approach improves performance in maze navigation by 10-15% and robot task planning, while reducing inference time by 30-40% compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Diffusion models exhibit performance degradation in complex, long-horizon robotic tasks due to compounding uncertainty and increased trajectory variance.
- Existing hierarchical diffusion planners suffer from "loose coupling," where high-level subgoals are generated independently and remain fixed, hindering dynamic refinement based on low-level execution feedback.
- Achieving coherent and scalable planning for robots in diverse, unstructured environments over extended timeframes remains a challenge.

## Method

- Introduces **Coupled Hierarchical Diffusion (CHD)**, which jointly models high-level subgoals and low-level trajectories within a unified ==diffusion== framework.
- Enables bidirectional coupling through a ==coupled hierarchical classifier==, allowing low-level feedback to dynamically refine high-level subgoals during the reverse (sampling) process.
- Utilizes ==asynchronous parallel generation== for subgoals and trajectories, and ==segment-wise generation== for low-level planning, to reduce complexity and accelerate inference.

## Results

- In continuous 2D maze navigation, CHD achieved **10-15% higher** normalized rewards compared to baseline methods like SHD and Diffuser, yielding more efficient paths.
- For robot task planning in semantic environments, the method demonstrated superior task completion rates and reduced step counts across varying task difficulties, outperforming baselines including BHD, Diffuser, and VLM.
- CHD reduced inference (sampling) time by **30-40%** compared to Baseline Hierarchical Diffusion while maintaining or improving performance, validating its computational efficiency for real-time applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Bidirectional interaction between high-level subgoal generation and low-level trajectory execution is essential for robust and coherent long-horizon planning.
> - A unified diffusion process, guided by a coupled hierarchical classifier, can effectively model the joint generation and refinement of both abstract subgoals and concrete actions.
> - Asynchronous parallel generation significantly boosts the inference speed of hierarchical diffusion planners, making them more practical for real-time robotic applications.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning and autonomous systems is continually striving to enable robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks in diverse and unstructured environments. Traditional planning methods often struggle with the combinatorial explosion of possibilities, while recent learning-based approaches face challenges with scalability, generalization, and coherence over extended timeframes.

Diffusion models, initially gaining prominence in image and video generation, have recently emerged as powerful generative models for robot control and planning. They excel at synthesizing smooth, multi-modal trajectories, offering a promising alternative to traditional reinforcement learning and imitation learning methods. Papers like "Diffuser" and "Decision Diffuser" have showcased their potential for generating goal-conditioned trajectories and optimizing rewards.

However, the broader research landscape acknowledges a significant limitation: while diffusion models perform strongly in short-horizon tasks, their performance degrades in complex, long-horizon settings. This degradation is attributed to compounding uncertainty and increased trajectory variance over longer planning horizons.

To address this, hierarchical planning has gained traction. This approach decomposes a complex task into high-level (HL) subgoals and low-level (LL) trajectory segments, effectively reducing the planning horizon and distribution complexity for each sub-problem. Within the diffusion model paradigm, "Hierarchical Diffusion Planners" (e.g., HMDI and SHD) have been proposed. These methods attempt to leverage diffusion models at both hierarchical levels.

The critical gap identified by the authors of "Coupled Hierarchical Diffusion (CHD)" is the *loose coupling* inherent in these existing hierarchical diffusion planners (termed "Baseline Hierarchical Diffusion" or BHD). In BHD, HL subgoals are often generated independently and remain fixed throughout the LL trajectory generation process. This prevents the LL planner from providing feedback to refine or correct subgoals if they prove infeasible or sub-optimal, leading to a disconnect that limits coordinated planning and can result in inefficient or failed task execution. This problem resonates with long-standing challenges in traditional hierarchical planning architectures where maintaining coherence and effective communication between different levels of abstraction is crucial.

CHD directly confronts this limitation. By proposing a framework that models HL subgoals and LL trajectories *jointly* within a *unified diffusion process*, and crucially, allowing bidirectional interaction through a shared classifier, CHD advances the state-of-the-art. It aims to bridge the gap between the expressive power of diffusion models and the scalability requirements of hierarchical planning, thereby offering a more robust and efficient solution for long-horizon robot autonomy. This work is positioned as a significant step towards making diffusion models a more viable and practical tool for real-world robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the development of Coupled Hierarchical Diffusion (CHD) stems from a critical observation regarding the limitations of existing diffusion-based planners in handling complex, long-horizon tasks. While diffusion models have demonstrated remarkable success in generating smooth, coherent, and multi-modal trajectories for short-horizon robotic control, their efficacy diminishes significantly as planning horizons extend. The authors trace this degradation to two main issues: increased trajectory variance and the compounding of uncertainty, making it difficult to achieve high rewards over long sequences of actions.

Hierarchical planning offers a promising avenue to mitigate these challenges by decomposing the overall problem into more manageable sub-problems: high-level (HL) subgoal inference and low-level (LL) goal-conditioned trajectory generation. This decomposition inherently reduces the effective horizon length and simplifies the complexity of the underlying data distributions. However, the authors identify a crucial flaw in existing "Baseline Hierarchical Diffusion" (BHD) methods: a "loose coupling" between the HL and LL planners.

The **central motivation** for CHD is to overcome this loose coupling. In current BHD approaches, HL subgoals are typically generated first and then remain static while the LL planner attempts to execute them. This unidirectional flow of information means the LL planner cannot provide feedback to dynamically refine or adjust subgoals if they are discovered to be sub-optimal, infeasible, or lead to undesirable outcomes during LL trajectory generation. This disconnect severely limits the potential for coordinated planning and can result in incoherent or sub-optimal overall plans.

This leads to the **core objective** of the paper: to design a hierarchical diffusion planner that enables *joint generation and refinement* of both HL subgoals and LL trajectories. To achieve this, the authors specifically aim to satisfy three key properties that are essential for effective and scalable long-horizon hierarchical planning:

1. **P1: Bi-directional Coupling:** The HL planner should guide LL trajectory generation, and, critically, LL feedback must be able to refine HL subgoals as planning progresses. This ensures dynamic adaptation and coherence.
2. **P2: Parallel Sampling:** To accelerate inference and make the planner practical for real-time applications, HL and LL components should be able to generate subgoals and trajectories concurrently.
3. **P3: Reduced Complexity:** The hierarchical decomposition should effectively lower the effective planning horizon and distribution complexity, thereby improving the tractability and scalability of the planning process.

By addressing these objectives, CHD seeks to establish a new paradigm for long-horizon planning with diffusion models, promising more robust, efficient, and coherent robotic behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of Coupled Hierarchical Diffusion (CHD) is built upon the idea of modeling high-level (HL) subgoals and low-level (LL) trajectories *jointly* within a unified diffusion process, with specific architectural innovations to ensure tight coupling, parallel generation, and reduced complexity.

The authors begin by conceptualizing a **Joint Diffusion Model (JDM)** as a foundational, principled approach for jointly generating multiple variables (in this case, HL subgoals $\tau_g$ and LL trajectories $\tau_x$). In a JDM, both variables are corrupted independently in the forward process and then denoised in a coupled, hierarchical reverse process where each level's denoising is conditioned on the other. While JDM inherently provides bi-directional coupling (P1), its diffusion over the entire joint space means it does not reduce planning horizon or data complexity (violating P3), nor does it allow independent, parallel sampling (violating P2). Thus, JDM is deemed impractical for hierarchical planning and is shown to be equivalent to a flat diffusion planner in terms of complexity.

To transform JDM into the practically scalable CHD, the authors introduce three key innovations:

1. **Coupled Hierarchical Classifier Guidance (Achieving P1: Bi-directional Coupling):**
 - **Simplification of JDM:** CHD first simplifies the JDM's reverse process by removing the *direct* dependency of the HL reverse model on the LL trajectory. Specifically, the HL denoising step is simplified to $p_{\theta_g}(\tau_{g_{t-1}} | \tau_{g_t})$, making it independent of $\tau_{x_t}$. This creates a more modular structure, but initially removes the direct LL-to-HL feedback.
 - **Reintroducing Coupling via Classifier:** The crucial innovation is embedding this simplified diffusion process within a hierarchical objective function guided by a *coupled hierarchical classifier* $p_{\phi}(O_{1:N}=1 | \tau_g, \tau_x)$. This classifier is trained to predict the optimality (e.g., cumulative reward) of the *joint* pair of HL subgoals and LL trajectories.
 - **Feedback Mechanism:** During the reverse (sampling) process, this classifier provides gradients to *both* the HL and LL denoising steps. The LL trajectories provide feedback *through the classifier term*, which then adjusts the HL subgoals at every reverse step. This dynamic, iterative adjustment based on LL outcomes realizes the desired bi-directional coupling, allowing subgoals to self-correct and improving overall optimality and coherence.

2. **Asynchronous Parallel Generation (Achieving P2: Parallel Sampling):**
 - **Challenge:** The probabilistic graph structure of the reverse process (where LL depends on HL: $p_{\theta_x}(\tau_{x_{t-1}} | \tau_{g_{t-1}}, \tau_{x_t})$) initially prevents synchronous parallel denoising of HL and LL at the *same* diffusion step $t$.
 - **Solution:** CHD reorganizes the reverse process into an *asynchronous* generation schedule. Instead of generating $(\tau_{g_{t-1}}, \tau_{x_{t-1}})$ at each step, it generates $(\tau_{g_{t-1}}, \tau_{x_t})$. This allows the HL and LL denoising to run in parallel for most diffusion steps.
 - **Classifier Guidance in Asynchronous Setup:** A challenge arises because the classifier scores $(\tau_{g_t}, \tau_{x_t})$, but the HL denoising affects $\tau_{g_{t-1}}$. CHD addresses this by backpropagating the classifier score through the HL denoiser using the chain rule, effectively using $\tau_{g_t}$ to guide the generation of $\tau_{g_{t-1}}$ while considering the $\tau_{x_t}$ context. This ensures that the benefits of coupled guidance are retained even with asynchronous updates.

3. **Segment-wise Generation (Achieving P3: Reduced Complexity):**
 - **Decomposition:** The LL planning process is explicitly decomposed into $N$ shorter, independent segments, each with a horizon $h$. This is a common approach in hierarchical planning to manage complexity.
 - **Factorization:** Both the LL reverse model $p_{\theta_x}(\tau_{x_{t-1},1:N} | \tau_{g_{t-1}}, \tau_{x_{t,1:N}})$ and the coupled hierarchical classifier $p(O_{1:N}=1 | \tau_{g_t}, \tau_{x_{t,1:N}})$ are factorized into segment-wise components. For the LL, this means $p_{\theta_x}(\tau_{x_{t-1},i} | g_{t-1,i}, \tau_{x_{t,i}})$, where each segment's trajectory is conditioned on its corresponding subgoal. For the classifier, it becomes a product of probabilities for each segment's optimality $p_{\phi}(O_i=1 | g_{t,i}, \tau_{x_{t,i}})$.
 - **Benefits:** This approximation significantly reduces the data complexity and effective planning horizon for the LL components, making the overall model more tractable and scalable for long-horizon tasks.

**Training:** The model is trained by minimizing variational bounds on the negative log-likelihood, similar to standard diffusion models. The HL and LL diffusion models (typically U-Nets) and the hierarchical classifier are trained simultaneously. For discrete state/action spaces (like in robot task planning), the Bits Diffusion method is employed for token generation.

By synergistically combining these three innovations, CHD constructs a robust and efficient hierarchical diffusion planner that addresses the core limitations of prior methods, enabling scalable and coherent long-horizon task execution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted for Coupled Hierarchical Diffusion (CHD) across diverse environments consistently demonstrate its superiority over both flat and existing hierarchical diffusion baselines. The findings confirm that CHD effectively improves trajectory coherence, reward maximization, and sampling efficiency, validating its design principles.

#### 4.1. Maze Navigation (Continuous 2D Space)

- **Performance Metric:** Normalized cumulative reward, where higher values indicate fewer steps to reach the goal.
- **Quantitative Results:** CHD significantly outperformed all baselines (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser (DD), Baseline Hierarchical Diffusion (BHD), HMDI, SHD) by 10-15% in normalized rewards across various Maze2D (fixed goal) and Multi2D (randomized goal) environments (U-Maze, Medium, Large). For instance, in Maze2D Large, CHD achieved 179.1 compared to SHD's 155.8 and Diffuser's 123.0.
- **Qualitative Explanation:** Visualizations revealed that CHD's tight coupling between HL subgoals and LL trajectory segments led to more efficient paths. Unlike BHD, which often placed subgoals at sub-optimal corners resulting in redundant steps or collisions due to fixed subgoal planning, CHD adapted subgoals dynamically, reducing overall path length and improving feasibility.

#### 4.2. Robot Task Planning (Kitchen World, Discrete Semantic Tokens)

- **Task:** Mobile dual-arm robot planning a sequence of semantic actions to "cook meals" (e.g., Pick Pot, Put On-stove) with up to 90 subtasks.
- **Performance Metrics:** Number of completed tasks (higher is better) and normalized cumulative steps (lower is better).
- **Quantitative Results (Task Completion):** CHD achieved the highest task completion rates across Easy, Medium, and Hard single-target and multi-target tasks. For example, in "Single Easy," CHD completed 8.01 tasks compared to BHD's 6.94, Diffuser's 6.34, and VLM's 4.71.
- **Quantitative Results (Efficiency):** CHD consistently yielded the lowest normalized step count across sub-tasks, indicating greater planning efficiency compared to all baselines. VLMs and Transformers struggled with "repetition traps" in later subtasks, while BHD's fixed subgoals limited recovery from early sub-optimal decisions.
- **Qualitative Explanation:** CHD's ability to incorporate LL feedback enabled it to dynamically adjust HL subgoals when infeasible actions were detected, significantly improving robustness and allowing the robot to recover and complete complex, multi-step tasks that baffled other planners.

#### 4.3. Analysis and Discussion (Ablation Studies and Computational Efficiency)

- **LL Feedback via Coupled Classifier Guidance:** Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the coupled hierarchical classifier. Removing classifier guidance or using only HL-conditioned classifiers (like BHD) led to significant performance drops, highlighting that LL feedback is essential for refining HL subgoals and achieving higher optimality.
- **Segment-wise Generation:** Replacing the segment-wise classifier with one conditioned on full trajectories reduced performance, underscoring the benefits of hierarchical decomposition in reducing distribution complexity and variance.
- **Computational Efficiency:**
 - **Training:** CHD (and BHD) demonstrated significantly faster training times than flat Diffuser models (e.g., 5.2 hours for Maze Nav vs. 15.0 hours for Diffuser), primarily due to the reduced data complexity and effective planning horizon, allowing fewer diffusion steps (32 vs. 256).
 - **Inference (Sampling):** Crucially, CHD’s asynchronous parallel generation led to a 30-40% reduction in sampling time compared to BHD (e.g., 0.54 seconds for Maze Nav vs. 0.95 seconds for BHD), while simultaneously providing superior planning performance. This makes CHD considerably more viable for real-time applications.

#### 4.4. Real-Robot Demonstration (Fetch Mobile Manipulator, Household Settings)

- **Task:** Organizing a cluttered dining table and preparing a meal, involving complex mobile and articulated manipulation tasks (e.g., "Serve burger," "Sponge in cabinet").
- **Results:** CHD achieved higher success rates in real-world scenarios, demonstrating practical viability. For instance, in "Serve burger," CHD had a 0.75 success rate compared to BHD's 0.60.
- **Significance:** CHD's joint planning of HL subgoals and LL actions through coupled diffusion resulted in significant gains in both planning robustness and downstream policy execution. It effectively handled hand occupancy constraints and logical action sequences, areas where auto-regressive models (VLM, Transformer) and even flat diffusion models often failed.

In summary, the main findings strongly support CHD's claims: its novel coupled hierarchical diffusion process, enabled by bidirectional feedback, parallel sampling, and segment-wise generation, leads to demonstrably better performance in terms of task success, trajectory efficiency, and computational speed across a range of challenging long-horizon robot planning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Coupled Hierarchical Diffusion (CHD) represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning and long-horizon planning, with substantial implications and potential impact.

**1. Enabling Robust and Scalable Long-Horizon Planning:** CHD directly addresses a core challenge in robot autonomy: effectively planning and executing complex tasks over extended horizons. By successfully coupling high-level subgoal generation with low-level trajectory execution within a unified diffusion framework, CHD overcomes the limitations of previous diffusion planners that struggled with accumulating errors and inconsistencies over long sequences. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in real-world scenarios that demand intricate, multi-step operations.

**2. Bridging the High-Level/Low-Level Planning Gap:** The paper's most impactful contribution is its novel solution to the pervasive "loose coupling" problem in hierarchical planning. By enabling bidirectional feedback, where low-level trajectory outcomes can dynamically refine high-level subgoals, CHD ensures a level of coherence and adaptability that was previously lacking. This tight integration guarantees that abstract plans are grounded in realistic low-level capabilities and can self-correct when faced with unforeseen circumstances or initial sub-optimal choices, leading to more robust and achievable plans.

**3. Enhanced Efficiency and Practicality:** The asynchronous parallel generation strategy is a critical innovation for practical application. By accelerating the inference (sampling) process by 30-40% compared to sequential hierarchical methods, CHD makes diffusion-based planning significantly more viable for real-time robotic control. Combined with the reduced complexity from segment-wise generation, CHD pushes diffusion models from impressive generative tools to efficient, deployable planners. This could lead to faster development cycles and more responsive robotic systems.

**4. Broad Applicability Across Task Domains:** The effectiveness of CHD demonstrated across diverse tasks—from continuous 2D maze navigation to discrete semantic task planning in complex 3D environments, and its successful deployment on a real mobile manipulator—underscores its generalizability. This suggests that CHD's principles could be applied to a wide array of robotic applications, including logistics, domestic service, industrial automation, and exploration, where long-horizon decision-making is paramount.

**5. Advancing the State-of-the-Art in Diffusion for Robotics:** This work solidifies the role of diffusion models as powerful generative planners in robotics. It moves beyond simply adapting diffusion models for trajectory generation by introducing sophisticated architectural designs that leverage their strengths (multi-modality, smoothness) while mitigating their weaknesses (scalability, coherence in hierarchy). CHD's theoretical insights into better approximating joint diffusion models and achieving higher optimality further contribute to the foundational understanding of generative models in control.

**Potential Impact:**
- **More Autonomous Robots:** CHD could enable robots to perform more complex, human-like tasks in unstructured environments without constant human intervention.
- **Improved Robot Reliability:** The robustness offered by coupled feedback makes robot plans less fragile to minor perturbations or initial planning errors.
- **Faster Development and Deployment:** The efficiency gains in training and inference could accelerate the development and testing of new robot skills and applications.
- **New Research Avenues:** The identified limitations (e.g., manual task segmentation, fixed subgoal lengths, reliance on learned primitives) provide clear directions for future research, such as developing adaptive hierarchical structures, integrating advanced perception, and ensuring safety in real-world scenarios. This work lays a strong foundation for future advancements in adaptive, intelligent, and scalable robot learning systems.
