# Quantum deep reinforcement learning for humanoid robot navigation task

> [!summary] Summary
> The Carnegie Mellon University research applies Quantum Deep Reinforcement Learning (QDRL) to humanoid robot navigation, integrating Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs) into the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm. This QuantumSAC approach achieved an 8% higher average return and required 92% fewer training steps compared to classical SAC in high-dimensional MuJoCo environments, demonstrating enhanced learning efficiency and significant parameter reduction.

## Problem

- Classical reinforcement learning (RL) faces limitations in complex, high-dimensional environments, such as humanoid robot navigation, including exponential parameter growth, training instability, and slow convergence.
- Many complex environments are characterized by stochastic and non-deterministic elements, introducing significant variance in classical RL training results and necessitating vast training episodes and computational resources.
- Prior quantum RL models primarily focused on simplified or specific tasks (e.g., wheeled robots, robotic arms, or only specific components), leaving a gap in applying QDRL to full humanoid robotics with their substantial observation and action spaces.

## Method

- Developed a hybrid quantum-classical ==Quantum Deep Reinforcement Learning (QDRL)== model by adapting the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm into a quantum-enhanced version, termed QuantumSAC.
- Implemented a hybrid policy network architecture for QuantumSAC that incorporates a 17-qubit data re-uploading Parameterized Quantum Circuit (PQC) to process high-dimensional state features and output action distributions.
- Evaluated QuantumSAC's performance against classical SAC in the MuJoCo Walker2d-v4 environment, a continuous control task, to compare learning efficiency, stability, and resource requirements.

## Results

- QuantumSAC achieved an **8%** higher average return (**246.40**) compared to classical SAC (**228.36**) in the MuJoCo Walker2d-v4 environment.
- The quantum implementation required **92%** fewer steps to reach its performance level and completed training in 169,000 episodes, while classical SAC needed 192,000 episodes, indicating accelerated learning.
- QuantumSAC demonstrated significant parameter efficiency, utilizing only 41 learnable parameters in its quantum circuit policy network, in contrast to 1,250 parameters for an equivalent classical network.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Quantum properties like superposition and entanglement can potentially reduce parameter space requirements and enhance convergence speed in complex reinforcement learning tasks compared to classical methods.
> - A hybrid quantum-classical setup utilizing parameterized quantum circuits can directly navigate high-dimensional state spaces, offering an alternative to traditional mapping and planning reliance in robotics.
> - Quantum-enhanced policies can achieve comparable or superior performance with significantly fewer learnable parameters and computational steps, attributed to the expressive power of quantum circuits and more efficient state space sampling.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Classical reinforcement learning (RL) methods have demonstrated capabilities across a range of tasks, but their application in complex, high-dimensional environments, such as those encountered in humanoid robot navigation, faces significant limitations. These limitations include the exponential growth in parameter requirements, challenges with training stability, and slow convergence speeds, particularly in stochastic and non-deterministic settings. The need for agents to process extensive sensory input and perform intricate motor control in such environments exacerbates these issues, often demanding a large number of training episodes and substantial computational resources.

To address these challenges, the field has seen increasing interest in integrating quantum computing with machine learning. Quantum Deep Reinforcement Learning (QDRL) has emerged as a hybrid approach that aims to leverage quantum properties like superposition, entanglement, and quantum interference to potentially reduce parameter space requirements and enhance convergence speed.

Prior research in quantum RL has explored various applications, primarily focusing on simplified or specific tasks. For instance, early work by Dong et al. (2012) explored quantum-inspired RL for mobile robot navigation, demonstrating its effectiveness but also highlighting the need for application in more varied and complex environments. Dragan et al. (2022) investigated hybrid quantum-classical models in stochastic environments like the frozen lake, showing improvements in performance and parameter reduction but primarily within simplified gym environments. Heimann et al. (2022, 2024) applied Quantum Deep Reinforcement Learning to wheeled robot navigation, achieving higher performance than classical methods in complex indoor environments. Samsani et al. (2021) utilized deep reinforcement learning for social robot navigation in crowded environments, focusing on safety rather than broader interactive behaviors. Acuto et al. (2022) applied a Variational Quantum Soft Actor-Critic approach to control a robotic arm, noting parameter reduction but acknowledging limitations in real-world applicability and the relatively lower observation and action spaces of robotic arms compared to humanoids. More recently, Sinha et al. (2025) introduced Nav-Q, a quantum-supported deep reinforcement learning algorithm for collision-free navigation of self-driving cars, implementing a hybrid quantum-classical critic component and a specialized data encoding strategy. This work, while addressing complex real-world challenges, differs in its specific application domain (self-driving cars) and the extent of quantum integration (only the critic component).

This current study by Lokossou et al. extends the application of QDRL to the more complex and high-dimensional domain of **humanoid robotics**. While previous quantum RL models focused on environments such as wheeled robots, robotic arms, or specific components (like the critic in Nav-Q), this work specifically targets humanoid robot navigation. The paper pioneers the application of a fully quantum Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) implementation to environments with substantial observation and action spaces, specifically MuJoCo’s Humanoid-v4 and Walker2d-v4. By directly navigating high-dimensional state spaces using parameterized quantum circuits in a hybrid quantum-classical setup, this research aims to bypass traditional mapping and planning reliance, addressing a gap in learning directly from environmental interactions in highly dynamic and unpredictable settings. The work contributes to understanding and applying quantum computing in tasks characterized by intricate dynamics and the need for dynamic balance and coordination.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The research is primarily motivated by the inherent limitations faced by classical reinforcement learning (RL) methods when applied to complex, high-dimensional environments, particularly in humanoid robotics. These limitations include:
- **Parameter Explosion:** Classical RL algorithms often require an extensive number of parameters to effectively learn in environments with large observation and action spaces, such as those found in humanoid robot navigation. This exponential growth in parameters leads to increased computational demands and challenges in training, stability, and convergence.
- **Stochasticity and Non-Determinism:** Many complex environments are characterized by stochastic and non-deterministic elements, which can introduce significant variance in training results for classical RL methods. This variability often necessitates a larger number of training episodes to achieve reliable performance, further increasing computational burden.

To address these challenges, the authors highlight the potential of quantum computing. Quantum computing offers inherent properties such as superposition, entanglement, and quantum interference, which could provide breakthroughs in processing power and efficiency. Quantum Deep Reinforcement Learning (QDRL) is presented as a promising hybrid approach that integrates quantum computing with deep RL to potentially reduce the parameter space and enhance the speed of convergence in complex tasks. Previous quantum RL models have shown promise in simplifying problem complexities and reducing training steps for navigation tasks in smaller environments.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this study are:
- **Pioneer QDRL Application to Humanoid Robotics:** To extend existing quantum RL techniques to the more complex domain of humanoid robotics, specifically in environments with substantial observation and action spaces, such as MuJoCo’s Humanoid-v4 and Walker2d-v4.
- **Efficient Learning of Complex Navigation Tasks:** To develop models that can efficiently learn complex navigation tasks in humanoid robots, encompassing not only walking but also optimizing walking speed while maintaining stability.
- **Direct Navigation of High-Dimensional State Spaces:** To explore a hybrid quantum-classical setup using parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) to directly navigate high-dimensional state spaces, thereby bypassing traditional mapping and planning methods.
- **Evaluate Quantum SAC Performance:** To conduct experiments comparing the performance of the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm in its classical form against its quantum implementation, with a focus on evaluating learning efficiency and computational resource requirements. This comparison specifically aims to demonstrate the accelerated learning potential of quantum computing in RL tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a hybrid quantum-classical approach to develop a Quantum Deep Reinforcement Learning (QDRL) model for humanoid robot navigation. The core methodology involved adapting the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm, a state-of-the-art classical deep RL technique, into a quantum-enhanced version (QuantumSAC) and evaluating its performance in a simulated environment.

**A. Learning Environment Setup:**
The primary testbed for this study was the **MuJoCo Walker2d-v4 gym environment**, provided by the OpenAI Gym toolkit. This environment simulates a bipedal walker, presenting a continuous control task with high-dimensional observation and action spaces.
- **Observation Space:** Comprises 17 continuous dimensions, including joint angles, velocities, and body orientation, providing detailed information about the robot's state.
- **Action Space:** Consists of 6 continuous actions, representing torque commands applied to the robot's joints.
The Walker2D environment was selected due to its complexity, reflecting the rich sensory information and wide range of control possibilities characteristic of humanoid agents, making it suitable for evaluating quantum-inspired approaches in high-dimensional continuous control tasks. The Humanoid-v4 environment was also mentioned as a target, though the Walker2D-v4 was primarily used due to current quantum hardware limitations regarding qubit count.

**B. Algorithm Choice: Soft Actor-Critic (SAC):**
The Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm was chosen for this comparative study. SAC is a prominent algorithm in classical Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) known for its robustness in addressing common challenges in DRL. Its selection was justified by:
- **Maximum Entropy Framework:** SAC incorporates a maximum entropy framework, which promotes efficient exploration in high-dimensional state and action spaces. This framework balances long-term rewards with policy entropy, providing a principled approach to navigate complex environments and reducing reliance on rigid hyperparameter tuning.
- **Architectural Design:** SAC utilizes multiple neural networks for the actor (policy), value estimation, and critics. The inclusion of twin critics and a target value function enhances stability and resilience, mitigating issues related to value function instability.
- **Probabilistic Policy:** SAC's probabilistic policy modeling introduces stochasticity into the decision-making process, facilitating effective exploration while maintaining a structured approach to policy optimization.

**C. Variational Quantum Soft Actor-Critic (QuantumSAC) Implementation:**
The core innovation lies in the implementation of QuantumSAC, which integrates parameterized quantum circuits (PQCs) into the SAC framework.
- **Parameterized Quantum Circuits (PQCs):** PQCs are hybrid quantum-classical models that utilize a sequence of quantum gates with adjustable parameters. These parameters are optimized classically during the learning process, similar to weights in neural networks, to achieve desired quantum state outputs.
- **Hybrid Quantum-Classical Policy Network Architecture:** The policy network of QuantumSAC was designed as a hybrid architecture (Figure 2, 3), incorporating:
 - **Input Layer:** Receives a 17-dimensional input vector representing the state features from the MuJoCo environment.
 - **Re-uploading PQC:** A quantum layer comprising 17 qubits. This layer repeatedly applies a parameterized quantum circuit on the input data, leveraging a data re-uploading Variational Quantum Circuit (VQC) approach. Data re-uploading encodes the input multiple times throughout the circuit, aiming to enhance the expressivity of the quantum circuit. The circuit processes the 17-dimensional input and produces measurements.
 - **Observables-Policy:** A classical sequential neural network layer that processes the outputs (measurements) from the quantum layer. This layer generates a 6-dimensional output vector, representing the means (μ) and standard deviations (σ) of the action distribution (corresponding to torque values for robot joints).
- **Objective Function:** The QuantumSAC algorithm follows the maximum-entropy reinforcement learning framework, optimizing both the expected return and policy entropy as described by the objective function:
 $J(\theta) = E_{S \sim D, \epsilon \sim N(0,1)} [ \min_{i=1,2} Q_{\phi_i} (S, \tilde{A}_{\theta}) - \alpha \log(\pi_{\theta} (\tilde{A}_{\theta}|S)) ]$
 where $\tilde{A}_{\theta}$ represents actions sampled using the reparameterization trick, and $\alpha$ is the temperature parameter controlling the importance of entropy.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** A key characteristic highlighted is the parameter efficiency of the QuantumSAC. The 2-layer data re-uploading quantum circuit requires significantly fewer learnable parameters (41) compared to an equivalent classical network (1,250 parameters), while achieving comparable performance. This reduction is considered particularly valuable for high-dimensional humanoid robot control.

**D. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Criteria:**
Experiments were conducted in the MuJoCo Walker2d-v4 environment to compare the efficiency and effectiveness of classical SAC against QuantumSAC. The performance metrics included:
- **Average Return:** Cumulative reward achieved by the agent over an episode.
- **Return Over Episode:** Reward obtained in each individual episode.
- **Average Return Over Episode:** Mean reward obtained over multiple episodes.
- **Speed of Running Over Episode:** Indicating the speed at which the agent completes tasks within an episode (steps per second).
- **Cumulative Steps Over Episode:** Total number of steps taken by the agent across episodes.
These metrics aimed to provide a comprehensive assessment of learning efficiency, stability, and overall performance, focusing on the speed at which high rewards are achieved and the efficiency of environmental exploration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of the Quantum Deep Reinforcement Learning (QDRL) approach, specifically the Quantum Soft Actor-Critic (QuantumSAC), against its classical counterpart in the MuJoCo Walker2d-v4 environment yielded several findings regarding performance and learning efficiency.

**Overall Performance Comparison:**
The study reported that the Quantum SAC achieved an 8% higher average return (246.40) than the classical SAC (228.36) while requiring 92% fewer steps to reach this performance level. This indicates an accelerated learning potential attributed to the quantum implementation.

**Return Over Episodes (Figure 4):**
Both classical and quantum SAC algorithms demonstrated an increase in return over time, indicating successful learning. However, QuantumSAC consistently exhibited a higher average return throughout the training period. It achieved peak performance faster and with fewer fluctuations in returns, suggesting a more stable and efficient learning process. QuantumSAC reached higher returns more quickly, demonstrating enhanced exploration and exploitation capabilities.

**Average Return Over Episodes (Figure 5):**
Analysis of the average return over episodes showed that the QuantumSAC algorithm achieved higher average returns and maintained these levels consistently over multiple episodes. QuantumSAC displayed a more rapid increase in average return early in the training process compared to classical SAC, reinforcing its efficiency in learning and adapting to the environment more quickly.

**Steps per Second over Average Return (Figure 6):**
A performance comparison in terms of steps per second (SPS) over average return revealed distinct operational characteristics:
- **Classical SAC:** Initially operated at approximately 1200 SPS, gradually decreasing to 300-400 SPS as training progressed. This high initial step rate corresponded to lower average returns, which improved over time, reaching a maximum of approximately 350.
- **Quantum SAC:** Maintained a consistently low SPS, close to 0, throughout the training process. Despite this low SPS, it achieved higher average returns more rapidly, peaking at approximately 250.
The interpretation provided for this discrepancy is that QuantumSAC achieved comparable or superior performance with significantly fewer *computational steps*. This is attributed to the properties of quantum computers, where quantum superposition may enable simultaneous exploration of multiple environmental states, leading to more comprehensive state space sampling. Quantum entanglement could facilitate more effective capture and processing of complex correlations within the environment. The expressive power of quantum circuits in representing policy and value functions was also suggested to contribute to a more nuanced and efficient modeling of environmental dynamics, resulting in a more stable learning curve for QuantumSAC with less fluctuation in both SPS and average return.

**Metric Comparison (Table I):**
A summary table comparing specific metrics further supported these findings:
- **Total Episodes:** QuantumSAC completed training in 169,000 episodes, whereas Classical SAC required 192,000 episodes.
- **Average Return:** QuantumSAC achieved an average return of 172.66, which was higher than Classical SAC's 135.07.
- **Maximum Return:** QuantumSAC achieved a maximum return of 716.71, exceeding Classical SAC's maximum of 500.52.
These results indicate that QuantumSAC, despite being trained over fewer episodes, consistently outperformed Classical SAC. The authors suggest this is due to quantum-inspired techniques enhancing its learning process, leading to more efficient and effective exploration. This includes advanced exploration methods like quantum probabilistic sampling, which potentially allows for the discovery of more optimal strategies and avoidance of local optima. The balance between exploration and exploitation in QuantumSAC and its robustness to noise were also noted as factors contributing to its superior performance and ability to search for global optima.

In summary, the QuantumSAC implementation demonstrated superior learning efficiency, faster convergence to higher returns, greater stability, and higher overall performance compared to the classical SAC, all while operating with significantly fewer learnable parameters.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by demonstrating the application and effectiveness of Quantum Deep Reinforcement Learning (QDRL) in a complex, high-dimensional domain. The study provides evidence that quantum-enhanced algorithms can address some of the persistent challenges faced by classical reinforcement learning in sophisticated tasks.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Extending QDRL to Humanoid Robotics:** This work is notable for pioneering the application of QDRL to humanoid robotics, specifically in environments with substantial observation and action spaces (MuJoCo’s Humanoid-v4 and Walker2d-v4). This marks a progression from previous quantum RL applications that largely focused on simpler robotic systems or partial quantum integration.
- **Enhanced Learning Efficiency and Stability:** The comparative analysis robustly indicates that the Quantum Soft Actor-Critic (QuantumSAC) achieves higher average returns, reaches peak performance more rapidly, and maintains greater stability in its learning process compared to the classical SAC. This suggests that quantum principles can lead to more efficient exploration and exploitation strategies.
- **Parameter Efficiency:** The implementation of QuantumSAC utilizing a 2-layer data re-uploading quantum circuit demonstrates significant parameter efficiency, requiring only 41 learnable parameters compared to 1,250 in an equivalent classical network. This reduction in parameters is crucial for managing computational complexity in high-dimensional control problems.
- **Reduced Computational Steps:** QuantumSAC achieved its superior performance with 92% fewer steps, highlighting a substantial reduction in the computational resources required to train effective policies. This addresses a critical limitation of classical RL methods in complex environments.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancements in Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** The demonstrated benefits of QDRL could accelerate the development and deployment of more capable and adaptive humanoid robots, self-driving cars, and other autonomous agents. More efficient training means faster iteration cycles and potentially more robust behaviors in complex, real-world scenarios.
- **Reduced Computational Burden:** The parameter efficiency and accelerated learning capabilities offered by quantum-enhanced methods could significantly reduce the computational resources and energy required for training complex AI models. This may make advanced AI solutions more accessible and sustainable.
- **New Avenues for AI Research:** This study encourages further exploration into the integration of quantum computing with other machine learning paradigms beyond reinforcement learning. It validates the potential for quantum computing to offer genuine advantages in specific AI tasks, opening doors for developing more powerful quantum-based AI technologies.
- **Guidance for Hybrid Algorithm Design:** The success of the hybrid quantum-classical architecture provides insights for designing future algorithms that optimally leverage the strengths of both classical and quantum computation.
- **Influence on Quantum Hardware Development:** The demonstrated benefits in complex tasks could further motivate investment and research into more powerful and stable quantum hardware capable of handling larger qubit counts and more complex quantum circuits, which would be essential for broader real-world applications of QDRL.
