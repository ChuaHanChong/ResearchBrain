# A Modular Vision-Language-Action Robotics Framework for Indoor Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at TCS Research developed a modular Vision-Language-Action (VLA) robotics framework for indoor environments. This system dynamically constructs a semantic voxel map from real-time sensor data, interprets natural language instructions using a VLM, and executes corresponding robotic tasks within time constraints.

## Problem

- Autonomous systems struggle to interpret high-level, ambiguous natural language instructions and translate them into concrete physical actions.
- Existing robotic approaches often rely on pre-existing 3D models or limited environmental memory, restricting their functionality in novel, unmapped real-world scenarios.
- The CMU VLA Challenge demands efficient integration of perception, language understanding, and action generation to navigate unknown indoor environments and answer diverse queries within a strict 10-minute time limit.

## Method

- A ROS-based modular framework integrates LiDAR-based 2D mapping and camera-based ==3D semantic voxel map== construction using **OwlViT** feature embeddings.
- A VLM-powered pipeline, utilizing **Gemini 2.0 Flash**, classifies user intent and generates ==context-aware prompts== by fusing natural language queries with real-time semantic map data.
- A ==state-driven execution flow== manages autonomous exploration, query handling, and action generation, including ==waypoint-based navigation== and dynamic path modification.

## Results

- The framework successfully responded to numerical, object reference, and instruction-following queries, correctly counting objects, locating items with 3D coordinates, and executing navigation paths.
- Performance optimizations reduced the exploration and mapping phase for a representative office scene by over **50%**, from **8 minutes 42 seconds** to **4 minutes 17 seconds**.
- The system demonstrated the ability to generate and execute navigation plans based on high-level commands, including dynamic path modification for avoidance instructions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic construction of a structured, persistent world model from real-time sensor data is essential for natural language understanding and action generation in previously unseen environments.
> - A VLM-driven pipeline, featuring query classification and context-aware prompt generation, effectively grounds abstract linguistic commands within the robot's geometric and semantic world model.
> - Targeted optimizations in navigation parameters and semantic map construction are crucial for meeting stringent time constraints in embodied AI challenges while maintaining functional integrity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous systems capable of understanding and acting upon high-level, natural language instructions from humans is a central objective in robotics and artificial intelligence. This capability necessitates the integration of perception, language understanding, and action generation within a single system. Traditional robotic approaches often encounter challenges with the ambiguity and variability inherent in human language, frequently relying on predefined commands or limited semantic understanding.

The CMU Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Challenge serves as a benchmark for evaluating systems in this domain. It tasks autonomous agents with navigating complex indoor environments and responding to diverse user queries in natural language. This challenge highlights the need for robust frameworks that can construct meaningful representations of the environment, interpret linguistic commands, and translate them into actionable robotic behaviors.

Prior work has explored various facets of this problem. Approaches like VLA-3D leverage pre-existing 3D models and structured scene graphs to generate descriptive language, demonstrating the utility of a detailed world model for spatial and semantic reasoning. However, such methods are often limited by their reliance on prior environmental data, making them less suitable for novel, unmapped real-world scenarios where an agent must build its understanding from sensor inputs. Another relevant framework, SORT3D, focuses on zero-shot 3D referential grounding by decomposing the task into instance-level semantic mapping, perception enhancement with 2D captions from VLMs, object filtering, and LLM-based spatial reasoning. While effective for object identification, SORT3D, in its benchmark evaluations, sometimes utilizes ground truth bounding boxes and instance segmentations, which may not always be available in fully autonomous, unknown environments.

This paper addresses the gap by focusing on a system that dynamically builds its own structured, persistent world model from real-time sensor data during exploration, thereby enabling natural language understanding and action generation in previously unseen indoor environments. It aims to develop a modular and robust framework that can systematically process linguistic commands, ground them within a rich, semantic environmental representation, and execute corresponding tasks, without depending on pre-generated 3D models or extensive manual intervention.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a modular and robust Vision-Language-Action (VLA) framework for autonomous agents operating in indoor environments. This framework is designed to systematically parse natural language instructions, ground these commands within a rich semantic representation of the environment, and execute the corresponding robotic tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and requirements in embodied AI:

- **Bridging Human Language and Robotic Action:** A core goal in robotics is to enable seamless interaction between humans and robots using natural language. This requires robots to interpret high-level, often ambiguous, linguistic commands and translate them into concrete physical actions.
- **Addressing the CMU VLA Challenge:** The CMU VLA Challenge serves as a specific benchmark, motivating the development of a system capable of navigating unknown indoor environments and responding to a variety of natural language queries within strict time constraints (10 minutes per task). This includes numerical questions (e.g., counting objects), object reference questions (e.g., locating specific items), and waypoint-based navigation instructions.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Memoryless Systems:** Initial approaches that relied solely on an agent's immediate camera feed for answering questions proved insufficient due to a lack of environmental memory and global spatial awareness. These reactive models could not answer questions about objects outside the field of view or reason about overall scene layout. This highlighted the necessity for a structured, persistent world model.
- **Real-Time, Dynamic Environment Understanding:** While frameworks like VLA-3D offer conceptual insights into using structured 3D data for language generation, they typically rely on pre-existing 3D models. A truly autonomous agent needs to construct its understanding of the environment solely from its own sensor data during exploration, without prior knowledge. This motivated the development of a system that dynamically builds a semantic map in real-time.
- **Computational Efficiency within Time Constraints:** The CMU VLA Challenge's 10-minute time limit necessitates an efficient system for exploration, mapping, reasoning, and navigation. This motivated optimizations in mapping speed and exploration strategies, as well as the selection of computationally efficient Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and fallback mechanisms to minimize latency.

The specific contributions outlined by the authors to achieve these objectives include:
1. **A semantic voxel map:** This map stores geometric information alongside rich feature embeddings for detected objects using the OwlViT model, constructed under a strict time limit.
2. **A novel VLM-driven pipeline:** This pipeline first classifies the user's intent and then generates a context-aware prompt by fusing the query with real-time semantic map data.
3. **A state-driven execution flow:** This manages autonomous exploration, query handling, and navigation, creating a complete instruction-to-action loop.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The system presented is implemented as a modular framework within the Robot Operating System (ROS), comprising interconnected nodes that automate the process from environmental perception to task execution. The overall architecture follows a parallel pipeline design for mapping and query processing, structured into four primary stages: Autonomous Exploration, Semantic Mapping, VLM-Powered Query Processing, and Action Generation.

**A. Autonomous Exploration and 2D Grid Mapping**
The system initiates exploration by building a 2D grid map from LiDAR sensor input. This map forms the basis for navigation. A planner node then executes a full-coverage exploration strategy. This involves:
- **Identifying Traversable Points:** The planner samples points across the grid, maintaining a minimum distance (initially 0.6m, later optimized to 1.25m) between them using a distance transform method to ensure safe clearance from obstacles.
- **Optimal Path Calculation:** These traversable points are treated as a network, and an efficient route is calculated to visit all points, minimizing travel time.
- **Waypoint Execution:** The generated sequence of waypoints is passed to a dedicated waypoint controller, which uses odometry data to drive the robot along the calculated path. The waypoint arrival threshold was optimized from 0.65m to 1.0m to reduce time spent on fine positioning.

**B. Semantic Voxel Map Construction**
Concurrent with the 2D navigation mapping, a 3D semantic voxel map is constructed. This module processes real-time camera feeds to detect objects and generate high-dimensional feature embeddings for them using the `google/owlvit-base-patch32` model. These embeddings, along with their corresponding 3D coordinates and scale, are iteratively integrated into the semantic voxel map. The map is updated progressively, providing an immediate, albeit potentially incomplete, representation of the world.
A critical aspect is the **time-constrained exploration**: the mapping phase is limited to 500 seconds. If the full-coverage plan is completed within this time, a "Complete Voxel Map" is finalized. If the time limit is exceeded, exploration halts, and the system proceeds with the "Partial Voxel Map" constructed up to that point. The voxel size was optimized to 0.15m (from an unspecified initial size) to accelerate map construction, balancing resolution with computational efficiency.

**C. VLM-Powered Query Processing**
After exploration and map construction, a central question handling module processes user queries:
- **Query Classification:** The user's natural language instruction is sent to a Vision-Language Model (VLM), specifically `Gemini 2.0 Flash`, with a carefully engineered prompt. The VLM classifies the instruction into one of three categories: Numerical, Object Reference, or Instruction-Following.
- **Object Grounding:** The VLM is used again to extract key object nouns from the instruction. A localization module loads the saved voxel map, computes a text embedding for the query noun, and identifies corresponding 3D points in the map by calculating the similarity between the query embedding and the feature embeddings stored in the voxel map.
- **Contextual Prompt Generation:** The retrieved information (object names, 3D coordinates, scale, and 2D grid map locations) is formatted into a detailed, context-rich prompt. This prompt grounds the abstract language query within the robot's geometric and semantic world model, preparing it for reasoning by the VLM.

**D. Post-Processing and Action Generation**
The final context-rich prompt is sent to the Gemini VLM for reasoning. The VLM's textual response is then parsed by a post-processing module, which triggers different actions based on the initial query classification:
- **Numerical Queries:** The module extracts the integer answer from the VLM's response and returns it.
- **Object Reference Queries:** The module parses grid coordinates from the VLM's answer, cross-references them with localized points to find the object’s global 3D coordinates and scale, and publishes a Marker in RViz to visually highlight the identified object.
- **Instruction-Following Queries:** This involves parsing a high-level plan from the VLM (e.g., `Goto(x,y)`, `Avoidbetween((x1,y1),(x2,y2))`). The `Avoidbetween` command dynamically modifies the 2D grid map by marking the specified region as non-traversable. A pathfinding algorithm then generates a path, which is converted to global coordinates and executed by the waypoint controller.

**E. System Optimizations**
To meet the 10-minute time limit of the CMU VLA Challenge, the system underwent optimizations to reduce the exploration and mapping phase from approximately 8 minutes and 42 seconds to 4 minutes and 17 seconds. These included:
- **Navigation and Planning:** Increasing waypoint spacing from 0.6m to 1.25m and relaxing the waypoint arrival threshold from 0.65m to 1.0m to generate shorter, more efficient paths and enable faster traversal.
- **Semantic Map Construction:** Increasing the voxel size to 0.15m to create a lower-resolution but computationally lighter voxel map, accelerating the process of adding and updating semantic features.

**F. Implementation Details**
The system uses ROS for modularity and PyTorch for deep learning components. Key models include:
- **OwlViT (`google/owlvit-base-patch32`):** Used for generating CLIP embeddings for object detection and text queries. Chosen over newer models like SigLip2 for its computational efficiency during time-constrained exploration.
- **Gemini (`gemini-2.0-flash`):** The primary VLM for high-level reasoning and classification. The 'Flash' variant was selected for its low latency, crucial for the time-critical application. Deepseek v3 is employed as a fallback API.
A Flask and Socket.IO-based web interface provides user interaction, allowing submission of natural language commands and displaying system logs and results.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates the development of a modular and integrated framework capable of addressing the CMU VLA Challenge by combining semantic mapping, natural language understanding, and autonomous action. The system successfully interprets and executes a diverse range of commands within simulated indoor environments.

Key findings and results include:

- **Effective Integration of Modalities:** The framework successfully integrates disparate components, including LiDAR-based 2D mapping, camera-based 3D semantic mapping with OwlViT embeddings, and VLM-driven language processing (Gemini), into a cohesive robotic system. This allows the autonomous agent to perceive its environment, understand natural language instructions, and execute corresponding actions.
- **Robust Semantic World Model:** The iterative construction of a semantic voxel map, incorporating geometric information and rich feature embeddings, enables the system to maintain a persistent and queryable representation of the environment. The time-constrained mapping approach ensures that the system can operate even if full exploration is not feasible within the given limits, by utilizing a partial map.
- **VLM-Powered Language Understanding:** The VLM-driven pipeline for query processing, which involves initial classification of user intent (Numerical, Object Reference, Instruction-Following) followed by context-aware prompt generation, proved effective. This approach grounds abstract linguistic queries in the geometric and semantic context of the robot's world model, leading to actionable outputs.
- **Successful Task Execution:** The system demonstrated the ability to respond to all three categories of challenge questions:
 - **Numerical Questions:** Correctly identified and counted objects in the environment (e.g., "how many chairs are there" -> "12").
 - **Object Reference Questions:** Accurately located specified objects and provided their 3D coordinates (e.g., "Find the potted plant on the file cabinet." -> "Object 'potted plant' is located at coordinates: [1.15, 1.26, 1.33]").
 - **Instruction-Following Queries:** Generated and executed navigation paths based on high-level commands, including path modification for avoidance instructions.
- **Significant Performance Optimizations:** Through targeted parameter adjustments in navigation, planning, and semantic map construction, the exploration and mapping phase for a representative office scene was reduced from approximately 8 minutes and 42 seconds to 4 minutes and 17 seconds. This 50% reduction in exploration time is critical for meeting the CMU VLA Challenge's strict 10-minute total task limit, allowing more time for reasoning and action execution.
- **Usable Interface and Fallback Mechanisms:** A web-based user interface facilitated interaction and demonstration. The inclusion of Deepseek v3 as a fallback API for Gemini enhanced system reliability by mitigating potential issues with the primary VLM service.

The successful demonstration of these capabilities in a simulated environment, without reliance on pre-existing 3D models, indicates the framework's capacity for autonomous operation in novel settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that makes contributions to the field of embodied AI and robotic autonomy. Its significance and potential impact can be understood in several dimensions:

- **Advancing Human-Robot Interaction:** The framework directly addresses the challenge of enabling robots to understand and execute commands given in natural human language. This capability is fundamental for enhancing human-robot collaboration in various settings, moving beyond predefined scripts or complex programming interfaces. It contributes to making robots more accessible and intuitive for end-users.
- **Robustness in Unstructured Environments:** By dynamically building a semantic map from real-time sensor data during exploration, the system does not rely on pre-existing 3D models or prior knowledge of the environment. This capability is important for deploying autonomous agents in novel, unknown, and potentially changing indoor settings, such as homes, offices, or industrial facilities.
- **Modular and Flexible Architecture:** The ROS-based modular design allows for independent development, testing, and easy interchangeability of components. This flexibility facilitates future research and development, enabling researchers to integrate newer perception models, language models, or navigation algorithms as they become available, without requiring a complete system overhaul.
- **Contribution to Embodied AI Benchmarking:** The successful application of this framework to the CMU VLA Challenge demonstrates a capable solution for complex, multimodal tasks. It provides a benchmark for future systems, highlighting effective strategies for integrating perception, language, and action within the constraints of such challenges.
- **Efficiency in Time-Critical Applications:** The explicit focus on time-constrained exploration and the significant optimizations achieved in mapping and navigation are crucial for real-world robotic applications where tasks must be completed within specific deadlines. The choice of low-latency VLMs further underscores this practicality.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations, such as reliance on online VLM APIs and assumptions of a static environment, provide clear directions for subsequent research. Addressing these limitations could lead to more resilient, real-time, and adaptive robotic systems capable of operating in dynamic, offline environments. Potential impacts include advancements in edge computing for VLMs, active perception, and continuous mapping for dynamic scene understanding.

In summary, this work provides a practical and integrated approach to a central problem in robotics, demonstrating how current advances in vision-language models and semantic mapping can be leveraged to enable more intelligent and interactive autonomous agents.
