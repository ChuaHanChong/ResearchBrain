# Enhancing Sketch Animation: Text-to-Video Diffusion Models with Temporal Consistency and Rigidity Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Indraprastha Institute of Information Technology Delhi developed a method for animating hand-drawn sketches from text prompts, integrating Length-Area regularization and As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) loss into text-to-video diffusion models. This approach improves sketch-to-video consistency to 0.8561 and text-to-video alignment to 0.1893, yielding smoother animations with better shape preservation compared to existing baselines.

## Problem

- Animating hand-drawn 2D sketches traditionally involves labor-intensive and skill-dependent processes like keyframe-based animation or rotoscoping.
- Recent learning-based methods for sketch animation often produce sequences lacking temporal consistency, resulting in jerky motions.
- These methods also frequently fail to preserve the original shape and topological integrity of sketches during deformation, leading to visual artifacts and distortions.

## Method

- The system uses a parametric representation of sketch strokes as cubic Bézier curves, enabling precise control over deformation.
- It extends a text-to-==video diffusion model== guided by ==Score Distillation Sampling== (==SDS==) loss to predict sketch motion based on text prompts.
- Two novel constraints are introduced: Length-Area (LA) regularization for temporal consistency and a differentiable As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) loss for shape preservation, both optimized via MLPs.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a sketch-to-video consistency score of **0.8561** (measured by CLIP), surpassing LiveSketch (**0.8287**) and VideoCrafter1 (**0.7064**).
- Qualitative evaluations demonstrated enhanced temporal coherence and better preservation of original sketch shapes (e.g., stable wine glass base, consistent squirrel body) compared to baseline methods.
- An ablation study confirmed that both Length-Area regularization and the ARAP loss individually contribute to improved temporal consistency and shape preservation, respectively, with their removal degrading animation quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit geometric regularization and rigidity constraints into text-to-video diffusion models can effectively resolve issues like temporal inconsistency and shape distortion in vector graphics animation.
> - Formulating the As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) loss as a differentiable function allows its direct optimization within a deep learning framework, enabling robust local rigidity preservation for sketch strokes.
> - Guiding motion through a text-to-video diffusion model while simultaneously enforcing geometric and rigidity priors on vector control points yields higher quality, more stable, and visually coherent sketch animations.
