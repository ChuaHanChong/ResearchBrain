# Enhancing Sketch Animation: Text-to-Video Diffusion Models with Temporal Consistency and Rigidity Constraints

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Indraprastha Institute of Information Technology Delhi developed a method for animating hand-drawn sketches from text prompts, integrating Length-Area regularization and As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) loss into text-to-video diffusion models. This approach improves sketch-to-video consistency to 0.8561 and text-to-video alignment to 0.1893, yielding smoother animations with better shape preservation compared to existing baselines.

## Problem

- Animating hand-drawn 2D sketches traditionally involves labor-intensive and skill-dependent processes like keyframe-based animation or rotoscoping.
- Recent learning-based methods for sketch animation often produce sequences lacking temporal consistency, resulting in jerky motions.
- These methods also frequently fail to preserve the original shape and topological integrity of sketches during deformation, leading to visual artifacts and distortions.

## Method

- The system uses a parametric representation of sketch strokes as cubic Bézier curves, enabling precise control over deformation.
- It extends a text-to-==video diffusion model== guided by ==Score Distillation Sampling== (==SDS==) loss to predict sketch motion based on text prompts.
- Two novel constraints are introduced: Length-Area (LA) regularization for temporal consistency and a differentiable As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) loss for shape preservation, both optimized via MLPs.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a sketch-to-video consistency score of **0.8561** (measured by CLIP), surpassing LiveSketch (**0.8287**) and VideoCrafter1 (**0.7064**).
- Qualitative evaluations demonstrated enhanced temporal coherence and better preservation of original sketch shapes (e.g., stable wine glass base, consistent squirrel body) compared to baseline methods.
- An ablation study confirmed that both Length-Area regularization and the ARAP loss individually contribute to improved temporal consistency and shape preservation, respectively, with their removal degrading animation quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating explicit geometric regularization and rigidity constraints into text-to-video diffusion models can effectively resolve issues like temporal inconsistency and shape distortion in vector graphics animation.
> - Formulating the As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) loss as a differentiable function allows its direct optimization within a deep learning framework, enabling robust local rigidity preservation for sketch strokes.
> - Guiding motion through a text-to-video diffusion model while simultaneously enforcing geometric and rigidity priors on vector control points yields higher quality, more stable, and visually coherent sketch animations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The animation of two-dimensional (2D) sketch illustrations constitutes a significant area within computer animation research, finding applications across video editing, entertainment, e-learning, and visual representation. Traditionally, animating hand-drawn sketches involves labor-intensive processes such as keyframe-based animation or rotoscoping, which demand substantial time, effort, and artistic skill. Video-driven animation methods often present limitations, being restricted to specific types of motion or requiring considerable manual input for tasks like defining control points or skeletons.

Recent advancements in deep learning have introduced methods that aim to automate or semi-automate sketch animation. Approaches like AnimationDrawing generate animations using pose mapping but are typically confined to biped motion. Other techniques, such as Su et al., animate sketches based on video input but still necessitate manual intervention. The emergence of learning-based approaches, exemplified by LiveSketch, represents a step towards reducing manual effort by leveraging a sketch and a text prompt to produce animated sketches. This particular method utilizes pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models and Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss to guide motion.

Despite these advancements, existing learning-based sketch animation methods often encounter challenges, specifically with maintaining temporal consistency across animated sequences and preserving the original shape and topology of the sketch during deformation. These issues can result in jerky motions, visual artifacts, and undesirable distortions of the sketch's identity.

Concurrently, the broader field of generative artificial intelligence has witnessed significant progress in text-to-video generation, particularly with diffusion models. These models have demonstrated an ability to synthesize video content from textual descriptions. However, applying these general video generation techniques directly to vectorized sketch inputs often struggles to preserve the unique characteristics of hand-drawn lines, such as their specific style, stroke integrity, and local rigidity.

This work by Rai and Sharma positions itself as an extension and refinement of recent learning-based sketch animation paradigms, specifically building upon LiveSketch. It aims to address the identified limitations of temporal consistency and shape preservation, which are critical for producing high-quality and visually coherent sketch animations, by integrating novel regularization and shape-preserving mechanisms within a text-to-video diffusion model framework. The paper seeks to improve the fidelity and stability of text-prompt-driven sketch animation, thereby pushing the capabilities of automated animation generation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an automated method for animating hand-drawn sketches based solely on a descriptive text prompt, requiring no manual user input. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations in existing sketch animation techniques:

1. **Complexity and Labor-Intensiveness of Traditional Animation:** Traditional methods for animating 2D sketch illustrations, such as keyframe-based approaches or rotoscoping, are recognized as tedious, cumbersome, time-consuming, and demanding of significant artistic skill. This constitutes a barrier for novice users and limits the efficiency of animation production.
2. **Limitations of Existing Automated Methods:** While recent deep learning-based approaches have aimed to reduce manual intervention, they still exhibit shortcomings.
 - **Specificity of Motion:** Some methods are restricted to animating specific types of motion, such as facial or biped animation, or rely on predefined motion trajectories or external video references that may not generalize well to diverse sketch content.
 - **Manual Input Requirements:** Even more advanced techniques often necessitate some form of manual input, such as defining control points, skeletons, or providing reference videos, which detracts from full automation.
3. **Challenges in Learning-Based Sketch-to-Video Animation:** Specifically, methods like LiveSketch, which utilize text prompts and pre-trained text-to-video diffusion models, have demonstrated promising initial results but suffer from critical issues:
 - **Lack of Temporal Consistency:** The generated animations often exhibit choppy or non-smooth motion, where the displacement of sketch strokes between consecutive frames is not adequately coherent. This leads to a visually unstable and unconvincing animation sequence.
 - **Shape Distortion and Topology Changes:** During the animation process, the shapes of the sketch strokes can deform unnaturally, leading to a loss of the original sketch's identity or topological integrity. This issue compromises the visual fidelity and recognition of the animated object.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out specific technical objectives:
- To leverage a parametric representation of sketch strokes, specifically cubic Bézier curves, for precise control over deformation.
- To utilize a pre-trained text-to-video diffusion model in conjunction with Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) loss to guide the motion of the sketch strokes according to a textual description.
- To introduce a novel **Length-Area (LA) regularization** framework designed to enhance temporal coherence by minimizing abrupt changes in stroke lengths and the swept area between consecutive frames, thereby ensuring smoother and more accurate motion.
- To implement a **shape-preserving As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) loss**, formulated as a differentiable function, to maintain the local rigidity and prevent topological changes or undesirable shape distortions of the sketch strokes during animation.
- To quantitatively and qualitatively demonstrate that the proposed method surpasses the performance of state-of-the-art techniques in terms of sketch-to-video consistency and text-to-video alignment.

These objectives collectively aim to produce high-quality, non-rigid, and smooth sketch deformations solely from text prompts, advancing the capabilities of automated sketch animation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology extends an existing framework for sketch animation guided by text descriptions, specifically building upon the principles of LiveSketch. The approach focuses on enhancing temporal consistency and shape preservation through the introduction of novel regularization and rigidity constraints.

**Sketch Representation:**
Each input sketch is composed of a set of strokes. These strokes are parametrically represented as cubic Bézier curves. A Bézier curve is defined by a set of control points. For a single frame, the set of control points is denoted as $B = \{p_i\}_{i=1}^{4k}$, where $p_i \in \mathbb{R}^2$ and $k$ is the total number of strokes. An animated sketch sequence over $n$ frames is represented by a set of moving control points $Z = \{B_i\}_{i=1}^{n}$, where $Z \in \mathbb{R}^{4k \times n \times 2}$.

**Core Animation Framework (Extended from LiveSketch):**
The system takes an initial sketch and a descriptive text prompt as input. A neural network architecture processes an initial set of control points, $Z_{\text{init}}$, and outputs corresponding displacements, $\Delta Z$, for each frame.
1. **Latent Space Projection:** Control points are initially projected into a higher-dimensional latent space using a shared mapping function, $g_{\text{shared}}: \mathbb{R}^2 \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^D$. This process enriches the point features with positional encoding.
2. **Motion Prediction Branches:** The projected features are then fed into two parallel branches:
 - **Local Motion Predictor ($M_l$):** Implemented as a multi-layer perceptron (MLP), this branch computes unconstrained local motion offsets for the control points.
 - **Global Motion Predictor ($M_g$):** This branch estimates global transformation matrices ($M_i$) for each frame, accounting for scaling, shear, rotation, and translation, thereby yielding global motion offsets.
3. **Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) Loss:** The overall optimization of the motion prediction blocks is guided by the SDS loss. This loss, calculated at every step of the diffusion generation process for all frames, ensures that the generated animation sequence aligns with the provided text prompt. The diffusion network predicts the positions of the sketch points, and the SDS loss guides the backpropagation to update the control points towards text-conditioned images.

**Novel Enhancements:**
The paper introduces two primary enhancements to address the limitations of temporal consistency and shape preservation:

1. **Length-Area (LA) Regularization:**
 - **Objective:** To maintain temporal coherence and produce smooth motion by minimizing abrupt changes in stroke lengths and reducing undesirable deformations between consecutive frames.
 - **Length Minimization:** The length of each Bézier curve stroke, $L = \int_0^1 |\dot{f}(u)| du$, is estimated. The regularization minimizes the absolute difference in stroke lengths between consecutive frames: $|L_{i+1} - L_i|$. This is computed relative to the initial frame's length to prevent error propagation.
 - **Area Minimization:** To further enhance temporal stability and reduce unwanted deformations, an area loss term is introduced. This term minimizes the surface area swept by a stroke between consecutive frames. The space-time Bézier surface, $f(u,t)$, is defined by time-varying control points $p_j(t) = M(t)p_{i,j} + \Delta p_{i,j}(t-t_i)/(t_{i+1}-t_i)$, where $M(t)$ interpolates transformation parameters. The swept area $A_i = \int_{t_i}^{t_{i+1}} \int_0^1 |\frac{\partial f}{\partial u} \times \frac{\partial f}{\partial t}| du dt$ is computed.
 - **Formulation:** The LA regularization is defined as $L_{\text{LA}} = \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (\lambda_l |L_{i+1} - L_i| + \lambda_a A_i)$.
 - **Optimization:** This loss is optimized using a multilayer perceptron (MLP). The MLP takes the control points updated after the LiveSketch SDS-based optimization as input and outputs further optimized control points. Hyperparameters $\lambda_l$ and $\lambda_a$ are set to 0.1 and $1e-5$, respectively.

2. **Shape-Preserving As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) Loss:**
 - **Objective:** To preserve the local rigidity of sketch strokes, maintain their original shape, and prevent topological changes or severe distortions during animation.
 - **Mesh Construction:** A global mesh structure is formed by applying Delaunay triangulation to the Bézier control points within each frame. The triangulation topology, denoted as $T$, remains fixed across all frames of the animation.
 - **Differentiable ARAP Formulation:** The standard ARAP loss is extended into a differentiable function, enabling its optimization through gradient-based methods and backpropagation within the network. The ARAP method works by minimizing distortion across the triangular mesh by optimizing local transformations (primarily rotations) for each triangle while maintaining global consistency.
 - **Loss Calculation:** The ARAP loss $L_{\text{ARAP}}$ is calculated by identifying all triangles in the mesh. For each edge $e$ in the initial sketch's triangulated mesh and its corresponding deformed edge $e'$ in subsequent frames, the loss minimizes the difference between the deformed edge and the product of an ARAP transformation matrix $D$ and the initial edge. The formula is $L_{\text{ARAP}} = \sum_{e \in T} \alpha_e \|e' - De\|^2$, where $\alpha_e$ is a weight proportional to the edge length.
 - **Optimization:** Similar to LA regularization, this loss is optimized via an MLP, which further refines the control points after the LiveSketch updates. The hyperparameter $\lambda_{\text{arap}}$ is set to 0.1.

**Implementation Details:**
The method utilizes a text-to-video diffusion model for pixel-space motion generation. The SDS loss guides the training for a specific timestep to derive updated control points. These control points are then further optimized by the MLP-based optimization procedures for the LA and ARAP losses. The MLP is trained for 1000 iterations of the LiveSketch model. A sequence of 24 animated sketches at 256x256 resolution is reported to take approximately 2 hours to generate.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents both quantitative and qualitative evaluations to assess the performance of the proposed method in enhancing sketch animation.

**Quantitative Evaluation:**
The method's performance was quantitatively compared against state-of-the-art baselines, VideoCrafter1 and LiveSketch. Evaluation metrics included:
- **Sketch-to-video consistency:** Measured using CLIP, which quantifies how well the animated sketch sequence maintains the visual identity and structure of the initial sketch.
- **Text-to-video alignment:** Measured using X-CLIP, which assesses how accurately the generated animation's motion and content correspond to the input text prompt.

Using 20 unique sketch samples and text prompts, the results were reported as follows:
- **VideoCrafter1:** Sketch-to-video consistency of 0.7064; Text-to-video alignment of 0.0876.
- **LiveSketch:** Sketch-to-video consistency of 0.8287; Text-to-video alignment of 0.1852.
- **Proposed Method (Ours):** Sketch-to-video consistency of 0.8561; Text-to-video alignment of 0.1893.

These results indicate that the proposed method achieved superior performance in sketch-to-video consistency compared to both baselines. In terms of text-to-video alignment, it demonstrated a slight improvement over LiveSketch and a more substantial improvement over VideoCrafter1.

**Qualitative Evaluation:**
Qualitative comparisons (Figures 4 and 5) highlighted improvements in temporal consistency and shape preservation:
- **Temporal Consistency:** Examples such as the "wine glass" and "squirrel" demonstrate that the proposed method produced more temporally consistent animations. The base of the wine glass and the squirrel's tail and body shape maintained stability and coherence across frames, whereas baselines like VideoCrafter1 and LiveSketch exhibited visible distortions or inconsistencies.
- **Shape Preservation:** The "surfer" and "squirrel" examples illustrated that the method effectively preserved the original shape and topology of the sketch during animation. The mesh-based rigidity loss contributed to smoother deformations without losing the characteristic features of the drawn objects, a notable improvement over baseline methods where shapes could become distorted. The animated sequences generated by the proposed method were described as producing high-quality, non-rigid, and smooth sketch deformations.

**Ablation Study:**
An ablation study was conducted to evaluate the individual contributions of the Length-Area (LA) regularization and the shape-preserving ARAP loss (Figure 6, Table II):
- **Without LA Regularization (W/o LA reg.):**
 - **Qualitative:** Animations exhibited a failure to maintain temporal consistency, characterized by rapid, uncontrolled movements and excessive variations in stroke length (e.g., the lizard's tail and legs).
 - **Quantitative:** Sketch-to-video consistency was 0.8306, and text-to-video alignment was 0.1864, both lower than the full proposed method.
- **Without Shape-Preserving ARAP (W/o Shape-preserving ARAP):**
 - **Qualitative:** Animations showed shape distortion, particularly when local motion was pronounced, indicating a failure to preserve the sketch's topology (e.g., the lizard's body distorting).
 - **Quantitative:** Sketch-to-video consistency was 0.8489, and text-to-video alignment was 0.1891. While text-to-video alignment was comparable, sketch-to-video consistency was slightly lower than the full method, reinforcing the ARAP loss's role in maintaining visual fidelity.

The ablation study confirmed that both the LA regularization and the ARAP loss contribute to the overall performance of the method, addressing temporal consistency and shape preservation, respectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of computer animation by providing an advanced method for animating hand-drawn sketches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized in several aspects:

1. **Enhanced Automation in Sketch Animation:** The method furthers the goal of fully automated sketch animation. By taking only a sketch input and a text prompt, it reduces the need for extensive manual input, artistic skill, and labor-intensive processes traditionally associated with 2D animation. This accessibility could democratize sketch animation for a broader range of users, including novices.
2. **Improved Animation Quality and Fidelity:** The introduction of Length-Area (LA) regularization and shape-preserving As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) loss directly addresses critical limitations of previous learning-based approaches, namely a lack of temporal consistency and shape distortion. The resulting animations are reported to be smoother, more temporally coherent, and better preserve the original topology and identity of the sketch, leading to higher visual quality and believability.
3. **Advancement in Generative Models for Vector Graphics:** The work demonstrates an effective integration of geometric and shape-preserving constraints within a text-to-video diffusion model framework. This approach could influence future research in applying generative AI to vector graphics, where precise control over shape, structure, and motion is crucial, unlike pixel-based image or video generation. The formulation of ARAP loss as a differentiable function for optimization via an MLP is a notable methodological contribution.
4. **Broader Applicability:** The enhanced capabilities of sketch animation offered by this method have potential implications across various domains:
 - **Entertainment Industry:** Streamlining the production of cartoons, animated characters, and visual effects by automating the animation of hand-drawn elements.
 - **E-learning and Education:** Facilitating the creation of dynamic, engaging visual explanations and tutorials using animated sketches to illustrate concepts more effectively.
 - **Video Editing and Content Creation:** Empowering content creators with a tool to quickly generate animated elements for videos, presentations, and interactive media.
 - **Visual Communication:** Providing a more efficient way to create compelling and clear visual narratives or representations from static drawings.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges certain limitations. The method's reliance on a pre-trained text-to-video prior can lead to artifacts if the prior struggles with specific motion types. More critically, the approach primarily focuses on single-object animations and demonstrates a decline in quality when dealing with multi-object scenarios, particularly when functional relationships exist between objects (e.g., a human and a bicycle). This can result in incorrect object separation and unnatural motion.

Future work outlined by the authors includes addressing these limitations by employing more advanced text-to-video priors for higher accuracy and implementing object-specific translations rather than relying solely on relative motion in multi-object contexts. Overcoming these challenges would further broaden the applicability and robustness of the method.
