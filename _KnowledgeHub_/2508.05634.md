# Towards Generalizable Safety in Crowd Navigation via Conformal Uncertainty Handling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of California, Riverside, developed a framework for robotic crowd navigation that uses adaptive conformal inference to quantify prediction uncertainty and integrates these estimates into a constrained reinforcement learning policy. This approach improves generalizable safety and robustness against out-of-distribution scenarios, reducing collisions by over 3.72 times and intrusions by 2.43 times compared to state-of-the-art baselines, and was successfully deployed on a physical robot.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning (RL) policies for crowd navigation exhibit performance degradation when confronted with out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.
- Overfitting during training and reliance on potentially inaccurate human trajectory predictions lead to safety risks and hinder generalization in diverse real-world crowds.
- Existing methods lacked a systematic approach to address prediction errors and integrate uncertainty quantification directly into RL decision-making for enhanced generalizability.

## Method

- A ==constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP)== framework is proposed, integrating ==adaptive conformal inference (ACI)== for real-time uncertainty quantification of human trajectory predictions.
- Uncertainty estimates are incorporated directly into the robot's observation space, feeding into an ==attention-based policy network==.
- A ==constrained reinforcement learning (CRL)== approach, using **PPO Lagrangian** with a physically meaningful, ==intrusion-based cost function==, guides robot behavior to maintain safety within dynamically sized uncertainty areas.

## Results

- The method achieved a **96.93%** success rate in-distribution, reducing collisions by over **3.72×** and intrusions by over **2.43×** compared to leading RL baselines, albeit with a trade-off in navigation efficiency.
- It demonstrated superior robustness in out-of-distribution scenarios, including varying human velocities, different human interaction policies (e.g., Social Force model), and group dynamics, maintaining high success rates (above **94%**) where other models showed significant degradation.
- The simulation-trained policy was successfully deployed on a physical robot **without fine-tuning**, exhibiting stable, safe navigation, dynamic visualization of uncertainty areas, and real-time computational efficiency in both sparse and dense crowd settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly quantifying and integrating prediction uncertainty into the observation space and learning process of an RL agent substantially enhances its generalizability and robustness in dynamic crowd navigation.
> - Constrained Reinforcement Learning with an intrusion-based cost function, informed by dynamic uncertainty estimates, provides fine-grained control over safety constraints, allowing for controllable trade-offs between safety and efficiency.
> - Adaptive Conformal Inference (ACI) effectively generates real-time, dynamically adjusted prediction uncertainty estimates, enabling the robot to adapt its behavior based on the reliability of human trajectory forecasts.
