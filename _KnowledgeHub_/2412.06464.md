# Gated Delta Networks: Improving Mamba2 with Delta Rule

> [!summary] Summary
> Gated Delta Networks introduce a "gated delta rule" that unifies Mamba2's adaptive decay with DeltaNet's targeted memory updates, leading to a more robust and flexible memory management mechanism for efficient sequence models. This approach consistently outperforms prior linear recurrent models across language modeling, retrieval, and long-context understanding benchmarks while maintaining hardware efficiency.

## Problem

- Mamba2's uniform decay mechanism (alpha_t) leads to inefficient information retention and "memory collisions" for tasks requiring long-term memory or precise retrieval of older, specific information.
- DeltaNet, while strong at selective updates, lacks a mechanism for rapidly clearing large chunks of irrelevant or outdated information, hindering performance in tasks with context switches or noise filtering.
- Existing efficient linear Transformers struggle to effectively manage complex memory for long-context understanding and precise retrieval while maintaining hardware efficiency.

## Method

- A novel ==gated delta rule== is introduced, unifying **Mamba2**'s adaptive decay (alpha_t) with **DeltaNet**'s targeted update (beta_t) to enable flexible control over memory: rapid clearance or precise, selective updates.
- A hardware-efficient ==chunkwise parallel training algorithm== is developed, extending previous work on DeltaNet to efficiently implement the new gated delta rule, preserving ==linear-time complexity==.
- Two hybrid architectures are proposed: **GatedDeltaNet-H1** combines Gated DeltaNet layers with ==Sliding Window Attention==, and **GatedDeltaNet-H2** stacks Mamba2, Gated DeltaNet, and SWA layers for multi-layered sequence modeling.

## Results

- Gated DeltaNet outperforms Mamba2 and DeltaNet across language modeling, common-sense reasoning, and both synthetic and real-world retrieval benchmarks, showing less S-NIAH degradation, superior pattern memorization, and stronger filtering via gating.
- The model achieves the lowest perplexity among recurrent models in length extrapolation up to **20K tokens** and demonstrates advantages in long-context understanding on the LongBench suite, particularly in retrieval and few-shot in-context learning.
- Gated DeltaNet maintains hardware efficiency, with training throughput comparable to DeltaNet, and its hybrid variants (GatedDeltaNet-H1/-H2) demonstrate strong throughput across various sequence lengths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The scalar gating term from Mamba2, enabling rapid memory clearance, and the delta rule from DeltaNet, facilitating precise, selective updates, are complementary for effective memory management in sequence models.
> - The proposed gated delta rule can be interpreted within an online learning framework, where the gating term acts as an adaptive weight decay in the SGD update for the fast weight matrix.
> - Hybrid architectures that combine the gated delta rule with other token mixing mechanisms, such as sliding window attention (SWA) or Mamba2 layers, can leverage distinct strengths for enhanced performance and efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the rapidly evolving landscape of efficient sequence modeling, particularly focusing on alternatives to the Transformer architecture for Large Language Models (LLMs). The standard Transformer, while powerful due to its attention mechanism, suffers from quadratic computational complexity with respect to sequence length, which limits its application to very long contexts and increases training and inference costs.

To address these limitations, a significant research thrust has emerged around **linear Transformers** and **recurrent neural networks (RNNs)** with linear state-space models (SSMs). These models aim to achieve linear or near-linear complexity by reformulating the attention mechanism as a linear recurrence or by leveraging state-space models. Early linear Transformers often underperformed standard Transformers, but recent innovations, particularly the introduction of **data-dependent gating mechanisms**, have significantly improved their capabilities.

Key advancements in this domain include:
- **Mamba and Mamba2 (Gu & Dao, 2023; Dao & Gu, 2024a)**: These models integrate selective state spaces with data-dependent gating, allowing the model to selectively propagate or forget information based on the input. Mamba2, specifically, uses a gated update rule (S_t = α_t S_{t-1} + v_t k_t^T) where α_t provides a uniform decay. While effective for language modeling, this uniform decay can be inefficient for precise memory management, especially in retrieval tasks where specific information needs to be retained or precisely updated.
- **Delta Networks (DeltaNet) (Schlag et al., 2021a; Yang et al., 2024b)**: In contrast, DeltaNet employs a "delta rule" that selectively updates memory by (softly) replacing an old key-value pair with the incoming one. This method has shown promise in synthetic associative recall tasks. However, its limitation lies in the lack of a mechanism for *rapidly clearing* outdated or irrelevant information across the entire memory, leading to moderate performance on some real-world tasks where context switching or filtering is crucial.

The paper identifies these two mechanisms—gating (as in Mamba2) and the delta rule (as in DeltaNet)—as complementary. Gating enables rapid memory erasure or decay, while the delta rule facilitates targeted, precise updates. The challenge is to combine these strengths into a unified architecture that maintains computational efficiency.

Furthermore, the paper situates itself within the broader context of **hybrid architectures** that combine different token mixing mechanisms, such as linear recurrences and sliding window attention (SWA). This approach aims to leverage the strengths of both: the global context modeling ability of linear RNNs and the local context understanding of attention, as seen in models like Griffin (De et al., 2024) and Samba (Ren et al., 2024).

The work also touches upon theoretical interpretations of these models through the lens of **online learning frameworks** and **fast weight programming**, connecting recurrent state updates to online optimization objectives like stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with adaptive weight decay. This theoretical perspective helps to understand the underlying principles governing memory management in these architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of existing efficient linear Transformers, specifically Mamba2 and DeltaNet, in effectively handling complex memory management for tasks requiring long-context understanding and precise retrieval.

The **key objectives** are:

1. **Overcome Memory Management Limitations**:
 - Mamba2, while incorporating data-dependent gating (α_t), applies a uniform decay to its state (S_t = α_t S_{t-1} + v_t k_t^T). This uniform decay can lead to "memory collisions" and inefficient information retention, especially for tasks requiring long-term memory or precise retrieval of older, specific information, where it struggles to distinguish between important and irrelevant historical data.
 - DeltaNet, with its delta rule (S_t = S_{t-1} (I - β_t k_t k_t^T) + β_t v_t k_t^T), excels at selective, associative updates, making it good for recalling specific key-value pairs. However, it lacks a mechanism for rapidly clearing or forgetting large chunks of irrelevant or outdated information, which is crucial for tasks involving context switches or filtering out noise.

2. **Propose a Unified Memory Control Mechanism**: The authors observe that the strengths of Mamba2's gating (rapid memory erasure) and DeltaNet's delta rule (targeted updates) are complementary. The core objective is to introduce a "gated delta rule" that combines both, enabling flexible memory control: rapid clearance when α_t → 0 and precise, selective updates when α_t → 1.

3. **Develop a Hardware-Efficient Training Algorithm**: Building on previous work that parallelized the delta rule, the authors aim to extend this efficiency to the new gated delta rule. This ensures that the proposed architecture, Gated DeltaNet, can be trained efficiently on modern GPUs, leveraging chunkwise parallelism and tensor core optimizations, which is critical for scaling to large models and datasets.

4. **Demonstrate Superior Performance Across Diverse Benchmarks**: The ultimate goal is to empirically validate that Gated DeltaNet consistently surpasses existing linear recurrent models (like Mamba2 and DeltaNet) across a comprehensive suite of benchmarks, including language modeling, common-sense reasoning, in-context retrieval (both synthetic and real-world), length extrapolation, and long-context understanding.

5. **Explore Hybrid Architectures**: Recognizing that pure linear recurrent models still have limitations (e.g., in modeling local shifts), another objective is to investigate hybrid architectures that combine Gated DeltaNet layers with other mechanisms like sliding window attention (SWA) or Mamba2 layers. This aims to achieve even higher performance and potentially better training efficiency by distributing the "memory burden" across complementary mechanisms.

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the performance gap between efficient linear models and Transformers by developing a more sophisticated and flexible memory management mechanism, all while maintaining and improving hardware efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research revolves around the introduction of the "gated delta rule" and the development of a hardware-efficient algorithm for its implementation.

#### 3.1. The Gated Delta Rule

The paper proposes a novel recurrent update rule for the state matrix $S_t$:
$S_t = S_{t-1} (\alpha_t (I - \beta_t k_t k_t^T)) + \beta_t v_t k_t^T$

Here:
- $\alpha_t \in (0, 1)$ is a data-dependent scalar gating term, akin to Mamba2's decay factor. When $\alpha_t \to 0$, it allows for rapid memory clearance, effectively "forgetting" the previous state.
- $\beta_t \in (0, 1)$ (or potentially (0, 2) for advanced state tracking) is the "writing strength" from the delta rule, controlling the degree to which a new key-value pair ($v_t k_t^T$) updates the memory and replaces existing associations. When $\alpha_t \to 1$, the rule effectively reverts to the pure delta rule.
- $I - \beta_t k_t k_t^T$ is a generalized Householder transition matrix, characteristic of the delta rule, which enables selective modification of existing key-value associations.

This formulation elegantly unifies the strengths of both Mamba2's global decay and DeltaNet's localized update.

#### 3.2. Online Learning Perspective

The authors provide a theoretical underpinning for the gated delta rule by framing it within an online learning objective, similar to the framework by Liu et al. (2024). They interpret the hidden state $S$ as a "fast weight matrix" that is updated via test-time stochastic gradient descent (SGD).
The gated delta rule can be viewed as optimizing the objective:
$\|S_t - \alpha_t S_{t-1}\|^2_F - 2\langle S_t k_t, \beta_t (v_t - \alpha_t S_{t-1} k_t)\rangle$

This formulation suggests that the $\alpha_t$ term acts as an adaptive weight decay in the SGD update for the fast weight matrix $S_t$, a common technique in deep learning for regularization. This perspective highlights how the model adaptively "forgets" previous information while learning new associations, making it more robust against state saturation and memory collisions.

#### 3.3. Hardware-Efficient Training Algorithm

A significant challenge for recurrent models is efficient training, which often suffers from the sequential nature of recurrence. The paper extends the chunkwise parallel training algorithm introduced by Yang et al. (2024b) for DeltaNet to incorporate the gating terms of Gated DeltaNet. This approach relies on the **WY representation** (Bischof & Loan, 1985) and **UT transform** (Joffrain et al., 2006) to express the cumulative products of the state transitions ($F_r^{[t]}$ and $G_r^{[t]}$ matrices, which are generalizations of $P_r^{[t]}$ and $H_r^{[t]}$ from DeltaNet) in a parallelizable matrix form.

The chunkwise algorithm partitions the input sequence into blocks. For each block, the output is computed based on the final state of the previous chunk and the query/key/value blocks of the current chunk. This allows for parallel computations within each chunk and efficient state passing between chunks. The resulting equations are rich in matrix multiplications (matmuls), which are highly optimized on modern GPUs (e.g., using tensor cores). This preserves linear-time complexity with respect to sequence length during training, crucial for handling long contexts.

#### 3.4. Architecture and Hybrid Models

- **Gated DeltaNet Token Mixer Block**: The basic Gated DeltaNet adopts a macro architecture similar to Llama, interleaving token mixer layers with SwiGLU MLP layers. Within each token mixer block, queries ($q$), keys ($k$), and values ($v$) are generated through linear projection, a short convolution, and SiLU activation. L2 normalization is applied to $q$ and $k$ for training stability. The gating parameters $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are generated via linear projections. The output is processed through normalization and gating before a final output projection.
- **Hybrid Architectures**: To further enhance performance, particularly for tasks benefiting from local context and global context, the authors propose two hybrid models:
 - **GatedDeltaNet-H1**: Combines Gated DeltaNet layers with Sliding Window Attention (SWA). SWA efficiently captures local dependencies within a fixed window.
 - **GatedDeltaNet-H2**: Stacks Mamba2 layers, Gated DeltaNet layers, and SWA layers. This creates a multi-layered approach to sequence modeling, aiming to leverage the distinct strengths of each mechanism.

All models are trained with 1.3B parameters on 100B tokens from the FineWeb-Edu dataset, using standard optimization practices (AdamW, cosine annealing learning rate schedule).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents a comprehensive empirical evaluation comparing Gated DeltaNet and its hybrid variants against a wide range of state-of-the-art architectures, including pure Transformers, various RNN-based models (RetNet, HGRN2, Mamba, Mamba2, DeltaNet), and other hybrid models (Samba).

#### 4.1. Memory Management Case Study (S-NIAH)

A key case study on the Single Needle-In-A-Haystack (S-NIAH) benchmark suite provides crucial insights into the memory management properties:
- **Decay Hurts Memory Retention (S-NIAH-1)**: DeltaNet shows near-perfect retention for pass-key retrieval. Mamba2 significantly degrades with longer sequences due to rapid uniform decay. Gated DeltaNet degrades less severely than Mamba2, demonstrating the benefits of the delta rule in retaining specific information.
- **Gating Facilitates Filtering (S-NIAH-2/3)**: In real-world contexts, DeltaNet's performance drops significantly at longer sequences due to memory collisions caused by a lack of efficient memory clearance. Mamba2 and Gated DeltaNet maintain better performance by using gating mechanisms to filter irrelevant information.
- **Delta Rule Helps Memorization (S-NIAH-3)**: For complex pattern memorization (UUIDs), Mamba2's performance rapidly drops, while Gated DeltaNet performs notably better, validating the delta rule's superior memorization capability.

These results directly support the paper's core hypothesis: gating and the delta rule are complementary for effective memory management.

#### 4.2. Language Modeling and Common-sense Reasoning

- **Superiority over Recurrent Baselines**: Gated DeltaNet consistently outperforms all other pure linear recurrent models (RetNet, HGRN2, Mamba, Mamba2, DeltaNet) in both language modeling perplexity (WikiText, LAMBADA) and zero-shot common-sense reasoning tasks (PIQA, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC, SIQA, BoolQ) at both 400M and 1.3B parameter scales. This indicates its robust general-purpose language understanding capabilities.
- **Hybrid Advantage**: The hybrid variants, Gated DeltaNet-H1 and Gated DeltaNet-H2, further enhance performance, often achieving the best results across the board.

#### 4.3. In-Context Retrieval

- **Pure Recurrent Models**: Among pure recurrent models, Gated DeltaNet outperforms both DeltaNet and Mamba2 on real-world recall-intensive tasks (SWDE, SQD, FDA, TQA, NQ, Drop). This confirms its improved real-world retrieval capabilities, despite the instruction-unaligned nature of the models leading to smaller performance margins compared to synthetic tasks.
- **Hybrid vs. Transformer**: Hybrid models (Gated DeltaNet-H1, Gated DeltaNet-H2, Samba) show compelling performance in retrieval tasks, often outperforming pure attention models like Transformer++ in many cases, highlighting the effectiveness of combining different token mixing mechanisms.

#### 4.4. Length Extrapolation and Long-Context Understanding

- **Length Extrapolation**: Gated DeltaNet achieves the lowest overall perplexity among RNN models when extrapolating to sequences up to 20K tokens across six long-context benchmarks (GovReport, QMSum, NarrativeQA, Qasper, CodeParrot, PG19). This suggests better memory management for very long sequences. Hybrid models further improve upon this.
- **Long Context Understanding (LongBench)**: On the LongBench suite of 14 tasks, Gated DeltaNet consistently shows advantages over other recurrent models, particularly in single-doc QA, few-shot in-context learning, and code-related tasks. This demonstrates its superior capabilities in retrieval, in-context learning, and state tracking for real-world long documents.

#### 4.5. Training Throughput

- **Efficiency**: The proposed gated delta rule introduces only marginal overhead. Gated DeltaNet achieves essentially the same training throughput as DeltaNet, although both are slightly slower than Mamba2 (by 2-3K tokens/sec) due to their more expressive transition matrices.
- **Hybrid Throughput**: Hybrid approaches combining SWA with other token mixers (Samba, Gated DeltaNet-H1, Gated DeltaNet-H2) demonstrate higher throughput than standalone mixers, particularly for shorter sequences (2K window). Gated DeltaNet-H1 shows strong throughput across all evaluated sequence lengths.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Gated DeltaNet represents a significant advancement in the field of efficient sequence modeling, particularly for Large Language Models (LLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Unified and Robust Memory Management**: The introduction of the "gated delta rule" is the most significant contribution. It successfully unifies two distinct, complementary memory mechanisms: the rapid, adaptive memory decay offered by gating (seen in Mamba2) and the precise, targeted memory updates of the delta rule (seen in DeltaNet). This unification addresses critical limitations of prior linear Transformers, offering a more robust and flexible approach to managing information over long sequences. This is crucial for real-world applications where varying degrees of forgetting and precise recall are simultaneously required.

2. **Improved Performance Across Tasks**: Gated DeltaNet consistently demonstrates superior performance across a broad spectrum of benchmarks, including general language modeling, common-sense reasoning, synthetic and real-world in-context retrieval, length extrapolation, and long-context understanding. This empirical validation underscores its general applicability and effectiveness, bridging the performance gap between purely recurrent models and attention-based Transformers, especially in long-context scenarios.

3. **Hardware-Efficient Training and Scalability**: By extending the chunkwise parallel algorithm, the authors ensure that Gated DeltaNet can be trained efficiently on modern hardware. This linear-time complexity during training and inference, combined with optimized GPU utilization (tensor core operations), makes it a practical and scalable alternative to quadratic-scaling Transformers for training larger models and handling longer sequences. The marginal throughput overhead compared to DeltaNet further emphasizes its practical viability.

4. **Foundation for Hybrid Architectures**: The success of the hybrid Gated DeltaNet models (GatedDeltaNet-H1 and -H2) highlights a promising direction for future research. By strategically combining the strengths of recurrent mechanisms (like the gated delta rule) with local attention, these models can achieve a synergistic effect, further boosting performance and efficiency. This suggests that the optimal LLM architecture might not be a single monolithic design but rather a judicious combination of different token mixers.

5. **Theoretical Understanding**: The interpretation of the gated delta rule through the lens of online learning and adaptive weight decay provides deeper theoretical insights into how these models learn and manage their internal states. This can inspire further theoretical work and lead to the development of even more sophisticated and interpretable recurrent architectures.

6. **Addressing Key LLM Limitations**: By improving in-context retrieval and long-context understanding, Gated DeltaNet directly addresses some of the most pressing challenges in LLM development. Its ability to manage memory efficiently is vital for applications requiring factual accuracy, complex reasoning, and understanding of extensive documents or conversations.

In conclusion, Gated DeltaNet pushes the boundaries of efficient sequence modeling by introducing a powerful and versatile memory update rule. Its demonstrated performance gains and hardware efficiency position it as a strong candidate for developing the next generation of scalable and capable large language models, contributing significantly to overcoming the computational bottlenecks of current Transformer-based systems.
