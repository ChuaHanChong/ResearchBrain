# When Does Self-supervision Improve Few-shot Learning?

> [!summary] Summary
> A systematic investigation into self-supervised learning (SSL) reveals its effectiveness in improving few-shot learning (FSL) performance, especially for challenging tasks and small datasets. The study also introduces a method for selecting domain-relevant unlabeled images to leverage generic data pools without suffering from negative domain shift.

## Problem

- Modern deep learning models require extensive labeled datasets, creating a challenge in domains where data collection or labeling is difficult or costly.
- Existing few-shot learning (FSL) methods, when trained on small base datasets, can overfit or discard semantic information critical for generalizing to novel classes.
- The benefits and applicability of self-supervised learning (SSL) in low-data few-shot settings and the impact of domain shift from external unlabeled data were not thoroughly understood.

## Method

- Integrates ==self-supervised learning (SSL)== as an auxiliary task with its loss combined with the supervised meta-learning loss to enhance feature representation in a shared backbone network.
- Employs two effective SSL tasks, ==Jigsaw Puzzle== and ==Rotation Prediction==, and evaluates their impact across various meta-learning frameworks and diverse fine-grained classification datasets.
- Develops a ==domain selection technique== using a binary logistic regression classifier and ==importance weighting== to select domain-similar unlabeled images from large, generic pools for SSL.

## Results

- SSL, particularly the jigsaw puzzle task, reduced the relative error rate of Prototypical Networks by **4%** to **27.8%** across seven diverse datasets, even when using only within-dataset images.
- Performance gains from SSL were considerably larger in scenarios with degraded inputs or significantly reduced base training set sizes, providing up to a **7.0%** accuracy improvement for specific tasks.
- The proposed domain-aware image selection method from generic pools achieved performance improvements over baselines, successfully mitigating the negative effects of domain shift and outperforming random selection.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised learning functions as an effective data-dependent regularizer that consistently improves few-shot learning performance, with gains amplified for harder tasks and smaller datasets.
> - The effectiveness of SSL is highly sensitive to the domain similarity between supervised and unlabeled data; out-of-domain unlabeled data can detrimentally impact performance.
> - SSL enhances supervised learning by refining feature representations for better generalization, rather than acting as a standalone solution or merely an initial feature extractor.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Modern machine learning algorithms, particularly deep neural networks, typically require extensive labeled datasets for effective training. This demand presents a significant challenge in domains where data collection or labeling is inherently difficult or costly, such as biology and medicine. Few-shot learning (FSL) emerged as a paradigm to address this issue, aiming to enable models to learn new concepts from a very limited number of labeled examples per class, akin to human learning from past experience. Existing FSL methods largely fall into categories such as optimization-based meta-learners (e.g., MAML) and distance-based classifiers (e.g., Prototypical Networks), which train a feature representation on a set of "base" classes with the intent of generalizing to "novel" classes.

However, training solely on base class classification, especially with small base datasets, can lead to representations that only encode features useful for distinguishing these specific classes, potentially discarding semantic information crucial for novel classes. Self-supervised learning (SSL) offers a complementary approach by learning statistical regularities within unlabeled data, thereby acquiring rich, generalizable feature representations without explicit human annotations. While SSL has demonstrated considerable success on large, generic unlabeled datasets (like internet imagery), its applicability and benefits in the context of small datasets and the specific challenges of few-shot learning have been less thoroughly investigated.

This work specifically investigates the utility of integrating SSL into few-shot learning frameworks. It positions SSL as a data-dependent regularizer that can augment standard supervised training, particularly when labeled data is scarce. The paper differentiates itself from previous SSL research, which primarily focused on large unlabeled datasets and often observed SSL representations lagging behind fully-supervised ones. Instead, this study explores a scenario where SSL can enhance supervised training in low-data regimes without necessarily relying on external datasets. The research builds upon prior work that also explored SSL for few-shot learning, notably Gidaris et al., but extends it by employing deeper network architectures, evaluating across a wider range of fine-grained classification tasks, and introducing a novel analysis of the impact of domain shifts in unlabeled data used for SSL. Furthermore, it proposes a practical method for selecting relevant unlabeled images from a generic pool to maximize SSL benefits. The study also relates to multi-task learning, where SSL tasks are treated as auxiliary objectives alongside the primary few-shot learning task, demonstrating a mutually beneficial interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct a systematic investigation into the role and effectiveness of self-supervised learning (SSL) in improving few-shot learning (FSL) performance. The authors are motivated by the observation that while current machine learning often requires large amounts of labeled data, real-world applications frequently encounter scenarios with limited labeled data.

The key motivations and specific questions addressed in this study are:

1. **Enhancing Few-Shot Generalization with Limited Data:** Current FSL approaches, when trained on small "base" datasets, might overfit to the base classes or discard semantic information not immediately relevant for base class discrimination but crucial for novel classes. The authors hypothesize that SSL, by learning statistical regularities without class labels, could recover this useful semantic information, prevent overfitting, and thus improve generalization to novel classes in the few-shot setting.
2. **Applicability to Small Datasets and Challenging Tasks:** Traditional SSL often assumes the availability of large unlabeled datasets. A central motivation is to determine if SSL can provide benefits even when applied to small datasets, utilizing only the images present within those datasets, or when the learning tasks are inherently more challenging (e.g., fine-grained classification, degraded image quality, extremely limited training examples).
3. **Impact of Domain Shift in Unlabeled Data:** For novel problem domains where abundant unlabeled data might not be readily available, it is common to consider leveraging generic external unlabeled image pools. This raises the critical question of whether additional unlabeled images always improve performance, and specifically, how the *domain similarity* between the images used for supervised learning (D_s) and those used for self-supervision (D_ss) affects generalization. The authors aim to systematically study the degree to which domain shifts between D_s and D_ss influence few-shot learning performance.
4. **Developing Domain-Aware Image Selection for SSL:** Based on the analysis of domain shift, a practical motivation is to develop a method that can automatically select appropriate unlabeled images for SSL from a large, generic pool. This addresses the challenge of leveraging external data effectively without negative impact, particularly for domains where specific unlabeled data is scarce. The goal is to provide a "powerful, general, and practical approach" for improving few-shot learning in such scenarios.

In summary, the research seeks to quantify and characterize the benefits of integrating SSL into FSL, especially in data-constrained environments, and to provide actionable insights and methods for leveraging unlabeled data effectively when domain shifts are a concern.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a framework that combines meta-learning for few-shot classification with auxiliary self-supervised learning tasks. This integration aims to improve the feature representation learned by a shared backbone network.

#### 3.1. Few-shot Learning Setup

- **Standard N-way K-shot Protocol:** The study adheres to the conventional few-shot learning setup, where a model is trained on a set of "base" classes (D_b) and then evaluated on "novel" classes (D_n) with very few examples (K-shot) per class for N-way classification.
- **Dataset Splits:** Classes within each dataset are divided into disjoint base, validation (val), and novel sets. The model is trained on base categories, validated on val categories, and tested on novel categories.
- **Datasets:** Experiments were conducted on diverse domains including mini-ImageNet, tiered-ImageNet, and five fine-grained datasets: Caltech-UCSD Birds, Stanford Cars, FGVC Aircrafts, Stanford Dogs, and Oxford Flowers. These fine-grained datasets are notably smaller than ImageNet variants.
- **Meta-learners:**
 - **Primary:** Prototypical Networks (ProtoNet), chosen for its strong baseline performance with deeper networks as per Chen et al.. ProtoNet performs episodic training, calculating class prototypes from a support set and classifying query examples based on distances to these prototypes.
 - **Other Meta-learners:** Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML) and a standard cross-entropy classifier (softmax) are also evaluated to demonstrate broader applicability.
- **Feature Backbone:** ResNet-18 is used as the shared feature backbone network across all meta-learners, following best practices for strong few-shot baselines.

#### 3.2. Self-Supervised Learning (SSL) Integration

- **Auxiliary Task Framework:** SSL is integrated as an auxiliary task, with its loss (L_ss) combined with the supervised meta-learning loss (L_s) to form a total loss: L = L_s + L_ss. This setup positions SSL as a data-dependent regularizer for the shared feature backbone.
- **Self-Supervised Tasks:** Two tasks, identified as effective in large-scale SSL comparisons, are employed:
 - **Jigsaw Puzzle Task:** Input images are tiled into 3x3 regions and randomly permuted. The network predicts the index of the permutation (reduced to one of 35 distinct permutations).
 - **Rotation Task:** Input images are rotated by angles from {0°, 90°, 180°, 270°}. The network predicts the angle of rotation.
- **Loss Functions:** Cross-entropy loss is used for both supervised and self-supervised prediction tasks.
- **Stochastic Sampling and Training:** When D_s = D_ss (SSL uses images from the labeled training dataset), the same batch is used for both losses. When D_s ≠ D_ss (SSL uses images from a different or additional domain), a separate batch is used for L_ss. The two losses are simply averaged for gradient updates.

#### 3.3. Experimental Scenarios Investigated

The methodology systematically explores three key aspects:

1. **Effectiveness of SSL within the Dataset (D_s = D_ss):**
 - SSL is applied using only the images from the base categories, acting as an internal regularizer.
 - Performance is evaluated across standard few-shot benchmarks and "harder" settings: degraded inputs (low-resolution, greyscale) and reduced training data (20% of base images).

2. **Analyzing the Effect of Domain Shift (D_ss ≠ D_s):**
 - **Scaling with Same-Domain Unlabeled Data:** Experiments vary the amount of unlabeled data used for SSL, drawing it from the *same domain* as the base classes (e.g., using remaining 80% of data from base categories as unlabeled for SSL when 20% is used for supervised training).
 - **Impact of Out-of-Domain Data:** A fraction of unlabeled images for SSL is progressively replaced with images from *other diverse datasets* to simulate increasing domain shift.

3. **Selecting Images for Self-supervision (D_ss from a Generic Pool):**
 - **Domain Selection Technique:** A binary logistic regression domain classifier is trained. This classifier distinguishes images from the target supervised domain (D_s) from images drawn from a large, generic pool of unlabeled images (D_p, e.g., Open Images V5, iNaturalist 2018).
 - **Importance Weighting:** Images from D_p are selected for SSL based on importance weights, defined as the ratio p(x ∈ D_s) / p(x ∈ D_p), aiming to pick images most similar to the target domain D_s.
 - **Comparison:** The performance with selected images is compared against baselines of no SSL, SSL with only within-dataset images, and SSL with randomly sampled images from the generic pool.

#### 3.4. Optimization and Implementation Details

- **Optimizer:** ADAM optimizer with a learning rate of 0.001.
- **Episodes:** 60,000 training episodes.
- **Evaluation:** Mean accuracy and 95% confidence intervals over 600 test episodes.
- **Data Augmentation:** Extensive augmentation, including cropping, flipping, and color jittering, is applied for both supervised and self-supervised tasks.
- **Batch Normalization:** For the jigsaw task, running mean and variance for batch normalization are not tracked but estimated per batch due to varied input statistics (full images vs. patches).
- **Loss Weighting:** A fixed trade-off term λ = 0.5 is typically used to combine L_s and L_ss, with some specific adjustments (e.g., λ = 0.3 for mini-/tiered-ImageNet with jigsaw).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the interaction between self-supervised learning (SSL) and few-shot learning (FSL):

1. **SSL Improves Few-Shot Learning, Even with Small Internal Datasets:**
 - Adding self-supervised tasks as auxiliary objectives significantly improved the performance of existing few-shot meta-learners. Specifically, the jigsaw puzzle task reduced the relative error rate of the Prototypical Network (ProtoNet) baseline by 4% to 27.8% across seven diverse datasets (mini-ImageNet, tiered-ImageNet, Birds, Cars, Aircrafts, Dogs, Flowers).
 - The rotation prediction task also yielded improvements on most datasets, though it was less effective for intrinsically symmetrical objects (e.g., flowers) or predominantly horizontal objects (e.g., aircrafts), suggesting task-specific efficacy.
 - Combining both jigsaw and rotation tasks could sometimes offer further benefits.
 - A critical finding is that these improvements were observed even when SSL was performed solely on images *within* the base classes, without relying on additional external unlabeled data.

2. **Benefits of SSL are Amplified for Harder Tasks and Smaller Datasets:**
 - The performance gains from SSL were more pronounced when the few-shot learning task was more challenging.
 - For tasks with degraded inputs (e.g., low-resolution images for Cars/Aircrafts or greyscale images for Birds/Dogs/Flowers), SSL provided higher relative improvements.
 - When the base training set size was substantially reduced (e.g., using only 20% of the original images), SSL delivered considerably larger gains. For instance, the 5-way 5-shot car classification accuracy improved by 7.0% with jigsaw puzzle loss when using 20% training data, compared to 0.7% with the original dataset size. This suggests SSL acts as a more effective regularizer in data-scarce scenarios.

3. **Generalizability Across Meta-Learners:**
 - The benefits of SSL were not exclusive to Prototypical Networks. When combined with other meta-learners like MAML and a standard cross-entropy (softmax) classifier, the jigsaw puzzle loss consistently improved their performance across most fine-grained datasets. ProtoNet combined with jigsaw loss generally achieved the best performance.

4. **SSL as a Regularizer, Not a Standalone Solution:**
 - Models trained *only* with self-supervision (without supervised labels) performed significantly worse than those trained with supervised labels, even from a simple cross-entropy loss. This indicates that SSL functions effectively as a feature regularizer that augments supervised learning, rather than replacing it for few-shot tasks.
 - Initializing a meta-learner with a purely SSL-trained model also did not offer improvements over random initialization, further supporting the regularizer role.

5. **Critical Impact of Domain Shift in Unlabeled Data:**
 - **Same-Domain Data Benefits:** Increasing the amount of unlabeled data for SSL from the *same domain* as the base classes led to improved performance, albeit with diminishing returns.
 - **Out-of-Domain Data Harms Performance:** Conversely, replacing a fraction of the unlabeled data for SSL with images from *different domains* progressively decreased the effectiveness of SSL. In several cases, training with out-of-domain images for SSL even resulted in *worse* performance than using only the limited in-domain labeled images for SSL, highlighting the detrimental effects of domain mismatch.

6. **Effective Image Selection from Generic Pools:**
 - Based on the domain shift analysis, the proposed method successfully selected similar-domain unlabeled images from a large, generic pool (Open Images V5, iNaturalist 2018) using a domain classifier and importance weights.
 - This domain-aware selection process led to significant performance improvements over baselines including "no SSL" and "SSL with 20% dataset" (using only in-domain labeled images). Crucially, it outperformed random selection from the generic pool, which often negatively impacted performance.
 - The selected images provided performance closer to an "oracle" scenario where additional unlabeled data was known to come from the exact same distribution.

7. **Saliency Map Visualization (Appendix):**
 - Saliency maps indicated that models trained with self-supervision tended to focus more on foreground object regions and less on potentially confounding background features, which could contribute to improved generalization, especially in fine-grained classification.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides substantial contributions to the fields of few-shot learning and self-supervised learning, with several significant implications:

1. **Enhanced Few-Shot Learning in Data-Constrained Domains:** The most direct impact is providing a robust method to improve few-shot learning performance, especially in domains characterized by inherently small datasets (e.g., medical imaging, rare species classification, specialized industrial vision). By demonstrating that SSL benefits increase with task difficulty and smaller training sets, the work offers a practical solution for scenarios where obtaining large, labeled datasets is prohibitive.

2. **Reduced Reliance on Large External Datasets for Initial Gains:** A key finding is that self-supervision can be effective even when utilizing only images *within* the small target dataset. This is significant because it allows practitioners to leverage SSL benefits without the immediate need for massive external unlabeled datasets, simplifying deployment and reducing computational overhead for initial improvements.

3. **Deeper Understanding of SSL's Role in Low-Data Regimes:** The systematic analysis clarifies that SSL acts primarily as a data-dependent regularizer in few-shot settings, rather than a replacement for supervised learning. This insight is crucial for guiding future research and practical applications of SSL in scenarios with limited supervised data, emphasizing its role in refining representations rather than initial feature extraction from scratch.

4. **Addressing the Domain Shift Challenge in Unlabeled Data:** The detailed study on the impact of domain shift between supervised and self-supervised data is a critical contribution. It explicitly quantifies how out-of-domain unlabeled data can harm performance, a previously underexplored aspect. This provides a clear warning for practitioners against indiscriminately adding generic unlabeled data for SSL.

5. **Practical Domain-Aware Image Selection:** The proposed method for automatically selecting relevant unlabeled images from a large, generic pool is highly significant. It offers a practical strategy for overcoming the challenge of limited domain-specific unlabeled data. By using a domain classifier to identify similar-domain images, the approach enables safe and effective utilization of broadly available unlabeled data, making SSL more robust and widely applicable in real-world scenarios.

6. **Guidance for Future Research in SSL and FSL:** The work opens several avenues for future investigation, such as exploring other cutting-edge SSL tasks (e.g., contrastive learning) within the few-shot framework and more deeply analyzing the complex interplay and generalization properties between different self-supervised and supervised objectives. The observation regarding saliency maps suggesting improved foreground focus also warrants further exploration into how SSL shapes feature representations to generalize better.

In essence, this research not only validates the utility of self-supervision for few-shot learning but also provides a nuanced understanding of *when* and *how* it is most effective, particularly addressing the practical constraints of real-world data scarcity and domain heterogeneity.
