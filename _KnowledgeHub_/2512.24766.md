# Dream2Flow: Bridging Video Generation and Open-World Manipulation with 3D Object Flow

> [!summary] Summary
> Dream2Flow introduces a framework that enables robots to perform diverse manipulation tasks in open-world settings by converting high-level language instructions into 3D object flow extracted from AI-generated videos. This method successfully guides robots to manipulate rigid, articulated, deformable, and granular objects in both simulation and real-world scenarios without task-specific training, outperforming alternative intermediate representations by achieving up to 8/10 success rates on tasks like "Put Bread in Bowl" and "Open Oven".

## Problem

- Bridging the "embodiment gap" to translate human-centric video content from generative AI models into robot-executable actions.
- Enabling robots to perform zero-shot, generalizable manipulation tasks on novel objects in unstructured environments.
- Developing a scalable method for task specification that moves beyond brittle programming or extensive demonstrations.

## Method

- **Dream2Flow** utilizes off-the-shelf ==image-to-video generation== models to synthesize videos depicting a task based on an initial RGB image and a language instruction.
- Extracts ==3D object flow== by estimating depth, segmenting the target object, tracking 2D points across video frames, and lifting them to 3D coordinates.
- Infers robot actions by formulating an optimization problem where the robot's objective is to follow the extracted 3D object flow, adapting the planning mechanism based on the task domain (e.g., ==particle-based dynamics==, rigid-grasp trajectory optimization, or RL rewards).

## Results

- Achieved successful manipulation across diverse objects and tasks in both simulation (e.g., Push-T) and real-world (e.g., Put Bread in Bowl, Open Oven, Sweep Pasta), with up to **8/10** success rates on real-world tasks.
- Outperformed alternative intermediate representations like AVDC and RIGVID, demonstrating superior robustness and smoother planned motions.
- 3D object flow successfully served as a reward function for reinforcement learning, enabling policies to learn embodiment-specific strategies that reproduce the desired object motion on different robot platforms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - 3D object flow serves as an effective, embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation that can guide robots to manipulate diverse object types (rigid, articulated, deformable, granular).
> - State-of-the-art video generation models can implicitly capture intuitive physics and common-sense priors, acting as a form of "visual planning" for robotic tasks.
> - Decoupling the desired object state changes ("what") from the specific robot actions ("how") allows for greater flexibility and generalizability in robotic manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Dream2Flow positions itself at a critical juncture in the fields of robotics, computer vision, and generative AI, addressing the challenge of **generalizable, open-world robotic manipulation**. The broader research landscape it contributes to includes several key trends:

- **Rise of Generative Models and Foundation Models in AI:** Recent years have seen an explosion in the capabilities of large-scale generative models, particularly in natural language processing (e.g., LLMs) and image/video generation (e.g., OpenAI's Sora, Google's Veo). These models can synthesize complex, realistic content based on high-level prompts. A major frontier in AI research is to "ground" these abstract generative capabilities into physical actions for embodied agents like robots. Dream2Flow directly tackles this by leveraging state-of-the-art video generation models.
- **The Embodiment Gap in Robotics:** A perennial challenge in robotics is the "embodiment gap." Most human demonstrations or internet videos, which are abundant sources of data, depict human interactions. Translating these human-centric actions into low-level robot control, considering different kinematics, dynamics, and morphologies, is non-trivial. Dream2Flow proposes a solution by extracting embodiment-agnostic information (3D object flow) rather than directly mimicking human movements.
- **Intermediate Representations for Robot Control:** Effective robotic control often requires bridging high-level task specifications (e.g., "open the oven") with low-level motor commands. Researchers have explored various intermediate representations, including symbolic plans, visual goals, affordance maps, 3D value maps, keypoint relations, and 2D/3D dense motion fields. Dream2Flow advocates for **3D object flow** as a robust, embodiment-agnostic intermediate representation, complementing other flow-based methods that have shown promise in manipulating deformable or articulated objects. This approach aligns with a growing trend of using "general flow as foundation affordance for scalable robot learning," as cited in related works.
- **Zero-Shot and Few-Shot Robotic Learning:** The ultimate goal for open-world robots is to perform novel tasks without extensive task-specific training or demonstrations. Large pre-trained models offer a pathway to achieve this "zero-shot" generalization by providing rich priors about the world. Dream2Flow's ability to operate in a zero-shot manner by directly converting language instructions into executable robot actions via generated videos is a significant contribution to this domain.
- **Learning from Videos for Robotics:** Video data, especially "in-the-wild" human videos, contains vast knowledge about object interactions and physical dynamics. Researchers are increasingly exploring how robots can learn from such videos, either through auxiliary training objectives, reward models, policies, or as simulators/world models. Dream2Flow utilizes video generation as a form of "visual planning" or "imagination" to generate desired object state changes, which can then be directly acted upon by a robot.

By proposing 3D object flow as a universal interface, Dream2Flow contributes a practical, effective, and scalable framework for integrating high-level generative intelligence with low-level robotic control, pushing the boundaries towards more autonomous and versatile robot systems that can interpret and act upon human intent in unstructured environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind Dream2Flow are driven by the recent advancements in generative AI and the persistent challenges in robotic manipulation:

**Key Objectives:**

1. **Bridge Video Generation and Robotic Control:** The primary objective is to create a seamless framework that effectively translates the high-level visual understanding and plausible physical interactions synthesized by state-of-the-art video generation models into concrete, low-level actions executable by a robotic system.
2. **Enable Zero-Shot Open-World Manipulation:** To allow robots to perform novel, diverse manipulation tasks in unstructured, unseen environments, using only an initial visual observation (RGB-D) and a language instruction, without requiring any task-specific demonstrations or training data.
3. **Propose a General, Embodiment-Agnostic Interface:** To identify and validate an intermediate representation that can effectively distill actionable information from human-centric video predictions for various types of objects (rigid, articulated, deformable, granular) and across different robot embodiments. The paper proposes **3D object flow** for this purpose.
4. **Decouple "What" from "How":** To separate the desired state changes and object motions in the environment ("what needs to happen") from the specific low-level actions a robot takes to achieve those changes ("how a particular embodiment realizes it" given its constraints). This allows for greater flexibility and generalizability.

**Motivation:**

1. **Exploiting Generative Video Models' Capabilities:** Recent video generation models have shown remarkable ability to synthesize long, high-fidelity clips of physical interactions from text prompts and initial images. These models implicitly capture intuitive physics, common-sense priors about object properties, and plausible interaction dynamics, making them a "compelling tool to zero-shot reason about plausible physical interactions."
2. **Overcoming the Embodiment Gap:** While generative video models are powerful, they often produce videos showing human interactions, as human activity data is far more abundant. Directly translating these human motions to robots is challenging due to the "embodiment gap" (differences in kinematics, dynamics, and action spaces between humans and robots). A key motivation is to extract *actionable signals* from these visual predictions that a robot can then enact, rather than directly mimicking human movements.
3. **Scaling Task Specification:** Traditional robotic task specification methods (e.g., symbolic programming, extensive demonstrations) are often brittle or data-intensive. Leveraging large, pre-trained video models allows for "open-ended task instruction" through natural language, providing a highly scalable mechanism for specifying tasks.
4. **Handling Diverse Object Categories:** Many robotic manipulation tasks involve objects with varied physical properties (e.g., rigid blocks, articulated doors, deformable cloths, granular materials). The motivation is to find a unified representation that naturally captures the necessary motion for all these categories without specialized modules for each. 3D object flow, being point-based, offers this flexibility.
5. **Efficiency and Autonomy:** The aspiration is to develop an autonomous pipeline that directly goes from visual perception and language to robot action, significantly reducing the manual effort and expertise traditionally required to program or train robots for new tasks.

In essence, Dream2Flow is motivated by the desire to unlock the vast potential of generative AI for practical, general-purpose robotics, enabling robots to "imagine" solutions to novel problems and execute them in the real world with minimal human intervention.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Dream2Flow proposes a novel framework that integrates off-the-shelf vision and video generation foundation models to facilitate zero-shot, open-world robotic manipulation. Its methodology can be broken down into three main stages: Video Generation, 3D Object Flow Extraction, and Action Inference with 3D Object Flow.

**A. Problem Formulation**
Given an initial RGB-D observation (I₀, D₀), a camera projection Π, and a task instruction ℓ, the goal is to output an action sequence u₀:H-1 that accomplishes the task by following an object motion inferred from a generated video. The action space U is general, accommodating various robot control schemes.

**B. Core Pipeline - Separating "What" from "How"**

1. **Video Generation:**
 - **Input:** An initial RGB image of the workspace (without the robot visible, I₀) and a natural language task instruction (ℓ).
 - **Model:** An off-the-shelf image-to-video generation model (e.g., Veo 3, Wan2.1, Kling 2.1) is used to synthesize an RGB video {V_t} depicting the task being performed.
 - **Rationale:** The authors empirically found that current video models produce more physically plausible interactions when the robot is not in the initial frame and not mentioned in the prompt, as they are primarily trained on human-centric data. Prompts are carefully constructed (e.g., "by one hand," "camera holds a still pose") to optimize video quality and consistency with downstream processing. For some tasks (e.g., Push-T), a goal image can also be provided as part of the prompt.

2. **3D Object Flow Extraction:**
 - **Video Depth Estimation:** SpatialTrackerV2 is employed to estimate per-frame depth {Z̃_t} from the generated video. To address the scale-shift ambiguity of monocular depth, the estimated depth of the first frame is aligned to the initial real-world depth D₀ obtained from the robot's sensor to compute a global scale and bias (s*, b*), yielding calibrated depths {Z_t}.
 - **Object Localization and Masking:** Grounding DINO generates a bounding box for the task-relevant object based on (I₀, ℓ). This bounding box then prompts SAM 2 to produce a precise binary mask of the object in the initial frame.
 - **2D Point Tracking:** From the masked region in the first frame, 'n' pixels are sampled. CoTracker3 is then used to track these sampled points across all frames of the generated video, producing 2D trajectories (c^i_t) and visibility flags (v^i_t).
 - **3D Lifting:** The visible 2D points are lifted to 3D using the calibrated depths ({Z_t}) and known camera intrinsics/extrinsics, transforming them into the robot's coordinate frame. This results in the **3D object flow** (P_1:T ∈ R^(T × n × 3)), which represents the desired 3D trajectories of points on the task-relevant object. For articulated objects, a heuristic based on point motion and SAM 2 masks is used to filter points belonging to the movable part. For grasp selection, AnyGrasp proposes candidates, and HaMer (for thumb detection in the video) helps select a grasp near the intended interaction point.

3. **Action Inference with 3D Object Flow:**
 - **Objective:** The robot's goal is to manipulate the object to closely follow the reconstructed 3D object flow. This is formulated as an optimization problem:
 ```
        min_{{u_t ∈ U}} Σ_{t=0}^{H-1} [λ_task ||x̂_obj_t - P̃_t||₂² + λ_control(x̂_t, u_t)]
        s.t. x̂_{t+1} = f(x̂_t, u_t), x̂_0 = x_0
        ```
 where x̂_obj_t are the predicted object points, P̃_t is the time-aligned target 3D object flow from the video, and λ_task / λ_control are weights for task and control costs.
 - **Dynamics Models and Planning:** Dream2Flow adapts its action inference mechanism based on the task domain:
 - **Simulated Push-T Domain (Non-prehensile):** Uses a learned **particle-based dynamics model** (based on Point Transformer V3) that predicts the delta positions of all scene particles given the current state and a push skill primitive. Planning is done via random-shooting, selecting the push parameters that minimize the cost relative to an intermediate subgoal from the 3D object flow.
 - **Real-World Domain (Prehensile/Rigid Grasp):** Assumes a rigid-grasp dynamics model. Once an object is grasped, the action space is defined by end-effector poses. Trajectory optimization is performed using PyRoki to minimize the objective, incorporating pose smoothness, reachability, and manipulability costs (λ_control) in addition to the 3D object flow tracking cost (λ_task).
 - **Simulated Door Opening Domain (Reinforcement Learning):** For tasks where learning a policy is suitable, SAC (Soft Actor-Critic) is used to train sensorimotor policies. The 3D object flow is directly incorporated into the reward function, effectively teaching the robot to reproduce the generated object motion. This compiles the optimization process into a parametric policy.

The overall approach is a sophisticated orchestration of various foundation models for vision and generation, culminating in an object-centric 3D flow representation that guides robotic action through either direct optimization or reinforcement learning, all in a zero-shot manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Dream2Flow demonstrates significant capabilities and robustness across a variety of manipulation tasks in both simulation and the real world. The key findings and results are as follows:

1. **Effectiveness of 3D Object Flow as an Interface:**
 - Dream2Flow successfully performs diverse manipulation tasks, including "Push-T" (simulated), "Put Bread in Bowl," "Open Oven," and "Cover Bowl" (real-world).
 - It handles objects of varying physical properties: **rigid** (bread, T-block, can), **articulated** (oven, door, drawer, chair), **deformable** (scarf), and **granular** (pasta). This versatility is a major strength, as 3D object flow naturally captures the necessary motion for diverse object types.
 - Further "in-the-wild" real-world tasks like "Pull Out Chair," "Open Drawer," "Sweep Pasta," and "Recycle Can" highlight its applicability in more complex, unstructured settings (Fig. 1, Fig. 11).

2. **Superiority over Alternative Intermediate Representations:**
 - Comparative experiments against AVDC and RIGVID (which rely on dense optical flow or rigid transform estimations) show Dream2Flow's superior performance (Table I).
 - Dream2Flow achieves 8/10 success rates for "Bread in Bowl" and "Open Oven" tasks, significantly outperforming AVDC (7/10, 0/10) and RIGVID (6/10, 6/10). For "Cover Bowl," Dream2Flow achieves 3/10, still better than AVDC (2/10) and RIGVID (1/10), reflecting the inherent difficulty of manipulating deformable objects.
 - The authors attribute this to 3D object flow being less affected by noisy transform estimates and more robust when most points are occluded, allowing smoother planned motions. It's also better suited for deformable objects, which lack rigid transformations.

3. **Robustness and Generalization:**
 - **Object Instances, Backgrounds, Viewing Angles:** Dream2Flow exhibits robustness to variations in object instances (e.g., different types of bread), backgrounds, and camera viewing angles, maintaining performance with only slight drops in challenging scenarios (Fig. 4). This suggests it inherits generalization capabilities from the underlying vision and video generation foundation models.
 - **Multiple Tasks in the Same Scene:** The system can perform different manipulation tasks within the exact same physical scene simply by altering the language instruction, demonstrating its ability to adapt object-flow targets based on high-level goals (Fig. 5).

4. **3D Object Flow as an Effective Reward for Reinforcement Learning:**
 - When used as a reward function in an RL setting (Open Door task), policies trained with 3D object flow rewards (using SAC) achieve comparable performance to those trained with handcrafted object state rewards across different robot embodiments (Franka Panda, Boston Dynamics Spot, GR1 humanoid arm) (Table II).
 - Crucially, these policies learn diverse, embodiment-specific strategies to accomplish the same object motion, such as the Spot using its base for better reachability or the GR1 leveraging its palm for stability (Fig. 6). This highlights 3D object flow's power as an abstract, embodiment-agnostic goal signal.

5. **Impact of Video Generation Model Choice:**
 - The choice of the upstream video generation model significantly impacts Dream2Flow's performance (Table III). Veo 3 performed best on real-world tasks (8/10 for Open Oven), while Wan2.1 was more effective in the simulated Push-T task (52/100, though with significant challenges). Kling 2.1 generally performed worse.
 - Common failure modes from video generation include "object morphing" (e.g., bread turning into crackers) and "hallucination" (e.g., new objects appearing), which severely disrupt downstream tracking and execution (Fig. 12). Excessive camera motion in generated videos also causes issues for depth estimation.

6. **Importance of Dynamics Model Choice:**
 - For planning in the Push-T task, a learned **particle-based dynamics model** (52/100 success) substantially outperformed models that predict only block pose (12/100) or use simple heuristics (17/100) (Table IV). This finding underscores the necessity of precise, per-point predictions for capturing complex object dynamics, especially for tasks involving rotation and intricate interactions.

7. **Failure Analysis:**
 - A breakdown of failures in real-robot experiments (Fig. 7) reveals the main bottlenecks: video generation artifacts (morphing, hallucination - 12 failures), flow extraction errors (due to severe rotations or occlusions leading to tracking dropouts - 4 failures), and robot execution failures (e.g., incorrect grasp selection, insufficient motion - 4 failures). These pinpoint clear areas for future improvement.

In summary, the findings robustly support 3D object flow as a powerful and generalizable interface for translating high-level, language-conditioned video predictions into effective robot actions across diverse manipulation scenarios and object types, all without task-specific training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Dream2Flow represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in its approach to open-world, zero-shot task completion. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

1. **Democratizing Robotic Capabilities:** By enabling robots to understand and perform novel tasks directly from natural language instructions and visual observations, Dream2Flow lowers the barrier to entry for deploying robots in new scenarios. It reduces the need for extensive, costly, and time-consuming human demonstrations or expert programming, potentially accelerating the adoption of robotics in a wider range of applications, from domestic assistance to industrial automation.

2. **Generalizable Robotic Manipulation:** The ability to handle rigid, articulated, deformable, and granular objects within a unified framework is a major leap. Most robotic systems are specialized for specific object types or tasks. Dream2Flow's generalization capability, derived from rich priors encoded in foundation models and the flexibility of 3D object flow, moves closer to the vision of general-purpose robots capable of interacting with the complexity of the real world.

3. **A Scalable Paradigm for Task Specification:** Language-conditioned video generation offers a highly intuitive and scalable way to specify tasks. Instead of coding complex policies or providing numerous demonstrations, a user can simply describe a goal. Dream2Flow provides a concrete mechanism to convert this high-level intent into actionable robot commands, transforming how humans interact with and instruct robots.

4. **Bridging the AI Perception-Action Gap:** Dream2Flow offers a compelling solution to the long-standing "embodiment gap." It demonstrates that the impressive generative capabilities of AI models can be effectively "grounded" into the physical world of robotics. By extracting embodiment-agnostic 3D object flow, it elegantly separates the abstract "what" (desired state change) from the robot-specific "how" (kinematic and dynamic execution), allowing robots to leverage high-level reasoning without being constrained by their physical form.

5. **Unlocking the Value of "In-the-Wild" Data:** The work highlights how pre-trained vision and video generation models, largely trained on internet-scale "in-the-wild" data, can directly endow robots with a vast repertoire of intuitive physics and interaction knowledge. This approach bypasses the traditional bottleneck of collecting robot-specific datasets for every new task.

6. **Advancing Foundation Models in Robotics:** Dream2Flow is a prime example of how foundation models can be synergistically combined for complex robotic tasks. It provides a blueprint for integrating advanced capabilities in video generation, depth estimation, object segmentation, and point tracking into an end-to-end robotic system, further cementing the role of these powerful general models in robotics research.

7. **Clear Directions for Future Research:** The detailed failure analysis is itself a significant contribution, providing concrete avenues for future work. Improving robustness in video generation (reducing morphing, hallucination, camera motion), enhancing 3D tracking under severe occlusion, and developing more sophisticated grasp and manipulation planning for complex objects are crucial next steps that Dream2Flow clearly identifies. Addressing the computational latency (3-11 minutes for flow extraction) is also vital for practical deployment.

In conclusion, Dream2Flow is more than just a new method; it proposes a new conceptual bridge between the rapidly advancing fields of generative AI and practical robotics. Its emphasis on 3D object flow as a universal interface could profoundly influence how robots learn, generalize, and interact with the complex, open-ended tasks of our world.
