# Oryx: a Scalable Sequence Model for Many-Agent Coordination in Offline MARL

> [!summary] Summary
> Oryx presents a scalable sequence model for offline Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), leveraging a retention-based architecture with autoregressive Implicit Constraint Q-learning to mitigate extrapolation error and miscoordination. The approach achieved state-of-the-art performance on 79% of SMAC datasets, 14 out of 16 Multi-Agent MuJoCo datasets, and all RWARE datasets, demonstrating robust coordination capabilities for up to 50 agents.

## Problem

- Existing offline Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) methods struggle with accumulating extrapolation error, which rapidly escalates as the joint action space grows exponentially with many agents.
- Offline training leads to miscoordination issues, as agents must learn from historical, potentially suboptimal data without the benefit of active environmental feedback.
- Scaling robust offline MARL to scenarios involving a large number of agents and long temporal dependencies remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- **Oryx** combines the retention-based ==Sable sequence model== architecture with a dual-output decoder and an autoregressive Implicit Constraint Q-learning (ICQ) objective.
- The dual-output decoder simultaneously predicts policy logits and Q-value estimates, enabling explicit coordination and counterfactual advantage estimation.
- A sequential updating scheme processes agents in a random permutation, conditioning each agent's policy update on previously chosen actions to foster stable and coherent multi-agent policies.

## Results

- Oryx achieved near-optimal performance, with mean episode returns up to **0.99**, in a custom T-Maze environment, validating the necessity of its integrated mechanisms.
- The algorithm exhibited superior scalability in the Connector environment, maintaining near-expert performance with up to 50 agents, significantly outperforming MAICQ beyond 23 agents.
- Oryx established new state-of-the-art performance across multiple established benchmarks, matching or surpassing existing methods on **79%** of SMAC datasets, 14 out of 16 Multi-Agent MuJoCo datasets, and all 6 RWARE datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sequential action selection, long-term memory via retention mechanisms, and autoregressive ICQ regularization are individually critical for effective long-horizon multi-agent coordination in offline settings.
> - The Sable-based sequence modeling architecture enhances scalability, allowing Oryx to maintain robust performance with up to 50 agents in complex coordination tasks.
> - Integrating autoregressive policies with offline MARL through sequence models provides a robust framework for learning coordinated behaviors from static data and opens a new research direction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), specifically addressing challenges in its offline variant. MARL, which involves multiple agents learning to cooperate or compete within an environment, exhibits potential across various real-world applications, including autonomous driving, warehouse logistics, and intelligent rail network management. However, a significant obstacle to its practical deployment is the requirement for extensive and costly interaction with the environment, which is often infeasible in safety-critical or resource-constrained domains.

Offline MARL emerges as an approach to mitigate this challenge by training multi-agent policies exclusively from pre-collected, static datasets, eliminating the need for further environment interaction. This paradigm aims to leverage existing large volumes of logged data, such as historical operational records, to distil robust cooperative policies.

The offline multi-agent setting introduces two primary technical difficulties:
1. **Accumulating Extrapolation Error:** This occurs when agents select actions during training that deviate from the distribution of actions present in the offline dataset. The problem escalates rapidly as the joint action space expands exponentially with the number of agents. While prior research has made progress in addressing extrapolation error through policy constraints or conservative value estimation, the scalability of these methods to scenarios involving a large number of agents remains an open question.
2. **Miscoordination:** This issue arises because offline training prevents agents from actively interacting with the environment, forcing them to learn solely from historical behaviors, which may originate from suboptimal policies. This can lead to the development of incompatible individual policies that fail to coordinate effectively in cooperative tasks. Existing approaches have attempted to tackle miscoordination, but their performance with long temporal dependencies and numerous agents is not well-established.

A related area involves the application of sequence modeling techniques to MARL. These models are designed to capture long-term dependencies and complex joint-agent behaviors, which are particularly relevant in partially observable environments and tasks with extended horizons. Previous works have explored Transformer-based architectures for offline MARL and other attention or diffusion-based strategies. In online MARL, transformer-based autoregressive action selection mechanisms and scalable architectures have been developed.

The presented work introduces Oryx, an algorithm designed to simultaneously address the challenges of extrapolation error and miscoordination, particularly in many-agent settings. It does this by integrating concepts from scalable sequence modeling, specifically the retention-based architecture of Sable (Mahjoub et al., 2025), with robust offline policy improvement mechanisms derived from Implicit Constraint Q-learning (ICQ) (Yang et al., 2021). This situates Oryx at the intersection of offline reinforcement learning, multi-agent coordination, and advanced sequence modeling, aiming to bridge existing gaps in scalability and robustness for complex cooperative tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel algorithm for offline cooperative Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) capable of achieving effective many-agent multi-step coordination in complex environments. This objective is motivated by the recognized challenges hindering the practical deployment of MARL in real-world scenarios, particularly those that are safety-critical or economically constrained.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving the development of Oryx are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Scalability Challenges in Offline MARL:** The work aims to tackle the limitations of existing offline MARL methods in scaling to environments with a large number of agents. Current approaches often struggle with the exponential growth of the joint action space, which exacerbates issues like extrapolation error. Oryx is motivated by the need for an algorithm that can maintain performance as agent populations increase.
- **Mitigating Extrapolation Error:** A core objective is to reduce the accumulating extrapolation error, which occurs when a learned policy proposes actions that are out-of-distribution relative to the offline dataset. This error can lead to unreliable policy performance. The motivation here is to develop a method that provides robust policy improvement while adhering to the data distribution.
- **Solving Miscoordination Issues:** The paper identifies miscoordination as a critical problem in offline MARL, where agents learn from historical, potentially suboptimal, data without the benefit of active environmental feedback. This can lead to agents developing incompatible policies. Oryx aims to address this by fostering stable and coherent multi-agent policies.
- **Integrating Advanced Sequence Modeling:** A key motivation is to leverage recent advancements in sequence modeling, specifically the retention-based architecture of Sable, to model long-term temporal dependencies and large agent populations efficiently. This is intended to enable Oryx to maintain temporal coherence over extended trajectories, which is vital for multi-step coordination.
- **Developing a Robust Offline Policy Update Scheme:** The research aims to combine Sable's architecture with a sequential form of Implicit Constraint Q-learning (ICQ). This integration is intended to result in a novel autoregressive policy update scheme that is both robust to offline data limitations and capable of explicit coordination. The dual-decoder architecture, simultaneously predicting actions and Q-values, is designed to enable the use of counterfactual advantage, facilitating extrapolation-safe policy updates.
- **Empirical Validation across Diverse Benchmarks:** A significant objective is to rigorously evaluate Oryx across a broad spectrum of challenging MARL benchmarks, including discrete and continuous control tasks (SMAC, RWARE, Multi-Agent MuJoCo), varying in scale, difficulty, and episode lengths. This extensive evaluation is intended to demonstrate the algorithm's robustness, generalization capabilities, and its ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance.
- **Pushing the Limits of Many-Agent Coordination:** The authors aim to introduce and evaluate Oryx on novel datasets featuring an increased number of agents (up to 50) in complex coordination tasks (e.g., Connector environment). This is specifically designed to stress-test Oryx’s ability to scale effectively and to highlight its advantages in settings beyond those typically explored in the literature.
- **Facilitating Real-World Application:** Ultimately, the motivation is to contribute to the development of offline MARL methods that can reliably deploy cooperative, multi-agent policies learned entirely from static data in complex, real-world domains, thereby unlocking the full potential of MARL in practical settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on the development and evaluation of Oryx, an algorithm that combines a scalable sequence modeling architecture with a sequential form of Implicit Constraint Q-learning (ICQ) for offline cooperative MARL. The approach is structured to address both extrapolation error and miscoordination, particularly in many-agent, long-horizon settings.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Theoretical Foundation

The work models cooperative MARL as a Decentralized Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (Dec-POMDP). The objective is to learn a joint policy $\pi(\mathbf{a}|\tau)$ that maximizes the expected sum of discounted rewards. The notation for multi-agent Q-functions and advantage functions is adopted from Zhong et al. (2024b), which allows for evaluating actions of ordered subsets of agents while marginalizing out others.
A key theoretical underpinning is the multi-agent advantage decomposition theorem (Kuba et al., 2021), which states that the total advantage $A(\tau, \mathbf{a})$ can be decomposed into a sum of individual advantages $A_{i_j}(\tau, \mathbf{a}_{i_{1:j-1}}, a_{i_j})$. This theorem guarantees monotonic policy improvement if sequential policy updates maintain positive advantage for each agent.

#### 3.2. Network Architecture

Oryx modifies the **Sable** architecture (Mahjoub et al., 2025), a retention-based sequence model known for efficiently handling long sequential dependencies in large agent populations.
- **Retention Mechanism:** Sable uses a retention mechanism, inspired by RetNet (Sun et al., 2023), which replaces softmax-based attention with a decaying matrix component. This design allows for flexible sequence modeling (recurrent, parallel, or chunkwise).
- **Oryx's Adaptation:** For offline training, Oryx primarily uses Sable's chunkwise training mode. This mode processes fixed-length temporal chunks and maintains hidden state transitions across chunk boundaries, ensuring temporal coherence. Unlike online settings, Oryx does not maintain a persistent recurrent retention state across training phases; instead, it starts with a `None` hidden state.
- **Dual-Output Decoder:** A key modification to Sable for Oryx is the implementation of a dual-output decoder structure. This decoder simultaneously produces both policy logits (action probabilities for each agent) and Q-value estimates. This allows the network to explicitly capture the relative value of each agent's actions within the current historical context.
- **Encoder and Value Head:** The encoder processes per-agent observations into embeddings. Unlike the original Sable, Oryx removes the value head from the encoder, training the encoder and the dual-output decoder end-to-end with a combined critic and policy loss.

#### 3.3. Autoregressive Implicit Constraint Q-learning (ICQ) Loss

Oryx integrates the autoregressive policy structure of sequence models with an enhanced offline multi-agent objective based on ICQ (Yang et al., 2021).
- **Autoregressive Policy:** The joint policy is represented as a product of factors, where each agent's action probability is conditioned on the actions of previously ordered agents: $\pi(\mathbf{a}|\tau) = \prod_{j=1}^{n} \pi_{i_j}(a_{i_j}|\tau, \mathbf{a}_{i_{1:j-1}})$.
- **ICQ Regularization:** Original ICQ constrains policy updates to remain close to the behavior policy $\mu$ (from the dataset) by using a specific Bellman operator that implicitly incorporates an importance sampling weight. The optimal policy takes the form $\pi^*_{k+1}(\mathbf{a}|\tau) = \frac{1}{Z(\tau)} \mu(\mathbf{a}|\tau) \exp \left( \frac{Q_{\pi_k}(\tau,\mathbf{a})}{\alpha} \right)$.
- **Autoregressive ICQ Loss (Theorem 1):** Building on the autoregressive decomposition and advantage decomposition theorem, Oryx derives a sequential optimization objective for each agent's policy $\pi_{i_j}^*$. The policy loss minimizes a weighted KL divergence, where weights involve the exponentiated advantage and a normalizing partition function $Z_{i_{1:j}}(\tau)$.
- **Critic Update:** The critic network parameters are updated by minimizing a squared error loss, where target Q-values are computed using the ICQ operator. This involves sampling target actions from the dataset and applying implicit importance weights.
- **Counterfactual Advantage Baseline:** To manage the high variance associated with centralized advantage estimates in multi-agent settings, Oryx employs a counterfactual baseline, as introduced in Foerster et al. (2018) and analyzed in Kuba et al. (2021). This baseline decomposes the advantage to account for individual agent contributions, reducing the variance growth that can be linear with the number of agents.

#### 3.4. Sequential Updating Scheme (Algorithm 1)

The training algorithm for Oryx proceeds iteratively:
1. Initialize policy and critic network parameters.
2. In each training step:
 - A mini-batch of trajectories is sampled from the offline dataset.
 - A random permutation of agents is drawn.
 - For each agent $j$ in the chosen permutation (from 1 to $n$):
 - The critic $Q_{i_j}$ is updated using the autoregressive ICQ loss, which incorporates the implicit importance weights.
 - The counterfactual advantage $A_{i_{1:j}}$ is calculated.
 - The policy $\pi_{i_j}$ is updated using the autoregressive ICQ loss, which leverages the calculated advantage.

This sequential updating scheme explicitly addresses miscoordination by conditioning each agent's policy update on the actions already executed by other agents within the current sequence, promoting stable policy improvement.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Strategy

Oryx was evaluated across three main categories:
1. **Validation of Core Mechanisms:** A custom T-Maze environment was designed to isolate the importance of sequential action selection, memory, and offline sequential ICQ regularization. Baselines included independent and centralized ICQ variants, alongside targeted Oryx ablations.
2. **Scalability to Many-Agent Settings:** The Connector environment (Bonnet et al., 2024), which features increasing coordination complexity with agent count, was used to stress-test Oryx with up to 50 agents. New datasets were generated for larger agent counts. MAICQ served as a primary baseline.
3. **Benchmark Performance:** Extensive benchmarking was conducted across 65 datasets from established MARL benchmarks (SMAC, Multi-Agent MuJoCo, RWARE), comparing Oryx against state-of-the-art offline MARL algorithms.
4. **Architecture Comparison:** An ablation study compared Oryx (Sable backbone) with an offline variant of the Multi-Agent Transformer (MAT) integrated with the same autoregressive ICQ loss, using aggregated metrics for comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of Oryx involved several experiments designed to validate its core mechanisms, assess its scalability, and compare its performance against existing state-of-the-art methods across diverse benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Validation of Oryx’s Core Mechanisms in T-Maze

The T-Maze environment was used to isolate the importance of Oryx's key components: sequential action selection, long-term memory, and autoregressive ICQ regularization.
- **Performance:** Oryx, with all its components active, achieved optimal performance, with mean episode returns of 0.99 ± 0.01 on a replay dataset and 0.94 ± 0.03 on an expert dataset.
- **Ablation Results:** All tested baselines (independent ICQ (I-ICQ) and MAICQ) and Oryx ablations (Oryx - w/o Auto-Regressive Actions, Oryx - w/o Memory, Oryx - w/o ICQ) failed to solve the task, exhibiting mean episode returns of 0.0 ± 0.0. This indicates that each of Oryx’s integrated mechanisms—sequential action selection, memory retention, and ICQ-based offline regularization—is individually necessary for effective long-horizon multi-agent coordination in this setting.

#### 4.2. Scalability in Complex Many-Agent Settings (Connector)

Oryx's ability to handle large agent populations and increasing coordination complexity was evaluated using the Connector environment with up to 50 agents.
- **Comparison with MAICQ:** At lower agent counts, Oryx and MAICQ showed comparable performance. However, as the number of agents increased beyond approximately 23, Oryx demonstrated significantly superior performance. Oryx achieved near-expert performance, whereas MAICQ's performance remained around 25% of the expert level in highly dense scenarios.
- **Robustness at Scale:** Oryx maintained robust performance as agent numbers escalated from 30 to 50, effectively managing the increasing coordination demands. MAICQ's performance degraded in these larger settings. This suggests Oryx's architecture and learning scheme are more suited for scaling to many-agent coordination tasks.

#### 4.3. State-of-the-Art Performance on Existing Offline MARL Datasets

Oryx was benchmarked against leading offline MARL algorithms across 65 datasets from SMAC, Multi-Agent MuJoCo, and RWARE.
- **SMAC:** Across 43 datasets spanning 9 unique scenarios, Oryx matched or surpassed the current state-of-the-art performance on 34 datasets (79%).
- **Multi-Agent MuJoCo:** On 16 datasets from scenarios like 2x3 HalfCheetah, 6x1 HalfCheetah, 2x4 Ant, and 3x1 Hopper, Oryx matched or surpassed the current state-of-the-art on 14 datasets.
- **RWARE:** Oryx established new state-of-the-art performance on all 6 available RWARE datasets, improving upon previous best results by nearly 20% in some cases. RWARE is notable for its long episodes and sparse reward signals, highlighting Oryx's capacity for long-horizon coordination.

#### 4.4. Comparison of Autoregressive Sequence Modeling Architectures

An ablation study compared Oryx (using the Sable network backbone) with an offline variant of the Multi-Agent Transformer (MAT+ICQ) that shared the same autoregressive ICQ loss. This was to isolate the architectural impact.
- **Aggregated Metrics:** Across all SMAC and RWARE datasets, aggregated metrics (median, interquartile mean (IQM), mean, and optimality gap) were reported after normalizing scores.
- **Performance Advantage:** Oryx consistently outperformed MAT+ICQ across all metrics and environments. For instance, on SMAC, Oryx achieved a median normalized score of 0.91 compared to MAT+ICQ's 0.71, and an IQM of 0.87 compared to 0.67. The optimality gap for Oryx was lower (0.23) than for MAT+ICQ (0.38). Similar trends were observed for RWARE. This indicates that the Sable-based architecture contributes to improved performance, scalability, efficiency, and stability in the offline MARL context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development and empirical validation of Oryx present several points of significance and potential impact within the field of Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning and its applications.

**Advancement in Offline Multi-Agent Coordination:** Oryx contributes to addressing fundamental challenges in offline MARL, specifically the issues of accumulating extrapolation error and miscoordination. Its integration of a scalable sequence model with an autoregressive, implicitly constrained policy update scheme demonstrates a robust approach to learning coordinated behaviors from static data, particularly in settings characterized by many agents and long-term dependencies.

**Enhanced Scalability for Complex Environments:** The work shows that Oryx maintains performance and scales effectively in complex environments with a high density of agents, such as the Connector environment with up to 50 agents. This ability to handle increased agent populations and coordination demands extends the applicability of offline MARL to more intricate real-world scenarios than previously demonstrated.

**Establishment of New Performance Benchmarks:** Oryx achieved state-of-the-art performance on a substantial majority of the diverse benchmark datasets tested across SMAC, Multi-Agent MuJoCo, and RWARE. This includes notable improvements in environments requiring long-horizon coordination and sparse rewards, which are particularly challenging. These results indicate a competitive advancement in the field's empirical capabilities.

**Validation of Architectural Choices:** The comparative study between Oryx (Sable-based) and a Multi-Agent Transformer variant using the same learning objective validates the selection of Sable's retention-based architecture for its benefits in performance, scalability, and stability within the offline MARL context. This provides insights into effective architectural designs for sequence modeling in multi-agent systems.

**Pathways Towards Real-World Deployment:** By mitigating key difficulties in offline MARL, Oryx moves closer to enabling the reliable training of cooperative multi-agent policies using only pre-recorded data. This is crucial for domains where direct environment interaction is expensive or unsafe, such as autonomous systems, logistics, and resource management. The potential for deploying robust policies learned from existing operational data could accelerate the adoption of MARL in industrial settings.

**Stimulation of Future Research:** The introduction of autoregressive policies to offline MARL via sequence models opens a new research direction. The authors suggest that future work could explore extending other offline MARL methods to utilize autoregressive policies and sequence models, or integrating diverse offline learning techniques into this paradigm. Furthermore, the release of new, challenging datasets for many-agent coordination in the Connector environment (with up to 50 agents) provides a valuable resource for the research community, encouraging further investigation into scalable offline MARL.

**Identified Limitations and Future Directions:** The paper acknowledges that Oryx's evaluation, while extensive, is still within research benchmarks and not yet fully representative of real-world industrial complexity. Consequently, future work is proposed to extend Oryx to hybrid offline-online settings and to evaluate its effectiveness in broader, more varied real-world applications.
