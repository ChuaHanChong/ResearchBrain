# Oryx: a Scalable Sequence Model for Many-Agent Coordination in Offline MARL

> [!summary] Summary
> Oryx presents a scalable sequence model for offline Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), leveraging a retention-based architecture with autoregressive Implicit Constraint Q-learning to mitigate extrapolation error and miscoordination. The approach achieved state-of-the-art performance on 79% of SMAC datasets, 14 out of 16 Multi-Agent MuJoCo datasets, and all RWARE datasets, demonstrating robust coordination capabilities for up to 50 agents.

## Problem

- Existing offline Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) methods struggle with accumulating extrapolation error, which rapidly escalates as the joint action space grows exponentially with many agents.
- Offline training leads to miscoordination issues, as agents must learn from historical, potentially suboptimal data without the benefit of active environmental feedback.
- Scaling robust offline MARL to scenarios involving a large number of agents and long temporal dependencies remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- **Oryx** combines the retention-based ==Sable sequence model== architecture with a dual-output decoder and an autoregressive Implicit Constraint Q-learning (ICQ) objective.
- The dual-output decoder simultaneously predicts policy logits and Q-value estimates, enabling explicit coordination and counterfactual advantage estimation.
- A sequential updating scheme processes agents in a random permutation, conditioning each agent's policy update on previously chosen actions to foster stable and coherent multi-agent policies.

## Results

- Oryx achieved near-optimal performance, with mean episode returns up to **0.99**, in a custom T-Maze environment, validating the necessity of its integrated mechanisms.
- The algorithm exhibited superior scalability in the Connector environment, maintaining near-expert performance with up to 50 agents, significantly outperforming MAICQ beyond 23 agents.
- Oryx established new state-of-the-art performance across multiple established benchmarks, matching or surpassing existing methods on **79%** of SMAC datasets, 14 out of 16 Multi-Agent MuJoCo datasets, and all 6 RWARE datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sequential action selection, long-term memory via retention mechanisms, and autoregressive ICQ regularization are individually critical for effective long-horizon multi-agent coordination in offline settings.
> - The Sable-based sequence modeling architecture enhances scalability, allowing Oryx to maintain robust performance with up to 50 agents in complex coordination tasks.
> - Integrating autoregressive policies with offline MARL through sequence models provides a robust framework for learning coordinated behaviors from static data and opens a new research direction.
