# Spatial-Aware VLA Pretraining through Visual-Physical Alignment from Human Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Peking University, Renmin University of China, and BeingBeyond introduced a pretraining paradigm for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, explicitly aligning 2D visual perception with 3D physical space by leveraging comprehensive annotations from human videos. This method significantly enhances spatial grounding and generalization in robotic manipulation tasks across both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- VLA models face a fundamental "2D-to-3D gap," processing 2D visual inputs for actions in a 3D physical world.
- This results in weak spatial grounding, limiting a robot's ability to perceive object geometry, understand spatial relationships, and execute precise physical actions.
- Current VLA pretraining methods do not explicitly integrate 3D spatial awareness to bridge this perception-action divide, often relying on implicit features or suffering from embodiment mismatch.

## Method

- Developed **VIPA-VLA**, a VLA model featuring a ==dual-encoder== architecture that integrates a standard VLM backbone with a 3D vision encoder and a fusion layer to combine semantic and spatial visual information.
- Introduced ==Hand3D==, a large-scale dataset of human videos with explicit 3D visual and action annotations (e.g., 3D hand poses, object localizations, discretized wrist trajectories).
- Proposed a two-stage pretraining paradigm: first, aligning 2D semantic and 3D spatial visual features, then learning 3D motion priors by predicting discretized motion tokens from human demonstrations.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **96.8%** on the LIBERO benchmark (two-view input) and **45.8%** on the RoboCasa benchmark (three-view), outperforming many previous methods and baselines like π-series.
- Demonstrated superior performance in real-world robot tasks, achieving **60%** and **50%** whole-task success rates on "Wipe-Board" and "Water-Plant" respectively, and robust generalization to unseen environments (e.g., **50%** on "Wipe-Board-Unseen" where baselines scored 0-**10%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the spatial-aware pretraining paradigm and the VIPA-VLA dual-encoder architecture significantly contribute to improved spatial grounding and overall task success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale human demonstration videos are a scalable and rich source for extracting explicit 3D visual and action annotations, providing valuable visual-physical correspondences.
> - Explicitly aligning 2D semantic vision with 3D physical space during pretraining is crucial for developing robust spatial grounding in VLA models, enhancing their ability to reason about 3D geometry and relationships.
> - A multi-stage pretraining approach, progressively learning from coarse-grained 3D visual relationships to fine-grained 3D action trajectories, effectively instills comprehensive spatial awareness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in large-scale Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have significantly propelled the field of robot learning. VLA models, which extend VLMs to enable language-guided action execution in physical environments, represent a promising paradigm for developing generalist robot policies. However, a fundamental limitation persists: these models often process 2D visual inputs (images, video frames) but operate in a 3D physical world. This inherent "2D-to-3D gap" leads to weak spatial grounding, hindering a robot's ability to accurately perceive object geometry, understand spatial relationships, and execute precise physical actions, thereby limiting generalization.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap. Existing VLA approaches either tokenize robot actions and fine-tune pretrained VLMs on robot datasets (e.g., RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA), or incorporate action experts. While models like GR00T and π-series combine massive internet data with robot datasets for large-scale VLA pretraining, they do not explicitly focus on *spatial-aware* pretraining to bridge the 2D-3D perception-action divide.

The work also differentiates itself from existing 3D-aware models and research on learning from human videos:
- **3D Vision-Language Models (3D VLMs):** Recent 3D VLMs incorporate 3D spatial information (e.g., from explicit 3D annotations or depth estimation) to enhance perception and reasoning about spatial relationships and object geometry. However, their primary focus is perception, not explicitly linking 3D perception to physical *action space* for robot policy learning. This paper explicitly extends 3D understanding to action grounding.
- **Learning from Human Videos:** Prior research has leveraged human demonstration videos for representation learning or aligning human/robot action spaces. However, representation learning often yields implicit features, and direct action space alignment suffers from embodiment mismatch between humans and robots. This work argues that human videos are a rich, easily obtainable source of "visual-physical correspondences" that reveal how actions unfold in 3D, and it proposes a method to extract *explicit 3D visual and action annotations* to overcome the limitations of previous human video-based approaches.

In essence, this research introduces a novel pretraining paradigm that synergizes the strengths of large-scale VLMs with explicit 3D spatial awareness derived from readily available human videos, a unique combination designed to directly tackle the perception-action grounding problem in VLA models, making it a significant contribution to embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a fundamental challenge in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: the inherent disconnect between their 2D visual perception capabilities and the 3D physical environment in which robots operate. While VLMs have shown remarkable abilities in learning joint representations across modalities, their application to robotics is constrained by a "significant gap between perception and action grounding." This gap manifests as poor spatial understanding, limiting a robot's ability to interpret visual cues in terms of 3D geometry and how physical actions interact with the environment.

The key objectives are:

1. **Bridge the 2D-3D Gap:** To overcome the limitation where VLA models rely on 2D visual inputs for actions in a 3D world, thereby improving their ability to ground actions in physical space.
2. **Acquire 3D Spatial Awareness Pre-policy Learning:** To enable VLA models to develop a robust 3D spatial understanding *before* they are adapted to specific robot policy learning tasks. This pretraining aims to provide a strong foundation for subsequent, more effective robot learning.
3. **Leverage Human Videos as a Rich Supervision Source:** To utilize large-scale human demonstration videos, which implicitly contain abundant 2D visual observations aligned with 3D physical actions, as a novel and scalable source of supervision for visual-physical alignment.
4. **Develop a Spatial-Aware VLA Pretraining Paradigm:** To propose a structured pretraining methodology that explicitly aligns visual space with physical space using 3D annotations extracted from human videos.
5. **Design an Architecture for Visual-Physical Alignment:** To instantiate this paradigm with a new VLA architecture, VIPA-VLA, capable of augmenting semantic visual representations with explicit 3D spatial features.
6. **Enhance Grounding, Robustness, and Generalization:** To demonstrate that the proposed paradigm and model significantly improve the grounding between 2D vision and 3D action, leading to more robust and generalizable robotic policies across various simulation and real-world tasks.

In summary, the core motivation is to equip VLA models with an innate understanding of 3D physical space, making their visual perception directly relevant to physical action execution, thereby fostering more capable and adaptable robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the **Spatial-Aware VLA Pretraining** paradigm, instantiated by the **VIPA-VLA** model and supported by the novel **Hand3D** dataset.

#### 3.1. Hand3D Dataset Construction

The paper introduces **Hand3D**, a large-scale dataset built from diverse human manipulation videos, designed to provide explicit 3D visual and action annotations.
- **Human Video Collection:** Aggregates videos from nine heterogeneous sources (e.g., Arctic, HOI4D, EgoDex, Taste-Rob) covering various manipulation tasks. All hand annotations are unified to the MANO parametric hand model, ensuring consistent 3D hand pose and shape information.
- **3D Aware Annotations:**
 - **3D Visual Annotations:**
 - **Point Cloud Estimation:** Uses Cut3R to obtain dense per-frame 3D point clouds.
 - **Object Localization:** Employs Gemini-2.5-flash and GroundingDINO to generate 2D object bounding boxes, which are then combined with depth information to localize objects in 3D.
 - **Hand Pose Integration:** MANO-based hand poses are transformed to the camera coordinate system to yield 3D joint locations.
 - **Scale Calibration:** A crucial step to unify the relative scale from point cloud estimation with the absolute physical scale of hand joint positions, ensuring consistent 3D representations.
 - **Instructional Data Curation (Hand3D-visual):** From the 3D visual annotations, Gemini-2.5-flash is used to generate ~300K VQA-style pairs across four categories:
 1. **Spatial Relationship:** Describes 3D relations (position, distance, contact) between hands and objects.
 2. **Task Completion:** Formulates instructions on 3D hand movement for object interaction.
 3. **Hand Movement:** Annotates 3D hand trajectories (direction, distance) between frames.
 4. **Camera Movement:** Describes relative 3D camera transformation (rotation, translation).
 Direction is discretized into axis-aligned language tokens, and distance is represented by Euclidean norm.
 - **3D Action Annotations (Hand3D-action):** Extracts 3D wrist trajectories (x, y, z series) from human videos, tokenizes them via uniform binning into "motion tokens" (K=1024 bins for each axis over a 1m³ physical space). Combines these with text instructions from Gemini-2.5-Flash/Pro to yield ~1M video-instruction-motion pairs, covering instructional motion generation, motion translation, and contextual motion prediction.

#### 3.2. VIPA-VLA Model Architecture

VIPA-VLA employs a dual-encoder architecture to integrate both semantic and spatial visual information (Figure 4).
- **Semantic Vision Encoder:** A standard pretrained VLM backbone (InternVL3.5-2B) to extract high-level visual semantics.
- **3D Vision Encoder:** Cut3R, which provides explicit geometric understanding of the scene, producing spatial embeddings.
- **Fusion Layer:** A cross-attention module that combines semantic (V_sem) and spatial (V_spa) features. Semantic features query 3D spatial features, and the output is integrated back into the visual features via a residual connection with a learnable scaling parameter (α).
- **Motion Tokens:** The LLM's vocabulary is extended to include discrete motion tokens, representing discretized 3D wrist trajectory points.

#### 3.3. Spatial-Aware VLA Pretraining

The pretraining is a two-stage process (Figure 1), building progressively on visual-physical alignment:
- **Stage 1: 3D-Visual Pretraining:**
 - Initializes with a pretrained VLM, pretrained 3D vision encoder, and a randomly initialized fusion layer.
 - **Objective:** Trains *only* the fusion layer using the Hand3D-visual VQA data (freezing other pretrained parameters). This stage focuses on aligning 2D semantic embeddings with 3D spatial embeddings, encouraging reasoning about 3D spatial relations.
- **Stage 2: 3D-Action Pretraining:**
 - Extends the LLM vocabulary with motion tokens.
 - **Objective:** Trains the LLM (and implicitly the fusion layer) on Hand3D-action annotations, predicting motion tokens conditioned on fused visual and textual inputs. Semantic and spatial encoders remain frozen. This stage focuses on fine-grained spatial reasoning and action-level understanding, learning physical motion patterns.

#### 3.4. Post-Training

After pretraining, VIPA-VLA is adapted to downstream robot tasks:
- An **action head** (implemented as a diffusion transformer, DiT) is attached to the pretrained VLM backbone.
- The model generates conditional context (h_cond) from fused visual and language features.
- The DiT, conditioned on h_cond and robot state, predicts action chunks using a **flow matching** objective, minimizing the deviation between its prediction and an oracle transport direction.
- Only the LLM backbone and the action head are updated during this fine-tuning stage.

This multi-stage approach systematically builds spatial awareness, starting from coarse-grained 3D visual relationships to fine-grained 3D action trajectories, all derived from human demonstrations, before adapting to robot control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that VIPA-VLA achieves significant improvements in spatial grounding and generalization across both simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Simulation Robot Tasks

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** VIPA-VLA showed strong performance across all four task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), achieving an average success rate of 92.4% with single-view input and 96.8% with two-view input. Notably, it surpassed many previous methods and achieved results comparable to or slightly better than powerful baselines like π-series and GR00T models, despite not using any robot data during pretraining. This highlights the effectiveness of spatial reasoning learned from human videos in generalizing to simulated robot environments.
- **RoboCasa Benchmark:** Using a three-view input and only 50 human demonstrations per task, VIPA-VLA achieved the best overall performance with an average success rate of 45.8%. It showed particularly strong improvements in "Doors/Drawers" tasks (+9.9% over π0.5), which demand precise spatial localization. This indicates robust generalization in complex, visually diverse, and geometrically challenging environments with limited robot data.

#### 4.2. Real Robot Tasks

Evaluations on a 7-DoF Franka Research 3 arm confirmed VIPA-VLA's ability to transfer learned spatial understanding to real-world scenarios.
- **Performance on Seen Environments:** VIPA-VLA achieved the best overall performance across "Put-Three-Obj," "Wipe-Board," and "Water-Plant" tasks, with significantly higher whole-task success rates for "Wipe-Board" (60%) and "Water-Plant" (50%) compared to baselines. Although its whole-task success rate on "Put-Three-Obj" was lower than GR00T N1.5, its *sub-task success rate* was higher (52% vs 48%), indicating more consistent progress and robustness through the task stages rather than early failures.
- **Generalization to Unseen Environments:** When tested in unseen environments (e.g., different tablecloth colors, marker colors), VIPA-VLA maintained strong performance, outperforming all baselines by a significant margin. For "Wipe-Board-Unseen," it achieved a 50% whole-task success rate, whereas most baselines failed completely (0-10%). This is a crucial finding, demonstrating that the Spatial-Aware Pretraining provides transferable 3D spatial understanding, enabling reliable generalization to new scenes.
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Failure analysis revealed that VIPA-VLA's failures typically occur in fine-grained manipulation steps (slight misalignments), while overall spatial localization and planning remain correct. In contrast, baseline models frequently fail due to inaccurate spatial grounding, misjudging object positions or reaching incorrect regions, indicating a weaker 2D-3D correspondence.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Architecture and Pretraining Contribution:** Ablations on the LIBERO benchmark confirmed that both the Spatial-Aware Pretraining and the dual-encoder architecture contribute significantly to performance. Removing either led to a consistent performance drop, with combined removal resulting in the largest decline (-3.7% in average success rate), validating their synergistic effect.
- **Effect of Spatial-Aware VLA Pretraining:**
 - **3D Visual Annotations (Stage 1):** Evaluation on the Hand3D-test (unseen VQA pairs) showed that pretraining with Hand3D-visual significantly improved 3D spatial understanding. VIPA-VLA-PT (only fusion layer trained) achieved a lower distance error (0.12m vs. 0.18m for InternVL3.5) and higher direction score (1.82/3 vs. 1.22/3), demonstrating that aligning semantic and spatial features via the fusion layer yields substantial gains in 3D spatial reasoning.
 - **3D Action Annotations (Stage 2):** Qualitative visualization of predicted motion trajectories showed VIPA-VLA producing smoother, more goal-directed trajectories that closely align with instructions and reflect learned affordance knowledge, outperforming noisy ground-truth human motions.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that explicit Spatial-Aware VLA Pretraining using 3D annotations extracted from human videos, combined with a dual-encoder architecture, is highly effective in endowing VLA models with robust 3D spatial understanding, leading to superior performance, generalization, and robustness in diverse robotic manipulation tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence and robotics, particularly for the development of generalist robot policies. Its primary impact lies in effectively bridging the long-standing "2D-3D gap" that has hindered VLA models from achieving robust and generalized physical interaction.

The key significance points are:

1. **Enhanced Spatial Grounding and Generalization:** By providing VLA models with explicit 3D spatial awareness during pretraining, the work enables a fundamental improvement in how robots perceive and interact with the physical world. This leads to significantly more robust and generalizable policies, as evidenced by superior performance in complex simulation benchmarks (RoboCasa) and critical real-world tasks, especially in unseen environments. This directly translates to robots that can adapt better to novel situations and diverse task variations.
2. **Leveraging Accessible Data Sources:** The methodology demonstrates the power of large-scale human demonstration videos as a rich and readily available source of "visual-physical correspondences." Unlike costly and time-consuming robot data collection, human videos are abundant and diverse. The proposed pipeline for extracting precise 3D visual and action annotations from these videos makes this vast resource actionable for robot learning. This reduces reliance on massive robot datasets for initial grounding, democratizing access to powerful pretraining data.
3. **Novel Pretraining Paradigm:** The Spatial-Aware VLA Pretraining paradigm is a conceptual breakthrough. By systematically training VLA models to align 2D vision with 3D physical understanding in a progressive two-stage process (3D visual annotations for coarse grounding, 3D action annotations for fine-grained motion priors), it offers a structured way to instill essential physical intelligence. This paradigm can inspire future research into pretraining strategies that prioritize specific inductive biases crucial for robotic control.
4. **Architectural Innovation:** The VIPA-VLA dual-encoder architecture, combining semantic and 3D vision encoders with an intelligent fusion layer, is a practical instantiation of the paradigm. It effectively integrates complementary visual information, demonstrating that explicit architectural designs can significantly enhance spatial reasoning beyond data-level supervision alone. The introduction of motion tokens for discretized 3D action representation is also a notable contribution to making continuous action spaces learnable by LLM-based architectures.
5. **Addressing Embodiment Mismatch:** While leveraging human videos, the methodology cleverly sidesteps the traditional embodiment mismatch problem by focusing on *spatial understanding* and *action priors* rather than direct imitation of human kinematics. The acquired spatial intelligence is then transferred to the robot's action space during post-training, making the human data broadly applicable.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robot Learning:** By providing a strong spatial foundation, robots can learn new tasks with fewer robot-specific demonstrations, making robot deployment faster and more efficient.
- **Development of More Capable Generalist Robots:** This work moves closer to truly generalist robots that can understand and perform a wide variety of tasks in open-world settings, mimicking human-level adaptability.
- **Broader Research Applications:** The Hand3D dataset and annotation pipeline can serve as a valuable resource for the research community, fostering further advancements in 3D-aware perception, human-robot interaction, and embodied AI.
- **Future Directions:** The paper suggests combining this human-video-based pretraining with robot data pretraining. This hybrid approach could yield even more comprehensive and effective VLA models, representing a powerful future research avenue.

In conclusion, "Spatial-Aware VLA Pretraining through Visual-Physical Alignment from Human Videos" significantly advances the state-of-the-art in robot learning by tackling a core perception-action challenge. Its innovative use of human video data and a dedicated pretraining paradigm paves the way for more intelligent, adaptable, and generalizable robotic systems.
