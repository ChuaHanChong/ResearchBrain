# Unlabeled Data Improves Fine-Grained Image Zero-shot Classification with Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UCLA developed AutoSEP, a self-supervised prompt learning framework that improves fine-grained image classification performance of Multimodal Large Language Models using only unlabeled data and black-box access. The framework consistently boosted classification accuracy by an average of 13% over standard zero-shot performance across various MLLMs and datasets.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) generally struggle with fine-grained image classification tasks, often overlooking subtle yet discriminative visual details.
- Existing methods to improve MLLM performance on fine-grained tasks typically rely on expensive, large-scale labeled datasets for training or fine-tuning, which are resource-intensive to acquire.
- Current prompt optimization techniques often require labeled data for supervision or are designed for text-only tasks, making them less effective for black-box multimodal scenarios without label access.

## Method

- ==AutoSEP== employs an iterative self-supervised =='Reflect-and-Modify' strategy== to optimize a description-generation prompt without using any labeled data.
- The MLLM first generates a detailed textual description of an image using the optimized prompt, then performs classification by combining the image with its generated description.
- Prompt optimization is guided by an ==unsupervised scoring function== based on instance-level classification, which measures how well the MLLM can correctly distinguish an image's own generated description from those of other images.

## Results

- AutoSEP consistently achieved an average improvement of **13%** over standard zero-shot classification and **3%** over the best unsupervised baselines across eight fine-grained datasets and three state-of-the-art MLLMs (Gemini 1.5 Flash, GPT-4o, Qwen2-VL-72B-Instruct).
- The proposed instance-level classification accuracy as an unsupervised scoring signal significantly outperformed other prompt optimization strategies that used pseudo-labeling or pairwise output evaluations.
- The framework demonstrated consistent performance gains with increasing unlabeled sample sizes, showing reliable improvements even when utilizing as few as **60 unlabeled images** for optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Principled optimization of description-generation prompts is crucial for MLLMs to produce task-relevant, discriminative descriptions, as unoptimized prompts often yield marginal or negative performance changes.
> - Instance-level classification accuracy provides a robust and effective unsupervised signal for guiding prompt optimization in MLLMs, outperforming alternative self-supervised scoring strategies.
> - The iterative prompt refinement process leads to increased prompt diversity and semantic richness, which positively correlates with improved fine-grained classification performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), zero-shot learning, fine-grained image classification (FGIC), and self-supervised prompt optimization.

**MLLMs and Zero-Shot Classification:** In recent years, MLLMs, such as GPT-4o, Gemini, Qwen-VL, and Flamingo, have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in understanding and processing both visual and textual information. Their ability to perform "zero-shot" classification—classifying images into categories they haven't explicitly been trained on—is a significant advancement. These models are typically pretrained on vast web-scraped datasets, allowing them to generalize across a broad spectrum of general image classification tasks.

**Challenges in Fine-Grained Image Classification:** Despite their general prowess, MLLMs often struggle with fine-grained classification tasks. These tasks demand the identification of subtle visual distinctions between highly similar subcategories (e.g., differentiating between various species of birds, dog breeds, or plant types). The paper highlights that MLLMs, without explicit guidance, tend to overlook these critical, subtle details or misinterpret key characteristics, leading to performance degradation compared to basic-level classification. This limitation is a well-recognized problem in the MLLM community, with several ongoing efforts to address it.

**Existing Approaches and Identified Gaps:**
- **Supervised Fine-tuning:** A common approach to improve MLLM performance on FGIC is extensive training or fine-tuning with large-scale labeled datasets specific to the fine-grained categories. However, the authors point out that collecting and annotating such datasets is exceptionally resource-intensive, demanding significant computational power and human effort due to the subtle nature of the distinctions. This makes supervised approaches impractical for many real-world scenarios, especially in low-resource settings.
- **Prompt Engineering:** The broader field of prompt optimization for LLMs and MLLMs has seen substantial progress, with various techniques for automatically searching or refining prompts to elicit better model performance. However, many of these methods primarily rely on labeled data for supervision during the optimization process or are designed for text-only tasks.
- **Self-Supervised Learning (SSL):** Traditional SSL methods leverage unlabeled data to learn robust feature representations (e.g., through contrastive learning) by training encoders to pull positive pairs closer and push negative pairs further apart in an embedding space. While highly effective, these methods typically involve training or fine-tuning the model's internal weights, which is often not feasible when only black-box access to a proprietary MLLM is available.

**The Niche of This Work:** This paper fills a crucial gap by exploring how *unlabeled data* can be effectively leveraged to enhance *zero-shot fine-grained classification* for MLLMs within a *black-box setting*. Unlike prior work, AutoSEP does not require labeled data for optimization nor does it necessitate access to the MLLM's internal parameters for training or fine-tuning. It innovatively adapts the principles of self-supervised learning to the realm of MLLM prompting, transforming the problem of learning better visual features into learning better *textual descriptions* that MLLMs can utilize. This positions AutoSEP as a practical and resource-efficient solution for improving MLLM capabilities in challenging visual tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **significantly improve the performance of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) on fine-grained image classification (FGIC) tasks**. This improvement is sought under two stringent constraints:
1. **Utilization of only unlabeled data:** The framework must function without any class-specific labels during its optimization process.
2. **Black-box access to MLLMs:** The method should not require any training or fine-tuning of the MLLM's internal parameters, relying solely on its inference capabilities.

The core idea underpinning AutoSEP is to **leverage abundant unlabeled data to learn an optimal description prompt**. This prompt is designed to guide MLLMs in generating textual descriptions that precisely highlight the crucial discriminative features within an image, thereby boosting their fine-grained classification accuracy.

The authors are motivated by several critical observations and challenges in the current MLLM landscape:
- **MLLM Deficiency in Fine-Grained Tasks:** Despite impressive general zero-shot capabilities, MLLMs frequently fail to differentiate between visually similar subcategories, primarily because they tend to overlook subtle yet critical visual details without explicit direction. This "ignorance of MLLMs" is a significant hurdle for deploying them in tasks requiring high precision.
- **Resource-Intensive Supervised Approaches:** Existing methods to enhance FGIC often necessitate large-scale, meticulously labeled datasets for training or fine-tuning. The collection and annotation of such data, especially for fine-grained distinctions, are prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. This makes traditional supervised learning impractical for many real-world applications and limits the scalability of MLLM improvements.
- **Abundance of Unlabeled Data:** In stark contrast to labeled data, unlabeled images are readily available. The authors identify an unexplored opportunity: how can this abundant unlabeled data be harnessed to improve MLLM performance in a self-improving manner, without human intervention or expensive computational resources?
- **Need for a Lightweight and Generalizable Solution:** There is a demand for a framework that can enhance MLLMs' capabilities without modifying their underlying architecture, making it applicable to proprietary, black-box models. Such a solution would be highly practical for low-resource or open-world scenarios where data labeling is unfeasible.

By addressing these motivations, AutoSEP aims to provide a novel, efficient, and practical solution for pushing the boundaries of MLLM performance in fine-grained visual recognition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces **AutoSEP (Automatic Self-Enhancing Prompt Learning)**, an iterative self-supervised framework designed to improve MLLM fine-grained classification through optimized image description generation.

#### 3.1. Image Description Generation for Enhanced Classification

AutoSEP's core approach involves an intermediate step where the MLLM first generates a detailed textual description of an image before performing classification.
1. **Description Generation:** Given an image `x`, a "description generation prompt" `p` is used to instruct the MLLM to produce a textual description `t` (`t = M(x, p)`).
2. **Classification with Description:** The MLLM then uses both the original image `x` and the generated description `t` (concatenated with the classification prompt `q`) to predict the final label `ŷ` (`ŷ = M(x, [q, t])`).
This description generation step is conceptualized as an "encoder," mapping raw visual input into a text format that highlights discriminative features relevant for the classification task, analogous to how encoders in contrastive learning extract salient numerical features. The challenge then becomes how to optimize this description generation prompt `p` using only unlabeled data.

#### 3.2. Automatic Self-Enhancing Prompt Learning

The optimization of the description-generation prompt `p` is framed as an iterative process to maximize an unsupervised scoring function `Ψ(X, p)`, where `X` is the set of unlabeled images.

#### 3.2.1. Instance-level Classification for Unsupervised Scoring

Drawing inspiration from traditional self-supervised learning, which learns to distinguish between instances, AutoSEP proposes to optimize `p` such that the MLLM generates highly distinctive descriptions for each individual unlabeled image. This aims to maximize the inter-image distinctiveness of the generated descriptions.
1. **Instance-level Task:** The task is framed as determining whether an image `x_i` correctly matches its own generated description `t_i` when presented alongside a "negative" description `t_j` (generated for a different image `x_j`).
2. **Binary-Choice Prompt:** For a given image `x_i` and two descriptions `t_a`, `t_b` (one of which is `t_i` and the other `t_j`), the MLLM is prompted with "Text 1: {t_a}. Text 2: {t_b}. Which description correctly describes the image? The first text or the second text?". A random shuffling `Z` prevents positional bias.
3. **Correctness Indicator `V(x_i, x_j)`:** This function returns 1 if the MLLM correctly selects `t_i` as describing `x_i` (over `t_j`), and 0 otherwise.
4. **Scoring Function `Ψ(X, p)`:** The unsupervised scoring function sums `V(x_i, x_j)` over all unique pairs of images in `X`. In practice, it's approximated by sampling `k` random negative samples for each image, normalized by `k * |X|` to get an average correctness score. This score quantifies how well the MLLM can distinguish its own generated descriptions.

#### 3.2.2. Description-generation Prompt Optimization (Algorithm 1)

The optimization follows an iterative "Reflect-and-Modify" strategy, a feedback-driven approach common in advanced prompt engineering:
1. **Initialization:** The process starts with a simple, initial description-generation prompt (e.g., "Describe the object...").
2. **Iterative Refinement:** For each iteration `t`:
 - **Generate Descriptions:** Candidate prompts from the previous iteration are used to generate descriptions for a random mini-batch of unlabeled images `X_t`.
 - **Instance-level Evaluation:** The instance-level classification scoring function `Ψ` is used to evaluate the current set of prompts, identifying "error pairs" `J_error` where the MLLM failed to correctly match an image to its description.
 - **Reflect Operator:** A diagnostic prompt is constructed, presenting the MLLM with the current prompt and examples of `J_error`. The MLLM is asked to analyze why the prompt might have led to these errors (e.g., vagueness, irrelevant focus). This generates a set of potential reasons.
 - **Modify Operator:** Based on the MLLM's reflections, another prompt instructs the MLLM to revise the current description-generation prompt by explicitly addressing the identified shortcomings. This produces multiple candidate revised prompts.
 - **Candidate Evaluation and Selection:** All candidate prompts (from previous iteration and newly generated) are evaluated using `Ψ(X, p)`. Only the `b` top-performing prompts are retained for the next iteration.
3. **Termination:** The process continues for `N` predefined iterations, and the best-performing prompt found throughout the iterations is selected as the final optimized prompt.

Crucially, this entire framework requires only black-box access to the MLLM, meaning it interacts with the model via input-output without needing to modify its internal weights or architecture.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across multiple fine-grained classification datasets and MLLMs validate the effectiveness and robustness of the AutoSEP framework.

#### 4.1. Consistent Outperformance Across Baselines and MLLMs

- AutoSEP consistently outperforms all tested unsupervised baselines (both optimization-free and other prompt optimization methods) across eight fine-grained classification tasks (CUB-200-2011 bird subsets, iNaturalist subsets, Stanford Dogs terrier subsets, and VegFru subsets) and three state-of-the-art MLLMs (Gemini 1.5 Flash, GPT-4o, and Qwen2-VL-72B-Instruct).
- Quantitatively, AutoSEP achieves an average improvement of **13% over standard zero-shot classification** and **3% over the best-performing baselines**. This highlights its significant contribution to boosting MLLM performance in challenging fine-grained scenarios.

#### 4.2. Necessity of Prompt Optimization for Descriptions

- The paper observes that using an initial, human-crafted description generation prompt often yields only marginal improvements or, in some cases, even degrades classification performance compared to vanilla zero-shot. This underscores that simply generating descriptions is insufficient; principled guidance and optimization (as provided by AutoSEP) are crucial for the MLLM to produce informative and task-relevant descriptions. Optimized prompts lead to more accurate and reliable visual descriptions, as qualitatively illustrated in Figure 5.

#### 4.3. Efficacy of Instance-level Classification as an Unsupervised Signal

- A key finding is the superior performance of the proposed instance-level classification accuracy as an unsupervised scoring signal for prompt optimization.
- It significantly outperforms alternative unsupervised scoring strategies, including optimization with randomly assigned labels, optimization with majority vote pseudo-labels, and Self-Supervised Prompt Optimization (SPO) using pairwise output evaluations.
- Notably, SPO, despite its success in text-only tasks, struggles in this multimodal context. The authors hypothesize that MLLMs, while adept at comparing reasoning paths in text, are less consistent in evaluating the "informativeness" of image descriptions for fine-grained classification, leading to noisy feedback for SPO. This emphasizes the robustness of AutoSEP's instance-level distinctiveness measure.

#### 4.4. Positive Learning Dynamics and Correlation

- Analysis of learning dynamics (Figure 3a, 3b) demonstrates that both instance-level and class-wise classification performance consistently improve over increasing optimization iterations. This indicates that the iterative feedback loop effectively refines the prompts.
- A strong positive Pearson correlation (Table 2) between instance-level classification performance and class-wise classification performance is observed across different datasets and MLLMs. This empirical evidence strongly supports the hypothesis that instance-level supervision serves as an effective proxy to guide prompt optimization for better fine-grained class discrimination without labels.

#### 4.5. Increased Prompt Diversity and Semantic Richness

- The diversity score of the generation prompts (Figure 3c) steadily increases throughout the optimization process. This suggests that AutoSEP encourages the MLLM to explore a broader and more semantically rich prompt space, introducing more varied and informative instructions, which contributes to improved classification.

#### 4.6. Impact of Unlabeled Sample Size

- Experiments show a clear positive correlation between the number of unlabeled samples used during optimization and the resulting classification performance (Figure 4). More samples lead to stronger, more robust instance-level signals, enabling more stable optimization and consistent gains. AutoSEP demonstrates reliable performance even with as few as 60 unlabeled samples, highlighting its practicality for various data availability scenarios.

#### 4.7. Computational Feasibility and Generalizability to Other MLLMs

- The computational complexity of AutoSEP is approximately O(60n) MLLM queries per iteration (for typical settings), which is feasible for practical use (e.g., 12 minutes for 60 images with Gemini 1.5 Flash).
- AutoSEP also shows consistent improvements when applied to Gemini 2.0 Flash (Table 4) and even smaller MLLMs like Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct (Table 5) when a stronger MLLM is leveraged for the reflection and modification steps, showcasing its broad applicability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AutoSEP framework represents a significant advancement in enhancing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for challenging visual recognition tasks, particularly in fine-grained image classification. Its potential impact spans both theoretical understanding and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Novelty and Theoretical Contribution

- **First of its kind:** To the best of the authors' knowledge, this is the first work to successfully demonstrate how unlabeled data can be systematically leveraged to improve zero-shot fine-grained image classification performance in a black-box MLLM setting. This addresses a critical unmet need in the field.
- **Bridging SSL and Prompt Engineering:** AutoSEP innovatively adapts core principles from self-supervised learning (SSL), specifically instance-level discrimination, to the domain of prompt optimization. Instead of learning numerical feature representations, it teaches MLLMs *how to generate better textual descriptions* that serve as discriminative "verbal features." This paradigm shift offers a new avenue for MLLM self-improvement.
- **Robust Unsupervised Signal:** The paper's empirical validation of instance-level classification as a highly effective and robust unsupervised signal for prompt optimization is a key contribution, offering a reliable alternative to methods reliant on labeled data or less effective proxy signals.

#### 5.2. Practicality and Broad Applicability

- **Elimination of Labeled Data Dependency:** By operating entirely on unlabeled data for optimization, AutoSEP bypasses the most significant bottleneck in fine-grained classification: the exorbitant cost and effort of collecting and annotating large, high-quality labeled datasets. This makes it a highly practical solution for low-resource languages, niche domains, or rapidly evolving classification tasks.
- **Black-box Compatibility:** The framework's reliance solely on black-box MLLM access is crucial. It enables enhancements for proprietary models (like GPT-4o or Gemini) where internal fine-tuning is impossible, thereby extending the utility of powerful foundation models without needing to modify their architecture or weights.
- **Lightweight and Feasible:** With a demonstrated computational complexity that is practical for real-world scenarios, AutoSEP offers a lightweight solution for MLLM improvement that can be deployed without massive computational overhead typically associated with model training.
- **Enhanced MLLM Capabilities:** By consistently improving zero-shot performance on challenging fine-grained tasks by an average of 13%, AutoSEP makes MLLMs more reliable and powerful tools for precise visual discrimination.

#### 5.3. Potential Impact on Real-World Applications

- **Scientific Research:** Applications in biology, botany, and zoology for automated species identification, monitoring biodiversity, or accelerating scientific discovery by enabling more precise categorization of natural phenomena.
- **Healthcare:** Potentially aiding in fine-grained medical image analysis (e.g., distinguishing subtle disease variants, categorizing tissue types) where expert labeling is extremely expensive and time-consuming.
- **Manufacturing and Quality Control:** Improving automated inspection systems to detect subtle defects or variations in products.
- **Robotics and Autonomous Systems:** Enhancing the perceptual capabilities of robots or autonomous vehicles to make finer distinctions in their environment (e.g., recognizing subtle differences between objects or terrains).
- **Accessibility and Personalization:** Developing more accurate visual aids for visually impaired individuals or creating highly personalized visual content recommendations.

#### 5.4. Acknowledged Limitations

The authors acknowledge that the method's effectiveness relies on the MLLM's ability to accurately capture and verbalize subtle, class-discriminative visual features, which might not hold for extremely fine-grained or difficult-to-articulate distinctions. Additionally, while unsupervised, the method still requires a sufficient number of unlabeled samples for stable and effective prompt optimization, and its performance, though significantly improved, is inherently limited compared to fully supervised methods with explicit class-level guidance.

Despite these limitations, AutoSEP paves the way for a more autonomous and efficient approach to enhancing MLLM perception, fostering a future where powerful multimodal AI can self-improve its understanding of the visual world with minimal human intervention.
