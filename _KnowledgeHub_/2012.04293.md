# CRAFT: A Benchmark for Causal Reasoning About Forces and inTeractions

> [!summary] Summary
> CRAFT introduces a video question answering benchmark to evaluate AI models' causal reasoning about forces and object interactions in dynamic physical environments. The benchmark features question types derived from Force Dynamics Theory (cause, enable, prevent) and reveals a substantial performance gap, with human subjects achieving 76.60% accuracy compared to the best neural baseline's 47.31% in challenging generalization settings.

## Problem

- Existing AI models demonstrate limitations in performing robust causal reasoning about physical forces and object interactions within dynamic visual scenes.
- Many visual question answering (VQA) datasets do not require deep understanding of physics, counterfactual scenarios, or nuanced human-like causal categories beyond simple event prediction.
- AI systems struggle to generalize physical reasoning to novel, unseen environments, often exploiting dataset biases rather than learning transferable principles.

## Method

- The paper introduces **CRAFT**, a large-scale synthetic video QA benchmark comprising approximately 57,000 question-video pairs generated from 10,000 unique Box2D physics simulations.
- It employs a functional program-based question generation system to create descriptive, counterfactual, and novel 'cause, enable, prevent' causal questions, formally defined based on Force Dynamics Theory.
- A 'hard setting' is included where test data originates from entirely unseen scene layouts to rigorously evaluate generalization capabilities, along with sophisticated heuristics and validation steps to mitigate dataset biases.

## Results

- A significant performance gap exists between human subjects (**76.60%** overall accuracy in the hard setting) and the best performing neural baseline (MAC-V, **47.31%**) on the CRAFT benchmark.
- Multimodal models experience a substantial performance degradation when transitioning from the 'easy' to the 'hard' setting, highlighting their struggle to generalize physical reasoning to novel scene layouts.
- Oracle models, provided with a natural language description of the causal graph, achieved up to **86.20%** accuracy (BERT-D), closely approaching human performance and demonstrating the value of explicit causal information.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating cognitive science concepts, specifically the Force Dynamics Theory, into AI benchmarks provides a more human-like and nuanced framework for evaluating causal understanding.
> - The substantial performance of 'oracle' models, which received explicit causal graph descriptions, indicates that robust extraction and reasoning with underlying causal event structures are critical for success.
> - Current multimodal AI models exhibit significant limitations in generalizing their understanding of physical interactions to novel visual contexts not encountered during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of artificial intelligence, specifically at the intersection of computer vision, natural language processing, and cognitive science, by addressing the challenge of causal reasoning in dynamic physical environments.

**Visual Question Answering (VQA) and Temporal Reasoning:** The work is positioned within the context of Visual Question Answering, where models process visual data (images or videos) and natural language questions to provide answers. Existing VQA datasets vary in their use of real-world versus synthetic data and methods of question generation (crowdsourcing vs. automatic). While some prior VQA research has focused on temporal reasoning, the paper notes that many datasets do not require deep understanding of physics or the ability to imagine counterfactual scenarios. Notable exceptions that explore hypothetical questions and intuitive physics include TIWIQ, CLEVRER, CLEVR\_HYP, and TVR. CRAFT aims to extend these efforts by presenting scenes with increased temporal complexity and incorporating a broader spectrum of causal reasoning.

**Causal Reasoning in Cognitive Science:** The paper draws inspiration from cognitive science theories on how humans perceive and reason about causal events. It specifically highlights the Force Dynamics Theory, which proposes that humans represent various causal relationships (such as "cause," "enable," and "prevent") by understanding the patterns of forces between an "affector" and a "patient." This theory moves beyond simpler notions of causality to address complex interactions. Previous cognitive studies have indicated that both adults and young children distinguish these semantic categories. The authors state that CRAFT represents the first attempt to integrate these complex, linguistically-grounded causal relationships into a VQA benchmark for artificial intelligence models.

**Understanding Physics in Artificial Intelligence:** There has been increasing interest in developing AI systems that understand and reason about the physical world. Prior work in this domain includes tasks like predicting object stability, motion trajectories, and planning object configurations. Benchmarks such as PHYRE and Tools focus on 2D environments and predicting action outcomes, while CoPhy deals with counterfactual physical interventions. The paper observes that many of these physical reasoning tasks lack a significant language component. Conversely, some efforts in Natural Language Processing (e.g., PIQA, PROST) evaluate physical commonsense in language models, but without direct visual grounding. CRAFT endeavors to bridge this gap by combining complex physical simulations with natural language questions that require nuanced causal reasoning, thereby merging insights from both the visual reasoning and cognitive science communities. By introducing question types centered on "cause, enable, and prevent," CRAFT aims to test models' ability to acquire a form of commonsense knowledge regarding object interactions and their outcomes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce CRAFT (Causal Reasoning About Forces and inTeractions), a novel video question answering dataset designed to benchmark and advance artificial intelligence models' capabilities in understanding and reasoning about physical forces and object interactions in dynamic scenes.

The motivations underpinning the creation of CRAFT are multi-faceted:

- **Bridging the Human-AI Causal Reasoning Gap:** The authors observe that while humans possess a robust ability to perceive, understand, and reason about causal events, current artificial learning systems demonstrate limitations in processing complex causal scenes and inferring physical actions of objects. A key motivation is to provide a challenging benchmark that quantifies this gap and encourages the development of AI models with more human-like physical and causal understanding.
- **Integrating Vision and Language for Causal Understanding:** The research is motivated by the need to explore the interplay between language and vision in performing plausible causal reasoning. CRAFT aims to analyze the extent to which existing neural models can comprehend and reason about physical and causal relationships between dynamic objects using both visual and textual information.
- **Incorporating Cognitive Science Insights:** Inspired by the Force Dynamics Theory from cognitive linguistics, a significant motivation was to introduce new categories of causal questions beyond previously studied descriptive and counterfactual types. These new categories, "cause," "enable," and "prevent," require models to understand the nuanced causal interactions between objects, thereby pushing towards a deeper, cognitively-informed understanding of causality.
- **Developing a Rigorous Benchmark for Physical Reasoning:** The paper identifies a need for VQA datasets that specifically test "strong reasoning capabilities" and a "deep understanding of physics," including the ability to perform mental simulations for counterfactual scenarios. CRAFT is designed with complex physical interactions and questions that necessitate such advanced reasoning.
- **Addressing Dataset Bias:** A recurrent challenge in VQA dataset creation is minimizing dataset bias, where models might exploit shortcuts rather than genuinely learn the task. The authors were motivated to generate a dataset with sophisticated heuristics and validation steps to mitigate strong biases and ensure that models must truly understand scene dynamics and questions to succeed.
- **Generalization to Unseen Environments:** By introducing a "hard setting" where test data originates from scene layouts not present during training, a core motivation was to evaluate models' generalization capabilities to novel physical configurations and interactions, moving beyond mere memorization of training patterns.

In essence, CRAFT is motivated by the goal of pushing AI systems towards more sophisticated, human-like causal reasoning in physical domains, leveraging insights from cognitive science and addressing limitations in existing benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CRAFT dataset is a synthetically generated video question answering benchmark designed to evaluate temporal and causal reasoning. It comprises approximately 57,000 question and video pairs derived from 10,000 unique videos.

**Dataset Structure and Splits:**
The dataset is divided into training, validation, and test sets with a 60:20:20 ratio based on videos, ensuring no video overlap between splits. Two primary settings are provided:
- **Easy Setting:** Videos in all splits (train, validation, test) can originate from any of the 20 distinct scene layouts.
- **Hard Setting:** The test split exclusively contains videos generated from scene layouts that were not encountered during the training phase (12 layouts for training, 4 for validation, and 4 for testing). This setting is designed to assess generalization capabilities to novel environments.

**Video Generation:**
- **Physics Engine:** Scenes are created using the Box2D physics simulator (Catto, 2010), a 2D rigid body physics engine.
- **Scene Characteristics:** Videos are 10 seconds long with a spatial resolution of 256x256 pixels. There are 20 distinct scene layouts.
- **Object Composition:**
 - **Static Scene Elements:** Seven types (ramp, platform, button, basket, left wall, right wall, ground) are rendered in black. Their attributes (position, orientation) are initialized randomly within predefined intervals for each scene layout and remain fixed.
 - **Dynamic Objects:** Comprise three shapes (cube, triangle, circle), two sizes (small, large), and eight colors. These objects' attributes (position, velocity) continuously change due to physical interactions and gravity.
- **Variation Videos:** For each original simulation, additional "variation videos" are generated by removing each dynamic object individually from the scene. These variations are crucial for determining ground-truth answers to counterfactual and causal questions.

**Event Extraction and Causal Graph Representation:**
To formally represent interactions, various event types are extracted from the simulations: Start, End, Collision, Touch Start, Touch End, and Enter Basket. These events are used to construct a "causal graph," a directed graph where nodes represent events and edges indicate a causal relationship between events, typically due to shared objects or sequential occurrences. This graph serves as a key component for automated question generation.

**Simulation Representation:**
Each simulation instance is represented by its initial state (object attributes at the start), final state (object attributes at the end, capturing causal relationships), and the generated causal graph. This comprehensive representation contains sufficient information to answer CRAFT questions programmatically.

**Question Generation:**
- **Functional Programs:** Questions are generated using a functional program approach, similar to CLEVR, but with an expanded set of functional modules tailored for dynamic scenes (e.g., filtering events, filtering objects based on motion).
- **Language Variety:** To enhance natural language diversity, the system uses multiple paraphrased versions of question templates and integrates synonyms for certain words, chosen randomly during generation while maintaining grammatical correctness.
- **Bias Reduction:** To prevent models from exploiting superficial dataset biases:
 - The generation process employs heuristics to minimize strong biases.
 - A verification step is applied: after initial generation, minor random perturbations are introduced to dynamic objects, and if the answer to a question changes, that question-video pair is excluded. This filters out "counter-intuitive" cases that humans also struggle with.
 - The overall answer distribution is balanced across different simulation and task pairs to reduce shortcuts based on answer frequency.

**Question Types:** CRAFT features 48 distinct question types categorized into three main categories:
- **Descriptive Questions:** Focus on object attributes and temporal aspects, often requiring counting or identifying properties based on events.
- **Counterfactual Questions:** Require inferring what would occur if an object were removed from the scene. Some questions involve exploring multiple counterfactual scenarios simultaneously.
- **Causal Questions:** A unique category in CRAFT, these questions demand an understanding of "cause," "enable," and "prevent." They necessitate comparing the original video's outcome with a counterfactual video (simulated by removing an affector object) to determine the causal relationship.
 - The distinction between "enable," "cause," and "prevent" is formally defined based on the *initial velocity* (intention) of the patient object and the outcome with/without the affector.
 - An affector **enables** if the patient originally intended the task but failed without the affector.
 - An affector **causes** if the patient was not intended to execute the task.
 - An affector **prevents** if the patient was intended to do the task and succeeded without the affector.

**Baseline Models:** The study evaluates a range of baseline models:
- **Heuristic Models:** Random guessers and frequency-based predictors.
- **Text-only Models:** LSTM and BERT (ignoring visual input).
- **Visual-Textual Models:**
 - Single-Frame: LSTM-CNN-F/L, MAC-F/L (using first/last frame).
 - Video: LSTM-CNN-V, MAC-V (using R3D for video features), TVQA, TVQA+ (state-of-the-art video QA models adapted), G-SWM (an object-centric model).
- **Oracle Models:** LSTM-D and BERT-D, which receive a natural language description of the causal graph in addition to the question, providing an upper bound for models with perfect access to event information.

**Human Evaluation:** A small-scale human study was conducted with 101 adults (56 qualified participants) answering 481 randomly selected CRAFT questions to establish a human performance benchmark.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental analysis on the CRAFT dataset revealed several findings regarding the performance of current AI models compared to human capabilities in causal reasoning about physical interactions.

**Performance Gap between Humans and Baselines:**
A primary finding is the substantial performance gap between human subjects and the tested neural baselines. In the "hard setting," humans achieved an overall accuracy of 76.60%, while the best performing neural baseline achieved 47.31% (MAC-V), indicating a gap of over 29 percentage points. Even in the "easy setting," the best baseline (LSTM-CNN-V) reached 53.01%, still significantly below human performance. This suggests that CRAFT effectively challenges existing models.

**Impact of Easy vs. Hard Settings:**
The performance of multimodal models generally degraded substantially from the easy to the hard setting. For instance, LSTM-CNN-V dropped from 53.01% to 46.50% overall. This performance drop was not observed in text-only baselines, as expected. This result indicates that current multimodal methods struggle to generalize their understanding of physical interactions to novel scene layouts not encountered during training, highlighting a limitation in their ability to detect interactions and localize events in new visual contexts.

**Performance Across Question Types:**
- **Descriptive Questions:** Models consistently performed weakest on descriptive questions, with accuracies ranging from 23.5% to 48.12% in the easy setting and 23.2% to 42.9% in the hard setting. This is attributed to the diverse range of answer types required for these questions, including counting, shape, and color identification.
- **Counterfactual and Causal Questions:** Models achieved relatively higher accuracies on these question types, ranging from 32.7% to 61.4% in the easy setting and 30.1% to 56.2% in the hard setting. While better than descriptive questions, these scores still indicate significant room for improvement.

**Baseline Model Comparison:**
- **Heuristic and Text-only Models:** These baselines performed poorly, with text-only models showing an inability to leverage visual information.
- **Single-Frame vs. Video Models:** Single-frame methods generally performed lower than video models (e.g., LSTM-CNN-L vs. LSTM-CNN-V), underscoring the importance of temporal information in the videos for understanding dynamic interactions.
- **Video Models:**
 - LSTM-CNN-V showed reasonable performance in the easy setting but less robust generalization.
 - TVQA and TVQA+ performed less effectively than some other video baselines (e.g., LSTM-CNN-V), suggesting they might be more tailored to video question answering in contexts with rich auxiliary information like subtitles or concept detectors, which are absent in CRAFT.
 - MAC variants, particularly MAC-V, demonstrated the best performance among the video models in the challenging hard setting. This implies that their compositional visual reasoning architecture with spatio-temporal attention may be more effective for generalizing to unseen physical scenarios.
 - G-SWM, an object-centric model, performed poorly in these experiments. The authors suggest this might be due to the complexity of scenes with many objects, making object decomposition challenging, and propose pre-training as a potential solution for future work.

**Oracle Model Performance:**
The oracle models, LSTM-D and BERT-D, which were provided with a natural language description of the simulation's causal graph, significantly outperformed all other tested neural models. BERT-D, in particular, achieved an impressive 86.20% accuracy in the easy setting and 84.90% in the hard setting, closely approaching human performance. Notably, BERT-D even slightly outperformed human subjects on descriptive questions. This finding strongly suggests that models require a robust ability to extract and process the causal interactions between dynamic objects and the environment to succeed in CRAFT.

In summary, the results establish CRAFT as a demanding benchmark that reveals the current limitations of AI in complex physical and causal reasoning, particularly concerning generalization to novel scenarios. The performance of oracle models highlights the critical role of accurately capturing and reasoning with underlying causal event structures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CRAFT benchmark dataset and the accompanying analysis contribute to the field of artificial intelligence with several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Advancing Causal Reasoning in AI:** CRAFT provides a dedicated and challenging benchmark specifically tailored to evaluate causal reasoning about physical forces and interactions. By introducing novel question types (cause, enable, prevent) derived from cognitive linguistics' Force Dynamics Theory, it pushes AI research beyond mere descriptive or simple temporal understanding towards more nuanced and human-like causal inference. This can stimulate the development of AI models with a deeper "commonsense" understanding of how objects interact in the physical world.
- **Highlighting Current AI Limitations:** The substantial gap between human performance and even state-of-the-art neural baselines on CRAFT effectively quantifies current AI limitations. This diagnostic value is crucial for identifying areas where models fall short, particularly in tasks requiring complex temporal analysis, counterfactual simulation, and generalization to unseen physical scenarios (demonstrated by the "hard setting").
- **Driving Research into Generalization and Robustness:** The significant performance drop observed in the "hard setting" underscores the challenge of achieving generalization in physical reasoning. This aspect of CRAFT can drive research into more robust and generalizable AI architectures that do not simply memorize training examples but learn transferable principles of physics and causality, which is vital for real-world applications.
- **Fostering Interdisciplinary Research:** By integrating concepts from cognitive science (Force Dynamics Theory) with computer vision and natural language processing, CRAFT encourages interdisciplinary research. It provides a common ground for cognitive scientists to formalize human causal understanding for computational modeling and for AI researchers to develop models inspired by human cognition.
- **Guiding Model Development:** The superior performance of the "oracle" models, which were provided with explicit causal graph information, indicates the critical importance of effective causal event extraction and representation. This finding can guide future model development towards architectures that can implicitly or explicitly build such causal representations from raw video data and integrate them with language understanding. It suggests potential benefits for neuro-symbolic approaches or object-centric models capable of decomposing complex scenes into interpretable components.
- **Foundation for Real-World Applications:** Improvements in causal reasoning capabilities, as benchmarked by CRAFT, have potential implications for a wide range of real-world AI applications. These include:
 - **Robotics:** Robots that can predict the causal outcomes of their actions and understand how to manipulate objects to achieve goals.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Systems that can reason about potential collisions, understand why certain events occur, and plan interventions.
 - **Intelligent Assistants:** Assistants capable of answering complex "why" and "what if" questions about dynamic events.
 - **Simulation and Gaming:** More realistic and interactive virtual environments that adhere to intuitive physical laws and causal relationships.

In conclusion, CRAFT serves as a valuable tool for pushing the boundaries of AI in understanding the physical world through causal reasoning, offering a clear measure of progress and pointing toward promising directions for future research.
