# DreamSim: Learning New Dimensions of Human Visual Similarity using Synthetic Data

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamSim introduces a new perceptual similarity metric that aligns with human judgments, especially for mid-level visual variations, by leveraging a novel synthetic dataset (NIGHTS) created with generative AI and efficient fine-tuning of large vision models. It achieves 96.16% accuracy in predicting human judgments on its curated dataset, outperforming prior metrics and demonstrating enhanced capabilities in various computer vision applications.

## Problem

- Existing low-level perceptual metrics (e.g., LPIPS, DISTS) primarily focus on pixel/patch-level differences and fail to capture holistic, mid-level visual variations like object pose or spatial layout.
- Embeddings from large vision models (e.g., CLIP, DINO) excel at high-level semantic similarity but are not consistently aligned with human perception of finer-grained visual structure and mid-level nuances.
- A significant lack of human judgment datasets specifically designed to capture and evaluate mid-level visual similarity, hindering the development of better metrics.

## Method

- Developed NIGHTS, a novel dataset of over 20,000 human-judged image triplets generated using Stable Diffusion, specifically designed to capture mid-level variations and filtered for unanimous, 'cognitively impenetrable' judgments.
- Employed an ensemble of state-of-the-art large vision models (DINO, CLIP, OpenCLIP) as feature extractors.
- Fine-tuned the ensemble using Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) and a triplet loss objective on the NIGHTS dataset to align the metric with human perceptual judgments.

## Results

- DreamSim achieved 96.16% accuracy in predicting human judgments on the NIGHTS dataset, outperforming prior learned metrics like LPIPS (70.7%) and untuned large vision models like DINO (90.1%).
- The metric demonstrated improved performance on low-level distortion datasets (BAPPS) and competitive results on image quality assessment benchmarks, despite being optimized for mid-level differences.
- DreamSim showed superior performance in image retrieval tasks, finding more 'meaningful neighbors' by balancing low-level appearance and high-level semantics, and when used as a loss function, it better preserved original image characteristics during feature inversion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative AI can be used to systematically create psychophysical stimuli for studying specific dimensions of human visual perception, overcoming data scarcity.
> - Efficient fine-tuning methods like LoRA effectively adapt large pre-trained vision models to align with nuanced human judgments using relatively smaller, targeted datasets.
> - Human visual similarity judgments often prioritize appearance-based, mid-level features over abstract semantic categories, a distinction not fully captured by existing metrics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The ability to perceive and quantify visual similarity is fundamental to both human cognition and artificial intelligence. In computer vision, accurately measuring image similarity is crucial for a myriad of applications, including image retrieval, compression, generation, and quality assessment. This paper addresses a critical gap in the current landscape of perceptual similarity metrics.

Historically, image similarity has been measured using **classical low-level metrics** like Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR) and Structural Similarity Index (SSIM). While these metrics are useful for assessing pixel-wise differences and structural distortions, they fall short in capturing the holistic, semantic, or "mid-level" differences that humans readily perceive, such as changes in object pose, spatial layout, or subtle semantic variations.

The advent of deep learning led to **learned perceptual metrics**, such as LPIPS (Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity) and DISTS (Deep Image Structure and Texture Similarity). These metrics leverage features extracted from pre-trained convolutional neural networks (CNNs) like VGG or AlexNet and are tuned on datasets of human judgments, showing improved alignment with human perception over classical methods. However, as the authors note, these learned metrics often operate at the patch level (e.g., 64x64 patches for LPIPS), still emphasizing low-level color and texture rather than broader image understanding.

More recently, the rise of **large vision models** or "foundation models" like CLIP, DINO, and MAE, often based on transformer architectures, has provided powerful image-level embeddings. These models, trained on vast amounts of data using self-supervised or contrastive learning (e.g., image-text pairs for CLIP), excel at capturing high-level semantic similarities and differences between images. They have been increasingly adopted for measuring image-to-image distances in various applications. However, their alignment with human *fine-grained visual structure* and *mid-level* perceptual judgments has remained underexplored and, as this paper shows, imperfect. Existing high-level human datasets, like THINGS, focus primarily on broad categorical similarity (e.g., "is a kitchen more like a giraffe or a beach?"), not the nuanced visual attributes.

"DreamSim" positions itself directly in this landscape by seeking to **bridge the gap** between these low-level patch-based metrics and high-level categorical embeddings. The core innovation lies in its focus on *mid-level variations*—changes in object pose, perspective, foreground color, number of items, and object shape—which are neither purely pixel-level nor solely semantic category-level. To achieve this, the authors tackle a major data challenge: the scarcity of human judgment datasets that capture these specific mid-level distinctions. They ingeniously leverage **recent text-to-image generative models (Stable Diffusion)** to create a novel dataset of synthetic images that exhibit controlled mid-level perturbations, allowing for the systematic collection of relevant human similarity judgments. This approach to dataset creation is a significant contribution, demonstrating a new paradigm for psychophysical research in the age of generative AI. By fine-tuning large vision models on this new, targeted human dataset, DreamSim aims to create a metric that truly reflects human holistic visual perception, offering a more nuanced and accurate tool for assessing image similarity in a wide range of computer vision tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a new perceptual similarity metric, named DreamSim, that assesses images holistically and aligns more closely with human judgments, particularly for mid-level visual variations.** This metric aims to bridge the gap between existing low-level, patch-based metrics and high-level, semantic-categorical comparisons.

The key motivations driving this objective are:

- **Limitations of Existing Metrics:** Current state-of-the-art perceptual metrics, such as LPIPS and DISTS, primarily focus on low-level visual properties like pixel colors and textures. While effective for tasks like image compression artifact detection, they fail to capture more complex, mid-level similarities and differences related to image layout, object pose, object shape, and subtle semantic content.
- **Incomplete Alignment of Large Vision Models:** While embeddings from large vision models like DINO and CLIP are increasingly used for measuring image distances and excel at broad semantic similarity, their alignment with human perception of richer, fine-grained, and mid-level visual structures remains unclear and often suboptimal. These models might group images by category but miss crucial perceptual nuances within those categories.
- **Need for Mid-Level Similarity Data:** A significant barrier to developing a metric that captures mid-level similarity is the lack of appropriate datasets of human judgments. Prior datasets either focus on elementary pixel-level distortions (e.g., blurring, noise) or highly abstract categorical distinctions. There was no established benchmark for judging visual similarity across diverse mid-level variations.
- **Leveraging Generative AI for Data Creation:** The recent advancements in text-to-image generative models (like Stable Diffusion) presented a novel opportunity to overcome this data scarcity. These models can generate diverse images adhering to specific prompts, allowing for the creation of controlled variations (e.g., different poses of the same object) that are ideal for studying mid-level similarity perception. This allows for the generation of "cognitively impenetrable" stimuli—image sets that lead to consistent and intuitive decisions across observers.
- **Enhancing Downstream Computer Vision Applications:** A more human-aligned perceptual metric has direct practical implications. It can serve as a better loss function for image generation and reconstruction tasks, ensuring that generated images are not only visually plausible but also perceptually similar to human preferences. It can also improve image retrieval systems, allowing for more intuitive and perceptually meaningful search results.

In essence, the paper aims to create a more sophisticated and human-centric way for AI systems to "see" and compare images, moving beyond purely technical measures to capture the nuanced visual understanding inherent in human perception.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of DreamSim is characterized by a two-pronged approach: the creation of a novel human-judged synthetic dataset and the subsequent learning of an improved perceptual metric through fine-tuning state-of-the-art deep learning models.

#### 3.1. Perceptual Dataset Collection: NIGHTS (Novel Image Generations with Human-Tested Similarity)

To address the lack of suitable data for mid-level similarity, the authors developed NIGHTS, a dataset of human similarity judgments over image triplets.

- **Synthetic Image Generation:**
 - **Tool:** Stable Diffusion v1.4, a powerful text-to-image generative model, was used.
 - **Process:** Images were generated using prompts of the structure "An image of a <category>". Categories were drawn from popular classification datasets like ImageNet, CIFAR-10/100, Oxford 102 Flower, Food-101, and SUN397. This ensured diversity across objects and scenes.
 - **Goal:** The aim was to produce images with underlying semantic commonality but showcasing diverse mid-level variations such as pose, perspective, style, color, number of items, and object shape. This qualitatively differs from prior datasets that focused on low-level (e.g., blurring) or high-level (e.g., categorical) changes.
 - **Triplets:** An initial set of 100,000 image triplets was generated, each consisting of a reference image (x) and two "distortions" (x̃0, x̃1) generated from the *same* prompt.

- **Human Perceptual Judgments:**
 - **Cognitive Impenetrability:** A critical design principle was to collect judgments that are *automatic, stable, and shared* across humans, minimizing subjective biases. This is referred to as "cognitively impenetrable."
 - **Two Alternative Forced Choice (2AFC) Task:**
 - **Setup:** Participants (via Amazon Mechanical Turk - AMT) were shown a reference image (x) and two distorted versions (x̃0, x̃1). They were asked to choose which distorted image was more similar to the reference.
 - **Filtering for Unanimity:** To ensure cognitive impenetrability, the dataset was iteratively filtered over 10 rounds. Only triplets that received a *unanimous vote* (e.g., all judges consistently chose x̃0 over x̃1) were retained. This rigorous filtering process, combined with sentinel tasks (e.g., showing an identical image vs. a completely different one) for quality control, resulted in 20,019 high-quality triplets with an average of 7 unanimous votes each.
 - **Just Noticeable Difference (JND) Task (Validation):**
 - **Purpose:** To corroborate the 2AFC findings and assess the reliability of judgments under different experimental settings.
 - **Setup:** On a subset of 411 triplets from the 2AFC test set, participants were shown interleaved image pairs (x → v → x̃ → v) and asked if the first and third images (x and x̃), and second and fourth images (v and v) were identical. A standardized 500ms viewing time and masking were used to prompt for an instinctive decision.
 - **Selection:** JND triplets were specifically chosen where humans found one distortion to be identical and the other to be noticeably different, providing a clear boundary.

#### 3.2. Perceptual Metric Learning: DreamSim

The collected NIGHTS dataset was then used to train a new perceptual metric.

- **Candidate Embeddings and Metrics:** The authors evaluated various state-of-the-art models as potential feature extractors for measuring image similarity:
 - **Prior Learned Metrics:** LPIPS and DISTS (CNN-based).
 - **Large Vision Models:** DINO, CLIP, OpenCLIP, and MAE (Transformer-based ViT architectures).
 - **Distance Metric:** Cosine distance was primarily used for transformer-based embeddings (1 - cos(fθ(x), fθ(x̃))) to compute similarity between image features. LPIPS and DISTS have their own internal distance calculations.

- **Learning an Improved Metric:**
 - **Objective Function:** A hinge loss (equivalent to triplet loss) was employed. For a given triplet (x, x̃0, x̃1) with human label y (indicating x̃y is more similar to x), the objective was to maximize the difference between the distances, making the distance to the human-preferred image (d_y) smaller than the distance to the other image (d_1-y) by a margin (m=0.05).
 L(y, ŷ) = max(0, m - (d0 - d1) * ȳ), where ȳ is -1 or 1 based on y.
 - **Tuning Strategy:** Given the large number of parameters in ViT backbones and the relatively small size of the NIGHTS dataset, efficient fine-tuning methods were explored:
 - **MLP Head:** An initial attempt involved adding a 1-hidden-layer MLP on top of pre-trained embeddings. This proved less effective.
 - **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA):** This technique was found to be significantly more effective. LoRA enables fine-tuning large pre-trained models by injecting small, trainable low-rank matrices into the original model's weights, drastically reducing the number of trainable parameters (only ~0.67% of total parameters were tuned). This allows for efficient adaptation without full fine-tuning.
 - **Feature Concatenation and Ensemble:** The highest performance was achieved by concatenating features from the best-performing models on the dataset (DINO, CLIP, and OpenCLIP) into a single ensemble. This ensemble was then fine-tuned using LoRA. The resulting best LoRA-tuned ensemble model is **DreamSim**.

This comprehensive methodology, from novel synthetic data generation to efficient model fine-tuning, enabled the creation of DreamSim, a metric specifically designed to capture the elusive mid-level aspects of human visual similarity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings across human-metric alignment, attribute sensitivity, and practical applications.

#### 4.1. Alignment with Human Judgments

- **Superiority of DreamSim on NIGHTS:** The most prominent finding is that DreamSim, the LoRA-tuned ensemble of DINO, CLIP, and OpenCLIP features, achieves **96.16% accuracy** in predicting human judgments on the NIGHTS dataset. This significantly outperforms existing metrics:
 - **Low-level metrics (PSNR, SSIM):** Around 57% accuracy (barely above chance).
 - **Prior learned metrics (LPIPS, DISTS):** LPIPS at 70.7%, DISTS at 86.0%.
 - **Untuned large vision models:** DINO B/16 at 90.1%, OpenCLIP B/32 at 87.6%. Even the untuned ensemble reached 90.8%.
- **Effectiveness of LoRA Fine-tuning:** Fine-tuning with LoRA dramatically improved performance over using raw embeddings or tuning with an MLP head. For instance, the ensemble model jumped from 90.8% (untuned) to 96.2% (LoRA-tuned).
- **Correlation between 2AFC and JND:** The high correlation between metric performance on the 2AFC and JND tasks validates the robustness and generality of the human judgments collected in NIGHTS, indicating that a coherent sense of similarity is captured across different experimental settings.

#### 4.2. Generalization to Other Similarity Tasks

- **BAPPS (Low-level similarity):** Training on NIGHTS generally improved DreamSim's alignment with human judgments on the BAPPS dataset, which focuses on low-level distortions. This suggests that while optimized for mid-level, DreamSim still implicitly captures relevant low-level information.
- **THINGS (High-level conceptual similarity):** Interestingly, fine-tuning on NIGHTS *decreased* alignment on the THINGS dataset, which assesses high-level conceptual similarity (e.g., object categories). This implies that human "automatic judgments" (as captured in NIGHTS) prioritize appearance-based visual differences over abstract semantic categories, a nuanced distinction.
- **Image Quality Assessment (IQA) Benchmarks (TID2013, KADID-10k):** DreamSim proved competitive with specialized IQA metrics (like LPIPS and FSIM) and outperformed most low-level metrics and base ViT models, despite not being trained for IQA directly. This further supports its versatility in capturing perceptually relevant information.

#### 4.3. Image Attribute Sensitivity Analysis

- **Robustness to Orientation:** DreamSim was least affected by flipping the reference image, indicating robustness to simple orientation changes.
- **Sensitivity to Core Attributes:** Ablating color, shading, or structure (e.g., using contours) had a larger impact on decisions, suggesting these attributes are critical for DreamSim.
- **Foreground Dominance:** Removing the foreground content (color, shading, texture) had a significantly larger impact than removing the background, highlighting that DreamSim places more importance on foreground objects for similarity recognition.
- **Alignment with Semantic Features:** Compared to LPIPS/DISTS, DreamSim is more sensitive to individual object instances ("things") than background elements ("stuff"). Compared to DINO/OpenCLIP, DreamSim retains higher sensitivity to color and layout. It showed particular alignment with the "presence of people" category in COCO triplets.

#### 4.4. Applications

- **Image Retrieval:** DreamSim consistently retrieved "meaningful neighbors" in ImageNet-R and MS-COCO datasets. Unlike baselines that sometimes focused solely on low-level (e.g., background color with LPIPS) or high-level (e.g., semantic class with OpenCLIP), DreamSim demonstrated a balanced sensitivity to a continuum of visual traits. User studies confirmed preference for DreamSim's retrievals (36.8% for ImageNet-R, 33.6% for COCO).
- **Feature Inversion:** When used as an objective for image optimization or guiding generative models (Deep Image Prior, Guided Diffusion), DreamSim better preserved the original colors, shapes, semantics, and global layout of target images compared to other metrics, showcasing its holistic understanding.
- **k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN) Classification:** DreamSim outperformed other baselines on the ObjectNet dataset and was competitive with DINO on ImageNet100, indicating its learned features are effective for classification based on visual similarity.
- **Dimensionality Reduction:** Performance remained high even after PCA dimensionality reduction, suggesting that the improvement isn't solely due to a higher-dimensional feature space but rather the enhanced capacity and diverse model priors obtained from ensembling and fine-tuning.

In summary, the findings establish DreamSim as a highly human-aligned perceptual metric, particularly effective at capturing nuanced mid-level visual similarities. Its performance across various benchmarks and applications demonstrates its practical utility and superior alignment compared to previous state-of-the-art methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "DreamSim" research paper presents a significant advancement in the field of computer vision and its intersection with human perception. Its contributions have multifaceted significance and potential impact:

#### 5.1. Advancement in Perceptual Similarity Metrics

- **Bridging the Mid-Level Gap:** DreamSim successfully addresses a long-standing challenge by developing a metric that reliably captures *mid-level* visual similarities—such as variations in object pose, perspective, layout, and fine-grained semantic content—which are beyond the scope of traditional low-level metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) and more granular than the high-level categorical distinctions typically handled by large vision models (CLIP, DINO). This provides a more nuanced and human-aligned tool for evaluating image similarity.
- **State-of-the-Art Alignment:** Achieving over 96% accuracy in predicting human judgments on a challenging dataset of synthetic image triplets sets a new benchmark for human-metric alignment in perceptual similarity, highlighting the effectiveness of the proposed fine-tuning and ensembling approach.

#### 5.2. Novel Data Collection Paradigm

- **Synthetic Data for Psychophysics:** The creation of the NIGHTS dataset is a groundbreaking contribution. It demonstrates a powerful and scalable methodology for generating controlled, diverse, and perceptually rich stimuli using modern text-to-image diffusion models. This approach overcomes the limitations of traditional dataset collection, which often struggles with generating sufficient variations or controlling specific attributes.
- **Cognitively Impenetrable Judgments:** The rigorous filtering process for unanimous human judgments on NIGHTS is crucial. It ensures that the collected data reflects "instinctive" and broadly shared human perception, rather than subjective or cognitively mediated interpretations, thereby enhancing the reliability and validity of the dataset for training robust perceptual models.

#### 5.3. Implications for AI and Computer Vision Applications

- **Improved Evaluation for Generative Models:** A more human-aligned similarity metric like DreamSim is invaluable for evaluating the output of image generation, reconstruction, and manipulation models. It allows researchers to quantify how "perceptually similar" generated content is to a target, going beyond simple pixel accuracy to assess whether the AI's output truly resonates with human visual preferences. This can guide the development of future generative models towards more human-preferred results.
- **Enhanced Image Retrieval and Search:** DreamSim's superior performance in image retrieval, finding more "meaningful neighbors" that balance low-level appearance and high-level semantics, can lead to more intuitive and effective visual search engines and content recommendation systems.
- **Better Loss Functions and Optimization Objectives:** DreamSim can be adopted as a more sophisticated loss function for various computer vision tasks that require perceptual consistency, such as image super-resolution, style transfer, and inverse problems. Its ability to guide feature inversion towards more perceptually faithful results highlights its potential in guiding image optimization.
- **Insights into Model Behavior:** The analysis of DreamSim's sensitivity to various image attributes (e.g., foreground objects, color, structure) provides valuable insights into what aspects of an image the metric prioritizes. This helps in understanding how large vision models, when fine-tuned on human data, align their internal representations with human visual hierarchies.

#### 5.4. Broader Scientific Impact

- **A Bridge to Cognitive Science:** This work contributes to the interdisciplinary understanding between computer vision and cognitive science. By systematically collecting human judgments on controlled synthetic stimuli and aligning AI models with these judgments, it offers new avenues for exploring the mechanisms of human visual perception and how they can be computationalized.
- **Template for Future Perceptual Studies:** The methodology for creating NIGHTS can serve as a template for developing other specialized perceptual datasets, allowing researchers to explore different dimensions of human visual cognition that are currently uncaptured by existing benchmarks.

While acknowledging limitations such as potential biases inherited from underlying foundation models and a dataset primarily focused on object-centric domains, DreamSim represents a significant leap forward. It underscores the power of combining advanced generative AI with rigorous human psychophysics to create more perceptually intelligent computer vision systems, paving the way for AI that "sees" more like humans do.
