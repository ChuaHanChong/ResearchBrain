# Consensus-based optimization (CBO): Towards Global Optimality in Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Consensus-Based Optimization (CBO) to robotics, demonstrating its ability to achieve global optimality in challenging trajectory and policy optimization problems. CBO consistently finds lower-cost solutions by avoiding local minima, outperforming common zero-order methods like MPPI and CMA-ES in complex, high-dimensional scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing trajectory and policy optimization methods in robotics, including gradient-based and popular zero-order techniques, frequently converge to local minima, leading to sub-optimal robot behaviors.
- Robotics problems often involve highly non-convex cost landscapes, high-dimensional control variables, and complex dynamics (e.g., contact interactions), which are difficult for local optimizers to navigate.
- Current state-of-the-art approaches often rely on extensive heuristics, careful cost function design, or large human demonstration datasets, which can be time-consuming and lack guarantees for optimal solutions.

## Method

- A unified framework is proposed to analyze existing zero-order methods and **CBO**, focusing on minimizing a ==smoothed surrogate objective function== by iteratively updating a control sequence distribution.
- CBO employs a ==non-parametric== approach where a population of particles explores the solution space, with each particle's dynamics governed by a ==stochastic differential equation (SDE)== that attracts it towards a cost-weighted ==consensus point==.
- The algorithm's exploration term is proportional to the particle's distance from the consensus point, promoting diverse exploration in less-visited regions and allowing the empirical particle distribution to adapt to complex cost landscape shapes.

## Results

- CBO achieved significantly lower costs than CMA and MPPI in long-horizon planning, successfully navigating cluttered spaces and avoiding local minima where baselines failed.
- On the double cartpole balancing task, CBO exhibited the best average performance with lower variance in best costs over iterations, while maintaining competitive per-iteration computation times.
- For demonstration-free humanoid locomotion, CBO found significantly lower costs compared to MPPI and CMA-ES, enabling successful and optimal locomotion behaviors for a **23 DoF** humanoid.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - CBO's particle dynamics are driven by attraction to a consensus point, not direct gradients, enabling particles to traverse local minima and achieve global exploration.
> - The non-parametric nature of CBO, represented by the particle population, overcomes the 'curse of distribution parametrization' of methods relying on fixed-form distributions like Gaussians.
> - Theoretical analysis demonstrates CBO's convergence to the global optimum under specific conditions, providing a mathematical foundation for its global search capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Trajectory and policy optimization are central to developing autonomous robotic systems capable of performing complex tasks. Current state-of-the-art approaches often rely on heuristics, careful design of cost functions, and extensive tuning of objectives. While alternative methods involving learning from human demonstrations or large datasets have shown promise, they do not inherently guarantee optimality and may not explore the full range of valid solutions. Consequently, there is a recognized need for robust optimization algorithms that can generate favorable robot behaviors without relying on human input, especially in high state-control dimensions and long planning horizons.

Traditionally, robotics has leveraged gradient-based optimization methods. However, these methods often encounter difficulties with problems involving complex contact interactions, as rigid contact introduces non-differentiability and hybrid dynamics. The emergence of parallelized simulation environments has enabled the efficient evaluation of numerous control trajectories, leading to increased interest in zero-order (gradient-free) optimization techniques. These methods bypass the need for differentiable simulators and are naturally suited for contact-implicit scenarios.

Popular zero-order optimization techniques in robotics include Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), and Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES). These algorithms typically operate by iteratively sampling from a local distribution, often a multivariate Gaussian centered at the current best estimate. The update mechanisms in these methods can be interpreted as approximating gradient descent steps, which inherently limits them to finding local optima. This means they are prone to getting stuck in sub-optimal solutions, particularly in objective landscapes with multiple local minima.

This paper introduces Consensus-Based Optimization (CBO) to the field of robotics as a potential solution to these limitations. CBO offers a distinct approach by employing a population of particles that explores the solution space globally, rather than relying on local sampling. While CBO has demonstrated success in general global optimization benchmarks and applications, its application to robotics problems, particularly in trajectory optimization, is novel. The work aims to provide a unifying mathematical framework for understanding meta-heuristic methods in global optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce Consensus-Based Optimization (CBO) to the robotics community and demonstrate its effectiveness in achieving global optimality for challenging trajectory and policy optimization problems.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current robotic optimization paradigms:

- **Local Optimality of Existing Methods:** Many prevalent optimization techniques in robotics, including both traditional gradient-based methods and popular zero-order methods like MPPI, CEM, and CMA-ES, are fundamentally local in nature. They tend to converge to local minima of the cost function, which can lead to sub-optimal or undesirable robot behaviors in complex, multi-modal environments.
- **Challenges with Complex Environments:** Robotics problems often feature highly non-convex cost landscapes, high-dimensional decision variables (control sequences over long horizons), and complex dynamics, including contact interactions. These characteristics make it difficult for local optimizers to escape suboptimal regions and find the true global optimum.
- **Reliance on Heuristics and Demonstrations:** State-of-the-art methods frequently depend on heavily engineered heuristics, intricate cost function designs, or large datasets from human demonstrations. This reliance can be time-consuming, difficult to tune, and may not guarantee optimal solutions or facilitate the discovery of the full set of valid robot behaviors.
- **Lack of Global Guarantees:** There is a need for optimization algorithms that can provide theoretical guarantees or practical demonstrations of convergence to global optima, especially as robotic systems tackle increasingly complex and safety-critical tasks.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines several key contributions:

- **Reinterpretation of Zero-Order Methods:** To frame the problem, the authors recontextualize widely used zero-order optimization methods in robotics within a general global optimization framework. This reinterpretation aims to systematically highlight their inherent limitations regarding global optimality.
- **Introduction and Explanation of CBO:** The paper provides an intuitive explanation of how CBO functions, detailing its mechanisms for global exploration and how it addresses the weaknesses of existing zero-order methods. It also draws connections between CBO and current methods through the lens of diffusion process dynamics.
- **Demonstration of CBO's Scalability and Performance:** A central objective is to empirically demonstrate CBO's capability to identify globally meaningful solutions in demanding robotic trajectory optimization tasks. This includes high-dimensional humanoid models and problems with long time horizons, illustrating how CBO can find global solutions where local sampling methods often fail.
- **First Application to Robotics:** The paper positions itself as the first to specifically demonstrate the applicability of CBO to robotics problems and to empirically compare its performance against other prominent zero-order optimization methods in this domain.
- **Unifying Framework:** The work aims to offer a rigorous mathematical framework under which various meta-heuristic global optimization methods can be formally analyzed.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research establishes a unified framework for global optimization, under which existing zero-order methods and the proposed Consensus-Based Optimization (CBO) are analyzed. The core idea is to minimize a smoothed surrogate objective function, $\xi(J | U)$, defined as the integral of the original cost function $J$ over a probability distribution $U$ of control sequences. The optimization then proceeds by iteratively updating this distribution $U$.

**Critique of Existing Zero-Order Methods (MPPI, CEM, CMA-ES):**
The paper first casts popular zero-order methods like MPPI, CEM, and CMA-ES into this framework to highlight their limitations in achieving global optimality.
- **Path Integral (PI) Methods (e.g., MPPI):** These methods update the mean of a control distribution (typically Gaussian) by weighting sampled trajectories according to their costs. The samples are drawn locally around the current mean with a fixed covariance. The update rule can be recovered by minimizing the surrogate function under a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence constraint, effectively approximating a gradient descent on the mean. A critical observation is that particles are "forgotten" after each iteration, leading to homogeneous exploration and lacking individual memory.
- **Covariance Matrix Adaptation (CMA-ES) and Cross Entropy Method (CEM):** These methods extend PI by adapting the covariance matrix of the sampling distribution, allowing the search to concentrate along promising directions. CMA-ES and CEM update both the mean and covariance based on a subset of elite samples. While this helps mitigate the "curse of dimensionality" by focusing samples, the parameterized (Gaussian) nature of the distribution restricts its expressive flexibility. Gaussian distributions, with their inherent symmetry, may not accurately capture complex, irregular solution spaces (e.g., asymmetric tails or disconnected regions), which the authors refer to as the "curse of distribution parametrization."

**Consensus-Based Optimization (CBO) Approach:**
CBO addresses the identified limitations by adopting a non-parametric approach using a population of particles to represent the distribution $U$. Instead of explicit parametrization, CBO relies on particle dynamics defined by a stochastic differential equation (SDE) for each particle $u^{(r,i)}$ at iteration $r$:

$du^{(r,i)}_{0:T} = -\lambda (u^{(r,i)}_{0:T} - \bar{u}^{(r)}_{0:T}) dr + \sigma |u^{(r,i)}_{0:T} - \bar{u}^{(r)}_{0:T}| dW^{(r,i)}$

Here:
- $\lambda > 0$ is a decay rate, governing the strength of attraction towards the consensus point.
- $\bar{u}^{(r)}_{0:T}$ is the **consensus point**, calculated as a weighted average of all particles, where weights $w^{(r,i)}$ are determined by a softmax function of their trajectory costs (Equation 9).
- $\sigma \ge 0$ controls the intensity of exploration via Brownian motion $dW^{(r,i)}$. The exploration term is proportional to the distance of the particle from the consensus point, meaning particles farther away explore more.

The CBO algorithm (Algorithm 1) outlines a discrete-time Euler-Maruyama integration of this SDE, where particles are iteratively updated by a drift term towards the consensus point and a particle-dependent exploration term. The algorithm is designed for parallel computation on GPUs, with empirical runtime comparisons showing it is competitive with MPPI and CMA-ES per iteration.

**Advantages of CBO (as theorized by the authors):**
- **Agnostic to Local Optima:** Particle dynamics are driven by attraction to the consensus point, not direct gradients of the objective, allowing particles to traverse local minima.
- **Inertia and Diverse Exploration:** Particles retain their positions and explore around them, leading to more diverse and adaptive exploration than methods that reset particles. The exploration term's dependence on distance to the consensus point means exploration is higher in less explored regions.
- **Non-parametric Distribution:** The empirical distribution formed by the particles can adapt to complex, irregular shapes in the cost landscape, overcoming the "curse of distribution parametrization" of Gaussian-based methods.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:** Under certain "inverse continuity" and "relaxed Lipschitz continuous" assumptions on the cost function, CBO is theoretically proven to converge to the global optimum. A condition `2λ > n_a × T × σ^2` (where `n_a` is control dimension and `T` is horizon) is derived to ensure convergence.

**Experimental Setup:**
The authors evaluate CBO against MPPI and a simplified CMA implementation across three challenging robotics scenarios, ensuring fair comparisons by using the same environment, initial particle populations, and aligned hyperparameters (e.g., temperature $\rho$, exploration $\sigma$).
1. **Long Horizon Planning:** A point mass navigating a cluttered, confined space with deliberately placed local minima. High-dimensional control (200 variables) for a 100-step horizon. Cost includes running cost, control regularization, and penalties for not reaching a tunnel or hitting obstacles.
2. **Double Cartpole:** A dynamic balance problem for a highly underactuated system. Long planning horizon (16s, 100 control knots) with a small maximum control force, making it challenging. Cost is terminal, based on pole tip position, alignment, cart position, and velocities.
3. **Humanoid Locomotion:** A high-dimensional (23 DoF) Unitree G1 humanoid. The task is demonstration-free locomotion within 2 seconds using 6 zero-order hold knots as PD target inputs. The cost is purely terminal, based on the se(3) difference between the final and target base configurations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results consistently demonstrate that CBO outperforms MPPI and CMA-ES in minimizing costs across the three challenging robotic trajectory optimization problems.

- **Long Horizon Planning:**
 - CBO achieved significantly lower costs compared to CMA and MPPI.
 - Visualizations (Figure 4) indicated that CBO successfully navigated the tunnel, circumventing local minima (e.g., the left wall barrier) and obstacles. In contrast, MPPI and CMA became trapped in local minima and failed to reach the intended goal tunnel.
 - The authors noted that while CBO performed substantially better, it still did not reach the exact goal in this "extremely difficult" scenario, suggesting that even larger particle populations might be necessary for such high complexity problems.

- **Double Cartpole:**
 - CBO exhibited the best average performance with relatively low variance in the best cost over iterations (Figure 6).
 - CMA-ES showed larger fluctuations in cost during the optimization process, indicating less consistent performance.
 - A runtime comparison (Table I) on this task showed that CBO's per-iteration computation time (336.43 ms) was comparable to CMA-ES (351.28 ms) and MPPI (372.48 ms), suggesting that its superior performance does not incur a significant computational penalty per iteration.

- **Humanoid Locomotion:**
 - CBO achieved significantly lower costs than both MPPI and CMA-ES for the demonstration-free locomotion task (Figure 7).
 - Although CBO showed a larger variance in its loss compared to baselines, its best runs still yielded much lower costs, indicating its ability to find superior solutions.
 - Snapshots of a CBO-optimized trajectory (Figure 8) illustrated a successful locomotion behavior for the humanoid, reaching the target configuration from a standing start.

In summary, the empirical evidence supports the claim that CBO's global exploration mechanism and non-parametric distribution adaptation enable it to find better solutions and avoid local minima where other common zero-order methods often fail, without imposing a substantial per-iteration computational overhead. The theoretical analysis also provides a mathematical foundation for CBO's convergence properties.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work holds several significant implications for the field of robotics and optimization:

- **Advancement in Global Optimization for Robotics:** The introduction of Consensus-Based Optimization (CBO) to robotics provides a new and theoretically grounded framework for tackling global trajectory and policy optimization problems. This is a critical step towards addressing the inherent limitations of local optimizers that frequently dominate current robotic control strategies.
- **Overcoming Local Minima:** The consistent demonstration of CBO's ability to escape local minima in complex, high-dimensional, and challenging robotic scenarios (long-horizon planning, underactuated systems, humanoid locomotion) is a substantial contribution. This capability can lead to the discovery of more robust, efficient, and novel robot behaviors that might be inaccessible to methods prone to local optima.
- **Reduced Reliance on Heuristics and Demonstrations:** By offering a method capable of achieving global optimality, CBO has the potential to lessen the dependence on labor-intensive manual tuning of cost functions and the collection of extensive human demonstrations. This could streamline the development process for complex robotic skills and allow robots to discover optimal behaviors autonomously.
- **Improved Performance in Difficult Scenarios:** The demonstrated success on problems with long horizons, high dimensionality, and only terminal costs indicates CBO's applicability to a broader range of complex robotic tasks that are currently challenging for existing methods. This expands the frontier of what can be achieved with autonomous optimization in robotics.
- **Unifying Theoretical Framework:** The reinterpretation of existing zero-order methods within a general global optimization framework provides a more coherent understanding of their underlying mechanisms and limitations. CBO offers a rigorous mathematical foundation that can serve as a basis for further theoretical development and analysis of particle-based optimization strategies in robotics.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly opens avenues for future work, including exploring CBO's potential for discovering multimodal behaviors, effectively incorporating various types of constraints, and transitioning the algorithm to real-world robotic experiments. These directions highlight the broad potential impact of CBO on developing more capable and versatile autonomous robotic systems.

In conclusion, this research presents CBO as a promising tool for achieving global optimality in robotics, offering a pathway to develop more advanced and robust robotic capabilities by addressing the fundamental challenge of local minima in complex optimization landscapes.
