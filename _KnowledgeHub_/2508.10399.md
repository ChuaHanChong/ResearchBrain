# Large Model Empowered Embodied AI: A Survey on Decision-Making and Embodied Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> An extensive survey consolidates recent advancements in using large models to enhance embodied artificial intelligence, specifically detailing their roles in hierarchical and end-to-end decision-making, as well as various embodied learning paradigms. The work outlines the current state, identifies challenges such as data scarcity and the sim-to-real gap, and proposes future research avenues.

## Problem

- Embodied AI systems face challenges in achieving human-level intelligence and generalizing to diverse, open-ended real-world scenarios.
- The rapidly evolving intersection of large models and embodied AI lacks a systematic, comprehensive review focusing on how large models empower decision-making and learning.
- Fragmented research efforts and outdated existing surveys make it difficult for researchers to navigate advancements in this interdisciplinary field.

## Method

- Conducts a comprehensive and structured literature review on how large models empower embodied AI, specifically in decision-making and embodied learning.
- Employs a dual analytical approach, horizontally comparing diverse methods and vertically tracing the evolution of core models.
- Integrates a novel discussion of world models, detailing their design and crucial functions within embodied decision-making and learning.

## Results

- Large models demonstrably enhance planning through structured, natural, and programming language outputs, and improve execution via dynamic invocation of pretrained models and closed-loop feedback.
- VLA models achieve improved precision and adaptability in end-to-end control, with enhancements in perception, trajectory action optimization using diffusion models, and reduced training costs through efficient architectures.
- The field still faces critical challenges such as embodied data scarcity, the need for robust continual learning strategies, high computational demands, and effective bridging of the sim-to-real gap.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large models fundamentally reshape embodied AI by providing advanced cognitive and generative capabilities that address long-standing challenges in decision-making and learning.
> - Hierarchical decision-making leverages LLMs for high-level planning and diverse models for low-level execution, while end-to-end VLA models offer streamlined perception-action loops.
> - Large models significantly empower embodied learning through improved policy network construction (e.g., diffusion, Transformers) and automated reward function design, with world models playing a crucial role in simulation and data generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This survey paper is positioned at a crucial intersection of two of the most rapidly advancing fields in artificial intelligence: Embodied AI and Large Models. Embodied AI, a long-standing pursuit, aims to develop intelligent systems with physical forms that can perceive, make decisions, act, and learn in real-world environments, ultimately striving towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). Despite decades of effort, achieving human-level intelligence for general-purpose tasks in open, dynamic environments remains a significant challenge.

Simultaneously, recent breakthroughs in large models—including Large Language Models (LLMs), Large Vision Models (LVMs), Vision-Language Models (VLMs), and Multimodal Large Models (MLMs)—have revolutionized various AI domains by demonstrating unprecedented capabilities in perception, reasoning, and generation. The core hypothesis driving a substantial portion of current AI research is that these large models can endow embodied agents with advanced cognitive and operational abilities, overcoming many of the limitations of earlier, more rigid or task-specific embodied systems.

This survey directly addresses the burgeoning synergy between these two fields. The authors highlight a gap in the existing literature: while there are surveys on large models themselves or on specific components of embodied AI (e.g., planning, learning, simulators), there is a lack of systematic, comprehensive reviews that specifically focus on *how large models empower embodied AI*, particularly in the critical areas of autonomous decision-making and embodied learning. The paper aims to fill this void by providing a structured overview of the latest advancements, thereby integrating scattered studies into a coherent framework. By doing so, it serves as a critical resource for researchers and practitioners navigating this complex, interdisciplinary landscape, helping to consolidate knowledge and accelerate future innovation towards robust, general-purpose embodied agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this survey stems from the recognized challenges in achieving human-level intelligence in Embodied AI and the transformative potential of recent large model breakthroughs. The authors observe that despite significant advances, embodied agents still struggle with generalization and transferability to diverse, open-ended scenarios. While large models have shown promise in enhancing various aspects of embodied AI (perception, interaction, planning, learning), the field is still in its nascent stages, with research efforts being fragmented and lacking systematic categorization. Existing surveys are often limited in scope, focusing either on large models in isolation or on specific sub-components of embodied AI, without adequately addressing the synergistic integration of the two, or they have become outdated given the rapid pace of development.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of this survey are clearly articulated through its stated contributions:

1. **Focus on Large Model Empowerment in Embodied AI:** The survey specifically examines how large models enhance embodied AI from a holistic perspective. This includes their role in hierarchical decision-making (high-level planning, low-level execution, feedback enhancement), end-to-end decision-making (via Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models), and embodied learning (imitation learning and reinforcement learning).
2. **Comprehensive Review of Decision-Making and Embodied Learning:** The paper aims to provide an in-depth and broad review of both hierarchical and end-to-end decision-making paradigms, contrasting their strengths and weaknesses. For embodied learning, it covers not only imitation and reinforcement learning but also introduces transfer learning and meta-learning, along with a novel integration of world models within the survey of embodied AI, detailing their design and critical roles.
3. **Dual Analytical Approach for In-Depth Insights:** To provide a thorough understanding, the survey adopts a unique dual analytical methodology. This involves a "horizontal" analysis that compares diverse approaches (e.g., different large models, hierarchical vs. end-to-end decision-making, imitation vs. reinforcement learning, various learning strategies). Concurrently, a "vertical" analysis traces the evolution of core models and methods, detailing their origins, subsequent advances, and current challenges. This dual perspective enables both a macro-level overview and deep insights into mainstream methods.
4. **Identification of Challenges and Future Directions:** A crucial objective is to systematically analyze and categorize current studies, identify the latest progress, and, importantly, pinpoint remaining challenges and potential future research directions. This aims to provide researchers with a clear theoretical framework and practical guidance for advancing the field towards AGI.

In essence, the motivation is to consolidate, clarify, and guide research in the rapidly expanding area of large model-empowered embodied AI, addressing the existing fragmented knowledge and paving the way for more robust, general-purpose intelligent agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey paper, the methodology adopted by Liang et al. is primarily a structured and analytical literature review, designed to systematically categorize and critically assess the vast and fast-evolving landscape of large model-empowered embodied AI. The authors explicitly state a "dual analytical approach" to achieve their objectives.

1. **Comprehensive Literature Search and Categorization:** The initial step involves an extensive investigation of current studies in large models and embodied AI. The authors acknowledge that current studies are often scattered and complex, necessitating a systematic categorization. They explicitly mention identifying mainstream large models (LLM, LVM, LVLM, MLM, VLA models) and techniques for enhancing their general capabilities (In-Context Learning, X of Thoughts, RAG, ReAct, RLHF, MCP).
2. **Horizontal Analysis:** This dimension of their methodology involves reviewing and comparing diverse approaches across several key aspects:
 - **Large Models:** Comparing the roles and capabilities of different types of large models (LLM, LVM, LVLM, MLM, VLA) in empowering embodied intelligence.
 - **Decision-Making Paradigms:** Detailed comparison between hierarchical and end-to-end decision-making, analyzing their architectural differences, performance characteristics, interpretability, generalization abilities, real-time applicability, and computational costs.
 - **Embodied Learning Methods:** Comparing imitation learning, reinforcement learning, transfer learning, and meta-learning, outlining their strengths, limitations, and application contexts within embodied AI.
 - **Strategies within Paradigms:** For instance, within hierarchical planning, comparing structured language, natural language, and programming language planning with LLMs; or within VLA models, comparing different enhancement strategies for perception, action optimization, and cost reduction.
3. **Vertical Analysis:** This aspect traces the evolution of core models or methods within the embodied AI landscape. It involves detailing their origins, subsequent advancements, and the challenges they currently face. For example, in the context of world models, the survey traces the development from latent space models (RSSM, PlaNet, Dreamer) to Transformer-based, diffusion-based, and Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA). Similarly, the evolution of VLA models from RT-2 and its subsequent enhancements are detailed.
4. **Focus on Decision-Making and Learning:** The core of the survey is structured around these two central aspects of embodied intelligence.
 - **Decision-Making:** Divided into hierarchical (perception/interaction, high-level planning, low-level execution, feedback) and end-to-end (VLA models, enhancements like perception, trajectory action optimization, and cost reduction).
 - **Embodied Learning:** Concentrates on imitation learning (diffusion-based, Transformer-based policy networks) and reinforcement learning (reward function design, policy network construction via diffusion, Transformers, or LLMs), also including a discussion of transfer learning and meta-learning.
5. **Integration of World Models:** A notable methodological contribution is the explicit integration of world models into the survey, discussing their design methods (latent space, Transformer-based, diffusion-based, JEPA) and their critical roles in enhancing both decision-making (simulated validation, knowledge augmentation) and embodied learning (state transitions, data generation). This represents a novel synthesis for such surveys, as noted by the authors.
6. **Identification of Challenges and Future Prospects:** The survey concludes by leveraging the comprehensive review to distill the major open challenges facing large model-empowered embodied AI (e.g., data scarcity, continual learning, computational efficiency, sim-to-real gap) and proposes potential future research directions.

The overall approach is systematic, analytical, and comprehensive, designed to provide a structured understanding of a complex and rapidly evolving domain.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey "Large Model Empowered Embodied AI: A Survey on Decision-Making and Embodied Learning" does not present novel experimental results but rather synthesizes the findings and advancements from a vast body of existing literature. Its "findings" are therefore the key insights and conclusions drawn from this comprehensive review.

**A. Empowerment of Large Models in Embodied AI:**
- **Cognitive Backbone:** LLMs primarily serve as the cognitive backbone, processing natural language, grasping context, and generating actionable responses.
- **Perception and APIs:** LVMs are crucial for perception tasks or as callable APIs (e.g., object recognition, segmentation) during execution.
- **Multimodal Integration:** LVLMs and MLMs integrate language with other modalities (vision, audio) to enhance understanding of human instructions and generate contextually relevant responses.
- **End-to-End Control:** VLA models represent a significant shift, offering end-to-end mapping from multimodal inputs to direct action outputs, streamlining the perception-action loop.
- **Generative Capabilities:** LMs are increasingly leveraged for their generative abilities to advance embodied learning (e.g., data generation) and assist in constructing world models.

**B. Autonomous Decision-Making Paradigms:**
- **Hierarchical Decision-Making:**
 - **High-Level Planning:** LLMs significantly enhance planning by generating structured language plans (e.g., PDDL via LLV, FSP-LLM, LLM+P), natural language plans (e.g., Zero-shot, SayCAN, Text2Motion, Grounded Decoding), and programming language plans (e.g., CaP, Instruct2Act, ProgPrompt). They improve adaptability and reduce manual effort compared to traditional rule-based methods.
 - **Low-Level Execution:** This layer has evolved from traditional control algorithms (PID, LQR, MPC) to learning-driven control (imitation learning, reinforcement learning empowered by LLMs like Embodied-GPT, Hi-Core) and modular control, where LLMs dynamically invoke pretrained models (e.g., CLIP, SAM via DEPS, PaLM-E).
 - **Feedback and Enhancement:** Closed-loop feedback mechanisms, including LLM self-reflection (Re-Prompting, Self-Refine, Reflexion, Voyager), human feedback (KNOWNO, EmbodiedGPT, YAY Robot, IRAP), and environment feedback (Inner Monologue, TaPA, DoReMi, RoCo, ViLaIn, ViLA, Octopus, Voxposer), are critical for iterative refinement of decision-making.
- **End-to-End Decision-Making (VLA Models):**
 - **Unified Framework:** VLA models (e.g., RT-2) integrate perception, language understanding, planning, and action execution directly, leveraging large model knowledge to achieve precise and adaptable task execution.
 - **Key Components:** Involve tokenization/representation (vision, language, state, action tokens), multimodal information fusion (cross-modal attention in Transformers), and action detokenization (discrete or continuous action generation).
 - **Enhancements:** Significant progress in addressing VLA limitations through:
 - **Perception Capability:** Enhancements like runtime observation intervention (BYO-VLA), visual trajectory prompts (TraceVLA), and 3D integration (3D-VLA, SpatialVLA).

**C. Embodied Learning Methodologies:**
- **Imitation Learning Empowerment:** LMs enhance policy network construction, overcoming challenges of capturing complex behaviors and ensuring generalization.
 - **Diffusion-based Policy Networks:** Diffusion models (Pearce, DABC, Diffusion Policy, 3D-Diffusion) capture diverse action distributions, enhancing robustness and expressiveness for continuous action generation.
 - **Transformer-based Policy Networks:** Transformers (RT-1, RT-Trajectory, ALOHA, Mobile ALOHA, HiveFormer, RVT, RoboCat, RoboAgent, CrossFormer) model long-term dependencies in trajectories, improving consistency, accuracy, and generalization across tasks and embodiments.
- **Reinforcement Learning Empowerment:** LMs address challenges in reward function design and policy network construction.
 - **Reward Function Design:** LMs generate reward signals (Kwon et al., L2R) or dense reward functions (Text2Reward, Eureka, Auto MC-Reward) from natural language, significantly reducing manual effort and improving reward quality.
 - **Policy Network Construction:**
 - **Diffusion Models:** (Diffusion-QL, EDP) model complex action distributions, particularly for offline RL.
 - **Transformer-based Architectures:** (Decision Transformer, Prompt-DT, ODT, Q-Transformer, Gato) reframe RL as sequence modeling, leveraging self-attention for policy learning from trajectories.
 - **LLMs:** (GLAM, LaMo, Reid) use pretrained knowledge and sequence modeling to streamline offline RL, improving efficiency and transferability.

**D. Role of World Models:**
- **Design Methods:** World models have evolved from latent space models (RSSM, Dreamer) to Transformer-based (IRIS, Genie, TWM, STORM), diffusion-based (Sora, UniPi, UniSim), and Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA).
- **In Decision-Making:** Enable simulated validation (NeBula, UniSim) to "try out" actions virtually and augment knowledge (WKM, Agent-Pro, GovSim) for better planning and common sense.
- **In Embodied Learning:** Facilitate model-based reinforcement learning by simulating state transitions (RobotDreamPolicy, DayDreamer, SWIM) and generating synthetic data (SynthER, MTDiff, VPDD), enhancing sample efficiency and learning robustness.

**E. Identified Challenges and Future Prospects:**
- **Embodied Data Scarcity:** Real-world robot data is insufficient; solutions involve world model-based data synthesis and integration of large human datasets, but face reality gaps and alignment issues.
- **Continual Learning:** Catastrophic forgetting, inefficient autonomous exploration, and unpredictability of the real world hinder long-term adaptability. Solutions involve experience replay, regularization, and data mixing.
- **Computation and Deployment Efficiency:** High computational costs and memory requirements of large models limit real-time deployment on edge devices. Solutions include PEFT, model compression (knowledge distillation, quantization), and hardware acceleration.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Discrepancies between simulated and real environments lead to poor transferability. Advanced simulators and robust policies are needed to bridge this gap.

In summary, the survey finds that large models are fundamentally reshaping embodied AI by providing advanced cognitive and generative capabilities that address long-standing challenges in decision-making and learning, but significant hurdles related to data, learning adaptability, efficiency, and reality transfer remain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey by Liang et al. holds significant importance and potential impact across multiple facets of AI research and development, particularly for the fields of embodied AI, robotics, and large models.

**A. Consolidation and Guidance for a Nascent Field:**
The most immediate impact is providing a much-needed, comprehensive, and systematically organized overview of a rapidly evolving and inherently interdisciplinary field. The authors explicitly state that current studies are "scattered" and "lack of systematic categorization." By synthesizing this fragmented knowledge, the survey offers a unified theoretical framework and practical guidance. This is invaluable for:
- **New Researchers:** It provides a clear entry point into the complex landscape of large model-empowered embodied AI, outlining key concepts, paradigms, and state-of-the-art methods.
- **Experienced Researchers:** It serves as a reference point, highlighting the latest progress, offering a critical comparison of diverse approaches, and identifying prominent open problems, thereby helping to align and direct future research efforts.

**B. Accelerating Progress Towards AGI:**
Embodied AI is considered a promising path to Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). By detailing how large models enhance an agent's ability to perceive, decide, act, and learn in complex environments, the survey contributes to a deeper understanding of the capabilities required for AGI. The systematic analysis of decision-making (hierarchical vs. end-to-end) and embodied learning (imitation, reinforcement, world models) mechanisms, particularly when empowered by large models, offers insights into building more general-purpose and adaptable intelligent systems.

**C. Bridging Interdisciplinary Gaps:**
The research area naturally spans machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, and robotics. This survey explicitly bridges these domains by showing how advancements in large models (often from NLP/CV) are being successfully integrated into robotic and embodied systems. This can foster greater collaboration and cross-pollination of ideas between researchers in these distinct yet interconnected fields.

**D. Identifying Critical Research Directions:**
A major strength of the survey is its clear articulation of open challenges and future prospects. Issues such as embodied data scarcity, continual learning (catastrophic forgetting, efficient exploration), computational and deployment efficiency, and the persistent sim-to-real gap are highlighted. This serves as a roadmap for future research, guiding funding agencies, academic institutions, and industry labs towards critical areas that require urgent attention to realize the full potential of embodied AI. For instance, the discussion on parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) and model compression (e.g., TinyVLA) directly impacts the deployability of advanced AI on resource-constrained physical robots.

**E. Practical Implications for Industry:**
For engineers and developers in robotics and automation, the survey provides practical insights into the current capabilities and limitations of large model-empowered embodied systems. The detailed comparison of hierarchical and end-to-end approaches, along with discussions on various VLA models and their enhancements, can inform design choices for real-world applications in areas like manufacturing, logistics, autonomous vehicles, and domestic robots. Understanding the trade-offs between performance, interpretability, computational cost, and generalization is crucial for practical system deployment.

**F. Emphasizing World Models' Role:**
The survey's explicit integration of world models and their functions in both decision-making and embodied learning (simulated validation, knowledge augmentation, data generation) is a notable contribution. This emphasis underscores the increasing recognition of internal predictive models as essential components for intelligent agents, moving beyond purely reactive or model-free approaches. This could spur more dedicated research into advanced world model architectures and their symbiotic relationship with large models.

In conclusion, this survey is a timely and critical contribution that systematically maps out the state-of-the-art in large model-empowered embodied AI. By providing clarity, identifying key advancements, and charting future directions, it is poised to significantly impact the trajectory of research and development towards creating more capable, adaptable, and genuinely intelligent embodied systems.
