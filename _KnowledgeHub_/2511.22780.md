# Distracted Robot: How Visual Clutter Undermine Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huawei Technologies Canada developed the Dual-View Feature Congestion (DvFC) measure to systematically quantify visual clutter in robotic manipulation environments. Their evaluation of state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies demonstrates that increasing clutter can reduce task success rates by up to 34%, with an 18% improvement observed from data augmentation, yet not fully resolving the issue.

## Problem

- Robotic systems face significant challenges operating reliably in real-world cluttered environments where distractors can cause misperception, target confusion, or physical obstruction.
- Existing evaluation protocols for robotic manipulation often lack systematic characterization of distractor properties, quantities, or arrangements, making it difficult to quantify their precise impact.
- Current methods treat distractors as a monolithic factor or analyze environmental elements in isolation, preventing a nuanced understanding of how specific clutter properties degrade performance.

## Method

- Introduced a novel evaluation protocol employing a ==psychophysical perspective==, utilizing the **DvFC** (==Dual-View Feature Congestion==) measure to holistically quantify scene clutter.
- Systematically constructed ==6000 simulated and 216 real-world== evaluation scenarios with varying distractor types, quantities (1-16), and arrangements, then conducted extensive experiments across ==five state-of-the-art VLA policies== (Octo, OpenVLA, CogACT, π0, SpatialVLA) in both settings.
- Investigated a ==data enhancement strategy== by finetuning the π0 policy with additional distractor-inclusive training data to improve robustness.

## Results

- All evaluated policies experienced substantial performance degradation, with success rates dropping up to **34%** in cluttered scenarios compared to baseline, alongside high collision rates (e.g., π0 at **0.287**).
- The **DvFC** measure showed a consistent inverse correlation with policy success (initial occlusion increases up to **20%** caused a drastic decline); real-world UR5e experiments largely validated this downward trend, especially for complex tasks.
- Finetuning the π0 policy with augmented training data improved its overall success rate by **18%** in cluttered environments, although reductions in grasp failure rates were less substantial (**6%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual clutter significantly degrades SOTA VLA manipulation policies, with success-rate drops up to 34%; the Dual-View Feature Congestion (DvFC) measure predicts this degradation via a clear inverse clutter-success relationship.
> - Different VLA policies exhibit unique vulnerabilities and complementary strengths in cluttered scenes, even with similar average performance, suggesting potential for combined or specialized architectural approaches.
> - While data enhancement by finetuning with distractor-inclusive training data can improve clutter robustness by 18% in success rate, it does not fully mitigate all negative impacts of clutter, indicating a need for more fundamental architectural innovations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of robotic systems in real-world environments necessitates robustness to varying environmental conditions, particularly visual clutter. Prior research has indicated that distractors—non-target objects—can negatively impact robotic policy performance through misperception due to occlusion, target confusion stemming from visual or semantic similarity, incorrect task associations, or physical obstruction.

Existing evaluation protocols in robotic manipulation primarily focus on assessing specific robotic skills, the characteristics of target objects, or the reasoning capabilities required for tasks, often under controlled or arbitrarily varied conditions. While some studies introduce distractors, they frequently do so without systematically characterizing their properties, quantities, or arrangements, thereby limiting the ability to quantify their precise impact. A recent study identified various environmental factors, including distractors, as influential on manipulation policies. However, it treated distractors as a monolithic factor and analyzed environmental elements in isolation, neglecting their potential compounding effects. This approach prevents a nuanced understanding of how specific distractor properties or their interactions with other scene elements contribute to performance degradation.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing a novel evaluation protocol that approaches the problem from a psychophysical perspective. It aims to provide a more unified and systematic method for characterizing clutter, thereby enabling a quantitative analysis of its impact on robotic manipulation policies. By considering distractors' inherent properties, spatial arrangements, and their combined effect with environmental factors, this research seeks to establish a more comprehensive framework for understanding and evaluating robot robustness in complex, cluttered scenes, thereby bridging a gap in current evaluation methodologies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the critical need for robotic systems to operate reliably in unstructured and cluttered real-world environments. The presence of distractors—objects irrelevant to the task—is a ubiquitous challenge that can severely compromise robot performance by leading to issues such as target misidentification, perceptual ambiguities due to occlusion, and physical collision. Current evaluation methods for robotic manipulation often overlook the systematic assessment of these challenges, relying on generalized scenarios or isolated environmental factors. This gap in evaluation protocols hinders the development of policies that are truly robust to real-world complexities.

Against this background, the paper articulates several key objectives:

- **Propose a Novel Evaluation Protocol:** To introduce an evaluation framework specifically designed for examining robotic manipulation policies in cluttered scenes, moving beyond current protocols that treat distractors insufficiently or arbitrarily.
- **Psychophysical Perspective and Unified Clutter Measure:** To adopt a psychophysical approach, utilizing a unified measure of clutter—the Dual-View Feature Congestion (DvFC)—that holistically accounts for environmental factors, distractor quantity, characteristics, and arrangement. This objective aims to quantify the "degree of clutter" in a scene systematically.
- **Systematic Scenario Construction:** To construct diverse evaluation scenarios in both hyper-realistic simulation and real-world environments, ensuring systematic variation of distractor types, quantities, and arrangements, including scenarios with partial occlusion, while guaranteeing target reachability without scene rearrangement.
- **Extensive Experimentation on VLA Models:** To conduct comprehensive experiments on state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) manipulation policies to quantify the significant impact of scene clutter on their performance.
- **Identify Policy Vulnerabilities and Differences:** To highlight how different VLA policies, despite achieving similar average performance across tasks, possess unique vulnerabilities and exhibit relatively low agreement on successful scenarios, thereby providing a deeper understanding of their individual strengths and weaknesses.
- **Validate Clutter Measure Effectiveness:** To demonstrate that the proposed DvFC clutter measure serves as an effective indicator for predicting performance degradation in manipulation tasks.
- **Analyze Distractor Impact:** To analyze the specific impact of distractors concerning their quantity and occluding influence on policy performance.
- **Assess Data Enhancement Strategy:** To investigate whether finetuning VLA policies on enhanced training data, specifically designed to include distractors, can effectively mitigate the negative effects of clutter on performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a systematic methodology centered around a novel psychophysical clutter measure and extensive experimentation in both simulated and real-world environments.

**A. Problem Formulation and Robot Skills:**
The core problem addresses robotic manipulation where the objective is to generate 6-Degrees-of-Freedom (DOF) gripper poses from visual observations and language instructions. The study focuses on fundamental robotic skills adopted from the SIMPLER framework, including "pick (lift) object," "move object next to another object," "stack objects," and "place (smaller) objects on another (bigger) one." These tasks require accurate target detection amidst distractors, reaching and grasping in potentially confined spaces, and precise placement while avoiding collisions. The premise is that if clutter affects these core abilities, it will likely impact other manipulation tasks as well.

**B. Clutter Measure: Dual-View Feature Congestion (DvFC):**
To quantify scene complexity, the authors propose the Dual-View Feature Congestion (DvFC) measure. This metric is based on the psychophysical Feature Congestion Measure (FCM), which combines covariances of scene color, contrast, and orientation at different scales to capture distractor quantity, spatial distribution, and similarity to the target. To address FCM's limitation as a 2D image-based metric and account for operational complexity and occluded objects, DvFC extends it to a dual-view approach. It combines FCM calculations from both the robot's primary camera view (capturing visual clutter) and a top-down view (capturing operational complexity and spatial arrangement that might affect reachability). This unified measure is designed to account for the compounding effects of different scene elements.

**C. Scenario Generation:**
Evaluation scenarios are systematically generated from base scenarios within the SIMPLER simulation environment. Distractors are randomly selected (1–12 objects) from a set of 61 YCB objects and randomly placed within the robot's operational space. Two key constraints are applied during placement:
1. A minimum spatial gap (δ) between objects is enforced to prevent stacking or piling.
2. The space around the target object is constrained to maintain its grasping affordance.
For each generated scene, the DvFC score is computed. To ensure feasibility and diversity, scenarios where the target object is more than 50% visually occluded or has no grasping affordance are discarded. The remaining scenes are then binned based on their DvFC scores, and uniform sampling is performed from these bins, resulting in a total of 6000 simulated scenarios.

**D. Experimental Setup (Simulated World):**
- **Tasks:** Six specific skills are evaluated: "Move near," "Stack cube," "Pick coke," "Put spoon," "Put eggplant," and "Put carrot."
- **Distractor Selection and Placement:** Distractors are randomly selected YCB objects, with those causing instruction ambiguity removed. Placement ensures a distance threshold from the target and limits visual occlusion from the robot's default perspective to a maximum of 50%.
- **Models:** Five state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are evaluated: Octo, OpenVLA, CogACT, π0, and SpatialVLA. Octo and OpenVLA are trained on specific datasets (Bridge and Fractal, respectively) and serve as references, while the other three are trained on both.
- **Metrics:** Performance is assessed using:
 - **Success Rate (SR):** Percentage of completed tasks (including those with collisions).
 - **Hard Success Rate (h-SR):** Percentage of completed tasks without any collisions.
 - **Collision Rate (CR):** Percentage of scenarios where the robot contacts a distractor.
 - **Grasp Failure Rate (GFR):** Percentage of scenarios where the robot fails to grasp the target.
 - **Efficiency Rate (ER):** Normalized number of steps taken to complete the task.

**E. Experimental Setup (Real World):**
To validate findings, a subset of experiments is replicated using a UR5e robot manipulator. Due to time constraints, scenario generation is sparser, with 6 variations of distractor quantities (0, 1, 2, 4, 8, and 16 distractors) for four core skills (pick, move, stack, put). This totals 216 real-world scenarios. The π0 policy, chosen for its balanced performance in simulation, is used. Scenarios are again binned by DvFC for analysis.

**F. Data Enhancement Strategy:**
The study investigates whether augmented training data can improve clutter robustness. The π0 model is finetuned using a dataset that includes both base samples (target objects only) and 45 additional scenarios per skill featuring distractors with randomized arrangements. This finetuned model is then re-evaluated on the test scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted in both simulated and real-world environments, yielded several key findings regarding the impact of visual clutter on robotic manipulation policies.

**A. Significant Impact of Distractors on Policy Performance:**
The addition of distractors substantially reduced the performance of all evaluated policies. Across tasks, policies experienced a performance degradation of up to 34% (as stated in the abstract) in success rate (SR) compared to baseline scenarios. For instance, the π0 policy’s SR dropped from 0.711 in base scenarios to 0.470 in cluttered scenes. Scenarios involving visual occlusion of the target consistently resulted in significantly lower success rates, indicating increased risk of target confusion and collision. Policies generally exhibited high collision rates (CR), with π0 at 0.287 and CogACT at 0.395, suggesting limitations in obstacle avoidance mechanisms. SpatialVLA demonstrated a comparatively lower CR (0.16) and a higher hard success rate (h-SR), which might be attributed to its optimization for spatial understanding. Grasp failure rates (GFR) varied, with π0 showing relative robustness in grasping affordance estimation.

**B. Differential Impact of Distractors on Policies:**
Despite achieving similar average success rates, the policies demonstrated unique strengths and weaknesses when confronted with distractors. Per-task performance varied, with all models performing best on the simplest task, "Pick coke." SpatialVLA and CogACT performed better on "Move" tasks, while π0 excelled in "Stack" and "Put" tasks, indicating a more balanced overall performance for π0. A Venn diagram analysis revealed that different policies exhibited low agreement on successful scenarios; CogACT shared only about 45% of its successes with π0. This suggests complementary strengths, as a combined approach of these policies could achieve an approximately 67% SR, higher than any single policy. Failure analysis further detailed these differences: CogACT was generally more successful at reaching the correct target but suffered from higher grasping failures, while π0 was better at grasping but less successful at overall task completion. "Fail to reach target" cases often involved grasping wrong objects due to visual confusion or the robot passing over the target. π0’s failure points for "Fail to reach target" were more dispersed, extending further from the target, suggesting greater vulnerability to target misidentification.

**C. Clutter Measure as a Performance Indicator:**
The Dual-View Feature Congestion (DvFC) measure proved to be an effective indicator of expected policy performance. A clear inverse relationship was observed: as DvFC values increased (indicating higher clutter), the success rates of all policies generally declined. The rate of decline varied among policies; SpatialVLA’s performance dropped rapidly at lower DvFC values, while CogACT and π0 showed an initial decline, then stabilized, before further decreasing at the highest clutter levels. π0 exhibited more stability at higher clutter levels. Collision rates generally increased with DvFC, and grasp failure rates, particularly for SpatialVLA, showed significant degradation, confirming the measure’s utility in predicting performance trends.

**D. Impact of Distractor Aspects:**
Further analysis revealed that specific aspects of distractors had distinct impacts:
- **Set Size (Number of Distractors):** Increasing the number of distractors negatively impacted performance, but the success rate tended to stabilize after approximately five distractors for all policies.
- **Visual Occlusion:** Initial increases in target visual occlusion (up to roughly 20%) caused a drastic decline in success rates across all policies before stabilizing. π0 demonstrated greater robustness at higher occlusion levels compared to CogACT, while SpatialVLA was the most vulnerable, dropping to 0% SR at the highest occlusion.

**E. Real-World Validation:**
Real-world experiments using a UR5e robot and the π0 policy largely corroborated the simulation findings. A downward trend in performance was observed as clutter levels (DvFC) increased. The impact of clutter was more pronounced with increasing task complexity. Qualitative examples of real-world failures mirrored those in simulation, with target confusion being a prevalent issue, even with minimal distractors. Real-world collision rates increased more rapidly with clutter, whereas grasp failure rates, after an initial rise, tended to stabilize, consistent with simulated results.

**F. Effectiveness of Data Enhancement:**
Finetuning the π0 policy with additional real-world data containing distractors demonstrated an increase in the policy’s robustness to clutter. The overall success rate improved by 18%. However, improvements in other metrics, such as grasp failure rate, were less substantial (a 6% reduction). This finding suggests that while data enhancement can be beneficial, it may not fully address all negative impacts of clutter, particularly given the controlled environment and limited variability of distractors used.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the field of robotic manipulation by systematically examining the impact of visual clutter, a pervasive and critical challenge for real-world robot deployment. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancement in Evaluation Protocols:** The introduction of the Dual-View Feature Congestion (DvFC) measure provides a novel, psychophysically-grounded, and unified metric for systematically quantifying scene clutter. This metric moves beyond arbitrary or isolated factor-based evaluations, offering a more comprehensive tool for benchmark design and performance assessment in cluttered environments. It highlights the inadequacy of average success rates and advocates for more granular analysis across varying clutter conditions.
- **Deeper Understanding of Policy Limitations:** The study demonstrates that visual clutter significantly degrades the performance of state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, with performance drops as high as 34%. This underscores the fundamental vulnerability of current robotic policies to environmental complexity. By dissecting failure modes (e.g., target confusion, collision, grasping failures) and relating them to specific aspects of clutter (e.g., set size, occlusion), the research provides critical insights for identifying weaknesses in existing architectures.
- **Guidance for Policy Development:** The finding that different policies exhibit unique vulnerabilities and complementary strengths, even with similar average performance, suggests avenues for future research. This could lead to the development of specialized architectures, ensemble methods, or hybrid approaches that leverage the strengths of multiple policies to enhance overall robustness in diverse cluttered scenarios. It also informs developers about which types of clutter (e.g., high occlusion vs. high distractor count) disproportionately affect different policy designs.
- **Emphasis on Clutter-Robust Design:** The research explicitly shows that simply scaling training data with distractors, while beneficial, does not fully resolve the robustness issue across all metrics. This finding has a profound impact, as it implies that architectural innovations, explicit scene reasoning capabilities, or more sophisticated learning paradigms beyond data augmentation may be necessary to achieve truly clutter-robust robotic manipulation.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The validation of key findings in real-world experiments strengthens the credibility of the simulated results and highlights the predictive power of the DvFC measure. This contributes to bridging the sim-to-real gap, offering confidence that insights gained in simulation can inform real-world robot development.
- **Towards Generalist Robots:** Ultimately, by systematically addressing the challenge of visual clutter, this work contributes to the foundational understanding required to develop more generalist and robust robotic systems capable of operating autonomously and reliably in dynamic, unconstrained real-world environments, such as homes, factories, or service industries.
