# Distracted Robot: How Visual Clutter Undermine Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huawei Technologies Canada developed the Dual-View Feature Congestion (DvFC) measure to systematically quantify visual clutter in robotic manipulation environments. Their evaluation of state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies demonstrates that increasing clutter can reduce task success rates by up to 34%, with an 18% improvement observed from data augmentation, yet not fully resolving the issue.

## Problem

- Robotic systems face significant challenges operating reliably in real-world cluttered environments where distractors can cause misperception, target confusion, or physical obstruction.
- Existing evaluation protocols for robotic manipulation often lack systematic characterization of distractor properties, quantities, or arrangements, making it difficult to quantify their precise impact.
- Current methods treat distractors as a monolithic factor or analyze environmental elements in isolation, preventing a nuanced understanding of how specific clutter properties degrade performance.

## Method

- Introduced a novel evaluation protocol employing a ==psychophysical perspective==, utilizing the **DvFC** (==Dual-View Feature Congestion==) measure to holistically quantify scene clutter.
- Systematically constructed ==6000 simulated and 216 real-world== evaluation scenarios with varying distractor types, quantities (1-16), and arrangements, ensuring target reachability.
- Conducted extensive experiments on ==five state-of-the-art Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies==, including Octo, OpenVLA, CogACT, \u03c00, and SpatialVLA, in both simulated and real-world settings.
- Investigated a ==data enhancement strategy== by finetuning the \u03c00 policy with additional distractor-inclusive training data to improve robustness.

## Results

- All evaluated policies experienced substantial performance degradation, with success rates dropping up to **34%** in cluttered scenarios compared to baseline, alongside high collision rates (e.g., \u03c00 at **0.287**).
- The **DvFC** measure showed a consistent inverse correlation with policy success rates; as DvFC increased, policy performance declined, with initial increases in target visual occlusion (up to **20%**) causing a drastic decline.
- Finetuning the \u03c00 policy with augmented training data improved its overall success rate by **18%** in cluttered environments, although reductions in grasp failure rates were less substantial (**6%**).
- Real-world experiments using a UR5e robot largely validated simulation findings, demonstrating a clear downward trend in performance with increasing clutter, particularly for more complex tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual clutter significantly degrades the performance of state-of-the-art VLA manipulation policies, with observed success rate drops as high as 34%.
> - The Dual-View Feature Congestion (DvFC) measure effectively predicts policy performance degradation, demonstrating a clear inverse relationship between increasing clutter and task success rates.
> - Different VLA policies exhibit unique vulnerabilities and complementary strengths in cluttered scenes, even with similar average performance, suggesting potential for combined or specialized architectural approaches.
> - While data enhancement by finetuning with distractor-inclusive training data can improve clutter robustness by 18% in success rate, it does not fully mitigate all negative impacts of clutter, indicating a need for more fundamental architectural innovations.
