# Rethinking Generalization in Reasoning SFT: A Conditional Analysis on Optimization, Data, and Model Capability

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and Shanghai Jiao Tong University found that supervised fine-tuning (SFT) can foster cross-domain generalization in large language models for reasoning tasks, provided there is sufficient optimization, high-quality long chain-of-thought (CoT) data, and capable base models. This reasoning generalization, however, was observed to correlate with a decrease in model safety.

## Problem

- The common belief that supervised fine-tuning (SFT) primarily leads to memorization and lacks cross-domain generalization for large language models (LLMs).
- Previous studies supporting this view often used inconsistent experimental conditions, such as short training durations, varying data quality, or smaller base models.
- Unclear whether observed generalization failures in SFT were intrinsic limitations or outcomes of specific experimental setups.

## Method

- Systematically investigated the roles of ==optimization dynamics==, ==training data characteristics== (quality, CoT presence), and ==base-model capability== in ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== generalization.
- Conducted controlled experiments on pretrained base models using ==math-only reasoning SFT== to isolate the acquisition of new generalization.
- Employed a comprehensive evaluation suite to measure in-domain, out-of-domain reasoning, general capabilities, and potential safety degradation.

## Results

- Extended SFT training demonstrated a **dip-and-recovery** pattern for out-of-domain (OOD) reasoning, with performance eventually surpassing base model levels.
- Long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision and high data quality were found to be essential, leading to stronger generalization, including from abstract procedural tasks.
- Larger base models consistently showed broad generalization benefits, while smaller models exhibited minimal or negative gains.
- A consistent trade-off was observed where increased reasoning generalization from long-CoT SFT correlated with a degradation in model **safety** performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SFT's perceived lack of generalization can be an artifact of under-optimization, as performance often shows a "dip-and-recovery" pattern with extended training.
> - High-quality, long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data, especially that conveying abstract reasoning procedures, is critical for fostering generalization, while low-quality data impedes it.
> - The ability of SFT to generalize is strongly dependent on the inherent capability of the base model, with larger models demonstrating more robust transfer.
