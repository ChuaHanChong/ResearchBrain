# POCA: Pareto-Optimal Curriculum Alignment for Visual Text Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tohoku University and Amazon Web Services developed POCA, a framework for visual text generation that employs Pareto-optimal set selection and adaptive curriculum alignment. This approach stabilizes multi-reward reinforcement learning and improves data efficiency, leading to higher text accuracy, image coherence, and aesthetic quality, outperforming state-of-the-art models like AnyText2 in CLIP and HPS scores.

## Problem

- Existing multi-reward reinforcement learning for visual text generation experiences training instability from scalarizing conflicting reward signals (e.g., text accuracy versus aesthetic quality).
- Current RL methods face challenges in efficient and effective prompt selection, leading to high computational costs and slow convergence when managing diverse, multi-objective training data.
- Achieving a balanced trade-off between precise text rendering and overall image aesthetic quality remains difficult for visual text generation models.

## Method

- A Joint ==Pareto Set== Selection component re-frames multi-reward optimization, using bi-directional Pareto sorting to filter ambiguous signals within GRPO groups for clearer policy updates.
- The Pareto-guided GRPO objective only incorporates samples from the identified Pareto set, guiding the model towards optimal trade-off solutions.
- An Adaptive Curriculum Planning strategy dynamically measures prompt difficulty via OCR reward, creating an "easy-to-hard" learning path through multi-stage data partitioning for efficient training.

## Results

- POCA consistently outperforms the AnyText base model and the GRPO baseline in text accuracy (Sentence Accuracy, Normalized Edit Distance), text-image alignment (CLIP score), and aesthetic quality (HPS score).
- Ablation studies confirm that Pareto-guided optimization achieves significantly higher OCR and CLIP rewards with more stable convergence compared to weighted-sum approaches.
- The adaptive curriculum achieves performance on 1k images comparable to Pareto-guided methods on 10k images, demonstrating substantial data efficiency, and user studies confirm superior outputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scalarizing conflicting multi-reward signals destabilizes RL training, demonstrating that multi-objective Pareto-optimal selection is crucial for achieving superior trade-offs in visual text generation.
> - Bi-directional Pareto sorting provides clear, unambiguous positive and negative optimization signals, enabling stable convergence to better text accuracy, image coherence, and aesthetic quality.
> - Dynamically adjusting the training curriculum based on prompt difficulty significantly enhances data efficiency and accelerates the learning process in complex multi-reward RL environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image generation has advanced significantly in recent years, with visual text generation emerging as a specialized and challenging sub-area. State-of-the-art models are capable of rendering plausible text within high-quality images. However, practical applications often necessitate precise control over text attributes (e.g., regions, fonts) and overall image characteristics, leading to research in controllable visual text generation that conditions the process on auxiliary signals like text glyphs or positional cues.

A fundamental challenge in this domain is the inherent trade-off between the fidelity of the rendered text and the overall coherence and aesthetic quality of the generated image. Existing methods frequently struggle to balance these conflicting objectives during training. Reinforcement Learning (RL) from Human Feedback (RLHF) has recently been adopted to refine generative models post-training by aligning them with multiple reward objectives, such as prompt alignment and aesthetic criteria. Frameworks like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) represent advances in this area.

However, current RLHF approaches, particularly for visual text generation, encounter two primary limitations. Firstly, the common practice of aggregating multiple reward signals using a weighted-sum approach can lead to unstable training and suboptimal convergence. This instability arises from contradictory reward signals (e.g., optimizing for text accuracy might conflict with aesthetic appeal), making it difficult to find an unambiguous optimization target or to appropriately balance reward weights. Secondly, the selection of training prompts for RL remains an open problem. While larger datasets often improve performance, they incur high computational costs and extended training times. Moreover, multi-reward optimization benefits from a diverse mix of prompts, but such diversity can create a complex optimization landscape, slowing convergence. This highlights a need for efficient and effective prompt selection strategies.

POCA positions itself within this landscape by addressing these two specific limitations. It seeks to provide a more stable multi-objective optimization framework for RL-based visual text generation, moving beyond scalarized rewards, and simultaneously introduces an adaptive curriculum learning strategy for efficient and effective data management in multi-reward environments. This work aims to enhance visual text generation models' ability to produce images that are accurate in text, coherent in visuals, and aesthetically pleasing, under complex instructions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust framework that can effectively manage the inherent trade-off between text accuracy and overall image quality in visual text generation, particularly within reinforcement learning paradigms. The motivation for POCA stems from identified deficiencies in current approaches:

1. **Addressing the Instability of Multi-Reward Optimization in Visual Text Generation:**
 - **Problem:** Current visual text generation models, when subjected to multi-reward optimization via RL (e.g., GRPO-based methods), often exhibit instability. This instability is attributed to conflicting reward signals (e.g., maximizing Optical Character Recognition (OCR) accuracy might reduce aesthetic quality, and vice versa).
 - **Mechanism of Instability:** Existing RL methods typically aggregate multiple reward signals into a single scalar value using a weighted-sum approach. This scalarization can lead to an ambiguous optimization target when rewards are contradictory, resulting in suboptimal convergence and an inability to achieve a balanced trade-off across objectives.
 - **Motivation for Pareto-Optimal Approach:** The researchers observe that a simple weighted-sum approach destabilizes training. Therefore, a core motivation is to move beyond scalarization by reframing the problem as a multi-objective optimization task. The aim is to identify a Pareto-optimal set of solutions, representing the best possible trade-offs where no single objective can be improved without degrading another. This approach seeks to provide clearer, non-conflicting signals for policy updates, thereby stabilizing the training process.

2. **Improving Efficiency and Effectiveness of Prompt Selection for RL Training:**
 - **Problem:** Reinforcement learning requires a set of training instructions (prompts). The size and composition of this dataset significantly impact training efficiency and model performance. Using very large datasets is computationally expensive and time-consuming. Conversely, small, static datasets might not provide sufficient diversity or targeted signals for all reward objectives, especially in multi-reward settings where prompts biased towards different criteria (e.g., aesthetics vs. textual accuracy) might be needed. This can create a complex optimization landscape that hinders convergence.
 - **Motivation for Curriculum Alignment:** The challenge of efficiently selecting prompts for effective training is recognized as an unsolved problem. The motivation is to design an adaptive curriculum alignment strategy. This strategy aims to dynamically manage the sequence of learning tasks, exposing the model to increasingly complex environments in a structured, "easy-to-hard" progression, similar to human learning. This approach is intended to ensure optimal convergence, improve data efficiency, and achieve better final performance by tailoring the learning experience to the model's evolving capabilities, particularly within a multi-reward context where RL methods explore in a limited data environment.

In synergy, POCA seeks to identify optimal trade-off solutions from different rewards under an organized optimization landscape, leading to improved text accuracy, image coherence, and instruction-following capability in visual text generation models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

POCA (Pareto-Optimal Curriculum Alignment) is a synergistic framework designed to address the challenges of multi-reward optimization and data selection in visual text generation using GRPO-based reinforcement learning. The methodology consists of two core components: Joint Pareto Set Selection and Adaptive Curriculum Planning.

#### 3.1. Reinforcement Learning Foundation

POCA builds upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework, which models the denoising process of a diffusion model as a Markov Decision Process (MDP).
- **Denoising in MDP:** The iterative denoising steps are formulated as transitions within an MDP, where the state `s_t` includes the prompt `c`, time step `t`, and noisy latent `z_t`. The action `a_t` is the denoised sample `z_{t-1}` predicted by the model, and the policy `π(a_t | s_t)` is the diffusion model's prediction function. Rewards `R(s_t, a_t)` are only given at the final step (`t=0`) for the generated image `z_0`.
- **GRPO Objective:** GRPO optimizes the policy `π_θ` by maximizing an objective function that evaluates "group-relative advantages." For a given prompt `c`, a group of `G` outputs `{o_1..., o_G}` is sampled. The advantage `A_i` for each output `o_i` is calculated relative to the mean and standard deviation of rewards within its group. This relative advantage stabilizes policy updates compared to standard policy gradient methods.

#### 3.2. Joint Pareto Set Selection

This component addresses the instability caused by scalarizing conflicting reward signals.
- **Problem with Scalarization:** Traditional GRPO, when applied to visual text generation, aggregates rewards (e.g., OCR accuracy, CLIP score, HPS score) using a weighted sum. This is found to be unstable due to conflicting objectives, leading to suboptimal convergence.
- **Pareto-Optimal Reframing:** POCA re-frames multi-reward optimization by identifying the Pareto set in a joint reward space. A sample is Pareto-optimal if no other sample can improve on one reward without worsening another.
- **Bi-directional Pareto Sorting (Algorithm 1):** Within each GRPO group of `G` sampled outputs, a group-based Pareto sorting is performed to obtain a Pareto set `P`.
 - **Non-dominated Set (P+):** Identifies samples that represent the best trade-offs, where no other sample in the group dominates them (i.e., is better or equal in all rewards and strictly better in at least one). These provide positive optimization signals.
 - **Fully Dominated Set (P-):** Identifies samples that are clearly poor trade-offs. This is achieved by applying non-dominated sorting to *negated* reward vectors. These provide negative optimization signals.
 - By considering both `P+` and `P-`, the model learns to generate outputs closer to the best trade-offs and further away from the worst trade-offs.
- **Pareto-guided GRPO (Eq. 4):** The GRPO objective function is modified such that only samples included in the identified Pareto set `P` (comprising `P+` and `P-`) contribute to policy updates. Samples not in `P` are assigned a zero advantage, effectively filtering out inconsistent or ambiguous reward signals and guiding the optimization along a clearer path.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Curriculum Planning

This component aims to manage training data efficiently and guide the policy model through a stable, "easy-to-hard" learning path.
- **Difficulty Measurer:**
 - **Reward Selection:** The OCR reward model is chosen as the primary difficulty measurer, as empirical analysis showed it exhibits greater variance and finer ranking resolution across samples compared to CLIP and HPS rewards.
 - **Difficulty Scoring:** For every prompt `c` in the dataset `D`, its mean OCR reward `μ_c` is estimated by sampling `N` times with the current policy model `π_θ`.
 - **Normalization and Ranking:** These mean OCR rewards are then normalized using the Empirical Cumulative Distribution Function (ECDF). The ECDF value `ECDF(μ_c)` for a prompt represents its percentile rank, serving as a normalized relative difficulty score.
 - **Adaptive Assessment:** This process is re-invoked at the beginning of each of `S` training stages, allowing the curriculum to adapt dynamically to the model's evolving capabilities.
- **Training Scheduler:**
 - **Data Partitioning:** Based on the ECDF values, the entire dataset `D` is partitioned into three discrete difficulty bins: `D_easy` (e.g., ECDF ≥ 0.7), `D_medium` (0.3 < ECDF < 0.7), and `D_hard` (ECDF ≤ 0.3). A small fraction of the easiest and hardest samples are optionally trimmed.
 - **Curriculum Progression:** The Training Scheduler then assigns a different mixture ratio `w_s` for the easy, medium, and hard prompt subsets at each training stage `S`. For example, early stages might heavily favor easy prompts, while later stages progressively introduce more medium and hard prompts. This "bucketing curriculum" progressively exposes the model to more challenging environments, facilitating learning of robust reward trade-offs.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** AnyText is adopted as the base visual text generation model.
- **Reward Models:** Three reward models are used:
 - **CLIP:** Measures text-image alignment.
 - **EasyOCR:** Measures text accuracy using Normalized Edit Distance (NED), formulated as `1 - NED` for reward maximization.
 - **HPS-v2.1:** Quantifies the aesthetic appeal of generated images.
- **Dataset (POCA-20k):** A mixed dataset of 20,000 images, combining 5k general images from SynthText, 10k scene text images from AnyWord-3M, and 5k art/movie posters from LeX-Art. Descriptive prompts for these images were generated using Gemini 2.5.
- **Training Details:** The base model is trained for 300 steps using LoRA (rank 128), with 16 samples generated per prompt and a batch size of 4, following Dance-GRPO configurations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance and mechanisms of POCA:

#### 4.1. Quantitative Evaluation

- **Overall Performance:** POCA demonstrates improvements over the original AnyText base model across all evaluated metrics. It surpasses the performance of AnyText2, a current state-of-the-art model, in terms of text-image alignment (CLIP score) and aesthetic quality (HPS score).
- **Comparison with GRPO Baseline:** POCA outperforms the standard GRPO baseline (which uses an equally-weighted scalarization of rewards) in both text accuracy (Sentence Accuracy, Normalized Edit Distance) and CLIP score. This indicates that POCA successfully identifies a superior balance between conflicting rewards.
- **Generalizability:** The framework was also validated on an SDXL-based model, showing similar improvements, which suggests its applicability to larger and more recent models.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis

- **Baseline Comparison:** Visual comparisons (Fig. 4) on both English and Chinese prompts from the AnyText-benchmark indicate that POCA achieves higher overall reward scores. It produces visually superior images, characterized by seamless text blending, harmonious color combinations, and finer image details. This is attributed to the multi-reward alignment with filtered, unambiguous signals and an efficient learning path.
- **Aesthetic Appeal and Instruction-Following:** On a curated test set of complex prompts from POCA-20k, POCA demonstrates stronger instruction-following capabilities than AnyText and AnyText2 (Fig. 5, 16). Specifically, it integrates text more coherently with backgrounds and produces more aesthetically pleasing results, particularly in situations requiring fine-grained detail.

#### 4.3. Human Study

- A user study involving 22 participants evaluating 50 image sets generated by POCA, AnyText, and GRPO revealed that POCA significantly outperforms the base model and the GRPO baseline across three categories: text accuracy, prompt following, and aesthetics (Fig. 8).

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Weighted-sum vs. Pareto-guided Optimization:**
 - The GRPO baseline (weighted-sum) converges to a suboptimal state, performing competitively only on the HPS reward, while the Pareto-guided method achieves significantly higher OCR and CLIP rewards (Fig. 6).
 - The GRPO baseline shows unstable improvements in multi-reward optimization, particularly in text-image alignment, whereas the Pareto-guided approach demonstrates stable and consistent gains (Fig. 7). This supports the hypothesis that bi-directional Pareto selection provides a cleaner optimization path by filtering signals.
 - Further comparison showed that POCA's bi-directional sorting strategy consistently achieves higher reward curves across OCR, CLIP, and HPS metrics compared to one-directional non-dominated or fully dominated sorting baselines (Fig. 10, Table 3a). It also leads to a higher probability of producing optimal trade-off states in a larger solution space (best@k protocol, Table 3b) and contributes the largest proportion of non-dominated solutions to the global Pareto front (Fig. 9).
- **Adaptive Curriculum vs. Naive Mixing:**
 - The OCR-oriented curriculum yields faster convergence and higher sentence accuracy without sacrificing other reward objectives, supporting its effectiveness for data selection (Fig. 7).
 - POCA-1k (trained on 1k images) achieves performance comparable to the Pareto-guided method trained on 10k images (Table 2), indicating strong data efficiency due to curriculum planning.
 - The multi-stage adaptive design achieves higher sentence accuracy than a single-stage variant, further justifying this approach (Table 2).
- **Difficulty Measurer Justification:** Analysis of reward model variance confirmed that OCR reward distribution exhibits a larger dynamic range and finer ranking resolution across samples compared to CLIP and HPS, justifying its selection as the difficulty measurer (Fig. 11).

#### 4.5. Additional Comparisons (Supplementary Material)

- POCA outperforms RPO, a harmonic-mean weighted-sum approach, particularly in Sentence Accuracy, avoiding the need for manual hyperparameter balancing (Table 4).
- POCA also surpasses Curriculum-DPO, a DPO-based curriculum design, across multiple metrics, suggesting its advantage in handling conflicting multi-reward environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

POCA addresses two key limitations in the advanced field of visual text generation: the instability of multi-reward optimization in reinforcement learning and the inefficiency of static data selection. Its contributions offer a method for enhancing the performance and practical applicability of text-to-image models.

The primary significance lies in its **Pareto-optimal selection algorithm**, which reframes multi-reward optimization from a scalarized, potentially conflicting objective into a multi-objective problem. By identifying non-dominated and fully dominated samples, POCA stabilizes the training process and enables models to converge to superior trade-off solutions that simultaneously improve text accuracy, image coherence, and aesthetic quality. This advancement has implications beyond visual text generation, potentially offering a more robust approach for multi-objective reinforcement learning in other domains where reward signals may conflict.

Secondly, the **adaptive curriculum alignment strategy** contributes to data efficiency and accelerated learning. By dynamically assessing prompt difficulty and creating an "easy-to-hard" learning sequence, POCA optimizes the training path. This approach reduces computational costs, accelerates convergence, and improves final model performance, particularly in environments with limited data or complex reward landscapes. The data-efficient nature of this curriculum design could be valuable for training large generative models where data collection and processing are significant bottlenecks.

The combined synergy of these two components enables the generation of higher-quality visual text images that are faithful to textual instructions, aesthetically pleasing, and semantically coherent. The empirical evidence, including quantitative benchmarks, qualitative analysis, and human studies, demonstrates POCA's ability to produce visually superior and more accurate outputs compared to prior methods. Its generalizability to different base models, including SDXL-based architectures, further underscores its potential for broad impact.

Potential real-world applications include:
- **Graphic Design and Advertising:** Automating the creation of posters, banners, and advertisements with precise text placement and styling, ensuring both readability and visual appeal.
- **Content Creation:** Generating images for narratives, presentations, or digital media that accurately incorporate specified text while maintaining high artistic quality.
- **Personalization:** Enabling users to create customized images with embedded text for various personal or commercial uses, requiring detailed instruction following.

Overall, POCA represents a methodological step forward in controllable visual text generation, providing a framework that is both more stable in training and more efficient in data utilization, thereby pushing the capabilities of AI in visual content creation.
