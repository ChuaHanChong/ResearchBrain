# Emergence of Exploration in Policy Gradient Reinforcement Learning via Retrying

> [!summary] Summary
> A new reinforcement learning objective called ReMax enables exploration to emerge intrinsically, rather than through explicit bonus terms, by optimizing for the expected maximum return over multiple action retries under reward uncertainty. The resulting RePPO algorithm achieves competitive performance on various benchmarks without requiring exploration bonuses, while also demonstrating higher policy entropy.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning exploration strategies often rely on explicit bonuses (e.g., entropy, count-based, prediction error) which increase algorithmic complexity and require careful hyperparameter tuning.
- Many bonus-based methods necessitate additional models (e.g., density, dynamics) or specific architectural designs to estimate statistics or uncertainty, adding overhead.
- A need for an intrinsic mechanism where exploratory behavior naturally arises from the agent's objective function, rather than being engineered separately.

## Method

- The ReMax objective is introduced, formalizing exploration as greedy reward maximization under conditions of uncertainty and the opportunity for retrying actions, effectively evaluating a policy by the expected maximum return over multiple action samples.
- A novel Expected Improvement (EI)-based policy gradient formulation is derived, transforming the gradient into an expectation over a single action, which is estimable from single-trajectory returns.
- The ReMax PPO (RePPO) algorithm integrates this gradient into the PPO framework, using a Q-critic, an EI-based advantage, and a Q-replacement strategy for stable optimization, with a continuous parameter 'm' to control the exploration-exploitation trade-off.

## Results

- RePPO achieved the best overall performance among PPO variants on MinAtar games and was competitive with strong Q-learning baselines, all without using explicit exploration bonuses, while consistently maintaining higher policy entropy during training.
- In bandit experiments, ReMax induced stochastic policies for M ">= 2" and adaptively increased exploration with growing reward uncertainty, demonstrating empirically sublinear cumulative regret comparable to classic bandit baselines.
- On the large-scale Craftax environment, RePPO performed comparably to tuned entropy-regularized and RND-augmented PPO variants, confirming its ability to promote sustained exploration in complex, open-ended settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Exploration emerges as an intrinsic property of an agent's objective if it accounts for the ability to make multiple attempts (retries) and the inherent uncertainty in reward outcomes.
> - The continuous retry parameter 'm' in ReMax provides fine-grained control over the exploration-exploitation trade-off, systematically influencing policy stochasticity and convergence speed.
> - A reformulation using an Expected Improvement (EI) baseline allows for an efficient policy gradient that can be estimated from single trajectories, making the multi-sample objective practical for deep RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Exploration is a fundamental challenge in reinforcement learning (RL), as agents must gather sufficient information about an environment to discover high-rewarding policies without prematurely committing to suboptimal actions. The existing landscape of exploration strategies can be broadly categorized into several approaches.

One prevalent category involves the addition of **explicit bonuses** to the environment's reward signal. These include:
- **Entropy regularization**: Encourages stochastic policies to increase the diversity of actions, as seen in methods like Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and maximum entropy inverse RL.
- **Count-based exploration**: Incentivizes visiting states or state-action pairs infrequently encountered, often by assigning bonuses based on pseudo-counts or visitation frequencies.
- **Prediction-error-based methods**: Reward the agent for visiting states where its internal models (e.g., dynamics models) make inaccurate predictions, implying novelty or insufficient understanding.
- **Information-gain-based methods**: Seek to maximize the reduction in model uncertainty.
- **Novelty-based methods**: Directly incentivize visiting "novel" states, using various metrics for novelty.
These bonus-based methods often require additional models to estimate statistics (e.g., density models, dynamics models) or careful tuning of bonus coefficients, which can increase algorithmic complexity and computational overhead.

Another significant line of work focuses on **posterior sampling** (e.g., Thompson sampling), which involves maintaining a distribution over possible reward functions or Q-functions and sampling from this distribution to guide exploration. This often utilizes ensembles of Q-networks or Bayesian neural networks to model epistemic uncertainty.

The presented work introduces a distinct mechanism for exploration by formalizing it as a problem of **greedy reward maximization under uncertainty**, specifically by considering the opportunity for **retrying** actions. This approach deviates from methods that rely on explicit bonus terms or direct posterior sampling. It posits that exploration naturally emerges if an agent's objective accounts for the ability to make multiple attempts (retries) from similar states and for the inherent uncertainty in reward outcomes. This positions ReMax as an alternative to traditional exploration strategies, aiming to achieve exploration as an emergent property of a modified reward objective rather than through a separately engineered exploration mechanism.

The concept shares similarities with **retry-style objectives** such as `pass@K` and `max@K` used in Large Language Model (LLM) training for reasoning tasks, where the success of a task is measured by the best outcome over multiple generated samples. However, this paper differentiates ReMax by explicitly accounting for reward uncertainty in episodic RL settings, where multiple direct retries from the same state are often infeasible, and by developing a continuous retry parameter for fine-grained control. The work also contrasts with **risk-sensitive RL**, which modifies criteria for return distributions to encode risk preferences, and **distributional RL**, which models the full return distribution. While related, ReMax's primary motivation is epistemic uncertainty and exploration rather than risk management.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether exploration can be effectively promoted in reinforcement learning without the need for explicit exploration bonuses. The authors propose an alternative mechanism where exploratory behavior emerges intrinsically from the agent's objective function.

The motivation for this objective stems from a core intuition about how exploration benefits decision-making in real-world scenarios:
1. **Value of Retrying**: An agent benefits from exploration primarily because it anticipates repeatedly encountering similar states. In such situations, attempting various actions allows the agent to discover better outcomes or reduce uncertainty. If an agent had only one opportunity to act in a given state, a purely greedy policy (choosing the action currently believed to yield the highest reward) would be optimal, and exploration would be unnecessary. The ability to retry actions enables learning from diverse experiences and leveraging potentially better outcomes in subsequent attempts.
2. **Impact of Uncertainty**: Environmental uncertainty further motivates exploration. When the true values of actions are not precisely known, attempting alternative actions serves as a form of information gathering. Without any uncertainty, the problem reduces to pure optimization, and exploration is not required.

Based on this intuition, the paper introduces the **ReMax objective**. Its primary goal is to formalize exploration as a form of reward maximization under conditions of uncertainty and the opportunity for retries. Specifically, the ReMax objective evaluates a policy not by the expected return of a single action, but by the expected maximum return observed over *M* samples (or "retries") of actions drawn from the policy, while explicitly accounting for uncertainty in these returns. The underlying hypothesis is that optimizing such an objective will inherently induce stochastic and exploratory behaviors as an emergent property, rather than requiring separate exploration bonus terms.

A secondary objective is to develop an efficient policy optimization method for ReMax in deep RL. This involves deriving a policy gradient formulation that can be estimated from single-trajectory returns, despite the objective inherently involving multiple action samples. Furthermore, the motivation extends to generalizing the discrete retry count *M* to a continuous parameter *m > 0*. This generalization aims to provide finer-grained control over the exploration-exploitation trade-off, allowing for more precise tuning of the agent's exploratory drive.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology developed in this paper proceeds through several stages, starting from a bandit setting, extending to full reinforcement learning, deriving a novel policy gradient, and culminating in a practical algorithm.

#### 3.1. ReMax Objective Formulation

- **Bandit Setting Intuition**: The paper first introduces ReMax in a multi-armed bandit context (Equation 1). For a policy $\pi$ and *M* retries, the objective $J_M^{\text{ReMax}}(\pi)$ is defined as the expectation over the uncertain action values $\mu$ (from a distribution $\Pi$) of the maximum return observed over *M* actions sampled from $\pi$. This contrasts with the standard RL objective $J_{\text{RL}}(\pi)$, which takes the expectation over a single action. The formulation explicitly highlights two components: "retrying" (taking the maximum over *M* samples) and "epistemic uncertainty" (expectation over $\mu \sim \Pi$). The paper empirically demonstrates in this setting that $M \ge 2$ induces stochastic policies, and ReMax adapts exploration to the scale of reward uncertainty. It also shows that even with deterministic rewards, the retry parameter *m* reshapes the objective's curvature, influencing convergence speed.
- **Extension to Reinforcement Learning**: For episodic Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), direct retrying from the exact same state multiple times is generally infeasible due to state transitions. To address this, the ReMax objective for RL (Equation 2) reinterprets retries as multiple "queries" to a Q-function, $Q_{\pi}^M(s, a)$, which represents the expected episodic return from a state-action pair $(s,a)$ under policy $\pi$ within a specific MDP *M*. Uncertainty is modeled as a distribution over possible MDPs, $P(\mathcal{M})$, which induces a distribution $Q$ over Q-functions (Equation 3). This effectively reduces the problem to a bandit-like scenario for a fixed state *s*, where the Q-values $Q(s, \cdot)$ form the action values.
- **Modeling Q-function Uncertainty**: Two approaches are proposed for handling the distribution over Q-functions:
 1. **Explicit modeling**: Using techniques like ensembles or Bayesian methods to explicitly represent Q-function uncertainty.
 2. **Implicit modeling**: Recognizing that in deep RL, Q-critics are inherently nonstationary due to training dynamics, the Q-function at each update can be treated as a sample from an implicit distribution.

#### 3.2. Efficient Computation of Expected Maximum

- **Closed-Form Expression**: To avoid high-variance sampling-based estimators for the inner expectation (the expected maximum over *M* trials for a fixed Q-value vector), the paper derives a closed-form expression (Proposition 3.2). This formula efficiently computes $J_M^{\text{ReMax}}(\pi, s, q)$ by sorting the Q-values $q_{(1)} \ge \dots \ge q_{(K)}$ and summing terms weighted by $(1 - C_j)^M$, where $C_j$ is the cumulative policy mass on the top *j* actions. This computation has a time complexity of $O(K \log K)$, making it practical for discrete action spaces.

#### 3.3. Policy Gradient Formulation for ReMax

- **Challenge with Naive Policy Gradient**: A direct application of the policy gradient theorem to $J_M^{\text{ReMax}}$ (Equation 6) results in an expectation over *M* coupled actions, which cannot be estimated from a single trajectory and is therefore infeasible for episodic RL.
- **Policy Gradient via Expected Improvement (EI)**: To overcome this, the paper introduces a reformulation using a baseline that decouples the max operator (Proposition 4.1). By choosing the baseline for an action $A_m$ as the maximum Q-value among the other $M-1$ sampled actions ($W_{-m}$), the term $(q_{A_m} - W_{-m})_+$ emerges. This term measures the positive improvement of action $A_m$ over the best of the other $M-1$ draws.
- **EI-based Policy Gradient**: The reformulation leads to an "EI-based Policy Gradient" (Definition 4.2), which can be expressed as $M E_{a \sim \pi_{\theta}}[\nabla_{\theta} \log \pi_{\theta}(a|s) \text{EI}_M(q_a, \pi_{\theta}, q)]$. This critical step transforms the gradient into an expectation over a single action, allowing it to be estimated from single-trajectory returns.
- **Closed-Form Computation and Generalization of EI**: A closed-form expression for $\text{EI}_M(R; \pi, q)$ is derived (Proposition 4.3), similar to the ReMax objective itself, also leveraging sorted Q-values. This computation is $O(K \log K)$. Crucially, the formulation allows the integer draw count *M* to be generalized to a positive real parameter *m > 0* by replacing $(1-C_j)^{M-1}$ with $(1-C_j)^{m-1}$, enabling continuous control over the exploration-exploitation trade-off.

#### 3.4. ReMax PPO (RePPO) Algorithm

- **PPO Integration**: The derived policy gradient is integrated into the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) framework, leading to the ReMax PPO (RePPO) algorithm. RePPO modifies standard PPO in two main ways:
 1. It uses a **Q-critic** ($Q_{\phi}$) instead of a state-value critic ($V_{\phi}$).
 2. It employs an **EI-based advantage** ($A^+(t)$) in its clipped, importance-weighted surrogate objective (Equation 12). The advantage is defined as $R^+(t) - b^+(s_t)$, where $R^+(t) = \text{EI}_m(R_{\lambda}^t, \pi_{\theta}, Q_{\phi}(s_t, \cdot))$ is the EI of the lambda-return, and $b^+(s_t)$ is an action-independent baseline based on the expected EI of Q-values.
- **Q-replacement for Exploration Stability**: To mitigate potential issues where an under-specified critic might cause the policy to overfit to a sampled action (by yielding $R(s,a) > Q_{\phi}(s,a)$), the Q-replacement strategy is introduced. When computing EI for a trajectory return $R(s,a)$, the a-th element of $q = Q_{\phi}(s,\cdot)$ is replaced with $R(s,a)$. This ensures the improvement term for the sampled action itself is zero, stabilizing the advantage estimate and preventing spurious "self-improvement."

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical validation across bandit problems and various reinforcement learning benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Bandit Experiments

- **Stochastic Policy Emergence**: In a simple binary bandit with reward uncertainty, increasing the retry parameter $M$ from 1 to $M \ge 2$ shifts the optimal policy from deterministic to stochastic, demonstrating that the retry mechanism induces exploration. Larger $M$ values led to more exploratory policies.
- **Adaptation to Reward Uncertainty**: ReMax demonstrated adaptive exploration based on the magnitude of reward uncertainty. In a Bernoulli bandit where one arm offered rare but high-reward outcomes, ReMax (with $M=2$) increasingly favored this arm as its reward uncertainty (scale $\alpha_1$) grew, whereas a softmax policy (entropy-regularized) remained fixed.
- **Control over Convergence with Deterministic Rewards**: Even with deterministic rewards, the continuous parameter *m* in ReMax reshaped the objective's curvature. Values $m > 1$ flattened the objective, reducing gradient magnitudes and slowing convergence (encouraging exploration), while $m < 1$ sharpened curvature and amplified gradients, accelerating updates. This suggests *m* can modulate the exploration-exploitation trade-off even when epistemic uncertainty is low.
- **Empirically Sublinear Regret**: In multi-armed bandit settings with posterior updates (Beta-Bernoulli and Gaussian-Gaussian), ReMax exhibited empirically sublinear cumulative regret, performing comparably to classic sublinear-regret baselines like UCB and Thompson sampling, and outperforming a Softmax baseline. This suggests ReMax enables effective information gathering in a principled manner.

#### 4.2. MinAtar Experiments

- **Superior Performance without Bonuses**: RePPO with $m \in [1.2, 1.4]$ achieved the best overall performance among PPO variants across four MinAtar games (Breakout, Asterix, Freeway, Space Invaders). It outperformed PPO-V, PPO-Q with an entropy bonus, and PPO-V + RND, all without requiring an explicit exploration bonus. RePPO was also competitive with PQN (a strong Q-learning baseline) on median and IQM metrics, and often outperformed it on mean scores, indicating better tail performance.
- **Emergent Exploration (Higher Entropy)**: RePPO maintained higher policy entropy during training compared to PPO with an entropy bonus, despite not using an explicit entropy term. This finding supports the claim that ReMax intrinsically promotes exploratory behavior.
- **Effect of Continuous Parameter *m***: Varying *m* from 0.9 to 3 systematically influenced policy behavior. Larger *m* values slowed entropy decay, encouraging more exploration. Returns peaked around $m \approx 1.2–1.4$, with performance degrading for values significantly larger or smaller, indicating that *m* provides fine-grained control over the exploration-exploitation balance.
- **Ablation Study**: Both the action-independent baseline and the Q-replacement strategy were found to be crucial for RePPO's performance. Removing either component led to substantial degradation, suggesting they are necessary for realizing the full benefits of the ReMax objective and its policy gradient.

#### 4.3. Craftax Experiments

- **Competitive Performance in Open-Ended Environments**: On Craftax, a large-scale, open-ended RL environment, RePPO (with $m=1.2$) achieved performance comparable to tuned entropy-regularized PPO and RND-augmented PPO variants, despite not using any explicit exploration bonuses. It outperformed PPO variants without such bonuses.
- **Sustained Exploration at Scale**: Similar to MinAtar, RePPO on Craftax maintained higher policy entropy throughout training compared to PPO baselines without bonuses, further supporting its ability to promote exploration in more complex settings.

#### 4.4. Atari Experiments (Appendix F)

- **Exploration Promotion Confirmed**: On a suite of 10 dense-reward Atari games identified as hard exploration problems, RePPO again demonstrated its capacity to promote exploration, with larger *m* values leading to higher policy entropy.
- **Contextual Exploration Needs**: Performance peaked at smaller *m* values (0.9-1.0) on these Atari environments, and PPO *without* an entropy bonus sometimes outperformed PPO *with* an entropy bonus. This finding suggests that these specific Atari environments may require less aggressive exploration than other benchmarks, and RePPO's continuous *m* parameter allowed for adapting to this requirement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a novel approach to the problem of exploration in reinforcement learning, with several significant contributions and potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Intrinsic Exploration Mechanism**: The core significance lies in demonstrating that exploration can emerge as an intrinsic property of a carefully designed reward objective, ReMax, rather than requiring auxiliary exploration bonuses. This shifts the paradigm from engineering explicit exploration mechanisms to embedding exploration directly into the agent's goal.
- **Formalization of Intuition**: The paper formalizes the intuition that exploration is beneficial when repeated attempts are possible under uncertainty. By explicitly incorporating "retries" and "uncertainty" into the objective, ReMax provides a theoretical framework for understanding and promoting exploratory behavior.
- **Efficient Policy Optimization**: The derivation of a novel, estimation-friendly policy gradient for ReMax, along with closed-form expressions for the objective and Expected Improvement (EI), makes the approach computationally efficient and practical for deep reinforcement learning. This is particularly important for discrete action spaces where Q-values for all actions can be computed and sorted.
- **Fine-Grained Control**: The generalization of the discrete retry count *M* to a continuous parameter *m > 0* provides a flexible mechanism for tuning the exploration-exploitation trade-off. This continuous control allows practitioners to adapt the level of exploration more precisely to specific tasks and environments.
- **Empirical Validation**: The comprehensive empirical evaluation across various benchmarks, from bandit problems to MinAtar and the large-scale Craftax environment, supports the effectiveness and scalability of the proposed RePPO algorithm in inducing and leveraging emergent exploration.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Reduced Algorithmic Complexity**: By obviating the need for explicit exploration bonuses or separate posterior models, ReMax could simplify the design and implementation of RL algorithms. This may lead to more robust and less hyperparameter-sensitive agents, as practitioners would not need to tune additional exploration coefficients or build auxiliary models.
- **Broader Applicability**: The method's independence from explicit bonus design or posterior modeling could broaden the applicability of RL to domains where designing reliable bonuses (e.g., in sparse reward settings) or explicitly modeling uncertainty is challenging or computationally prohibitive.
- **Advancements in Reasoning Tasks**: The connection to `pass@K` objectives in LLM reasoning tasks suggests a potential for cross-domain impact. ReMax's ability to account for uncertainty and optimize for multiple attempts could be beneficial in scenarios where LLMs or other agents need to explore multiple paths or generate diverse solutions to complex problems.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: ReMax provides a new theoretical and practical foundation for exploring how different objective functions can implicitly shape agent behavior. This could lead to further research into alternative reward formulations that inherently promote desirable behaviors like curiosity, information seeking, or robustness, without explicit extrinsic motivators.
- **Understanding Emergent Behavior**: The work contributes to the understanding of how complex behaviors like exploration can emerge from simpler, reward-maximizing principles when the objective function is appropriately structured to reflect the fundamental conditions under which such behaviors are advantageous.

#### 5.3. Scope and Future Work

The current study primarily focuses on discrete-action problems with a relatively small number of actions. Future work could explore extensions to large discrete or continuous action spaces, potentially through sampling-based estimators. Investigating alternative instantiations of ReMax beyond the PPO-based implementation, such as integration with Q-learning or off-policy actor-critic methods, is also a potential avenue. Further research could delve into distinguishing between stochastic and deep exploration under ReMax, potentially by modeling epistemic uncertainty in Q-values explicitly, and establishing formal regret bounds.
