# Multi Time Scale World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Karlsruhe Institute Of Technology and collaborators at SAP SE and Robert Bosch GmbH developed Multi Time Scale State Space (MTS3) models, a hierarchical probabilistic framework for learning world dynamics. The model achieves accurate long-horizon predictions and robust uncertainty quantification across various complex simulated and real-world robotic environments by explicitly modeling dynamics at multiple temporal granularities.

## Problem

- Existing single-time scale world models struggle with error accumulation and computational cost during long-horizon predictions.
- Such models often fail to capture long-term trends or higher-level patterns essential for efficient planning.
- Providing reliable uncertainty estimates over extended prediction horizons remains a challenge for intelligent agents.

## Method

- MTS3 employs a hierarchical architecture with coupled 'fast time-scale' and 'slow time-scale' state-space models to represent dynamics at different granularities.
- It utilizes (locally) linear Gaussian dynamics, enabling closed-form Gaussian inference with factorized covariances for computational efficiency.
- The model incorporates Bayesian aggregation of abstract actions and observations to drive the slow time-scale dynamics and uses an imputation-based training scheme for robust long-term predictions.

## Results

- MTS3 consistently achieved accurate long-term action-conditional future predictions (RMSE) and robust uncertainty estimates (NLL) on 6 diverse datasets, including D4RL, real robotic manipulation, and mobile robotics scenarios.
- The model outperformed single-time scale deep Kalman models and traditional RNNs, and often matched or surpassed Transformer-based baselines, particularly for principled uncertainty estimation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that latent state splitting (memory component), action abstraction, and imputation-based training are critical components for MTS3's performance in long-horizon prediction tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling dynamics at multiple temporal scales significantly improves the accuracy and robustness of long-horizon predictions and uncertainty estimates.
> - The use of abstract actions and observations, coupled with Bayesian aggregation, is crucial for effectively learning and propagating information across different time scales.
> - An imputation-based training strategy, where the model learns to fill in masked observations, is essential for developing strong long-term predictive capabilities.
