# Multi Time Scale World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Karlsruhe Institute Of Technology and collaborators at SAP SE and Robert Bosch GmbH developed Multi Time Scale State Space (MTS3) models, a hierarchical probabilistic framework for learning world dynamics. The model achieves accurate long-horizon predictions and robust uncertainty quantification across various complex simulated and real-world robotic environments by explicitly modeling dynamics at multiple temporal granularities.

## Problem

- Existing single-time scale world models struggle with error accumulation and computational cost during long-horizon predictions.
- Such models often fail to capture long-term trends or higher-level patterns essential for efficient planning.
- Providing reliable uncertainty estimates over extended prediction horizons remains a challenge for intelligent agents.

## Method

- MTS3 employs a hierarchical architecture with coupled 'fast time-scale' and 'slow time-scale' state-space models to represent dynamics at different granularities.
- It utilizes (locally) linear Gaussian dynamics, enabling closed-form Gaussian inference with factorized covariances for computational efficiency.
- The model incorporates Bayesian aggregation of abstract actions and observations to drive the slow time-scale dynamics and uses an imputation-based training scheme for robust long-term predictions.

## Results

- MTS3 consistently achieved accurate long-term action-conditional future predictions (RMSE) and robust uncertainty estimates (NLL) on 6 diverse datasets, including D4RL, real robotic manipulation, and mobile robotics scenarios.
- The model outperformed single-time scale deep Kalman models and traditional RNNs, and often matched or surpassed Transformer-based baselines, particularly for principled uncertainty estimation.
- Ablation studies confirmed that latent state splitting (memory component), action abstraction, and imputation-based training are critical components for MTS3's performance in long-horizon prediction tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling dynamics at multiple temporal scales significantly improves the accuracy and robustness of long-horizon predictions and uncertainty estimates.
> - The use of abstract actions and observations, coupled with Bayesian aggregation, is crucial for effectively learning and propagating information across different time scales.
> - An imputation-based training strategy, where the model learns to fill in masked observations, is essential for developing strong long-term predictive capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "world models" – internal representations of environment dynamics that can predict future states given actions – is a cornerstone of intelligent agents, crucial for model-predictive control and model-based reinforcement learning. However, a significant challenge in this field is creating models that can effectively reason and predict across **multiple levels of temporal abstraction** while also providing **robust uncertainty estimates** over long horizons.

Historically, world models have often operated at a single, fine-grained temporal resolution (e.g., milliseconds). This approach struggles to capture long-term trends, becomes computationally expensive for long-horizon predictions due to accumulating errors, and may miss higher-level patterns essential for efficient planning (e.g., a robot's precise grip vs. overall task goal). Early theoretical work by Sutton and Precup & Sutton highlighted the importance of multi-time scale models, but practical, scalable implementations have remained a "major technical hurdle".

This paper addresses this gap by building upon two main lines of research:

1. **Deep State Space Models (SSMs):** Previous work by Neumann's group (e.g., RKN, HiP-RSSM) and others has combined the probabilistic foundations of SSMs with the representational power of deep neural networks. These models offer tractable probabilistic inference and scalability, often relying on exact (or locally linear Gaussian) inference schemes. However, most of these models have been primarily evaluated on short-term prediction tasks. MTS3 extends this by introducing a hierarchical structure that explicitly supports multi-time scale operation.
2. **Temporal Abstraction in Reinforcement Learning:** Concepts like "feudal reinforcement learning" and multi-time models have long recognized the utility of hierarchical control and planning, where high-level policies dictate abstract goals over longer timescales, and low-level policies execute fine-grained actions. MTS3 provides a principled probabilistic framework for learning the underlying world dynamics required to support such hierarchical reasoning, moving beyond tabular settings to complex, high-dimensional systems.

The work also positions itself against recent advancements in **Transformers**, which have shown remarkable success in capturing long-range dependencies in various domains, including time series forecasting and world models. While Transformers excel at long-range sequence modeling, the authors argue that many existing Transformer-based world models are deterministic and do not provide principled uncertainty estimation or explicit temporal abstractions in the same manner as probabilistic hierarchical models. By developing MTS3, the authors aim to combine the strengths of probabilistic, exact inference for robust uncertainty with the necessity of multi-time scale reasoning for efficient long-horizon predictions, effectively extending the capabilities of deep SSMs into a hierarchical domain relevant for complex real-world agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **devise a principled probabilistic formalism and architecture capable of learning world models that operate at multiple levels of temporal abstractions.** This aims to overcome the limitations of existing single-time scale world models in dealing with complex uncertainty predictions and efficient long-horizon planning.

The specific motivations and desiderata for the proposed Multi Time Scale State Space (MTS3) model are:

- **Modeling Dynamics at Multiple Time Scales:** To explicitly capture both fast-changing, short-term dynamics (e.g., precise joint movements in a robot) and slow-changing, long-term trends or "task" dynamics (e.g., the overall goal or configuration of an environment).
- **Accurate Long-Term Predictions and Uncertainty Estimates:** To enable predictions several seconds into the future, providing not just mean estimates but also reliable uncertainty quantification, which is crucial for risk-aware planning and decision-making in real-world systems.
- **Computational Efficiency:** To maintain tractability during learning and inference, especially for long horizons, by leveraging efficient inference schemes. This contrasts with single-time scale models where errors and computational costs accumulate over long sequences.
- **Robustness to Changing Environment Configurations:** To allow for a single global model that can adapt to varying task structures or non-stationarities, rather than requiring re-learning for every new scenario. The higher-level abstraction should ideally capture these adaptable aspects.
- **Support for Model-Based Control and Planning:** To serve as a key component for advanced model-predictive control and model-based reinforcement learning algorithms that require accurate, robust, and temporally abstract predictions.

In essence, the authors are motivated to create a "more intelligent" world model that mirrors human or biological intelligence in its ability to reason about actions and their consequences across various timescales, from immediate movements to long-term task completion.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology is the **Multi Time Scale State Space (MTS3) model**, defined as a hierarchical sequential latent variable model. It leverages principles of (locally) linear Gaussian State Space Models (SSMs) and factorized inference to achieve principled probabilistic modeling and computational efficiency.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Structure (2-Level MTS3)

The paper primarily details a 2-level MTS3, which can be extended to N levels. It consists of two coupled SSMs:

- **Fast Time-Scale SSM (fts-SSM):** This model operates at the original fine-grained time step (∆t) of the dynamical system. It models the short-term, precise dynamics of the observable latent state `z_t` conditioned on primitive actions `a_t`. Crucially, its dynamics (`f_fts`) are *parameterized* by a "task descriptor" `l_k`.
- **Slow Time-Scale SSM (sts-SSM):** This model updates only every `H` steps, effectively operating at a coarser time step (`H∆t`). Its latent state is the task descriptor `l_k`, which captures slow-moving, long-term trends or contextual information. It propagates based on abstract actions `α_k` and is updated by abstract observations `β_k,t`.

#### 3.2. Coupling and Inference

The key to MTS3 is the coupling between the two SSMs:
- The prior belief `p(l_k | β_{1:k-1}, α_{1:k})` from the `sts-SSM` provides the distribution over the task descriptor `l_k` that then parameterizes the dynamics of the `fts-SSM`. This allows the `fts-SSM` to adapt its dynamics based on the inferred long-term context.
- Both SSMs utilize **(locally) linear Gaussian dynamics** and **observation models**. This choice enables **closed-form Gaussian inference**, which means Kalman prediction and update rules can be applied. The authors leverage the factorized inference scheme from RKN, where only diagonal and one off-diagonal vector of the covariance matrix are computed, greatly enhancing computational efficiency and differentiability for end-to-end learning.

#### 3.3. Abstract Actions and Observations

To facilitate the `sts-SSM` operating at a coarser granularity, the paper introduces abstract representations:
- **Abstract Observations (`β_k,t`):** Instead of processing all `H` raw observations, a specialized encoder (`enc_β`) transforms each raw observation `o_k,t` (along with a temporal encoding) into a latent abstract observation `β_k,t` and its uncertainty `ν_k,t`. These `H` abstract observations within a window `k` are then aggregated using **Bayesian aggregation** into a consistent belief about the `l_k` latent state. This aggregation is permutation-invariant and enables parallel processing.
- **Abstract Actions (`α_k`):** Similarly, primitive actions `a_k,t` over a window `k` are encoded (`enc_α`) into latent abstract actions `α_k,t` and their uncertainties `ρ_k,t`. These are also aggregated using Bayesian aggregation to form a single abstract action `α_k` (and its uncertainty `Σ_α_k`), which drives the `sts-SSM` dynamics.

#### 3.4. Training Objective and Imputation

The model is trained by **maximizing the posterior predictive log-likelihood**. This involves predicting future observations `o_k,t+1` conditioned on past (latent and abstract) observations and actions. The objective is approximated using a Gaussian distribution, and its parameters are decoded from the mean and variance estimates of the prior belief.

To improve **long-term prediction performance**, the authors introduce an **imputation-based training scheme**. During training, a fraction of observations are randomly masked out. The network is then explicitly tasked to "impute" these missing observations, effectively forcing it to learn robust long-horizon predictions even when intermediate ground truth observations are unavailable. This masking occurs at both time scales (single time steps or entire time windows).

#### 3.5. N-level MTS3 Generalization

The framework is designed to be extensible to an arbitrary number of `N` temporal abstractions. An `N`-level MTS3 comprises `N` SSMs (`S_0` to `S_{N-1}`), where `S_i` is parameterized by the latent state of `S_{i+1}` and operates at a time step `H_i∆t`, with `H_0 = 1` and `H_i < H_{i+1}`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluate MTS3's performance in action-conditional long-horizon future predictions across a diverse set of simulated and real-world dynamical systems.

#### 4.1. Environments and Datasets

- **D4RL Datasets:** HalfCheetah, Medium Maze, and Franka Kitchen environments, chosen for varied properties like sub-optimal trajectories, multi-task interactions, and non-Markovian collection policies.
- **Manipulation Datasets:** Real-world data from a heavy machinery excavator arm and a Panda robot with varying payloads, representing highly non-linear, non-Markovian, and non-stationary dynamics.
- **Mobile Robotics Dataset:** A simulated four-wheeled mobile robot navigating uneven terrain, posing challenges due to wheel-terrain interactions and non-stationary dynamics.
In all datasets, only position information is provided, and velocities are masked to create partially observable settings.

#### 4.2. Baselines

- **Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs):** LSTMs and GRUs.
- **Deep State Space Models (RSSMs):** RKN and HiP-RSSM (from the same research group), known for short-term prediction and exact inference.
- **Transformers:** AR-Transformer (GPT-like autoregressive) and Multi-Transformer (direct multi-step loss).

#### 4.3. Deterministic Predictions (Mean Estimates - RMSE)

- MTS3 consistently delivers accurate long-term action-conditional future predictions across all 6 datasets, as measured by Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE).
- Single-time scale deep Kalman models (RKN, HiP-RSSM) and traditional RNNs (LSTM, GRU) generally fail to provide meaningful mean estimates beyond a few milliseconds or short horizons.
- AR-Transformers also perform poorly, likely due to error accumulation in autoregressive predictions.
- Multi-Transformers are strong baselines, outperforming MTS3 slightly in Medium Maze and Panda datasets. However, for complex tasks like Franka Kitchen (involving multi-object, multi-task interactions), MTS3 is uniquely capable of providing meaningful long-horizon predictions.

#### 4.4. Probabilistic Predictions (Variance Estimates - NLL)

- MTS3 yields the most accurate uncertainty estimates (quantified by Negative Log-Likelihood - NLL) in most datasets, with the exception of Medium Maze where Multi-Transformer performs slightly better.
- Crucially, AR-Transformers and deep Kalman models (RKN, HiP-RSSM) struggle to learn meaningful uncertainty representations for long-term predictions, often resulting in highly divergent or unstable variance estimates. This highlights MTS3's strength in principled uncertainty propagation.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies on Modeling Assumptions and Training Scheme

The ablation studies confirm the importance of key design choices:
- **Latent State Splitting (Memory Component):** An MTS3 variant without the split into observable (`p_t`) and unobservable (`m_t`) latent states (i.e., `h_fts = I`) leads to worse long-term predictions. This suggests that explicitly modeling an unobservable "memory" part in the latent state is critical for handling non-Markovian dynamics.
- **Action Abstraction:** Removing action abstractions in the `sts-SSM` significantly degrades long-horizon prediction performance, demonstrating the necessity of abstract actions for effective multi-time scale dynamics learning.
- **Imputation-Based Training:** Training MTS3 solely for one-step-ahead predictions (without masking/imputation) results in significantly poorer long-term prediction capabilities. This confirms the vital role of the imputation scheme in fostering robust long-horizon prediction.

#### 4.6. Role of Discretization Step `H∆t`

- Ablations on the `H∆t` parameter (which defines the slow time scale) on the hydraulics dataset show that both very small values (e.g., H=2,3,5,10) and very large values (e.g., H=75) lead to performance degradation. An optimal intermediate value (H=30 in this case) achieves the best performance.
- Visualization of predictions for different `H` values reveals that the slow-changing task dynamics, inferred by the `sts-SSM`, "reconfigures" the fast dynamics periodically. For very large `H`, the `fts-SSM` has to perform too many steps within a window, leading to error accumulation, while very small `H` might not capture sufficiently abstract long-term trends.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Multi Time Scale World Models" (MTS3) paper presents a significant advancement in the field of intelligent agents and model-based learning, particularly for complex, real-world dynamical systems. Its main significance lies in:

- **Principled Probabilistic Formalism for Multi-Time Scale Learning:** MTS3 offers a mathematically sound framework for integrating temporal abstractions into world models. By combining the strengths of deep state-space models with a hierarchical structure, it provides a powerful tool for reasoning at multiple levels of granularity, a capability considered crucial for autonomous machine intelligence.
- **Highly Accurate Long-Horizon Predictions with Uncertainty:** A key challenge in model-based reinforcement learning and planning is the accumulation of errors and uncertainty over long prediction horizons. MTS3 demonstrates superior performance in both deterministic (mean) and probabilistic (variance) long-term predictions, spanning several seconds into the future, across various challenging benchmarks. This principled uncertainty propagation is vital for robust and risk-aware decision-making in real-world applications.
- **Robustness to Complex Dynamics:** The model proves effective on diverse systems, including D4RL environments, real robotic manipulators (Panda, excavator), and mobile robots. Its ability to handle partially observable, non-linear, non-Markovian, and non-stationary dynamics is a testament to its robustness and practical applicability.
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** By leveraging closed-form Gaussian inference and factorized covariance representations (inspired by RKN), MTS3 remains computationally efficient and scalable during learning and inference, despite its hierarchical complexity. This efficiency is critical for deploying world models in real-time control systems.
- **Foundational for Hierarchical Control and Planning:** The ability to predict at multiple temporal abstractions opens new avenues for hierarchical planning and control. High-level plans can leverage the slow-changing task dynamics, while low-level controllers can use the fast-changing state dynamics for precise execution. This could lead to more efficient, robust, and generalizable robotic behaviors.
- **Competitive Performance with Simpler Models:** The paper highlights that MTS3, based on "simple linear models with principled modeling assumptions," can compete with or even outperform much larger Transformer-based models, which often require significantly more parameters and may lack principled uncertainty estimation. This suggests a more data-efficient and interpretable approach.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advanced Robotics:** Enabling robots to perform complex, long-horizon tasks with greater autonomy, safety, and adaptability by reasoning about both immediate actions and overarching goals. This is particularly relevant for industrial automation (e.g., excavators, manufacturing) where precise control and task-level understanding are critical.
- **Model-Based Reinforcement Learning:** Providing more accurate and reliable world models for agents to plan and explore, leading to faster learning and better performance in complex environments.
- **Simulation and Digital Twins:** Creating more realistic and predictive digital models of physical systems, useful for design, optimization, and training without relying solely on physical prototypes.
- **General AI:** Contributing to the fundamental understanding and construction of artificial intelligence systems capable of type 2 reasoning, mirroring the hierarchical cognitive processes observed in biological intelligence.

The identified limitations, such as restricting experiments to two hierarchies and proprioceptive sensors, along with the call for future work on hierarchical control and image-based inputs, indicate clear pathways for further research and development to broaden the scope and impact of MTS3.
