# Adaptive Conformal Guidance for Learning under Uncertainty

> [!summary] Summary
> AdaConG, a framework from researchers at the University of Maryland, North Carolina State University, and Adobe Research, integrates split conformal prediction directly into the training loop to dynamically modulate guidance signals based on their quantified uncertainty. This approach enhances learning robustness and improves performance across supervised, semi-supervised, and reinforcement learning tasks, achieving up to +10.89% accuracy gains in knowledge distillation under domain shift.

## Problem

- Machine learning systems often rely on noisy or unreliable guidance signals, such as pseudo-labels or expert demonstrations, which can degrade performance if blindly trusted.
- Existing methods for addressing uncertainty are often heuristic-based, poorly calibrated, or limited to post-hoc analysis, lacking dynamic integration into the learning process.
- The challenge lies in adaptively balancing reliance on imperfect guidance with the model's self-learning capabilities to prevent error propagation and improve generalization.

## Method

- **AdaConG** embeds ==split conformal prediction== directly into the machine learning training loop to provide statistically rigorous and model-agnostic uncertainty quantification for guidance signals.
- It quantifies guidance uncertainty based on the size of prediction sets generated by conformal prediction, then uses this measure to adaptively weight the influence of guidance loss in the overall objective function.
- The framework is applied and validated across diverse learning paradigms, including knowledge distillation (supervised), semi-supervised learning, and imitation-guided reinforcement learning.

## Results

- Achieved up to +**10.89%** improvement in Top-1 accuracy for knowledge distillation under domain shift, outperforming training from scratch and other uncertainty-aware methods.
- Consistently improved Top-1 accuracy in semi-supervised image classification across multiple baselines and datasets, with gains of up to +**5.98%** by mitigating pseudo-label noise.
- Demonstrated significantly faster convergence and over **6x** higher accumulated rewards in imitation-guided reinforcement learning in domain-shifted environments compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating conformal prediction directly into the training process provides a principled, dynamic mechanism for managing uncertainty in guidance, moving beyond static or heuristic approaches.
> - Adaptively reducing reliance on highly uncertain guidance signals enhances robustness to noisy data, domain shifts, and suboptimal teachers.
> - The approach offers a generalizable solution for effective learning under uncertainty, applicable across various machine learning settings and tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Adaptive Conformal Guidance for Learning Under Uncertainty" addresses a critical and pervasive challenge in modern machine learning: the effective utilization of guidance signals when those signals are inherently noisy, unreliable, or prone to generalization issues. Machine learning systems widely employ various forms of guidance—ranging from explicit labeled datasets in supervised learning to pseudo-labels in semi-supervised learning and expert demonstrations in reinforcement learning—to improve performance, bootstrap learning in data-scarce environments, and enhance sample efficiency. However, the efficacy of this guidance hinges on its quality. Domain shifts, limited data, inherent model uncertainty, or environmental variations can render guidance noisy, misleading, or non-generalizable, leading to degraded model performance if blindly trusted.

The broader research landscape has explored several avenues to tackle uncertainty in machine learning. These include:
- **Heuristic Uncertainty Estimates:** Methods like Monte Carlo dropout, entropy-based reweighting, or perturbing distillation losses attempt to quantify and integrate uncertainty. However, these often rely on heuristics, can be overly confident, poorly calibrated, and are typically confined to specific tasks like supervised classification.
- **Domain-Specific Solutions:** Some approaches address uncertainty within narrow application domains, such as medical imaging or human-robot interaction. While effective in their niches, they lack broad applicability.
- **Post-hoc Calibration:** Conformal Prediction (CP) has emerged as a statistically rigorous, distribution-free, and model-agnostic framework for quantifying predictive uncertainty by constructing prediction sets with guaranteed coverage. Traditionally, CP has been applied *after* a model has been trained, primarily for output calibration or flagging unreliable predictions.

The key innovation of AdaConG is its departure from these existing paradigms by being the *first framework* to directly embed split conformal prediction into the *training loop* of machine learning models. This is a significant shift. Instead of treating uncertainty as a post-training diagnostic or relying on ad-hoc heuristics, AdaConG leverages CP's robust uncertainty quantification *during* the learning process to dynamically modulate the influence of guidance signals. This allows the model to adaptively reduce its reliance on potentially misleading information, thereby enhancing learning performance and robustness across diverse learning settings. By integrating a rigorous uncertainty measure directly into the optimization objective, AdaConG bridges the gap between theoretical guarantees of CP and the practical demands of adaptive, robust learning in real-world, uncertain environments. This positions AdaConG as a foundational advancement towards more trustworthy and adaptable AI systems that can learn effectively from imperfect sources of information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a robust and broadly applicable framework that enables machine learning systems to effectively leverage guidance signals while appropriately accounting for their inherent uncertainty. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Noisy and Unreliable Guidance:** Machine learning systems frequently rely on external guidance, such as annotated data, pseudo-labels, or expert demonstrations. However, these guidance signals are often imperfect due to factors like domain shifts between training and deployment environments, limited availability of high-quality data, or generalization limitations of pre-trained models. Blindly trusting such noisy or misleading signals can lead to error propagation, overfitting to incorrect information, and ultimately, suboptimal model performance.
2. **Balancing Trust and Self-Reliance:** While noisy guidance can be detrimental, discarding potentially valuable guidance altogether is wasteful of computational resources and domain knowledge. The challenge lies in finding a principled way to discern when to trust guidance and when to rely more on the model's own learning and exploration.
3. **Limitations of Existing Uncertainty Methods:** Previous attempts to incorporate uncertainty into learning often suffer from drawbacks. Many rely on heuristic uncertainty estimates (e.g., entropy, softmax probability) which can be overconfident, poorly calibrated, and fail under distribution shifts. Others are domain-specific, limiting their general utility. Methods like Conformal Prediction, while rigorous, have primarily been applied post-hoc to calibrate model outputs, rather than dynamically informing the learning process itself.
4. **Desire for a Generalizable Solution:** The authors aim for a solution that is not confined to a single machine learning paradigm or application. They seek a "simple yet effective, and broadly applicable solution for incorporating uncertainty-aware guidance" that can span supervised, semi-supervised, and reinforcement learning settings.

In light of these motivations, the **key objective** of AdaConG is to dynamically modulate the influence of guidance signals based on a rigorous quantification of their associated uncertainty using split conformal prediction. This aims to empower models to reduce reliance on potentially misleading signals, enhance learning performance, and improve robustness under imperfect guidance, thereby addressing the fundamental question of how to effectively learn under uncertainty.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AdaConG proposes a general framework that integrates split conformal prediction (CP) directly into the training loop to adaptively modulate the influence of guidance signals. The core idea is to quantify the uncertainty of guidance and use this quantification to dynamically weight the guidance loss component of the overall objective function.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Split Conformal Prediction (CP)

AdaConG leverages split CP, a distribution-free and model-agnostic method that provides prediction sets with guaranteed coverage levels.
- **Nonconformity Score ($s$):** This score measures how unusual a prediction is. For regression, it's typically the absolute residual $|y - \hat{y}(x)|$. For classification, it's $1 - p_{\bar{y}}(x)$, where $p_{\bar{y}}(x)$ is the model's probability for the true class $\bar{y}$.
- **Calibration Set ($D_{cal}$):** A held-out dataset used to compute the empirical distribution of nonconformity scores.
- **Quantile ($q_{1-\alpha}$):** From the nonconformity scores on $D_{cal}$, a $(1-\alpha)$-quantile is computed, representing a threshold.
- **Prediction Set ($C(x)$):** For a new test input $x_{test}$, the prediction set $C(x_{test}) = \{y: s(x_{test}, y) \leq q_{1-\alpha}\}$ is constructed. This set contains the true label with a probability of at least $1-\alpha$, assuming exchangeability.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Conformal Guidance (AdaConG) Framework

AdaConG builds upon split CP to create an adaptive weighting mechanism.
- **Guidance Uncertainty Quantification ($u(x)$):** The uncertainty of a guidance signal for an input $x$ is primarily quantified by the *size* of its prediction set, $|C(x)|$. A mapping function $g$ (e.g., $g(n) = (n-1)/(K-1)$ for a K-class problem) normalizes $u(x)$ to be within $$. Larger prediction sets imply higher uncertainty.
- **Adaptive Weighting ($w(x)$):** An adaptive weight $w(x)$ is computed from the uncertainty $u(x)$ using a monotonically decreasing function $h$ (e.g., exponential decay $h(u) = \exp(-\gamma u)$ with temperature $\gamma > 0$). This ensures that higher uncertainty leads to a lower weight, thereby reducing the influence of unreliable guidance.
- **Loss Function Integration:** The overall loss function for training the target model is defined as $L = \lambda_{task} L_t + w(x) \cdot \lambda_{guide} L_g$, where $L_t$ is the primary task loss, $L_g$ is the guidance loss, and $\lambda_{task}$, $\lambda_{guide}$ are coefficients. This dynamically weighted guidance loss is added to the task loss.

#### 3.3. Application-Specific Instantiations

- **Supervised Learning (Knowledge Distillation):**
 - **Scenario:** A pretrained teacher model $f_p$ guides a student model $f_t$ under potential domain shift.
 - **Data Split:** The target domain dataset $D_t$ is split into training ($D_{t,train}$), calibration ($D_{cal}$), and testing ($D_{test}$) sets. $D_{cal}$ is crucial for conformalizing $f_p$ on target domain data.
 - **Guidance Loss:** $L_g$ is typically KL divergence between student and teacher logits.
 - **Uncertainty:** RAPS algorithm is used to compute prediction sets for $f_p$.

- **Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL):**
 - **Scenario:** Leveraging a limited labeled dataset $D_l$ and a large unlabeled dataset $D_u$.
 - **Pseudo-labeling:** A model $f$ generates pseudo-labels $\tilde{y}$ for unlabeled samples $x$ (weakly augmented $x_{weak}$).
 - **Calibration:** $D_{cal}$ is constructed from $D_l$ with weak augmentation. CP generates prediction sets $C(x)$ for unlabeled samples.
 - **Unsupervised Loss:** The unsupervised loss $L_u$ for consistency regularization (e.g., cross-entropy between strongly augmented $f(x_{strong})$ and $\tilde{y}$) is adaptively weighted by $w(x)$.
 - **Total Loss:** $L = L_s + \lambda_u L_u$, where $L_s$ is supervised loss on $D_l$.

- **Imitation-Guided Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Scenario:** An imitation policy $\pi_I$ (from expert demonstrations) guides an RL policy $\pi_R$.
 - **Nonconformity Score:** $s(s, a) = -\log \pi(a|s)$.
 - **Calibration:** A static calibration set $D_{cal,I}$ from $\pi_I$ rollouts for $\hat{q}_I$. For $\pi_R$, an adaptive CP approach is used with a dynamic calibration set $D_{cal,R}$ (sliding window) and an exponentially moving average (EMA) for $\hat{q}_R$.
 - **Guidance Weight:** $w(s) = h(u_I(s), u_R(s))$, e.g., $w(s) = \frac{\exp(-u_I(s))}{\exp(-u_I(s)) + \exp(-u_R(s))}$, balancing reliance on $\pi_I$ vs. $\pi_R$ based on their relative uncertainties.
 - **Action Selection:** Actions are sampled from $\pi_I$ or $\pi_R$ based on $w(s)$. An exploration probability $\epsilon$ also encourages independent RL exploration.
 - **Total Loss:** $L = L_t + w(s) \cdot L_g$, where $L_t$ is the RL objective (e.g., advantage-weighted policy gradient) and $L_g$ is KL divergence between $\pi_R$ and $\pi_I$.

In essence, AdaConG introduces a unified, flexible framework where the quality of guidance is continuously assessed via CP's uncertainty quantification and dynamically incorporated into the learning objective.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental validation across diverse tasks demonstrates that AdaConG consistently improves performance and robustness under imperfect guidance.

#### 4.1. Knowledge Distillation (Supervised Learning)

- **Improved Performance under Domain Shift:** When the teacher model underperforms due to domain shift (e.g., Gaussian noise in CIFAR-100), traditional knowledge distillation (KD) methods lead to students performing worse than training from scratch. AdaConG, however, significantly improves performance, with gains of up to **+10.89%** in Top-1 accuracy for KD, allowing the student to surpass even the scratch-trained student. This highlights AdaConG's ability to selectively leverage "dark knowledge" from an unreliable teacher.
- **Superior to Heuristic Methods:** AdaConG combined with KD consistently outperforms other uncertainty-aware KD methods that rely on heuristic estimates (e.g., Entropy, MC Dropout, MSP, PTLoss, EA-KD). This is attributed to CP's more rigorous and reliable uncertainty estimates, especially under domain shifts.
- **Architectural Flexibility:** AdaConG's effectiveness extends to both homogeneous (teacher and student of same architecture type) and heterogeneous (different architectures) teacher-student setups.
- **Robustness to Hyperparameters:** Ablation studies confirm that AdaConG's performance is robust to choices of the error rate ($\alpha$) for CP and the temperature parameter ($\gamma$) for adaptive weighting.
- **Quantile Computation is Key:** Direct use of nonconformity scores performs worse than AdaConG, validating the necessity of computing quantiles to transform non-conformalized uncertainty measures into rigorous ones via prediction sets.
- **Computational Efficiency:** AdaConG adds minimal computational overhead compared to standard KD (0.08ms latency per sample) and is significantly more efficient than MC dropout, which requires multiple forward passes.

#### 4.2. Semi-Supervised Image Classification

- **Consistent Accuracy Gains:** AdaConG consistently improves the Top-1 accuracy across various SSL baselines (UDA, FixMatch, FlexMatch) and datasets (CIFAR-10/100, STL-10) with varying numbers of labeled samples. Gains of up to **+5.98%** (FixMatch on CIFAR-10 with 40 labels) were observed.
- **Mitigation of Pseudo-Label Noise:** By adaptively reweighting pseudo-labels based on their uncertainty, AdaConG reduces reliance on noisy supervision, thereby mitigating error propagation during self-training.
- **Flexibility in Loss Functions:** The method remains effective even when using different guidance loss functions, such as Mean Squared Error (MSE) instead of cross-entropy, demonstrating its generality.

#### 4.3. Gridworld Navigation (Imitation-Guided Reinforcement Learning)

- **Faster Convergence and Higher Rewards:** Both AdaConG and its hard variant (Hard AdaConG) demonstrate significantly faster convergence and achieve higher accumulated rewards compared to purely RL (SAC) and other imitation-bootstrapped RL methods (IBRL, Soft IBRL) in all tested environments (Lava 1, Lava 2, Door).
- **Robustness in Shifted Environments:** In the domain-shifted Lava 2 environment, where the imitation policy severely underperforms due to generalization constraints, AdaConG achieves rewards **over 6x higher** than the best-performing baselines after convergence. This is a critical finding, showcasing AdaConG's ability to extract useful knowledge even from a suboptimal guidance policy by dynamically shifting reliance.
- **Adaptive Policy Learning:** The prediction uncertainty of the RL policy learned with AdaConG decreases over time and approaches that of the IL policy, indicating the development of a well-learned and calibrated RL policy that can eventually outperform the initial guidance.

#### 4.4. Autonomous Driving (Steer Prediction)

- **Enhanced Steering Accuracy:** In the real-world task of autonomous driving steer prediction, AdaConG consistently enhances mean accuracy by **3.1% to 3.8%** across various KD methods (KD, FitNet, PKT, FT) when transferring knowledge from a multi-modal teacher to a unimodal student under domain shifts (Gaussian noise on RGB images).
- **Practical Applicability:** This result validates AdaConG's utility in safety-critical applications where model robustness to imperfect sensory input and guidance is paramount.

Overall, the findings strongly support AdaConG as a broadly applicable and highly effective solution for learning under uncertainty, consistently demonstrating performance improvements and enhanced robustness across a diverse range of machine learning tasks and scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Adaptive Conformal Guidance (AdaConG) represents a significant advancement in the field of machine learning, particularly concerning robust learning from imperfect information sources. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Uncertainty Integration:** AdaConG introduces a novel paradigm by embedding statistically rigorous conformal prediction (CP) directly into the *training loop* for adaptive guidance weighting. This moves beyond traditional post-hoc calibration or reliance on heuristic uncertainty estimates, offering a more principled and dynamic approach to managing uncertainty during learning. This is a fundamental methodological contribution.
2. **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** The core benefit of AdaConG is its ability to enable robust learning even when guidance signals are noisy, incomplete, or misaligned with the target domain. By dynamically modulating the influence of guidance based on its quantified uncertainty, models can intelligently reduce reliance on misleading information and enhance overall performance. This improved reliability is crucial for deploying ML systems in real-world, dynamic, and safety-critical environments.
3. **Broad Applicability Across ML Paradigms:** The demonstration of AdaConG's effectiveness across supervised learning (knowledge distillation), semi-supervised learning (image classification with pseudo-labels), and reinforcement learning (imitation-guided navigation) highlights its generality. This broad applicability suggests that AdaConG could serve as a foundational component for many future ML systems that rely on diverse forms of guidance.
4. **Improved Performance and Efficiency:** In practical terms, AdaConG consistently leads to measurable improvements in accuracy and faster convergence, even surpassing strong baselines. For instance, in reinforcement learning, it achieves significantly higher rewards in challenging, domain-shifted environments where other methods fail to adapt. This translates to more efficient learning and better performing agents.
5. **Unlocking Value from Imperfect Teachers:** AdaConG's capacity to extract "useful dark knowledge" even from underperforming or uncertain teachers is a major breakthrough. It allows valuable information to be leveraged judiciously without suffering the detrimental effects of blind trust, thus maximizing the utility of available guidance resources.
6. **Towards More Trustworthy AI:** By providing a rigorous and adaptive mechanism for uncertainty quantification and integration, AdaConG contributes directly to the development of more trustworthy and explainable AI systems. Understanding when a model relies on external guidance and how confident that guidance is can increase transparency and user confidence.
7. **Future Research Directions:** The paper itself points to promising future work, such as extending AdaConG to settings with ambiguous or imprecise labels, and integrating it with concepts from Imprecise Probabilistic Machine Learning, Active Learning, and Continual Learning. This suggests that AdaConG is not merely an incremental improvement but a stepping stone for deeper research into adaptive and uncertainty-aware intelligent systems.

In summary, AdaConG offers a versatile, robust, and computationally efficient solution to a pervasive problem in machine learning. Its novel integration of conformal prediction into the training loop significantly advances the state-of-the-art in learning under uncertainty, with broad implications for making AI systems more reliable, adaptable, and performant across a wide spectrum of applications.
