# LAMP: Latent Motion Prior-Guided Real-World Learning for Dexterous Hand Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University, Tsinghua University, Peking University, and PsiBot developed LAMP, a framework for dexterous hand manipulation that leverages a latent motion prior to enable efficient real-world imitation and reinforcement learning. The approach attained near-perfect success rates on complex physical tasks by structuring the high-dimensional hand-action space and guiding exploration.

## Problem

- Real-world dexterous manipulation faces challenges with sim-to-real transfer due to the "reality gap" and difficulties in accurately simulating complex multi-finger contact dynamics.
- Imitation learning struggles with high-degrees-of-freedom (DoF) action spaces, leading to accumulated errors, out-of-distribution states, and limited ability for contact recovery or adaptation to perturbations.
- Unconstrained online reinforcement learning for dexterous hands is sample-inefficient and risky on physical hardware, as erratic exploration can disrupt objects or break delicate contact patterns.

## Method

- A ==Latent Motion Prior Module (LMPM)== is developed to learn a continuous, decodable, and history-conditioned ==latent action space== for dexterous hand movements directly from offline demonstration trajectories, trained with a ==KL-regularized reconstruction objective==.
- The **LAMP** framework integrates ==imitation learning (IL)== and ==residual reinforcement learning (RL)== by using the LMPM as a shared interface, allowing policies to predict ==latent offsets== for hand commands.
- Online ==residual RL== (via ==Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)== with RLPD-style updates) is performed within this latent space, enabling local, contact-preserving corrections to the learned imitation policy, thereby guiding exploration more efficiently and safely.

## Results

- LAMP achieved an average imitation learning success rate of **56.25%** and, after online reinforcement learning, an average final success rate of **98.75%** across four real-world dexterous manipulation tasks.
- The framework outperformed alternative action interfaces, reaching **100%** success on three tasks (Grasp & Place, Open Drawer, Assemble Box) and **95%** on Pull Tissue, whereas a raw joint space achieved only **15%** and PCA **65%**.
- LAMP produced significantly smoother hand motions and maintained the lowest fraction of off-manifold exploration, indicating more stable and contact-preserving online learning compared to other methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning a history-conditioned, continuous, and decodable latent motion prior (LMPM) effectively structures high-dimensional dexterous hand action spaces, simplifying the policy learning problem.
> - Integrating imitation learning and residual reinforcement learning through a shared latent interface enables stable initialization from demonstrations and robust, contact-preserving online refinement.
> - Confining real-world exploration to local corrections within a learned, contact-consistent latent manifold leads to significantly smoother actions and more sample-efficient learning on physical hardware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly with dexterous hands, is progressing towards more complex and contact-rich tasks. However, deploying these capabilities on physical hardware faces significant challenges. Current research largely relies on sim-to-real transfer and imitation learning (IL).

Sim-to-real transfer is hindered by the "reality gap," primarily due to the difficulty of accurately simulating chaotic multi-finger contact dynamics, micro-friction, and soft-material compliance. This discrepancy often leads to degraded policy performance when transferred to physical robots.

Imitation learning, while effective for replicating demonstrated trajectories, lacks inherent mechanisms for active exploration and error correction. In high-degrees-of-freedom (DoF) action spaces, minor execution errors can quickly compound, causing the robot hand to drift into out-of-distribution (OOD) configurations. Without the exploratory capabilities of reinforcement learning (RL), pure IL struggles with contact recovery and adaptation to perturbations beyond the scope of limited human demonstrations.

Online reinforcement learning, a promising alternative for fine-tuning behaviors directly in the real world, presents its own set of difficulties. Unconstrained stochastic exploration in high-DoF action spaces can lead to erratic movements that disturb objects or break delicate contact patterns. Such irreversible state transitions result in extremely sparse successful trajectories and poor sample efficiency, making real-world RL prohibitively expensive and risky for physical hardware. This contrasts with biological learning, where early actions, termed "structured motor babbling," are guided by intrinsic neuromuscular priors.

Previous attempts to address these challenges include exploiting the intrinsic low-dimensional structure of dexterous hand movements. Studies have shown that complex grasping postures can be explained by a few principal modes, leading to research into compressed or latent action spaces such as linear hand subspaces (e.g., PCA) or learned continuous/discrete latent policies (e.g., VQ-VAE). However, existing methods often lack the ability to decode latent commands into executable hand targets, support locally smooth residual updates that preserve contact, or incorporate recent motion history to ensure exploration remains within contact-consistent regions of the action space.

This work aims to bridge these gaps by introducing a continuous, decodable, history-conditioned latent action space that can be shared across both supervised policy learning and online residual exploration, thereby providing a more structured and stable interface for real-world dexterous manipulation learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to overcome the fragility and inefficiency of existing real-world learning paradigms for dexterous hands. High-dimensional hand actions exacerbate imitation errors and render reinforcement learning exploration prone to unsafe, contact-breaking movements, which are detrimental to physical hardware. While combining IL with online RL can reduce the need for extensive manual supervision, unconstrained exploration in raw hand-action spaces remains sample-inefficient and risky.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the paper are:

1. **To introduce the Latent Motion Prior Module (LMPM):** The goal is to develop a module that learns a continuous, decodable manifold of dexterous hand motion directly from offline demonstration trajectories. This module is designed to map recent hand-action histories to a compact, history-conditioned latent prior and decode continuous latent commands into executable high-dimensional hand targets. This aims to provide a structured, low-dimensional representation that captures the essential dynamics of feasible hand movements.

2. **To develop LAMP as a unified real-world learning framework:** LAMP is conceived as a three-stage framework that integrates imitation learning and residual reinforcement learning. This integration is designed to occur through the shared LMPM latent hand-action interface. The explicit motivation is to enable residual exploration to make localized corrections near demonstrated, contact-consistent hand motions, as opposed to perturbing every finger joint independently and potentially breaking contact.

3. **To validate LAMP's performance on real-robot dexterous manipulation tasks:** The research seeks to empirically demonstrate that LAMP can achieve higher learning efficiency and robustness compared to alternative hand-action interfaces (e.g., raw joint space, linear compression like PCA, or discrete action spaces like VQ-VAE). The validation specifically targets challenging real-world tasks that require sustained hand-object contact and precise control.

In essence, the overarching objective is to make real-world learning for dexterous hands more reliable, safer, and more sample-efficient by providing a structured, guided exploration mechanism within a learned, task-relevant action manifold.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LAMP framework employs a three-stage approach to enable real-world learning for dexterous hand manipulation, centered around a novel Latent Motion Prior Module (LMPM).

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Learning the Latent Motion Prior (LMPM Pretraining)

The initial stage focuses on creating a robust hand-action interface by learning the LMPM from the hand-motion component of teleoperated trajectories.
- **Input:** A history of recent hand targets, denoted as $H_t = (h_{t-K+1}, \dots, h_t)$, where $K$ is the history length (8 steps in this work).
- **Components:**
 - **Encoder ($\mathrm{E}_\phi$):** This component maps $H_t$ to the parameters ($\mu_\phi(H_t)$, $\sigma_\phi(H_t)$) of a local Gaussian latent prior $q_\phi(z_t|H_t) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_\phi(H_t), \text{diag}(\sigma^2_\phi(H_t)))$ in a compact latent action space ($R^2$).
 - **Decoder ($\mathrm{D}_\theta$):** This component maps a sampled latent vector $z_t$ back to the next 6-DoF absolute hand target, $\hat{h}_{t+1} = \mathrm{D}_\theta(z_t)$.
- **Training Objective:** The LMPM is trained using a reconstruction objective, $L_{prior} = \mathbb{E}_{(H_t,h_{t+1})} [\|\mathrm{D}_\theta(z_t) - h_{t+1}\|^2_2 + \beta D_{KL}(q_\phi(z_t|H_t) \| \mathcal{N}(0, I))]$. The first term ensures that the decoded hand target closely matches the true target, preserving executability in the original hand-command space. The second term, a KL-regularized bottleneck, encourages a compact and smooth latent coordinate system.
- **Outcome:** After this stage, the encoder $\mathrm{E}_\phi$ and decoder $\mathrm{D}_\theta$ are fixed. They provide a shared latent action space and a history-conditioned distribution that serves as a local prior center for the current motion phase.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Imitation Learning in the Latent Action Space

This stage leverages the pre-trained LMPM to learn an initial visuomotor policy from a small set of demonstrations.
- **Input:** At time $t$, the policy receives an observation $o_t$ (RGB images and arm proprioceptive state $x_{arm,t}$) and the recent hand-target history $H_t$.
- **Policy Architecture:**
 - The frozen LMPM encoder $\mathrm{E}_\phi$ first computes the prior statistics ($\mu_t, \sigma_t$) from $H_t$.
 - A behavior cloning (BC) policy, $\pi_{BC}^\psi$, processes $o_t, \mu_t, \sigma_t$.
 - It predicts two outputs: a native 6-DoF arm command ($\hat{a}_{arm,t}$) and a 2-DoF latent hand-action offset ($\Delta\hat{z}_t$).
 - The final 6-DoF hand target ($\hat{h}_{t+1}$) is then generated by decoding the sum of the prior mean and the offset using the frozen LMPM decoder: $\hat{h}_{t+1} = \mathrm{D}_\theta(\mu_t + \Delta\hat{z}_t)$.
- **Training Objective:** The supervised objective, $L_{BC} = \|\hat{a}_{arm,t} - a_{arm,t}\|^2_2 + \lambda_h \|\hat{h}_{t+1} - h_{t+1}\|^2_2 + \lambda_z \|\Delta\hat{z}_t\|^2_2$, minimizes the difference between predicted and demonstrated arm commands, and between decoded and demonstrated hand targets. A regularization term on the latent offset $\Delta\hat{z}_t$ is also included.
- **Rationale:** Predicting a latent offset allows the policy to adapt the learned hand-motion prior to the current visual observation while ensuring the decoded hand commands remain on the learned, contact-consistent manifold. The arm commands are kept in their native space to allow for task-level variation.

#### 3.3. Stage 3: Residual RL in the Latent Action Space

The final stage improves the BC policy through online interaction using residual reinforcement learning, operating within the same latent hand interface.
- **Process:**
 - New transitions collected during online interaction are added to a replay buffer, which is mixed with the initial demonstration data (RLPD-style updates).
 - The residual actor ($\pi_\eta$) observes the current state $s_t = (o_t, H_t)$ and outputs a residual action $u_t = (\rho_{arm,t}, \rho_{z,t})$, comprising a native arm residual and a latent hand residual.
 - The executed action ($\tilde{a}_t$) is a combination of the BC policy's output and the residual action: $\tilde{a}_t = (a_{arm,t,bc} + s_{arm}\rho_{arm,t}, \mathrm{D}_\theta(z_{t,bc} + s_z\rho_{z,t}))$. Here, $z_{t,bc} = \mu_t + \Delta z_{t,bc}$ is the latent hand action from the BC policy.
- **Training Objective:** Standard Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) losses are used to update the residual actor and critic, drawing from batches of both online and demonstration data. Sparse task rewards are provided by a separately trained visual classifier.
- **Rationale:** Applying the hand residual in the latent space (Z) confines exploration to local corrections around the history-conditioned hand command, preventing abrupt, contact-breaking perturbations common in raw joint spaces. This promotes more sample-efficient and stable online learning.

#### 3.4. Real-World System and Tasks

- **Hardware:** Franka Research 3 robotic arm equipped with a Ruiyan dexterous hand.
- **Sensing:** A front-view RealSense D435 camera and a wrist-mounted RealSense D405 camera provide dual RGB observations.
- **Demonstration Collection:** Human-in-the-loop teleoperation using a Synglove for finger control and a SpaceMouse for TCP (Tool Center Point) translation and rotation.
- **Tasks:** Four distinct dexterous manipulation tasks are used for evaluation: Grasp & Place (of a bottle into a box), Open Drawer, Pull Tissue, and Assemble Box. These tasks require sustained hand-object contact and precise movements.
- **Evaluation:** During online RL and evaluation, the TCP pose is randomized to assess spatial generalization. Sparse rewards and success labels are determined by a visual classifier.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates LAMP against alternative hand-action interfaces (Raw, PCA, VQ-VAE (DQ-RISE)) across four real-world dexterous manipulation tasks, focusing on imitation learning (IL) and subsequent online residual reinforcement learning (RL) performance.

#### 4.1. Comparison of Action Spaces

- **Imitation Learning Performance:**
 - **Raw Interface:** Policies trained with the raw 6-DoF hand-action space exhibited low success rates (average 15% across tasks). The high dimensionality led to noticeable hand jitter and frequent failure to establish or maintain contact with objects, confirming the difficulty of direct regression in high-DoF spaces.
 - **PCA Interface:** Linear compression to 2 dimensions improved IL performance substantially over the Raw interface (average 35% success). This indicated that a compact hand-action representation aids dexterous policy learning. However, its fixed linear projection could not fully capture the complex, non-linear manifold of hand motion.
 - **VQ-VAE (DQ-RISE) Interface:** This discrete hand-action representation, while reducing dimensionality, often resulted in frequent switching between codebook entries during execution. This caused visible jitter and non-smooth motion, particularly impacting rigid-object grasping where slips occurred. Its average IL success rate was 35%.
 - **LAMP (Ours):** Utilizing the history-conditioned continuous latent action interface, LAMP achieved the highest average IL success rate of 56.25%. It produced stable and smooth hand motion, leading to more reliable object contact compared to the other interfaces.

- **Reinforcement Learning Performance:**
 - **Raw Interface:** Due to its low initial IL success rate and unconstrained exploration in the raw joint space, online RL for the Raw variant was highly inefficient and failed to converge effectively. The average final RL success rate remained at 15%.
 - **PCA Interface:** While improving over its IL baseline, the fixed linear projection of PCA could still lead to residuals that decoded into contact-breaking finger motions. Its average final RL success rate was 65%.
 - **VQ-VAE (DQ-RISE) Interface:** Despite improvements from RL, codebook switches in the discrete action space continued to introduce abrupt gesture jumps, limiting its final evaluation success. The average final RL success rate was 36.25%.
 - **LAMP (Ours):** Benefiting from a continuous decoder and history-conditioned prior, LAMP organized demonstrated hand motions into a better-conditioned latent space. This provided residual RL with a smoother, more contact-preserving neighborhood for refinement, leading to significant improvements. LAMP achieved an average final RL success rate of 98.75%, reaching 100% success on three tasks (Grasp & Place, Open Drawer, Assemble Box) and 95% on the remaining task (Pull Tissue). The exploration in LAMP remained local to the imitation policy, making RL sample-efficient.

#### 4.2. Action-Interface Ablations

Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of LMPM's design choices: the compact latent bottleneck and the history-conditioned prior center.

- **"w/o low-dimensional bottleneck":** Expanding the latent dimension (from 2-D) generally lowered final performance (e.g., Assemble Box RL success dropped from 100% to 20%). This suggests that a larger hand space increases the burden on arm-hand coordination and online exploration, leading to more arm-action failures.
- **"w/o history-conditioned encoder":** Without the history-conditioned encoder, the visual policy predicted latent points without a local prior center. This weakened imitation initialization. While the decoder still mapped actions onto a learned hand-motion manifold, making training more stable than raw control, it resulted in lower RL success rates (e.g., Pull Tissue RL success dropped from 95% to 60%). This indicates the prior center's importance for coordinating arm approach and hand contact timing.

#### 4.3. Action-Flow Visualization

Visualizations of latent action flow during real-world rollouts confirmed LAMP's structured exploration. The LMPM encoder output consistently kept hand actions on a feasible motion manifold. The IL-predicted offset provided the main vision-guided displacement, while the RL residual made local online adjustments in the same latent coordinates. When IL left a larger performance gap, the residual provided more substantial shifts towards successful regions. When IL was already proficient, residuals remained small and structured, demonstrating localized and contact-preserving exploration.

#### 4.4. Off-Manifold Exploration Analysis

Quantitative analysis of off-manifold exploration (ΔNN/Disp ratio) revealed that Raw exploration steps were consistently far from the demonstrated manifold. PCA, despite dimension reduction, remained substantially off-manifold because its linear coordinates could not follow the curved hand-motion structure. LAMP consistently maintained the lowest off-manifold fraction on most tasks, indicating that its continuous latent residuals provided a more appropriate local neighborhood for real-robot exploration, which helps preserve contact.

#### 4.5. Action Smoothness Analysis

Measurement of second-order hand-target variation (jitter) showed that Raw and VQ-VAE policies exhibited frequent high-jitter bands during execution. In contrast, LAMP maintained significantly smoother hand motion across most rollouts, contributing to its observed contact stability and higher success rates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LAMP framework introduces a method for real-world dexterous manipulation that addresses core limitations of current robot learning paradigms, primarily the brittleness of imitation learning and the inefficiency and risk of unconstrained exploration in high-dimensional action spaces.

**Significance of Findings:**
- **Structured Action Space for Dexterous Manipulation:** The research demonstrates the effectiveness of a history-conditioned, continuous, and decodable latent motion prior (LMPM) in structuring the high-dimensional hand-action space. This approach effectively simplifies the policy learning problem for dexterous hands.
- **Unified IL and RL Framework:** LAMP successfully unifies imitation learning and residual reinforcement learning through this shared latent interface. This allows for effective initialization from demonstrations and subsequent robust, contact-preserving refinement via online exploration.
- **Improved Real-World Performance:** The empirical results on four challenging real-world tasks show that LAMP significantly outperforms raw, linear (PCA), and discrete (VQ-VAE) hand-action representations, achieving near-perfect success rates after online refinement. This highlights the practical utility of the proposed latent prior.
- **Efficient and Safe Exploration:** The study provides evidence that framing exploration as local corrections within a learned, contact-consistent latent manifold leads to smoother actions, reduced off-manifold excursions, and more sample-efficient online learning on physical hardware.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** LAMP could facilitate the development of more capable and autonomous dexterous manipulation robots, particularly in scenarios requiring fine motor skills and sustained object interaction.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By enabling more robust and sample-efficient real-world learning, LAMP could potentially reduce the extensive engineering and data collection efforts typically associated with deploying dexterous manipulation skills on physical robots.
- **Foundation for Generalization:** The principle of a history-conditioned, learned motion prior might be extended to more general manipulation tasks or different robot morphologies, paving the way for more generalizable dexterous policies.
- **Influence on Action Representation Learning:** This work contributes to the broader field of action representation learning in robotics, suggesting that context-aware and decodable latent spaces are crucial for high-DoF control.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The current LMPM is trained from task-specific offline hand-motion data, implying that suitable demonstrations are required for each new task. Future work could focus on learning more general or multi-task hand-motion priors from broader datasets and higher-capacity models. Additionally, the validation on a 6-DoF hand and 2-D latent space suggests potential for further exploration with higher-DoF hands and more diverse contact regimes, leveraging observations from human hand synergy research. Overall, LAMP represents a step towards making real-robot dexterous manipulation learning more practical and effective.
