# Multi-Agent LLM Actor-Critic Framework for Social Robot Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Purdue University developed SAMALM, a decentralized multi-agent LLM actor-critic framework for socially-aware multi-robot navigation that generates direct low-level control signals. Experiments showed SAMALM, especially when using GPT-4o, achieved success rates up to 68% and social scores up to 72% in dynamic, human-populated simulated environments, significantly outperforming existing LLM baselines.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)-based social navigation models lack adaptability to novel, unseen environments despite strong benchmark performance.
- Existing LLM-based navigation frameworks often rely on centralized decision-making, struggle to account for individual robot characteristics, and lack robust mechanisms for verifying generated actions.
- Current LLM-based approaches face inconsistency challenges when translating high-level, macro-actions into precise, low-level robot control signals.

## Method

- ==Proposed SAMALM==, a decentralized multi-agent LLM actor-critic framework for multi-robot socially-aware navigation, structured around an LLM-powered actor-critic for each robot.
- Developed a multi-LLM world model that constructs personalized spatio-temporal knowledge of task scenarios for each robot based on its local observations of human-robot and robot-robot interactions.
- Implemented a two-tier self-verification mechanism with individual (local) LLM critics and a global LLM critic, combined with an entropy-based score fusion to adaptively refine actions and re-query when necessary.

## Results

- SAMALM consistently outperformed all baseline LLMs across various configurations, with the GPT-4o-based SAMALM achieving a **68%** success rate (SR) and **72%** social score (SS), significantly higher than the GPT-4o baseline's SR of **20%** and SS of **18%**.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the critical contribution of the multi-LLM critic modules, showing improvements of 25-**30%** in SR and 23-**36%** in SS when critics were included in SAMALM.
- Smaller or older LLMs (e.g., LLaMA-8B, GPT-**3.5**) exhibited negligible performance in multi-robot social navigation, yielding near-zero average SR and SS, indicating their inability to effectively process multi-variable inputs for this task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A decentralized multi-LLM actor-critic architecture effectively enables personalized robot behaviors, direct low-level control, and robust action verification for multi-robot social navigation.
> - The direct generation of low-level control commands ([vx, vy]) by LLM actors improves inference consistency and avoids instability associated with translating high-level macro-actions.
> - An adaptive, entropy-based fusion of local and global critic scores provides a robust self-verification and re-querying mechanism, mitigating LLM 'hallucinations' and enhancing decision quality in dynamic, social environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of robotics and artificial intelligence, particularly with advancements in large language models (LLMs), has driven increased research into human-robot collaboration and embodied intelligence. A critical area within this domain is socially-aware robot navigation (SAN), which focuses on enabling robots to operate effectively and acceptably in human-populated environments.

Prior research in SAN has largely utilized deep reinforcement learning (DRL) approaches, which integrate human-robot interaction (HRI) and robot-robot interaction (RRI) with path planning. While DRL-based methods have demonstrated strong performance in benchmark scenarios following extensive training, they often exhibit limitations in adapting to novel environments or situations outside their original training datasets. This lack of adaptability restricts their broader deployment.

More recently, LLMs have emerged as a promising technology for robot navigation due to their capabilities in commonsense inference, which can potentially facilitate zero-shot navigation—the ability to perform tasks in unfamiliar settings without prior specific training. Existing LLM-based frameworks for navigation, however, often encounter several challenges. These include a reliance on centralized decision-making paradigms, which may not adequately account for the unique characteristics or preferences of individual robots within a multi-robot system. Furthermore, many current LLM-based approaches lack robust mechanisms for verifying the generated actions, increasing the risk of inconsistent or erroneous outputs (sometimes referred to as "hallucinations"). A significant challenge also lies in the difficulty of consistently translating high-level, macro-actions inferred by LLMs into precise, low-level control signals required for direct robot movement.

This research positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified limitations in LLM-based multi-robot SAN. It seeks to develop a decentralized framework that leverages the commonsense reasoning of LLMs while incorporating mechanisms for personalization, direct low-level control, and robust action verification in multi-robot social navigation tasks. The work draws inspiration from successful multi-LLM coordination paradigms in other multi-agent contexts, such as communication and cooperative software development, and applies these concepts to the specific complexities of social robot navigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, adaptable, and socially compliant framework for multi-robot social navigation that overcomes the limitations of existing deep reinforcement learning and centralized LLM-based approaches. The authors propose a novel decentralized multi-agent LLM actor-critic framework, named SAMALM (Socially-Aware Multi-Agent actor-critic LLM framework), specifically designed for multi-robot SAN tasks.

The motivation for developing SAMALM stems from several critical observations and unaddressed challenges:

1. **Limited Adaptability of DRL-based Methods:** Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) models, despite their benchmark performance, are typically constrained to the environmental configurations and datasets on which they were trained. This significantly limits their ability to generalize and adapt to new, unseen scenarios, which is a crucial requirement for real-world robotic deployment in dynamic human environments.

2. **Deficiencies in Centralized LLM-based Navigation:** While LLMs offer strong commonsense inference capabilities for zero-shot navigation, existing LLM-based frameworks often rely on a centralized decision-making system. This centralized paradigm struggles to account for the heterogeneous attributes, unique configurations, or individual preferences (e.g., speed, social distance) of different robots within a multi-robot system. A single LLM attempting to manage all robots collectively may not optimize for individual robot specificities.

3. **Inconsistency in Action Translation:** Many current LLM-based approaches infer high-level macro-actions or instructions, which then need to be translated into precise, low-level robot control signals (e.g., specific velocities `[vx, vy]`). This two-step process can introduce instability and inconsistency in the reasoning chain, leading to errors in robot execution. The authors aim to enable LLM-actors to generate direct low-level control signals.

4. **Lack of Robust Verification in LLM Systems:** The absence of a verification step in many LLM-based systems can undermine the robustness and cooperativeness of multi-robot systems. Without a mechanism to evaluate the quality and social appropriateness of generated actions, there is an increased risk of LLM-induced "hallucinations" or numerical errors leading to unsafe or non-compliant behaviors. A verification mechanism is essential for reliable operation.

To address these motivations, SAMALM's specific objectives and proposed contributions are:
- To propose SAMALM, a novel framework designed to enhance multi-robot social norm compliance and cooperative navigation through a multi-LLM actor-critic architecture.
- To develop a multi-LLM world model that constructs personalized knowledge of task scenarios for each robot based on its local observations and interactions, thereby enabling a contextual understanding of environmental dynamics, including Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) and Robot-Robot Interaction (RRI).
- To implement a multi-LLM agent system with personalized prompt engineering. This allows each LLM actor to reflect distinct robot preferences and characteristics, facilitating asynchronous queries and tailored action generation.
- To establish a robust self-verification and group cooperation mechanism using a multi-LLM actor-critic framework. This involves a global LLM critic to evaluate team-level behaviors and a set of local LLM critics to assess individual robot actions.
- To integrate an entropy-based score fusion mechanism within the critic system. This mechanism is intended to adaptively combine evaluations from local and global critics, thereby enhancing robustness, coordination, and enabling re-querying of actions until satisfactory results are achieved.
- To demonstrate that SAMALM can produce socially compliant behaviors and exhibit strong adaptability across diverse multi-robot navigation scenarios, balancing local autonomy with global oversight.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SAMALM is presented as a decentralized multi-robot cooperative social navigation planner, structured around an LLM-powered actor-critic framework for each individual robot. The overall architecture is depicted in Figure 2 of the paper.

**Problem Formulation:** The multi-robot social navigation task is reformulated as a textual decentralized partially observable Markov decision process (Dec-POMDP) where joint observations and actions are transformed into textual representations. This textualization is crucial for enabling effective interaction with the LLM-based actor-critic framework.

**A. Multi-Agent World Model Construction:**
Inspired by human cognitive frameworks, SAMALM develops a spatio-temporal graph structural multi-robot world model for textually representing HRI and RRI. Each robot constructs its own dedicated spatial-temporal graph from its unique local observations, ensuring tailored and contextual representations.
- **Nodes:** The world model for the i-th robot (`V_i`) comprises a self-node (containing the robot's location, velocity, and goal) and nodes for observed humans and other robots (providing their positions and velocities).
- **Edges:** Spatial edges model HRI and RRI, describing relative distance dynamics between the robot and observed entities. Temporal edges represent the relative distance to the robot's destination for its self-node, and for observed humans/robots, they capture acceleration and estimated next-timestep positions based on uniform linear motion kinematics.

**B. Multi-Agent LLM Actors:**
LLMs are directly employed as the robot's policy network, leveraging their commonsense inference capabilities.
- **Direct Control Signals:** Unlike previous approaches, SAMALM's LLM-actors generate direct low-level control commands (`[vx, vy]`) rather than macro-actions, aiming to maintain inference consistency.
- **Prompt Engineering:** The prompt for each LLM-actor includes a shared task configuration textual description (overall task and data descriptions) and an individual environmental dynamics textual description derived from its specific world model.
- **Personalization:** Robot-specific preferences, such as preferred speed and designated socially acceptable distance, are integrated into the prompt engineering to accommodate the diverse attributes of different robotic platforms.
- **Reasoning Enhancement:** Auto-CoT (Automatic Chain of Thought) inference technology is used to enhance the reasoning ability of the LLM-actors.

**C. Multi-Agent LLM Critics:**
A self-verification mechanism evaluates actions generated by the LLM-actors. This involves a two-tier critic system:
- **Individual (Local) LLM Critics (`Q_i`):** Each local critic evaluates its corresponding robot's action based on current observations and potential future impacts. Its prompt engineering incorporates a rule-based checklist and robot personality. Penalties (`γ_i(l)`) are applied for short-term factors (e.g., relative distance to humans, risk of collision) and long-term factors (e.g., involvement in high-risk areas with multiple humans in future timesteps).
- **Global LLM Critic (`Q_g`):** This critic assesses group-level behaviors of the entire multi-robot system. Penalties (`γ(g)`) consider both intra-group factors (e.g., cooperative navigation, robot-robot collision detection) and inter-group factors (e.g., multi-robot system interaction with humans, overall navigation task effectiveness and completion time).
- **Communication:** A bi-directional message chain facilitates information sharing among robots, directing whole team data to a designated leader robot for the global critic's evaluation.

**D. Entropy Fusion Mechanism:**
An entropy-based mechanism adaptively integrates local and global critic scores.
- **Confidence Distribution:** Each local critic score (`Q_i`) is normalized to produce a confidence distribution (`C_i`).
- **Disagreement Level Estimation:** The entropy (`H_i`) of each local score is calculated to estimate the level of disagreement among local critic evaluations. The system local critic score entropy (`H`) is then obtained.
- **Disagreement Weight:** A disagreement weight (`ω`) is defined based on the ratio of the system local critic score entropy (`H`) to its maximum possible entropy (`H_max`), indicating how centralized or disordered the local scores are.
- **Critic Fusion Score (`Z`):** The final fusion score is a weighted average of the average local critic scores and the global critic score: `Z = ω * (average(Q_i)) + (1 - ω) * Q_g`.
- **Self-Verification and Re-query:** If the fusion score `Z` falls below a predefined threshold (`Z_th`), actions are re-queried. Any local action with a critic score lower than `Z` is updated by its respective actor, incorporating textual evaluation reasoning records (feedback) from both the local and global critics. If `Z` meets or exceeds `Z_th`, the actions are executed. This iterative refinement process aims to enhance robustness and decision quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SAMALM was conducted in a simulated multi-robot social navigation environment, consistent with prior research. Scenarios involved 3 to 5 robots and 5 to 10 humans, with varying robot fields-of-view (FOV) (90° and 360°). Robots were assigned heterogeneous types (mobile robot, robot dog, drone), each with distinct preferred velocities and social distances, reflecting personalized preferences. Human pedestrians were simulated with personalized policies, allowing dynamic changes in velocity and goals, and their sensitive information was inaccessible to robots.

**Evaluation Metrics:** Two primary metrics were used over 50 test cases:
1. **Success Rate (SR):** The percentage of episodes where robots successfully reached their goals without collisions or timeouts.
2. **Social Score (SS):** A measure of social compliance based on path quality, adherence to personalized safe distances, and frequency of human discomfort incidents.

**Comparison with Baselines:**
- **Superior Performance of SAMALM:** The SAMALM framework consistently outperformed all baseline LLMs across various configurations. For instance, in a scenario with a 90° FOV, 5 robots, and 3 humans, SAMALM-L (using LLaMA as backbone) achieved an SR of 44% and an SS of 31%, which represented a doubling of performance compared to the LLaMA-405B baseline (SR 22%, SS 15%). Similarly, SAMALM-G (using GPT-4o as backbone) recorded an SR of 68% and an SS of 72%, substantially surpassing the GPT-4o baseline.
- **Limited Capability of Smaller/Older LLMs:** Earlier LLM models like LLaMA-8B and GPT-3.5 showed negligible performance in multi-robot SAN tasks, yielding near-zero average SR and SS. Their outputs frequently consisted of fixed, non-responsive actions, indicating an inability to process multi-variable inputs and task requirements effectively.
- **Improved but Inconsistent Performance of Larger Baselines:** Larger models such as LLaMA-70B and GPT-4 performed better than their smaller counterparts but still demonstrated inconsistencies, often making value errors and variable miscalculations. LLaMA-405B and GPT-4o exhibited more promising reasoning abilities, accurately performing calculations and utilizing variables, resulting in average scores (SR=12%, SS=8% for LLaMA-405B; SR=20%, SS=18% for GPT-4o) that were notably higher than the smaller LLMs, though still significantly lower than SAMALM.

**Ablation Study:**
- **Validation of Critic Modules:** The ablation studies confirmed the substantial contribution of the multi-LLM actor-critic framework. Removing the critic modules (Ablation-L and Ablation-G) led to a significant impairment in performance compared to the full SAMALM models.
- **Quantitative Improvements with Critics:** For the LLaMA-based SAMALM, the average SR increased from 23% (Ablation-L) to 33% (SAMALM-L), representing a 30% improvement. The average SS rose from 17% (Ablation-L) to 22% (SAMALM-L), a 23% gain. Similarly, for the GPT-4o-based SAMALM, the average SR was enhanced from 42% (Ablation-G) to 56% (SAMALM-G), a 25% increase, and the average SS improved from 35% (Ablation-G) to 55% (SAMALM-G), a 36% improvement.
- These enhancements underscore the effectiveness of incorporating the two-tier critic architecture, along with the self-verification and entropy-based score fusion mechanisms, in refining action proposals and ensuring both robust navigation and social appropriateness.

**Qualitative Observations:** Beyond the quantitative metrics, qualitative assessments indicated that SAMALM generated smoother and more adaptive trajectories. The framework was also better equipped to handle dynamic changes in the environment. The textual feedback provided by the critics proved valuable, enabling actors to adjust their actions to address specific issues at both individual and group levels, contributing to improved decision-making.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of multi-robot social navigation by introducing SAMALM, a novel decentralized multi-agent LLM actor-critic framework. The work addresses several critical limitations of existing approaches and contributes to the development of more capable and reliable autonomous systems.

**Novelty and Framework Contribution:** SAMALM represents a pioneering effort in implementing a multi-LLM framework specifically for multi-robot socially-aware navigation tasks. By explicitly considering dynamic semantic inference in both Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) and Robot-Robot Interaction (RRI) within a multi-LLM actor-critic architecture, the framework pushes beyond previous centralized LLM-based planners and traditional DRL approaches.

**Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability:** A key impact of SAMALM is its ability to overcome the adaptability challenges inherent in many DRL-based methods. Leveraging LLMs' commonsense inference allows for zero-shot navigation in unfamiliar environments. Furthermore, the two-tier verification process, involving both local and global LLM critics, coupled with an entropy-based score fusion mechanism, significantly enhances the robustness of action generation. This self-verification and re-querying capability helps mitigate the risks of LLM-induced errors, leading to more reliable robot behaviors.

**Improved Social Compliance:** The consistently higher Social Scores achieved by SAMALM indicate that the framework effectively enables robots to maintain appropriate social distances, navigate smoothly through human crowds, and generally exhibit behaviors that are more acceptable in human-populated spaces. This is crucial for fostering trust and acceptance of robots in daily life.

**Decentralized Decision-Making and Personalization:** By employing individual LLM actors with personalized prompt engineering, SAMALM allows each robot to make decisions tailored to its unique characteristics and preferences (e.g., speed, social distance). This decentralized approach contrasts with centralized systems that often struggle to account for such heterogeneity, leading to more effective and contextually appropriate individual robot behaviors while maintaining overall system coordination.

**Direct Low-Level Control:** The ability of LLM actors to directly generate low-level control signals (`[vx, vy]`) is a notable methodological advancement. This bypasses the potential instability and inconsistency associated with translating high-level macro-actions into precise movements, thereby improving the coherence and reliability of robot execution.

**Potential for Broader Deployment:** The combined attributes of enhanced adaptability, social compliance, robustness, and personalized decentralized control make SAMALM a promising step towards the broader and safer deployment of multi-robot systems in complex, dynamic, and human-populated environments such as offices, hospitals, or public spaces. It lays a foundation for robots to seamlessly integrate into human society, performing tasks cooperatively and safely.

**Future Research Directions:** The SAMALM framework provides a strong foundation for future research in several areas, including the development of more sophisticated world models, exploration of diverse LLM architectures for actor and critic roles, and investigation into real-world deployments to validate its effectiveness outside of simulation. Further work could also focus on optimizing the entropy fusion mechanism and exploring adaptive thresholding for the re-query process.
