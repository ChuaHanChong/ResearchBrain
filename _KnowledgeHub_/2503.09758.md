# Multi-Agent LLM Actor-Critic Framework for Social Robot Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Purdue University developed SAMALM, a decentralized multi-agent LLM actor-critic framework for socially-aware multi-robot navigation that generates direct low-level control signals. Experiments showed SAMALM, especially when using GPT-4o, achieved success rates up to 68% and social scores up to 72% in dynamic, human-populated simulated environments, significantly outperforming existing LLM baselines.

## Problem

- Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL)-based social navigation models lack adaptability to novel, unseen environments despite strong benchmark performance.
- Existing LLM-based navigation frameworks often rely on centralized decision-making, struggle to account for individual robot characteristics, and lack robust mechanisms for verifying generated actions.
- Current LLM-based approaches face inconsistency challenges when translating high-level, macro-actions into precise, low-level robot control signals.

## Method

- ==Proposed SAMALM==, a decentralized multi-agent LLM actor-critic framework for multi-robot socially-aware navigation, structured around an LLM-powered actor-critic for each robot.
- Developed a multi-LLM world model that constructs personalized spatio-temporal knowledge of task scenarios for each robot based on its local observations of human-robot and robot-robot interactions.
- Implemented a two-tier self-verification mechanism with individual (local) LLM critics and a global LLM critic, combined with an entropy-based score fusion to adaptively refine actions and re-query when necessary.

## Results

- SAMALM consistently outperformed all baseline LLMs across various configurations, with the GPT-4o-based SAMALM achieving a **68%** success rate (SR) and **72%** social score (SS), significantly higher than the GPT-4o baseline's SR of **20%** and SS of **18%**.
- Ablation studies demonstrated the critical contribution of the multi-LLM critic modules, showing improvements of 25-**30%** in SR and 23-**36%** in SS when critics were included in SAMALM.
- Smaller or older LLMs (e.g., LLaMA-8B, GPT-**3.5**) exhibited negligible performance in multi-robot social navigation, yielding near-zero average SR and SS, indicating their inability to effectively process multi-variable inputs for this task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A decentralized multi-LLM actor-critic architecture effectively enables personalized robot behaviors, direct low-level control, and robust action verification for multi-robot social navigation.
> - The direct generation of low-level control commands ([vx, vy]) by LLM actors improves inference consistency and avoids instability associated with translating high-level macro-actions.
> - An adaptive, entropy-based fusion of local and global critic scores provides a robust self-verification and re-querying mechanism, mitigating LLM 'hallucinations' and enhancing decision quality in dynamic, social environments.
