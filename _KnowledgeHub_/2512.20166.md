# LoLA: Long Horizon Latent Action Learning for General Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> LoLA is a Vision-Language-Action model for long-horizon robot manipulation that fuses high-resolution current-frame observations with downsampled historical motion frames, then grounds vision-language features in robot proprioception via a State-Aware Latent Re-representation module before a flow-matching action expert decodes multi-step actions. It beats pi-0 and other baselines on SIMPLER, LIBERO, and real Franka and Bi-Manual Aloha tasks.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models mostly use single-frame inputs, lacking temporal awareness to track multi-step states or maintain action consistency, causing repetitive errors over long horizons.
- Prolonged robot-environment interaction compounds small perturbations into out-of-distribution states, and current models cannot recover, driving eventual policy failure.
- Prior VLA fusion methods concatenate robot proprioception with vision-language embeddings as a parallel input, leaving embeddings abstract, ungrounded, and vulnerable to irrelevant background cues.

## Method

- ==Current Observation Encoding== processes high-resolution multi-view frames at the present timestep for precise low-level spatial understanding needed for immediate action generation.
- ==Selective Spatial-Temporal Sampling== downsamples long historical frame sequences to a lower resolution for ==Historical Motion Encoding==, cutting compute while retaining trajectory and task-phase context.
- ==State-Aware Latent Re-representation (SALR)== runs a parallel ==State Transformer== that multiplicatively fuses robot-state queries with VLM key-value caches via ==outer product== and learnable masks, feeding a ==Conditional Flow Matching== action expert.

## Results

- On LIBERO, LoLA reaches **96.2%** average success versus pi-0's **92.2%**, including **88.2%** on the long-horizon LIBERO-Long suite versus **85.4%**.
- On SIMPLER WidowX, LoLA achieves **71.9%** average success, a **20.6%** relative improvement over pi-0's **41.7%**; on real Franka multi-step tasks it reaches up to **2.67 times** pi-0's success rate.
- Ablations show adding proprioception grounding raises LIBERO average success from **84.7%** to **91.2%**, and combining multi-frame history with SALR lifts WidowX success from **30.3%** to **57.3%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Grounding abstract vision-language embeddings in physically-coupled robot state via multiplicative fusion transfers better to action generation than late-fusion concatenation.
> - Asymmetric resolution allocation, high-fidelity current frame plus downsampled history, is an efficient way to inject long temporal context without prohibitive compute cost.
> - Gains from added context and history modules are largest precisely on long-horizon, error-accumulating tasks, where naive single-frame policies degrade fastest.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "LoLA" positions itself within the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, specifically addressing a critical limitation: the capability to perform long-horizon, language-guided tasks.

Recent advancements in robotic manipulation have largely been driven by VLA models, which leverage powerful pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like RT-2, OpenVLA, and π0. These models have demonstrated impressive cross-modal understanding, interpreting natural language instructions and grounding them in visual observations, leading to strong generalization capabilities across novel objects, environments, and tasks. However, the authors correctly identify that "most existing works focus on short-horizon tasks," which typically involve simple, quickly completable actions (e.g., "pick up a blue cup"). This focus severely limits their applicability to complex, real-world scenarios that demand sequential, goal-oriented behaviors over extended periods, such as "pizza making" or setting up an oven.

The key challenges for learning long-horizon tasks, as articulated by the authors, are:
1. **Temporal Context Understanding:** Traditional single-frame models struggle to track multi-step states or maintain action consistency over time, often leading to repetitive errors. LoLA directly addresses this by integrating long-term multi-view observations.
2. **Robustness to Out-of-Distribution Situations:** Prolonged interactions increase the likelihood of unexpected perturbations, which can accumulate and drive the robot into unfamiliar states, causing policy failure. LoLA aims to mitigate this risk by grounding its representations in physical reality.
3. **Resource Demands:** Collecting and processing long-duration data with frequent human resets is exponentially more expensive and computationally intensive than for short-horizon tasks. LoLA tackles this with an efficient encoding strategy.

The paper contextualizes itself against existing related work:

- **Vision-Language-Action Models:** While models like RT-2 and OpenVLA fine-tune VLMs on robotic data for action prediction, and π0 uses a VLM for scene understanding with a separate action expert, the paper criticizes their prevalent reliance on single-frame inputs. This oversight of temporal relationships hinders consecutive action generation, making them unsuitable for long-horizon tasks. LoLA's explicit incorporation of long-horizon history frames is a direct counterpoint.
- **Diffusion-based Robot Policies:** Diffusion models, initially popular in image generation, have shown promise in simulating robot trajectories (e.g., Diffusion Policy, Octo, RDT, CogACT). The paper notes that while these methods are effective, they often use vision-language embeddings that are "not aligned with actions as conditions to guide action generation." LoLA's State-Aware Latent Re-representation (SALR) module is specifically designed to overcome this by aligning vision-language embeddings with robot state information, providing a more grounded condition for action generation.
- **Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation:** Previous approaches in this area often rely on decomposing complex tasks into sub-goals (e.g., PerAct, ARM, VAPO), which can be task-specific and difficult to generalize. Other methods predict future world states (ReflectVLM) or forecast trajectories (DTP). LoLA distinguishes itself by emphasizing the leverage of "rich historical information" beyond just immediate actions or single-frame observations, arguing that historical frames contain crucial context about the effects of actions on the environment and object occlusions.

In essence, LoLA directly addresses the fundamental challenges of temporal coherence, physical grounding, and computational efficiency in long-horizon robot manipulation, thereby extending the capabilities of VLA models from rudimentary, short-burst actions to complex, multi-stage, goal-oriented robotic behaviors essential for real-world deployment. Its novel approach to explicitly grounding vision-language features in robot proprioception offers a significant conceptual and practical advancement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the LoLA framework is to develop a generalizable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of enabling various robotic platforms to accurately perform diverse **long-horizon manipulation tasks**. This capability is intended to be driven by a comprehensive understanding of historical frame information, multi-view images, and natural language instructions.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical limitations observed in the current state of robotic manipulation research:

1. **Limited Applicability of Existing VLA Models:** While VLA models have shown remarkable progress in interpreting language and generating actions, the vast majority focus on short-horizon tasks, which are completed in mere seconds. This severely restricts their utility in real-world scenarios that demand complex, goal-oriented behaviors involving sequences of actions over extended periods (e.g., a multi-step cooking process). The paper explicitly highlights the gap between simple tasks like "pick up a blue cup" and intricate ones like "pizza making."

2. **Challenges of Long-Horizon Task Learning:** The authors identify three core difficulties inherent in long-horizon tasks:
 - **Temporal Coherence:** Maintaining a consistent understanding of the temporal context across many steps, accurately tracking multi-step states, and generating coherent action sequences is difficult for models that lack robust temporal awareness. This often leads to cumulative errors and policy failures.
 - **Robustness to Out-of-Distribution States:** Longer interactions with the environment increase the probability of encountering unexpected or novel situations. Without proper grounding and contextual awareness, minor perturbations can accumulate, driving the robot into states far from its training distribution and causing policy breakdown.
 - **Resource Intensity:** Both training and deployment of long-horizon tasks demand substantial resources. Collecting minute-long data with frequent human resets is significantly more expensive than short-horizon data, and processing long historical sequences incurs heavy computational and memory overhead.

3. **Weak Alignment between Perception and Action in Existing Models:** Current VLA approaches often suffer from a "modality gap" where vision-language embeddings are not sufficiently aligned with the physical action space of the robot. Simple concatenation of robot proprioception (e.g., joint angles) with vision-language features is seen as a "late fusion" approach that doesn't fundamentally transform or filter abstract visual representations with physical context. This leads to ungrounded embeddings that can be easily misled by irrelevant visual information, hindering precise action generation.

To address these motivations, LoLA sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

- **Efficiently Integrate Long-Term Multi-View Observations:** Develop a strategy to incorporate extensive historical context and multi-view information without incurring prohibitive computational or memory costs, using selective spatial-temporal sampling.
- **Bridge the Modality Gap with Physical Grounding:** Introduce a novel mechanism, the State-Aware Latent Re-representation (SALR) module, to explicitly ground high-level vision-language features in the robot's physical action space. This creates an "embodiment-anchored" latent space that leverages robot proprioception to constrain learned representations to physically plausible motion patterns.
- **Filter Action-Irrelevant Information:** Implement a learnable mask operation to actively suppress noise and focus on critical action-relevant signals within the fused representations.
- **Generate Coherent Multi-Step Actions:** Utilize a Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) action expert that is conditioned on these physically-grounded, context-rich representations to generate smooth and precise multi-step action sequences.
- **Demonstrate Superior Performance and Robustness:** Validate the proposed framework's effectiveness across diverse simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic platforms, particularly in challenging long-horizon tasks, showing significant improvements over state-of-the-art methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LoLA (Long-horizon Latent Action Learning) is a comprehensive framework designed to tackle long-horizon robot manipulation by integrating long-term observations and physically grounding vision-language representations. The model aims to predict a temporal action sequence for a robot given historical multi-frame inputs, current multi-view images, and a language instruction. It comprises three core components: a pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM), the State-Aware Latent Re-representation (SALR) module, and an Action Expert.

#### 3.1. Pretrained VLM for Visual Encoding

The initial stage focuses on processing visual and linguistic inputs to build a rich contextual understanding. This is achieved through two specialized encoding components:

- **Current Observation Encoding (COE):** This component processes high-fidelity, high-resolution images from the current timestep. It includes a primary view, secondary views (for spatial ambiguity resolution and occlusions), and a wrist-mounted camera view (for fine-grained manipulation). These inputs are fed into a vision encoder to extract `F_coe`, a detailed, static snapshot of the current scene's spatial relationships.
- **Historical Motion Encoding (HME):** To capture dynamic context, object motion, and task progress over time, this component processes a sequence of historical frames. A key innovation here is **Selective Spatial-Temporal Sampling**: all historical frames are downsampled to a lower resolution (e.g., 224x224 to 112x112). This design choice is based on the insight that while immediate actions require high-resolution current state information, historical context (like velocity or task phase) can be efficiently gleaned from lower-resolution sequences, significantly reducing computational and memory burdens. The downsampled sequence is processed by the same vision encoder to extract `F_hme`, encoding motion information.

The embeddings from COE (`F_coe`), HME (`F_hme`), and the language instruction embeddings (`F_l`) are concatenated to form `F_in`. A multi-layer VLM (specifically Qwen2.5-VL-7B, a 28-layer transformer) then processes `F_in`, generating intermediate Key and Value embeddings (`{K_i, V_i}`) at each layer. These layer-wise VLM embeddings are crucial inputs for the subsequent SALR module.

#### 3.2. State-Aware Latent Re-representation (SALR)

This module is the core innovation of LoLA, designed to bridge the significant "modality gap" between abstract vision-language embeddings and concrete robot actions. Unlike prior methods that simply concatenate robot proprioception (joint angles, end-effector pose) as a "late fusion" or "additive" conditional input, SALR fundamentally transforms the representations.

- **Problem with Existing Approaches:** Simple concatenation fails to explicitly ground vision-language embeddings in the physical scale. The abstract embeddings remain untransformed by physical context, forcing subsequent attention layers to implicitly learn complex physical grounding, which can be inefficient and prone to misguidance by irrelevant visual features.
- **LoLA's Solution: Deep Multiplicative Fusion:** SALR introduces a dedicated **State Transformer** that runs *in parallel* to the VLM, sharing the same L-layer depth (28 layers).
 1. **State Projection:** The raw robot state vector `s_t` (containing joint angles `q_t` and end-effector pose `p_t`) is projected to match the VLM's hidden dimension, creating an initial state embedding.
 2. **State Transformer Processing:** This initial state embedding is processed through self-attention within each layer of the State Transformer to produce a refined state representation.
 3. **Multiplicative Outer-Product Fusion:** Instead of standard cross-attention, the refined state representation generates a robot state's Query vector (`Q_r`). This `Q_r` is then used to *modulate* the VLM's KV cache (`{K_i, V_i}`) from the corresponding layer using an element-wise (outer-product) fusion:
 - `K* = Q_r ⊙ K_i`
 - `V* = Q_r ⊙ V_i`
 This explicit multiplicative interaction grounds the vision-language features directly in the robot's physical scale, creating an "embodiment-anchored" latent space. This process expands the feature space, explicitly aligning visual perception with real-world physical dimensions and constraining the learned representation to adhere to physically plausible motion patterns.
- **Learnable Mask Operation:** To extract action-relevant cues and suppress noise (e.g., background distractors), learnable masks (`M_k`, `M_v`) are applied to `K*` and `V*` (`K' = K* ⊙ M_k`, `V' = V* ⊙ M_v`).
- **Latent Space Compression:** The filtered features are then distilled into compact, re-encoded key (`K_a`) and value (`V_a`) embeddings. These form the physically-grounded input for the action generation module.

#### 3.3. Action Expert for Action Prediction

The Action Expert is responsible for translating the physically-grounded, high-level representations from SALR into a concrete, multi-step action sequence.

- **Architecture:** It is implemented as a Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) model, specifically an M-layer Transformer Decoder (28 layers). CFM models are adept at trajectory modeling by predicting noise to iteratively refine an action sequence.
- **Conditioning:** The decoder is conditioned on three inputs:
 1. The final aligned latent condition `{K_a, V_a}` from the SALR module.
 2. A noisy action trajectory sampled at time `z`.
 3. The noise timestep `z`, encoded via sinusoidal embeddings.
- **Action Generation:** The decoder's Self-Attention layers model the temporal structure of the action sequence using the noisy action and `z` embeddings. A Cross-Attention mechanism at each layer then injects the state-aware, multi-modal guidance by attending to `K_a` (as Key) and `V_a` (as Value).
- **Training and Inference:** During training, the expert is optimized using the CFM objective to predict the noise. During inference, it takes the `{K_a, V_a}` condition and performs multiple denoising steps to progressively decode a smooth and precise action sequence from pure noise.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

LoLA is a large model with approximately 10 Billion parameters, leveraging Qwen2.5-VL-7B as its VLM backbone. It's pre-trained on diverse datasets like OXE and AgiBot (1.1 million real-world episodes). Training involves a cluster of 32 NVIDIA A100 GPUs, utilizing a hybrid sharding strategy (intra-node ZeRO-3, inter-node DDP) and gradient checkpointing for memory efficiency with long video sequences. The model uses 25 historical frames by default and processes multi-view images (primary, secondary, wrist) at different resolutions to balance fidelity and efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LoLA's performance was rigorously evaluated across a variety of simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic platforms, consistently demonstrating superior capabilities, particularly in challenging long-horizon manipulation tasks.

#### 4.1. Manipulation Benchmark on SIMPLER

The SIMPLER benchmark, designed to test sim-to-real generalization, included tasks for both Google Robot and WidowX Robot under "Visual Matching" and "Variant Aggregation" settings.

- **Google Robot Tasks (Table 1):** LoLA achieved state-of-the-art performance.
 - In the "Visual Matching" setting, LoLA attained an average success rate of **61.5%**, significantly outperforming the π0 baseline (52.7%) and surpassing the larger, closed-source RT-2-X model (46.3%).
 - In the "Variant Aggregation" setting, LoLA scored **54.6%**, again outperforming π0 (46.0%) and being comparable to RT-2-X (54.4%).
- **WidowX Robot Tasks (Table 2):** LoLA's advantages were even more pronounced on the complex, multi-step tasks for the WidowX robot.
 - LoLA achieved a remarkable **71.9%** average success rate, representing a substantial **20.6% relative improvement over π0 (41.7%)**. This result strongly validates LoLA's effectiveness in leveraging historical context to maintain coherence over long-horizon tasks.

#### 4.2. Manipulation Benchmark on LIBERO

The LIBERO benchmark assesses generalization across diverse objects, goals, spatial relationships, and long-horizon tasks, with many tasks formatted as multi-step instructions.

- **Overall Performance (Table 3):** LoLA achieved the highest average success rate across all task suites at **96.2%**, surpassing existing state-of-the-art methods, including π0 (92.2%).
- **LIBERO-Long Suite:** Crucially, LoLA demonstrated robust performance on the challenging LIBERO-Long suite, designed specifically for long-horizon capabilities, achieving **88.2%** success. This result directly underscores LoLA's ability to effectively utilize historical information to guide the robot through complex, multi-step tasks.

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation

Real-world experiments were conducted on a Franka Research 3 robot and a Bi-Manual Aloha setup, providing crucial validation in non-simulated environments.

- **Franka Research 3 (Cooking-themed tasks):** A benchmark of 28 tasks, including 22 atomic sub-tasks grouped into 7 sequential long-horizon tasks and 6 end-to-end continuous long-horizon tasks, was used.
 - **Single-Step Tasks (Table 4):** LoLA outperformed baselines (Diffusion Policy, π0) with an average success rate of **46.1%** compared to π0's 36.8% and Diffusion Policy's 18.0%.
 - **Multi-Step / Long-Horizon Tasks (Table 6):** In challenging long-horizon sequences (e.g., "Set up the oven," which is T4 → T5 → T6), LoLA demonstrated significant improvements, achieving up to **2.67 times the success rate of π0**. For instance, LoLA achieved 33.1% for "Set up the oven" compared to π0's 12.4%. While absolute success rates for all models were lower due to the inherent difficulty and error accumulation in real-world, multi-step tasks, LoLA's substantial *relative improvement* validates the robustness of its long-term context integration and SALR module.
- **Bi-Manual Aloha BusyBox (Supplementary Table 9):** This setup tested fine-grained bimanual manipulation on reconfigurable modules.
 - LoLA achieved an average success rate of **46.7%**, demonstrating superior generalization capabilities compared to π0 (30.0%) and Diffusion Policy (8.3%). This gain is attributed to SALR's ability to precisely ground intricate visual features of the BusyBox components into the robot's physical end-effector space.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation experiments were conducted to understand the impact of key components.

- **Components Analysis (Table 5 - WidowX robot):**
 - The incorporation of **Multiple Historical Frames (MF)** significantly improved overall manipulation success.
 - The application of the **State-Aware Latent Re-representation (SALR)** module, especially in conjunction with MF, led to the best performance. This underscores the crucial role of both leveraging historical information and explicitly aligning vision-language embeddings with actions.
- **Impact of Robot Proprioception (Table 7 - LIBERO):**
 - Explicitly grounding the Vision-Language (VL) embeddings with robot proprioception ("w/ state") significantly improved the average success rate from 84.7% to **91.2%** (a **6.5% gain**). This unequivocally confirms the critical role of the SALR module in achieving robust performance by providing physical grounding.

In summary, the findings consistently demonstrate that LoLA significantly outperforms prior state-of-the-art methods across diverse embodiments and environments, particularly excelling in the complex and challenging domain of long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks due to its effective integration of temporal context and physically-grounded perception-action alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LoLA represents a significant step forward in the field of general robot manipulation, particularly for tasks requiring extended temporal reasoning and precise physical interaction. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Long-Horizon Robot Manipulation:** The most direct and impactful contribution of LoLA is its ability to address the long-standing challenge of long-horizon tasks in robotic manipulation. By moving beyond short-horizon capabilities, LoLA expands the scope of VLA models, making them suitable for complex, real-world applications that involve multi-step, sequential actions over sustained periods. This capability is crucial for scenarios like assistive robotics, industrial automation, and household tasks, where robots need to perform intricate workflows without constant human intervention.

2. **Novel and Effective Physical Grounding (SALR):** The State-Aware Latent Re-representation (SALR) module is a core innovation that fundamentally improves the alignment between high-level vision-language perception and low-level physical robot actions. By explicitly grounding VLM embeddings in robot proprioception through a multiplicative outer-product fusion, LoLA creates an "embodiment-anchored" latent space. This approach is significantly more sophisticated than simple concatenation or "late fusion" methods, effectively bridging the "modality gap" and mitigating critical issues like state drift and cumulative errors that plague long-horizon tasks. This principled grounding mechanism has the potential to become a standard component in future generalist robot policies.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The extensive evaluations across diverse simulation benchmarks (SIMPLER, LIBERO) and real-world robots (Franka, Bi-Manual Aloha) demonstrate LoLA's superior robustness and generalization capabilities. The significant performance gains, especially in multi-step and error-prone real-world scenarios, indicate that LoLA can handle variations in environments, objects, and task sequences more effectively than prior methods. This robustness is paramount for deploying robots in uncontrolled, dynamic real-world settings.

4. **Efficient Temporal Context Integration:** The selective spatial-temporal sampling strategy allows LoLA to integrate rich historical context efficiently. By downsampling historical frames while maintaining high-fidelity current observations, the framework balances the need for extensive temporal information with the computational and memory constraints inherent in processing long video sequences. This efficiency is vital for scalable and practical deployment of long-horizon robot policies.

5. **Path Towards More Autonomous and Intelligent Robots:** LoLA pushes the boundaries of how robots interpret natural language commands and execute complex actions. By enabling robots to understand and perform multi-stage, goal-oriented tasks, it paves the way for more autonomous and intelligent robotic systems that can operate with less human supervision and adapt to dynamic task requirements. This could accelerate the adoption of robots in various sectors, from healthcare to logistics, by enabling them to perform more meaningful and comprehensive tasks.

6. **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper not only offers a powerful solution but also highlights future research directions. Acknowledging limitations in achieving robust execution for *more complex, novel, or perturbation-rich* long-horizon tasks, it suggests focusing on "enhancing this robustness, for example, by enabling dynamic, closed-loop recovery from significant failures." This provides a clear roadmap for the community to build upon LoLA's foundation, exploring advanced error recovery mechanisms and continuous adaptation strategies to further realize truly general-purpose robotic intelligence.

In conclusion, LoLA makes a substantial contribution by addressing a critical bottleneck in robot manipulation. Its innovative approach to combining efficient temporal context integration with a physically-grounded perception-action alignment provides a robust and generalizable framework, significantly advancing the field towards practical, long-horizon robotic capabilities.
