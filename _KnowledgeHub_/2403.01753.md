# Training-Free Pretrained Model Merging

> [!summary] Summary
> Merging under Dual-Space Constraints (MuDSC) is a training-free framework that combines multiple pre-trained neural networks into a single multi-talent model. It achieves this by iteratively optimizing unit matching using similarity measures from both weight and activation spaces, demonstrating consistent performance improvements over baselines across homogeneous and heterogeneous tasks.

## Problem

- Existing training-free model merging methods, like Task Arithmetic, require all models to be fine-tuned from the same pre-trained initialization, limiting their real-world applicability.
- Prior unit matching algorithms for merging often rely on unit similarity in either weight space or activation space, which frequently exhibit inconsistencies, leading to suboptimal merged models.
- The proliferation of specialized deep neural networks creates challenges in model storage and computational overhead, necessitating efficient consolidation into multi-functional entities without additional training.

## Method

- ==MuDSC== proposes an iterative algorithm that optimizes ==unit matching== by considering a weighted linear combination of similarity measures from both the ==activation space and the weight space==.
- The method iteratively refines ==permutation matrices== for each layer, updating weight-space representations based on adjacent layers' permutations and combining with static activation-space similarities until convergence.
- It includes adaptations for neural network architectures with group structures (e.g., Group Normalization, Multi-Head Attention) through ==group alignment and group zip strategies== to ensure broad compatibility.

## Results

- MuDSC consistently outperforms state-of-the-art training-free merging baselines across various homogeneous tasks (e.g., CIFAR-100, ImageNet) for models trained from scratch or fine-tuned from different pre-trained backbones.
- The framework shows significant scaled performance improvements in heterogeneous task merging on the Taskonomy dataset, with `MuDSC_Align` maintaining or surpassing SOTA in **83%** of tasks (e.g., **+5.12%** in 'Segment Semantic') and `MuDSC_Zip` in **75%** of tasks (e.g., **+11.42%** in 'Segment Semantic').
- Loss landscape visualizations demonstrate that MuDSC places the merged model directly at the lowest point of the multi-task loss basin, achieving a unified lower loss for each task, indicating better generalization and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unit similarity in the weight space and activation space often do not align, with many units showing high similarity in one space but low in the other, which empirically motivates a dual-space approach for effective merging.
> - Considering both weight and activation space similarities during unit matching allows the merged model to reside in a more favorable multi-task loss landscape, achieving lower, overlapping loss basins for individual tasks.
> - The iterative refinement of permutations across layers is crucial for effectively integrating information from both dual spaces, as the optimal weight-space representation for a layer depends on the permutations of adjacent layers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the rapidly evolving field of deep learning, specifically addressing the challenge of efficiently combining knowledge from multiple pre-trained neural networks. As the complexity and number of deep neural networks continue to grow, particularly with the proliferation of specialized models for diverse tasks, the issues of model storage, computational overhead, and flexible deployment become increasingly critical.

The broader landscape of combining models can be categorized into:

1. **Training-based knowledge amalgamation:** This includes techniques like knowledge distillation, where a "student" model is trained to mimic the outputs of multiple "teacher" models, or model stitching, which involves connecting layers of different networks. While effective, these methods necessitate additional, often expensive, training or fine-tuning processes, which can be computationally intensive and require access to training data (raising privacy concerns).
2. **Training-free model merging:** This paradigm aims to combine models by directly manipulating their parameters, avoiding further training. Existing prominent approaches in this category include:
 - **Task Arithmetic:** This method constructs multi-task models by performing simple arithmetic operations (like addition) on "task vectors" (differences between fine-tuned and pre-trained model parameters). However, a significant limitation is its prerequisite that all models to be merged must have been fine-tuned from the *same pre-trained initialization*. This severely restricts its applicability in real-world scenarios where models might originate from different base models or training histories.
 - **Unit Matching:** This emerging technique focuses on identifying and aligning functionally similar neurons (or "units") across different models. It leverages the permutation invariance property of neural networks, where swapping neurons within a layer, along with corresponding adjustments in adjacent layers, does not change the network's function. While promising, prior unit matching methods typically rely on similarity measures either in the *weight space* (comparing synaptic weights) or the *activation space* (comparing neuron outputs for a given input).

The paper identifies a crucial gap and common drawback in existing unit matching methods: the **inconsistency of unit similarity between the weight space and the activation space**. As illustrated in Figure 1 of the paper, units with high similarity in weights might exhibit low similarity in activations, and vice-versa. This discrepancy means that relying solely on one space for matching can lead to suboptimal or "poor artifact" merged models, especially when trying to combine models trained for different tasks. This work directly addresses this limitation, aiming to provide a more robust and generally applicable training-free model merging solution by unifying these two perspectives. By proposing a framework that considers *both* weight and activation similarity, this research pushes the boundaries of training-free model merging towards greater flexibility and performance, particularly for models with diverse origins and functionalities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an innovative and effective training-free model merging framework, termed **Merging under Dual-Space Constraints (MuDSC)**, capable of combining multiple single-talent models into a single multi-talent model. The authors aim to overcome critical limitations observed in existing training-free model merging techniques.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving this work are:

- **Addressing the Proliferation of Specialized Models:** The increasing number of task-specific deep neural networks leads to significant challenges in terms of model storage and computational resources. Merging these models into a single, multi-functional entity can mitigate these issues.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Training-Based Merging:** Existing training-based knowledge amalgamation methods require extensive and costly retraining or fine-tuning, which is inefficient and may not always be feasible due to data privacy or resource constraints. The goal is to avoid any additional training.
- **Relaxing Prerequisites of Existing Training-Free Methods:** Current training-free methods, particularly task arithmetic, are highly restrictive, requiring that models originate from the *same pre-trained initialization*. This severely limits their practical applicability. MuDSC aims to enable merging of models trained from scratch or fine-tuned from different initializations.
- **Resolving the Inconsistency in Unit Similarity:** The core motivation stems from the observation that prior unit matching algorithms, which are crucial for training-free merging, rely solely on either weight space or activation space for determining unit similarity. The authors rigorously identify and demonstrate that these two spaces often exhibit inconsistent similarity patterns (as shown in Figure 1). This inconsistency leads to suboptimal matches and hinders the performance of merged models. MuDSC is specifically designed to address this fundamental inconsistency.
- **Enhancing Merged Model Performance and Robustness:** By leveraging a more comprehensive similarity measure, the objective is to significantly boost the performance of the merged multi-task models across various task combinations and architectures. The authors also aim to show that MuDSC enables the merged model to reside in a more favorable (lower loss, overlapping) segment of the multi-task loss landscape.
- **Ensuring Practical Usability and Architectural Adaptability:** The framework is designed to be usable with common neural network architectures, including those with group structures like Multi-Head Attention and Group Normalization, which pose challenges for standard unit matching algorithms.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the need for a more general, robust, and efficient training-free model merging technique that can effectively integrate diverse knowledge without additional training overhead or restrictive architectural prerequisites.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology revolves around **Merging under Dual-Space Constraints (MuDSC)**, an iterative algorithm that explicitly leverages unit similarity in both activation and weight spaces to find optimal permutation matrices for merging.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries of Unit Matching

The general procedure for model merging via unit matching involves fusing units that exhibit high similarity to maximize the cumulative global similarity across layers. This is mathematically formulated as finding a set of permutation matrices ($P_l$) for each layer ($l$) that maximizes the Frobenius inner product between a similarity matrix ($C_l$) and the permutation matrix (Eq. 1), subject to constraints ensuring valid merging (each unit mapped at most once, and merged units form a cohesive block). The merged weights ($W_l^1$) are then obtained by averaging the permuted unit parameters (Eq. 2).

#### 3.2. Merging under Dual-Space Constraints (MuDSC)

The core innovation of MuDSC is to address the inconsistency of unit similarity between the weight space and the activation space.

- **Dual-Space Objective:** Instead of optimizing for similarity in a single space, MuDSC proposes a combined objective (Eq. 3) that linearly weights the similarity in the activation space ($C(A_l)$) and the weight space ($C(Z_l)$) using a balanced factor $\alpha \in$. This formulation is elegantly shown to be equivalent to computing a *linear combination of the similarity matrices themselves* (Eq. 4), making the optimization tractable.
- **Unit Representation:**
 - **Activation Space ($A_l$):** Units are represented by their activation values, typically obtained by passing sample data through the network. This captures the feature-learning aspect of neurons.
 - **Weight Space ($Z_l$):** Following advanced solutions in prior work, units in the $l$-th layer are represented by concatenating their incoming weights from layer $l-1$ ($W_l$) and their outgoing weights to layer $l+1$ ($W_{l+1}$) (Eq. 6). This comprehensive representation captures the unit's connectivity to both preceding and succeeding layers.
- **Iterative Algorithm (Algorithm 1):** A key challenge arises because the optimal weight-space representation ($Z_l$) for a layer $l$ depends on the permutation matrices ($P_{l-1}, P_{l+1}$) of its adjacent layers. This dependency necessitates an iterative approach.
 - **Initialization:** The algorithm starts by initializing permutation matrices ($P_l$) based solely on activation-based similarity, as activation-based matching can be performed in a single pass.
 - **Iteration:** In each subsequent iteration, the algorithm updates the permutation matrices for each layer $l$:
 1. It computes the weight-space representation ($Z_l$) for all models based on the current permutation matrices of layers $l-1$ and $l+1$.
 2. It then computes a combined similarity matrix for layer $l$ by linearly combining the weight-space similarity matrix ($C(Z_l)$) and the initial activation-space similarity matrix ($C(A_l)$) using the balance factor $\alpha$.
 3. Finally, it derives new permutation matrices ($P_l$) from this combined similarity matrix.
 - This process continues until convergence, gradually refining the unit matches by simultaneously considering both spatial similarities.

#### 3.3. Adaptation to Group Structure

Recognizing that many modern neural networks employ group structures (e.g., Group Normalization, Multi-Head Attention), which invalidate simple permutation invariance, MuDSC incorporates adaptations for these architectures:

- **Group Alignment:** This approach first performs internal alignment (matching units) within each group pairwise. Then, it conducts external alignment (matching groups) based on the average similarity obtained from the internal alignments. This method, while potentially having quadratic time complexity for external matching, aims for a globally optimal match.
- **Group Zip:** For greedy matching algorithms (like the "zip" operation), a simplified scheme is proposed. It first determines which groups to merge (using the results of group alignment in experiments for fair comparison), and then "zips" units within these pre-merged groups.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The methodology is evaluated extensively across various tasks and model architectures:

- **Homogeneous Tasks:** Merging models trained on classification tasks with different subsets of categories.
 - **Datasets:** CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 (small-scale), ImageNet (large-scale).
 - **Models:** ResNet-20, ResNet-20GN, ResNet-26, ResNet-50GN, ViT-Small, DINO-Small, Swin-Tiny. This covers models trained from scratch, fine-tuned from ImageNet pre-trained models, and those with group structures.
 - **Metrics:** Joint accuracy and per-task accuracy.
- **Heterogeneous Tasks:** Merging models trained on fundamentally different tasks.
 - **Dataset:** Taskonomy (12 diverse tasks like segmentation, depth estimation, edge detection).
 - **Models:** Encoders from Taskonomy's encoder-decoder architectures.
 - **Metric:** Scaled performance (normalized between an average estimator and the original pre-trained model performance) to intuitively compare diverse task outcomes.
- **Baselines:** A comprehensive comparison against state-of-the-art training-free merging methods, including:
 - `Average` (direct parameter averaging), `Rebasin` (alignment-based SOTA), `A.Align` (activation-based alignment), `Zipit` (zip-based SOTA), and `W.Zip` (weight-based zip).
- **Hyperparameters:** A balance factor $\alpha$ is searched on training data for homogeneous tasks to find the best per-task accuracy, while it is fixed at 0.5 for heterogeneous tasks.
- **Loss Landscape Visualization:** Conducted on a simple MNIST binary classification multi-task setup (prime vs. odd numbers) using a four-layer MLP, reduced to 2D via PCA, to visually demonstrate how MuDSC positions the merged model within the loss landscape.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MuDSC provides compelling evidence for its effectiveness and superiority over existing training-free model merging techniques.

- **Empirical Demonstration of Dual-Space Inconsistency:** Figure 1 serves as a crucial foundational finding, empirically demonstrating that unit similarity in weight space and activation space often do not align. Many units show high similarity in one space but low in the other, directly motivating the need for a dual-space approach. The "best merged" unit is consistently found where both similarities are high.

- **Superiority in Homogeneous Task Merging:**
 - **Randomly Initialized Models (Table 1):** MuDSC consistently outperforms all baselines. For instance, with ResNet20GN on CIFAR100, `MuDSC_Align` significantly boosts joint accuracy by 8.41% over the second-best method (`A.Align`), showcasing its strength when models are trained from scratch. `MuDSC_Zip` also shows improvements, especially for ResNet20.
 - **Pre-trained and Fine-tuned Models (Table 2 & S7):** This is a particularly challenging scenario for previous methods. `Rebasin` and `W.Zip` often perform no better than direct averaging (e.g., for ResNet26, Resnet50GN, ViT on CIFAR100), implying that their single-space approach struggles with models that share a common pre-training but diverge in fine-tuning. Activation-based methods (`A.Align`) can even perform worse than averaging in some cases. MuDSC, however, effectively navigates this complexity, consistently achieving improvements in merged accuracy across ResNet26, ResNet50GN, ViT, DINO-S, and Swin-T architectures. This demonstrates MuDSC's ability to overcome the limitations of single-space methods when dealing with models that initially shared high weight similarity but developed distinct functionalities.
 - **Large-Scale Data (ImageNet, Table 3):** The superior performance of MuDSC extends to large-scale ImageNet data, with `MuDSC_Align` and `MuDSC_Zip` surpassing baselines when merging ResNet-50 models fine-tuned from a DINO-pretrained backbone on disjoint subsets of ImageNet-1k.

- **Significant Gains in Heterogeneous Task Merging (Table 4):**
 - MuDSC demonstrates remarkable improvements when merging models trained on vastly different tasks from the Taskonomy dataset.
 - `MuDSC_Align` either maintains or surpasses the current state-of-the-art in 83% of the tasks, with notable scaled performance improvements of 5.12% in "Segment Semantic," 2.68% in "Reshading," 4.58% in "Autoencoding," and 3.88% in "Denoise."
 - `MuDSC_Zip` similarly outperforms SOTA methods in 75% of tasks, achieving an outstanding 11.42% improvement in "Segment Semantic."
 - The paper also notes a nuanced finding: alignment-based methods perform better on semantic tasks (e.g., classification, semantic segmentation), while zip-based methods excel in pixel-to-pixel tasks (e.g., depth estimation, denoising). Crucially, MuDSC consistently enhances *both* types of base matching algorithms regardless of their specific strengths.

- **Favorable Loss Landscape Visualization (Figure 3):**
 - The visualization on the MNIST multi-task experiment reveals that MuDSC positions the merged model directly at the lowest point of the multi-task loss basin, in contrast to activation-based methods (e.g., Zipit) which are near, and weight-based methods (e.g., W.Zip) which are further away.
 - More profoundly, MuDSC facilitates the discovery of overlapping loss basins at lower points for individual tasks. This implies that MuDSC effectively balances the performance across different tasks, leading to a "unified lower loss for each task" in the merged model, which is a strong indicator of better generalization and robustness.

- **Computational Aspects (Supplementary Material S4):** The supplementary material indicates that MuDSC typically converges in a reasonable number of rounds (3-10 rounds for various models and datasets), making the iterative approach practically feasible despite the increased per-round complexity compared to single-pass activation-based methods.

In summary, the main findings clearly establish MuDSC as a superior training-free model merging framework, capable of producing higher-performing and more robust multi-task models by meticulously integrating information from both weight and activation spaces, even for complex scenarios involving fine-tuned or diverse models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Training-Free Pretrained Model Merging" paper introduces MuDSC, a significant advancement in the field of efficient AI model deployment and multi-task learning, with several important implications and potential impacts:

- **Broadening the Applicability of Training-Free Merging:** MuDSC addresses a critical limitation of previous training-free merging methods by not requiring models to originate from the same pre-trained initialization. This makes it applicable to a much wider range of real-world scenarios where models might be acquired from different sources, developed with varying pre-training strategies, or fine-tuned for specialized tasks. This flexibility is a game-changer for efficiently combining a diverse "zoo" of pre-trained models.
- **Enhancing Efficiency and Resource Utilization:** By enabling the creation of "multi-talent" models without additional training or fine-tuning, MuDSC significantly reduces the computational and data-access costs associated with integrating new functionalities. This leads to substantial savings in terms of GPU time, energy consumption, and storage, as a single merged model can replace multiple specialized ones. This is particularly impactful for resource-constrained environments or edge devices.
- **Improved Multi-Task Model Performance and Robustness:** The consistent empirical superiority of MuDSC across diverse homogeneous and heterogeneous tasks demonstrates its ability to generate higher-performing merged models. The loss landscape visualization further solidifies this, showing that MuDSC guides the merged model to a more robust, low-loss region that simultaneously optimizes for multiple tasks. This translates to better generalization and balanced performance across different functions.
- **Advancing the Understanding of Neural Network Representations:** By explicitly demonstrating and addressing the inconsistency between weight-space and activation-space similarity, the paper contributes to a deeper understanding of how different aspects of a neural network's internal state (parameters vs. feature outputs) relate to its learned function and how these aspects should be considered for effective model manipulation. This could inspire further research into multi-modal representations of neural network units.
- **Practical Applicability to Modern Architectures:** The integrated adaptations for group structures like Multi-Head Attention and Group Normalization ensure that MuDSC is compatible with a wide array of contemporary and future neural network architectures (e.g., Transformers), which are increasingly prevalent in computer vision and beyond. This foresight ensures the method's long-term relevance.
- **Catalyst for AI System Integration in Industry:** The involvement of the State Grid Shandong Electric Power Company strongly suggests the practical relevance of this work to industrial applications. In sectors like energy, healthcare, or manufacturing, where numerous AI models might monitor different aspects of complex systems, MuDSC offers a pathway to consolidate these models into more efficient, integrated, and manageable AI solutions. This could lead to streamlined deployment, easier maintenance, and potentially novel capabilities arising from the synergistic combination of specialized models.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The observed differences in performance between alignment-based and zip-based methods for semantic vs. pixel-to-pixel tasks, even with MuDSC's dual-space enhancement, opens up new avenues for research into task-specific model merging strategies or more adaptive combination techniques. The work also paves the way for exploring how $\alpha$ could be dynamically learned or adapted for different layers or task combinations.

In conclusion, MuDSC represents a significant step forward in training-free model merging, offering a robust, efficient, and versatile framework that addresses key limitations of prior art. Its impact is likely to be felt across both academic research into neural network interpretability and practical applications requiring scalable and adaptable AI system deployments.
