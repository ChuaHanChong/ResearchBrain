# Training-Free Pretrained Model Merging

> [!summary] Summary
> Merging under Dual-Space Constraints (MuDSC) is a training-free framework that combines multiple pre-trained neural networks into a single multi-talent model. It achieves this by iteratively optimizing unit matching using similarity measures from both weight and activation spaces, demonstrating consistent performance improvements over baselines across homogeneous and heterogeneous tasks.

## Problem

- Existing training-free model merging methods, like Task Arithmetic, require all models to be fine-tuned from the same pre-trained initialization, limiting their real-world applicability.
- Prior unit matching algorithms for merging often rely on unit similarity in either weight space or activation space, which frequently exhibit inconsistencies, leading to suboptimal merged models.
- The proliferation of specialized deep neural networks creates challenges in model storage and computational overhead, necessitating efficient consolidation into multi-functional entities without additional training.

## Method

- ==MuDSC== proposes an iterative algorithm that optimizes ==unit matching== by considering a weighted linear combination of similarity measures from both the ==activation space and the weight space==.
- The method iteratively refines ==permutation matrices== for each layer, updating weight-space representations based on adjacent layers' permutations and combining with static activation-space similarities until convergence.
- It includes adaptations for neural network architectures with group structures (e.g., Group Normalization, Multi-Head Attention) through ==group alignment and group zip strategies== to ensure broad compatibility.

## Results

- MuDSC consistently outperforms state-of-the-art training-free merging baselines across various homogeneous tasks (e.g., CIFAR-100, ImageNet) for models trained from scratch or fine-tuned from different pre-trained backbones.
- The framework shows significant scaled performance improvements in heterogeneous task merging on the Taskonomy dataset, with `MuDSC_Align` maintaining or surpassing SOTA in **83%** of tasks (e.g., **+5.12%** in 'Segment Semantic') and `MuDSC_Zip` in **75%** of tasks (e.g., **+11.42%** in 'Segment Semantic').
- Loss landscape visualizations demonstrate that MuDSC places the merged model directly at the lowest point of the multi-task loss basin, achieving a unified lower loss for each task, indicating better generalization and robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unit similarity in the weight space and activation space often do not align, with many units showing high similarity in one space but low in the other, which empirically motivates a dual-space approach for effective merging.
> - Considering both weight and activation space similarities during unit matching allows the merged model to reside in a more favorable multi-task loss landscape, achieving lower, overlapping loss basins for individual tasks.
> - The iterative refinement of permutations across layers is crucial for effectively integrating information from both dual spaces, as the optimal weight-space representation for a layer depends on the permutations of adjacent layers.
