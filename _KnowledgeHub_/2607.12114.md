# GaitSpan: Growing Humanoid Locomotion from Walking to Running

> [!summary] Summary
> The GaitSpan framework enables a humanoid robot to expand its learned walking skill into a continuous range of faster gaits, including jogging and running. It reuses the robot's foundational walking motor structure, adapting it with new rhythms and residual corrections to achieve robust command tracking and dynamic motion across diverse robot morphologies and terrains.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots struggle to acquire a continuous spectrum of locomotion gaits (walking, jogging, running) from a single policy.
- Existing methods like discrete experts or direct fine-tuning are inefficient, lack smooth transitions, or may overwrite stable slower gaits when aiming for faster, more dynamic movements.
- Traditional control and learning approaches often limit flexibility, generalization, and are challenged by designing reward functions that stabilize both slow and fast gaits simultaneously.

## Method

- **GaitSpan** employs a ==skill growth== paradigm where a frozen ==seed walking policy== is expanded by regenerating its motor structure at new rhythms and applying ==residual adaptations==.
- **GaitWave** uses ==phase-scaled canonical action waves== from the seed policy combined with a ==hierarchical multi-resolution coefficient memory== to enable command-conditioned rhythm expansion.
- **H-SLIP** introduces ==hierarchical virtual-leg dynamic shaping== with physically grounded rewards for compression, rebound, touchdown, and flight, guiding the policy towards dynamic jogging and running behaviors.

## Results

- GaitSpan successfully generated a **continuous progression of gaits** from stable walking to jogging and running-like behaviors, demonstrating smooth transitions and **increasing flight time** with higher commanded speeds.
- The framework maintained **low command tracking error** across multiple robot embodiments and out-of-distribution commands, outperforming expert assembly baselines in dynamic gait emergence and exhibiting competitive **energy efficiency** against human-demonstration baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both GaitWave and H-SLIP are complementary and essential, with the structured skill reuse strategy proving more effective than training from scratch or direct fine-tuning for acquiring a coherent gait family.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An acquired walking skill can serve as a generative prior, systematically expanding into a family of faster gaits rather than requiring independent learning or discrete switching.
> - The synergistic combination of structured rhythm expansion (GaitWave) and physically grounded dynamic shaping (H-SLIP) is crucial for achieving accurate command tracking and natural emergence of dynamic locomotion with flight phases.
> - Freezing and expanding around a pretrained seed policy, coupled with self-anchored regularization, preserves low-speed stability while enabling adaptable, command-driven dynamic gait growth.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid locomotion is a foundational challenge in robotics, requiring robust balance, whole-body coordination, and adaptive motion across various commands and environments. Prior research has approached this problem through several paradigms, each with inherent limitations that GaitSpan aims to address.

Historically, model-based control methods have been employed, utilizing concepts like the Zero Moment Point (ZMP), Linear Inverted Pendulum (LIP), and Spring-Loaded Inverted Pendulum (SLIP) models. These approaches provide structural priors for stability and can achieve robust walking and running. However, they often rely on explicit model assumptions, rigid gait schedules, or morphology-specific controllers, limiting their flexibility in adapting continuously across a broad range of locomotion speeds or environmental conditions.

More recently, learning-based approaches, particularly reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL), have gained prominence. RL has demonstrated capabilities in producing agile motions like walking, running, and parkour. Nevertheless, obtaining diverse-speed locomotion from a single RL policy remains challenging because reward functions that stabilize slower gaits may inhibit faster, more dynamic motions. A common workaround involves training discrete skills or "experts" for different gaits and then distilling or switching between them. This strategy can be inefficient for continuous speed ranges, introduce interference between skills, lead to averaged behaviors at regime boundaries, and result in brittle transitions.

Imitation learning, which leverages human motion data, can reproduce natural and expressive behaviors. However, learned policies are tied to the demonstration data distribution and may not generalize well across different terrains, robot morphologies, or command ranges. Furthermore, imitation learning imports motion diversity from external sources rather than developing it from the robot's intrinsic motor capabilities.

Curriculum learning is a common RL strategy where tasks progress in complexity, improving training efficiency but often requiring manual design and potentially overwriting earlier learned skills without explicit preservation mechanisms. Continual learning focuses on acquiring, retaining, and reusing knowledge over extended experiences. While related to GaitSpan's goal of reusing prior knowledge, existing continual learning methods typically fine-tune policies, select modules from libraries, or learn latent skill priors.

GaitSpan differentiates itself by proposing a "skill growth" paradigm. Instead of treating different gaits as separate skills to be prescribed, imitated, distilled, or switched among, it views an acquired walking skill as a reusable "seed." This seed motor structure for balance, support, and contact transition is then expanded into a family of related behaviors (jogging, running) by regenerating it at new rhythms, extending strides, and applying residual adaptations. This approach aims to provide a single, command-conditioned policy that continuously spans diverse gait regimes, offering a potentially more efficient, scalable, and higher-performing route for humanoid skill acquisition that generalizes across morphologies and unseen terrains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable a humanoid robot that has learned to walk to expand this fundamental skill into a broader repertoire of faster locomotion, specifically jogging and running, without requiring the robot to learn each new gait independently or from scratch.

The motivation for this objective stems from observed limitations in current humanoid locomotion learning approaches:
- **Lack of Continuous Gait Spectrum:** Existing methods often yield discrete gait competencies. A humanoid trained for one athletic motion typically does not acquire command-conditioned locomotion across a continuous range encompassing walking, jogging, and running. Even when running is demonstrated, the policies may be narrow in scope (e.g., specific speed ranges or task settings).
- **Inefficiency of Separate Skill Acquisition:** The common practice of training discrete gait experts and then distilling or switching between them is considered inefficient for handling a continuous speed range. This can lead to issues such as cross-skill interference, behaviors that average between gaits near transition points, and fragile transitions.
- **Limited Flexibility and Generalization:** Model-based control, while robust, can be too rigid due to reliance on hand-designed templates and specific models. Imitation learning is constrained by its training data distribution, potentially limiting generalization across varying terrains, robot embodiments, or commanded speeds beyond the demonstrated range. Standard reinforcement learning struggles with reward design that can simultaneously stabilize walking and encourage dynamic, high-speed motions.
- **Absence of a Skill-Growth Principle:** The authors argue that a fundamental principle is missing: how an acquired skill can systematically expand into a family of related behaviors, akin to how humans adapt their motor repertoire. They propose that walking, as a foundational skill, contains reusable motor structures (for balance, support, center-of-mass shifting, foot placement, body coordination, and contact transition) that should be leveraged rather than discarded when transitioning to faster movements.

The core motivation is to develop a framework, GaitSpan, that treats walking as a "seed skill" from which faster gaits can naturally grow. This involves regenerating the seed's motor structure at new rhythms, extending strides, and applying residual corrections, leading to a single policy that can smoothly transition between walking, jogging, and running.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GaitSpan is a framework designed to expand a pretrained, basic walking policy (referred to as the "seed policy" $\pi_{seed}$) into a broader family of faster locomotion gaits. It operates on a seed-residual adaptation architecture, where the frozen walking policy is treated as a generator of reusable motor structures rather than a static base action. The overall action $a_t$ is formulated as a combination of seed-derived "action waves" ($a_{wave,t}$) and a residual term ($a_{res,t}$), clipped to joint limits.

The framework comprises three main aspects:

1. **GaitWave: Hierarchical Rhythm Growth from Seed Walking:**
 - **Phase-scaled Canonical Action Waves:** The seed walking policy uses a sinusoidal phase encoding in its observation. GaitWave reuses this phase input by querying the *same frozen seed policy* under multiple phase scales $P = \{\rho_0, \rho_1, \dots, \rho_K\}$. Each scale $\rho_k$ modifies the phase input (e.g., $\sin(\rho_k \phi_t)$, $\cos(\rho_k \phi_t)$) to generate distinct "canonical action waves" $a_{seed}^{(k)}_t$. These waves are not separate experts or hand-designed templates but self-generated action structures from the robot's own foundational walking policy at alternative internal rhythms.
 - **Hierarchical Multi-Resolution Coefficient Memory:** To combine these canonical action waves in a command-conditioned manner, GaitWave introduces a hierarchical memory bank. This bank learns coefficients at multiple resolutions ($H = \{2, 4, \dots, l\}$). At each resolution $L$, the command interval is represented by $L$ learnable memory units $M^{(L)}_{\psi,j}$, each predicting coefficients $\beta^{(L)}_{t,j}$ for the $K$ canonical action waves. This design allows for both global coherence and local expressiveness. A soft-blending mechanism is used to combine outputs from neighboring memory units, ensuring continuous transitions. Coefficients from different resolutions are aggregated to produce the final set of coefficients $\alpha_t$. The $a_{wave,t}$ component is then formed by combining the base seed action and the scaled canonical action waves: $a_{wave,t} = a_{seed,t} + \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_{t,k} a_{seed,t}$. At low speeds, GaitWave can recover behavior close to the original walking policy, while at higher speeds, it adaptively reshapes the action using alternative rhythms.

2. **H-SLIP: Hierarchical Virtual-Leg Dynamic Shaping:**
 - **Motivation:** Rhythm expansion alone does not guarantee physically meaningful dynamic gaits. Faster locomotion requires coordinated dynamic events like stance loading, compression, rebound, flight, and touchdown.
 - **Hierarchical Virtual Legs:** H-SLIP introduces a physically grounded objective inspired by the Spring-Loaded Inverted Pendulum (SLIP) model. It defines three levels of "virtual-leg" abstractions for each foot: a global virtual leg (root to foot), and local virtual legs (root to knee, and knee to foot). These capture whole-body support dynamics and local lower-limb articulation.
 - **Hierarchical Dynamics Shaping:** This component rewards four key stages:
 - **Compression & Rebound:** Rewards $r^x_{comp,t}$ (ReLU of negative temporal length change) and $r^x_{reb,t}$ (ReLU of positive temporal length change) for each virtual-leg level $x$. Rebound is modulated by a velocity-tracking gate to ensure it contributes to commanded locomotion.
 - **Touchdown:** Rewards $r_{td,t}$ for controlled touchdowns, discouraging large planar foot velocities at contact.
 - **Flight:** Rewards $r_{flight,t}$ when no feet are in ground contact, encouraging the policy to leave quasi-static walking for more dynamic motion.
 - An additional penalty $r_{slip,t}$ discourages undesirable stance slips.
 - These terms are combined into a hierarchical SLIP reward $r^{H-SLIP}_t$, providing a gait-agnostic physical bias to guide motions towards jogging- and running-like regimes while maintaining flexibility.

3. **Residual Adaptation:**
 - A residual policy $a_{res,t} = \delta_{\theta}(s_t, v_t, a_{seed,t})$ learns state-dependent corrections beyond what GaitWave and H-SLIP provide. This term captures motion details not accounted for by the structured expansion of the seed policy. The final action is $a_t = \text{clip}(a_{wave,t} + a_{res,t}, a_{min}, a_{max})$.

### 4. Total Reward and Self-Anchored Regularization

The total reward $r_{total,t}$ combines a standard locomotion objective, the H-SLIP dynamic-shaping reward, and auxiliary "self-anchored" (SA) regularization terms. These SA terms include:
- **Seed anchoring:** Encourages the final action to stay close to the nominal canonical walking action at low commanded speeds.
- **Residual compactness:** Penalizes large residual corrections to bias the policy towards using the expanded canonical structure.
- **Phase-aware self-similarity:** Encourages recurrent locomotion structure without explicit gait trajectories.
- **Temporal action consistency:** Promotes smoothness in the expanded policy relative to the foundational skill.
These regularization terms preserve the inherited walking structure while enabling command-driven dynamic gait growth.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several key findings regarding GaitSpan's ability to grow diverse humanoid locomotion gaits.

#### 5.1. Beyond Walking - Emergence of Jogging, Striding, and Running

- **Continuous Gait Progression:** GaitSpan successfully generated a continuous progression of gaits from stable walking to longer-stride, jogging-like, and running-like behaviors as commanded speed increased. Foot-contact plots (Figure 3) illustrated smooth transitions from regular alternating support at low speeds to reorganized contact frequencies and denser contact-flight transitions at higher speeds.
- **Smooth Transitions:** Time-lapse sequences (Figure 1) visually demonstrated smooth motion transitions from walking to faster dynamic regimes on real-world terrains, supporting the claim that new gaits emerge through expansion rather than discrete switching.
- **Embodiment Adaptation:** Gait emergence was observed to adapt to specific robot morphologies. Different humanoids (Booster T1, Booster K1, Unitree G1) exhibited distinct foot-contact patterns at the same commanded speed, reflecting morphology-specific adjustments without relying on rigid designs or a singular gait schedule. GaitSpan allowed each embodiment to develop its own command-appropriate locomotion patterns.

#### 5.2. Baseline Comparison

GaitSpan was compared against several baselines: the frozen walking Seed policy, an Energy Multi-Experts approach, and a Human Demonstration baseline (using AMP).
- **Command Tracking and Dynamic Gait:** GaitSpan maintained low tracking error across all three evaluated embodiments (Booster T1, K1, Unitree G1) and both in-distribution and out-of-distribution (OOD) commands, while progressively developing substantial flight time at higher speeds (Figure 4). This indicated that dynamic gait emergence did not compromise command following.
- **Superiority over Expert Assembly:** The Energy Multi-Experts baseline, despite combining separately trained policies, showed degraded high-speed tracking and negligible flight phases due to its conservative, energy-oriented objectives (Figure 11, 13). This suggested that composing discrete experts did not yield a coherent continuous gait family as effectively as GaitSpan's growth approach.
- **Command-Responsiveness vs. Human Priors:** Compared to the human-demonstration baseline (AMP), GaitSpan tracked commands more accurately across the evaluated range and exhibited clearer speed-dependent contact dynamics (Figure 5). It maintained grounded walking at low speeds and developed pronounced no-contact intervals only at higher speeds, in contrast to AMP which showed substantial flight even at walking speeds (Figure 12).
- **Energy Efficiency:** GaitSpan consumed less energy than the human-demonstration baseline over most of the command range and remained competitive at the highest speeds (Figure 10). This implied that human-like imitation is not always necessary for energy-efficient and command-adaptive gaits for robots.

#### 5.3. Ablation Study

Ablation studies investigated the contributions of GaitSpan's key components and training strategies.
- **Complementary Roles of GaitWave and H-SLIP:** Neither the vanilla seed-residual baseline nor variants using only GaitWave or only H-SLIP achieved simultaneous accurate tracking and meaningful dynamic gait emergence across the full range (Figure 6a). GaitWave alone provided limited flight, while H-SLIP alone, though generating flight, suffered from tracking deterioration under OOD commands due to overly aggressive dynamic motion. The full GaitSpan model achieved the best balance, maintaining accurate tracking and progressive flight development, demonstrating a synergistic coupling where GaitWave provides structured rhythm expansion and H-SLIP provides physical bias for dynamic strides.
- **Importance of Skill Reuse Strategy:** Training from scratch (with or without a residual branch) failed to recover a coherent gait family, often losing tracking or producing transient, inappropriate flight (Figure 6b, 13). Direct fine-tuning of the seed policy resulted in rapidly increasing tracking error and little sustained flight, indicating that directly overwriting the seed could degrade its stable walking structure. GaitSpan, by freezing and expanding around the seed policy, preserved low-speed locomotion accuracy while introducing dynamic patterns only as commanded speed increased, proving its structured expansion approach more effective.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

GaitSpan represents a conceptual shift in humanoid skill acquisition by demonstrating a "skill growth" paradigm. This work signifies that a learned motor skill, specifically walking, can serve not merely as an endpoint of training, but as a foundational prior from which richer families of related behaviors can emerge through structured expansion.

The potential impacts of this research include:
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** By leveraging existing skills and avoiding the need to relearn locomotion from scratch for each new gait, GaitSpan offers a more efficient and scalable pathway for developing diverse humanoid behaviors. This reduces training time and computational resources compared to multi-expert or from-scratch approaches.
- **Continuous and Flexible Locomotion:** The framework delivers a single, command-conditioned policy that continuously spans walking, jogging, and running-like regimes. This enables smoother transitions between gaits and greater flexibility in responding to continuous speed commands, a capability often lacking in systems that rely on discrete skill switching.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** GaitSpan demonstrates robustness across multiple humanoid morphologies and generalizes zero-shot to unseen terrains (sim-to-sim and real-world), despite being trained only on flat and mildly uneven surfaces. This improved generalization is critical for deploying humanoids in complex, unstructured environments.
- **Bio-Inspired Learning Principle:** The approach aligns with observations of human motor learning, where individuals reuse fundamental motor structures while adapting rhythm and stride for different speeds. This bio-inspired principle could inform the development of more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
- **Foundation for Future Skill Acquisition:** GaitSpan positions existing skills as "generative priors" for behavior families, suggesting a broader route for acquiring complex skills. This could lead to hierarchical learning systems where foundational skills are incrementally expanded and refined, rather than re-evaluated or re-trained for every new task.
- **Reduced Reliance on External Data:** The system learns dynamic, energy-conscious, and command-adaptive gaits without relying extensively on human motion demonstrations, which may not always be optimal for robot morphology and energy objectives. This reduces the dependency on potentially mismatched external data.

Overall, GaitSpan contributes to the development of humanoids capable of more natural, versatile, and adaptable locomotion, paving the way for advanced applications in various real-world scenarios.
