# ViserDex: Visual Sim-to-Real for Robust Dexterous In-hand Reorientation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETH Zurich and NVIDIA developed ViserDex, a sim-to-real framework enabling robust monocular RGB-based in-hand reorientation of complex objects by integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting with novel pre-rasterization augmentations for visual data generation. The system demonstrated sustained reorientation across diverse objects and under challenging adversarial lighting conditions, achieving up to 37.6 consecutive reorientations and reducing computational training requirements to a single consumer-grade GPU.

## Problem

- Dexterous in-hand manipulation with monocular RGB cameras struggles with robust object pose estimation due to rapid motions, self-occlusions, and motion blur.
- Traditional rendering for sim-to-real reinforcement learning is computationally expensive for generating diverse, photorealistic visual data, while standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) lacks mechanisms for dynamic scene randomization.
- Existing sim-to-real dexterous control often avoids primary reliance on monocular RGB, leading to a robustness bottleneck or requiring complex multi-modal sensing setups.

## Method

- A modular ==sim-to-real== framework consisting of teacher policy training (with full state access), student distillation (for real-world deployment), and a visual pose estimator.
- Integration of ==3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)== (3DGS) rendering directly into the simulation loop to generate high-fidelity visual data efficiently.
- Novel ==pre-rasterization augmentations== applied to 3DGS attributes (spherical harmonic coefficients) to achieve structured domain randomization, simulating diverse lighting and material properties within a static Gaussian representation.

## Results

- The pose estimation pipeline achieved **65.4**% accuracy on real-world data under nominal conditions and **56.3**% under adversarial conditions, outperforming tiled rendering baselines.
- The system achieved a mean of **37.6** consecutive reorientations on the Allegro Hand under nominal lighting and maintained approximately **25** consecutive successes under severe adversarial lighting.
- Policy training was significantly more resource-efficient, converging in **26****-90 hours** on a single/dual consumer-grade GPU and achieving **1.6x** faster rendering throughput compared to traditional tiled rendering.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust dexterous manipulation with monocular RGB is achievable by addressing the 'perception-control gap' through a computationally efficient and visually realistic sim-to-real pipeline.
> - Domain randomization can be effectively performed directly within the 3D Gaussian Splatting representation space, overcoming its inherent limitations for dynamic scene rendering and providing diverse training data.
> - A performance-based curriculum for reinforcement learning and a belief encoder-decoder for student distillation are crucial for efficient policy training and robustness to real-world sensor noise and partial observations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic dexterous manipulation has seen advancements, particularly with the application of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) for acquiring complex in-hand reorientation skills. However, current methods often exhibit limitations when faced with objects possessing realistic textures, complex geometries, and varied appearances. A significant challenge lies in the perception-control gap, where rapid in-hand motions and severe self-occlusions hinder accurate object pose estimation from real-world sensors. Existing solutions often resort to alternative sensing modalities such as tactile arrays, depth cameras, or multi-camera setups, which introduce overheads, calibration complexities, or scalability limitations. Consequently, sim-to-real dexterous control has typically avoided primary reliance on monocular RGB camera observations, creating a robustness bottleneck.

A fundamental issue in RGB-based manipulation is the computational cost of generating photorealistic visual data for high-throughput reinforcement learning training within simulation. Traditional mesh-based rendering can be computationally intractable for generating sufficient visual diversity over extended training periods, often necessitating large compute clusters. Recent advancements in 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) offer real-time, photorealistic rendering superior to traditional mesh rasterization due to its compact and flexible scene representation. However, standard 3DGS is typically optimized for static scenes and inherently entangles illumination with geometry, precluding independent manipulation of lighting and material properties. This limitation prevents its direct application for effective Domain Randomization (DR), which is crucial for robust sim-to-real transfer.

This work addresses these challenges by integrating 3DGS into a sim-to-real framework for monocular RGB in-hand reorientation. It specifically tackles the limitations of standard 3DGS by performing domain randomization in the Gaussian representation space, enabling the generation of photorealistic and randomized visual data for object pose estimation without requiring computationally expensive ray tracing. By doing so, the research aims to bridge the visual sim-to-real gap and enable robust dexterous manipulation using a single monocular RGB camera, thus overcoming a key perception–robustness bottleneck in the current landscape.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and computationally efficient sim-to-real framework for monocular RGB-based in-hand reorientation of complex objects. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and addresses limitations in current robotic manipulation systems:

1. **Overcoming the Perception-Control Gap in Dexterous Manipulation:** In-hand reorientation demands precise estimation of object pose, a challenging task given the rapid motions, frequent self-occlusions by fingers, and motion blur encountered during manipulation. Monocular RGB sensing, while offering rich semantic cues, has been difficult to leverage robustly due to these perceptual challenges. Existing solutions that rely on multi-camera setups or other modalities (tactile, depth) introduce additional hardware and complexity. The motivation is to enable accurate and reliable pose estimation from a single, inexpensive monocular RGB camera.

2. **Bridging the Visual Sim-to-Real Gap with Computational Efficiency:** Achieving the photorealism required for robust sim-to-real transfer through standard mesh-based rendering is computationally expensive, especially for the high-throughput training cycles needed in reinforcement learning. This often necessitates significant computational resources, such as large compute clusters. The paper is motivated to find a computationally efficient rendering approach that can generate diverse, photorealistic visual data in simulation to reduce this gap.

3. **Extending 3D Gaussian Splatting for Dynamic Scene Randomization:** While 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) offers real-time, high-fidelity rendering, its conventional application is limited to static scenes, and it inherently intertwines illumination with geometry. This prevents independent manipulation of lighting and material properties, which are prerequisites for effective domain randomization. A key motivation is to devise strategies that overcome these limitations, enabling 3DGS to generate diverse, dynamic visual data essential for training robust perception models for dexterous manipulation tasks.

4. **Enabling Efficient and Accessible Training of Manipulation Policies:** Prior work in sim-to-real dexterous manipulation often relies on complex and computationally intensive Domain Randomization (DR) schemes, such as Automatic Domain Randomization (ADR), requiring significant compute resources. The researchers are motivated to simplify the reinforcement learning process and improve training efficiency, making it possible to learn complex manipulation behaviors on consumer-grade hardware.

In summary, the work aims to enable robust, monocular RGB-only dexterous manipulation by developing a computationally efficient sim-to-real pipeline that integrates and extends 3D Gaussian Splatting for realistic visual data generation and utilizes a structured approach to policy learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system for robust in-hand reorientation using monocular RGB vision is designed as a modular pipeline, separating the challenges of learning dexterous motor skills, inferring object state from partial observations, and overcoming the visual sim-to-real gap. The pipeline consists of four main phases: Teacher Training using Reinforcement Learning, Student Distillation, Visual Pose Estimator Training, and Deployment.

**A. Teacher Training using Reinforcement Learning**
The initial phase involves training a teacher policy in simulation with full access to the system state. The task is formulated as a goal-conditioned Markov Decision Process (MDP) for rotating an object to a target orientation, utilizing Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The policy commands 16-DoF Allegro Hand joint positions. A dense reward function incentivizes orientation alignment and provides a sparse success bonus, while penalizing aggressive actions, high joint velocities, and energy consumption. Episodes terminate if the object is dropped or if a goal is not achieved within a specified time.

Observations are categorized into:
- **Proprioceptive (O_prop):** Robot joint positions, action history, object goal, remaining episode time.
- **Exteroceptive (O_exte):** Object's current pose in hand frame, orientation relative to goal.
- **Privileged (O_priv):** Ground-truth state information available only to the teacher, including object velocity, fingertip contact forces, and randomized physical properties (e.g., mass, scale).

To improve sample efficiency and stabilize training, a performance-based curriculum is employed. This curriculum gradually increases task complexity based on the agent's average consecutive success count by:
1. Incrementally increasing regularization penalties.
2. Progressively increasing random action latency.
3. Gradually narrowing the allowed time window for consecutive successes.
Domain randomization is also applied to physical properties (mass, friction, restitution) and geometric properties of the robot and object, along with external disturbances. The policy uses a multi-encoder network with a deep MLP backbone and ELU activations.

**B. Student Training using Distillation**
The teacher policy, relying on privileged information, is distilled into a recurrent student policy for real-world deployment, where privileged signals are unavailable. The student receives noisy proprioceptive (o_noisy_prop) and noisy exteroceptive (o_noisy_exte) observations. A belief encoder-decoder network allows the student to implicitly infer the system state.

To simulate real-world sensor imperfections, a Perception Noise Generator corrupts simulated object pose observations with:
- Temporal downsampling for low frame rates.
- Stochastic jitter for variable latency.
- Systematic bias for calibration errors.
- Injected random poses for occasional tracking failures.
The belief encoder updates a latent belief state, which is then used by a belief decoder to reconstruct the teacher's observations, trained with a reconstruction loss. A control head outputs actions, supervised by a behavior cloning loss that minimizes the L2 distance between student and teacher actions. Training uses a composite loss function and an online DAgger variant to mitigate covariate shift.

**C. Visual Object Representation and Augmentations**
The student policy requires object pose estimates from RGB images, necessitating a robust perception model trained on diverse visual data. The system integrates 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) rasterization into the simulation loop to avoid the computational overhead of ray-tracing.

1. **3D Gaussian Object Representation:** Objects are represented by 3D Gaussians, each characterized by position, covariance, opacity, and spherical harmonic (SH) coefficients up to degree L=3 for view-dependent color.
2. **Simulation-Integrated GS Rendering:** For moving objects, the inverse of the object's transform is applied to the camera, rendering the scene as static to satisfy 3DGS assumptions. To account for occlusions from robot fingers, a low-fidelity depth map of the hand (D_phys) is generated from the physics simulator and used to mask out occluded pixels in the 3DGS-rendered RGB image.
3. **Pre-Rasterization Augmentations:** This is a core contribution, addressing the static scene and entangled illumination limitations of vanilla 3DGS. Instead of post-process image augmentations or computationally expensive scene parameter randomizations, perturbations are applied directly to the Gaussian attributes (specifically, SH coefficients) before rasterization. To maintain photometric consistency and generate plausible diversity, Gaussians are clustered based on geometric or photometric correlations, and perturbations are applied to these clusters as a group.
 - **Random Noise Group:** Independent perturbations to each Gaussian simulate high-frequency sensor noise.
 - **Spatial Cluster Group:** K-means clustering (64 clusters) by spatial location, with perturbations mimicking localized variations like shadows or surface damage.
 - **Color Cluster Group:** Clustering by 0th-order SH (32 clusters), with perturbations simulating material-specific shifts (albedo, reflectance).
 - **Global Shift Group:** Treating the entire scene as one cluster, with global shifts and scaling to SH coefficients, simulating macro-level environmental changes (ambient brightness, color temperature, exposure).
Each augmentation is applied stochastically, allowing different perturbations to compound and generate diverse scene appearances from a single static representation.

**D. Visual Object Pose Estimator Training**
A keypoint-based pose estimator is trained to recover the 6D object pose from RGB images, providing the exteroceptive input for the student policy. The training dataset is generated by rolling out the expert teacher policy in simulation, rendering images through the 3DGS pipeline with the pre-rasterization augmentations. Random ISO noise and motion blur are added to enhance robustness. The pose estimator uses a ResNet-34 backbone, predicting nine 2.5D keypoints (u, v, d) which are then resolved to a 6D pose using the Rigid Procrustes algorithm.

**E. Experimental Setup**
The system is deployed on a 16-DoF Allegro Hand with a wrist-mounted Intel RealSense D435i camera. All models operate on a single workstation with an Intel Core i9 CPU and NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada GPU, with a policy inference rate of 30 Hz. SAM2 is fine-tuned per object for real-time object masking. Five diverse objects (Cube, Globe, Tablet Bottle, 3D Printed Toy, Rubber Duck) are used, with high-fidelity meshes obtained via Polycam. Evaluations are conducted under nominal lighting (white distant light) and adversarial lighting (low-illumination point sources with dynamic hue shifts).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Pose Estimation using Different Rendering Pipelines**
The pose estimation pipeline was evaluated on real-world data under nominal and adversarial lighting conditions against three baselines: Standard Tiled Rendering, Domain Randomized (DR) Tiled Rendering, and Naïve Gaussian Splatting (GS). Evaluation metrics included Average Distance of Model Points (ADD) and a prediction accuracy metric (error < 10mm and < 10°).

- **Nominal Conditions:** The proposed method achieved a mean accuracy of 65.4% and mean ADD of 10.2 mm, outperforming Standard Tiled (53.3%) and DR Tiled (55.6%) baselines. Performance gains were particularly notable for geometrically and texturally complex objects (e.g., 3D Printed Toy, +24% improvement over randomized tiled rendering).
- **Adversarial Conditions:** Under challenging lighting, the proposed method demonstrated superior robustness with a mean accuracy of 56.3%, significantly higher than Randomized Tiled Rendering (47.2%). The Naïve GS baseline performed poorly (36.5% accuracy), indicating that high-fidelity rendering alone is insufficient without tailored randomization strategies to capture visual diversity under varying illumination.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The integrated 3DGS pipeline achieved a 1.6× faster rendering throughput and was substantially more memory-efficient (12 GB VRAM for 1,024 environments vs. 34 GB for tiled renderer). The pre-rasterization augmentations introduced negligible overhead (< 2 ms per batch, ≈ 4% of total frame rendering time).

**B. Effect of Pre-Rasterization Augmentations**
A "leave-one-out" ablation study was conducted to quantify the contribution of each pre-rasterization augmentation group.

- **Nominal Conditions:** The complete augmentation pipeline yielded the highest robustness. Removing Random Noise or Structured Clustering resulted in moderate performance dips (accuracy 57-59%). Critically, removing Global Shift augmentations caused a significant performance collapse (mean accuracy 51.2%, ADD error nearly doubling to 16.9 mm), particularly for reflective objects like the Tablet Bottle. This indicates the importance of simulating global environmental variations.
- **Adversarial Conditions:** Removing Random Noise resulted in a moderate drop (mean accuracy 48.6%). Omitting Spatial Clustering (42.5%) or Color Clustering (44.7%) led to significant degradation, confirming the importance of correlated perturbations for localized effects. The exclusion of Global Shift augmentations caused a catastrophic performance collapse (mean accuracy 23.6%), underscoring its role in generalizing to diverse real-world lighting.

**C. In-hand Reorientation Policy Learning**

- **Resource-Efficient Policy Training:** The teacher RL training converged in 26 hours on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 for simple geometries and approximately 90 hours on a dual-GPU setup for complex objects, consuming 30 GB VRAM. This represents an order-of-magnitude improvement in VRAM efficiency and reduced training time compared to prior work (e.g., DeXtreme requiring eight A40 GPUs over 60 hours). Student-teacher distillation completed in 16 hours on a single RTX 4090.
- **Impact of Performance-based Curriculum:** The curriculum components significantly accelerated convergence and achieved higher consecutive successes. Removing Action Latency or Time Window components slowed learning, while removing the Regularization Penalty curriculum resulted in complete failure. The curriculum also provided a self-regulating mechanism for reward balancing, eliminating the need for per-object tuning.

**D. Hardware Deployment**
The system was validated through zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on the Allegro Hand under nominal and adversarial lighting. Success was defined as orientation error within 0.4 radians, and consecutive successes (CS) were measured.

- **Comparison to Baselines:** Under nominal lighting, the system achieved a mean of 37.6 consecutive reorientations. It outperformed DeXtreme on the shared Cube object (35.4 CS vs. 27.8 CS). Replacing the custom pose estimator with FoundationPose resulted in near-total failure (0.4 CS), attributed to FoundationPose's lower operating frequency (4 Hz vs. ~18 Hz) and susceptibility to rapid motion and occlusions.
- **Object-Specific Performance:** Robust performance was observed across diverse geometries. The Globe achieved over 200 CS in one trial, and non-convex objects like the 3D Printed Toy and Rubber Duck obtained more than 20 CS on average. A sim-to-real gap was noted for the Tablet Bottle, attributed to unmodeled low surface friction.
- **Robustness to Adversarial Lighting:** Under severe adversarial lighting, the policy maintained an average of ~25 consecutive successes. This is reported as the first demonstration of sustained dexterous in-hand manipulation under such extreme visual perturbations. Performance decreased relative to nominal lighting, indicating a tight coupling between perception and control, where small perceptual errors can compound into significant control failures.

**E. Additional Results (from Appendix)**

- **Simulation Results:** The teacher policy consistently outperformed the student policy in simulation, even with noiseless observations. However, the student policy exhibited minimal degradation with noisy observations, demonstrating robustness against the noise model. A quantitative performance gap between simulation and hardware was observed due to inevitable physical disparities (e.g., contact models, actuator dynamics).
- **Belief State Analysis:** During real-world rollouts with artificial noise injection, the belief decoder effectively filtered high-amplitude noise and rejected outliers (e.g., a catastrophic 180° flip from the pose estimator), maintaining stable state estimates.
- **Pose Estimation During Rollouts:** During hardware deployment, the pose estimator achieved a mean translation error of 10.39 mm and a mean rotation error of 27.54°. The Pearson correlation coefficient between occlusion ratio and errors was low (0.20 for translation, 0.19 for rotation), suggesting robustness to partial occlusions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for robust in-hand reorientation that leverages monocular RGB vision, offering several contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotics:

1. **Addressing the Perception Bottleneck in Dexterous Manipulation:** The work highlights that a primary bottleneck in achieving real-world dexterity often resides in perceptual fidelity rather than control complexity. By demonstrating robust manipulation performance with physically grounded vision, the system shows that complex and precise tasks, traditionally reliant on proprioceptive or tactile sensing, can be effectively supported by monocular RGB feedback.
2. **Advancing Sim-to-Real Transfer for Visual Robotics:** The integration of 3D Gaussian Splatting directly into the simulation loop provides a computationally efficient pathway for generating high-fidelity visual data. The novel pre-rasterization augmentations introduce structured diversity into static Gaussian scenes, effectively bridging the visual sim-to-real gap by simulating diverse lighting and material conditions without the high cost of traditional ray tracing. This approach enhances the realism and generalizability of simulated training data.
3. **Enabling Accessible and Efficient Robot Learning:** The proposed pipeline allows for the training of both perception and control models on consumer-grade hardware (a single GPU). This significantly reduces the computational resources and time previously required for high-fidelity reinforcement learning in dexterous manipulation, making advanced robotic capabilities more accessible to a broader range of researchers and practitioners.
4. **Demonstrating Robustness to Challenging Real-World Conditions:** The system achieved sustained dexterous in-hand reorientation across diverse object geometries, even under severe adversarial lighting conditions. This demonstration of robustness under extreme visual perturbations is a notable step towards deploying robots in uncontrolled, dynamic real-world environments.
5. **Modular and Decomposed System Design:** By decomposing the complex problem into distinct phases (teacher training, student distillation, pose estimator training), the framework offers a principled approach to tackling partial observability and the sim-to-real gap, which can be beneficial for developing other complex robotic skills.

The findings suggest that enhancing visual perception with sufficient physical grounding can enable sophisticated manipulation tasks. Future directions, such as integrating dense visual feedback with high-frequency tactile sensing, could further improve robustness to unmodeled surface properties. Extending this instance-specific manipulation pipeline to broader generalization remains a key step toward general-purpose dexterous manipulation.
