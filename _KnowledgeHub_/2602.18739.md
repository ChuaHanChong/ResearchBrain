# When World Models Dream Wrong: Physical-Conditioned Adversarial Attacks against World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanyang Technological University and the University of Szeged developed PhysCond-WMA, an adversarial attack that subtly perturbs the physical condition inputs of generative world models, leading to semantic and logical distortions in generated videos while maintaining visual fidelity. The attack degraded 3D object detection mean average precision by up to 1.3% and increased open-loop planning collision rates by 26.7%, revealing critical vulnerabilities in autonomous driving systems.

## Problem

- Generative World Models (WMs) for autonomous driving rely on specific physical priors like HDMaps and 3D-box features, which expose uninvestigated attack surfaces.
- Existing adversarial attack research primarily focuses on direct image/text manipulations or general diffusion models, not the unique physical conditioning of WMs.
- Even minor perturbations to physical conditions could cascade through the generative process, leading to physically implausible or semantically incorrect outputs that bias downstream decision-making.

## Method

- Introduced PhysCond-WMA, a white-box, inference-time adversarial attack that perturbs physical-condition channels (HDMap embeddings, 3D-box features) of generative world models.
- Employed a two-stage optimization: a 'quality-preserving guidance stage' to maintain perceptual fidelity, followed by a 'momentum-guided denoising optimization stage' to steer the generation towards target semantics.
- The attack guides the model's reverse ==diffusion== process using an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of target-directed gradients, ensuring stable semantic shifts while preserving visual and temporal coherence.

## Results

- PhysCond-WMA achieved human-evaluated attack success rates up to 0.55 on DriveDreamer2, while maintaining high perceptual quality with average Fréchet Inception Distance increases of **9%** and Fréchet Video Distance increases of **3.9%**.
- The attack significantly impacted downstream tasks, decreasing 3D object detection mean average precision by up to **1.3%** and increasing open-loop planning collision rates by **26.7%** (from **0.15%** to **0.19%**).
- Jointly perturbing HDMap and 3D Box conditions yielded substantially higher attack success rates compared to attacking single channels, demonstrating the necessity of comprehensive manipulation for effective semantic shifts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative world models for autonomous driving are vulnerable to subtle, white-box adversarial perturbations in their physical conditioning inputs, leading to significant semantic and logical distortions.
> - Maintaining perceptual fidelity is crucial for the attack's effectiveness, achieved by carefully guiding the reverse-diffusion trajectory to remain on the data manifold.
> - Stronger generative models, with robust conditional coupling, appear more susceptible to PhysCond-WMA, as they more effectively propagate and accumulate subtle physical condition perturbations into coherent, cross-frame semantic shifts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generative World Models (WMs) have emerged as a significant advancement in artificial intelligence, particularly in their ability to learn compact, structured representations of environments and predict future observations. This capability is leveraged across various applications, including sample-efficient policy learning, simulation-driven evaluation, and long-horizon reasoning in fields such as robotics and autonomous driving. Recent progress in generative WMs has enabled the synthesis of temporally coherent, sensor-conditional videos of complex urban scenes, conditioned on diverse inputs like high-definition maps (HDMaps), planned trajectories, or sparse multimodal sensor data. These models thus function as controllable simulators, offering a scalable method for generating corner cases and extensive scenarios, which can reduce the reliance on expensive real-world data collection and facilitate new avenues for closed-loop testing in autonomous driving research and validation.

However, the increasing sophistication and reliance on these models also introduce new security vulnerabilities. The performance of these generative WMs is critically dependent on learned latent dynamics and the quality of conditional inputs. Existing research has explored adversarial attacks against deep learning models, including those based on diffusion models, which form the core structure of many generative WMs. These prior works, such as AdvDiff and AdvDiffVLM, focus on generating adversarial examples by manipulating the diffusion sampling process to induce changes in generated images or to create targeted, transferable attacks for vision-language models. Yet, a specific examination of adversarial attacks directly targeting the "physical-condition channels" of generative world models, especially in the context of autonomous driving, has been largely unexplored.

This paper addresses this gap by investigating how subtle, structured perturbations in physical conditions—such as HDMap shifts or mislabeled 3D-box features—can be amplified through the generative pipeline, leading to physically implausible or semantically incorrect outcomes that propagate over time. Such distortions in generated data could potentially bias downstream planning systems, misguiding decision-making in safety-critical applications like autonomous driving. The work positions itself as the first to systematically study and quantify these security risks in generative world models, aiming to identify novel attack surfaces and subsequently promote the development of more comprehensive security evaluation frameworks for these advanced systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this research stems from the increasing integration of generative world models (WMs) into critical applications, particularly autonomous driving, where their ability to synthesize controllable, sensor-conditioned videos is highly valued. The paper highlights that these models, while powerful, inherently rely on specific physical priors, such as HDMap embeddings and 3D-box features, to ensure realism and temporal coherence in their generated outputs. This reliance, however, inadvertently exposes novel attack surfaces that have not been thoroughly investigated.

The core motivation is the understanding that even minor, structured perturbations to these physical-condition channels can have significant, cascading effects. Such perturbations could be amplified through the generative diffusion pipeline, propagating over time and leading to outputs that are physically implausible (e.g., object hallucination or disappearance, violations of causality). When these flawed generated videos are used in downstream tasks, such as training perception modules or evaluating planning algorithms, they can critically bias cost estimations, underestimate collision risks, or induce excessively conservative behaviors, ultimately misguiding the decision-making processes of autonomous systems. Existing adversarial attack methodologies primarily focus on direct image or text manipulations or general diffusion models, without specifically considering the unique structure and physical conditioning of generative world models in autonomous driving contexts.

Given this context, the key objectives of this work are articulated as follows:

1. **To Propose a Novel Adversarial Attack:** The primary objective is to introduce Physical-Conditioned World Model Attack (PhysCond-WMA), a white-box adversarial attack specifically designed for generative world models. This attack aims to manipulate the model's diffusion process by perturbing its physical-condition channels (e.g., HDMap embeddings, 3D-box features).
2. **To Induce Semantic, Logic, or Decision-Level Distortion with Perceptual Fidelity:** A crucial goal is to ensure that the induced distortions are significant at semantic, logical, or decision-making levels (e.g., changes in object identity, illogical object behavior, or incorrect traffic light states) while maintaining high perceptual quality and temporal coherence in the generated videos. This stealthiness is critical for the attack's effectiveness and real-world implications.
3. **To Overcome Key Technical Challenges:** The authors identify two main technical challenges in achieving the above objectives:
 - Directly optimizing HDMap and 3D-box conditions can easily push the reverse-diffusion trajectory off the data manifold, resulting in noticeable visual artifacts and degraded output quality (large FID/FVD). The attack needs to remain stealthy.
 - Even when perceptual fidelity is maintained, the denoising update is predominantly governed by the model's clean prediction. Naive injection of target gradients might be too weak to induce semantic changes or too strong, thereby breaking temporal consistency. The attack must effectively guide semantic shifts without compromising temporal coherence.
4. **To Quantify Security Vulnerabilities:** The research aims to be the first to systematically reveal and quantify the security vulnerabilities inherent in generative world models for autonomous driving. This includes evaluating the attack's success rate and its tangible impact on downstream tasks like 3D object detection and open-loop planning.
5. **To Drive Comprehensive Security Checkers:** Ultimately, the work seeks to raise awareness of these newly identified vulnerabilities, motivating the development of more robust and comprehensive security evaluation frameworks for generative world models, extending beyond simple fidelity metrics to include perceptual, temporal, physical, and policy compliance dimensions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces Physical-Conditioned World Model Attack (PhysCond-WMA), a white-box, inference-time adversarial attack designed to perturb the physical-condition channels of generative world models. The attacker is assumed to have access to the victim model's pretrained weights and gradient information but is not allowed to modify model parameters through training or fine-tuning.

**Problem Definition:**
A generative world model `WM` produces a video `V = {x_m}` based on physical priors `R` (HDMaps, 3D bounding boxes, BEV representations) and input multimodal conditions `C` (text, images, videos). The attack aims to create a subtle perturbation `δ` to the physical conditions `R`, resulting in an attacked video `V_att = WM(R + δ, C*)`, where `C*` represents targeted attack conditions. The objective is to change video semantics (directionally or non-directionally) while preserving the perceptual quality of the generated results.
The optimization problem for the attacker is defined as minimizing a loss function:
`δ(t) = arg min_δ [λ_R ||δ||^2_2 + λ_L E_t [L_diff(x_t, x_att_t)] - λ_tar A(x_att_t, C*)]`
Here, `λ_R`, `λ_L`, and `λ_tar` are scaling factors controlling perturbation magnitude, perceptual fidelity preservation (using `L_diff`, the standard diffusion model loss), and semantic alignment with the target `C*` (using `A`).

**Two-Stage Optimization:**
PhysCond-WMA employs a two-stage optimization process to address the challenges of maintaining perceptual fidelity while inducing semantic shifts.

1. **Quality-preserving Guidance Stage:**
 - **Goal:** To gently guide the reverse denoising trajectory away from the clean generation flow, ensuring perceptual fidelity is maintained. Directly injecting large perturbations can lead to outputs that drift off the data manifold, causing noticeable artifacts.
 - **Mechanism:** During the reverse diffusion steps, a small corrective perturbation is added to the noise prediction `ϵ_θ(x_t)` in Equation (2). Specifically, the estimated noise `˜ϵ_t` is modified as: `˜ϵ_t = ϵ_t(x_t, t) + α_A · ∇_{x_t} L_diff(x_t, x_att_t)`. Here, `α_A` is a scale factor for the gradient of the diffusion loss. This perturbation aims to slightly reduce the diffusion loss, pushing the trajectory in a controlled manner.
 - **Termination Criterion:** This stage is terminated at the first diffusion step `Δ` where the diffusion loss `L_diff(x_t; x_att_t)` falls below a calibrated threshold `τ`. This ensures that the generated state remains sufficiently close to the data manifold, preventing severe quality degradation. The authors determined `τ = 0.15` through ablation studies.

2. **Momentum-Guided Denoising Optimization Stage:**
 - **Goal:** Once the trajectory is within the quality-preserving region (i.e., `L_diff < τ`), this stage aims to effectively steer the denoising process towards the desired target semantics. The challenge is that the model's intrinsic denoising `ϵ(x_t)` still dominates updates, potentially diluting target-aligned gradients or causing instability.
 - **Mechanism:** An Exponential Moving Average (EMA) mechanism is introduced to accumulate target-directed gradients, creating a smooth and coherent semantic offset trajectory. The modified noise prediction `˜ϵ_t` in this stage is: `˜ϵ_t = λϵ_t + (1 − λ)[∇_{x_t} A(x_att_t, C*) − β · ∇_{x_t} L_diff(x_t, x_att_t)]`.
 - `λ` is the momentum factor (set to `0.9`), ensuring stability by weighting the model's natural prediction `ϵ_t` with the adversarial guidance.
 - `A(x_att_t, C*)` is a semantic alignment model (SSCD in this paper) used to quantify the semantic consistency between the denoised result `x_att_t` and the target `C*`. The gradient `∇_{x_t} A` pushes towards the target.
 - `β` (set to `0.6`) controls the regularization term `∇_{x_t} L_diff`, which helps prevent excessive quality degradation while aligning with the target.
 - For **untargeted attacks**, the `∇_{x_t} A(x_att_t, C*)` term is omitted, and the perturbation primarily focuses on diverging from the clean manifold: `˜ϵ_t = λϵ_t + (1 − λ) ∇_{x_t} L_diff(x_t, x_att_t)`.
 - **Rationale:** EMA helps in suppressing short-term fluctuations, ensuring stable semantic transitions and preserving visual quality and spatio-temporal coherence in the final video.

**Attack Pipeline and Implementation Details:**
The PhysCond-WMA framework operates in the latent video space, conditioned on physical priors (HDMap, 3D-box) and input target contexts (images, text, multimodal prompts). The clean reverse-diffusion path is duplicated into an adversarial branch. Stage 1 constrains the diffusion loss, while Stage 2 injects momentum-guided, target-aligned gradients. The framework outputs distortions at semantic, logical, and decision levels. The diffusion process involves 25 steps. Target images are generated using an SDXL-inpainting 1.0 model to insert objects or modify scenes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted using the nuScenes dataset, comprising 850 driving videos. The victim models evaluated were DriveDreamer and DriveDreamer2, prominent generative world models for autonomous driving. Evaluation metrics included Fréchet Inception Distance (FID) and Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) for perceptual quality, and a novel Attack Success Rate (ASR) metric. ASR was determined by both GPT-5 and human evaluators across semantic, logical, and decision levels of distortion, where a score `S > 0.5` indicated a successful attack. Downstream impacts were assessed on 3D object detection (using FCOS3D) and open-loop planning (using ST-P3 and VAD).

**Attack Performance:**
Table 1 demonstrates PhysCond-WMA's attack performance on both DriveDreamer and DriveDreamer2.
- **Perceptual Quality:** The attack induces a relatively minor degradation in perceptual quality. For DriveDreamer2, untargeted attacks increased FID by 3.2 and FVD by 2.4, while targeted attacks increased FID by 2.1 and FVD by 0.8. On average, FID increased by approximately 9% and FVD by 3.9%, suggesting the attack largely preserved visual fidelity.
- **Attack Success Rate (ASR):**
 - Human evaluators consistently reported higher ASR than GPT-5. For DriveDreamer2, targeted attacks achieved a Human ASR of 0.55, compared to GPT-5 ASR of 0.30. This disparity is attributed to the attack's stealthiness: single frames often appear perceptually normal, but the video-level semantics or temporal coherence are altered (e.g., an oncoming bus changing into a truck). Human raters, capable of detecting cross-frame semantic and causal violations, were more effective at identifying these "visually normal but behaviorally abnormal" samples, whereas GPT-5, potentially biased towards appearance cues, under-reported successes.
 - Targeted attacks demonstrated higher ASR compared to untargeted attacks, with an approximate 60% improvement in ASR, indicating the method’s ability to guide the reverse diffusion toward specific semantic targets.
 - Stronger generative models, such as DriveDreamer2, exhibited higher Human ASR (28% increase for untargeted, 34.1% for targeted over DriveDreamer). This suggests that in models with robust conditional coupling and temporal modeling, small physical condition perturbations are more stably propagated and accumulated into coherent, cross-frame semantic shifts, enhancing attack effectiveness.

**Downstream Impact:**
The study quantified the tangible risks introduced by PhysCond-WMA:
- **3D Object Detection:** When 800 frames from attacked videos were used to augment the training set for 3D detection, mean Average Precision (mAP) decreased by 1.1% (DriveDreamer) and 1.3% (DriveDreamer2) compared to using clean synthetic data. The nuScenes Detection Score (NDS) also decreased by 0.7% and 1.9%, respectively. This indicates that despite perceptual similarity, the semantic and physical inconsistencies introduced by PhysCond-WMA contaminate the training distribution, degrading detection performance.
- **Open-loop Planning:** Using attacked videos for open-loop planning (on DriveDreamer) increased the L2 displacement error (L2 Avg.) from 0.29m to 0.33m (a 13.8% relative increase). The collision rate increased from 0.15% to 0.19% (a 26.7% relative increase). This demonstrates that the attack can perturb trajectories and worsen collision rates, directly impacting critical downstream decision-making.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Loss Function Threshold (τ):** Varying `τ` for the quality-preserving stage showed `τ = 0.15` as optimal, balancing perceptual quality (FVD) and attack success (ASR). Too low a `τ` hindered target convergence, while too high a `τ` degraded image quality.
- **Effect of Two Stages:** Both stages were found to be complementary and indispensable. Using only Stage 1 (quality-preserving) resulted in low ASR (87.5% reduction for untargeted, 94.5% for targeted) because perturbations attenuated without explicit semantic guidance. Using only Stage 2 (momentum-guided) led to higher FVD (worse quality) and still substantially lower ASR, underscoring Stage 1's role in stabilizing the trajectory and preserving quality, which is crucial for Stage 2's effectiveness.
- **Physical Conditions:** Jointly perturbing both HDMap and 3D Box conditions yielded significantly higher ASR compared to attacking only one channel (e.g., 52.4% and 28.0% increase in untargeted ASR over HDMap-only and 3D Box-only, respectively). This is because HDMap constrains road topology, and 3D Box anchors objects, and attacking both concurrently allows for more comprehensive semantic shifts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant contribution to the understanding of security vulnerabilities in generative artificial intelligence, particularly in the context of autonomous driving. By introducing PhysCond-WMA, the paper systematically identifies and quantifies, for the first time, security risks in generative world models when their physical-condition channels are perturbed.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that even subtle, white-box manipulations of input conditions—such as HDMap embeddings and 3D-box features—can induce temporally coherent semantic, logical, or decision-level distortions in generated videos, while perceptually preserving visual fidelity. This stealthy nature of the attack is crucial because it highlights a vulnerability that may not be detectable by conventional quality metrics or short-horizon automated evaluators. The finding that human evaluators identify a higher attack success rate than advanced language models (GPT-5) underscores this point, suggesting that current automated screening methods may be insufficient for detecting such covert attacks in large-scale pipelines.

The potential impact of these findings is substantial, particularly for safety-critical applications like autonomous driving.
- **Security for Autonomous Systems:** The work exposes a critical attack surface within the perception and planning pipelines of autonomous vehicles. If adversarial actors can manipulate the synthetic data used for training or validation, they could introduce biases that lead to misjudgments, increased collision risks, or overly conservative behaviors in real-world driving scenarios. This necessitates a re-evaluation of the robustness and security of generative models used in these systems.
- **Development of Robust Generative Models:** The identified vulnerabilities provide concrete directions for enhancing the resilience of generative world models. Future research can focus on developing defense mechanisms that make these models less susceptible to perturbations in physical condition inputs, potentially through robust training, input sanitization, or anomaly detection.
- **Advanced Evaluation Frameworks:** The study advocates for a shift towards more comprehensive safety checkers. It emphasizes that evaluating generated data solely based on fidelity metrics (like FID/FVD) is insufficient. Future evaluation frameworks should incorporate perceptual, temporal, physics, and policy compliance dimensions to thoroughly assess the trustworthiness and safety of synthetic data and the models that produce it. This could involve developing new metrics or integrating human-in-the-loop validation more systematically.
- **Broader AI Security Implications:** Beyond autonomous driving, the principles demonstrated by PhysCond-WMA may extend to other domains where generative models are conditioned on structured, domain-specific priors (e.g., medical imaging, architectural design, robotics). The findings could encourage a broader examination of how adversarial perturbations in conditioning inputs can lead to semantically meaningful but perceptually subtle errors across various AI applications.

The paper acknowledges certain limitations, such as the current focus on image targets as input conditions, with multimodal targets (video and text) requiring further exploration. Additionally, the ASR evaluation methodology, while novel, is presented as a valuable step rather than a complete standard for world model safety. These limitations indicate avenues for future research to further generalize and refine the understanding of adversarial attacks against generative world models.
