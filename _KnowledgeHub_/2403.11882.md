# ReGenNet: Towards Human Action-Reaction Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> ReGenNet is a diffusion-based generative model that synthesizes a human reaction conditioned on another person's ongoing action. A Transformer decoder with a directional attention mask enables online, auto-regressive generation without future frames, while an explicit distance-based interaction loss constrains relative pose, orientation and translation. On three newly actor-reactor-annotated datasets it cuts FID sharply versus motion-diffusion baselines at sub-millisecond per-frame latency.

## Problem

- Prior human-centric generative models condition on static scenes or objects, so they cannot handle a dynamic human partner whose motion changes spatially and temporally throughout the interaction.
- Existing multi-person motion synthesis treats both people symmetrically or targets narrow action categories, ignoring the causal actor-reactor asymmetry of everyday atomic interactions.
- No interaction dataset annotates actor-reactor order, and sparse skeleton or body-only representations discard hand gestures needed for fine-grained interactions; concurrent reaction work stays offline.

## Method

- NTU120-AS, InterHuman-AS and Chi3D-AS: three datasets re-annotated with actor-reactor order, with NTU120 lifted to ==SMPL-X== by pose estimation so hands and face are represented.
- **ReGenNet**, a conditional motion diffusion model whose denoiser stacks eight Transformer decoder units; a ==directional attention mask== blocks future actor frames, enabling auto-regressive online reaction synthesis.
- An explicit ==distance-based interaction loss== supervises relative joint positions, orientation rotation matrices and root translation between actor and reactor; actor features fuse by concatenation, intention label optional.

## Results

- On NTU120-AS online unconstrained, test-conditioned FID drops to **11.00** versus **24.25** for MDM-GRU and **44.94** for AGRoL; Chi3D-AS train-conditioned FID falls from **4.96** to **0.27**.
- On InterHuman-AS it reaches **2.265** FID and **0.407** Top-3 R-Precision, beating RAIG at **2.915** and InterGen at **13.237**; cross-camera transfer gives **8.16** FID versus **25.57**.
- Ablations: removing the interaction loss raises train FID from **0.90** to **1.96**; shuffling actor-reactor labels drops accuracy from **0.749** to **0.656**; **5** DDIM steps cost **0.76 ms** per frame.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Role asymmetry is a usable supervision signal: labelling who acts and who reacts beats symmetric or shuffled training, so annotate causal order rather than assume it.
> - Causal masking in a Transformer decoder converts an offline diffusion motion model into a real-time reactive one, a cheap architectural swap rather than a new objective.
> - Distance-based interaction losses borrowed from human-scene synthesis transfer to human-human settings, halving train-conditioned FID, so relative geometry can be supervised directly rather than learned implicitly.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human-centric generative models has seen substantial progress, primarily focusing on synthesizing human interactions with *static* scenes and objects. This includes generating motions for humans navigating environments [26, 27, 71, 81–83] or manipulating objects. While these areas are well-explored, the domain of *dynamic human-human interaction synthesis*, particularly focusing on action-reaction dynamics, has remained relatively underdeveloped.

Existing works on multi-person motion generation often treat all individuals equally or limit their scope to specific interaction types (e.g., martial arts). They frequently utilize sparse skeleton joints or simplified SMPL body models, which inherently restrict the fidelity and detail of intricate interactions involving hands, faces, and subtle body language. Furthermore, the critical aspects of asymmetry (actor vs. reactor roles), synchronicity (online generation), and fine-grained detail have largely been overlooked. Critically, there has been a notable absence of dedicated human-human interaction datasets annotated with explicit actor-reactor orders.

This paper directly addresses these gaps, positioning itself at the forefront of a nascent yet crucial sub-field: **causal human action-reaction synthesis**. It builds upon the recent success of diffusion models, which have emerged as powerful generative models for human motion synthesis, by adapting and extending them to the complex interpersonal domain. The use of Transformer architectures, already proven effective in sequential data modeling, further aligns with current state-of-the-art practices in deep learning.

ReGenNet distinguishes itself from concurrent works, such as Interaction Transformer and RAIG, by emphasizing richer SMPL-X representations (including hands and faces) and specifically tackling the "online" and "unconstrained" settings (instantaneous reactions without prior knowledge of the actor's intention). By introducing the first multi-setting benchmark and annotated datasets, this work aims to standardize and accelerate research in this complex area, moving beyond mere co-presence to model the nuanced causal dynamics of human interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to advance human-centric generative models by developing a robust framework for **human action-reaction synthesis**, specifically generating realistic human reactions conditioned on the actions of another individual. This task holds significant implications for various interactive technologies.

The core motivations stem from the inherent complexity and ubiquity of human-human interactions, which present distinct challenges compared to human-environment interactions:

1. **Asymmetry:** Human-human interactions are fundamentally causal and asymmetric. One person typically acts, and the other reacts. Existing models often treat all interacting parties symmetrically, failing to capture this causal flow. The authors aim to explicitly model and leverage this actor-reactor distinction.
2. **Dynamic Nature:** Interactions are rarely static. They involve continuous, spatiotemporal changes in body parts, relative positions, and orientations. The model must capture this fluid, dynamic interplay.
3. **Synchronicity (Online Generation):** In real-world interactions, reactions are often instantaneous. This necessitates an "online" generative model that can predict a reaction in real-time without foreknowledge of the actor's future movements. This is a crucial practical requirement for applications like AR/VR or human-robot interaction.
4. **Detailed Representation:** Interactions involve more than just coarse body movements. Fine-grained details such as hand gestures, jaw movements, and even facial expressions contribute significantly to the plausibility and realism of an interaction. The authors aim to incorporate these details into their synthesis.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the paper are:

- **To propose the first comprehensive multi-setting human action-reaction synthesis benchmark:** This involves defining the problem rigorously and establishing evaluation criteria.
- **To create dedicated annotated datasets:** Given the lack of existing datasets with explicit actor-reactor order, the authors undertake the crucial task of annotating the NTU120, InterHuman, and Chi3D datasets, extending NTU120 to SMPL-X representation to capture fine details.
- **To develop ReGenNet, a novel diffusion-based generative model:** This model is designed to synthesize physically and semantically plausible human reactions, specifically addressing the asymmetric, dynamic, synchronous, and detailed characteristics identified.
- **To enable online, intention-agnostic generation:** A key functional requirement is the ability to generate reactions instantly, even when the actor's full motion sequence or underlying intention is unknown to the reactor.
- **To demonstrate generalization capabilities:** The model should be able to generate plausible reactions for unseen actor motions and be robust to environmental changes like viewpoint variations.
- **To ensure modularity and flexibility:** ReGenNet should be adaptable to different interaction scenarios, such as offline generation or scenarios where actor intention is known.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of ReGenNet is grounded in a robust data representation, novel dataset annotation, and a sophisticated generative model architecture combining diffusion models with Transformer decoders, augmented by a specialized interaction loss.

**Data Representation:** The authors adopt the **SMPL-X body model** for representing human motion sequences. This choice is critical as SMPL-X provides a comprehensive parametric model that includes not only body pose and shape but also detailed articulated hand poses, jaw, and eyeballs (K=54 joints). This allows for capturing the "detailed" nature of human-human interactions, crucial for realism in gestures and subtle expressions. A motion sequence for a person is represented as `[θ, q, γ]`, encompassing pose parameters, global orientation, and root translation.

**Dataset Formulation and Annotation:** Recognizing the absence of asymmetry annotations in existing multi-person interaction datasets, the authors meticulously curate three new datasets: **NTU120-AS, InterHuman-AS, and Chi3D-AS**.
- **NTU120-AS:** The large-scale NTU120 dataset is extended to SMPL-X representation using the PyMAF-X pose estimation method and then annotated for actor-reactor order.
- **InterHuman-AS:** The InterHuman dataset (largest multi-human interaction dataset) is annotated for asymmetry.
- **Chi3D-AS:** The Chi3D dataset (with ground-truth SMPL-X) is also annotated.
The annotation process involves manually labeling the actor-reactor order for each interaction sequence, often requiring double-checking, ensuring the causal relationship is correctly identified.

**Generative Model: ReGenNet Architecture:** ReGenNet is built upon a **diffusion model framework** known for its high-quality synthesis capabilities.
- **Forward Diffusion:** A standard Markov chain-based process progressively adds Gaussian noise to a clean reaction sequence `x_0` to produce a noised version `x_t` over `T` timesteps (Eq. 1).
- **Reverse Denoising Process:** A neural network `F` (the core of ReGenNet) learns to reverse this process, predicting the clean body poses `x_0` from a noised `x_t` conditioned on the actor's action `y` and other signals.
- **Transformer Decoder Backbone:** The network `F` is implemented by stacking `ℓ_dec` **Transformer decoder units**. This is a deliberate choice for "online" generation. Unlike Transformer encoders which can access all input tokens, the decoder's masked multi-head attention mechanism inherently prevents it from "seeing" future information of the reaction sequence. This allows for auto-regressive inference, where the reaction at time `t` is generated based only on past and current observations, satisfying the synchronous requirement.
- **Conditioning Scheme:** Actor motion `y` and noised reactor motion `x_t` are linearly projected, concatenated, and fused to form input tokens `z_t`. The noising timestep `t` and an optional action label `a` (for constrained settings) are also projected and summed to provide conditional information.

**Explicit Interaction Loss (L_inter):** A novel and crucial component of ReGenNet is `L_inter`, designed to explicitly model the dynamic human-human interaction. Inspired by distance-based representations in human-scene/object interaction models, `L_inter` quantifies the relative spatiotemporal relationships between the predicted reactor and the actor. It measures the L2 distance between the ground-truth and predicted relative values for:
- **Joint Positions:** Calculated using forward kinematics (`FK`) to convert rotation poses into joint positions.
- **Global Orientations:** Computed using rotation matrices (`RM`).
- **Root Translations:** Simple difference in root positions.
This loss encourages the generated reactions to maintain physically and semantically plausible spatial and orientational relationships with the actor.

**Overall Training Objective:** The total training loss `L_all` combines the standard diffusion model loss `L_dm` (mean squared error between true and predicted `x_0`) with the explicit interaction loss `L_inter`, weighted by `λ_inter`.

**Customization Support:** ReGenNet is designed to be modular and flexible. The intention branch (action label `a`) can be enabled or disabled for constrained vs. unconstrained settings, and the directional attention mask can be removed for offline (non-real-time) generation, demonstrating the model's adaptability to various scenarios. During inference, DDIM sampling is used to accelerate the denoising process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments comprehensively evaluate ReGenNet's performance against state-of-the-art baselines across multiple datasets and settings, primarily focusing on the challenging **online, unconstrained setting** (instant reaction generation without knowing actor's future or intention).

**Comparison to State-of-the-art Baselines:**
- ReGenNet consistently **outperforms baselines** (cVAE, AGRoL, MDM, MDM-GRU, T2M, RAIG, InterGen) across various quantitative metrics on NTU120-AS, Chi3D-AS, and InterHuman-AS datasets.
- **FID (Frechet Inception Distance):** ReGenNet achieves significantly lower FID scores, often by a large margin (e.g., 0.90 vs. 5.31 for MDM-GRU on NTU120-AS train-conditioned), indicating that its generated reactions are closer to the distribution of real human reactions. This is a crucial metric for evaluating generation quality.
- **Action Recognition Accuracy:** The model often achieves perfect or near-perfect accuracy (1.000) for train-conditioned settings, demonstrating its ability to learn the correct action patterns, and competitive accuracy for test-conditioned settings.
- **Diversity and Multi-modality:** ReGenNet also shows strong performance in generating diverse and multi-modal reactions, indicating its capability to produce varied and realistic responses for a given action, closely matching real data distributions.
- **Generalization to Unseen Motions:** For test-conditioned settings (actor motions not seen during training), ReGenNet maintains superior FID and multi-modality, affirming its strong generalization capability to novel actor movements.
- **InterHuman-AS (Text-Conditioned):** On this dataset, ReGenNet also yields the best R Precision (0.407 vs. 0.374 for InterGen), FID, and MM Dist, demonstrating its effectiveness in a text-conditioned interaction synthesis scenario.

**Generalization Experiments (Viewpoint Changes):**
- When trained on one camera view (camera 1 of NTU120-AS) and tested on another (camera 2), ReGenNet exhibits **robustness to viewpoint changes**, achieving the best FID, action recognition accuracy, and multi-modality among baselines (e.g., 8.16 FID vs. 25.57 for MDM-GRU).

**Ablation Studies:** These studies confirm the effectiveness of ReGenNet's design choices:
- **Conditioning Scheme:** Concatenating actor and reactor features proved more effective than simply adding them, yielding better FID and accuracy.
- **Positional Embedding (PE):** Including positional embedding was found to be beneficial, resulting in lower FID scores.
- **Explicit Interaction Loss (`L_inter`):** Removing `L_inter` led to inferior performance (higher FID), demonstrating its importance in enhancing reaction quality by explicitly modeling relative dynamics.
- **Number of Decoder Layers (`ℓ_dec`):** Eight decoder layers were identified as the optimal configuration, balancing performance and complexity.
- **DDIM Sampling Timesteps:** While `T=1000` is used for training, experiments showed that just **5 DDIM sampling steps** achieved the best FID score with significantly **low latency (0.76 ms)** per frame, making real-time applications feasible.
- **Necessity of Actor-Reactor Annotations:** Randomly shuffling actor-reactor roles for an "unsupervised" approach resulted in inferior performance, empirically validating the necessity of explicit asymmetry annotations.

**Extension to Other Settings:**
- **Offline Setting:** By replacing the Transformer decoder with an encoder (disabling the attention mask), ReGenNet successfully adapted to the offline scenario, achieving superior performance on most metrics.
- **Constrained Setting:** When provided with the actor's intention (action labels), ReGenNet's performance further improved significantly (e.g., 5.89 FID for test-conditioned vs. 11.00 in unconstrained), as expected, highlighting the value of explicit conditioning.

**Qualitative Evaluation:**
- Visualizations (e.g., Fig. 3) showcase that ReGenNet synthesizes visually plausible reactions, including appropriate relative head orientations, realistic positional changes (e.g., stepping back from a hit), accurate body part movements, and detailed hand gestures (e.g., for handshaking or finger-guessing). The generated actions demonstrate semantic reasonableness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "ReGenNet: Towards Human Action-Reaction Synthesis" paper marks a significant advancement in the field of human-centric generative AI, addressing a critical and previously underexplored area. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**1. Pioneering a New Research Frontier:**
This work is the first to comprehensively analyze and model the asymmetric, dynamic, synchronous, and detailed characteristics inherent in human-human interactions. By formally defining the task of "human action-reaction synthesis," it opens up a new, highly relevant research avenue beyond static scene or object interactions.

**2. Crucial Dataset Contribution:**
The creation and public release of dedicated, actor-reactor annotated datasets (NTU120-AS, InterHuman-AS, Chi3D-AS) is a monumental contribution. This addresses a fundamental bottleneck in research, providing the necessary ground truth for training and evaluating models that understand and generate causal human interaction. The extension of NTU120 to SMPL-X is particularly valuable for detailed motion analysis.

**3. State-of-the-Art Methodological Innovation:**
ReGenNet's architecture, combining a diffusion model with a Transformer decoder, represents a sophisticated technical solution. The use of the Transformer decoder with masked attention is ingenious for enabling online, synchronous generation, a key requirement for real-time applications. The novel explicit distance-based interaction loss (`L_inter`) is a significant innovation, effectively guiding the model to learn physically plausible relative movements between interacting individuals. This methodology sets a new benchmark for quality and realism in generated human interactions.

**4. Enabling Real-World Interactive Applications:**
The ability to generate instant and plausible human reactions, even without prior knowledge of an actor's intentions, has profound implications for a wide range of applications:
- **AR/VR:** Enhancing immersion by enabling virtual characters (NPCs) to react naturally and dynamically to user movements.
- **Gaming:** Creating more believable and interactive game environments with NPCs that respond organically to player actions.
- **Human-Robot Interaction:** Equipping robots with the capacity to anticipate and react appropriately to human gestures and movements, fostering more intuitive and safer collaborations.
- **Embodied AI:** Advancing the development of AI agents that can physically interact with humans in a natural and responsive manner.

**5. Foundational for Future Research:**
The paper not only provides solutions but also clearly articulates current limitations, such as focusing on atomic interaction periods, dataset quality issues (noise, lack of natural facial expressions), and the need for longer-duration interaction modeling. This transparency effectively guides future research directions, inviting the community to build upon ReGenNet's benchmark, datasets, and modular framework to tackle even more complex, multi-modal, and long-term human-human interactions.

**6. Promotion of Open Science:**
The commitment to making the benchmark, annotated data, models, and code publicly available will significantly accelerate research and foster collaboration within the computer vision, graphics, and AI communities.

In conclusion, ReGenNet represents a significant leap forward in understanding and synthesizing the intricate dynamics of human-human interaction. By providing both a novel methodological framework and essential annotated datasets, this work lays a strong foundation for a future where digital humans can react with unprecedented realism and spontaneity, profoundly impacting interactive technologies.
