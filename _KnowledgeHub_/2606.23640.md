# Learning Process Rewards via Success Visitation Matching for Efficient RL

> [!summary] Summary
> UC Berkeley researchers developed Success Visitation Matching (SVM) process rewards, an automated method that converts sparse outcome rewards into dense, step-level feedback for reinforcement learning. This approach utilizes a discriminator to differentiate between successful and unsuccessful state-action visitations, accelerating learning in robotic control tasks while provably maintaining the optimal policy. Experiments showed SVM enabled over 80-90% success rates in simulations and real-world robot tasks, often requiring approximately 2x fewer environment steps for convergence compared to sparse reward baselines.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning often faces sparse reward functions, where agents receive feedback only upon task completion, leading to a difficult credit assignment problem.
- The sparsity of rewards makes learning inefficient, slow, or ineffective, as agents struggle to identify which intermediate actions contribute to eventual success.
- Existing dense reward shaping methods typically rely on extensive domain-specific knowledge, human engineering, or additional supervision, limiting their generalizability and risking alteration of the original task's optimal policy.

## Method

- The paper introduces Success Visitation Matching (SVM) process rewards, which automatically generate dense, step-level feedback from sparse outcome rewards.
- This is achieved by training a discriminator to distinguish between state-action pairs observed in successful episodes (D+D^+D+) and unsuccessful episodes (D−D^-D−) collected online.
- The SVM reward function integrates the original outcome reward with a clipped log ratio of these visitation probabilities, encouraging actions more likely to lead to success and theoretically preserving the optimal policy in deterministic settings.

## Results

- SVM process rewards consistently led to significantly faster and more stable reinforcement learning, achieving over 80-**90%** success rates across various simulated robotic manipulation tasks (LIBERO, RoboCasa) and often requiring **2x** fewer samples for convergence than baselines.
- The method successfully scaled to real-world robotic control, enabling online improvement for tasks like 'Pick and Place' and 'Open Drawer' on a WidowX arm, where training with only sparse outcome rewards failed to learn.
- SVM also improved the efficiency of finetuning large Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (e.g., π0), reducing the required environment steps by approximately **2x** to reach a **90%** success rate without compromising final performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense, informative rewards can be learned dynamically and automatically from sparse outcome signals by matching the visitation patterns of successful trajectories, rather than requiring static expert demonstrations or human-engineered features.
> - Framing the reward as a log ratio of successful to unsuccessful state-action visitation probabilities allows for principled reward shaping that guides learning while ensuring the optimal policy for the original sparse task is preserved.
> - Continuously updating the positive example set (D+D^+D+) with newly observed successful episodes, similar to an online form of inverse reinforcement learning, is critical for enabling efficient and stable policy improvement in sparse reward environments.
