# 3D-LLM: Injecting the 3D World into Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The 3D-LLM framework from a collaboration including MIT and UMass Amherst enables large language models to understand and reason about the 3D physical world. It achieves this by generating large-scale 3D-language data and deriving 3D features from multi-view 2D images, demonstrating improved performance across tasks like 3D question answering and object grounding compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) lack grounding in the 3D physical world, limiting their understanding of spatial relationships, object affordances, and scene layouts.
- Existing 3D-language models are typically task-specific, lacking the generalizability of foundation models to handle diverse 3D-related tasks comprehensively.
- There is a severe scarcity of high-quality, diverse 3D-language paired data, which hinders the training of large-scale 3D foundation models.

## Method

- A novel ==data generation pipeline== leverages LLMs like ChatGPT and 2D VLMs to create over 300,000 diverse 3D-language data points from existing 3D datasets such as Objaverse, ScanNet, and HM3D.
- Meaningful ==3D features== are extracted by rendering 3D scenes from multiple views and robustly constructing 3D features from pre-trained 2D image encoders using methods like ==direct reconstruction==, ==feature fusion==, or ==neural fields==.
- **3D-LLMs** are trained on pre-trained 2D VLM backbones (e.g., Flamingo, BLIP-2), with a novel ==3D localization mechanism== that augments 3D features with ==position embeddings== and extends LLM vocabularies with discrete ==location tokens==.

## Results

- 3D-LLMs significantly outperformed state-of-the-art baselines on the ScanQA dataset, achieving an approximate **9%** increase in BLEU-1 score on the validation set and **7%** on the test set.
- The models demonstrated superior performance over 2D VLMs and language-only LLMs on custom-generated datasets for 3D captioning, 3D-assisted dialogue, and task decomposition.
- For 3D grounding on ScanRefer, 3D-LLMs achieved an average distance between predicted and ground-truth bounding box centers of around **1.03 to 1.1 meters** without relying on explicit object proposal modules.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Holistic understanding of 3D scenes can be achieved directly from raw 3D features, outperforming methods reliant on explicit object detection.
> - Leveraging pre-trained 2D vision-language models and generating large-scale 3D-language data via advanced prompting significantly mitigates the scarcity of paired 3D-language datasets.
> - Injecting explicit spatial context through position embeddings for 3D features and dedicated location tokens in the language model's vocabulary enables robust 3D localization and reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen transformative advancements with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) such as GPT-4, which demonstrate remarkable capabilities in communication, reasoning, and various language-based tasks. Subsequent research extended these capabilities into the multimodal domain with Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like Flamingo and BLIP-2, enabling LLMs to understand and reason about 2D images and videos.

However, a critical limitation persists: these powerful models lack grounding in the 3D physical world. They excel at processing abstract language and interpreting static 2D visual information but struggle with richer 3D concepts such as spatial relationships, object affordances (what an object can be used for), physical interactions, and scene layout. This deficiency fundamentally limits their applicability in scenarios requiring real-world interaction, such as robotics, augmented reality, or complex human-robot collaboration.

"3D-LLM" directly addresses this crucial gap. It proposes to inject 3D world understanding into large language models, thereby introducing a new paradigm of "3D-LLMs." While previous works have explored specific 3D-language tasks like 3D Question Answering (ScanQA), 3D Object Localization (ScanRefer), or 3D Captioning, these solutions have typically been task-specific, lacking the generalization and versatility inherent in modern foundation models. 3D-LLM aims to create a unified model capable of handling a diverse array of 3D-related tasks, moving beyond isolated capabilities towards a more holistic understanding of 3D environments.

This work represents a significant step towards creating truly intelligent agents and embodied AI systems that can perceive, reason about, and plan within complex 3D environments, mirroring the intuitive understanding humans possess. It positions itself as a foundational effort to bridge the cognitive abilities of LLMs with the physical reality of the 3D world, thereby pushing the boundaries of multimodal AI beyond current 2D constraints. The project also confronts the inherent challenge of data scarcity in the 3D domain, a bottleneck that has historically hampered the development of large-scale 3D models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **inject the 3D world into large language models**, leading to the creation of a new class of models termed "3D-LLMs." The motivation for this ambitious goal stems from several key observations and desires:

- **Bridging the Reality Gap:** Despite the prowess of LLMs and VLMs in abstract reasoning and 2D visual understanding, they are fundamentally "ungrounded" in the 3D physical world. This means they cannot intuitively grasp concepts vital for real-world interaction, such as precise spatial relationships between objects, their functional affordances, basic physics, and the overall layout of an environment.
- **Enabling Embodied Intelligence:** The ultimate aspiration is to develop AI assistants akin to those depicted in science fiction – robots or virtual agents that can deeply understand 3D environments, perform complex reasoning based on this understanding, and execute sophisticated planning. Current models fall short of this vision.
- **Leveraging 3D Representation Advantages:** By taking holistic 3D representations (specifically, 3D point clouds with features) as input, 3D-LLMs gain two significant advantages:
 - **Long-term Memory:** Holistic 3D representations can store comprehensive information about an entire scene, offering "long-term memory" compared to episodic, partial-view observations from 2D images.
 - **Richer Reasoning:** 3D properties like affordances and complex spatial relationships can be reasoned directly from 3D data, a capability far beyond what language-only or 2D image-based LLMs can achieve.
- **Achieving Task Generalization in 3D:** Unlike existing task-specific 3D-language models, the objective is to build a single, general-purpose 3D-LLM that can perform a diverse range of 3D-related tasks. This includes tasks such as:
 - 3D Captioning (describing entire scenes)
 - Dense Captioning (describing specific objects/regions)
 - 3D Question Answering (answering questions about the 3D environment)
 - Task Decomposition (breaking down high-level goals into low-level 3D actions)
 - 3D Grounding (localizing objects from language descriptions)
 - 3D-Assisted Dialogue (engaging in natural conversation grounded in 3D scenes)
 - Navigation (planning paths within 3D environments).
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity:** A major practical motivation is to address the severe scarcity of paired 3D-language data, which is a significant bottleneck for training large 3D foundation models, analogous to the vast internet-scale 2D image-text datasets.

In essence, the project aims to push AI closer to human-like understanding and interaction with the physical world by providing LLMs with a foundational, intuitive grasp of 3D space.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed 3D-LLM framework is designed to overcome the challenges of data scarcity and the lack of pre-trained 3D encoders by leveraging existing strengths of 2D Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs). The methodology can be broken down into three core components: data generation, 3D feature extraction, and 3D-LLM training.

#### 3.1. 3D-Language Data Generation

Recognizing the severe scarcity of high-quality, diverse 3D-language paired data, the authors devised novel data generation pipelines using advanced LLMs like ChatGPT. Over 300,000 3D-language data points were collected covering a wide array of tasks. Three main prompting mechanisms were employed:

1. **Boxes-Demonstration-Instruction based Prompting:** This method provides ChatGPT with the axis-aligned bounding boxes (AABB) of rooms and objects within 3D scenes, including semantic and spatial information. Coupled with specific instructions and 0-3 few-shot demonstration examples, ChatGPT is prompted to generate diverse data types (e.g., task decomposition, 3D-assisted dialogue, Q&A).
2. **ChatCaptioner based Prompting:** Inspired by recent work, this technique involves ChatGPT iteratively asking informative questions about an image, with BLIP-2 providing answers based on different views of the 3D scene. This iterative questioning and answering process allows for the compilation of comprehensive, global 3D descriptions of entire scenes.
3. **Revision based Prompting:** This mechanism facilitates the transformation of one type of 3D data into another, increasing data diversity and utility.

The data generation primarily leverages existing 3D datasets:
- **Objaverse:** Used to generate high-quality 3D-related descriptions for individual 3D objects, addressing the issue of noisy or missing existing descriptions.
- **ScanNet:** Utilized for its richly annotated 3D indoor scenes, providing semantics and bounding boxes for scene-level understanding.
- **Habitat-Matterport (HM3D) and HM3DSem:** Employed for their large-scale 3D environments and semantic annotations, crucial for tasks like embodied AI and navigation.

#### 3.2. 3D Feature Extractor

Since training 3D encoders from scratch on limited 3D-language data is infeasible, the approach extracts meaningful 3D features by leveraging powerful pre-trained 2D image encoders. This is achieved by rendering 3D scenes from multiple views and constructing 3D features from the extracted 2D image features. Three methods are used to construct 3D features from these rendered multi-view images, depending on the nature of the 3D data:

1. **Direct Reconstruction:** For RGB-D data with accurate camera poses and intrinsics, 3D point clouds are directly reconstructed, and 2D features are mapped onto these 3D points.
2. **Feature Fusion (using gradslam):** For 3D data with potentially noisy depth maps or camera parameters, 2D features are robustly fused into 3D maps, extending beyond just depth and color.
3. **Neural Field (using neural voxel field):** For 3D data primarily with RGB renderings and without depth information (or with very noisy camera poses), a compact 3D representation is constructed where each voxel holds a feature. 3D and 2D features are aligned using Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss.

This process yields `N`-dimensional 3D features (where `N` is the number of points in the point cloud) with a feature dimension `Dv` that is compatible with 2D VLM backbones.

#### 3.3. Training 3D-LLMs

The core training strategy for 3D-LLMs involves leveraging pre-trained 2D VLMs as backbones, given that the extracted 3D features reside in the same feature space as 2D image features. This allows for efficient training without needing billion-scale 3D datasets.

- **2D VLM Backbones:** The models are built upon well-established 2D VLMs like Flamingo 9B and BLIP-2 (specifically, Vit-g Opt2.7B and Vit-g FlanT5-XL). These VLMs often utilize "perceiver" architectures (e.g., Flamingo's perceiver, BLIP-2's QFormer) that can handle large inputs of arbitrary sizes, making them suitable for variable-sized point cloud features.
- **Parameter Tuning:** During pre-training, most parts of the underlying LLMs (e.g., Opt and FlanT5) are kept frozen. The trainable parameters are primarily within the VLM-specific components like the QFormer in BLIP-2 or the perceiver and gated cross-attention layers in Flamingo.
- **3D Localization Mechanism:** To imbue 3D-LLMs with an inherent understanding of 3D spatial information—a crucial distinction from purely 2D or language models—a novel localization mechanism is introduced:
 1. **Augmenting 3D features with Position Embeddings:** Sin/cos position embeddings corresponding to the x, y, z coordinates are concatenated with the extracted 3D features. This provides the model with explicit spatial context for each feature point.
 2. **Augmenting LLM Vocabularies with Location Tokens:** The LLM's vocabulary is extended to include discrete location tokens (e.g., `<loc1>` to `<loc64>`) that represent discretized bounding box coordinates (AABB). The weights for these new tokens in the input and output embeddings of the language model are unfrozen, allowing the model to both interpret and generate precise spatial locations based on language descriptions.

The overall framework trains the 3D-LLMs with a standard language modeling loss, enabling them to generate diverse responses conditioned on both 3D features and language prompts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the superior performance and versatility of 3D-LLMs across a range of 3D-related tasks.

#### 4.1. Held-Out Evaluation on ScanQA

The models were finetuned and evaluated on the ScanQA dataset, a benchmark for 3D question answering.
- **Performance Leap:** 3D-LLMs significantly outperformed state-of-the-art baselines like ScanQA, ScanRefer+MCAN, and VoteNet+MCAN. For instance, the BLEU-1 score saw an approximate 9% increase on the validation set and 7% on the test set compared to the SOTA ScanQA model. CIDEr scores also showed considerable gains (e.g., ~5% higher than ScanQA).
- **Holistic Understanding:** A key finding is that 3D-LLMs achieve this superior performance without relying on explicit object representations or pre-trained object detectors (which baselines like VoteNet use). This indicates that the models possess a more holistic understanding of 3D scenes and can implicitly reason about objects and their relationships from raw 3D features.
- **Superiority over 2D VLMs:** Comparisons with 2D VLM baselines (Flamingo and BLIP-2 models trained with single-view or multi-view images) revealed that 3D-LLMs consistently perform better. This underscores the importance of proper 3D representations over fragmented multi-view 2D images, as the latter can lose critical 3D spatial information.

#### 4.2. More Extensive Evaluations

- **Held-In Evaluation (3D Captioning, 3D-Assisted Dialogue, Task Decomposition):** Experiments on the custom-generated held-in datasets showed that 3D-LLMs generated high-quality responses, significantly outperforming both 2D VLMs and language-only LLMs (FlanT5). This validates the model's ability to handle the diverse array of tasks it was designed for.
- **3DMV-VQA (Held-Out, Appendix):** On the 3DMV-VQA dataset, 3D-LLMs outperformed the state-of-the-art 3D-CLR, particularly in "concept" and "relation" based question types, and in overall performance. This further corroborates the advantage of 3D representations.
- **3D Grounding (ScanRefer, Held-Out, Appendix):** When finetuned on the ScanRefer benchmark for 3D object localization, 3D-LLMs achieved decent performance. Crucially, they outperformed most baselines even without using explicit object proposal modules or ground-truth bounding boxes, instead directly outputting location tokens. The average distance between predicted and ground-truth bounding box centers was impressively low (around 1.03 to 1.1 meters), confirming the effectiveness of the 3D localization mechanism.
- **Object Navigation (Qualitative, Appendix):** Qualitative examples demonstrate the 3D-LLM's capacity for progressive environmental understanding and navigation. It could predict appropriate 3D waypoints for an agent to find a target object, even when initially unobserved, showcasing its reasoning capabilities in dynamic 3D environments.
- **Qualitative Examples:** The paper presents numerous qualitative examples (Figure 1, 4, and additional figures in the Appendix) illustrating the model's ability to perform tasks like scene description, object dense captioning, answering 3D questions, decomposing tasks into actionable steps, and engaging in multi-turn 3D-assisted dialogues.
- **Ablation Study:** An ablation study on the Flamingo backbone confirmed that the "perceiver resampler" module significantly benefits the training and performance of 3D-LLMs, highlighting its role in efficiently processing large 3D inputs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "3D-LLM" project marks a significant advancement in the field of artificial intelligence, offering several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Pioneering 3D Grounding for LLMs:** This work introduces a groundbreaking family of models, 3D-LLMs, which represents a crucial leap towards truly grounding large language models in the physical 3D world. By enabling LLMs to understand spatial relationships, object affordances, and scene layouts, it addresses a fundamental limitation of current AI systems that are primarily confined to text or 2D images.
- **Enabling General-Purpose Embodied AI:** The ability of 3D-LLMs to handle a diverse array of 3D-related tasks—from captioning and question answering to task decomposition and navigation—within a single unified model signifies a major step towards creating more capable and versatile embodied AI agents (e.g., robots, virtual assistants). These agents could perform complex tasks in real-world environments more intelligently and intuitively.
- **Innovative Data Generation Paradigms:** The development of novel prompting pipelines leveraging LLMs like ChatGPT to generate large-scale, high-quality 3D-language datasets is a crucial methodological contribution. This approach provides a blueprint for overcoming data scarcity in other complex multimodal domains, accelerating research in areas traditionally limited by manual annotation efforts.
- **Efficient Training Strategy:** The methodology of extracting 3D features from 2D multi-view images and then utilizing pre-trained 2D VLMs as backbones offers an efficient and resource-friendly training paradigm. This strategy makes the development of sophisticated 3D-aware foundation models more accessible, democratizing research in this complex domain.
- **Enhanced Human-AI Interaction:** With 3D-LLMs, human-AI interaction can become more natural and effective, particularly in contexts involving physical spaces. Users could verbally instruct robots or virtual assistants about tasks in a room, ask questions about objects' locations, or seek assistance with navigation, leading to more seamless and intuitive interactions.
- **Applications Across Industries:** The potential impact spans various industries:
 - **Robotics:** Smarter robots capable of sophisticated scene understanding, planning, and task execution in complex environments.
 - **Augmented/Virtual Reality:** More immersive and interactive virtual experiences where AI agents can genuinely understand and respond to the virtual 3D environment.
 - **Smart Homes/Environments:** AI systems that can intelligently manage and interact within physical spaces, understanding user needs and environmental context.
 - **Architecture and Design:** Tools that can reason about spatial layouts and functional aspects of designs.
- **Future Research Directions:** The work opens numerous avenues for future research, including exploring more direct 3D feature learning methods that reduce reliance on multi-view rendering, improving the robustness of 3D localization, and expanding the types of 3D reasoning (e.g., fine-grained physics simulation, material properties). The planned release of the 3D-LLMs, dataset, and features will serve as a vital resource for the broader research community to build upon.

In conclusion, "3D-LLM" represents a significant stride in bridging the gap between advanced language understanding and physical world perception, laying foundational groundwork for a new generation of intelligent agents capable of truly understanding and interacting with our three-dimensional reality.
