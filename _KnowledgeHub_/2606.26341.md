# Scaling Nonlinear Optimization: Many Problems One GPU

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley developed `jaxipm`, the first GPU-batched Nonlinear Program (NLP) solver, designed to concurrently solve thousands of independent optimization problems on a single GPU. It achieved up to 25x higher throughput than CPU-based IPOPT on quadrotor control tasks while maintaining algorithmic parity.

## Problem

- Existing gradient-based NLP solvers (e.g., IPOPT) are CPU-bound, creating a computational bottleneck in modern GPU-batched robotics workflows and limiting throughput.
- Sampling-based methods leverage GPU parallelism but typically encode constraints as soft penalties, offering no guarantees regarding feasibility or optimality.
- The demand for large, high-quality datasets of optimal, constraint-satisfying trajectories for robot learning is unmet by current sequential NLP solvers.

## Method

- Implemented `jaxipm`, a complete redesign of the IPOPT algorithm in JAX, enabling just-in-time compilation for efficient **GPU** execution and automatic differentiation.
- Introduced **heterogeneous iteration fusion** to transform IPOPT's complex, conditional control flow into a fixed sequence of operations, where all possible branch computations are performed in parallel, then the correct result is selected.
- Developed **iteration level batching** to minimize GPU idle time by dynamically replacing converged problems with new ones within the active batch, ensuring continuous high throughput.

## Results

- `jaxipm` demonstrated up to **25.25x** higher throughput compared to CPU-based IPOPT and significantly outperformed other GPU-accelerated but non-batched solvers on quadrotor NMPC benchmarks.
- The solver maintained its throughput advantage with increasing problem difficulty, showing up to **12.28x** speedup over IPOPT for challenging trajectories, though speedup decreased with higher problem dimensionality due to GPU memory limits.
- Numerical comparisons showed deviations below 10^-8 between `jaxipm` and IPOPT states, confirming algorithmic parity and correctness in replicating IPOPT's full range of control flow branches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transforming complex, heterogeneous control flow of robust NLP solvers into uniform, parallelizable GPU operations is achievable by computing all possible branches simultaneously and then selecting the appropriate outcome.
> - Maximizing GPU utilization for batch optimization requires dynamically managing problem completion, continuously feeding new problems into available computational slots as others converge, rather than waiting for entire batches to finish.
> - Implementing NLP solvers in JAX enables deep integration with modern GPU-batched machine learning and robotics frameworks, leveraging automatic differentiation and vectorized operations for performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Many fundamental problems in robotics, such as trajectory optimization, inverse kinematics, and contact-rich motion planning, can be formulated as Nonlinear Programs (NLPs). Historically, two primary approaches have been employed to solve these problems: sampling-based (SB) methods and gradient-based (GB) methods.

Sampling-based methods, which include techniques like reinforcement learning (RL), Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control, and the cross-entropy method, have seen a significant increase in prominence within modern robotics research. This rise is largely attributable to the development of GPU-batched simulators (e.g., MuJoCo XLA, Isaac Lab, Brax). These simulators can generate synthetic dynamics rollouts orders of magnitude faster than traditional CPU-based alternatives, enabling rapid data generation for learning. However, SB methods typically encode constraints as soft penalties, offering no guarantees regarding feasibility or optimality. They also require exponentially more samples as problem dimensions increase.

In contrast, gradient-based methods rely on mature, well-tested, CPU-based NLP solvers such as IPOPT, KNITRO, and SNOPT. These solvers offer direct enforcement of hard constraints, converge to solutions satisfying optimality conditions, scale favorably with problem dimension, and can refine solutions to high precision by exploiting model information. Despite these advantages, GB methods have been limited by their computational bottleneck: the underlying NLP optimizers remain CPU-bound. This prevents their integration into modern GPU-batched robotics workflows, where they would otherwise become the slowest link, throttling the entire pipeline to single-problem throughput and leaving the GPU idle. This limitation prevents GB methods from achieving the significant parallelization speedups observed in SB methods.

Existing efforts to accelerate NLP solvers on GPUs have focused on porting the most computationally intensive parts of IPOPT-like algorithms, such as the solution of sparse symmetric indefinite linear (KKT) systems and sparse derivative evaluations. Examples include HyKKT and MadNLP, which reimplements IPOPT in Julia and uses GPU-accelerated linear solvers like cuDSS. While these efforts successfully accelerate per-iteration costs, their algorithmic logic, including the complex control flow branches essential for robustness, remains CPU-bound. Consequently, these solvers do not support GPU-batching, meaning they cannot concurrently solve multiple independent NLPs.

A separate line of research has developed GPU-batched solvers for *convex* optimization problems. These solvers, employing methods like interior-point, primal-dual hybrid-gradient, or operator-splitting, can leverage GPU parallelism due to their simpler algorithmic structure, which typically avoids the complex control flow branches found in general NLP solvers. However, these methods do not extend to problems with nonlinear dynamics or constraints.

The present work addresses this gap by introducing `jaxipm`, the first GPU-batched NLP solver. It aims to bridge the performance disparity between SB and GB methods by enabling GB methods to leverage GPU parallelism, thus making it possible to generate dataset-scale collections of optimal and constrained solutions for robotics applications. The demand for such high-quality batched NLP data is increasingly recognized in research areas such as training humanoid RL policies on optimal trajectories, distilling MPC controllers into quadruped policies via behavior cloning, and general efforts to close the "robot learning data gap" through hybrid model-free and model-based optimization pipelines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop the first GPU-batched Nonlinear Program (NLP) solver capable of concurrently solving thousands of independent NLPs on a single GPU. This objective is motivated by several factors concerning the current state of robotics optimization and machine learning integration:

1. **Addressing the Bottleneck of CPU-Bound Solvers**: Existing mature NLP solvers, such as IPOPT, SNOPT, and KNITRO, are designed for sequential execution on CPUs. While effective for single-problem optimization, their inability to exploit parallel processing on GPUs creates a significant bottleneck in modern robotics workflows. These workflows increasingly rely on GPU-batched simulators and learning frameworks that can process data orders of magnitude faster. When integrated into such pipelines, a CPU-bound NLP solver limits the entire batch to solving one problem at a time, leading to substantial GPU idle time and underutilization of computational resources.

2. **Unlocking Parallelism for Gradient-Based Methods**: Sampling-based methods have achieved unprecedented speeds by leveraging GPU-batched simulators. The motivation is to extend similar orders-of-magnitude speedups to gradient-based methods. This would enable the rapid generation of large datasets of optimal, constraint-satisfying solutions, which is currently impractical with CPU-bound solvers.

3. **Meeting the Demand for Large-Scale, High-Quality Optimization Data**: Recent advancements in robotic learning, particularly in imitation learning and hybrid model-predictive control (MPC) with reinforcement learning, highlight a growing need for extensive datasets of high-quality, optimal, and physically feasible trajectories. These datasets are crucial for training robust and performant robot policies. Current solvers cannot meet this demand at the required scale. A GPU-batched NLP solver could generate such data efficiently, bridging the "robot learning data gap."

4. **Maintaining IPOPT's Robustness and Advantages**: IPOPT was chosen as the basis for `jaxipm` due to its broad applicability to general NLPs and its established robustness, which has made it a default choice in many engineering and robotics applications. A key motivation was to adapt IPOPT's algorithmic strengths—including its ability to handle hard constraints, provide optimality guarantees, and scale well with problem dimension—to a GPU-batched environment without sacrificing its robustness. This required addressing IPOPT's complex internal control flow (e.g., various fallback mechanisms for edge cases like backtracking line search, second-order correction, and feasibility restoration), which is inherently sequential and thus challenging for GPU parallelism.

5. **Minimizing GPU Idle Time**: Beyond enabling parallel execution, a specific objective was to maximize GPU utilization. This meant addressing the problem of varying convergence rates within a batch of optimizations. A naive parallel implementation would wait for the slowest problem to finish before processing the next batch, leading to significant GPU idle time. The aim was to design a mechanism that continuously feeds new problems to the GPU as others converge, ensuring sustained high throughput.

In summary, the motivation is to modernize NLP solving by introducing GPU-batching capabilities, thereby enabling gradient-based optimization to integrate seamlessly into and accelerate modern GPU-accelerated robotics and machine learning frameworks, while retaining the benefits of robust and optimal solutions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology involves a complete redesign of the IPOPT algorithm from scratch, implemented in JAX, to enable high-throughput batch solving on GPUs while preserving the per-optimization control flow and robustness characteristic of IPOPT. This was achieved through two primary contributions: **heterogeneous iteration fusion** and **iteration level batching**.

#### 3.1. Implementation in JAX

The choice of JAX as the implementation framework was strategic. JAX provides just-in-time (JIT) compilation to XLA (Accelerated Linear Algebra), which allows Python/NumPy programs to run efficiently on GPUs and TPUs. Furthermore, JAX's native support for automatic differentiation and vectorized operations (`vmap`) aligns well with the requirements for high-performance, GPU-batched numerical optimization and its interoperability with modern GPU-batched learning frameworks.

#### 3.2. Heterogeneous Iteration Fusion

IPOPT's robustness stems from its sophisticated handling of edge cases through various control flow branches (e.g., backtracking line search, second-order correction, feasibility restoration, watchdog mechanism, tiny-step rule, barrier parameter adaptation). In a batch of NLP problems, each individual optimization might require a different control flow branch at any given iteration. This heterogeneity poses a challenge for GPUs, which operate most efficiently under a Single Instruction, Multiple Data (SIMD) paradigm, requiring uniform execution across parallel threads.

`jaxipm` addresses this by transforming IPOPT's conditional control flow into a fixed sequence of operations suitable for GPU parallelism:
1. **Decomposition of Branches**: Each control flow branch and its associated computations (e.g., calculating a Newton step, updating multipliers, checking convergence criteria) were analyzed and decomposed into two categories:
 - **Shared Computations**: These are fundamental calculations that are common across multiple, or all, control flow branches within an iteration. For instance, the formation and factorization of the KKT system (Equation 7) are central to generating search steps, regardless of whether a line search or second-order correction is subsequently applied. These shared computations are performed once per iteration for the entire batch in parallel.
 - **Unique Computations**: These are calculations specific to a particular control flow branch. For example, the specific modifications to the search direction or objective function, or additional linear solves, that only apply if a second-order correction is chosen, or scalar-vector multiplications for a line search.
2. **Parallel Execution of All Unique Paths**: Instead of executing a single conditional branch for each problem, `jaxipm` computes *all* possible branch-specific "unique" computations for *every* problem in the batch.
3. **Selection of Correct Output**: After all computations for all branches are performed in parallel, a selection mechanism is used to choose the correct result for each individual optimization problem, based on the specific control flow branch it was determined to need. This means that while a problem might only logically *need* a line search, its GPU thread will still perform computations for a second-order correction, and then simply discard that result.

This approach ensures that every optimization in the batch advances in lockstep, performing an identical sequence of computational operations at each iteration. This effectively fuses the heterogeneous iterations into a single, uniform iteration body that is highly amenable to GPU parallel execution, without compromising the algorithmic robustness and benefits derived from IPOPT's complex control flow.

#### 3.3. Iteration Level Batching

Even with heterogeneous iteration fusion, problems within a batch are unlikely to converge simultaneously. A naive "solve-level" batching strategy would require all problems in the current batch to complete before a new batch could be initiated. This would lead to significant GPU idle time as faster-converging problems wait for the slowest one to finish (visualized in Figure 2, Left).

`jaxipm` implements **iteration level batching** to minimize GPU downtime and maximize throughput:
1. **Synchronization at Iteration Boundaries**: The entire batch of optimizations progresses synchronously, one iteration at a time.
2. **Hot-Restarting Converged Problems**: When an individual optimization problem within the batch reaches convergence (or a termination criterion), its computational slot is not left idle.
3. **Dynamic Replacement**: At the beginning of the subsequent iteration, any converged problem is immediately "hot-restarted" with a new, fresh optimization problem. This new problem takes the place of the converged one, occupying the now-available computational resources within the batch.
4. **Continuous Processing**: Unfinished problems continue iterating without interruption, while the GPU is continuously supplied with active work.

This dynamic replacement strategy ensures that the GPU is consistently utilized, continuously solving new problems as others finish. This contrasts with solve-level batching and leads to a higher overall throughput by minimizing the impact of varying convergence times among problems (visualized in Figure 2, Right).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The `jaxipm` solver was evaluated on various quadrotor Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) benchmarks, comparing its performance against CasADi/IPOPT (a CPU-based baseline) and ExaModels/MadNLP (a GPU-accelerated, but non-batched, IPOPT implementation). All experiments used 13-DoF quadrotors with a timestep of 0.1s and a 30-step prediction horizon, resulting in at least 506 optimization variables per problem.

#### 4.1. Throughput

- **Scenario**: Batches of quadrotors navigating to a common goal from varying initial positions distributed in 90° and 180° circular arcs, in the presence of obstacles. The 180° scenario inherently introduced problems with diverse iteration counts, testing the effectiveness of heterogeneous iteration fusion and iteration level batching.
- **Results (Table II)**:
 - `jaxipm` demonstrated significant throughput acceleration: 25.25x faster than IPOPT for the 90° sector and 24.74x faster for the 180° sector.
 - MadNLP showed comparable or slightly lower throughput than IPOPT in these scenarios (0.99x and 1.03x, respectively), highlighting its inability to batch problems.
 - The average final cost function values for `jaxipm` were comparable to IPOPT, indicating similar solution quality, achieved with a reasonable average number of iterations. The similar speedups in both scenarios (90° more homogeneous, 180° more heterogeneous in iteration counts) support the effectiveness of iteration level batching in mitigating performance degradation from diverse problem types.

#### 4.2. Scalability

Scalability was assessed in two dimensions: problem difficulty and problem dimensionality.

- **Scaling Difficulty (Table III)**:
 - **Scenario**: Quadrotor reference tracking while avoiding three time-varying obstacles. Difficulty was increased by varying the average velocity (`v`) of the reference trajectory (1.0, 1.5, 2.0 m/s), requiring the quadrotor to operate closer to actuator limits.
 - **Results**:
 - `jaxipm` consistently maintained the highest throughput across all difficulty levels.
 - Its throughput advantage over IPOPT generally increased with problem difficulty (4.63x at 1.0 m/s, 9.43x at 1.5 m/s, and 12.28x at 2.0 m/s). This was attributed to `jaxipm` requiring a similar number of iterations across difficulty levels, while baselines struggled more.
 - MadNLP's performance varied, sometimes being slower than IPOPT at higher difficulties.

- **Scaling Dimensionality (Table IV)**:
 - **Scenario**: Multi-quadrotor consensus task, where multiple quadrotors swap positions while maintaining minimum separation. Dimensionality was increased by scaling the number of quadrotors from 2 (1012 variables) to 4 (2024 variables).
 - **Results**:
 - `jaxipm` maintained an order-of-magnitude throughput advantage over IPOPT (32.85x for 2 quadrotors).
 - However, the speedup decreased to 16.03x when scaling to 4 quadrotors. This reduction was attributed to GPU memory limitations, which restricted the maximum batch size for larger problems, thereby reducing the available parallelism despite the increased problem size.
 - MadNLP showed better speedups in this test (5.30x and 6.05x for 2 and 4 quads, respectively) compared to the throughput tests, but still significantly less than `jaxipm`.

#### 4.3. Correctness

- **Scenario**: A challenging single-quadrotor navigation task, designed to activate a wide range of IPOPT's control flow branches (e.g., second-order correction, soft/hard feasibility restoration, barrier update switches) throughout its optimization.
- **Method**: The complete IPOPT state was saved at each iteration. `jaxipm` was then stepped once from this state, and its output was compared against the next IPOPT state. The largest scaled element-wise deviation was computed for quantities common to both solvers.
- **Results (Figure 6)**: All measured deviations between `jaxipm` and IPOPT states fell below 10^-8. This empirical evidence supports `jaxipm`'s algorithmic parity with IPOPT, demonstrating that it is a feature-complete recreation capable of executing IPOPT's full range of control flow branches with high numerical accuracy.

In summary, `jaxipm` consistently outperformed IPOPT and MadNLP in terms of throughput across various NMPC tasks, demonstrating effective GPU utilization through its novel batching and fusion mechanisms. While its speedup can be affected by GPU memory constraints with increasing problem dimensionality, it maintains a substantial performance advantage.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of `jaxipm` represents a significant development in the field of nonlinear optimization for robotics and beyond, primarily due to its capability as the first GPU-batched NLP solver. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Gap for Gradient-Based Methods**: `jaxipm` enables gradient-based (GB) optimization methods to leverage GPU parallelism, similar to how GPU-batched simulators have accelerated sampling-based (SB) methods. This can unlock orders-of-magnitude speedups in generating constrained, locally optimal solutions, thereby making GB methods more competitive and applicable in contexts where real-time performance or large-scale data generation is crucial.

2. **Transforming Robotics Learning Pipelines**:
 - **Dataset Generation for Imitation Learning**: `jaxipm` can facilitate the creation of large datasets of optimal, dynamically feasible, and constraint-satisfying trajectories. Such datasets are highly valuable for training robust robot policies via imitation learning, addressing the "robot learning data gap" by providing higher-quality and more diverse training data than traditional human demonstrations or kinematics-based baselines.
 - **Integration with Reinforcement Learning**: GPU-based reinforcement learning frameworks can integrate NLPs natively into their training loops. This allows RL policies to directly benefit from hard constraint satisfaction and optimality guarantees provided by model-based optimization, potentially leading to safer and more predictable robot behaviors.
 - **Hybrid Control Architectures**: The ability to solve many NLPs concurrently can make complex hybrid control schemes, such as those combining Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) with Model Predictive Control (MPC), computationally feasible. This could lead to more sophisticated and adaptive robot controllers.

3. **Increased Computational Throughput**: By employing heterogeneous iteration fusion and iteration level batching, `jaxipm` maximizes GPU utilization and minimizes idle time. This allows for the concurrent solution of thousands of independent NLPs, vastly increasing the throughput of complex optimization problems. This is particularly impactful for applications requiring rapid exploration of solution spaces or repeated optimizations with varying parameters.

4. **Maintaining Algorithmic Robustness**: The methodology ensures that `jaxipm` retains the algorithmic robustness and convergence guarantees of IPOPT, a widely trusted NLP solver. This means the achieved speedups do not come at the cost of solution quality or reliability, which is critical for safety-critical robotics applications.

5. **Democratization of Advanced Optimization**: The open-source nature of `jaxipm`, coupled with its implementation in JAX, makes this advanced optimization capability accessible to a broader research community. JAX's differentiability and compatibility with machine learning ecosystems further lower the barrier to integrating complex optimization into end-to-end learning systems.

In conclusion, `jaxipm` addresses a long-standing bottleneck in robotics by enabling GPU-batched NLP solving. This innovation has the potential to accelerate research in model-based control, improve the efficiency and quality of data generation for robot learning, and foster the development of more capable and robust autonomous systems.
