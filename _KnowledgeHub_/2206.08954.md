# Bag of Image Patch Embedding Behind the Success of Self-Supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research reinterprets the success of self-supervised learning (SSL) by formally linking joint-embedding SSL to co-occurrence modeling of image patches. It introduces "BagSSL," a method showing that training with fixed-scale patches and aggregating their embeddings yields competitive performance, and demonstrates that aggregating local patch embeddings can consistently improve baseline SSL model performance by over 1%.

## Problem

- The underlying mechanisms for the empirical success of joint-embedding self-supervised learning (SSL) in learning image representations are not fully understood.
- The prevailing "invariance perspective" provides an incomplete theoretical explanation for why SSL methods effectively learn robust representations.
- Potential for enhancing existing SSL representations by better utilizing their inherent structure remains unexplored.

## Method

- Formally connected the ==spectral contrastive loss== (a joint-embedding SSL objective) to a ==co-occurrence statistics modeling== loss function for image patches.
- Developed "**BagSSL**," a training pipeline that exclusively uses ==fixed-scale image patches== for SSL training and aggregates their embeddings to form whole-image representations.
- Investigated ==linear aggregation== techniques for patch embeddings from both BagSSL and standard multi-scale pretrained SSL models to assess and improve representation quality.

## Results

- The BagSSL approach achieved performance comparable to or better than baseline multi-scale SSL methods on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-100, reaching **62%** top-1 linear probing accuracy on ImageNet-1K.
- Applying global linear aggregation of patch embeddings consistently improved the linear evaluation performance of state-of-the-art SSL models on ImageNet by over **1%** compared to standard central crop evaluation.
- Advanced local aggregation techniques significantly boosted k-NN accuracy on CIFAR-10, surpassing the linear evaluation accuracy of baseline models by a larger margin.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint-embedding SSL can be theoretically interpreted as learning distributed representations of image patches by modeling their co-occurrence statistics, drawing parallels to word embeddings in NLP.
> - The 'embedding' space of SSL models, prior to the projector head, retains substantial local information and fine-grained visual details, contrasting with the more invariant 'projection' space.
> - Whole-image representations from standard multi-scale pretrained SSL models can be effectively approximated by linearly aggregating their local patch embeddings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Self-supervised learning (SSL) has demonstrated significant empirical advancements in learning robust image representations, often matching or surpassing supervised methods in various applications. A prominent paradigm within SSL is joint-embedding SSL, which typically employs Siamese network architectures to bring different augmented views of the same image instance closer in a learned representation space, while simultaneously preventing representational collapse. The prevailing understanding of these methods attributes their success primarily to learning representations that are invariant to the applied data transformations.

This research paper positions itself within this context by proposing a supplementary perspective. While acknowledging the importance of invariance, the authors suggest that joint-embedding SSL methods fundamentally learn representations of image patches, modeling their co-occurrence statistics. This proposal draws a parallel between modern image SSL and historical approaches to word embedding in natural language processing (NLP), such as Word2Vec, which are based on modeling word co-occurrence within a context.

The work builds upon existing research in several areas:
- **Joint-embedding SSL methods:** It references foundational works like SimCLR, BYOL, VICReg, and TCR, which represent diverse strategies for achieving invariance without collapse.
- **Patch-based representations:** It relates to prior work in both supervised learning (e.g., Bagnet, Vision Transformers) and unsupervised learning (e.g., Gidaris et al.), which have explored the efficacy of local, patch-level features.
- **Co-occurrence statistics modeling:** It explicitly connects to NLP models that learn representations by capturing statistical relationships between entities (e.g., words) based on their co-occurrence in a given context.

By establishing a formal connection between SSL and co-occurrence modeling and empirically demonstrating the effectiveness of patch-based SSL, this work aims to deepen the theoretical understanding of SSL's success, moving beyond a sole focus on invariance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the fundamental understanding of the principles underlying the empirical success of joint-embedding self-supervised learning (SSL) methods in learning image representations. Despite the significant advancements and widespread adoption of SSL, the authors note that the theoretical understanding of *why* these methods work effectively remains limited.

The detailed objectives and motivations can be broken down as follows:

1. **Demystify Self-Supervised Representation Learning:** The overarching motivation is to provide clearer insights into the learning process within joint-embedding SSL, moving beyond a purely empirical observation of success. The paper aims to take a step towards explaining the mechanism behind the learned representations.

2. **Propose and Validate a Co-occurrence Modeling Hypothesis:** The core hypothesis is that joint-embedding SSL approaches primarily learn a representation of image patches that reflects their co-occurrence patterns within images. The authors aim to formally establish this connection, supplementing the prevailing "invariance perspective" with a "co-occurrence modeling" perspective.

3. **Demonstrate the Efficacy of Fixed-Scale Patch Representation Learning (BagSSL):** A key objective is to empirically show that training SSL models using only fixed-scale image patches, and subsequently aggregating these local patch representations, can achieve comparable or even superior performance to baseline methods that utilize multi-scale cropping augmentation. This involves introducing and evaluating the "BagSSL" pipeline.

4. **Investigate the Nature of Whole-Image Representations in Multi-scale Pretrained Models:** The work aims to demonstrate that, in standard SSL models pretrained with multi-scale crops, the resultant whole-image embedding can be approximated as a linear aggregation (average) of local patch embeddings. This would suggest an inherent patch-level understanding even in models not explicitly trained on fixed patches.

5. **Examine Locality Preservation in Learned Representations:** While SSL aims for global invariance, another objective is to explore whether locality is preserved within the learned representations when examining them at a finer, patch-level scale. This seeks to provide a more nuanced understanding of the representation space's structure.

6. **Leverage New Insights to Improve Existing SSL Baselines:** Beyond theoretical understanding, a practical motivation is to demonstrate that the insights gained from understanding patch co-occurrence and aggregation can be used to enhance the quality of representations learned by various state-of-the-art SSL baseline methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a combination of theoretical formalization and extensive empirical experimentation to investigate the role of image patch co-occurrence in self-supervised learning.

**Theoretical Formalization:**
The authors formally connect joint-embedding SSL to co-occurrence statistics modeling. They achieve this by demonstrating an equivalence between the spectral contrastive loss function (a form of joint-embedding SSL loss) and a co-occurrence statistics modeling loss function. This equivalence (Proposition 3.1) shows that minimizing the spectral contrastive loss implicitly encourages the inner product of embedding vectors to reflect the ratio of joint probability of two patches co-occurring to their marginal probabilities of occurring independently. The first term of the spectral loss pulls co-occurring patches closer, while the second term acts as a regularization to prevent collapse by pushing non-co-occurring patches apart.

**BagSSL Pipeline:**
The core experimental methodology involves a proposed pipeline termed "BagSSL."
- **Training:** Unlike conventional SSL methods that employ multi-scale cropping for data augmentation, BagSSL training exclusively uses fixed-scale image patches. From each image in a batch, two fixed-size patches are randomly selected and undergo color augmentation. These augmented patches are then fed through a deep neural network encoder (f) to produce embeddings ($\vec{h}$) and a projector network (g) to produce projections ($\vec{z}$). The SSL objective then optimizes these projections: co-occurring patch projections (from the same image) are pulled closer, while non-co-occurring patch projections are pushed apart through an anti-collapse regularization.
- **Image Representation Generation:** After training, to obtain a representation for an entire image, fixed-scale patches are extracted from the image. Each patch's embedding ($\vec{h}$) is generated by the trained encoder, and these multiple patch embeddings are then linearly aggregated, specifically by averaging, to form the final image representation ($\vec{R}_{img}$). The embedding layer output ($\vec{h}$) is preferred over the projector output ($\vec{z}$) for aggregation due to empirical performance.

**Empirical Evaluation:**
The BagSSL pipeline is tested with four representative joint-embedding SSL methods: SimCLR, VICReg, TCR, and BYOL. Experiments are conducted on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, ImageNet-100, and ImageNet-1K datasets, using ResNet-34 for CIFAR and ResNet-50 for ImageNet as backbone architectures.

The evaluation protocols include:
- **Linear Probing and k-NN Classification:** The quality of the learned representations is assessed using linear probing accuracy (top-1) and k-nearest neighbor (k-NN) classification, with consistent results observed between the two.
- **Evaluation Settings:**
 - **Standard Central Crop Evaluation:** A classifier is trained and evaluated on a single, fixed central crop of the image (e.g., the entire image for CIFAR, 224x224 for ImageNet).
 - **Multi-Patch/Crop Aggregated Evaluation:** Classifiers are trained and evaluated on the linearly-aggregated embedding of multiple patches or crops (e.g., 1, 16, 48, or 256 patches/crops), with the scale matching the pretraining setting.

**Specific Experimental Investigations:**
1. **BagSSL vs. Baselines Comparison:** The performance of BagSSL (fixed-scale patch training + aggregated evaluation) is directly compared against baseline SSL methods (multi-scale crop training + central crop or aggregated evaluation).
2. **Patch Embedding Aggregation Convergence:** For a multi-scale pretrained baseline model (VICReg), the authors investigate whether the linear aggregation of random patch embeddings converges to the whole-image embedding by calculating cosine similarity as the number of aggregated patches increases.
3. **Locality Preservation Visualization:**
 - **Cosine Similarity Heatmaps:** For a given image, cosine similarity heatmaps are generated between a query patch's embedding/projection and other patches' embeddings/projections from the same image. This visualizes the spatial extent of similarity.
 - **k-Nearest Neighbor (k-NN) Visualization:** For a given query patch, its k-nearest neighbors in both the projection and embedding spaces are visualized from the dataset. This helps assess whether the representation preserves local feature details or collapses them into broader categorical information.
4. **Enhancement of SSL Baselines through Aggregation:**
 - **Global Aggregation:** Pretrained SOTA SSL models are re-evaluated using global linear aggregation of multiple patch embeddings, comparing this performance to their standard single-crop evaluation.
 - **Local Aggregation:** A variation involving local averaging and concatenation of patch embeddings is explored with SOTA SSL models to assess further performance improvements, particularly for k-NN accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings that contribute to the understanding of joint-embedding self-supervised learning:

1. **Formal Connection between SSL and Co-occurrence Modeling:** The paper formally establishes that the spectral contrastive loss, a variant of joint-embedding SSL, is equivalent to a co-occurrence statistics modeling loss function. This provides a theoretical basis for interpreting SSL as learning distributed representations of image patches by capturing their co-occurrence probabilities.

2. **Competitive Performance of BagSSL:**
 - The "BagSSL" approach, which involves training with fixed-scale image patches and aggregating their embeddings, achieved performance comparable to or better than baseline multi-scale SSL methods on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and ImageNet-100 datasets when evaluated with aggregated patch embeddings.
 - On ImageNet-1K, BagSSL with 32x32 patch representation attained 62% top-1 linear probing accuracy. While there was a performance gap of 5.3% compared to VICReg on ImageNet-1K (67.9% vs. 73.2% with 16-patch aggregation), the authors suggest this might be due to a lack of hyperparameter optimization for BagSSL on this large dataset.
 - The evaluation consistently showed that aggregating more patch embeddings (e.g., 256 patches over 16) generally improved performance across datasets and methods.

3. **Whole-Image Embedding as Aggregation of Patch Embeddings:** For multi-scale pretrained baseline SSL models (e.g., VICReg), the study demonstrated that the whole-image representation obtained from a central crop is approximately equivalent to the linear aggregation (average) of multiple local patch embeddings. The cosine similarity between aggregated patch embeddings and the central crop embedding increased with the number of aggregated patches, indicating convergence.

4. **Preservation of Locality in Embedding Space:**
 - Through visualizations of cosine similarity heatmaps and k-nearest neighbors, the research showed that while the "projection" space (after the projector head) exhibits strong invariance to transformations, the "embedding" space (before the projector head) preserves significant local information.
 - Cosine similarity heatmaps indicated that for smaller patches, embedding similarities were localized to spatially proximate or visually similar features within an image. In contrast, projection similarities were broader, encompassing regions that contributed to the overall object class.
 - k-NN visualizations on CIFAR-10 patches revealed that nearest neighbors in the embedding space were consistently visually similar to the query patch, often representing the same specific "part" (e.g., a wheel component). In contrast, nearest neighbors in the projection space, while belonging to the same object category, could represent different, less visually similar "parts" (e.g., a door for a wheel query). This suggested a collapse of local details in the projection space, while the embedding space maintained fine-grained local distinctions.

5. **Enhancement of Baseline Models through Patch Aggregation:**
 - **Global Aggregation:** Re-evaluating existing state-of-the-art SSL models (SimCLR, TCR, VICReg, BYOL) on ImageNet using global linear aggregation of patch embeddings consistently improved their linear evaluation performance by over 1% compared to the standard central crop evaluation.
 - **Local Aggregation:** An advanced aggregation technique involving local averaging and concatenation of patch embeddings further boosted performance. On CIFAR-10, this local aggregation method achieved a notable increase in k-NN accuracy, surpassing the linear evaluation accuracy of baseline models by a larger margin.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research paper offers a significant contribution to the field of self-supervised learning by providing a new perspective on the mechanisms underlying the success of joint-embedding SSL methods.

1. **Enhanced Theoretical Understanding:** By formally connecting joint-embedding SSL to the modeling of image patch co-occurrence, the work supplements the widely accepted "invariance perspective." This deeper theoretical understanding can guide future research in SSL, potentially leading to the development of new algorithms and architectures that explicitly leverage co-occurrence statistics. It demystifies aspects of how SSL constructs its robust representations.

2. **Bridge between Computer Vision and NLP:** Establishing a formal analogy between image SSL and word embedding techniques like Word2Vec creates a conceptual bridge between computer vision and natural language processing. This interdisciplinary connection could foster cross-pollination of ideas and methodologies, inspiring novel approaches in both domains.

3. **Practical Performance Improvements:** The demonstration that aggregated patch embeddings, particularly through local aggregation, can consistently improve the performance of various state-of-the-art SSL models provides an immediate practical benefit. This offers a straightforward method for practitioners to enhance the quality of learned representations for downstream tasks without re-training models from scratch.

4. **Simplified Training Paradigms:** The finding that fixed-scale patch training (BagSSL) can achieve competitive results suggests that the complexity introduced by multi-scale cropping augmentation might not always be strictly necessary. This could simplify the training process for some SSL applications, potentially reducing computational overhead or hyperparameter tuning efforts.

5. **Nuanced Understanding of Representation Spaces:** The distinction between the locality-preserving embedding space and the more invariant projection space provides a nuanced view of the hierarchical information captured by SSL models. This insight could be valuable for tasks requiring either global, invariant features (from projection) or fine-grained, spatially sensitive information (from embedding), enabling more targeted use of different layers of SSL models.

6. **Future Research Directions:** The paper also highlights several limitations and areas for future work, such as a more thorough understanding of the projector head's role, optimization of two-crop training for patch co-occurrence, and extensive hyperparameter tuning for BagSSL on large datasets. These open questions indicate fertile ground for continued research to further refine and optimize SSL methodologies.
