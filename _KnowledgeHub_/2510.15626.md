# Adaptive Legged Locomotion via Online Learning for Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> An adaptive control algorithm is introduced for quadrupedal robots, which combines online learning of residual dynamics with Model Predictive Control. This approach allows robots to adapt to unknown payloads and varying terrains, yielding up to 67% better trajectory tracking accuracy in simulations and enabling performance in scenarios where non-adaptive methods fail.

## Problem

- Legged robots face challenges in achieving robust and adaptive control in real-world environments due to uncertainties such as imperfect models, unknown payloads, and unpredictable terrains.
- Current state-of-the-art control methods like Reinforcement Learning and Robust Control have limitations related to extensive offline training, sim-to-real gaps, conservatism, or restrictive assumptions about uncertainty structures.
- Enabling autonomous legged robot deployment in complex tasks requires continuous online adaptation to changes in both internal robot dynamics and external environmental disturbances.

## Method

- A two-module control architecture integrates ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) with an Online Learning module designed to estimate unknown residual dynamics in real-time.
- Residual dynamics, encompassing modeling errors and external disturbances, are approximated using random Fourier features within a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) to capture complex, non-linear uncertainties.
- The parameters of the approximated residual dynamics are updated online via an efficient Online Gradient Descent (OGD) mechanism, which infers the true residual from observed state transitions.

## Results

- In Gazebo simulations, the algorithm demonstrated up to **67%** improvement in trajectory tracking accuracy over a Nominal MPC and enabled successful task completion in scenarios with large external forces (e.g., 12g equivalent payload) where non-adaptive controllers failed.
- The proposed method outperformed a Heuristic L1 MPC by up to **21%**, showing faster adaptation to disturbances in complex terrain and force conditions.
- MuJoCo "sim2sim" tests confirmed stable locomotion and accurate trajectory tracking for quadrupedal robots with unknown payloads (up to 8kg) and varying ground friction, in contrast to the Nominal MPC which failed under heavy loads.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Approximating complex, non-linear residual dynamics with random Fourier features in an RKHS offers expressive uncertainty modeling while maintaining computational efficiency for real-time control.
> - Directly integrating this one-shot online learning into the MPC formulation allows for continuous adaptation to disturbances without the need for extensive offline training or prior system identification.
> - The algorithm provides theoretical guarantees, including sublinear dynamic regret, indicating that its long-term performance asymptotically converges to that of an optimal clairvoyant controller.
