# Adaptive Legged Locomotion via Online Learning for Model Predictive Control

> [!summary] Summary
> An adaptive control algorithm is introduced for quadrupedal robots, which combines online learning of residual dynamics with Model Predictive Control. This approach allows robots to adapt to unknown payloads and varying terrains, yielding up to 67% better trajectory tracking accuracy in simulations and enabling performance in scenarios where non-adaptive methods fail.

## Problem

- Legged robots face challenges in achieving robust and adaptive control in real-world environments due to uncertainties such as imperfect models, unknown payloads, and unpredictable terrains.
- Current state-of-the-art control methods like Reinforcement Learning and Robust Control have limitations related to extensive offline training, sim-to-real gaps, conservatism, or restrictive assumptions about uncertainty structures.
- Enabling autonomous legged robot deployment in complex tasks requires continuous online adaptation to changes in both internal robot dynamics and external environmental disturbances.

## Method

- A two-module control architecture integrates ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) with an Online Learning module designed to estimate unknown residual dynamics in real-time.
- Residual dynamics, encompassing modeling errors and external disturbances, are approximated using random Fourier features within a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) to capture complex, non-linear uncertainties.
- The parameters of the approximated residual dynamics are updated online via an efficient Online Gradient Descent (OGD) mechanism, which infers the true residual from observed state transitions.

## Results

- In Gazebo simulations, the algorithm demonstrated up to **67%** improvement in trajectory tracking accuracy over a Nominal MPC and enabled successful task completion in scenarios with large external forces (e.g., 12g equivalent payload) where non-adaptive controllers failed.
- The proposed method outperformed a Heuristic L1 MPC by up to **21%**, showing faster adaptation to disturbances in complex terrain and force conditions.
- MuJoCo "sim2sim" tests confirmed stable locomotion and accurate trajectory tracking for quadrupedal robots with unknown payloads (up to 8kg) and varying ground friction, in contrast to the Nominal MPC which failed under heavy loads.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Approximating complex, non-linear residual dynamics with random Fourier features in an RKHS offers expressive uncertainty modeling while maintaining computational efficiency for real-time control.
> - Directly integrating this one-shot online learning into the MPC formulation allows for continuous adaptation to disturbances without the need for extensive offline training or prior system identification.
> - The algorithm provides theoretical guarantees, including sublinear dynamic regret, indicating that its long-term performance asymptotically converges to that of an optimal clairvoyant controller.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robotics is rapidly advancing, with robots demonstrating impressive agility and dynamic locomotion. However, deploying these robots in real-world environments for tasks like search and rescue, payload delivery, or industrial inspection necessitates robust and adaptive control in the face of significant uncertainties. These uncertainties stem from imperfect robot models, unknown payloads, and diverse, unpredictable terrains with varying friction and elevation.

Current state-of-the-art methods to address these challenges generally fall into three categories:

- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** RL algorithms have achieved remarkable results in dynamic legged locomotion. However, they typically require extensive offline training in high-fidelity simulators, leading to a "sim-to-real" gap when transferring policies to physical hardware. While techniques like domain randomization or environment encoders attempt to mitigate this, they can trade optimality for robustness or require complex offline data collection.
- **Robust Control:** These methods focus on ensuring stability and performance even in the presence of worst-case disturbances. While providing guarantees, they often result in conservative control policies and can be computationally expensive, limiting their real-time application in dynamic systems like legged robots.
- **Adaptive Control:** Adaptive control methods update controller parameters online to compensate for uncertainties, often assuming parametric forms of unknown dynamics (e.g., vector-valued or linear functions of state). While more flexible than robust control, their applicability can be limited by these specific assumptions about the uncertainty structure.

This paper positions itself within the adaptive control paradigm but significantly extends it by integrating online learning with Model Predictive Control (MPC). It leverages the strengths of online learning algorithms, particularly those based on online convex optimization (OCO), which quantify performance using "regret" – the suboptimality against an optimal clairvoyant controller. Previous work in online learning for control has either focused on directly updating control inputs (which can be sensitive to tuning) or learning disturbance models for use in MPC.

The key differentiation of this work is its approach to learning the *residual dynamics* online. Unlike RL, it requires no offline training, offering "one-shot" online learning. In contrast to robust control, it adapts to the actual disturbance realization rather than assuming the worst-case. Crucially, it moves beyond the typical parametric assumptions of adaptive control by learning a rich functional representation of residual dynamics within a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS) using random Fourier features. This allows for a more expressive model of uncertainty without requiring offline system identification of basis functions, bridging the gap between model-based control and advanced function approximation techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is to enable legged robots, specifically quadrupeds, to autonomously perform complex tasks in real-world environments characterized by significant unknown uncertainties. Such tasks necessitate accurate and efficient reference trajectory tracking despite unknown dynamics (e.g., changes in robot mass/inertia due to unknown payloads) and external disturbances (e.g., varying terrain friction, rough surfaces, external forces).

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop an Adaptive Control Algorithm:** To create a novel control algorithm that allows legged robots to adapt online to unknown residual dynamics (modeling errors and external disturbances) in a self-supervised manner.
2. **Integrate Online Learning with MPC:** To combine the predictive capabilities of Model Predictive Control with a real-time online learning module that continuously updates its understanding of the unknown dynamics.
3. **Utilize Expressive Function Approximation:** To employ random Fourier features to approximate the residual dynamics in Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Spaces (RKHS), enabling the learning of complex, non-linear uncertainties while maintaining computational efficiency for real-time control.
4. **Achieve Asymptotic Optimality:** To provide theoretical guarantees for the algorithm's performance, specifically demonstrating sublinear dynamic regret, meaning its long-term performance asymptotically converges to that of an optimal clairvoyant controller that knows the disturbances a priori.
5. **Validate in Challenging Simulations:** To thoroughly validate the proposed algorithm in high-fidelity simulation environments (Gazebo and MuJoCo) under a variety of realistic and demanding scenarios, including unknown external forces, varying terrains (flat, slope, rough), unknown payloads, and changing ground friction coefficients.

In essence, the authors aim to deliver a control algorithm that is both theoretically sound (asymptotically optimal) and practically effective (real-time, adaptive, robust) for legged locomotion under realistic and complex uncertainties.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed algorithm for Adaptive Legged Locomotion via Online Learning and Model Predictive Control (Algorithm 1) is characterized by a tightly integrated, two-module architecture: a Model Predictive Control (MPC) module and an Online Learning of Residual Dynamics module (Figure 2).

**System Dynamics Formulation:**
The methodology begins by modeling the quadruped's dynamics using a single-rigid body model (Eq. 1). This is then reformulated into a control-affine system (Eq. 2) and discretized (Eq. 3). A critical component is `h(z)`, which represents the unknown residual dynamics—encompassing modeling errors and external disturbances (forces `f_u` and torques `τ_u`). This `h(z)` is defined as a function of relevant state and control features `z` (e.g., body velocity, angular velocity, and contact forces).

**Model Predictive Control (MPC) Module:**
At each time step `t`, the MPC module is responsible for computing the optimal control input `u_t`. It does this by solving a constrained optimization problem (Eq. 4a-4c) over a finite look-ahead horizon `N`. The key distinction from a nominal MPC is that it incorporates an *estimated* model of the residual dynamics, `h_hat(z_k; α_hat)`, into its prediction model for future states (`x_{k+1}`). The cost function `c_k(x_k, u_k)` typically penalizes deviations from a reference trajectory and control effort. The control inputs `u_k` are subject to physical constraints (e.g., friction cones, actuator limits).

**Online Learning of Residual Dynamics Module:**
This module is responsible for continuously updating the estimate `h_hat(z_k; α_hat)` of the unknown residual dynamics.
1. **Function Approximation via Random Fourier Features:** The core idea is to approximate the unknown function `h(·)` within a Reproducing Kernel Hilbert Space (RKHS). To achieve computational efficiency while retaining expressiveness, `h(·)` is approximated as a finite-dimensional linear combination of random Fourier features: `h_hat(·; α) = (1/M) * sum(Φ(·, θ_i) * α_i)`.
 - `M` is the number of random Fourier features.
 - `Φ(z, θ_i)` is the feature map, generated by randomly sampling parameters `θ_i` (comprising `w_i` and `b_i`) from a base measure `ν`.
 - `α_i` are the parameters (weights) that are learned online.
 Proposition 1 guarantees that this approximation can achieve a uniform approximation error of `O(1/√M)`, indicating the expressiveness of this approach.
2. **Online Least-Squares Estimation via Online Gradient Descent (OGD):** After the MPC module applies `u_t` and the system evolves to `x_{t+1}`, the true residual dynamics `h(z_t)` at that instant can be inferred using the observed state `x_{t+1}` and the known nominal dynamics (Eq. 3). This observed `h(z_t)` is then used to update the parameters `α_t` of `h_hat`.
 - An estimation loss function `l_t(α_hat_t)` is formulated, measuring the squared difference between the inferred `h(z_t)` and the current estimate `h_hat(z_t)`.
 - The gradient `∇_t` of this loss function with respect to `α_hat_t` is calculated.
 - The parameters are updated using a gradient descent step: `α'_t+1 = α_t - η∇_t`, where `η` is the learning rate.
 - Finally, `α'_t+1` is projected back onto a compact domain `D` (e.g., `||α_i|| <= B_h`), though the paper notes this projection step was not explicitly applied in experiments, likely implying `D` was sufficiently large or not strictly enforced.
 Proposition 2 states that this OGD-based estimation achieves an `O(√T)` regret bound, meaning it asymptotically performs as well as the best fixed parameter `α*` chosen in hindsight.

**Regret Guarantee:**
The overall algorithm (Algorithm 1) benefits from a strong theoretical guarantee: Theorem 1 demonstrates that it achieves sublinear dynamic regret, `O(T^(3/4))`, in the absence of unmodeled noise. This ensures that the algorithm's cumulative suboptimality against a hypothetical clairvoyant controller that always knows the true `h(·)` grows slower than linearly with the total time horizon `T`, implying asymptotic optimality (i.e., `Regret_D_T / T → 0` as `T → ∞`).

**Implementation Details:**
In the numerical experiments, the MPC runs at 200 Hz, with a look-ahead horizon `N=20` (0.6s). The features `z_t` for learning include body velocity, Euler angles, angular velocity, and the product of the contact Jacobian and control input. Fifty random Fourier features (`M=50`) are used, with a learning rate `η=0.003`. The non-linear programming problem for MPC is constructed using CasADi and solved by IPOPT. The online update for `α` is computationally lightweight due to the linearity of `h_hat` with respect to `α`, allowing the system to maintain real-time control frequencies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed algorithm was rigorously evaluated through simulations in two high-fidelity physics engines: Gazebo and MuJoCo, using the Unitree Go2 quadruped robot model.

**A. Gazebo Simulations:**
- **Scenarios:** The robot was tasked with tracking a reference trajectory ( to at 0.3m height) across flat terrain (0.75m/s), 20° slope terrain (0.75m/s), and rough terrain with 0.25m height variation (0.5m/s). Unknown external forces `f_u` (up to 12g, where `g` is the gravity vector) were applied as disturbances.
- **Benchmarks:** Compared against a "Nominal MPC" (ignoring uncertainty) and a "Heuristic L1 MPC" (estimating a constant uncertainty vector `h_bar`).
- **Results (Table I, Figure 3):**
 - **No Disturbance (`f_u = 0`):** All methods performed similarly, indicating the nominal model is sufficient without uncertainty.
 - **Significant Improvement over Nominal MPC:** Algorithm 1 consistently demonstrated substantial performance gains in tracking accuracy when disturbances were present. It achieved up to a **67% improvement** in tracking performance (e.g., in slope terrain with `f_u = 8g`).
 - **Robustness to Failure:** Crucially, Algorithm 1 enabled the quadruped to successfully reach the goal position in scenarios where the Nominal MPC completely failed (e.g., with `f_u = 12g` in flat/slope terrains due to heavy load, or `f_u = 2[||g||, 0, ||g||]^T` in rough terrain, causing Nominal MPC to stall).
 - **Superiority over L1-MPC:** Algorithm 1 also outperformed the L1-MPC, particularly in more complex terrain and force conditions. It showed up to a **21% improvement** (e.g., in rough terrain with `f_u = 2[||g||, 0, ||g||]^T`). Qualitative results (Figure 3) indicated that Algorithm 1 exhibited faster adaptation and response to disturbances compared to L1-MPC, leading to better tracking in specific directions (e.g., z-direction in flat/slope, x-direction in rough terrain).
 - **Learned Residuals:** Analysis of the learned residual forces and torques (Figure 4) confirmed that the online learning module effectively identified the dominant unknown dynamics, specifically the z-direction force component of `f_u`.

**B. MuJoCo Simulations:**
- **Scenarios:** A "sim2sim" setting using the high-fidelity MuJoCo simulator, testing flat terrains with constant and time-varying friction coefficients. Unknown payloads (0kg, 4kg, 8kg) were added, introducing time-varying disturbances in both forces and torques due to changes in mass, inertia, and ground interaction.
- **Results (Figure 5, Figure 6):**
 - Algorithm 1 successfully enabled the quadruped to maintain stable locomotion, track the desired 0.5m/s velocity, and keep its body height at 0.3m across all payload conditions (0kg, 4kg, 8kg) in both constant and varying friction terrains.
 - In contrast, the Nominal MPC showed significantly larger tracking errors in position (z-direction) and velocity (x-direction) and critically *failed* under the 8kg payload condition.

**C. Failure Cases:**
The authors acknowledge limitations, noting that even with online adaptation, the method (both theirs and L1-MPC) may fail under extremely challenging conditions, such as very large forces in rough terrain (over 4g in Gazebo) or 8kg payload with rapid friction changes in MuJoCo. These failures are attributed to difficulties in stable walking or learning residual dynamics amidst sudden changes in foothold positions or unexpected contact events.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of adaptive control for legged robots, particularly in addressing the pervasive challenge of real-world uncertainty. The proposed algorithm holds substantial significance and potential impact for several reasons:

1. **Bridging Theory and Practice:** The work provides a theoretically rigorous framework with a **sublinear dynamic regret guarantee**, assuring asymptotic optimality. This is a crucial contribution, offering strong performance assurances that are often lacking in heuristic or purely data-driven methods, while simultaneously demonstrating impressive practical performance in complex simulations.
2. **Enhanced Autonomy and Robustness:** By enabling quadrupeds to adapt online to unknown payloads, varying terrains, and external forces, the algorithm significantly enhances their robustness and autonomy. This is critical for future applications where robots must operate reliably in unstructured and unpredictable environments without human intervention or extensive pre-calibration.
3. **One-Shot Online Learning:** The "one-shot" online learning approach, which requires no offline training, offers immense practical advantages. It eliminates the need for time-consuming and expensive offline data collection and simulator training inherent in many reinforcement learning methods, making deployment faster and more flexible across different robots and environments.
4. **Expressive Uncertainty Modeling:** The use of random Fourier features to approximate residual dynamics in RKHS allows for learning rich, non-linear functional forms of uncertainty. This is a step beyond traditional adaptive control methods that often assume simpler parametric forms, enabling the robot to adapt to more complex and realistic unmodeled dynamics.
5. **Computational Efficiency:** Despite its sophistication, the online learning module is designed to be lightweight, integrating seamlessly into the real-time MPC loop. This is demonstrated by maintaining a 200 Hz control frequency with standard computational resources, proving its feasibility for onboard implementation in resource-constrained robotic systems.
6. **Paving the Way for Future Robotics:** This research contributes to a paradigm shift towards truly adaptive and intelligent robotic systems. The ability to learn and adapt to unknown environmental and internal dynamics on-the-fly opens doors for advanced applications in disaster response, logistics, exploration, and dynamic manufacturing, where robots can continuously improve their performance based on real-world interactions.

In conclusion, this paper offers a robust and theoretically sound solution for adaptive legged locomotion, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in autonomous navigation under uncertainty. Its blend of online learning, model predictive control, and advanced function approximation with strong guarantees sets a new benchmark for developing resilient and adaptable robotic systems.
