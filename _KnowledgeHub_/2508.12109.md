# Simple o3: Towards Interleaved Vision-Language Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Simple o3 is an open-source framework designed to enhance Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by enabling dynamic, interleaved vision-language reasoning through the use of visual tools. It includes a scalable data synthesis pipeline to create a high-quality dataset, TWI-Tools-146K, and demonstrates superior performance across diverse multimodal benchmarks, including a 27-point lead over GPT-4o on MME reasoning and a 12.9% improvement on VStarBench.

## Problem

- Existing MLLMs struggled with long Chain-of-Thought reasoning in multimodal scenarios due to fixed visual perception and the separation of perception from reasoning.
- A lack of scalable data generation pipelines and systematic methodologies hindered the development of tool-augmented reasoning for hierarchical task decomposition in multimodal settings.
- There was insufficient understanding of how different visual tools and varying input resolutions impact MLLM performance in interleaved vision-language reasoning.

## Method

- Simple o3 introduces an end-to-end open-source framework that integrates ==dynamic visual tools== (cropping, zooming, reusing) into MLLMs via supervised fine-tuning to enable iterative "thinking with images."
- It develops an automated, scalable ==data synthesis pipeline== to generate high-quality interleaved vision-language reasoning chains, curating the ==TWI-Tools-146K== dataset from diverse sources.
- The framework employs a ==multi-step inference process== where the model iteratively generates reasoning, executes visual tool commands (e.g., cropping images), and appends the transformed visual input as new observations.

## Results

- Simple o3 achieved **702.1** points on the MME reasoning subset, outperforming its base model by nearly **50** points and GPT-4o by **27** points, demonstrating enhanced multimodal reasoning.
- The framework showed substantial improvements in fine-grained perception (**+7.4%** on HR-Bench 4K, **+12.9%** on VStarBench) and spatial reasoning (**+16.6%** on MMVet, **+10.3%** on LogicVista).
- It demonstrated improved robustness by reducing hallucination rates by **1.6%** on POPE and **2.3%** on HallusionBench, indicating better grounding in visual reality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic interaction with visual inputs through tools like image cropping (`focus_area`) and re-attending to the original image (`reuse`) significantly enhances both fine-grained perception and complex multimodal reasoning capabilities.
> - A scalable, automated data synthesis pipeline using an MLLM orchestrator and a two-step verification module is effective for generating high-quality tool-augmented reasoning data, circumventing the need for expensive human annotation or RL.
> - Training with diverse, heterogeneous datasets (e.g., mathematical reasoning, perceptual reasoning) is crucial for fostering higher-order iterative reasoning, improving logical inter-object relations and strategic planning.
> - Optimal `focus_area` implementation involves image cropping during inference, and while high input resolution is beneficial for perception-oriented tasks, it can sometimes be detrimental to certain reasoning tasks due to an "explosion of visual tokens."

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "Simple o3" is situated at the crucial intersection of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and advanced reasoning capabilities, specifically extending "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) to multimodal scenarios.

**Background:**
Recent years have seen remarkable progress in MLLMs, enabling models to perform tasks ranging from image captioning to visual question answering (VQA) by integrating visual encoders with powerful large language models (LLMs). Concurrently, LLMs have been exploring "slow-thinking" or deliberative reasoning processes, inspired by human cognition, through techniques like CoT prompting.

**The Gap Identified by the Authors:**
Despite these advancements, the authors highlight a significant gap: the underexplored nature of *long Chain-of-Thought* in multimodal scenarios, particularly those involving *interleaved visual-language reasoning pathways*. Existing MLLMs often face limitations:
1. **Fixed Visual Perception:** Many MLLMs use visual encoders constrained by fixed input resolutions, leading to only partial capture of visual features or difficulties with fine-grained details in high-resolution images. While dynamic resolution strategies exist, they don't fully address the *reasoning capacity* limitations.
2. **Separation of Perception and Reasoning:** Early MLLMs often adopted a two-stage training paradigm (pretraining then instruction fine-tuning) that inherently separated perception from reasoning, hindering deep multimodal understanding.
3. **Lack of Tool-Augmented Reasoning:** While tool-augmented reasoning has shown promise in language-only domains, a systematic methodology to activate such capabilities in multimodal settings for hierarchical task decomposition (like humans revisiting visual stimuli) has been missing.
4. **Data Scarcity for Tool Use:** Current approaches for eliciting tool-use behaviors in MLLMs rely heavily on resource-intensive Reinforcement Learning (RL) or costly human annotation, lacking scalable pipelines for generating high-quality interleaved vision-language reasoning data.
5. **Underexplored Tool Selection and Resolution Impact:** There is a lack of systematic examination of which tools effectively enhance multimodal reasoning and how input resolution affects interleaved vision-language reasoning.

**Inspiration and "Simple o3"'s Contribution:**
A critical inspiration for this work is OpenAI's groundbreaking **o3 model**, which introduced the concept of "thinking with images." This iterative paradigm allows a model to transform images (e.g., cropping, zooming) while simultaneously performing linguistic reasoning to plan subsequent visual operations, thereby bridging continuous visual and discrete linguistic reasoning. "Simple o3" aims to reproduce and significantly extend this paradigm in an open-source and computationally affordable manner.

By proposing "Simple o3," the authors directly address the aforementioned gaps. They introduce an end-to-end framework that integrates *dynamic tool interactions* (cropping, zooming, reusing) into interleaved vision-language reasoning via supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Crucially, they develop a *scalable data synthesis pipeline* to generate the necessary high-quality reasoning chains, releasing the resulting TWI-Tools-146K dataset. Furthermore, they provide the *first in-depth analysis* of various interleaved reasoning strategies, tool selection, and input resolution impacts, offering crucial insights for the future development of "thinking with images" research. This work positions itself as a significant advancement towards enabling MLLMs to perform more human-like, deliberative, and interactive multimodal reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objectives of the "Simple o3" research are driven by the identified limitations in current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) regarding their long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and interleaved vision-language reasoning capabilities. The primary motivations and objectives are:

- **Objective 1: Develop an End-to-End Framework for Interleaved Vision-Language Reasoning.** The core goal is to create "Simple o3," a powerful yet computationally affordable open-source framework that integrates dynamic visual tool interactions into MLLMs. This framework aims to enable models to "think with images" in an iterative, human-like manner, extending beyond simple perception-then-reasoning sequences.
 - **Motivation:** Existing MLLMs struggle with complex, multi-step multimodal reasoning tasks that require dynamic interaction with visual input, similar to how humans might re-examine or manipulate an image to solve a problem. The success of OpenAI's proprietary o3 model highlighted the potential of this paradigm, motivating the creation of an accessible, open-source alternative.

- **Objective 2: Create a Scalable Data Synthesis Pipeline for High-Quality Interleaved Reasoning Data.** The research aims to design and implement an automated pipeline for generating interleaved vision-language reasoning chains, complete with executable visual operations and rigorous verification.
 - **Motivation:** A significant hurdle for training MLLMs in tool-augmented reasoning is the lack of high-quality, large-scale training data. Current methods relying on expensive human annotation or resource-intensive Reinforcement Learning (RL) are not scalable. A scalable synthesis pipeline is essential to overcome this data bottleneck and foster broader research.

- **Objective 3: Provide a Comprehensive Open-Source Dataset (TWI-Tools-146K).** A direct outcome of the data synthesis pipeline is the creation and release of a high-quality, open-source dataset featuring interleaved image-text reasoning data with executable function calls.
 - **Motivation:** The release of such a dataset will democratize research in this advanced area, allowing other researchers to train and evaluate models on complex multimodal reasoning tasks without needing to replicate the expensive data generation process.

- **Objective 4: Systematically Analyze the Impact of Visual Tools and Input Resolution.** The paper seeks to conduct an in-depth analysis of how different visual tools (cropping, zooming, reusing) and varying input resolutions influence MLLM performance in interleaved vision-language reasoning.
 - **Motivation:** While the concept of "thinking with images" is gaining traction, there is limited understanding of the specific contributions and optimal deployment of different visual manipulation tools. Furthermore, the interplay between visual input resolution and reasoning effectiveness remains largely unexplored. This analysis is crucial for optimizing future multimodal model designs and training strategies.

- **Objective 5: Demonstrate Superior Performance Across Diverse Benchmarks.** A key objective is to empirically validate "Simple o3"'s enhanced reasoning and perceptual capabilities against state-of-the-art proprietary and open-source MLLMs across a wide array of multimodal benchmarks.
 - **Motivation:** Proving "Simple o3"'s effectiveness and robustness is essential to establish its value as a novel paradigm for advancing multimodal reasoning and fine-grained perception.

In essence, the motivation for "Simple o3" stems from the desire to push MLLMs beyond superficial understanding to deep, deliberative, and interactive reasoning by integrating dynamic visual tool use, while simultaneously addressing the practical challenges of data generation and providing fundamental insights into the underlying mechanisms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Simple o3" framework is meticulously designed as an end-to-end system for learning tool-augmented interleaved image-text reasoning, comprising a scalable data generation pipeline, a novel training methodology, and a multi-step inference process.

#### 3.1. Scalable Data Generation Pipeline

The cornerstone of Simple o3 is its automated, high-quality data synthesis pipeline, designed to create interleaved vision-language reasoning chains in an "observe-reason-act" cycle.

- **Reasoning Path Generation:**
 - An MLLM (specifically, `gemini-2.5-Flash-Preview-05-20` in non-thinking mode for efficiency) acts as an orchestrator. Given a question-image pair, it iteratively generates atomic reasoning steps, visual operation plans, and corresponding visual tool commands.
 - Each step `s_t` in the reasoning chain `S` includes reasoning content (`R_t`), a tool command (`C_t`), and the resulting transformed image (`I_t`). This process is conditioned on the historical reasoning path (`H_{t-1}`).
 - **Structured Tagging:** Special XML-like tokens are used to delineate different components: `<reasoning>` for atomic reasoning steps and visual planning, `<function>` for tool invocations (JSON format specifying `name` and `arguments`), and `<observation>` for embedding transformed images. This structure ensures a parseable and complete record of multimodal reasoning.
 - **Visual Tools:** Three predefined visual tools are available:
 - `focus_area`: Returns an image with a drawn bounding box (bbox) around a specified region.
 - `zoom_in`: Magnifies the entire image area through interpolation.
 - `reuse`: Outputs the original image, effectively enhancing visual memory or allowing re-evaluation.

- **Two-step Verification Module:**
 - This module ensures the quality and consistency of the generated data.
 - **Geometric Validity (for `focus_area`):** Uses `gemini-2.5-flash-lite-preview-06-17` to verify if the bounding box either precisely matches or fully contains the target entity.
 - **Semantic Consistency (for `zoom_in` and `reuse`):** Verifies if the tool command (`C_t`) semantically aligns with the visual planning description (`p_t`).
 - **Answer Verification:** Compares the system's generated answer against the ground truth using `Qwen3-turbo`.
 - Samples are generated for a maximum of two attempts to enhance validity, filtering out low-quality or inconsistent examples.

- **TWI-Tools-146K Data Curation:**
 - The dataset is curated from diverse sources to maximize distribution and generalization: MATHV360K, LLaVA-CoT-100K, and ArxivQA.
 - Specific sub-tasks (e.g., geometry math problems) are excluded to focus on relevant reasoning types.
 - An initial 150K candidate entries are generated, yielding approximately 100K high-quality samples after rigorous verification. These samples cover perceptual VQA, knowledge-based reasoning, chart analysis, and logical reasoning.

#### 3.2. Training Incorporating Image Masking

The generated multi-turn dialogue data is adapted for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with a specific loss computation mechanism.

- **Data Reformatting:** Multi-turn "user-assistant-tool" dialogues are consolidated into single-turn "user-assistant" sequences.
- **Coordinate System Alignment:** All spatial coordinates are converted to the target model's native representation format to resolve discrepancies.
- **Modality-Aware Masking:** A binary mask `M_t` is applied during loss computation:
 - `M_t = 1` for textual tokens (loss computed).
 - `M_t = 0` for visual tokens (loss ignored).
 - This ensures that the model attends to visual data for contextual understanding but restricts gradient updates solely to textual outputs, which are the generative components. Visual tokens (demarcated by `<|image pad|>` and similar special tokens) serve as context.
- **Training Objective:** A masked cross-entropy loss is employed, optimizing textual generation while zeroing out gradients for visual tokens.
- **Implementation Details:** Full parameter fine-tuning is performed on `Qwen2.5-VL-7B`. Key parameters include a maximum sequence length of 8,192 tokens, flexible input image resolutions (4x28x28 to 1024x28x28 pixels), a cosine learning rate scheduler, 1 epoch of training, and a batch size of 8. The vision encoder and multimodal projector are kept frozen to leverage their pre-trained capabilities.

#### 3.3. Multi-step Inference with Tool Interaction

After SFT, the model becomes proficient in generating structured formats, enabling dynamic tool usage and multi-step interleaved reasoning during inference.

- **Iterative Process:** At each reasoning step `t`, the model generates reasoning content (`<reasoning>...</reasoning>`).
- **Tool Command Extraction:** Upon detecting a `</function>` tag, the system extracts the JSON-formatted tool command (`C_t`) between the `<function>` tags.
- **Visual Operation:** Crucially, during inference, the `focus_area` tool *crops* the image based on the returned coordinates, rather than just drawing a bounding box (as done during data generation for robustness). This processed image (`I_t`) is then embedded as visual tokens (`<observation>...</observation>`) and appended to the model's history for the next step.
- **Termination:** The cycle repeats until the model generates an answer (`<answer>...</answer>`) or hits a predefined round limit. This dynamic interaction allows the model to refine its visual perception based on its linguistic reasoning and vice versa.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

"Simple o3" demonstrates substantial improvements across a diverse set of benchmarks, validating its effectiveness in advancing multimodal reasoning and perception.

- **Overall Performance Superiority:** Simple o3 consistently outperforms its base model, `Qwen2.5-VL-7B`, as well as competitive open-source MLLMs and even proprietary models like `GPT-4o` and `GPT-4o-mini` on several key metrics.

- **Enhanced Multimodal Reasoning:**
 - On the **MME reasoning subset**, Simple o3 achieves a remarkable 702.1 points, outperforming its base model by nearly 50 points and GPT-4o by 27 points. This strongly supports the effectiveness of the "thinking with images" paradigm, where increasing attended visual tokens directly enhances reasoning.
 - For **CharXiv** and **VisuLogic** benchmarks, it shows improvements of 4.2% and 3.3% respectively, indicating its ability to handle abstract entities and complex, multi-step logical reasoning.
 - Significant gains of 16.6% on **MMVet (spatial reasoning)** and 10.3% on **LogicVista (spatial reasoning)** highlight Simple o3's superior understanding of relationships between entities and its unified comprehension of object locations, critical for spatial reasoning tasks.

- **Superior Fine-Grained and Coarse-Grained Perception:**
 - **Fine-grained perception** (HR-Bench 4K, VStarBench) sees substantial improvements of approximately 7.4% and 12.9% respectively, outperforming existing approaches including RL-based methods like DeepEyes and Chain-of-Focus. This shows the model's ability to focus on and interpret small, critical details within high-resolution images.
 - For **coarse-grained perception** (COCO Caption), Simple o3 improves performance by 3.1 points, suggesting that reusing the original image enhances holistic detailed comprehension.

- **General VQA and Chart Comprehension:**
 - **ScienceQA:** Achieves 90.0%, a 1.3% improvement, reflecting better integration of multidisciplinary knowledge.
 - **RealWorldQA:** Improves by 1.1%, demonstrating enhanced real-world applicability and generalization.
 - **MMStar** (+2.6 points) and **MMVet** (+1.5 points) show exceptional effectiveness in fusing visual-language data and handling complex multimodal tasks.
 - On **InfoVQA** (chart comprehension), Simple o3 records a 2.0% improvement, indicating significantly enhanced capabilities in parsing key information from charts. Performance on DocVQA remains competitive.

- **Reduced Hallucination:** Simple o3 shows improvements on hallucination benchmarks, specifically 1.6% on POPE and 2.3% on HallusionBench, indicating a stronger grounding in visual reality.

- **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Impact of Visual Tools:**
 - The `reuse` operation provides significant gains (e.g., +31.2 MME, +4.8% VStarBench), proving that re-attending to the original image via additional visual tokens greatly enhances perception and reasoning.
 - `zoom_in` offers marginal benefits (mostly <1%), suggesting its utility is more niche.
 - `focus_area` is a critical tool, particularly for fine-grained perception (e.g., +4.9% HR-Bench 4K, +6.9% VStarBench), by enabling precise visual localization and eliminating redundant information. The synergistic use of these tools maximizes the model's reasoning potential.
 - **Impact of Heterogeneous Training Data:**
 - Adding **MATHV360K** data led to a nearly 27-point leap in MME reasoning, strengthening logical inter-object relations and strategic planning.
 - Incorporating **LLaVA-CoT-100K** (perceptual data) boosted fine-grained perception significantly.
 - **ArxivQA** (abstract diagrams) further fortified logical reasoning, adding 13.5 points to MME reasoning.
 - This demonstrates that combining diverse, heterogeneous datasets is key to enhancing higher-order iterative reasoning.

- **Further Analysis – Tool Implementation and Resolution:**
 - **`focus_area` (Cropping vs. Drawing Bbox vs. Reusing):** During inference, *image cropping* based on coordinates yielded optimal performance. This indicates that directly injecting focused visual information, even if it leads to distribution mismatch (cropping in inference vs. bbox drawing in training), effectively enhances reasoning. Drawing bounding boxes or simply reusing the original image were less effective due to redundant tokens or scattered attention.
 - **Input Resolution:** Low resolution severely impairs performance. While trained primarily on low-resolution images, the model generalizes well to higher resolutions. Medium resolution suffices for many reasoning tasks, with high resolution being especially beneficial for *perception-oriented* tasks, reducing hallucination, and improving document parsing. However, high resolution can sometimes slightly *degrade* performance on certain reasoning tasks (e.g., CharXiv), possibly due to an "explosion of visual tokens" disrupting attention allocation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Simple o3" paper represents a significant step forward in the field of multimodal AI, offering crucial contributions and insights with broad potential impact.

- **Advancement in Multimodal Reasoning:** The most direct impact of Simple o3 is its substantial advancement in MLLMs' reasoning capabilities. By effectively reproducing and extending OpenAI's "thinking with images" paradigm in an open-source framework, Simple o3 demonstrates that MLLMs can engage in complex, multi-step, deliberative reasoning processes that dynamically interact with visual information. This moves MLLMs closer to human-like cognitive abilities in problem-solving.

- **Open-Source Accessibility and Reproducibility:** The release of the "Simple o3" framework and the accompanying TWI-Tools-146K dataset is a major contribution to the research community. By providing an open-source, computationally affordable solution, the authors significantly lower the barrier to entry for researchers interested in tool-augmented multimodal reasoning. This fosters greater collaboration, innovation, and reproducibility within the field, accelerating future research and development.

- **Scalable Data Generation Paradigm:** The innovative, scalable data synthesis pipeline is a critical methodological breakthrough. It addresses the long-standing challenge of creating high-quality, large-scale training data for complex multimodal tasks, traditionally hampered by resource-intensive human annotation or RL-based methods. This pipeline provides a blueprint for generating synthetic data for various interactive AI systems, extending beyond the current visual tools.

- **Enhanced Perception and Robustness:** Simple o3's superior performance across a wide range of benchmarks, including fine-grained perception, spatial reasoning, and general VQA, demonstrates its practical utility. Its improved ability to accurately perceive subtle details, understand spatial relationships, and integrate multidisciplinary knowledge makes it a more robust and versatile MLLM. The observed reduction in hallucination further enhances its reliability.

- **Actionable Insights for Future Model Design:** The in-depth ablation studies and further analyses regarding tool selection and input resolution provide invaluable practical and theoretical insights.
 - The finding that *image cropping* during inference is optimal for `focus_area`, despite training with bounding boxes, offers critical guidance for implementing dynamic visual tools.
 - The nuanced understanding of how *input resolution* affects different types of tasks (perception vs. reasoning) highlights the importance of adaptive resolution strategies and careful token management in high-resolution scenarios. These insights will directly inform the design and optimization of future "thinking with images" models.

- **Foundation for Agentic AI Systems:** The paper explicitly mentions future work exploring more tools and extending the paradigm from vision-language *reasoning* models to vision-language *action* models. This positions Simple o3 as a foundational step towards building more sophisticated, agentic AI systems that can not only understand and reason about the world but also actively manipulate it through iterative observation and action.

In summary, "Simple o3" not only delivers a high-performing MLLM framework but also provides essential tools, data, and analytical insights that will significantly shape the trajectory of research in multimodal AI, particularly in the domain of complex, interactive reasoning.
