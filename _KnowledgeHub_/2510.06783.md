# TTRV: Test-Time Reinforcement Learning for Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> TTRV introduces the first test-time reinforcement learning framework for decoder-based Vision-Language Models, enabling unsupervised adaptation on unlabeled test data by combining frequency-based and entropy-controlled self-supervised rewards with GRPO. This method yields substantial accuracy gains, averaging 28.97% on image recognition and 17.50% on VQA for InternVL3-8B, often outperforming GPT-4o.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) remain static post-training and struggle to adapt to new or out-of-distribution data without costly, labeled fine-tuning.
- Existing reinforcement learning methods for VLM adaptation rely on labeled data or curated training splits to generate reward signals, limiting their real-world applicability.
- Many Test-Time Training (TTT) approaches are not directly applicable to autoregressive, decoder-based VLMs, which generate token sequences, or rely on class-level distributions.

## Method

- TTRV adapts pre-trained decoder-based VLMs on-the-fly at inference time using only unlabeled test data by integrating ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==.
- It generates multiple candidate responses for each input to form an empirical output distribution and derives a novel ==self-supervised reward== from this distribution.
- The reward mechanism combines a ==frequency-based signal== to encourage consistency and an ==entropy-based signal== for diversity control, guiding the VLM policy optimization via GRPO.

## Results

- TTRV consistently improved Top-1 accuracy across **16** diverse benchmarks, with average gains of **28.97%** for InternVL3-8B on image recognition and **17.50%** on VQA tasks.
- The method enabled open-source InternVL3-8B to surpass GPT-4o by an average of **2.3%** on image recognition and remain highly competitive on VQA tasks.
- Impressive gains were achieved with remarkable data efficiency, using only **20** randomly chosen test instances per dataset, with non-trivial improvements observed even with a single adaptation sample.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective unsupervised adaptation of Vision-Language Models is achievable at test-time, enabling continuous model refinement without reliance on external labels.
> - Latent visual recognition and reasoning capabilities within large pre-trained VLMs can be significantly activated and amplified through self-supervised reinforcement learning.
> - A sophisticated self-supervised reward combining consistency (frequency) and certainty (low entropy) proves effective for guiding policy optimization in autoregressive multimodal models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work sits at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Reinforcement Learning (RL) for model adaptation, and Test-Time Training (TTT). It specifically addresses a critical gap in the current capabilities of VLMs by proposing a novel framework for unsupervised, on-the-fly adaptation.

**Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** The past few years have seen an explosion in VLM capabilities, with models like CLIP, LLaVA, and GPT-4o demonstrating impressive performance across a wide array of multimodal tasks such as object recognition, visual question answering (VQA), and captioning. These models typically operate in two main paradigms: dual-encoder models (e.g., CLIP) which learn aligned representations of vision and text, and large multimodal models (LMMs) which couple vision encoders with large language models (LLMs) for open-ended generative reasoning. While powerful, current VLMs are largely "static" once trained, meaning their performance can degrade in new or out-of-distribution environments, and adapting them usually requires costly, labeled data and fine-tuning. TTRV specifically targets *decoder-based VLMs* (LMMs), which are often more challenging to adapt with traditional TTT methods designed for class-level distributions.

**Reinforcement Learning for Model Adaptation:** RL, particularly through techniques like Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), has become a cornerstone for aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with human preferences and enhancing their reasoning capabilities. More recently, rule-based methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) have extended RL's utility to improving reasoning. While RL has shown promise for VLMs (e.g., VLM-R1, Perception-R1), these approaches still predominantly rely on labeled data or curated training splits to generate reward signals. This dependence limits their real-world applicability, as human-like learning often occurs directly from unlabeled interaction with the environment.

**Test-Time Training (TTT):** TTT methods aim to adapt models at inference time using unlabeled test data, often by optimizing self-supervised objectives like entropy minimization (e.g., TENT) or auxiliary losses. While effective for unimodal and some dual-encoder VLM architectures, many TTT approaches impose architectural constraints (e.g., requiring batch normalization layers) or rely on class-level probability distributions, which are not directly available in autoregressive, decoder-based VLMs. Existing TTT for VLMs typically focuses on dual-encoders or prompt-level adaptation. The most closely related work, TTRL for LLMs, introduced test-time RL using majority voting for rewards.

**TTRV's Unique Position:** TTRV carves out a unique niche by introducing the **first test-time reinforcement learning framework for *decoder-based VLMs* that operates entirely on *unlabeled test data***. It integrates GRPO with a novel, self-supervised reward mechanism that combines frequency-based signals (from repeated model rollouts) and diversity control (via entropy regularization). This distinguishes TTRV from:
- Traditional VLM training/fine-tuning that requires labeled data.
- RL-based fine-tuning for VLMs that still relies on curated training splits or labeled feedback.
- Many TTT methods that are not directly applicable to decoder-based VLMs or rely on simpler self-supervision signals.
- Prior test-time RL work (TTRL) by extending it to multimodal models and proposing a more sophisticated reward formulation beyond naive majority voting.

By enabling VLMs to continuously refine their understanding and reasoning directly from ambiguous, unlabeled experiences at inference time, TTRV represents a significant step towards more autonomous and human-like learning in multimodal AI, bridging the gap between static pre-trained models and dynamic, adaptive intelligent systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of TTRV stem from recognized limitations in the current paradigm of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL) for their adaptation:

1. **Overcoming Static VLM Behavior:** Current VLMs, despite their advanced capabilities, largely remain static once pre-trained and instruction-tuned. Unlike humans who continuously learn and adapt from novel experiences, VLMs struggle to refine their reasoning when encountering new domains or unseen tasks without extensive, costly re-training. The objective is to imbue VLMs with a dynamic, self-improving capability at inference time.

2. **Eliminating Reliance on Labeled Data for Adaptation:** Existing RL-based methods for improving VLM performance (e.g., RLHF, DPO, GRPO for VLMs) predominantly rely on human-labeled data or carefully curated training splits to derive reward signals. This dependence is a major bottleneck, as acquiring vast amounts of high-quality labeled data is expensive, time-consuming, and often impractical in real-world "in the wild" scenarios where train-test distinctions do not naturally exist. A key motivation is to move towards a paradigm where models can learn directly from *unlabeled data*, making adaptation more scalable and aligned with continuous learning from experience.

3. **Enhancing Generalization and Robustness:** The static nature and reliance on labeled data often limit VLM performance on out-of-distribution data, adversarial examples, or entirely new domains. The objective is to develop a method that enhances the model's core visual and linguistic understanding capabilities, making it more robust and capable of generalizing across diverse and challenging settings, rather than merely adapting to a specific data distribution.

4. **Extending RL to Unsupervised Test-Time Settings for Decoder-Based VLMs:** While Test-Time Training (TTT) and some test-time RL (for LLMs) exist, applying RL to *decoder-based VLMs* in a fully unsupervised, test-time setting presents unique challenges. Decoder-based VLMs generate autoregressive token distributions, making direct application of entropy-minimization or class-probability-based objectives non-trivial. The motivation is to design an effective reward formulation that can operate directly on the empirical distribution of VLM outputs without external supervision, specifically for these more complex multimodal architectures.

In essence, TTRV aims to realize the "true promise of RL" by enabling VLMs to learn and adapt directly from raw, unlabeled inference-time interactions, much like humans continuously refine their understanding by observing and interacting with their environment. This vision seeks to transform static VLMs into dynamic, self-improving systems capable of continuous self-optimization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TTRV (Test-Time Reinforcement Learning for Vision Language Models) proposes a novel framework to adapt pre-trained decoder-based VLMs on-the-fly at inference time, utilizing only unlabeled test data. The core of its methodology involves bootstrapping an off-the-shelf VLM with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and introducing a carefully designed self-supervised reward mechanism.

The overall approach can be broken down into the following key steps, as illustrated in Figure 2 of the paper:

1. **VLM Rollouts and Empirical Distribution Generation:**
 - For each unlabeled test sample `x` (consisting of an image and a text prompt), the current VLM policy `π_θ(·|x)` is used to generate `N` candidate responses `{ˆy_1..., ˆy_N}`.
 - These `N` sampled responses induce an *empirical probability distribution* over the set of unique outputs `{˜y_1..., ˜y_M}`. This step is crucial as it creates internal "feedback" from the model's own predictions.

2. **Novel Self-Supervised Reward Formulation:** The paper introduces two complementary reward signals extracted directly from this empirical distribution, designed to guide the VLM towards more consistent and confident predictions:

 - **Frequency-Based Reward (`r1`):**
 - The intuition is that if a model repeatedly produces the same answer for a given input, that answer is likely to be correct or at least self-consistent.
 - For each unique output `˜y_m`, its empirical probability `p(˜y_m)` is calculated as the frequency of its occurrence among the `N` sampled responses.
 - The reward `r1(ˆy_j)` for an individual sampled response `ˆy_j` is then defined as `p(˜y_m)` if `ˆy_j` is `˜y_m`. This provides a "soft, probabilistic supervision signal" where more frequent responses receive higher rewards.
 - This differs from a "best-of-N" (majority voting) scheme by not discarding less frequent responses but instead rewarding them proportionally, thereby retaining uncertainty and diverse reasoning paths.

 - **Diversity Control Reward (`r2`):**
 - While the frequency-based reward encourages consistency, it's also important to ensure the model converges to a confident prediction rather than spreading its probability mass too thinly.
 - This reward is based on the Shannon entropy `H(P)` of the empirical response distribution.
 - The auxiliary reward `r2` is defined as `-H(P)`, meaning it penalizes high entropy (i.e., high dispersion or uncertainty in outputs) and rewards low entropy (i.e., high certainty and convergence). This term regulates diversity, encouraging the model to consolidate its probability mass towards stable, high-probability answers.

 - **Combined Reward (`R`):** The final reward for any sampled response `ˆy_j` is a weighted sum: `R(ˆy_j) = r1(ˆy_j) + α * r2`, where `α` is a tunable hyperparameter balancing consistency and diversity.

3. **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):**
 - The combined reward `R(ˆy_j)` is then used within the GRPO framework, a stable approach for maximizing reward while constraining policy deviation from a reference policy `π_ref` (the base VLM).
 - Instead of using raw rewards, GRPO computes an *advantage score* for each sampled response `A_i` by normalizing its reward relative to the mean and standard deviation of rewards within the sampled group. This relative advantage stabilizes optimization.
 - The model parameters `θ` are updated via gradient ascent on the expected reward, with the policy update performed via clipped importance-weighted objectives to maintain stability and KL regularization to keep the fine-tuned model close to the original.

**Key Differentiators and Innovations:**

- **Unsupervised Test-Time Adaptation:** TTRV operates entirely without labeled data or dedicated training splits at inference time, making it highly suitable for real-world deployment and continuous learning.
- **Targeting Decoder-Based VLMs:** Unlike many TTT methods designed for dual-encoder models or class-level distributions, TTRV is specifically tailored for autoregressive, decoder-based VLMs, which generate token sequences.
- **Sophisticated Reward Design:** The combination of frequency-based and entropy-based rewards provides a nuanced self-supervision signal that encourages both consistency and convergence, going beyond simpler majority voting schemes.
- **Leveraging GRPO:** The integration with GRPO ensures a stable and effective policy optimization process, even with self-generated, potentially noisy reward signals.

By continuously performing this process on incoming unlabeled test data, TTRV transforms static pre-trained VLMs into dynamic, adaptive systems capable of self-improvement and robust generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TTRV was extensively evaluated across 16 diverse datasets covering image recognition and visual question answering (VQA), utilizing various open-source VLM backbones, primarily from the InternVL family, and comparing against strong proprietary models like GPT-4o. The main findings and results are highly compelling:

1. **Consistent and Substantial Performance Gains:**
 - **Image Recognition (Table 1):** TTRV consistently boosts Top-1 accuracy across all evaluated InternVL backbones and 8 diverse image recognition benchmarks (ImageNet, ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-R, ImageNet-S, ImageNet-A, Food101, DTD, Resisc45).
 - For InternVL3-2B, average gains were **32.95%**, with individual dataset improvements up to **52.49%** (DTD) and **42.31%** (ImageNet).
 - For InternVL3-8B, average gains were **28.97%**, achieving over **99% accuracy on ImageNet** (+19.84%).
 - **Visual Question Answering (Table 2):** TTRV also delivered consistent gains across 8 VQA datasets (MathVerse, MathVista, SEED, MME, RealWorldQA, Capture, CRPE, AI2D).
 - For InternVL3-2B, average gains were **9.69%**, with individual improvements up to **28.07%** (AI2D) and **23.86%** (SEED).
 - For InternVL3-8B, average gains were **17.50%**, with improvements up to **29.75%** (MME) and **27.04%** (SEED).

2. **Outperforming State-of-the-Art Proprietary Models:**
 - On image recognition, TTRV applied to InternVL3-8B **surpassed GPT-4o by an average of 2.3%** across 8 benchmarks, establishing new state-of-the-art results for open-source VLMs. For instance, InternVL3-8B with TTRV achieved 99.31% on ImageNet compared to GPT-4o's 98.30%.
 - On VQA tasks, TTRV remained **highly competitive with GPT-4o**, lagging by only ~2% on average but outperforming it on challenging benchmarks like MathVista.

3. **Remarkable Data Efficiency:**
 - The impressive gains were achieved by sampling **only 20 randomly chosen test instances per dataset** for adaptation.
 - Even in **extremely data-scarce scenarios**, TTRV showed effectiveness: applying adaptation on a *single randomly chosen test example* still yielded non-trivial improvements of up to **5.5%** in recognition tasks (Table 6). This suggests that TTRV is not simply adapting to the test data distribution but rather activating latent capabilities learned during pre-training.

4. **Cross-Dataset Generalization:**
 - Ablation studies (Figure 3, Table 12) demonstrated strong cross-dataset generalization. Training TTRV on one dataset (e.g., Food101) and evaluating on a completely different one (e.g., DTD) still yielded substantial improvements (e.g., +52.03% for InternVL3-2B from Food to DTD). This reinforces the hypothesis that TTRV enhances the model's underlying task abilities rather than merely overfitting to the adaptation data.

5. **Validation of Reward Design:**
 - Ablations on reward designs (Table 3) confirmed that the combination of frequency-based and diversity-control rewards significantly outperformed individual components, majority voting, and vanilla entropy minimization (TENT-like approach).

6. **Robustness and Generalization to Model Families:**
 - TTRV demonstrated robustness with minor variations across 5 independent runs (Table 11).
 - It generalized effectively to other VLM architectures beyond InternVL, showing consistent gains with Qwen2.5-VL (Table 7) and other models like MM-Eureka, ThinkLite-VL, and VisionReasoner (Table 13).

7. **Insights into Latent Capabilities:** The consistent improvements with minimal adaptation data and strong cross-dataset generalization suggest that TTRV primarily recovers and amplifies visual recognition and reasoning capabilities that were present during large-scale pre-training but might have been attenuated during instruction tuning or remained sub-optimal.

One minor observed limitation was a decrease in performance in specific cases (e.g., Resisc45 with InternVL2.5-4B), which the authors attribute to the base model's low performance leading to low-quality rollouts or GRPO instability.

In summary, TTRV provides a robust, data-efficient, and highly effective method for improving VLM performance across diverse tasks, challenging traditional assumptions about the necessity of labeled data for model adaptation and post-training with RL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TTRV represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and reinforcement learning, with far-reaching implications for future research and real-world applications.

1. **Enabling Continuous, Unsupervised Adaptation:** The most profound impact of TTRV is its demonstration of effective test-time reinforcement learning without reliance on labeled data. This capability moves VLMs closer to human-like learning, where continuous refinement occurs directly from interaction with the environment. This paradigm shift could enable VLMs to self-improve and remain relevant in dynamic, evolving real-world scenarios, drastically reducing the cost and effort associated with manual data labeling and traditional fine-tuning.

2. **Bridging the Gap Between Pre-training and Deployment:** TTRV highlights a critical insight: powerful capabilities often exist latently within large pre-trained models but may not be fully activated or optimally aligned during standard instruction tuning. By using self-supervised reward signals, TTRV can "recover and amplify" these inherent abilities, effectively bridging the performance gap between a VLM's pre-training potential and its actual downstream deployment performance, even on out-of-distribution data.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The consistent gains across diverse and challenging benchmarks, including distribution shifts (ImageNet-R/S/A) and complex reasoning tasks (MathVista, AI2D), indicate that TTRV makes VLMs more robust and capable of generalizing. This is crucial for deploying AI systems in safety-critical or open-world environments where unseen inputs are common.

4. **Democratization of Advanced VLM Performance:** By achieving (and in some cases surpassing) the performance of leading proprietary models like GPT-4o using open-source VLMs, TTRV democratizes access to high-performing multimodal AI. This could foster innovation in academic research and smaller industrial settings that lack the resources for massive proprietary model development.

5. **New Directions for Reinforcement Learning:** TTRV expands the utility of reinforcement learning beyond traditional supervised settings. It introduces a powerful mechanism for generating internal reward signals, opening new avenues for research into self-supervised RL, intrinsic motivation, and how models can learn from their own uncertainty and consistency. The findings regarding minimal data requirements (even single-sample adaptation) further challenge conventional wisdom about the data demands of RL.

6. **Inspiration for Next-Generation AI Systems:** The principles demonstrated by TTRV—on-the-fly adaptation, learning from self-generated feedback, and activating latent capabilities—could inspire the design of next-generation AI systems that are inherently more adaptive, autonomous, and efficient. This could lead to more dynamic AI agents capable of continuous self-improvement in robotics, autonomous systems, and personalized AI applications.

7. **Practical Implications for VLM Deployment:** For practitioners, TTRV offers a practical tool to boost the performance of existing VLMs without extensive re-engineering or access to new labeled datasets. This can significantly extend the lifespan and utility of deployed models, particularly in domains with rapid data drift or evolving task requirements.

While the paper acknowledges the need for theoretical explanations for its observed behavior, the empirical evidence is compelling. TTRV represents a significant step towards developing more intelligent, adaptable, and autonomous multimodal AI systems, paving the way for a future where models continuously learn and refine their understanding directly from the world around them.
