# When Thinking Drifts: Evidential Grounding for Robust Video Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> A study reveals that multimodal large language models often exhibit "Visual Thinking Drift" when applying Chain-of-Thought to video, generating verbose but factually ungrounded reasoning. The proposed Visual Evidence Reward (VER) system uses reinforcement learning to explicitly ground these reasoning processes in visual evidence, achieving state-of-the-art results with an average +4.0% accuracy gain across 10 video understanding benchmarks.

## Problem

- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, while successful in text-based LLMs, often degrades performance in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for video understanding tasks.
- MLLMs applying CoT to video exhibit "Visual Thinking Drift," where generated reasoning becomes unmoored from actual visual evidence, leading to hallucinations and incorrect conclusions.
- This drift occurs because MLLMs prioritize internal language priors and amplify biases over visual likelihoods, especially as reasoning chains extend.

## Method

- Develop a novel reinforcement learning framework, ==Visual Evidence Reward== (VER), to explicitly reward reasoning traces that are verifiably grounded in visual evidence.
- An auxiliary, powerful LLM acts as a judge, assigning a binary score to evaluate the factual alignment of generated reasoning with video content.
- Utilize an "inverted prompting" strategy with a strong external MLLM to generate question-dependent visual evidence, ensuring high-quality, verifiable training signals for grounding.

## Results

- Systematic evaluation showed that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting consistently reduced accuracy across 10 diverse video understanding benchmarks for open-source MLLMs.
- The Video-VER model achieved state-of-the-art performance, ranking first in 9 out of 10 evaluations and demonstrating an average +**4.0%** absolute accuracy gain across all benchmarks compared to CoT baselines.
- Ablation studies validated that question-dependent visual evidence is critical for grounding, and performance improved with increased input video frames, demonstrating effective temporal context utilization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, contrary to its benefits in text, frequently degrades performance in video reasoning for multimodal large language models by encouraging ungrounded speculation.
> - "Visual Thinking Drift" is a prevalent failure mode where MLLMs generate verbose, logically coherent but factually ungrounded rationales, overriding correct visual intuitions with language priors.
> - Reinforcement learning with explicit rewards for visual grounding can effectively counteract this drift, ensuring reasoning processes are tethered to actual video evidence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated at the crucial intersection of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and video understanding, a rapidly evolving domain critical for advanced artificial intelligence. The broader research landscape in video reasoning aims to empower machines to infer multi-step logic from dynamic visual content. Traditionally, two main strategies have emerged:

1. **Agentic or Modular Workflow:** This approach decomposes complex video understanding into specialized sub-problems (e.g., event parsing, object tracking, temporal grounding), often addressed by dedicated, specialized models.
2. **End-to-End MLLMs:** More recently, the advent of MLLMs has enabled holistic inference by integrating vision encoders (for extracting visual representations) with language models (for reasoning and textual rationale generation).

Building upon the latter, a significant trend involves adapting Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, initially successful in text-based tasks, to MLLMs for video. This has involved developing high-quality reasoning datasets with spatio-temporal annotations and applying reinforcement learning (RL) post-training to refine MLLM reasoning pathways. Early results in this direction often suggested that encouraging models to "think before answering" could be beneficial.

However, this paper critically challenges the uncritical application of CoT to video understanding. It identifies a crucial gap: while CoT shows promise in text, its specific challenges in video-centric tasks have remained underexplored. The authors meticulously demonstrate that CoT, far from universally enhancing performance, often *degrades* it in video reasoning, particularly for open-source models. This failure mode, termed "Visual Thinking Drift," occurs when MLLMs generate verbose but misleading internal monologues, hallucinating visual details, and overriding correct intuitions by amplifying internal biases or language priors rather than grounding their reasoning in actual visual evidence.

The work differentiates "Visual Thinking Drift" from general MLLM hallucination by emphasizing its emergence *within* the multi-step reasoning process, where early errors propagate through the chain, leading to conclusions unmoored from the video. Unlike previous efforts to mitigate hallucination through test-time interventions or preference modeling, this paper proposes a novel approach focusing on *reinforcement fine-tuning to improve the integrity of the logical progression itself*. By systematically exposing the limitations of current CoT applications in video and proposing a targeted solution, this research significantly advances the understanding of how to build robust and trustworthy multimodal reasoning systems. It underscores that true video intelligence requires not just "thinking," but deeply "seeing" throughout the thought process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a critical observation: while Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting has revolutionized reasoning capabilities in text-based large language models, its direct application to multimodal large language models (MLLMs) for video understanding often proves counterproductive. The authors identify a specific and prevalent failure mode, which they term "Visual Thinking Drift," where MLLMs generate plausible but factually ungrounded reasoning traces. This drift causes models to hallucinate visual details, prioritize internal language priors, and ultimately degrade overall performance, undermining the very goal of robust video reasoning. The overarching motivation is to bridge the gap between the expressive power of CoT and the imperative for visual fidelity in dynamic content analysis, ensuring that MLLMs not only "think before answering" but critically "see while thinking."

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Systematically Expose CoT Limitations in Video:** To conduct a comprehensive empirical study across a wide array of 10 diverse video understanding benchmarks, multiple MLLM architectures (including both open-source and proprietary models), and 20 granular video QA subtasks. The aim is to unequivocally demonstrate that CoT reasoning frequently diminishes performance in video understanding, contrary to its benefits in text.
2. **Characterize "Visual Thinking Drift":** To thoroughly explain the underlying mechanisms of this performance degradation. This involves illustrating through concrete examples how reasoning chains become disconnected from visual evidence and providing a Bayesian interpretation that elucidates how language priors can overpower visual likelihoods as the reasoning chain extends.
3. **Develop a Grounding Mechanism:** To propose a novel and effective solution to counteract "Visual Thinking Drift." This involves introducing the Visual Evidence Reward (VER), a reinforcement learning framework specifically designed to explicitly reward reasoning traces that are verifiably and granularly grounded in the actual visual evidence present in the video.
4. **Demonstrate Superior Performance:** To rigorously evaluate the proposed Video-VER model. The objective is to show that, unlike ungrounded CoT, Video-VER consistently achieves state-of-the-art or competitive performance across the diverse benchmarks, significantly improving accuracy by actively tethering intermediate thought processes to perceived visual content.
5. **Foster Robust, Visually Grounded AI:** To contribute a foundational understanding of the distinct challenges in video-centric reasoning and to encourage the broader development of AI systems that prioritize robust evidential grounding in visual data for their inferences, ultimately leading to more trustworthy and capable multimodal intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this paper is twofold: first, a systematic diagnostic study to identify and characterize the "Visual Thinking Drift" phenomenon; and second, the development and evaluation of a novel reinforcement learning framework, Visual Evidence Reward (VER), to counteract this drift.

#### 3.1. Systematic Analysis of CoT Failures in Video Reasoning

The authors began by conducting a comprehensive empirical investigation comparing two core prompting strategies for video MLLMs:
- **Direct Response Generation:** The model directly produces an answer `a` based on a question `q` and video context `v` (i.e., `p(a | q, v)`).
- **Reasoning-Driven Generation (Chain-of-Thought - CoT):** The model first infers a rationale sequence `c_1:T` and then conditions the final answer `a` on both the rationale and original inputs (i.e., `p(c_1:T | q, v) * p(a | c_1:T, q, v)`).

This comparison was performed across three leading open-source MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Video-R1-7B) and further analyzed with the proprietary GPT-4o model, spanning 10 diverse video understanding benchmarks, including general understanding, temporal reasoning, spatial reasoning, and hallucination robustness tasks.

The key finding was that CoT often *degraded* accuracy, especially for tasks requiring rapid visual perception. Analysis of 20 MVBench subtasks revealed that CoT reduced accuracy on simple perceptual judgments while improving it on cognitively demanding tasks like object counting. This paradoxical behavior was attributed to "Visual Thinking Drift," where verbose CoT prompts invited over-rationalization, hallucinated details, and context dilution, causing models to override correct first impressions.

To theoretically explain this, a **Bayesian lens** was adopted. The authors posited that CoT tokens are unsupervised during training. In the autoregressive generation process, visual likelihood is gradually diluted as self-attention increasingly focuses on already-generated tokens (language priors), leading to a high-variance latent state. A single hallucinated detail in an early token could then cascade, collapsing the posterior mass around a fictional narrative, from which recovery is nearly impossible due to the lack of backward message-passing to re-verify the video. The result is a logically sound but visually ungrounded chain of thought.

#### 3.2. Visual Evidence Reward (VER) Framework

To counter "Visual Thinking Drift," the paper introduces VER, a novel reward mechanism integrated into a reinforcement learning (RL) post-training framework.
- **Foundation:** VER enhances Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a lightweight, rule-based RL algorithm.
- **Core Idea:** Explicitly reward reasoning traces that are verifiably grounded in visual evidence. This compels models to "see while thinking."
- **Mechanism:**
 1. **Response Generation:** A policy model (Video-VER, based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B) generates a group of responses (reasoning chains and answers).
 2. **LLM-based Judge:** An auxiliary, strong LLM (Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct with temperature-0 decoding) acts as a judge. It evaluates each generated reasoning trace for its factual alignment with the visual evidence `v` for a given question `q`.
 3. **Binary Evidence Score:** The judge assigns a binary score `e_i ∈ {0, 1}`, where 1 indicates a proper reference to visual evidence and 0 otherwise.
 4. **Evidence Reward Coefficient:** An evidence reward `r_e` is assigned as `α` (tunable weight, set to 0.3) if `e_i = 1`, and `0` otherwise.
 5. **Evidence-Augmented Reward:** The total reward `r_evid_i` for a response is `r_i + r_e` if the answer `o_i` is correct AND `e_i = 1`; otherwise, it's just the base accuracy reward `r_i`. This means the model receives a bonus *only if* it's both correct and grounded.
 6. **GRPO Optimization:** These rewards are normalized and used within the clipped GRPO objective, along with accuracy, format, and length rewards, to update the policy.
- **Visual Evidence Generation (for training data):**
 - To obtain high-quality, question-dependent visual evidence for training the VER component, a powerful external MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) is used *offline*.
 - **Inverted Prompting:** Instead of asking the MLLM to generate a CoT and an answer, it is given the `(video, question, ground-truth answer)` triplet. It is then prompted to generate a list of precise visual observations that *support this already known answer*. This "inverted prompting" minimizes hallucination and ensures the generated evidence is question-relevant and visually verifiable.
 - The external VLM is *only* used to create this training data; Video-VER performs inference independently at test time.

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

The Video-VER model undergoes a two-stage training pipeline:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model is initially fine-tuned on the Video-R1-COT-165k dataset, which provides explicit CoT annotations, to bootstrap its foundational reasoning capabilities.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Following SFT, the model is further trained using GRPO with the VER mechanism on a mixture of datasets (Reversed-in-Time and Video-R1-260k). This RL phase allows the model to refine its reasoning patterns, specifically learning to ground its thoughts in visual evidence through exploration and the VER signal.

The approach rigorously tackles the identified problem with a data-efficient RL method, ensuring that linguistic fluency is coupled with robust visual grounding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several critical findings that collectively highlight the challenges of ungrounded Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in video understanding and demonstrate the superior efficacy of the proposed Visual Evidence Reward (VER) framework.

1. **CoT Degrades Performance in Video Reasoning:**
 - A systematic study across 10 diverse video benchmarks, using leading open-source MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B, Video-R1-7B), revealed a consistent and significant performance drop when CoT prompting was applied, compared to direct answer generation. For instance, Qwen2.5-VL-7B experienced a drop from 63.6% to 59.8% on MVBench and 59.2% to 54.7% on Video-MME.
 - Even the highly capable proprietary model, GPT-4o, showed a considerable portion of questions better answered directly than with CoT (Figure 4), implying a fundamental issue beyond model scale.
 - Detailed analysis of 20 subtasks from MVBench (Figure 3) demonstrated that CoT *reduces* accuracy on lightweight perceptual tasks (e.g., scene transition detection), where it introduces unnecessary reasoning steps, hallucinations, and context dilution, overriding correct "first impressions." Conversely, CoT sometimes helped with more cognitively demanding tasks like object counting.

2. **Confirmation of "Visual Thinking Drift":**
 - The observed performance degradation is attributed to "Visual Thinking Drift," a phenomenon where reasoning chains, despite appearing logically coherent, become unmoored from the actual video content.
 - The Bayesian explanation elucidated that CoT tokens, being unsupervised, allow language priors to dominate visual likelihoods as reasoning chains grow longer. This leads to linguistically plausible but visually ungrounded narratives, where even a single hallucinated detail can cascade and render subsequent reasoning incorrect.
 - Qualitative examples (Figure 1, 6, 12, 13) vividly illustrate how baseline models frequently introduce speculative or hallucinated details not present in the video, demonstrating this drift.

3. **Self-Consistency Indicates Instability, But Is Costly:**
 - Experiments with self-consistency decoding (majority voting over 20 CoT responses) showed a clear accuracy improvement across models and benchmarks (Figure 7). This indicates that CoT reasoning traces are often unstable and susceptible to visual thinking drift, and aggregating multiple responses can mitigate this.
 - However, this improvement comes at a significant computational overhead, highlighting the need for a more efficient, inherent grounding mechanism rather than a post-hoc aggregation strategy.

4. **Video-VER Achieves Superior, Grounded Performance:**
 - The proposed Video-VER model, integrating the Visual Evidence Reward into GRPO, consistently surpassed existing open-source video MLLMs across the comprehensive suite of 10 benchmarks (Table 1). It ranked first in 9 out of 10 evaluations and second in the remaining one (VSI-Bench).
 - Video-VER demonstrated notable absolute accuracy gains compared to its base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B with CoT), showing improvements as high as +9.0% on VideoHallucer and an average of +4.0% across all 10 benchmarks.
 - It showed particular strength in temporally nuanced tasks like TempCompass (74.0%) and TVBench (52.8%), indicating its enhanced ability to interpret dynamic visual content accurately.
 - Qualitative analysis confirmed that Video-VER's reasoning traces maintained strong alignment between intermediate steps and observable visual evidence, effectively avoiding the speculative and hallucinated content seen in baselines.

5. **Ablation Studies Validate VER Design Choices:**
 - **Question-Dependent Visual Evidence (QD-VE):** Ablation showed that generating visual evidence specifically tailored to the question (QD-VE) significantly outperformed using generic video captions (VC) on 9 out of 10 benchmarks (Table 2). This confirms the importance of context-specific grounding.
 - **Scalability with Frames:** Performance consistently improved as the number of input video frames increased (Table 3), with 32 frames yielding the best results on 8 out of 10 benchmarks. This indicates that Video-VER effectively leverages longer temporal contexts, confirming its ability to scale with richer visual input.

In summary, the findings robustly establish "Visual Thinking Drift" as a major impediment to effective CoT in video reasoning and present Video-VER as a powerful and principled solution, demonstrating that explicit grounding in visual evidence is paramount for achieving true video intelligence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work holds significant implications for the advancement of artificial intelligence, particularly in the realm of multimodal understanding and reasoning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing a Critical and Previously Underexplored Failure Mode:** The paper's most immediate and profound impact is the identification and characterization of "Visual Thinking Drift." By systematically demonstrating that Chain-of-Thought (CoT) often degrades performance in video reasoning, rather than universally enhancing it, the authors expose a fundamental limitation in current MLLMs. This insight shifts the research focus from merely adapting text-based CoT to fundamentally rethinking how reasoning must be grounded in dynamic visual contexts. This is crucial for building robust AI, as ungrounded reasoning can lead to unreliable and untrustworthy systems.

2. **Advancing Robust Multimodal Reasoning:** The introduction of the Visual Evidence Reward (VER) framework offers a principled and effective solution to the identified problem. By explicitly incentivizing reasoning traces that are verifiably grounded in visual evidence, VER ensures that MLLMs truly "see while thinking." This moves beyond superficial explanations to truly tether abstract logical steps to concrete perceptual observations, representing a significant step towards more robust, interpretable, and factually aligned multimodal AI.

3. **Methodological Innovation in Reinforcement Learning:** The VER mechanism, integrated into a reinforcement learning framework, provides a novel approach for injecting critical desiderata (visual grounding) into the training loop. The use of an auxiliary LLM as a judge and the innovative "inverted prompting" strategy for generating question-dependent visual evidence demonstrate clever ways to bootstrap complex reward signals without extensive human annotation. This methodology could inspire similar reward shaping techniques for other complex, fuzzy objectives in multimodal or even language-only domains.

4. **Improved Model Reliability and Trustworthiness:** By mitigating hallucinations and ensuring reasoning is tied to observable facts, Video-VER leads to more accurate and reliable video understanding systems. This enhanced reliability is critical for real-world deployment in sensitive applications where erroneous inferences could have severe consequences. It fosters greater trust in AI systems that can not only provide answers but also explain *how* those answers are derived from the visual input.

5. **Practical Accessibility of Advanced Reasoning:** The fact that Video-VER achieves state-of-the-art performance with a 7B parameter model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) makes advanced, grounded video reasoning more accessible. This contrasts with reliance on much larger, proprietary models, enabling broader research and application development within resource-constrained environments.

6. **Broader Societal Benefits:** The potential societal impact is substantial. More accurate and robust video understanding systems can:
 - **Enhance Accessibility:** Provide richer and more reliable descriptions of visual media for individuals with visual impairments.
 - **Improve Education:** Power intelligent tutoring systems that can better analyze instructional videos and assess understanding.
 - **Automate Content Analysis:** Lead to more dependable systems for content moderation, video summarization, and event detection, reducing errors caused by AI hallucinations.
 - **Aid in Safety-Critical Applications:** Contribute to more reliable autonomous systems (e.g., self-driving cars, surveillance) by ensuring their decision-making processes are firmly grounded in perceived reality.

7. **Future Research Directions:** This work opens several exciting avenues for future research, including extending VER to open-ended video QA tasks, handling very long-form videos with sparse critical information, and exploring more sophisticated visual representation techniques or dynamic frame selection methods. It also highlights the ongoing challenge and importance of optimizing LLM-based judges and refining visual evidence generation processes.

In conclusion, "When Thinking Drifts" provides a critical diagnosis of a pervasive problem in multimodal AI and offers a robust, principled solution. Its impact will likely be felt in both foundational research into AI reasoning and the practical deployment of more intelligent, reliable, and trustworthy video understanding systems across diverse applications.
