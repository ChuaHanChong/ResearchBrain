# Rethinking On-Policy Distillation of Large Language Models II: One Training Example

> [!summary] Summary
> On-policy distillation (OPD) trains a student on its own rollouts under dense token-level teacher supervision. This work runs OPD on a single training query and finds the student keeps improving for hundreds of steps, recovering 72% of full-data OPD's gain. A state-coverage analysis explains why: one query's rollouts already reach 71.5% of the states full-data training visits, while a steadily falling absorption rate, not data supply, limits progress.

## Problem

- Prior OPD studies analyze only algorithmic behavior, objective geometry and failure modes, leaving the role of the training data and its interaction with the algorithm unexamined.
- Data-efficiency work on reasoning post-training assumes supervision hinges on curated problems, either verifiable outcomes under sparse-reward RL or reference solutions under supervised fine-tuning.
- Post-training pipelines still curate large query sets for OPD and multi-teacher OPD without knowing whether query count, rather than optimization speed, is the binding constraint.

## Method

- **One-shot OPD**: an extreme controlled setting training the student repeatedly on a single query, spanning math, code, instruction following and agentic tool use across three student-teacher families.
- ==State coverage==: states are embedded by the teacher's final-layer hidden signature, pooled from full-data rollouts, reduced by ==PCA== and partitioned into 200 ==K-means clusters==; coverage is the fraction reached.
- ==Alignment diagnostics==: a ==teacher-student distance== from per-token log-probability gaps plus an ==absorption rate== measuring the share of remaining distance one update closes, tracked across query-set sizes.

## Results

- One-shot OPD reaches **68.5** versus **69.8** for full-data on math averages, recovering **87%** of full-data's gain at step **300** and **72%** at step **1000**, holding across domains and families.
- State coverage rises from **71.5%** for one query to **98.9%** for **16** semantically diverse queries, which match full-data OPD; **16** per domain recover **101%** of full-data multi-teacher OPD's gain.
- All query-set sizes remove **78-84%** of step-**30** distance by step **300** at similar decaying rates; content-light templates and off-domain WildChat prompts track the real-query baseline within about one point.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training inputs matter through the reasoning states they induce, not their task content, so data selection should target state coverage rather than problem count or difficulty.
> - Dense teacher supervision is abundant but absorbed slowly; the optimizer, not the dataset, sets run length, redirecting effort toward step efficiency and trust-region style reuse.
> - Dense per-token signal outlasts outcome reward: OPD keeps learning after a query is solved and on never-solved queries, but its ceiling is the teacher's distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of large language model (LLM) post-training, specifically focusing on On-Policy Distillation (OPD). OPD is a method that combines student-generated rollouts with dense, token-level supervision from a teacher model. It has gained increasing prominence in frontier LLM development, with its adoption in models such as Qwen3, MiMo, GLM-5, DeepSeek-V4, and Kimi K3, often alongside supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL).

Prior research on OPD has predominantly investigated its algorithmic behavior, training dynamics, and underlying mechanisms from an algorithmic perspective. This includes studies on its objective and update geometry, its effectiveness in specific scenarios, and common failure modes. However, the role of training data in shaping OPD's performance and the interaction between the data and the algorithm have received less attention.

This paper addresses this gap by examining OPD at the data-minimal limit, specifically by training on a single query, a setting termed "one-shot OPD." This approach is inspired by previous work on "one-shot RLVR" (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards) by Wang et al. (2026a), which explored the role of data in sparse outcome-reward RL settings. By applying a similar experimental lens to OPD, this research aims to disentangle the contributions of data supply and algorithmic absorption speed, which are typically intertwined in standard training scenarios. The study's findings contribute to a re-evaluation of data efficiency in post-training methods, moving the focus from the quantity of problems to the diversity of states induced during training. It complements existing work on data-efficient reasoning post-training (e.g., LIMA, LIMO, s1 for SFT, and LIMR for RLVR) and synthetic/unsupervised data generation by proposing a state-level view of data utility for OPD, where the input's primary role is to generate useful reasoning states for teacher supervision rather than providing explicit task content or verifiable outcomes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to understand the surprising phenomenon observed in one-shot On-Policy Distillation (OPD): despite being trained on a single query, a language model continues to improve for hundreds of optimization steps and achieves substantial performance gains. This observation parallels findings in one-shot Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), prompting a focused investigation into the underlying reasons for such sustained learning efficiency with minimal data in the context of dense, token-level supervision provided by OPD.

The central question driving this work is: "Why can OPD, when trained on just one example, keep learning for so long and improve by so much?" This question is broken down into two distinct aspects to facilitate analysis:
1. **"Keep learning for so long"**: This pertains to the efficiency with which the OPD algorithm absorbs supervision over time. The motivation here is to understand the factors that govern the duration and trajectory of learning, particularly the rate at which the student model integrates teacher signals.
2. **"Improve by so much"**: This concerns the sufficiency of a small amount of data to supply adequate supervision for substantial performance enhancement. The motivation is to uncover why a single training example can provide enough signal to drive significant improvements across various tasks and model architectures.

The motivation for this investigation stems from the recognized gap in current understanding of OPD. While previous studies have largely focused on the algorithmic aspects of OPD, the role of training data and its interaction with the algorithm remained underexplored. By isolating the data supply to its minimum (a single query), the authors aim to disentangle these two interwoven aspects of OPD efficiency, providing insights into its data requirements and the mechanisms of its learning process. Understanding these factors is considered essential for a comprehensive understanding of OPD's effectiveness and for guiding future developments in data curation and algorithmic design for LLM post-training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The study employs a systematic experimental approach to investigate one-shot OPD, comprising several ablations and analyses across different domains and model families.

### 4. Experimental Setup (Section 3.1)

- **Generality Test:** One-shot OPD was established across four task domains: mathematical reasoning, code generation, instruction following, and agentic tool use.
- **Models:** Student-teacher pairs from the same family were utilized.
 - For mathematical reasoning, code generation, and instruction following, the student was DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (R1-Distill-1.5B). Teachers were JustRL-DeepSeek-1.5B (JustRL-1.5B), Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B-v2-RLVE (Nemotron-1.5B), and a specifically post-trained instruction-following variant (UltraData-IF-1.5B), respectively.
 - To test model family robustness, additional mathematical reasoning pairs included Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct with GT-Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct-MATH, and OLMo-3-7B-Instruct-DPO with OLMo-3-7B-Instruct.
 - For agentic tool use, Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct was the student, paired with Hammer2.1-1.5b.
- **Datasets:** Domain-specific datasets were used: DAPO-Math-17K for math, Open-R1 Codeforces for code, a subset of UltraData-SFT-2605 for instruction following, and xLAM-function-calling-60K for agentic tool use.
- **One-Shot Query Selection:** For mathematics, three queries of varying initial difficulty (easy, medium, hard, based on student pass rate) were selected. For other domains, a single query was randomly sampled.
- **Training:** OPD was implemented in veRL. Mathematical reasoning runs optimized the top-𝑘 advantage (with 𝑘=16), while other domains used the sampled-token advantage. Each update involved a batch of 64 rollouts. AdamW optimizer was used with a learning rate of 10$^{-6}$, rollout temperature of 1.0, and a gradient clip norm of 1.0.
- **Evaluation:** Validation accuracy was measured on established benchmarks for each domain (e.g., MATH-500, AMC 2023, AIME 2025 for math, LiveCodeBench v6 for code, Multi-IF for instruction following, BFCL v3 for agentic tool use). Metrics included avg@16 or avg@3/8, and final-turn scores. Improvements were reported as gap recovery and full-data recovery ratios.

### 5. Data Perspective: State Coverage (Section 4.1)

- **Hypothesis:** A single query provides sufficient supervision if its rollouts cover a broad part of the state space visited by full-data OPD.
- **State Representation:** Each state (prefix) was represented by its teacher signature, the teacher's final-layer hidden vector at the state's last token.
- **Reference Space:** States visited by full-data OPD over its entire run on DAPO-Math-17K were pooled. PCA reduced these to 50 dimensions, followed by K-means clustering (K=200) to define semantic clusters.
- **State Coverage Metric:** The fraction of these K clusters reached by a given setting's rollouts was measured.
- **Ablation for Diversity (Section 4.2):**
 - **Response Diversity (off-policy):** Varying the number of distinct trajectories (1, 4, 16, 64) sampled once from the initial student and fixed for all updates, while keeping other factors constant.
 - **Query Diversity (on-policy):** Building 4- and 16-shot query sets by clustering DAPO-Math-17K with BGE-M3 and selecting semantically distinct representatives.

### 6. Algorithm Perspective: Slow Alignment (Section 5.1)

- **Alignment Metrics:**
 - **Distance ($d_t$):** Average magnitude of the per-token teacher-student disagreement (log probability difference) over visited states.
 - **Absorption Rate ($v_t$):** Proportion of the remaining distance absorbed by one update ($ (d_t - d_{t+1}) / d_t $). These were tracked from step 30 to avoid initial gradient clipping effects.
- **Ablation on Fixed States (Section 5.3):** Compared an "always-on-policy" run (fresh rollouts each step) with an "always-off-policy" run (reusing 64 fixed trajectories sampled once from the initial student), to see if a steady supply of new states is necessary for sustained learning.

### 7. Multi-Teacher OPD (MOPD) Extension (Section 6)

- Investigated if findings extend to MOPD, where a single student is trained on multiple domains with domain-specific teachers.
- Setup included one-shot, 16-shot (16 diverse queries per domain), and full-data MOPD across mathematical reasoning, code generation, and instruction following.

### 8. Discussion and Stress Tests (Section 7)

- **Content-Light Inputs (Section 7.1):** Evaluated OPD effectiveness using content-light templates (e.g., an empty user turn ending in `<think>`) and off-domain general-purpose WildChat queries to test if task content is necessary for useful states.
- **Comparison with One-Shot RLVR (Section 7.2):** Conducted a controlled comparison with one-shot RLVR on the same query to highlight differences in signal extraction and learning dynamics between dense token-level OPD and sparse outcome-level RLVR.

### 9. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the efficiency and mechanisms of On-Policy Distillation (OPD), particularly in data-minimal settings.

### 10. The One-Shot Phenomenon (Section 3)

- **Significant Gain:** One-shot OPD consistently recovers a substantial portion of the performance gain achieved by full-data OPD. In mathematical reasoning, training on a single query for 300 steps recovered 69% of the teacher-student gap and 87% of full-data OPD's gain. Over 1000 steps, it recovered 72% of full-data OPD's gain, reaching 68.4% accuracy compared to 72.1% for full-data.
- **Robustness Across Settings:** This effect was robust across various model families (R1-Distill-1.5B, Llama-3B-It, OLMo-7B-It-DPO for math) and task domains (mathematical reasoning, code generation, instruction following, agentic tool use). For non-math domains, one-shot OPD recovered 73%, 66%, and 64% of their respective teacher-student gaps.
- **Insensitivity to Query Properties:** The gain was largely unaffected by query difficulty (easy, medium, hard), response length limits, or rollout sampling temperature. Queries that were never solved by the student yielded comparable benchmark gains to those always or eventually solved.
- **Consistent Alignment:** One-shot OPD exhibited training dynamics similar to full-data OPD, with the top-K token overlap ratio increasing, overlap-token advantage approaching zero, and the absolute entropy gap nearly closing, indicating progressive alignment with the teacher on visited states.

### 11. Data Perspective: Abundant States (Section 4)

- **High State Coverage from a Single Query:** A single query, through repeated on-policy rollouts, was found to cover a substantial portion of the state space visited by full-data OPD. Specifically, one-shot OPD reached 71.5% state coverage by step 300, with most (65.9%) achieved within the first 100 steps. This indicates that a single query, despite its limited problem scope, generates a broad set of unique interaction states for supervision.
- **Diversity Drives Coverage and Accuracy:** Increasing the semantic diversity of queries directly correlated with increased state coverage and validation accuracy.
 - In off-policy training, increasing the number of distinct trajectories (states) from 1 to 64 monotonically improved validation accuracy.
 - In on-policy training, a set of 16 semantically distinct queries achieved 98.9% state coverage and matched the validation accuracy of full-data OPD. This suggests that the value of an additional training example is primarily determined by its ability to induce novel states not covered by existing examples.

### 12. Algorithm Perspective: Slow Alignment (Section 5)

- **Slow, Sustained Alignment:** The student continuously narrowed the teacher-student gap throughout training, but the absorption rate (proportion of remaining teacher-student disagreement resolved per update) diminished steadily. This slowdown, rather than an exhaustion of signal, explained why hundreds of steps were required for significant improvement.
- **Insensitivity to Query Count:** The rate at which alignment slowed was largely consistent across different training set sizes (1, 4, 16, or full DAPO-Math-17K queries). All settings removed 78% to 84% of their initial (step 30) distance by step 300, and their absorption rates fell by a similar factor. This indicates that the pace of alignment is an intrinsic property of the OPD algorithm, not dictated by the amount of data.
- **Fixed States Sustain Learning:** An off-policy run that trained on a *fixed* set of states for all updates still improved significantly over hundreds of steps, demonstrating that a continuous supply of novel states from on-policy rollouts is not a prerequisite for sustained long-term learning in OPD.

### 13. One-Shot Extends to Multi-Teacher OPD (Section 6)

- **16 Queries Match MOPD:** For Multi-Teacher OPD (MOPD) across three domains, 16 semantically diverse queries per domain were sufficient to match the performance of full-data MOPD. This 16-shot MOPD recovered 101% of full-data MOPD's gain, achieving nearly the same average validation accuracy (52.9% vs. 52.8%) as full-data training. Full-data MOPD itself proved effective, achieving 79% gap recovery, comparable to separate full-data OPD runs.

### 14. Discussion: Inputs as State Generators (Section 7)

- **Content-Light Inputs are Effective:** Inputs with minimal or no task-specific content, such as basic chat templates or general-domain WildChat queries, were nearly as effective as real, curated training queries in driving OPD for mathematical reasoning and code generation. These inputs induced trajectories that still exposed useful teacher supervision, often with a fraction of the rollout tokens.
- **OPD vs. RLVR Signal Extraction:** In a controlled comparison on the same query, OPD demonstrated a more effective signal extraction and greater validation gain than one-shot RLVR. OPD's validation accuracy gain over 1000 steps was more than double that of RLVR. While RLVR's signal diminished as the training query became consistently solved (reducing outcome variation), OPD continued to learn from local teacher-student gaps, which persist even on correct rollouts. This highlights OPD's independence from task verifiability and outcome-level variation.

### 15. Significance and Potential Impact

This research significantly reframes the understanding of data efficiency in On-Policy Distillation (OPD) for large language models (LLMs). The central finding that OPD is "data-overfed but algorithm-starved" challenges the conventional view that large, diverse datasets are the primary limiting factor in post-training. Instead, the work suggests that the rate at which the OPD algorithm can absorb supervision is a more critical bottleneck.

The potential impacts and significance of these findings are multi-faceted:

1. **Re-evaluation of Data Curation Strategies:** The demonstration that a few semantically diverse queries can achieve performance comparable to full datasets, and that content-light or off-domain inputs can still induce useful reasoning states, shifts the paradigm for data curation. The focus may move from collecting a vast number of problems with specific content to selecting queries that strategically expose the student to a broad and complementary set of "states" or prefixes during rollouts, thereby maximizing state coverage. This could lead to more efficient and targeted data collection efforts, potentially reducing the cost and effort associated with creating large, high-quality human-annotated datasets.

2. **Algorithmic Improvements for Step Efficiency:** By identifying the absorption rate of the algorithm as the binding resource rather than the data supply, the research highlights a new avenue for algorithmic innovation. Future work can prioritize increasing the step efficiency of OPD—how much is learned per optimization step—rather than solely focusing on data augmentation or synthesis. This could involve exploring techniques such as reusing batches for multiple epochs under trust regions, or weighting tokens based on their remaining teacher signal.

3. **Enhanced Understanding of LLM Learning Mechanisms:** The disentanglement of data supply and absorption rate provides deeper insights into how LLMs learn from dense, token-level supervision. The finding that a fixed set of states can sustain learning over hundreds of steps, and that OPD's signal persists even when queries are solved, contrasts with outcome-reward RL and suggests a distinct learning dynamic that is less reliant on external reward variation. This contributes to the broader understanding of learning processes in large neural networks.

4. **Implications for Multi-Teacher and General-Purpose Alignment:** The extension of the one-shot and few-shot efficiency to Multi-Teacher OPD (MOPD) suggests that a carefully selected, small set of diverse queries per domain could be sufficient for training a single student across multiple specialized teachers. This has implications for developing more scalable and efficient multi-task or general-purpose LLMs, where the challenge of combining diverse expert knowledge bases can be addressed with optimized data subsets.

5. **Efficiency in Frontier Post-training:** Given that OPD is increasingly integrated into frontier LLM post-training pipelines, these findings offer practical guidance for optimizing these resource-intensive processes. Reducing the need for massive datasets while maintaining performance can lead to faster iteration cycles, lower computational costs, and greater accessibility for researchers and developers. The study prompts a re-examination of the underlying mechanisms driving the success of recent post-training methods, potentially leading to more principled and efficient designs.
