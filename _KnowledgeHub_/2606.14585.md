# Sensitivity Shaping for Latent Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Generative latent dynamics models can become locally insensitive to control inputs, so unsupported actions yield predictions that look in-distribution and evade OOD detection. This paper adds a support-conditioned regularizer that enforces a non-vanishing control-Jacobian Frobenius norm on the highest-support training samples, identified by latent kNN distance. Across simulated obstacle avoidance, block plucking, and a real quadruped, it improves OOD detection and safety filtering.

## Problem

- Prior OOD methods treat learned dynamics as fixed and attach post hoc surrogates - ensembles, density models, nearest neighbors - that measure support compatibility, not true prediction error.
- Conformal calibration of these surrogates bounds false rejection of in-distribution inputs but gives no guarantee that genuinely unsupported transitions are reliably rejected.
- Broadening training data via web-scale corpora or foundation-model priors does not cover task-critical counterfactuals, and failure-free demonstrations near safety boundaries carry little control diversity.

## Method

- ==Control-Jacobian Frobenius norm== as a sensitivity measure over the latent transition map, exposing regions where distinct controls collapse to near-identical next-latent predictions.
- **Support-conditioned control-sensitivity regularizer**: a ==hinge loss== penalizing squared Jacobian norm below margin g, applied only to the beta-fraction of samples with smallest ==latent kNN distance==.
- High-support set refreshed every 10,000 steps after a warm-up phase; ==Hutchinson's identity== with 5 Gaussian probes estimates the norm via ==Jacobian-vector products==, avoiding full Jacobian materialization.

## Results

- Dubins obstacle avoidance one-step filter: success rises **0.600** to **0.800** (kNN), **0.910** to **0.960** (flow matching), **0.380** to **0.440** (ensemble), with OOD violation rates falling.
- Block plucking reachability filter: mediocre policy failure **0.196** to **0.150** at alpha=**0.1**; imagination-trained policy, regularized alpha=**0.05** gets **0.085** failure and **0.100** interventions versus vanilla alpha=**0.1** at **0.118** and **0.154**.
- Real Go2 quadruped: flow-matching filter reaches **1.000** success versus **0.900** vanilla, ensemble **0.750** versus **0.150**; beta ablation shows **0.3** best, **0.6** degrades performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Post hoc uncertainty scores inherit the blind spots of the representation they read; detectability must be built into dynamics training, not bolted on afterwards.
> - Regularize only where data support is strong - enforcing sensitivity everywhere raises local Lipschitz constants and amplifies extrapolation error, hurting reconstruction and downstream filtering.
> - Sharper latent transition geometry trades off against reconstruction smoothness: latent consistency improves as the support fraction grows while reconstruction error worsens.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Generative dynamics models, often referred to as world models, have emerged as a significant development in robotics. These models learn latent representations of high-dimensional observations and their transition dynamics, enabling predictive reasoning crucial for tasks such as vision-based manipulation, robot navigation, and locomotion, especially in scenarios where analytical dynamics are unavailable. Examples include recurrent state-space models (RSSMs) and the Dreamer family of algorithms, which optimize policies through imagined latent rollouts.

A critical challenge for these models, particularly in safety-critical applications, is their vulnerability to deployment-time distribution shift. This occurs when the model encounters out-of-distribution (OOD) state-action inputs, often due to differences between the deployed policy and the behavior policy that generated the training data. In such OOD situations, bounding prediction errors becomes difficult. Previous attempts to address this issue have focused on expanding the training data distribution, for instance, through large-scale multimodal datasets or foundation model priors. However, the inherent scarcity of robot interaction data limits the ability to achieve comprehensive coverage of all task-critical counterfactuals through data scaling alone.

This limitation has driven research into uncertainty-aware analysis, aiming to detect transitions that deviate from the training data support. Existing OOD detection methods typically employ post-hoc diagnostic approaches, such as using inter-model disagreement in ensembles, estimating likelihood with density models, or measuring distance to observed transitions via nearest-neighbor techniques. These methods often incorporate surrogate scores as penalties during planning to discourage unsupported controls, and their thresholds are calibrated using conformal prediction to bound the false rejection rate for in-distribution inputs.

The current work identifies a fundamental limitation of these existing OOD analysis methods: they treat the learned dynamics as fixed and attach post-hoc support surrogates. The paper argues that these surrogates can fail when the underlying learned dynamics are locally insensitive to control inputs. In such cases, unsupported control actions may produce latent predictions that spuriously resemble demonstrated transitions, thereby suppressing OOD signals despite large true predictive errors. This research aims to address this gap by proposing to *shape the dynamics learning process itself* to improve OOD detectability, rather than relying solely on post-hoc analysis.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are as follows:

- **Identify a Critical Failure Mode:** The paper's initial objective is to analyze and formally demonstrate a specific failure mode in generative dynamics models: control insensitivity. This occurs when the learned dynamics respond weakly to control inputs, underrepresenting the true system responses. This insensitivity leads to a situation where OOD control actions, which should generate distinct and detectable outcomes, instead produce latent predictions that appear compatible with the training distribution, thus masking true prediction errors.
- **Improve OOD Detection Reliability:** The core motivation is to enhance the reliability of OOD detection in model-based robotic systems, especially for safety-critical applications. In these domains, policies must reliably detect and avoid unsupported or potentially unsafe transitions. Current OOD surrogates are shown to be insufficient if the underlying dynamics model itself fails to capture control-dependent variations accurately.
- **Shape Dynamics Learning for OOD Awareness:** Instead of purely post-hoc OOD analysis, a key objective is to integrate OOD awareness directly into the dynamics learning process. The hypothesis is that by shaping the local sensitivity of the learned dynamics, the model can be made to better differentiate between control inputs, thereby improving the contrast between in-distribution and OOD transitions in the latent space.
- **Prevent Collapse of Latent Predictions:** A specific objective is to prevent undemonstrated or perturbed controls from collapsing to overly similar latent transitions. If different control inputs lead to nearly indistinguishable latent states, then any OOD detection mechanism operating in that latent space will struggle to identify true deviations.
- **Support-Conditioned Regularization:** The work aims to achieve this improved sensitivity without introducing instability. A detailed objective is to apply sensitivity regularization selectively, targeting only high-support regions of the training distribution. This approach seeks to promote responsive dynamics where sufficient empirical data exists, while avoiding the amplification of extrapolation errors in weakly supported or unseen regions.
- **Demonstrate Practical Impact:** Finally, the research aims to empirically demonstrate that the proposed method leads to improved OOD detection capabilities and enables safer closed-loop planning across various robotic tasks, ranging from simulated vision-based obstacle avoidance to real-robot navigation and manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research adopts a multi-faceted approach involving theoretical analysis, a novel regularization method, and comprehensive experimental validation across diverse robotic tasks.

#### 3.1. Analysis of Control Insensitivity in Latent Dynamics

The paper first empirically investigates the phenomenon of control insensitivity.
- **Running Example:** A vision-based Dubins obstacle avoidance system is used to illustrate the issue. This system involves an agent with constant-speed Dubins dynamics and a scalar steering-rate input, observing bird's-eye-view images. Generative dynamics models (DreamerV3) are trained on collision-free trajectories.
- **Data Analysis:** The training data is analyzed for spatial density and empirical control variance. It is observed that while state coverage can be dense near obstacles, control diversity is often low in these safety-critical regions because failure trajectories are excluded. This reduced control diversity is hypothesized to weaken the identifiability of control-dependent dynamics.
- **Measuring Control Sensitivity:** Control sensitivity is formalized by the Frobenius norm of the control Jacobian (∥J_u F_θ(z_t, u_t)∥_F), which quantifies the aggregate first-order response of the predicted next latent state to control input perturbations.
- **Quantifying Mismatch:** The model-predicted dynamics response (E_F_θ) is compared against the true system response (E_O) in the latent space. The analysis reveals that in low-sensitivity regions, the learned dynamics significantly underestimate control-induced variation, with the gap between predicted and true responses increasing with perturbation magnitude.
- **Impact on OOD Surrogates:** The paper demonstrates how this control insensitivity degrades the performance of existing OOD surrogates (kNN, Flow Matching, Ensemble). When dynamics are insensitive, perturbed controls produce predictions that appear close to the training distribution in the latent space, causing these surrogates to yield low OOD scores despite significant true prediction errors. kNN is shown to be particularly susceptible, while ensembles can exhibit non-monotonic responses.

#### 3.2. Support-Conditioned Control Sensitivity Regularization

To mitigate control insensitivity, the authors propose a new regularization term integrated into the dynamics model training.
- **Core Principle:** The regularization promotes non-trivial Frobenius norms on the control Jacobian within specific regions. This prevents latent predictions for distinct (even if undemonstrated) controls from collapsing into overly similar representations, thereby enhancing the detectability of unsupported transitions.
- **Regularization Formulation:** The regularization loss, `L_reg`, is defined as the expected positive difference between a margin `g` and the squared Frobenius norm of the control Jacobian. This penalizes vanishing sensitivity while allowing the norm to grow above the margin.
 `L_reg = E_{(o_t,u_t)∼D_bar, z_t∼ϕ_θ(·|o_t)} [ (g - ||∂F_θ(z_t, u_t)/∂u_t||_F^2)_+ ]`
- **Support-Conditioned Targeting:** Crucially, `L_reg` is applied only to high-support regions of the training distribution, denoted `D_bar`. This is to prevent the regularization from amplifying extrapolation errors in weakly supported regions. `D_bar` is identified by retaining a `β`-fraction of training samples with the smallest kNN distances in the latent space, serving as a parameter-free proxy for local support. This set `D_bar` is periodically updated during training.
- **Overall Training Objective:** The full training objective combines the standard generative dynamics loss (`L_dyn`, e.g., DreamerV3's objective) with the proposed regularization term: `L = L_dyn + λ_reg L_reg`, where `λ_reg` is a regularization weight.
- **Computational Efficiency:** To reduce the computational cost of calculating the Frobenius norm for each sample, Hutchinson’s identity is employed. This method uses Monte Carlo sampling with Jacobian-vector products (computable via automatic differentiation) instead of explicitly constructing the full Jacobian matrix.

#### 3.3. Experimental Validation

The proposed method is evaluated in three distinct robotic domains:
- **Dubins Obstacle Avoidance:** Used to validate improved sensitivity and OOD-aware one-step safety filtering for a random reference policy. Metrics include success rate, filtered fraction, and OOD violation rate.
- **Block Plucking Manipulation:** A Franka arm task involving extracting a block from a stack. Evaluation includes filtering a mediocre policy and filtering a policy trained with imagined rollouts, using a reachability-based safety filter. Metrics include failure rate, incompletion rate, filtered fraction, and reconstruction error.
- **Real-Robot Static-Obstacle Avoidance:** A Unitree Go2 quadruped avoiding obstacles, with observations from body-mounted cameras and discrete control commands. One-step and reachability-based filters are evaluated for a random reference policy.

#### 3.4. OOD Surrogates

The paper evaluates three types of OOD surrogates:
- **k-Nearest-Neighbor (kNN):** Measures the average Euclidean distance from a predicted successor latent state to its `k` nearest neighbors in the calibration set.
- **Flow Matching (FM):** Estimates conditional density by training a flow model to map predicted successors to a Gaussian base distribution, using the negative log-density as the score.
- **Ensemble (ENS):** Uses the Jensen–Rényi divergence of predictions from multiple independently trained transition predictors as a measure of disagreement.

All surrogates are calibrated using inductive conformal prediction to set OOD thresholds at a prescribed level `α`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings across its analyses and experimental validations:

- **Evidence of Control Insensitivity:** The initial analysis in the Dubins obstacle avoidance system demonstrated that generative dynamics models, particularly in safety-critical regions with limited control diversity, exhibit low control sensitivity. This was quantified by low Frobenius norms of the control Jacobian for the vanilla model. This insensitivity led to predicted latent transitions that substantially underestimated the true control-induced variation, even for small perturbations.
- **Degradation of Post-Hoc OOD Detection:** Empirical results showed that control insensitivity directly compromises the effectiveness of existing OOD surrogates. When the learned dynamics map distinct controls to overly similar latent states, kNN, Ensemble, and Flow Matching surrogates fail to reliably flag genuinely unsupported transitions as OOD. kNN was particularly poor, often assigning in-distribution scores to safety-critical OOD inputs, while ensemble scores could be non-monotonic.
- **Improved Control Sensitivity with Regularization:** The proposed support-conditioned control sensitivity regularization successfully induced stronger responsiveness in the learned dynamics. In the Dubins environment, the regularized model exhibited substantially higher control-Jacobian norms in previously insensitive near-obstacle regions. Latent space visualizations (PCA+t-SNE) further indicated improved separation between latent transitions generated by demonstrated and undemonstrated controls, confirming that the regularization effectively encourages the model to capture more control-induced variation. The regularized model's latent response was shown to more closely track the ground-truth latent response.
- **Enhanced OOD-Aware Planning:**
 - **Dubins Obstacle Avoidance:** Safety filters built upon sensitivity-regularized dynamics consistently achieved higher success rates and lower OOD violation rates compared to filters using vanilla dynamics. This improvement was observed across all three OOD surrogates (kNN, Flow Matching, Ensemble), with Flow Matching generally performing best.
 - **Block Plucking Manipulation:** A reachability-based safety filter, when trained with sensitivity-regularized dynamics, resulted in a lower task failure rate and lower reconstruction error, suggesting that the filtered trajectories remained closer to the training support. Furthermore, the regularized dynamics allowed for less conservative OOD calibration (i.e., smaller `α` values to maintain safety), indicating that the improved control sensitivity reduced the need for excessively cautious thresholding.
 - **Real-Robot Static-Obstacle Avoidance:** On the Unitree Go2 robot, the sensitivity-regularized dynamics, particularly when paired with the Flow Matching surrogate, achieved a perfect success rate in obstacle avoidance. The ensemble filter also showed a significant improvement in success rate, suggesting that reachability-based filtering benefits substantially from improved control-conditioned transition capture.
- **Trade-offs and Hyperparameter Sensitivity:** Ablation studies demonstrated the importance of support-conditioned targeting. While increasing the support fraction (`β`) initially improved latent separation and consistency, too broad an application of the regularizer (large `β`) could degrade reconstruction quality and downstream safety filtering performance. Similarly, an optimal sensitivity margin (`g`) was identified, with overly strong regularization potentially harming performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work makes a significant contribution to the field of safe robot learning and model-based control by addressing a fundamental limitation in existing OOD detection paradigms for generative dynamics models.

- **Novel Identification of a Failure Mode:** The paper's primary significance lies in identifying and thoroughly analyzing control insensitivity as a critical, previously underexplored failure mode for OOD analysis in learned dynamics. By showing how insensitive dynamics can suppress OOD signals, it reorients the focus from solely post-hoc uncertainty quantification to shaping the model's fundamental representational properties.
- **Enhanced Safety and Reliability in Robotics:** The proposed support-conditioned sensitivity regularization directly improves the reliability of OOD detection. This is crucial for deploying robotic systems in real-world, safety-critical applications where encountering unexpected state-action inputs is inevitable. By making models more capable of discerning control-induced deviations, the method contributes to building safer and more trustworthy autonomous systems.
- **Broader Applicability and Generalization:** The approach is demonstrated to be compatible with various generative dynamics models (e.g., DreamerV3) and multiple OOD surrogates (kNN, Flow Matching, Ensemble). Its effectiveness across diverse robotic tasks—including vision-based simulated obstacle avoidance, complex manipulation, and real-robot navigation—suggests broad applicability and potential for generalization across different robot platforms and problem settings.
- **Reduced Reliance on Extensive Data Coverage:** By enabling models to better capture and differentiate control-induced variations, the method may reduce the need for exhaustively large and diverse training datasets that cover every possible counterfactual. Instead, it allows for more robust detection of deviations from the *learned structure* of the dynamics.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** The paper also outlines limitations, such as the computational cost of periodic support set updates and potential issues with latent kNN-based support estimates in complex visual environments. These limitations open promising avenues for future research into more scalable, adaptive, and robust methods for support estimation, potentially integrating richer visual representations, and further extensive hardware validation. This work provides a foundation for developing a new class of OOD-aware dynamics models that are inherently more sensitive to control inputs and thus more reliable in uncertain environments.
