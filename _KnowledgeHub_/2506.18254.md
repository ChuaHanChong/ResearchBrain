# RLPR: Extrapolating RLVR to General Domains without Verifiers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and NUS developed RLPR, a verifier-free reinforcement learning framework that enhances Large Language Model reasoning across general domains by using an intrinsic probability-based reward. This method achieved an average 24.9% improvement on general-domain benchmarks and consistently outperformed existing RLVR and concurrent verifier-free approaches by removing the need for external verification.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs heavily rely on domain-specific external verifiers.
- Handcrafting rule-based verifiers is laborious, complex, and leads to brittle systems, while training LLM-based verifiers introduces high annotation costs and complexity.
- Applying RLVR to general, open-ended natural language reasoning is difficult due to the inability to devise general-purpose verifiers, limiting scalability.

## Method

- ==RLPR== introduces a =='Probability-based Reward' (PR)== that uses the mean of an LLM's per-token decoding probabilities for a ground-truth reference answer as a reward signal.
- A =='Reward Debiasing'== mechanism is employed to calculate the reward as the improvement in probability gained by generating reasoning, by subtracting a base score obtained without intermediate reasoning.
- A =='Standard Deviation Filtering'== approach dynamically filters out training prompts with low reward standard deviation, focusing learning on more informative samples and stabilizing training.

## Results

- RLPR achieved an average **24.9%** improvement on four general-domain benchmarks using Qwen2.5-7B, demonstrating its ability to extend RL to broader reasoning tasks.
- The framework consistently outperformed traditional RLVR baselines and specialized model-based verifiers, for example, surpassing General Reasoner-7B by **1.6 average points** across seven benchmarks.
- Quantitative analysis showed the Probability-based Reward (PR) achieved high discrimination (e.g., **0.95** ROC-AUC on math data), significantly outperforming rule-based verifiers (**0.61** on general data).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An LLM's intrinsic probability of generating a correct free-form answer can serve as an internal evaluation of reasoning reward.
> - The mean of per-token probabilities for a reference answer is a more robust reward signal than naive sequence likelihood, which is highly sensitive to minor variations.
> - Debiasing the reward by subtracting a base score (probability without reasoning) isolates the contribution of the generated reasoning process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of advanced reasoning capabilities in Large Language Models (LLMs) is a central pursuit in Artificial Intelligence. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a promising paradigm in this landscape, demonstrating significant success in enhancing LLM performance, particularly in structured domains like mathematics and code generation. Methods such as AlphaCode, Minerva, and DeepSeekMath have leveraged RLVR to achieve superhuman performance on specific tasks by employing external, often rule-based, verifiers to provide reward signals.

However, a critical limitation of existing RLVR approaches is their heavy reliance on these domain-specific verifiers. Handcrafting rules for verifiers is a laborious and complex engineering task, often leading to brittle systems that struggle with edge cases and lack generalizability. For open-ended, free-form natural language reasoning, devising such verifiers becomes exceedingly difficult, if not impossible, due to the inherent diversity and complexity of human language. While some recent efforts have explored training specialized LLMs as "verifier models," this introduces additional complexity, data annotation costs, and potential for unsatisfactory reward quality. This "scalability problem" is the key bottleneck preventing RLVR from being widely applied to general-domain reasoning.

RLPR (Reinforcement Learning with Reference Probability Reward) directly addresses this fundamental limitation. By proposing a "verifier-free" framework, RLPR aims to extrapolate the benefits of RLVR to broader, general domains without the need for external, domain-specific, or even model-based verifiers. It differentiates itself from existing RLVR by removing the explicit verifier requirement, effectively simplifying the training pipeline and making RL more broadly applicable to complex, free-form reasoning tasks.

The paper also positions RLPR against concurrent verifier-free methods, such as VeriFree, which uses likelihood of short reference answers as a reward. RLPR claims superiority by using per-token probabilities for rewards, demonstrating better robustness across different answer lengths and domains. Furthermore, it distinguishes itself from "self-reward optimization" techniques (e.g., those focusing on entropy minimization or consistency with majority votes) by explicitly deriving rewards from the LLM's intrinsic probability of generating *reference answers*, thereby grounding the reward in external truth while eliminating the external verifier dependency. This positions RLPR as a significant step towards more scalable, general, and practical reinforcement learning for LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **extend Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) to broader, general reasoning domains without relying on external verifiers**.

The key motivations driving this objective are:

- **Addressing the Scalability Limitation of RLVR:** Existing RLVR methods, despite their success in domains like mathematics and code, are heavily constrained by their dependence on domain-specific verifiers (e.g., rule-based or trained verifier models). Creating and maintaining these verifiers is labor-intensive, complex, and becomes practically impossible for the diverse and free-form nature of general-domain reasoning.
- **Eliminating Prohibitive Engineering and Annotation Costs:** Handcrafting rule-based verifiers requires extensive heuristic engineering. Training separate LLM-based verifier models demands significant and costly data annotation. This work aims to bypass these challenges entirely.
- **Leveraging Intrinsic LLM Capabilities:** The authors observe that an LLM's "intrinsic probability of generating a correct free-form answer directly indicates its own evaluation of the reasoning reward." This insight suggests that the model itself possesses a built-in signal that can be leveraged for reward, making external verifiers redundant.
- **Enabling Broader Application of RL for LLMs:** By removing the verifier bottleneck, RLPR seeks to unlock the full potential of RL to improve LLMs' reasoning capabilities across a much wider array of general-domain tasks, contributing to the path towards more general artificial intelligence.
- **Improving Reward Signal Quality:** The paper also aims to demonstrate that an intrinsic probability-based reward can be more robust and higher quality than naive approaches (like sequence likelihood) or even some model-based verifiers, especially for handling the nuances of natural language.

In essence, RLPR seeks to simplify and democratize the application of RL to LLM reasoning by making the reward signal acquisition process self-contained within the LLM, thereby facilitating wider adoption and exploration of RL-driven improvements for general AI tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RLPR introduces a novel verifier-free framework built on the core insight that an LLM's internal token probabilities for a reference answer can serve as a potent reward signal. The methodology comprises three key innovations:

#### 3.1. Probability-based Reward (PR)

Instead of relying on external verifiers, RLPR leverages the LLM's own intrinsic decoding probabilities. Given an input question, the policy model generates a reasoning process (`z`) and a final answer (`y`). To calculate the reward, the generated answer `y` is replaced with the ground-truth reference answer (`y*`) from the training data, forming a modified sequence `o'`. This `o'` is then fed back into the policy model to obtain per-token decoding probabilities.

Crucially, RLPR does not use the naive product of probabilities (sequence likelihood) as the reward, as this approach is highly sensitive to minor variations and low-probability tokens, leading to high variance and instability (a known issue that concurrent work like VeriFree attempts to mitigate by filtering short answers). Instead, RLPR proposes using the **mean of the per-token probabilities** of the reference answer (`f_seq = (1/|y*|) Σ p_i | o'_i ∈ y*`). This mean probability (`PR`) is found to be more robust and better correlated with answer quality.

#### 3.2. Reward Debiasing

The raw probability reward (`r`) might be influenced by factors inherent to the question and reference answer themselves, rather than solely reflecting the quality of the generated reasoning. To isolate the contribution of the reasoning process (`z`), RLPR introduces a debiasing mechanism. It calculates a "base score" (`r'`) by computing the probability score of directly generating the reference answer (`y*`) *without* any intermediate reasoning (`z`). The debiased probability reward (`ˆr`) is then calculated as the difference between the raw reward and this base score: `ˆr = clip(0,1, r - r')`. This effectively models the reward as the *improvement* in probability gained by generating the reasoning `z`, thus removing bias from question or reference answer characteristics. The clipping ensures the reward remains within a favorable range. The policy then optimizes the expected debiased reward: `E[ˆr * ∇logπ_θ(o|x)]`.

#### 3.3. Standard Deviation Filtering

Reinforcement learning training can be unstable, especially when rewards are noisy or when samples are either too easy (always high reward) or too difficult (always low reward). Traditional RLVR methods often employ "accuracy filtering" to remove such prompts. However, with continuous probability-based rewards, setting discrete thresholds for "correct" or "incorrect" becomes challenging.

RLPR addresses this by proposing a **standard deviation filtering** mechanism. The core idea is that prompts yielding low standard deviation in reward values (across multiple sampled responses) indicate samples that are consistently easy or consistently hard. These samples provide less informative gradients for policy improvement. To adapt to shifts in reward distribution during training, the filtering threshold (`β`) is dynamically updated using an exponential moving average of the average standard deviation of rewards from previous training steps. Prompts whose reward standard deviation falls below `β` are filtered out, effectively creating an adaptive curriculum learning mechanism that stabilizes training and improves final performance by focusing on more informative samples.

These three components – a robust probability-based reward, a reasoning-focused debiasing strategy, and an adaptive sample filtering mechanism – collectively enable RLPR to extend the benefits of RL to general-domain reasoning tasks without external verification.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive experiments conducted across various LLM families (Qwen, Llama, Gemma) and seven diverse reasoning benchmarks (MMLU-Pro, GPQA, TheoremQA, WebInstruct for general domains; MATH-500, Minerva, AIME24 for mathematics) demonstrate the strong effectiveness of RLPR.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

- **Significant General-Domain Reasoning Enhancement:** RLPR substantially improves performance on general-domain reasoning tasks. For instance, using Qwen2.5-7B, RLPR achieves a 24.9% average improvement on four general-domain benchmarks, showcasing its ability to extrapolate RLVR beyond its traditional confines.
- **Outperformance of RLVR Baselines:** RLPR consistently outperforms standard RLVR (which uses rule-based verifiers) across all tested base models (Qwen, Llama, Gemma). Specifically, it achieved 1.4, 3.9, and 1.4 average points higher general reasoning performance on Gemma, Llama, and Qwen, respectively, despite being verifier-free.
- **Competitive with Mathematical Reasoning Frameworks:** Despite being trained on non-mathematical data (WebInstruct excluding math prompts), RLPR demonstrates strong mathematical reasoning capabilities, even surpassing specialized math-focused RLVR frameworks like Oat-Zero and SimpleRL-Zoo on Minerva. This indicates that the general reasoning improvements generalize well to structured mathematical problems.
- **Superior to Model-Based Verifiers:** Notably, RLPR (using Qwen2.5-7B) surpasses General Reasoner-7B, which employs a specially trained 1.5B-parameter verifier model, by an average of 1.6 points across all seven benchmarks. This suggests that the intrinsic probability reward is more effective and less complex than relying on a separate, trained verifier model.
- **Clear Advantage over Concurrent Verifier-Free Methods:** Compared to VeriFree, a concurrent verifier-free approach, RLPR achieves significant improvements, scoring 7.6 points higher on TheoremQA and 7.5 points higher on Minerva. This highlights the robustness of RLPR's probability reward, which does not impose length constraints on answers unlike VeriFree.

#### 4.2. Probability-Based Reward (PR) Analysis

The paper provides strong evidence for the quality of its Probability-based Reward (PR):

- **High Discrimination Capability:** Quantitative analysis using ROC-AUC shows that PR consistently achieves high AUC values (e.g., 0.95 on math data, 0.90-0.91 on general data for Qwen models), indicating its superior ability to distinguish between correct and incorrect responses compared to rule-based verifiers (0.61 on general data) and even some verifier models (0.69 on general data).
- **Robustness Across Model Sizes:** Even small-scale Qwen2.5-0.5B using PR outperforms a specially trained General-Verifier on both mathematical and general data, demonstrating the inherent effectiveness and scalability of the PR concept.
- **Independence from Length and Entropy:** PR values show negligible correlation with the length and decoding entropy of generated responses, reinforcing its robustness as a reward signal that is not biased by these factors.
- **Essential for General-Domain Data Utilization:** The study confirms that general-domain data enhances performance, but only when coupled with a reward mechanism like PR that can handle natural language complexity; traditional rule-based verifiers struggle severely with such data.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies confirm the contribution of each proposed component:
- Removing **reward debiasing** (`w/o debiasing`) leads to performance drops (e.g., -2.7 on TheoremQA, -2.4 on Minerva).
- Disabling **standard deviation filtering** (`w/o std-filtering`) also results in lower performance (e.g., -2.9 on TheoremQA, -1.4 on Minerva).
- Replacing the proposed **mean per-token probability** with naive sequence likelihood (`w/o token prob.`) causes drastic performance degradation (e.g., -21.9 on TheoremQA, -22.3 on Minerva), highlighting the critical importance of the specific PR formulation.

#### 4.4. Robustness and Verifiable Domains

- **Robustness to Prompt Templates:** RLPR demonstrates consistent performance across different training prompt templates, unlike VeriFree which shows high sensitivity, indicating RLPR's generalizability and reliability.
- **Enhancement in Verifiable Domains:** Even in mathematical domains where rule-based verifiers are available, combining PR with rule-based rewards further improves performance, suggesting that the fine-grained discrimination offered by PR can complement even reliable binary correctness labels.

Overall, the findings strongly support RLPR's claim of providing a simple, scalable, and effective verifier-free RL framework that significantly boosts LLM reasoning capabilities across diverse domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of RLPR carries significant implications for the future of Large Language Model development and the broader field of Artificial Intelligence:

- **Breaking the Verifier Dependency Barrier:** RLPR's primary significance lies in eliminating the critical bottleneck of domain-specific external verifiers for Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). This moves RL-based training for LLMs from being a highly specialized, domain-locked technique to a more general and broadly applicable paradigm.
- **Enhanced Scalability and Generalizability:** By leveraging the LLM's intrinsic probability as a reward signal, RLPR dramatically simplifies the training pipeline, making it more scalable to diverse and complex general reasoning tasks that lack clear-cut, verifiable answers. This is a crucial step towards developing truly general-purpose AI systems that can reason across open-ended domains.
- **Reduced Development Costs and Complexity:** The framework obviates the need for expensive, time-consuming, and labor-intensive manual heuristic engineering for rule-based verifiers or extensive data annotation for training separate verifier models. This democratizes access to powerful RL techniques for LLM fine-tuning.
- **Improved Reward Signal Quality:** The findings demonstrate that RLPR's Probability-based Reward (PR), with its mean-token probability and debiasing, offers a more robust, stable, and discriminative reward signal compared to simpler likelihood-based approaches and even some model-based verifiers. This improved signal quality directly translates to better model performance.
- **Catalyst for General AI Research:** By making RL applicable to general-domain reasoning, RLPR opens new avenues for research into incentivized exploration and evolution of LLM capabilities. It brings the "scaling test-time computation" approach of RLVR to bear on a wider array of problems, potentially accelerating the path toward Artificial General Intelligence (AGI).
- **Broader Adoption of RL for LLM Tuning:** The simplicity and effectiveness of RLPR could encourage wider adoption of reinforcement learning as a post-training paradigm for enhancing LLMs, moving beyond purely supervised fine-tuning or RLHF reliant on human preferences (which also have scalability issues).
- **Applicability Across Model Families:** Demonstrating consistent improvements across Gemma, Llama, and Qwen models confirms the method's generality and not just its effectiveness on a single architecture.

In conclusion, RLPR represents a foundational advance that removes a major practical hurdle in applying reinforcement learning to complex LLM reasoning. Its verifier-free nature, coupled with its robust reward mechanism and training stabilization strategies, makes it a highly significant contribution that can accelerate the development of more capable, general, and autonomous LLM agents. The open-sourcing of code, data, and models will further facilitate future research in this critical area.
