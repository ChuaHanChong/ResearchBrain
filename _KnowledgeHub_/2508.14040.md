# ComputerRL: Scaling End-to-End Online Reinforcement Learning for Computer Use Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> The COMPUTERRL framework scales online reinforcement learning for computer use agents, achieving new state-of-the-art accuracy of 48.9% on the OSWorld benchmark and 43.3% on the new OfficeWorld benchmark by integrating an API-GUI interaction paradigm, a scalable distributed environment, and the Entropulse training strategy.

## Problem

- Developing robust LLM-based GUI agents is challenging due to the inherent human-centric design of graphical user interfaces, making machine interaction complex.
- Existing methods like Behavior Cloning suffer from scalability issues, limited generalization, and poor error recovery for diverse and complex desktop tasks.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning to desktop automation has been hindered by computational intensity, slow convergence rates, instability, and a tendency for policies to lose exploratory capacity (entropy collapse) during extended training.

## Method

- An ==API-GUI== interaction paradigm unifies direct GUI interactions with programmatic API calls, complemented by an LLM-based automated workflow for API development to enhance efficiency and versatility.
- A scalable and stable Ubuntu environment with a distributed multi-node clustering infrastructure was developed, capable of orchestrating thousands of concurrent virtual desktop environments for efficient data collection.
- A full-asynchronous RL framework is implemented, featuring multi-LLM driven Behavior Cloning, step-level Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with verifiable rewards, and the Entropulse strategy for sustained exploration through periodic RL-SFT alternations.

## Results

- AUTOGLM-OS-**9B**, trained with COMPUTERRL, achieved a new state-of-the-art accuracy of **48.9%** on the OSWorld benchmark, representing a **66%** performance gain from the RL phase and surpassing leading baselines.
- The framework also introduced the OFFICEWORLD benchmark and demonstrated a superior average success rate of **43.3%** on office-oriented tasks, outperforming agents like OpenAI CUA o3 (**33.9%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed the API-GUI paradigm significantly improved task completion with fewer steps, and the Entropulse strategy led to substantial performance improvements in the second RL phase by restoring policy entropy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The API-GUI paradigm significantly improves agent efficiency and versatility, demonstrating a 134% performance gain over GUI-only approaches in desktop automation by effectively combining programmatic and visual interactions.
> - A robust, distributed, and asynchronous RL infrastructure is crucial for overcoming the computational and stability challenges of training agents in complex, high-dimensional desktop environments.
> - The Entropulse strategy successfully counteracts entropy collapse in large-scale RL training, enabling sustained exploration and further performance gains by periodically rejuvenating the policy's exploratory capacity through SFT.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed dramatic advancements with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs), which have significantly expanded AI capabilities across numerous domains. A particularly exciting and challenging frontier is the development of LLM-based Graphical User Interface (GUI) agents, often referred to as "computer use agents" or "desktop intelligence." These agents aim to autonomously perceive, reason, and execute complex tasks within digital workspaces, fundamentally transforming human-computer interaction. The paper positions itself directly within this rapidly evolving landscape.

Historically, challenges in this area stem from the inherent human-centric design of GUIs, which makes machine imitation of human actions complex and cumbersome. Prior approaches, such as Behavior Cloning (BC) via manual annotation or model distillation, suffer from scalability issues, limited generalization, and poor error recovery. Manual annotation is prohibitively labor-intensive, while model distillation is constrained by the performance and diversity of existing "teacher" models.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a promising alternative, offering the potential for agents to learn and adapt more robustly. However, its widespread adoption in desktop automation has been hampered by significant practical hurdles: the computational intensity of complex environments, slow convergence rates, and general inefficiencies in RL training. These limitations severely restrict large-scale online RL for computer use agents, preventing the kind of iterative improvement and generalization seen in other RL domains.

COMPUTERRL directly confronts these challenges by proposing an end-to-end algorithmic framework designed to scale RL training for desktop agents. It contributes to several sub-areas:

- **LLM-based Agents and Multimodal AI**: By leveraging advanced open models like GLM-4 and GLM-4.1V-Thinking, the work contributes to the growing trend of empowering LLMs with multimodal perception and control capabilities over real-world interfaces.
- **GUI Agent Development**: It builds upon and pushes the boundaries of existing computer use agents such as CogAgent, AutoGLM, OS-Atlas, Aguvis, PC-Agent-E, UI-TARS, Agent S2, and OpenAI CUA, by providing a robust training framework.
- **RL for Complex Environments**: The paper specifically addresses the unique difficulties of applying RL to desktop environments, which are dynamic, high-dimensional, and human-oriented. Its contributions to distributed training infrastructure and algorithmic stability directly tackle known inefficiencies in RL for such complex settings.
- **Benchmarks and Evaluation**: By evaluating on established benchmarks like OSWorld and introducing the new OFFICEWORLD benchmark, the research not only demonstrates its own advancements but also contributes to the critical need for standardized and realistic evaluation methodologies in the field.
- **Scalable RL Training for LLMs**: The Entropulse strategy specifically targets the problem of entropy collapse in extended RL training runs for LLMs, a known issue in the broader field of training large models with RL (related to works like PPO, GRPO, DAPO, ARPO).

In essence, COMPUTERRL aims to unlock the full potential of RL for creating highly capable and generalizable computer use agents, moving beyond the limitations of previous methods and addressing the core technical barriers to large-scale online policy optimization in desktop environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and scalable framework, COMPUTERRL, that enables machine agents to skillfully operate complex digital workspaces autonomously. The authors are driven by the ambition to fundamentally transform human-computer interactions and elevate the capabilities of AI by making desktop environments fully accessible and manipulable by intelligent agents.

The core motivations and specific challenges addressed by COMPUTERRL can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Human-Machine Interface Gap**: A fundamental motivation is to overcome the "inherent mismatch between machine agents and human-centric desktop environments." GUIs are designed for human perception and interaction, making it difficult for machine agents to effectively interpret and act upon them. The authors aim to introduce a more "machine-oriented" approach that retains versatility while enhancing efficiency.
- **Overcoming Scalability and Generalization Limitations of Existing Methods**: Current state-of-the-art methods, primarily Behavior Cloning (BC) via manual annotation or model distillation, are limited in their ability to scale to complex, diverse tasks and generalize to unseen scenarios. They also struggle with error recovery. The authors seek a method that can continuously improve and generalize.
- **Addressing RL Inefficiencies for Desktop Tasks**: While Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers promise for robust learning and adaptation, its application to desktop automation has been restricted by several practical issues:
 - **Environmental Inefficiency**: Running complex desktop environments for thousands of training iterations is computationally demanding and often unstable.
 - **Slow Convergence and Instability**: Standard RL algorithms can be slow to converge in complex, high-dimensional action spaces, and susceptible to instability during extended training runs.
 - **Entropy Collapse**: A specific challenge identified is the tendency for policies to prematurely converge and lose their exploratory capacity (entropy collapse), hindering further learning and performance gains.
- **Enabling End-to-End Online Policy Optimization**: The ultimate goal is to enable efficient end-to-end online RL training, allowing agents to continuously learn, adapt, and improve their desktop operation skills in a self-improving loop, moving beyond static datasets.

By tackling these intertwined challenges, COMPUTERRL aims to create agents that not only achieve high performance on specific tasks but also possess the robustness, adaptability, and generalized intelligence required for real-world autonomous desktop use.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

COMPUTERRL is designed as an end-to-end algorithmic framework that integrates several innovations across interaction paradigms, infrastructure, and training algorithms to enable scalable and robust online reinforcement learning for computer use agents.

#### 3.1. The API-GUI Interaction Paradigm

To address the mismatch between human-centric GUIs and machine agents, COMPUTERRL introduces the API-GUI paradigm. This approach unifies direct GUI interactions (like clicks, scrolls, types) with programmatic API calls. The core idea is to allow agents to leverage the efficiency of APIs where available, while retaining the versatility of GUI interactions for unsupported or novel functionalities.

A key innovation is an LLM-based automated workflow for API development, minimizing human intervention. Users provide example tasks, and an LLM autonomously generates API code and test cases through three stages:
1. **Requirement Analysis**: LLMs analyze tasks, identify essential functionalities, compare them against existing APIs, and automatically generate new, general-purpose API interfaces for uncovered functionalities.
2. **API Implementation**: For each interface, Python libraries are used to implement functionalities, including error-handling and logging.
3. **Test Case Generation**: Unit tests verify API correctness (runtime errors, correct results), providing feedback for autonomous correction.
This methodology generates application-specific API sets for multiple Ubuntu applications. The agent's observation space is enriched with GUI element attributes (semantic type, text, coordinates, dimensions) extracted via `pyatspi` for text-based models, or screenshots (1280x720) for multimodal models. Contextual metadata, such as open window information and last action feedback, is also provided. The action space dynamically offers relevant APIs based on the active application, presented as Python function calls within the system prompt to facilitate LLM interpretability.

#### 3.2. Scalable and Stable Ubuntu Environment for Large-Scale Parallelism

Recognizing the computational intensity and instability of existing desktop environments for large-scale RL, the authors developed a robust, parallelizable infrastructure building upon OSWorld. Key innovations include:
- **Standardized, Decoupled Interface**: Refactoring the environment via AgentBench API to decouple execution from the computational backend, allowing flexible resource management.
- **Lightweight VM Deployment**: Utilizing `qemu-in-dockere` to deploy containerized Ubuntu VMs with streamlined images, reducing resource usage and network issues.
- **Distributed Multi-Node Clustering**: Employing gRPC for communication to link CPU nodes, enabling centralized resource allocation and orchestration for thousands of concurrent environments.
- **Web-based Visualization and Monitoring**: A web interface provides real-time status, agent states, and resource allocation for improved usability and debugging.

#### 3.3. Full-Asynchronous RL Framework for Efficient Training

To overcome the inefficiencies of synchronous RL training, COMPUTERRL integrates with the AgentRL framework, facilitating fully asynchronous RL. This involves:
- **Resource Partitioning**: Decoupling data collection (rollouts) and model training by assigning dedicated resources, preventing mutual blocking.
- **Dynamic Batch Sizing**: The trainer processes incoming data with flexible batch sizes to optimize GPU utilization and reduce idle time.
- **Modular Component Isolation**: Actor, reference, and critic modules run independently, using PyTorch distributed groups and NCCL for efficient parameter sharing.
- **Off-policy Bias Mitigation**: Limiting replay buffer size and frequent trajectory synchronization to keep training data relevant to the latest policy.

#### 3.4. The COMPUTERRL Training Strategy

The training strategy is divided into three progressive stages:

- **Behavior Cloning (BC) Setup**:
 - **Trajectory Collection with Multiple LLMs**: To generate a diverse and high-quality cold-start dataset, the authors employ a multi-LLM data pipeline. Initial sampling uses closed-source LLMs to generate several trajectories per task. These outcomes are stratified into "Fully Solved," "Partially Solved," and "Unsolved" categories. For "partially solved" tasks, the backbone model is fine-tuned (SFT) and then used to sample more trajectories. For "unsolved" tasks, a pool of high-performing models is used, leveraging inter-model variance to generate diverse solutions.
 - **SFT**: Successful trajectories (180k+ steps) from this process are aggregated, filtered, and used for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of the backbone model, providing foundational desktop manipulation and reasoning abilities.

- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards**:
 - **Step-Level Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**: The GRPO algorithm is extended to the step-level. For each task, multiple trajectories are sampled, and advantages (`A_i,j`) are computed for each step `(o_i,j)`. The loss function `J_StepGRPO` aggregates these advantages, incorporating a clipping mechanism and a KL divergence penalty.
 - **Reward Design**: A rule-based verification function provides binary rewards: 1 for correctly formatted actions contributing to a solution, and 0 for failed trajectories or improperly formatted actions. This direct reward assignment, based on the final trajectory outcome rather than step-wise Bellman updates, simplifies feedback and couples behaviors with task success.

- **Entropulse for Scaling RL Training**:
 - To counteract performance plateaus and entropy collapse during extended RL training, the authors propose Entropulse. This novel strategy periodically alternates between RL and SFT phases.
 - During initial RL, all successful rollout trajectories are collected. Instead of discarding them, these diverse trajectories (from different policies and training steps) are curated into a new SFT training set.
 - SFT on this curated dataset, despite maintaining stable evaluation performance, significantly increases the policy's entropy, restoring its exploratory capacity.
 - This "re-pulsed" exploration enables a second round of RL training to achieve further, substantial performance improvements, leading to state-of-the-art results.

Together, these methodological components form a comprehensive system that addresses the multi-faceted challenges of scaling end-to-end online RL for robust and generalizable computer use agents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with COMPUTERRL demonstrate significant advancements in desktop automation, establishing new state-of-the-art performance on challenging benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Main Results on OSWorld

- **State-of-the-Art Performance**: AUTOGLM-OS-9B, trained with COMPUTERRL on open models GLM-4-9B-0414 and GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking, achieved a new state-of-the-art accuracy of **48.9%** on the OSWorld benchmark. This represents a substantial 66% performance gain directly attributable to the RL phase.
- **Outperformance of Baselines**: AUTOGLM-OS-9B significantly outperformed other leading agents, including proprietary and open models:
 - OpenAI CUA o3 (42.9%)
 - UI-TARS-1.5 (42.5%)
 - Agent S2 w/ Gemini-2.5-Pro (41.4%)
 - Claude 4.0 Sonnet (30.7%)
- **Execution Efficiency**: The API-GUI strategy enabled AUTOGLM-OS to complete tasks using at most 1/3 of the steps required by strong baseline approaches, demonstrating remarkable gains in operational efficiency.

#### 4.2. Office Application Performance on OfficeWorld

- **New Benchmark**: The authors introduced OFFICEWORLD, a new benchmark comprising 180 challenging tasks from SpreadsheetBench, PPTC, and in-house Writer tasks, specifically designed to evaluate agents in office-oriented scenarios.
- **Superior Office Automation**: AUTOGLM-OS (with GLM-4.1V-9B-Thinking) achieved an average success rate of **43.3%** on OfficeWorld, surpassing all common baselines. For instance, it outperformed OpenAI o3 (33.9%), GPT-4.1 (25.0%), and Claude 4.0 Sonnet (24.4%).
- **Domain Strengths**: The agent showed particularly strong performance in LibreOffice Calc (58.3%) and LibreOffice Writer (30.0%) tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Framework Ablation (API-GUI vs. GUI-Only)**: Using GPT-4o, the API-GUI paradigm achieved an average success rate of 26.2%, a remarkable 134% improvement over the GUI-only approach (11.2%). This gain was most pronounced in Office (27.9% vs. 6.2%, a 350% improvement) and Professional (41.6% vs. 14.3%, a 191% improvement) domains, validating the effectiveness and efficiency of combining API calls with GUI interactions.
- **Training Ablation (Impact of Stages)**: An ablation study on Qwen2.5-14B demonstrated the progressive impact of each training stage:
 - Untrained model: 15.2%
 - Behavior Cloning (BC): Jumped to 31.9%, establishing a strong foundation.
 - RL Phase 1: Further increased performance to 42.0% (+10.1% gain).
 - Entropulse: While maintaining similar evaluation performance (41.5%), it *significantly increased action entropy*, which is crucial for enhanced exploration.
 - RL Phase 2: Benefiting from the renewed exploration capacity from Entropulse, performance rose to **45.8%** (+3.8% from RL1), achieving the best overall. The Workflow domain showed the most dramatic improvement (10.8% → 27.2%).
- **RL Scalability (Entropulse Effect)**: Analysis of reward and entropy curves confirmed that Entropulse effectively restores the model's exploratory capacity, enabling "substantially scaled effective training steps" and leading to improved overall performance in subsequent RL phases, even when compared to simply resetting the reference model.

#### 4.4. Case Study and Error Analysis

An error analysis revealed four primary categories of failure: multi-application coordination failures (34.4%), visual perception errors (25.8%), operational illusions (14.2%), and other errors (25.6%). This highlights multi-application scenarios as the most challenging area, offering clear directions for future research.

In summary, COMPUTERRL consistently delivers superior performance across a range of desktop tasks and benchmarks. The results underscore the effectiveness of its integrated approach—combining an innovative interaction paradigm, a scalable infrastructure, and a robust training methodology—to push the boundaries of autonomous computer use agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The COMPUTERRL framework represents a significant leap forward in the field of autonomous desktop intelligence, with profound implications for both fundamental AI research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Advancing the State-of-the-Art in Desktop Automation

By achieving new state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks like OSWorld and OfficeWorld, COMPUTERRL demonstrates that highly capable and generalizable computer use agents are becoming a reality. The 66% performance gain attributed to RL highlights the transformative power of scalable online policy optimization in this domain. This work sets a new benchmark for what is achievable in end-to-end desktop automation.

#### 5.2. Bridging the Human-Machine Interaction Gap

The proposed API-GUI paradigm is a critical innovation that effectively addresses the long-standing challenge of enabling machine agents to interact with human-designed interfaces. By unifying efficient programmatic API calls with flexible GUI interactions, COMPUTERRL allows agents to operate digital workspaces more naturally and robustly. The automated workflow for API development further lowers the barrier to extending agent capabilities to new applications, accelerating the development of versatile agents.

#### 5.3. Enabling Scalable Reinforcement Learning for Complex Environments

A core contribution of this work is the development of a robust, distributed RL infrastructure capable of orchestrating thousands of parallel virtual desktop environments. This infrastructure overcomes critical bottlenecks that have previously limited the application of RL to complex, high-dimensional GUI tasks. By enabling large-scale, efficient online RL training, COMPUTERRL opens up new avenues for experimentation and iterative improvement that were previously computationally intractable. This infrastructure is a valuable resource for the broader research community.

#### 5.4. Algorithmic Innovations for Sustained Learning

The Entropulse training strategy is a notable algorithmic contribution that tackles a fundamental problem in extended RL training: entropy collapse. By strategically alternating between RL and SFT phases, Entropulse effectively restores the policy's exploratory capacity, leading to sustained performance gains. This innovation enhances the stability and efficiency of RL training for LLM-based agents, making long-term, continuous learning more viable.

#### 5.5. Practical Applications and Economic Impact

The practical implications of COMPUTERRL are immense. As a foundational framework adopted in projects like AUTOGLM, it paves the way for a new generation of autonomous agents that can automate complex, multi-application workflows. This could revolutionize productivity in various sectors, from administrative tasks and data analysis to software development and creative work. Imagine agents that can independently manage schedules, process documents, analyze spreadsheets, or even troubleshoot software, significantly augmenting human capabilities.

#### 5.6. Laying Groundwork for Future Research

The authors themselves point to crucial future directions, including:
- **Robust Performance**: Adapting to constantly changing GUIs, unfamiliar applications, and novel edge cases in dynamic real-world environments.
- **Long-Horizon Autonomy**: Endowing agents with hierarchical planning and reasoning capabilities to orchestrate complex, multi-step objectives over extended periods, automating entire workflows rather than just discrete tasks.
- **Safe and Aligned Autonomy**: Developing rigorous frameworks for granular permissions, pre-action validation, and multi-stage approval protocols to ensure trustworthiness and mitigate risks associated with autonomous control over sensitive desktop platforms.

In conclusion, COMPUTERRL is a landmark achievement that not only pushes the boundaries of autonomous desktop intelligence but also provides essential infrastructure and algorithmic solutions that will catalyze future advancements in intelligent human-computer interaction. It moves us closer to a future where AI agents can truly operate as skilled and reliable collaborators within our digital workspaces.
