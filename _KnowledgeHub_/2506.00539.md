# ARIA: Training Language Agents with Intention-Driven Reward Aggregation

> [!summary] Summary
> ARIA is a framework designed to enhance language agent training in open-ended language action tasks by addressing reward sparsity and high variance through intention-driven reward aggregation. It consistently achieved superior performance across adversarial and single-agent language games, demonstrating up to a 9.83% higher win rate in negotiation tasks and significant reductions in policy gradient variance.

## Problem

- Training language agents in open-ended language action environments faces extreme reward sparsity due to the exponentially large and unstructured action space of natural language.
- High reward variance in these environments leads to unstable policy optimization and slow convergence for traditional reinforcement learning algorithms.
- Existing RL methods struggle to efficiently learn robust policies given the "combinatorial explosion" and noisy feedback from free-form language actions.

## Method

- **ARIA** constructs a compact "==intention space==" by embedding natural language actions and observations into semantic vectors, then clustering them hierarchically.
- It aggregates sparse rewards within this intention space by averaging temporally discounted rewards for all history-action pairs that map to the same semantic intention, thereby densifying the reward signal.
- A novel `SplitScore` metric adaptively determines the optimal clustering granularity, directly aligning with the objective of maximizing RL performance rather than just geometric properties.

## Results

- ARIA achieved superior performance across diverse open-ended language tasks, with win rates up to **9.83%** higher in negotiation and an average of **9.82%** reward improvement in single-agent games compared to baselines.
- The framework consistently demonstrated continuous policy improvement over multiple training iterations and enabled faster, more stable online training with consistently higher returns.
- Empirical evidence confirmed a significant reduction in reward variance, and theoretical analysis proved that ARIA reduces the total variance of the policy gradient estimator while maintaining a bounded bias.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Despite the vastness of natural language, underlying "intentions" can be semantically clustered, allowing for a lower-dimensional and more manageable representation of the action space.
> - Aggregating rewards based on these intentions significantly reduces policy gradient variance, leading to more stable and efficient reinforcement learning.
> - Optimizing clustering granularity with a reward-oriented metric like `SplitScore` is crucial for effectively compressing the action space and improving the quality of the reward signal for RL.
