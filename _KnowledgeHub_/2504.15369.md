# Solving New Tasks by Adapting Internet Video Knowledge

> [!summary] Summary
> This work explores adapting large-scale pre-trained video generative models to solve robotic tasks specified through natural language. A novel Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation technique is introduced, demonstrating strong generalization and improving task success rates by up to 3x compared to unadapted models.

## Problem

- Large-scale pre-trained video generative models possess broad knowledge but lack the specific environment understanding crucial for robotic applications.
- Traditional robotics learning heavily relies on expensive, in-domain data collection, which limits generalization to novel tasks.
- A challenge exists in effectively balancing the broad generalization capabilities of pre-trained models with the specific knowledge required for particular robotic environments.

## Method

- The research investigates three adaptation techniques: ==Direct Finetuning==, ==Subject Customization==, and ==Probabilistic Adaptation==, to integrate in-domain information into pre-trained ==text-to-video models==.
- A novel ==Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation== technique is introduced, which keeps the pre-trained model frozen and subtracts the score of a small 'distractor' model trained on irrelevant content.
- Adapted models are evaluated on two downstream robotics tasks: ==Visual Planning== (generating trajectories) and ==Policy Supervision== (training a policy from generated rollouts).

## Results

- All adaptation techniques improved the ability of pre-trained models to solve novel robotic tasks, with the most successful techniques achieving up to a **3x** improvement in task success rates.
- The novel Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation technique consistently delivered strong generalization performance across different robotic environments and evaluation methods.
- Subject Customization demonstrated substantial performance improvements while requiring only static images for adaptation, making it data-efficient.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained video generative models contain valuable motion priors that can be effectively adapted for robotic tasks.
> - Subtracting irrelevant information from a pre-trained model's score, as in Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation, can be highly effective for generalization.
> - Visual metrics for video quality (e.g., FVD) do not necessarily correlate with performance on downstream robotic tasks, emphasizing task-specific evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Robotic systems that can adapt to new tasks through natural language instructions represent a significant step toward more versatile and user-friendly automation. This paper explores how large-scale pre-trained video generative models, which contain extensive knowledge from internet videos, can be effectively adapted to solve specific robotic tasks despite lacking environment-specific knowledge.

#### 1.2. Background

Recent advances in large-scale text-to-video generative models have demonstrated impressive capabilities in producing diverse, high-quality videos based on text prompts. Models like AnimateDiff (built on Stable Diffusion) can generate convincing videos across a wide range of scenarios. However, these models often struggle with environment-specific details crucial for robotics applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. Objectives

The researchers address a fundamental challenge in robotics: how to balance the broad generalization capabilities of large-scale pre-trained models with the specific understanding needed for particular robotic environments. The paper contributes a comprehensive analysis of different adaptation techniques and introduces a novel approach called "Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation" that achieves strong generalization performance across different robotics tasks.

#### 2.2. Motivation

Traditional approaches to robotics learning rely heavily on in-domain data collection, which is expensive and limits generalization. The key motivation for this research is to find an optimal middle ground that leverages the extensive knowledge contained in pre-trained video models while incorporating the specific knowledge needed for robotic tasks.

This balance is particularly important because:
1. Pre-trained models have broad knowledge but lack environment-specific details
2. In-domain models understand specific environments but have limited generalization
3. Combining these strengths could enable robots to perform novel tasks specified through natural language

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper investigates three main adaptation techniques for integrating in-domain information into large-scale pre-trained text-to-video models:

#### 3.1. Direct Finetuning

This approach involves continuing the training of the pre-trained model (specifically the motion module of AnimateDiff) using in-domain video demonstrations. Direct finetuning modifies the original model's parameters to better align with the specific robotic environment.

The optimization objective can be formulated as:

```
L(θ) = E_{x_0,t,ε,c}[||ε - ε_θ(x_t, t, c)||²]
```

Where θ represents the model parameters, x_0 is the original video, t is the timestep, ε is noise, and c is the text condition.

#### 3.2. Subject Customization

This technique adapts the image and text encoders of the base model using only static images of the robot and environment, rather than full videos. It's particularly data-efficient as it requires only images rather than complete video demonstrations.

Subject customization employs textual inversion and LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) methods to learn embeddings and parameter updates that represent the specific robot and environment.

#### 3.3. Probabilistic Adaptation

This approach keeps the pre-trained model frozen and trains a separate, smaller in-domain video model. During inference, the predictions from both models are combined through score composition:

```
s_combined(x_t, t) = s_pretrained(x_t, t) + λ · s_in-domain(x_t, t)
```

Where s represents the score function (negative gradient of log probability), and λ is a weighting parameter.

#### 3.4. Proposed Method: Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation

The paper introduces a novel adaptation technique called "Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation." This method recognizes that pre-trained models already contain rich motion priors that can be valuable for robotic tasks. Instead of adding in-domain knowledge to the pre-trained model, it subtracts irrelevant information using a "distractor" model.

The approach works as follows:
1. Train a small video model on randomly generated videos that represent "distractor" content
2. During inference, subtract the score of this distractor model from the pre-trained model's score
3. This effectively removes irrelevant information while preserving the valuable knowledge in the pre-trained model

The score composition is formulated as:

```
s_combined(x_t, t) = s_pretrained(x_t, t) - λ · s_distractor(x_t, t)
```

This technique shows remarkable robustness and generalization capabilities, even when the in-domain data is of suboptimal quality.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Evaluation Methodology

The researchers evaluate their adaptation techniques using two downstream tasks:

#### 4.2. Visual Planning

In this approach, the adapted video generative model first produces a trajectory visualization based on the current observation and a text prompt. A separate action decoder then extracts actions from the generated video frames. This method directly leverages the generation capabilities of the adapted model.

#### 4.3. Policy Supervision

This approach uses the adapted video model to generate synthetic rollouts that are then used to train a separate policy model. The policy learns to map observations to actions by mimicking the behavior shown in the generated videos.

#### 4.4. Evaluation Environments and Metrics

The evaluation environments include:
- Manipulation tasks from the MetaWorld benchmark
- Continuous control tasks from the DeepMind Control Suite

Performance metrics included:
- Task success rate
- Ground-truth rewards
- Fréchet Video Distance (FVD) to measure visual quality

#### 4.5. Results and Analysis

The experiments revealed several important findings:

1. **Adaptation significantly improves generalization**: All adaptation techniques improved the ability of pre-trained models to solve novel robotic tasks, with the most successful techniques achieving up to 3x improvement in task success rates compared to unadapted models.

2. **Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation excels**: The novel Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation technique consistently achieved strong generalization performance across different robotic environments and downstream evaluation methods.

3. **Different techniques suit different scenarios**: Direct Finetuning performed well on tasks requiring novel text-conditioned generation, while Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation showed superior overall generalizability.

4. **Subject Customization is data-efficient**: This technique achieved substantial performance improvements despite requiring only static images rather than full video demonstrations, making it particularly valuable when data is limited.

5. **Visual metrics can be misleading**: High FVD scores (indicating visual similarity to real videos) did not necessarily correlate with high task success rates, highlighting the importance of evaluating adaptation through downstream robotic performance.

6. **Robustness to data quality**: Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation demonstrated remarkable robustness to the quality of adaptation data, successfully solving novel tasks even with suboptimal in-domain demonstrations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Implications and Applications

The research has several important implications for robotics and machine learning:

1. **Data-Efficient Robot Learning**: The adaptation techniques enable robots to learn from relatively small amounts of in-domain data, potentially reducing the data collection burden that has traditionally limited robotic learning.

2. **Natural Language Interface**: By effectively adapting text-to-video models, the approach enables more intuitive control of robots through natural language instructions, making robotics more accessible to non-experts.

3. **Generalization to Novel Tasks**: The ability to generalize to unseen tasks specified through text prompts significantly expands the flexibility of robotic systems, allowing them to perform a wider range of functions without explicit retraining.

4. **Practical Applications**: The techniques could be applied in various domains:
 - Manufacturing: Robots that can adapt to new assembly tasks
 - Healthcare: Assistive robots that can understand novel requests
 - Household: Domestic robots that can perform a variety of tasks
 - Exploration: Robots that can adapt to unexpected environments

5. **Resource Considerations**: The different adaptation techniques offer varying trade-offs between performance, data requirements, and computational resources, allowing practitioners to choose the most appropriate approach for their specific constraints.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

This research demonstrates that large-scale pre-trained video generative models can be effectively adapted to solve novel robotics tasks specified through natural language. The proposed Inverse Probabilistic Adaptation technique offers a particularly robust and effective approach to combining the generalization capabilities of pre-trained models with environment-specific knowledge.

The work addresses the fundamental tension between broad generalization and specific environmental understanding in robotic learning. By finding effective adaptation strategies, the research enables robots to leverage the vast knowledge contained in internet-scale video models while still performing effectively in specific environments.

Future work could explore:
- Adaptation techniques for more complex robotic tasks
- Combining multiple adaptation approaches for even better performance
- Real-world deployment and evaluation of these techniques
- Extending the approach to multi-modal inputs beyond text and video

The code for this research has been released, facilitating reproduction and extension of these findings by the robotics research community.
