# PromptLoop: Plug-and-Play Prompt Refinement via Latent Feedback for Diffusion Model Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> PromptLoop, developed by researchers at KAIST, introduces a plug-and-play reinforcement learning framework for diffusion models that uses a multimodal large language model (MLLM) to iteratively refine prompts based on latent feedback during the image generation process. This method consistently achieved higher scores on target reward functions, such as an ImageReward score of 1.0948 for SDXL, and demonstrated robust generalization to unseen diffusion models and orthogonal composability with existing alignment techniques.

## Problem

- Direct RL-based fine-tuning of diffusion models often struggles with generalization, composability, and robustness against reward hacking or over-optimization.
- Existing prompt refinement methods typically operate in a feed-forward manner, applying a single prompt without leveraging the dynamic, step-wise evolution of latent states during the diffusion process.
- A need existed for an alignment approach that could offer fine-grained, adaptive control akin to parameter-level tuning while retaining the modularity, flexibility, and generalization benefits of prompt-based methods.

## Method

- **PromptLoop** formulates the prompt refinement process as a ==Markov Decision Process== (MDP), where an MLLM acts as a policy to generate refined prompts (actions) at different timesteps based on intermediate visual latent states (states).
- The MLLM policy is trained using online, on-policy reinforcement learning with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to maximize a terminal reward signal from the generated image.
- A sparse prompt refinement strategy and pre-generation of prompts during inference are implemented to minimize computational overhead, ensuring the framework's practical efficiency for real-world applications.

## Results

- PromptLoop consistently achieved higher scores on target reward functions, such as an ImageReward score of 1.0948 for SDXL, outperforming various prompt-based and direct diffusion model fine-tuning baselines.
- The framework demonstrated robust generalization to diffusion models not encountered during training (e.g., SD3.5-large, FLUX.1-dev) and exhibited orthogonal composability, further enhancing performance when combined with other aligned models (e.g., DDPO, DanceGRPO).
- The method incurred a marginal inference overhead, with an approximate **23%** increase in total inference time for SDXL with 5 prompt refinement steps, while effectively mitigating over-optimization and reward hacking compared to some direct fine-tuning approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating latent feedback from intermediate denoised estimates into an MLLM policy allows for adaptive, step-wise prompt refinement that structurally approximates the fine-grained control of parameter-level tuning.
> - Decoupling reward optimization from direct weight modification by operating solely on prompts provides enhanced generalization to unseen diffusion models and enables orthogonal composability with existing alignment techniques.
> - The iterative, timestep-aware prompt evolution learned by the MLLM helps mitigate issues like over-optimization and reward hacking by providing dynamic, interpretable guidance that adapts throughout the image generation process.
