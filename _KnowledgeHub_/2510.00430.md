# PromptLoop: Plug-and-Play Prompt Refinement via Latent Feedback for Diffusion Model Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> PromptLoop, developed by researchers at KAIST, introduces a plug-and-play reinforcement learning framework for diffusion models that uses a multimodal large language model (MLLM) to iteratively refine prompts based on latent feedback during the image generation process. This method consistently achieved higher scores on target reward functions, such as an ImageReward score of 1.0948 for SDXL, and demonstrated robust generalization to unseen diffusion models and orthogonal composability with existing alignment techniques.

## Problem

- Direct RL-based fine-tuning of diffusion models often struggles with generalization, composability, and robustness against reward hacking or over-optimization.
- Existing prompt refinement methods typically operate in a feed-forward manner, applying a single prompt without leveraging the dynamic, step-wise evolution of latent states during the diffusion process.
- A need existed for an alignment approach that could offer fine-grained, adaptive control akin to parameter-level tuning while retaining the modularity, flexibility, and generalization benefits of prompt-based methods.

## Method

- **PromptLoop** formulates the prompt refinement process as a ==Markov Decision Process== (MDP), where an MLLM acts as a policy to generate refined prompts (actions) at different timesteps based on intermediate visual latent states (states).
- The MLLM policy is trained using online, on-policy reinforcement learning with Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to maximize a terminal reward signal from the generated image.
- A sparse prompt refinement strategy and pre-generation of prompts during inference are implemented to minimize computational overhead, ensuring the framework's practical efficiency for real-world applications.

## Results

- PromptLoop consistently achieved higher scores on target reward functions, such as an ImageReward score of 1.0948 for SDXL, outperforming various prompt-based and direct diffusion model fine-tuning baselines.
- The framework demonstrated robust generalization to diffusion models not encountered during training (e.g., SD3.5-large, FLUX.1-dev) and exhibited orthogonal composability, further enhancing performance when combined with other aligned models (e.g., DDPO, DanceGRPO).
- The method incurred a marginal inference overhead, with an approximate **23%** increase in total inference time for SDXL with 5 prompt refinement steps, while effectively mitigating over-optimization and reward hacking compared to some direct fine-tuning approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating latent feedback from intermediate denoised estimates into an MLLM policy allows for adaptive, step-wise prompt refinement that structurally approximates the fine-grained control of parameter-level tuning.
> - Decoupling reward optimization from direct weight modification by operating solely on prompts provides enhanced generalization to unseen diffusion models and enables orthogonal composability with existing alignment techniques.
> - The iterative, timestep-aware prompt evolution learned by the MLLM helps mitigate issues like over-optimization and reward hacking by providing dynamic, interpretable guidance that adapts throughout the image generation process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image generation has seen significant advancements with the emergence of diffusion models. A contemporary challenge in this area involves aligning these generative models with specific user preferences or predefined reward functions. Historically, two primary approaches have been explored for this alignment:

Firstly, **Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based fine-tuning of diffusion model parameters** involves directly applying RL algorithms, such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), to adjust the weights of diffusion models. These methods aim to steer model behavior towards desired outcomes, such as improved aesthetic quality, safety, human preference, or prompt alignment, without requiring new training data. However, this approach has demonstrated limitations including issues with generalization across different diffusion models, difficulty in composing additional enhancements after fine-tuning, and susceptibility to pathological behaviors like reward hacking or over-optimization.

Secondly, **Prompt refinement using Large Language Models (LLMs) or Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)** has gained traction. This approach involves modifying the input prompt to guide the diffusion model, rather than directly altering its internal parameters. Prompt refinement is considered attractive due to the inherent shareability of prompts across various text-to-image (T2I) models, which facilitates generalization and orthogonal composability. Furthermore, the abstract and discrete nature of prompts may offer a buffer against reward hacking by decoupling direct parameter updates from reward optimization. However, most existing RL-based prompt refinement methods operate in a feed-forward manner. They generate a single refined prompt and apply it uniformly throughout the entire diffusion sampling trajectory. This prevents them from fully leveraging the sequential and iterative nature of the diffusion process, where intermediate latent variables evolve with each denoising step.

PromptLoop is introduced to bridge the gap between these two paradigms. It aims to achieve a structural analogy to weight-level RL fine-tuning (e.g., Diffusion RL) while preserving the modularity, flexibility, and generality characteristic of prompt-based alignment methods. By integrating latent feedback into a step-wise prompt refinement process, PromptLoop positions itself as an approach that offers the fine-grained, adaptive control typically associated with parameter-level adjustments, but within the more adaptable and robust framework of prompt manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of PromptLoop stem from identified limitations in existing diffusion model alignment strategies:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Direct RL Fine-tuning:** Current direct RL fine-tuning methods for diffusion models, while effective in specific contexts, frequently face challenges with generalization to unseen models, composability with other enhancements, and robustness against undesirable behaviors like reward hacking or over-optimization. The authors seek an alignment method that can circumvent these issues.

2. **Overcoming the "Feed-Forward" Nature of Existing Prompt Refinement:** While prompt refinement offers advantages in modularity and generalization, most current approaches apply a single, pre-determined refined prompt throughout the entire diffusion sampling process. This neglects the dynamic, step-wise evolution of latent states within diffusion models, thereby missing an opportunity for adaptive, fine-grained control that could enhance alignment. The motivation is to introduce a feedback loop that leverages these intermediate latent states.

3. **Achieving Structural Analogy to Diffusion RL without Weight Modification:** A core objective is to emulate the closed-loop, sequential control mechanism of Diffusion RL, where diffusion model parameters interact directly with evolving latent variables. PromptLoop aims to achieve this functional equivalence but through prompt modifications rather than direct parameter fine-tuning. This design choice is motivated by the desire to retain the plug-and-play nature, generalization capabilities, and robustness of prompt-based methods.

4. **Enabling Fine-Grained Adaptive Control:** By refining prompts iteratively at different timesteps based on intermediate latent feedback, PromptLoop seeks to allow for adaptive adjustments to the sampling dynamics. This fine-grained control is intended to facilitate more precise alignment with complex reward functions and user preferences throughout the image generation process.

5. **Maintaining Practicality and Efficiency:** The introduction of an iterative MLLM-based policy for prompt refinement could potentially incur significant computational overhead. A key motivation is to design the framework to keep this inference-time overhead at a practically negligible or manageable level, ensuring its viability for integration into existing image generation pipelines.

6. **Ensuring Broad Applicability and Robustness:** The authors aim to develop a framework that is generalizable across diverse diffusion models and various reward functions, including both single and composite rewards. Furthermore, the method is motivated by the goal of orthogonal composability with existing alignment techniques and improved mitigation of over-optimization and reward hacking, which are common challenges in reward-driven model steering.

In summary, PromptLoop's main objective is to provide a plug-and-play, RL-based reward alignment framework that leverages step-wise latent feedback for prompt refinement. This approach aims to combine the adaptive control of parameter-level tuning with the flexibility and robustness of prompt-based methods, ultimately leading to more reliable, generalizable, and composable generative models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PromptLoop introduces a plug-and-play reinforcement learning (RL) framework that employs a multimodal large language model (MLLM) to iteratively refine prompts during the diffusion sampling process, guided by latent feedback.

#### 3.1. Markov Decision Process (MDP) Formulation

The core of PromptLoop's methodology lies in its MDP formulation, which establishes a structural analogy to direct Diffusion RL while operating at the prompt level.
- **State ($s_t$):** At each timestep $t$ in the reverse diffusion process, the state is defined as a tuple $(x_t, c_t, q, t)$. Here, $x_t$ represents the current intermediate visual latent state, $c_t$ is the prompt applied at the current step (which was generated at step $t+1$), $q$ is the initial user query, and $t$ is the current timestep.
- **Action ($a_t$):** The action is the refined text prompt $c_{t-1}$, which is generated by the MLLM policy. This refined prompt will then be used to guide the diffusion model in generating the next visual state $x_{t-1}$.
- **Transition:** The transition from state $s_t$ to $s_{t-1}$ is determined by the frozen diffusion model, which updates the visual state $x_t$ to $x_{t-1}$ using the action (refined prompt $c_{t-1}$).
- **Reward ($R$):** A terminal reward $r(x_0, q)$ is assigned only at the final step, after the full image $x_0$ has been generated. This reward evaluates the quality of the final image based on various criteria (e.g., aesthetic quality, prompt alignment, human preference).

This MDP contrasts with traditional Diffusion RL, where the diffusion model itself acts as the policy, and its actions are the next latent states ($x_{t-1}$). By making the prompt the action, PromptLoop approximates the functional role of weight-level control, providing timestep-aware guidance without modifying the diffusion model's parameters directly. This design aims to preserve modularity, generalization, and composability, and to improve robustness against reward hacking compared to direct parameter tuning.

#### 3.2. Optimization

PromptLoop trains the MLLM policy model to maximize the terminal reward using policy gradient methods.
- **Black-box components:** The diffusion model and the reward model are treated as black-box components, meaning no gradients flow through them to update the MLLM policy. The policy learns solely from observed rewards.
- **Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO):** The widely used PPO algorithm is employed for its stability, utilizing a clipped surrogate objective and a KL penalty to constrain policy updates.
- **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** To further enhance training stability and reduce variance, PromptLoop incorporates GRPO. This method replaces the standard advantage estimator with a group-normalized reward, where rewards from multiple sampled outputs for the same prompt are normalized relative to their group mean and standard deviation.

Training proceeds via an online, on-policy reinforcement learning procedure, with each episode initialized from user prompts drawn from a dataset.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- **MLLM Policy Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct is used as the MLLM policy model.
- **Diffusion Backbones:** Experiments utilize Stable Diffusion v1.5 (SD1.5), Stable Diffusion XL (SDXL), and Stable Diffusion XL Turbo (SDXL-turbo).
- **MLLM Input - Denoised Estimates:** Instead of raw noisy latent states ($x_t$), the MLLM policy model receives denoised latent estimates ($\hat{x}_t$). These estimates are obtained by applying the diffusion model's noise prediction to $x_{t+1}$ and the current prompt $c_t$. This choice is motivated by $\hat{x}_t$ being closer to the data manifold and thus providing more semantically meaningful visual feedback to the MLLM.
- **Sparse Refinement Strategy:** To mitigate the computational overhead of invoking the MLLM at every denoising step, a sparse refinement strategy is adopted. Prompt refinement occurs only at a predefined set of timesteps $R \subseteq \{1, \ldots, T\}$. During training, $R$ is sampled uniformly, while during inference, it is set deterministically at even intervals. The prompt remains fixed between refinement steps.
- **Inference-time Optimization:** The authors observe that explicit visual feedback from intermediate denoised states, while crucial during training, is not strictly necessary at inference. Once trained, the policy can generate effective refinements without explicit access to intermediate visual signals. This allows for pre-generating all refined prompts for an entire sampling trajectory, enabling the diffusion process to proceed without interruptions and significantly reducing inference overhead. Placeholder tokens are used for visual feedback during inference.
- **Training Parameters:** GRPO training uses a learning rate of $5 \times 10^{-6}$, batch size 8, group size 8, and a KL-regularization coefficient ($\beta$) of 0.005 (or 0 for composite rewards). Parameter-efficient fine-tuning (LoRA) is applied to the MLLM with specific rank and scaling factors.
- **Prompting the MLLM:** The MLLM is guided by an instruction prompt that provides context, including the user input, the previously applied improved prompt, current/total timesteps, and the name of the target reward function. The MLLM is required to output a refined prompt in a JSON format.
- **Reward Models:**
 - **Single Reward:** ImageReward, aesthetic score models, compressibility/incompressibility models are used.
 - **Composite Reward:** A RePrompt-style design is adopted, combining ImageReward, an MLLM-based reward (gpt-5-mini-2025-08-07), and task-specific signals like format and length rewards.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding PromptLoop's performance across diverse reward functions and diffusion backbones, alongside an analysis of its underlying mechanisms.

#### 4.1. Effective Reward Optimization

- **Single Reward Alignment:** Quantitative evaluations (Table 1) demonstrate that PromptLoop consistently achieves higher scores on target reward functions (e.g., ImageReward) compared to various baselines, including other prompt-based methods (ReFL, Qwen2.5-VL-3B, GPT-5, RePrompt) and direct diffusion model fine-tuning (DDPO). For instance, with SDXL and ImageReward, PromptLoop achieved an ImageReward score of 1.0948, exceeding RePrompt's 1.0148. This indicates effective optimization towards the desired reward.
- **Composite Reward Alignment:** In more complex composite reward settings (RePrompt-style multi-reward alignment with SDXL-turbo), PromptLoop achieved high performance across object-centric prompt alignment benchmarks (GenEval) and human-preference benchmarks (ImageReward, HPSv2) (Table 2). This suggests the method's capability to balance and optimize multiple heterogeneous objectives.

#### 4.2. Generalization Capabilities

- **Unseen Diffusion Models:** PromptLoop exhibits robust generalization to diffusion models not encountered during its training. The policy model, trained on vanilla diffusion model environments, effectively improved performance when applied to different SD versions (e.g., SD1.5 policy on SDXL and vice-versa) and even to recent flow-matching models like SD3.5-large and FLUX.1-dev without additional fine-tuning (Table 6). This is achieved by aligning timesteps via noise-to-signal ratio.

#### 4.3. Orthogonal Composability

- **Enhancement of Existing Methods:** The framework demonstrates orthogonal composability, meaning a PromptLoop-trained policy can be applied to other diffusion models that have already been fine-tuned or augmented with existing human preference alignment modules (e.g., DDPO, DanceGRPO, Diffusion-DPO, ReFL, NPNet). This often results in further performance improvements, indicating that PromptLoop can complement existing techniques rather than replace them, without requiring retraining. For example, applying PromptLoop to SD1.5 already aligned with DDPO increased the ImageReward score from 0.6051 to 0.9842 (Table 1).

#### 4.4. Mitigation of Over-optimization and Reward Hacking

- **Qualitative Observations:** Qualitative comparisons (Figure 4, 5) illustrate that PromptLoop helps in mitigating over-optimization and reward hacking, an aspect not always captured by quantitative metrics. For example, some baseline methods optimized the target reward in ways that led to visually undesirable or unnatural images (resembling reward hacking), while PromptLoop-generated images maintained higher fidelity and aesthetic quality. This robustness is also supported by consistent performance across multiple evaluation metrics (e.g., HPSv2, MLLM Score), even when optimizing for a specific reward.

#### 4.5. Minimal Inference Overhead

- **Runtime Analysis:** PromptLoop introduces a marginal increase in inference time. For SDXL, going from 0 to 5 prompt improvement steps increased the total inference time from 15.00 seconds/image to 18.43 seconds/image, representing a relative increase of approximately 23% (Table 4). This overhead is considered practically manageable, particularly as the relative cost is expected to decrease with larger diffusion models.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Component Effectiveness:** Ablation studies (Table 3) confirmed the contribution of each proposed component. Simply applying an untrained policy model degraded performance. Incorporating GRPO training led to significant improvements. Multiple prompt refinements further boosted performance. The inclusion of training-time visual feedback substantially increased the target reward without degrading other metrics, suggesting its role in mitigating reward hacking.
- **Impact of Refinement Steps:** Increasing the number of prompt refinement steps consistently enhanced reward alignment and other evaluation metrics (Figure 6). This effect was more pronounced when visual feedback was included, underscoring the importance of both iterative refinement and latent feedback.

#### 4.7. Prompt Evolution Analysis

- **Timestep-wise Prompt Dynamics:** Analysis of prompt evolution (Table 5) showed that early timesteps typically emphasized meta-level descriptors (quality, style), intermediate timesteps introduced more concrete and fine-grained details (object properties, environment), and later timesteps either preserved specificity or reverted to prototypical atmospheric cues. This progression aligns with known scheduling strategies for classifier-free guidance, where early steps focus on global structure and later steps on fine details.
- **Prompt Length and Semantic Drift:** Iterative refinement caused a gradual increase in prompt length, but it remained within text encoder limits (Figure 11). Semantic drift was found to be limited, with core user intent well-preserved, attributed to the MLLM's access to the original prompt and the terminal reward signal (Figure 12).

#### 4.8. Training Dynamics and Stability

- **Stable Optimization:** Despite using RL, the training process was observed to be stable (Figure 14). This stability is attributed to strong MLLM policy initialization, parameter-efficient LoRA updates, a fixed diffusion environment, and the use of GRPO. Stronger initial policies were found to lead to faster convergence and more effective reward alignment (Table 7).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PromptLoop represents a significant contribution to the field of generative artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of aligning diffusion models with user preferences. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging Methodological Gaps:** The framework successfully bridges the gap between direct parameter-level RL fine-tuning of diffusion models and prompt-based refinement. By achieving structural analogy to Diffusion RL through latent feedback while operating solely on prompts, PromptLoop offers a method that combines the fine-grained, adaptive control of the former with the modularity and flexibility of the latter.

2. **Enhanced Generalization and Composability:** A key impact of PromptLoop is its demonstrated ability to generalize robustly to unseen diffusion models and to compose orthogonally with existing alignment techniques. This addresses critical limitations of many parameter-level fine-tuning methods, which often suffer from model-specificity and difficulties in combining different alignment strategies. This characteristic makes PromptLoop a versatile tool that can be integrated into diverse and evolving generative AI ecosystems without extensive retraining.

3. **Improved Robustness against Undesirable Behaviors:** The research indicates that PromptLoop mitigates issues such as over-optimization and reward hacking. By decoupling reward optimization from direct manipulation of model weights and by providing a continuous, human-interpretable control signal (prompts), the system appears less prone to generating pathological outputs that satisfy the reward function superficially but fail human evaluation. This contributes to more reliable and trustworthy generative models.

4. **Practicality and Efficiency for Real-World Deployment:** Despite introducing an iterative MLLM-based policy, the sparse refinement strategy and the ability to pre-generate prompts during inference keep the computational overhead at a manageable level. This practicality is crucial for integrating such advanced alignment mechanisms into user-facing applications and production environments, where latency and resource consumption are critical factors.

5. **Interpretable Control and Dynamic Guidance:** The analysis of prompt evolution reveals that the RL-trained MLLM policy learns to adapt prompt specificity throughout the diffusion process, mirroring optimal guidance scheduling strategies. This not only offers an interpretable control mechanism but also suggests that the policy develops an implicit understanding of the diffusion model's internal dynamics at different timesteps. Such dynamic, interpretable guidance can open avenues for more intuitive and powerful user control.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** PromptLoop's plug-and-play nature and its demonstrated effectiveness across diverse reward types and diffusion architectures position it as a foundational framework. It could inspire further research into advanced prompt engineering, multi-modal policy learning, and new strategies for aligning complex generative models with human intentions, potentially leading to the development of more sophisticated and robust generative AI systems.

Overall, PromptLoop contributes a practical, effective, and flexible solution for aligning diffusion models, addressing long-standing challenges in the field and enhancing the potential for high-quality, user-controlled image generation.
