# Resa: Transparent Reasoning Models via SAEs

> [!summary] Summary
> Resa introduces SAE-Tuning, a two-stage training procedure that efficiently elicits strong reasoning abilities in language models using only verified question-answer data. The method reduces training costs by over 2000x and time by over 450x compared to RL-based approaches, achieving comparable performance while offering transparency into reasoning features.

## Problem

- Existing LLM reasoning elicitation methods (RL-based) are computationally intensive, demanding vast resources and long training times.
- Traditional fine-tuning methods (RL or SFT) are largely 'black boxes,' obscuring how reasoning abilities are instilled and activated within the model.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) often relies on costly and labor-intensive Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning traces, limiting scalability.

## Method

- Resa introduces ==SAE-Tuning==, a novel two-stage training procedure that leverages ==Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)== with only verified, CoT-free question-answer data.
- Stage I: Train an SAE on a source model's internal layer activations to explicitly extract and capture ==reasoning-related features==.
- Stage II: Elicit reasoning abilities in a target model by guiding its ==Low-Rank Adapters (LoRA)== based SFT with the frozen, pre-trained SAE, which is removed at test time.

## Results

- Resa-STILL-v5 achieves **48.06%** average performance, nearly matching the RL-trained Tina-STILL (**48.16%** avg) while reducing training costs by over **2000x** (to ~**$1**) and time by over **450x** (to under **20 minutes**).
- Reasoning abilities extracted from one dataset generalize to different, even non-overlapping, elicitation datasets, with Resa-STILL2DeepScaleR (**48.77%** avg) performing on par with Tina-DeepScaleR (**48.38%** avg).
- Reasoning abilities captured in low-rank adapters during SAE-guided SFT can be treated as portable modules, demonstrating competitive performance when attached to different base models at test time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SAEs can extract and explicitly represent latent reasoning abilities from language model activations, enabling transparent analysis of how reasoning is encoded.
> - Reasoning capabilities can be modularized and transferred as portable adapters, suggesting a composable AI system architecture where abstract abilities are separate from foundational knowledge.
> - The overall structural organization of reasoning features across model layers correlates with the final reasoning performance, providing a data-driven path to optimizing SAE-Tuning by strategically selecting SAE hookpoints.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) has witnessed remarkable progress in complex tasks such as mathematical, coding, and scientific reasoning. However, current methods for eliciting these reasoning abilities, primarily Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) data, present significant challenges.

**Challenges with Existing Paradigms:**
- **RL-based workflows** (e.g., DeepSeek-R1, Shao et al., 2024): While effective in improving reasoning, they are notoriously resource-intensive, demanding vast computational power and long training times. Furthermore, RL often operates as a "black box," making it difficult to understand *how* the model's internal representations are altered to achieve better reasoning. Recent work (Zhao et al., 2025; Chu et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2025a, 2025b) has explored the "elicitation hypothesis," suggesting that RL primarily surfaces pre-existing latent reasoning abilities rather than instilling them from scratch.
- **CoT-based SFT** (e.g., Lightman et al., 2023; Muennighoff et al., 2025): This approach relies on meticulously curated step-by-step reasoning traces (CoT), which are costly and time-consuming to create at scale. The quality and availability of such data remain a bottleneck.

**Resa's Contribution to the Landscape:**
Resa directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel paradigm that aims for a "three-birds-one-stone" solution: effective, computationally efficient, and transparent reasoning elicitation.
- **Efficiency:** Resa offers a drastic reduction in training costs (over 2000x) and time (over 450x) compared to RL-based methods, making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible. This democratizes research into reasoning models by providing a low-cost, high-efficiency alternative.
- **Transparency and Interpretability:** By leveraging Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) (Anthropic, 2023, 2024), Resa moves beyond the "black box" nature of traditional fine-tuning. SAEs are interpretability tools designed to deconstruct dense internal activations into sparse, human-interpretable features. This work extends the application of SAEs beyond pure interpretability to active model steering and ability elicitation, building on prior work in model steering (Panickssery et al., 2024; Bayat et al., 2025). The introduction of a "prompt-only" method to quantify reasoning features and correlate them with performance is a significant step towards a more transparent understanding of how reasoning is encoded and leveraged within LLMs.
- **Modularity and Generalizability:** The demonstration that extracted reasoning abilities can generalize across different data distributions and be attached as portable "adapters" (similar to LoRA) at test time represents a significant conceptual advance. This aligns with a growing trend in LLM research to view complex abilities as composable modules, paving the way for more flexible and efficient model design (e.g., "Reasoning-as-an-Adapter" concept).

In essence, Resa positions itself at the intersection of efficient LLM training, interpretability, and the elicitation of complex cognitive abilities. It offers a practical and theoretically insightful alternative to current dominant paradigms for achieving strong reasoning performance in smaller LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for eliciting strong reasoning abilities in language models that is simultaneously **effective, computationally efficient, and transparent**. The authors identify a critical gap in the current landscape where existing methods, despite their performance, suffer from significant drawbacks:

- **Motivation for Efficiency:** Reinforcement Learning (RL) has shown great promise in enhancing reasoning, but its exorbitant computational cost and time requirements (often thousands of dollars and hundreds of hours) make it inaccessible for many researchers and practitioners. The authors seek a method that can achieve comparable performance at a fraction of the cost and time.
- **Motivation for Transparency:** Traditional fine-tuning methods, whether RL or SFT, are largely "black boxes." While they improve reasoning, the specific internal mechanisms and representations that contribute to this improvement remain opaque. The authors aim to shed light on *how* reasoning abilities are instilled and activated within the model.
- **Motivation for Reduced Data Dependency:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) often relies on high-quality Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning traces, which are labor-intensive and expensive to curate. The authors are motivated to explore a method that can function effectively using only readily available, verified question-answer data, without requiring explicit step-by-step reasoning.

To bridge this gap, the key objective is to leverage Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) as a mechanism to first understand and then actively "instill" reasoning capabilities. The authors hypothesize that SAEs can capture the "fundamental building blocks of reasoning" within a source model's activations and then guide a target model to develop these abilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Resa achieves its objectives through a novel two-stage training procedure called **SAE-Tuning**, which leverages Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) with only verified, CoT-free question-answer data.

#### 3.1. Resa Model Family and Core Idea

Resa is presented as a family of 1.5B reasoning models, primarily derived from DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (R1-Distill) and Tina models (RL-trained variants of R1-Distill). The "transparent" designation reflects the methodology's focus on interpretability, using SAEs to explicitly isolate and extract implicit reasoning abilities, and then controllably instilling them into a target model.

#### 3.2. SAE-Tuning: The Two-Stage Pipeline

- **Stage I: SAE Training (Reasoning Ability Extraction)**
 - **Goal:** To train an SAE to capture reasoning-related features from a `source model`.
 - **Process:** A `trigger dataset` (verified, CoT-free question-answer pairs) is fed into the source model. The activations from a specific internal layer (the "SAE hookpoint," typically the MLP output of a middle layer, e.g., 12th out of 28) are captured. An SAE is then trained on these activations to reconstruct them, minimizing the reconstruction error. The core insight is that a subset of the sparse features learned by the SAE corresponds to the source model's implicit reasoning abilities triggered by the `<think>` and `</think>` tokens in the prompt.
 - **SAE Architecture:** The SAE consists of an encoder ($W_{enc}$) and a decoder ($W_{dec}$) with unit-norm columns, along with biases. It projects the dense activation into a much higher-dimensional sparse latent space, where `Top-k` features are selected.
 - **SAE Training Modes:** The authors explore three modes for training the SAE:
 - *Pre-trained:* Using an existing SAE (e.g., from EleutherAI). This bypasses Stage I.
 - *Fine-tuned:* Further fine-tuning a pre-trained SAE on activations from the specific source model and trigger dataset.
 - *Trained-from-Scratch:* Training an SAE from random initialization solely on activations from the source model and trigger dataset.

- **Stage II: SAE-Guided SFT (Reasoning Ability Elicitation)**
 - **Goal:** To elicit reasoning abilities in a `target model` by guiding its SFT with the trained SAE.
 - **Process:** The trained SAE from Stage I is **frozen** and integrated into the target model's architecture at a chosen layer (same hookpoint as in Stage I). The target model is then fine-tuned using Low-Rank Adapters (LoRA) (rank `r`) in each MLP and attention sublayer.
 - **Loss Function:** The training objective is the KL divergence between the next token probability distribution *with* the SAE inserted (`ỹ`) and *without* the SAE (`y`). This forces the target model to produce internal representations compatible with the frozen SAE's reasoning features, effectively instilling those features.
 - **Crucial Aspect:** After SFT, the SAE is **entirely removed** from the model at test time. The reasoning abilities are "instilled" into the target model's own LoRA-adapted parameters, allowing for standard inference.
 - **Elicitation Dataset:** This dataset, typically identical to the trigger dataset, comprises verified CoT-free question-answer pairs. The authors highlight that standard SFT on this data *without* an SAE is ineffective, underscoring the SAE's vital role.

#### 3.3. Conceptual Perspectives

The authors offer two intuitive perspectives on SAE-Tuning:
- **Knowledge Distillation:** The SAE acts as a "knowledge bottleneck" in Stage I, learning compressed reasoning representations. In Stage II, it becomes a "teacher," guiding the target model (student) via the KL divergence objective to replicate the source model's reasoning behavior, influenced by the SAE's explicit features.
- **Alternating Optimization:** The process alternates between optimizing SAE parameters to capture reasoning features (Stage I) and optimizing model adapters (LoRA) to integrate these features (Stage II), with the SAE serving as a bridge.

#### 3.4. Practical Implementation Setup

- **Models:** Primarily 1.5B DeepSeek-R1-Distill (target model) and Tina models (source models).
- **Datasets:** STILL and DeepScaleR (verified, CoT-free Q&A data). Prompt template uses `<think>` and `</think>` tokens to activate latent reasoning paths, despite containing no intermediate steps.
- **Computational Resources:** Constrained to a minimal hardware footprint (2 NVIDIA L40S or RTX 6000 Ada GPUs) to demonstrate cost-efficiency. Total cost for all experiments was approximately $436, with main tasks costing around $53, and the best Resa model costing about $5.
- **Frameworks:** Built on OpenR1 and Sparsify for training, and lighteval with vLLM for efficient evaluation.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Zero-shot Pass@1 performance on a diverse suite of six reasoning benchmarks (AIME24/25, AMC23, MATH500, GPQA Diamond, Minerva).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling empirical evidence for the effectiveness, efficiency, generalizability, modularity, and transparency of the SAE-Tuning procedure.

#### 4.1. Efficient Reasoning Ability Elicitation

- **Proof of Concept (Reasoning Ability Replication):** SAE-Tuning successfully replicates the performance of expensive RL-trained models. Resa-STILL-v1 (47.28% avg) recovers 98.2% of the performance of the RL-trained Tina-STILL (48.16% avg) on the STILL dataset. Resa-DeepScaleR-v1 (48.95% avg) even slightly surpasses its RL-trained counterpart, Tina-DeepScaleR (48.38% avg).
- **Necessity of SAEs:** Ablations confirm that standard SFT on the same CoT-free data *without* an SAE yields significantly lower performance (39.00% avg for STILL-CoT-free-SFT), highlighting the critical role of the SAE-guided process.
- **Practical Utility (End-to-End Elicitation):**
 - **SAE Simplification:** Training an SAE from scratch is found to be comparably effective (Resa-STILL-Trained-from-Scratch-SAE: 47.36% avg) to fine-tuning a pre-trained SAE (Resa-STILL-Finetuned-SAE: 47.28% avg). This eliminates the dependency on costly pre-trained SAEs.
 - **Source Model Simplification:** The method can elicit reasoning directly from the *base model* (R1-Distill, referred to as Tina-0-step) without needing a specialized, heavily RL-trained source. Resa-STILL-v5, using the base model as its own source and a trained-from-scratch SAE, achieves 48.06% avg, nearly identical to the fully RL-trained Tina-STILL (48.16%). This demonstrates that necessary reasoning features are latent in base models and can be elicited with high efficiency.
- **Cost and Time Reductions:** Compared to RL-based workflows, SAE-Tuning achieves peak training cost reductions of over **2000x (to approximately $1)** and time reductions of over **450x (to under 20 minutes)** while maintaining comparable performance.

#### 4.2. Generalizable and Modular Reasoning Ability

- **Out-of-Distribution Generalization:** Reasoning abilities extracted from one dataset (STILL) successfully generalize to different, even non-overlapping, elicitation datasets (DeepScaleR, Open-S1, II-Thought, OpenR1). For instance, Resa-STILL2DeepScaleR achieves 48.77% avg, on par with Tina-DeepScaleR (48.38%) which was trained natively on DeepScaleR. This indicates the extracted features represent a general reasoning process, not dataset-specific artifacts.
- **Modular Reasoning-as-an-Adapter:** The research demonstrates that the reasoning ability, captured in low-rank adapters during SAE-guided SFT, can be treated as a portable "adapter." Adapters trained on Qwen-Math or Qwen and then attached to R1-Distill at test time (without retraining) yield competitive performance (47.86% and 47.54% avg, respectively), comparable to models fully SAE-tuned on R1-Distill. This supports the hypothesis that a strong reasoning model can be conceptualized as a combination of "Abstract Reasoning Ability" (the adapter) and "Foundational Knowledge" (the base model).

#### 4.3. Transparent Reasoning Feature Extraction

- **Prompt-Only Feature Extraction Method:** A novel method is introduced to quantify "reasoning features" by identifying SAE features exclusively and simultaneously activated by `<think>` and `</think>` tokens in a system prompt.
- **Correlation with Performance:** Analyzing the layer-wise distribution of these features reveals a "tri-modal" pattern in the base R1-Distill model. Crucially, a large-scale ablation study (26 Resa-STILL models, one for each hookpoint layer) shows that the final reasoning performance of the Resa models exhibits a very similar tri-modal distribution.
- **GMM Analysis:** Fitting a 3-component Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to both the feature count distribution and the reasoning performance distribution shows a strong structural alignment. The means and component weights of the GMMs are nearly identical, suggesting that while individual layer feature counts may not directly predict performance, the *overall structural organization* of reasoning features across layers *is* a robust predictor of how performance will be distributed. This provides a data-driven path to optimizing SAE-Tuning by strategically selecting SAE hookpoints.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Resa project presents a highly significant advancement in the field of language model reasoning, with profound potential impacts across research and application domains:

- **Democratization of Reasoning Model Research:** The most immediate and striking impact is the drastic reduction in the computational resources required to elicit strong reasoning abilities. By cutting costs by over 2000x and time by over 450x, Resa makes advanced LLM reasoning research accessible to a much broader community, including academic labs and smaller organizations, without requiring access to supercomputing clusters or massive cloud budgets. This could accelerate innovation in reasoning-focused LLMs.

- **Enhanced Model Transparency and Interpretability:** Resa directly tackles the "black box" problem of LLMs. By using SAEs to extract and quantify reasoning features, the work provides a more transparent understanding of where and how reasoning capabilities reside within a model's internal representations. The novel prompt-only feature extraction method and the correlation between feature distribution and performance offer practical tools for model analysis and debugging. This transparency fosters trust and enables more targeted interventions for improving model behavior.

- **Foundations for Composable AI Systems:** The demonstration of modular reasoning abilities, transferable as "adapters" across models, is a conceptual breakthrough. This suggests a future where complex AI capabilities can be developed, optimized, and then "plugged in" as discrete modules to different foundational models. This could lead to more efficient model development pipelines, where specialized reasoning modules are trained once and then combined with various base models, rather than retraining entire models from scratch for every specific task or domain. It also aligns with a vision of AI where abstract abilities are separated from foundational knowledge, enabling more flexible and adaptable systems.

- **New Paradigm for Ability Elicitation:** Resa moves beyond the limitations of RL and CoT-based SFT. Its ability to elicit strong reasoning using only CoT-free, verified question-answer data simplifies data curation, reducing a major bottleneck in model training. This "SAE-guided SFT" paradigm offers a powerful alternative for instilling complex abilities without explicit step-by-step supervision, potentially applicable to a wider range of cognitive skills beyond reasoning.

- **Improved Model Optimization and Design:** The finding that the overall structural organization of reasoning features across layers correlates with performance provides a data-driven heuristic for optimizing model training. Researchers can now analyze the latent feature distribution of a source model to strategically select SAE hookpoints, leading to more efficient and effective fine-tuning. This mechanistic understanding can guide future architectural designs for models intended to excel at reasoning tasks.

In conclusion, Resa offers a significant leap forward by presenting an effective, highly efficient, and interpretable method for eliciting reasoning in LLMs. Its contributions promise to make advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible, understandable, and composable, fundamentally impacting how LLMs are developed and deployed in the future.
