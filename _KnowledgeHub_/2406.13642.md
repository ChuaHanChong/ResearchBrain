# SpatialBot: Precise Spatial Understanding with Vision Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SpatialBot enhances Vision Language Models' (VLMs) ability to precisely understand 3D space by integrating RGB-D information and training on novel, depth-centric datasets. The model achieves over 99% accuracy in direct depth estimation, improves general VLM benchmark performance, and yields higher success rates in robotic manipulation tasks by leveraging its enhanced spatial reasoning.

## Problem

- Most Vision Language Models (VLMs) are trained on 2D RGB images, lacking precise depth comprehension for real-world 3D spatial understanding in embodied AI applications.
- A scarcity of large-scale, depth-specific datasets prevents VLMs from learning fine-grained spatial reasoning across various precision requirements (e.g., millimeter-level for robotics).
- Existing VLM evaluation benchmarks do not comprehensively assess precise spatial understanding capabilities, especially for metric depth and complex spatial relationships.

## Method

- ==Integrates RGB== and depth images into the VLM architecture to enable explicit depth comprehension, using a consistent depth encoding scheme for various scales.
- Introduces `SpatialQA` (general depth VQA) and `SpatialQA-E` (embodied AI spatial relationships) datasets, generated with a progressive three-step QA pipeline and GPT-4o.
- Implements a unique `Depth API` allowing the VLM to query precise metric depth values for specific pixels or regions during inference, enhancing quantitative spatial reasoning.

## Results

- Achieves over **99%** accuracy in direct depth value estimation and consistently outperforms baselines on dedicated spatial understanding tasks within the `SpatialBench` evaluation.
- Demonstrates enhanced performance on general VLM benchmarks (e.g., MME, GQA) beyond spatial tasks, indicating a generalized improvement from depth integration.
- Enables significantly higher success rates in robotic pick-and-place manipulation tasks when using RGB-D input, translating enhanced spatial understanding into improved real-world robot control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating precise depth information directly into VLM training and inference is critical for robust 3D spatial understanding, moving beyond 2D image interpretation.
> - Specialized, multi-level depth-augmented datasets (like SpatialQA and SpatialQA-E) effectively teach VLMs fine-grained spatial reasoning, from low-level depth values to complex relationships.
> - Enhancing a VLM's foundational spatial understanding through depth training can improve its performance on general visual reasoning benchmarks, indicating a synergistic effect.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision Language Models (VLMs) has seen rapid advancements, enabling impressive performance in 2D image understanding, including tasks like perception, reasoning, and Optical Character Recognition (OCR). Models such as LLaVA, BLIP-2, QWen-VL, Gemini, and GPT-4V have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in interpreting visual information when paired with textual queries.

However, a critical limitation persists: these VLMs primarily operate on 2D RGB images and often struggle with robust spatial understanding. This deficiency becomes particularly pronounced in real-world applications, especially those involving embodied AI, such as robotic manipulation, navigation, and human-robot interaction. The paper identifies three main challenges contributing to this struggle:
1. **Limited Depth Comprehension:** Most popular VLMs are trained solely on RGB images, without exposure to depth information. Consequently, their internal representations lack the necessary capacity for precise depth perception.
2. **Absence of Depth-Specific Datasets:** Existing VLM training datasets largely omit depth maps and depth-related tasks, preventing models from learning fine-grained spatial reasoning.
3. **Inconsistent Depth Scales:** The varying precision requirements for indoor (millimeter-level) versus outdoor (broader range) tasks pose a significant challenge for uniform depth processing.

Prior work has attempted to address spatial understanding in various ways. Some methods infer spatial relationships purely from 2D images (e.g., SpatialVLM, SpatialRGPT), but their accuracy for metric depth is limited. Other approaches leverage point clouds or depth maps, with depth maps being preferred for their ease of collection and processing. While Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) models (like ZoeDepth) have improved, they often lack the millimeter-level precision crucial for embodied tasks and cannot provide the precise sensor data required. Furthermore, while Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (e.g., RT, Octo, OpenVLA) exist for robotic control, they typically do not explicitly focus on enhancing deep spatial understanding as a foundational VLM capability.

`SpatialBot` positions itself directly at the intersection of these challenges. It proposes a novel approach to fundamentally enhance VLMs' spatial understanding by:
- **Integrating RGB-D Information:** Moving beyond 2D RGB, `SpatialBot` explicitly leverages both RGB and depth images, acknowledging that precise spatial reasoning requires explicit depth cues.
- **Curating Specialized Datasets:** The introduction of `SpatialQA` and `SpatialQA-E` directly addresses the data scarcity issue, providing multi-level, depth-centric question-answering pairs for general spatial understanding and specific embodied tasks, respectively. This progressive training paradigm aims to teach VLMs depth perception from basic to complex levels.
- **Developing a Robust Evaluation:** `SpatialBench` is designed as a comprehensive benchmark to rigorously assess spatial understanding capabilities, which was previously lacking in existing general VLM benchmarks.
- **Introducing a Depth API:** This innovative component allows the VLM to query specific, precise depth values, bridging the gap between high-level reasoning and low-level sensor data, and addressing the need for metric accuracy.

By tackling these limitations, `SpatialBot` aims to enable VLMs to transition from general 2D image comprehension to truly precise spatial reasoning, a critical step towards more capable and autonomous embodied AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of `SpatialBot` is to significantly enhance the spatial understanding capabilities of Vision Language Models (VLMs), moving beyond their current impressive but limited 2D image comprehension. This overarching goal is driven by the recognition that precise spatial understanding is fundamental for real-world applications, especially in embodied AI domains such as robotic manipulation and navigation.

The specific motivations and key objectives of this research can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing VLM Limitations in Depth Perception:** A core motivation is to overcome the inherent challenge VLMs face in comprehending spatial information from RGB images alone. Current VLMs, trained predominantly on 2D data, exhibit "limited capacity to understand depth information," making them unsuitable for tasks requiring precise metric spatial reasoning. `SpatialBot` aims to directly address this by explicitly incorporating depth images into the VLM pipeline.
- **Developing Purpose-Built Training Data:** The authors identify a significant gap in the research landscape: the absence of large-scale, well-designed datasets tailored for training VLMs in depth perception. To fill this void, a key objective is to "introduce the `SpatialQA` and `SpatialQA-E` datasets." `SpatialQA` focuses on general depth-related questions across various scenarios, while `SpatialQA-E` specializes in multi-level depth questions crucial for embodied AI tasks, specifically robot manipulation.
- **Enabling Robust Evaluation of Spatial Understanding:** To accurately gauge improvements in spatial reasoning, a dedicated evaluation framework is needed. Therefore, the paper aims to develop `SpatialBench`, a comprehensive benchmark designed to assess VLMs' spatial understanding across different levels of complexity, from low-level depth estimation to high-level spatial relationships.
- **Facilitating Precise Interaction with Spatial Data:** For embodied AI tasks requiring high precision (e.g., millimeter-level for robot manipulation), simply interpreting a depth map may not suffice. A crucial objective is to enable VLMs to "accurately obtain depth information" by designing a "depth API that allows the model to query the depth values of individual pixels or regions." This allows for both qualitative spatial reasoning and quantitative data retrieval.
- **Demonstrating Practical Utility in Embodied AI:** Ultimately, the motivation is to showcase `SpatialBot`'s real-world applicability. A key objective is to fine-tune and deploy `SpatialBot` on robots to perform manipulation tasks that inherently rely on sophisticated spatial reasoning. This demonstrates how enhanced VLM spatial understanding can translate into tangible improvements for robotic control and autonomy.
- **Improving General VLM Performance:** The research also implicitly motivates the hypothesis that by enriching VLMs with spatial understanding, their performance on general VLM benchmarks might also improve, especially for questions involving counting, existence, and positional reasoning that can benefit from clearer spatial cues.

In essence, `SpatialBot` is driven by the ambition to equip VLMs with a foundational, precise understanding of 3D space, which is critical for pushing the boundaries of intelligent systems that can perceive, reason about, and interact with the physical world effectively.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The `SpatialBot` framework is meticulously designed to imbue Vision Language Models (VLMs) with precise spatial understanding by integrating depth information. The approach involves a novel model architecture, a sophisticated data generation pipeline, and a tailored evaluation benchmark.

**Model Architecture:**
`SpatialBot` employs a standard VLM structure comprising an image encoder (SigLIP, 384x384 resolution) and a multi-modal projector, which convert visual information into tokens. These tokens are then fed, alongside text tokens, into a Large Language Model (LLM) backbone (e.g., Phi-2-3B, Phi-3-4B, QWen-1.5-4B, Llama-3-8B) to generate responses. The key innovation is its ability to accept both RGB and *optional* depth images as input.

**SpatialBot Depth API:**
A unique feature of `SpatialBot` is its `Depth API`. This API allows the VLM to dynamically query precise depth values during inference. When `SpatialBot`'s internal reasoning determines a need for accurate metric depth (e.g., to compare distances or determine object touch), its output can contain a specific format like "Depth(point)". The API intercepts this, retrieves the corresponding depth value from the depth map (or computes it for a region/object), and feeds this numerical value back into the VLM. This enables `SpatialBot` to perform fine-grained quantitative spatial reasoning that a purely qualitative VLM might struggle with. During training, the model is exposed to API calls on a subset of data, while for others, it's encouraged to infer directly from the depth map, enhancing its direct understanding.

**Depth Map Encoding:**
To ensure consistency and precision across varied indoor and outdoor scenarios, `SpatialBot` uses a specific depth encoding scheme. It stores depth values in millimeters, supporting a broad range (1mm to 131.071m), utilizing either `uint24` or three-channel `uint8`. For the `uint8` format, values are distributed across three channels with units of $2^0$, $2^5$, and $2^{10}$ millimeters, ensuring distinct representation for different depth scales. The paper emphasizes using raw sensor depth when available (e.g., from RGB-D cameras on robots) or leveraging a robust Monocular Depth Estimation (MDE) model like ZoeDepth for RGB-only datasets, specifically avoiding inverse relative depth conversions for metric accuracy.

**Object Depth Description:**
To provide comprehensive spatial context for objects, `SpatialBot` utilizes four depth values—max, min, mean, and center—derived from an object's mask. To account for mask or depth map inaccuracies, the 95th and 5th percentile values are used for max and min depths, respectively. If masks are unreliable (e.g., from inaccurate bounding boxes), it defaults to using only the center point depth.

**Dataset Construction: `SpatialQA` and `SpatialQA-E`**

The core of `SpatialBot`'s training lies in its specially curated datasets:

- **`SpatialQA` (General RGB-D VQA):** Built upon existing VLM datasets like Bunny695k (which includes COCO and Visual Genome), `SpatialQA` augments them with depth information and depth-related QA pairs. Raw sensor depth is used for datasets like NYU Depth v2 and subsets of RT-X, while ZoeDepth provides estimated metric depth for others (COCO, VG, KITTI, SA-1B). A `three-step QA pipeline` is designed to progressively teach spatial understanding:
 1. **Low-Level:** Focuses on direct depth map understanding and point depth queries (e.g., "What is the depth value of point (x,y)?").
 2. **Middle-Level:** Teaches RGB-depth alignment, object depth description (mean/min/max/center depth), and proximity relationships (e.g., "Which object is closer?").
 3. **High-Level:** Applies learned depth knowledge to complex tasks like counting, enumeration, and understanding spatial relationships (e.g., "Is object A above object B?"). GPT-4o is heavily utilized to generate diverse conversations and QA pairs based on visual and depth cues.

- **`SpatialQA-E` (Embodied AI Dataset):** This dataset extends spatial reasoning to real-world robotic manipulation. It comprises 2000 episodes collected using a 7-axis Franka Research 3 Robotic Arm. Tasks involve picking and placing objects on a table, avoiding obstacles, and following instructions rich in spatial relationships (positional, size, distinguishing between real and "illusion" objects like printed images based on depth). Human experts annotated bounding boxes and depth information for specific RT-X images, and GPT-4o generated conversations focusing on robot actions and spatial relationships.

**`SpatialBot` in Embodiment Tasks:**
For robot manipulation, `SpatialBot` is fine-tuned on `SpatialQA-E` to operate as a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. It processes multi-frame RGB or RGB-D inputs to predict 7 Degrees of Freedom (DoF) action vectors (delta changes in position and rotation, and gripper closure). Each dimension of the action space is binned into 100 possible values for precise control. To enable multi-task training (general QA + robotics), natural language elements are integrated into the robot's responses (e.g., "The robot should...").

**Evaluation Benchmark: `SpatialBench`**
To comprehensively evaluate spatial understanding, `SpatialBench` was developed. It consists of 120 manually annotated images with questions covering positional relationships, object existence, reaching, and size comparison. Questions are in Yes/No or multiple-choice formats, with specific depth and proximity questions included.

**Training Details:**
`SpatialBot` is pretrained on two million image-text pairs from LAION-2B (Bunny-pretrain-LAION-2M) and then fine-tuned on the combined `Bunny695k` and `SpatialQA` datasets. LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) is used during fine-tuning. For manipulation tasks, specific learning rates are applied, and multi-modal projector is made trainable.

This comprehensive methodology, from data creation and encoding to model architecture and API integration, underpins `SpatialBot`'s ability to achieve precise spatial understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on `SpatialBot` across dedicated spatial benchmarks, general VLM benchmarks, and embodied AI tasks demonstrate its significant improvements in spatial understanding and practical applications.

#### 4.1. Spatial Understanding on `SpatialBench`

The results on the custom-designed `SpatialBench` clearly highlight `SpatialBot`'s superior spatial reasoning capabilities compared to baselines, including general VLMs and even GPT-4o.
- **Depth Perception:** `SpatialBot` with RGB-D input achieved "greater than 99%" accuracy in direct depth value estimation (e.g., `SpatialBot-Phi2-3B-RGBD` on "Depth"). This is a remarkable improvement over models trained only on RGB or even GPT-4o with RGB-D input, validating the effectiveness of its depth encoding and API integration.
- **High-Level Spatial Reasoning:** `SpatialBot` models, particularly with RGB-D input, consistently outperformed "Bunny" baselines across various high-level spatial tasks on `SpatialBench`:
 - **Position, Existence, Counting:** Showed notable improvements, indicating that depth information helps in better object grounding and localization within a scene.
 - **Reaching and Size Comparison:** `SpatialBot` demonstrated significantly higher accuracy, crucial for embodied tasks where judging physical contact or relative dimensions is vital. For instance, `SpatialBot-Phi2-3B-RGB` (even without explicit depth input during inference, but trained with depth) substantially improved "Reaching" over `Bunny-Phi2-3B-RGB`.
- **Comparison with GPT-4o:** While GPT-4o showed decent performance, `SpatialBot` (especially its RGB-D variant) either matched or surpassed it on several spatial metrics, underscoring its specialized training benefit. The qualitative examples in the paper (Fig. 1 and Fig. 12) visually demonstrate `SpatialBot`'s ability to make correct spatial judgments where GPT-4o fails, particularly regarding contact ("Has the gripper touched the rag?").

#### 4.2. General VLM Benchmarks

A significant finding is that training `SpatialBot` on the depth-augmented `SpatialQA` dataset not only improves spatial understanding but also **enhances its performance on general VLM benchmarks** (MME, MMBench, SEED-I, VQAv2, GQA, POPE).
- `SpatialBot` models consistently achieved higher scores than their respective "Bunny" counterparts across most evaluated benchmarks, even those where depth information is not strictly necessary for solving the questions.
- This suggests a synergistic effect: incorporating precise spatial and depth reasoning during training helps VLMs develop a more robust and comprehensive understanding of visual scenes, which generalizes to broader visual reasoning tasks. Improvements were particularly noted in MME (Perception and Cognition) and GQA, which include tasks like counting and object existence that can implicitly benefit from better spatial awareness.

#### 4.3. `SpatialBot` in Embodiment Tasks

The ultimate validation of `SpatialBot`'s utility comes from its deployment on real robotic platforms. Fine-tuned on `SpatialQA-E`, `SpatialBot` functioned as a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for manipulation tasks.
- **Improved Success Rates with Depth:** Experiments showed that `SpatialBot` using RGB-D inputs achieved significantly higher success rates in pick-and-place tasks compared to its RGB-only counterpart (Fig. 8). This improvement was consistent across different task categories: "Positional," "Size," and "Illusion" (where distinguishing real objects from printed ones relies heavily on depth).
- This directly demonstrates that `SpatialBot`'s enhanced spatial understanding capabilities translate into more accurate and effective robotic control in complex real-world scenarios requiring spatial reasoning (e.g., "pick up the teacup in the middle and place it on the closest cutting board").

#### 4.4. Monocular Depth Estimation by VLM (Self-Evaluation)

The paper also explored whether `SpatialBot` could perform MDE *itself* through text output. While `SpatialBot-RGB` showed some capacity, the results (Table 1, "Depth" score for RGB-only models) suggest that VLMs are not yet fully capable of accurate pixel-level MDE in a text-only output fashion. This finding reinforces the necessity and effectiveness of the `Depth API` to provide precise metric depth values from an external source or pre-computed depth maps, rather than relying on the VLM to infer them perfectly.

In summary, the results unequivocally demonstrate that `SpatialBot` successfully achieves its core objective of enhancing VLM spatial understanding, translates this into improved performance on both general VLM tasks and critical embodied AI applications, and provides valuable datasets and benchmarks for future research.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

`SpatialBot` represents a crucial advancement in the capabilities of Vision Language Models (VLMs), addressing a fundamental limitation that has hindered their deployment in many real-world applications. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Bridging the 2D-3D Gap in VLMs:** The most significant contribution of `SpatialBot` is its effective integration of precise depth information into VLMs. While previous VLMs excelled at 2D image understanding, their struggles with spatial relationships and metric depth limited their utility in physical environments. `SpatialBot` directly addresses this by demonstrating that by training VLMs with both RGB and depth data, and providing mechanisms for precise depth querying (via the Depth API), they can achieve a sophisticated understanding of 3D space. This moves VLMs closer to truly comprehending the physical world, rather than just interpreting flat images.

- **Enabling Robust Embodied AI:** This work has profound implications for embodied AI. Tasks like robotic manipulation, navigation, and human-robot interaction demand highly accurate spatial reasoning, often at a millimeter level. By demonstrating `SpatialBot`'s superior performance in robotic pick-and-place tasks with spatial instructions, the paper provides a clear pathway for developing more capable and reliable intelligent robots. Robots powered by `SpatialBot` could potentially operate with greater autonomy, precision, and adaptability in complex, unstructured environments, leading to advancements in manufacturing, logistics, assistive robotics, and more.

- **Pioneering New Data Resources and Benchmarks:** The creation and open-sourcing of `SpatialQA`, `SpatialQA-E`, and `SpatialBench` are major contributions to the research community.
 - `SpatialQA` is a large-scale RGB-D VQA dataset that can serve as a foundational resource for training future spatial VLMs, facilitating research on how models learn to interpret and reason about depth. Its progressive training paradigm (low to high-level spatial tasks) is a valuable blueprint.
 - `SpatialQA-E` is particularly novel as the "first manipulation dataset that focuses on spatial relationships." This is critical for training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that need to parse complex spatial commands and execute precise actions in a robotic context.
 - `SpatialBench` provides a standardized, comprehensive tool for evaluating spatial understanding, allowing researchers to rigorously compare models and track progress in this vital area. These resources will accelerate future research and development in spatial AI.

- **Demonstrating Generalizability of Spatial Training:** The finding that training on depth-augmented data for specific spatial tasks can also improve performance on *general VLM benchmarks* is highly significant. This suggests that enhancing a VLM's understanding of space leads to a more holistic and robust visual comprehension, indicating a synergistic relationship between spatial and general visual reasoning abilities. This could guide future VLM pre-training strategies.

- **Practical Applicability:** The reliance on readily available RGB-D cameras (common on modern robots) and the use of robust MDE models (like ZoeDepth) to generate depth maps for existing RGB datasets make `SpatialBot` highly practical. This approach avoids the need for specialized hardware or computationally expensive 3D reconstruction pipelines, lowering the barrier to adoption for developers and researchers. The `Depth API` offers a flexible mechanism to combine the VLM's high-level reasoning with precise sensor data when needed.

- **Future Research Directions:** `SpatialBot` also opens up new avenues for research. The observation that VLMs struggle with text-only pixel-level MDE suggests that integrating specialized decoders for depth prediction could be a future research direction to create truly end-to-end spatial understanding models. Furthermore, the integration of `SpatialBot` into more complex embodied tasks, such as long-horizon planning, multi-robot coordination, and adaptive manipulation in dynamic environments, represents compelling future work.

In conclusion, `SpatialBot` represents a significant leap towards more intelligent and capable AI systems that can not only "see" but also "understand" the spatial reality of their surroundings. Its contributions in model development, dataset creation, and practical demonstration lay a strong foundation for the next generation of embodied AI and real-world VLM applications.
