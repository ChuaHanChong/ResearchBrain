# Composite Flow Matching for Reinforcement Learning with Shifted-Dynamics Data

> [!summary] Summary
> The COMPFLOW framework, developed by researchers at Harvard University, introduces a method for reinforcement learning with shifted-dynamics data by leveraging composite flow matching to robustly estimate dynamics gaps using the Wasserstein distance. It achieved a 14.2% average relative improvement over the strongest baseline on Gym-MuJoCo tasks and an 8.8% improvement on a wildlife conservation task under limited online interactions.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) in real-world scenarios faces limited online interaction data, necessitating the use of pre-collected offline datasets.
- A significant challenge arises when transition dynamics in offline datasets differ from online environments (shifted dynamics), leading to distribution mismatch and unstable learning.
- Existing methods for quantifying dynamics gaps often rely on statistical measures like KL divergence, which can be ill-defined or unstable when offline and online dynamics have mismatched support.

## Method

- **COMPFLOW** estimates dynamics gaps using ==composite flow matching== to compute the Wasserstein distance between conditional transition distributions, which is robust even with mismatched support.
- A composite flow architecture first trains an offline dynamics model, then initializes the online dynamics model from its output, leveraging structural knowledge for improved generalization with limited online data.
- An optimistic active data collection strategy guides the agent to explore state-action regions with high estimated dynamics gaps, enhancing policy adaptation and collecting valuable online experience.

## Results

- COMPFLOW consistently outperformed or matched strong baselines, achieving the highest mean return on 19 out of 27 Gym-MuJoCo tasks and tying for best on 5 others, with a **14.2%** average relative improvement over the strongest baseline.
- An ablation study demonstrated that the composite flow architecture significantly reduced the Mean Squared Error in learning online transition dynamics, supporting its theoretical benefits.
- In a simulated wildlife conservation task, COMPFLOW achieved the highest reward, outperforming the best baseline by **8.8%** and the SAC baseline by **20.8%** with limited online interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Wasserstein distance, computed via flow matching, provides a more stable and theoretically grounded measure of dynamics shifts than information-theoretic measures, especially when distribution supports do not perfectly overlap.
> - Composing an online flow from a pre-trained offline flow effectively transfers structural knowledge, significantly improving the accuracy and generalization of learned online dynamics models from scarce data.
> - Actively seeking out and collecting data in areas of high dynamics discrepancy can lead to more robust policy adaptation and superior performance compared to conservative data filtering alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes in complex sequential decision-making tasks, often requiring extensive online interaction with the environment. However, real-world applications in domains such as robotics, healthcare, and wildlife conservation frequently face constraints on interaction data due to high costs, safety concerns, or logistical challenges. To mitigate this, a common strategy is to leverage pre-collected offline datasets to improve sample efficiency during online learning.

A significant challenge in this "offline-to-online" RL paradigm arises when the transition dynamics in the offline dataset differ from those in the online environment, a phenomenon referred to as "shifted dynamics." This discrepancy can lead to distribution mismatch, introduce bias into policy updates, and destabilize the learning process. Prior work has attempted to address shifted dynamics through methods like penalizing rewards or value estimates of offline transitions with high dynamics gaps, or by filtering out such transitions. These methods often rely on estimators of the dynamics gap, such as KL divergence or mutual information. However, these information-theoretic measures can become ill-defined or unstable when the offline and online dynamics have mismatched support, meaning that certain state-action pairs lead to next states in one environment that are impossible in the other. Value-based filtering methods, on the other hand, can be susceptible to bootstrapping bias and target non-stationarity, leading to instability.

This research contributes to the field by proposing a principled framework, COMPFLOW, that addresses the limitations of existing dynamics-gap estimation methods. It builds on the theoretical connections between flow matching and optimal transport, offering a more robust and well-defined measure of the dynamics gap using the Wasserstein distance. This approach positions the work within the growing area of applying generative models, specifically flow matching, to RL problems, moving beyond their typical use for policy learning or dynamics modeling within a single environment to tackle cross-domain transfer learning under dynamics shifts. It also introduces an active data collection strategy that goes beyond conservative data filtering to proactively explore regions with high dynamics discrepancy, aiming to improve policy adaptation and generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust and effective framework for reinforcement learning in environments where there is a shift in transition dynamics between a large pre-collected offline dataset and the online environment. This is motivated by the critical need to improve sample efficiency in real-world RL applications where online interactions are limited, expensive, or risky.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Dynamics Gap Estimators:** Current methods for quantifying the dynamics gap often rely on statistical measures like KL divergence or mutual information. These measures face a fundamental limitation: they can be ill-defined or numerically unstable when the support of the offline and online transition distributions do not overlap (i.e., when certain state-action pairs lead to next states in one environment that are impossible in the other). This issue prevents accurate and reliable quantification of dynamics shifts, which in turn compromises the effectiveness of methods that filter or penalize offline data based on these gaps. The research aims to overcome this by introducing a dynamics gap estimator based on the Wasserstein distance, which is well-defined even with mismatched supports.

- **Improving Generalization of Online Dynamics Learning with Limited Data:** Learning accurate transition dynamics models from scratch in the online environment, especially when interaction data is scarce, is prone to overfitting and poor generalization. The authors aim to improve the fidelity of the online dynamics model by leveraging the information present in the more abundant offline data. This objective motivates the development of a composite flow architecture that incorporates structural knowledge from a pre-trained offline dynamics model into the online dynamics learning process, thereby enhancing the model's ability to generalize from limited online interactions.

- **Developing a Principled Framework for Dynamics Gap Estimation:** The proposed method seeks to establish a theoretically grounded approach for estimating the dynamics gap. By drawing on the connection between flow matching and optimal transport, the framework aims to directly compute the Wasserstein distance between conditional transition distributions. This approach is intended to provide a more stable and robust measure compared to prior techniques.

- **Enhancing Policy Adaptation through Active Data Collection:** Merely filtering out high-discrepancy offline data or applying conservative penalties can restrict the agent's experience diversity and hinder its ability to adapt to new dynamics. The research aims to move beyond purely conservative approaches by integrating an active data collection strategy. This strategy is designed to encourage exploration in state-action regions where the dynamics gap is estimated to be high, thereby guiding the agent to gather valuable online experience in areas critical for adaptation and potentially leading to a more robust and optimal policy.

- **Empirical Validation and Performance Improvement:** A key objective is to empirically demonstrate that the proposed COMPFLOW framework, with its principled dynamics gap estimation and active exploration strategy, can consistently outperform existing state-of-the-art baselines across a range of RL benchmarks with various types and severities of dynamics shifts. The goal is to show improved performance under limited online interaction budgets.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The COMPFLOW framework introduces a novel approach for handling shifted dynamics in reinforcement learning by leveraging composite flow matching for dynamics gap estimation and an active data collection strategy.

#### 3.1. Estimating Dynamics Gap via Composite Flow

The core of COMPFLOW lies in its method for estimating the discrepancy between offline and online transition dynamics using flow matching.

1. **Flow Matching Background:** Flow matching (FM) is a generative modeling technique that learns a time-dependent velocity field to transport samples from a simple source distribution (e.g., an isotropic Gaussian, $p_0(x)$) to a complex target distribution ($p_1(x)$) via a deterministic ordinary differential equation (ODE). The training objective, $L(\theta) = E_{t \sim U, x_0 \sim p_0(x_0), x_1 \sim p_1(x_1)}[||v_{\theta}(x_t, t) - (x_1 - x_0)||^2_2]$, encourages the flow to follow linear paths, improving computational efficiency. Optimal Transport Flow Matching (OT-FM) connects this to Optimal Transport (OT) by drawing $(x_0, x_1)$ pairs from an optimal coupling, such that the learned flow's transport cost approximates the squared 2-Wasserstein distance, $W^2_2(p_0, p_1)$.

2. **Composite Flow Architecture:** To address the challenge of limited online data, COMPFLOW proposes a composite flow formulation rather than learning the online dynamics model directly from scratch.
 - **Offline Flow:** A conditional flow model, $\psi^{off}_{\theta}(x_0, t | s, a)$, is first trained on the abundant offline data. This flow maps a Gaussian latent variable $x_0 \sim N(0, I)$ at $t=0$ to a next state $x_1 = s'$ that follows the offline transition distribution $p_{off}(s'|s,a)$ at $t=1$.
 - **Online Flow:** The online transition model, $p_{on}(s'|s,a)$, is then learned as a second flow, $\psi^{on}_{\phi}(x, t | s, a)$, but it is *initialized* from the output of the offline flow. Specifically, it starts from $x_1$ (the next state generated by the offline flow) at $t=1$ and transports it to the online next state $s'$ (from $p_{on}(s'|s,a)$) at $t=2$. This sequential composition allows the online flow to leverage the structural knowledge embedded in the well-trained offline flow.
 - **Theoretical Justification (Theorem 3.1):** This composite structure is theoretically shown to reduce generalization error compared to learning the online dynamics directly from a Gaussian prior, provided that the offline dynamics distribution is closer to the online dynamics distribution in Wasserstein distance than a Gaussian distribution ($W_2(p_G, p_{on}) > W_2(\hat{p}_{off}, p_{on})$).

3. **Wasserstein Distance Estimation:** To compute the dynamics gap $\Delta(s,a)$ (the Wasserstein distance between $p_{off}(\cdot|s,a)$ and $p_{on}(\cdot|s,a)$), the online flow is trained using an OT-FM objective adapted for conditional distributions.
 - **Augmented Transport Cost:** Since $(s,a)$ pairs exist in a continuous space, a batch of samples for a *fixed* $(s,a)$ is not feasible. The approach incorporates the conditioning variables directly into the transport cost for OT-FM: $c((s_{off}, a_{off}, s'_{off}), (s_{on}, a_{on}, s'_{on})) = ||s'_{off} - s'_{on}||^2_2 + \eta(||s_{off} - s_{on}||^2_2 + ||a_{off} - a_{on}||^2_2)$. The hyperparameter $\eta$ controls the alignment strength of conditioning variables.
 - **Matched Marginals (Proposition 3.3, Remark 3.4):** To ensure this augmented cost recovers the conditional Wasserstein distance as $\eta \rightarrow \infty$, the (s,a)-marginals of the two empirical distributions used for OT coupling must be matched. This is achieved by constructing the "offline-synthetic" batch by first sampling $(s_{off}, a_{off})$ from the *online* dataset $D_{on}$, then generating $s'_{off}$ using the pre-trained offline flow. The "online" batch $(s_{on}, a_{on}, s'_{on})$ is directly sampled from $D_{on}$. This ensures the (s,a) marginals are matched in expectation.
 - **Monte Carlo Estimator:** The dynamics gap $\Delta(s,a)$ is estimated via a Monte Carlo average of the squared displacement between the output of the offline flow and the final output of the composite online flow for a given $(s,a)$, for samples initialized from $N(0,I)$.

#### 3.2. Data Collection at High Dynamics Gap Region

COMPFLOW integrates the estimated dynamics gap into both replay buffer management and active exploration.

1. **Replay Buffer Construction:** The replay buffer $B$ is composed of all online transitions $D_{on}$ and a selectively filtered subset of offline transitions from $D_{off}$. Offline transitions $(s,a)$ are included only if their estimated dynamics gap, $\hat{\Delta}(s,a)$, is below a predefined threshold $\tau$ (or, in practice, a fixed percentage $\xi\%$ of lowest-gap transitions in a batch are selected).

2. **Optimistic Active Data Collection:** To address potential limitations in state-action coverage from purely filtering low-gap data, an optimistic exploration strategy is employed. The policy is encouraged to explore regions with high dynamics gap by adding an exploration bonus to the Q-value function during action selection: $a = \arg\max_{a \in A} (Q(s,a) + \beta \hat{\Delta}(s,a))$, where $\beta$ is a hyperparameter balancing return and exploration. This guides the agent to collect online data in regions where the dynamics are most different from the offline data.

3. **Theoretical Justification (Theorem 3.5):** Theoretical analysis suggests that this targeted exploration in high-gap regions, specifically by collecting online samples to replace offline samples with dynamics gaps exceeding a threshold $\kappa$, can reduce the performance gap to the optimal online policy.

#### 3.3. Practical Implementation

COMPFLOW is instantiated using a standard actor-critic algorithm (Soft Actor-Critic, SAC).
- **Critic Update:** The critic's Q-functions are trained by minimizing a Bellman loss. The loss combines online transitions with selected offline transitions (the $\xi\%$-quantile of lowest-gap samples). Critically, the target value $y$ for *all* transitions (both online and selected offline) incorporates the estimated dynamics gap as an exploration bonus: $y = r + \gamma Q_{\zeta}(s',a') + \beta \hat{\Delta}(s,a)$.
- **Policy Update:** The actor (policy $\pi_{\phi}$) is updated to maximize its Q-values. It also includes a behavior cloning regularization term, which encourages the policy's actions to stay close to the actions observed in the offline dataset, consistent with insights from prior works on mitigating policy-behavior mismatch.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence for the effectiveness of COMPFLOW across standard RL benchmarks and a real-world inspired task.

#### 4.1. Gym-MuJoCo Experiments

**Experimental Setup:**
The evaluation was conducted on three continuous control tasks from OpenAI Gym (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d), using D4RL datasets (medium, medium-replay, medium-expert) as offline data. Three types of dynamics shifts were applied to create the online environments: morphology changes (body part sizes), kinematic changes (joint angle constraints), and friction changes (surface coefficients). COMPFLOW was compared against several baselines: SAC (training from scratch), BC-SAC (SAC with behavior cloning), H2O, BC-VGDF, and BC-PAR. Evaluation was based on return after 40,000 environment interactions, reflecting a limited-interaction setting.

**Main Results:**
- **Superior Performance:** COMPFLOW consistently outperformed or matched strong baselines. It achieved the highest mean return on 19 out of 27 tasks and tied for the best on 5 additional tasks. On average, COMPFLOW yielded a return of 2193, representing a 14.2% relative improvement over the strongest baseline, BC-SAC (1920).
- **Benefit of Offline Data & Dynamics Awareness:** Compared to training from scratch with SAC, COMPFLOW showed a 149.8% average improvement, highlighting the benefit of leveraging offline data. Furthermore, COMPFLOW outperformed BC-SAC (which uses all offline data directly) on 23 of 27 tasks and matched it on 3 others, demonstrating the effectiveness of its dynamics-gap-aware design in selective data utilization and exploration.
- **Robust Dynamics Gap Estimation:** The strong performance of COMPFLOW, particularly compared to baselines relying on KL divergence or mutual information (H2O, BC-PAR) or value estimates (BC-VGDF), suggests that its principled dynamics gap estimation via Wasserstein distance is more robust and stable, especially under significant dynamics shifts and mismatched support.

**Ablation and Hyperparameter Analysis:**
- **Composite Flow Effectiveness (Figure 3):** An ablation study showed that the composite flow architecture significantly reduced the Mean Squared Error (MSE) in learning online transition dynamics compared to a direct flow model initialized from a Gaussian prior. This empirical finding supports Theorem 3.1, indicating improved generalization by reusing structural knowledge from offline data.
- **Impact of Data Selection Ratio $\xi\%$ (Figure 4):** The selection ratio, which determines the percentage of lowest-gap offline data admitted into the replay buffer, was found to be task-dependent. Moderate values (e.g., 30% or 50%) generally performed well, balancing the use of relevant offline data with the avoidance of high-discrepancy samples. Excessive inclusion (e.g., 70%) could degrade performance, while overly conservative filtering (e.g., 20%) sometimes led to slower learning.
- **Impact of Exploration Strength $\beta$ (Figure 5):** The hyperparameter $\beta$, controlling the exploration bonus in high-dynamics-gap regions, also showed task-dependent optimal values. For "Friction" tasks, larger $\beta$ values often led to higher asymptotic returns, indicating the benefit of incentivized exploration. For "Morphology" tasks, moderate $\beta$ values were generally optimal, suggesting that excessive exploration might not always be effective. Notably, the absence of an exploration bonus ($\beta=0$) consistently resulted in lower performance across all tested tasks, underscoring the importance of this exploration strategy.

#### 4.2. Patrol Policy Learning for Wildlife Conservation

**Task Setup:**
The method was evaluated in a simulated wildlife conservation task involving patrol policy learning. The scenario involved learning an effective patrol policy in Queen Elizabeth National Park using an offline dataset collected from Murchison Falls National Park, where ecological conditions and poacher behaviors induce a dynamics mismatch between the parks.

**Results (Figure 6):**
COMPFLOW achieved the highest reward in this real-world inspired task, outperforming the best baseline (BC-PAR) by 8.8% and SAC (training from scratch) by 20.8% under the same online interaction budget. This demonstrates the method's potential for practical application in critical domains where data efficiency and robust adaptation to shifted dynamics are essential.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The COMPFLOW framework presents a significant advancement in reinforcement learning with shifted-dynamics data, addressing a critical challenge for deploying RL in real-world scenarios.

1. **Robust Dynamics Gap Estimation:** The most prominent significance lies in its principled approach to estimating the dynamics gap using the Wasserstein distance via composite flow matching. This overcomes the fundamental limitations of traditional information-theoretic measures (like KL divergence or mutual information) which can be ill-defined or unstable under mismatched support. This robust estimation is crucial for reliable data filtering and adaptive learning in diverse environments.
2. **Improved Generalization with Limited Online Data:** The composite flow architecture effectively leverages structural knowledge from abundant offline data to improve the generalization ability of the online dynamics model, even with limited online interaction. This is a practical advantage in contexts where online data collection is severely restricted.
3. **Enhanced Policy Adaptation through Active Exploration:** By integrating the estimated dynamics gap into an optimistic active data collection strategy, COMPFLOW moves beyond purely conservative data filtering. It strategically guides the agent to explore high-discrepancy regions, enabling more comprehensive adaptation to the online environment and potentially leading to more optimal policies.
4. **Strong Empirical Performance:** The consistent outperformance against strong baselines across a variety of RL benchmarks with different types of dynamics shifts, including a real-world inspired conservation task, validates the practical efficacy and generalizability of the proposed method.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Increased Real-World Applicability of RL:** By making RL more robust and sample-efficient in the presence of dynamics shifts, COMPFLOW has the potential to accelerate the deployment of RL systems in high-stakes, data-scarce domains such as robotics (e.g., adapting robot policies trained in simulation to physical systems with different friction or joint dynamics), healthcare (e.g., personalizing treatment strategies using historical patient data despite individual physiological variations), and environmental conservation (e.g., transferring patrol strategies between ecologically distinct protected areas).
2. **Safer and More Reliable RL Systems:** The ability to accurately quantify and adapt to dynamics shifts contributes to the development of safer and more reliable RL agents, as it reduces the risk of deploying policies that are brittle to environmental changes not captured in offline training data.
3. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work provides a foundation for future research directions, including:
 - **Uncertainty Quantification:** Incorporating uncertainty estimates in dynamics gap estimation could further refine data filtering and exploration strategies, leading to even more robust learning.
 - **Broader Domain Shifts:** Extending the method to scenarios with mismatched state and action spaces by learning dynamics in shared latent embeddings.
 - **Real-world Deployment:** Testing and validating COMPFLOW directly in physical systems and real-world applications to assess its performance beyond simulated environments.
 - **Interpretable Dynamics Transfer:** The flow-based dynamics models could potentially offer more insights into the nature of the dynamics shift, aiding in system design or environmental management.

Overall, COMPFLOW represents a step towards making reinforcement learning more practical, adaptable, and deployable in complex, dynamic real-world settings.
