# Composite Flow Matching for Reinforcement Learning with Shifted-Dynamics Data

> [!summary] Summary
> The COMPFLOW framework, developed by researchers at Harvard University, introduces a method for reinforcement learning with shifted-dynamics data by leveraging composite flow matching to robustly estimate dynamics gaps using the Wasserstein distance. It achieved a 14.2% average relative improvement over the strongest baseline on Gym-MuJoCo tasks and an 8.8% improvement on a wildlife conservation task under limited online interactions.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) in real-world scenarios faces limited online interaction data, necessitating the use of pre-collected offline datasets.
- A significant challenge arises when transition dynamics in offline datasets differ from online environments (shifted dynamics), leading to distribution mismatch and unstable learning.
- Existing methods for quantifying dynamics gaps often rely on statistical measures like KL divergence, which can be ill-defined or unstable when offline and online dynamics have mismatched support.

## Method

- **COMPFLOW** estimates dynamics gaps using ==composite flow matching== to compute the Wasserstein distance between conditional transition distributions, which is robust even with mismatched support.
- A composite flow architecture first trains an offline dynamics model, then initializes the online dynamics model from its output, leveraging structural knowledge for improved generalization with limited online data.
- An optimistic active data collection strategy guides the agent to explore state-action regions with high estimated dynamics gaps, enhancing policy adaptation and collecting valuable online experience.

## Results

- COMPFLOW consistently outperformed or matched strong baselines, achieving the highest mean return on 19 out of 27 Gym-MuJoCo tasks and tying for best on 5 others, with a **14.2%** average relative improvement over the strongest baseline.
- An ablation study demonstrated that the composite flow architecture significantly reduced the Mean Squared Error in learning online transition dynamics, supporting its theoretical benefits.
- In a simulated wildlife conservation task, COMPFLOW achieved the highest reward, outperforming the best baseline by **8.8%** and the SAC baseline by **20.8%** with limited online interaction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Wasserstein distance, computed via flow matching, provides a more stable and theoretically grounded measure of dynamics shifts than information-theoretic measures, especially when distribution supports do not perfectly overlap.
> - Composing an online flow from a pre-trained offline flow effectively transfers structural knowledge, significantly improving the accuracy and generalization of learned online dynamics models from scarce data.
> - Actively seeking out and collecting data in areas of high dynamics discrepancy can lead to more robust policy adaptation and superior performance compared to conservative data filtering alone.
