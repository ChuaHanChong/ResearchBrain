# Contrastive Decoding: Open-ended Text Generation as Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Contrastive Decoding (CD) improves open-ended text generation by leveraging a contrastive objective between a large "expert" language model and a smaller "amateur" model. This decoding-time strategy produces text that is highly coherent and fluent while maintaining lexical diversity, without requiring any model retraining or fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Maximum probability decoding methods (e.g., greedy, beam search) lead to repetitive, degenerate, and uninteresting text in open-ended generation.
- Sampling-based methods (e.g., nucleus, top-k sampling) improve diversity but often result in topic drift and incoherence over longer generated sequences.
- Training-based solutions to these issues are computationally expensive and impractical for increasingly large language models.

## Method

- **Contrastive Decoding (CD)** uses a ==search-based approach== to optimize a ==contrastive objective==, maximizing tokens that are highly probable under a large ==expert LM== and less probable under a smaller ==amateur LM==.
- An ==adaptive plausibility constraint (V_head)== filters candidate tokens, ensuring only those sufficiently plausible by the expert LM are considered, preventing implausible or nonsensical outputs.
- The method employs ==beam search== to select tokens based on this contrastive score, favoring sequences that align with the expert's nuanced understanding while avoiding the amateur's common failure modes.

## Results

- Contrastive Decoding consistently achieved the highest **MAUVE** scores, indicating generated text distributions closest to human reference text across various open-ended domains.
- Human evaluations showed overwhelming preference for CD's outputs in terms of coherence (**2.6x** over nucleus sampling, **6.4x** over typical decoding) and superior fluency.
- Optimal performance was achieved with a large capacity gap between the expert and amateur models (e.g., **OPT-13B** expert vs. **OPT-125M** amateur), with the amateur's temperature and context window further enhancing quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherent differences in generation quality between large (expert) and small (amateur) language models can be exploited at decoding time to produce higher-quality text.
> - Penalizing tokens that are highly predictable by a less capable (amateur) model encourages the generation of more informative, diverse, and less repetitive text.
> - A plausibility constraint is essential to ground the contrastive objective, preventing the selection of rare or implausible tokens that might coincidentally have high contrastive scores.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of natural language generation (NLG) has seen a paradigm shift with the advent of large pre-trained language models (LLMs). These models exhibit remarkable capabilities in generating human-like text across diverse tasks. However, a significant challenge remains in *how* to extract the best possible text from these powerful models, a process known as decoding. The broader research landscape is characterized by an ongoing struggle to find decoding strategies that balance text quality attributes like fluency, coherence, diversity, and factual consistency.

Traditional decoding methods primarily fall into two categories, each with well-documented shortcomings:

1. **Search-based methods (e.g., greedy decoding, beam search):** These methods aim to find the most probable sequence of tokens. While effective for goal-oriented tasks (like summarization or machine translation where accuracy is paramount), they often produce text that is short, repetitive, and lacks lexical diversity in open-ended generation tasks. This is due to the "neural text degeneration" problem, where maximizing token probability at each step can lead to local optima that are not globally desirable.
2. **Sampling-based methods (e.g., nucleus sampling, top-k sampling, typical decoding):** These methods introduce stochasticity by sampling tokens from a truncated or re-weighted probability distribution. They successfully address the diversity issue, producing more varied and interesting text, which is highly desirable for open-ended generation (e.g., story writing, chatbots). However, their stochastic nature can lead to problems like topic drift and incoherence, especially over longer generated sequences, as "unlucky" sampling choices compound.

Existing research efforts to address these issues include:

- **Training-based approaches:** Methods like unlikelihood training (Welleck et al., 2020) explicitly penalize repetitive sequences during training, while contrastive learning (Su et al., 2022) aims to separate token representations to boost diversity. While effective, these require re-training or fine-tuning, which is computationally expensive and time-consuming for ever-larger LLMs.
- **Decoding-time modifications:** Some methods, like DExperts (Liu et al., 2021), use an "expert" and "anti-expert" model for *controllable* generation (e.g., generating non-toxic text), but typically require specialized anti-expert models. MMI decoding (Li et al., 2016) uses mutual information to promote diverse responses in dialogue systems. Contrastive search (Su et al., 2022) also leverages a form of contrast.

This paper positions **Contrastive Decoding (CD)** as a novel decoding-time strategy that directly tackles the core trade-off between coherence and diversity in open-ended generation, without incurring the high cost of model re-training. It synthesizes insights from prior contrastive approaches and the observed failure modes of LLMs. By proposing a method that leverages readily available pre-trained models of different scales, CD offers an efficient and broadly applicable solution to a fundamental problem in LLM deployment. It seeks to bridge the gap between the high coherence of search-based methods and the high diversity of sampling-based methods, while being easily adoptable by practitioners.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and effective decoding strategy for **open-ended text generation** that can produce **fluent, lexically diverse, and coherent textual continuations** from pre-trained language models.

The motivation stems directly from the inherent limitations of prevailing decoding algorithms:

1. **Failure of Maximum Probability (Greedy/Beam Search):** When LMs are decoded by simply selecting the most probable next token or sequence, the generated text tends to be short, repetitive, and uninteresting. This "degeneration" problem makes the output unsuitable for creative or expansive generation tasks. Maximizing probability does not align with human preferences for natural, varied language.
2. **Failure of Sampling Methods (Nucleus/Top-K):** While sampling introduces diversity and reduces repetition, it often comes at the cost of coherence. Over longer generations, random sampling choices can accumulate, causing the text to drift off-topic, introduce contradictions, or become outright nonsensical. This "topic drift" makes the output unreliable for maintaining a consistent narrative or informational flow.
3. **Need for Training-Free Solutions:** As language models grow exponentially in size (e.g., GPT-3, OPT-175B), the cost and resources required for further training or fine-tuning become prohibitive for many researchers and practitioners. There is a strong motivation to find decoding-time solutions that can improve the quality of existing, frozen LMs "off-the-shelf."

The central insight motivating Contrastive Decoding is the observation that the failure modes of language models (repetition, incoherence, generic phrases) are **more pronounced in smaller LMs** compared to larger, more capable LMs. The authors hypothesize that this *difference* in behavior can be exploited. If a large "expert" LM assigns high probability to a desirable token, and a small "amateur" LM assigns comparatively lower probability to it (or vice-versa for undesirable tokens), this contrast signals which tokens to prefer. By amplifying the "expert" characteristics and diminishing the "amateur" ones, the system aims to generate text that captures the best of the larger model's knowledge and linguistic prowess, without its common pitfalls or the amateur's pronounced weaknesses.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Contrastive Decoding (CD) is introduced as a search-based decoding method that optimizes a novel contrastive objective, subject to a plausibility constraint. Its core components and design choices are:

1. **Expert and Amateur Language Models:**
 - **Expert LM (p_EXP):** A standard, large pre-trained autoregressive language model (e.g., OPT-13B, GPT2-XL). This model is assumed to capture a richer understanding of language and world knowledge.
 - **Amateur LM (p_AMA):** A smaller pre-trained autoregressive language model, ideally from the same family as the expert (e.g., OPT-125M, GPT2-small). The amateur is chosen because its "failure modes" (repetition, generic phrases, lack of coherence) are more pronounced.

2. **The Contrastive Objective (L_CD):**
 The objective to maximize for a generated continuation `x_cont` given a prompt `x_pre` is defined as:
 `L_CD(x_cont, x_pre) = log p_EXP(x_cont | x_pre) - log p_AMA(x_cont | x_pre)`
 This objective rewards sequences that are highly probable under the expert LM but *less probable* under the amateur LM. It effectively "filters out" patterns that are easily predictable by the less capable amateur, which often correspond to repetitive or generic text. Conversely, patterns that are uniquely favored by the expert (e.g., complex factual statements, nuanced phrasing) receive a higher score.

3. **Adaptive Plausibility Constraint (V_head):**
 The contrastive objective alone can sometimes be problematic:
 - **False Positives:** Implausible or rare tokens might coincidentally have a large `log p_EXP - log p_AMA` difference, leading to nonsensical outputs.
 - **False Negatives:** Very common and correct tokens (e.g., continuation of a common phrase) might have a low contrastive score if both LMs are highly confident, even if they are desirable.
 To mitigate these issues, CD introduces `V_head(x_<i)`, an adaptive plausibility constraint at each decoding step `i`. This constraint restricts the set of candidate tokens to only those `x_i` for which `p_EXP(x_i | x_<i) ≥ α * max_w p_EXP(w | x_<i)`. Here, `α` is a hyperparameter (set to 0.1).
 - `V_head` ensures that only tokens considered "sufficiently plausible" by the expert LM are even considered.
 - When the expert is highly confident about a token (i.e., `max_w p_EXP(w|x_<i)` is very high for a single token), `V_head` might restrict the candidate set to just that token, effectively overriding the contrastive objective and ensuring correctness for "easy" decisions.

4. **Full Method and Decoding:**
 The full Contrastive Decoding formulation aims to:
 `maximize L_CD(x_cont, x_pre)`
 `subject to x_i ∈ V_head(x_<i), ∀x_i ∈ x_cont`
 Since optimizing this sequence-level objective is intractable, it is factored into token-level scores:
 `CD-score(x_i; x_<i) = { log(p_EXP(x_i|x_<i) / p_AMA(x_i|x_<i)), if x_i ∈ V_head(x_<i) ; -inf, otherwise }`
 The decoding process uses **beam search** (with a beam size of 5). At each step, tokens are first filtered by the `V_head` constraint, and then the remaining tokens are scored using the CD-score for beam expansion. This ensures plausible tokens are chosen based on their "contrast" with the amateur.

5. **Design Choices for the Amateur LM:**
 The authors explore three aspects for defining the amateur's behavior:
 - **Scale:** The most effective amateur is the *smallest* model in the same family as the expert, creating the largest capacity gap. Extremely low-capacity models like n-gram LMs perform poorly as amateurs, as their errors don't sufficiently highlight the expert's nuanced failure modes.
 - **Temperature (τ):** Applying a temperature to the amateur's probability distribution (`p_AMA(x_i|x_<i, τ)`) can manipulate its behavior. Lower temperatures (τ < 1, e.g., 0.5) make the amateur's distribution spikier, emphasizing its most likely (and often repetitive) predictions, which CD then penalizes more effectively. τ ∈ [0.5, 1.5] is found to be robust.
 - **Context Window:** The amateur LM's context can be intentionally restricted (e.g., only conditioning on the last token of `x_pre` instead of the full prompt). This further weakens the amateur's coherence, compelling CD to select tokens that are more globally coherent under the expert.

6. **Pragmatic Interpretation:**
 The authors offer a pragmatic view inspired by Gricean maxims. The expert LM acts as a "knowledgeable speaker," producing high-quality, fluent, and relevant language. The amateur LM models a "less-informed listener." By upweighting tokens based on the expert and downweighting tokens predictable by the amateur, CD encourages text that is both high quality and *informative* (not predictable by a simpler model), thus satisfying communicative goals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Contrastive Decoding (CD) involved extensive automatic and human assessments across three distinct open-ended text generation domains: Wikipedia, news, and stories. Experiments were conducted with different expert-amateur LM combinations (GPT2-XL vs. GPT2-small, OPT-13B vs. OPT-125M).

#### 4.1. Automatic Evaluation (Table 1)

CD was compared against four strong decoding baselines: nucleus sampling, top-k sampling, typical decoding, greedy decoding, and contrastive search (CS).

- **MAUVE Score:** CD consistently achieved the highest MAUVE scores across all domains and model sizes. MAUVE measures the distributional similarity between generated text and human reference text, indicating that CD outputs are closest to human-quality distributions.
- **Coherence (COH):** CD significantly outperformed all other baselines in coherence scores by a substantial margin. This indicates that CD effectively reduces topic drift and produces text that stays on-topic with the prompt. Greedy decoding also showed good coherence (but was highly repetitive), which the authors attribute to its degenerate nature (repeating phrases is a form of coherence). CD's coherence gain is attributed to its search-based nature, the implicit reward for coherence from larger LMs, and the restricted context for the amateur.
- **Diversity (DIV):** CD achieved comparable or slightly worse diversity scores compared to highly diverse sampling methods (nucleus, typical sampling), but demonstrated substantially better diversity than other search-based methods (greedy, CS). The diversity gap with sampling methods was observed to shrink with larger expert models (e.g., from GPT2-XL to OPT-13B), suggesting CD's diversity improves with model scale.

#### 4.2. Human Evaluation (Table 2)

To directly assess perceived quality, human evaluators (Amazon Mechanical Turkers) compared CD outputs against nucleus sampling (a standard high-MAUVE method) and typical decoding (a high-diversity method) for fluency and coherence.

- **Coherence:** CD was overwhelmingly preferred for coherence. On average, human evaluators preferred CD's outputs 2.6 times more than nucleus sampling and 6.4 times more than typical decoding. This is a very strong validation of CD's ability to maintain topic consistency.
- **Fluency:** CD also demonstrated superior or comparable fluency. It was preferred 1.4 times more than nucleus sampling and 3.5 times more than typical decoding. This indicates CD generates grammatically sound and naturally flowing text without unnatural repetitions or stylistic inconsistencies.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Examples (Table 3)

Illustrative examples confirmed the quantitative findings. Nucleus sampling outputs often exhibited topic shifts, style shifts (e.g., from third-person narrative to first-person conversational), and even email formats, reflecting noisy pre-training data. CD outputs, in contrast, consistently stayed on topic, elaborated coherently, and maintained a consistent style.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Size of Amateur and Expert LMs (Figure 2):** The experiments confirmed that a larger scale difference between the expert and amateur LMs yielded better generation quality (higher diversity and MAUVE). The optimal setup was a combination of the largest available expert and the smallest available amateur (e.g., GPT2-XL expert with GPT2-small amateur). Using the same model as both expert and amateur, or an expert smaller than the amateur, resulted in inferior text quality (often highly repetitive). N-gram models as amateurs performed poorly, indicating that the amateur needs to be a "realistic" model of common LM errors.
- **Impact of Amateur Temperature (τ) (Figure 3):** CD showed robust performance for amateur temperatures `τ` within the range of [0.5, 1.5]. Lower temperatures (e.g., 0.5) tend to make the amateur's distribution spikier, which helps in identifying and penalizing common errors.
- **Sampling vs. Search (Table 4, 5):** The search-based optimization of the CD objective consistently produced higher quality text (better fluency and coherence) than simply sampling from a softmax-normalized CD-score distribution.
- **Plausibility Constraints (V_head) (Table 4):** The `V_head` plausibility constraint was found to be critical. Removing it resulted in severe fluency issues and drastically low MAUVE scores (e.g., 0.01), confirming its role in preventing implausible tokens from being selected.
- **Prompt Inclusion for Amateur (Table 5):** Restricting the amateur LM to condition only on the last token of the prompt (instead of the full prompt) slightly improved coherence and fluency, further emphasizing the expert's ability to maintain long-range context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Contrastive Decoding (CD) represents a significant advancement in the practical application and quality control of large language models for open-ended text generation. Its impact stems from several key aspects:

1. **Zero-Training Paradigm:** Perhaps the most significant contribution is CD's ability to dramatically improve generation quality *without requiring any additional training or fine-tuning* of the underlying language models. This makes it an incredibly cost-effective and resource-efficient solution, especially pertinent in an era of ever-growing, multi-billion-parameter LMs where retraining is prohibitively expensive. It allows practitioners to immediately leverage existing frozen models more effectively.
2. **Superior Generation Quality:** CD successfully addresses the long-standing trade-off between coherence and diversity in open-ended generation. It produces text that is both highly coherent (maintaining topic and avoiding drift) and highly fluent, while also being lexically diverse, surpassing leading sampling and search-based methods in both automatic and human evaluations. This directly enhances the utility of LLMs for creative writing, content generation, and conversational AI, where natural and consistent output is paramount.
3. **Model Agnostic Applicability:** The method's effectiveness was demonstrated across different LM families (GPT-2, OPT) and various scales. This broad applicability suggests that CD is a generalizable technique that can benefit a wide range of current and future language models.
4. **Theoretical and Pragmatic Foundations:** The paper offers both an intuitive and a pragmatic interpretation of the contrastive objective, linking it to concepts of informative communication and the distinguishability of expert-generated text. This provides a stronger conceptual framework for why the method works, beyond purely empirical observations.
5. **Catalyst for Future Research:** CD opens up new avenues for exploring how to best leverage the inherent differences and capabilities of multiple pre-trained LMs. Future work could investigate different definitions of "amateur" (e.g., models trained on specific types of undesirable text, earlier checkpoints of the same model, or models with restricted architectural features) to target other specific aspects of text quality or controllable generation. The paper explicitly mentions extending this idea to task-oriented generation, which is a promising direction.
6. **Ethical Considerations and Limitations:** The authors responsibly highlight potential ethical risks, such as the generation of more convincing disinformation due to improved text quality. This calls for further research into countermeasures like watermarking generated outputs. They also acknowledge current limitations, particularly in task-oriented generation settings (e.g., summarization, machine translation), where the "amateur" LM's desirable mode might be too close to the "expert's," thus not providing a useful contrast. This indicates that the definition of an effective "amateur" might need to be task-specific beyond simply model scale.

In summary, Contrastive Decoding offers a highly practical, robust, and theoretically grounded approach to enhancing open-ended text generation from LLMs. Its zero-training requirement, coupled with significant improvements in output quality, positions it as an important development for the widespread and effective deployment of large language models.
