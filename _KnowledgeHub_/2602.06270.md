# VowelPrompt: Hearing Speech Emotions from Text via Vowel-level Prosodic Augmentation

> [!summary] Summary
> VowelPrompt, developed by researchers from Meta Superintelligence Labs, Arizona State University, and MIT, enables large language models to interpret speech emotions from text by augmenting transcripts with fine-grained, natural language descriptions of vowel-level prosodic cues. This framework consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods in zero-shot, fine-tuned, cross-domain, and multilingual settings, while providing interpretable explanations for emotion predictions.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-based speech emotion recognition (SER) methods either rely on opaque acoustic embeddings, sacrificing interpretability, or use coarse, utterance-level prosodic descriptions, missing subtle emotional cues.
- Traditional SER methods struggle to provide both high performance and clear interpretability, often requiring an audio encoder during inference which limits deployment in text-only or resource-constrained environments.
- There is a need for a linguistically grounded and fine-grained approach to integrate prosodic information into LLMs to improve their ability to reason about speech emotions.

## Method

- **VowelPrompt** extracts precise pitch, intensity, and duration features from individual, time-aligned vowel segments, normalizing and discretizing them into five ordinal categories.
- These vowel-level ==prosodic== features are converted into concise, natural language descriptions and appended to the text transcript, allowing LLMs to jointly process lexical and prosodic information.
- A two-stage LLM adaptation pipeline, combining supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reward (RLVR) using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), enhances reasoning quality, output adherence, and generalization.

## Results

- VowelPrompt achieved up to **7.80%** UACC and **7.11%** WF1 gains on IEMOCAP over zero-shot baselines and consistently outperformed fine-tuned state-of-the-art methods with absolute WF1 improvements up to **3.14%** on IEMOCAP and **2.17%** on MELD.
- The framework demonstrated improved robustness under domain shift, yielding gains of up to **6.96%** in WF1 in cross-domain evaluations, and extended effectively to multilingual SER across French, German, and mixed-lingual datasets.
- Ablation studies confirmed the contribution of all vowel-level features, with pitch being most significant, and counterfactual analysis showed predictions were genuinely driven by prosodic cues; human evaluators rated VowelPrompt's reasoning traces significantly higher for prosodic grounding and coherence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vowel-level prosodic cues are crucial carriers of affective prosody and provide more fine-grained and interpretable information for emotion recognition than utterance-level descriptors or opaque acoustic embeddings.
> - Integrating linguistically grounded, discrete prosodic features as natural language prompts enables LLMs to achieve superior SER performance while also generating human-readable explanations for their predictions.
> - A two-stage LLM fine-tuning process, particularly with RLVR and a composite reward function, is effective in refining reasoning, enforcing structured output, and improving generalization across diverse conditions including cross-domain and multilingual settings.
