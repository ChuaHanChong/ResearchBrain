# Merging and Disentangling Views in Visual Reinforcement Learning for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC San Diego introduce Merge And Disentanglement (MAD), an actor-critic algorithm that enhances visual reinforcement learning for robotic manipulation by simultaneously merging multi-view features for improved sample efficiency and disentangling individual view representations for robustness to sensor reduction. The framework achieves average success rates 30% higher on Meta-World and 36% higher on ManiSkill3 compared to baselines, while maintaining performance when operating with fewer cameras.

## Problem

- Visual reinforcement learning policies struggle to achieve a comprehensive 3D understanding from 2D observations, impacting depth perception and control in robotic manipulation.
- Multi-view policies, while beneficial for sample efficiency and occlusion mitigation, are often complex to deploy and fragile if sensors malfunction or become unavailable.
- Existing methods typically prioritize either multi-view merging for performance or view disentanglement for robustness, but not both concurrently and efficiently.

## Method

- The **MAD** algorithm employs a shared ==CNN encoder== to process each input camera view, generating singular-view feature representations.
- These singular-view features are merged via summation to create a combined multi-view representation, maintaining consistent dimensionality for downstream networks.
- ==Feature-level data augmentation== is applied by feeding both the merged multi-view representation and individual singular-view features to the actor and critic networks, using a ==SADA==-inspired weighted loss to stabilize learning.

## Results

- MAD achieved an average of **30%** higher success rates on 15 Meta-World tasks and **36%** higher on 5 ManiSkill3 tasks compared to baselines, demonstrating superior sample efficiency.
- The algorithm showed strong robustness to view reduction, maintaining consistent success rates when evaluated with only a single camera view and outperforming all baselines on individual third-person perspectives.
- MAD successfully adapted to scenarios with occluded/uninformative views and different modalities (RGBD), performing comparably to single-camera baselines despite 'overloaded' inputs, and generalizing well to RGB-Only and Depth-Only evaluations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Merging features through summation effectively combines information from multiple views while preserving feature magnitude and avoiding architectural changes for reduced inputs.
> - Applying data augmentation at the feature level, rather than on raw images, and selectively weighting augmented vs. unaugmented streams in the loss (using `alpha = 0.8` as optimal) is crucial for both sample efficiency and disentanglement.
> - The integrated approach of MAD, which simultaneously leverages all views for learning and individual views for disentanglement, addresses the limitations of baselines that prioritize one aspect, leading to more practical and robust policies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual reinforcement learning (RL) has achieved progress in various applications, from Atari games to complex real-world robotics tasks such as manipulation and autonomous flight. A fundamental challenge in these systems, particularly in robotics, is the inherent limitation of learning from 2D observations to understand a 3D environment, which impacts depth perception and control.

Multi-view approaches offer a solution to this limitation by leveraging multiple camera perspectives. These methods can merge information from various views to learn more comprehensive representations, mitigate occlusions, and potentially enhance sample efficiency in Q-learning algorithms. However, policies conditioned on multi-view representations introduce practical challenges. They can be complex to deploy due to increased sensor requirements and may exhibit fragility if one or more sensors malfunction or become unavailable during deployment.

Conversely, another line of research focuses on disentangling view representations during training to achieve policies that are robust to a reduction in available camera views. Such disentanglement aims to create lightweight policies that can generalize even when the input sensory data changes, such as from multiple cameras to a single camera.

This work, Merge And Disentanglement (MAD), positions itself at the intersection of these two research directions. It seeks to combine the benefits of multi-view learning (improved sample efficiency through merged representations) with the advantages of disentangled representations (robustness to sensor failures and flexibility in deployment). The paper references extensive prior work in data augmentation for visual RL, highlighting the use of input image transformations to improve generalization and sample efficiency. It also acknowledges existing methods for robot manipulation using multiple views, which aim to improve performance by fusing different perspectives to overcome occlusions. Furthermore, the paper draws on research in computer vision and visual RL concerning feature merging and disentanglement, noting that previous multi-feature input policies often suffer performance degradation when inputs become unavailable. MAD aims to address these identified gaps by simultaneously pursuing both merging and disentanglement without requiring specific input view order, auxiliary losses, extra forward passes, or additional learnable parameters.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method that enhances the sample efficiency of visual reinforcement learning policies for robotic manipulation by leveraging multiple camera views, while simultaneously ensuring these policies are robust to a reduction in the number of available camera views during deployment. This dual objective aims to create policies that are both effective in learning and practical for real-world application.

The motivation for merging views stems from several observations in visual RL:
- **3D World Understanding:** The inherently 3D nature of robotic manipulation tasks requires a comprehensive understanding of the environment. Learning solely from 2D observations limits a system's depth perception and overall understanding. Multiple camera views provide richer contextual information, facilitating a better approximation of the true 3D state.
- **Improved Representations:** Merging information from diverse viewpoints can lead to more robust and informative feature representations for Q-learning. These enhanced representations can, in turn, train policies that are more sample efficient, meaning they require less interaction with the environment to achieve a desired performance level.
- **Occlusion Mitigation:** Different camera angles can offer complementary perspectives, helping to overcome occlusions that might block the robot's view of critical objects or task components from a single camera.

The motivation for disentangling views addresses practical challenges associated with multi-view policies:
- **Deployment Burden:** Policies that are strictly conditioned on receiving inputs from multiple cameras can be cumbersome to deploy in varied real-world scenarios. Setting up and maintaining multiple synchronized cameras, especially in novel or constrained environments, can be logistically challenging.
- **Fragility to Sensor Failure:** Multi-view policies that do not adequately disentangle view features are susceptible to performance degradation if one or more cameras malfunction, become occluded, or are intentionally removed during deployment. This sensitivity reduces the reliability and usability of such systems.
- **Lightweight Deployment:** To enable more flexible and robust deployment, policies should be able to function effectively even with a reduced set of camera inputs, ideally down to a single view. Disentangling view representations during training is identified as a mechanism to achieve this robustness and enable lightweight deployment.

The researchers hypothesize that a method capable of both merging multi-view data for efficient learning and disentangling individual view features for robustness would overcome the limitations of existing approaches that typically prioritize one aspect over the other. Such a method would allow policies to benefit from the rich information of multiple views during training while maintaining adaptability and performance when fewer views are available during inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Merge And Disentanglement (MAD), is an actor-critic algorithm built upon Data Regularized Q-Learning (DrQ). It is designed to process multiple camera views to enhance sample efficiency while simultaneously disentangling view representations to ensure robustness during deployment with reduced camera inputs.

The methodology can be broken down into three main components:

#### 3.1. Merge Module

Given `n` input camera views, denoted as `o_m_t = (o_1_t, o_2_t, ..., o_n_t)` at time `t`, MAD processes each view independently.
1. **Shared CNN Encoder:** Each individual view `o_i_t` is passed through a single shared Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) encoder, `f_ξ`. This encoder produces a singular-view feature representation `V_i_t = f_ξ(o_i_t)`.
2. **Feature Summation:** After encoding all `n` singular views (`V_1_t, V_2_t, ..., V_n_t`), these features are merged by summation to create a combined multi-view representation `M_t = Σ V_i_t`.
The choice of feature summation as the merging method is motivated by two factors:
- **Dimensionality Consistency:** Feature summation ensures that the merged representation `M_t` and any singular-view representation `V_i_t` have the same dimensionality. This avoids the need for architectural modifications in the downstream actor and critic networks when input views are missing during deployment.
- **Magnitude Preservation:** Summation preserves the magnitude of different view features, which provides the downstream networks with an implicit signal regarding the number of views currently available.

#### 3.2. Feature-level Data Augmentation

MAD distinguishes itself from conventional visual RL data augmentation, which typically applies transformations (e.g., random cropping, color jittering) directly to input images or states. Instead, MAD performs augmentation at the *feature level*, specifically between the image encoder and the downstream actor and critic networks.
- Traditional multi-view algorithms would typically only pass the merged representation `M_t` to the actor and critic.
- MAD, however, augments these downstream inputs by including all individual singular-view feature representations (`V_i_t`) in addition to the merged representation `M_t`. This aims to improve the robustness of the actor and critic to scenarios where some camera views might be unavailable.

#### 3.3. Augmentation Strategy (Based on SADA)

Naively training on both merged multi-view features and all singular-view features can destabilize learning and decrease sample efficiency, as the downstream networks might perceive them as distinct states. To mitigate this, MAD adapts the Selective Augmentation for Data Augmentation (SADA) framework. The core idea of SADA is to selectively apply augmentation to different parts of the actor-critic update process to stabilize learning.

MAD modifies the RL loss objectives as follows:
- **Critic Update:** The target Q-value is predicted exclusively from the *unaugmented* merged feature stream (`M_t`). In contrast, the online Q-value, used for calculating the Bellman residual, is predicted from both the unaugmented merged stream (`M_t`) and the augmented singular-view feature streams (`V_i_t`). This reduces variance in the target Q-value.
- **Actor Update:** Similarly, the Q-value for policy evaluation is predicted purely from the *unaugmented* merged feature stream (`M_t`). The policy action, however, is predicted from both the unaugmented merged stream (`M_t`) and the augmented singular-view feature streams (`V_i_t`).

The modified loss functions incorporate a hyperparameter `α` to weigh the contribution of the unaugmented and augmented streams:
- **Actor Loss (L_MAD_π_ϕ):** A weighted sum of `L_UnAug_π_ϕ` (policy trained on `M_t`) and the average of `L_Aug_π_ϕ` (policy trained on each `V_i_t`).
 `L_MAD_π_ϕ(D) = α * E[ -Q_θ(M_t, π_ϕ(M_t)) ] + (1 - α) * (1/n) * Σ E[ -Q_θ(M_t, π_ϕ(V_i_t)) ]`
- **Critic Loss (L_MAD_Q_θ):** A weighted sum of `L_UnAug_Q_θ` (critic trained on `M_t`) and the average of `L_Aug_Q_θ` (critic trained on each `V_i_t`). The target Q-value `Q_θ(M_t+1, a')` always uses the unaugmented merged representation.
 `L_MAD_Q_θ(D) = α * E[ (Q_θ(M_t, a_t) - r_t - γQ_θ(M_t+1, a'))^2 ] + (1 - α) * (1/n) * Σ E[ (Q_θ(V_i_t, a_t) - r_t - γQ_θ(M_t+1, a'))^2 ]`

Setting `α = 0.5` recovers the original SADA loss formulation. Tuning `α` allows for fine-grained control over the learning process.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Backbone:** DrQ is used as the base algorithm for continuous control.
- **Encoder:** A single shared DQN CNN encoder processes all input camera views.
- **Observations:** Input observations consist of a stack of three recent RGB images per view (3x84x84).
- **Proprioception:** Proprioceptive robot state input is also provided to the actor and critic networks.
- **Image Regularization:** Identical small random shifts are applied to all input camera views at each timestep, consistent with DrQ's regularization strategy.
- **Environments:** Evaluated on 15 Meta-World v2 tasks and 5 ManiSkill3 tasks, using a consistent setup of one first-person camera and two third-person cameras.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on 20 visual RL tasks across Meta-World and ManiSkill3 benchmarks demonstrated several findings regarding the performance, robustness, and adaptability of the MAD algorithm compared to baseline methods.

#### 4.1. Robustness and Sample Efficiency

- **Overall Performance:** MAD exhibited superior sample efficiency and robustness compared to baselines (MVD, VIB, MV-MWM, and Single Camera DrQ). On Meta-World, MAD achieved an average of 30% higher success rates over 15 tasks. On ManiSkill3, it attained an average of 36% higher success rates over 5 tasks, both within the same number of environment steps. These improvements indicate a more efficient learning process.
- **Robustness to View Reduction:** When evaluated with a reduction in camera views (i.e., using only a single view input), MAD maintained consistent success rates. Specifically, it demonstrated stronger robustness on individual Third Person A and Third Person B views, outperforming all baselines, including single-camera agents trained exclusively on those views. This suggests that MAD effectively leveraged multi-view representations during training to generalize better to individual third-person perspectives.
- **First Person View:** On the First Person View, MAD achieved competitive success rates, performing similarly to the Single Camera baseline and the VIB baseline, while generally outperforming other multi-view baselines.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Baseline Failures

- **MVD (Disentanglement Baseline):** While MVD successfully disentangles representations for robustness to view reduction, it suffered from lower sample efficiency. This is attributed to its design, which involves randomly selecting individual views for encoding into shared and private representations, thereby avoiding reliance on all views simultaneously for speed.
- **VIB (Merge Baseline):** VIB showed a strong dependency on the first-person view, as its variational information bottleneck was applied only to third-person views. Consequently, it achieved high sample efficiency only when the first-person view was available, failing otherwise.
- **MV-MWM (World Model Baseline):** MV-MWM demonstrated effective disentanglement but exhibited slow learning when no expert demonstrations were provided, primarily due to its complex auxiliary objective of learning masked multi-view representations.
- **MAD's Comparative Advantages:** MAD addressed these limitations by leveraging all input views for increased learning speed while using singular-view features as augmentations for disentanglement. It is also agnostic to input perspectives (unlike VIB) and avoids the need for heavy auxiliary objectives or expert demonstrations (unlike MV-MWM).

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Component Ablations:** Experiments on 5 Meta-World hard tasks showed that MAD's integrated approach was crucial. Training naively on both merged and singular view features ("MAD - Naive Both"), training only on merged features ("MAD - Merged Only"), or training only on singular features ("MAD - Singular Only") all resulted in substantially lower success rates (MAD outperformed by 29% success rate). This indicates that each component of MAD's formulation contributes to its performance.
- **Alpha Hyperparameter Ablations:** Testing different `α` values for the loss function revealed that `α = 0.8` yielded the best performance, outperforming the original SADA formulation (`α = 0.5`) and a purely unaugmented objective (`α = 1.0`).

#### 4.4. Multi-view Merging Method Comparison

- A comparison of various merging methods (Frame Stack, Concatenation, Sum, Attention, ViT, Q-Mean) on Meta-World tasks, using two and three views, indicated that most methods achieved similar sample efficiency.
- Q-Mean, a late merging strategy, slightly underperformed compared to other methods, suggesting that early or middle merging strategies might be more effective for multi-view RL. Feature summation, as used in MAD, proved to be an effective merging strategy.

#### 4.5. Adaptability to Occluded Views

- In an experiment where two out of three cameras were made uninformative (occluded), and only one (Third Person A) was useful, MAD successfully solved the tasks with a 63% success rate on ManiSkill3. This performance was comparable to the Single Camera baseline (62%), despite MAD being "overloaded" with uninformative views, demonstrating its ability to adapt.
- MVD and VIB performed significantly worse in this occluded scenario, highlighting their limitations when views are not uniformly useful or when specific views are absent.

#### 4.6. Adaptability to Different Modalities (RGBD)

- MAD was adapted to process different modalities by summing features from RGB and Depth images (from a single camera view). When trained on RGBD inputs, MAD achieved similar sample efficiency to an RGBD baseline.
- Furthermore, MAD generalized well to RGB-Only and Depth-Only evaluations, surprisingly outperforming the dedicated Depth baseline on Depth-Only evaluation. This indicates that MAD can leverage complementary information from different modalities effectively, even enabling the training of robust depth-only policies from RGBD inputs.

#### 4.7. Camera Scalability (Appendix)

- Extended experiments showed MAD maintained its performance advantages when scaled to five camera views, outperforming baselines by 48% success rate on ManiSkill3 tasks.
- It also effectively handled different input camera resolutions by resizing them to a fixed size as a preprocessing step, maintaining performance similar to its original setup.
- Analysis of camera effectiveness indicated that the First Person view often provided the best representations, but its limited observability in some tasks underscored the value of combining it with other views for overall task success.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Merge And Disentangle (MAD) algorithm presents a method that addresses a prevalent trade-off in visual reinforcement learning for robotic manipulation: the choice between achieving high sample efficiency through multi-view integration and ensuring policy robustness in scenarios of reduced or failing sensors. By demonstrating a framework that accomplishes both concurrently, MAD contributes to the development of more practical and resilient robotic systems.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency:** By effectively merging features from multiple camera views, MAD enables the learning of richer, more comprehensive representations of the 3D environment. This leads to policies that can acquire skills more quickly, requiring fewer interactions with the environment, which is crucial for real-world robot learning where data collection can be costly and time-consuming.
- **Robustness to Sensor Degradation:** The ability of MAD to disentangle view representations means that learned policies can maintain performance even when faced with a reduction in available camera views or the failure of individual sensors. This robustness is vital for deployment in dynamic and unpredictable real-world settings where sensory inputs may vary or be partially compromised.
- **Lightweight and Flexible Deployment:** Policies trained with MAD can function with a single view input during deployment, eliminating the need for all cameras used during training to be present and operational. This flexibility simplifies the deployment process, reduces hardware requirements, and allows for adaptation to environments with varying sensor configurations.
- **Practical Algorithm Design:** MAD achieves its dual objectives without introducing additional learnable parameters, requiring auxiliary losses, or necessitating extra forward passes. This design contributes to its practicality, making it potentially easier to integrate into existing visual RL pipelines and reducing computational overhead.
- **Versatile Applicability:** The demonstrated adaptability of MAD to occluded views and its successful application to different modalities (e.g., combining RGB and Depth features) suggests a broader applicability beyond just multiple camera views. This versatility indicates that the core principles of merging and disentangling features could be beneficial across various sensory inputs and potentially enable novel ways to train robust modality-specific policies from multi-modal data.
- **Advancement in Multi-view RL:** The research establishes a strong baseline for future work in multi-view reinforcement learning by providing a framework that unifies previously separate objectives. This could foster further innovation in designing robot learning systems that are both powerful in their learning capacity and reliable in their operational deployment.

A recognized limitation of the current study is the absence of real-world experimental validation. Future work could aim to transfer MAD-trained policies to physical robots using techniques like simulation-to-real transfer or demonstration-bootstrapped methods. Furthermore, addressing the challenge of generalizing to novel, unseen viewpoints remains an area for continued research, potentially leveraging generative foundational models. Despite these limitations, the theoretical and empirical contributions of MAD provide a step towards more accessible and democratized visual reinforcement learning.
