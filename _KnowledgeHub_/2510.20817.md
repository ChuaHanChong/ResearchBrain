# KL-Regularized Reinforcement Learning is Designed to Mode Collapse

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NYU and EPFL demonstrate that KL-regularized reinforcement learning objectives inherently lead to mode collapse, proving that optimal solutions are often unimodal by design rather than due to optimization failures. They introduce Mode Anchored Reward Augmentation (MARA), a simple algorithm that modifies rewards to foster diverse, high-quality outputs across generative AI applications.

## Problem

- Diversity collapse (mode collapse) is a pervasive issue in reinforcement learning post-training of foundation models, leading to models producing similar outputs despite multiple valid solutions.
- Existing approaches to combat diversity collapse are often heuristic and address symptoms rather than the fundamental theoretical cause.
- A lack of rigorous understanding of why diversity collapse occurs inherently within KL-regularized reinforcement learning objectives.

## Method

- The authors conducted a rigorous theoretical analysis using ==variational inference== to derive the ==globally optimal target distributions== implicitly defined by ==KL-regularized RL objectives==.
- They developed ==Mode Anchored Reward Augmentation (MARA)==, a simple algorithm that redefines the ==reward function== to ensure the optimal target distribution places uniformly high mass over all high-quality samples.
- The theoretical claims and **MARA**'s efficacy were validated through didactic simulations and experiments on Large Language Models (LLMs) and ==Chemical Language Models (CLMs)==.

## Results

- Theoretical analysis revealed that optimal KL-regularized policies exhibit exponential sensitivity to even minor reward differences and merely preserve reference policy ratios for equally rewarded samples, thereby leading to mode collapse.
- MARA successfully maintained diversity and quality in LLM post-training, outperforming baselines in tasks like verifiable integer generation and creative question answering by achieving higher entropy and distinctness.
- Applied to Chemical Language Models for drug discovery, MARA significantly increased diversity of generated candidate molecules.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - KL-regularized RL objectives, under common settings, are intrinsically designed to yield unimodal optimal policies, indicating that diversity collapse is an inherent outcome rather than an optimization failure.
> - The choice between reverse and forward KL regularization does not primarily determine mode coverage; instead, the regularization strength (β), and relative magnitudes of rewards and reference probabilities are the dominant factors.
> - The proposed MARA algorithm promotes multimodality by modifying the reward function to equalize the effective reward-to-reference-probability ratio for all high-quality samples, thus directly shaping the target distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical and widely observed problem in the rapidly evolving field of **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for post-training foundation models**, particularly Large Language Models (LLMs). RL, especially through techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and its variants, has become the dominant paradigm for aligning and refining pre-trained LLMs to human preferences and specific task objectives.

However, a significant empirical observation, widely reported in recent literature (e.g., Kirk et al., 2023; Cui et al., 2025; Yang & Holtzman, 2025), is that while RL post-training demonstrably improves the quality and alignment of model outputs, it often comes at the severe cost of **diversity**. This phenomenon, often termed "mode collapse" or "entropy collapse," manifests as the model consistently producing very similar answers, even when multiple valid and diverse solutions exist. This lack of diversity is detrimental across various applications, from creative writing and open-ended conversation to scientific discovery (e.g., novel drug molecules or mathematical proofs) where exploring a wide solution space is paramount.

Existing approaches to combat diversity collapse have largely been heuristic or driven by empirical observations. These include:
- **Explicit diversity rewards:** Adding separate reward signals to encourage diverse outputs (Li et al., 2025).
- **Modifying KL regularization:** Exploring different f-divergences (Wang et al., 2023) or directly manipulating KL terms.
- **Data selection strategies:** Choosing diverse data for training (Lanchantin et al., 2025).
- **Count-based exploration bonuses:** Encouraging visits to less frequent states or generating less common outputs (Song et al., 2025).

This paper distinguishes itself by taking a **"step back to diagnose a more fundamental problem."** Instead of proposing another ad-hoc solution, it delves into the theoretical underpinnings of KL-regularized RL objectives themselves. It challenges commonly held intuitions about the "mode-seeking" nature of reverse KL and "mass-covering" nature of forward KL, particularly in the context of highly flexible generative models. The work argues that diversity collapse is not merely an optimization artifact or a quirk of training dynamics, but rather an *inherent consequence of the objective function itself* under commonly used settings. By proving that the optimal target distribution in many typical scenarios is *unimodal by construction*, the authors shift the focus from how to *train* a diverse policy to how to *define* a diverse target distribution in the first place. This theoretical reframing offers a principled pathway to designing RL objectives that naturally encourage multimodality, thereby fundamentally addressing the root cause of diversity collapse.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this research is to rigorously understand and fundamentally address the pervasive issue of diversity collapse in RL post-training of foundation models. The authors posit that current empirical efforts to mitigate this problem are often treating symptoms rather than the root cause.

The key objectives are articulated as follows:

1. **Diagnose the intrinsic nature of KL-regularized RL objectives:** The primary goal is to mathematically and empirically investigate whether the target distributions implicitly defined by standard KL-regularized RL objectives (using both reverse and forward KL divergences) are inherently designed to be diverse or to collapse into single modes.
2. **Challenge common intuitions about KL divergences:** The paper aims to re-evaluate the widely held beliefs that reverse KL leads to "mode seeking" and forward KL leads to "mass covering." It seeks to demonstrate that these intuitions may not transfer directly to the complex, flexible distributions found in modern foundation models.
3. **Derive the characteristics of optimal policies:** Through variational inference tools, the authors aim to analytically derive the functional form of the optimal policy (target distribution) for both reverse and forward KL-regularized reward maximization problems.
4. **Identify conditions for mode collapse:** Based on the derived optimal forms, the research aims to pinpoint specific conditions, particularly related to regularization strength (β), reward function characteristics, and reference policy probabilities, under which the optimal solution will be unimodal, thus leading to diversity collapse.
5. **Propose a theoretically justified algorithm for multimodality:** Leveraging the insights gained from the theoretical analysis, the authors intend to construct a simple, scalable algorithm that modifies the RL objective to explicitly optimize for a target distribution that places high probability over *all* high-quality sampling modes, thereby directly promoting diversity without requiring external diversity signals.
6. **Empirically validate the theoretical findings and proposed algorithm:** The research aims to support its theoretical claims with didactic simulations and demonstrate the practical efficacy of the proposed algorithm (MARA) across real-world, complex scenarios, including post-training Large Language Models for creative tasks and Chemical Language Models for drug discovery.

In essence, the authors are motivated to move beyond empirical fixes for diversity collapse by providing a foundational understanding of *why* it occurs in KL-regularized RL and then offering a principled, theoretically grounded solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper is a rigorous combination of **theoretical analysis** and **empirical validation**.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Analysis (Diagnosis of Mode Collapse)

The core of the theoretical approach involves analyzing KL-regularized RL through the lens of **variational inference (VI)**. This allows the authors to derive and dissect the *globally optimal target distributions* that these objectives implicitly define, rather than focusing solely on the optimization dynamics.

1. **Formulating KL-regularized RL as Distribution Matching:** The authors frame the problem as maximizing an objective function $J(\pi_\theta) = E_{\pi_\theta(y)}[R(y)] - \beta D(\pi_\theta, \pi_{ref})$, where $D(\cdot, \cdot)$ is a KL divergence and $\beta$ is the regularization coefficient.
2. **Analysis of Reverse KL Regularization:**
 - They derive the optimal solution $\pi^*$ for the reverse KL case ($D_{KL}(\pi_\theta || \pi_{ref})$) as $G_\beta(y) = \frac{1}{\zeta} \pi_{ref}(y) \exp(R(y)/\beta)$. This closed-form solution is crucial for subsequent analysis.
 - They show that maximizing this objective is equivalent to minimizing the reverse KL divergence between the current policy $\pi_\theta$ and the derived target $G_\beta$.
3. **Analysis of Forward KL Regularization:**
 - They derive the optimal solution for the forward KL case ($D_{KL}(\pi_{ref} || \pi_\theta)$) as $G_{fwd}(y) = \frac{\beta \pi_{ref}(y)}{\Lambda - R(y)}$, where $\Lambda$ is a normalization constant. Notably, this is a different family of distributions without a simple closed form.
 - They demonstrate that the gradient of this objective is *not* a forward KL gradient toward a fixed target, challenging common intuitions about its behavior.
4. **Probability Ratio Analysis:** The central theoretical tool is the derivation of the log probability ratio between any two samples ($y_1, y_2$) under the optimal reverse-KL distribution $G_\beta$: $\log \frac{G_\beta(y_1)}{G_\beta(y_2)} = \log \frac{\pi_{ref}(y_1)}{\pi_{ref}(y_2)} + \frac{1}{\beta}(R(y_1) - R(y_2))$. This elegant formula allows for direct examination of how relative probabilities are shaped by rewards, reference policy, and regularization strength.
5. **Identifying Conditions for Mode Collapse:**
 - **Equal Reference Support:** They show that with equal $\pi_{ref}$ probabilities, even small reward differences lead to *exponential* differences in optimal policy probabilities, especially with low $\beta$, driving mode collapse to the highest reward.
 - **Equal Rewards:** For tasks with verifiable rewards (e.g., math problems) where correct answers get the same reward, the probability ratio in the optimal solution is simply the ratio from $\pi_{ref}$. This means RL, by construction, *never* promotes lower-support answers, leading to collapse on high-support modes.
 - **Unequal Rewards and Supports:** They derive a condition (Equation 10) where a specific $\beta$ can balance reward differences and reference policy probabilities, indicating that $\beta$ primarily acts as a knob to trade off high-reward/low-support solutions against lower-reward/high-support solutions.

#### 3.2. Proposed Algorithm (Mode Anchored Reward Augmentation - MARA)

Based on the theoretical insights, the authors propose a novel, simple algorithm called **Mode Anchored Reward Augmentation (MARA)**.

1. **Constructing a Multimodal Target:** The core idea is to directly engineer the reward function such that the optimal target distribution places *uniformly high mass over all high-quality samples* (defined as those with reward $R(y) \ge \tau$).
2. **Augmented Reward Function:** The augmented reward $\bar{R}(y)$ is defined as:
 - $\bar{R}(y) = R(y)$ if $R(y) < \tau$ (low-reward regions remain unchanged).
 - $\bar{R}(y) = R(z) + \beta (\log \pi_{ref}(z) - \log \pi_{ref}(y))$ if $R(y) \ge \tau$, where $z$ is a fixed "anchor" sample (e.g., the highest $\pi_{ref}$ sample among high-quality samples). This augmentation effectively normalizes the reward-to-reference-probability ratio for all high-quality samples, making them equally desirable in the optimal solution.
3. **Implementation Simplicity:** The method is presented as a minimal, two-line modification to standard RL algorithms (Algorithm 1) and can be used as a drop-in replacement. An equivalent alternative (Algorithm 2) that augments both reward and reference probability is also provided for reverse KL.

#### 3.3. Empirical Validation

The theoretical claims and the efficacy of MARA are validated through experiments across three distinct settings:

1. **Didactic Simulations:** Toy categorical distribution problems are used to visually and quantitatively demonstrate the theoretical predictions about mode collapse under various $\beta$ settings and reward/reference policy configurations.
2. **Large Language Model (LLM) Post-training:**
 - **Verifiable 1-2 Task:** Training Qwen2.5-3B to generate random integers 1 or 2. This task has known correct answers with potentially different base policy likelihoods, allowing for direct assessment of diversity collapse and MARA's ability to maintain it.
 - **Creative Question Answering:** Training Qwen3-1.7B on WildChat text using a parametric reward model. This non-verifiable task evaluates MARA's performance on more complex, open-ended generation tasks against baselines like GRPO and RLOO, using metrics like n-gram diversity, semantic diversity, and distinct functional classes.
3. **Chemical Language Model (CLM) for Drug Discovery:** Applying MARA to REINVENT (a state-of-the-art RL algorithm for CLMs) on drug discovery tasks (SYNTH and ALL-AMIDE). This tests MARA's ability to generate diverse and high-quality novel molecules, a critical aspect of molecular optimization where mode collapse is highly detrimental. Metrics include "Yield" (unique high-reward molecules) and "OB100" (efficiency).

This multi-faceted approach, combining deep theoretical analysis with practical, domain-diverse empirical validation, forms a robust methodology to address the research problem.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several significant findings, both theoretical and empirical:

#### 4.1. Theoretical Findings

1. **KL Type is Not the Primary Determinant of Mode Coverage:** Contrary to common intuitions, the choice between reverse and forward KL regularization does *not* intrinsically determine mode coverage. Instead, both KL types define distinct families of optimal distributions, and mode coverage primarily depends on other factors: regularization strength ($\beta$), and the relative magnitudes of rewards ($R(y)$) and reference probabilities ($\pi_{ref}(y)$).
2. **Optimal Solutions are Often Unimodal by Construction:** A critical finding is that under commonly used RL settings (e.g., weak KL regularization with varied rewards, or any KL regularization when correct answers have identical rewards but vastly different reference policy supports), the globally optimal solution distribution is often *unimodal*. This implies that diversity collapse is not an optimization failure but a *natural, intended outcome* of correctly solving the defined RL objective.
3. **Probability Ratio Reveals Mechanisms of Collapse:**
 - **Exponential Sensitivity to Reward Differences:** When samples have similar reference probabilities ($\pi_{ref}(y_1) \approx \pi_{ref}(y_2)$), even minor differences in rewards lead to *exponential* differences in their probabilities in the optimal policy ($G_\beta(y_1)/G_\beta(y_2) = \exp((R(y_1) - R(y_2))/\beta)$). Low $\beta$ (weak regularization) dramatically exaggerates this, driving rapid collapse to the highest reward mode.
 - **Preservation of Reference Ratios for Equal Rewards:** When multiple correct answers share the same reward ($R(y_1) = R(y_2)$), their probability ratio in the optimal solution is simply their ratio in the reference policy ($G_\beta(y_1)/G_\beta(y_2) = \pi_{ref}(y_1)/\pi_{ref}(y_2)$). This proves that standard KL-regularized RL, by design, will *never* up-weight a lower-support, equally correct answer.
 - **$\beta$ as a Trade-off Knob:** The regularization coefficient $\beta$ balances the preference between higher-reward, low-support solutions and lower-reward, high-support solutions, rather than fundamentally altering the "mode-seeking" or "mass-covering" behavior.
4. **Forward KL Gradient is Not Standard:** The paper analytically shows that optimizing the forward-KL regularized objective does not yield a standard forward-KL gradient towards a fixed target distribution, complicating intuitions about its behavior.

#### 4.2. Empirical Results

1. **Didactic Simulations:** These experiments visually confirm the theoretical predictions, showing how different $\beta$ values and reward/reference configurations lead to unimodal or multimodal target distributions as predicted by the probability ratio analysis. They illustrate the extreme sensitivity to reward differences and the inability to promote low-support modes with equal rewards.
2. **LLM 1-2 Verifiable Task:**
 - Vanilla RL (both forward and reverse KL) consistently results in mode collapse, with policies learning to generate only one of the two correct answers (e.g., all "1"s) due to higher likelihood under the base policy.
 - **MARA successfully preserves diversity** (higher entropy of valid answers) while maintaining high reward (quality). The Pareto front analysis clearly shows MARA achieving better diversity at comparable quality levels than vanilla RL.
3. **Creative Question Answering (LLM):**
 - MARA significantly **outperforms baseline methods (GRPO, RLOO)** in terms of out-of-distribution reward, indicating better generalization and quality.
 - MARA also achieves **higher diversity** across most metrics (Ngram EAD, Mean Distinct) compared to baselines, demonstrating its effectiveness in complex, non-verifiable tasks. Semantic diversity was slightly lower but still competitive.
4. **Drug Discovery with Chemical Language Models (CLMs):**
 - MARA, applied as a drop-in replacement in the REINVENT algorithm, consistently leads to **higher "Yield"** (more unique high-reward molecules discovered) and **lower "OB100"** (greater efficiency in finding high-reward molecules) across challenging SYNTH and ALL-AMIDE tasks and different reward thresholds.
 - Crucially, MARA achieves these benefits **without sacrificing global diversity** (measured by IntDiv1 and #Circles), demonstrating its utility in applications where generating novel, diverse, and high-quality outputs is paramount.

In summary, the paper's main findings fundamentally reinterpret diversity collapse in KL-regularized RL as a design outcome of the objective rather than a training flaw. It then offers a simple, theoretically sound intervention (MARA) that empirically proves effective across diverse generative AI applications, addressing this core issue by directly shaping the target distribution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work carries significant implications for the field of reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of training and aligning foundation models.

1. **Fundamental Shift in Understanding Diversity Collapse:** The most significant contribution is the paradigm shift from viewing diversity collapse as an optimization problem to understanding it as a **consequence of the objective's inherent design**. By mathematically demonstrating that commonly used KL-regularized RL objectives, under typical hyperparameters, are constructed to yield unimodal optimal distributions, the authors provide a foundational diagnosis. This reframing is critical because it directs research efforts towards designing better objectives rather than merely tuning optimization procedures.
2. **Demystifying KL Divergence Intuitions:** The paper challenges deeply ingrained intuitions about "mode seeking" (reverse KL) and "mass covering" (forward KL). It clarifies that these properties are context-dependent and do not universally apply to flexible approximate distributions in RL. This theoretical precision can prevent misapplication and misinterpretation of KL regularization in future research.
3. **Principled Algorithm Design:** The proposed **Mode Anchored Reward Augmentation (MARA)** algorithm is a direct and elegant outcome of the theoretical analysis. Its simplicity (minimal code changes) and theoretical justification make it a highly impactful contribution. Instead of relying on heuristic diversity bonuses or complex objective modifications, MARA directly sculpts the target distribution to be multimodal. This offers a powerful and interpretable method for promoting diversity.
4. **Broad Applicability and Generalization:** The empirical validation across diverse domains—Large Language Models (for both verifiable and creative tasks) and Chemical Language Models (for drug discovery)—underscores MARA's versatility. This suggests that the underlying theoretical principles and the solution are not domain-specific but rather applicable to a wide range of generative AI tasks where diversity and quality are both crucial. Its success in drug discovery, a field where computational efficiency and novelty are paramount, highlights its practical value.
5. **Improved Model Capabilities:** By allowing RL-trained models to maintain diversity while achieving high quality, MARA can unlock new capabilities for foundation models. This includes:
 - **Enhanced creativity and exploration:** In LLMs, it can lead to more varied and interesting outputs for creative writing, brainstorming, and open-ended dialogue.
 - **Accelerated scientific discovery:** In domains like drug discovery and materials science, it can enable the exploration of a much wider and more diverse range of high-quality candidate molecules or designs, increasing the probability of discovering truly novel and effective solutions.
 - **Better reasoning and uncertainty quantification:** Diversity can reflect uncertainty over competing hypotheses, which is fundamental to scientific inquiry and robust decision-making.

In conclusion, this paper provides a robust theoretical explanation for a widespread practical problem in RL and offers a simple, effective, and theoretically grounded solution. Its impact will likely be felt across the rapidly expanding landscape of generative AI, pushing the boundaries of what foundation models can achieve in terms of both quality and diversity.
