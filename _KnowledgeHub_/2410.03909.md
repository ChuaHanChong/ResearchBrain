# Improving Efficiency of Sampling-based Motion Planning via Message-Passing Monte Carlo

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel sampling strategy, Message-Passing Monte Carlo (MPMC), is introduced to enhance the efficiency of sampling-based motion planning by generating low-discrepancy point sets. This approach achieves superior planning efficiency with fewer samples across various benchmarks and real-world robot tasks, backed by theoretical guarantees relating discrepancy to path optimality.

## Problem

- Sampling-based motion planners often suffer from inefficiencies due to non-uniform exploration of high-dimensional configuration spaces.
- Traditional random and quasi-Monte Carlo sampling methods provide irregular point distributions, leading to high computational costs for pathfinding.
- Existing learning-based sampling techniques typically require problem-specific conditioning, limiting their unbiased generalizability.

## Method

- ==Message-Passing Monte Carlo (MPMC)==, a ==GNN==-based method, is introduced to generate ==low-discrepancy point sets== for configuration space exploration.
- A novel training objective using ==Hickernell L2-discrepancy== is developed to enable efficient MPMC point generation in high-dimensional spaces.
- The MPMC sampling strategy is integrated into a pre-sampled PRM (**ps-PRM**) framework to demonstrate its enhanced planning efficiency.

## Results

- MPMC-generated point sets consistently exhibited **3 to 5 times** lower Hickernell L2-discrepancy than Uniform and Halton sampling in **10-dimensional** spaces.
- Across 2D mazes and OMPL benchmarks (e.g., hypercube corridors, kinematic chain), MPMC consistently achieved higher success rates with significantly fewer samples.
- The method demonstrated **3 to 8 times** higher success rates for a **5-D** UR5 robot arm planning task and was successfully deployed on physical robot hardware.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing the discrepancy of sampled points directly improves motion planning performance, leading to a tighter upper bound on the distance from an optimal path.
> - Graph Neural Networks, such as MPMC, can learn to generate high-quality, low-discrepancy point sets in high-dimensional spaces without requiring problem-specific conditioning.
> - Enhancing the fundamental sampling process can yield universal efficiency improvements across a broad range of sampling-based motion planners.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Sampling-based motion planning, exemplified by methods like Probabilistic Roadmaps (PRMs) and Rapidly-exploring Random Trees (RRTs), is a widely adopted approach for robot navigation in complex, high-dimensional spaces. Much of the existing research in this domain has focused on refining the planning algorithms themselves to improve efficiency, optimality, and ability to handle various constraints or dynamic environments.

A parallel trend involves incorporating machine learning to enhance sampling-based planners. Some approaches learn and reuse past solutions or adapt sampling distributions based on the workspace environment or specific start-goal configurations. These methods often involve biasing the sampling process with learned knowledge to accelerate convergence.

Another significant area of research is low-discrepancy constructions. These methods, largely rooted in number theory and abstract algebra (e.g., Halton, Sobol, Faure sequences, lattice rules), aim to generate point sets that uniformly cover a space, thereby improving the efficiency of Monte Carlo integration and similar applications. More recently, some work has explored optimizing permutations of existing sequences or using non-linear programming to construct low-discrepancy sets, though these often face computational limitations, especially for higher dimensions or larger point counts.

This current work departs from algorithm-centric refinements and many existing learning-based methods by focusing on the fundamental sampling process itself, aiming for a problem-agnostic and unbiased approach. It introduces a novel sampling strategy that leverages Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to generate low-discrepancy point sets, fundamentally distinct from traditional number-theoretic constructions and conditional learning-based sampling. By enhancing the uniformity of sampled points, the research positions itself to improve the foundational efficiency of a broad class of sampling-based planners without requiring prior planning examples or conditioning on environmental details.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency of sampling-based motion planning methods. These methods, while effective for high-dimensional problems, frequently encounter inefficiencies because their sampling distributions may not uniformly explore the configuration space, leading to suboptimal coverage and increased computational costs.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:
- **Inefficiencies in existing sampling:** Traditional random sampling often leads to irregular point distributions, requiring a large number of samples to adequately cover the configuration space and find a valid path. Quasi-Monte Carlo methods, while offering better uniformity, can still suffer from correlation issues in higher dimensions.
- **Fundamental building block:** The authors identify the core sampling process as a critical bottleneck. Instead of developing more complex planning algorithms, they aim to improve this fundamental step, which could then universally benefit a wide array of sampling-based planners.
- **Need for unbiased and generalizable sampling:** Many existing learning-based sampling techniques rely on conditioning on workspace descriptions, steer functions, past examples, or start/goal information. The authors seek a deterministic sampling strategy that does not depend on such prior knowledge or problem-specific conditioning, making it more versatile.
- **Leveraging novel GNN capabilities:** The recent development of Message-Passing Monte Carlo (MPMC), a Graph Neural Network (GNN) architecture capable of generating point sets with exceptionally low discrepancy, presents an opportunity to address the sampling uniformity challenge in motion planning.
- **Addressing high-dimensionality challenges:** High-dimensional spaces exacerbate the problems of irregular sampling. The authors specifically aim to adapt MPMC to effectively generate uniform point sets in these challenging scenarios, which are common in robotics.
- **Theoretical justification:** The work is motivated by the desire to provide a rigorous theoretical link between low-discrepancy sampling and improved motion planning performance, specifically by showing how reduced discrepancy can lead to tighter bounds on the distance from an optimal path.

The key objectives are summarized by the authors' contributions:
- Introduce MPMC neural network point set generation as a novel, unbiased sampling technique in motion planning.
- Propose a new training objective for MPMC tailored to high-dimensional configuration spaces.
- Provide theoretical justification connecting MPMC's training objective to a tighter upper bound on the distance from the optimal path.
- Demonstrate superior planning efficiency compared to current standard sampling approaches across various PRM benchmarks.
- Validate the practical applicability of the sampling technique on a real-world robot arm.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed methodology involves integrating Message-Passing Monte Carlo (MPMC) to generate low-discrepancy point sets for sampling-based motion planning.

#### 3.1. Message-Passing Monte Carlo (MPMC) Sampling

MPMC, as described in, utilizes Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) to produce point sets that uniformly cover a space, typically a unit hypercube. The uniformity of these point sets is quantified using discrepancy measures. The primary measure employed is the $L_p$-discrepancy, defined as the $L_p$-norm of the difference between the empirical distribution of points and the uniform distribution. Specifically, the $L_2$-discrepancy is used as the training loss for the MPMC model due to its differentiable closed-form solution (Warnock's formula).

The MPMC model functions by taking random input points, encoding them into a high-dimensional representation, passing these representations through a deep GNN (where the graph is constructed based on nearest neighbors of the initial input points), and finally decoding the node-wise output representations back into the unit hypercube.

#### 3.2. Fast MPMC Training in Higher Dimensions

For higher-dimensional spaces, the standard $L_2$-discrepancy can become less effective at distinguishing uniformly distributed points from random ones. To address this, the Hickernell $L_2$-discrepancy ($D_{H,2}$) is employed. This measure sums over the $L_2$-discrepancies of all possible k-dimensional projections of the point set. A direct computation of $D_{H,2}$ is computationally intensive, scaling as $O((2^d - 1)N^2d)$. To mitigate this, the authors leverage a closed-form solution for the Hickernell $L_2$-discrepancy, which reduces the computational complexity to $O(N^2d)$, allowing for efficient training in high dimensions by minimizing discrepancy across all projections. This constitutes a novel training objective tailored for high-dimensional configuration spaces.

#### 3.3. Motion Planning via Probabilistic Road Maps (ps-PRM)

To evaluate the efficiency of their sampling technique, the authors employ a specific instantiation of the Probabilistic Roadmap (PRM) algorithm, termed "pre-sampled PRM" (ps-PRM). This algorithm operates as follows:
1. **Sampling:** N points are sampled from the entire configuration space using the chosen sampling method (Uniform, Halton, Sobol, or MPMC).
2. **Pruning:** Invalid samples (those in collision) are removed, retaining only valid milestones.
3. **Graph Construction:** The valid milestones are combined with the start and goal configurations to form the set of vertices (V). For each vertex, nearby nodes within a defined radius ($r_N$) are identified.
4. **Edge Creation:** Collision-free paths between nearby nodes are checked, and bidirectional edges are added to the graph (E) if a path is clear.
5. **Pathfinding:** A shortest path from the start to the goal is sought using A* algorithm on the constructed roadmap.

This ps-PRM framework allows for direct assessment of sampling efficiency based on the success rate of finding a path given a fixed number of samples, as it isolates the sampling process from iterative roadmap growth strategies.

#### 3.4. Theoretical Guarantee

The methodology includes a theoretical justification for minimizing discrepancy in motion planning. This is established by relating discrepancy to dispersion, another measure of point set uniformity. Dispersion ($D_p$) quantifies the maximum distance from any point in the space to the nearest sample point. A known inequality links $D_\infty$ dispersion to $L_\infty$ discrepancy: $D_\infty \leq L_\infty^{1/d}$.

Building on previous work, a deterministic sampling guarantee for PRM is established: assuming mild conditions (e.g., feasible path $\delta$-clearance) and choosing a connection radius $r_N$ proportional to $L_\infty^{1/d}$, it is possible to guarantee that the cost of the returned path is within a factor of $\frac{1}{\alpha-1}$ of the optimal $\delta$-clear path. This implies that a low-discrepancy point set directly contributes to finding paths that are closer to optimal. The MPMC points are generated using Hickernell $L_2$-discrepancy, and while the theoretical guarantee is framed in terms of $L_\infty$-discrepancy, empirical evidence suggests a close relationship between $L_2$ and $L_\infty$ discrepancies for moderate numbers of points.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation compares MPMC sampling against Uniform, Halton, and Sobol (for 2D maze only) sampling techniques across various simulated environments and a physical robot task. Performance is assessed based on success rate in finding a path for a given number of sampled points.

#### 4.1. Discrepancy Comparison

Initial evaluation in a 10-dimensional space demonstrated that MPMC-generated point sets consistently achieve substantially lower Hickernell $L_2$-discrepancy compared to Uniform and Halton sampling. MPMC points reached discrepancies close to 3 times lower than Halton and up to 5 times lower than Uniform sampling for larger point sets (e.g., N=1024), indicating superior spatial uniformity. Halton sequences showed only a marginal advantage over Uniform sampling, and sometimes performed worse for smaller N.

#### 4.2. 2-D Maze Experiments

Experiments across three levels of 2-D mazes, designed with increasing difficulty, yielded the following results (Figure 5):
- **MPMC Superiority:** MPMC consistently exhibited the best performance per sampled point set size across all three levels, with only two minor exceptions where Halton or Sobol slightly outperformed it by a small margin.
- **Efficiency on Harder Levels:** On the most challenging Level 3 maze, MPMC achieved close to 90% success with 256 points, a performance level that required Halton and Sobol twice as many points and was unattainable by Uniform sampling even with 4 times that number.
- **Relative Performance:** Uniform sampling was considerably less efficient, often requiring at least four times the points of low-discrepancy methods to reach similar success rates. Sobol and Halton sequences performed comparably overall but frequently fell short of MPMC's success rates by significant margins.

#### 4.3. OMPL and UR5 Benchmarks

The benchmarks included SE(3) rigid body puzzles (Cubicle, Twistycool), d-D hypercube corridors (d=2, 3, 10), a 10-D kinematic chain, and a 5-D UR5 robot arm task (Table 1):
- **SE(3) Puzzles (Cubicle, Twistycool):** MPMC generally achieved the best performance, though with marginal advantages. This was attributed to the uniform sampling of the SO(3) quaternion component across all methods.
- **Hypercube Corridors ($R^2$, $R^3$):** In 2D and 3D hypercube scenarios, MPMC significantly outperformed competitors. For example, with 128 and 256 points, MPMC solved approximately twice as many runs as its best competitor. With larger point sets, MPMC maintained a success rate advantage of around 50%.
- **Higher-Dimensional Hypercube ($R^{10}$):** In 10 dimensions, MPMC maintained its top-performing status. With 512 points, MPMC achieved an 80% success rate, significantly higher than Halton (56%) and Uniform (62%).
- **10-D Kinematic Chain:** MPMC showed notable performance gains, reaching a 24% success rate with 256 samples, compared to 12% for Uniform and 14% for Halton.
- **UR5 Robot Arm ($R^5$):** In the realistic 5-D UR5 planning task, MPMC consistently surpassed Uniform and Halton sampling, demonstrating 3 to 8 times higher success rates across various sample counts.
- **Real-world Deployment:** The approach was successfully demonstrated on physical UR5 robot hardware.

Across multiple benchmarks, MPMC consistently demonstrated superior planning efficiency, often achieving higher success rates with fewer sampled points compared to Uniform, Halton, and Sobol methods, particularly in higher-dimensional and complex environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the efficiency of sampling-based motion planning by targeting the foundational sampling process.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**
- **Novel Sampling Paradigm:** The work introduces the first application of Message-Passing Monte Carlo (MPMC) neural networks for point set generation in motion planning. This represents a novel, unbiased, and deterministic approach to sampling, departing from traditional number-theoretic methods and prior learning-based techniques that often require problem-specific conditioning.
- **Enhanced High-Dimensional Performance:** By proposing a novel training objective based on the Hickernell $L_2$-discrepancy with an efficient closed-form solution, the method is specifically tailored for generating high-quality, low-discrepancy point sets in high-dimensional configuration spaces. This directly addresses a critical challenge in robotic motion planning.
- **Theoretical Grounding:** The research provides rigorous theoretical justification, linking the minimization of discrepancy (specifically, via MPMC's training objective) to a tighter upper bound on the distance from an optimal path. This strengthens the scientific basis for using low-discrepancy sampling in motion planning.
- **Empirical Superiority:** Extensive experimental results across various PRM benchmarks, including 2D mazes, OMPL scenarios, and a real-world UR5 robot arm task, consistently demonstrate that the MPMC-based sampling strategy outperforms traditional uniform sampling and other quasi-Monte Carlo methods like Halton and Sobol sequences in terms of planning efficiency and success rates, often requiring significantly fewer samples.
- **Practical Applicability:** The successful demonstration on a physical UR5 robot arm highlights the method's potential for practical deployment in real-world robotic systems, offering a path to more efficient and reliable robot operations.

**Potential Impact:**
The MPMC-based sampling strategy has the potential to broadly impact the field of robotics and motion planning. By providing a more efficient way to explore configuration spaces, it could lead to:
- **Faster Planning:** Reduced sample requirements directly translate to faster planning times, which is critical for real-time applications and dynamic environments.
- **Improved Success Rates:** Higher success rates with fewer samples mean more reliable pathfinding, especially in complex or constrained environments where current methods struggle.
- **Broader Applicability:** As a general-purpose sampling technique, MPMC can be seamlessly integrated into existing and future sampling-based planners (e.g., RRTs, FMT*), potentially enhancing their performance without requiring extensive modifications to the core algorithms.
- **Reduced Computational Resources:** Lower sample counts can reduce the computational overhead associated with collision checking and graph construction, which are major costs in motion planning.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
One identified limitation is the current requirement to retrain the MPMC model for each specific number of points ($N$) and dimensionality ($d$). This can be computationally intensive, particularly in applications with varying configuration space requirements. Future research aims to address this by:
- Developing neural network architectures and training procedures that allow for generalization across different $N$ and $d$ values (e.g., inductive graph learning for varying node counts).
- Exploring methods for transforming large MPMC point sets into low-discrepancy sequences to support iterative or variable-N planning.
- Utilizing canonical projections of high-dimensional MPMC points for lower-dimensional problems.

Further research will also explore integrating MPMC into more sophisticated sampling-based planners beyond the PRM framework and adapting the technique for compute-critical applications requiring real-time performance, such as acrobatic flight or high-speed driving, by extending MPMC to non-Euclidean manifolds.
