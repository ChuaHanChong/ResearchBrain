# CogDriver: Integrating Cognitive Inertia for Temporally Coherent Planning in Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> A new framework named CogDriver introduces "cognitive inertia" into autonomous driving agents, enabling temporally coherent planning by learning persistent intent from narrative-rich vision-language-action data. This approach yields a 22% relative improvement in Driving Score and a 63% increase in Success Rate on the Bench2Drive closed-loop benchmark, along with an L2 distance of 0.34 meters on nuScenes for open-loop planning.

## Problem

- Current end-to-end autonomous driving systems operate as "cognitively stateless agents," processing environmental snapshots in isolation, leading to erratic decision jitter and fragmented internal representations.
- Existing systems struggle with complex, multi-step maneuvers due to a lack of continuous understanding and persistent intentions, unlike human drivers.
- Available language-enhanced datasets often provide atomic, snapshot-based rationales, lacking the "continuous why" or evolving causal narratives needed to learn temporal coherence.

## Method

- The **CogDriver** framework introduces ==CogDriver-Data==, a suite of large-scale VLA datasets with narrative annotations capturing persistent intent and causal reasoning, generated by a Multi-View Spatiotemporal MLLM.
- The CogDriver-Agent is an end-to-end architecture that instills ==cognitive inertia== by integrating spatiotemporal perception with language-conditioned planning.
- A Temporal Coherence Module (TCM) within the agent maintains a dynamic, memory-efficient representation of the world state, enabling robust long-range reasoning through geometric propagation, motion-conditioned refinement, and state reconciliation.

## Results

- Achieved a Driving Score of 78.21 and a Success Rate of **56.93%** on the Bench2Drive closed-loop planning task, representing **22%** and **63%** relative improvements, respectively, over previous state-of-the-art methods.
- Demonstrated an average L2 distance of **0.34 m** and an infraction rate of **3.18%** on the nuScenes open-loop trajectory planning task, establishing new competitive benchmarks.
- Showed substantial performance gains in Driving Visual Question Answering, with a **37.6%** relative improvement in CIDEr on CogDriver-nuScenes and consistent gains across other multimodal reasoning metrics, validating enhanced understanding and reasoning capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling "cognitive inertia" through continuous narrative annotations and dedicated memory modules can significantly improve temporal coherence and reduce decision jitter in autonomous driving.
> - Learning from vision-language-action data that captures the evolving causal narrative of driving intent is crucial for agents to form stable internal representations and execute complex, multi-step maneuvers.
> - A hierarchical architecture that combines multi-view spatiotemporal perception with a frozen VLM and trainable adapters can effectively integrate rich environmental context with high-level language instructions for coherent trajectory planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving research has made advancements in perception and prediction, with increasing interest in end-to-end systems and the integration of large vision-language models (VLMs). Much of the existing literature focuses on "world models" designed to predict future states, often in pixel or latent space. However, a limitation identified in current end-to-end systems is their operation as "cognitively stateless agents." These agents process each environmental snapshot in isolation, lacking a continuous understanding or "cognitive inertia"—the persistence of intentions that underlies stable human cognition. This leads to issues such as erratic decision jitter and difficulty in executing complex, multi-step maneuvers.

Existing language-enhanced datasets for autonomous driving, such as BDD-X and DRAMA, typically provide atomic, snapshot-based rationales. While more recent datasets like DriveLM and CoVLA offer continuous trajectories, they are described as lacking a corresponding "continuous why," meaning the evolving causal narrative that connects decisions over time. Consequently, these datasets do not sufficiently capture the temporal coherence required to learn cognitive inertia. Similarly, current multimodal large language models (MLLMs) applied to driving tasks often function as stimulus-response mechanisms, failing to model the causal relationships within dynamic scenes necessary for explainable decision-making.

This work addresses these identified gaps by proposing a framework that explicitly instills cognitive inertia and temporal coherence into autonomous driving agents. It aims to move beyond reactive prediction to enable agents that develop stable internal representations, perform long-term planning, and exhibit human-like reasoning and decision consistency. By focusing on learning persistent intent and causal dynamics from narrative annotations, CogDriver seeks to enable a paradigm shift from stateless, reactive models to cognitively coherent agents capable of more reliable and trustworthy operation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop autonomous agents capable of temporally coherent planning, addressing the identified deficiency of "cognitive inertia" in current vision-language models (VLMs). The authors argue that existing autonomous driving systems, particularly those relying on VLMs, operate on isolated snapshots of the environment, resulting in fragmented internal representations. This "cognitively stateless" operation hinders the formation of a continuous understanding, leading to undesirable behaviors such as erratic decision jitter and the inability to execute complex, multi-step maneuvers reliably.

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that human drivers exhibit stable, multi-step planning and commitment to chosen strategies, guided by a coherent understanding of the environment over time. For instance, a human driver planning an overtake maneuver maintains the intent to overtake, adapting the specific execution based on evolving traffic conditions, rather than abandoning and re-evaluating the entire maneuver repeatedly. The absence of such "cognitive inertia" in autonomous systems can lead to unpredictable and potentially unsafe driving.

Specifically, the key objectives are:
1. **Develop a mechanism to instill cognitive inertia:** The core goal is to enable an autonomous agent to form and maintain a stable internal representation of the world, fostering consistent decision-making and continuous understanding across temporal sequences.
2. **Create a dataset for learning temporal dynamics and persistent intent:** To supervise the learning of cognitive inertia, there is a need for large-scale vision-language-action (VLA) datasets featuring narrative annotations that capture the "story" of persistent driving intent, rather than disconnected, per-frame rationales.
3. **Design an agent architecture capable of maintaining a stable internal state:** This involves developing an architecture that can process multi-view, temporal data and incorporate memory mechanisms to build and update a coherent world model.
4. **Promote decision coherence through explicit learning:** The system should be trained using methods that explicitly teach the model to maintain consistency in its decisions and reasoning over time.
5. **Validate the effectiveness in long-term decision-making and imitation accuracy:** The developed framework and agent should demonstrate improvements in metrics related to both complex, closed-loop driving scenarios and open-loop trajectory planning, with an emphasis on reduced decision jitter and successful execution of long-term plans.

In summary, the research aims to address a fundamental limitation in autonomous driving—the lack of temporal coherence and cognitive inertia—by introducing a novel data generation paradigm and an agent architecture designed to learn and maintain stable, human-like intentions and reasoning processes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CogDriver framework consists of two primary components: CogDriver-Data, a novel dataset suite, and CogDriver-Agent, an autonomous driving agent architecture.

#### 3.1. CogDriver-Data

CogDriver-Data comprises two large-scale VLA datasets, CogDriver-nuScenes and CogDriver-Bench2Drive, designed to provide narrative-rich annotations that capture persistent intent, causal reasoning, and temporally coherent actions. The data annotation pipeline is structured to instill spatiotemporal and logical consistency.

- **Multi-View Spatiotemporal MLLM (MVST-MLLM):** This novel architecture serves as the annotator. Unlike models processing static images or single video streams, the MVST-MLLM's vision encoder processes multi-view streams concurrently. It utilizes a hierarchy of Conv3D and window attention layers to extract and fuse features across both space (all camera views) and time. This holistic perception enables reasoning about complex dynamic events by correlating information from multiple viewpoints over time.
- **Cognitive Inertia Injection:** This mechanism enforces temporal coherence during annotation. The MLLM is conditioned on a structured prompt containing high-level rules and tasks. The model is trained to generate a single, continuous narrative that explains an entire temporal sequence of visual inputs, rather than disconnected per-frame descriptions. This process aligns static principles with dynamic visual evidence, fostering causally-linked explanations.
- **Future History Alignment:** To ensure physical plausibility and consistency, the generated narrative is rigorously verified against the ground-truth vehicle trajectory.
- **Human-Centric Reasoning Priors:** Explicit prompts regarding driving rules, visual reasoning principles, and scenario-specific heuristics are incorporated to guide the captioning process towards human-like thought processes, capturing high-level explanations and intention recognition.
- **Scene-Aware Grounding:** To combat model hallucination, verifiable data (map context, vehicle dynamics, trajectory profiles, surrounding agent states, traffic signals) is systematically extracted from driving logs and structured into a dedicated prompt component. This forces the MLLM to condition its output on objective ground truth, reducing factual errors.
- **Narrative Generation:** The MVST-MLLM (a 72B model) is conditioned on both structured prompts from scene-aware grounding and expert driving principles, as well as multi-view, multi-frame video (temporal windows of 5 frames). This allows the model to perceive state transitions and generate narrative fragments explaining current actions by linking them to past observations and future goals.

#### 3.2. CogDriver-Agent

CogDriver-Agent is an end-to-end framework engineered to instill cognitive inertia by integrating spatiotemporal perception with language-conditioned planning. The architecture employs a hierarchical vision backbone to distill multi-view inputs into spatiotemporal tokens. These tokens are then projected into the embedding space of a frozen VLM, where they are fused with historical state information and natural language instructions. Lightweight, trainable adapters steer the VLM core to generate coherent trajectories.

- **Temporal Coherence Module (TCM):** This central module maintains a dynamic, memory-efficient representation of the world state to enable robust long-range reasoning. It operates in three stages:
 1. **Geometric Propagation:** Historical 3D object queries are geometrically warped into the current frame's coordinate system using ego-motion transformations, providing a physical prior for initial state estimates.
 2. **Motion-Conditioned State Refinement:** A motion-aware normalization scheme refines propagated states. Conditional affine transformation coefficients (α and β) are parameterized as a function of the full motion context (ego-motion, velocity, time difference). These coefficients modulate positional embeddings and propagated context features, allowing feature-level compensation for object dynamics or occlusions.
 3. **State Reconciliation and Fusion:** Refined memory queries (temporal prior) are concatenated with new perception queries (current frame evidence). A self-attention mechanism reconciles historical beliefs with new observations, updating tracks and suppressing redundant detections. Cross-attention grounds these reconciled queries into current visual evidence, with modulated positional embeddings providing explicit spatial guidance for object localization and state updates.
- **Training Objectives:** A composite loss function optimizes 3D object detection and structured scene understanding:
 - **Perception Loss ($L_{pc}$):** Combines categorical classification (Focal Loss) and spatial localization (L1 loss) for 3D bounding boxes, along with similar losses for lane and road structure analysis.
 - **Language Loss ($L_{ce}$):** Uses auto-regressive cross-entropy loss for the large language model.
 - **Total Loss ($L_{total}$):** Aggregates $L_{pc}$ and $L_{ce}$.
- **Implementation Details:** EVA-02-L serves as the vision encoder, pretrained via masked image modeling with knowledge distillation from CLIP. LLaVA v1.5 is the base VLM. Training is performed in two stages:
 1. **Vision-Language Alignment:** The vision encoder and lightweight adapters are trained to generate ground-truth narrative explanations from multi-view video and structured prompts using CogDriver-Data.
 2. **End-to-End VLA Fine-tuning:** The vision encoder and adapters are further fine-tuned, with the LLM core frozen, to jointly predict narratives and future trajectory waypoints. AdamW optimizer is used with differentiated learning rates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to validate CogDriver's paradigm in enhancing temporal coherence and cognitive inertia in autonomous driving agents.

#### 4.1. Closed-Loop Trajectory Planning Task

Evaluations on the Bench2Drive benchmark for closed-loop trajectory planning demonstrated that CogDriver-Agent achieved a Driving Score of 78.21 and a Success Rate of 56.93%. These represent a 22% and 63% relative improvement, respectively, over previous state-of-the-art methods. The model also achieved competitive results in open-loop Avg. L2 score (0.63 vs. 0.62 for the best baseline) and Efficiency (169.52 vs. 170.21). These results indicate improved long-term planning success and reduced decision jitter.

#### 4.2. Open-Loop Trajectory Planning Task

On the nuScenes dataset, CogDriver-Agent achieved an average L2 distance of 0.34 m, outperforming several baselines including BEV-Planner (0.55 m) and DriveVLM (0.40 m). The collision rate (CR) was 0.40%, which was slightly higher than the best baseline (Senna: 0.12%) but lower than most others. The infraction rate (IR) was 3.18%, establishing a new state-of-the-art compared to methods like BEV-Planner (4.26%) and VAD-Base (3.82%). These results suggest superior or competitive performance in both safety and trajectory accuracy.

#### 4.3. Driving Visual Question Answering Task

CogDriver-Agent showed substantial performance improvements across various metrics on both CogDriver-nuScenes and CogDriver-Bench2Drive benchmarks when compared to state-of-the-art open-source MLLMs.
- **CogDriver-nuScenes:** Achieved a 37.6% relative improvement in CIDEr, 100.1% in BLEU-1, and 224.0% in BLEU-4 scores.
- **CogDriver-Bench2Drive:** Demonstrated 9.0% improvement in CIDEr, 53.6% in BLEU-1, and 210.8% in BLEU-4.
Consistent gains were also observed in the ROUGE-L metric across both datasets, validating enhanced multimodal reasoning capabilities.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Analysis on Different Language Components:** The full configuration of language components yielded the best performance in VQA (51.54 BLEU-1). Environment context provided a 7.6% boost in VQA BLEU-1. Dynamic and static object descriptions were critical for safety metrics, achieving low collision (0.37) and intersection rates (3.08). Trajectory prediction (0.34 L2 error) remained stable across configurations.
- **Effectiveness on Temporal Memory Module (TCM):** The introduction of the sparse temporal memory module led to improvements in open-loop driving metrics: L2 distance decreased from 0.38 to 0.34, collision rate from 0.44 to 0.40, and intersection rate from 3.65 to 3.18. While VQA BLEU-1 and precision slightly reduced, recall improved, indicating enhanced sensitivity to temporal information.
- **Inference Efficiency and Throughput:** CogDriver-Agent achieved significantly higher throughput on a single NVIDIA A800 GPU compared to Qwen2.5VL 32B, with 3410.81 input tokens/s and 391.43 output tokens/s, indicating suitability for real-time applications.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

Qualitative analysis demonstrated decision coherence and causal cognitive coherence. In a 'Left Turn' scenario, the agent consistently maintained the high-level plan across frames, and its underlying rationale evolved from reacting to a 'car ahead' to anticipating an 'upcoming junction'. Similar strategic path planning was observed in an adverse weather scenario. This contrasts with stateless models that might remain fixated on immediate visual cues without forming a coherent, evolving causal narrative.

#### 4.6. Generalizability on OmniDrive Dataset

CogDriver-Agent established new benchmarks across all evaluation metrics on the OmniDrive dataset, achieving a CIDEr score of 86.67 (3.0% improvement over LLaVa-next 72B) and a BLEU-1 score of 36.40 (7.1% gain). The BLEU-4 score showed a 42.4% improvement. The model also surpassed larger 72B parameter models in several metrics, indicating its architectural innovations provide superior results irrespective of model scale.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CogDriver framework represents a methodological advancement in autonomous driving by directly addressing the issue of cognitive inertia and temporal coherence. The introduction of CogDriver-Data, with its narrative annotations capturing persistent intent and causal reasoning, provides a novel supervisory signal for training more sophisticated autonomous agents. This data bridges a gap in existing datasets that often treat driving as a series of disconnected moments.

The CogDriver-Agent, with its Temporal Coherence Module, demonstrates a concrete approach to building a stable internal representation, enabling the agent to maintain consistent intentions and plans over time. This architectural innovation moves beyond purely reactive decision-making by integrating memory-efficient mechanisms for object permanence and coherent perception.

The experimental results indicate substantial improvements across key performance indicators, including a 22% increase in closed-loop Driving Score and a 63% increase in Success Rate on Bench2Drive, alongside reductions in L2 error and infraction rates on nuScenes. These quantitative gains, coupled with qualitative demonstrations of evolving, coherent reasoning, suggest that the agent is developing a more reliable understanding of dynamic driving scenarios.

The significance of this work lies in its potential to enhance the reliability, safety, and interpretability of autonomous driving systems. By reducing decision jitter and enabling the execution of complex, long-term plans, CogDriver could contribute to autonomous vehicles that are more predictable and trustworthy in real-world environments. The framework's ability to generate context-aware and explainable driving decisions also contributes to the development of more transparent AI systems, which is crucial for regulatory acceptance and public confidence. The observed improvements in generalizability across different datasets further suggest the robustness of the proposed paradigm.
