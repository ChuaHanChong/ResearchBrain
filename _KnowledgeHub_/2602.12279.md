# UniT: Unified Multimodal Chain-of-Thought Test-time Scaling

> [!summary] Summary
> The UniT framework, developed by Meta Superintelligence Labs and Stanford University, introduces iterative reasoning and refinement for unified multimodal models. It extends chain-of-thought test-time scaling to the multimodal domain, yielding an average 10.34% improvement in compositional generation tasks and a 225.19% increase in human preference for multi-turn editing, while proving 2.5 times more compute-efficient than parallel sampling.

## Problem

- Existing unified multimodal models operate in a single-pass mode, lacking inherent mechanisms for self-evaluation, reflection, or iterative refinement of outputs.
- This single-pass limitation hinders performance on complex multimodal tasks that inherently require multi-step reasoning and self-correction, such as compositional generation or multi-turn editing.
- The systematic application of iterative, sequential test-time scaling, which has benefited text-only models, was not yet extended to unified multimodal models.

## Method

- An ==agentic framework== is employed to automatically synthesize high-quality ==multimodal chain-of-thought trajectories==, encompassing prompt generation, initial image creation, iterative reflection by a VLM, and refinement by an image editing model.
- These synthesized multi-round trajectories are used to fine-tune a unified multimodal model (==Bagel==), enabling it to internalize complex multimodal reasoning, verification, and refinement patterns.
- At inference time, the single trained **UniT** model autonomously performs all multi-round planning, generation, reflection, and refinement, guided by techniques like ==Nested Classifier-Free Guidance==.

## Results

- UniT models demonstrate emergent generalization, performing effectively on inference chains averaging **4.7 rounds** after training on shorter **3.6-round trajectories**.
- Sequential chain-of-thought scaling consistently outperforms parallel sampling, achieving better performance across tasks (OneIG-Bench, CompBench, ImgEdit, MIRA) with **2.5× less computational cost**.
- The framework yields substantial improvements in multimodal tasks, including a **10.34%** alignment increase on OneIG-Bench, a **225.19%** human preference improvement for multi-turn editing, and a **53.33%** gain in out-of-distribution visual reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Sequential chain-of-thought reasoning is a more scalable and computationally efficient test-time scaling strategy for multimodal tasks compared to parallel best-of-N sampling.
> - Unified multimodal models can be trained to internalize cognitive behaviors (verification, subgoal decomposition, content memory) that enable iterative self-correction and multi-round reasoning.
> - Models trained on shorter reasoning trajectories can effectively generalize and extrapolate to longer, more complex inference chains at test time, indicating an emergent problem-solving capability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements in multimodal models, which aim to process and generate information across various modalities such as vision and language. A subset of these, unified multimodal models, integrates both understanding and generation capabilities within a single architecture, offering potential for improved cross-modal grounding and continuous contextual tracking compared to modular pipelines. However, a prevailing limitation in existing unified models is their typical operation in a single-pass mode, producing outputs without explicit mechanisms for iterative evaluation, reflection, or refinement. This single-pass limitation restricts their performance on complex tasks requiring multi-step reasoning, such as compositional generation, multi-turn editing, and intricate visual reasoning.

Concurrently, a paradigm known as test-time scaling (TTS) has emerged in the domain of large language models (LLMs). TTS involves allocating additional computational resources during inference to enable iterative reasoning, verification, and refinement, leading to substantial performance gains in tasks like mathematics, coding, and logic. Early explorations of multimodal chain-of-thought have shown benefits for single-round visual understanding and generation. However, extending the TTS paradigm to unified multimodal models, which integrate diverse capabilities like image generation, vision-language verification, and image editing within a single coherent framework, has remained an open challenge.

This work addresses this gap by proposing a unified framework that enables iterative reasoning and self-correction in multimodal models, thereby bridging the established benefits of test-time scaling from text-only domains to unified multimodal architectures. It aims to integrate capabilities that were previously scattered across specialized models into a single, cohesive system capable of multi-round multimodal chain-of-thought reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable scalable multimodal inference that allows unified models to iteratively generate, reflect upon, and refine their outputs. This objective is motivated by several observations and challenges:

1. **Limitations of Single-Pass Unified Multimodal Models**: Existing unified models, despite their ability to handle multiple modalities, typically operate in a single inference pass. This mode of operation is insufficient for complex tasks that inherently require iterative decomposition of instructions, verification of intermediate results, and corrective actions. Tasks involving complex spatial compositions, multiple interacting objects, or evolving instructions often necessitate a multi-step approach.
2. **Success of Test-Time Scaling in Language Models**: The demonstrated effectiveness of test-time scaling (TTS) in large language models for complex reasoning tasks highlights the potential benefits of allocating additional inference compute for iterative processes. This success suggests that a similar approach could enhance multimodal models if appropriately adapted.
3. **Need for Integrated Multimodal Reasoning**: Current multimodal tasks requiring iterative refinement often rely on a collection of specialized models (e.g., separate models for image generation, vision-language verification, and image editing). This modular approach introduces complexities such as inter-model communication overhead and difficulties in maintaining continuous multimodal context. There is a motivation to develop a unified framework where a single model can perform all these functions coherently.
4. **Eliciting Cognitive Behaviors**: The research aims to induce specific cognitive behaviors in unified multimodal models, including:
 - **Verification**: The ability to evaluate generated outputs against given instructions to identify discrepancies.
 - **Subgoal Decomposition**: The capability to break down complex, multi-faceted instructions into a sequence of manageable, executable steps.
 - **Content Memory**: The capacity to maintain an understanding of visual content and contextual information across multiple rounds of interaction and refinement.
 These behaviors are deemed crucial for effective iterative reasoning and problem-solving in multimodal domains.

In summary, the motivation stems from the recognition that current unified multimodal models are constrained by their single-pass nature, while iterative reasoning has proven effective in unimodal contexts. The objective is to develop a unified framework that systematically integrates data synthesis, model training, and inference mechanisms to facilitate scalable, iterative, and self-correcting multimodal inference, thereby enabling emergent cognitive behaviors within a single model architecture.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The UniT framework for multimodal chain-of-thought test-time scaling integrates three core components: agentic data synthesis, unified model training, and flexible test-time inference.

#### 3.1. Multimodal Chain-of-Thought Data Synthesis

An automated agentic pipeline is developed to synthesize multi-round multimodal chain-of-thought trajectories. This pipeline iteratively generates and refines images based on user prompts, inducing specific cognitive behaviors:

1. **Prompt Generation**: Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E generates 20,000 diverse prompts covering compositional attributes, spatial relations, and complex multimodal tasks, based on T2I-CoReBench.
2. **Initial Generation**: Flux Pro produces initial images from these prompts. For complex prompts, Qwen3-VL decomposes the prompt into subgoals and guides the initial generation for the first step.
3. **Reflection (Verification and Planning)**: Qwen3-VL acts as a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to evaluate whether the generated image satisfies the prompt. If not, it generates explicit chain-of-thought reasoning by:
 - Identifying deficiencies in the current image.
 - Planning concrete improvements (subgoal decomposition).
 - Specifying detailed editing instructions.
 - This process also maintains "content memory" by referencing previous images and instructions. The VLM uses a structured prompt template guiding it through image description, comparison analysis, and action decision (edit, backtrack, or complete).
4. **Refinement**: Flux Kontext or Qwen-Image-Edit applies the editing instructions provided by the VLM to modify the image.
5. **Iteration**: Steps 3-4 repeat until the VLM determines that the output image fully satisfies the original prompt.

This iterative loop produces multi-turn reasoning trajectories that inherently embed the cognitive behaviors of verification, subgoal decomposition, and content memory.

**Data Filtering**: To ensure training efficiency and quality, several filters are applied to the synthesized trajectories:
- **Length constraint**: Trajectories exceeding 8 rounds are removed.
- **Quality regression**: Trajectories where the final image is worse than any of the first three (measured by Qwen3-VL quality score) are discarded.
- **Relevance filtering**: Individual rounds with editing prompts semantically irrelevant to the original task (measured by Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E) are removed.
- **Minimal visual changes**: Rounds with perceptual editing distance (LPIPS) below 0.03 between consecutive images are excluded.
- **Benchmark deduplication**: Training prompts are deduplicated from evaluation benchmarks using 5-gram matching to prevent data leakage.
This process yields approximately 12,000 high-quality trajectories for training.

#### 3.2. Unified Model Training

The Bagel (Deng et al., 2025b) unified multimodal model, which possesses both understanding and generation capabilities, is fine-tuned on the collected dataset.
- **Base Model**: Bagel is pretrained on large-scale interleaved text-image sequences.
- **Fine-tuning**: The fine-tuning process involves 700 H100 GPU hours.
- **Loss Aversion**: For multi-turn editing, 10% of intermediate image editing instructions are excluded from loss calculation to simulate user prompts that do not strictly require losses for every intermediate step.
This training enables the single Bagel model to internalize the multimodal reasoning patterns and perform understanding and refinement without relying on external models at inference time.

#### 3.3. Flexible Test-Time Inference and Budget Forcing

At inference time, the trained UniT model (a single Bagel model) autonomously performs all operations: planning, generation, reflection, and refinement. It utilizes a framework that incorporates two nested classifier-free guidance (CFG) schemes:
1. **Text CFG**: Conditions the generation with and without the current text instruction.
2. **Image CFG**: Conditions the generation with and without all images in the generation history (initial input and previous outputs).
The nested application (image guidance applied on top of text-guided prediction) enables independent control over prompt adherence and visual consistency, particularly for multi-turn editing.

**Budget Forcing**: The approach adapts "budget forcing" from text-only to multimodal test-time scaling by controlling the number of image generation rounds (C), which is the dominant factor in inference latency.
- **Forcing Extended Reasoning**: If the model terminates before C rounds, an End-of-Sequence (EOS) token suppression mechanism is used, appending a "Let's edit the image" prompt to force continued reasoning and image generation.
- **Budget Constraint**: If the model generates more than C images, only the final image from round C is used.
This allows for the study of "sequential chain-of-thought scaling" (iterative refinement) versus "parallel best-of-N scaling" (generating N independent images and selecting the best). The model trained on trajectories averaging 3.6 rounds demonstrates the ability to generalize to longer inference chains, averaging 4.7 rounds at test time, indicating emergent extrapolation capabilities beyond its training distribution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The UniT framework was evaluated across various multimodal tasks, demonstrating improvements in both generation and understanding, and exhibiting key emergent behaviors.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance Improvements

- **Compositional Generation (OneIG-Bench)**: UniT achieved an alignment score of 0.843, representing a 10.34% improvement over the base Bagel model (0.764) at C=10 rounds. This indicates enhanced ability to follow complex compositional instructions in image generation. Compared to Bagel+CoT (text-only chain-of-thought), UniT showed a 5.3% improvement (0.843 vs 0.790).
- **Compositional Editing (CompBench)**: On the multi-object compositional editing subset, UniT achieved an overall normalized score of 0.988, marking a 5.56% improvement from C=1 to C=10 rounds compared to the base Bagel model (0.936).
- **Multi-Turn Editing (ImgEdit)**: For multi-turn editing, UniT demonstrated substantial gains, achieving a human evaluation score of 4.26 (on a 0-10 scale), which is a 225.19% improvement from C=1 to C=4 rounds compared to the base Bagel model (1.31). This improvement was particularly noted in "Content Memory" and "Content Understanding" sub-scores.
- **Visual Reasoning (MIRA)**: For out-of-distribution visual reasoning tasks, UniT achieved an overall accuracy of 11.5%, representing a 53.33% improvement from C=1 to C=10 rounds over the base Bagel model (7.5%). While still below frontier models (e.g., GPT-5, Qwen2.5-VL), this demonstrates the transferability of multimodal chain-of-thought benefits to comprehension tasks.

#### 4.2. Sequential vs. Parallel Scaling

UniT demonstrated that sequential chain-of-thought scaling consistently outperforms parallel best-of-N sampling across all evaluated tasks.
- **Performance Advantage**: At C=10 (or C=4 for ImgEdit), sequential scaling achieved 4.85% better performance than parallel on OneIG-Bench, 3.89% on CompBench, 71.77% on ImgEdit, and 33.72% on MIRA.
- **Compute Efficiency**: Sequential scaling achieved comparable performance to parallel scaling with approximately 2.5 times fewer generated images (e.g., C=4 sequential matched N=10 parallel on OneIG-Bench).
- **Scaling Behavior**: Sequential scaling showed steeper performance improvements per additional image and sustained gains up to C=10, whereas parallel scaling tended to plateau earlier. This indicates that iterative refinement with explicit reasoning utilizes inference compute more efficiently than independent sampling.

#### 4.3. Emergent Cognitive Behaviors and Generalization

- **Beyond-Training Generalization**: Models trained on trajectories averaging 3.6 refinement rounds effectively generalized to longer inference chains, averaging 4.7 rounds at test time. This indicates an emergent ability to extrapolate problem-solving capabilities beyond the distribution of the training data.
- **Cognitive Behavior Ablations**:
 - Removing **subgoal decomposition** particularly impacted compositional tasks (3.8% drop on OneIG-Bench, 2.5% on CompBench), highlighting its role in multi-step generation planning.
 - Removing **content memory** significantly degraded multi-turn editing (42.5% relative drop on ImgEdit), confirming its criticality for coherent interactions across rounds.
 - Removing **verification** had the largest impact on visual reasoning (1.9% drop on MIRA), underscoring its importance for validating reasoning steps.
These ablations confirm that each induced cognitive behavior serves a distinct functional role.

#### 4.4. Data Quality Analysis

Ablation studies on data filtering demonstrated the importance of curated training data:
- Removing **relevance filtering** caused the largest degradation in compositional tasks (3.1% on OneIG-Bench, 2.5% on CompBench).
- Removing the **minimal visual changes filter** most significantly hurt multi-turn editing (1.16 points on ImgEdit).
- Removing the **quality regression filter** had the largest impact on visual reasoning (1.5% on MIRA).

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples illustrated how multimodal chain-of-thought test-time scaling progressively refines outputs. Initial rounds often contained compositional errors, which were then systematically corrected in subsequent rounds through explicit reasoning, subgoal decomposition, and error verification. This also transferred to visual reasoning, where the model's self-critique mechanism allowed it to identify and revise flawed analyses.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the capabilities of unified multimodal models by introducing UniT, a framework that extends test-time scaling (TTS) from text-only domains to combined visual understanding and generation.

The **primary significance** lies in establishing multimodal chain-of-thought TTS as a viable paradigm. It demonstrates that iteratively allocating additional computational resources during inference, coupled with explicit reasoning, can substantially enhance performance on complex multimodal tasks. This unified approach offers a contrast to modular pipelines, integrating diverse capabilities within a single model architecture and reducing inter-model communication overhead, which contributes to more streamlined deployment.

A key finding is the **emergent generalization to longer reasoning chains** at test time, exceeding the length of trajectories seen during training. This mirrors observations in text-only models and suggests that the learned iterative reasoning patterns are robust and scalable. This property implies that models trained on moderate-length reasoning can effectively tackle more complex problems at inference time without requiring prohibitively long training trajectories.

The work also highlights the **superiority of sequential chain-of-thought scaling over parallel sampling** in terms of both performance and compute efficiency. This finding suggests that iterative refinement, where each step builds upon and corrects the previous one with explicit reasoning, is a more effective use of inference compute than generating multiple independent samples. This has implications for optimizing resource allocation in future multimodal AI systems.

The **induction of cognitive behaviors**—verification, subgoal decomposition, and content memory—through the agentic data synthesis pipeline is crucial. The ablations confirm that these behaviors are integral to UniT's performance, each contributing distinctly to different task types, such as multi-step generation planning or maintaining coherence in multi-turn interactions. This contributes to a deeper understanding of the mechanisms underpinning effective multimodal reasoning.

The demonstrated improvements across a broad range of tasks—including compositional generation, multi-turn editing, and visual reasoning—suggest that UniT's methodology is **versatile and broadly applicable**. This unified benefit for both generation and comprehension tasks positions multimodal chain-of-thought TTS as a general paradigm for future advancements in AI.

**Potential impact** includes:
- **Enhanced Multimodal Interaction**: Improved capabilities for complex image editing, content creation, and interactive visual reasoning could lead to more sophisticated AI assistants and creative tools.
- **Foundation for Future Multimodal AI**: As base unified models continue to improve in scale and capacity, the UniT framework provides a methodological scaffold that can directly leverage these advancements, further amplifying their performance.
- **Efficiency in Inference**: The finding on the efficiency of sequential scaling provides guidance for designing more compute-optimal inference strategies for multimodal models, potentially leading to faster or higher-quality results within constrained computational budgets.
- **Advancement in AI Cognition**: The successful induction and identification of distinct cognitive behaviors contribute to the ongoing research into how AI models can emulate and extend human-like reasoning processes.

While the approach requires additional computational resources at inference, the research lays a foundation for future work to explore more efficient reflection mechanisms and adaptive budget allocation strategies, further optimizing the balance between performance gains and computational cost.
