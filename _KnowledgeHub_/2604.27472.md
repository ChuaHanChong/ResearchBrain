# PRTS: A Primitive Reasoning and Tasking System via Contrastive Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> PRTS introduces a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation model that integrates reward-label-free Contrastive Reinforcement Learning (CRL) into its pretraining, equipping it with an intrinsic understanding of "goal reachability." This enables the model to quantify the feasibility of achieving language-specified objectives, leading to enhanced performance in long-horizon tasks and zero-shot generalization across diverse robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, primarily relying on supervised behavior cloning, often lack an intrinsic understanding of "goal reachability" and temporal progress towards objectives.
- Prior methods to inject value awareness into VLAs typically require explicit reward annotations, separate value networks, or suffer from bootstrapping errors and architectural decoupling.
- VLAs struggle with robust long-horizon planning and decision-making because they focus on immediate action prediction rather than broader temporal progress evaluation.

## Method

- **PRTS** reformulates VLA pretraining through ==Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning==, specifically adapting ==Contrastive RL (CRL)== for language-conditioned settings.
- Employs a ==temporal weighting scheme== in a ==bidirectional contrastive objective== to implicitly extract dense goal-reachability supervision from offline trajectories.
- A novel ==role-aware causal mask==, implemented via a custom FlashAttention kernel, integrates CRL into the VLM backbone with minimal overhead.

## Results

- **PRTS** achieved SOTA **98.4%** on LIBERO; highest zero-shot **81.4%** on LIBERO-Plus and **58.8%** on LIBERO-Pro.
- Strong real-world robustness: **73.8%** average success across visual, layout, and object instance shifts.
- Qualitative stress tests confirm consistent recovery from human interventions; CRL values accurately track task progress.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Language-conditioned Contrastive Reinforcement Learning (LC-CRL) can learn a unified embedding space where the inner product of state-action and goal embeddings approximates the log-discounted goal occupancy, quantifying task progress.
> - Implicit, dense goal-reachability supervision can be extracted directly from the temporal structure of offline demonstration trajectories, eliminating the need for explicit reward labels or separate value networks.
> - A custom role-aware causal mask allows the VLM backbone to efficiently compute action predictions, state-action representations, and goal representations in a single forward pass, ensuring scalability for large-scale pretraining.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic control has seen advancements through Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage Vision-Language Models (VLMs) as backbones. These VLA architectures typically employ a dual-system approach: a VLM handles high-level semantic understanding and task reasoning (System 2), while an action expert manages low-level continuous control (System 1).

A prevalent paradigm in VLA pretraining is supervised behavior cloning, where models learn visuomotor priors by directly imitating demonstrated actions. While effective for immediate action prediction and encoding static visual-linguistic semantic correlations, this approach exhibits limitations. It often overlooks a fundamental aspect of embodied interaction: a robotic trajectory is inherently a goal-reaching process over time. Consequently, existing VLA representations often lack an intrinsic notion of "goal reachability"—a quantitative estimate of how likely a given state-action pair is to eventually achieve a language-specified objective. This deficiency means that while VLAs can reason qualitatively about "what to do," they often lack the quantitative awareness required to evaluate execution difficulty or distinguish difficult-yet-reachable states from easy-yet-erroneous ones.

Some previous efforts have aimed to augment VLAs with value awareness, such as π\*0.6 and VLAC. However, these methods typically rely on explicit reward or progress annotations, require a separately trained value network that incurs additional computational cost, or use discretized scalar returns or hand-engineered progress fractions as targets. These approaches can suffer from bootstrapping errors inherent in Temporal-Difference (TD) learning or from architectural decoupling between the value modules and the core VLM backbone, preventing direct shaping of shared vision-language representations by value signals.

PRTS addresses these limitations by reformulating VLA pretraining through the lens of Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL), specifically utilizing Contrastive Reinforcement Learning (CRL). CRL has demonstrated scalability to deep networks and avoids the bootstrapping errors of TD learning by reframing value estimation as a classification problem. By integrating CRL directly into the VLM backbone during pretraining, PRTS aims to inject temporal awareness of goal reachability into the model's representation space without requiring explicit reward annotations or separate networks. This approach seeks to provide a unified framework where semantic reasoning, action generation, and goal-reachability estimation are learned jointly within the same architecture, thus shaping a more robust and generalizable visuomotor prior for robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind PRTS is to overcome a fundamental limitation in existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their predominant reliance on supervised behavior cloning for pretraining, which often neglects the temporal and goal-oriented nature of robot learning. While current VLAs demonstrate proficiency in visual-linguistic semantic understanding, they typically lack an intrinsic mechanism to quantify "goal reachability"—the probability or feasibility of achieving a language-specified objective from a given state-action pair. This absence limits their capacity for robust long-horizon planning and decision-making, as they primarily focus on immediate action prediction rather than understanding the broader temporal progress towards a goal.

The key objectives of this work are:

1. **To reformulate VLA pretraining through Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL)**: The aim is to move beyond pure behavior cloning by treating language instructions as explicit goals within a GCRL framework. This reframing is intended to naturally equip the VLM backbone with an understanding of temporal goal progress.
2. **To learn a unified embedding space with goal-reachability awareness**: PRTS seeks to create an embedding space where the inner product of state-action and goal embeddings approximates the log-discounted goal occupancy. This measure quantifies the physical feasibility of reaching a language-specified goal from a current state-action, offering a quantitative assessment beyond static semantic matching.
3. **To extract dense goal-reachability supervision implicitly from offline trajectories**: A crucial objective is to derive this dense, temporal supervision directly from existing offline demonstration data, eliminating the need for manual reward annotations or progress labels. This objective leverages the inherent structure of goal-reaching behavior within expert trajectories.
4. **To integrate contrastive reinforcement learning (CRL) into the VLM backbone with minimal overhead**: The methodology aims to fold the goal-reachability awareness into the VLM architecture via a role-aware causal mask, integrated into a custom FlashAttention kernel. This design is motivated by the need to incur negligible additional overhead compared to vanilla behavior cloning, ensuring the scalability of the approach for large-scale pretraining.
5. **To unify semantic reasoning, discrete action prediction, and goal-conditioned representation learning**: PRTS intends to achieve joint optimization and shared vision-language representations within a single VLM architecture. This unification allows the model to simultaneously perform high-level semantic reasoning, predict discrete actions, and estimate goal reachability.
6. **To enhance execution success and long-horizon planning, particularly in challenging settings**: The ultimate motivation is to improve the performance of general-purpose robotic foundation policies. Specific targets for improvement include long-horizon tasks, contact-rich manipulations, and zero-shot generalization to novel instructions, where explicit goal-reachability awareness is hypothesized to be most beneficial.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PRTS (Primitive Reasoning and Tasking System) is a VLA foundation model that reformulates pretraining by integrating Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) principles, specifically Contrastive Reinforcement Learning (CRL), into the Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone.

#### 3.1. Problem Setup and Theoretical Foundation

The problem is cast as language-conditioned robotic manipulation under an imitation learning paradigm, using a large-scale dataset of demonstrations with language instructions. PRTS leverages a key insight from GCRL: for goal-reaching reward functions, the Q-function is equivalent to the conditional occupancy probability of reaching a goal from a given state-action pair under a discounted state occupancy measure. Contrastive RL (Eysenbach et al., 2022) estimates this Q-function by learning state-action representations (ϕ) and goal representations (ψ) whose inner product approximates the log-ratio of goal occupancy probability to marginal goal probability, thereby avoiding the bootstrapping errors of Temporal-Difference (TD) learning.

#### 3.2. Language-Conditioned Contrastive Reinforcement Learning (LC-CRL)

The core innovation lies in adapting standard CRL to the language-conditioned setting where the goal (`l`) is a natural language instruction, constant across an entire trajectory. This presents a "multi-positive problem" as all state-action pairs within a trajectory share the same language goal.

- **Temporal Weighting as Geometric Sampling**: To address the multi-positive problem and introduce temporal awareness, PRTS employs a temporal weighting scheme. For a language instruction `l_i` and its associated state-action pairs `(s_j, a_j)` at timestep `t_j` (within a trajectory of length `T_j`), a weight `q_ij` is assigned proportional to `γ^(T_j - t_j)`. This weighting emulates the geometric distribution used in standard CRL for sampling future states, assigning higher "value" to states closer to task completion and enabling the representation similarity to encode discounted goal occupancy.

- **Bidirectional Contrastive Objectives**: Two complementary objectives are optimized using the InfoNCE formulation:
 1. **State-Action to Language (`s, a → l`)**: For each `(s_i, a_i)` anchor, its own language instruction `l_i` is the positive goal. The loss `L_sa→l` uses a `γ^(T_i - t_i)` weight for the state-action pair. This objective primarily aligns state-action pairs with their correct task-level language goals, preventing interference between different tasks.
 2. **Language to State-Action (`l → s, a`)**: For each language anchor `l_i`, all state-action pairs `(s_j, a_j)` from the same task in the batch form a positive set `S(i)`. The loss `L_l→sa` uses the `q_ij` soft targets. This direction introduces the temporal signal, forcing the inner product `ψ(l_i)⊤ϕ(s_j, a_j)` to reflect the temporal distance to task completion, encoding log-discounted goal occupancy.
 The combination ensures both task identity and task progress are captured in the learned representations. The theoretical framework demonstrates that this temporal weighting scheme makes the inner product `ψ(l)⊤ϕ(s, a)` proportional to the log-discounted state occupancy measure, effectively quantifying `log Q_π_l(s, a)`.

#### 3.3. PRTS Architecture

PRTS is built upon the Qwen3-VL backbone. The input sequence includes multi-view visual tokens, textual proprioceptive-state tokens, language instruction tokens, and auto-regressive (AR) action tokens (tokenized via FAST).

- **Auxiliary Token Blocks**: Two additional token blocks, `<CRL_action>` and `<CRL_goal>`, are appended to the input sequence.
 - The hidden state of a learnable `<|action_end|>` token within `<CRL_action>` encodes the state-action representation `ϕ(s_t, a_t)`.
 - The hidden state of a learnable `<|goal_end|>` token within `<CRL_goal>` encodes the goal representation `ψ(l)`.
 These representations are passed through lightweight MLP heads, followed by ℓ2-normalization and temperature scaling for contrastive learning.

- **Role-Aware Causal Mask**: To ensure disentangled representations and efficient computation within a single forward pass, a custom FlashAttention kernel implements a role-aware causal mask:
 - AR action tokens maintain standard causal attention to all preceding tokens.
 - `CRL_action` tokens attend only to vision and proprioception tokens, and themselves (blocked from language and AR actions) to ensure `ϕ(s, a)` is independent of the language goal.
 - `CRL_goal` tokens use self-only causal attention within their block, encoding `ψ(l)` from the language instruction alone.
 This design, optimized with a CuTe kernel, allows computing action predictions, `ϕ(s, a)`, and `ψ(l)` in a single forward pass, achieving throughput comparable to pure behavior cloning.

#### 3.4. Training Scheme and Dataset

PRTS uses a two-stage training approach:
1. **Pre-training**: The VLM backbone is jointly optimized using:
 - `L_BC`: Cross-entropy loss for AR action tokens.
 - `L_RobotQA`, `L_Subtask`: Auxiliary cross-entropy losses for robot-QA and sub-task prediction.
 - `λ_crl * (L_sa→l + L_l→sa)`: The bidirectional contrastive losses. (`λ_crl = 1.0`, `γ = 0.995`).
 A cross-device contrastive strategy is used to maximize the negative pool (approx. 2K in-batch negatives).
2. **Post-training**: A randomly initialized DiT-based flow-matching action expert (675M parameters) is attached and fine-tuned on downstream data using `L_FM` (conditional flow matching loss) to produce continuous action chunks. Gradients continue to flow through the VLM backbone.

The model is pretrained on a large-scale dataset (404M samples, 167.8B tokens) comprising:
- **Action-Labeled Data**: Aggregated from AgiBotWorld, RoboMind, Open X-Embodiment, and PRTS Self-Collected datasets, covering diverse hardware, contact-rich, and long-horizon tasks.
- **Visual-Reasoning Data**: Provides supervision for spatial grounding (RefCOCO, Pixmo-Point, RoboPoint, RoboRefIt, RefSpatial, RoboAfford), task planning and high-level reasoning (Cosmos-Reason1, EgoPlanIT, RoboVQA), and general semantic understanding (LLaVA-Instruct).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PRTS was evaluated across a range of simulation and real-world benchmarks, with a focus on overall performance, the specific effect of Contrastive RL, generalization capabilities, robustness to human interventions, and pre-training efficiency.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **LIBERO Benchmark**: PRTS achieved a 98.4% average success rate (SR), matching state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance while utilizing substantially less post-training compute (e.g., global batch size 32, 30K steps) compared to several competitive baselines. Specifically, it demonstrated strong performance on long-horizon tasks, reaching 96.6% SR.
- **LIBERO-Plus (Zero-shot)**: PRTS achieved the highest average SR of 81.4%, surpassing π0.5 (80.7%) despite an 8x smaller post-training batch. Notable gains were observed on challenging perturbations like Robot state (+14.4 SR), Background (+15.3 SR), and Language (+1.1 SR), indicating robustness to distribution shifts.
- **LIBERO-Pro (Zero-shot)**: PRTS achieved an average SR of 58.8%, a 5.5-point improvement over π0.5. The most significant gains were on the *Task* axis (31.5% vs. 0.8% for π0.5), demonstrating superior zero-shot novel instruction following. It also led on *Position* (24.3% vs. 20.8%).
- **SimplerEnv (WidowX)**: PRTS achieved a 77.1% average SR, outperforming GR00T-N1.5 by 15.2 points and Qwen3-VL-PI by 16.2 points, highlighting its adaptability across different robot embodiments.
- **Real-World Evaluation (RealMan Dual-Arm)**: PRTS attained a 95.9% average SR across 11 tasks with mixed-task post-training, significantly outperforming π0.5 (85.5%) and π0 (67.3%). Its advantage was pronounced in contact-rich and long-horizon tasks, such as "Office Long Term" (95% for PRTS vs. 40% for π0.5).
- **Real-World Evaluation (Flexiv Single-Arm)**: PRTS achieved a 90.0% average SR, exceeding π0.5 by 15.0%. It demonstrated stronger spatial grounding in "Block Combination" (100% vs. 90%) and mitigated compounding errors in the long-horizon "Flower Arrangement" (70% vs. 35%).

#### 4.2. Effect of Contrastive RL (Ablation Study)

A controlled experiment compared PRTS with and without the Contrastive RL (CRL) objective during pre-training.
- **LIBERO**: CRL improved the average SR by 0.6%, with specific gains on Spatial (+1.0%) and Long (+1.0%) tasks.
- **LIBERO-Plus**: CRL yielded a 4.9% increase in average SR, with substantial improvements in robustness against Robot state (+16.3%) and Noise (+9.0%) perturbations.
- **LIBERO-Pro**: CRL resulted in a 5.0% increase in average SR, primarily driven by enhanced generalization on the *Position* (+10.5%) and *Task* (+11.4%) axes. Detailed analysis showed significant gains in re-grounding scenarios (e.g., Object-Position: 4.8% to 36.0%) and adapting to new task logics (e.g., Spatial-Task: 34.4% to 62.2%).
- **t-SNE Visualization**: Analysis of learned state-action representations showed that PRTS with CRL organized the embedding space into tightly clustered, well-separated neighborhoods corresponding to reusable manipulation primitives (e.g., Hand-Pick, Gripper-Pick). The non-CRL variant exhibited entangled and diffuse representations, suggesting overfitting to trajectory-specific correlations.

#### 4.3. Controlled Real-World Generalization

PRTS demonstrated high robustness to visual (Illumination), layout (Spatial), and object instance (Object) shifts in four representative RealMan tasks, maintaining high success rates (e.g., 100% for "Paper Rubbish" and "Place Block"). On *Task generalization* (novel instruction following), PRTS averaged 73.8% success, significantly outperforming π0.5 (35.0%) and π0 (13.8%), indicating its ability to transfer learned skills and adapt to new language-specified goals.

#### 4.4. Robustness and Recovery under Human Interventions

Qualitative stress tests on four tasks with human-induced perturbations (e.g., moving objects, reverting task progress) showed that PRTS consistently recovered and completed tasks. It adapted to changed states, abandoned stale actions, and re-entered necessary subgoals. Baselines often failed to re-plan effectively when strong interventions invalidated execution history.

#### 4.5. CRL Value Visualization

Direct examination of the pre-trained PRTS (without downstream fine-tuning) revealed that the `ϕ(s, a)⊤ψ(l)` inner product faithfully tracked the log-transformed goal-conditioned action value. For a held-out "Pick Shoes" trajectory, the value under the correct instruction showed a clear upward trend with peaks aligned to task milestones, rising from 0.28 to 0.57. In contrast, the value under a mismatched instruction remained consistently low (0.04-0.20), confirming temporal-progress-sensitive value estimation and strong goal-conditioned discriminability.

#### 4.6. Pre-Training Efficiency

- **Kernel Microbenchmark**: The custom CuTe kernel for the role-aware mask achieved a per-layer attention forward time of 0.531 ms, which is only 1.18 times that of the FlashAttention 3 (FA3) baseline (0.45 ms) without CRL tokens. This demonstrates minimal overhead.
- **Throughput Scaling**: The end-to-end training throughput scaled nearly linearly up to 64 H100 GPUs, achieving 85.1% efficiency at 64 GPUs, consistent with expected communication overhead in distributed training (DeepSpeed ZeRO-2). This indicates the practicality of large-scale contrastive pre-training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of PRTS marks a notable advancement in the field of robotic learning by integrating reward-label-free Contrastive Reinforcement Learning (CRL) directly into the pretraining of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This represents a shift from models primarily trained via behavior cloning to those endowed with an intrinsic "goal-reachability awareness."

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness**: PRTS demonstrates substantial improvements in crucial areas such as long-horizon task execution, zero-shot generalization to novel instructions, and recovery from dynamic human interventions. These capabilities are critical for deploying robots in unstructured, real-world environments where variations and unexpected events are common. The ability to reason about goal reachability, rather than simply imitating trajectories, allows the robot to adapt to significant distribution shifts, including changes in object layouts, appearances, lighting, and even task semantics.

2. **Addressing the Temporal Reasoning Gap**: By explicitly embedding temporal task progress and goal feasibility into its representations, PRTS addresses a key limitation of prior VLA models. This enables the policy to not only understand "what to do" but also "how likely it is to succeed" and "how close it is to achieving the goal," providing a more comprehensive basis for decision-making in goal-directed tasks.

3. **Architectural and Computational Efficiency**: The novel architectural design, particularly the role-aware causal mask fused into FlashAttention via a custom CuTe kernel, is a significant technical contribution. This innovation ensures that the benefits of CRL can be incorporated into large-scale VLA pretraining with minimal additional computational overhead compared to vanilla behavior cloning. This efficiency makes it feasible to scale value-aware learning to foundation models, which is essential for realizing general-purpose robot intelligence.

4. **Reward-Free Value Learning**: The ability to derive dense goal-reachability supervision directly from offline trajectory structure, without requiring manual reward annotations or separate value networks, simplifies the data collection and training pipeline. This approach reduces the dependency on labor-intensive reward engineering, a common bottleneck in traditional reinforcement learning.

5. **Foundation for Goal-Directed Intelligence**: The integration of goal-reachability awareness into the VLM backbone suggests that VLA pretraining can evolve beyond merely creating "more-capable imitators." It paves the way for developing robotic policies that are truly goal-directed, capable of robust planning, and intrinsically aware of task progress, positioning PRTS as an emerging foundation for more sophisticated and general-purpose robotic intelligence.

6. **Future Research Directions**: This work opens avenues for further exploration of how other principles from Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning can be effectively integrated into large-scale multimodal foundation models for robotics, potentially leading to even more advanced adaptive and autonomous robotic systems.
