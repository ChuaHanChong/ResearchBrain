# IPR-1: Interactive Physical Reasoner

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University introduced the Interactive Physical Reasoner (IPR), an agent that acquires human-like physical and causal reasoning from diverse interactive environments. IPR leverages a physics-centric action code (PhysCode) and integrates vision-language models with a world model, demonstrating robust zero-shot transfer across a new 1000+ game benchmark (G2U) and showing performance improvements that scale with increased interactive experience.

## Problem

- Existing AI agents often overfit to superficial visual details, struggling to generalize physical reasoning across diverse interactive environments due to a lack of understanding invariant physical and causal mechanisms.
- Current embodied AI paradigms (VLMs, VLAs, RL, World Models) exhibit limitations such as lack of predictive grounding, reliance on rote memorization, or difficulty in modeling complex dynamics, hindering robust transfer to novel interactive tasks.
- A scalable benchmark was needed to systematically study interactive physical reasoning in highly heterogeneous environments, encompassing diverse visual styles, physics, and causal structures.

## Method

- The Interactive Physical Reasoner (IPR) paradigm was proposed, blending ==VLM-based semantic priors==, world-model-based imagination, and reinforcement learning within a unified latent action space.
- PhysCode, a physics-centric latent action code, was developed to unify action semantics with visual dynamics, addressing issues of semantic aliasing in raw controls and imprecise dynamic control via natural language.
- A prediction-reinforced GRPO (Guaranteed Risk Policy Optimization) mechanism was introduced to update the VLM's policy using short-horizon rollouts from a latent-level world model, strengthening its interactive reasoning.

## Results

- IPR achieved robust overall performance across 200 diverse games, obtaining high mean scores in Survival (**0.252**), Curiosity (1.173), and Utility (**0.493**) objectives, significantly outperforming various baselines including GPT-5.
- The model demonstrated strong zero-shot transfer capabilities to unseen games, with its performance on Survival, Curiosity, and Utility steadily improving as the number of training games and interaction steps increased.
- Ablation studies confirmed that integrating world model prediction via GRPO updates is critical for strengthening long-horizon physical reasoning, as evidenced by a higher Curiosity score of 2.77 compared to variants without this component.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalizable physical and causal reasoning can be acquired through interaction with diverse environments, and this ability consistently improves with accumulated experience, indicating that agents can distill invariant mechanisms.
> - A physics-centric latent action space (PhysCode) is essential for disentangling core dynamics from domain-specific visual appearances and action interfaces, enabling robust zero-shot transfer of physical understanding.
> - Integrating world model predictions to reinforce VLM policies in an interactive loop effectively bridges the gap between high-level reasoning and low-level predictive grounding, leading to superior performance in complex physical tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of embodied artificial intelligence (AI), specifically addressing the development of agents capable of human-like physical and causal reasoning in interactive environments. The current landscape involves several distinct paradigms, each with limitations the authors aim to address:

- **Pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**: While capable of broad semantic understanding from large-scale pretraining, VLMs typically acquire static, pattern-matching behaviors, often functioning as open-loop planners. Fine-tuning (SFT) enhances text-based declarative reasoning but often lacks the predictive grounding necessary for dynamic, interactive physical tasks.
- **Behavior-Cloned Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Agents**: These agents are constrained by the quality of demonstration data, relying on rote memorization of trajectories. This can limit their adaptability and make them prone to failure under environmental variations.
- **Model-Based Approaches (Control Theory, Model-Based RL)**: These methods offer stability through accurate dynamics models but encounter difficulties in complex environments where creating a faithful, high-fidelity model is computationally or practically infeasible.
- **Model-Free Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: While avoiding explicit modeling, RL often requires extensive samples and dense rewards. It can overfit to task-specific shortcuts rather than underlying causal mechanisms, which hinders transfer and generalization to novel tasks.
- **World Models and Prediction-Based Approaches**: These methods learn latent dynamics and enable agents to simulate future states. While effective for exploration and sparse-reward learning, they can sometimes collapse into short-horizon target chasing or imitation of superficial visual patterns, lacking robust causal reasoning and suffering from compounding errors in complex or novel environments.

The authors identify a fundamental gap: existing paradigms frequently overfit to superficial visual details, rather than capturing the invariant physical and causal mechanisms that govern interactions. This work proposes a "blended" approach, aiming to integrate the strengths of these paradigms—exploration from RL, essential latent prediction from world models, and semantic priors from VLMs—to achieve robust physical reasoning and generalization across diverse interactive domains. It attempts to learn invariant dynamics by disentangling core mechanisms from domain-specific visual appearances and action interfaces.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether an agent can acquire human-like physical and causal reasoning through interaction with diverse environments, and if this ability can consistently improve with accumulated experience. This core question is motivated by the observation that humans develop their understanding of physics and causality primarily through interaction, rather than explicit labels, and that their predictive and reasoning abilities scale with experience.

The specific objectives and motivations include:

- **Addressing Overfitting to Visual Details**: Existing AI systems often struggle to generalize across environments with significant visual domain gaps because they tend to overfit to visual patterns rather than internalizing underlying physical and causal principles. The motivation is to develop an agent that can distill shared mechanisms and transfer this understanding.
- **Developing a Generalizable Learning Paradigm**: The authors hypothesize that if an agent is exposed to a diverse set of interactive worlds and trained to identify shared physical and causal mechanisms, rather than environment-specific appearances or action interfaces, its physical reasoning ability would scale reliably and transfer to new scenarios.
- **Introducing a Scalable Benchmark (Game-to-Unseen, G2U)**: To systematically study physical reasoning from interaction at scale, the paper introduces the Game-to-Unseen (G2U) benchmark. This benchmark comprises over 1,000 heterogeneous games, spanning diverse visual styles, control interfaces, physics configurations, and causal structures. The motivation behind G2U is to provide an ideal testbed for physical reasoning, offering rich interaction and realistic physics at low cost, while the heterogeneity introduces substantial domain gaps that challenge traditional agents.
- **Diagnosing Limitations of Current Approaches**: The research aims to empirically diagnose the specific failure modes of prevalent prediction-based, RL-based, and VLM-based methods when faced with the G2U problem. For instance, VLMs/VLAs may lack look-ahead capabilities, while world models might imitate visual patterns without deeply understanding underlying physics.
- **Proposing a Blended Architectural Approach (IPR)**: Motivated by the limitations of existing methods, the authors propose the Interactive Physical Reasoner (IPR). This involves combining VLM-based semantic priors for policy formulation with world-model-based imagination for predictive grounding. The goal is to create a system that models only essential latent dynamics, interacts with multimodal signals, and reinforces its policy using physically feasible predictive feedback.
- **Developing a Physics-Centric Action Space (PhysCode)**: A key motivation is to overcome limitations of traditional action spaces, such as raw key semantic aliasing (e.g., the "UP" key having different meanings across games) and the inability of language to express fine-grained visual dynamics (e.g., precise jump height). PhysCode aims to provide a shared, physics-centric latent action code that aligns semantic intent with dynamics, fostering cross-domain transfer.
- **Establishing a Hierarchical Evaluation Framework**: Inspired by Maslow’s hierarchy of needs, the research proposes a three-level evaluation: Survival, Curiosity, and Utility. This is motivated by the need to assess an agent’s capabilities across a spectrum from basic physical intuition (survival) to exploratory behavior (curiosity) and complex goal-driven reasoning (utility), thereby providing a comprehensive diagnostic tool for interactive physical reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Interactive Physical Reasoner (IPR) paradigm integrates components from world models, VLMs, and reinforcement learning within a unified latent action space. The methodology comprises four main stages: (1) learning a physics-centric action code vocabulary (PhysCode), (2) training a latent-conditioned world model, (3) aligning a VLM with PhysCode, and (4) reinforcing the VLM policy using world model rollouts.

#### 3.1. PhysCode: Physics-centric Action Code

PhysCode addresses the challenges of semantic aliasing in raw controls and imprecise dynamic control via natural language. It is a discrete latent action representation derived from a Vector Quantized (VQ) codebook.

- **Input Cues**: Each action code is conditioned on three multimodal cues:
 - **Domain-specific visual appearance**: Extracted via DINOv3 features (ϕ_img(x_t)) from the current and future frames.
 - **Domain-agnostic motion**: Captured by optical flow (ϕ_flow(Flow(x_t, x_{t+1}))). Optical flow acts as privileged information during pretraining to shape a physics-centric codebook, then dropped at inference.
 - **Lightweight semantic hints**: Obtained from a T5 encoder (ϕ_sem(y_t)) based on textual descriptions of actions.
- **Gated Modality Fusion**: A small gated MLP fuses these projected modalities into a combined representation (h_t). Softmax-normalized gates determine the contribution of each modality. Flow dropout (p=0.5) is applied during training, replacing flow features with zeros, to ensure the encoder can rely on appearance and semantics alone at test time. An L1 penalty regularizes gate sparsity.
- **Spatio-Temporal Encoder and Codebook**: A 6-layer Transformer encoder maps sequences of fused representations to a continuous latent vector (z_t). This vector is then quantized to the nearest entry in a VQ codebook (C = {c_k}) to obtain a discrete index (a_t). An action is represented as a short sequence of these indices (a_LAT_t = ⟨c_t,1:L⟩).
- **Training Objective**: PhysCode is trained using a VQ-VAE objective, minimizing the reconstruction loss of future DINOv3 features (L_LA = ||ˆf_{t+∆} - f_{t+∆}||^2_2) combined with commitment losses for the codebook and latent vector. A temporal head predicts feature changes (∆f_{t+∆}) to encourage alignment with dynamical changes. PhysCode is pretrained on the entire 1,000+ game corpus.
- **Inference**: At inference, optical flow is unavailable, and its gate is disabled. The encoder uses appearance and semantic cues to derive PhysCode indices.

#### 3.2. Latent-Level World Model with a Critic

With the PhysCode vocabulary fixed, a feature-level world model is trained to predict future features and rewards, conditioned on latent actions.

- **Prediction**: For a given triple (f_t, a_t, f_{t+∆}), the world model (P_θ) predicts future features (ˆf_{t+∆}) and a scalar value (V_θ(f_t, a_t)). This prediction occurs in the latent feature space, which compresses visual variance and noise, promoting dynamics transferability. The world model is implemented as an 8-layer Transformer unrolled over short latent trajectories (H=5).
- **Training Phases**:
 1. **Prediction Phase**: The model is initially trained with a feature-prediction loss (L_pred = ||ˆf_{t+∆} - f_{t+∆}||_1) on pre-collected trajectories, focusing on physics-consistent feature evolution.
 2. **Value-Learning Phase**: After freezing the dynamics layers, a critic head is trained using a Q-learning-style objective (L_value = ℓ_Q(V_θ(f_t, a_t), y_t)), where y_t is a target value from rollout returns and a slowly updated target network (θ_-) stabilizes learning.

#### 3.3. Prediction-Reinforced Interactive Reasoning

The core of IPR involves strengthening VLM reasoning using world model predictions within an interactive loop.

- **VLM Backbone and PhysCode Integration**: Qwen3-VL-8B is adopted as the VLM backbone. Its tokenizer is extended with PhysCode tokens, allowing the VLM to directly output discrete latent actions autoregressively.
- **Perception-Action Alignment**: The VLM undergoes supervised training on human (f_t, c_t) pairs, where f_t is the DINOv3 feature of the current frame and c_t is the learned PhysCode. The input includes an interleaved image-text prompt (current frame, goal), and the target is the PhysCode sequence. This stage teaches the VLM to parse visual context, understand goals, and output structured PhysCode sequences.
- **Prediction-Reinforced GRPO (Guaranteed Risk Policy Optimization)**: In an interactive setting, the VLM's policy is refined using world model rollouts.
 1. **Observation and Candidate Generation**: At each environment step, the VLM processes the current frame (x_t) and task prompt (g) and samples B candidate PhysCode sequences (a^(b)_t).
 2. **Imagined Rollouts and Value Estimation**: For each candidate action sequence, the world model performs short-horizon rollouts in the feature space, yielding a predicted return (ˆR^(b)_t) from its critic head.
 3. **Advantage Computation**: Returns are normalized within the candidate set to compute advantages (A^(b)).
 4. **Policy Update**: The VLM policy is updated using a GRPO objective (L_GRPO), which incorporates the advantages to prefer actions leading to better imagined futures, while a KL-divergence term (β_KL KL[π_ϕ || π_ϕ0]) maintains a conservative trust region relative to the initial aligned VLM (π_ϕ0).
- **Interleaving Training**: Real environment interaction and imagination-based updates are interleaved (1:k ratio) to improve data efficiency, with recent contexts stored in a replay buffer.
- **Inference**: During inference, the VLM proposes candidate PhysCode sequences, the world model scores them via imagination, and the highest-scoring action is selected. A router (T_env) maps the selected PhysCode to environment-specific controls using a pre-learned lookup table.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several findings regarding the necessity of PhysCode, IPR's performance across diverse game types, and its zero-shot transfer capabilities.

#### 4.1. Necessity of PhysCode

The investigation into PhysCode's necessity compared to raw keyboard/mouse inputs and natural language instructions yielded the following:

- **Robustness under Joint Training (Table 1a)**: When trained jointly across games with heterogeneous physics, raw keyboard/mouse inputs exhibited cross-game conflicts, leading to performance degradation in latent and pixel prediction. Language interfaces partially mitigated this by providing explicit semantics. PhysCode, by separating actions based on underlying dynamics, showed minimal degradation, indicating its ability to reduce interface aliasing.
- **Leave-n-out Transfer (Table 1b)**: In a zero-shot transfer setting where models were trained on all but 10 games and evaluated on the held-out set, PhysCode demonstrated more reliable transfer compared to keyboard or language instructions in both latent and pixel prediction metrics.
- **Physics-Conditioned Transfer (Table 1c)**: When transfer was conditioned on the physical mechanisms of the environment, PhysCode exhibited higher zero-shot performance when target environments matched the training set’s physical principles (e.g., projectile, gravity, inertia, impulse). This indicates that PhysCode captures reusable physical mechanisms rather than memorizing game-specific bindings.

#### 4.2. Performance in Diverse Physical Worlds

IPR was evaluated against various baselines (RL, VLM, World Model, IL) on 200 games across the hierarchical objectives: Survival, Curiosity, and Utility (Table 2).

- **Overall Performance**: IPR, using a Qwen3-VL-8B backbone, achieved a high average rank (4.9/28, where lower is better), indicating robust performance across all three objectives. It obtained high mean scores across Survival (0.252), Curiosity (1.173), and Utility (0.493), and ranked highest in Survival (72% in top-3) and Curiosity (13.4% in top-3) and well in Utility (22% in top-3).
- **Comparison to Baselines**:
 - **Prediction-based Methods (World Models like DreamerV3, V-JEPA2, GenieRedux)**: These models showed strength in Curiosity, indicating broad exploration and dynamics understanding, but were weaker in Utility and often in Survival, struggling with goal pursuit or risk avoidance.
 - **RL-based Methods (PPO, DQN)**: Strong at Utility when rewards were well-shaped, but weaker in Curiosity and often inconsistent in Survival. They could overfit to task-specific signals and suffered from brittleness.
 - **Experience-based Methods (ACT-BC, Qwen3-VL-8B-BC)**: Demonstrated competence in Survival and often in Utility by imitating human trajectories, but were weaker in Curiosity, lacking exploration, and limited by demonstration quality.
 - **Reasoning-based Pretrained VLMs (GPT-4o, GPT-5, Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B)**: Capable of goal-conditioned reasoning and showed moderate Utility, but generally weaker in Survival and Curiosity, as they lacked forward consequence prediction for interactive physical tasks. IPR with an 8B backbone achieved higher overall scores than GPT-5.
- **Summary**: IPR integrated the strengths of these paradigms, with VLMs providing goal-driven reasoning, the world model supplying rollout prediction, and RL optimizing decisions using imagined rewards, leading to consistent performance across all three levels.

#### 4.3. Zero-shot Transfer to Unseen Games

The study validated the Game-to-Unseen (G2U) setting by evaluating IPR's zero-shot transfer capabilities on a held-out set of 50 unseen games.

- **Scaling with Training Games (Figure 7)**: As the number of training games (N) increased, IPR's zero-shot performance on Survival, Curiosity, and Utility steadily improved on the unseen game set. The steepest gains were observed early for Survival, followed by sustained improvements for Curiosity and Utility with more diverse interactions. This suggests that training in varied physically and causally related environments helps IPR generalize underlying physical and causal patterns rather than memorizing domain-specific features.

#### 4.4. Ablations and Analysis

An ablation study on IPR components (World Model prediction, GRPO) using a Qwen3-VL-8B backbone showed:

- **Prediction Helps VLM Reasoning (Table 3)**: Comparing variants, IPR (VLM + WM + GRPO) achieved the highest Curiosity (2.77) while maintaining strong Survival (0.76) and Utility (1.34). This indicates that integrating world model prediction via GRPO updates is critical for strengthening long-horizon physical reasoning, beyond simply pushing for immediate high scores.
- **Impact of Behavior Cloning**: Naive Behavior Cloning (BC) on top of the pretrained VLM showed marginal survival improvement but degraded curiosity and utility, suggesting that low-quality demonstrations could overwrite beneficial priors. Combining BC with PPO or GRPO also led to reduced performance compared to IPR without BC.
- **GRPO vs. PPO**: VLM + GRPO showed comparable Utility and Survival to VLM + PPO, but GRPO led to higher Curiosity. This suggests GRPO, with its trust region approach, provides a more stable reinforcement signal in this context.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and indicates potential impacts:

- **Formulation of the G2U Problem and Benchmark**: The introduction of the Game-to-Unseen (G2U) benchmark, comprising over 1,000 heterogeneous games with a hierarchical evaluation (Survival, Curiosity, Utility), provides a standardized and scalable testbed for advancing research in interactive physical reasoning. This benchmark is crucial for diagnosing the strengths and weaknesses of different AI methodologies and promoting the development of generalist agents that can generalize beyond specific domains.
- **Introduction of PhysCode**: The development of PhysCode, a physics-centric latent action code, offers a novel approach to unify action semantics with visual dynamics. By creating a shared action space that captures reusable dynamical patterns across diverse games, PhysCode addresses the limitations of domain-specific controls and imprecise language-based actions. This could impact the design of general-purpose action interfaces for various embodied AI applications, including robotics.
- **The IPR Paradigm for Scalable Physical Reasoning**: The proposed Interactive Physical Reasoner (IPR) paradigm demonstrates a method for reinforcing a VLM's policy with world-model rollouts in a shared latent action space. This blend allows the agent to internalize physical and causal regularities directly from interactive consequences, rather than relying solely on static data. This approach offers a pathway for agents to steadily improve their physical reasoning abilities as they accumulate interactive experience.
- **Empirical Demonstration of Generalization and Scaling**: The results show that IPR performs robustly across diverse game types, from primitive intuition to goal-driven reasoning, and achieves strong zero-shot transfer to unseen games. The observation that performance scales with the number of training games and interaction steps supports the hypothesis that interactive learning can lead to scalable and transferable physical knowledge. This suggests a viable path towards agents that can adapt to novel interactive environments without extensive fine-tuning.
- **Bridging Gaps in Existing AI Paradigms**: IPR effectively combines the strengths of VLMs (goal-driven reasoning), world models (predictive imagination), and RL (optimization from imagined rewards). This integration helps mitigate the individual limitations of these approaches, such as VLM's lack of foresight, world models' tendency to overfit visual patterns, and RL's sample inefficiency or brittle generalization.
- **Future Directions for Embodied AI**: The findings suggest a promising direction for developing interactive agents that acquire reusable physical and causal knowledge. While currently limited to game environments and short-horizon imagination, the framework provides a foundation for future work, including extending this paradigm to real-world interactive environments and robotic tasks, potentially leading to more intelligent and adaptable robotic systems.
