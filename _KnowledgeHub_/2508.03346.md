# Making Slow Thinking Faster: Compressing LLM Chain-of-Thought via Step Entropy

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from The Chinese University of Hong Kong and Huawei Technologies introduces "step entropy" to quantify the informational contribution of each reasoning step in large language models. The method enables significant compression of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories by identifying and pruning redundant, low-entropy steps, achieving up to 57% token reduction while maintaining or improving accuracy on mathematical benchmarks.

## Problem

- Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting in LLMs, while enhancing reasoning accuracy, often generates verbose and redundant reasoning paths, leading to increased inference latency and computational costs.
- Current CoT methods lack a systematic and principled way for LLMs to discern crucial reasoning steps from superfluous ones.
- Previous explicit CoT compression techniques are often heuristic or token-level, failing to capture the semantic integrity of reasoning steps.

## Method

- Developed "==step entropy==," an information-theoretic metric that quantifies the uncertainty of an LLM when generating each reasoning step, with low entropy indicating redundancy.
- Implemented an entropy-based pruning strategy that identifies and replaces up to 80% of the lowest-entropy steps with a `[SKIP]` token to create compressed CoT training data.
- Designed a two-stage training approach, combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for imitation and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a multi-objective reward function, to enable LLMs to autonomously generate compressed CoTs during inference.

## Results

- Experiments demonstrated that pruning up to **80%** of low-entropy steps maintained consistent accuracy, validating redundancy.
- The two-stage training enabled autonomous compressed CoT generation, achieving **16%** to **57%** reductions in thinking tokens across various models (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3, QwQ) and mathematical benchmarks (GSM8k, AIME 2024).
- The method maintained or slightly improved Pass@1 accuracy on several benchmarks, for instance, increasing DeepSeek-R1-**7B** accuracy on GSM8k from **80.36%** to **80.82%**, and Qwen3-**8B** on AIME 2024 from **79.31%** to **81.48%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial majority (up to 80%) of reasoning steps generated by LLMs in CoT are informationally redundant and can be pruned without sacrificing final answer accuracy, as shown by low step entropy.
> - Step-level pruning based on entropy is significantly more effective and preserves semantic integrity better than direct token-level pruning for CoT compression.
> - A balanced multi-objective reward function in reinforcement learning, incorporating correctness, skip ratio, skip number penalty, and response length penalty, is crucial for training LLMs to generate adaptively compressed and accurate reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models have revolutionized complex reasoning tasks, largely due to techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. By externalizing intermediate reasoning steps, CoT-enabled models (often referred to as Large Reasoning Models or LRMs, such as DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3) can break down problems, leading to improved accuracy across various domains like mathematics, coding, and logic. However, a significant drawback of this "slow thinking" approach is the generation of verbose and often redundant reasoning paths. This verbosity translates directly into increased inference latency, higher computational costs, and diminished efficiency, posing a substantial barrier to the scalable and practical deployment of LLMs. Prior research has identified this issue, noting instances of "overthinking" where excessively long CoTs do not yield proportional gains in accuracy.

The broader research landscape addressing this inefficiency can be broadly categorized into two main avenues:

1. **Implicit or Latent Reasoning:** This approach aims to internalize reasoning steps, either by fine-tuning models to perform reasoning without explicit verbalization (e.g., iCOT, COCONUT) or by distilling reasoning processes into the model's hidden states (e.g., knowledge distillation, dynamic latent compression). While highly efficient, these methods sacrifice the interpretability and verifiability that are key advantages of explicit CoT.

2. **Explicit CoT Compression:** This direction focuses on shortening the generated text of the reasoning chain at various granularities:
 - **Token-level pruning:** Removing individual tokens from the input context or enabling controllable token-level skipping during generation.
 - **Chunk-based compression:** Compressing larger segments of the reasoning chain.
 - **Length-constrained tuning:** Integrating penalties into reinforcement learning (RL) reward functions to encourage shorter CoTs or developing architectures tailored for length-compressible CoTs.
 - **Learning to skip steps:** Recent efforts have explored teaching LLMs to skip certain reasoning steps.

This paper positions itself firmly within the explicit CoT compression paradigm, but with a novel and principled contribution. Unlike previous methods that might prune based on token counts, length constraints, or arbitrary skipping, this work introduces an *information-theoretic* metric—step entropy—to systematically identify and quantify the informational contribution of each reasoning step. The authors argue that operating at the "step level" better mimics human cognition (skipping entire thoughts rather than just words) and that an explicit entropy signal provides a verifiable, semantic basis for pruning. By focusing on preserving the benefits of explicit reasoning while drastically improving efficiency, this research carves a distinct path that differentiates it from both latent reasoning strategies and less principled explicit compression techniques. The introduction of a two-stage training strategy to autonomously generate compressed CoTs further enhances its practical relevance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the inherent trade-off in current LLM reasoning: while Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting significantly boosts performance on complex multi-step problems, it often leads to overly verbose and redundant thought processes. This verbosity results in substantial practical drawbacks, including:
- **Increased inference latency:** Longer outputs take more time to generate.
- **Higher computational costs:** Generating more tokens consumes more computational resources.
- **Diminished overall efficiency:** Both latency and cost hinder the scalability and economic viability of LLM deployment.

The authors observe that humans naturally omit obvious or predictable thoughts when solving problems, focusing only on "key milestones." They identify a fundamental gap in current LLM capabilities: the lack of a systematic and principled method for LLMs to identify which reasoning steps are crucial and which are superfluous.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of this research are:

1. **Introduce and Validate Step Entropy:** To propose "step entropy" as a novel, principled metric that quantifies the informational contribution of individual reasoning steps within a CoT trajectory, and to theoretically analyze its relationship with the final solution.
2. **Empirically Demonstrate Redundancy:** To provide strong empirical evidence that steps identified as having low entropy are indeed highly redundant and can be pruned without significant loss of accuracy.
3. **Develop an Entropy-Based Compression Framework:** To build a practical CoT compression framework that leverages step entropy to selectively remove redundant steps.
4. **Enable Autonomous Compressed CoT Generation:** To design and implement a novel two-stage training strategy that allows LLMs to autonomously learn to generate compressed reasoning trajectories during inference, incorporating special `[SKIP]` tokens for redundant steps.
5. **Enhance LLM Inference Efficiency:** To significantly improve the inference efficiency of LLMs (in terms of token usage and computational cost) while rigorously preserving or even improving final answer accuracy.
6. **Advance Understanding of Reasoning Structures:** To offer profound implications for practical LLM deployment and contribute to a deeper theoretical understanding of the underlying reasoning structures employed by large language models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is built upon a novel entropy-based metric and a two-stage training strategy to enable autonomous CoT compression.

#### 3.1. Step Entropy Formulation and Theoretical Justification

The core of the method is **step entropy**, a metric designed to quantify the informational contribution of each reasoning step. The foundational premise is that steps generated with high confidence (low uncertainty) by the LLM are more likely to be redundant.

1. **Segmentation:** A generated CoT sequence is first segmented into distinct steps, `C = (S1, S2..., SN)`.
2. **Token-level Entropy:** During autoregressive generation, for each token `t_i,j` within a step `S_i`, the model produces a probability distribution `p(·|c_i,j)` over its vocabulary. The Shannon entropy `H(t_i,j | c_i,j)` is calculated from this distribution, representing the model's uncertainty at that specific token generation.
3. **Step Entropy Calculation:** The step entropy `H(S_i | S_<i)` is defined as the sum of the token-level entropies for all tokens within that step. A high step entropy implies high uncertainty during generation, while low step entropy indicates deterministic generation.
4. **Theoretical Justification:** The authors provide a theoretical argument using conditional mutual information `I(S_j; A | ~S_j) ≤ H(S_j | S_<j)`. This inequality suggests that when the entropy of a step `S_j` is low, its conditional mutual information with the final answer `A` (given all other steps) is also minor. This implies a weak relationship between the low-entropy step and the final solution, thereby denoting it as less informative and potentially redundant. This theoretical grounding underpins the empirical pruning strategy.

#### 3.2. Low-Entropy Steps Pruning Strategy (for Static Compression & Data Generation)

Based on the step entropy, a practical CoT compression approach is proposed for generating compressed CoTs and preparing training data:

1. **Full CoT Generation:** An LRM (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) generates a complete CoT trajectory for a given problem, typically within `<think>` and `</think>` tags, with steps delimited by `\n\n`.
2. **Step Entropy Calculation:** The step entropy `H(S_i)` is computed for each extracted step.
3. **Entropy-Based Pruning:** Steps are ranked by their entropy scores in ascending order. A proportion `κ` of the lowest-entropy steps are identified for pruning. These pruned steps are replaced with a special `[SKIP]` token, while high-entropy steps are retained. An empirically determined `κ = 0.8` (80% lowest-entropy steps) is used for data generation.
4. **Inference with Compressed CoT:** The modified CoT (containing `[SKIP]` tokens) is then used in conjunction with the user query to prompt the model to generate only the final answer.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Training Strategy (for Autonomous Compressed CoT Generation)

To enable LLMs to autonomously generate compressed reasoning during inference, the authors propose a two-stage training methodology:

- **Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
 - **Objective:** To teach the model to imitate the compressed reasoning style.
 - **Data:** A large dataset of (problem, compressed CoT) pairs is created using the 80% entropy-based pruning strategy on existing mathematical problems (DeepScaleR and OpenR1-Math datasets), filtering for manageable sequence lengths.
 - **Process:** The model is fine-tuned on this dataset by minimizing cross-entropy loss, learning to predict compressed reasoning paths and to strategically insert `[SKIP]` tokens. This stage provides a robust initialization.

- **Stage 2: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**
 - **Objective:** To further optimize the model's ability to balance accuracy and efficiency, moving beyond static imitation to reward-driven learning.
 - **Method:** GRPO (a reinforcement learning technique) is employed. For each input prompt, the model generates a group of `K` completions. A policy `π_θ` is then optimized to maximize a composite reward function `R(C)` for each generated completion `C`.
 - **Composite Reward Function `R(C)`:** This function sums four components:
 1. **Correctness Reward (`R_correctness`):** A large positive reward (+2.0) for generating the correct final answer, otherwise 0.0.
 2. **Skip Ratio Reward (`R_skip ratio`):** Tiered rewards based on the ratio of `[SKIP]` tokens to total steps. Higher ratios (e.g., ≥ κ_high = 0.8) receive a higher reward (+1.0), intermediate ratios (κ_low = 0.5 to κ_high) receive a moderate reward (+0.5), and lower ratios receive 0.0. This encourages aggressive but controlled compression.
 3. **Skip Number Penalty (`R_sn`):** A penalty (-1.0) if the number of `[SKIP]` tokens exceeds a threshold (`τ_skip num = 100`). This prevents degenerate behavior where the model might over-generate `[SKIP]` tokens.
 4. **Response Length Penalty (`R_rl`):** A penalty (-1.0) for responses exceeding a maximum token length (`τ_length = 3500`). This encourages overall conciseness.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

The base LRM used is DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B. SFT involves 3 epochs with DeepSpeed Stage 2 and LoRA. GRPO uses 10k samples, DeepSpeed Stage 3, LoRA, and AdamW optimizer, with specific hyperparameters for group size (`G=14`) and KL coefficient (`0.04`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several significant findings, demonstrating the efficacy and generalizability of the proposed step entropy-based compression framework.

#### 4.1. Optimal Pruning Ratio Validation

Controlled experiments on DeepSeek-R1-7B using samples from DeepScaleR revealed critical insights into pruning strategies (Figure 1):
- **Low-Entropy Steps Pruning:** Final answer accuracy remained stable and unaffected even when **up to 80%** of the lowest-entropy steps were masked. Beyond this threshold, accuracy began to decline, eventually converging to "no-thinking" performance. This strongly validates that a vast majority of low-entropy steps are indeed redundant.
- **High-Entropy Steps Pruning:** Accuracy degraded immediately and severely upon masking even a small fraction of steps (e.g., dropping below "no-thinking" performance beyond 40% mask ratio), indicating these steps are critical for reasoning.
- **Random Steps Pruning:** Performance fell between the two extremes, confirming the strategic advantage of entropy-based pruning.
Based on these findings, the core strategy of pruning 80% of the lowest-entropy steps was established.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness and Generalizability of Low-Entropy Pruning

Extensive experiments across multiple models (DeepSeek-R1-7B, DeepSeek-R1-14B, Qwen3-8B, QwQ-32B) and mathematical benchmarks (GSM8k, Math500, AIME 2024, AIME 2025) demonstrated (Table 1 & 5):
- **Substantial Token Reductions:** The method consistently achieved significant reductions in "thinking tokens," ranging from 0.6% (DeepSeek-R1-7B on GSM8k) to an impressive 55.1% (QwQ-32B on AIME 2024).
- **Accuracy Maintenance or Improvement:** Crucially, these efficiency gains were achieved while maintaining or even slightly improving Pass@1 accuracy on several benchmarks (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-7B on GSM8k: 80.36% → 80.82%; Qwen3-8B on AIME 2024: 79.31% → 81.48%).
- **Cross-Architectural Consistency:** The consistent performance across diverse model families (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3, QwQ) highlights that step entropy is a robust and generalizable principle for identifying redundancy, independent of specific model architecture or size.

#### 4.3. Scalability for Dataset Creation

The 80% low-entropy pruning strategy was successfully applied to large-scale datasets (40k DeepScaleR and 90k OpenR1-Math), confirming its robustness and scalability for creating compressed CoT training data while preserving accuracy (Table 2).

#### 4.4. Step-level vs. Token-level Pruning

A comparative analysis (Figure 4) showed that step-level pruning is superior:
- Our step-pruning method maintained baseline accuracy even after removing up to 40% of total thinking tokens.
- Direct token-level pruning, however, led to a sharp and immediate decline in performance after just a 20% token mask ratio. This supports the argument that the reasoning step, not individual tokens, is the appropriate semantic unit for compression to preserve structural and semantic integrity.

#### 4.5. Two-Stage Training Strategy Performance

The two-stage SFT + GRPO training strategy proved highly effective for autonomous compressed CoT generation (Table 3):
- **SFT Stage:** Successfully taught the model a compressed reasoning style, leading to dramatic initial token reductions (e.g., 43% on GSM8k) with minimal accuracy impact.
- **SFT + GRPO Stage:** Further optimized the balance between compression and accuracy. The fine-tuned model achieved overall impressive token reductions (e.g., 44% on GSM8k, 57% on AIME 2024) while maintaining or improving accuracy (e.g., GSM8k accuracy slightly increased from 78.54% to 79.15%). This demonstrated the model's ability to learn adaptive, reward-driven compression.

#### 4.6. Reward Components Ablation Study

An ablation study on the GRPO reward function (Table 4) underscored the necessity of each component:
- Using only correctness and skip ratio rewards led to catastrophic performance degradation and paradoxically *increased* token usage, indicating degenerate policies.
- Adding the skip number penalty restored competitive accuracy.
- The complete reward function (including response length penalty) was crucial for achieving optimal balance, maintaining near-baseline accuracy while delivering substantial efficiency gains. This confirms that effective CoT compression requires carefully balanced multi-objective optimization.

#### 4.7. Domain-aware Experiments (Appendix)

Experiments on MMLU benchmarks (College Medicine and High School History) with DeepSeek-R1-7B and QwQ-32B (Tables 7 & 8 in Appendix) revealed domain-specific compression characteristics:
- **Generalizability:** The step entropy method generalized beyond mathematical tasks, effectively compressing CoTs in knowledge-based domains.
- **High Redundancy in Knowledge-based Tasks:** Both models showed that MMLU tasks contain substantial redundancy, tolerating significant compression levels (e.g., DeepSeek-R1-7B maintained/improved accuracy with 18.6-20.8% token reduction on College Medicine/History). QwQ-32B maintained perfect accuracy on High School History even at 90% pruning, with up to 20.1% token reduction.
- **Domain-Specific Tolerance:** Factual recall/historical reasoning (High School History) showed higher compression tolerance than analytical/medical reasoning (College Medicine), suggesting varying degrees of redundancy across cognitive processes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes significant contributions with both practical and theoretical implications for the field of large language models.

#### 5.1. Practical Significance

- **Enhanced LLM Efficiency and Cost Reduction:** By demonstrating that up to 80% of reasoning steps can be pruned without accuracy degradation, the work offers a powerful solution to the critical challenge of inference costs and latency in LLMs. This can lead to substantial reductions in computational overhead, making LLMs more economically viable, scalable, and environmentally sustainable for real-world deployment. The achieved token reductions (16-57%) translate directly into faster response times and lower API costs for LLM providers and users.
- **Preserved Interpretability and Verifiability:** Unlike latent reasoning approaches, this method maintains the explicit nature of Chain-of-Thought, providing a transparent and verifiable reasoning path. This is crucial for applications requiring explainability, debugging, or auditing, allowing practitioners to retain the benefits of explicit reasoning while gaining significant efficiency.
- **Autonomous and Adaptive Compression:** The two-stage training strategy enables LLMs to autonomously learn to generate compressed CoTs during inference. This moves beyond static compression to dynamic, on-the-fly optimization, making the models inherently more efficient without requiring external post-processing. This capability is a vital step towards self-optimizing LLMs.

#### 5.2. Theoretical Significance

- **Deeper Understanding of LLM Reasoning Redundancy:** The work provides fundamental insights into the internal workings of LLM reasoning. The discovery that a vast majority of generated thought steps are informationally redundant challenges assumptions about the necessity of verbose reasoning and informs future research into how LLMs construct and refine their reasoning processes.
- **Principled Metric for Informational Value:** The introduction of "step entropy" as a principled, information-theoretic metric to quantify the contribution of individual reasoning steps offers a robust tool for analyzing and optimizing LLM outputs. This metric can be applied to other aspects of LLM generation beyond CoT, potentially leading to new methods for content summarization, task-specific adaptation, or even evaluating the "quality" of reasoning.
- **Foundation for More Efficient Architectures:** The findings suggest that future LLM architectures and training methodologies could be designed to inherently generate more concise and less redundant reasoning, moving towards "sparse thinking" models from the ground up, rather than relying solely on post-hoc compression.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors acknowledge certain limitations, which also hint at future research avenues:
- **Threshold Generalizability:** The empirically derived 80% pruning threshold, while effective on tested models (DeepSeek-R1, Qwen3, QwQ) and mathematical tasks, may not be universally optimal for all LLM architectures or diverse application domains. This calls for the development of **adaptive, task-aware, and model-aware compression strategies** that can dynamically adjust the pruning ratio based on context.
- **Domain Validation:** While initial domain-aware experiments on MMLU tasks showed promising results and interesting domain-specific patterns (e.g., higher compression tolerance in factual recall tasks), the primary validation remains in mathematical reasoning. Further extensive validation across a wider array of domains (e.g., code generation, scientific reasoning, creative writing) would solidify the method's universal applicability.

In conclusion, this paper presents a compelling and well-validated approach to address a critical efficiency problem in LLMs. By grounding CoT compression in the information-theoretic concept of step entropy and integrating it with a robust two-stage training strategy, the authors offer a practical and principled solution that significantly advances the state of the art in efficient and interpretable large language model reasoning.
