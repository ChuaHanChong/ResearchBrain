# HOIDiffusion: Generating Realistic 3D Hand-Object Interaction Data

> [!summary] Summary
> HOIDiffusion generates realistic 3D hand-object interaction data in two stages: a pretrained grasp VAE produces MANO grasp trajectories from an object mesh, then a fine-tuned Stable Diffusion conditioned on rendered normals, segmentation and projected hand skeletons plus text synthesizes images, with background regularization preserving prompt editability. It reaches FID 55.22, 95.49% contact recall, and improves downstream 6D pose estimation.

## Problem

- 3D hand-object datasets need mocap rigs, multi-camera setups or CAD-based manual labeling, so they cover only dozens of objects and cannot scale.
- Text-to-image diffusion models produce physically implausible grasps, extra or missing fingers, and offer no handle on the underlying 3D shape and pose.
- Detector- and segmenter-based auto-annotation pipelines give imprecise 3D labels, while repetitive source video keeps pose and appearance diversity low.

## Method

- Stage one applies pretrained ==GrabNet== to predict ending ==MANO== grasp parameters from an object mesh, then spherical linear interpolation from a zeroed start pose yields a grasping trajectory.
- **Structure control** injects three rendered conditions, a ==MiDaS==-estimated normal map, hand-object segmentation, and projected hand skeleton, via per-condition ResNet encoders that shift ==Stable Diffusion U-Net== encoder features.
- **Appearance regularization** co-trains on a background buffer of scenery images with blank conditions as ==classifier-free guidance==, weight 1, plus ==LLaVA==-generated detailed captions for text supervision.

## Results

- FID improves to **55.22** versus **63.71** for fine-tuned LDM, **87.99** ControlNet, **134.40** DreamBooth; substituting NOCS renders lifts DualPoseNet 10-degree-2cm accuracy from **47.8** to **55.0**.
- Mean hand contact recall reaches **95.49%** against **89.85%** for ControlNet and **84.33%** for Affordance Diffusion, with hand keypoint PCK **0.85** versus **0.67**.
- Ablations: dropping normals, keypoints or segmentation raises FID from **77.64** to **82.40**, **81.93** and **78.57**; removing regularization cuts CLIPScore from **0.79** to **0.66**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Disentangling geometry conditioning from text appearance turns a generative model into an annotation-consistent data engine: labels come free from the rendering that drives the conditions.
> - Fine-tuning on a small clean-background dataset collapses style diversity; mixing synthetic background images with blank conditions restores the base model's prompt editability.
> - Synthetic realism, not only correct geometry, drives downstream perception gains, since overly artificial renders limit RGB encoders more than pose supervision does.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The understanding of human hand-object interactions is a persistent area of research in computer vision, with applications spanning robotics, virtual reality (VR), and augmented reality (AR). Prior work has focused on collecting large-scale 2D video datasets of hand-object interactions and, more recently, 3D annotated datasets using multi-camera setups or refined labeling approaches with CAD models. However, these 3D data collection processes are time-consuming and resource-intensive, resulting in datasets with limited diversity in objects and interaction types. This scarcity of high-quality, diverse 3D hand-object interaction data impedes the development of robust perception systems.

Concurrently, generative models, particularly diffusion models, have demonstrated significant advancements in synthesizing realistic images from text descriptions. Models like DALL-E2 and Stable Diffusion can generate photorealistic images, but they frequently struggle with accurately depicting fine-grained details of hand poses and object interactions, sometimes producing geometrically implausible results such as hands with an incorrect number of fingers. Furthermore, these models typically lack precise control over the 3D geometry and pose of generated objects and hands, limiting their utility for creating structured 3D data.

HOIDiffusion addresses these gaps by proposing a method to leverage the realism of diffusion models while providing explicit 3D geometric control. It aims to generate synthetic, diverse, and realistic 3D hand-object interaction data (images accompanied by 3D ground truths), thereby mitigating the limitations of real-world data collection. The work positions itself at the intersection of 3D vision, hand-object interaction understanding, and advanced generative modeling, offering a controlled synthesis approach that disentangles geometry from appearance. This enables the creation of data that is both visually realistic and geometrically accurate, which is a requirement for training downstream perception tasks like 6D object pose estimation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for generating realistic and diverse 3D hand-object interaction data, which includes both high-quality images and their corresponding 3D ground truths. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Data Scarcity and Scalability Issues**: The collection of real-world 3D hand-object interaction data is inherently challenging due to hardware constraints, the complexity of 3D annotation, and the time-consuming nature of the process. Existing datasets often contain a limited number of objects and interaction types. The motivation is to overcome this scarcity by enabling the scalable generation of synthetic data that is both diverse and realistic.

2. **Enhancing Control in Image Synthesis**: While state-of-the-art diffusion models can generate visually compelling images, they often lack the ability to produce physically plausible hand-object interactions and offer limited control over the geometric configurations. A key objective is to provide a more controllable synthesis process where users can specify both the 3D geometric structure (hand and object shape and pose) and the visual style (appearance and background) in a disentangled manner. This level of control is crucial for tailoring data to specific research or application needs.

3. **Improving Realism and Geometric Plausibility**: General diffusion models frequently generate artifacts in hand depictions (e.g., incorrect finger count or unnatural poses) and may not accurately represent physical interactions with objects. A central motivation is to generate images that are not only photorealistic but also geometrically consistent and physically plausible, ensuring that the synthesized data is reliable for downstream tasks.

4. **Facilitating Downstream Perception Tasks**: The ultimate motivation extends beyond data generation to its practical utility. The researchers aim to demonstrate that the generated 3D data can effectively improve the performance of perception systems, specifically by training models for tasks such as 6D object pose estimation. This highlights the practical value of a scalable, controllable, and realistic data generation pipeline for advancing computer vision and robotics.

In essence, the work seeks to bridge the gap between high-fidelity image generation and precise 3D structural control, creating a novel pipeline for data synthesis that addresses fundamental challenges in hand-object interaction research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed HOIDiffusion framework operates as a two-stage pipeline designed to synthesize realistic hand-object interaction images conditioned on both 3D geometric structure and text descriptions.

**Stage 1: Hand Grasping Trajectory Generation**
The first stage focuses on generating the 3D geometric configuration of the hand and object. Given a 3D object model (potentially with random transformations), the system needs to establish a plausible hand grasp.
1. **Grasp Pose Prediction**: A pre-trained GrabNet model is utilized. GrabNet is a variational autoencoder (VAE) trained on large-scale 3D physical data, capable of predicting diverse and accurate hand grasp poses (represented by MANO parameters, which include joint pose and hand shape) given an object mesh. It has demonstrated strong generalization capabilities to unseen objects.
2. **Trajectory Interpolation**: After obtaining the final grasp pose from GrabNet and an initial hand starting position (approximated vertically above the contact surface with zeroed MANO parameters), spherical linear interpolation is employed. This process generates a smooth hand-grasping trajectory, providing intermediate hand poses leading to the final grasp. Along this trajectory, various 3D ground-truth annotations, such as segmentation masks, normal maps, and depth maps, can be obtained through rendering.

**Stage 2: Hand-Object-Interaction Image Synthesis**
The second stage involves training a conditional diffusion model to synthesize an RGB image based on the 3D geometric configurations from Stage 1 and a text description for appearance. This stage is composed of two main components: Structure Control and Appearance Regularization.

1. **Structure Control**: To ensure accurate and physically plausible image generation, the system distills three types of geometric conditions from the 3D hand-object configurations:
 - **Hand Keypoint 2D Projection ($s_i$)**: To address common issues in diffusion models like missing or distorted fingers, the 3D hand skeleton information is projected onto the 2D image plane. This provides precise visual guidance for hand position and pose.
 - **Hand-Object Segmentation ($m_i$)**: Segmentation maps are used to define clear boundaries between the hand and the object, and the background. This helps mitigate interference between regions and provides coarse object shape priors, crucial for accurate object representation and occlusion handling.
 - **Estimated Normal Map ($n_i$)**: A normal map is derived from the 3D geometry to capture surface details and lighting interactions. This condition is essential for maintaining geometric consistency and realism in the synthesized image.
 These three conditions ($s_i$, $m_i$, $n_i$) are encoded and fed into the fine-tuned Stable Diffusion U-Net architecture. The original forward process of the denoising diffusion model is modified to take these conditions as inputs, allowing the model to generate images consistent with the specified 3D structure. The conditions are injected into the U-Net encoder, shifting the feature maps of each encoding channel, similar to adapter methods.

2. **Appearance Regularization**: To prevent the diffusion model from overfitting to the style of the training dataset (which might have uniform backgrounds) and to preserve its text-driven editing capabilities, an appearance regularization strategy is introduced:
 - **Background Buffer**: In addition to the primary hand-object interaction (HOI) training dataset, batches of high-quality scenery images are synthesized using the pre-trained text-to-image diffusion model. The prompts for these images are generated by the large language model ChatGPT, creating a "background buffer."
 - **Regularization during Training**: During training, these background images are intermittently utilized for regularization. For these images, vacant skeleton projection and mask conditions are provided, which act as classifier-free guidance corresponding to the background region in HOI data. This encourages the model to retain its ability to generate diverse backgrounds and styles.
 - **Detailed Text Descriptions**: The LLaVA large multimodal model is used to caption the HOI training images with detailed descriptions of foreground appearance and background. These descriptions are processed by a CLIP text encoder, making the model aware of diverse text prompts and enhancing its ability to map text to visual appearance.
 The training loss function is augmented to include a regularization term that accounts for the background buffer, ensuring that the model leverages both geometry information from new conditional variables and rich appearance information from the pre-trained model while maintaining text-driven control over appearance. The Stable Diffusion decoder is trained, while other U-Net parameters and the text encoder are fixed.

**Implementation Details**: The DexYCB dataset is chosen for training due to its high-quality images, varied viewpoints, and human demonstrations. A learning rate of 10⁻⁵ is used for fine-tuning over approximately 50,000 steps. The model leverages eight A100 GPUs for training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The HOIDiffusion model demonstrates capabilities in generating realistic and diverse 3D hand-object interaction data, exhibiting both precise geometry control and flexible appearance manipulation.

**Image Synthesis Quality and Appearance Alignment:**
- **Quantitative Metrics**: The model's generation quality was assessed using FID, sFID, IS, and CLIPScore metrics.
 - HOIDiffusion achieved an FID of 55.22, sFID of 91.28, IS of 7.73, and CLIPScore of 0.78.
 - These scores generally indicate superior performance compared to baseline methods, including fine-tuned LDM (FID 63.71, sFID 119.10, IS 6.81, CLIPScore 0.68), DreamBooth (FID 134.40, sFID 92.82, IS 7.99, CLIPScore 0.75), and ControlNet (FID 87.99, sFID 248.56, IS 6.60, CLIPScore 0.77).
 - The lower FID and sFID values indicate higher fidelity to real datasets, while a higher IS suggests greater diversity in generated images. The improved CLIPScore demonstrates a stronger alignment between generated images and their corresponding text prompts, particularly for appearance control.

**Hand Pose Evaluation:**
- **Geometric Consistency**: Hand grasping status and pose precision were evaluated using Hand Contact Recall and Percentage of Correct Keypoints (PCK).
 - HOIDiffusion achieved a mean Hand Contact Recall of 95.49% across various objects (mug, bowl, bottle, can) and a PCK of 0.85.
 - This performance surpasses baselines such as LDM (72.91% recall, 0.15 PCK), DreamBooth (73.72% recall, 0.10 PCK), ControlNet (89.85% recall, 0.67 PCK), and Affordance Diffusion (84.33% mean recall).
 - These results indicate that HOIDiffusion is capable of synthesizing images with accurate grasps and firm contact between the hand and object, as well as precise hand poses that are consistent with the provided 2D skeleton projections.

**Geometry and Appearance Disentanglement:**
- **Geometry Manipulation (Qualitative)**: The model demonstrates the ability to generate diverse images for various object shapes, poses, and hand skeletons while maintaining geometric consistency. Figure 4 illustrates the synthesis of realistic hand-object interactions for objects like mugs, cans, bottles, and bowls, accurately aligning with the input 3D geometry derived from estimated normal maps, skeleton projections, and segmentation masks. The generated images exhibit a consistent "laboratory or stereo environment" style when using plain prompts, reflecting the training dataset's characteristics.
- **Appearance Control (Qualitative)**: With fixed geometric conditions, the model shows robustness in depicting diverse backgrounds and object appearances by leveraging text prompts (Figure 5 and 8). The appearance regularization component enables the model to effectively disentangle style from structure, allowing for flexible style transformations (e.g., "on the beach," "in the snow," "metallic object," "Gothic-style object") without distorting the underlying geometry. This indicates the model retains knowledge from pre-trained models for novel appearance generation.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Structural Control**: Evaluation of individual structural conditions (normal map, hand keypoint projection, hand-object segmentation) showed that each component contributes to the overall performance. The full HOIDiffusion model (FID 77.64 for 1k images) consistently outperformed versions missing any one of these modules (e.g., w/o estimated normal maps: FID 82.40; w/o hand keypoint projection: FID 81.93; w/o hand-object segmentation: FID 78.57). This confirms the necessity of all three physical conditions for robust and accurate generation.
- **Appearance Regularization**: The regularization strategy significantly improved the model's text-editing ability. Without regularization, the fine-tuned model tended to overfit to the training dataset's style (e.g., laboratory environment), resulting in a lower CLIPScore (0.66) and reduced diversity. The inclusion of appearance regularization increased the CLIPScore to 0.79, demonstrating its effectiveness in maintaining flexible style control.

**Applications:**
- **Video Generation**: By leveraging zero-shot video generation techniques (refactoring self-attention layers to cross-attention modules between an anchor frame and current frames), the model could synthesize relatively smooth hand-grasping trajectories from sequential geometric inputs. This effectively mitigates flickering issues observed in directly concatenated frames.
- **Downstream Tasks (6D Object Pose Estimation)**: The generated data was used to augment or replace synthetic data in the NOCS dataset for training categorical 6D object pose estimators (SPD and DualPoseNet). When trained with HOIDiffusion-generated images, DualPoseNet showed improved performance across all metrics (IoU@25, IoU@50, IoU@75, 5°2cm, 5°5cm, 10°2cm, 10°5cm) compared to training with the original NOCS dataset. SPD also showed improvements in the `cm` metrics. This demonstrates the practical utility of HOIDiffusion's realistic synthetic data in enhancing perception systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HOIDiffusion framework represents a significant contribution to the field of computer vision and robotics by addressing the persistent challenge of generating realistic and diverse 3D hand-object interaction data. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Alleviating Data Scarcity**: The primary impact is the provision of a scalable method for generating synthetic 3D hand-object interaction data. This directly tackles the limitations of real-world data collection, which is often expensive, time-consuming, and yields datasets with limited diversity. By generating data that comes with rich 3D ground truths, HOIDiffusion can provide a virtually limitless source of training examples, potentially accelerating research and development in areas reliant on such data.

2. **Enhanced Control and Realism**: The disentanglement of geometry and appearance in the synthesis process offers unprecedented control over data generation. Researchers and developers can precisely specify 3D hand and object poses, shapes, and interactions, while also controlling visual styles, textures, and backgrounds through text prompts. This capability ensures that the generated data is not only photorealistic but also geometrically accurate and physically plausible, addressing a common shortcoming of previous generative models.

3. **Advancement in Perception Systems**: The demonstrated effectiveness of HOIDiffusion's generated data in improving downstream tasks, specifically 6D object pose estimation, highlights its practical utility. This indicates that synthetic data can be a viable and valuable resource for training robust perception models, potentially reducing the reliance on costly real-world annotations and opening avenues for training models in challenging or rare scenarios.

4. **Broader Applications in Robotics and VR/AR**: The ability to generate controlled, realistic hand-object interaction data has direct implications for robotics (e.g., training robotic grasping, manipulation planning), virtual reality, and augmented reality (e.g., creating realistic interactive environments, training hand tracking systems). It could enable more robust and generalizable solutions in these domains where understanding and simulating human-like interactions are crucial.

5. **Foundation for Future Research**: HOIDiffusion establishes a strong foundation for future research in generative models for 3D content. The approach of integrating explicit 3D structural guidance with powerful 2D diffusion models could be extended to other complex interaction types, multi-body interactions, or even full-body human-object interactions, pushing the boundaries of what generative models can achieve in structured 3D synthesis.

In conclusion, HOIDiffusion offers a powerful and flexible tool for creating high-quality, diverse, and controllable 3D hand-object interaction data. Its potential to overcome data acquisition bottlenecks and enhance the performance of various perception systems could have a transformative impact on several related fields.
