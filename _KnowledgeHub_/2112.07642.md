# EgoBody: Human Body Shape and Motion of Interacting People from Head-Mounted Devices

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoBody is a large-scale dataset for 3D human pose, shape and motion of an interaction partner seen from a head-mounted device. A HoloLens2 (RGB, depth, gaze, head/hand tracking) is calibrated to a multi-Azure-Kinect rig, and SMPL-X meshes are fitted to multi-view RGB-D plus egocentric frames in scanned 3D scenes. It yields 125 sequences and the first egocentric interactee pose benchmark, where fine-tuning cuts errors sharply.

## Problem

- Third-person motion-capture datasets replicate neither AR/VR viewpoints nor two-person interaction, and most capture a single isolated subject with no interaction partner.
- Egocentric datasets give only coarse action labels, only the camera wearer's pose, skeletons without body shape or 3D scene, or no 3D ground truth at all.
- Pose-and-shape methods assume near-full-body views, so they degrade under the truncation, motion blur, and partners entering or leaving the headset field of view.

## Method

- Capture rig pairs a HoloLens2 (RGB, long-throw depth, eye gaze, head and hand tracking) with three to five hardware-synchronized Azure Kinects, plus iPhone-scanned 3D scene meshes.
- ==Keypoint-based calibration refinement== optimizes the Kinect-HoloLens2 extrinsics by minimizing reprojection error between fitted ==SMPL-X== joints and OpenPose detections in egocentric frames.
- Two-stage ==SMPL-X fitting==: per-frame multi-view objective with joint, depth, prior, contact and collision terms, then ==LEMO==-based temporal fitting adding smoothness, friction and egocentric reprojection terms.

## Results

- Dataset spans **125** sequences, **36** subjects, **15** indoor scenes: **219,731** multi-view frames, **199,111** egocentric frames, **175,611** with the interactee visible, split subject-disjointly.
- State-of-the-art methods average **77%** higher joint error on EgoBody than on 3DPW; fine-tuning cuts MPJPE **42%** for SPIN, **36%** for METRO, **18%** for EFT.
- Fine-tuned models transfer: You2Me PA-MPJPE drops from **152.8** to **87.9** (SPIN) and **117.7** to **88.2** (METRO); blur-level error deviation falls **27.3** to **2.4**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Benchmark saturation on third-person data hides brittleness: the same methods spread widely apart once viewpoint truncation and embodied camera motion are introduced.
> - Truncation, not blur, dominates egocentric error; most methods stay insensitive to blur except extreme cases, and error tracks per-joint invisibility closely.
> - Global-feature regression fails on extremities even when visible, arguing for local, part-attentive image features rather than a single pooled representation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "EgoBody" addresses a critical gap in the research landscape concerning the understanding of human social interactions, particularly from an egocentric perspective. This field is highly relevant for numerous applications, including assistive robotics, augmented reality (AR), and virtual reality (VR), where machines often perceive the world through head-mounted devices (HMDs). A foundational challenge in these applications is accurately estimating the 3D body pose, shape, and motion of an interaction partner (referred to as the "interactee") from the HMD wearer's viewpoint.

**Existing Limitations:**
Historically, 3D human pose and shape (3DHPS) estimation research has predominantly relied on datasets captured from a *third-person view*. These datasets, such as Human3.6M, AMASS, PROX, Panoptic Studio, and 3DPW, have been instrumental in advancing the field. However, they typically focus on single individuals, are captured in controlled studio environments, or do not accurately replicate the unique challenges of egocentric perception.

In the realm of *egocentric vision*, existing datasets primarily suffer from several limitations:
- **Lack of 3D Ground Truth:** Many egocentric datasets (e.g., Ego4D, EPIC-Kitchens) provide extensive video but lack high-quality 3D ground truth for human pose, shape, and motion, instead focusing on action recognition or coarse interaction labels.
- **Focus on Camera Wearer:** Some datasets, like Mo2Cap2 and xR-EgoPose, provide 3D pose for the *camera wearer* but do not consider the interaction partner.
- **Limited Interaction Data:** Datasets that do involve interactees, such as HPS or You2Me, often have very limited interaction scenarios, lack 3D body shape information, or miss crucial scene context. You2Me, for instance, provides 3D skeletons but no body shape or 3D scene.
- **Specific Challenges of HMDs:** Egocentric views from HMDs introduce unique difficulties that traditional 3DHPS methods are not equipped to handle, including severe body truncations (due to close proximity and limited field of view), motion blur (exacerbated by head movement), and people entering/exiting the frame dynamically.

**EgoBody's Contribution:**
EgoBody directly fills this significant gap by proposing **the first large-scale, multi-modal egocentric dataset** that provides high-quality 3D human shape, pose, and motion ground truth for *both* interacting subjects, alongside comprehensive egocentric data streams and accurate 3D scene reconstructions. By employing Microsoft HoloLens2 headsets synchronized with a multi-Kinect rig, the dataset captures rich egocentric modalities (RGB, depth, eye gaze, head/hand tracking) and precise 3D body meshes (using SMPL-X) for interactions in complex 3D scenes. This holistic approach addresses the deficiencies of prior work and provides a unique resource for developing robust models for egocentric human interaction understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the EgoBody project is to overcome the severe data scarcity that has hindered research into understanding social interactions from egocentric views. This primary goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Enabling Robust Egocentric 3D Human Pose, Shape, and Motion Estimation:** The core motivation stems from the recognition that current state-of-the-art 3DHPS methods, primarily developed and evaluated on third-person view data, perform poorly when applied to egocentric views. This performance degradation is attributed to the unique challenges of HMD data, such as frequent body truncations, motion blur, and dynamic viewpoints. EgoBody aims to provide the necessary data to train and evaluate models that can robustly handle these specific egocentric conditions.

2. **Facilitating Research in Assistive Robotics and AR/VR:** Understanding human interactions from an embodied, first-person perspective is crucial for the advancement of assistive robotics (e.g., robots that can intelligently collaborate with humans) and immersive AR/VR experiences (e.g., realistic social interactions within virtual environments). The lack of suitable datasets has been a major bottleneck for developing these applications, and EgoBody seeks to provide a foundational resource.

3. **Providing Comprehensive Multi-modal Data Streams:** Recognizing that real-world interactions involve more than just visual cues, the authors aimed to collect rich multi-modal data. This includes not only RGB and depth from the HMD but also crucial eye gaze tracking, and head/hand tracking information. The inclusion of eye gaze is particularly motivated by its potential to reveal human attention during interactions, opening new avenues for studying the relationship between attention, social cues, and motion.

4. **Delivering High-Quality 3D Ground Truth for *Both* Interacting Subjects:** Unlike many existing egocentric datasets that only annotate the camera wearer or provide coarse 2D/3D skeletons without body shape, EgoBody's objective is to reconstruct accurate 3D full-body meshes (using the expressive SMPL-X model) for *both* the camera wearer and the interaction partner. This level of detail is essential for a comprehensive understanding of body dynamics and non-verbal communication during interactions.

5. **Integrating 3D Scene Context:** To provide a holistic understanding of the physical world around the camera wearer and to enable studies of human-scene interactions, EgoBody also includes accurate 3D reconstructions of the indoor environments where interactions take place.

6. **Establishing a Standardized Benchmark:** By proposing EgoBody, the authors aim to create the first standardized benchmark for 3DHPS estimation of the interactee from egocentric views during social interactions. This benchmark will allow for systematic evaluation and comparison of new methods, driving progress in the field.

In essence, the motivation for EgoBody is to bridge the gap between the critical need for egocentric human interaction understanding in advanced applications and the current limitations imposed by insufficient and unrepresentative training data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The creation of the EgoBody dataset involves a sophisticated multi-modal data acquisition and ground-truth reconstruction pipeline, designed to capture high-quality human motions and scene context from both egocentric and third-person perspectives.

#### 3.1. Data Acquisition Setup

The system leverages a combination of consumer-grade hardware for a lightweight and deployable setup:
- **Egocentric Capture:** A **Microsoft HoloLens2 headset** is worn by one subject (the "camera wearer"). It records multi-modal egocentric data streams, including RGB video (1920x1080 at 30 FPS), long-throw depth frames (512x512 at 1-5 FPS), and integrated eye gaze, head, and hand tracking data (60 FPS). The Research Mode API is used to access these streams.
- **Third-Person Multi-View Capture (Motion Capture Rig):** Three to five **Azure Kinect cameras** are positioned around the interaction space. These cameras capture synchronized multi-view RGB (1920x1080) and depth (640x576) frames at 30 FPS, serving as the primary input for ground-truth 3D reconstruction. The cameras are fixed during recording.
- **3D Scene Representation:** The indoor environments are pre-scanned using an **iPhone12 Pro Max running a 3D Scanner app**. This generates triangulated 3D meshes of the scenes, providing essential spatial context.

#### 3.2. Calibration and Synchronization

Accurate spatial and temporal alignment of all sensor data is paramount:
- **Kinect-Kinect Synchronization:** Azure Kinects are hardware-synchronized using audio cables.
- **Kinect-HoloLens2 Synchronization:** Since hardware synchronization is not possible, a software-based approach is used. A flashlight visible to all devices signals the first frame, allowing for temporal alignment based on timestamps.
- **Spatial Calibration:** Initial spatial calibration for Kinect-Kinect and Kinect-HoloLens2 is performed using a checkerboard. This is refined by an Iterative Closest Point (ICP) algorithm on the reconstructed scene point clouds from depth sensors.
- **Keypoint-Based HoloLens2 Calibration Refinement:** A crucial innovation is a proposed refinement scheme for the Kinect-HoloLens2 calibration. This addresses inaccuracies stemming from imperfect HoloLens2 factory depth calibration and tracking drift. It optimizes the transformation between the Kinect world coordinate frame and the HoloLens2's fixed coordinate system by minimizing the 2D error between projected 3D SMPL-X joints (from the multi-Kinect reconstruction) and 2D OpenPose detections in the egocentric RGB frames. This ensures accurate ground truth across all sensor coordinates.
- **Scene Registration:** The pre-scanned 3D scene meshes are registered into the coordinate frame defined by one of the Kinect cameras (Cam1).

#### 3.3. Ground-Truth Acquisition Pipeline (Marker-less Motion Capture)

The pipeline reconstructs the 3D full-body pose, shape, and motion of both subjects using the expressive **SMPL-X body model**, which defines parameters for global translation (γ), body shape (β), pose (θ), and facial expression (ϕ).
- **Data Preprocessing:**
 - **2D Joint Detection:** OpenPose is used to detect 2D body joints in all RGB frames (Kinect and HoloLens2).
 - **Human Segmentation:** Mask-RCNN and DeepLabv3 are applied to Kinect depth frames to segment human body point clouds.
 - **Data Cleaning:** Manual inspection and cleaning are performed to address false positives, ensure consistent subject identification across frames/views, and fix inaccurate 2D joint detections caused by occlusions. Depth information from Kinects is also used to filter out spurious 2D joint detections.
- **Per-Frame Fitting:** An initial optimization stage estimates SMPL-X parameters for each subject and frame independently. This minimizes an objective function balancing:
 - **Multi-view 2D Joint Error (E_J):** Distance between projected SMPL-X joints and OpenPose detections across all Kinect views.
 - **Depth Term (E_D):** Discrepancies between the estimated body surface and segmented human depth point clouds.
 - **Priors (E_prior):** Body pose, shape, and expression priors.
 - **Scene Interaction Terms (E_contact, E_coll):** Encourage scene-body contacts and penalize collisions.
- **Temporally Consistent Fitting:** To improve smoothness and temporal consistency, a second optimization stage is run. This leverages **LEMO priors** to obtain realistic human motion dynamics and further incorporates egocentric 2D joint detections (E_J_ego) from the HoloLens2 for the interactee (when visible). This stage also includes temporal smoothing (E_smooth) and contact friction (E_fric) terms. The optimization is performed for each subject separately.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that EgoBody provides highly accurate, multi-modal, and spatially/temporally consistent 3D ground truth, uniquely tailored for egocentric human interaction understanding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The EgoBody dataset and its associated benchmark experiments yield several key findings that highlight the challenges and opportunities in egocentric 3D human pose and shape estimation.

#### 4.1. Dataset Statistics and Characteristics

- **Scale and Diversity:** EgoBody comprises 125 sequences from 36 subjects (18 male, 18 female) engaging in diverse social interactions across 15 indoor scenes. This totals 219,731 multi-view (MV) frames and 199,111 egocentric (EgoSet) RGB frames, with 175,611 EgoSet frames having the interactee visible (EgoSet-interactee).
- **Ground Truth:** For every MV frame, the dataset provides 3D full-body pose and shape annotations (SMPL-X parameters) for both subjects, along with the 3D scene mesh. EgoSet frames include HoloLens2 head, hand, and eye tracking data, plus depth frames.
- **Interaction Characteristics:** The dataset covers interaction distances between 0.90m and 3.48m, and diverse social interaction categories (cooperation, social exchange, conflict, conformity, etc.).
- **Joint Visibility and Attention:** Analysis reveals that egocentric views frequently suffer from severe body truncation, particularly for lower body parts. Knees are invisible in ~50% of frames, and feet in ~80%. The camera wearer's eye gaze is strongly focused on the interactee, predominantly on the upper body joints, which correlates with the lower visibility of the interactee's lower body.

#### 4.2. Benchmark Evaluation of State-of-the-Art (SoTA) 3DHPS Methods

The paper establishes the first benchmark for 3DHPS estimation of the interactee from egocentric images, evaluating SoTA methods (CMR, SPIN, LGD, METRO, PARE, EFT) on the EgoBody's EgoSet-interactee test set.
- **Significant Performance Drop:** SoTA methods, despite their strong performance on third-person view datasets like 3DPW, exhibit a substantial performance drop on EgoBody, with an average of **77% higher 3D joint error (PA-MPJPE)**. This highlights that existing models do not generalize well to the unique egocentric perspective.
- **Sensitivity to Motion Blur:** While most methods show surprising insensitivity to mild motion blur, their performance significantly degrades in heavily blurred scenarios (sharpness score < 10).
- **Impact of Joint Visibility (Truncation):** This is identified as a major challenge. All methods perform worse with decreased body visibility. PARE (employing part attention) and EFT (using aggressive crop augmentations) show relatively better robustness against truncation, suggesting that explicit or implicit handling of partial body views is crucial. The analysis shows a direct correlation between lower joint visibility (e.g., feet, knees) and higher estimation errors for those body parts. Errors for wrist joints, despite good visibility, also remain high, pointing to a general challenge in accurately estimating extremities.

#### 4.3. Baseline Improvement through Fine-tuning on EgoBody

To demonstrate the utility of the EgoBody dataset, three representative SoTA methods (SPIN, METRO, EFT) were fine-tuned on the EgoBody training set.
- **Substantial Performance Gains:** Fine-tuning leads to significant improvements across all metrics:
 - SPIN-ft achieves a 42% lower MPJPE and 35% lower V2V error.
 - METRO-ft achieves a 36% lower MPJPE and 33% lower V2V error.
 - EFT-ft achieves an 18% lower MPJPE and 14% lower V2V error.
- **Increased Robustness to Motion Blur:** Fine-tuned models not only achieve lower errors but also demonstrate significantly increased robustness to motion blur, with standard deviations of MPJPE across sharpness levels dramatically reduced (e.g., SPIN from 27.3 to 2.4, EFT from 11.1 to 1.3).
- **Ineffectiveness of Simple Augmentation:** Additional experiments with motion blur augmentation on original training sets of SPIN showed no improvement, indicating that EgoBody's real-world egocentric data and high-quality ground truth are essential, not just synthetic augmentations.

#### 4.4. Cross-Dataset Generalization (You2Me)

The effectiveness of fine-tuning on EgoBody is further validated through a cross-dataset evaluation on You2Me.
- **Generalizable Improvements:** Models fine-tuned on EgoBody consistently show improved performance on You2Me (e.g., SPIN-ft (87.9) significantly outperforms original SPIN (152.8) in PA-MPJPE). This indicates that EgoBody helps models learn features and robustness that generalize to other egocentric datasets and scenarios.

In summary, the findings reveal a clear deficiency of current 3DHPS methods in egocentric settings, primarily due to truncation and motion blur. Crucially, EgoBody provides the necessary data to significantly overcome these limitations, demonstrated by the substantial performance improvements achieved through fine-tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EgoBody dataset represents a significant advancement in the field of computer vision and human-computer interaction, with far-reaching potential impacts:

1. **Addresses a Critical Data Gap:** The most profound significance of EgoBody is its role in filling a long-standing and critical data gap. It provides the first large-scale, multi-modal egocentric dataset with high-quality 3D human pose, shape, and motion ground truth for *both* interacting subjects, along with precise 3D scene reconstructions and eye-gaze data. This unique combination of features makes it an indispensable resource for researchers tackling the complexities of egocentric social interaction understanding.

2. **Enables Development of Robust Egocentric 3DHPS Models:** By providing data that directly addresses the unique challenges of HMD-captured egocentric views—such as severe body truncation, motion blur, and dynamic viewpoints—EgoBody will be instrumental in developing the next generation of 3D human pose and shape estimation models. The paper already demonstrates that fine-tuning existing state-of-the-art models on EgoBody leads to substantial performance gains and increased robustness, validating its utility as a training resource. This will drive the creation of more capable algorithms for real-world applications.

3. **Advances Social Interaction Understanding from a First-Person Perspective:** The dataset’s focus on diverse social interaction scenarios, combined with detailed 3D body models and eye-gaze tracking, opens up entirely new research avenues. Researchers can now deeply investigate human attention dynamics, non-verbal cues, and subtle behavioral patterns during social interactions from the camera wearer's perspective. This could lead to breakthroughs in understanding human behavior, emotions, and intentions in social contexts.

4. **Accelerates Innovation in AR/VR and Assistive Robotics:** The insights and models developed using EgoBody have direct and immediate applications in immersive technologies and intelligent robotics. In AR/VR, accurate understanding of an interactee’s 3D body and gaze enables more realistic virtual social encounters and intuitive user interfaces. For assistive robotics, it allows robots to better perceive and anticipate human actions and intentions, leading to safer, more effective collaboration in shared physical spaces.

5. **Establishes a New Benchmark:** EgoBody sets a new standard for evaluating 3D human pose and shape estimation in egocentric settings. This benchmark will facilitate systematic comparisons of new methods, ensuring measurable progress and healthy competition within the research community. The identified limitations of current SoTA methods on egocentric data also provide clear directions for future research.

6. **Potential for Multidisciplinary Research:** The rich modalities (RGB-D, gaze, 3D body models, 3D scenes) and diverse interaction scenarios make EgoBody valuable not only for computer vision but also for fields like cognitive science, psychology, and human-computer interaction, allowing for multidisciplinary studies into the nature of social communication and perception.

Looking forward, the authors suggest enriching the dataset with more participants, audio recordings, and natural language descriptions of motions, which would further amplify its impact and foster even more complex research into human behavior and interaction. In essence, EgoBody is poised to be a foundational dataset that will significantly propel the fields of egocentric vision, human interaction understanding, and their practical applications in AR/VR and robotics.
