# MiMo-Embodied: X-Embodied Foundation Model Technical Report

> [!summary] Summary
> Xiaomi's MiMo-Embodied introduces the first open-source cross-embodied foundation model, unifying autonomous driving and embodied AI tasks. It achieves state-of-the-art performance across 29 benchmarks in both domains, demonstrating positive knowledge transfer between diverse physical environments.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for embodied AI and autonomous driving are highly specialized and fragmented, leading to a significant 'domain gap' between indoor robotics and outdoor driving tasks.
- This fragmentation prevents cross-domain generalization, limiting a single model's ability to understand and interact across diverse physical environments.
- A comprehensive evaluation framework was lacking to assess the overall performance and generalizability of embodied VLMs across both autonomous driving and embodied AI tasks.

## Method

- Developed **MiMo-Embodied**, a unified ==VLM architecture== comprising a ==Vision Transformer==, ==MLP Projector==, and Large Language Model, building on the **MiMo-VL** foundation.
- Constructed a diverse multimodal dataset spanning general visual-language understanding, embodied AI tasks (==affordance==, planning, spatial), and autonomous driving scenarios (perception, prediction, planning).
- Implemented a ==progressive four-stage training strategy==, including ==supervised fine-tuning== for embodied AI and autonomous driving, ==Chain-of-Thought reasoning==, and ==Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning==, to mitigate interference and foster synergistic learning.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance across **17** embodied AI benchmarks, demonstrating strong capabilities in affordance prediction, task planning, and spatial understanding.
- Set new performance records across **12** autonomous driving benchmarks, excelling in environmental perception, status prediction, and driving planning tasks.
- Qualitative evaluations showed superior performance in real-world embodied navigation, manipulation, and autonomous driving trajectory planning compared to other advanced VLMs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Autonomous driving and embodied AI tasks exhibit strong positive transfer and mutually reinforce one another when integrated within a unified model using a progressive training strategy.
> - A multi-stage training approach, incorporating specialized fine-tuning, Chain-of-Thought reasoning, and Reinforcement Learning, is crucial for mitigating cross-domain interference and achieving synergistic performance improvements.
> - The specialized cross-embodiment training not only improves task-specific performance but also enhances general visual understanding capabilities without compromise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The broader research landscape in artificial intelligence is witnessing advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which are demonstrating capabilities in multimodal perception, understanding, and reasoning. Within this context, specialized embodied VLMs have emerged, focusing on specific interactions with the physical world. These include models for embodied AI (e.g., RoboBrain, VeBrain) that prioritize indoor tasks like task planning and spatial understanding, and models for autonomous driving (e.g., RoboTron-Drive, DriveLMM-o1) that focus on outdoor scenarios such as environmental perception, status prediction, and driving planning.

While these specialized models have shown progress within their respective domains, a significant domain gap exists between indoor embodied AI and outdoor autonomous driving. This fragmentation limits the cross-domain generalization of capabilities and hinders the development of unified systems capable of effective interaction in diverse physical environments. Existing evaluations for VLMs typically assess partial capabilities within a single domain, lacking a comprehensive framework to evaluate cross-embodiment performance. MiMo-Embodied positions itself within this landscape by addressing these limitations, proposing a unified VLM designed to integrate and achieve proficiency in both autonomous driving and embodied AI tasks. It aims to bridge the identified domain gap and provide a comprehensive evaluation across these traditionally disparate areas.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The development of MiMo-Embodied is driven by two primary challenges identified within the current VLM research landscape:

1. **Lack of Unified Embodied VLMs:** Existing VLMs are typically specialized for either embodied AI (focused on indoor robotics) or autonomous driving (focused on outdoor vehicle operation). This specialization results in a fragmented approach, where a unified VLM capable of integrating both autonomous driving and embodied AI functionalities does not readily exist. This fragmentation restricts the generalization of crucial capabilities, such as spatial understanding and complex reasoning, across diverse indoor and outdoor environments. Consequently, the capacity of models to interact effectively with the physical world in dynamic and varied settings is limited. The objective is to develop a single VLM that can competently handle tasks from both domains, thereby fostering a more generalized understanding and interaction capability.

2. **Absence of Comprehensive Cross-Embodiment Capability Evaluation:** Current evaluation methodologies for VLMs primarily assess partial capabilities within either autonomous driving or embodied AI. There is a perceived lack of a unified, comprehensive cross-embodiment evaluation framework that can thoroughly assess the overall performance and synergistic benefits of specialized embodied VLMs across these combined domains. The motivation here is to establish a new standard for evaluating such integrated models, allowing for a holistic assessment of their performance across a wide spectrum of tasks pertinent to both fields.

To address these challenges, the key objectives for MiMo-Embodied include:
- Developing a unified VLM capable of merging autonomous driving and embodied AI tasks into a single model.
- Enhancing understanding and reasoning in dynamic physical environments by leveraging positive transfer between the two domains.
- Establishing new performance benchmarks across a wide range of tasks in both embodied AI and autonomous driving, outperforming existing open-source, closed-source, and specialized baselines.
- Providing a detailed analysis of the model design and training methodologies, including multi-stage learning, curated data construction, and CoT/RL fine-tuning, to facilitate further research.
- Open-sourcing the model and code to promote advancements in the field of cross-embodied foundation models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Architecture

The MiMo-Embodied architecture comprises three main components designed for processing diverse visual inputs and generating coherent responses:
1. **Vision Transformer (ViT):** This component is responsible for encoding various visual inputs, including single images, multiple images, and videos. It employs a self-attention mechanism to extract features and discern patterns and relationships within the visual data. The visual tokens produced are prepared for integration with the subsequent LLM. The ViT is adopted from MiMo-VL to process high-resolution inputs.
2. **Projector:** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) serves as the projector, mapping the high-dimensional visual encodings from the ViT into a latent space compatible with the Large Language Model (LLM). This step ensures seamless integration of visual and textual information while preserving essential characteristics. The projector is initialized using pre-trained weights from MiMo-VL.
3. **Large Language Model (LLM):** This component performs text understanding and reasoning. Once visual tokens are projected into its latent space, the LLM processes this combined multimodal input to interpret data and generate contextually relevant responses. The LLM backbone is initialized using pre-trained weights from MiMo-VL, leveraging its existing vision-language alignment and reasoning capabilities.

#### 3.2. Training Dataset

The training dataset for MiMo-Embodied is designed to provide a broad foundation in general visual-language understanding, along with specialized data for embodied AI and autonomous driving scenarios. It encompasses three main categories:

1. **General Dataset:** This foundational dataset leverages the MiMo-VL training corpus, which includes high-resolution images, videos, long texts, long documents, and synthetic reasoning data. It supports:
 - **Visual Grounding:** For fine-grained object-level localization and cross-modal alignment.
 - **Document and Chart Comprehension:** For structural understanding of complex layouts, tables, and information graphics.
 - **Video Understanding:** For temporal reasoning, event captioning, and dynamic scene analysis.
 - **Multimodal Reasoning:** For complex logical inference across mathematical, scientific, and symbolic domains.

2. **Embodied AI Dataset:** This dataset enhances capabilities for physical interaction and reasoning and is divided into:
 - **Affordance Prediction:** Data from PixMo-Points (fine-grained localization, object counting), RoboAfford (object-level and scene-level interaction reasoning), and RoboRefIt (referential understanding in cluttered scenes).
 - **High-level Task Planning:** Data from Cosmos-Reason1 (grounding language in physical reality, multi-step physical reasoning), EgoPlan-IT (human-like planning from a first-person perspective), and RoboVQA (long-horizon reasoning through QA pairs).
 - **Spatial Understanding:** Data from SQA3D and self-curated 3D datasets (3D Question Answering, 3D bounding box localization), VLM-3R (spatio-temporal QA, embodied route planning), RefSpatial (spatial reasoning for robotic referring tasks), and EmbSpatial (spatial relationship identification, object localization).

3. **Autonomous Driving Dataset:** This dataset focuses on core functional modules for autonomous driving systems:
 - **Environmental Perception:** Data from CODA-LM (general perception, corner-case objects), scenery dataset (driver actions, observations), DriveLM, related research, nuScenes-QA (scene graphs, object recognition), MME-RealWorld (object identification, driver attention), IDKB (traffic laws), MAPLM (road-centric analysis), and DRAMA (regional object localization).
 - **Status Prediction:** Data from DriveLM (motion prediction, object interaction) and MME-RealWorld (traffic elements intention prediction).
 - **Driving Planning:**
 - **Action Decision:** Data from DriveLM (safe/unsafe action classification, goal action definition), MME-RealWorld (ego intention prediction), and IDKB (vehicle control knowledge).
 - **Driving Reasoning:** Data from CODA-LM (driving advice, reasoning logic), Nuinstruct (high-level decision-making), related research (action justifications), (video-based action reasoning), DriveLM (planning and reasoning), and IDKB (driver responsibility, defensive driving).

#### 3.3. Training Strategy

MiMo-Embodied employs a progressive four-stage training strategy building upon MiMo-VL:
1. **Stage 1: Embodied AI Supervised Fine-tuning:** Establishes core vision-language understanding and embodied reasoning by fine-tuning on general data (MiMo-VL corpus) and curated embodied AI datasets (affordance, task planning, spatial understanding).
2. **Stage 2: Autonomous Driving Supervised Fine-tuning:** Specializes the model for autonomous driving by integrating driving-specific samples, focusing on multi-view spatial reasoning, temporal consistency, and complex traffic scenario analysis (environment comprehension, status prediction, driving planning). This stage uses a mix of autonomous driving and embodied data.
3. **Stage 3: Chain-of-Thought Reasoning Supervised Fine-tuning:** Enhances reasoning transparency and logical coherence using Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methodology. Sampled training data includes explicit reasoning chains for analyzing context, generating solutions, evaluating alternatives, and justifying decisions across both embodied and autonomous driving domains.
4. **Stage 4: Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning:** Further refines precision and reliability using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm. This stage optimizes correctness and response quality for diverse multimodal tasks, addressing corner cases and failure modes. Task-specific reward signals are used for multi-choice visual reasoning, spatial grounding, and format compliance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Quantitative Comparisons on Benchmarks

MiMo-Embodied was evaluated against various open-source, closed-source, and specialized VLMs across 17 embodied AI benchmarks and 12 autonomous driving benchmarks.

##### 4.1.1. Embodied AI Benchmarks

- **Affordance Prediction:** MiMo-Embodied achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance across all five affordance prediction benchmarks (RoboRefIt, Where2Place, VABench-Point, Part-Afford, RoboAfford-Eval), demonstrating capabilities in fine-grained affordance reasoning. For instance, it outperformed other embodied VLMs by a notable margin on VABench-Point, Part-Afford, and RoboAfford-Eval.
- **Task Planning:** The model demonstrated advanced task planning capabilities, outperforming other models on RoboVQA in causal inference and goal-oriented outcomes. It also achieved competitive performance on the long-horizon planning benchmark EgoPlan2.
- **Spatial Understanding:** MiMo-Embodied achieved SOTA results on CV-Bench (comprehensive spatial intelligence) and led performance on RoboSpatial, RefSpatial-Bench, and the relation subset of CRPE (spatial relationship reasoning and language grounding). It also demonstrated competitive performance on EmbSpatial, SAT, and the VQA subset of MetaVQA.

##### 4.1.2. Autonomous Driving Benchmarks

- **Perception Capability:** MiMo-Embodied achieved strong performance across all perception benchmarks (CODA-LM, DriveAction, DRAMA, MME-RealWorld, IDKB, DriveLM, MAPLM, nuScenes-QA, LingoQA, OmniDrive). It delivered SOTA results in panoramic semantic understanding and robustness in local perception scenarios, adapting to diverse real-world perception demands.
- **Prediction Capability:** The model showed strong performance on both MME-RealWorld (single image) and DriveLM (multi-view images), accurately capturing individual behavioral intentions and modeling complex multi-agent interactions.
- **Planning Capability:** MiMo-Embodied achieved superior performance across all planning-oriented benchmarks (DriveLM, MME-RealWorld, IDKB for Action Decision; LingoQA, CODA-LM, OmniDrive, NuInstruct, BDD-X for Driving Reasoning). This indicated a strong capacity to generate accurate, context-appropriate driving decisions and produce coherent, interpretable reasoning aligned with traffic logic.

##### 4.1.3. General Visual Understanding (Appendix A.1)

After multi-stage embodied and autonomous driving fine-tuning, MiMo-Embodied's general visual understanding capabilities were preserved and, in some cases, improved. It achieved an absolute gain of 9.71 points on MMMU-Pro standard and 9.88 points on MMMU-Pro vision. It also delivered competitive results on Mantis and AI2D, achieved the best performance on V*, and improved over MiMo-VL on PixmoCount (4.15 points) and CountBench (2.03 points). This suggests a positive transfer of learning from specialized training to fundamental visual comprehension.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Evaluation in Real-world Tasks

##### 4.2.1. MiMo-Embodied for Embodied Navigation & Manipulation

- **Embodied Navigation:** In long-horizon navigation tasks, MiMo-Embodied inferred spatial regions and target objects from high-level instructions (e.g., "I want to sleep"). It successfully marked points on top-down maps and identified target objects in first-person images, demonstrating effective spatial reasoning and object localization in household scenarios (e.g., locating a bed, vacuum cleaner, plants, toilet). Comparative visualizations showed that MiMo-Embodied achieved precise center localization for target objects and comprehended plural queries, outperforming GPT-4o, Qwen2.5-VL, and RoboBrain-2.0, which sometimes produced scattered or ambiguous localizations.
- **Embodied Manipulation:** The model performed hierarchical pick-and-place tasks, decomposing high-level instructions into precise actions. This included distinguishing graspable parts (e.g., pot lid, spoon handle), determining placement locations, and executing tasks requiring counting and spatial reasoning (e.g., grasping the "third orange from the left" and placing it "between the oranges"). MiMo-Embodied consistently identified functionally appropriate regions, while other models exhibited limitations in identifying graspable components or relational positions for object arrangement.

##### 4.2.2. MiMo-Embodied for Autonomous Driving

- **Trajectory Planning on Public Benchmark (NAVSIM):** MiMo-Embodied consistently outperformed InternVL3-8B and ReCogDrive-Large (similar scale models) on the NAVSIM planning dataset across metrics such as No At-Fault Collision (NC), Drivable Area Compliance (DAC), Time-to-Collision (TTC), Comfort (Conf), Ego Process (EP), and Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS). MiMo-Embodied+RL achieved a PDMS of 91.0, compared to ReCogDrive-Large-RL's 90.4. The model demonstrated the ability to handle diverse situations like intersection turning, U-turns, car-following, and lane-change overtaking.
- **Trajectory Planning on Proprietary Data:** Evaluated on a large-scale proprietary dataset, MiMo-Embodied consistently outperformed the Qwen2.5-VL 7B baseline across 11 complex driving scenarios (e.g., U-Turn, Detour VRUs, Safety-critical LC), showing a mean relative performance improvement of 7.7% in L2 distance error reduction. Gains were most pronounced in complex, interactive maneuvers, indicating superior reasoning in challenging driving situations.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

A systematic ablation study validated the effectiveness of the multi-stage training strategy.
- Training solely on embodied data (MiMo-VL w/ Embodied) showed strong performance in both domains but autonomous driving tasks suffered.
- Training solely on autonomous driving data (MiMo-VL w/ AD) excelled in its domain but exhibited a significant performance drop on embodied tasks.
- Directly training embodied and autonomous driving tasks together (MiMo-VL w/ Embodied+AD) improved embodied tasks but slightly reduced autonomous driving performance.
- MiMo-Embodied, using the proposed multi-stage training strategy, achieved a 4% improvement on embodied tasks (average 63.7%) and an 8.1% improvement on autonomous driving tasks (63.4%) compared to the direct combined training (Embodied+AD). This indicates that the multi-stage approach effectively mitigates cross-domain task interference and enables synergistic improvements without sacrificing single-task performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

MiMo-Embodied represents a step towards unifying traditionally distinct domains of embodied AI and autonomous driving within a single vision-language model. Its open-source release aims to contribute to the research community by providing a foundation model that demonstrates significant positive transfer and mutual reinforcement between these domains.

The model's demonstrated superior performance across a wide array of embodied AI benchmarks (affordance prediction, task planning, spatial understanding) and autonomous driving benchmarks (environmental perception, status prediction, driving planning) indicates its capacity to set new performance standards. Its ability to maintain and even enhance general visual understanding capabilities after specialized training suggests that the integration of diverse embodied experiences can lead to more robust and general-purpose AI systems.

Potential impacts include:
- **Accelerated Research:** Providing an open-source, high-performing cross-embodied foundation model can accelerate research in unified AI for physical interaction.
- **Enhanced Autonomous Systems:** The model's capabilities in complex reasoning, multi-agent interaction, and interpretable driving planning could lead to more intelligent, safer, and adaptable autonomous vehicles.
- **Advanced Robotics:** Improved affordance prediction, long-horizon task planning, and spatial understanding could enable more capable and versatile robotic systems for navigation and manipulation in complex environments.
- **Foundation for VLA Models:** MiMo-Embodied provides a basis for future Vision-Language Action (VLA) models in both embodied AI and autonomous driving, enabling more intuitive task execution through natural language and real-time response to dynamic scenarios.
- **Multi-Modal Integration:** The work lays groundwork for integrating additional modalities, such as 3D point clouds, to further enhance long-term planning, decision-making, and human-robot interaction.

Overall, MiMo-Embodied offers a unified framework and demonstrated capabilities that could influence the development of more generalist AI agents capable of operating effectively across a broader spectrum of real-world physical tasks.
