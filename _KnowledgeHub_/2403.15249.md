# Spectral Motion Alignment for Video Motion Transfer using Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST developed Spectral Motion Alignment (SMA), a framework that refines motion representations for video motion transfer using spectral analysis techniques like Fourier and Wavelet transforms. This approach significantly enhances motion accuracy and temporal consistency in generated videos, reducing artifacts and improving overall motion coherence across various diffusion-based video customization models.

## Problem

- Existing methods for distilling motion from video often struggle to capture global motion context, focusing primarily on local patterns.
- Pixel- or feature-space motion representations are highly susceptible to motion-independent spatial distortions (e.g., background changes, lighting shifts), leading to unreliable motion transfer.
- These limitations result in generated videos that lack coherent, overarching motion and may erroneously reproduce high-frequency artifacts.

## Method

- The Spectral Motion Alignment (**SMA**) framework refines motion representations by operating in the ==frequency domain==, integrating with existing diffusion-based video customization models.
- It employs a 1D Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) for "spectral global motion alignment," capturing multi-resolution motion dynamics across an entire sequence of frames.
- It utilizes a 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) for "spectral local motion refinement," aligning amplitude and phase spectra while weighting low-frequency components to mitigate local, non-motion-related distortions.

## Results

- **SMA** consistently improved **Text-Alignment** and **Temporal Consistency** metrics, and user study scores for Editing Accuracy, Temporal Consistency, and Motion Accuracy across MotionDirector, VMC (Show-1, Zeroscope), Tune-A-Video, and ControlVideo.
- Qualitative evaluations demonstrated that **SMA** generates more accurate and consistent motion patterns, better distinguishes dynamic from static objects, and reduces flickering artifacts compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the local (FFT-based) and global (Wavelet-based) spectral alignment components contribute significantly, with their combined use yielding the most substantial performance improvements in metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spectral analysis, specifically DWT and FFT, can robustly capture and refine motion information by disentangling meaningful motion dynamics from irrelevant high-frequency noise and capturing motion across multiple scales.
> - Combining global (Wavelet-based) and local (Fourier-based) spectral alignment components is crucial for comprehensively addressing both long-range motion coherence and artifact mitigation.
> - The SMA framework serves as an orthogonal enhancement, broadly compatible with diverse diffusion-based video customization models (T2V and T2I adapted for video), offering improved fidelity with negligible computational overhead.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of video customization has seen advancements with the emergence of diffusion models, which facilitate the adaptation of input video appearance while aiming to preserve its motion patterns. Current methodologies for distilling motion information from video frames primarily rely on estimating motion representations through frame differences or correlations in either pixel or feature space. Examples include approaches that leverage connectivity between latent frames or treat residual vectors between consecutive frames as "motion vectors" for fine-tuning pre-trained video diffusion models (VDMs).

However, these existing pixel- or feature-space methods have demonstrated limitations. They often struggle to capture the global context of motion, focusing instead on local patterns, which can lead to a fragmented understanding of whole-frame dynamics. Additionally, these representations are susceptible to motion-independent spatial distortions arising from abrupt changes in background, lighting, or other frame inconsistencies. Such artifacts can degrade the reliability of motion representations and be erroneously reproduced in generated videos.

This work addresses these identified limitations by introducing spectral analysis techniques, such as Fourier and Wavelet transforms, into the motion distillation process. While frequency-aware methods have been explored in visual generation for tasks like motion estimation, video compression, and architectural understanding in deep learning, their application to explicitly refine and align motion representations within the context of diffusion-model-based video motion transfer is a distinct contribution. The proposed Spectral Motion Alignment (SMA) framework positions itself as an enhancement that can be integrated with various existing diffusion-based motion customization models, operating orthogonally to their pixel or feature-space learning objectives. It aims to improve motion fidelity and temporal consistency by leveraging the frequency domain to filter artifacts and capture multi-scale motion dynamics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework that enhances the accuracy and reliability of motion transfer in video customization tasks utilizing diffusion models. This is achieved by refining and aligning motion representations within the spectral domain.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from two main limitations observed in current diffusion-based video motion transfer methods:

1. **Lack of Global Motion Context:** Existing methods, which often approximate motion by analyzing pixel-space or feature-space differences between consecutive frames, tend to focus on local motion patterns. This localized perspective can hinder the comprehensive understanding of whole-frame, long-range motion dynamics. Consequently, the models may fail to capture overarching motion trajectories or global transitions across multiple frames, leading to outputs that lack coherent, overarching motion. The researchers posit that a more holistic understanding of motion dynamics requires considering the global context inherent in a sequence of frames.

2. **Vulnerability to Motion-Independent Spatial Distortions:** The pixel- or feature-space residual vectors, while containing motion information, are also prone to incorporating disruptive variations unrelated to actual motion. These can include transient background changes, illumination shifts, camera jitters, or other inconsistencies between frames. When diffusion models are fine-tuned using these raw, artifact-laden motion representations, they may erroneously learn and reproduce these high-frequency artifacts, leading to less reliable and visually inconsistent motion transfer. The objective is to mitigate these motion-independent distortions to ensure that the learned representations primarily encode actual motion.

To address these challenges, the authors propose that motion information might be more robustly captured and refined by analyzing its inherent frequency components. This intuition motivates the development of a spectral domain approach to motion alignment, aiming to disentangle meaningful motion dynamics from irrelevant high-frequency noise and to capture motion across multiple scales.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces the Spectral Motion Alignment (SMA) framework, which refines and aligns motion representations in the frequency domain. The framework is designed to be compatible with existing diffusion-based video motion transfer models.

#### 3.1. Denoised Motion Vector Estimation

The initial step involves estimating motion representations from pixel space, following the approach of VMC (Jeong, Park, and Ye 2023). This method treats residual vectors between consecutive frames as motion information. Specifically, for an *n*-th frame, the motion vector at time *t* is defined as δv*n*t = v*n+1*t - v*n*t. The ground-truth motion vector in pixel space, δv*n*0, is derived using the diffusion kernel properties. Similarly, a denoised estimate of these motion representations, δv̂*n*0, is obtained using Tweedie's formula from the denoised estimate of the original frames. The baseline objective for motion transfer involves fine-tuning a pre-trained Video Diffusion Model (VDM) to align these ground-truth and estimated motion vectors through a pixel-space alignment loss (ℓ_align).

#### 3.2. Spectral Global Motion Alignment

To address the limitation of locally focused motion understanding, SMA incorporates a spectral global motion alignment component. This component aims to capture comprehensive motion dynamics across all frames.

- **Approach:** The method utilizes a 1D Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT), specifically the Haar wavelet, on a sequence of motion vectors. For each spatial pixel dimension *s* (e.g., (x,y) coordinate), a 1D array is formed from the motion vector components across all frames (N-1 length). This transformation allows for multi-resolution analysis of motion patterns.
- **Loss Function:** A global motion alignment loss (ℓ_global) is defined as the L1 distance between the wavelet coefficients of the ground-truth motion vector sequence (δv_0,s) and its denoised estimate (δv̂_0,s(t)). This encourages the learning of multi-scale motion dynamics by leveraging the rich wavelet-domain representations that consider global frame transitions.

#### 3.3. Spectral Local Motion Refinement

To mitigate the presence of high-frequency local distortions and non-motion-related artifacts in the estimated motion representations, SMA includes a spectral local motion refinement component.

- **Approach:** The method employs 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) on individual motion vectors. It aligns the amplitude and phase spectra of the ground-truth and predicted motion vectors.
- **Loss Functions:** Two losses are defined: ℓ_A_local for amplitude matching and ℓ_P_local for phase matching.
- **Weighting:** A frequency-domain weighting function, ω(a, b), is applied to these losses. This weighting prioritizes low-frequency components by assigning higher weights to central frequencies (representing coarser details) and lower weights to high-frequency components (which often correspond to noise or artifacts). This strategy is designed to prevent the fine-tuned VDM from overfitting to motion-independent high-frequency distortions.

#### 3.4. Overall Optimization and Inference Pipeline

The complete SMA framework integrates the baseline pixel-space alignment loss (ℓ_align) with the proposed spectral losses:

min_θ E[ℓ_align(δv_n_0, δv̂_n_0(t)) + λ_g ℓ_global(δv_0, δv̂_0(t)) + λ_l ℓ_local(δv_n_0, δv̂_n_0(t))]

Here, λ_g and λ_l are hyperparameters controlling the influence of the global and local spectral alignment terms, respectively. Upon optimizing the model parameters (θ) with this combined objective, inference is performed using new text prompts to transform appearances while preserving the learned motion patterns.

#### 3.5. Extension to Feature Space

The SMA framework is demonstrated to be adaptable beyond pixel-space motion vectors. It can be extended to diffusion features (e.g., from DIFT or DMT), where motion vectors are constructed from pairwise differences in space-time diffusion features. The spectral alignment objectives (ℓ_global and ℓ_local) are applied to these feature residuals, thereby demonstrating the generality of the approach.

#### 3.6. Experimental Settings

- **Dataset:** A curated dataset of 30 text-video pairs from DAVIS and WebVid-10M, featuring diverse motion types and subjects, with video lengths between 8 and 16 frames.
- **Base Models:**
 - Text-to-Video (T2V) Diffusion Models: Zeroscope (non-cascaded) and Show-1 (cascaded).
 - Text-to-Image (T2I) Diffusion Models adapted for video: Stable Diffusion v1-5 and ControlNet-Depth.
- **Baselines:** MotionDirector, VMC, DMT (for T2V); Tune-A-Video, ControlVideo (for T2I).
- **Hyperparameters:** Haar wavelets with 3 levels for 8-frame videos and 4 levels for 16-frame videos for DWT. Frequency domain weighting factor δ = 0.05. Loss weights λ_g = 0.4 and λ_l = 0.2 were used, with recommendations for fine-tuning.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Automatic Metrics:** Text-Alignment (average cosine similarity between target text prompt and generated frames using CLIP), Temporal Consistency (average cosine similarity of CLIP image features between all frame pairs).
 - **User Study:** 42 participants rated Editing Accuracy, Temporal Consistency, and Motion Accuracy on a 5-point scale.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents both qualitative and quantitative evidence demonstrating the efficacy of the Spectral Motion Alignment (SMA) framework across various diffusion-based video motion transfer models.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Comparisons

- **Improved Motion Transfer:** Visual comparisons (Figures 3, 4, 5, and supplementary figures) indicate that videos generated with SMA exhibit more accurate and consistent motion patterns compared to baselines. Without SMA, videos might capture the target appearance but fail to replicate the input motion faithfully, sometimes producing static objects that should be dynamic or vice versa.
- **Distinguishing Dynamic from Static Objects:** SMA demonstrates an ability to differentiate between moving and stationary elements within a scene. For example, in one instance, SMA correctly depicted an eagle moving from right to left while the ground remained static, whereas the baseline without SMA incorrectly showed the ground moving with the eagle.
- **Reduced Artifacts:** SMA contributed to alleviating flickering artifacts observed in foreground objects, particularly when integrated with frameworks like Tune-A-Video.
- **Enhanced Motion Accuracy:** For methods like ControlVideo, which use structural cues (e.g., depth maps) to maintain structural integrity, SMA further improves motion accuracy, suggesting that structural consistency alone is insufficient for precise motion capture.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparisons

The quantitative evaluations consistently indicate that SMA enhances the performance of various motion transfer frameworks across multiple metrics.

- **T2V Diffusion-based Frameworks (Table 1):**
 - When integrated with **MotionDirector**, SMA improved Text-Align (0.7550 to 0.8081), Temporal Consistency (0.9780 to 0.9784), and user study scores for Editing Accuracy (3.19 to 4.14), Temporal Consistency (3.07 to 3.89), and Motion Accuracy (2.67 to 3.88).
 - With **VMC (Show-1)**, SMA showed improvements in Text-Align (0.8066 to 0.8193), Temporal Consistency (0.9742 to 0.9776), and user study scores (3.25 to 3.85, 3.22 to 3.84, 2.72 to 3.92 respectively).
 - For **VMC (Zeroscope)**, similar improvements were observed: Text-Align (0.8223 to 0.8425), Temporal Consistency (0.9560 to 0.9578), and user study scores (2.95 to 4.07, 2.83 to 3.84, 2.47 to 4.07).
 - These results demonstrate SMA's consistent positive impact across different foundational T2V models and baseline methods.

- **T2I Video Diffusion Models (Table 2):**
 - For **Tune-A-Video**, SMA increased Text-Align (0.8289 to 0.8633), Temporal Consistency (0.8951 to 0.9568), and user study scores (2.71 to 3.41, 2.11 to 2.88, 2.27 to 3.25).
 - With **ControlVideo**, SMA improved Text-Align (0.8686 to 0.8781), Temporal Consistency (0.9451 to 0.9590), and user study scores (2.88 to 3.56, 2.25 to 3.16, 2.69 to 3.40).
 - A notable advantage was observed in motion accuracy across both T2I-based frameworks.

#### 4.3. Analysis of SMA Components

- **Artifact Mitigation (Figure 6):** Visualization of spatial frequency spectra and motion vectors revealed that pixel-space motion vectors often contain high-frequency artifacts (e.g., background textures, stair/fence patterns) unrelated to motion. The local motion refinement component of SMA, by prioritizing low spatial frequency components, effectively mitigates these distortions, leading to cleaner and more reliable motion representations.
- **Global Motion Coherence (Figure 7, Table 3):** Without global motion alignment, some frameworks occasionally generated "reversed" or illogical motions (e.g., an astronaut skateboarding upwards). The global motion alignment component, by leveraging wavelet transforms to analyze motion dynamics across the entire video sequence, successfully addresses these issues, ensuring a more accurate understanding and transfer of global motion patterns.
- **Ablation Study (Table 3):** Quantitative ablation studies confirmed the individual contributions of both the local (ℓ_local) and global (ℓ_global) spectral alignment terms. Integrating either term improved performance over the baseline, and their combined use yielded the most substantial improvements in metrics like Text-Align and Frame-Consistency, further supporting their complementary roles.
- **Training Progress and Alternative Approaches (Figure 8):** Visualization of training progress showed a gradual improvement in spatial layout alignment when SMA was incorporated. Furthermore, an exploration of alternative frequency-domain approaches found that while 2D DWT also improved motion transfer by prioritizing low-frequency components, the proposed 2D DFT method for local refinement generally performed better and was less sensitive to hyperparameter tuning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Spectral Motion Alignment (SMA) framework presents a methodological advancement in the field of video motion transfer using diffusion models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations:** SMA directly addresses critical shortcomings of existing pixel- or feature-space motion distillation methods, specifically their inability to capture global motion context and their susceptibility to motion-independent spatial artifacts. By operating in the spectral domain, the framework provides a more robust and comprehensive mechanism for understanding and encoding motion dynamics.

- **Enhanced Motion Fidelity and Temporal Consistency:** The integration of SMA consistently leads to improved motion accuracy and temporal consistency in generated videos. This translates to outputs that are not only visually more plausible but also adhere more closely to the intended motion patterns, reducing artifacts such as flickering or erroneous global movements.

- **Orthogonal and Broad Compatibility:** A key aspect of SMA is its design as an orthogonal enhancement that can be seamlessly integrated with a variety of existing diffusion-based video customization frameworks. This includes both text-to-video (T2V) diffusion models (e.g., MotionDirector, VMC) and text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models adapted for video (e.g., Tune-A-Video, ControlVideo). This broad compatibility suggests that SMA can serve as a widely applicable module for improving motion transfer capabilities across diverse architectures and parameterizations.

- **Computational Efficiency:** The authors state that SMA imposes negligible memory and computational burdens. For instance, its integration with VMC demonstrated lightweight training requirements (15GB vRAM, less than 5 minutes), indicating its practical applicability without necessitating significant hardware upgrades or extended training times. This efficiency is important for the widespread adoption of such methods in research and potential applications.

- **Improved Video Understanding and Generation:** By refining how motion information is distilled and aligned, SMA contributes to a deeper understanding of motion patterns in video. This improved motion representation has implications beyond motion transfer, potentially benefiting other video understanding tasks, such as activity recognition, and future video generation models aiming for higher realism and controllability.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The success of spectral analysis in refining motion representations opens avenues for further exploration into frequency-domain techniques for various aspects of video generation and editing. This could include investigating different wavelet families, advanced Fourier-based filtering, or multi-dimensional spectral approaches to capture even more intricate spatio-temporal dynamics.

In conclusion, SMA offers a robust and efficient approach to overcome critical limitations in current video motion transfer techniques, contributing to the development of more accurate, consistent, and controllable video generation systems.
