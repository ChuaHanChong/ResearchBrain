# Spectral Motion Alignment for Video Motion Transfer using Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from KAIST developed Spectral Motion Alignment (SMA), a framework that refines motion representations for video motion transfer using spectral analysis techniques like Fourier and Wavelet transforms. This approach significantly enhances motion accuracy and temporal consistency in generated videos, reducing artifacts and improving overall motion coherence across various diffusion-based video customization models.

## Problem

- Existing methods for distilling motion from video often struggle to capture global motion context, focusing primarily on local patterns.
- Pixel- or feature-space motion representations are highly susceptible to motion-independent spatial distortions (e.g., background changes, lighting shifts), leading to unreliable motion transfer.
- These limitations result in generated videos that lack coherent, overarching motion and may erroneously reproduce high-frequency artifacts.

## Method

- The Spectral Motion Alignment (**SMA**) framework refines motion representations by operating in the ==frequency domain==, integrating with existing diffusion-based video customization models.
- It employs a 1D Discrete Wavelet Transform (DWT) for "spectral global motion alignment," capturing multi-resolution motion dynamics across an entire sequence of frames.
- It utilizes a 2D Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) for "spectral local motion refinement," aligning amplitude and phase spectra while weighting low-frequency components to mitigate local, non-motion-related distortions.

## Results

- **SMA** consistently improved **Text-Alignment** and **Temporal Consistency** metrics, and user study scores for Editing Accuracy, Temporal Consistency, and Motion Accuracy across MotionDirector, VMC (Show-1, Zeroscope), Tune-A-Video, and ControlVideo.
- Qualitative evaluations demonstrated that **SMA** generates more accurate and consistent motion patterns, better distinguishes dynamic from static objects, and reduces flickering artifacts compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the local (FFT-based) and global (Wavelet-based) spectral alignment components contribute significantly, with their combined use yielding the most substantial performance improvements in metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spectral analysis, specifically DWT and FFT, can robustly capture and refine motion information by disentangling meaningful motion dynamics from irrelevant high-frequency noise and capturing motion across multiple scales.
> - Combining global (Wavelet-based) and local (Fourier-based) spectral alignment components is crucial for comprehensively addressing both long-range motion coherence and artifact mitigation.
> - The SMA framework serves as an orthogonal enhancement, broadly compatible with diverse diffusion-based video customization models (T2V and T2I adapted for video), offering improved fidelity with negligible computational overhead.
