# Dual-IPO: Dual-Iterative Preference Optimization for Text-to-Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Fudan University and Shanghai Academy of AI for Science researchers introduced Dual-Iterative Preference Optimization (Dual-IPO), an iterative framework that refines both text-to-video generation models and their reward models. The method significantly improves video quality and human preference alignment, enabling smaller models to outperform larger baselines and achieving up to 81.33% accuracy in aligning with human preferences.

## Problem

- Text-to-video (T2V) models frequently produce outputs that do not consistently align with user preferences regarding subject consistency, motion smoothness, and aesthetic quality.
- Existing post-training alignment techniques like DPO and KTO require extensive, costly human-annotated preference datasets, which are resource-intensive for video.
- External reward models often suffer from distribution mismatch and unreliable signals, while fine-tuning on static datasets can lead to overfitting and degradation of synthesis quality.

## Method

- Dual-IPO implements an iterative framework that simultaneously refines a T2V generation model and a ==reward model==, using a small human-annotated dataset for an initial warm-start.
- A Self-Refined Preference Optimization (**SRPO**) loop enhances the reward model through Chain-of-Thought (CoT) guided annotations, voting-based self-consistency via multi-path inference, and a Preference Certainty Estimator (PCE) for reliable pseudo-label generation.
- The refined reward model then iteratively aligns the T2V generation model using Diffusion-DPO for pairwise supervision or Diffusion-KTO for pointwise alignment, with automatic critic updates triggered upon performance degradation.

## Results

- The Dual-IPO reward model achieved 81.33% accuracy in aligning with human preference labels, significantly outperforming other open-source models like VisionReward (68.44%) and VideoAlign (65.21%).
- Optimized with Dual-IPO, a smaller 2B parameter T2V model (CogVideoX-2B) achieved a VBench score of 82.74, surpassing a larger 5B parameter baseline (CogVideoX-5B, 81.61) across all quality dimensions.
- Models fine-tuned with Dual-IPO demonstrated consistent performance gains, with Wan-1.3B +Ours achieving a total VBench score of 88.32 after five iterations, outperforming state-of-the-art models like Vidu Q1 (87.41) and Open-Sora-2.0 (84.34).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Iterative co-refinement of both the reward model and the generative model is crucial for robust and adaptive preference alignment in T2V generation, preventing issues like reward misalignment and overfitting.
> - High-quality preference signals can be generated with minimal human supervision through self-refined pseudo-labeling, leveraging CoT-guided reasoning, multi-path inference, and a preference certainty estimator.
> - A customized and dynamically updated reward model, specifically tailored and refined for the target T2V generator, is necessary for effective downstream optimization, outperforming static or generic external reward models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of text-to-video (T2V) generation has advanced significantly, driven by scalable diffusion transformers and large datasets. These advancements have enabled the creation of increasingly realistic videos, impacting various domains such as movie production, story generation, and scene synthesis. Despite these technical breakthroughs, a notable gap persists between the capabilities of current T2V models and the specific demands and preferences of users. Existing models frequently fail to produce outputs that consistently align with user expectations regarding subject consistency, motion smoothness, and overall aesthetic quality.

To address this alignment challenge, post-training techniques, such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Kahneman-Tversky Optimization (KTO), have been adopted from the domain of Large Language Models (LLMs) and applied to generative models. However, these methods typically necessitate extensive datasets of human-annotated preferences, which are resource-intensive to construct. An alternative approach involves utilizing external reward models, such as HPS, ImageReward, VideoAlign, or VisionReward, to automatically evaluate and assign preference labels to generated content. Nevertheless, these external reward models often exhibit limitations, including distribution mismatch across different video generation models and evaluation criteria, leading to unreliable reward signals. Furthermore, fine-tuning T2V models on fixed, offline preference datasets can result in overfitting, potential collapse to the preference signals, and degradation of synthesis quality and generalization. The effectiveness of reward signals is also observed to diminish over time as model artifacts become subtler, necessitating an evolving reward mechanism.

This research introduces Dual-Iterative Preference Optimization (Dual-IPO) as a framework designed to address these identified limitations. It positions itself within the landscape of post-training and preference learning for T2V models, specifically targeting the challenges of unreliable reward signals, the high cost of human annotation, and the static nature of existing alignment methods. By iteratively refining both the reward model and the video generation model, Dual-IPO aims to enhance synthesis quality and human preference alignment in a more robust, adaptive, and data-efficient manner, thereby advancing the practical utility and performance of T2V systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the synthesis quality and human preference alignment of text-to-video generation models. This overarching goal is motivated by several specific challenges and shortcomings observed in the current state of T2V technology and preference learning:

1. **Bridging the Quality Gap with User Preferences:** Current advanced video generation models, despite producing realistic videos, frequently fail to generate outputs that fully satisfy users' authentic demands. Specific areas of deficiency include subject consistency, motion smoothness, and aesthetic quality. The motivation here is to develop a mechanism that can systematically refine model outputs to better match human expectations across these critical dimensions.

2. **Addressing the Cost and Scarcity of Human Preference Annotations:** Traditional preference learning techniques, such as DPO and KTO, rely on large datasets of human-annotated preferences. The manual annotation of such datasets, especially for video, is laborious, time-consuming, and expensive. A key motivation is to reduce this dependency on extensive manual annotation, making preference-based alignment more accessible and scalable.

3. **Enhancing the Reliability and Robustness of Reward Models:** Existing external reward models (e.g., VideoAlign, VisionReward) often suffer from issues like distribution mismatch when applied to different video generation models or evaluation criteria. This can lead to unreliable reward signals that do not accurately reflect human preferences. The motivation is to develop a reward model that provides robust, reliable, and consistent feedback for guiding T2V model optimization.

4. **Mitigating Overfitting and Generalization Issues:** Aligning video generation models on static, offline preference datasets can cause overfitting to the training data, potentially leading to a degradation in generalization ability and even "collapse" where the model's outputs become overly biased towards the learned preferences without genuine quality improvement. The research aims to develop an adaptive alignment strategy that avoids these pitfalls.

5. **Ensuring Reward Signals Evolve with Model Improvements:** As a video generation model undergoes refinement, the nature of its imperfections can become subtler. A static reward model may become biased and ineffective if it does not adapt to these evolving artifacts. The motivation is to create a dynamic system where the reward model itself can progressively improve and remain relevant to the current state of the generation model, thus facilitating stable and continuous optimization.

To address these motivations, the authors propose the Dual-Iterative Optimization (Dual-IPO) framework. This framework aims to sequentially and mutually optimize both the reward model and the video generation model within an iterative loop. This dual-optimization strategy is intended to ensure reliable reward signals through mechanisms like CoT-guided reasoning, voting-based self-consistency, and preference certainty estimation, while concurrently guiding the T2V model towards improved synthesis quality in areas such as subject consistency, motion smoothness, and aesthetic appeal, all without requiring continuous manual preference annotations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Dual-Iterative Preference Optimization (Dual-IPO) framework is designed as an iterative paradigm that simultaneously refines both a reward model and a text-to-video (T2V) generation model to enhance synthesis quality and human preference alignment. The methodology involves an initial warm-start phase followed by a continuous dual-optimization loop.

#### 3.1. Method Overview

The process begins with the collection of a small human preference dataset, which includes both pairwise and pointwise annotations. This dataset is used to initially train the reward model. Once a foundational reward model is established, it is integrated with the T2V generation model into an iterative optimization loop, where each component progressively improves the other.

#### 3.2. Self-Refined Preference Optimization (SRPO) for the Reward Model

SRPO is a self-iterative training loop designed to enhance the reward model with minimal human supervision:

- **CoT-guided Annotation:** The initial phase involves creating a small set of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) guided annotations. This leverages a Vision-Language Model (VLM) for structured reasoning and preference construction. Videos are generated from diverse prompts (spanning semantic categories and motion types, built by Qwen2.5) using a T2V model (e.g., CogVideoX-2B). Each prompt yields four videos from different seeds, and the dataset is expanded iteratively. Both pairwise ranked and pointwise scored preference data are collected across three dimensions: text-video consistency, content faithfulness, and motion smoothness. This small, high-quality dataset serves as a cold start for the reward model.

- **Self-consistency via Multi-path Inference:** To scale preference data and improve reliability, SRPO employs a multi-path inference mechanism. By applying the CoT-enabled reward model across multiple reasoning paths, responses are aggregated through a voting-based self-consistency approach. This process counts answer frequencies to automatically construct stable pseudo-preference labels, thereby reducing noise from individual inference paths.

- **Preference Certainty Estimator (PCE):** Despite self-consistency, pseudo-labels may still contain noise. To filter out unreliable labels, a Preference Certainty Estimator (PCE) is introduced. Based on the DPO reward formulation, PCE measures the probability that a preferred video ($y_w$) has a reward score higher than the average reward ($Q$) across the current dataset: $PCE(y_w | x) = P_\theta(R(y_w | x) > Q)$. Only data points where $PCE(y_w | x) > 0.5$ are retained. This criterion ensures that selected preferences not only reflect a relative ranking but also exceed a global quality threshold, enhancing label robustness.

- **Iterative Pseudo-Label Refinement:** The refined pseudo-labels, generated through CoT-guided annotation, self-consistency, and PCE filtering, are used to iteratively fine-tune the critic (reward) model. The updated critic then scores newly generated video samples for the subsequent iteration, creating a bootstrapping process where the critic progressively aligns with human preferences and generalizes to evolving data distributions.

- **Reward Model Training Objective:** The reward model is trained using a modified DPO objective, $L_{SRPO}(\theta)$. This loss is essentially the standard DPO loss, but it is weighted by the PCE metric: $L_{SRPO}(\theta) = E_{x,y_w,y_l}[PCE(y_w | x) \cdot L_{DPO}(\theta)]$. This formulation prioritizes reliable preferences during training, mitigating the impact of noisy supervision and fostering a more stable and generalizable reward model. The framework supports both pairwise (for Diffusion-DPO) and pointwise (for Diffusion-KTO) preference data for flexible alignment strategies.

#### 3.3. Iterative Alignment for Video Generation Model

Once a reliable reward model is established, it is used to align the T2V generation model within an iterative optimization loop.

- **Optimization Loop:** In each iteration, the T2V model generates a batch of videos from diverse prompts. The current reward model evaluates these videos, producing updated preference signals (pairwise for DPO, pointwise for KTO). The T2V model is then optimized using these signals. The framework monitors key indicators such as VBench metrics and training loss curves. If performance degradation or abnormal loss dynamics are detected, a critic update via SRPO is automatically triggered. This ensures an accurate and distribution-aware reward model, which then re-scores videos and reconstructs preference datasets for the next T2V optimization iteration. This mechanism aims to mitigate reward misalignment and overfitting.

- **Preference Strategies:**
 - **Diffusion-DPO for Pairwise Alignment:** For pairwise supervision, the method employs Diffusion-DPO, an extension of DPO for diffusion models. It minimizes a loss function that increases the preference margin between preferred ($x_{w_0}$) and less-preferred ($x_{l_0}$) samples, using noise predictions ($\epsilon$) from the trained ($\epsilon_\theta$) and reference ($\epsilon_{ref}$) models. Auxiliary Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) terms are added to improve generalization, incorporating both sampled preference data ($D_{sample}$) and real video data ($D_{real}$) for regularization.
 - **Diffusion-KTO for Pointwise Alignment:** For pointwise alignment, Diffusion-KTO is utilized, which optimizes a utility-based objective without requiring explicit preference pairs. The core loss considers the desirability $w(x_0) \in \{\pm 1\}$ and a reference utility $Q_{ref}$. Similar to DPO, a regularization term using real-world data ($D_{real}$) is added to stabilize training.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Reward Model:** Pre-trained VILA models (13B/40B parameters) are fine-tuned. Initial training uses a small CoT-guided annotation set, followed by 5 epochs (LR 2e-5, batch size 4 on 32 GPUs). SRPO generates 20K pseudo-labels per iteration for subsequent iterative fine-tuning. Videos are represented by 16 uniformly sampled frames.
- **T2V Alignment Training:** A batch size of 512 and a learning rate of 2e-6 (raised to 2e-5 after stabilization) are used across 128 GPUs. For each caption, 8 candidate videos are generated, and the top-1 and bottom-1 (based on critic ranking) form preference pairs. Approximately 100K such pairs are constructed per iteration. A single training round typically takes about two weeks.
- **Datasets:** A pool of structured elements (subjects, attributes, actions) is used to generate approximately one million natural language captions via an LLM. The target T2V model then generates corresponding videos. A subset of real video-text pairs from VidGen-1M is incorporated for joint fine-tuning and improved diversity.
- **Evaluation:** Both automatic metrics (VBench, including semantic consistency, motion smoothness, visual quality) and human assessments are used.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate Dual-IPO demonstrate its effectiveness in improving text-to-video generation quality and human preference alignment across various settings.

#### 4.1. Reward Model Performance and Necessity

- **Superior Accuracy:** A customized reward model is identified as necessary for reliable alignment. The Dual-IPO reward model achieved an accuracy of 81.33% in aligning with human preference labels. This significantly outperformed other open-source models, including VisionReward (68.44%), VideoAlign (65.21%), and VideoScore (63.58%). This performance is attributed to the integration of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning and VLM-based inference.
- **Downstream Impact:** When used to fine-tune CogVideoX-2B via Diffusion-DPO, the Dual-IPO critic boosted the VBench score from 80.91 to 81.54 (+0.67). In contrast, VideoScore led to a slight degradation, and other external models yielded only minor gains (~+0.3). This indicates that reward models must be specifically tailored and refined for the target T2V generator.
- **Data Efficiency:** The Dual-IPO reward model achieved strong alignment using only 6,000 annotated pairs, a smaller dataset compared to those used for other models, suggesting its data efficiency.

#### 4.2. Performance Gains across Diverse Baselines and Scales

- **Model Scale Advantage:** Dual-IPO enabled a smaller T2V model (2B parameters) to surpass a larger baseline (5B parameters). Specifically, the optimized CogVideoX-2B model achieved a VBench total score of 82.74 after three iterations, outperforming the baseline CogVideoX-5B's score of 81.61 across all dimensions (quality, semantic, motion smoothness, aesthetic quality, etc.).
- **Consistent Improvements:** The framework demonstrated consistent performance gains during iterative training. CogVideoX-5B improved from 81.61 to 84.63 on VBench after three iterations, and Wan-1.3B increased from 84.26 to 86.28, indicating enhanced visual quality and prompt consistency.
- **State-of-the-Art Comparison:** After five Dual-IPO iterations, the fine-tuned models achieved competitive performance against other state-of-the-art T2V models. For example, Wan-1.3B +Ours achieved a total VBench score of 88.32, surpassing models like Vidu Q1 (87.41) and Open-Sora-2.0 (84.34).
- **Qualitative Improvements:** Qualitative comparisons showed that the optimized models produced videos with improved semantic fidelity (better reflection of prompt intent), enhanced temporal consistency (reduced artifacts and smoother transitions), and increased realism (fewer structural errors).

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Self-Refined Preference Optimization (SRPO)

- **Necessity of Critic Update:** Ablation studies demonstrated the crucial role of SRPO in maintaining and improving performance. Without updating the critic model between the third and fourth IPO iterations, performance declined from 82.74 to 82.33. In contrast, applying the full SRPO procedure led to an increase from 82.74 to 82.91, underscoring the importance of in-the-loop reward model optimization.
- **SRPO Component Efficacy:** The SRPO loss formulation was shown to be critical for critic robustness and signal quality. Removing the Preference Certainty Estimator (PCE) while retaining SRPO loss caused only a minimal change (82.69). However, replacing SRPO loss with a standard DPO objective, even with PCE, resulted in a greater performance drop (to 82.83).

#### 4.4. Effectiveness of Iterative Alignment

- **Importance of Multi-Round Iteration:** A comparison with a static Diffusion-DPO baseline (without updating preference data or the reward model) highlighted the benefits of iterative optimization. The static baseline initially improved but then degraded due to overfitting and misaligned rewards. Dual-IPO, conversely, maintained steady performance gains through progressive data improvement and reward optimization, as evidenced by VBench score progression.
- **Benefits of Real Video Data:** Incorporating real-world video data (from VidGen-1M) during fine-tuning, alongside diffusion loss and preference optimization, led to consistent gains in total, quality, and semantic VBench scores (from 81.17 to 81.69 for CogVideoX-2B-IPO1). Real data alone, however, did not yield similar benefits, suggesting its effectiveness is maximized when combined with preference guidance.
- **Flexibility of Preference Strategies:** Both Diffusion-DPO and Diffusion-KTO strategies, when integrated into Dual-IPO, outperformed the baseline. KTO showed a slight advantage in Total and Semantic scores, while DPO led in Quality scores. This demonstrates the framework's flexibility in accommodating different alignment paradigms.

#### 4.5. Human Evaluation

Human visual evaluation corroborated the automatic metric findings. Dual-IPO consistently outperformed the CogVideo-2B baseline across dimensions such as "Consistency," "Motion," and "Faithfulness," indicating more natural movement and better text alignment in generated videos.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Dual-Iterative Preference Optimization (Dual-IPO) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of text-to-video generation, addressing several critical limitations of existing approaches and offering substantial potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Synthesis Quality and Human Alignment

The core significance lies in its demonstrated ability to consistently improve the quality of generated videos across multiple dimensions, including subject consistency, motion smoothness, and aesthetic appeal. By iteratively aligning the generation process with human preferences, Dual-IPO directly addresses the common complaint that advanced T2V models often fail to produce truly satisfactory outputs. This leads to more user-aligned and contextually relevant video content, moving T2V models closer to practical deployment scenarios where user satisfaction is paramount.

#### 5.2. Data Efficiency and Reduced Annotation Burden

A major impact of Dual-IPO is its capacity to reduce the reliance on laborious and expensive manual human preference annotations. By introducing a self-refined preference optimization (SRPO) strategy, the framework can generate reliable pseudo-labels with minimal initial human supervision. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for fine-tuning T2V models, making advanced alignment techniques more accessible to researchers and developers with limited resources. The ability to achieve strong alignment with a small cold-start human preference dataset marks a notable improvement in data efficiency.

#### 5.3. Robust and Adaptive Reward Modeling

Dual-IPO's iterative refinement of the reward model is crucial. By incorporating mechanisms such as CoT-guided reasoning, voting-based self-consistency, and preference certainty estimation, the framework ensures that the reward signals are robust, reliable, and continuously adapt to the evolving capabilities and artifacts of the T2V model. This mitigates issues of distribution mismatch and static reward bias, leading to more stable and effective optimization over extended training periods.

#### 5.4. Scalability and Accessibility of High-Quality Generation

The finding that Dual-IPO can enable a smaller 2B parameter model to surpass a 5B parameter baseline model is particularly impactful. This demonstrates that substantial quality improvements and preference alignment can be achieved without necessarily requiring prohibitively large generative models. This enhanced alignment efficiency and effectiveness make high-quality T2V generation more accessible and computationally feasible for a broader range of research institutions and commercial entities.

#### 5.5. Flexible and Unified Alignment Framework

The framework's support for both pairwise (Diffusion-DPO) and pointwise (Diffusion-KTO) preference strategies offers flexibility in deployment, allowing adaptation to different alignment objectives and available data formats. This unified approach can streamline the development of diverse preference-aligned generative systems.

#### 5.6. Broader Applicability and Future Research

The dual-iterative optimization paradigm presented in Dual-IPO is not strictly limited to text-to-video generation. Its principles—iterative refinement of both a generative model and its corresponding evaluative (reward) model, driven by adaptive pseudo-labeling and human validation—could potentially be extended to other generative AI domains, such as text-to-image, text-to-audio, or even multi-modal generation, where human preference alignment is a critical challenge. This opens new avenues for research into more generally applicable, self-improving generative AI systems.

In conclusion, Dual-IPO contributes a systematic and effective approach to bridging the gap between raw T2V model outputs and human preferences. Its advancements in reward model reliability, data efficiency, and model scalability are poised to impact the development of more sophisticated, user-centric, and practically deployable video generation technologies.
