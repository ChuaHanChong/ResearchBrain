# MultihopSpatial: Multi-hop Compositional Spatial Reasoning Benchmark for Vision-Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Electronics and Telecommunications Research Institute and KAIST introduced MultihopSpatial, a benchmark and training corpus designed to evaluate and enhance multi-hop compositional spatial reasoning in Vision-Language Models. The benchmark revealed a significant "spatial blind spot" where models often provide correct answers without precise visual grounding, and post-training on the corpus improved both VLM metrics and downstream robotic manipulation performance.

## Problem

- Existing VLM spatial reasoning benchmarks focus on elementary, single-hop relations and do not adequately assess the multi-step, compositional reasoning required for real-world interactions.
- Traditional multiple-choice questions (MCQs) allow models to select correct answers via linguistic shortcuts without precisely locating the target object, creating a "spatial blind spot" for embodied AI.
- There is a gap between current VLM evaluation metrics and the robust perception and action requirements for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) agents in physical environments.

## Method

- Developed **MultihopSpatial**, a VQA benchmark featuring 4,500 manually annotated samples that require multi-hop compositional spatial reasoning, each with ground-truth bounding boxes for joint evaluation.
- Introduced ==Acc@50IoU==, an evaluation metric that considers a prediction correct only if both the multiple-choice answer is accurate and the predicted bounding box has at least 0.5 ==Intersection over Union (IoU)== with the ground truth.
- Created **MultihopSpatial-Train**, a large-scale corpus of 6,791 grounded VQA samples, and demonstrated its utility through ==reinforcement learning post-training== to enhance VLM spatial intelligence.

## Results

- The best-performing model achieved **4**0.6%** Acc@50IoU**, with scores plummeting under 3-hop ego-centric conditions (e.g., **8.**5%** Acc@50IoU** for GPT-5.2-Thinking).
- Reinforcement learning post-training on MultihopSpatial-Train significantly improved the base VLM's performance across all MultihopSpatial metrics and showed consistent gains on five external spatial reasoning benchmarks.
- Enhanced VLM spatial reasoning directly translated to improved performance in downstream embodied robotic manipulation tasks, increasing average task completion on CALVIN ABC→D and yielding a **4.**2%** absolute improvement** on Libero.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Performance on MultihopSpatial consistently degrades with increasing reasoning hops, especially under ego-centric (first-person) conditions, indicating that perspective-taking compounds the difficulty of multi-step reasoning.
> - A significant "grounding gap" exists, where 59% of correctly answered questions across models lacked proper spatial localization (Acc@50IoU < 0.5), particularly prominent in proprietary instant models.
> - Scaling the capacity of visual spatial representations, rather than solely relying on scaling the language model backbone, is critical for advancing multi-hop spatial reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has advanced significantly, particularly with the emergence of embodied agents, known as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, designed to interact with physical environments. Spatial reasoning is a fundamental capability for these agents, enabling them to perceive and execute actions accurately in complex real-world scenarios.

However, existing benchmarks for VLM spatial reasoning primarily focus on elementary, single-hop relations. These typically involve questions verifying simple spatial relationships (e.g., "Is X to the right of Y?") and often rely on multiple-choice questions (MCQs) without requiring precise visual localization of the target objects. This design presents several limitations:
- **Lack of Compositional Reasoning:** Real-world interactions frequently demand multi-step, compositional spatial reasoning, which current benchmarks do not adequately assess. For example, understanding an instruction like "move the round cup on my right—the one furthest away" requires a sequence of inferences involving perspective-taking, attribute filtering, and relational comparisons.
- **Insufficient Visual Grounding:** Models can sometimes select the correct answer in MCQs through linguistic shortcuts or shallow understanding, without genuinely locating the target object in the image. This creates a "spatial blind spot" where high VLM performance on benchmarks does not necessarily translate to accurate perception or action execution in VLA frameworks.
- **Gap between VLM Metrics and VLA Performance:** While some recent efforts provide training sets alongside benchmarks, they often lack validation in end-to-end VLA execution, leaving a disconnection between VLM spatial reasoning capabilities and their practical application in embodied agents.

MultihopSpatial addresses these identified gaps by introducing a benchmark specifically designed for multi-hop compositional spatial reasoning that also integrates precise visual grounding. By requiring both answer selection and bounding box prediction, it aims to provide a more rigorous evaluation aligned with the demands of VLA deployment. Furthermore, the accompanying training corpus seeks to enhance VLM spatial intelligence and demonstrate its direct impact on downstream embodied manipulation tasks. This work, therefore, aims to bridge the current disparities between VLM evaluation methodologies and the requirements of robust, real-world embodied AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a comprehensive benchmark and associated training resources that more accurately evaluate and enhance the multi-hop compositional spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly in the context of their application as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) agents in physical environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the existing VLM research landscape:

1. **Addressing the Insufficiency of Single-Hop Benchmarks:** Current VLM spatial reasoning benchmarks predominantly focus on elementary, single-hop relations. While these are foundational, real-world scenarios for embodied agents often require a sequence of intermediate inferences, combining multiple spatial concepts (e.g., attribute, position, relation) and perspectives (e.g., ego-centric, exo-centric). The existing benchmarks do not adequately capture this multi-hop compositional reasoning, leading to an incomplete assessment of VLM capabilities.

2. **Overcoming the "Spatial Blind Spot" of Traditional MCQs:** Many existing benchmarks evaluate spatial understanding solely through multiple-choice questions. This method allows models to potentially select correct answers based on linguistic cues or partial understanding, without precisely locating the described object in the visual scene. For VLA agents, accurate visual grounding – the ability to localize the referred object with precision – is critical for successful manipulation and interaction. A key motivation is to eliminate this "spatial blind spot" by requiring precise bounding box predictions in addition to answer selection.

3. **Developing a Grounded Evaluation Metric for VLA Deployment:** The deployment of VLMs as VLA agents necessitates robust perception and action capabilities. Accurate visual grounding is paramount for tasks such as grasping specific objects or navigating to precise locations. There is a need for an evaluation metric that rigorously assesses both the reasoning correctness and the spatial localization accuracy, thus directly reflecting the practical requirements for VLA deployment.

4. **Fostering Spatial Intelligence through a Dedicated Training Corpus:** Beyond evaluation, there is a motivation to provide a training resource that can actively improve VLM spatial intelligence. By offering a large-scale corpus of grounded VQA samples, the research aims to enable post-training of VLMs to enhance their multi-hop spatial reasoning capabilities, thereby demonstrating the utility of such a dataset not just as an evaluation tool, but also as a developmental resource.

5. **Bridging the Gap to Embodied Action Performance:** A significant motivation is to demonstrate a clear link between improved VLM spatial reasoning metrics and actual performance in downstream embodied robotic tasks. By showing that enhancements on the proposed benchmark translate to better results in VLA benchmarks, the work aims to reinforce the importance of compositional spatial reasoning and precise grounding as foundational capabilities for embodied AI.

In summary, the research aims to move beyond superficial evaluations by introducing a challenging, grounded benchmark and a complementary training corpus to drive the development of VLMs that possess genuine, robust multi-hop compositional spatial reasoning and visual grounding abilities essential for intelligent embodied agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces MultihopSpatial, a benchmark, an evaluation metric, and a training corpus, along with a reinforcement learning (RL) post-training methodology, to address the identified gaps in VLM spatial reasoning evaluation and enhancement.

#### 3.1. MultihopSpatial Benchmark Design

- **Scale and Content:** MultihopSpatial comprises 4,500 manually annotated VQA pairs. These samples are designed for multi-hop, compositional spatial queries that reflect real-world complexity. Each sample includes ground-truth bounding boxes to facilitate joint evaluation of reasoning and spatial localization.
- **Data Source:** Images were curated from COCO and PACO-Ego4D, encompassing 3,563 spatially complex images from diverse indoor/outdoor scenes and varying perspectives.
- **Annotation and Verification:** All QA pairs and bounding boxes were strictly annotated by ten trained human experts. A rigorous multi-stage verification process, involving three rounds of independent cross-checking, was employed. This protocol aimed to ensure high quality and reliability, achieving a Krippendorff’s α of 0.90 for inter-annotator agreement. Verification confirmed the existence of option entities in images, precise bounding box matches, and uniquely supported correct answers.
- **Reasoning Categories:** Three fundamental spatial reasoning categories are defined:
 - **Attribute (ATT):** Visual properties (e.g., color, shape, state).
 - **Position (POS):** Spatial location and orientation (e.g., left, right, in front of).
 - **Relation (REL):** Spatial relationships (e.g., closest, farthest, highest).
- **Multi-hop Structure:** Questions systematically compose these categories into 1- to 3-hop complexities:
 - **1-Hop:** Single-step questions targeting one spatial category (POS or REL). ATT is excluded as a standalone category.
 - **2-Hop:** Questions combining two categories (ATT+POS, ATT+REL, or POS+REL), typically requiring a two-stage inference.
 - **3-Hop:** Questions incorporating all three categories (ATT+POS+REL), designed to mirror complex human references in cluttered scenes and test disambiguation.
- **Viewpoints:** Questions cover both ego-centric (first-person perspective) and exo-centric (third-person perspective) viewpoints, balanced at 750 samples per hop per viewpoint.

#### 3.2. Acc@50IoU Metric

To evaluate both reasoning and precise visual grounding, the paper introduces **Acc@50IoU**. This metric considers a prediction correct only if two conditions are met:
1. The multiple-choice answer is correct (ˆy = y∗).
2. The predicted bounding box (ˆB) has an Intersection over Union (IoU) of at least 0.5 with the ground-truth bounding box (B∗) (IoU(ˆB, B∗) ≥ 0.5).
This metric aims to eliminate the "spatial blind spot" of traditional MCQs, where models might select correct answers without accurately locating the target.

#### 3.3. MultihopSpatial-Train Corpus

An auxiliary large-scale corpus, **MultihopSpatial-Train**, is provided. It consists of 6,791 grounded VQA samples explicitly designed to support the post-training of VLMs for enhancing spatial intelligence.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning (RL) Post-training

The research investigates the utility of MultihopSpatial-Train as a training corpus through reinforcement learning.
- **Framework:** Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is adopted, utilizing deterministic, rule-based reward functions for unambiguous correctness signals.
- **Algorithm:** Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed. This algorithm optimizes the policy by sampling a group of responses and using normalized sequence-level advantage scores, eliminating the need for a critic model.
- **Base VLM:** Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct serves as the base policy model, with LoRA applied to its LLM backbone.
- **Composite Reward Function:** The total reward (R) for a generated sequence is a sum of three components, with weighting coefficients α = β = 1:
 - **R_format:** Binary reward (1 if output follows "Answer: (X) Bounding Box: [x1, y1, x2, y2]" format, 0 otherwise).
 - **R_mcq:** Discrete reward (1 if parsed MCQ choice matches ground-truth, 0 otherwise).
 - **R_bbox:** Continuous reward derived from the Generalized Intersection over Union (GIoU) between predicted and ground-truth bounding boxes. The GIoU score (∈ [−1, 1]) is normalized to a range to provide a dense, positive signal.
This composite reward encourages simultaneous improvement in compositional spatial reasoning and precise visual localization.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Protocol

- **VLM Benchmarking:** 37 state-of-the-art VLMs are evaluated, categorized into: (i) proprietary instant, (ii) proprietary reasoning, (iii) open-weight instant, (iv) open-weight reasoning, and (v) specialized spatial reasoning models. All models are prompted with a consistent instruction template requiring both a multiple-choice answer and a bounding box prediction.
- **Metrics:** In addition to **Acc@50IoU**, two other metrics are used:
 - **MCQ Accuracy:** Percentage of correct multiple-choice predictions, without considering localization.
 - **Avg. IoU:** Computed only for MCQ-correct samples, to isolate grounding precision from reasoning errors.
- **Downstream VLA Evaluation:** The GRPO-trained VLM is integrated into the VLM4VLA framework and evaluated on two embodied robotic manipulation benchmarks: CALVIN ABC→D and Libero. This assesses the transferability of enhanced spatial reasoning to practical robotic control tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation of 37 state-of-the-art VLMs on MultihopSpatial, complemented by analysis of the training corpus's utility, yielded several key findings:

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Benchmark Difficulty

- **Top Performers:** Gemini-3-Pro achieved the highest MCQ accuracy (64.7%) and Acc@50IoU (40.6%) among all models. In the open-weight category, Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking led. Qwen3-VL-235B demonstrated strong average IoU, indicating good grounding when the answer was correct.
- **Benchmark Challenge:** MultihopSpatial is a challenging benchmark, with even the best models peaking at 40.6% Acc@50IoU. Performance consistently degrades with increasing hop count, particularly under 3-hop ego-centric conditions where many models, including advanced reasoning ones like GPT-5.2-Thinking, performed poorly (e.g., 8.5% Acc@50IoU). This indicates that the benchmark effectively evaluates both compositional reasoning and spatial grounding.

#### 4.2. Metric-Dependent Rankings and Grounding Gap (A6)

- **Decoupling of Reasoning and Grounding:** A significant finding is the decoupling of compositional reasoning and visual grounding capabilities in current VLMs. Models often showed high MCQ accuracy but plummeted in Acc@50IoU (e.g., Claude-Opus-4.5 dropped from 7th in MCQ to 29th in Acc@50IoU), suggesting they answer correctly without precise localization. Conversely, some smaller models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-4B) improved their rank under Acc@50IoU due to robust grounding.
- **Ungrounded Ratio:** On average, 59% of correctly answered questions across all models lacked proper spatial localization (Acc@50IoU < 0.5). This ungrounded ratio was particularly high for proprietary instant models (93%) and models like Gemma-3-IT, Molmo2, and Claude-Sonnet-4.5 (over 98%), indicating reliance on linguistic shortcuts rather than genuine spatial understanding. Acc@50IoU is thus validated as an essential metric to uncover these limitations.

#### 4.3. In-depth Analysis (Eight Key Insights)

- **A1. Performance Degradation Across Reasoning Hops:** There is a consistent and steep decline in both MCQ accuracy and Acc@50IoU as the number of reasoning hops increases from 1-hop to 3-hop. This degradation is more pronounced under ego-centric evaluation, indicating that perspective-taking compounds the difficulty of multi-step reasoning.

- **A2. Ego vs. Exo: Perspective-Taking as a Compounding Bottleneck:** Ego-centric evaluation serves as a significant bottleneck. While Acc@50IoU differentiates grounding capabilities under exo-centric conditions (e.g., Qwen3-VL vs. InternVL-3.5), it compresses performance to a low floor (20-25%) for most models under ego-centric conditions, masking underlying capability gaps. This highlights the additional challenge posed by perspective transformation.

- **A3. Instant vs. Reasoning: Diminishing Returns:** Reasoning models (with "thinking mode" enabled) generally outperform instant models. However, this advantage diminishes significantly under multi-hop pressure, with reasoning models still struggling to achieve high scores on 3-hop ego-centric tasks. This suggests that inference-time reasoning, such as chain-of-thought, yields diminishing returns as compositional complexity increases.

- **A4. Open-weight vs. Proprietary Models:** Proprietary models exhibited a slight MCQ advantage in ego-centric tasks, implying stronger perspective-taking abilities. However, open-weight models, particularly Qwen3-VL and GLM-4.6V families, demonstrated consistently more reliable spatial grounding and overall higher Acc@50IoU, forming a dense cluster at over 60% in exo-centric Acc@50IoU. This suggests that the open-weight ecosystem currently offers more robust spatial grounding.

- **A5. Generalist vs. Specialist Models:** Counterintuitively, specialized spatial reasoning models (SRMs) generally underperformed comparable general-purpose VLMs (≤10B) across all metrics and hop counts. This is attributed to the lack of robust native grounding in most SRMs, which are typically fine-tuned on single-step QA and fail to generalize to multi-hop scenarios. General-purpose VLMs appear to offer a stronger foundation for compositional spatial understanding.

- **A7. The Limits of LLM Scaling in Spatial Reasoning:** Scaling the language backbone of VLMs alone shows limited gains in Acc@50IoU, which largely plateaus or remains near zero for many open-weight families. A notable exception, InternVL-3.5 at 38B, saw a significant grounding jump, but this coincided with a substantial upgrade to its vision encoder. This finding indicates that scaling the capacity of visual spatial representations is critical for multi-hop spatial reasoning, more so than relying solely on the language component's reasoning capacity.

- **A8. Error Analysis on Tag Compositions:** Analysis of error rates across spatial tag combinations revealed that multi-tag compositions, especially the pos-rel (P-R) combination, incur significantly higher errors. Models struggle to jointly handle positional localization and relational comparison. Qualitative analysis (Fig. 15) showed models acknowledging conditions during reasoning but failing to incorporate them into the final prediction, indicating a breakdown in maintaining intermediate conditions through the reasoning chain.

#### 4.4. MultihopSpatial-Train: Utility as a Training Corpus

- **In-domain Improvement:** Reinforcement learning post-training (GRPO) on MultihopSpatial-Train significantly improved the base VLM (Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct) across all MultihopSpatial metrics, including MCQ Accuracy and Acc@50IoU.
- **Out-of-Domain Generalization:** The RL-trained model achieved consistent performance improvements on five external spatial reasoning benchmarks (BLINK, 3DSRBench, OmniSpatial, VSI-Bench, SpatialMQA), demonstrating that learned spatial reasoning capabilities generalize beyond the in-domain data.
- **Downstream VLA Transfer:** The enhanced spatial reasoning directly translated to improved performance in embodied robotic manipulation tasks. On the CALVIN ABC→D benchmark, the average task completion score increased, with benefits widening for longer task chains. Similarly, a notable absolute improvement of 4.2% was observed on the Libero benchmark. These results reinforce the idea that spatial reasoning acquired from MultihopSpatial-Train contributes to robust robotic policy execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MultihopSpatial research introduces several significant contributions that collectively aim to advance the field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly in the context of embodied AI.

1. **Novel and Comprehensive Benchmark:** MultihopSpatial fills a critical gap by providing a benchmark specifically designed for multi-hop, compositional spatial reasoning. This moves beyond the limitations of single-hop evaluations, offering a more realistic assessment of VLM capabilities for complex real-world interactions. Its inclusion of diverse spatial perspectives (ego-centric and exo-centric) further enhances its applicability to embodied agents.

2. **Introduction of a Grounded Evaluation Metric (Acc@50IoU):** The Acc@50IoU metric is a crucial innovation. By simultaneously requiring both correct answer selection and precise visual grounding (bounding box prediction), it addresses the "spatial blind spot" prevalent in traditional MCQ-only benchmarks. This metric provides a more reliable and stringent measure of genuine spatial understanding, essential for VLMs intended for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) deployment where accurate localization is paramount.

3. **Dedicated Training Corpus for Spatial Intelligence:** MultihopSpatial-Train provides a large-scale, high-quality corpus for training VLMs. The research demonstrates that reinforcement learning (RL) post-training on this dataset effectively enhances VLM spatial intelligence, showing utility beyond mere evaluation.

4. **Demonstrated Impact on Embodied Agents:** A key significance is the explicit demonstration that improved spatial reasoning, achieved through training on MultihopSpatial-Train, translates directly to better performance in downstream embodied robotic manipulation tasks (CALVIN and Libero benchmarks). This finding establishes a vital link between VLM cognitive capabilities and practical robotic control, reinforcing spatial reasoning as a foundational capability for effective embodied AI.

5. **New Insights into VLM Architectures and Capabilities:** The extensive evaluation of 37 state-of-the-art VLMs yields valuable insights:
 - It highlights the inherent difficulty of compositional spatial reasoning and perspective-taking for current models.
 - It reveals a significant "grounding gap," where many models achieve high MCQ accuracy without true visual localization.
 - It suggests that scaling visual encoders, rather than just language model backbones, is critical for advancing multi-hop spatial reasoning.
 - It indicates that open-weight models, particularly certain families, currently offer more reliable spatial grounding compared to some proprietary counterparts.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** By providing a challenging benchmark, a robust evaluation methodology, and a valuable training resource, MultihopSpatial is positioned to catalyze future research in Embodied AI. It encourages developers to focus on building VLMs with stronger, more integrated spatial priors for real-time, dynamic robotic control in uncontrolled environments. The identified challenges (e.g., compounding difficulty of ego-centric multi-hop reasoning, breakdown in maintaining intermediate conditions) point to fruitful avenues for architectural and algorithmic innovation.

In conclusion, MultihopSpatial represents a substantial step towards more rigorous and practical evaluation and development of spatial reasoning in Vision-Language Models, directly addressing the requirements for their successful deployment in embodied AI applications.
