# Action Tokenizer Matters in In-Context Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces LipVQ-VAE, a Lipschitz-constrained vector quantization variational autoencoder designed to enhance temporal smoothness in action representations for in-context imitation learning (ICIL). The method improved average success rates in robotic manipulation by 5.3% on RoboCasa and by 6% on ManiSkill compared to strong baselines, while also demonstrating improved sim-to-real transferability with 12-14% higher success rates on real robotic hardware.

## Problem

- Traditional imitation learning often struggles with generalization and requires extensive task-specific fine-tuning for novel robotic manipulation tasks.
- Existing in-context imitation learning (ICIL) approaches largely overlook the importance of action representation, particularly the preservation of temporal smoothness.
- Current action tokenization methods for ICIL fail to adequately maintain temporal smoothness, leading to unstable or unreliable robotic execution and suboptimal performance.

## Method

- A novel Lipschitz-constrained ==Vector Quantization Variational AutoEncoder (LipVQ-VAE)== is proposed as an action tokenizer.
- LipVQ-VAE enforces the Lipschitz condition in its latent action space through weight normalization within the encoder, propagating smoothness from raw action inputs to a quantized latent codebook.
- This tokenizer is integrated into an In-Context Robotic Transformer (ICRT) framework, allowing it to discretize and encode continuous actions into smooth latent representations for autoregressive prediction during ICIL.

## Results

- ICRT with LipVQ-VAE achieved an average success rate of **0.530** on RoboCasa (a **5.3%** improvement over the BC-Transformer baseline) and **0.617** on ManiSkill (outperforming ACT+LFQ-VAE by **6%**).
- LipVQ-VAE demonstrated the best quantitative smoothness score (**0.63**) among all evaluated tokenizers, confirming a positive correlation between smoother action tokenization and higher manipulation success rates.
- Real-world experiments on a Kinova Gen3 arm showed ICRT+LipVQ-VAE achieving 12-**14%** higher success rates compared to baselines, indicating robust sim-to-real transfer and more reliable physical execution due to smoother motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal smoothness in action representations is a critical, often overlooked, factor directly correlating with higher success rates in robotic manipulation tasks.
> - Explicitly enforcing Lipschitz continuity in the latent space of action tokenizers significantly improves temporal smoothness and consequently enhances the performance of in-context imitation learning.
> - The choice and design of the action tokenizer are fundamental to the overall success and robustness of ICIL, with a strong impact on generalization and real-world applicability.
