# Action Tokenizer Matters in In-Context Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces LipVQ-VAE, a Lipschitz-constrained vector quantization variational autoencoder designed to enhance temporal smoothness in action representations for in-context imitation learning (ICIL). The method improved average success rates in robotic manipulation by 5.3% on RoboCasa and by 6% on ManiSkill compared to strong baselines, while also demonstrating improved sim-to-real transferability with 12-14% higher success rates on real robotic hardware.

## Problem

- Traditional imitation learning often struggles with generalization and requires extensive task-specific fine-tuning for novel robotic manipulation tasks.
- Existing in-context imitation learning (ICIL) approaches largely overlook the importance of action representation, particularly the preservation of temporal smoothness.
- Current action tokenization methods for ICIL fail to adequately maintain temporal smoothness, leading to unstable or unreliable robotic execution and suboptimal performance.

## Method

- A novel Lipschitz-constrained ==Vector Quantization Variational AutoEncoder (LipVQ-VAE)== is proposed as an action tokenizer.
- LipVQ-VAE enforces the Lipschitz condition in its latent action space through weight normalization within the encoder, propagating smoothness from raw action inputs to a quantized latent codebook.
- This tokenizer is integrated into an In-Context Robotic Transformer (ICRT) framework, allowing it to discretize and encode continuous actions into smooth latent representations for autoregressive prediction during ICIL.

## Results

- ICRT with LipVQ-VAE achieved an average success rate of **0.530** on RoboCasa (a **5.3%** improvement over the BC-Transformer baseline) and **0.617** on ManiSkill (outperforming ACT+LFQ-VAE by **6%**).
- LipVQ-VAE demonstrated the best quantitative smoothness score (**0.63**) among all evaluated tokenizers, confirming a positive correlation between smoother action tokenization and higher manipulation success rates.
- Real-world experiments on a Kinova Gen3 arm showed ICRT+LipVQ-VAE achieving 12-**14%** higher success rates compared to baselines, indicating robust sim-to-real transfer and more reliable physical execution due to smoother motions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal smoothness in action representations is a critical, often overlooked, factor directly correlating with higher success rates in robotic manipulation tasks.
> - Explicitly enforcing Lipschitz continuity in the latent space of action tokenizers significantly improves temporal smoothness and consequently enhances the performance of in-context imitation learning.
> - The choice and design of the action tokenizer are fundamental to the overall success and robustness of ICIL, with a strong impact on generalization and real-world applicability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically addressing challenges in imitation learning (IL) and the emerging paradigm of in-context imitation learning (ICIL). Traditional imitation learning approaches often face limitations in generalization, primarily due to the dependency on high-quality and extensive demonstration datasets. While large-scale datasets such as Octo, Open X-Embodiment, and DROID have been developed to mitigate data scarcity, adapting to novel tasks typically necessitates additional task-specific fine-tuning, which restricts practical application.

In response to these generalization challenges, in-context imitation learning (ICIL) has emerged, drawing inspiration from the in-context learning capabilities observed in large language models (LLMs). ICIL aims to enable robots to infer and execute new tasks directly from expert demonstrations without requiring explicit retraining for each novel scenario. This paradigm holds potential for enhancing flexibility and efficiency in real-world robotic deployment.

Despite the promise of ICIL, current research has largely overlooked the importance of action representation within contextual demonstrations. Many existing ICIL methods tend to rely on LLMs, which are noted for their difficulties in precise, long-horizon motion planning, partly attributed to inaccuracies in action representation and inherent latency. Effective contextual representation, including action representation, is recognized as a factor in improving in-context learning performance.

The paper focuses on action tokenization, which involves discretizing and encoding continuous action spaces, a fundamental aspect of sequential decision-making in robotic manipulation. While various action representation methods exist (e.g., discretization, function approximation, latent space representation, language tokens), and techniques like positional encoding or vector quantization can address temporal ordering, a critical gap in ICIL is identified: the preservation of temporal smoothness in action trajectories. Temporal smoothness is crucial for stable and reliable robotic execution, as it contributes to continuity in manipulation outputs and reduces noise and uncertainties. The paper notes an empirical correlation between smoother action representations and higher robotic manipulation success rates.

This work contributes to bridging these identified gaps by systematically investigating the role of action tokenization in ICIL. It specifically targets the limitation of temporal smoothness in existing action tokenizers, proposing a method to enhance this aspect within the ICIL framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observation that while in-context imitation learning (ICIL) offers a promising avenue for improving robotic generalization, the field has not adequately explored the impact of action representation. Current ICIL approaches often default to action representations that may not fully leverage the potential of contextual demonstrations, leading to suboptimal performance, particularly in scenarios requiring precise and smooth robotic movements. The authors note that existing LLM-centric methods in ICIL struggle with accurate action representation and temporal consistency.

A core premise of the paper is the empirical finding that a smoother representation of actions directly correlates with higher success rates in robotic manipulation tasks. This observation highlights temporal smoothness as a critical, yet largely overlooked, factor in the design of effective action tokenizers for ICIL. Existing action tokenization methods, while capable of encoding action trajectories, are identified as failing to preserve this essential temporal smoothness, which can lead to unstable or unreliable robotic execution.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of this research are multi-fold:

1. **Systematically Evaluate Existing Action Tokenizers in ICIL:** The authors aim to conduct a comprehensive evaluation of current action tokenization methods when integrated into an ICIL framework. This evaluation is intended to reveal their strengths and, more importantly, their limitations, particularly concerning temporal smoothness.
2. **Identify Temporal Smoothness as a Critical Limitation:** Through their evaluation, the researchers intend to formally establish temporal smoothness as a significant deficiency in prevalent action tokenizers within the ICIL context. This identification underscores the necessity for novel approaches that explicitly address this issue.
3. **Propose a Smoothness-Enhanced Action Tokenizer:** To address the identified limitation, the paper aims to introduce LipVQ-VAE, a novel variational autoencoder. This tokenizer is designed to enforce the Lipschitz condition in its latent action space, specifically through weight normalization. The objective is for LipVQ-VAE to propagate smoothness constraints from raw action inputs to a quantized latent codebook, thereby generating smoother actions.
4. **Demonstrate Improved ICIL Performance:** The ultimate objective is to empirically show that integrating LipVQ-VAE into an ICIL framework leads to improved performance in robotic manipulation tasks. This includes demonstrating enhanced success rates in high-fidelity simulators and confirming its ability to produce smoother, more reliable trajectories in real-world robotic experiments.
5. **Underscore the Significance of Action Tokenization:** By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to highlight the critical, yet often underexplored, role that action tokenization plays in the overall success and robustness of in-context imitation learning.

In summary, the motivation is to fill a critical gap in ICIL research by focusing on action representation, specifically temporal smoothness, and providing a robust solution that demonstrably enhances robot performance and generalization capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology outlined in the paper centers on enhancing the action tokenizer component within an established in-context imitation learning (ICIL) framework. The authors adopt the In-Context Robotic Transformer (ICRT) framework, which models robotic manipulation as a next-token prediction problem.

**A. In-Context Imitation Learning Framework (ICRT)**
The ICRT framework operates by processing a batch of prompt demonstrations, which provide contextual information, alongside a query sequence for new observations.
1. **Tokenization:** Both observations and actions from prompt demonstrations and query sequences are processed through a layer of tokenizers.
 - **Observation Backbone:** This component uses encoder networks to handle various sensory inputs. For RGB images (from RoboCasa) and RGB-D images (from ManiSkill), ResNet-18 is employed. Language conditions are embedded using CLIP, and other sensory data are processed via Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs).
 - **Action Tokenizer:** This is the specific focus of the paper. It encodes actions at each timestep into a latent representation. The core contribution of this work is the proposed LipVQ-VAE tokenizer that replaces or enhances existing action tokenizers within this framework.
2. **Latent Space Projection:** Tokenized observations and actions are then projected into a shared latent space, ensuring dimensional consistency through an MLP.
3. **Causal Transformer:** An autoregressive causal transformer processes the sequence of observation and action tokens. This architecture ensures that each predicted token is conditioned only on preceding tokens.
 - Prompt tokens (observation-action pairs from `M` timesteps) provide context: `(h_o_p,1, h_a_p,1..., h_o_p,M, h_a_p,M)`.
 - Query tokens (observations for `N` timesteps) are provided for prediction: `(h_o_q,1, h_o_q,2..., h_o_q,N)`.
 - The transformer generates action predictions for the query sequence, masking tokens corresponding to the prompt.
4. **Action Decoding:** Non-masked tokens are decoded via MLPs to generate continuous actions for the query observations.
5. **Training:** The network is trained using a behavior cloning approach, supervising predicted actions with ground truth actions from expert demonstrations.
6. **Inference:** During inference, the model processes one query observation at a time and predicts the corresponding action autoregressively, leveraging the context provided by prompt demonstrations to generalize to unseen tasks without retraining.

**B. Lipschitz-Constrained Vector Quantization Variational AutoEncoder (LipVQ-VAE)**
The proposed LipVQ-VAE is an action tokenizer designed to enhance temporal smoothness in action representation. It builds upon the structure of a Variational AutoEncoder (VAE) and incorporates Vector Quantization (VQ) with Lipschitz regularization.

1. **VQ-VAE Foundation:**
 - **Encoder:** An encoder `q_θ(x)` maps the continuous input action `x` (e.g., relative gripper pose) to a latent vector.
 - **Vector Quantization:** To enable discrete representation suitable for autoregressive models, the latent vector is then mapped to the nearest entry `e_k` in a learnable codebook `Z = {e_1..., e_K}`. The closest codebook entry is determined by L2 distance: `z = e_k, k = arg min_j ||q_θ(x) - e_j||^2_2`.
 - **Decoder:** A decoder `p(x|z)` reconstructs the action `x_hat` from the quantized latent vector `z`.

2. **Lipschitz Regularization for Smoothness:**
 - **Principle:** To enforce smoothness in the latent space, Lipschitz regularization is applied to the encoder network. A function `q_θ` is `c`-Lipschitz continuous if `||q_θ(t0) - q_θ(t1)|| <= c ||t0 - t1||` for any inputs `t0, t1`. This condition bounds the rate of change of the function, thereby promoting smoothness.
 - **Implementation (Weight Normalization):** For fully connected networks with 1-Lipschitz activation functions (e.g., ReLU), the overall Lipschitz bound can be controlled by bounding the per-layer Lipschitz constants. The authors adopt a method that normalizes the weight matrix `W^(ℓ)` of each layer `ℓ` in the encoder. Specifically, each row `W^(ℓ)_i` is scaled such that its absolute row sum does not exceed a trainable Lipschitz bound `softplus(c_ℓ)`:
 `W^(ℓ)_i = (W^(ℓ)_i / Σ_j |W^(ℓ)_i,j|) * softplus(c_ℓ)`
 This normalization is applied after each linear layer and before each ReLU activation. The `softplus` function `ln(1 + e^(c_ℓ))` ensures positivity of the Lipschitz bounds.

3. **Training Objective:**
 LipVQ-VAE is trained jointly with the ICRT framework using a multi-component loss function:
 - `L_reconstruction`: The L2 reconstruction error between the original action `x` and the reconstructed action `x_hat`. This ensures the latent space is meaningful for action representation.
 - `L_codebook`: A term `||sg[q_θ(x)] - z||^2_2` (where `sg` is the stop-gradient operation) that updates the codebook entries to move closer to the encoder outputs, ensuring the codebook effectively represents the action space.
 - `L_commit`: A commitment loss `||q_θ(x) - sg[z]||^2_2` that penalizes the encoder output for being far from the selected codebook entry, preventing the codebook from being underutilized.
 - `L_Lipschitz`: A regularization term `Σ_ℓ softplus(c_ℓ)` which directly penalizes large Lipschitz bounds, thus promoting smoothness.

 The overall objective function is a weighted sum: `L = L_reconstruction + αL_codebook + βL_commit + γL_Lipschitz`, with `α=1.0, β=0.25, γ=10^-6`.

By integrating LipVQ-VAE as the action tokenizer within the ICRT framework, the methodology aims to produce action representations that are not only discrete and effective for in-context learning but also temporally smooth, leading to more stable and robust robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted in both simulation environments (RoboCasa and ManiSkill) and on real robotic hardware, addressing three primary questions regarding LipVQ-VAE's effectiveness, its impact on smoothness, and its sim-to-real transferability.

**A. Simulation Robotic Manipulation Results**

1. **Performance on RoboCasa (Table I):**
 - ICRT with LipVQ-VAE achieved an average success rate of 0.530 across seven foundational skill families, which represents a 5.3% improvement over the BC-Transformer baseline (0.477), a state-of-the-art method.
 - The choice of action tokenizer significantly influenced ICRT performance, with an 8.8% difference between the best (LipVQ-VAE) and worst (MLP) tokenizers. The MLP tokenizer (0.442) even underperformed BC-Transformer, indicating that an ineffective tokenizer can degrade ICIL performance.
 - LipVQ-VAE surpassed other action tokenizers, including LFQ-VAE, by 4.1%, demonstrating the effectiveness of Lipschitz regularization for smoothness and performance.

2. **Performance on ManiSkill (Table II):**
 - In ManiSkill, for tasks like Pick Cube, Push Cube, and Stack Cube, LipVQ-VAE again achieved the highest average success rate (0.617).
 - It outperformed ACT+LFQ-VAE by 6% and the original ACT (which addresses action smoothing but without explicit latent feature regularization) by 19.7%. This suggests that explicit regularization of latent features, as in LipVQ-VAE, contributes to performance gains.

3. **Smoothness Evaluation (Table III & Fig. 4):**
 - A quantitative smoothness score (least energy, lower is smoother) revealed LipVQ-VAE to have the best score (0.63) among all tokenizers. This confirmed a positive correlation between smoother action tokenization and higher manipulation success rates across methods.
 - Qualitative visualization using t-SNE (Fig. 4) of latent trajectories further supported these findings, showing that LipVQ-VAE produced visually smoother latent representations compared to other tokenizers.

**B. Ablation Study**

1. **Impact of Lipschitz Regularization (Table IV):**
 - Adding Lipschitz regularization consistently improved performance for both MLP-based tokenizers (2.3% increase in success rate) and VQ-VAE-based tokenizers (5.5% increase in success rate) in RoboCasa.
 - VQ-VAE consistently outperformed MLP in both regularized and non-regularized settings, indicating that vector quantization into discrete latent vectors contributes to a more structured representation.

2. **Impact of Codebook Size (Fig. 5):**
 - Performance in RoboCasa generally improved with larger codebook sizes for LipVQ-VAE, peaking at a size of 2^10 (1024 entries), which yielded the best success rate (0.530). Further increases did not yield significant performance gains but introduced additional latency.

3. **Cross-dataset Results (MimicGen → Human, Table VI):**
 - When transferring from the MimicGen dataset to a human demonstration dataset in RoboCasa, ICRT frameworks (including those with weaker tokenizers like MLP) showed better adaptation to new contexts compared to BC-Transformer.
 - LipVQ-VAE within ICRT maintained its superior performance, improving over other action tokenizers by an average of 2.3% (0.525 avg success rate).

4. **Zero-shot Manipulation Results (Table VII):**
 - In zero-shot tasks (trained on 8 atomic tasks, evaluated on others), in-context learning methods generally enhanced performance.
 - LipVQ-VAE achieved a 2.4% higher average success rate (0.052) compared to BC-Transformer (0.028), and marginally outperformed other tokenizers by at least 0.2%, suggesting that temporal smoothness is beneficial even for unseen action trajectories.

**C. Real-world Robotic Manipulation Results (Table V)**

- Experiments using a Kinova Gen3 arm for "open/close drawer" and "open/close door" tasks demonstrated successful sim-to-real transfer.
- ICRT+LipVQ-VAE achieved notably higher success rates (around 12-14%) on real hardware, while baseline approaches like BC-Transformer and ICRT+Bin struggled (not exceeding 4%). This indicates that LipVQ-VAE produced smoother motions that were more reliable in the physical environment.

**D. Discussion of Limitations:**

- The approach does not explicitly address the integration of multiple modalities (vision, language, proprioception, action) into a shared latent space, which remains an open research challenge.
- While zero-shot performance improved, it remained modest, indicating that challenges in achieving broad generalization persist.
- Despite advancements, real-world performance still faces limitations, highlighting ongoing sim-to-real challenges.

In summary, the main findings confirm that action tokenization, particularly with an emphasis on temporal smoothness through Lipschitz regularization, significantly enhances ICIL performance in robotic manipulation across diverse simulated and real-world tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by systematically investigating a previously underexplored aspect of in-context imitation learning (ICIL): the role of action tokenization. The findings establish that the method of discretizing and encoding actions has a direct and substantial impact on the success and stability of robotic tasks, particularly highlighting temporal smoothness as a critical factor.

The introduction of LipVQ-VAE represents a significant methodological advancement. By incorporating Lipschitz regularization into a vector quantized variational autoencoder, the proposed tokenizer effectively enforces smoothness constraints in the latent action space. This direct approach to improving temporal continuity in action representations has been empirically demonstrated to enhance robotic manipulation performance. The consistent improvements observed across various tasks in high-fidelity simulators (RoboCasa and ManiSkill), as well as the successful transfer to real-world robotic hardware (Kinova Gen3 arm), underscore the practical utility and robustness of LipVQ-VAE.

The potential impact of this work is multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness in ICIL:** By providing a more effective and smoother action representation, LipVQ-VAE can lead to more generalized and robust policies within ICIL frameworks. This means robots may be able to learn from a smaller set of demonstrations and adapt to novel tasks and environments more reliably without requiring extensive retraining.
2. **Improved Real-world Robotic Execution:** The demonstrated ability of LipVQ-VAE to generate smoother and more reliable trajectories in real-world experiments is crucial. Smoother actions translate to more stable control signals, reduced wear and tear on robotic hardware, and a higher likelihood of task completion in complex, unstructured environments where minor oscillations or jerky movements can lead to failure.
3. **Guidance for Future Action Representation Research:** This paper highlights that the design of action tokenizers is not merely a technical detail but a fundamental component influencing the capabilities of ICIL. It provides a strong impetus for future research to consider temporal smoothness and robust latent space properties when developing new action representation methods.
4. **Broader Applicability:** While demonstrated in manipulation tasks, the principles of enforcing latent space smoothness could be applicable to other domains requiring sequential decision-making and continuous action spaces, potentially impacting areas like autonomous driving, human-robot interaction, and reinforcement learning.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations. The current approach does not explicitly integrate multiple modalities (vision, language, proprioception) into a unified shared latent space, which remains an open challenge. The zero-shot performance, while improved, is still modest, indicating that further work is needed to achieve truly broad generalization across entirely novel tasks or objects. Additionally, the inherent complexities of sim-to-real transfer persist, suggesting that bridging the reality gap requires ongoing effort beyond improvements in action tokenization alone.

Overall, this research significantly advances the understanding and implementation of action representation in in-context imitation learning, offering a concrete solution that improves robot performance and reliability.
