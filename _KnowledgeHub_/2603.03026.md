# Any Resolution Any Geometry: From Multi-View To Multi-Patch

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAUST developed the Ultra Resolution Geometry Transformer (URGT) for joint depth and surface normal estimation from a single image at arbitrary resolutions. This method reinterprets a high-resolution image as a set of patches for a multi-view transformer, significantly enhancing detail and global consistency at resolutions up to 8K, achieving a 49% reduction in AbsRel for depth and lower mean angular error for normals on UnrealStereo4K.

## Problem

- Existing joint depth-normal estimation models operate at low resolutions due to computational constraints, leading to a loss of fine detail in high-resolution imagery.
- Patch-based refinement methods improve local detail but often introduce discontinuities or inconsistencies at patch boundaries due to a lack of global reasoning.
- Multi-view transformer architectures offer global reasoning but are primarily designed for multi-view scenarios at moderate resolutions, not high-resolution monocular inputs.

## Method

- The ==Ultra Resolution Geometry Transformer (URGT)== re-imagines a high-resolution single image as a 'set of patches' processed by a ==multi-view transformer architecture==.
- It employs a specialized attention mechanism, combining ==intra-patch attention== for local detail with ==cross-patch attention== using global positional encoding (RoPE) to ensure global coherence.
- A ==GridMix patch sampling strategy== is introduced during training to improve spatial robustness and generalization by probabilistically using various grid configurations.

## Results

- URGT reduced the AbsRel for depth by **49%** (from **0.0582** to **0.0291**) and RMSE by **35%** (from **2.17** to **1.31**) on UnrealStereo4K compared to prior state-of-the-art.
- For surface normal estimation, the joint model reduced the mean angular error from **23.36°** to **18.51°** on UnrealStereo4K, outperforming Metric3D v2.
- The method demonstrates strong zero-shot and cross-domain generalization, preserving sharp boundaries, fine details, and smooth transitions across resolutions up to 8K in real-world images with an inference time of approximately **0.97 seconds** for a 4K image.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating a single high-resolution image as a collection of interacting patches processed by a transformer allows for simultaneous high-detail local refinement and global consistency.
> - Global positional encoding (RoPE) effectively maintains spatial relationships across patches, crucial for seamless geometric transitions and preventing boundary artifacts.
> - Probabilistic patch sampling (GridMix) acts as a data augmentation strategy, enhancing the model's ability to generalize across different image compositions and improve inter-patch coherence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The joint estimation of depth maps and surface normals from a single image is a fundamental task in 3D scene understanding, crucial for applications ranging from 3D reconstruction and augmented reality to robotics and autonomous driving. Depth maps provide the global spatial layout, while surface normals encode fine-grained surface orientations, making them complementary cues. However, achieving high-resolution predictions for both simultaneously has remained a significant challenge, primarily due to two main limitations in existing methods:

- **Computational Constraints and Resolution Trade-off:** Most modern joint depth-normal estimation models (ee.g., Metric3D V2, GeoWizard) operate at relatively low input resolutions due to memory and computational demands. This limits their ability to capture fine details, leading to over-smoothed results and a degradation of quality when applied to high-resolution imagery. Scaling these models directly to 4K or 8K inputs is often infeasible.
- **Local Consistency vs. Global Coherence:** Patch-based refinement methods (e.g., PatchFusion, PatchRefiner, PRO) have emerged to address high-resolution needs by dividing images into smaller patches, processing them, and then merging the results. While effective at improving local detail, these methods frequently suffer from discontinuities or inconsistencies at patch boundaries because they lack effective mechanisms for global reasoning across the entire image. Furthermore, many existing high-resolution pipelines are designed primarily for depth prediction, making joint depth and normal estimation at scale difficult.
- **Leveraging Multi-View Architectures:** In a parallel development, multi-view transformer architectures like DUSt3R and Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer (VGGT) have demonstrated strong capabilities in dense geometry estimation by jointly encoding multiple views and using attention mechanisms for global information propagation. This "set-based formulation" allows scaling by reasoning across a collection of inputs. However, these models are typically geared towards multi-view scenarios at moderate resolutions and do not directly tackle the problem of high-resolution joint depth-normal estimation from a *single* image.

This paper by Cui et al. brilliantly bridges these gaps. It draws inspiration from the global reasoning capabilities of multi-view transformers like VGGT and reinterprets a single high-resolution image as a "set of patches" (or "virtual views"). By adapting a multi-view transformer to a multi-patch setting, the authors propose a unified framework that simultaneously addresses the computational challenges of high-resolution inputs, the local-global consistency trade-off, and the need for joint depth and normal estimation. This work pushes the boundaries of monocular 3D scene understanding by delivering high-fidelity geometry at previously unattainable resolutions with strong global coherence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a unified, efficient, and scalable framework for high-resolution joint estimation of depth maps and surface normals from a single monocular image. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

- **Overcoming Resolution Limitations:** The primary motivation stems from the inherent difficulty of processing high-resolution images (e.g., 4K, 8K) for geometry estimation due to memory and computational constraints. Existing methods either downsample images, leading to loss of detail, or use patch-wise processing that introduces inconsistencies. The authors aim to enable prediction at "any resolution" without sacrificing detail or consistency.
- **Achieving Joint Geometric Consistency:** Depth and surface normals are inherently linked. Jointly estimating them should naturally lead to more geometrically consistent and accurate predictions compared to estimating them independently. The paper aims to leverage this coupling effectively within a high-resolution context.
- **Enhancing Local Detail and Global Coherence:** Current patch-based methods struggle to reconcile sharp local details with smooth global transitions across patch boundaries. The authors seek a method that can preserve fine-grained structures (like thin objects or high-frequency textures) while maintaining global geometric plausibility and seamless continuity across the entire image.
- **Improving Generalization:** The scarcity of high-resolution ground-truth data necessitates methods with strong generalization capabilities, especially for zero-shot and cross-domain applications. The authors aim to design a system robust enough to perform well on diverse in-the-wild images.
- **Efficiency and Practicality:** The solution should not only be accurate but also computationally efficient enough for practical real-world applications, avoiding excessively long inference times.

In essence, the paper aims to create a "Ultra Resolution Geometry Transformer" (URGT) that can provide high-quality, geometrically coupled depth and normal maps at unprecedented resolutions from a single image, overcoming the limitations of prior work in terms of detail, consistency, and scalability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the proposed method is the **Ultra Resolution Geometry Transformer (URGT)**, which fundamentally re-imagines the problem of high-resolution single-image geometry estimation as a multi-patch task processed by a transformer architecture.

#### 3.1. Formulation

1. **Input Preparation:** The URGT takes a high-resolution RGB image ($I$) as input. To guide the refinement process, it also incorporates coarse depth ($D_{coarse}$) and surface normal ($n_{coarse}$) estimates generated by pre-trained, robust foundation models like Depth-Anything v2 and Metric3D v2, respectively. These coarse predictions are upsampled to match the resolution of the input RGB image.
2. **Patchification and Encoding:** The high-resolution image and its upsampled coarse priors are partitioned into a grid of fixed-size patches. For each patch $k$, its RGB content ($J_k$) and corresponding coarse depth ($D_{coarse,k}$) and normal ($n_{coarse,k}$) crops are independently encoded using a powerful visual encoder, DINOv2. This yields visual tokens ($t_{J_k}$), depth tokens ($t_{D_{coarse,k}}$), and normal tokens ($t_{n_{coarse,k}}$).
3. **Unified Representation:** These independently encoded tokens for each patch are summed together to form a "unified representation" ($t_{k}^{joint}$), integrating visual and geometric information.
4. **Transformer Processing:** All unified tokens from all patches are concatenated into a single input sequence, which is then fed into a series of $L$ transformer blocks.
5. **Offset Prediction and Refinement:** A lightweight DPT-style prediction head is applied to the transformer's output. Instead of directly predicting depth and normals, the head predicts *offsets* ($\Delta_{Depth_k}$ and $\Delta_{Normal_k}$) relative to the initial coarse estimates. These offsets are then added to the coarse maps to produce the final refined depth ($D_{refined_k} = D_{coarse_k} + \Delta_{Depth_k}$) and normal ($n_{refined_k} = n_{coarse_k} + \Delta_{Normal_k}$) maps.

#### 3.2. Globally Consistent Multi-Patch Attention

The key to URGT's ability to balance local detail and global consistency lies in its specialized attention mechanism and positional encoding:

1. **Global Positional Encoding (RoPE):** To maintain spatial correlation across patches, each token is assigned a global spatial coordinate ($p_g$) by offsetting its local patch coordinate by the patch's top-left corner position in the original image. This global coordinate is used with Rotary Positional Encoding (RoPE) when computing queries and keys ($\tilde{q}, \tilde{k}$). This ensures that the transformer understands the absolute spatial relationships between tokens, regardless of which patch they originate from.
2. **Intra-Patch Attention:** Within each transformer block, the network first performs self-attention restricted to tokens *within the same patch*. This allows the model to capture fine-grained local structures and refine details specific to each patch.
3. **Cross-Patch Attention:** Subsequently, *cross-patch attention* is applied over the concatenated token sequence from *all* patches. This enables each token to attend to tokens from any other patch, facilitating long-range information exchange. This global communication is crucial for enforcing consistency, propagating context, and eliminating artifacts at patch boundaries.
By alternating these two attention mechanisms, the URGT first consolidates local details and then ensures global coherence.

#### 3.3. GridMix Patch Sampling Strategy

To improve spatial robustness and generalization, especially given the scarcity of high-resolution training data, the authors introduce **GridMix**. During training, instead of using a fixed grid configuration, the model probabilistically samples different $M \times M$ grid arrangements (where $M \in \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$) at each iteration.
- $M=1$: A single randomly sampled patch.
- $M \in \{2, 3\}$: An $M \times M$ grid of patches, randomly sampled from the image.
- $M=4$: A fixed $4 \times 4$ grid that covers the entire image.
This strategy acts as a data augmentation technique, exposing the model to various ways of partitioning the image, thereby improving inter-patch consistency and the model's ability to handle diverse spatial layouts during inference.

#### 3.4. Geometrically Consistent Supervision

The model is trained with a unified loss function that encourages geometric consistency between predicted depth and normals:

1. **Depth Loss ($L_{depth}$):** Combines a pixel-wise Mean Squared Error (MSE) term and a gradient-based term ($L_{grad}$) to promote both accurate depth values and sharp boundaries.
2. **Normal Loss ($L_{normal}$):** Uses pseudo-normals ($n_{pseudo}$) derived from the ground-truth depth map via a standard least-squares method. The loss combines an angular error term ($L_{angle}$) to penalize orientation differences and an MSE term to enforce per-pixel alignment.
3. **Total Loss ($L_{total}$):** A weighted sum of $L_{depth}$ and $L_{normal}$. Crucially, since $n_{pseudo}$ is derived from $D_{gt}$, both heads are ultimately constrained by the same underlying 3D geometry, forcing their predictions to be geometrically consistent. The framework also supports separate training for depth or normals alone.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of URGT demonstrates its superior performance across multiple benchmarks and scenarios.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **State-of-the-Art on UnrealStereo4K:** On the high-resolution UnrealStereo4K dataset, URGT achieves significant improvements over prior methods.
 - **Depth Estimation:** The joint depth-normal model reduces AbsRel from 0.0582 to 0.0291 (a 49% improvement over PatchRefiner), RMSE from 2.17 to 1.31 (a 35% improvement), and boosts $\delta_1$ accuracy from 0.956 to 0.983. It also achieves the lowest Consistency Error (CE, 0.0415) and improved Pseudo Depth Boundary Error (PDBE), indicating sharper and more consistent depth boundaries.
 - **Surface Normal Estimation:** The joint model substantially outperforms Metric3D v2, reducing the mean angular error from 23.36° to 18.51° and RMS angular error from 13.90 to 9.60. It also shows significantly higher accuracy thresholds (e.g., 29.37% within 5° vs. 11.74% for Metric3D v2).
- **Computational Efficiency:** Despite its advanced capabilities, URGT maintains a reasonable inference time of approximately 0.97 seconds for a 4K image.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Superiority

Visual comparisons in the paper (e.g., Figure 1, 4, 6) consistently show that URGT produces:
- **Sharper Boundaries and Finer Details:** The method effectively preserves thin structures and high-frequency textures that are often blurred or lost by other models.
- **Smoother Transitions and Global Consistency:** URGT eliminates the "block-wise" artifacts and discontinuities common in other patch-based methods, yielding seamless geometry across the entire image.

#### 4.3. Zero-Shot Generalization

URGT demonstrates strong zero-shot and cross-domain generalization capabilities, outperforming prior refinement approaches on real-world datasets like Booster, ETH3D, and Middlebury 2014. For instance, on Booster, the joint model achieves the lowest AbsRel (0.0248) and matches PRO on $\delta_1$ (0.997). This highlights its robustness to diverse real-world scenes.

#### 4.4. Scalability to Arbitrary Resolutions

A key strength of URGT is its ability to scale effectively to "any resolution" (2K, 4K, 6K, 8K, and beyond) without requiring resolution-specific training. This is achieved by adaptively scaling the patch grid to cover the full image while keeping the patch size and transformer backbone fixed. The teaser image (Figure 1) and supplementary materials (Figure 10, 13) showcase its performance on an 8K image and even an out-of-domain 8K manga-style image, where it still recovers geometrically plausible depth and normals.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies confirm the importance of key methodological components:
- **GridMix Patch Sampling:** Probabilistic sampling of different grid configurations significantly improves performance and generalization (AbsRel 0.0295, RMSE 1.35) compared to using a single fixed grid (e.g., AbsRel 0.0321, RMSE 1.42 for 4x4 fixed grid), especially for inter-patch coherence (lower CE).
- **Global Positional Encoding:** Global RoPE is crucial for cross-patch geometric alignment, leading to much lower Consistency Error (0.0635 vs. 0.2830 for local RoPE) and smoother transitions across patch boundaries.
- **Cross-Patch Attention:** Disabling cross-patch attention results in significantly higher errors (AbsRel 0.0678 vs. 0.0500) and visible boundary discontinuities between neighboring regions, underscoring its role in maintaining inter-patch geometric consistency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "Any Resolution Any Geometry: From Multi-View To Multi-Patch" holds significant implications for the field of 3D computer vision and beyond:

- **Enabling High-Fidelity 3D Scene Understanding:** By delivering high-resolution, geometrically consistent, and detail-preserving depth and normal maps, URGT provides foundational data for a wide array of downstream applications. These include high-quality 3D reconstruction, realistic scene relighting, accurate object detection and segmentation in 3D, and advanced applications in augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) where precise environmental geometry is critical for believable interactions. The ability to process 4K and 8K imagery directly opens up new possibilities for creating detailed virtual worlds from real-world captures.
- **Unified and Scalable Solution:** The paper presents a robust and flexible framework that overcomes a long-standing challenge in balancing resolution, detail, and global consistency in monocular 3D geometry estimation. Its scalability to arbitrary resolutions without retraining is a major practical advantage, allowing deployment in diverse scenarios with varying input image quality.
- **New Paradigm for High-Resolution Processing:** The novel approach of reinterpreting a single high-resolution image as a collection of patches processed by a multi-view transformer offers a powerful new paradigm. This "multi-patch as multi-view" concept could inspire similar architectural adaptations for other high-resolution image processing tasks that require both local precision and global coherence.
- **Improved Robustness and Generalization:** The GridMix patch sampling strategy and the use of global positional encoding contribute to a highly robust model with strong zero-shot and cross-domain generalization. This is particularly valuable given the difficulties of acquiring large-scale, high-resolution ground-truth datasets for training, making the model more applicable to real-world, unconstrained environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** URGT provides a strong baseline and a versatile architecture for future advancements. Researchers could explore integrating it with other generative models, refining other geometric primitives, or adapting it for video input to achieve temporally consistent high-resolution 3D geometry. The modularity, particularly the offset prediction from coarse priors, also suggests avenues for creating more generic, plug-and-play refinement modules independent of specific coarse predictors.
- **Practical Efficiency:** Achieving state-of-the-art results with reasonable inference times (under 1 second for 4K images) makes this method highly practical for real-time or near-real-time applications, moving it from a purely academic exercise to a deployable solution.

In conclusion, "Any Resolution Any Geometry" represents a significant leap forward in monocular 3D geometry estimation. By elegantly adapting advanced transformer architectures and introducing innovative training strategies, the authors have developed a method that is not only highly accurate and scalable but also capable of producing unprecedented levels of detail and consistency, thereby broadening the horizons for 3D scene understanding in various applications.
