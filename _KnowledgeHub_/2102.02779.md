# Unifying Vision-and-Language Tasks via Text Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UNC Chapel Hill developed VL-T5 and VL-BART, unified transformer models that reformulate diverse vision-and-language tasks as multimodal conditional text generation. These models achieve performance on par with specialized architectures while demonstrating enhanced generalization for open-ended visual question answering.

## Problem

- Diverse vision-and-language (VL) tasks traditionally require distinct model architectures and loss functions, leading to fragmentation and high engineering overhead.
- Existing VL transformers, while using shared backbones, still depend on task-specific "heads" for different objectives.
- Discriminative VL models often struggle with open-ended responses and rare cases, particularly in tasks like visual question answering.

## Method

- Develop **VL-T5** and **VL-BART**, unified ==encoder-decoder transformer== architectures adapted from T5 Base and BART Base.
- Reformulate all vision-and-language tasks as a ==multimodal conditional text generation== problem using a single ==language modeling objective==.
- Integrate task-specific ==text prefixes== and ==visual sentinel tokens== into the input to guide the model and represent visual outputs generatively.

## Results

- VL-T5 and VL-BART achieved performance comparable to or slightly surpassed recent state-of-the-art task-specific VL transformers across 7 diverse benchmarks.
- The generative approach demonstrated superior generalization for open-ended VQA, outperforming discriminative baselines by **6-6.2 percentage points** on questions with rare answers.
- A single VL-T5 model, trained on all **7 downstream tasks** simultaneously, maintained performance similar to single-task models, indicating effective multi-task learning and parameter sharing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diverse vision-and-language tasks can be effectively unified under a single multimodal conditional text generation framework.
> - Pretrained text-to-text models like T5 and BART can be successfully extended to handle visual inputs, providing a powerful foundation for multimodal AI.
> - Recasting discriminative VL tasks as generative text generation enhances generalization, particularly for open-ended answers and rare cases.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a significant challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Vision-and-Language (VL) learning: the proliferation of task-specific models. Historically, distinct VL tasks—such as visual question answering (VQA), referring expression comprehension (REC), and image captioning—have necessitated specialized model architectures, loss functions, and inference procedures. This approach leads to considerable engineering overhead, limited knowledge transfer between tasks, and difficulties in scaling to a growing number of VL applications.

The paper draws a clear parallel with the success of the **pretraining-finetuning paradigm** in Natural Language Processing (NLP), exemplified by models like BERT, GPT, T5, and BART. These large language models have demonstrated remarkable versatility by reformulating diverse NLP tasks (e.g., question answering, text summarization, classification) into a unified text-to-text format, often through conditional text generation. Existing VL transformers (e.g., LXMERT, ViLBERT, UNITER, Oscar) have adopted the pretraining-finetuning paradigm but often still rely on task-specific "heads" (e.g., a multi-label classifier for VQA, a region scorer for REC) attached to a shared backbone.

This work steps further by proposing a **fully unified framework** for VL tasks. It positions itself within the trend of "unified frameworks" in NLP (like T5) that aim to solve a multitude of tasks with a single model architecture and objective function. By converting all VL tasks into a multimodal conditional text generation problem, the authors challenge the conventional discriminative modeling approaches prevalent in many VL tasks. This paradigm shift offers several advantages:
- **Architectural Simplification:** Eliminates the need for hand-crafted task-specific layers.
- **Enhanced Generalization:** Potentially better handling of open-ended generation and rare cases, where discriminative classifiers struggle.
- **Efficient Multi-task Learning:** Facilitates training a single model across multiple tasks simultaneously, promoting parameter sharing and knowledge transfer.

The paper thus represents a significant step towards creating more general-purpose and efficient multimodal AI systems, mirroring the advancements seen in text-only NLP towards truly unified models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **propose and evaluate a unified framework for learning diverse vision-and-language tasks via multimodal conditional text generation**. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Alleviate Task-Specific Design Hassles:** Existing VL methods typically require designing unique architectures and objectives for each task. For instance, VQA often uses a multi-label answer classifier, while referring expression comprehension might employ a region scorer, and image captioning a language decoder. This proliferation of specialized components is inefficient and cumbersome. The authors aim to simplify this by using a single, consistent approach.

2. **Leverage Pretrained Language Models for Multimodality:** Building on the success of transformer-based language models (like T5 and BART), the authors seek to extend their powerful text generation capabilities to the multimodal domain. They hypothesize that these models, when properly extended with visual understanding, can directly generate textual labels or responses for various VL tasks, rather than relying on discriminative classification.

3. **Improve Generalization to Open-Ended and Rare Scenarios:** Discriminative models often operate over a fixed, predefined set of answers or regions, limiting their ability to handle open-ended questions or answers not frequently seen during training. By adopting a generative approach, the models are intrinsically capable of producing novel or less common textual outputs, which is particularly beneficial for tasks like VQA with rare answers.

4. **Enable Efficient Multi-Task Learning:** With a unified architecture and objective, the framework is designed to facilitate simultaneous training on multiple VL tasks using a single set of parameters. This promises better knowledge sharing, reduced computational resources compared to training separate models, and the potential to develop more robust, general-purpose VL agents.

In essence, the research is motivated by the desire to move beyond fragmented, task-specific solutions in VL learning towards a more elegant, generalized, and efficient paradigm inspired by recent breakthroughs in unified NLP.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the development of **VL-T5** and **VL-BART**, unified encoder-decoder transformer architectures based on the pre-trained text-to-text models T5 Base and BART Base, respectively. The methodology centers on reformulating all vision-and-language tasks as **multimodal conditional text generation**.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The models adopt a standard **transformer encoder-decoder architecture**.
- **Multimodal Encoder:** This component takes concatenated text and visual embeddings as input. It is a stack of transformer blocks responsible for creating contextualized joint representations of the multimodal input.
- **Autoregressive Text Decoder:** This component generates output text tokens sequentially. It attends to previously generated tokens (via self-attention) and the encoder's output (via cross-attention) to predict the next token.
- **Backbone Extension:** The original T5/BART text encoders are extended to multimodal encoders by incorporating image region embeddings.

#### 3.2. Input Representation

- **Visual Embeddings:** An input image `v` is represented by `n=36` object regions detected by a Faster R-CNN model pre-trained on Visual Genome. Each region is encoded as a sum of four feature types: (i) RoI object features, (ii) RoI bounding box coordinates, (iii) image IDs (for tasks with multiple images like NLVR2), and (iv) region IDs. Crucially, the paper introduces **visual sentinel tokens** (e.g., `<vis_1>`, `<vis_3>`) whose text embeddings are reused as region ID embeddings. This sharing is key to unifying text-based and region-based outputs.
- **Text Embeddings:** Input text `x` is augmented with **task-specific prefixes** (e.g., "vqa:", "visual grounding:") to guide the model on the nature of the task. These prefixes allow the single model to adapt to different tasks without changing its architecture. The text is tokenized and embedded using the original T5/BART vocabulary, extended with the aforementioned visual sentinel tokens. Positional embeddings (relative for T5, absolute for BART) are applied.

#### 3.3. Unified Objective Function

All pretraining and downstream tasks are trained with the same **language modeling objective**: minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the target label text `y` given the input text `x` and image `v`. This means the model learns to generate the correct sequence of tokens for any task.

#### 3.4. Task Formulation as Text Generation

This is the central innovation. Discriminative tasks are reframed as generative:
- **Visual Question Answering (VQA):** Instead of a multi-label classifier, the model generates the answer text (e.g., "vqa: what is the man jumping over?" -> "fire hydrant").
- **Referring Expression Comprehension (RefCOCOg):** Instead of a region classifier, the model generates the visual sentinel token corresponding to the target region (e.g., "visual grounding: yellow fire hydrant" -> "<vis_3>").
- **Image-Text Matching:** The model generates "true" or "false" to indicate correspondence.
- **Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR):** For multiple-choice, the model concatenates the question and each candidate answer/rationale, then generates "true" or "false" for correctness, effectively ranking candidates by probability.
- **Image Captioning:** Naturally a generation task, the model generates the descriptive caption.
- **Multimodal Machine Translation:** The model translates the source text into the target language, conditioned on the image.

#### 3.5. Pretraining Strategy

The models are pretrained in a multi-task setup on aggregated data from MS COCO and Visual Genome (9.18M image-text pairs on 180K images). The pretraining tasks, also formulated as text generation, include:
- **Multimodal Language Modeling:** Masked span prediction (VL-T5) or token denoising (VL-BART) on image-caption pairs.
- **Visual Question Answering:** Generating answers to VQA-style questions.
- **Image-Text Matching:** Generating "true" or "false" for image-caption pairs.
- **Visual Grounding:** Generating region IDs from object descriptions.
- **Grounded Captioning:** Generating region descriptions from region IDs.

#### 3.6. Finetuning Strategy

After pretraining, the models are finetuned on 7 diverse downstream benchmarks (VQA, GQA, NLVR2, RefCOCOg, VCR, COCO Caption, Multi30K En-De). Both single-task and multi-task finetuning (with a single set of shared parameters across all tasks) are explored.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presents compelling evidence for the efficacy of the unified generative framework across a wide array of vision-and-language tasks:

1. **Comparable Performance to Task-Specific SOTA:** On 7 popular VL benchmarks, including tasks traditionally modeled discriminatively (VQA, GQA, NLVR2, RefCOCOg, VCR), the generative VL-T5 and VL-BART models achieve performance comparable to or even slightly exceeding recent state-of-the-art task-specific VL transformers. This is a significant finding given that the proposed models use a single unified architecture and a consistent language modeling objective for all tasks, contrasting with the varied architectures and objectives of baselines.

2. **Superior Generalization in Visual Question Answering:** A key advantage of the generative approach is demonstrated in VQA, particularly for "out-of-domain" questions where the ground truth answers are rare or not part of a predefined frequent answer set. Generative VL-T5 and VL-BART significantly outperform their discriminative counterparts (and strong discriminative baselines like UNITER) by 6 to 6.2 percentage points on these rare-answer scenarios. This highlights the framework's better generalization ability and its suitability for open-ended VQA.

3. **Effective Multi-Task Learning:** The framework successfully supports multi-task learning. A single VL-T5 model, trained on all 7 downstream tasks with a single set of parameters, achieves performance similar to separately optimized single-task models. This indicates efficient parameter sharing and knowledge transfer across tasks, demonstrating the framework's potential for building more universal VL models. Furthermore, the single shared language modeling head performs nearly as well as task-specific heads while requiring significantly fewer total parameters.

4. **Robustness Across Task Types:**
 - **NLVR2:** VL-T5 shows comparable performance to UNITER in the computationally lighter "Triplet" encoding setting.
 - **RefCOCOg:** VL-T5 significantly outperforms older modular models like MAttNet and achieves reasonable performance compared to UNITER, despite UNITER being pretrained on a much larger corpus. This validates the generative formulation of grounding tasks. (VL-BART, however, showed divergence, hypothesized to be due to its absolute positional encoding leading to memorization.)
 - **Visual Commonsense Reasoning (VCR):** With second-stage in-domain pretraining, VL-T5 achieves comparable (slightly better) performance to UNITER for the challenging Q→AR task.
 - **Image Captioning (COCO Caption):** The models demonstrate competitive caption generation capabilities.
 - **Multimodal Machine Translation (Multi30K En-De):** VL-T5-based models surpass baselines, including those using strong data augmentation, indicating that visual context can benefit translation even if V&L pretraining doesn't always yield a large boost.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step towards the development of truly unified and general-purpose multimodal AI systems, with several profound implications:

1. **Reduced Engineering Complexity and Cost:** By eliminating the need for task-specific architectures and objectives, the framework substantially reduces the development and engineering overhead associated with creating new VL models. Researchers and practitioners can adapt models to new tasks simply by defining appropriate text prefixes and expected text outputs, akin to how large language models are used for various NLP tasks.

2. **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The demonstrated ability of generative models to handle open-ended questions and rare answers more effectively than discriminative methods is crucial for real-world applications. This capability is vital for scenarios where the range of possible outputs is vast and unpredictable, moving beyond the limitations of fixed classification bins. This makes VL-T5 and VL-BART more robust to out-of-distribution data.

3. **Efficient Multi-Task Learning and Resource Utilization:** The success in multi-task finetuning with a single set of parameters showcases a path towards more efficient use of computational resources. Instead of training and deploying multiple specialized models, a single unified model can handle various VL tasks. This has implications for sustainability in AI research and deployment, especially for large-scale models.

4. **Broader Applicability of Pretrained Language Models:** The work highlights the power of extending existing, highly performant pretrained language models (like T5 and BART) into the multimodal domain. This strategy leverages the immense knowledge encoded in these language models, accelerating progress in multimodal AI without necessarily having to build entirely new multimodal foundational models from scratch.

5. **Inspiration for Future Research:** By demonstrating that a diverse set of discriminative VL tasks can be effectively recast as generative problems, this paper opens new avenues for research. It encourages further exploration into unifying even more complex multimodal tasks within a single generation framework, potentially leading to models that exhibit more holistic understanding and reasoning abilities across modalities.

In conclusion, "Unifying Vision-and-Language Tasks via Text Generation" offers a compelling vision for the future of multimodal AI, advocating for architectural simplicity, improved generalization, and efficiency through a unified generative approach. The public availability of the code (VL-T5) further ensures that this impactful work can be built upon by the wider research community.
