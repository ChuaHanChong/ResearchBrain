# Masked World Models for Visual Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Masked World Models (MWM) introduces a decoupled framework for visual model-based reinforcement learning, utilizing a specialized autoencoder that employs convolutional feature masking and an auxiliary reward prediction objective to learn highly detailed visual representations. This method enables more accurate latent dynamics modeling, demonstrating superior performance and sample efficiency in complex robotic control tasks requiring fine-grained perception, such as those in Meta-world and RLBench.

## Problem

- Current visual model-based RL methods often use a single, monolithic model, leading to a trade-off between visual representation quality and dynamics accuracy, particularly for fine-grained details critical in robotic manipulation.
- Standard Masked Autoencoders (MAE), which mask raw pixel patches, struggle to capture fine-grained details *within* those patches, limiting their utility for precise object interaction in control tasks.
- Pure reconstruction objectives for visual representation learning may not inherently prioritize task-relevant information, potentially leading to representations that are not optimally suited for guiding a robot's policy.

## Method

- MWM decouples visual representation learning from ==latent dynamics== learning, continuously updating both components with online experience.
- A specialized autoencoder employs convolutional feature masking on early convolutional outputs (rather than raw pixels) and incorporates an auxiliary reward prediction objective to learn detailed, task-relevant visual representations.
- A Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM) learns environment dynamics based on these *frozen*, detailed latent representations, with its decoder reconstructing the latent features instead of raw pixels, and an actor-critic model is trained using imagined rollouts.

## Results

- MWM achieved an **81.7%** aggregate success rate across 50 Meta-world visual robotic manipulation tasks, significantly outperforming DreamerV2's **67.9%**, especially on tasks requiring precise small object interaction.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both convolutional feature masking and the auxiliary reward prediction objective were critical for MWM's performance, with their removal leading to substantial degradation.
- Qualitative analysis showed MWM's world model accurately predicted task-relevant object positions, and learned representations demonstrated transferability, achieving strong performance on unseen manipulation tasks when used frozen.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling visual representation learning from dynamics modeling, especially when using Vision Transformers, is crucial for achieving accurate world models that can capture fine-grained visual details required for complex robotic control.
> - Masking and reconstructing convolutional features (rather than raw pixel patches) in an autoencoder significantly improves the ability to capture fine-grained visual details within patches, which are essential for precise robotic interactions.
> - Incorporating an auxiliary reward prediction objective during visual representation learning explicitly guides the autoencoder to embed task-relevant information into its latent space, substantially enhancing downstream policy performance and representation generalizability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of visual model-based reinforcement learning (RL), which aims to enable robots to learn efficiently from visual observations by building a "world model" of their environment. Historically, model-based RL approaches have shown promise for sample efficiency, either by using the learned model for planning or for generating synthetic experience to train policies.

Current visual model-based RL methods often train a single, monolithic model end-to-end to learn both visual representations and environment dynamics. The authors identify a limitation in this integrated approach: it can create a trade-off between the quality of visual representations and the accuracy of dynamics modeling. This trade-off can make it difficult for world models to capture fine-grained visual details, particularly those related to interactions with small objects, which are crucial for many robotic manipulation tasks.

Previous attempts to decouple representation learning from dynamics learning, such as using Variational Autoencoders (VAEs), have faced challenges because the separately learned VAE representations might not be optimally suited for dynamics prediction or may fail to encode task-relevant information.

More recently, Masked Autoencoders (MAE), which utilize Vision Transformers (ViTs) and self-supervised learning by reconstructing masked pixel patches, have emerged as effective and scalable methods for visual representation learning. However, the authors observe that standard MAE might not capture the fine-grained details within patches that are essential for visual control tasks, where precise object positions and interactions are critical.

This work, "Masked World Models (MWM)," positions itself by addressing these limitations. It proposes a novel decoupled framework that aims to harness the benefits of ViT-based self-supervised representation learning while overcoming the specific challenges of capturing fine visual details for robotic control. By introducing convolutional feature masking and an auxiliary reward prediction objective within the representation learning phase, MWM seeks to generate representations more amenable to accurate latent dynamics learning and subsequent policy optimization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a visual model-based reinforcement learning framework that can achieve sample-efficient robot learning from visual observations, particularly in environments involving complex visual details and interactions with small objects.

The motivations underpinning this objective are multi-faceted:

- **Addressing the End-to-End Learning Bottleneck:** Current visual model-based RL algorithms commonly learn visual representations and environment dynamics within a single, end-to-end world model. The authors hypothesize that this integrated optimization process forces a compromise, limiting the model's ability to accurately capture intricate visual details and predict future states precisely. For instance, tasks involving robot interaction with small objects necessitate a high degree of visual fidelity for the model to predict outcomes accurately. Decoupling these learning processes is motivated by the idea that each component can then be optimized for its specific function without interference from the other, potentially leading to more accurate and robust models.

- **Enhancing Visual Representation Learning for Fine-Grained Details:** While Masked Autoencoders (MAE) have demonstrated success in general visual representation learning, they typically operate by masking and reconstructing large pixel patches. The authors argue that this approach can hinder the learning of fine-grained details *within* these patches, such as the exact position of a small object. Such details are indispensable for robotic control tasks. The motivation here is to adapt the MAE framework to capture these critical visual specifics while retaining MAE's advantages of stability, computational efficiency, and scalability, which are beneficial for online RL. This led to the idea of masking convolutional features rather than raw pixel patches, as convolutional layers inherently process and mix local visual information.

- **Integrating Task-Relevant Information into Representations:** Pure reconstruction objectives, whether of pixels or latent features, may not inherently prioritize task-relevant information. For a model to effectively guide a robot's policy, its learned representations should encapsulate features that are predictive of rewards and facilitate successful task completion. Therefore, the authors are motivated to introduce an auxiliary reward prediction objective during the visual representation learning phase. This aims to explicitly encourage the autoencoder to encode task-salient information into its latent space, thereby enriching the representations for subsequent dynamics learning and policy optimization.

- **Improving Latent Dynamics Modeling:** Once high-quality, task-relevant visual representations are learned, the objective is to leverage them to train a more effective latent dynamics model. By providing the dynamics model with abstract yet detailed representations rather than raw pixels, the motivation is that the dynamics model can focus predominantly on learning temporal transitions and state evolution, without simultaneously needing to extract salient visual features from high-dimensional pixel data. This specialization is expected to improve the accuracy and efficiency of dynamics learning.

In summary, the key objectives are to overcome the limitations of monolithic world models and standard self-supervised vision transformers in robotic control by decoupling representation and dynamics learning, designing a representation learner that excels at fine-grained detail capture via convolutional feature masking, and incorporating task-relevance through auxiliary reward prediction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Masked World Models (MWM) framework operates by decoupling visual representation learning from latent dynamics learning, updating both continually with online samples. The core approach involves two main iterative processes: training a specialized autoencoder and then learning a latent dynamics model on its representations.

#### 3.1. Visual Representation Learning

The first component involves training an autoencoder with a novel masking strategy and an auxiliary objective.

- **Autoencoder Architecture:** The autoencoder is designed with early convolutional layers, a Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder, and a ViT decoder.
 - **Convolution Stem:** Instead of a traditional pixel-patchification stem, the input raw visual observation (e.g., 64x64x3 RGB image) is first processed by a convolution stem comprising a few convolutional layers (e.g., 3 convolutional layers with 4x4 kernel, stride 2, followed by a 1x1 convolution). This stem transforms the raw pixels into a grid of convolutional features.
 - **Convolutional Feature Masking:** A key innovation is the application of random masking directly to these *convolutional features* (`h_c,t`), rather than to raw pixel patches as in standard MAE. A high masking ratio (e.g., 75%) is employed. This results in `h_c,m,t`, the masked convolutional features. The rationale is that early convolutional layers, by mixing low-level details, enable the autoencoder to reconstruct fine-grained information within patches by inferring from surrounding unmasked features, a capability often limited by pixel-patch masking.
 - **ViT Encoder:** The unmasked convolutional features (along with a learnable CLS token) are fed into a ViT encoder (e.g., 4 layers).
 - **ViT Decoder:** The output of the ViT encoder, combined with learnable mask tokens (including one for reward prediction), is then processed by a ViT decoder (e.g., 3 layers).
 - **Pixel Reconstruction:** The ViT decoder's output is used to reconstruct the *original raw pixels* (`o_t`). Unlike standard MAE, the loss is computed on the entire image, not just masked patches.
- **Auxiliary Reward Prediction Objective:** To ensure task-relevant information is embedded in the representations, the autoencoder is concurrently trained to predict the reward (`r_t`) associated with the observation. A dedicated learnable mask token is introduced into the ViT decoder's input, and its corresponding output is used by a linear head to predict the reward. This objective is optimized jointly with the pixel reconstruction loss.
- **Optimization (L_vis):** The autoencoder's parameters ($\phi$) are optimized to minimize the sum of the negative log-likelihood of pixel reconstruction and the negative log-likelihood of reward prediction for a given batch of observations and rewards.
- **Initialization with Warm-up:** The autoencoder parameters are initialized with a warm-up schedule, training for a certain number of gradient steps (e.g., 5000) using a linear warm-up over initial steps (e.g., 2500) from an initial random exploration phase. This helps stabilize training from the beginning.

#### 3.2. Latent Dynamics Learning

Once the visual representations are learned (and continually updated), a latent dynamics model is trained on these representations.

- **Frozen Representations:** The autoencoder is used in an unmasked state (masking ratio `m=0`) to encode raw observations into detailed latent representations (`z_c,0,t`). Crucially, the autoencoder's parameters are *frozen* during this dynamics learning phase.
- **RSSM Adaptation:** MWM adapts the Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM) architecture, a core component of DreamerV2.
 - **Input to RSSM:** Instead of raw pixels, the RSSM's representation model receives the autoencoder's latent features (`z_c,0,t`), previous state (`s_t-1`), and previous action (`a_t-1`) to infer the current state (`s_t`).
 - **Reconstruction Target:** The RSSM's decoder is trained to reconstruct `z_c,0,t` from its latent states (`s_t`), rather than raw pixels.
 - **Full Latent Utilization:** Unlike some MAE-based approaches that use only the CLS token for downstream tasks, MWM utilizes *all* elements of the `z_c,0,t` representation, which typically contain richer spatial information, for dynamics learning.
 - **Other RSSM Components:** The transition model, which predicts future states without observation, and the reward predictor (within the RSSM), which predicts future rewards from latent states, are retained from the DreamerV2 architecture.
- **Optimization (L_dyn):** The latent dynamics model's parameters ($\theta$) are optimized to minimize a variational lower bound, which includes terms for reconstructing the latent features (`z_c,0,t`), predicting rewards (`r_j`), and a KL divergence term to regularize the latent state distributions.
- **Behavior Learning (Actor-Critic):** Similar to DreamerV2, an actor-critic model is trained using imagined rollouts from the learned latent dynamics model. The actor learns to maximize imagined returns by backpropagating gradients through the world model, and the critic learns to estimate state values.

#### 3.3. Overall Training Loop

The MWM algorithm iteratively repeats these steps:
1. **Collect Transitions:** The agent interacts with the environment, collecting observations, actions, and rewards, which are stored in a replay buffer. The autoencoder's current representations are used to update the model state, and the actor samples actions based on this state.
2. **Visual Representation Learning:** A batch of samples is drawn from the replay buffer, and the autoencoder's parameters ($\phi$) are updated by minimizing `L_vis`.
3. **Dynamics Learning:** Another batch is drawn, and the latent dynamics model's parameters ($\theta$) are updated by minimizing `L_dyn`, using the *frozen* representations from the autoencoder.
4. **Actor-Critic Learning:** Imagined future rollouts are generated using the updated latent dynamics model and the current actor. The actor's parameters ($\psi$) and critic's parameters ($\xi$) are then updated based on these imaginary experiences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Masked World Models (MWM) demonstrates its effectiveness and robustness across various visual control benchmarks, consistently outperforming a prominent state-of-the-art model-based RL baseline, DreamerV2.

- **Meta-world Experiments:**
 - MWM achieved a significantly higher aggregate success rate of 81.7% across 50 visual robotic manipulation tasks, compared to DreamerV2's 67.9%.
 - On individual challenging tasks, particularly those requiring precise interaction with small objects (e.g., "Pick Place," "Coffee Pull," "Hand Insert"), MWM demonstrated substantial gains in both sample efficiency and final performance. For instance, MWM achieved over 80% success rate on "Pick Place," where DreamerV2 struggled.
 - The consistent outperformance across a diverse set of 50 tasks indicates that MWM's decoupled approach and specialized representation learning are beneficial for complex robotic manipulation.

- **RLBench Experiments:**
 - On selected RLBench tasks ("Reach Target," "Push Button") that are characterized by sparse rewards and require fine visual understanding, MWM again significantly outperformed DreamerV2.
 - MWM achieved over 80% success rate on "Reach Target," whereas DreamerV2 struggled with less than 20%. This was attributed to DreamerV2's failure to capture target object positions accurately, a challenge MWM addressed.

- **DeepMind Control Suite Experiments:**
 - MWM showed competitive performance with DreamerV2 on visual locomotion tasks (e.g., "Quadruped Run," "Quadruped Walk"), indicating the generality of the approach beyond manipulation.
 - For manipulation tasks within this suite, such as "Reach Duplo," MWM outperformed DreamerV2, reinforcing the finding that MWM is effective in environments requiring the capture of fine-grained object details.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Convolutional Feature Masking:** Directly comparing convolutional feature masking with traditional pixel patch masking (MAE) demonstrated that the former significantly improved performance. This supports the hypothesis that masking features from early convolutional layers is more effective for visual control, as it enables the model to capture fine-grained details within patches.
 - **Masking Ratio:** An optimal masking ratio of 75% was identified. Lower ratios (25%, 50%) yielded worse performance, suggesting that a strong regularization prevents shortcut learning. However, an excessively high ratio (90%) also led to performance degradation, indicating a balance is needed.
 - **Reward Prediction:** Removing the auxiliary reward prediction objective from the autoencoder's training significantly degraded performance. This highlights the importance of explicitly encoding task-relevant information into visual representations for successful control.
 - **World Model Input (CLS vs. All Representations):** Utilizing all generated latent representations (including spatial information) as input to the dynamics model proved more effective than using only the CLS token, indicating the value of retaining spatial context.
 - **Autoencoder Model Size:** For the Meta-world tasks studied, varying the number of layers in the autoencoder's ViT encoder and decoder did not yield significant performance differences, suggesting that the complexity of these environments might not necessitate larger models.
 - **DreamerV2 with ViT:** A direct replacement of DreamerV2's CNN components with a ViT architecture resulted in severe training instability and poor performance, underscoring the critical role of MWM's decoupled training strategy for effectively leveraging ViT architectures in RL.

- **Qualitative Analysis:**
 - Visualizations of future frame predictions revealed that MWM's world model accurately captured and predicted the positions of task-relevant objects (e.g., a red target block), which DreamerV2 often failed to do.
 - Interestingly, while MWM's autoencoder reconstructs all visual details, its latent dynamics model appeared to implicitly ignore task-irrelevant components (e.g., other colored blocks) during its predictions, focusing on the critical elements for task completion. This illustrates how the decoupling allows the autoencoder to learn general visual fidelity while the dynamics model specializes in task-relevant dynamics.

- **Generalizability of Representations (Appendix L):**
 - Representations learned with reward prediction on one task (e.g., "Push") showed transferability, achieving comparable or even better performance when used frozen for other unseen manipulation tasks (e.g., "Push Back," "Pick Place," "Drawer Open") than MWM trained from scratch on those tasks.
 - A state regression analysis confirmed that representations learned with reward prediction provided better predictive power for proprioceptive states on both seen and unseen tasks, suggesting they embed broadly useful information for robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Masked World Models (MWM) framework represents a notable advancement in visual model-based reinforcement learning, offering several significant implications for future research and applications in robotics and AI.

- **Enhanced World Model Accuracy for Complex Visual Tasks:** The primary significance of MWM lies in demonstrating that decoupling visual representation learning from dynamics learning, when implemented with an appropriate architecture, can lead to more accurate world models. This is particularly crucial for robotic tasks that demand fine-grained perception and precise interaction with small objects, where traditional end-to-end approaches have shown limitations. The ability to better model these complex visual dynamics directly translates to more reliable planning and control.

- **Effective Integration of Vision Transformers in Online RL:** MWM provides a robust method for incorporating Vision Transformers (ViTs) into online reinforcement learning. ViTs, known for their strong performance in computer vision, have been challenging to integrate into model-based RL due to potential training instability. MWM's decoupled approach stabilizes ViT training for representation learning, demonstrating that these powerful architectures can be effectively leveraged for visual control tasks, leading to improved performance and sample efficiency.

- **Novel Self-Supervised Representation Learning for Robotics:** The introduction of convolutional feature masking as an alternative to pixel-patch masking in masked autoencoders is a significant methodological contribution. This approach enables the autoencoder to capture finer visual details within feature patches, which is critical for robotic manipulation where precise object states are paramount. This technique offers a new direction for designing self-supervised learning objectives tailored to the specific demands of visual control.

- **Importance of Task-Relevant Representation Shaping:** The ablation studies clearly highlight the crucial role of the auxiliary reward prediction objective in shaping effective visual representations. This finding emphasizes that reconstruction alone might not be sufficient for generating representations that are optimal for control. Incorporating task-specific signals during representation learning can significantly boost policy performance and suggests that even "general" visual representations benefit from task-specific tuning. The observed generalizability of these task-informed representations to unseen tasks further indicates their robustness and utility.

- **Improved Sample Efficiency:** MWM's consistent outperformance and often superior sample efficiency compared to DreamerV2 signify a step forward in reducing the data requirements for learning complex visual control policies. This is a practical benefit for real-world robotics, where data collection can be expensive and time-consuming.

**Potential Impact:**

The implications of MWM extend to various areas:

- **Robotic Manipulation:** The improved ability to model interactions with small objects opens avenues for more sophisticated and dexterous robotic manipulation in unstructured environments, impacting applications in manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics.
- **Autonomous Systems:** Enhanced visual world models could improve the perception and planning capabilities of autonomous vehicles and drones, enabling them to navigate and interact more effectively with dynamic and visually complex surroundings.
- **Foundation Models for Control:** The framework's ability to learn transferable representations, even from task-specific reward signals, suggests a path towards developing "foundation models" for robotic control that can be pre-trained and then fine-tuned for a wide range of tasks, reducing the need for learning from scratch each time.
- **Theoretical Understanding:** The work contributes to a deeper understanding of the interplay between visual representation learning and dynamics modeling in reinforcement learning, informing the design of future RL architectures.

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges limitations such as dependence on reward signals and current sample inefficiency for real-world deployment. Future work exploring video-based representation learning, multimodal inputs, and leveraging demonstrations or pre-trained models could further enhance MWM's capabilities and address these remaining challenges.
