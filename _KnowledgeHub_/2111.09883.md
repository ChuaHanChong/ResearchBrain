# Swin Transformer V2: Scaling Up Capacity and Resolution

> [!summary] Summary
> Swin Transformer V2, developed by Microsoft Research Asia, successfully scales vision models to 3 billion parameters and high resolutions by addressing training instability, resolution transfer, and data efficiency. This enabled new state-of-the-art results across various computer vision tasks while significantly reducing the dependency on massive labeled datasets.

## Problem

- Training large vision models (billions of parameters) often leads to instability and divergence, with activation values varying by orders of magnitude across layers.
- Existing methods struggle to effectively transfer models pre-trained on low-resolution images to high-resolution downstream tasks due to a significant 'resolution gap'.
- Scaling vision models traditionally requires proprietary, super-huge labeled datasets (e.g., 3 billion images), limiting accessibility and increasing training costs.

## Method

- Introduced ==Residual Post-Normalization== and ==Scaled Cosine Attention== to stabilize training by controlling activation amplitudes and attention map values in deeper, larger models.
- Developed ==Log-Spaced Continuous Position Bias (Log-CPB)== using a meta-network and log-transformed coordinates to enable seamless and effective transfer across varying input and window resolutions.
- Employed ==self-supervised pre-training with SimMIM== and leveraged GPU memory-saving techniques like ZeRO optimizer, activation checkpointing, and sequential self-attention computation for efficient large model training.

## Results

- Successfully trained a **3-billion-parameter** Swin Transformer V2 (SwinV2-G) capable of processing images up to **1,**536×**1,536** resolution, representing the largest dense vision model reported.
- Achieved new state-of-the-art performance across diverse vision tasks: **84.0%** top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-V2, **63.1/54.4** box/mask AP on COCO test-dev, **59.9** mIoU on ADE20K, and **86.8%** top-1 accuracy on Kinetics-400.
- Utilized SimMIM for self-supervised pre-training, enabling SwinV2-G to achieve SOTA results with **40x** less labeled data and training time compared to previous billion-level vision models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision models can be successfully scaled to billions of parameters, akin to large language models, by addressing architectural stability and resolution transfer challenges.
> - Self-supervised pre-training (SimMIM) significantly reduces the reliance on vast labeled datasets for training multi-billion parameter vision models, making them more accessible.
> - Novel techniques like residual post-normalization, scaled cosine attention, and log-spaced continuous position bias are crucial for stable training and effective resolution transfer in large vision Transformers.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research paper "Swin Transformer V2" is a pivotal contribution within the rapidly evolving landscape of deep learning, particularly at the intersection of computer vision and large-scale model development. Its context is best understood by examining the trends in NLP, the emergence of Vision Transformers, and the specific challenges of scaling vision models.

- **The NLP Precedent and Scaling Laws**: The paper explicitly draws a parallel to the "incredibly successful" scaling of language models. Models like BERT, GPT-3, and Switch Transformers have demonstrated that increasing model parameters by orders of magnitude (from millions to trillions) leads to significant performance improvements, often without saturation, and unlocks emergent capabilities like few-shot learning. This success in NLP has established a "scaling paradigm" where larger models, trained on more data, perform better and are more versatile.
- **The Rise of Vision Transformers (ViTs)**: Traditionally, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) dominated computer vision. However, the introduction of the Vision Transformer (ViT) in 2020 marked a paradigm shift, demonstrating that Transformers could effectively process images and achieve competitive performance on tasks like ImageNet classification. Early large ViTs, however, faced challenges: they often required massive proprietary labeled datasets like JFT-3B (3 billion images) for pre-training and were primarily applied to image classification, struggling with dense prediction tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation due to their global attention mechanism and high computational cost at higher resolutions.
- **The Original Swin Transformer (V1)**: The original Swin Transformer (introduced in 2021, also by Microsoft Research Asia) addressed many of ViT's limitations. It introduced a hierarchical architecture and shifted window attention, which limited self-attention computation to local windows while allowing cross-window connections. This made Swin Transformer computationally efficient, linear with image resolution, and capable of serving as a general-purpose backbone for various vision tasks, including detection and segmentation, often outperforming CNNs. Swin V1 was a significant step towards making Transformers practical for broader computer vision applications.
- **The "Scaling Gap" in Vision**: Despite Swin V1's success, a "capacity gap" remained between vision and language models. Vision models had not yet reached the multi-billion or trillion-parameter scales seen in NLP, nor did they exhibit the same level of generalizability or few-shot capabilities across diverse tasks without extensive task-specific fine-tuning. Existing large vision models still largely focused on image classification and were heavily reliant on massive labeled datasets.
- **Swin Transformer V2's Position**: Swin Transformer V2 directly addresses this "scaling gap." It aims to push the boundaries of vision model size and resolution, applying lessons learned from large-scale NLP model training while also tackling the unique challenges of vision: training instability at scale, the need for effective transfer across varying image resolutions (especially between pre-training and fine-tuning), and the prohibitive cost of acquiring vast *labeled* datasets. By proposing architectural improvements and integrating self-supervised learning, Swin V2 attempts to enable vision models to truly scale up in capacity and generalize effectively, potentially paving the way for "foundation models" in computer vision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind Swin Transformer V2 is to replicate the immense success of large-scale models in Natural Language Processing (NLP) within the domain of computer vision. The authors observe that while NLP models have scaled up by over 1,000 times in recent years, exhibiting significant performance gains and few-shot learning capabilities, vision models have lagged. The paper explicitly aims to explore and address the challenges associated with building and deploying such large-scale vision models.

The key objectives and motivations can be broken down into three primary issues the authors seek to tackle:

1. **Addressing Training Instability in Large Vision Models**: As the capacity of vision models increases, especially with deeper architectures, the training process becomes highly unstable. The authors specifically identify that activation amplitudes across layers can vary dramatically (by a factor of 10^4), leading to training divergence. Their motivation is to design architectural modifications that ensure stable training even for models with billions of parameters.
2. **Bridging Resolution Gaps Between Pre-training and Fine-tuning**: Many downstream vision tasks, such as object detection and semantic segmentation, require high-resolution input images. However, pre-training models on such high resolutions is computationally expensive. The common practice of pre-training on low-resolution images and then fine-tuning on high-resolution images leads to a "resolution gap." Existing methods, like bi-cubic interpolation of position biases, are often sub-optimal. The objective is to develop a method that allows seamless and effective transfer of models trained with low-resolution inputs to tasks requiring significantly higher resolutions and larger attention windows.
3. **Reducing Hunger on Labeled Data**: Training multi-billion parameter models typically demands extremely large datasets. Previous state-of-the-art large vision models (e.g., ViT-G, CoAtNet-7) have relied on proprietary datasets like JFT-3B (3 billion labeled images), which are unavailable to most researchers and costly to collect. The motivation is to reduce this dependency on "super-huge labelled data" by leveraging more efficient data strategies, such as self-supervised learning, making large-scale vision model training more accessible and resource-efficient.

Beyond these specific technical challenges, the overarching motivation is to **"close the capacity gap between vision and language models and facilitate the joint modeling of the two domains."** By successfully demonstrating the benefits of scaling up vision models in capacity and resolution across a broad range of general vision tasks, the authors aim to stimulate further research in this direction, ultimately contributing to the development of more general, powerful, and versatile AI systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Swin Transformer V2 builds upon the successful hierarchical architecture of Swin Transformer V1, introducing several key modifications to enable scaling up capacity and resolution while addressing training stability, resolution transfer, and data efficiency.

#### 3.1. Architectural Adaptations for Scaling Capacity (SwinV2 Core)

To tackle the training instability observed when scaling up Swin Transformer V1, the authors propose two main architectural changes:

- **Residual Post-Normalization (Res-post-norm)**: In Swin Transformer V1 (and most vision Transformers like vanilla ViT), Layer Normalization (LN) is applied at the beginning of each Transformer block (pre-normalization). This can lead to activation values accumulating and exploding in deeper layers of very large models. SwinV2 adopts a "res-post-norm" configuration, moving the LN layer to the end of each residual unit, *after* the residual connection. This ensures that the amplitude of the main branch does not accumulate, leading to much milder activation values across network layers and significantly improving training stability for larger models. For the largest models (SwinV2-H and SwinV2-G), an additional LN layer is strategically inserted on the main branch every 6 Transformer blocks for further stabilization.
- **Scaled Cosine Attention**: The original self-attention mechanism uses a dot product between query and key vectors. For large models, this can lead to attention maps being dominated by a few pixel pairs, especially with res-post-norm. SwinV2 replaces this with a "scaled cosine attention" mechanism: `Sim(q_i, k_j) = cos(q_i, k_j) / τ + B_ij`. Here, `cos` denotes cosine similarity, `B_ij` is the relative position bias, and `τ` is a learnable scalar (non-shared across heads and layers). Cosine similarity naturally normalizes the attention values, making them less sensitive to amplitude variations of input features and less prone to extreme values, thereby contributing to training stability and improved accuracy, particularly for larger models.

#### 3.2. Scaling Up Window Resolution

To effectively transfer models pre-trained on low-resolution images to high-resolution downstream tasks, SwinV2 introduces:

- **Log-Spaced Continuous Position Bias (Log-CPB)**: The original Swin Transformer used a parameterized relative position bias (RPB) that was interpolated (e.g., bi-cubic) when transferring to different window sizes. This interpolation often proved sub-optimal, especially for large resolution changes. Log-CPB replaces this with a small "meta network" (`G`, typically a 2-layer MLP with ReLU) that generates bias values based on relative coordinates: `B(∆x,∆y) = G(∆x,∆y)`.
 - **Continuous Nature**: Since `G` can take any coordinate as input, the model can inherently handle arbitrary window sizes during fine-tuning without rigid interpolation.
 - **Log-Spaced Coordinates**: Crucially, the input coordinates (`∆x, ∆y`) to the meta network are transformed into log-space: `sign(x) * log(1 + |∆x|)`. This transformation significantly reduces the "extrapolation ratio" when transferring from a smaller pre-training window size to a much larger fine-tuning window size. For instance, transferring from 8x8 to 16x16 window size, linear spacing would require extrapolating by 1.14x, while log-spacing reduces it to 0.33x, making the transfer much smoother and more effective.

#### 3.3. Data and Memory Efficiency

To address the "data hunger" and high GPU memory consumption of large models:

- **Self-Supervised Pre-training (SimMIM)**: Instead of relying on massive labeled datasets like JFT-3B (3 billion images), SwinV2 leverages SimMIM, a masked image modeling self-supervised pre-training method. This allows the 3-billion-parameter SwinV2-G model to achieve state-of-the-art results using only 70 million *labeled* images (ImageNet-22K-ext), representing a 40x reduction in labeled data compared to previous large vision models. This significantly reduces training costs and makes large model training more accessible.
- **GPU Memory Saving Implementations**:
 - **Zero-Redundancy Optimizer (ZeRO)**: The DeepSpeed framework's ZeRO stage-1 option is employed. This technique partitions and distributes model parameters and their corresponding optimizer states across multiple GPUs, drastically reducing the memory footprint per GPU (e.g., from 48GB to a fraction for a 3B model with AdamW).
 - **Activation Checkpointing**: This technique recomputes intermediate activation maps during the backward pass (gradient calculation) rather than storing them in memory during the forward pass. This saves significant memory, especially for high-resolution inputs, at the cost of a moderate increase (up to 30%) in training time.
 - **Sequential Self-Attention Computation**: For the most memory-intensive scenarios (e.g., 1536x1536 images with 32x32 windows on 40GB A100 GPUs), the self-attention computation is implemented sequentially rather than in a single batch. This fine-grained optimization within the attention module further reduces peak memory consumption with minimal impact on overall training speed.

#### 3.4. Model Configurations and Training Protocol

The authors define several model sizes, ranging from SwinV2-T (Tiny) to SwinV2-G (Giant, 3 billion parameters), with increasing channel counts and block depths. For SwinV2-G, a 2-step pre-training approach is used: first, self-supervised pre-training with SimMIM on ImageNet-22K-ext, followed by supervised pre-training on the same dataset with class labels. This multi-stage approach, combined with the proposed architectural and memory optimizations, enables the successful training and fine-tuning of the largest Swin Transformer model on various vision benchmarks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper demonstrates the successful scaling of Swin Transformer to unprecedented capacities and resolutions, establishing new state-of-the-art results across a diverse set of computer vision tasks.

- **Successful Training of a 3 Billion-Parameter Model**: The paper successfully trained Swin Transformer V2 (SwinV2-G) with 3 billion parameters, making it the largest dense vision model reported at the time. This model is capable of training with images up to 1,536×1,536 resolution, a significant leap for general-purpose vision models.

- **Improved Training Stability**:
 - The proposed **res-post-norm** and **scaled cosine attention** mechanisms effectively stabilize the training process. Experiments show that these changes mitigate the dramatic increase in activation values at deeper layers observed in the original Swin Transformer (Figure 2), which previously led to training instability and divergence for larger models (Figure 3).
 - Ablation studies (Table 6) confirm that both techniques individually contribute to accuracy improvements across different model sizes (Swin-T, S, B), with larger models benefiting more significantly. The combination also shows gains for ViT architecture.

- **Effective Resolution Transfer with Log-CPB**:
 - The **Log-spaced Continuous Position Bias (Log-CPB)** method substantially improves the model's ability to transfer across different window resolutions, especially from low-resolution pre-training to high-resolution fine-tuning.
 - Table 1 demonstrates that Log-CPB consistently outperforms the conventional parameterized position bias (used in SwinV1) and even a linear-spaced continuous position bias, particularly when the resolution difference is large (e.g., from 8x8 pre-training window to 24x24 fine-tuning window, showing a +10.4% gain without fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K). This highlights its ability to generalize position biases effectively.
 - The flexibility of Log-CPB also allows for "test-time window adjustment," where using a larger window size during inference can provide additional performance gains on detection and segmentation tasks (Tables 3, 4, 5).

- **Reduced Data Dependency and Training Efficiency**:
 - By leveraging **SimMIM** for self-supervised pre-training, SwinV2-G achieves state-of-the-art results using a significantly smaller labeled dataset (ImageNet-22K-ext with 70 million images) compared to previous billion-level vision models that relied on JFT-3B (3 billion images). This represents a **40 times reduction in labeled data** and **40 times less training time** than Google's comparable models. This is a critical finding for the practical accessibility of large vision models.
 - The integration of memory-saving techniques (ZeRO optimizer, activation checkpointing, and sequential self-attention computation) further ensures that these massive models can be trained efficiently even on common high-end GPUs like Nvidia A100-40G, enabling high-resolution inputs up to 1536x1536.

- **New State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance Across Multiple Tasks**: SwinV2-G sets new performance records on four representative vision tasks, demonstrating its generalizability and superiority:
 - **ImageNet-V2 Image Classification**: Achieved 84.0% top-1 accuracy, surpassing the previous best by +0.7%.
 - **COCO Object Detection and Instance Segmentation**: Achieved 63.1 / 54.4 box / mask AP on COCO test-dev, an improvement of +1.8/+1.4 over the previous best.
 - **ADE20K Semantic Segmentation**: Achieved 59.9 mIoU, outperforming the previous best by +1.5.
 - **Kinetics-400 Video Action Classification**: Achieved 86.8% top-1 accuracy, a +1.4% improvement over the previous best.
 These results collectively demonstrate that scaling up vision models, when done effectively, yields significant performance gains across a wide range of vision challenges, akin to the benefits observed in NLP.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Swin Transformer V2: Scaling Up Capacity and Resolution" paper represents a landmark contribution to the field of computer vision and deep learning, with significant implications and potential impact:

- **Pioneering Large-Scale Vision Models**: The most direct significance is that Swin Transformer V2 successfully demonstrates the feasibility and benefits of scaling vision models to billions of parameters, a scale previously mostly associated with NLP. By achieving state-of-the-art results across diverse tasks with a 3-billion-parameter model, it effectively "closes the capacity gap" between vision and language models. This paves the way for a new generation of "foundation models" in computer vision, similar to how large language models serve as foundational general-purpose NLP models.

- **Enhanced Generalizability and Versatility**: Unlike previous large vision models that were often limited to image classification or required massive task-specific adjustments, SwinV2-G demonstrates strong performance across image classification, object detection, semantic segmentation, and video action recognition. This broad generalizability highlights its potential as a highly versatile backbone for a wide array of visual intelligence applications, reducing the need for entirely new architectures or retraining for each new task.

- **Practicality of High-Resolution Processing**: The Log-CPB method is a crucial practical innovation. Many real-world vision applications (e.g., medical imaging, high-resolution surveillance, autonomous driving) demand processing very high-resolution images. SwinV2's ability to seamlessly transfer knowledge from low-resolution pre-training to high-resolution fine-tuning without significant performance drops, and even allow for flexible test-time resolution adjustments, makes large pre-trained models much more adaptable and efficient for such demanding scenarios.

- **Reduced Resource Barrier for Large Models**: The reliance on self-supervised pre-training (SimMIM) to achieve SOTA results with 40 times less labeled data and training time than previous billion-parameter models is a game-changer. This drastically lowers the computational and data acquisition barriers for training and leveraging extremely large vision models. It democratizes access to state-of-the-art capabilities, making it more feasible for institutions without Google-scale resources to develop and deploy highly capable vision AI.

- **Stimulating Future Research in Multimodal AI**: The paper explicitly states its goal to "facilitate the joint modeling of the two domains" (vision and language). By demonstrating that vision models can achieve comparable scale and generalizability to language models, SwinV2 sets a strong precedent for future research into truly unified, multimodal AI systems that can understand and process information across different modalities simultaneously. This could lead to breakthroughs in areas like visual question answering, image captioning, and human-computer interaction.

- **Architectural Robustness and Stability**: The proposed `res-post-norm` and `scaled cosine attention` are critical architectural improvements that enhance training stability for deep, large-scale Transformers. These techniques can be adopted in other Transformer-based architectures, contributing to more robust and scalable designs beyond Swin Transformer itself.

In essence, Swin Transformer V2 marks a significant step towards creating more powerful, general-purpose, and accessible AI for computer vision, promising to accelerate progress in foundational AI research and its real-world applications.
