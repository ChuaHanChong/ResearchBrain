# InSpire: Vision-Language-Action Models with Intrinsic Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The InSpire framework from Southwest Jiaotong University, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, and Tongji University enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by integrating an intrinsic spatial reasoning Visual Question Answering (VQA) task. This approach mitigates spurious correlations, leading to a 10.0% absolute success rate improvement on unseen simulation tasks and a 26% enhancement on unseen real-world tasks, outperforming larger VLA models with fewer parameters.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics often suffer from limited generalization due to learning 'spurious correlations' between task-irrelevant visual features and actions.
- Current methods to improve VLA reasoning typically require additional training data, external large models, or explicit 3D information, limiting efficiency and scalability.
- Robots struggle to robustly perform in novel, unseen environments or with varied object configurations due to a lack of intrinsic spatial understanding.

## Method

- **InSpire** introduces a two-step policy execution within a single VLA, first generating a textual spatial reasoning answer via a ==Visual Question Answering (VQA)== task, then using this answer to guide action prediction.
- A structured VQA task asks, 'In which direction is the [object] relative to the robot?', with object names dynamically identified from the instruction and answers constrained to coarse directional labels.
- Ground-truth spatial relationships are automatically generated and integrated into expert demonstrations using rule-based position acquisition and direction inference, without requiring extra human annotation.

## Results

- InSpire boosted miniVLA-VQ's absolute success rate by **10.0%** on unseen LIBERO simulation tasks, with particularly strong gains of **+13.0%** on `LIBERO-Spatial` and **+20.1%** on `LIBERO-Object`.
- Real-world experiments showed InSpire improving the π0-FAST baseline's success rate by an average of **26%** on unseen tasks, demonstrating robustness to novel object colors, specific objects, and complex backgrounds.
- InspireVLA-1B, a 1B-parameter model, consistently outperformed five larger reasoning-based VLAs (e.g., CoT-VLA-7B) across LIBERO generalization datasets while using significantly fewer parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly forcing VLAs to reason about task-relevant spatial relationships through an intrinsic VQA task effectively mitigates spurious correlations and improves generalization.
> - Direction prediction-based VQA formulations (e.g., '1D Direction') are more effective in guiding action generation than quantitative measures like proximity or exact 3D coordinates.
> - Enhancing VLA reasoning does not necessarily require additional training data or external large models; a plug-and-play approach can leverage the VLM backbone's inherent world knowledge efficiently.
