# InSpire: Vision-Language-Action Models with Intrinsic Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The InSpire framework from Southwest Jiaotong University, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, and Tongji University enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by integrating an intrinsic spatial reasoning Visual Question Answering (VQA) task. This approach mitigates spurious correlations, leading to a 10.0% absolute success rate improvement on unseen simulation tasks and a 26% enhancement on unseen real-world tasks, outperforming larger VLA models with fewer parameters.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics often suffer from limited generalization due to learning 'spurious correlations' between task-irrelevant visual features and actions.
- Current methods to improve VLA reasoning typically require additional training data, external large models, or explicit 3D information, limiting efficiency and scalability.
- Robots struggle to robustly perform in novel, unseen environments or with varied object configurations due to a lack of intrinsic spatial understanding.

## Method

- **InSpire** introduces a two-step policy execution within a single VLA, first generating a textual spatial reasoning answer via a ==Visual Question Answering (VQA)== task, then using this answer to guide action prediction.
- A structured VQA task asks, 'In which direction is the [object] relative to the robot?', with object names dynamically identified from the instruction and answers constrained to coarse directional labels.
- Ground-truth spatial relationships are automatically generated and integrated into expert demonstrations using rule-based position acquisition and direction inference, without requiring extra human annotation.

## Results

- InSpire boosted miniVLA-VQ's absolute success rate by **10.0%** on unseen LIBERO simulation tasks, with particularly strong gains of **+13.0%** on `LIBERO-Spatial` and **+20.1%** on `LIBERO-Object`.
- Real-world experiments showed InSpire improving the π0-FAST baseline's success rate by an average of **26%** on unseen tasks, demonstrating robustness to novel object colors, specific objects, and complex backgrounds.
- InspireVLA-1B, a 1B-parameter model, consistently outperformed five larger reasoning-based VLAs (e.g., CoT-VLA-7B) across LIBERO generalization datasets while using significantly fewer parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly forcing VLAs to reason about task-relevant spatial relationships through an intrinsic VQA task effectively mitigates spurious correlations and improves generalization.
> - Direction prediction-based VQA formulations (e.g., '1D Direction') are more effective in guiding action generation than quantitative measures like proximity or exact 3D coordinates.
> - Enhancing VLA reasoning does not necessarily require additional training data or external large models; a plug-and-play approach can leverage the VLM backbone's inherent world knowledge efficiently.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "InSpire" is situated at the forefront of research into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for general-purpose robotic systems. This field aims to leverage the formidable capabilities of pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs), originally developed for tasks like image captioning and visual question answering, to enable robots to understand natural language instructions and visual observations, and translate them directly into low-level motor actions. Projects like RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA, and Octo represent significant milestones in this domain, demonstrating the potential for scaling robotic learning with large datasets and foundation models.

Despite these advancements, a critical challenge for existing VLAs is their limited generalization capacity beyond the specific training data distribution. This limitation primarily stems from their tendency to learn "spurious correlations" – associating task-irrelevant visual features or contextual cues with actions, rather than forming a deeper understanding of the causal relationships between observations, language instructions, and desired actions. For instance, a robot might learn to move a specific object because of a background color seen during training, rather than understanding the object itself and its spatial relation to the robot as specified by an instruction.

Previous research efforts have attempted to address this generalization issue, particularly by integrating various forms of "reasoning" into VLA models. Approaches like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, borrowed from large language models, aim to introduce step-by-step intermediate thoughts to guide action generation. Models such as CoT-VLA and RT-H have explored this direction, often by relying on auxiliary training data or interactions with external, larger models. Similarly, efforts like SpatialVLA have focused on explicitly injecting 3D spatial information into the input to improve spatial awareness. While these methods offer partial improvements, they often come with trade-offs in efficiency, require additional data, or introduce dependencies on complex external systems, limiting their practicality and scalability.

InSpire positions itself as a novel and more efficient solution to this fundamental problem. It directly tackles spurious correlations by intrinsically boosting the spatial reasoning ability of VLAs. Crucially, it achieves this without requiring extra training data (beyond the standard expert demonstrations for the task) or interacting with other large models. By reformulating spatial reasoning as an intrinsic Visual Question Answering (VQA) task that acts as a "bridge" between observations and actions, InSpire offers a plug-and-play enhancement for existing autoregressive VLAs. This approach represents a significant step towards creating more robust, generalizable, and less data-hungry robotic systems by moving beyond mere pattern matching to a more grounded understanding of spatial relationships and task-relevant factors. It contributes to the broader goal of making robotic AI more reliable and adaptable to novel, unseen environments and tasks, thereby accelerating the deployment of general-purpose robots in diverse real-world scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "InSpire" research is to significantly enhance the generalization capacity of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic control. This overarching goal is motivated by a critical limitation observed in current state-of-the-art VLAs: their tendency to develop "spurious correlations."

The key motivations and objectives can be detailed as follows:

- **Mitigate Spurious Correlations:** The central problem identified is that existing VLAs often learn to associate task-irrelevant visual features (e.g., background elements, specific object colors in training data) with particular actions. This "shortcut learning" prevents them from truly understanding the underlying causal relationships between relevant observations (like target objects and their positions), language instructions, and the required actions. The main objective is to overcome this issue to enable VLAs to generalize effectively to novel scenarios that deviate from the training distribution.
- **Boost Intrinsic Spatial Reasoning:** The authors hypothesize that VLAs resort to spurious correlations because task-relevant factors, particularly spatial relationships, are not explicitly represented or easily extractable from raw observations and language instructions. Inspired by human cognitive processes, where spatial awareness is fundamental for task execution, a core objective is to intrinsically equip VLAs with robust spatial reasoning capabilities. This means enabling the model to explicitly understand and reason about the relative directions and positions of objects with respect to the robot.
- **Achieve Generalization Without External Dependencies:** A significant motivation is to develop a solution that enhances reasoning capabilities *without* the common drawbacks of prior approaches. This translates into two specific objectives:
 - **No Extra Training Data:** Avoid the need to collect or annotate additional, specialized training data specifically for reasoning, which can be time-consuming and resource-intensive.
 - **No Interaction with Other Large Models:** Eliminate dependencies on external, potentially larger or slower, models (like separate Large Language Models or specialized visual encoders) for generating intermediate reasoning steps, thereby maintaining efficiency and self-sufficiency.
- **Develop a Plug-and-Play Enhancement:** The practical objective is to create a simple yet effective "plugin" approach that can be seamlessly integrated with and enhance existing autoregressive VLA architectures. This aims to maximize compatibility and ease of adoption for the robotics community, allowing current advanced VLAs to benefit from improved spatial reasoning without extensive architectural overhauls.
- **Improve Robustness and Accuracy in Diverse Environments:** Ultimately, by achieving the above, the research aims to lead to more accurate and robust robot actions across a wide array of tasks and environments, including those with novel objects, varied scenes, and previously unseen configurations, both in simulation and the real world. This will bring general-purpose robotic systems closer to practical deployment.

In essence, the research seeks to empower VLAs with a more "intelligent" and human-like understanding of their environment, moving beyond rote memorization of correlations to a causal and spatially-aware decision-making process, all while maintaining efficiency and ease of integration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "InSpire" (Intrinsic Spatial Reasoning) approach tackles the limitations of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by integrating an explicit spatial reasoning Visual Question Answering (VQA) task directly into the VLA's action generation process. The core idea is to guide the model's attention towards task-relevant spatial factors, thereby mitigating the learning of spurious correlations.

**A. Core Mechanism: Two-Step Policy Execution within a Single VLA**

InSpire reformulates the VLA's operation into a conceptual two-step process, although executed by the *same* underlying autoregressive VLA model:

1. **Extraction Policy (π_u'):** Given a visual observation `o` and a language instruction `l`, InSpire first constructs a textual question `q` designed to probe the spatial relationship of a target object relative to the robot. The VLA then takes `q`, `o`, and `l` as input to generate a textual answer `g`. This `[q, g]` pair forms an "extracted task-relevant representation" denoted as `u'`.
2. **Action Policy (π_θ):** Subsequently, this generated `u'` (the spatial reasoning question-answer pair) is prepended to the original language instruction `l` and current observation `o`. The VLA then processes this augmented input `[u', o, l]` to predict the final low-level robot actions `a`.

This innovative structure forces the VLA to first explicitly reason about crucial spatial information before attempting to predict actions, making task-relevant factors salient and guiding the model away from relying on spurious correlations.

**B. Spatial Reasoning VQA Task Formulation**

The design of the spatial reasoning VQA task is critical:

- **Question Structure:** The specific question employed is "In which direction is the [object] relative to the robot?". This direct phrasing ensures the VLA focuses on relative spatial relationships.
- **Object Identification:** To populate the `[object]` placeholder in the question, InSpire utilizes the Natural Language Toolkit (NLTK) to identify object names mentioned within the original language instruction `l`. This allows for dynamic question generation based on the current task.
- **Answer Space:** The VLA's output for `g` (the answer) is constrained to a predefined set of coarse-grained directional labels: "right," "left," "up," "down," "front," "back," and "grasped." This simplified set makes the reasoning task more manageable and directly relevant to typical robot manipulation actions.

**C. Automated Rule-based Object Direction Labeling for Training**

To enable supervised training for this spatial reasoning VQA task, ground-truth spatial relationships are automatically generated and integrated into the demonstration dataset:

- **Data Augmentation:** Each expert demonstration `(o_i, l_i, a_i)` is augmented with `u'_i`, which includes the self-generated question `q` and its corresponding ground-truth spatial relationship answer `g`.
- **Position Acquisition:**
 - **Simulation:** In simulated environments, the 3D positions of the robot's end-effector/gripper and target objects are directly available.
 - **Real World:** For real-world tasks, the end-effector's position at the moment of gripper closure or opening is recorded, serving as a proxy for the object's position during interaction.
- **Direction Inference:**
 1. A position difference vector `d = [x_i - x_0, y_i - y_0, z_i - z_0]` is calculated between the end-effector/gripper position `[x_i, y_i, z_i]` and the object position `[x_0, y_0, z_0]`.
 2. The axis corresponding to the component of `d` with the largest absolute value determines the primary direction (e.g., if `|x_i - x_0|` is largest, it's either "left" or "right"). The sign of the component then specifies the exact direction (e.g., positive `x_i - x_0` might be "right").
 3. If the gripper is closed and holding an object, the label "grasped" is assigned directly.
- **Viewpoint:** This rule-based strategy operates from a third-person viewpoint, ensuring consistent labeling.

**D. Training and Integration**

- **Autoregressive Loss:** During training, the standard autoregressive loss (typically used for action tokens) is extended to also apply to the tokens corresponding to the spatial reasoning answers. This additional loss term encourages the VLA to accurately predict these textual directional descriptions.
- **Plug-and-Play Compatibility:** InSpire is designed to be fully compatible with existing autoregressive VLA architectures, enabling its seamless integration as a plugin without requiring modifications to the VLA's core structure or its pre-training regimen. It leverages the inherent text-based world knowledge of the VLM backbone of the VLA.

**E. Evaluation**

Extensive evaluations were conducted across:

- **Simulation Benchmarks:** LIBERO (90 seen tasks, 4 unseen generalization datasets: Spatial, Object, Goal, Long) and CALVIN (challenging long-horizon, language-conditioned tasks).
- **Real-world Tasks:** 10 seen and 5 unseen manipulation tasks using an AGILEX PiPER 6DOF robot arm, designed to test spatial reasoning, novel objects, and unknown instructions.
- **Baselines:** Two state-of-the-art autoregressive VLAs: miniVLA-VQ (a lightweight version of OpenVLA) and π0-FAST (an upgraded version of π0).
- **Ablation Studies:** To assess the impact of different VQA formulations (e.g., 1D vs. 3D direction, proximity, 3D location, distance) and insertion positions of VQA tokens.

By systematically introducing this intrinsic spatial reasoning step, InSpire aims to create a more robust and generalizable VLA that bases its actions on a deeper understanding of task-relevant spatial cues rather than superficial correlations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across diverse simulation and real-world environments demonstrate that InSpire significantly enhances the performance and generalization capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

**A. Simulation Experiments (LIBERO and CALVIN)**

- **LIBERO Performance:**
 - InSpire achieved remarkable improvements on miniVLA-VQ, boosting its absolute success rate by **6.2% on seen tasks** and an even more significant **10.0% on unseen tasks**. This highlights its strong generalization capacity.
 - The gains were particularly pronounced on `LIBERO-Spatial` (+13.0% for miniVLA-VQ) and `LIBERO-Object` (+20.1% for miniVLA-VQ) tasks, confirming InSpire's effectiveness in scenarios requiring fine-grained spatial understanding and object interaction.
 - While InSpire also improved π0-FAST, the gains were more modest (+1.0% seen, +2.8% unseen average). The authors attribute this to π0-FAST's extensive pre-training on a much larger dataset than LIBERO-90, suggesting that the initial strong performance of such models might limit the *relative* impact of InSpire when fine-tuned on smaller datasets.
 - InSpire-enhanced VLAs consistently outperformed other VLAs and reasoning-based methods (e.g., SpatialVLA) in generalization to unseen tasks, which often showed very poor performance without InSpire.
- **CALVIN Performance:**
 - InSpire consistently outperformed baseline miniVLA and miniVLA-VQ across sequences of 1 to 5 sequential tasks. This demonstrates InSpire's superior performance in complex, long-horizon tasks requiring sustained decision-making and robustness. Its ability to complete more subtasks in a row underscores its efficacy in mitigating spurious correlations over extended task executions.

**B. Real-world Experiments**

- **Enhanced Success Rates:** InSpire consistently improved the success rates of the π0-FAST baseline in real-world settings, achieving an average enhancement of **25% on seen tasks** and **26% on unseen tasks**. This real-world validation is crucial, as real environments pose significantly greater challenges due to visual complexity and variability.
- **Robustness to Novelty:** The approach showed exceptional robustness against variations in object color, specific objects, and complex backgrounds in unseen tasks. Notably, InSpire increased the relative success rate of the baseline by 100% on 4 out of 5 unseen tasks, demonstrating its strong generalization in challenging, novel real-world scenarios.
- **Computational Overhead:** InSpire introduced a minor additional time cost of 0.18 seconds per step on average. The authors argue this trade-off is acceptable given the substantial improvements in success rates, particularly for a plugin-based approach.

**C. Comparison with State-of-the-Art (InspireVLA-1B)**

- A 1B-parameter model, InspireVLA-1B (miniVLA-VQ with InSpire), was developed and compared against leading reasoning-based VLAs like SpatialVLA-4B (4B parameters) and CoT-VLA-7B (7B parameters).
- InspireVLA-1B consistently **outperformed all five compared VLAs** (Diffusion Policy, Octo, OpenVLA, SpatialVLA-4B, CoT-VLA-7B) across the four LIBERO generalization datasets.
- Critically, InspireVLA-1B achieved these superior results with significantly fewer parameters (4x fewer than SpatialVLA-4B and 7x fewer than CoT-VLA-7B), highlighting its **parameter efficiency**.
- It demonstrated particular advantages on `LIBERO-Spatial` and `LIBERO-Object` tasks, reinforcing the effectiveness of its explicit spatial reasoning mechanism.

**D. Ablations on Design Decisions**

- **VQA Formulations:**
 - All tested VQA formulations (1D Direction, 3D Direction, Proximity, 3D Location, Distance) improved baseline performance.
 - The proposed "1D Direction" (e.g., "right/left") achieved the best overall performance, closely followed by "3D Direction" (e.g., "[right, up, front]"). This suggests that *direction prediction-based VQA tasks* are most effective in guiding action generation compared to quantitative measures like proximity or accurate 3D coordinates.
- **VQA Insertion Positions:**
 - Placing VQA tokens *before* the language instruction tokens ("VQA-First") generally yielded better performance on seen tasks. While slightly less effective on unseen tasks compared to placing them *after* the instruction ("Instruct-First"), both configurations still substantially boosted the baseline, confirming the overall benefit of the auxiliary VQA task.

**E. How InSpire Resolves Spurious Correlations (Qualitative Analysis)**

- **Addressing Shortcut Learning:** Qualitative examples showed that InSpire corrected instances where the baseline VLA would directly approach a frequently seen "drawer" (shortcut) without properly interpreting the language instruction. InSpire forced the model to correctly identify the target object and its spatial relation.
- **Improving Robustness to Distractors:** In complex scenes with numerous distractors, InSpire enabled the VLA to differentiate task-relevant objects from irrelevant elements, improving object identification and action accuracy. This was visually supported by attention maps showing InSpire focusing on the correct objects.
- **Enabling Continual Action Correction:** The approach allowed the model to continuously correct incorrect actions along a trajectory, demonstrating enhanced awareness of task execution status (e.g., relative direction of the target object, gripper status), a critical capability for complex, multi-step tasks.

In summary, InSpire consistently provides substantial and generalizable performance gains in robotic manipulation, both in simulation and the real world, by effectively addressing the problem of spurious correlations through intrinsic spatial reasoning, all while being efficient and easy to integrate.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The InSpire framework represents a significant step forward in the development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotics, offering several compelling aspects of significance and potential impact:

**A. Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:**
The most profound impact of InSpire is its direct and effective mitigation of spurious correlations, which is a fundamental bottleneck in the generalization of current VLA models. By intrinsically boosting spatial reasoning, InSpire enables robots to perform reliably in novel, unseen environments, with new object configurations, varying backgrounds, and even unfamiliar instructions. This enhanced generalization is crucial for moving robotic systems from controlled laboratory settings to real-world deployment, where variability is the norm. The qualitative results showing InSpire correcting shortcut learning, improving robustness to distractors, and enabling continual action correction further underscore its potential to create more intelligent and fault-tolerant robotic agents.

**B. Efficiency and Practicality:**
InSpire's design philosophy prioritizes efficiency and practical implementation. Unlike many reasoning-based approaches that require auxiliary training data or rely on computationally intensive interactions with external large models, InSpire achieves its gains without these dependencies. This "no extra data, no external models" approach makes it highly attractive for real-world applications where data collection and computational resources are often constrained. The fact that it outperforms larger, more parameter-heavy models (like CoT-VLA-7B and SpatialVLA-4B) with significantly fewer parameters (e.g., InspireVLA-1B) highlights its exceptional parameter efficiency and makes it more accessible for deployment on resource-limited robotic platforms.

**C. Plug-and-Play Integration:**
The plug-and-play nature of InSpire, allowing it to be seamlessly integrated into existing autoregressive VLA architectures, lowers the barrier to adoption for the robotics research and development community. This means that current state-of-the-art VLAs can readily benefit from improved spatial reasoning without requiring extensive architectural redesigns or retraining from scratch. This flexibility will likely accelerate the integration of advanced reasoning capabilities into a wider range of robotic systems.

**D. Advancing Human-like Reasoning in Robotics:**
By explicitly incorporating spatial reasoning—a cognitive ability fundamental to human understanding and task execution—InSpire moves VLAs closer to human-like intelligence. The ability to implicitly ask and answer "where is the object relative to me?" before acting imbues the robot with a deeper contextual awareness, transforming its decision-making from mere pattern matching to a more grounded, causal understanding of its environment. This can pave the way for more intuitive human-robot interaction and more sophisticated task execution.

**E. Broader Impact on Robotic AI Research:**
The success of InSpire validates the hypothesis that making task-relevant factors explicit can effectively combat spurious correlations. This insight could inspire further research into identifying and making other latent, task-relevant factors explicit within VLA models (e.g., affordances, intent, temporal reasoning), potentially leading to a new wave of intrinsically more robust and generalizable robotic learning paradigms. The ablation studies on VQA formulations also provide valuable guidance for future work on designing effective intermediate reasoning tasks.

**F. Future Directions:**
The identified limitations, such as exploring integration with diffusion policy-based VLAs and conducting deeper empirical studies into varied spurious correlations across different VLA pre-training paradigms, offer clear and impactful avenues for future research. Successfully integrating InSpire with diffusion models, which offer distinct advantages in action generation, could unlock even broader applicability.

In conclusion, InSpire represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation by offering an elegant, efficient, and highly effective solution to a core challenge. Its ability to endow VLAs with intrinsic spatial reasoning, without adding complexity or data requirements, pushes the boundaries of what is possible for general-purpose robotic systems, bringing us closer to a future where robots can operate intelligently and reliably in unpredictable real-world environments.
