# BiCoord: A Bimanual Manipulation Benchmark towards Long-Horizon Spatial-Temporal Coordination

> [!summary] Summary
> The BiCoord benchmark introduces a collection of bimanual manipulation tasks that necessitate extensive spatial-temporal coordination over long horizons. It reveals that current policies exhibit difficulties in precise alignment, robust reasoning, and maintaining sustained performance across multiple stages in these complex scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing bimanual manipulation benchmarks feature short-horizon tasks lacking complex, extended dependencies.
- Current benchmarks show weak or temporally disjoint bimanual coordination, rather than tightly coupled spatial-temporal interaction.
- A lack of granular metrics to quantitatively assess the intricate spatial and temporal aspects of bimanual robot arm cooperation.

## Method

- Established **BiCoord**, a benchmark with tasks specifically designed for ==long-horizon== and tightly coordinated ==bimanual manipulation==.
- Developed a suite of novel metrics (==MRD==, ==ARD==, ==SMT==, ==SMP==, ==STI==) to quantify spatial, temporal, and combined spatial-temporal coordination.
- Utilized an ==AI coding agent== with human-in-the-loop verification and automatic stage-wise trajectory annotations to construct diverse and complex tasks.

## Results

- VLA models generally achieved higher success rates than Diffusion Policy in single-task learning due to pre-trained knowledge.
- All evaluated policies struggled with tasks requiring precise alignment and exhibited limited reasoning capabilities under varied initial conditions.
- BiCoord tasks show a **4×** increase in spatial-temporal integral values vs prior benchmarks; policy performance consistently degraded in later stages of long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - BiCoord tasks significantly surpass existing benchmarks in complexity, demonstrating a fourfold increase in spatial-temporal integral values and substantially longer, multi-stage trajectories.
> - Current state-of-the-art policies struggle with the demanding coordination requirements of BiCoord tasks, particularly in precise alignment and flexible reasoning.
> - VLA models show an advantage from pre-training for overall success but exhibit rigidity, while Diffusion Policy is more efficient and stable for specific motions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bimanual manipulation, defined as the coordinated use of two robotic arms to complete tasks, is considered a critical capability for achieving human-level dexterity in robotics. The broader research landscape in robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements, particularly with the development of various simulators and benchmarks.

**Evolution of Robotic Manipulation Benchmarks:**
Initially, much of the research focused on unimanual manipulation. Benchmarks such as LIBERO, RLBench, and RoboCerebra provided diverse tasks and environments for single-arm robotic learning. With increasing recognition of the advantages of dual-arm systems, new benchmarks specifically for bimanual manipulation began to emerge, including RoboTwin and RLBench2. These platforms have facilitated data-driven learning approaches by providing bimanual tasks and expert demonstration data. Concurrently, various learning methods have been developed for robotic manipulation, ranging from specialized policies for single tasks (e.g., Diffusion Policy, DP) to more generalized Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models (e.g., RDT, Pi0, OpenVLA-OFT) that leverage large-scale pre-training for multi-task capabilities. Specific approaches have also addressed bimanual manipulation, focusing on aspects like action templates, communication, conflict reduction, and leader-follower architectures.

**Identified Gaps and BiCoord's Contribution:**
Despite these developments, the authors identify two primary limitations in existing bimanual manipulation benchmarks that hinder the capture of real-world coordination complexity:
1. **Short-Horizon Tasks:** Current benchmarks largely focus on tasks completable within a few motion primitives, lacking the long-horizon dependencies and hierarchical structures inherent in real-world scenarios. Human manipulation often requires continuous coordination across multiple sub-goals, object states, and contact transitions over extended periods.
2. **Loosely Coordinated Bimanual Tasks:** While many benchmarks include two-arm scenarios, the coordination between arms is often weak, with each arm operating largely independently or performing temporally disjoint actions. This contrasts with human-like manipulation, which typically involves tight spatial-temporal coupling, such as simultaneous stabilization and manipulation of an object, or dynamic exchange of forces and roles between arms.

BiCoord is introduced to address these identified gaps. It aims to provide a benchmark specifically designed for **long-horizon and tightly coordinated bimanual manipulation**, thereby pushing the boundaries of research in robotic dexterity towards more complex, human-like cooperative behaviors. By doing so, BiCoord seeks to uncover fundamental challenges that existing policies face when confronted with tasks requiring sustained and intricate inter-arm dependency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind developing BiCoord stems from the recognition that current robotic manipulation benchmarks do not adequately represent the complexity and coordination observed in human bimanual actions. Human-level dexterity often involves the simultaneous and coordinated use of two hands, a capability that offers significant advantages over single-arm manipulation, particularly in environments designed for two-handed interaction (e.g., using tools, handling household objects).

The authors highlight that human bimanual actions, such as cooking, exhibit strong inter-arm dependency characterized by a high degree of spatial and temporal coordination. This includes spatially complementary actions (e.g., one hand stabilizing while the other operates) and temporal synchronization for fluid, goal-oriented motion. Such "spatially and temporally entangled" interactions are crucial for achieving advanced dexterity.

However, existing bimanual benchmarks, despite their contributions, are limited in two main aspects:
- **Limited Task Horizon:** Tasks are typically short, composed of a few basic movements like picking and placing. This fails to simulate the extended dependencies and hierarchical task structures prevalent in real-world scenarios, where robots would need to continuously coordinate their actions across multiple sub-goals, manage dynamic object states, and handle various contact transitions over a longer duration.
- **Insufficient Coordination Complexity:** While these benchmarks involve two arms, the level of coordination required is often low. Arms may perform independent actions or actions that are separated in time, rather than demanding the tight spatial-temporal coupling characteristic of true human-like cooperation. This prevents models from learning and executing truly collaborative behaviors, such as concurrently stabilizing and manipulating an object or dynamically transferring roles between arms.

Based on these identified limitations, the key objectives of the BiCoord project are:
- **To establish a novel benchmark, BiCoord**, specifically designed for bimanual manipulation tasks that are both long-horizon and demand tight spatial-temporal coordination.
- **To incorporate diverse tasks** within BiCoord that necessitate continuous inter-arm dependency and dynamic role exchange throughout multiple sub-goals.
- **To propose a comprehensive suite of quantitative metrics** capable of evaluating bimanual coordination from spatial, temporal, and combined spatial-temporal perspectives, thereby enabling a systematic and detailed measurement of cooperative performance.
- **To provide a foundation** for studying long-horizon cooperative manipulation, facilitating the development of advanced robotic policies.
- **To inspire future research** into coordination-aware robotic learning by exposing the challenges faced by current manipulation policies when applied to tasks requiring intricate and prolonged bimanual cooperation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of BiCoord involved a systematic approach encompassing task design principles, an automated data generation pipeline with human oversight, and the introduction of novel metrics for quantifying bimanual coordination. The benchmark is built upon the RoboTwin 2.0 platform.

#### 3.1. Spatial-Temporal Coordination Metrics

To quantitatively assess bimanual coordination, the authors propose a new set of metrics that go beyond simple task success rates, considering both spatial and temporal aspects of dual-arm interaction.

- **Spatial Metrics:** These metrics quantify the proximity and interaction space between the two robot arms.
 - **Minimum Relative Distance (MRD):** Measures the smallest Euclidean distance observed between the end-effectors of the left and right arms during a trajectory. This value is normalized by the initial distance between the end-effectors to allow for comparison across different task setups. A smaller MRD suggests that the arms operate in close proximity, indicating tighter spatial coupling.
 - **Average Relative Distance (ARD):** Calculates the average Euclidean distance between the end-effectors over the entire trajectory, also normalized by the initial distance. Similar to MRD, a smaller ARD implies a generally closer spatial collaboration throughout the task.

- **Temporal Metrics:** These metrics evaluate the extent of simultaneous action execution by the two arms. An arm is defined as "taking an action" if its end-effector position changes or its gripper state is close (i.e., grasping).
 - **Simultaneous Movement Time (SMT):** Represents the total number of timesteps where both the left and right arms are simultaneously performing an action.
 - **Simultaneous Movement Percentage (SMP):** Normalizes the SMT by the total trajectory length (L), providing a percentage measure of simultaneous activity. A higher SMP indicates greater temporal synergy.

- **Spatial-Temporal Integral (STI):** This is a composite metric designed to capture the intertwining of spatial and temporal coordination. It integrates the Simultaneous Movement Percentage (SMP) over various relative distance thresholds. Specifically, `SMP<d` is defined as the percentage of timesteps where both arms move simultaneously *and* their relative distance is below a given threshold `d`. The STI is then computed as the integral of `SMP<1-d` with respect to `d` from 0 to 1. A higher STI value signifies that the arms not only move simultaneously for a significant duration but also do so while maintaining close spatial proximity, thus indicating a strong spatio-temporal coupling.

**Comparison with Existing Benchmarks:**
The authors applied these metrics to existing benchmarks, RoboTwin 2.0 and RLBench2. Their analysis showed that while RLBench2 had a high SMP (97.10%), indicating simultaneous movement, its ARD was also high (114.93%), suggesting the arms were often far apart. Conversely, RoboTwin 2.0 showed stronger spatial correlation with lower ARD, but a low SMP (26.10%), indicating a lack of temporal synergy. Both benchmarks therefore exhibited restricted STI values (11.20% and 8.13% respectively), confirming their limited combined spatial-temporal coordination. Furthermore, these benchmarks were characterized by short-horizon tasks, averaging 1-2 stages, 2-3 objects, and around 200 timesteps.

#### 3.2. BiCoord Construction Pipeline

The construction of BiCoord involved a three-stage pipeline to ensure tasks possessed the desired characteristics of long-horizon and tight coordination:

1. **Task Design:** Tasks were conceived using objects from the RoboTwin-OD library. A central focus was placed on requiring high-level bimanual coordination and multiple sequential manipulation stages. The design also supports diverse robot embodiments and scene textures. Key coordination characteristics were explicitly considered:
 - **Phased Coupling:** Arms alternate between cooperative and independent phases based on task requirements (e.g., separating to pick up distinct objects, then approaching to combine them, then separating again).
 - **Spatial-Temporal Constraints:** Arms must maintain specific temporal ordering and spatial relationships (e.g., reaching the same region simultaneously, maintaining relative vertical positions for throwing-catching).
 - **Predictive Coordination:** Each arm's movement anticipates the future motion or state of the other arm, enabling proactive planning (e.g., one arm moving away to avoid collision based on the other arm's expected action).

2. **Action Code Generation:** An AI coding agent was used to generate action scripts for the designed tasks. This agent was prompted with task details and a list of available APIs. The generated code underwent an initial verification step: it was tested in simulation across 10 random seeds, requiring a success rate exceeding 0.6. If this threshold was not met, a human-in-the-loop process was initiated to analyze the weaknesses and guide subsequent code generation until the success criterion was satisfied.

3. **Trajectory Generation & Annotation:** Once action codes were verified, 100 successful trajectories were generated for each task. Crucially, these trajectories were accompanied by automatic stage-wise annotations. These annotations included:
 - **Time Zones:** Delineating the temporal segments for each stage.
 - **Sub-Goals:** Explicitly stating the objective for each stage.
 - **Arm Behaviors:** Describing the specific actions and roles of each arm within that stage.
 This fine-grained annotation supports detailed policy training and provides a basis for stage-wise evaluation.

#### 3.3. Features of BiCoord

The resulting BiCoord benchmark exhibits distinct features aligned with its design objectives:
- **Tight Spatio-Temporal Coordination:** Quantitative analysis demonstrates BiCoord's superior coordination. It achieves an SMP of 92.81%, indicating extensive simultaneous arm activity. Its MRD and ARD are significantly lower (45.78% and 32.29% reduction, respectively, compared to previous benchmarks), signifying closer spatial collaboration. The STI value for BiCoord is 42.16%, which is approximately four times higher than previous benchmarks, confirming a strong coupling between simultaneous actions and close spatial proximity.
- **Long-Horizon Manipulation:** BiCoord tasks are considerably longer. On average, a task in BiCoord consists of 4.27 stages (nearly three times more than previous benchmarks), features an average trajectory length 63.35% longer, and involves 64.13% more objects.
- **Stage-Wise Annotation and Evaluation:** To facilitate granular analysis, BiCoord provides comprehensive stage-wise annotations for training and evaluation. For evaluation, beyond the final task success rate (SR), a **Stage-Wise Success Rate (SSR)** is introduced. This metric calculates a weighted sum of successful stage completions, where each stage is assigned a score based on its importance. SSR offers more detailed insights into a policy's performance throughout the manipulation process.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors evaluated four representative manipulation policies on BiCoord: Diffusion Policy (DP) and three Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models—RDT, OpenVLA-OFT, and Pi0. Experiments were conducted in both single-task and multi-task settings, using Success Rate (SR), Stage-Wise Success Rate (SSR), and Trajectory Length (TL) as evaluation metrics.

#### 4.1. Results of Single-Task Learning

- **VLA Model Performance:** VLA models generally exhibited higher SR and SSR compared to DP. This was attributed to their pre-trained knowledge from large-scale datasets, which appears to confer an advantage in handling complex manipulation tasks. This observation suggests that large-scale pre-training is beneficial in embodied intelligence, akin to its impact in vision-language models.
- **Diffusion Policy (DP) Efficiency:** DP typically completed tasks in fewer timesteps (lower TL) than the VLA models. For instance, on the "Place Plate And Cup" task, DP achieved a high SR (71%) with a trajectory length 9.75% shorter than Pi0. This indicates a potential for combining DP's efficiency with the prior knowledge embedded in VLA models.
- **Limitations in Reasoning Ability:** Current policies, including VLAs, demonstrated weaknesses in reasoning and adapting to variations in initial conditions. For example, in the "Divide Block Tower" task, Pi0 could succeed in a standard scenario but failed when the colors or order of blocks changed, despite the training data including such variations. This suggests a tendency towards rigid imitation learning rather than robust reasoning, pointing to areas where reinforcement learning or higher-level planning could offer improvements.
- **Deficiency in Precise Alignment:** All evaluated policies struggled with tasks requiring precise alignment, such as "Handover Block With Bowls." Policies often initiated pouring actions before the bowls were fully aligned, resulting in near-zero success rates. While DP appeared to exhibit slightly better accuracy in aligning objects than VLAs in some instances, the overall performance indicated a significant challenge in this area for all tested methods.
- **Stage-Wise Analysis:** The stage-wise evaluation (SSR) revealed that policy performance often degraded in later stages of long-horizon tasks. For example, in "Collect Pens" and "Jigsaw," success rates sharply dropped as the number of objects or task stages increased. This highlights that current policies still face difficulties in sustained, long-horizon manipulation.

#### 4.2. Results of Multi-Task Learning

- **General Performance Drop:** In the multi-task setting, models generally experienced a drop in SR compared to their single-task counterparts. For example, Pi0's average SR decreased from 46.4% to 27.2%. This outcome is consistent with the expectation that managing a broader range of tasks with a single model presents a greater challenge.
- **Unexpected Improvements:** Counter-intuitively, some multi-task models showed improved SR on specific tasks. OpenVLA-OFT, for instance, saw its SR on "Sweep Block" increase from 22.0% to 44.0%. This suggests that while different tasks might introduce competition, shared skills and commonalities across tasks could also foster mutual promotion and enhance generalization capabilities, warranting further investigation.

#### 4.3. Full-Trajectory Visualization (Cook Task)

Visual analysis of the "Cook" task provided qualitative insights:
- **Recovery Ability:** Pi0 demonstrated a degree of recovery ability. When it failed to grasp the first piece of bread, it re-attempted the action after placing another piece of bread, rather than adhering rigidly to a pre-defined sequence. This suggests that VLAs might possess some capacity for task state evaluation and error correction.
- **Stability:** DP exhibited more stable manipulation, such as holding the skillet horizontally, compared to RDT and OpenVLA-OFT, which sometimes tilted the skillet. DP's failures were often linked to inflexible adjustments of grasping poses, while VLA failures were more frequently due to unstable object handling (e.g., ingredients falling out). This indicates a trade-off between DP's stability and the generalization capabilities of VLAs.
- **Efficiency:** DP tended to perform movements with smaller ranges and closer to the target, leading to more efficient trajectories with fewer timesteps. VLAs, in contrast, often executed rotations with larger amplitudes. This difference in movement style was consistent with DP's generally lower TL values.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BiCoord represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for bimanual systems, by directly addressing limitations in existing benchmarks.

- **Advancing Bimanual Manipulation Research:** By focusing on long-horizon tasks with tight spatial-temporal coordination, BiCoord pushes the frontier of bimanual manipulation research beyond simple, short-duration actions. It provides a platform to explore the complexities of continuous inter-arm dependency and dynamic role exchange, which are crucial for developing truly dexterous robots.
- **Novel Quantitative Evaluation:** The introduction of a dedicated suite of metrics—Minimum Relative Distance (MRD), Average Relative Distance (ARD), Simultaneous Movement Time (SMT), Simultaneous Movement Percentage (SMP), and especially the Spatial-Temporal Integral (STI)—offers a more nuanced and quantitative way to assess bimanual cooperation. These metrics move beyond basic success rates to provide insights into the quality and intricacy of coordination, laying a theoretical foundation for analyzing multi-arm systems.
- **Granular Policy Analysis:** The provision of stage-wise annotations and evaluation (via Stage-Wise Success Rate, SSR) allows for fine-grained analysis of policy performance at different stages of a complex task. This level of detail is invaluable for identifying specific failure modes, understanding the progression of errors, and ultimately guiding the development of more robust and adaptive policies.
- **Revealing Fundamental Challenges:** The baseline experiments with state-of-the-art policies (Diffusion Policy and prominent Vision-Language-Action models) on BiCoord tasks have illuminated fundamental weaknesses in current robotic learning methods. These include:
 - **Limited Reasoning Capabilities:** Policies struggle to adapt to variations in task conditions or object properties, suggesting a reliance on rigid imitation rather than robust reasoning.
 - **Deficiencies in Precise Alignment:** Tasks requiring high precision in spatial alignment remain a significant hurdle.
 - **Challenges in Long-Horizon Coordination:** Sustained high performance across multiple task stages is difficult to achieve, indicating a need for improved long-term planning and control.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** BiCoord is positioned to inspire several key research directions:
 - Developing new robotic learning algorithms that can achieve robust reasoning, precise alignment, and stable, long-horizon bimanual coordination.
 - Investigating methods to combine the efficiency of approaches like Diffusion Policy with the generalization capabilities and prior knowledge of VLA models.
 - Exploring techniques to leverage commonalities across diverse tasks to enhance multi-task learning and generalization.
 - Advancing the understanding and modeling of human-like predictive coordination and dynamic role exchange in robotic systems.

In summary, BiCoord serves as a rigorous benchmark that not only quantifies existing limitations in bimanual manipulation but also provides the necessary tools and detailed data to accelerate progress toward more capable and human-like robotic dexterity.
