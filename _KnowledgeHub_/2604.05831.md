# BiCoord: A Bimanual Manipulation Benchmark towards Long-Horizon Spatial-Temporal Coordination

> [!summary] Summary
> The BiCoord benchmark introduces a collection of bimanual manipulation tasks that necessitate extensive spatial-temporal coordination over long horizons. It reveals that current policies exhibit difficulties in precise alignment, robust reasoning, and maintaining sustained performance across multiple stages in these complex scenarios.

## Problem

- Existing bimanual manipulation benchmarks feature short-horizon tasks lacking complex, extended dependencies.
- Current benchmarks show weak or temporally disjoint bimanual coordination, rather than tightly coupled spatial-temporal interaction.
- A lack of granular metrics to quantitatively assess the intricate spatial and temporal aspects of bimanual robot arm cooperation.

## Method

- Established **BiCoord**, a benchmark with tasks specifically designed for ==long-horizon== and tightly coordinated ==bimanual manipulation==.
- Developed a suite of novel metrics (==MRD==, ==ARD==, ==SMT==, ==SMP==, ==STI==) to quantify spatial, temporal, and combined spatial-temporal coordination.
- Utilized an ==AI coding agent== with human-in-the-loop verification and automatic stage-wise trajectory annotations to construct diverse and complex tasks.

## Results

- VLA models generally achieved higher success rates than Diffusion Policy in single-task learning due to pre-trained knowledge.
- All evaluated policies struggled with tasks requiring precise alignment and exhibited limited reasoning capabilities under varied initial conditions.
- BiCoord tasks show a **4×** increase in spatial-temporal integral values vs prior benchmarks; policy performance consistently degraded in later stages of long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - BiCoord tasks significantly surpass existing benchmarks in complexity, demonstrating a fourfold increase in spatial-temporal integral values and substantially longer, multi-stage trajectories.
> - Current state-of-the-art policies struggle with the demanding coordination requirements of BiCoord tasks, particularly in precise alignment and flexible reasoning.
> - VLA models show an advantage from pre-training for overall success but exhibit rigidity, while Diffusion Policy is more efficient and stable for specific motions.
