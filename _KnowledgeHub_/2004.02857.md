# Beyond the Nav-Graph: Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> The paper introduces Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE), a more realistic benchmark where agents execute low-level actions in 3D environments, moving beyond prior "nav-graph" assumptions. This setting significantly challenges existing models, with the best cross-modal attention agent achieving a 0.30 SPL (32% SR) on val-unseen, revealing a substantial performance gap compared to nav-graph-based methods.

## Problem

- Most Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) tasks operate on sparse "nav-graphs," assuming a known environment topology and perfect, oracle navigation between pre-defined nodes.
- Agents in prior VLN settings often receive their precise location and heading, bypassing the need for real-world localization and collision avoidance.
- These unrealistic assumptions simplify the problem to "visually-guided graph search," inflating performance metrics and hindering the transferability of agents to physical robots.

## Method

- Introduced ==Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments (VLN-CE)== using the ==Habitat simulator== and ==Matterport3D==, allowing free navigation with ==low-level actions== like `move forward 0.25m` and `turn-left/right 15 degrees`.
- Transferred existing **Room-to-Room (R2R)** instruction-path pairs to the continuous Habitat environment, converting ==nav-graph nodes== to ==navigable points== and verifying trajectory traversability.
- Developed and evaluated instruction-guided navigation models, including a ==Seq2Seq== baseline and a ==Cross-Modal Attention== model, incorporating auxiliary losses like ==Dataset Aggregation== and a ==Progress Monitor==.

## Results

- The best-performing `Cross-Modal Attention` model, trained with combined augmentations (PM+DA*+Aug), achieved a **0.30 SPL** (**32% Success Rate**) on val-unseen environments in VLN-CE.
- Input modality ablations demonstrated that both depth (for collision avoidance) and RGB imagery (for instruction grounding) are crucial, with models lacking either performing poorly.
- A significant performance gap exists between VLN-CE agents and state-of-the-art nav-graph VLN models (e.g., **0.21 SPL** for the best VLN-CE agent converted to nav-graph vs. **0.47 SPL** for SOTA VLN), quantitatively showing the impact of nav-graph assumptions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continuous VLN significantly increases task difficulty, with basic baselines achieving much lower success rates than in nav-graph settings, due to requirements for low-level control, collision avoidance, and continuous perception.
> - Depth perception is critical for effective navigation in continuous environments, as models without it perform poorly, highlighting its importance for collision avoidance.
> - The nav-graph paradigm provides a substantial "prior" to existing VLN models, inflating reported performance and masking the true challenges of embodied navigation.
