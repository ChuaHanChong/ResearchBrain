# Motion-I2V: Consistent and Controllable Image-to-Video Generation with Explicit Motion Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Motion-I2V introduces a two-stage framework for image-to-video generation that explicitly models motion, achieving improved temporal consistency even with large movements and enabling fine-grained user control via sparse trajectories and motion brushes. This approach from the Multimedia Laboratory at CUHK generates videos with 0.9871 frame consistency while producing an average displacement of 20.06, outperforming prior methods.

## Problem

- Lack of temporal consistency in open-domain Image-to-Video (I2V) generation, especially with large object movements or viewpoint changes, leading to flickering artifacts or identity shifts.
- Limited controllability in existing I2V models, primarily relying on input images and textual prompts, preventing precise manipulation of motion trajectories or animating specific regions.
- Difficulty for video latent diffusion models (VLDMs) with limited 1-D temporal attention to capture and propagate complex spatial-temporal patterns for consistent motion.

## Method

- Proposes a two-stage framework: explicit motion generation in Stage 1 and motion-guided video rendering in Stage 2.
- Stage 1 uses a fine-tuned Video Latent ==Diffusion Model== (**VLDM**) to predict a sequence of dense pixel-wise displacement fields (==optical flow==) from the reference image.
- Stage 2 employs "motion-augmented temporal attention" that forward-warps reference frame features according to the predicted motion and adaptively injects them into the ==attention mechanism== for subsequent frame generation.
- Integrates ControlNet for fine-grained user control, enabling sparse trajectory guidance and region-specific animation (motion brush) for precise motion manipulation.

## Results

- Achieved higher prompt consistency (34.86 CLIP score) and frame consistency (0.9871 CLIP similarity) compared to state-of-the-art methods like DynamiCrafter and I2VGen-XL.
- Generated videos with substantially larger average displacement (20.06) while maintaining high consistency, unlike other models which trade off motion magnitude for coherence.
- Qualitatively produces temporally coherent videos with significant motion, preserving identity and appearance, while comparable models often exhibit distortion or limited movement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling motion in a dedicated first stage significantly improves temporal consistency and enables generation of larger, more complex motions compared to implicit end-to-end approaches.
> - Enhancing temporal attention with motion-warped features is crucial for effectively leveraging predicted motion fields, improving consistency without sacrificing motion magnitude.
> - Factoring out motion prediction allows for the integration of fine-grained control mechanisms (like ControlNet with sparse trajectories or motion brushes) that were previously difficult to achieve.
