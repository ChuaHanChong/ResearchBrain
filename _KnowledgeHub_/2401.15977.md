# Motion-I2V: Consistent and Controllable Image-to-Video Generation with Explicit Motion Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Motion-I2V introduces a two-stage framework for image-to-video generation that explicitly models motion, achieving improved temporal consistency even with large movements and enabling fine-grained user control via sparse trajectories and motion brushes. This approach from the Multimedia Laboratory at CUHK generates videos with 0.9871 frame consistency while producing an average displacement of 20.06, outperforming prior methods.

## Problem

- Lack of temporal consistency in open-domain Image-to-Video (I2V) generation, especially with large object movements or viewpoint changes, leading to flickering artifacts or identity shifts.
- Limited controllability in existing I2V models, primarily relying on input images and textual prompts, preventing precise manipulation of motion trajectories or animating specific regions.
- Difficulty for video latent diffusion models (VLDMs) with limited 1-D temporal attention to capture and propagate complex spatial-temporal patterns for consistent motion.

## Method

- Proposes a two-stage framework: explicit motion generation in Stage 1 and motion-guided video rendering in Stage 2.
- Stage 1 uses a fine-tuned Video Latent ==Diffusion Model== (**VLDM**) to predict a sequence of dense pixel-wise displacement fields (==optical flow==) from the reference image, with an added ControlNet enabling sparse trajectory guidance and region-specific animation (motion brush).
- Stage 2 employs "motion-augmented temporal attention" that forward-warps reference frame features according to the predicted motion and adaptively injects them into the ==attention mechanism== for subsequent frame generation.

## Results

- Achieved higher prompt consistency (34.86 CLIP score) and frame consistency (0.9871 CLIP similarity) compared to state-of-the-art methods like DynamiCrafter and I2VGen-XL.
- Generated videos with substantially larger average displacement (20.06) while maintaining high consistency, unlike other models which trade off motion magnitude for coherence.
- Qualitatively produces temporally coherent videos with significant motion, preserving identity and appearance, while comparable models often exhibit distortion or limited movement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling motion in a dedicated first stage significantly improves temporal consistency and enables generation of larger, more complex motions compared to implicit end-to-end approaches.
> - Enhancing temporal attention with motion-warped features is crucial for effectively leveraging predicted motion fields, improving consistency without sacrificing motion magnitude.
> - Factoring out motion prediction allows for the integration of fine-grained control mechanisms (like ControlNet with sparse trajectories or motion brushes) that were previously difficult to achieve.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Image-to-Video (I2V) generation, the task of animating a static image into a dynamic video sequence while preserving visual appearance, has long been a challenging yet highly sought-after capability in computer graphics and vision. Traditionally, I2V methods were highly specialized, focusing on specific categories like human hair, fluids, or portraits. This specialization severely limited their applicability to diverse, open-domain scenarios.

The advent of **diffusion models** has revolutionized the field of generative AI, particularly with their success in high-quality, diverse image generation (e.g., DALL-E 2, GLIDE, Imagen, Stable Diffusion). This success naturally led researchers to extend these powerful models to video generation. Initial approaches typically adapted existing Text-to-Image (T2I) models by incorporating 1-D temporal attention modules to model temporal dependencies. Examples include VideoComposer, I2VGen-XL, and DynamiCrafter. While these methods achieved impressive open-domain animation, they faced significant hurdles:

1. **Temporal Consistency:** The reliance on limited 1-D temporal attention in Video Latent Diffusion Models (VLDMs) makes it difficult to ensure smooth and coherent temporal transitions, especially when large object movements or viewpoint changes occur. This often leads to flickering artifacts or identity shifts across frames. The models struggle to learn complex spatial-temporal priors solely from this limited temporal mechanism.
2. **Controllability:** Most existing I2V models offer coarse control, primarily relying on the input image and textual prompts. This severely limits user agency, preventing precise manipulation of motion trajectories or animating specific regions, a level of control commonly available in static image manipulation (e.g., drag-based or region-specific editing).

**Motion-I2V** directly addresses these two critical limitations by proposing a novel two-stage framework that introduces *explicit motion modeling*. This approach marks a significant departure from end-to-end I2V diffusion models that attempt to learn both motion and appearance generation simultaneously. By factoring out motion prediction into a dedicated first stage, Motion-I2V leverages and integrates insights from the well-established field of **motion modeling**, specifically optical flow and point tracking (e.g., FlowNet, RAFT, DOT, CoTracker). This integration of traditional motion estimation techniques with modern generative models is a key trend in hybrid AI systems, combining the strengths of specialized modules.

Furthermore, Motion-I2V integrates the **ControlNet** concept, a recent innovation in diffusion models that allows for fine-grained conditional control. By training a ControlNet specifically for motion prediction in the first stage, the work aligns with the broader trend of enhancing user control and interaction in generative AI, moving beyond simple text prompts to more intuitive, interactive modalities. The ability to perform zero-shot video-to-video translation also broadens the utility of the framework, positioning it as a versatile tool for content creation.

In summary, Motion-I2V situates itself at the forefront of generative video research by tackling the core challenges of consistency and controllability, offering a more robust and user-friendly solution than prior single-stage or implicitly-motion-learned approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the Motion-I2V research is to develop a novel framework for **consistent and controllable image-to-video generation**. The authors explicitly state their aim to remedy the shortcomings observed in existing I2V diffusion models.

The motivation behind this work stems from two core problems identified in the current I2V landscape:

1. **Lack of Temporal Consistency in Open-Domain I2V:** Previous general-purpose I2V diffusion models often struggle to maintain visual coherence and identity preservation across frames, especially when tasked with generating videos that involve significant motion or viewpoint changes. This limitation is attributed to their reliance on a "narrow temporal receptive field" provided by 1-D temporal attention mechanisms within the video latent diffusion models (VLDMs). Such mechanisms find it difficult to capture and propagate complex spatial-temporal patterns effectively, leading to visual distortions or "crashed results" as observed in the ablation studies. The authors are motivated to enable the generation of "more consistent videos even at the presence of large motion and viewpoint variation."

2. **Limited Controllability:** Current I2V methods primarily condition generation on a reference image and a text prompt. While powerful, this level of control is often insufficient for users who desire precise manipulation over the generated motion. Analogous to drag-based or region-specific controls prevalent in image manipulation, there is a strong need for similar fine-grained and interactive control in video generation. The authors aim to empower users to "more precisely control the motion trajectories and animated region with sparse trajectories and motion brush," moving beyond the limitations of text-only instructions.

To address these motivations, Motion-I2V proposes a fundamental shift in the I2V paradigm: **factorizing the complex image-to-video mapping into two distinct stages with explicit motion modeling.** This factorization is the core hypothesis for improving both consistency and controllability. By dedicating a stage to predicting plausible pixel-wise motion trajectories, the subsequent video rendering stage can explicitly leverage this dynamic information, thereby alleviating the pressure on the temporal attention mechanisms to implicitly learn complex motion patterns from scratch.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Motion-I2V introduces a novel, two-stage framework for image-to-video generation, designed to enhance both consistency and controllability by explicitly modeling motion.

**A. Stage 1: Explicit Motion Generation (Motion Field Predictor)**

The first stage focuses on deducing plausible motion fields—pixel-wise trajectories that animate the reference image.
- **Goal:** To predict a sequence of 2D displacement maps, {f0→i | i = 1..., N}, representing the optical flow from the reference frame (I0) to subsequent frames (Ii).
- **Backbone:** A pre-trained Stable Diffusion 1.5 model is adapted and fine-tuned to act as a **Video Latent Diffusion Model (VLDM)** for motion field prediction. The choice of a pre-trained LDM leverages its rich generative priors and semantic knowledge, enabling the model to associate motion distributions with corresponding objects.
- **Motion Fields Modeling:** Each optical flow map f0→i is encoded into a latent representation using a dedicated optical flow VAE encoder. The latent code of the clean reference image (E(I0)) is concatenated along the channel dimension as a condition. Frame stride `i` is also embedded and added to time embeddings to represent motion strength.
- **Training Strategy:** A three-step fine-tuning process is employed:
 1. Fine-tune an LDM to predict a *single* displacement field conditioned on the reference image and text.
 2. Freeze these tuned LDM parameters and integrate vanilla temporal modules (1-D temporal attention) to create a VLDM for training, learning the video's temporal motion distribution by denoising the entire sequence of motion fields.
 3. Finetune the *entire* VLDM model to obtain the final motion fields predictor.
- **Ground Truth:** Optical flow and multi-frame trajectories are estimated from training videos using state-of-the-art methods like FlowFormer++ and DOT to serve as ground truth for training the motion predictor.

**B. Stage 2: Motion-Guided I2V (Video Rendering)**

The second stage is responsible for generating the video frames, propagating the reference image's content based on the motion fields predicted in Stage 1.
- **Goal:** Synthesize future frames that maintain fidelity to the reference image and temporal consistency, guided by the explicit motion.
- **Backbone:** AnimateDiff v2 is chosen as the base VLDM, with its spatial modules initialized from pre-trained weights and frozen to inherit strong generative priors.
- **Motion-Augmented Temporal Attention:** This is the core innovation in Stage 2. It enhances the vanilla 1-D temporal attention by incorporating the predicted motion fields.
 - For each temporal layer, the latent feature map of the reference frame (z) is *forward-warped* according to the predicted motion field (f0→i) to create "warped feature maps" (z[i]’).
 - These warped feature maps (z[i]’) are *interleaved* with the original noisy latent features of the subsequent frames (z[i]).
 - Crucially, in the temporal attention mechanism (Softmax(QKᵀ)V), the *query (Q)* is derived from the original noisy features (z), while the *key (K)* and *value (V)* are derived from the *augmented features* (z_aug, which includes both original and warped features). This allows the attention mechanism to adaptively inject the warped, motion-guided reference features into the synthesized frames at multiple scales, effectively enlarging the temporal receptive field and simplifying the learning of complex spatial-temporal patterns.
- **Selective Noising:** During the denoising process, the clean latent code of the reference image (z_ref) is always concatenated with the noisy latent codes of other frames along the temporal axis. This ensures that the reference image’s visual details are faithfully preserved throughout generation.

**C. Fine-Grained Control Mechanisms**

Motion-I2V extends its capabilities beyond basic I2V through several control mechanisms, enabled by the explicit motion modeling:
- **Sparse Trajectory Guided I2V:** A **ControlNet** is trained for Stage 1. It clones the down-sample and middle blocks of the Stage 1 3D-Unet. This ControlNet takes sparse user-drawn pixel-wise trajectories (f_sparse) and a binary mask (m) as additional conditions. It then generates plausible dense displacement fields, which guide Stage 2. This offers intuitive, interactive control over object motion.
- **Region-Specific I2V (Motion Brush):** This is a specialized use of the ControlNet. By setting f_sparse to all-zero maps and the mask (m) to specify static (1) and animated (0) regions, users can animate only selected areas while keeping others static.
- **Zero-Shot Video-to-Video Translation (V2V):** Stage 2 of Motion-I2V can perform V2V translation. Users can first transform the *initial frame* of a source video into a target style using existing image-to-image tools. Then, the dense displacement fields are obtained from the *source video* using an off-the-shelf dense point tracker. Stage 2 then uses these predicted motions to animate the *transformed first frame*, consistently propagating the new style throughout the video while preserving the source video's dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted by the authors demonstrate the superior performance of Motion-I2V in terms of temporal consistency, prompt following, and the ability to generate larger, more complex motions compared to existing state-of-the-art methods.

**A. Quantitative Comparison:**
- **Dataset:** Evaluations were performed on a custom test set of 80 images from various categories (human activity, animals, vehicles, natural scenes, AI-generated) with ChatGPT-4V generated prompts, as no comprehensive open-domain I2V benchmark currently exists.
- **Metrics:**
 - **Prompt Consistency (↑):** Measured by CLIP text-image logits between the prompt and generated frames. Motion-I2V scored **34.86**, outperforming VideoComposer (32.62), I2VGen-XL (33.69), and DynamiCrafter (34.60). This indicates better adherence to textual instructions.
 - **Frame Consistency (↑):** Measured by the cosine similarity between consecutive generated frames in the CLIP embedding space. Motion-I2V achieved **0.9871**, slightly better than DynamiCrafter (0.9860) and significantly better than I2VGen-XL (0.9650) and VideoComposer (0.9393). This highlights its strong temporal coherence.
 - **Average Displacement:** Measures the magnitude of motion by estimating optical flow between the first and subsequent frames. Motion-I2V generated a substantial **20.06**, higher than I2VGen-XL (17.70) and much higher than DynamiCrafter (3.31), while still maintaining high consistency. This is a critical finding, as it shows Motion-I2V can produce *significant motion without sacrificing consistency*, a common trade-off in other models.

**B. Qualitative Comparison:**
- **Observation:** Comparative visual results against DynamiCrafter, Pika 1.0 (commercial), and Gen2 (commercial) reinforce the quantitative findings.
 - DynamiCrafter and Pika 1.0: Tend to generate videos with very small motions, often failing to respond effectively to explicit motion prompts.
 - Gen2: Capable of generating large motions, similar to Motion-I2V, but often suffers from "severe distortion" and fails to preserve the identity of the reference image across frames, indicating poor temporal consistency.
 - Motion-I2V: Consistently synthesizes temporally coherent videos even when large motions are involved, effectively preserving the identity and appearance of the reference image. This visually validates its key advantage.

**C. Ablation Study:**
- The ablation study strongly supports the design choices of the two-stage framework and the motion-augmented temporal attention:
 - **Without Stage 1:** A model relying solely on 1-D temporal attention for motion learning (like many prior methods) was highly unstable, often producing "crashed results," exhibiting low consistency (0.9505), and sometimes generating extremely large (66.44 average displacement) but nonsensical motions. This underscores the difficulty of implicitly learning complex motion patterns in an end-to-end manner.
 - **With Stage 1 (Naive Addition):** When Stage 1 was included, but the warped features were *naively added* (rather than adaptively injected via attention) to the subsequent frames in Stage 2, stability improved, leading to higher consistency (0.9542) and more vivid motions (48.91 average displacement) compared to no Stage 1. This confirms the benefit of explicit motion guidance.
 - **With Stage 1 (Attention-based Injection):** The full Motion-I2V model, utilizing attention to adaptively inject warped features, achieved the highest consistency (0.9871) and maintained a robust motion magnitude (20.06 average displacement) without distortion. This demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed motion-augmented temporal attention in leveraging the predicted motion fields for superior temporal coherence.

These results conclusively show that Motion-I2V effectively addresses the long-standing challenges of consistency and controllability in open-domain I2V.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Motion-I2V represents a significant advancement in the field of image-to-video generation, offering substantial improvements in both the quality and usability of generated video content. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Paradigm Shift in I2V Architecture:** The most profound contribution is the novel two-stage factorization of the I2V task into explicit motion prediction and motion-guided video rendering. This decouples the complex spatial-temporal modeling, allowing each stage to specialize: Stage 1 learns to deduce plausible dynamics, and Stage 2 focuses on content propagation guided by these dynamics. This architectural innovation provides a robust framework that can inspire future research in complex generative tasks by breaking them down into more manageable, interconnected sub-problems.
- **Unprecedented Temporal Consistency with Large Motions:** Motion-I2V effectively solves a critical trade-off prevalent in previous I2V models: generating either small, consistent motions or large, inconsistent (distorted) motions. By explicitly guiding the video generation with predicted motion fields and enhancing temporal attention with warped features, Motion-I2V achieves high temporal consistency even in the presence of significant object movement or viewpoint changes. This leads to more realistic, visually stable, and high-quality animated outputs.
- **Enhanced and Intuitive Controllability:** The integration of ControlNet for sparse trajectory guidance and region-specific animation (motion brush) is a game-changer for user interaction. Unlike text-only control, these mechanisms offer precise, intuitive, and interactive ways for users to define desired motions and animated regions. This level of fine-grained control moves I2V closer to being a practical creative tool for artists, designers, and content creators.
- **Versatile Framework with Zero-Shot Capabilities:** The demonstration of zero-shot video-to-video translation further highlights the versatility of the Motion-I2V framework. By leveraging existing image-to-image tools for style transfer and external motion tracking, the second stage of Motion-I2V can seamlessly propagate new styles while preserving original video dynamics. This expands its utility beyond pure I2V.
- **Practical Applications across Industries:** The ability to generate consistent and controllable videos from single images has immense practical implications:
 - **Film & Entertainment:** Pre-visualization, rapid prototyping of animated scenes, special effects generation, character animation.
 - **Advertising & Marketing:** Dynamic product showcases, engaging social media content, animated advertisements from static images.
 - **Augmented Reality (AR) / Virtual Reality (VR):** Animating static elements within virtual environments to create more immersive experiences.
 - **Content Creation:** Empowering artists, influencers, and small businesses to produce high-quality animated content without extensive animation skills or resources.
 - **Education:** Creating dynamic learning materials from static diagrams or images.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens several avenues for future exploration, such as integrating more sophisticated motion primitives, exploring different forms of user control (e.g., semantic control of motion), improving generation speed, and addressing current limitations like brightness consistency. The identified limitation regarding the noise scheduler (Zero-SNR schedulers) points towards promising areas for further refinement.

In conclusion, Motion-I2V significantly pushes the boundaries of image-to-video generation by offering a robust, consistent, and highly controllable framework. Its innovative two-stage design and user-centric control mechanisms make it a powerful tool with wide-ranging potential applications across various creative and industrial domains, marking a crucial step towards more accessible and high-quality video content creation.
