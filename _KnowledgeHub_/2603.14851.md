# AutoMoT: A Unified Vision-Language-Action Model with Asynchronous Mixture-of-Transformers for End-to-End Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> AutoMoT introduces a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for end-to-end autonomous driving, leveraging an asynchronous Mixture-of-Transformers architecture with a frozen VLM for scene understanding and a specialized action expert. This architecture achieves a 7.6x inference speedup while maintaining planning accuracy and preserving the general reasoning capabilities of pre-trained VLMs.

## Problem

- Existing autonomous driving systems face distributional misalignment between high-level VLM reasoning and low-level action spaces.
- Integrating VLMs often leads to underexploitation or catastrophic forgetting of their general reasoning capabilities when fine-tuned for specific driving tasks.
- Unified VLA models designed for synchronized reasoning and action generation suffer from substantial inference latency, hindering real-time autonomous driving requirements.

## Method

- **AutoMoT** proposes a ==Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT)== architecture comprising a frozen pre-trained VLM as a ==Scene Understanding Expert (UE)== and a task-specialized ==Action Expert (AE)== for decision-making and trajectory planning.
- An ==asynchronous inference== mechanism allows the UE and AE to operate at different temporal frequencies, with the AE reusing UE's scene representations via a shared ==KV cache== for high-frequency action updates.
- ==Joint attention sharing== between UE and AE conditions action generation on high-level scene understanding, ensuring decisions are semantically grounded while preserving the UE's general knowledge.

## Results

- Achieves competitive closed-loop driving on CARLA Bench2Drive (**Driving Score 87.34%**, **Success Rate 70.00%**) and SOTA open-loop collision rates on nuScenes (**0.07% collision rate**) with **0.32m** L2 displacement.
- Maintains strong scene understanding on VQA benchmarks (TallyQA **81.40%**, InfoVQA **89.30%**), showing the frozen VLM mitigates catastrophic forgetting.
- Asynchronous inference reduces overall latency by **86.8%** (from 0.38s to 0.05s) — a **7.6x speedup** — with only **1.24%** L2 increase.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preserving the pre-trained VLM's general knowledge by freezing its backbone for scene understanding effectively transfers knowledge to action policy learning and avoids catastrophic forgetting on general reasoning tasks.
> - Domain-specific training for action-level tasks (decision-making and trajectory planning) is crucial for robust autonomous driving performance, as general VLMs alone are insufficient for these specialized tasks.
> - Asynchronous inference significantly enhances real-time efficiency (7.6x speedup) for autonomous driving by decoupling reasoning and action generation frequencies without meaningfully degrading planning accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving (AD) has widely adopted hierarchical modular pipelines, typically encompassing perception, prediction, and planning components, particularly in end-to-end (E2E) systems. Recent advancements in vision-language models (VLMs) have contributed to AD by enhancing high-level scene understanding, a capability often limited in conventional data-driven E2E systems, especially in complex open-world environments. VLMs are recognized for their generalization and reasoning capabilities, which can improve the interpretability of AD systems.

Current approaches to integrating VLMs into E2E AD systems generally fall into several categories, each with identified limitations:
- **VLM as Scene Encoder**: In this paradigm, VLMs are incorporated into the upstream stages of the AD pipeline to provide rich scene understanding for downstream planning. A limitation of this approach is the potential for distributional misalignment between the reasoning space of VLMs and the action space of planners. Additionally, fine-tuning VLMs for generating intermediate conditioning signals can constrain them to task-specific roles, potentially diminishing their general pre-trained capabilities.
- **VLM as Secondary System**: This involves a dual-system architecture where the VLM functions as an auxiliary module, providing high-level conditioning signals to assist conventional E2E pipelines. This approach shares the same limitations regarding distributional misalignment and potential degradation of general VLM capabilities.
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Architectures**: More recent developments involve VLA architectures that integrate reasoning and planning within a single pre-trained VLM backbone, often using autoregressive modeling. While this unified design is compact and leverages the reasoning capabilities of VLMs, a significant challenge is the tight coupling of action policy execution with high-level reasoning at a synchronized temporal frequency. This coupling can lead to substantial inference latency, which is impractical for real-world autonomous driving, especially in complex interactive environments requiring low-latency control and rapid replanning. Furthermore, approaches that generate actions in textual form rely on textual token supervision, which is considered a weaker form of supervision compared to direct numerical action representations.

This work, AutoMoT, positions itself by addressing these limitations. It aims to unify reasoning and action generation within a single VLA model while preserving the general reasoning capabilities of pre-trained VLMs and enabling efficient, asynchronous inference. The objective is to mitigate distributional misalignment, avoid degradation of VLM intelligence, and reduce inference latency to meet real-time requirements, thereby differentiating itself from existing VLM integration strategies in autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified challenges in integrating vision-language models (VLMs) into end-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving (AD) systems. The authors pinpoint three core issues:
1. **Distributional Misalignment**: Existing integration strategies frequently exhibit a discrepancy between the reasoning space of VLMs and the action space required by AD planners. This misalignment can hinder effective knowledge transfer and coherent policy generation.
2. **Underexploitation of General VLM Capabilities**: When pre-trained VLMs are fine-tuned for specific AD tasks or to generate intermediate conditioning signals, their broad, general reasoning capabilities can be constrained or diminished, leading to a reduction in their overall utility.
3. **Substantial Inference Latency**: Tightly coupling high-level reasoning with low-latency action policy generation, especially in unified VLA models, often results in significant inference latency. This latency can critically degrade driving performance, particularly in dynamic and complex real-world scenarios that demand rapid decision-making and control.

To address these challenges, the authors pose a central question: "How can VLA models fully leverage the generalist intelligence of a pre-trained VLM while mastering domain-specific capabilities and simultaneously satisfying real-time inference requirements?"

In response, the key objectives of the AutoMoT project are:
- To propose an end-to-end AD framework, AutoMoT, that unifies asynchronous reasoning (scene understanding) and action generation (decision-making and trajectory planning) within a single vision-language-action (VLA) model.
- To design a system that avoids the degradation of VLM capabilities, specifically by preserving the general reasoning intelligence of the pre-trained VLM backbone.
- To resolve the distributional discrepancies that often arise between the reasoning space of VLMs and the action space of AD systems.
- To enable efficient asynchronous inference across various tasks, allowing different components (e.g., high-level reasoning and low-level action) to operate at different temporal frequencies, thereby reducing overall latency.
- To investigate the functional boundaries of pre-trained VLMs within the AD context, aiming to clarify when and to what extent AD-tailored fine-tuning is necessary for different task levels (e.g., scene understanding versus action planning).
- To explore the utility of a VLA-oriented action refiner that enhances driving performance through diffusion-based fine-tuning, building upon the outputs of the VLA model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AutoMoT is designed as an end-to-end autonomous driving framework that unifies scene understanding, decision-making, and trajectory planning within a single VLA model. The architecture is composed of three primary components: a Scene Understanding Expert (UE), an Action Expert (AE), and an Action Refiner, all implemented using transformer-based architectures.

#### 3.1. Network Architecture

- **Understanding Expert (UE)**:
 - **Function**: The UE is responsible for high-level scene understanding and generating Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, particularly for complex and rare scenarios. It also facilitates knowledge transfer to the action policy learning process.
 - **Implementation**: It employs the Qwen3-VL-4B dense model as its vision-language backbone.
 - **Input**: Multi-view and multi-frame RGB images from onboard cameras, along with textual prompts comprising system prompts and user instructions.
 - **Output**: Semantic reasoning results in textual form.
 - **Training Strategy**: The UE is kept frozen throughout the entire training process to preserve its general knowledge and prevent catastrophic degradation of its reasoning performance.

- **Action Expert (AE)**:
 - **Function**: The AE handles decision-making and trajectory planning within the unified VLA framework.
 - **Input**: At each timestep `t`, the AE receives an observation `o_t = {I_RGB_t, I_BEV_t, Q(t)}`, which includes current RGB images, LiDAR Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) features, and action queries.
 - **Output**: The AE generates action-side latent representations that are decoded into three specific outputs:
 - Concrete meta-actions (`Z_hat_t`) for the next three consecutive 1-second intervals.
 - Future temporal waypoints (`Y_hat_t`) sampled at 0.5-second intervals over a 3-second horizon.
 - Spatial route points (`Y_bar_t`) to parameterize the reference path.
 - **Architecture**: The AE is implemented as a task-specialized transformer with approximately 1.6 billion parameters. It is trained from scratch to acquire domain-specific knowledge for autonomous driving.
 - **Joint Attention Sharing**: The AE operates in a shared attention space with the UE. This allows the AE to condition its action generation process on the latent reasoning produced by the UE, thus grounding decision-making and planning in high-level scene understanding and enabling knowledge transfer from the pre-trained VLM.
 - **Attention Patterns**: Understanding, decision-making, and planning are regulated through cross-task causal attention. Specifically, decision representations are conditioned on understanding, and planning is further conditioned on both understanding and decision within the latent space. Within each task, latent features follow bidirectional attention across modalities, while cross-task interactions are governed by causal attention. The AE operates at a higher frequency than the UE.

- **Action Refiner**:
 - **Function**: This component enhances driving performance by refining the trajectory proposals generated by the AE.
 - **Implementation**: It is based on a Diffusion Transformer (DiT). Unlike standard diffusion processes that often start from Gaussian noise, this refiner uses the spatial-temporal trajectories predicted by the AE as informative priors. It performs truncated reverse denoising from this informed prior to generate refined trajectories, accelerating inference.
 - **Process**: AE trajectory proposals are perturbed with multiplicative Gaussian noise. These noisy trajectories are converted into temporal and spatial queries, which are processed by a stack of DiT decoder blocks. A conditioning signal (integrating diffusion timestep, current ego state, and state history) is injected via adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN).
 - **Mixture-of-Attention (MoA) Mechanism**: To effectively fuse heterogeneous information (latent decision states `h_de` from AE and BEV features `F_bev` from the vision encoder), a MoA mechanism is proposed. MoA employs a main-bypass fusion design, where attention is computed in parallel across self-attention (temporal and spatial queries), cross-attention to BEV features, and cross-attention to latent decision states. The contribution of latent decision states is adaptively modulated by a learnable factor. Residual bypass pathways (mean pooling for BEV features, attention pooling for reasoning tokens) preserve global contextual cues.

#### 3.2. Training Strategy

- **Decision Making**: This is formulated as a token-level sequence modeling problem over meta-actions, conditioned on multi-frame driving observations.
 - **Datasets**: NuSync (a custom-annotated multi-frame decision-making dataset based on nuScenes, supporting synchronous and asynchronous inference) and PDM-Meta (for CARLA simulation). NuSync includes 80.1K samples with meta-actions over a 3-second horizon.
 - **Loss Function**: The AE predicts a sequence of meta-action tokens, and the policy is optimized by minimizing the negative log-likelihood (`L_DM`) of the target decision tokens.
- **Trajectory Planning**:
 - **Datasets**: nuScenes and CARLA-Garage. For the Action Refiner, the PDM-Lite dataset (over 700,000 samples) is used.
 - **Trajectory Representation**: 3-second future trajectories are represented by temporal waypoints (for motion dynamics) and spatial route points (for road geometry).
 - **Loss Function**: An `ℓ_1` loss is used for both temporal (`L_temp_traj`) and spatial (`L_spatial_traj`) trajectory prediction.
 - **Joint Optimization**: Decision-making and trajectory planning are jointly optimized within the AE, aiming for coherent action policies grounded in the semantic representations from the UE.
- **Action Refiner**: The refiner is trained using an L1 reconstruction error as the loss function for trajectory refinement.

#### 3.3. Asynchronous Inference with KV Caching

AutoMoT implements asynchronous inference as a multi-rate process where reasoning and action inference evolve at different temporal resolutions, while remaining grounded in real-time visual observations.
- **Mechanism**: A shared Key-Value (KV) cache mediates the interaction between the UE and AE.
- **Process**:
 1. At any action timestep `t`, the AE generates layer-wise queries, keys, and values (`Q_l_act(t), K_l_act(t), V_l_act(t)`).
 2. At a less frequent update time `τ(t) <= t`, the UE produces a set of layer-wise KV representations (`K_l_scene(τ(t)), V_l_scene(τ(t))`) which are stored in a persistent KV cache.
 3. For attention computation, the AE combines the cached KV from the UE at `τ(t)` with its own derived KV representations at time `t`.
 4. The final joint attention is computed using the AE's queries and the combined keys and values.
- **Benefit**: This design allows action inference (decision-making and trajectory planning) to operate at a higher execution frequency by reusing potentially older but still relevant scene representations from the UE, which is updated less frequently. This aligns with the real-time requirements of autonomous driving by reducing redundant computation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on AutoMoT assessed its performance across various benchmarks, including closed-loop and open-loop settings, as well as scene understanding tasks.

#### 4.1. Closed-Loop Planning Benchmark Results

- Evaluations on the CARLA Bench2Drive leaderboard indicated that AutoMoT achieved state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance among VLM-augmented baseline methods.
- AutoMoT obtained a Driving Score (DS) of 87.34 and a Success Rate (SR) of 70.00%. This performance exceeded that of SimLingo (DS 85.07, SR 67.27%), a method that utilized action dreamer-based simulation for data augmentation, whereas AutoMoT was trained solely on the original dataset.
- The integration of the VLA-oriented action refiner (AutoMoT+) further improved closed-loop driving performance, achieving a DS of 89.42 and an SR of 74.09%. The report noted that the stochasticity of diffusion policies could lead to variability in closed-loop outcomes, thus reporting average performance.

#### 4.2. Open-Loop Planning Benchmark Results

- On the nuScenes open-loop planning benchmark, AutoMoT exhibited competitive performance in terms of L2 displacement and achieved SOTA results in collision rate.
- The average L2 displacement was 0.32 meters, competitive with top-performing methods. The average collision rate was 0.07%, which was noted as SOTA.
- A key observation was that AutoMoT, along with OpenEMMA, refrained from domain-specific VLM backbone adaptation, in contrast to most existing methods. Despite this, AutoMoT showed a performance distinction, particularly in L2 displacement, suggesting the critical role of policy learning in VLA models for action-level tasks beyond the inherent capabilities of pre-trained VLMs.
- The authors also indicated that the marginal gains (e.g., a few centimeters in L2 displacement) observed from fine-tuning the VLM backbone on AD datasets might come at the cost of significant degradation in scene understanding due to catastrophic forgetting.

#### 4.3. General VQA Benchmark Results

- To investigate the trade-off between AD-specific adaptation and general reasoning capabilities, AutoMoT (with a frozen VLM backbone) was compared against other open-source baselines on both AD-specific (LingoQA, OmniDrive, CODA-LM) and general-domain VQA datasets (TallyQA, InfoVQA).
- AutoMoT demonstrated competitive or superior performance on AD-specific VQA benchmarks (e.g., LingoQA: 67.00 Lingo-Judge score, OmniDrive: 0.89 GPT-Score), even against methods that fine-tuned their VLM backbones (e.g., ReCogDrive: 67.20 Lingo-Judge, 0.82 OmniDrive).
- On general-domain VQA benchmarks, AutoMoT significantly outperformed fine-tuned methods (e.g., TallyQA: 81.40 vs 63.40 for Robotron-Drive; InfoVQA: 89.30 vs 75.80 for ReCogDrive). This suggested that fine-tuning the VLM backbone on AD-tailored scene understanding tasks yielded limited benefits for planning metrics and led to substantial performance degradation on general-domain VQA, indicative of catastrophic forgetting.

#### 4.4. Performance Boundary of Pretrained Backbone (Ablation Study)

- A controlled ablation study focused on the impact of fine-tuning the VLM backbone of AutoMoT on AD-specific datasets (LingoQA for scene understanding, OmniDrive counterfactual reasoning for planning-related tasks) and its effect on general-domain knowledge benchmarks.
- Fine-tuning the VLM backbone resulted in only marginal improvements on LingoQA (67.00 to 67.20) but substantial gains on the OmniDrive counterfactual planning task (18.20 to 67.80). This suggested that pre-trained VLMs could support multi-task scene understanding through semantic prompting alone, while fine-tuning was more critical for action-level tasks.
- The impact on general-domain reasoning was task-dependent: minor changes for datasets with simpler answer spaces (ScienceQA, FigureQA), but substantial degradation for complex VQA benchmarks requiring compositional reasoning and multi-step inference (e.g., TallyQA: 81.40 to 52.40; InfographicVQA: 89.30 to 50.20). This provided evidence of catastrophic forgetting when VLMs were directly adapted to the AD domain.
- These findings led to the conclusion that high-level scene understanding might not require extensive domain-specific adaptation, while task-specific fine-tuning should primarily target action-level components. AutoMoT's design, which preserves UE general intelligence and transfers reasoning knowledge to the AE, aligned with this principle.

#### 4.5. Asynchronous versus Synchronous Inference (Ablation Study)

- A comparison between AutoMoT (asynchronous with KV cache) and AutoMoT-S (synchronized, without KV cache) revealed that asynchronous inference maintained nearly identical planning accuracy. The average L2 error increased only marginally from 0.322 to 0.324 (0.62% degradation), indicating that the slightly outdated visual context from asynchronous execution had a minor effect.
- Regarding efficiency, AutoMoT demonstrated significant latency reduction. Without the diffusion head, AutoMoT-S took 117.3 ms per step (8.5 Hz), while AutoMoT reduced this to 37.0 ms (27 Hz), a 68.5% reduction. With the diffusion head, latencies were 143.3 ms (7.0 Hz) for AutoMoT-S and 63.0 ms (16 Hz) for AutoMoT.
- Comparisons with other VLA baselines indicated that AutoMoT achieved an order-of-magnitude improvement in inference efficiency (37 ms vs. hundreds or thousands of milliseconds for others), attributed to decoupling reasoning and action generation through asynchronous execution and KV-cache reuse.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AutoMoT framework contributes to the field of autonomous driving by proposing a unified vision-language-action (VLA) model that integrates scene understanding, decision-making, and planning within a single architecture. Its significance lies in addressing several critical challenges encountered in the application of VLMs to end-to-end autonomous driving systems.

One notable impact is the demonstration of **efficient asynchronous inference**. By implementing a Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture with layer-wise joint attention sharing and a KV-caching mechanism, AutoMoT allows reasoning and action generation tasks to operate at different temporal frequencies. This approach substantially reduces inference latency compared to prior synchronous VLA models, making the system more compatible with the real-time processing requirements of autonomous driving platforms. This efficiency gain, achieved without significant degradation in planning performance, represents a step towards practical onboard deployment of VLA-scale models.

A further significant contribution is the clarification of the **functional boundaries of pre-trained VLMs in autonomous driving**. The research provides evidence that while pre-trained VLMs offer robust multi-task scene understanding through semantic prompting alone, domain-specific fine-tuning is more critical for action-level tasks such as trajectory planning and decision-making. Moreover, directly fine-tuning the VLM backbone on AD-specific datasets can lead to catastrophic forgetting, degrading general reasoning capabilities on broader VQA benchmarks. This finding suggests a targeted adaptation strategy where the general intelligence of the VLM backbone is preserved (e.g., by freezing it), and domain-specific knowledge is primarily transferred to and refined within action-oriented components. This approach aims to maximize the benefits of pre-trained models while mitigating the risks of over-specialization.

The framework's competitive and state-of-the-art performance on multiple simulation and real-world benchmarks, under both open-loop and closed-loop settings, validates its feasibility and effectiveness. By unifying reasoning and action within an asynchronous VLA model, AutoMoT offers a more coherent and integrated approach to autonomous driving compared to modular or tightly coupled synchronous systems.

From a broader perspective, this work prompts a rethinking of VLM adaptation strategies in complex embodied AI domains. The insights into balancing general intelligence with domain-specific mastery can inform the design of future large multimodal models for other real-time robotic applications. While deploying VLA-scale models remains resource-intensive for current onboard computational platforms, the authors anticipate that rapid advancements in hardware and model acceleration techniques (e.g., quantization, pruning, efficient caching) could make AutoMoT's onboard deployment increasingly practical.
