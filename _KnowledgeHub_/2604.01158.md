# SMASH: Mastering Scalable Whole-Body Skills for Humanoid Ping-Pong with Egocentric Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from The University of Hong Kong and Kinetix AI developed SMASH, a humanoid robot system capable of playing table tennis using only onboard egocentric vision. The system integrates scalable whole-body skill learning, a task-aligned policy, and a robust perception system, allowing the robot to perform diverse strikes like smashes and crouching shots in real-world settings.

## Problem

- Humanoid table tennis systems previously relied on external sensing (e.g., high-speed cameras or motion-capture) for ball tracking and robot localization.
- Existing approaches often struggled with agile whole-body coordination, either by decoupling movements or using a limited, predefined set of motions.
- Acquiring a sufficiently diverse and comprehensive set of human motion-capture data for whole-body strike motions across the entire reachable workspace is difficult and costly.

## Method

- A ==dual-modality egocentric onboard perception== system was developed for real-time ball observation and robot self-localization using head-mounted stereo cameras and ==AprilTag detection==.
- A conditional autoregressive ==Motion-VAE== augmented sparse human motion-capture data, creating a diverse and scalable library of whole-body strike motions.
- A ==reinforcement learning-based whole-body control policy== was trained using ==task-oriented motion matching==, optimizing for table tennis objectives while being regularized by motion priors from the augmented library.

## Results

- **SMASH** achieved the first outdoor consecutive humanoid table-tennis striking using only onboard egocentric vision, successfully executing diverse whole-body behaviors including smashes and crouching shots.
- The Motion-VAE data augmentation significantly expanded strike-space coverage and improved policy success rates compared to using only original motion-capture data.
- The egocentric perception system enabled a **93.7%** contact rate and **59.7%** successful returns in a continuous 50-minute hardware experiment involving 642 randomized ball launches, demonstrating tolerance to real-world sensing uncertainty.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Augmenting sparse human demonstrations with a generative Motion-VAE effectively scales motion data, improving policy performance and task success by providing diverse and physically plausible whole-body strike patterns.
> - Integrating task-level rewards with motion priors through nearest motion matching enables the learning of both effective and natural whole-body coordination for complex, dynamic tasks.
> - A robust egocentric perception pipeline, including adaptive state estimation and dynamic collision handling, is crucial for enabling autonomous humanoid operation in unpredictable, real-world environments without external sensing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is actively progressing from fundamental locomotion tasks to more complex, agile whole-body behaviors, especially in dynamic interaction scenarios like sports. Humanoid table tennis serves as a challenging benchmark due to its requirements for rapid contact transitions, dynamic balance recovery, tight upper- and lower-body coordination, and extremely limited reaction times.

Prior humanoid table tennis systems, such as HITTER and PACE, have relied on hierarchical planning with upper-body-centered imitation or end-to-end reinforcement learning. A common limitation has been their dependence on external sensing infrastructure (e.g., high-speed cameras or motion-capture systems) for ball tracking and robot localization. Furthermore, these systems often struggle to achieve truly agile whole-body coordination, either by decoupling upper and lower body movements or utilizing only a small, predefined set of motions. The acquisition of diverse, task-aligned strike motions for learning precise yet natural whole-body behaviors also presents a significant challenge.

This work, SMASH, addresses these limitations by integrating scalable whole-body skill learning with onboard egocentric perception. It aims to develop a system that can perform complex table tennis maneuvers, including diverse strike motions like smashes and crouching shots, using only internal sensing. By eliminating the need for external cameras, SMASH positions itself as a step towards enabling humanoid robots to operate autonomously and interact naturally in dynamic, unstructured environments, moving beyond laboratory settings. It differentiates itself by combining full-body strike tracking from human motion with task-driven reinforcement learning, in contrast to purely hierarchical or end-to-end approaches, to achieve more human-like and diverse whole-body coordination.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for the SMASH research are to overcome the core challenges currently limiting humanoid table tennis systems, thereby advancing humanoid capabilities in dynamic interaction tasks. These include:

- **Enabling Egocentric Onboard Perception:** A key motivation is to eliminate the reliance on external sensing for humanoid table tennis. This requires developing a low-latency and robust onboard egocentric perception pipeline that can accurately estimate ball state and robot pose under fast robot motion, despite the challenges posed by small, fast, and easily blurred ping-pong balls, ego-motion-induced instability, and a limited field of view from onboard cameras. The goal is to achieve continuous, real-time ball observation and robot localization using only internal cameras, making outdoor deployment feasible.

- **Achieving Agile Whole-Body Coordination:** Current systems often employ decoupled upper- and lower-body behaviors or a restricted set of pre-defined motions, which limits the robot's agility and expressiveness. A central objective is to develop a task-aligned whole-body control framework that learns tightly coordinated, physically natural, and task-effective striking behaviors. This involves moving beyond simple forehand/backhand strokes to encompass a diverse repertoire of motions, such as explosive whole-body smashes and low crouching shots, across a wide hitting workspace.

- **Developing Scalable Motion Generation:** Acquiring a sufficiently diverse and comprehensive set of human demonstrations (motion-capture data) for whole-body strike motions across the entire reachable hitting workspace is inherently difficult and costly. The motivation here is to introduce a scalable motion generation and matching framework. This involves augmenting sparse and unevenly distributed motion-capture demonstrations with a generative model (Motion-VAE) to create a diverse motion library that broadly covers the hitting workspace. This expanded dataset aims to improve task-aligned motion priors, enhance motion matching, and ultimately lead to more natural, robust, and efficient policy learning.

In summary, the research is motivated by the desire to enable humanoid robots to perform dynamic, human-like tasks like table tennis with full autonomy, agility, and diverse skills, by addressing the fundamental perception and control challenges associated with whole-body coordination and data scarcity in real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SMASH system is designed as a modular pipeline integrating scalable motion generation, task-aligned policy learning, and egocentric onboard perception.

#### 3.1. Overall System Architecture

SMASH connects these three core components:
1. **Data Module:** Motion-capture demonstrations are augmented with a Motion-VAE to build a comprehensive strike-motion dataset covering the reachable hitting workspace.
2. **Policy Module:** A whole-body control policy is trained using reinforcement learning, where task commands are integrated with motion priors through a nearest motion matching scheme. This allows for the selection and execution of appropriate strike behaviors.
3. **Deployment Module:** During real-world operation, the egocentric onboard perception system provides real-time estimates of ball and robot states. These estimates are fed into a planner and the motion matching module to generate closed-loop whole-body actions.

#### 3.2. Learning Scalable Whole-Body Skills

A central challenge is the scarcity of diverse motion-capture (mocap) data for whole-body strikes. The methodology addresses this through:

- **Task-aligned Strike Motion Generation:**
 - **Data Collection and Retargeting:** An initial dataset of 400 human mocap strike demonstrations (1.08s clips around strike instant) is collected and retargeted to the Unitree G1 robot kinematics. Each motion is paired with a strike target feature (relative racket position at strike).
 - **Motion-VAE Augmentation:** A conditional autoregressive Motion-VAE is trained on these strike-centered motion clips.
 - **Objective:** The VAE aims to synthesize new strike motions that preserve human-like coordination and timing while expanding workspace coverage. The loss function includes:
 - `L_VAE`: Combines multi-step motion reconstruction and latent regularization (KL divergence with cyclical annealing).
 - `L_phase`: Ensures preservation of the strike-cycle structure using a cyclic phase representation.
 - `L_smooth`: Imposes temporal smoothness at generation boundaries.
 - `L_foot`: Penalizes physically implausible foot ground penetration.
 - **Tracker-based Filtering:** Generated motions are filtered for physical executability. Each synthesized motion is fed to a motion-tracking policy in simulation. Only motions that can be stably tracked are retained. Further filtering ensures strike-event and phase consistency. The final augmented strike library merges these executable generated motions with the original mocap data.

- **Policy: Task-oriented Motion Matching:**
 - **Reinforcement Learning Framework:** The whole-body controller is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) within an asymmetric actor-critic framework. The actor receives noisy, history-stacked observations, while the critic has access to cleaner, privileged information.
 - **Motion Matching Scheme:** Instead of hierarchical control, a simple nearest-neighbor search retrieves a reference motion from the augmented library. The search is conditioned on the desired strike target (`p_rel_hit`), with small random perturbations (`ϵ`) simulating real-world target prediction errors. The matched motion is provided to the policy as a motion command observation.
 - **Reward Structure:** The policy is optimized for task-level table tennis objectives, regularized by the matched motion prior.
 - **Task Reward (`r_task`):** Focused around the strike event, it includes:
 - `r_pos`: Racket position error.
 - `r_ori`: Racket orientation error (alignment of racket face normal with desired strike velocity).
 - `r_vel`: Racket velocity error.
 - `r_succ`: A sparse success bonus.
 - Each term is shaped by a gated exponential reward (`I[τ_t ∈ W_j]`) to concentrate supervision around impact. Wrist joints are excluded from motion-tracking terms to allow fine-grained strike execution.
 - **Robustness and Domain Randomization:** Phase-dependent noise is injected into task commands (larger noise when strike is far, diminishing near contact). Standard domain randomization includes IMU noise, external push perturbations, and friction randomization to bridge the sim-to-real gap.
 - **Adaptive Training Techniques:**
 - **Adaptive Region Sampling:** The target workspace is partitioned, and regions with lower success rates are sampled with higher probability, redirecting training towards underperforming areas.
 - **Adaptive Task Tracking:** The reward scale (`σ_j`) for task tracking is adaptively updated based on running tracking errors, progressively sharpening strike constraints as the policy improves.

#### 3.3. Egocentric Onboard Perception System

The system employs a dual-modality perception framework for real-time ball observation and robot localization.

- **Coordinate Frame Definitions:** Five reference frames are defined (World (W), Table (T), Origin (O), Camera (C1), Camera (C2)) with pre-calibrated transformations.
- **MoCap-Based Sensing Pipeline (for controlled experiments):** A professional optical MoCap system at 120 Hz tracks a retro-reflective coated ball and a rigid-body tracker on the robot torso, providing high-accuracy ground truth for validation.
- **Ego-View Camera Sensing Pipeline (for in-the-wild deployment):**
 - **Hardware:** A head-mounted ZED X stereo camera (60 Hz) for ball detection, and a downward-tilted ZED X Mini (120 Hz) for robot localization.
 - **Ball Detection and 3D Localization:** A three-stage pipeline: 1) Coarse detection via YOLO, 2) Sub-pixel refinement via HSV segmentation, 3) Stereo triangulation for 3D position (`p_C1_ball`).
 - **Robot Self-Localization via AprilTag:** The ZED X Mini uses AprilTag detection on the table to estimate `C2_T_T` via RANSAC-PnP, maintaining 120 Hz performance with a dynamically updated region of interest (ROI).
 - Torso pose (`O_T_Torso`) and ball position in table frame (`T_p_ball`) are derived from these.
- **Ball State Estimation: Adaptive Extended Kalman Filter (AEKF):**
 - **State Space:** Position and velocity in Table coordinates (`x = [p_x, p_y, p_z, v_x, v_y, v_z]^T`).
 - **Physics-Based Prediction Model:** Uses a quadratic aerodynamic drag model and applies table collision handling with horizontal (`C_h`) and vertical (`C_v`) restitution coefficients when the ball interacts with the table.
 - **Adaptive Observation Noise:** The measurement noise covariance (`R(d_k)`) is isotropic and grows with camera-ball distance (`d_k`), reflecting lower confidence at longer ranges.
 - **Initialization and Resets:** Position is initialized from the first measurement; velocity from a fixed prior. The filter resets if no observation is received for too long or if a return is detected.
- **Ball Trajectory Prediction:** Based on the current filtered ball state, the future trajectory is propagated using the same flight model. Candidate arrival times (`τ`) are determined through an initial search and online refinement, selecting targets within an admissible strike volume.
- **Racket Strike Planning:** Given predicted impact position (`p_hit`) and incoming velocity (`v_in`), the required outgoing ball velocity (`v_out`) for a target landing position (`p_target`) is computed using a linearized drag model. A frictionless normal collision model then determines the required racket velocity (`V_r`) and surface normal (`ˆn`).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of SMASH encompassed simulation and real-world experiments, focusing on policy performance, the impact of motion-VAE data scaling, and the efficacy of the egocentric perception system.

#### 4.1. Policy Performance Evaluation

- **Baseline Comparisons (Simulation):** SMASH achieved a favorable trade-off between task execution and motion quality. Compared to a task-only PPO baseline, SMASH demonstrated a higher success rate and reduced position tracking error, indicating the benefit of motion guidance. Against HITTER (a state-of-the-art system that imitates upper-body swings), SMASH achieved comparable task success while significantly improving whole-body motion tracking accuracy and reducing applied torque, due to its full-body coordination approach. A pure motion-imitation baseline (Mimic) had low motion tracking and smoothness errors but was insufficient for solving the table tennis task, underscoring the necessity of task-driven optimization.
- **Impact of Motion-VAE Data Scaling:** The Motion-VAE-generated motions significantly expanded the strike-space coverage compared to the original mocap dataset, providing a broader distribution of strike targets. Quantitative ablations showed that enlarging the dataset with VAE-generated motions substantially improved policy performance and task success rates. For instance, datasets augmented with 5000 generated clips (e.g., 400-5k-gen) consistently outperformed policies trained on only mocap clips (e.g., 64, 200, or 400 clips). The tracker-based filtering stage for generated motions was critical; without it, motion tracking accuracy degraded, indicating that motions without this dynamic compatibility filter were harder for the robot to execute.
- **Detailed Training Techniques:** Adaptive region sampling was found to moderately improve success rates, particularly for underperforming regions like high strike targets, by reallocating training samples. Adaptive tracking sigma was crucial, with its removal leading to a substantial reduction in success rate and degraded task-tracking performance, as overly strict tracking constraints early in training hindered effective task learning.
- **Hardware Performance (MoCap-based Perception):** On real hardware with MoCap-based perception (near ground truth), the policy successfully executed diverse whole-body behaviors, including smashes, low-crouching saves, and large-range lateral movements with coordinated full-body motion. The system demonstrated the ability to accommodate substantially different motion styles (e.g., crouching vs. regular standing returns) by implicitly selecting motion patterns based on target ball position. This confirmed the transferability and expressiveness of the motion-matching mechanism.

#### 4.2. Analysis of the Egocentric Perception System

- **Qualitative Overview:** Real-time perception snapshots illustrated the sequential process: YOLO-based ball detection for coarse localization, HSV-based refinement for pixel-level accuracy, and subsequent state estimation and trajectory prediction by the AEKF. Predictions progressively converged and refined the strike point as the ball approached.
- **Evaluations Against Various Perception Settings:** A comparison across simulation (virtual ball), hardware (virtual ball), hardware (MoCap), and hardware (ego camera) settings revealed a sim-to-real gap in execution accuracy. While MoCap-based perception yielded higher return rates and lower execution errors than ego-view, the ego-view system still achieved a high strike rate (19/20) and a non-trivial return rate (12/20). This indicated that the onboard perception pipeline provided sufficient accuracy for closed-loop striking in real-world conditions and that the policy's training with task perturbations enabled tolerance to sensing uncertainty. Motion tracking metrics remained relatively stable across settings, suggesting perception noise primarily impacted strike accuracy rather than whole-body motion stability.
- **Egocentric Perception Noise:** Analysis of real-world error distributions (position, velocity, temporal estimation) showed a consistent convergent trend. As the time to strike decreased, estimation errors progressively diminished in both mean and standard deviation, demonstrating increasing precision and certainty as the ball neared the robot.
- **Module Ablation Study:**
 - The full perception pipeline achieved the best overall performance.
 - Removing the YOLO detector severely degraded performance, especially with visual distractors, highlighting its role in robust candidate generation.
 - Disabling HSV refinement led to reduced strike and return rates and increased estimation errors, underscoring the need for pixel-level accuracy.
 - Removing collision handling in the AEKF caused the most significant degradation, with large increases in position and velocity errors, confirming the necessity of modeling bounce discontinuities.
 - Disabling adaptive observation noise reduced accuracy and task success, validating the distance-aware uncertainty model.
 - Poor filter initialization (zero initialization) increased estimation errors.
 - Using the head-mounted ZED X for pose estimation instead of the dedicated downward-facing ZED X Mini resulted in reduced return rates and stability due to frequent loss of AprilTag visibility, validating the dual-camera design for stable, high-frequency localization.
- **Robustness Analysis (Long-term Experiment):** A continuous 50-minute hardware experiment involving 642 randomized ball launches yielded a 93.7% total contact rate (59.7% successful returns). An asymmetry was observed, with forehand strikes significantly outperforming backhand strikes in return rate (66.7% vs. 38.9%), attributed to backhand motions more frequently occluding the camera's field of view and introducing control instability from aggressive posture adjustments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SMASH represents an advancement in humanoid robotics by demonstrating capabilities that were previously limited or impossible. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Pioneering Outdoor Autonomous Humanoid Interaction:** SMASH achieves the first outdoor consecutive humanoid table-tennis striking using only onboard egocentric vision, eliminating reliance on external cameras or motion-capture systems. This significantly enhances the autonomy and deployability of humanoid robots in dynamic, unstructured environments, moving them closer to real-world applications beyond laboratory settings.
- **Advanced Whole-Body Skill Execution:** The system realizes highly dynamic and diverse whole-body behaviors, including the first whole-body smash on a humanoid robot, low crouching shots, and large coordinated lateral movements. This expands the repertoire of complex, agile motor skills achievable by humanoids, showcasing more human-like coordination than prior systems.
- **Scalable and Natural Motion Learning:** By augmenting sparse human motion-capture data with a generative motion model (Motion-VAE), SMASH addresses the challenge of data scarcity and uneven workspace coverage. This scalable approach to motion prior generation improves the naturalness, robustness, and diversity of learned striking behaviors across a wide hitting workspace, impacting how complex motor skills can be taught to robots.
- **Integrated Perception-Action Pipeline:** The robust integration of real-time egocentric perception with task-aligned whole-body control demonstrates a complete, closed-loop system for dynamic object interaction. This unified pipeline provides a template for developing similar capabilities in other dynamic humanoid tasks.
- **Foundational for Open-World Humanoid Interaction:** The ability to perceive, plan, and execute complex, dynamic actions using only onboard sensing in an outdoor environment is a significant step towards enabling humanoid robots to perform sophisticated interactions in open-world scenarios. This work contributes to the broader goal of developing general-purpose humanoid robots capable of operating in human environments.

Despite limitations such as the restricted field of view of current egocentric perception systems (highlighting the future need for active perception) and the absence of ball spin modeling, SMASH establishes new benchmarks for humanoid capabilities in sports and dynamic object interaction, indicating a path forward for increasingly autonomous and agile humanoid robots.
