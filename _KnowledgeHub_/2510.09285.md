# Spotlight on Token Perception for Multimodal Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Shanghai AI Laboratory and other institutions developed Visually-Perceptive Policy Optimization (VPPO), a reinforcement learning algorithm that enhances Large Vision-Language Models' multimodal reasoning by explicitly integrating visual perception into the policy update. VPPO achieved state-of-the-art performance, improving average accuracy by 2.2 percentage points for 7B models and 1.1 percentage points for 32B models across eight challenging multimodal reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) frameworks for Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) often overlook the critical role of visual perception during the optimization process.
- Current methods use a uniform, coarse learning signal, broadcasting a single reward to all tokens and trajectories, which dilutes the learning process and fails to prioritize visually-grounded reasoning.
- LVLMs may develop reasoning abilities that rely on linguistic priors or spurious correlations rather than robust, genuinely multimodal visual integration.

## Method

- Introduced ==Visually-Perceptive Policy== Optimization (**VPPO**), a novel policy gradient algorithm that quantifies a token's visual dependency using KL divergence between policy outputs conditioned on original and perturbed images.
- Implemented Token-level Gradient Filtering (TGF) by creating a binary mask to update gradients only for the top-k% most visually dependent tokens (empirically k=0.4).
- Applied Trajectory-level Advantage Shaping (TAS) to rescale the advantage for each trajectory based on its average visual dependency, adaptively amplifying updates for highly visually-grounded reasoning paths.

## Results

- VPPO achieved state-of-the-art average accuracy: **57.5%** for **7B** parameter models (2.2 percentage points over PAPO-D) and **64.6%** for **32B** models (1.1 percentage points over DAPO) across eight multimodal reasoning benchmarks.
- Demonstrated consistent improvements over GRPO and DAPO baselines, with a 4.4 percentage point gain over GRPO for **7B** models and 2.0 percentage points for **32B** models.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both Token-level Gradient Filtering (TGF) and Trajectory-level Advantage Shaping (TAS) individually contribute to performance gains, with their combination yielding optimal results, and performance peaked with a **40%** gradient filtering ratio.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The visual dependency of tokens within a reasoning trajectory is sparsely distributed, with only a minority of tokens exhibiting high visual dependence.
> - Different reasoning trajectories show heterogeneous overall visual grounding, indicating that only a subset of successful paths are genuinely perception-driven.
> - Targeted modulation of the learning signal at both token and trajectory levels, based on visual dependency, constructs a lower-variance policy gradient estimator, leading to more stable and efficient learning.
