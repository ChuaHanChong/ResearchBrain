# Prompt Tuning Decision Transformers with Structured and Scalable Bandits

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from King AI Labs, Microsoft Gaming, and Örebro University developed a structured bandit-based method to optimize trajectory prompt selection for the Prompting Decision Transformer (PDT), enhancing performance and generalization on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution tasks while maintaining computational efficiency. The approach leverages the pre-trained PDT as a feature extractor, provides theoretical regret bounds, and demonstrates robust improvements over uniform sampling and other baselines.

## Problem

- The Prompting Decision Transformer (PDT) uses a uniform sampling strategy for trajectory prompts from expert demonstrations, which is inefficient and disregards the varying informativeness of prompt segments.
- Suboptimal prompt selection leads to reduced task differentiation and degraded performance, especially when adapting to new tasks.
- Prior prompt-tuning methods often face limitations such as combinatorial scaling with prompt size or state-action space dimensionality, or are unsuitable for discrete, structured trajectory prompts.

## Method

- A ==structured bandit architecture== is introduced that factorizes the prompt selection problem across individual prompt segments, with J independent reward models reducing complexity from combinatorial to linear in prompt size.
- The pre-trained PDT model is utilized as a fixed-size feature extractor for trajectory segments, making the bandit's reward models efficient in high-dimensional (e.g., pixel-based) state-action spaces.
- At each online rollout, the bandit selects an optimal prompt by predicting the performance of each segment at each position, guided by an exploration strategy (e.g., UCB, Thompson Sampling), and updates all reward models based on the observed return.

## Results

- The bandit-based prompt-tuning consistently improved PDT performance by 10-**20%** on in-distribution tasks (MuJoCo, Meta-world) and enhanced generalization on out-of-distribution tasks, often surpassing CQL baselines.
- Achieved near-optimal performance with as little as **10%** expert demonstrations when prompts were sampled from mixed-quality datasets, demonstrating robustness to data quality.
- Provided theoretical regret bounds of O(√(K log |P|) + Kε), with empirical validation showing efficient exploration, outperforming traditional baselines, and demonstrating low interaction terms across synthetic tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factorizing the prompt selection problem into independent segment-level decisions with a structured bandit enables scalable and efficient optimization of discrete trajectory prompts.
> - The pre-trained Transformer within PDT can serve as a powerful and compact feature extractor, providing robust latent representations of trajectory segments for bandit reward models.
> - Optimizing context selection at inference time significantly boosts a pre-trained model's performance and generalization without requiring costly fine-tuning or weight updates to the Transformer backbone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The ability to leverage large offline datasets for training generalizable foundation models is a significant area of research in artificial intelligence. In Reinforcement Learning (RL), the **Decision Transformer (DT)** marked a pivotal advancement by reframing offline RL as a sequence modeling problem, enabling the application of powerful Transformer architectures. This approach mitigates common challenges in offline RL, such as instabilities of temporal-difference learning and distribution shifts, by modeling trajectories as sequences of return-to-go, states, and actions.

Building upon DT, the **Prompting Decision Transformer (PDT)** extended this paradigm to multi-task and few-shot offline RL settings. Analogous to prompting in Large Language Models (LLMs), PDT employs stochastic trajectory prompts as task-specific context. These prompts, prepended to the input sequence, allow the model to adapt its behavior at inference time to different tasks without requiring further weight updates to the Transformer backbone. This makes PDT particularly attractive for efficient and robust adaptation in multi-task scenarios.

However, a noted limitation of the original PDT is its uniform sampling strategy for trajectory prompts from expert demonstrations. This approach does not account for the informativeness of individual prompt segments, potentially leading to suboptimal task differentiation and performance degradation. Prior works have attempted to address this. For example, Yuan et al. (2024) explored goal-conditioning and hindsight relabeling, while Hu et al. (2023, 2024) proposed generative methods for prompt candidates. These approaches often suffer from limitations such as inapplicability in discrete settings, lack of adherence to causal relationships, or poor scaling with prompt size and state-action space dimensionality due to treating prompts as unstructured inputs.

In the broader context of prompt tuning, particularly in LLMs, methods like InstructZero (Chen et al., 2024) and INSTINCT (Lin et al., 2023) use Bayesian optimization or neural bandits to optimize continuous soft prompt vectors. Shi et al. (2024a) applied Multi-Armed Bandits (MABs) to learn optimal prompts within a fixed budget for LLMs. While these works focus on continuous optimization and reconstruction, the current paper specifically addresses prompt tuning directly within the discrete, structured trajectory prompt space of PDT. Other multi-task DT methods, such as hierarchical prompting (Wang et al., 2024a) or Hyper-Decision Transformer (Xu et al., 2023), often rely on large quantities of demonstration data or involve updating the Transformer backbone. This work distinguishes itself by focusing on optimizing prompt selection from a limited set of expert demonstrations at test time, without altering the pre-trained model weights, and specifically designing a bandit architecture for the structured prompt space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an identified inefficiency in the Prompting Decision Transformer (PDT): its reliance on uniform sampling of trajectory prompts from expert demonstrations. This uniform sampling strategy disregards the varying informativeness of different prompt segments, which can lead to suboptimal task differentiation and degraded performance, even within fully observable Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). The authors hypothesize that selecting more informative prompts can significantly enhance PDT's ability to adapt to diverse tasks.

The key objectives of this work are to:

1. **Develop a bandit-based prompt-tuning method:** Introduce an approach that learns to construct optimal trajectory prompts from available demonstration data at inference time, thereby moving beyond PDT's uninformed sampling.
2. **Achieve scalable prompt selection:** Design a structured bandit architecture that operates efficiently in the trajectory prompt space, addressing the combinatorial scaling issues associated with naive bandit implementations where each possible prompt combination would be an arm. The goal is to reduce this complexity from combinatorial to linear in prompt size (number of segments).
3. **Leverage the pre-trained PDT as a feature extractor:** Demonstrate that the pre-trained PDT model itself can serve as a powerful and compact feature extractor for the bandit's reward models. This objective aims to enable efficient reward modeling across various environments, especially high-dimensional ones (e.g., pixel-based), by providing fixed-size latent representations of prompt segments.
4. **Enhance downstream task performance without weight updates:** Optimize prompt selection to boost the performance of the underlying PDT model on downstream tasks, critically, without requiring costly weight updates or fine-tuning of the Transformer backbone.
5. **Provide theoretical guarantees:** Establish regret bounds for the proposed bandit architecture, grounding the empirical performance in a theoretical framework.
6. **Empirically demonstrate efficacy:** Show through experimentation that the proposed method consistently improves performance across a wide range of tasks, including high-dimensional environments and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, outperforming existing prompt-tuning baselines.

In essence, the research aims to bridge the gap between PDT pre-training and adaptation by enabling a robust, computationally efficient, and performance-enhancing mechanism for context selection at inference time.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a bandit-based prompt-tuning method for the Prompting Decision Transformer (PDT) at inference time. This approach aims to identify optimal trajectory prompts from a small set of task-specific demonstrations, maximizing the performance of a pre-trained PDT model without updating its weights.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The work considers the offline multi-task RL problem, where a PDT model `π*(x; θ)` is pre-trained on a dataset `D` comprising trajectories from multiple training tasks. The objective is to enhance the model's performance on a target task `T_i` (either in-distribution or out-of-distribution) by selecting an optimal stochastic trajectory prompt `ρ` from a set of expert demonstrations `P_i` for that task. A simulator for `M_i` is assumed available for online evaluation.

#### 3.2. Bandit-based Prompt Tuning

The core idea is to use a Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) framework to optimize prompt selection. A naive approach, treating each unique prompt (a combination of J segments) as a separate arm, would suffer from combinatorial scaling with the number of possible segments and the prompt length J. This would also ignore similarities between prompts.

To overcome these limitations, the authors propose a **structured bandit architecture**:

- **Factorization of Search Space:** Instead of a single bandit over the entire prompt space, the problem is factorized across prompt segments. The architecture features J independent "arms," one for each segment position `j ∈ {1..., J}` within the stochastic trajectory prompt `ρ = (˜τ_1..., ˜τ_J)`.
- **Independent Reward Models:** Each arm `j` maintains an independent reward model `ϕ_j`, which predicts the performance (return) of the underlying PDT when a specific segment `˜τ` is placed at position `j` in the prompt. This decomposition reduces the complexity of the search space from combinatorial to linear in J.
- **Prompt Selection Process:**
 - At each round `k`, for every available segment `˜τ` in the demonstration set `P_i`, each reward model `ϕ_j` predicts a reward `ˆy_j = ϕ_j(˜τ)` for placing `˜τ` at position `j`.
 - This generates a prediction matrix `Y ∈ R^(J x |P_i|)`.
 - The J segments with the highest predicted performance are selected by computing `arg max` over the segment dimension of `Y` for each position `j`, guided by an exploration strategy (e.g., ϵ-greedy, Upper Confidence Bounds (UCB), or Thompson Sampling (TS)). This yields the chosen prompt `ρ_k = (˜τ_k_1..., ˜τ_k_J)`.
- **Reward and Update:** The selected prompt `ρ_k` is used for the `k`-th online rollout of the PDT `π*(x_k; θ)` in the environment `M_i`. The resulting total return `G_k_i` serves as the reward for the bandit. All J reward models `ϕ_j` are then independently updated using the collected `⟨˜τ_k_j, G_k_i⟩` pairs. This process is iterated over a fixed number of bandit steps.

#### 3.3. Regret Analysis

The paper provides a theoretical analysis of the proposed bandit architecture, assuming that the true reward function `G(ρ)` for a prompt `ρ = (˜τ_1..., ˜τ_J)` can be approximated as the mean of J independent reward models `ϕ_j(˜τ_j)`, plus a bounded interaction term `h(.) ≤ ε`.

- **Assumption Justification:** The modular structure of PDT prompts, where segments correspond to localized behaviors, supports the idea that task identification often relies on a few informative state-action pairs within segments, minimizing inter-segment interactions. Additionally, PDT pre-training samples segments independently, not encouraging reliance on global interactions or order.
- **Theoretical Bound (Theorem 4.1):** The cumulative regret after K rounds for the proposed method is bounded as `Regret(K) <= (1/J) * Σ(Regret_j(K)) + 2Kε`, where `Regret_j(K)` is the slot-specific regret for each independent reward model.
- **Corollary 4.2:** If each slot-specific reward model `ϕ_j` is learned using a standard contextual bandit algorithm with regret `O(√(K log |P|))`, then the total regret for the structured bandit is `O(√(K log |P|) + Kε)`. This indicates that the sublinear regret bound of standard algorithms is preserved, with an additional linear term dependent on the approximation error `ε`.

#### 3.4. Arm Features

To handle high-dimensional state and action spaces (e.g., pixel observations), directly using unencoded trajectory segments `˜τ` as input to `ϕ_j` becomes computationally expensive. The authors propose leveraging the pre-trained PDT model itself as a feature extractor `Ψ: ˜τ → R^d`. The latent representation of prompt tokens from the PDT is used as a fixed-size embedding for each segment. This mitigates the scaling issue, as the input size to `ϕ_j` becomes `d` (embedding dimension) instead of `H × (|S| + |A| + 1)`, and aligns with the inductive biases of the pre-trained Transformer.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

The method is evaluated on various environments: MuJoCo Half Cheetah, MuJoCo Ant, Meta-world Pick-place, and a custom Sparse 2D Point environment. Datasets are typically derived from existing benchmarks or generated using PPO for the 2D environment. Baselines include: optimal policy oracle (CQL), vanilla Decision Transformer (DT), standard PDT (uniform sampling), Gaussian perturbation-based hill-climbing, and ZORankSGD (Hu et al., 2023). Experiments involve 250 online rollouts, reporting mean return from the final 10 rollouts, averaged over multiple seeds and tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that the proposed bandit-based prompt-tuning method consistently enhances the performance of a pre-trained PDT model across various tasks and scenarios.

#### 4.1. Performance Improvements on In-Distribution Tasks

- **Consistent Gains:** The bandit-based prompt-tuning method reliably and substantially improves the inference-time performance of the frozen PDT backbone across all tested MuJoCo and Meta-world environments. For instance, on MuJoCo Ant, the method (e.g., ϵ-greedy or TS) achieves returns of 815.17 to 835.38 for J=1, H=5, surpassing the CQL oracle (760.54) and significantly outperforming vanilla PDT (694.43).
- **Scaling with Prompt Size:** The performance gains provided by the bandit method are observed to be greater than those achieved by simply increasing prompt size (J and H) in standard PDT, underscoring the advantage of optimized prompt selection.
- **Feature Extraction Efficacy:** When using Transformer-encoded trajectory segments (denoted by Ψ), the method achieves performance comparable to or even better than variants using unencoded inputs. This indicates that the PDT model provides robust and compact representations suitable for bandit-based prompt tuning, making the approach scalable to high-dimensional state and action spaces.
- **Superiority over Baselines:** Hill-climbing and ZORankSGD baselines also show improvements over vanilla PDT but are less consistent and generally achieve weaker results compared to the bandit-based methods. This is attributed to their reliance on iterative, noise-driven perturbations.

#### 4.2. Performance on Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Tasks

- **Enhanced Generalization:** The bandit-based method significantly improves PDT’s generalization capability on OOD tasks across all evaluated environments. For example, on OOD MuJoCo Half Cheetah (J=1, H=5), TS (Ψ) achieves -38.56, substantially better than vanilla PDT's -64.78 and even outperforming the CQL oracle's -23.8 (given the negative reward structure). These improvements often bring OOD performance levels close to in-distribution results, provided the underlying PDT model has sufficient generalization capacity.
- **Baseline Instability in OOD:** Hill-climbing and ZORankSGD demonstrate reduced robustness in OOD settings, showing considerably smaller performance gains or even degradation compared to the proposed method, particularly in MuJoCo Half Cheetah and MuJoCo Ant.
- **Ineffectiveness of Fine-tuning:** Fine-tuning the PDT backbone for 250 epochs on target task data did not reliably improve performance and sometimes led to degradation. This is hypothesized to be due to the large Transformer size, lack of multi-task regularization, and less efficient full-model updates.

#### 4.3. Prompt Space Exploration Analysis (Sparse 2D Point)

- **Qualitative Exploration:** In the Sparse 2D Point environment, a qualitative analysis of prompt selection reveals distinct exploration strategies:
 - **Standard PDT:** Samples prompts uniformly, without refinement.
 - **Hill-climbing:** Gradually discovers high-performing prompts in the local neighborhood of an initial sample via Gaussian noise.
 - **ZORankSGD:** Moves prompts towards the task goal but can overshoot and generate out-of-distribution prompts due to its unconstrained generative nature.
 - **Bandit Methods (e.g., ϵ-greedy):** Initially sample prompts broadly (both low and high-performing), then leverage accumulated reward estimates to focus exploration on high-reward regions, typically selecting segments near the task goal.
- **Quantitative Performance:** The bandit methods achieve near-optimal returns in this environment, with Thompson Sampling (TS) performing slightly better than UCB or ϵ-greedy. This highlights the effectiveness of learned exploration. The difference between using Transformer-encoded prompts (Ψ) and unencoded prompts was negligible, likely due to the simplicity of the 2D environment.

#### 4.4. Bandit Regret Analysis (Synthetic Task)

- **Efficient Exploration:** On a synthetic task designed to mimic prompt tuning challenges, the structured bandit method matches the performance of standard "flat" MAB algorithms on small problems and consistently outperforms them as the problem size (combinatorial space) increases. This demonstrates more efficient exploration by leveraging the problem's inherent structure.
- **Bounded Interaction Term:** The estimated interaction term `h` remains low and increases only marginally with problem size, empirically supporting the bounded interaction assumption central to Theorem 4.1.

#### 4.5. Prompt Quality and Wall-clock Inference Time (Appendix D.2, F)

- **Robustness to Data Quality:** The bandit-based prompt optimization significantly enhances PDT's robustness to the quality of demonstration data. When prompts are sampled from mixed datasets (combining expert and novice demonstrations), the method achieves near-optimal performance with as little as 10% expert demonstrations, whereas standard PDT performance deteriorates proportionally to the percentage of non-expert data.
- **Inference Efficiency:** Using PDT-encoded prompt representations (Ψ) leads to lower wall-clock inference times, especially for larger prompts. This is because the encoded representation has a fixed dimension, making reward model updates more efficient compared to processing unencoded, higher-dimensional raw segments.

In summary, the main findings confirm that the proposed structured bandit approach with PDT-based feature extraction provides a robust, scalable, and highly effective method for prompt tuning in PDT, significantly outperforming existing baselines and extending the capabilities of PDT to more challenging and diverse scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents a notable advancement in the field of offline Reinforcement Learning, specifically in the context of sequence modeling with Decision Transformers. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enhanced Adaptability and Performance for PDT:** The primary impact is the significant improvement in the adaptability and performance of the Prompting Decision Transformer (PDT). By addressing the limitation of uninformed prompt sampling, the method allows PDT to leverage expert demonstrations more effectively, leading to higher returns on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution tasks. This enhances PDT's utility as a foundation model for multi-task and few-shot RL problems.
2. **Increased Robustness and Generalization:** The bandit-based approach demonstrates superior robustness in out-of-distribution scenarios compared to prior prompt-tuning baselines. Furthermore, its ability to achieve near-optimal performance even when relying on a small percentage of expert demonstrations within mixed-quality datasets reduces the stringent requirement for purely expert data, making PDT more practical in real-world settings where obtaining perfect demonstrations can be challenging.
3. **Computational Efficiency and Scalability:** The introduction of a structured bandit architecture, which reduces the prompt selection problem from combinatorial to linear complexity in prompt size, represents a significant step towards scalability. Leveraging the pre-trained PDT as a fixed-size feature extractor further enables the application of this method to high-dimensional environments (e.g., pixel-based observations) without proportional increases in computational cost for the bandit's reward models. This allows for efficient deployment at inference time without costly fine-tuning of the large Transformer backbone.
4. **Theoretical Foundation for Prompt Optimization:** The theoretical establishment of regret bounds for the structured bandit architecture provides a principled understanding of its learning dynamics. The empirical validation of a bounded interaction term `ε` in synthetic tasks supports the design choices, reinforcing the method's mathematical soundness.
5. **Broader Implications for Offline RL and Sequence Modeling:** This research contributes to the broader integration of offline RL and sequence modeling paradigms. By demonstrating effective, data-driven context selection, it opens avenues for similar prompt optimization strategies in other Transformer-based models for complex sequential decision-making tasks, potentially inspiring new designs for how foundation models in RL learn and adapt.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**

- **Combinatorial Search Space Expansion:** A key limitation acknowledged by the authors is that the search space still expands combinatorially as the number of available demonstrations increases, which can render online exploration impractical for very large demonstration datasets. Future work could focus on learning a sampler to pre-select high-potential segments or clustering similar prompts to reduce redundancy.
- **Offline RL Extrapolation Challenges:** While bandit-based tuning enhances OOD performance, pure offline RL inherently struggles with extrapolation to entirely novel scenarios. The authors suggest incorporating meta-learning techniques, such as In-Context RL, into PDT pre-training as a promising extension to improve generalization capabilities further.

In conclusion, this work offers a valuable and practical method for optimizing the contextual input of Decision Transformers, contributing to the development of more efficient, robust, and generalizable agents in offline multi-task reinforcement learning.
