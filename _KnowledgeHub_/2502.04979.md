# Prompt Tuning Decision Transformers with Structured and Scalable Bandits

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from King AI Labs, Microsoft Gaming, and Örebro University developed a structured bandit-based method to optimize trajectory prompt selection for the Prompting Decision Transformer (PDT), enhancing performance and generalization on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution tasks while maintaining computational efficiency. The approach leverages the pre-trained PDT as a feature extractor, provides theoretical regret bounds, and demonstrates robust improvements over uniform sampling and other baselines.

## Problem

- The Prompting Decision Transformer (PDT) uses a uniform sampling strategy for trajectory prompts from expert demonstrations, which is inefficient and disregards the varying informativeness of prompt segments.
- Suboptimal prompt selection leads to reduced task differentiation and degraded performance, especially when adapting to new tasks.
- Prior prompt-tuning methods often face limitations such as combinatorial scaling with prompt size or state-action space dimensionality, or are unsuitable for discrete, structured trajectory prompts.

## Method

- A ==structured bandit architecture== is introduced that factorizes the prompt selection problem across individual prompt segments, with J independent reward models reducing complexity from combinatorial to linear in prompt size.
- The pre-trained PDT model is utilized as a fixed-size feature extractor for trajectory segments, making the bandit's reward models efficient in high-dimensional (e.g., pixel-based) state-action spaces.
- At each online rollout, the bandit selects an optimal prompt by predicting the performance of each segment at each position, guided by an exploration strategy (e.g., UCB, Thompson Sampling), and updates all reward models based on the observed return.

## Results

- The bandit-based prompt-tuning consistently improved PDT performance by 10-**20%** on in-distribution tasks (MuJoCo, Meta-world) and enhanced generalization on out-of-distribution tasks, often surpassing CQL baselines.
- Achieved near-optimal performance with as little as **10%** expert demonstrations when prompts were sampled from mixed-quality datasets, demonstrating robustness to data quality.
- Provided theoretical regret bounds of O(√(K log |P|) + Kε), with empirical validation showing efficient exploration, outperforming traditional baselines, and demonstrating low interaction terms across synthetic tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factorizing the prompt selection problem into independent segment-level decisions with a structured bandit enables scalable and efficient optimization of discrete trajectory prompts.
> - The pre-trained Transformer within PDT can serve as a powerful and compact feature extractor, providing robust latent representations of trajectory segments for bandit reward models.
> - Optimizing context selection at inference time significantly boosts a pre-trained model's performance and generalization without requiring costly fine-tuning or weight updates to the Transformer backbone.
