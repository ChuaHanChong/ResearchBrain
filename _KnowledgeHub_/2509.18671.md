# N2M: Bridging Navigation and Manipulation by Learning Pose Preference from Rollout

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST and Vital Robotics developed N2M, a system that learns optimal base pose preferences for mobile manipulators directly from policy rollouts, bridging navigation and manipulation. This approach achieves higher success rates (e.g., 0.55-0.56 vs. 0.09-0.24 for a baseline) with rapid inference (0.07 seconds) and strong generalization across various tasks and unseen environments using limited data.

## Problem

- Traditional base positioning for mobile manipulators relies on geometric reachability, which is insufficient for modern learning-based manipulation policies sensitive to initial observation distributions.
- Existing policy-aware methods for base positioning suffer from practical limitations, including slow inference speeds and reliance on pre-built scene reconstructions, limiting real-time application and compatibility with diverse policies.
- Precisely defining and learning the optimal base pose is challenging due to the complex interplay of policy, task, environment, and the need to adapt to non-static environments and multi-modal pose preferences with data efficiency.

## Method

- **N2M** introduces a neural network that predicts a multi-modal distribution of preferable initial poses (modeled as a Gaussian Mixture Model) directly from an ego-centric RGB point cloud observation.
- A novel viewpoint augmentation strategy is employed during training, rendering multiple observations from different viewpoints for each successful rollout, enhancing data efficiency and viewpoint robustness.
- The system captures local observations at the navigation end pose, predicts a collision-free pose from the learned distribution, navigates there, and then executes the pre-trained manipulation policy.

## Results

- N2M achieved significantly higher manipulation success rates (e.g., **0.55**-**0.56**) compared to geometric baselines (**0.15**-**0.16**) and a prior policy-aware method, Mobi-π (**0.09**-**0.24**), on tasks like CloseDrawer.
- The method demonstrated strong data efficiency, achieving performance comparable to an Oracle with only 10 successful policy rollouts and surpassing it with 20 rollouts, and showed robust generalization to unseen environments.
- N2M exhibited rapid inference times of **0.07** seconds per inference, enabling real-time adaptation in dynamic environments, in contrast to Mobi-π's inference time of **273.52** seconds per inference.
- In sequential tasks, N2M improved pick success rates to 18/20 and drop success rates to 12/20, outperforming human intuition (12 picks, 5 drops) and geometric reachability (6 picks, 1 drop), significantly enhancing overall task completion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning policy preferences directly from a limited number of successful rollouts, rather than relying on geometric reachability or heuristic policy-awareness, enables mobile manipulators to consistently find optimal base poses for diverse manipulation tasks.
> - A key data augmentation technique, viewpoint augmentation, significantly improves data efficiency and allows the model to generalize robustly to unseen environments and varying initial robot viewpoints using minimal real-world data.
> - Predicting a multi-modal distribution of preferable poses (e.g., using a GMM) from a single ego-centric observation enables the system to capture multiple valid task-execution viewpoints and achieve rapid, real-time inference (0.07 seconds per inference).
