# N2M: Bridging Navigation and Manipulation by Learning Pose Preference from Rollout

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST and Vital Robotics developed N2M, a system that learns optimal base pose preferences for mobile manipulators directly from policy rollouts, bridging navigation and manipulation. This approach achieves higher success rates (e.g., 0.55-0.56 vs. 0.09-0.24 for a baseline) with rapid inference (0.07 seconds) and strong generalization across various tasks and unseen environments using limited data.

## Problem

- Traditional base positioning for mobile manipulators relies on geometric reachability, which is insufficient for modern learning-based manipulation policies sensitive to initial observation distributions.
- Existing policy-aware methods for base positioning suffer from practical limitations, including slow inference speeds and reliance on pre-built scene reconstructions, limiting real-time application and compatibility with diverse policies.
- Precisely defining and learning the optimal base pose is challenging due to the complex interplay of policy, task, environment, and the need to adapt to non-static environments and multi-modal pose preferences with data efficiency.

## Method

- **N2M** introduces a neural network that predicts a multi-modal distribution of preferable initial poses (modeled as a Gaussian Mixture Model) directly from an ego-centric RGB point cloud observation.
- A novel viewpoint augmentation strategy is employed during training, rendering multiple observations from different viewpoints for each successful rollout, enhancing data efficiency and viewpoint robustness.
- The system captures local observations at the navigation end pose, predicts a collision-free pose from the learned distribution, navigates there, and then executes the pre-trained manipulation policy.

## Results

- N2M achieved significantly higher manipulation success rates (e.g., **0.55**-**0.56**) compared to geometric baselines (**0.15**-**0.16**) and a prior policy-aware method, Mobi-π (**0.09**-**0.24**), on tasks like CloseDrawer.
- The method demonstrated strong data efficiency, matching an Oracle with only 10 policy rollouts and surpassing it with 20, generalizing robustly to unseen environments, and exhibited rapid inference of **0.07** seconds vs Mobi-π's **273.52** seconds.
- In sequential tasks, N2M improved pick success rates to 18/20 and drop success rates to 12/20, outperforming human intuition (12 picks, 5 drops) and geometric reachability (6 picks, 1 drop), significantly enhancing overall task completion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning policy preferences directly from a limited number of successful rollouts, rather than relying on geometric reachability or heuristic policy-awareness, enables mobile manipulators to consistently find optimal base poses for diverse manipulation tasks.
> - A key data augmentation technique, viewpoint augmentation, significantly improves data efficiency and allows the model to generalize robustly to unseen environments and varying initial robot viewpoints using minimal real-world data.
> - Predicting a multi-modal distribution of preferable poses (e.g., using a GMM) from a single ego-centric observation enables the system to capture multiple valid task-execution viewpoints and achieve rapid, real-time inference (0.07 seconds per inference).

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Mobile manipulators, which integrate robot mobility with environmental interaction capabilities, are designed for a range of real-world applications. These systems typically employ a modular architecture, where a navigation module guides the robot to a task area, followed by the execution of a pre-trained manipulation policy. A central challenge in this modular approach is determining the precise base pose for the manipulator to execute its task effectively.

Historically, this base positioning problem has been framed as a geometric search, primarily focusing on physical reachability using tools such as Inverse Reachability Maps (IRM). While sufficient for planner-based manipulation policies, this geometric criterion is often inadequate for modern learning-based policies. Learning-based policies are sensitive to their initial observation distributions; a pose that is kinematically feasible might still lead to policy failure if it falls outside the expected input distribution of the trained manipulation model.

Recent research efforts have attempted to introduce policy-awareness into base positioning. For instance, methods like MoManipVLA and MoTo extend fixed-base manipulation policies with mobile capabilities but still rely predominantly on geometric analysis. A concurrent work, Mobi-π, represents a policy-aware approach but exhibits practical limitations, including reliance on pre-built scene reconstructions, high inference latency, and potential incompatibility with policies trained on vast, external datasets or through continuous reinforcement learning.

This work, N2M, positions itself by reformulating the base positioning problem to explicitly learn policy preferences. It seeks to bridge the gap between navigation and manipulation by providing a practical, efficient, and policy-aware solution that addresses the shortcomings of prior geometric-centric and early policy-aware methods. The approach is orthogonal to efforts aimed at improving the intrinsic robustness or generalization capabilities of manipulation policies themselves; instead, N2M guides the robot to optimal viewpoints where existing policies are most likely to succeed, acting as a "plug-and-play" enhancement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of N2M is to develop a practical and policy-aware method for determining the optimal base pose for a mobile manipulator to execute a manipulation policy. This objective is driven by the identified limitations of existing approaches to the navigation-to-manipulation transition.

The motivation for N2M stems from several critical observations:

1. **Insufficiency of Geometric Reachability:** Traditional methods rely on geometric checks to ensure an object is physically reachable. However, modern learning-based manipulation policies are highly sensitive to the robot's initial base pose and the corresponding observation. A kinematically feasible pose may still lead to policy failure if the observed scene configuration deviates from the policy's training data distribution. This sensitivity necessitates a positioning strategy that is aware of the manipulation policy's specific preferences.

2. **Practical Limitations of Prior Policy-Aware Methods:** Existing policy-aware approaches, such as Mobi-π, face significant practical challenges. These include:
 - **Reliance on pre-built scene representations:** Methods requiring 3D scene reconstruction (e.g., using 3D Gaussian Splatting) are slow and demand re-reconstruction for dynamic environments, limiting their real-world applicability.
 - **Slow inference speeds:** Extensive search and iterative sampling processes lead to prohibitively long inference times, rendering them unsuitable for real-time operation or dynamic environments.
 - **Incompatibility with diverse policy training paradigms:** Some methods rely on access to the policy's original training data distribution, making them less compatible with state-of-the-art policies trained on large, diverse datasets or through continuous reinforcement learning.

3. **Addressing Fundamental Challenges in Base Positioning:** The authors identify five specific challenges that N2M aims to resolve:
 - **Adaptability to Non-Static Environments:** The need for rapid adaptation to changes in the environment. N2M aims to predict preferred poses efficiently from ego-centric sensor data in a single forward pass.
 - **Multi-Modality of Preferable Poses:** Multiple distinct poses might be equally optimal for policy execution. Predicting a single pose can lead to suboptimal interpolation. N2M seeks to model the distribution of preferable poses.
 - **Defining the Criterion for Preferable Poses:** Manipulation performance is influenced by complex interactions between policy architecture, training data, robot configuration, task, and environment. N2M aims to directly learn these preferences from actual policy execution outcomes (rollouts) rather than modeling underlying factors.
 - **Viewpoint Robustness:** The robot's initial navigation end pose can vary significantly. N2M needs to provide consistent and reliable predictions regardless of the robot's specific viewpoint within the task area.
 - **Data Efficiency:** Collecting successful policy rollouts can be time-consuming and labor-intensive. N2M aims to achieve robust learning with a minimal number of rollouts.

By addressing these motivations and challenges, N2M seeks to create a robust, efficient, and broadly applicable system that effectively aligns the navigation capabilities of mobile robots with the execution requirements of learning-based manipulation policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The N2M (Navigation-to-Manipulation) module systematically reformulates the base positioning problem by learning policy preferences directly from rollouts. The methodology encompasses a specific system overview, network architecture, and a data preparation strategy that includes a novel viewpoint augmentation technique.

#### 3.1. N2M Module Overview

The N2M module operates in four sequential steps during deployment (as depicted in Figure 1):
1. **Observation Capture:** At the navigation end pose, the robot captures an ego-centric RGB point cloud using its onboard RGB-D camera. This local observation is critical for adaptability to non-static environments.
2. **Pose Distribution Prediction:** The N2M network processes the captured point cloud to predict a distribution of preferable initial poses for manipulation.
3. **Collision-Free Pose Sampling:** A collision-free base pose is sampled from the predicted distribution. This step often involves a simplified collision check using an occupancy map derived from the scene's point cloud.
4. **Navigation and Manipulation:** The robot navigates to the sampled initial pose and subsequently executes the pre-trained manipulation policy.

#### 3.2. Network Architecture

The core of N2M is a neural network, denoted as $f_{\theta}$, designed to predict the multi-modal distribution of preferable initial poses.
- **Input:** An ego-centric RGB point cloud observation ($o$) captured by the robot's onboard RGB-D camera. This design choice avoids reliance on global or historical scene information during inference.
- **Output:** The network models the distribution of preferable initial poses, $P(\mathbf{p})$, using a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM):
 $P(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \alpha_k \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{p}|\mu_k, \Sigma_k)$
 where $K$ is the number of Gaussian kernels (e.g., $K=2$ for multi-modal tasks like closing a drawer from two sides, $K=1$ otherwise), $\alpha_k$ are the weights, and $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{p}|\mu_k, \Sigma_k)$ represents a Gaussian distribution with mean $\mu_k$ and covariance $\Sigma_k$. The network $f_{\theta}$ outputs the parameters $\{\alpha_k, \mu_k, \Sigma_k\}_{k=1}^K$.
- **Encoder:** The point cloud input is processed by a Point-BERT model, which encodes it into a fixed-length latent vector. Point-BERT is initialized with pre-trained weights and then fine-tuned during N2M training.
- **Decoder:** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) takes the latent vector from Point-BERT and generates the parameters for each Gaussian kernel of the GMM.
- **Loss Function:** The network is trained to maximize the probability of observing preferable initial poses from its training dataset $D$. The loss function is formulated as:
 $L(\theta) = \sum_{(\mathbf{o},\mathbf{p}) \in D} - \log P_{f_{\theta}(\mathbf{o})}(\mathbf{p})$
 Additionally, three regularization terms are incorporated to improve GMM fitting: maximizing the entropy of kernel weights (to prevent collapse to a single mode), enforcing inter-mode distance (to avoid mode convergence), and regularizing the weighted sum of mode entropies (to prevent overfitting and overly narrow modes).

#### 3.3. Data Preparation

The training data for the N2M network is prepared in two main steps: raw data collection and data augmentation.
1. **Raw Data Collection:**
 - The raw dataset $R$ consists of pairs $(\mathcal{S}, \mathbf{p}_{\pi})$, where $\mathcal{S}$ is a local scene reconstruction and $\mathbf{p}_{\pi}$ is a preferable initial pose.
 - For each entry:
 - Multiple RGB-D frames are captured and stitched to reconstruct the local task area $\mathcal{S}$. Relative poses are determined via odometry or point cloud registration.
 - The robot is positioned at various poses within the task area, and the manipulation policy $\pi$ is executed. If $\pi$ successfully completes the task, that pose is recorded as $\mathbf{p}_{\pi}$ for the current scene $\mathcal{S}$. The scene is then reset.
 - $\mathbf{p}_{\pi}$ and $\mathcal{S}$ share a common coordinate frame.
2. **Data Augmentation (Novel Viewpoint Augmentation):**
 - This strategy is critical for achieving viewpoint robustness and data efficiency.
 - For each collected raw pair $(\mathcal{S}, \mathbf{p}_{\pi})$:
 - $M$ (e.g., 300) distinct viewpoints ($v$) are uniformly sampled within the task area.
 - Invalid viewpoints (e.g., those that collide with the scene or from which the target object is not visible) are filtered out.
 - For each valid viewpoint $v$, a new ego-centric point cloud observation $o_v$ is rendered by projecting points from $\mathcal{S}$ onto the viewpoint using the robot's RGB-D camera intrinsics.
 - The preferable initial pose $\mathbf{p}_{\pi}$ remains constant for a given scene $\mathcal{S}$ regardless of the viewpoint. Thus, each rendered observation $o_v$ is paired with the same $\mathbf{p}_{\pi}$ to form a training sample $(o_v, \mathbf{p}_{\pi})$ for the augmented dataset $D$.
 - Additional augmentations applied during training include random rotations around the Z-axis, translations within a 1m radius on the XY-plane, and uniform downsampling of the point cloud to 8,192 points.

#### 3.4. Robot Setup and Collision Detection

- **Simulation:** Experiments are conducted in RoboCasa using a Franka Panda arm mounted on an Omron mobile base, with an RGB-D camera on the wrist. Ground truth depth and robot location are used for point cloud reconstruction. The initial joint configuration of the arm is fixed, simplifying pose prediction to the SE(2) space (x, y, θ).
- **Real World:** The Rainbow Robotics RB-Y1 platform is used, equipped with RealSense D405 (wrist), RealSense D435 (head), and ZED 2i (head) cameras. ZED 2i captures ego-centric point clouds. The RB-Y1 allows torso height adjustment, so initial poses are represented as a 4-dimensional vector (x, y, θ, h).
- **Collision Detection:** For both simulation and real-world, collision checking for sampled poses involves projecting the scene's point cloud onto a 2D plane to create an occupancy map. The robot's footprint is modeled as a rectangular boundary for efficient collision checks. In real-world, this map is derived from local point clouds, which is considered sufficient as the target object and preferred pose are typically within the camera's field of view.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted in both simulation (RoboCasa) and real-world environments evaluate N2M's applicability, data efficiency, generalizability, benchmark performance against alternatives, and efficacy in sequential tasks.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Applicability (Q1)

N2M demonstrated broad applicability by consistently outperforming the geometric-only "Reachability" baseline across various tasks (PnPCounterToCab, Close Double Door, Open Single Door, Close Drawer) and different manipulation policy architectures (BC Transformer, Diffusion Policy, Action Chunking with Transformers - ACT). The success rates achieved by N2M were comparable to an "Oracle" baseline (ideal fixed, pre-defined pose), and in some instances (e.g., PnPCounterToCab, Diffusion Policy in Open Single Door), N2M even surpassed the Oracle. This suggests that N2M can identify optimal policy preferences that may not align perfectly with the original training data distribution, highlighting the utility of learning directly from policy rollouts.

#### 4.2. Data Efficiency Analysis (Q2)

On the PnPCounterToCab task, N2M achieved success rates equivalent to the Oracle baseline with only 10 successful policy rollouts for training. With 20 rollouts, N2M's performance exceeded that of the Oracle. This indicates that N2M can capture policy preferences effectively and robustly from a limited amount of rollout data, addressing the challenge of costly data collection.

#### 4.3. Generalizability Analysis (Q3)

N2M exhibited generalization capabilities to unseen environments. In the PnPCounterToCab task, performance improved as the number of distinct training scenes increased, even when tested in entirely new scenes with varied furniture textures or layouts. The module was able to match or surpass the Oracle baseline, suggesting it learns general patterns of tasks and policies applicable to novel scenarios.

#### 4.4. Benchmark (Q4)

A comparative benchmark on the CloseDrawer task using two policies (BC Transformer and Diffusion Policy) evaluated N2M against "Reachability" and "Mobi-π," with "Oracle" serving as an upper bound.

- **Success Rate:**
 - **Reachability:** Showed low success rates (0.16±0.04 for DP, 0.15±0.03 for BC), confirming the insufficiency of non-policy-aware geometric methods.
 - **Mobi-π:** Also exhibited low success rates (0.24±0.05 for DP, 0.09±0.03 for BC). The authors attribute this to Mobi-π's reliance on observation similarity as a heuristic for policy performance and potential limitations of its pre-trained RGB encoder in distinguishing subtle observation differences.
 - **N2M:** Achieved significantly higher success rates (0.55±0.07 for DP, 0.56±0.04 for BC), demonstrating state-of-the-art performance by learning policy preferences directly from rollouts.
 - **Oracle:** Maintained the highest success rates (0.93±0.04 for DP, 0.97±0.02 for BC).

- **Time Cost:**
 - **Mobi-π:** Required significant pre-processing (5 minutes for scene scanning + 7 minutes for 3DGS training per scene) and had a slow inference time (273.52±13.06 seconds per inference). This limits its application in dynamic environments.
 - **N2M:** Required an initial data collection phase (20.7 minutes for rollouts) and training (3.4 hours), but achieved a rapid inference time (0.07±0.03 seconds per inference). This enables real-time adaptation in dynamic environments.

#### 4.5. Real-World Experiment (Q5)

In the "Lamp Retrieval" task, N2M demonstrated effective prediction of preferable initial poses in the real world with a small number of rollouts. N2M variants, even those trained on a subset of cells, showed reasonable predictions for lamp positions not seen during training, further supporting generalizability. The system also demonstrated robust and reliable performance through 10 consecutive successful task executions under randomized lamp placements and robot viewpoints.

#### 4.6. Performance in Sequential Task (Q6)

A multi-stage task (picking a chip box from a table and dropping it into a trash bin) highlighted the critical importance of policy-aware positioning in sequential mobile manipulation.
- **Reachability:** Showed poor performance throughout, with only 6 out of 20 successful picks, and only 1 successful drop out of those 6.
- **Human-Intuition:** Performed better than reachability (12 successful picks, 5 successful drops), but still struggled, demonstrating that human intuition without policy knowledge is not optimal. The observation that human performance improved over trials corroborated N2M's learning-from-rollouts approach.
- **N2M:** Significantly improved both subtask success rates (18 successful picks, 12 successful drops) and the overall sequential task completion. This underscores how N2M mitigates error compounding in sequential tasks by ensuring optimal base positioning at each stage.

#### 4.7. Further Analysis

- **Qualitative Analysis:** Visualizations confirmed N2M's real-time prediction capabilities, viewpoint robustness (10 consecutive successes), and generalization to unseen environments with limited data.
- **Ablation Study:** Viewpoint augmentation and positional augmentation were identified as the most impactful design choices for N2M's success. Performance gains from viewpoint augmentation saturated around 300 augmented views. The choice of encoder (PointBERT vs. PointNet++) and the use of pre-trained weights were less critical to overall performance but PointBERT with pre-trained weights yielded the highest results.
- **Visualization of Learned Representations:** Attention heatmaps showed that the N2M encoder consistently focused on salient, task-relevant regions of the point cloud (e.g., lamp, toybox, person), indicating data-efficient learning of critical scene features.
- **Failure Analysis:** Identified limitations included sensitivity to very small or distant objects (due to downsampling and out-of-distribution inputs), susceptibility to noisy sensor data, and fundamental limitations imposed by the underlying manipulation policy itself (e.g., if the policy's preferred pose distribution is inherently too narrow).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents N2M, a system that reformulates the problem of base positioning for mobile manipulators from a purely geometric search to a task of predicting preferable poses based on policy rollout outcomes. This shift in paradigm offers several areas of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Mobile Manipulation Performance:** N2M consistently demonstrates superior performance in terms of manipulation success rates compared to both non-policy-aware geometric baselines and existing policy-aware alternatives. By guiding the robot to poses where manipulation policies are most effective, it addresses a critical misalignment that often leads to failures in complex mobile manipulation tasks.

2. **Practicality and Efficiency:** N2M provides a practical solution for real-world deployment. Its fast inference speed (orders of magnitude faster than some policy-aware alternatives) enables real-time adaptation to dynamic environments. This efficiency is achieved by directly predicting pose distributions from ego-centric point clouds, circumventing the need for computationally intensive 3D scene reconstructions during inference.

3. **Broad Applicability and Generalization:** The methodology is designed to be agnostic to specific manipulation policy architectures, tasks, and robot hardware. Experiments validate its broad applicability across diverse settings and demonstrate robust generalization capabilities to unseen scenes, textures, and layouts, achieved with relatively data-efficient training.

4. **Novel Viewpoint Augmentation Strategy:** The proposed viewpoint augmentation technique is a key technical contribution. It enables the model to learn robust, viewpoint-invariant representations of policy preferences from limited rollout data. This strategy enhances data efficiency and could be applicable to other 3D scene prediction domains beyond mobile manipulation.

5. **Orthogonal Enhancement to Policy Capabilities:** N2M offers a "plug-and-play" enhancement that boosts overall system success rates without requiring modifications or intrinsic improvements to the manipulation policy itself. This means it can augment existing policies, including those relying on large foundation models, by optimizing the robot's physical position. This modularity avoids the computational overhead associated with intrinsically more capable but often heavier policy backbones.

6. **Enabling Complex and Sequential Tasks:** The research highlights N2M's critical role in sequential mobile manipulation tasks. By optimizing each subtask's base positioning, N2M mitigates error compounding, significantly improving overall long-horizon task completion rates. This capability is essential for deploying mobile manipulators in multi-stage real-world scenarios.

7. **Future Research Directions:** The predicted base pose distribution can serve as an indicator of policy proficiency, potentially guiding future active learning strategies. This could enable targeted data collection to continuously expand policy capabilities and boundary awareness, leading to more robust and versatile robotic systems.

In summary, N2M represents a significant advancement in bridging the gap between navigation and manipulation in robotics. Its practical design, demonstrated performance, and methodological innovations contribute to more effective and adaptable mobile manipulator systems for a variety of applications.
