# SpatialTracker: Tracking Any 2D Pixels in 3D Space

> [!summary] Summary
> SpatialTracker performs long-range point tracking in 3D rather than 2D. It lifts pixels with a monocular depth estimator, encodes each frame as triplane feature maps, and iteratively refines 3D trajectories with a transformer, regularized by an as-rigid-as-possible loss over learned rigidity embeddings. It reaches state-of-the-art results on TAP-Vid, BADJA, and PointOdyssey, especially under occlusion and out-of-plane rotation.

## Problem

- Optical flow only covers adjacent frames and feature tracking only sparse pixels, so neither recovers dense long-range pixel motion across an entire video.
- Prior point trackers correlate features purely in 2D image space, discarding the 3D structure where motion actually occurs, so rotation and occlusion become hard to express.
- Projection places spatially distant surfaces adjacent in the image, so 2D correlation neighborhoods near occlusion boundaries contain irrelevant context that corrupts motion reasoning.

## Method

- ==Triplane encoding==: monocular depth unprojects CNN features into a featured point cloud, then average-splats it onto three orthogonal planes, giving dense compact 3D features for any queried point.
- Iterative 3D trajectory prediction: a **transformer** with spatial and temporal attention refines point positions and features over M steps inside overlapping sliding windows, plus an MLP visibility head.
- ==As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP)== regularization with rigidity embeddings: per-trajectory embeddings give cosine rigidity affinities that weight a loss preserving pairwise 3D distances over time, enabling spectral clustering into rigid parts.

## Results

- On TAP-Vid the method averages **58.2** AJ, **73.3** position accuracy, and **88.2** occlusion accuracy across Kinetics, DAVIS, and RGB-Stacking, beating CoTracker's **57.4** / **72.2** / **87.8**.
- On PointOdyssey 3D tracking it reaches **0.22** ATE3D versus **0.77** for lifted CoTracker and **0.70** for chained RAFT-3D, with delta-0.2 rising to **0.76**.
- Removing the ARAP loss drops DAVIS accuracy from **61.1** to **55.1** AJ; swapping ZoeDepth for MiDaS or DPT yields **56.3** and **51.4** AJ respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Estimating motion in the space where it physically happens simplifies the problem: 3D rotation and occlusion have compact parameterizations that 2D representations only express awkwardly.
> - Triplanes are a cheap substitute for voxel volumes, giving dense queryable 3D features from 2.5D observations without the memory cost of 3D convolutions.
> - Rigidity grouping can be learned self-supervisedly from a distance-preservation loss, letting visible neighbors propagate motion to ambiguous or occluded points.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Dense pixel tracking in videos—the task of following every pixel across multiple frames—represents one of computer vision's most challenging problems. While applications ranging from video editing to robotics demand robust long-range pixel trajectories, existing methods primarily operate in 2D image space, facing fundamental limitations when dealing with occlusions, complex deformations, and rapid motion.

SpatialTracker, developed by researchers from Zhejiang University, UC Berkeley, and Ant Group, introduces a paradigm shift by lifting 2D pixels into 3D space and performing tracking in the three-dimensional domain. This approach leverages the inherent regularity of 3D motion compared to its complex 2D projections, enabling more robust and accurate pixel tracking across extended temporal sequences.

The method combines monocular depth estimation with a compact triplane representation for 3D features and introduces an "As-Rigid-As-Possible" (ARAP) constraint that encourages spatial consistency by identifying and preserving rigid motion patterns. Through extensive evaluation on standard benchmarks, SpatialTracker demonstrates state-of-the-art performance in both 2D and 3D tracking tasks.

### 2. The Challenge of 2D Pixel Tracking

Traditional approaches to pixel motion estimation fall into two primary categories: sparse feature tracking methods like SIFT and SURF that provide long-range trajectories for distinctive points, and optical flow techniques that estimate dense motion between adjacent frame pairs. Recent "tracking any point" methods attempt to bridge this gap by generating dense, long-range pixel trajectories using deep neural networks.

However, these 2D-centric approaches face inherent limitations stemming from the 3D-to-2D projection process. When objects rotate or undergo complex deformations, their 2D motion patterns can appear highly irregular despite following simple 3D transformations. Occlusions create discontinuities where pixels disappear and reappear, while the projection process can make spatially distant 3D points appear close in 2D space, leading to misleading feature correlations.

SpatialTracker addresses these challenges by operating directly in 3D space, where motion patterns follow more predictable physical principles. For instance, a rigid body rotation involves only three parameters in 3D, while its 2D projection creates complex apparent motion across the image plane. By reasoning in 3D, the method can leverage geometric priors and physical constraints that are naturally defined in three-dimensional space.

### 3. Core Methodology

SpatialTracker's methodology consists of three main components that work together to enable robust 3D pixel tracking:

#### 3.1. Monocular Depth Estimation and 3D Lifting

The process begins by estimating depth for each video frame using a pre-trained monocular depth estimator (ZoeDepth). This depth information, combined with assumed camera intrinsics, allows 2D pixels to be unprojected into 3D point clouds. Each 3D point is associated with features extracted from the corresponding image region, creating a rich 3D representation that captures both geometric and appearance information.

#### 3.2. Iterative Transformer-Based Tracking

The core tracking mechanism operates through an iterative refinement process. For each video sequence (processed in sliding windows of 8 frames), the method initializes 3D trajectories and iteratively updates them using a transformer network. At each iteration, the transformer takes as input:

- Positional encodings of current 3D point locations
- Point features from previous iterations or the triplane representation
- Correlation features computed by comparing point features with local 3D neighborhoods

The transformer outputs updated 3D positions and refined point features, with this process repeated for 6 iterations to achieve stable trajectory predictions. Additionally, the model predicts visibility scores to handle occlusions effectively.

#### 3.3. Training and Inference Strategy

The model is trained end-to-end on the TAP-Vid-Kubric dataset, which provides 24-frame RGBD sequences with full 3D trajectory annotations. During training, ground truth depth and camera intrinsics are used, while inference relies on monocular depth estimation. The training combines multiple loss functions: trajectory loss for 3D position accuracy, visibility loss for occlusion handling, and the ARAP loss for enforcing rigid motion consistency.

### 4. Triplane Representation for 3D Features

A key innovation in SpatialTracker is its use of triplane representations to efficiently encode 3D scene features. Raw point clouds are irregular and memory-intensive, making them unsuitable for dense feature extraction across arbitrary 3D locations. The triplane approach addresses this by projecting the 3D point cloud onto three orthogonal planes (XY, XZ, YZ) using orthographic projection and average splatting.

This representation offers several advantages:

1. **Efficiency**: The triplane format is compact and allows for efficient feature queries at any 3D location within the camera's field of view
2. **Completeness**: Unlike sparse point clouds, the triplane provides dense feature coverage across the entire 3D space
3. **Interpolation**: Features for arbitrary 3D points can be obtained by projecting onto the three planes, interpolating features, and fusing them

The triplane representation enables the transformer network to efficiently access rich 3D context when updating trajectory predictions, supporting the correlation computations that are crucial for robust tracking.

### 5. As-Rigid-As-Possible Constraint

One of SpatialTracker's most significant contributions is the integration of an As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) constraint that enforces spatial consistency in 3D motion. This constraint is motivated by the observation that many real-world objects contain rigid or semi-rigid parts that move together coherently.

#### 5.1. Rigidity Embedding Learning

The ARAP mechanism works by learning "rigidity embeddings" for each tracked trajectory. These embeddings are computed by aggregating trajectory features across time and are designed to capture which points belong to the same rigid component. The cosine similarity between rigidity embeddings defines the "rigidity affinity" between trajectory pairs.

#### 5.2. Mathematical Formulation

The ARAP loss function encourages point pairs with high rigidity affinity to maintain constant Euclidean distances over time:

```
L_arap = Σ s_ij * |d_ij(t) - d_ij(1)|²
```

where `s_ij` is the rigidity affinity between trajectories i and j, and `d_ij(t)` represents their 3D distance at time t. This formulation naturally handles rigid body transformations while allowing for flexible motion in non-rigid regions.

#### 5.3. Benefits and Applications

The ARAP constraint provides multiple benefits:

1. **Occlusion Handling**: When points become occluded, their motion can be inferred from visible points in the same rigid group
2. **Temporal Consistency**: The constraint reduces jitter and maintains smooth trajectories
3. **Semantic Understanding**: The learned rigidity embeddings can be used for rigid part segmentation through spectral clustering

### 6. Experimental Results

SpatialTracker demonstrates superior performance across multiple benchmarks for both 2D and 3D pixel tracking tasks.

#### 6.1. 2D Tracking Performance

On the TAP-Vid benchmark, SpatialTracker consistently outperforms strong baselines including TAP-Net, PIPs, TAPIR, and CoTracker. For example, on the Kinetics dataset, it achieves 50.1 Average Jaccard (AJ) compared to CoTracker's 48.7, while also improving Average Position Accuracy and Occlusion Accuracy metrics.

The method shows particularly strong performance on the BADJA dataset for animal pose tracking, achieving 69.2% segment-based accuracy versus CoTracker's 63.6%. This improvement demonstrates better boundary awareness and object understanding.

For very long trajectories on the PointOdyssey dataset (up to 2000 frames), SpatialTracker maintains superior performance across all metrics, with the gap increasing as trajectory length grows.

#### 6.2. 3D Tracking Evaluation

Since no direct baselines exist for long-range 3D tracking, the authors constructed comparison methods by chaining pairwise scene flow approaches and lifting 2D CoTracker results with depth maps. SpatialTracker significantly outperforms these baselines, achieving an Average Trajectory Error (ATE_3D) of 0.22 compared to 0.70 for chained RAFT-3D and 0.77 for lifted CoTracker.

#### 6.3. Ablation Studies

Key ablation studies reveal:

- **ARAP Loss Importance**: Removing the ARAP constraint causes substantial performance drops (e.g., AJ on DAVIS from 61.1 to 55.1)
- **Depth Estimator Impact**: ZoeDepth outperforms other monocular depth methods due to its metric depth estimation and temporal consistency
- **Rigid Part Discovery**: The learned rigidity embeddings enable meaningful segmentation of rigid object parts

### 7. Technical Contributions and Impact

SpatialTracker makes several significant contributions to the field of dense pixel tracking:

#### 7.1. Paradigm Shift to 3D

The most fundamental contribution is demonstrating that 3D-based tracking can substantially outperform 2D methods. By operating in the domain where motion naturally occurs, SpatialTracker achieves more robust and accurate results while handling challenging scenarios like occlusions and complex deformations more effectively.

#### 7.2. Integration of Modern Techniques

The method successfully combines several state-of-the-art components:
- Monocular depth estimation for 3D geometry inference
- Triplane representations for efficient 3D feature encoding
- Transformer networks for iterative trajectory refinement
- Learnable geometric constraints for motion regularization

#### 7.3. Novel ARAP Integration

The self-supervised learning of rigidity embeddings and their use in enforcing ARAP constraints represents a significant advancement. This approach allows the model to dynamically identify rigid components and use this information to improve tracking accuracy, particularly for occluded regions.

#### 7.4. Practical Impact

The improved tracking accuracy has broad implications for applications including:

- **Video Editing**: More stable motion estimation for effects and object manipulation
- **Robotics**: Better understanding of dynamic environments for navigation and interaction
- **AR/VR**: More robust tracking for seamless virtual content integration
- **Human-Computer Interaction**: Accurate motion analysis for pose estimation and behavior understanding

#### 7.5. Future Research Directions

The work explicitly acknowledges dependence on monocular depth estimation quality and points toward future research in joint optimization of depth estimation and motion tracking. This suggests a promising direction where advances in 3D reconstruction directly benefit motion estimation and vice versa.

SpatialTracker establishes 3D-aware pixel tracking as a compelling approach for dense, long-range motion estimation, offering a more principled and robust alternative to purely 2D methods. By demonstrating clear performance advantages across multiple benchmarks and providing insights into the benefits of 3D reasoning, this work is likely to influence future research in video understanding and motion analysis.
