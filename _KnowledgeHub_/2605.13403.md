# RotVLA: Rotational Latent Action for Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> RotVLA introduces a Vision-Language-Action model that represents latent actions as continuous elements of the n-dimensional rotation group SO(n). This method, supported by a triplet learning framework, effectively prevents degeneration into trivial solutions and enables the model to achieve state-of-the-art performance on robotic manipulation benchmarks like LIBERO (98.2% success rate) and RoboTwin2.0 (89.6% success rate), alongside robust real-world performance on a dual-arm robot.

## Problem

- Existing Latent Action Models (LAMs) for VLA pretraining are prone to learning trivial behaviors by merely reconstructing target frames, rather than capturing true underlying dynamics.
- Discrete quantization of latent action spaces in traditional LAMs imposes artificial discontinuity, limiting representational capacity and fine-grained control for inherently continuous physical actions.
- Discrete latent action tokens often lack physically meaningful structure or interpretability, hindering sophisticated action planning and generalization across diverse robot embodiments.

## Method

- **RotVLA** develops a ==continuous rotational latent action== representation, where each latent action is an n x n rotation matrix projected onto ==SO(n)== using ==SoftVQ== and ==SVD-based orthogonal projection==.
- Introduces a ==triplet learning framework== that enforces ==temporal compositionality== across three consecutive frames, supervising both single-step and composed two-step transitions to prevent degeneration.
- Leverages a ==flow-matching objective== for VLA pretraining and finetunes with a ==unified action expert== that jointly denoises latent actions (as high-level plans) and embodiment-specific robot actions.

## Results

- Achieved an average **98.2%** success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and **89.6%** on the RoboTwin2.0 bimanual manipulation benchmark, surpassing prior state-of-the-art VLA baselines.
- Demonstrated superior and robust performance in real-world dual-arm robotic tasks, including improved success rates and lower failure rates compared to baselines, especially under domain-shift conditions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of the rotational latent action design and the triplet learning framework, with their combination leading to a **+1.2%** performance improvement over SO(n) alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing latent actions as continuous elements of SO(n) with compositional supervision significantly enhances the capture of real-world, physically meaningful motion dynamics, moving beyond simple visual reconstruction.
> - A triplet learning framework is crucial for preventing latent action models from collapsing to trivial frame reconstruction by enforcing consistency in composed actions over time.
> - The learned continuous rotational latent actions provide an effective high-level planner that improves long-horizon manipulation, multi-task generalization, and cross-domain robustness for VLA models.
