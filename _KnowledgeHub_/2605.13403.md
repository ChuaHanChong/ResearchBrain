# RotVLA: Rotational Latent Action for Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> RotVLA introduces a Vision-Language-Action model that represents latent actions as continuous elements of the n-dimensional rotation group SO(n). This method, supported by a triplet learning framework, effectively prevents degeneration into trivial solutions and enables the model to achieve state-of-the-art performance on robotic manipulation benchmarks like LIBERO (98.2% success rate) and RoboTwin2.0 (89.6% success rate), alongside robust real-world performance on a dual-arm robot.

## Problem

- Existing Latent Action Models (LAMs) for VLA pretraining are prone to learning trivial behaviors by merely reconstructing target frames, rather than capturing true underlying dynamics.
- Discrete quantization of latent action spaces in traditional LAMs imposes artificial discontinuity, limiting representational capacity and fine-grained control for inherently continuous physical actions.
- Discrete latent action tokens often lack physically meaningful structure or interpretability, hindering sophisticated action planning and generalization across diverse robot embodiments.

## Method

- **RotVLA** develops a ==continuous rotational latent action== representation, where each latent action is an n x n rotation matrix projected onto ==SO(n)== using ==SoftVQ== and ==SVD-based orthogonal projection==.
- Introduces a ==triplet learning framework== that enforces ==temporal compositionality== across three consecutive frames, supervising both single-step and composed two-step transitions to prevent degeneration.
- Leverages a ==flow-matching objective== for VLA pretraining and finetunes with a ==unified action expert== that jointly denoises latent actions (as high-level plans) and embodiment-specific robot actions.

## Results

- Achieved an average **98.2%** success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and **89.6%** on the RoboTwin2.0 bimanual manipulation benchmark, surpassing prior state-of-the-art VLA baselines.
- Demonstrated superior and robust performance in real-world dual-arm robotic tasks, including improved success rates and lower failure rates compared to baselines, especially under domain-shift conditions.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of the rotational latent action design and the triplet learning framework, with their combination leading to a **+1.2%** performance improvement over SO(n) alone.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing latent actions as continuous elements of SO(n) with compositional supervision significantly enhances the capture of real-world, physically meaningful motion dynamics, moving beyond simple visual reconstruction.
> - A triplet learning framework is crucial for preventing latent action models from collapsing to trivial frame reconstruction by enforcing consistency in composed actions over time.
> - The learned continuous rotational latent actions provide an effective high-level planner that improves long-horizon manipulation, multi-task generalization, and cross-domain robustness for VLA models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, often built upon Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and trained on extensive robot-related data, represent a significant advancement in embodied AI. These models aim to enable robots to understand instructions, perceive environments, and execute actions, thereby facilitating more generalist robotic policies. A primary challenge in VLA pretraining involves managing heterogeneous datasets, which comprise data from diverse robot embodiments and unannotated human videos. The variability in visual observations, action spaces, and robot configurations across these datasets complicates the learning of a unified policy.

Latent Action Models (LAMs) have emerged as a promising approach to address this heterogeneity. LAMs seek to learn a consolidated, embodiment-agnostic action space by encoding transitions between sequential observations. Traditional LAMs commonly employ a discrete quantization encode-decode paradigm, often utilizing methods like VQ-VAE to convert continuous actions into discrete tokens. This approach aims to align latent actions with the next-token prediction framework prevalent in large VLMs. However, this discretization introduces several limitations: it can lead to trivial solutions where the model merely reconstructs the target frame rather than capturing underlying dynamics, it restricts the representational capacity of the latent space, and it often results in latent actions that lack physical interpretability or compositionality.

RotVLA positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome these limitations. It proposes a novel approach to latent action modeling that moves beyond discrete quantization, aiming for a continuous, physically grounded representation. By addressing the issues of limited representational capacity, degeneration into trivial reconstruction, and lack of physical meaning, RotVLA contributes to the development of more robust, generalizable, and scalable VLA models capable of operating across a wider range of robotic platforms and tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of RotVLA are rooted in addressing the fundamental limitations identified in existing Latent Action Models (LAMs) used for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) pretraining.

The paper highlights three key issues with current LAMs:
1. **Risk of Degeneration to Trivial Solutions:** Existing LAMs, particularly those relying on frame reconstruction objectives, are prone to learning trivial behaviors. Instead of capturing the dynamic underlying actions, the model may simply encode the target frame's appearance, leading to a latent space that does not represent meaningful transitions. This shortcut limits the model's ability to generalize motion semantics.
2. **Limited Representational Capacity and Discontinuity:** The common practice of discretizing the latent action space, often via vector quantization, imposes an artificial discontinuity on actions. Real-world physical actions are inherently continuous, and their discretization can severely restrict the model's ability to represent the full spectrum of possible movements accurately and with fine granularity. This also hinders the representation of subtle variations in actions.
3. **Lack of Physically Meaningful Structure:** Discrete latent action tokens typically lack an inherent physical structure or interpretability. There is no clear notion of scale, direction, or compositionality within such a space. This absence makes it difficult to define how actions combine or relate to real-world dynamics, limiting the potential for sophisticated action planning and generalization across diverse embodiments.

To address these limitations, RotVLA aims to achieve the following specific objectives:
- **Develop a Continuous Rotational Latent Action Representation:** The core motivation is to represent latent actions as elements of the n-dimensional rotation group, SO(n). This formulation is intended to naturally align with the continuous nature of physical actions and provide a richer representational capacity than discrete tokens. Furthermore, the mathematical properties of SO(n) (e.g., closure under matrix multiplication) are expected to enable a physically meaningful definition of action composition.
- **Enforce Meaningful Temporal Dynamics and Prevent Degeneration:** To mitigate the risk of trivial frame reconstruction, the paper seeks to introduce a "triplet learning framework." This framework is designed to explicitly enforce temporal compositionality by considering three consecutive frames. By supervising both single-step transitions and two-step composed transitions, the model is compelled to learn latent actions that genuinely capture frame-to-frame dynamics rather than mere visual similarity.
- **Create a Unified and Generalizable Action Space:** By learning a continuous, structured latent action representation from a diverse range of robot and human video data, RotVLA aims to establish a unified action space. This unified space is intended to generalize across various robot embodiments, enabling cross-embodiment learning and potentially zero-shot action generation capabilities, a critical step towards more generalist robot foundation models.
- **Integrate Latent Actions as a Planner for Robot Control:** For downstream tasks, the goal is to leverage the learned latent actions not just as a representation, but as a high-level planner. This involves designing a unified action expert that jointly denoises latent actions and embodiment-specific robot actions, with latent actions providing guidance for the robot control.

These objectives collectively aim to enhance the representational power, robustness, and generalizability of VLA models, ultimately contributing to more capable and adaptable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RotVLA is structured into three main stages: learning a continuous rotational latent action, pretraining the VLA model, and finetuning for downstream tasks.

#### 3.1. Stage I: Continuous Rotational Latent Action

This stage focuses on developing a latent action representation that is continuous, geometrically structured, and avoids the pitfalls of discrete quantization.

- **Continuous Latent Action:** The authors replace the conventional VQ-VAE method for latent action extraction with SoftVQ. SoftVQ, by using a soft categorical distribution to aggregate multiple codewords, maintains continuity in the latent space while retaining the benefits of a codebook structure. This addresses the limitation of discrete representations.

- **Rotational Latent Action (SO(n) Representation):** Building on the continuous formulation, RotVLA further constrains the latent action to lie within the n-dimensional rotation group, SO(n). Each latent action is represented as an *n x n* rotation matrix. To achieve this, the model first predicts an unconstrained matrix, which is then projected onto SO(n) using an SVD-based orthogonal projection (Eq. 4). This projection ensures orthogonality and unit determinant, maintaining the properties of a rotation matrix while allowing flexibility during learning. The latent action extraction is formalized as an operator F, incorporating SoftVQ and the SO(n) projection.

- **Triplet Learning Framework:** To prevent the latent action model (LAM) from degenerating into trivial frame reconstruction, RotVLA introduces a triplet learning framework. Given three consecutive frames (I_t, I_t+1, I_t+2), the framework operates as follows:
 - **Single-Step Latent Action Extraction:** Latent actions for consecutive frame pairs are extracted: z_t→t+1 = F(I_t, I_t+1) and z_t+1→t+2 = F(I_t+1, I_t+2).
 - **Single-Step Reconstruction:** A decoder D predicts the next frame based on the current frame and the extracted latent action (e.g., ˆI_t+1 = D(I_t, z_t→t+1)). A loss L_single supervises these single-step predictions.
 - **Identity Latent Action:** An identity latent action (z_I) is explicitly anchored by computing and projecting the batch-wise mean latent action from identical frame pairs (F(I_t, I_t)). This helps stabilize the latent space.
 - **Action Composition:** Due to the SO(n) representation, latent actions can be composed via matrix multiplication. A two-step composed latent action is calculated as z_comp_t→t+2 = z_t+1→t+2 · z^-1_I · z_t→t+1. The inverse of z_I is included to account for potential gauge ambiguity and ensure the composition remains within the SO(n) group.
 - **Two-Step Compositional Prediction:** The composed latent action is then applied to the initial frame to predict the final frame in the triplet: ˆI_comp_t+2 = D(I_t, z_comp_t→t+2). A reconstruction loss L_comp penalizes the difference between this prediction and the ground-truth I_t+2.
 - **Overall Triplet Loss:** The total loss for this stage, L_triplet, combines L_single, L_comp, and a Kullback-Leibler loss (L_soft) from SoftVQ for codebook learning. This objective encourages the LAM to capture meaningful temporal dynamics and compositional consistency, discouraging simple appearance memorization.

#### 3.2. Stage II: RotVLA Pretraining

After the continuous rotational latent action space is learned, these latent actions serve as supervision for pretraining the RotVLA model.
- **Architecture:** RotVLA utilizes a pretrained VLM backbone, specifically InternVL3.5-1B, to process multimodal inputs (consecutive frames and language instructions). This VLM backbone is integrated with a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) based action expert.
- **Latent Action Supervision:** The VLM extracts vision and language tokens (h). The corresponding latent action tokens z_t→t+1 (obtained from Stage I) are used as targets.
- **Flow-Matching Objective:** RotVLA is pretrained using a flow-matching objective (L_FM_LA). The action expert predicts a velocity field (v_θ) that transforms a noisy latent action (z_τ) back to the target latent action (z_t→t+1), conditioned on the VLM features (h). This generative modeling approach aims to produce smooth and coherent latent action sequences.

#### 3.3. Stage III: RotVLA Finetuning

This stage adapts the pretrained RotVLA model for specific downstream robotic manipulation tasks.
- **Unified Flow-Matching Action Expert:** The latent action head is extended to become a unified flow-matching action expert that simultaneously predicts two types of variables:
 - Latent actions (z ∈ SO(n)): These function as a high-level planner.
 - Robot actions (a ∈ R^(N×d)): These are d-dimensional robot control commands over a horizon N, serving as the robot controller. The absolute end-effector (abs EEF) pose, including xyz position and Rotate6D representation for orientation, is adopted for robot actions.
- **Joint Supervision:** A joint action variable x = (a, z_t→t+1) is defined. A unified flow-matching objective (L_FM_LA−RA) is applied to jointly supervise the denoising of both latent and robot actions within a single diffusion framework.
- **Structured Attention Mechanism:** To maintain a clear separation between planning and control while enabling effective cross-modal interaction, a structured attention mechanism is implemented in the action head. Latent action tokens attend only to vision-language tokens. Conversely, robot action tokens attend to both latent action tokens (for planning guidance) and vision-language tokens (for task context). This preserves the action semantics learned during cross-embodiment pretraining.

#### 3.4. Data

The pretraining datasets for both the LAM and RotVLA are aggregated from large-scale sources, totaling over 1700 hours. These include Open X-Embodiment, AGIBOT-beta, RoboMIND, RoboCOIN, and Ego4D. This diverse collection encompasses data from single-arm and bi-manual robots, dexterous hands, and human egocentric videos, facilitating cross-embodiment learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

RotVLA's performance was evaluated across simulation benchmarks and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, alongside detailed analyses of its proposed latent action representation.

#### 4.1. Simulation Results

- **LIBERO Benchmark:** RotVLA achieved an average success rate of 98.2% across the LIBERO-Spatial, LIBERO-Object, LIBERO-Goal, and LIBERO-Long suites. This result surpassed several established baselines, including UniVLA (95.4%), GR00T-N1.6 (97.0%), and X-VLA (98.1%).
- **RoboTwin2.0 Benchmark:** For bimanual robotic manipulation, RotVLA attained success rates of 89.6% on the clean setting and 88.5% on the randomized setting. These scores exceeded prior VLA baselines such as StarVLA (88.2% / 88.3%) and LingBot-VLA (88.6% / 86.7%).
The paper attributes this strong performance to the VLA pretraining strategy, which leverages the continuous rotational latent action representation to capture smooth and compositional dynamics. The triplet learning framework is cited for mitigating LAM collapse and fostering meaningful temporal representations, while latent actions are identified as effective high-level planners for stable long-horizon manipulation and multi-task generalization.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- RotVLA was evaluated on a dual-arm ARX R5 robotic platform for two single-arm tasks (pick-and-place, block in drawer) and one dual-arm task (cup stacking).
- Compared to the π0.5 baseline, RotVLA consistently demonstrated superior performance across all three tasks under standard evaluation. For the single-arm tasks, success rates exceeded 90%. In the dual-arm cup stacking task, which demands bimanual coordination and long-horizon planning, RotVLA exhibited a significantly lower failure rate than the baseline.
- Under domain-shift conditions, which involved varying backgrounds and distractor objects, the performance disparity between RotVLA and the baseline widened. This robustness is attributed to the continuous rotational latent action-based pretraining and latent planner finetuning, which emphasize high-level motion semantics over specific visual appearances.
- Inference latency for RotVLA was measured at 79ms per step, comparable to π0.5's 61ms, demonstrating competitive real-time performance.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Continuous Rotational Latent Action

- **Cross-Domain Generalization:** Visualizations (Fig. 4) illustrated that latent actions extracted from one domain (e.g., Ego4D human videos) could generalize effectively to different robotic platforms (e.g., Tian Qin, UR5) and even to unseen datasets (e.g., LIBERO). This suggested that the learned latent actions capture embodiment-independent, high-level motion semantics.
- **Training Stability (Degeneration Mitigation):** A comparison with a reconstruction-only baseline revealed that the triplet learning framework effectively mitigates the risk of degeneration. While both methods achieved low reconstruction error for the immediate next frame (ˆI_t+1), the reconstruction-only baseline produced significantly smaller differences between I_t+1 and an "imagined" frame (ˆI'_t+2) generated by applying the latent action to I_t+1. This indicated that the baseline's latent actions collapsed to encoding frame appearance rather than genuine motion dynamics. The triplet framework, in contrast, captured distinct dynamic changes, such as arm lifting, and successfully extrapolated future steps.
- **Representation Expressiveness:** On the LARY benchmark for evaluating latent action representations, RotVLA significantly outperformed existing methods. It achieved lower regression error for low-level robotic control and higher classification accuracy for high-level semantic actions. A variant, RotVLA*, trained on a reduced dataset (excluding overlapping benchmark datasets), still surpassed other LAMs on robotic data and performed comparably to LAPA-DINOv3 on human activity recognition, despite no human video training. This implies that the proposed latent action representation captures both fine-grained control dynamics and high-level semantic structure more effectively.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

Ablation studies investigated key design choices:
- **Latent Planner:** Removing latent action supervision during finetuning (w/o. Planner) led to a performance drop, particularly for long-horizon tasks (LIBERO-Long), indicating the importance of latent actions as a planning signal.
- **Latent Action Dimension (n):** An optimal dimension of n=16 was identified. Smaller dimensions limited expressiveness, while larger dimensions increased optimization difficulty and computational cost.
- **Latent Action Design:**
 - A discrete latent action paradigm showed limited improvement over the "w/o. Pretrain" baseline, confirming its restricted representational capacity.
 - Replacing discrete with continuous latent actions improved performance by 1.4%.
 - Adopting the SO(n) latent action alone yielded marginal gains.
 - However, when the compositional supervision from the triplet learning framework (L_comp) was introduced on top of the SO(n) latent action, performance improved by an additional 1.2%. This result indicated that the triplet training framework, in conjunction with SO(n), is crucial for enabling the LAM to capture true motion dynamics rather than just memorizing frame appearance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RotVLA introduces a novel approach to latent action modeling that carries significant implications for the development of generalizable Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The core innovation lies in its formulation of latent actions as continuous elements within the n-dimensional rotation group (SO(n)), coupled with a triplet learning framework that enforces temporal compositionality.

This approach offers several key advantages:
- **Enhanced Representational Capacity:** By moving beyond discrete tokens, RotVLA's continuous, rotational latent actions can capture the nuanced and inherently continuous nature of physical motions more accurately. This fine-grained representation is crucial for precise control and understanding of complex robot behaviors.
- **Physically Meaningful Structure and Compositionality:** Representing actions as elements of SO(n) provides a structured geometry that aligns with real-world action dynamics. The ability to compose actions via matrix multiplication offers a mathematically grounded way to combine movements, facilitating more sophisticated planning and hierarchical control. This intrinsic structure aids in learning meaningful and transferable motion dynamics.
- **Mitigation of Representation Collapse:** The triplet learning framework directly addresses the problem of latent action models degenerating into trivial frame reconstruction. By explicitly penalizing models that fail to capture compositional consistency across multiple time steps, RotVLA is compelled to learn latent actions that truly represent motion dynamics rather than mere visual appearance changes.
- **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The comprehensive pretraining on large-scale, heterogeneous datasets, including cross-embodiment robotic data and human videos, enables RotVLA to learn a unified action space that generalizes across diverse platforms. The robustness demonstrated in real-world experiments, particularly under domain-shift conditions, suggests a greater ability to adapt to unseen environments.
- **Efficient High-Level Planning:** The integration of latent actions as high-level planners during finetuning allows the model to leverage abstract motion semantics to guide embodiment-specific robot control. This hierarchical approach can lead to more stable and effective long-horizon manipulation capabilities.
- **Competitive Performance with Moderate Parameters:** Achieving state-of-the-art results on challenging benchmarks like LIBERO and RoboTwin2.0 with 1.7 billion parameters and 1700+ hours of pretraining data positions RotVLA as a computationally efficient solution relative to its performance.

The findings suggest that incorporating explicit geometric structure and continuity into latent action modeling is a promising direction for developing scalable and generalizable robot foundation models. The ability of RotVLA to learn transferable motion semantics, independent of specific embodiments or visual appearances, opens pathways for accelerating robot learning by leveraging vast amounts of diverse, unlabeled video data. Future work could explore scaling both the LAM and VLA models further, and more extensively integrating human video data to enhance robot learning capabilities.
