# Learning Robust Dexterous In-Hand Manipulation from Joint Sensors with Proprioceptive Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zürich developed a Proprioceptive Transformer that enables tendon-driven robotic hands to achieve dexterous in-hand manipulation using only joint sensor feedback. This approach demonstrated a 3.1x improvement in cube rotation speed over proprioceptive PPO baselines and maintained 100% rotation accuracy on a real ORCA hand.

## Problem

- External vision systems for dexterous manipulation suffer from occlusions, high computational demands, and large sim-to-real gaps, limiting their practical deployment.
- Tendon-driven robotic hands present challenges due to transmission nonlinearities like cable stretch and backlash, which complicate precise control with motor-side sensing.
- Extracting sufficient exteroceptive information (e.g., object state) solely from internal robot (proprioceptive) sensors for complex in-hand manipulation tasks remains a challenge.

## Method

- A teacher-student learning framework is employed, where a teacher policy trained with privileged information in simulation distills its knowledge into a student policy.
- The student policy, implemented as a Proprioceptive Transformer (PT), learns from only noisy joint position and velocity histories, incorporating auxiliary reconstruction objectives for object pose and clean joint states.
- The trained PT policy is deployed on a real tendon-driven ORCA hand using direct joint angle sensors, enabling zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for continuous cube reorientation.

## Results

- The Proprioceptive Transformer with direct joint sensing (PT-Joint) achieved 11.83 Rotations Per Minute (RPM) for a medium cube on the real ORCA hand, a 3.1x improvement over a proprioceptive PPO baseline.
- PT-Joint maintained 100% Rotation Accuracy and Drop-Free Success Rate across both medium and large cubes, demonstrating robust and stable performance.
- The Transformer architecture significantly reduced object position reconstruction RMSE to 13.70 mm from proprioceptive history alone, a 23.4% reduction compared to an MLP baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct joint sensing provides superior proprioceptive feedback compared to motor encoder readings for tendon-driven hands, effectively mitigating issues from transmission nonlinearities.
> - Temporal sequences of joint measurements implicitly encode sufficient information about object presence, size, and state, allowing dexterous manipulation without explicit external vision or tactile sensing.
> - Transformer architectures effectively leverage long-range temporal dependencies in proprioceptive data to implicitly estimate object states and denoise sensor readings, outperforming MLPs and LSTMs.
