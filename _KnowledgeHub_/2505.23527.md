# Normalizing Flows are Capable Models for RL

> [!summary] Summary
> This research demonstrates that Normalizing Flows are capable probabilistic models for reinforcement learning, performing competitively with or surpassing state-of-the-art diffusion and transformer models across a wide range of tasks. The work shows that NFs offer a simpler and more computationally efficient alternative for learning complex policies, Q-functions, and occupancy measures in continuous RL settings.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art probabilistic models in reinforcement learning (RL) like diffusion models are computationally intensive, requiring the solution of differential equations for training and inference.
- Autoregressive transformer models are highly expressive but primarily suited for discrete settings, often requiring complex machinery to adapt to continuous action spaces in RL.
- Normalizing Flows (NFs) have been largely overlooked in RL due to a prevailing belief that they lack sufficient expressivity or suffer from architectural restrictions and training instabilities.

## Method

- The authors utilize ==Normalizing Flows== (NFs) as the core probabilistic model, leveraging their ability to learn an invertible mapping between a complex data distribution and a simple prior, allowing for exact likelihood computation and efficient sampling.
- They propose a simple and efficient NF architecture built on RealNVP-style coupling networks with linear flows (GLOW-style) and standard Layer Normalization, designed for conditional modeling in RL contexts.
- The developed NF architecture is seamlessly integrated into various RL algorithms, including offline imitation learning (Behavior Cloning), offline RL (MaxEnt RL + BC), and unsupervised goal-conditioned RL (Q-function and goal occupancy estimation).

## Results

- NF-based Behavior Cloning (NF-BC) is competitive with or outperforms diffusion and transformer baselines on multi-modal imitation learning tasks (e.g., PushT, Kitchen), using **6x** fewer parameters than some diffusion policies.
- NF-Goal-Conditioned Behavior Cloning (NF-GCBC) dramatically outperforms its Gaussian counterpart on 40 out of 45 OGBench tasks and exceeds dedicated offline RL algorithms (e.g., GCIQL, QRL) on the same benchmark.
- NF-RLBC (MaxEnt RL + BC) is competitive with leading diffusion/flow-matching models in offline RL, outperforming all baselines on 15 out of 30 tasks and demonstrating superior performance on long-horizon reasoning tasks like `puzzle-3x3-play` by **230%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Normalizing Flows (NFs) can model complex, multi-modal distributions in continuous action spaces with exact likelihood computation and efficient sampling, capabilities often lacking or computationally expensive in other models.
> - A single, relatively simple NF architecture can be seamlessly integrated into diverse RL algorithms, serving as policies, Q-functions, and occupancy measures.
> - Adding Gaussian noise during training and applying a denoising trick during sampling significantly improves the robustness and sample quality of NFs for behavior cloning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning has increasingly relied on powerful probabilistic models to handle the complexity and high dimensionality of real-world data and control problems. Recent years have seen a surge in the adoption of architectures like autoregressive transformers, energy-based models (EBMs), and, most notably, diffusion models. These models have demonstrated impressive expressivity, allowing RL algorithms to learn complex, multi-modal policies, manage high-dimensional observations, and perform sophisticated planning.

However, the authors point out that these powerful models often come with significant trade-offs:
- **Diffusion models:** While highly expressive, they are computationally intensive, requiring the solution of differential equations for training, sampling, and likelihood evaluation. This computational burden can limit their scalability and real-time application.
- **Autoregressive transformer models:** These are scalable and expressive but are predominantly applied in discrete settings. Their application to continuous action spaces in RL often necessitates additional machinery, such as learning discrete representations of actions or states, which adds complexity and potential approximation errors.
- **Gaussian models:** Offer computational efficiency but fundamentally lack the expressivity needed for multi-modal or complex distributions often encountered in RL.

Within this landscape, Normalizing Flows (NFs) exist as an alternative class of generative models. NFs learn an invertible mapping between a complex data distribution and a simple base distribution (e.g., a standard Gaussian). This invertibility allows for efficient, exact likelihood computation and direct, non-iterative sampling. Despite these theoretical advantages, NFs have received comparatively less attention in the RL community than diffusion models or transformers. The prevailing belief, often cited, is that NFs inherently lack sufficient expressivity or suffer from architectural restrictions and training instabilities.

This paper directly challenges this prevailing belief. It positions NFs as a compelling alternative that can provide the necessary capabilities (exact likelihoods, efficient sampling, compatibility with variational inference) without the computational overhead or architectural compromises of other state-of-the-art models. By demonstrating NFs' competitive performance across a wide range of RL tasks, this work aims to re-evaluate their potential and encourage their broader adoption, potentially simplifying existing RL algorithms and opening new avenues for research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to challenge the widely held (mis)conception that Normalizing Flows (NFs) lack the expressivity required for complex reinforcement learning tasks. The authors aim to demonstrate that NFs are indeed highly capable probabilistic models that can seamlessly integrate into various RL algorithms, serving as policies, Q-functions, and occupancy measures.

The key motivations driving this research are rooted in the limitations of current state-of-the-art probabilistic models in RL:
- **Computational Intensity:** Diffusion models, while expressive, are computationally demanding for training, sampling, and likelihood evaluation due to their reliance on solving differential equations.
- **Architectural Constraints/Complexity:** Autoregressive transformer models, popular for their scalability and expressivity, typically require discrete representations for continuous RL tasks, adding an extra layer of complexity.
- **Need for a Unified Model:** Many RL algorithms require a probabilistic model that can efficiently perform multiple operations: exact likelihood computation (for behavior cloning, value function estimation), efficient sampling (for real-time control, policy execution), and compatibility with variational inference (for offline RL, MaxEnt RL). Existing models often support only a subset of these capabilities, making their application complicated or requiring auxiliary techniques.
- **Simplicity and Efficiency:** The authors hypothesize that a simpler, more efficient model that naturally supports all these required capabilities could lead to "much simpler algorithms" while achieving high performance. They seek to show that NFs, despite their prior limited attention in RL, offer this combination of flexibility, expressivity, and computational tractability.

In essence, the paper seeks to provide empirical evidence that NFs are not only expressive enough but also offer a more elegant and computationally efficient solution for a broad spectrum of RL problems, thereby simplifying the design and implementation of RL algorithms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the paper's methodology involves demonstrating the versatility and expressivity of Normalizing Flows (NFs) across diverse reinforcement learning settings. The approach is multi-faceted:

#### 3.1. Normalizing Flow Fundamentals

The paper begins by re-establishing the theoretical benefits of NFs. An NF learns an invertible mapping $f_\theta: \mathbb{R}^d \to \mathbb{R}^d$ from a data distribution $p(x)$ to a simple prior distribution $p_0(x)$ (e.g., a standard Gaussian). This invertibility is crucial because it allows for:
- **Exact Likelihood Computation:** The density $p_\theta(x)$ can be precisely calculated using the change of variables formula: $p_\theta(x) = p_0(f_\theta(x)) |\det(\frac{df_\theta(x)}{dx})|$. This is a direct, single-pass computation.
- **Efficient Sampling:** Samples $x$ from $p_\theta(x)$ are generated by first sampling $z \sim p_0(x)$ and then applying the inverse mapping $x = f_\theta^{-1}(z)$. This is also a single-pass operation.
- **Compatibility with Training Paradigms:** NFs can be efficiently trained using both Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) and Variational Inference (VI), as both require easy computation of likelihoods and sampling, and the ability to backpropagate gradients through these operations. This flexibility is key to integrating NFs into various RL algorithms.

#### 3.2. Proposed NF Architecture

The authors design a "simple and efficient" NF architecture specifically tailored for RL applications. This architecture is built upon established components from prior NF work, optimized for expressivity and speed:
- **Coupling Network (RealNVP-style):** Each NF block incorporates a coupling layer where a part of the input (e.g., first half of dimensions) is passed through unchanged, while the other part is transformed using a scaling and shifting operation conditioned on the first part. This design ensures that the inverse function and the log-determinant of the Jacobian are easily computable, as the Jacobian is triangular.
- **Linear Flow (GLOW-style):** After the coupling layer, a linear transformation is applied using a matrix decomposition ($W=PL_\theta U_\theta$, where P is a fixed permutation, $L_\theta$ is lower triangular, and $U_\theta$ is upper triangular). This "generalized permutation" allows for interaction between all dimensions, enhancing expressivity, while maintaining easy invertibility and log-determinant calculation.
- **Layer Normalization:** Unlike some prior NFs that used specialized normalization layers, the authors found that standard LayerNorm within the coupling network's weights was sufficient for stable training of deep NFs.
- **Conditional Modeling:** The architecture is designed to handle conditional distributions $p_\theta(x|y)$, where $y$ is conditioning information (e.g., state, goal). The coupling network's affine transformations are conditioned on $y$.
- **Noising and Denoising (for MLE training):** For NFs trained purely with MLE (e.g., Behavior Cloning), adding Gaussian noise to the training inputs and then applying a denoising trick during sampling was found to be crucial for performance. This technique, adapted from Zhai et al., helps improve the robustness and sample quality of the learned flow.

#### 3.3. Integration into RL Algorithms

The paper demonstrates the "seamless" integration of this single NF architecture into a range of RL algorithms across four distinct problem settings:

- **Offline Imitation Learning (IL):**
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC):** NF-BC models the conditional policy $\pi_\theta(a|s)$ using MLE (Eq. 7), maximizing the likelihood of expert actions given states.
 - **Goal-Conditioned Behavior Cloning (GCBC):** NF-GCBC extends BC by conditioning actions on future goals $g$ (Eq. 8), treating future states as goals.
- **Offline RL:**
 - **MaxEnt RL + BC:** NF-RLBC uses the NF as an expressive policy within an actor-critic framework. It leverages NF's ability to be optimized via both VI (for entropy regularization and maximizing Q-value, Eq. 10) and MLE (for behavior regularization, Eq. 10), allowing it to sample actions with high values while staying close to the expert data.
- **Goal-Conditioned RL (GCRL) / Unsupervised RL:**
 - **Q-function Estimation:** NF-UGS uses the NF to model the discounted state occupancy distribution $p_\theta(g|s,a)$ (Eq. 11), framing Q-function estimation as an MLE problem.
 - **Unsupervised Goal Sampling (UGS):** NF-UGS also uses the NF to estimate the marginal goal coverage density $p_\theta(g)$ (Eq. 12) via MLE, which is then used to select challenging goals for exploration. A single NF network is used for both conditional and unconditional density estimation via a masking scheme.

#### 3.4. Extensive Empirical Evaluation

The methodology culminates in a comprehensive empirical evaluation across 82 tasks from various RL benchmarks (e.g., PushT, Kitchen, OGBench, Ant environments). The NF-based methods are compared against strong baselines using diffusion models, transformers, and other probabilistic models, focusing on performance, computational efficiency (parameters, hyperparameters), and algorithmic simplicity.

By adopting this rigorous methodology, the authors aim to provide compelling evidence that NFs are a highly "capable" and underutilized model family for modern RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling empirical evidence across a wide array of reinforcement learning tasks, systematically challenging the notion that Normalizing Flows (NFs) lack expressivity or practicality for RL. The main findings and results are summarized as follows:

#### 4.1. Imitation Learning (IL)

- **Performance:** The NF-based Behavior Cloning (NF-BC) algorithm is competitive with, and often outperforms, state-of-the-art baselines using diffusion models (DiffPolicy-C, DiffPolicy-T) and transformers (VQ-BeT, BeT) on multi-modal behavior datasets like PushT, Kitchen, Multimodal Ant, and UR3 BlockPush. Specifically, NF-BC outperforms baselines on 2 out of 4 tasks (PushT and Kitchen).
- **Efficiency & Simplicity:** NF-BC achieves these results with significantly less computational overhead and greater algorithmic simplicity. It uses 6 times fewer parameters than convolutional diffusion policies (DiffPolicy-C) and requires 2 to 3 times fewer hyperparameters than VQ-BeT and DiffPolicy variants. This challenges the assumption that high expressivity necessitates complex, resource-intensive models.

#### 4.2. Conditional Imitation Learning (CIL)

- **Superior Performance:** The NF-based Goal-Conditioned Behavior Cloning (NF-GCBC) dramatically outperforms its Gaussian counterpart (GCBC) on 40 out of 45 tasks from the OGBench benchmark, underscoring the critical role of expressive policies.
- **Outperforming Offline RL Algorithms:** Remarkably, NF-GCBC, despite being a BC-based algorithm (which traditionally do not learn value functions), outperforms dedicated offline RL algorithms such as GCIQL, QRL, and CRL across the 45 tasks. It also significantly outperforms FM-GCBC (a flow-matching based policy with the same underlying algorithm) by 77%, suggesting that direct log-likelihood optimization of NFs may be more effective than indirect probabilistic modeling (via SDE/ODE learning) for imitation.

#### 4.3. Offline Reinforcement Learning (Offline RL)

- **Competitive & Strong Performance:** NF-RLBC, which integrates the NF architecture as a policy into a MaxEnt RL + BC framework, is competitive with strong baselines using diffusion/flow-matching models (IDQL, IFQL, FQL) on 30 tasks. NF-RLBC outperforms all baselines on 15 out of 30 tasks and achieves top-2 results on an additional 10 tasks.
- **Handling Long-Horizon Reasoning:** NF-RLBC demonstrates particularly strong performance on tasks requiring long-horizon and sequential reasoning, such as `puzzle-3x3-play` and `scene-play`. On `puzzle-3x3-play`, NF-RLBC performs 230% better than the next best baseline. The authors attribute this to NF's ability to use direct gradient-based optimization for action search through the policy, unlike other expressive policy classes that require intermediate steps like distillation or resampling.

#### 4.4. Unsupervised Goal-Conditioned RL (UGS)

- **Effective Density and Q-function Estimation:** NF-UGS, which leverages a single NF to estimate both the Q-function (as a conditional state occupancy density) and the marginal goal coverage density, consistently outperforms other unsupervised baselines, including `CRL-oracle` (which makes stronger assumptions about test-time goal availability), `CRL-uniform`, and `CRL-minmax` on tasks like `ant-u-maze`, `ant-big-maze`, and `ant-hard-maze`.
- **Sustained Exploration:** NF-UGS demonstrates better asymptotic success rates compared to baselines, indicating its effectiveness in continuously exploring novel and challenging goals by effectively estimating the non-stationary goal coverage distribution.

Overall, the main findings strongly suggest that Normalizing Flows are not only expressive enough to model complex, multi-modal distributions in RL but also offer a path to simpler, more efficient, and often more performant algorithms compared to current state-of-the-art approaches. The consistent success across diverse RL paradigms provides compelling evidence against the long-standing skepticism regarding NF capabilities in RL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research paper carries significant implications for the field of reinforcement learning, potentially shifting perspectives on the choice and application of probabilistic models.

#### 5.1. Re-evaluation of Normalizing Flows

The most immediate and impactful contribution is the compelling empirical evidence that Normalizing Flows are highly capable generative models for RL. This directly refutes the "prevailing belief" that NFs lack sufficient expressivity or are too restrictive for complex RL tasks. By demonstrating their competitive or superior performance across a broad spectrum of 82 tasks in imitation learning, offline RL, and goal-conditioned RL, the paper fundamentally challenges an entrenched misconception within the community.

#### 5.2. Algorithmic Simplicity and Efficiency

The work highlights that NFs can lead to "much simpler algorithms" while achieving higher performance. This simplicity stems from NFs' unique ability to provide exact likelihoods, efficient sampling, and compatibility with variational inference, all through a single, differentiable architecture. This contrasts sharply with diffusion models (computational intensity) and transformer models (need for discrete representations/additional machinery). For practitioners, simpler algorithms mean:
- **Reduced Development Overhead:** Easier to implement and debug.
- **Lower Hyperparameter Tuning:** Fewer hyperparameters to optimize, making deployment more straightforward.
- **Improved Reproducibility:** Simpler designs are generally more robust and easier to reproduce.
- **Computational Savings:** Fewer parameters and direct computations (no ODE solving) translate to faster training and inference, crucial for large-scale applications.

#### 5.3. Unifying Probabilistic Models in RL

The paper effectively argues for NFs as a "generally capable model family for RL" that can serve multiple roles (policy, Q-function, occupancy measure) within different algorithms. This provides a unified framework for probabilistic modeling in RL, potentially fostering a more cohesive understanding and development of RL algorithms.

#### 5.4. Opening New Research Avenues

By establishing the efficacy of NFs, the paper opens several promising directions for future research:
- **Distributional RL:** NFs can naturally model complex distributions over returns or values, enabling more sophisticated distributional RL algorithms.
- **Bayesian RL:** The ability to compute exact likelihoods makes NFs a strong candidate for modeling uncertainty in Bayesian RL frameworks, which is crucial for exploration and safety.
- **RL Safety:** Better uncertainty quantification and explicit density modeling via NFs could contribute to developing safer RL agents that can better predict and avoid dangerous situations.
- **Novel NF Architectures:** While this work leverages existing NF components, its success might spur the development of new NF architectures specifically optimized for RL challenges, potentially overcoming current limitations like invertibility constraints (as mentioned in the paper's limitations).

#### 5.5. Practical Implications for Robotics and Control

The strong performance on tasks involving fine-grained control (PushT), multi-modal behaviors (Multimodal Ant, UR3 BlockPush, Kitchen), and long-horizon manipulation (puzzle-3x3-play) suggests that NFs could be highly effective for real-world robotic control and decision-making systems, where efficiency and robustness are paramount.

In conclusion, this paper delivers a powerful message: Normalizing Flows are not just theoretically appealing but also empirically performant and practically advantageous models for modern reinforcement learning. It advocates for a shift towards simpler, yet equally expressive, probabilistic models, promising to streamline algorithmic design and broaden the applicability of RL in complex domains.
