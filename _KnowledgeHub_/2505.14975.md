# Flattening Hierarchies with Policy Bootstrapping

> [!summary] Summary
> Subgoal Advantage-Weighted Policy Bootstrapping (SAW) is an algorithm for offline Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning that trains a single, non-hierarchical policy for long-horizon tasks. SAW eliminates the need for a generative model over the subgoal space, achieving strong performance in complex environments such as a 35% success rate in `humanoidmaze-giant-navigate`.

## Problem

- Scaling Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) to complex, long-horizon tasks is challenging due to sparse rewards and temporal discounting.
- Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) methods, while effective, introduce significant architectural complexity, particularly from the need to learn a generative model over high-dimensional subgoal spaces.
- Current offline GCRL approaches face limitations in scalability and unified task representation, hindering their application as general 'foundation policies'.

## Method

- The ==Subgoal Advantage-Weighted== Policy Bootstrapping (**SAW**) algorithm was developed, introducing a flat (non-hierarchical) objective for a goal-conditioned policy.
- SAW eliminates the need for a generative model over the subgoal space by directly estimating expectations via advantage-weighted importance sampling on in-trajectory waypoint states.
- The method combines a one-step Advantage-Weighted Regression (AWR) objective with a policy bootstrapping component that leverages a separately trained subpolicy for stable action targets.

## Results

- SAW matched or surpassed state-of-the-art baselines across 20 state- and pixel-based offline GCRL datasets, achieving a **73%** success rate in `antmaze-giant-navigate`.
- The algorithm was the first to demonstrate non-trivial success (**35%** success rate) in `humanoidmaze-giant-navigate`, significantly improving upon the previous best of **12%**.
- SAW scaled effectively to pixel-based locomotion tasks like `visual-antmaze-large` and achieved state-of-the-art performance in state-based manipulation tasks, outperforming existing methods in 5 `scene` tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Empirical analysis revealed that the success of hierarchical methods in offline GCRL is significantly due to improved sampling efficiency from training low-level policies on nearby subgoals, in addition to better value function signal-to-noise ratio.
> - Hierarchical policy optimization can be reframed as probabilistic inference over optimal subgoal states, providing a unified theoretical framework.
> - Directly using advantage-weighted importance sampling for subgoals from the dataset, rather than learning a generative model, removes a major source of complexity and enhances scalability.
