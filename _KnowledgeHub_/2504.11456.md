# DeepMath-103K: A Large-Scale, Challenging, Decontaminated, and Verifiable Mathematical Dataset for Advancing Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepMath-103K is a meticulously curated mathematical dataset containing 103,000 challenging, decontaminated, and verifiable problems, designed to advance reasoning in Large Language Models. Models trained with this dataset achieve state-of-the-art performance on mathematical benchmarks and demonstrate improved generalizable reasoning across various scientific domains.

## Problem

- Existing mathematical datasets often lack sufficient difficulty and novelty, plateauing at moderate problem levels while recycling problems from common sources, hindering generalizable reasoning.
- Widespread data contamination from evaluation benchmarks within training datasets leads to unreliable performance metrics and an overestimation of model capabilities.
- Many current datasets lack verifiable answers, which is crucial for the effective application of rule-based reward functions in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR).

## Method

- The **DeepMath-103K** dataset was constructed with ==103,000 mathematical problems==, focusing on difficulty levels 5-9, primarily sourced from less structured platforms like Math StackExchange to ensure novelty.
- A rigorous decontamination pipeline was implemented using embedding similarity search and an LLM-Judge to remove any overlaps with 17 comprehensive mathematical and STEM benchmarks.
- To ensure verifiability for RLVR, a two-stage process involved GPT-4o for question filtering and standardization, followed by generating three DeepSeek-R1 solutions per problem and verifying consistency with a rule-based checker.

## Results

- DeepMath series models achieved state-of-the-art pass@1 accuracy, with DeepMath-Zero-Math-**7B** improving by +12.7 on AIME24 and +12.1 on AIME25 compared to strong baselines.
- DeepMath-Omn-**1.5B** established new SOTA performance for **1.5B**-scale models, reaching 64.0 on AIME24 and 57.3 on AIME25, outperforming larger proprietary models like o1-mini.
- Models trained on DeepMath-**103K** consistently outperformed baselines on the GPQA-Diamond benchmark, demonstrating enhanced reasoning generalization across biology, physics, and chemistry.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality, challenging, and decontaminated mathematical data is critical for advancing LLM reasoning beyond current limitations, enabling state-of-the-art performance and robust evaluation.
> - Rigorous data curation, particularly in preventing benchmark contamination and ensuring answer verifiability, is essential for reliable reinforcement learning and trustworthy model assessment.
> - Training on diverse and complex mathematical problems fosters generalizable reasoning skills that extend beyond mathematics to other scientific domains, as evidenced by performance on interdisciplinary STEM benchmarks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical bottleneck in the advancement of complex mathematical reasoning within Large Language Models (LLMs), particularly those leveraging Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). The broader landscape of LLM research has seen remarkable progress in various domains, but complex, multi-step mathematical reasoning remains a significant challenge.

Existing approaches to enhance LLMs' mathematical capabilities generally fall into three categories: continued pre-training (CPT), supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL). While SFT and RL have shown promise, their effectiveness is heavily contingent on the quality and characteristics of the training data. The authors contend that current mathematical datasets, while numerous, suffer from several fundamental limitations that hinder the development of truly advanced reasoning models. These limitations include:
- **Insufficient Difficulty:** Many datasets are skewed towards easier problems (as visually demonstrated in Figure 1a), failing to push the boundaries of current LLMs.
- **Data Contamination:** A prevalent issue where training data inadvertently includes problems from standard evaluation benchmarks (highlighted in Figure 3 and Appendix A), leading to unreliable performance metrics and an overestimation of model capabilities.
- **Lack of Verifiability:** For RLVR, the ability to automatically and rigorously verify answers is paramount for generating accurate rewards. Many datasets lack this feature or provide answers that are too complex for rule-based verification.
- **Limited Novelty and Diversity:** A common practice involves recombining and filtering problems from readily available sources like AIME, leading to significant overlaps between datasets (Figure 5 and Figure 6) and a lack of fresh, diverse problem-solving scenarios.

DeepMath-103K positions itself as a solution to these critical data deficiencies. It builds upon the foundational understanding that RL, especially with verifiable rewards, holds significant potential for complex reasoning (as demonstrated by works like DeepSeek-R1 and others cited). By meticulously curating a dataset that is large-scale, challenging, rigorously decontaminated, and equipped with verifiable answers and diverse solution paths, DeepMath-103K aims to provide the necessary fuel for LLMs to transcend current reasoning limitations. The paper's emphasis on sourcing novel problems from less structured environments like Math StackExchange further distinguishes it from existing datasets that primarily recycle common mathematical problems, thereby addressing the problem of data saturation and promoting genuine generalization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind the DeepMath-103K project stems from the observation that while Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Large Language Models (LLMs) demonstrates significant potential in complex mathematical reasoning, its advancement is severely constrained by the limitations of current training datasets. The authors identify specific shortcomings in existing data resources, which serve as the primary drivers for their work:
1. **Lack of Sufficient Difficulty:** Many available datasets do not pose enough challenge to effectively train and evaluate advanced reasoning models, leading to models that plateau at moderate difficulty levels.
2. **Benchmark Contamination:** The inadvertent inclusion of test set problems from standard evaluation benchmarks within training data compromises the integrity and reliability of model performance assessments.
3. **Absence of Verifiable Answers:** For effective Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), a crucial paradigm for training robust reasoning models, the ability to automatically and reliably verify final answers is essential for calculating accurate rewards. Many datasets lack this feature.
4. **Limited Data Novelty and Diversity at Scale:** A significant portion of existing datasets recycle or re-package problems from common sources, leading to overlaps and a diminished capacity to foster generalizable reasoning.

To address these critical gaps, the key objectives of DeepMath-103K are multifaceted:
- **To create a large-scale mathematical dataset (103K problems) with a high concentration of challenging problems, primarily at difficulty levels 5-9.** This is intended to push the boundaries of current LLM reasoning capabilities.
- **To ensure rigorous decontamination against a comprehensive suite of known mathematical and STEM benchmarks.** This objective aims to guarantee the integrity and trustworthiness of future model evaluations.
- **To provide verifiable final answers for every problem, suitable for rule-based reward functions in RLVR.** This is crucial for facilitating robust and efficient RL training paradigms.
- **To include three distinct "R1 solutions" (reasoning paths) for each problem.** This feature is designed to support diverse training methodologies, including supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and to offer multiple perspectives on problem-solving.
- **To source problems from novel and diverse, albeit less structured, origins like Math StackExchange.** This aims to overcome the limitations of data reuse, introduce unique problem varieties, and foster more generalizable reasoning skills in trained models.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to advance the state-of-the-art in mathematical and broader scientific reasoning for LLMs, demonstrating that high-quality, specialized data is a pivotal factor in achieving superior performance and generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The construction of DeepMath-103K is a meticulously designed, multi-stage data curation pipeline, ensuring high quality, challenge, decontamination, and verifiability. The dataset comprises 103,000 mathematical problems, with a core of 95,000 challenging problems (difficulty levels 5-9) supplemented by 8,000 problems (levels 3-5) from SimpleRL for broader difficulty coverage. Each data sample is comprehensively structured to include the mathematical question, a verifiable final answer, a numerical difficulty score, a hierarchical topic classification, and three distinct DeepSeek-R1-generated solutions.

The curation pipeline consists of four primary stages:

1. **Source Analysis and Collection:**
 - The authors first analyzed the difficulty distributions of various open mathematical reasoning datasets (e.g., MetaMathQA, dart-math-hard, MMIQC, WebInstructSub). They observed that datasets derived from common sources like GSM8K and MATH typically skewed towards lower difficulty levels.
 - Crucially, they identified MMIQC and WebInstructSub, which source content more broadly from the web (notably Math StackExchange), as having flatter difficulty distributions with a higher proportion of mid-to-high difficulty problems (levels 5-9).
 - Based on this, subsets of Math StackExchange problems from MMIQC and WebInstructSub were selected as primary sources, along with NuminaMath-CoT for topical diversity. This yielded an initial raw pool of 2,869,000 questions, emphasizing the authors' commitment to novel data sources over re-collected ones.

2. **Data Decontamination:**
 - Recognizing the pervasive issue of benchmark contamination (with raw sources showing up to 90% contamination with benchmarks like AIME24 and AMC23), a rigorous decontamination process was implemented.
 - This involved comparing candidate questions against a comprehensive suite of 17 mathematics and STEM benchmarks (e.g., MATH, AIME, Minerva Math, GPQA).
 - The decontamination method utilized embedding similarity search (using `paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2`) to identify the top-5 most similar examples from benchmark test sets. An LLM-Judge (Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) then determined if these were identical questions or paraphrases, discarding any overlapping samples. This semantic comparison is more robust than simple lexical matching.

3. **Difficulty Filtering:**
 - To focus on challenging problems, each decontaminated problem was assigned a difficulty level. This was achieved by prompting GPT-4o six times per problem, based on AoPS annotation guidelines, and averaging the ratings.
 - Only problems with an estimated difficulty level of 5 or higher were retained, aligning with the objective of creating a dataset that pushes the limits of LLM reasoning.

4. **Answer Verification:**
 - The availability of verifiable final answers is paramount for rule-based reward functions in RLVR. The authors addressed challenges posed by open-ended questions and complex answers through a two-stage process:
 - **Question Filtering and Formatting:** GPT-4o was used to filter out problem types unsuitable for verification and to rewrite conversational questions into a standardized format expecting a single, specific numerical or symbolic answer.
 - **Consistency Check:** For suitable questions, three distinct solution paths were generated using the DeepSeek-R1 model. A rule-based verifier then extracted the final answer from each of these three solutions, as well as from the original source solution. Only problems where all extracted final answers were identical were included in the final dataset, ensuring robust verifiability.

The entire curation process was computationally intensive, incurring an expenditure of 138,000 US dollars in GPT-4o API fees and a total of 127,000 H20 GPU hours, underscoring the significant resources invested to achieve the dataset's quality and scale. The result is a dataset characterized by high difficulty, rigorous decontamination, broad topical diversity (Figure 4), and significant data novelty and uniqueness compared to existing resources (Figure 5, Figure 6).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents compelling evidence for the efficacy and distinct characteristics of DeepMath-103K, both as a dataset and through the performance of models trained on it, referred to as "DeepMath series models."

**Dataset Characteristics:**
- **Higher Difficulty:** DeepMath-103K significantly surpasses existing open datasets in terms of problem difficulty. As shown in Figure 1a, its problem distribution is heavily skewed towards higher levels (5-9), offering a robust challenge for advanced reasoning models.
- **Rigorous Decontamination:** The meticulous decontamination process proved highly effective. DeepMath-103K exhibits negligible contamination with standard mathematical and STEM benchmarks, a stark contrast to other datasets and raw sources which showed alarmingly high overlap rates (Figure 3, Appendix A). This ensures reliable and unbiased evaluation of models.
- **Broad Topical Diversity:** The dataset covers a wide array of mathematical topics, from elementary algebra to advanced calculus and abstract algebra (Figure 4), providing a comprehensive training ground for generalizable mathematical reasoning.
- **Data Novelty and Uniqueness:** By sourcing problems from less structured forums like Math StackExchange, DeepMath-103K boasts significant novelty. A large portion of its problems (82.81K out of 103K) are unique and not found in other datasets, and its embedding distribution is distinctly different from highly overlapping existing datasets (Figure 5, Figure 6).

**Model Performance (DeepMath Series Models):**
DeepMath series models, trained using DeepMath-103K, consistently achieve state-of-the-art (SOTA) results across various mathematical benchmarks and demonstrate remarkable generalization capabilities.

- **Zero RL Training from Base Models:**
 - DeepMath-Zero-7B, starting from the base Qwen-2.5-7B model, shows substantial pass@1 accuracy improvements. For instance, it achieves +12.7 on AIME24 and +12.1 on AIME25 over the strong baseline Qwen-2.5-7B-SRL-Zoo.
 - DeepMath-Zero-Math-7B, initialized from Qwen-2.5-Math-7B, pushes these boundaries further, establishing new SOTA performance with pass@1 improvements of +12.7 (+23.0 over base) on AIME24 and +12.1 (+19.1 over base) on AIME25. These results highlight the dataset's effectiveness in training powerful mathematical reasoners from a foundational level.

- **RL Training from Instruction-tuned Models:**
 - Even when fine-tuning already instruction-tuned models, DeepMath-103K yields significant gains. DeepMath-1.5B, starting from R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B, demonstrates strong improvements, including +7.9 on AIME24 and +6.0 on AIME25.
 - DeepMath-Omn-1.5B, built upon OpenMath-Nemotron-1.5B, achieves unprecedented SOTA pass@1 accuracies for 1.5B-scale models, reaching 64.0 on AIME24 and 57.3 on AIME25. Notably, it surpasses larger proprietary models like o1-mini (63.6 on AIME24) and low-effort o3-mini (60.0 on AIME24), underscoring the dataset's capacity to boost even strong existing models.

- **Generalizable Reasoning Beyond Mathematics:**
 - A particularly significant finding is the generalization of reasoning abilities. DeepMath series models consistently outperform baselines on the GPQA-Diamond benchmark (Table 4), which assesses reasoning in biology, physics, and chemistry. This suggests that the rigorous and diverse mathematical training from DeepMath-103K fosters robust, transferable reasoning skills applicable across different scientific domains.

**Analysis of Zero RL Using DeepMath-103K:**
- During zero RL training (Figure 9), DeepMath-Zero-7B exhibited increasing rollout response lengths and a higher frequency of "cognitive behaviors" such as subgoal identification, verification, backtracking, and enumeration. This trend suggests the reproduction of the "aha moment" phenomenon, leading to models capable of long and intricate reasoning.
- The notably longer average response lengths of DeepMath-Zero-7B on test benchmarks (Figure 9c) indicate its utility for research into long reasoning models and related phenomena like over- and under-thinking.

In summary, the main findings robustly demonstrate that DeepMath-103K is a superior resource for training LLMs in complex mathematical reasoning, leading to new SOTA performances and broad generalization capabilities, while setting high standards for data integrity and novelty.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DeepMath-103K represents a significant contribution to the field of AI, particularly in advancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). Its potential impact spans several crucial areas:

- **Elevating LLM Reasoning Capabilities:** By providing a large-scale collection of challenging mathematical problems, DeepMath-103K directly addresses the critical need for training data that can push LLMs beyond rudimentary problem-solving. The observed state-of-the-art results on multiple mathematical benchmarks unequivocally demonstrate its effectiveness in developing more sophisticated and robust mathematical reasoners. This, in turn, can unlock advancements in AI systems requiring complex logical and quantitative understanding.

- **Enabling Robust Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR):** The inclusion of rigorously verifiable answers is a cornerstone of DeepMath-103K. This feature is crucial for the effective application of rule-based reward functions in RLVR, a paradigm that has proven highly successful in mitigating reward hacking and fostering genuine reasoning. DeepMath-103K therefore provides a robust foundation for further research and development in RL-based reasoning architectures.

- **Fostering Generalizable Intelligence:** Perhaps one of the most impactful findings is the generalization of reasoning abilities beyond mathematics. Models trained on DeepMath-103K show superior performance on interdisciplinary STEM benchmarks like GPQA-Diamond (biology, physics, chemistry). This suggests that training on high-quality, diverse, and challenging mathematical problems can cultivate more abstract, transferable reasoning skills, moving LLMs closer to truly generalizable artificial intelligence. This could have profound implications for AI applications in various scientific and engineering domains.

- **Setting New Standards for Data Integrity:** The paper's meticulous and computationally intensive data decontamination process against a wide array of benchmarks sets a new benchmark for dataset creation. By rigorously removing contamination, DeepMath-103K ensures that future evaluations of models trained on it are fair, reliable, and accurately reflect true reasoning capabilities rather than memorization of test examples. This is vital for maintaining the credibility and progress of AI research.

- **Promoting Data Novelty and Diversity:** The strategy of sourcing problems from less structured, more diverse platforms like Math StackExchange is crucial for overcoming the limitations of existing datasets that often overlap and recycle problems. This approach introduces a wealth of novel problem-solving scenarios, ensuring that models are exposed to a richer variety of mathematical concepts and reasoning styles, which is essential for fostering true generalization rather than pattern recognition on repetitive data.

- **Facilitating Research on Long Reasoning:** The analysis showing increased response lengths and the emergence of cognitive behaviors (subgoal decomposition, verification, backtracking) in models trained on DeepMath-103K highlights its utility for investigating "long reasoning" phenomena. This opens new avenues for understanding and mitigating challenges like over- and under-thinking in complex problem-solving.

- **Community Contribution and Open Science:** The public release of DeepMath-103K, along with the associated code and model weights, significantly contributes to the open science movement. By lowering the barrier to entry for researchers, it is poised to accelerate innovation in mathematical AI, encourage new methodologies, and foster collaborative efforts to push the boundaries of advanced reasoning.

While acknowledging limitations such as potential biases from LLM-based difficulty assessment and the high computational cost, the overall impact of DeepMath-103K is profoundly positive. It provides a foundational resource that is likely to drive the next wave of advancements in LLM reasoning, fostering models that are not only mathematically proficient but also possess more broadly applicable intelligence.
