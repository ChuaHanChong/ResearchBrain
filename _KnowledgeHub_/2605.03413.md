# Learning to Theorize the World from Observation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST introduced the Neural Theorizer (NEO), an AI system that learns to construct explicit, executable programs (theories) directly from raw sensory observations, enabling explanation-driven generalization to novel compositions and longer sequences. NEO demonstrated superior transferability and successfully discovered latent primitive operations compared to monolithic models across tasks like GridWorld and Image Editing.

## Problem

- Current AI world models primarily focus on prediction and reconstruction, often learning entangled representations that struggle with compositional generalization and interpretability.
- These models typically lack explicit mechanisms to discover reusable, compositional theories that explain observed phenomena, diverging from the 'theory-theory' of human cognitive development.
- Existing approaches for learning structured, programmatic knowledge often require symbolic supervision or predefined domain-specific languages, limiting their applicability to raw, unstructured sensory data.

## Method

- Proposed the Learning-to-Theorize (**L2T**) paradigm, formalizing 'theories' as executable, compositional programs inferred directly from raw observation pairs (x, y).
- Developed the ==Neural Theorizer== (**NEO**), a probabilistic neural model that learns a discrete 'Language of Thought' (primitives via **VQ-VAE**) and induces programs using an iterative theory programmer.
- Implemented a ==Minimum Description Length== (**MDL**) principle to favor concise explanations and a state grounding loss to ensure intermediate latent states correspond to valid observations.

## Results

- NEO consistently outperformed monolithic baseline models across GridWorld, Arithmetic Factorization, and Image Editing tasks, demonstrating robust compositional and length out-of-distribution generalization.
- Achieved high transfer accuracy in OOD settings; for instance, NEO showed 0.933 compositional OOD accuracy and 0.845 length OOD accuracy on GridWorld at the hardest setting (α=0.33).
- Successfully recovered latent primitive operations and their compositional structure, with learned codebook entries aligning with ground-truth actions, particularly when augmented with test-time search (NEO-S).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Operationalizing 'understanding' as the construction of explicit, executable, and compositional theories (programs) from observation enables robust 'explanation-driven generalization'.
> - A probabilistic neural architecture can discover reusable abstract primitives and compose them into explanatory programs solely from raw sensory data, without symbolic supervision.
> - Techniques like Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoders for discrete primitives, the Minimum Description Length principle for program length selection, and state grounding regularization are crucial for inducing compositional theories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research integrates insights from developmental cognitive science with advancements in artificial intelligence (AI) to address a limitation in current AI world models. The paper draws inspiration from Alan Turing's 1950 suggestion that AI should focus on simulating the child's mind to understand how intelligence is learned from its earliest stages. This perspective aligns with the "theory-theory" of cognitive development, or the "baby as a scientist" view, in developmental psychology. This view posits that human children, even before acquiring mature language, construct and revise internal theories that explain how the world operates. These theories are characterized by being structured, reusable, and compositional explanations of observed phenomena. This implies that human understanding extends beyond mere prediction; it involves inferring the underlying mechanisms and causal structures.

In contrast, the paper argues that most contemporary AI systems, including recent world models, primarily operationalize understanding as accurate future prediction in latent or observation spaces, reconstruction quality, or task-specific performance. While effective for these objectives, such systems often learn "entangled composite transformations" that capture correlations without explicitly discovering reusable, compositional mechanisms. This can lead to brittleness when faced with distribution shifts or compositional generalization challenges, where familiar components need to be recombined in novel ways. The lack of explicit, structured explanatory theories in many current AI models represents a gap in achieving human-like understanding and generalization.

This work aims to bridge this gap by introducing a learning paradigm that enables artificial systems to construct explicit explanatory theories of the world from raw, non-textual sensory inputs. It positions itself as a step towards world models that prioritize explanatory, compositional understanding over purely prediction-centric learning, thereby addressing a central open problem in AI. It relates to fields such as latent action and world models, neural program induction, abstract reasoning benchmarks like ARC-AGI, and compositional representation learning, but distinguishes itself by focusing on unsupervised discovery of explicit, transferable programmatic theories from observation alone.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and validate "Learning-to-Theorize (L2T)," a novel learning paradigm that enables artificial systems to infer explicit explanatory theories of the world directly from raw, non-textual observations. This objective is motivated by several considerations:

- **Mimicking Human Cognitive Development:** Inspired by developmental cognitive science, the authors aim to operationalize the human capacity for theory-building in AI. This involves moving beyond simply predicting what will happen next to constructing internal explanatory structures that capture *how* observations are generated and transformed. The goal is to build systems that act more like "baby scientists," discovering reusable, compositional explanations of phenomena.
- **Addressing Limitations of Current AI World Models:** Current world models are largely optimized for predictive accuracy, reconstruction, or task-specific performance. While successful in these areas, they often learn implicitly entangled representations that do not explicitly decompose transformations into their constituent mechanisms. This lack of explicit, compositional structure can limit their ability to systematically recombine known components to understand novel situations, making them vulnerable to failures under distribution shift or when compositional generalization is required. The research seeks to overcome this by shifting the learning objective from merely fitting input-output mappings to discovering structured, compositional mechanisms.
- **Enabling Explanation-Driven Generalization:** A core motivation is to achieve "explanation-driven generalization." This means that understanding an observation should involve inferring the latent program that generated it, allowing this inferred program to be reused and applied to novel inputs to predict new phenomena. This contrasts with models that might achieve high reconstruction accuracy but fail to transfer their internal representations to different contexts. The research aims to demonstrate that models learning explicit theories can systematically generalize to unseen program compositions and longer sequences of operations.
- **Learning from Minimally Curated Data:** The authors emphasize the importance of learning these theories solely from raw observation pairs, without relying on symbolic supervision, explicit program annotations, task-specific labels, or pre-defined task groupings. This design choice aims for applicability to large-scale, minimally curated observational data, such as sequences of frames from trajectories, mirroring how humans learn from continuous sensory input.
- **Developing a Robust Evaluation Framework:** To properly assess whether a model has learned a reusable theory rather than an instance-specific mapping, the research introduces the "Observation-to-Theory Induction Benchmark (OTIB)." This benchmark specifically tests the "program transferability" of induced theories, requiring a model to infer a theory from one observation pair and then successfully apply it to a distinct second input generated by the same underlying mechanism.

In summary, the key objective is to advance AI towards a more human-like, explanatory form of intelligence by developing a computational framework for the unsupervised induction of compositional, executable theories from raw visual data, and to demonstrate its capacity for robust generalization across various levels of novelty.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Learning-to-Theorize (L2T) paradigm and instantiates it with the Neural Theorizer (NEO), a probabilistic neural model designed to infer latent executable programs from observation pairs.

#### 3.1. Learning-to-Theorize (L2T) Framework

The L2T framework defines theorizing as a three-part capacity:
1. **Discovering Reusable Abstract Primitives:** Identifying fundamental operations that appear across different phenomena.
2. **Composing Structured Explanations:** Learning to combine these primitives into programs that explain complex observations.
3. **Explaining Novel Phenomena:** Forming new compositions of learned primitives to understand unseen situations.

In this framework, a "theory" is represented as an "executable, compositional program."
- **Phenomenon and Generative Process:** A phenomenon is modeled as an observation pair (x, y), where y is generated from x by an underlying, unobserved program (τ).
- **Compositional Structure of Programs:** A program τ is an ordered sequence of primitive operations (z_1..., z_K) drawn from a finite set Z. Each primitive z_i has an associated execution function f_zi: X → X (transforming an observation space X). Program execution corresponds to functional composition: f_τ = f_zK ◦ ... ◦ f_z1.
- **Compositional Generalization and Training Dataset:** Training data D_train consists of (x, y) pairs, where y = f_τ(x) and τ is sampled from a subset T_train of all possible programs. Crucially, the programs {τ_n} are latent. The framework emphasizes i.i.d. phenomena, unlike benchmarks requiring task grouping.
- **Test-Time Generalization and Program Transferability:** Evaluation occurs on T_test, a disjoint set of programs, potentially longer (K' > K) or involving unseen compositions. A model infers a program ˆτ from a "support" pair (x^(1), y^(1)) and then applies this ˆτ to a "query" input x^(2) to predict ˆy^(2). Success is measured by the observation-space error d_obs(ˆy^(2), y^(2)), assessing if the learned theory is reusable.

#### 3.2. Neural Theorizer (NEO) Architecture

NEO is a probabilistic neural model for inducing latent executable programs.
- **Probabilistic Modeling:** NEO maximizes the conditional likelihood p_θ(y | x) by introducing latent variables: a program τ and its execution trace s = (s_1..., s_K+1). The generative process factors into an encoder p_θ(s_1 | x), a theory programmer p_θ(z_ik | s_k), and a shared primitive execution model p_θ(s_k+1 | s_k, z_ik). A variational posterior q_ϕ(τ, s | x, y) is used to optimize an evidence lower bound (ELBO).
- **Theory Programmer:** This component, q_ϕ(z_ik | s_k, y), acts as a goal-conditioned policy. Given the current latent state s_k and an encoded target observation s_y, it selects the next primitive z_ik. This iterative selection induces a compositional program. The primitives themselves form a "Language of Thought"—abstract symbols whose operational semantics (how they transform latent states) are learned through the shared execution model. A Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) with a discrete codebook is used to implement this, allowing end-to-end training.
- **Minimum Description Length (MDL) Principle:** To handle variable program lengths and avoid overly long explanations, NEO incorporates the MDL principle. For each execution step k, a reconstruction ˆy_k = D_θ(s_k) is computed. The optimal theory length k* is selected as arg min_k (λ_MDL * ℓ(y, ˆy_k)), where λ_MDL penalizes longer programs. Only the loss from ˆy_k* is backpropagated, encouraging short yet accurate programs.
- **Practical Implementation Details:**
 - **Deterministic Execution:** For simplicity, primitive execution is modeled deterministically as s_k+1 = f_θ(s_k, z_ik).
 - **State Grounding:** To ensure intermediate latent states correspond to valid observations, a grounding loss L_state = Σ_k ||s_k - sg[E_θ(D_θ(s_k))]||^2 is applied. This encourages each s_k to lie on the manifold of valid latent representations, preventing "shortcut" transitions that lack reusability.
 - **Training Objective:** The overall objective combines the reconstruction loss, VQ-VAE-specific losses (codebook and commitment loss), and the state grounding loss, truncated at the MDL-selected length k*. Pretrained encoder/decoder parameters are used for stability.
- **Inference at Test Time:** Starting from s_1 = E_θ(x), NEO iteratively constructs a program by greedily selecting primitives via q_ϕ(z_ik | s_k, y). Execution stops when reconstruction error falls below a threshold. This process supports length generalization by allowing the model to compose programs longer than those seen during training. A sampling-based search variant (NEO-S) is also used to explore multiple plausible theories.

#### 3.3. Observation-to-Theory Induction Benchmark (OTIB)

OTIB evaluates models on three types of generalization: In-Distribution (ID), Compositional Out-of-Distribution (OOD) (unseen primitive combinations), and Length OOD (longer programs). The parameter α controls the fraction of programs included in the training set, with smaller α increasing the difficulty of primitive discovery. The benchmark is instantiated in GridWorld (object movement), Arithmetic Factorization Reasoning (multiplicative operations), and Image Editing (visual transformations).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate NEO's capability in learning explicit, compositional theories from raw observations and generalizing across various test conditions.

- **Superior Generalization Across Domains:** NEO consistently outperformed all baseline methods (Discrete Monolithic, Continuous Monolithic, and Continuous Monolithic with Program Optimization) in GridWorld, Arithmetic Factorization Reasoning, and Image Editing tasks. This superiority was observed not only for in-distribution performance but, more critically, for compositional Out-of-Distribution (OOD) and length OOD generalization.
 - **Baseline Limitations:** Monolithic baselines, while sometimes achieving high "self-explainability" (accurate reconstruction of the target observation *y* from the support *x* and the inferred latent code), exhibited near-zero "transferability" (applying the inferred code to a *new* input *x'* to accurately predict *y'*). This suggests that these models tend to encode instance-specific information rather than learning a reusable, generative mechanism.
 - **NEO's Strengths:** NEO maintained strong transferability across all α-ratings and OOD settings. For instance, in GridWorld, NEO achieved transferability scores of approximately 0.933 on compositional OOD and 0.845 on length OOD, even at the most challenging α=0.33 setting (where only a third of program compositions were seen during training). Similar trends were observed in Arithmetic Factorization and Image Editing, with NEO consistently yielding lower reconstruction errors (e.g., L1 distance) in the visual domain.

- **Discovery of Unseen Primitives:** The "primitiveness" metric, which measures how well learned codes align with ground-truth primitive operations, indicated that NEO successfully discovers reusable primitive units.
 - NEO achieved high primitiveness scores (often approaching 1.0) even when a significant portion of ground-truth primitives were not directly observable in isolation during training (low α values). This suggests that NEO decomposes complex, multi-step observed transformations into their finer, underlying primitive components, rather than merely memorizing observed composite actions.
 - Visualizations of the code-primitive alignment matrices further supported this, showing that learned codes often mapped directly to single ground-truth primitives.

- **Adaptive Program Length Selection (MDL Principle):** The Minimum Description Length (MDL) principle enabled NEO to automatically select the optimal explanation length (k*) for each phenomenon. Visualizations demonstrated that the selected k* often aligned closely with the ground-truth number of primitive steps required for a transformation. This indicates that NEO can adapt the complexity of its induced theory to the actual complexity of the underlying process, avoiding over-decomposition or under-decomposition.

- **Effective Test-Time Scaling (NEO-S):** Augmenting NEO with a sampling-based test-time search procedure (NEO-S) significantly boosted performance, especially for length OOD generalization.
 - In Arithmetic Factorization, NEO-S improved length OOD transfer accuracy from approximately 0.02 to over 0.70 with sufficient sampling budget.
 - This suggests that NEO successfully learns the necessary primitive operations, and the search primarily helps in finding correct long-horizon compositions of these primitives, highlighting the utility of a compositional program space for effective exploration.

- **Importance of State Grounding:** Ablation studies revealed that the state grounding loss is crucial for NEO's stability and performance. Removing it caused training to collapse, with primitiveness and transferability dropping to zero. This indicates that grounding intermediate latent states to a valid observation manifold is essential for learning meaningful and reusable primitive transitions.

- **Robustness to Codebook Size:** NEO demonstrated robustness to over-complete codebook sizes. Even with a codebook significantly larger than the number of ground-truth primitives, NEO still learned to induce primitive-level codes and compose them, rather than allocating separate codes to every observed composite action.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Learning-to-Theorize (L2T) as a paradigm for AI that emphasizes the induction of explicit, explanatory theories from raw observations, rather than solely focusing on predictive performance. The Neural Theorizer (NEO) model serves as a proof of concept, demonstrating that compositional, executable programs can be learned as latent theories in an unsupervised manner.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:

- **Shift Towards Explanatory AI:** By focusing on the discovery of underlying generative mechanisms and reusable primitives, this work contributes to a broader shift in AI research from prediction-centric models towards systems capable of more profound, explanatory understanding. This aligns AI more closely with human cognition, particularly the "theory-building" capabilities observed in developmental psychology.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The demonstrated ability of NEO to achieve explanation-driven generalization, including compositional OOD and length OOD generalization, suggests a path towards more robust AI systems that are less brittle under distribution shifts. When a model learns *how* things happen, it is better equipped to adapt to novel combinations or longer sequences of familiar operations.
- **Improved Interpretability:** Representing theories as explicit, compositional programs, even if latent, offers a potential avenue for enhanced interpretability in AI. By inspecting the learned primitives and their compositions, researchers may gain insights into the model's understanding of environmental dynamics, moving beyond opaque neural network representations.
- **Foundational for Scientific AI and World Modeling:** The capacity to infer abstract mechanisms from raw data has direct implications for scientific discovery and the development of advanced world models. Such models could potentially aid in automatically deriving "laws" or "rules" of a simulated or real-world environment.
- **Reduced Data Curation Requirements:** The ability to learn from raw, non-textual observation pairs without requiring explicit program annotations, task labels, or symbolic supervision broadens the applicability of such systems to large-scale, unstructured datasets.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** The authors acknowledge several limitations that also point to future research directions. The current formulation assumes relatively small, discrete sets of primitives and short program lengths, limiting its direct scalability to complex real-world domains with continuous or highly structured dynamics. Primitive semantics are derived purely through reconstruction, and alignment with human-interpretable or truly causal factors is not guaranteed. The deterministic execution and reconstruction-based stopping criteria may also be sensitive to noise or partial observability. Future work will need to address these challenges to extend L2T to richer, more complex environments with stochastic dynamics and open-ended theory spaces.

Overall, this research represents a foundational step towards building AI systems that learn not just to predict, but to theorize and explain the world they observe, potentially leading to more intelligent, adaptable, and understandable AI.
