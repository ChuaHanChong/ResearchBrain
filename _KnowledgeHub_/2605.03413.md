# Learning to Theorize the World from Observation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at KAIST introduced the Neural Theorizer (NEO), an AI system that learns to construct explicit, executable programs (theories) directly from raw sensory observations, enabling explanation-driven generalization to novel compositions and longer sequences. NEO demonstrated superior transferability and successfully discovered latent primitive operations compared to monolithic models across tasks like GridWorld and Image Editing.

## Problem

- Current AI world models primarily focus on prediction and reconstruction, often learning entangled representations that struggle with compositional generalization and interpretability.
- These models typically lack explicit mechanisms to discover reusable, compositional theories that explain observed phenomena, diverging from the 'theory-theory' of human cognitive development.
- Existing approaches for learning structured, programmatic knowledge often require symbolic supervision or predefined domain-specific languages, limiting their applicability to raw, unstructured sensory data.

## Method

- Proposed the Learning-to-Theorize (**L2T**) paradigm, formalizing 'theories' as executable, compositional programs inferred directly from raw observation pairs (x, y).
- Developed the ==Neural Theorizer== (**NEO**), a probabilistic neural model that learns a discrete 'Language of Thought' (primitives via **VQ-VAE**) and induces programs using an iterative theory programmer.
- Implemented a ==Minimum Description Length== (**MDL**) principle to favor concise explanations and a state grounding loss to ensure intermediate latent states correspond to valid observations.

## Results

- NEO consistently outperformed monolithic baseline models across GridWorld, Arithmetic Factorization, and Image Editing tasks, demonstrating robust compositional and length out-of-distribution generalization.
- Achieved high transfer accuracy in OOD settings; for instance, NEO showed 0.933 compositional OOD accuracy and 0.845 length OOD accuracy on GridWorld at the hardest setting (α=0.33).
- Successfully recovered latent primitive operations and their compositional structure, with learned codebook entries aligning with ground-truth actions, particularly when augmented with test-time search (NEO-S).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Operationalizing 'understanding' as the construction of explicit, executable, and compositional theories (programs) from observation enables robust 'explanation-driven generalization'.
> - A probabilistic neural architecture can discover reusable abstract primitives and compose them into explanatory programs solely from raw sensory data, without symbolic supervision.
> - Techniques like Vector-Quantized Variational Autoencoders for discrete primitives, the Minimum Description Length principle for program length selection, and state grounding regularization are crucial for inducing compositional theories.
