# EvoPrompt: Connecting LLMs with Evolutionary Algorithms Yields Powerful Prompt Optimizers

> [!summary] Summary
> EvoPrompt is a gradient-free framework for optimizing discrete natural-language prompts. It keeps a population of prompts, has an LLM carry out genetic-algorithm or differential-evolution operators to write new candidates, and keeps the best ones by dev-set score. It beats human-written prompts and prior automatic methods on 31 datasets, gaining up to 25% on BIG-Bench Hard.

## Problem

- Earlier automatic prompt methods often need token probabilities or gradients from the LLM, which black-box APIs such as GPT-3.5 do not expose.
- Methods that only enumerate and resample diverse prompts explore widely but waste budget. Methods that only edit the current prompt exploit it and stall in local optima.
- Standard evolutionary operators edit tokens independently, ignoring how tokens relate. That breaks the coherence and readability discrete prompts need, so EAs cannot be applied to prompts directly.

## Method

- **EvoPrompt** is a generic loop: initialize a population from manual and LLM-generated prompts, select parents, have an LLM apply ==evolutionary operators==, score offspring on a dev set, and update the population.
- The ==GA== instantiation picks two parents by ==roulette-wheel selection== and instructs the LLM to cross them over, then mutate the offspring. The top N of the merged old and new prompts survive.
- The ==DE== instantiation mutates only the differing parts of two prompts, merges them into the current best prompt, then crosses over with a base prompt. Each offspring replaces its base prompt only if it scores higher.

## Results

- On 7 language-understanding datasets with Alpaca-7b, EvoPrompt (DE) averages **77.05** and GA **76.25**. Manual instructions average 71.07 and APE 73.80. On Subj, DE reaches **75.55**, about **5 points** above GA.
- On SAMSum summarization with GPT-3.5, DE reaches ROUGE-1 of **46.49** against 43.95 for manual prompts. On ASSET simplification, both variants gain over **3 SARI points** over manual prompts.
- On 22 BIG-Bench Hard tasks, EvoPrompt improves every task. DE gains up to **25%** (**3.5%** average) and GA up to **15%** (**2.5%** average). Mutating only differing parts and using the best prompt as Prompt 3 both help in ablations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An LLM can act as a natural-language evolutionary operator. This keeps prompts fluent while the classical algorithm handles the exploration-exploitation balance, with no gradients or logits needed.
> - DE holds up better than GA when initial prompts are weak, and initial-population quality matters little: random starting prompts matched top-performing ones. Evolution adds most where manual prompts are poor.
> - The recipe of using an LLM as an operator inside a conventional search algorithm generalizes beyond prompts. Other algorithms such as particle swarm or quality-diversity search can plausibly be instantiated the same way.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have led to their widespread application across various natural language processing (NLP) tasks. A critical factor in harnessing the full potential of these models, especially when accessed via black-box APIs, is the design of effective prompts. This challenge has given rise to the field of "prompt engineering." Initially, prompt engineering relied heavily on manual human effort, requiring domain expertise and significant trial-and-error to craft optimal instructions for LLMs. This manual process is subjective, time-consuming, and often leads to suboptimal or inconsistent performance across different tasks and models.

To mitigate this human burden, the research community has explored various approaches for "automatic prompt optimization." These can be broadly categorized:

- **Continuous Prompt Tuning:** Early approaches like "prompt tuning" (Li & Liang, 2021; Liu et al., 2021b; Zhang et al., 2021) focused on optimizing continuous, differentiable "soft prompts" in the embedding space of LLMs. While effective in improving performance by directly optimizing model parameters, these methods suffer from two major drawbacks:
 1. They require access to the internal parameters and gradients of the LLMs, which is often not possible for proprietary models accessible only via black-box APIs (e.g., GPT-3.5, GPT-4).
 2. The resulting "soft prompts" are not human-readable, limiting interpretability and direct human intervention.

- **Discrete Prompt Optimization:** This category aims to optimize natural language prompts, which are human-readable and compatible with black-box LLMs. Existing methods within this space include:
 - **Gradient-based/Token Probability Methods:** Some approaches attempt to derive "gradients" or leverage token probabilities from the LLM's output layer to guide prompt search (Deng et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023a). However, these still often require more access than typical black-box APIs provide.
 - **Exploration-focused Methods:** These involve enumerating diverse prompts or using techniques like re-sampling to find the best prompt from a pool of candidates (e.g., APE by Zhou et al., 2022; Jiang et al., 2020). While effective in exploring the prompt space, they can be computationally expensive and may not efficiently converge to optimal solutions.
 - **Exploitation-focused Methods:** Other works emphasize iteratively refining existing prompts, often by analyzing incorrect predictions or applying "prompt editing" strategies (e.g., APO by Pryzant et al., 2023; Prasad et al., 2022; Guo et al., 2023). While good for local improvement, these methods risk getting stuck in local optima, failing to discover globally superior prompts.

Concurrently, there's a growing interest in understanding how LLMs can be leveraged beyond their core language generation capabilities. Recent studies have shown LLMs' potential to act as "black-box optimizers" (Zheng et al., 2023) or to "imitate" operations of conventional algorithms, such as mutation (Lehman et al., 2022) or crossover (Meyerson et al., 2023) in genetic algorithms. Some work has even combined LLMs with genetic algorithms for specific tasks like neural architecture search (Chen et al., 2023) or game design (Lanzi & Loiacono, 2023).

EVOPROMPT fits squarely into this landscape by addressing a critical gap in discrete prompt optimization. It proposes a novel framework that bridges the powerful language generation capabilities of LLMs with the robust optimization principles of Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs). By using LLMs themselves to perform the natural language-aware evolutionary operations (mutation and crossover) on discrete prompts, EVOPROMPT achieves several key advancements:
1. It is **derivative-free and parameter-agnostic**, making it fully compatible with black-box LLM APIs.
2. It inherently **balances exploration and exploitation**, a hallmark strength of EAs, thus overcoming the limitations of purely explorative or exploitative prompt optimization methods.
3. It ensures the generated prompts remain **coherent and human-readable**, preserving interpretability.
4. It further demonstrates and formalizes the concept of LLMs acting as versatile "algorithmic components," opening new avenues for combining LLMs with a broader range of traditional computational algorithms.

In essence, EVOPROMPT represents a significant step forward in automated prompt engineering, offering a robust, interpretable, and widely applicable solution for optimizing LLM performance on diverse tasks, especially in scenarios where internal model access is restricted.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a novel and effective framework for **automatic discrete prompt optimization** for Large Language Models (LLMs). This framework, named EVOPROMPT, aims to overcome the significant challenges associated with manual prompt engineering while addressing the limitations of existing automated methods.

The key motivations driving this research are multi-faceted:

- **Alleviating Human Effort:** As LLMs become ubiquitous, the manual crafting of effective prompts ("prompt engineering") has emerged as a bottleneck. It requires substantial human expertise, is often subjective, and involves considerable trial-and-error, making it an arduous and inefficient process for adapting LLMs to various downstream tasks. The primary motivation is to automate this laborious process.

- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Automatic Methods:**
 - **Black-box LLMs:** Many powerful LLMs, such as GPT-3.5 and GPT-4, are accessible only via black-box APIs, meaning their internal parameters, gradients, or even token probabilities are not exposed. This renders continuous prompt tuning methods and many discrete prompt optimization techniques (which rely on such internal access) impractical or impossible to apply. A key motivation for EVOPROMPT is to develop a method that *does not require any gradients or parameters* from the target LLM.
 - **Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off:** Existing discrete prompt optimization methods often fall into one of two extremes:
 - Purely explorative methods (e.g., enumerating prompts) can be inefficient and lead to indecisiveness, potentially wasting computational resources.
 - Purely exploitative methods (e.g., prompt editing or "gradient descent" analogies) are prone to getting stuck in local optima, failing to discover truly optimal prompts within the vast search space. The motivation here is to design a method that achieves a **balanced approach between exploration and exploitation**, a known strength of evolutionary algorithms.

- **Leveraging Strengths of Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs):** EAs are derivative-free optimization algorithms renowned for their ability to search complex, non-differentiable spaces, balance exploration and exploitation, and achieve good performance with relatively fast convergence. The authors were motivated to borrow the core ideas of EAs due to these advantages, which align well with the challenges of discrete prompt optimization.

- **Bridging LLMs and Conventional Algorithms:** A specific challenge arises when applying EAs to natural language prompts: traditional EA operators (like mutation and crossover) typically alter elements independently, which would destroy the coherence and readability of natural language expressions. A strong motivation for EVOPROMPT is to **connect LLMs with EAs**, allowing LLMs to act as intelligent "evolutionary operators." This approach leverages LLMs' inherent understanding and generation capabilities of natural language to ensure that new candidate prompts generated through mutation and crossover remain coherent and human-readable. This synergy between LLMs and conventional algorithms is a novel research direction the authors aim to explore and promote.

In summary, EVOPROMPT's objectives are to develop an automatic, robust, interpretable, and efficient discrete prompt optimizer that functions seamlessly with black-box LLMs by strategically integrating the language intelligence of LLMs with the optimization power of evolutionary algorithms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EVOPROMPT proposes a novel framework for discrete prompt optimization by synergistically connecting Large Language Models (LLMs) with Evolutionary Algorithms (EAs). The core idea is to leverage the LLM's natural language understanding and generation capabilities to implement the evolutionary operators (e.g., mutation and crossover) that generate new candidate prompts, while the EA paradigm guides the overall optimization process. This approach bypasses the need for gradients or internal parameters of the target LLM, making it suitable for black-box API models.

The general framework of EVOPROMPT (Algorithm 1) consists of three main iterative steps:

1. **Initial Population:**
 - Unlike some methods that start randomly, EVOPROMPT begins with an initial population of *N* prompts (P₀).
 - This population is designed to be diverse, incorporating both available human-engineered (manual) prompts (to leverage prior human knowledge) and prompts generated by LLMs themselves (to introduce diversity, mimicking random initialization in traditional EAs).
 - Each prompt's performance is evaluated on a development set (sᵢ = f_D(pᵢ)).

2. **Evolution:**
 - In each iteration, new candidate prompts are generated by applying evolutionary operators to existing prompts in the population.
 - Crucially, **LLMs are used as the "evolutionary operators"**. Instead of low-level token manipulations, the LLM is prompted to perform high-level linguistic operations akin to mutation and crossover. This ensures the generated prompts remain coherent, grammatically correct, and human-readable, which is a key challenge when applying EAs to natural language.
 - The process involves selecting "parent prompts" from the current population based on their performance scores.

3. **Update:**
 - The newly generated candidate prompts are evaluated on the development set.
 - Similar to the "survival of the fittest" principle in EAs, prompts with superior performance are retained to form the updated population. The specific update strategy varies depending on the type of EA instantiated.

The paper instantiates EVOPROMPT with two widely used evolutionary algorithms: Genetic Algorithm (GA) and Differential Evolution (DE).

**Instantiation with Genetic Algorithm (GA):**
- **Selection:** The roulette wheel selection method is employed, where prompts with higher performance scores on the development set have a greater probability of being selected as parents. Two parent prompts are typically selected.
- **Evolution (LLM-implemented):** The LLM is instructed to perform a two-stage operation to generate a new offspring prompt:
 1. **Crossover:** The LLM is given the two selected parent prompts and prompted to "crossover" them, generating a new prompt that inherits characteristics from both. (Illustrated in Figure 1, Step 1).
 2. **Mutation:** The LLM then "mutates" the prompt generated in the crossover step, introducing random linguistic alterations while maintaining coherence. (Illustrated in Figure 1, Step 2).
- **Update:** In each iteration, *N* new prompts are generated. These are merged with the existing *N* prompts, and the top *N* prompts (based on their scores) are selected to form the next generation's population, ensuring continuous quality improvement.

**Instantiation with Differential Evolution (DE):**
- DE typically operates on numerical vectors. EVOPROMPT adapts DE's principles to discrete prompts by designing LLM-based operations that mimic DE's mutation and crossover logic.
- **Evolution (LLM-implemented):** The LLM is guided through a multi-step process (illustrated in Figure 2):
 1. **Identify Different Parts (Mimicking b-c):** The LLM is prompted to identify the linguistic "differences" or distinct parts between two randomly selected "donor" prompts (pᵣ₁ and pᵣ₂ from the population). This mimics the differential vector in DE.
 2. **Mutate Different Parts (Mimicking F(b-c)):** The identified "different parts" are then randomly mutated by the LLM.
 3. **Combine with Best Prompt (Mimicking a + F(b-c)):** The LLM takes the current best-performing prompt from the population ("Prompt 3" in Figure 2, analogous to 'a' in DE) and selectively replaces parts of it with the mutated "different parts" identified in the previous step, generating a "new prompt." This operation exploits information from the current best solution.
 4. **Crossover:** Finally, the LLM performs a crossover operation between this "new prompt" and a "basic prompt" (a candidate from the current population), combining their features to produce the final candidate prompt.
- **Update:** For each prompt in the current population (pᵢ), a new candidate prompt (p'ᵢ) is generated. The one with the higher performance score (between pᵢ and p'ᵢ) is retained, keeping the population size constant while improving overall quality.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Target LLMs:** Prompt optimization is performed for both open-source Alpaca-7b and closed-source GPT-3.5 (text-davinci-003).
- **Evolutionary LLM:** GPT-3.5 is exclusively used to perform the evolutionary operations (mutation, crossover) due to its strong language generation capabilities.
- **Datasets:** Extensive evaluation is conducted on 31 datasets across diverse tasks, including language understanding (sentiment, topic, subjectivity classification), language generation (summarization, simplification), and challenging multi-step reasoning tasks from BIG-Bench Hard (BBH).
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against human-engineered prompts (Manual Instructions, PromptSource, Natural Instructions) and existing automatic prompt generation methods (APE, APO).
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Standard metrics for each task (accuracy, ROUGE, SARI) are used. Results for Alpaca are averaged over 3 random seeds; GPT-3.5 results are from one seed due to budget constraints.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across 31 diverse datasets on both open-source (Alpaca-7b) and closed-source (GPT-3.5) LLMs consistently demonstrate the superiority of EVOPROMPT.

- **Overall Performance Superiority:**
 - EVOPROMPT significantly outperforms human-engineered prompts (Manual Instructions, Natural Instructions, PromptSource) across language understanding, generation, and reasoning tasks. For instance, on language understanding tasks with Alpaca-7b, EVOPROMPT (DE) achieves an average accuracy of 77.05%, surpassing the best manual instruction baseline (71.07%) and APE (73.80%).
 - It also consistently outperforms existing automatic prompt generation methods like APE (iterative Monte Carlo Search) and APO (gradient descent-like refinement), indicating a better balance between exploration and exploitation. On BBH tasks, EVOPROMPT achieves up to a 25% improvement over the baseline and averages 2.5-3.5% higher normalized scores than APE.

- **Performance Across Task Types:**
 - **Language Understanding:** EVOPROMPT achieves notable gains. On the challenging subjectivity classification task (Subj), EVOPROMPT(DE) shows a substantial 5% accuracy advantage over EVOPROMPT(GA) and significantly outperforms baselines, suggesting DE's effectiveness in escaping local optima for complex classification tasks.
 - **Language Generation (Summarization & Simplification):** EVOPROMPT yields substantial performance gains. For summarization (SAMSum), EVOPROMPT(DE) outperforms EVOPROMPT(GA) and baselines (e.g., +3.82 ROUGE-L over MI for GPT-3.5). For simplification (ASSET), EVOPROMPT shows an improvement of over 3 SARI points compared to manual prompts.
 - **BIG-Bench Hard (BBH):** EVOPROMPT consistently finds better prompts for all 22 challenging multi-step reasoning tasks. EVOPROMPT(DE) achieves up to a 25% improvement on individual tasks and an average of 3.5% normalized score improvement across all tasks, outperforming EVOPROMPT(GA) on 6 tasks by over 2%. This indicates DE's robustness for highly challenging reasoning tasks.

- **Comparison between GA and DE Instantiations:**
 - Both EVOPROMPT(GA) and EVOPROMPT(DE) are highly effective.
 - EVOPROMPT(DE) generally shows a slight edge, especially on more complex tasks (e.g., Subj, summarization, BBH tasks). This is attributed to DE's particular strength in avoiding local optima and its potentially broader exploration capability as evidenced by generating longer prompts with higher variance and more new words (Figure 9).

- **Ablation Studies on Design Choices:**
 - **GA Selection Strategy:** Roulette wheel selection for parent prompts was found to be more effective than tournament or random selection (Table 4).
 - **DE Evolutionary Operators:**
 - **Mutation on Different Parts:** Mutating only the "different parts" of prompts (as in DE's differential vector concept) consistently yielded better performance than mutating all content (Table 5, "Diff" vs. "All" and "eliminate"). This confirms that preserving shared components is beneficial.
 - **Role of "Prompt 3" (Current Best):** Incorporating the current top-performing prompt ("Prompt 3" in Figure 2) during the mutation process is crucial. Choosing the *best* prompt for this role is significantly more effective than random sampling or eliminating its use (Table 5), validating the exploitation aspect of DE in guiding the search.

- **Population Initialization:**
 - The quality of initial prompts is not strictly essential; randomly selected prompts can achieve similar performance to top-performing ones.
 - Introducing randomness (variations generated by LLMs) to initial populations can slightly improve performance when starting with top-performing prompts.
 - Notably, EVOPROMPT(DE) demonstrates better performance than EVOPROMPT(GA) when starting with *bottom-performing* initial prompts (Table 6), suggesting DE's superior ability to explore and escape poor starting points.

- **Cost Analysis and Convergence:**
 - EVOPROMPT's total API requests (and token usage) are comparable to APE.
 - While EVOPROMPT(DE) might converge slightly slower than APE and EVOPROMPT(GA), it consistently achieves a higher performance ceiling, implying its potential for discovering more optimal prompts (Table 13).

- **Prompt Diversity Analysis:** EVOPROMPT(DE) tends to generate longer prompts with higher variance in length and more new words compared to EVOPROMPT(GA), indicating its stronger exploration capabilities (Figure 9).

In essence, the findings unequivocally establish EVOPROMPT as a state-of-the-art method for discrete prompt optimization, showcasing robust performance across a wide array of tasks and LLMs, and demonstrating the profound synergy achievable by integrating LLMs with conventional evolutionary algorithms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EVOPROMPT framework represents a significant advancement in the field of Large Language Model (LLM) prompt engineering and beyond, offering both immediate practical utility and broader scientific implications.

**Immediate Practical Utility:**
- **Automated Prompt Optimization for Black-Box LLMs:** The most direct impact is providing an effective, automated solution for discrete prompt optimization that works seamlessly with black-box LLM APIs (like GPT-3.5, GPT-4). This capability is crucial as many state-of-the-art LLMs are proprietary and only accessible via APIs. It eliminates the need for internal model access (gradients, parameters, token probabilities), which is a common limitation of other prompt tuning methods.
- **Reduced Human Effort and Expertise:** By automating the prompt design process, EVOPROMPT drastically reduces the substantial human effort, expertise, and subjective trial-and-error traditionally required in prompt engineering. This democratizes access to effective LLM utilization for a broader range of users and applications.
- **Improved LLM Performance:** The consistent and significant performance gains demonstrated across 31 diverse datasets, including challenging reasoning tasks on BIG-Bench Hard, directly translate to more effective and reliable LLM applications. This means LLMs can perform better on various tasks, from simple classification to complex generation and reasoning.
- **Human-Readable and Interpretable Prompts:** Unlike continuous prompt tuning methods that generate unintelligible "soft prompts," EVOPROMPT produces discrete, natural language prompts that are human-readable. This maintains interpretability, allowing developers and users to understand why a prompt is effective and potentially refine it further if needed.
- **Resource for the Community:** The authors' commitment to releasing the optimal prompts generated by EVOPROMPT for common tasks (sentiment classification, summarization, reasoning, etc.) provides a valuable public resource, fostering reproducibility and serving as a strong starting point for others working on these tasks.

**Broader Scientific and Research Impact:**
- **A New Paradigm for LLM Integration:** EVOPROMPT pioneers a novel paradigm where LLMs are not merely tools to be prompted but are themselves intelligent components within larger algorithmic frameworks. By demonstrating that LLMs can effectively implement complex operations like mutation and crossover within evolutionary algorithms, the paper opens up a new avenue for how we think about and utilize LLMs.
- **Synergy Between LLMs and Traditional Algorithms:** This work strongly advocates for and demonstrates the powerful synergy created by combining LLMs with conventional algorithms. It suggests that LLMs, with their vast knowledge and language capabilities, can act as sophisticated, language-aware "agents" that perform algorithmic steps. This has implications far beyond prompt optimization.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The paper explicitly encourages future research into combining LLMs with other traditional optimization and search algorithms, such as Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), Ant Colony Optimization (ACO), and Quality-Diversity (QD) algorithms. This could lead to breakthroughs in fields like:
 - **Automatic Code Generation:** LLMs guiding search for optimal code structures.
 - **Game Level Design:** LLMs evolving game environments or character behaviors.
 - **Non-trivial NP-hard Problems:** Leveraging LLMs' pattern recognition and reasoning for combinatorial optimization.
 - **Text-to-Image Generation (Prompting):** Optimizing prompts for generative AI models.
 The main challenge, as highlighted by the authors, will be adapting the specific elements and "movements" of these traditional algorithms into concepts that LLMs can understand and operate on in the language space.
- **Understanding LLM Capabilities:** This research also deepens our understanding of LLMs' inherent capabilities. It reveals their capacity to follow intricate multi-step instructions, generate creative and coherent variations, and even approximate complex algorithmic behaviors when properly prompted.

In conclusion, EVOPROMPT is a significant contribution that not only provides a highly practical and effective solution for a pressing challenge in LLM application but also lays foundational groundwork for a new research direction at the intersection of large language models and classical computational intelligence, promising a wealth of innovative applications across various domains.
