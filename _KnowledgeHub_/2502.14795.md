# Humanoid-VLA: Towards Universal Humanoid Control with Visual Integration

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces Humanoid-VLA, the first Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model designed for humanoid robots, enabling autonomous interaction and context-aware motion generation by integrating language, egocentric vision, and control. It addresses data scarcity through a self-supervised data augmentation strategy, outperforming prior text-to-motion models in quality and diversity, and achieving high success rates in real-world object interaction tasks on a Unitree G1 robot.

## Problem

- Current humanoid control frameworks are largely reactive, responding to explicit external inputs and lacking autonomous interaction capabilities.
- Existing methods often rely on third-person observations or lack integrated egocentric visual context, limiting context-aware motion generation from the robot's own perspective.
- The development of universal humanoid control models, especially those integrating vision and language, faces significant data scarcity of synchronized first-person visual, textual, and whole-body motion data.

## Method

- Proposes **Humanoid-VLA**, the first ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== model for humanoids, built on a two-stage framework: ==Language-Motion Pre-Alignment== and ==Vision-Conditioned Fine-Tuning==.
- Introduces a ==self-supervised data augmentation== strategy using ==compositional motion quantization== and automatic instructional prompt generation to generate large-scale pseudo-annotated training data.
- Integrates the VLA model's generated kinematic motion sequences with a ==goal-conditioned Reinforcement Learning (RL)== policy to control a humanoid robot with physically plausible joint torques.

## Results

- Humanoid-VLA outperformed baselines in text-to-motion synthesis, achieving an FID of **0.467** on HumanML3D (**47.5%** improvement over MDM) and **4.466** motion diversity on Humanoid-S (**6%** better than MDM).
- The integrated RL policy achieved robust motion imitation in physics simulations with consistently low errors, including mean position errors below **40mm** and pose accuracy at **1.18mm**.
- Demonstrated strong performance on a real Unitree G1 robot with high success rates (e.g., **10/10** for "Turn to an object," **9/10** for "Hold an object," "Kick a ball") in various real-world object interaction tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A self-supervised data augmentation pipeline is effective in significantly strengthening motion-language alignment and addressing data scarcity for humanoid VLA models.
> - Integrating egocentric visual perception allows humanoids to move beyond reactive control, enabling autonomous interaction and context-aware motion generation.
> - Combining large language models for motion generation with a goal-conditioned RL policy for whole-body control leads to robust and physically consistent humanoid behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid robot control has seen significant advancements, particularly with the advent of data-driven frameworks leveraging extensive human motion datasets. Initial research focused on developing whole-body controllers to translate basic kinematic sequences into humanoid motion, often relying on traditional methods like Model Predictive Control (MPC) for accuracy and stability, or early learning-based approaches for flexibility. More recently, the integration of multimodal perception, including natural language processing and visual information, has enabled humanoids to perform real-time mimicry and respond to language commands. Works like Exbody, H2O, and OmniH2O have pushed the boundaries of whole-body control and language-guided motion generation.

However, as the paper rightly points out, a critical limitation of these existing frameworks is their primarily **reactive nature**. They dynamically adjust motions in response to *external inputs* (e.g., direct commands, human demonstrations) but lack the ability to *autonomously perceive* and infer interaction targets within their immediate surroundings. This impedes their deployment in complex, unstructured environments requiring object manipulation or adaptive exploration.

This is where "Humanoid-VLA" carves out its niche. It directly addresses this gap by introducing the **first Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model specifically designed for humanoid robots.** While VLA models have made significant strides in other robotic domains, such as robotic arms (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA) and quadrupeds (e.g., QUAR-VLA), their application to humanoids has been hindered by a critical **data scarcity**—specifically, the lack of synchronized first-person visual information, textual motion descriptions, and whole-body motion data for humanoids.

Humanoid-VLA positions itself as a foundational step towards universal humanoid control by combining language understanding, egocentric scene perception, and motion control into a unified system. It moves beyond mere mimicry or reactive responses, aiming for a more "human-like capacity for adaptive and intelligent engagement" by enabling autonomous interaction and environment exploration. The proposed self-supervised data augmentation strategy is a direct response to the aforementioned data scarcity, differentiating this work from others that rely on expensive teleoperation or noisy VLLM annotations for data collection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind Humanoid-VLA are multifaceted, addressing fundamental limitations in current humanoid robot capabilities:

- **Overcoming Reactive Control:** Current humanoid control frameworks are largely reactive, meaning they respond to explicit external inputs (e.g., human demonstrations, direct commands). A key objective is to enable **autonomous interaction capabilities** where the robot can perceive its environment, infer intentions, and decide on actions without constant external prompting. This is crucial for deploying humanoids in real-world scenarios that demand object manipulation or adaptive exploration.
- **Integrating Egocentric Visual Perception:** A major motivation is to incorporate **egocentric visual context** into humanoid control. Existing methods often rely on third-person observations or lack integrated visual understanding from the robot's own perspective. Humanoid-VLA aims to enable context-aware motion generation by allowing the robot to "see" and understand its immediate surroundings from its own viewpoint, a prerequisite for intelligent interaction.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** The development of universal humanoid control models, especially those integrating vision and language, faces a significant bottleneck: **data scarcity**. Existing motion capture datasets lack synchronized first-person visual information and rich textual descriptions. Manual data collection through teleoperation is prohibitively expensive and hard to scale. The paper is motivated to propose a feasible and cost-effective paradigm to circumvent this, aiming to utilize large-scale, unlabeled video data effectively.
- **Achieving Universal Humanoid Control:** The overarching goal is to move "Towards Universal Humanoid Control." This implies developing a framework that is highly generalizable, capable of performing diverse tasks, and adaptable to various environmental conditions, ultimately leading to more intelligent and versatile humanoid robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Humanoid-VLA proposes a novel framework built on two main stages: Language-Motion Pre-Alignment and Vision-Conditioned Fine-Tuning, complemented by a unique self-supervised data augmentation strategy and integration with a whole-body controller.

- **A. Language-Motion Pre-Alignment:**
 - **Motivation:** To establish a robust foundation for motion generation by aligning non-egocentric human motion data with textual descriptions, learning universal motion patterns and action semantics without relying on egocentric visual input.
 - **Data Acquisition: Self-Supervised Data Augmentation:** This is a core innovation to combat data scarcity. Instead of manual annotation or noisy VLLM outputs, the approach generates pseudo-annotations directly from motion data using self-supervised tasks.
 - **Compositional Motion Quantization:** Body poses are decomposed into five distinct parts (left leg, right leg, torso, left arm, right arm). Each part has its own encoder and codebook, allowing for flexible manipulation at the token level (e.g., masking specific body parts). This facilitates the creation of diverse self-supervised tasks.
 - **Automatic Data Augmentation:** Four types of augmentations are introduced: `<Track>` (joint trajectory following), `<Time>` (motion duration control), `<Occlusion>` (reconstructing masked body parts), and `<State>` (moving between start/end poses). For each task, instructional prompts are automatically generated (e.g., "Missing left arm <Occlusion> motion data. Please complete the motion."), paired with the ground truth motion. This process converts raw motion data into question-answer pairs, significantly expanding the dataset scale and linguistic diversity.
 - **Training:** The training process is split into two stages. First, large-scale, lower-quality data (from videos with pseudo-annotations) establishes initial motion-language alignment. Second, a smaller, high-quality Mocap dataset refines the model for kinematic accuracy. The framework uses a Large Language Model (LLM, specifically Llama3-70B) to map input conditions (language and motion tokens) to generate motion sequences autoregressively. A shared vocabulary unifies motion codebook and language codebook, enabling seamless integration. The objective is to maximize the log-likelihood of the data distribution.

- **B. Vision-Conditioned Fine-Tuning:**
 - **Motivation:** To integrate egocentric visual information, enabling humanoids to understand their environment, react based on autonomous perception, and decide what actions to take.
 - **Approach:** The transformer layers from the pre-alignment phase are copied and frozen. A vision encoder is introduced to process first-person view images. Cross-attention layers are then utilized to fuse the encoded visual features with the language features, creating a unified embedding. This parameter-efficient approach preserves the pre-trained knowledge while allowing dynamic fusion of visual context. The fine-tuning optimizes the model using the same log-likelihood objective as the pre-alignment phase, but with a smaller dataset of real-world motion capture data paired with egocentric visuals.

- **C. Whole-Body Controller Integration:**
 - **Approach:** Once the VLA model generates kinematic motion sequences (15 universal humanoid joint points), a whole-body controller, implemented as a goal-conditioned Reinforcement Learning (RL) policy, maps these human motions onto the 24 joints of a humanoid robot. This policy generates joint torques to control the robot, using a reward strategy based on observation and goal, and optimized via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). This ensures physical plausibility and real-world executability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments comprehensively evaluate Humanoid-VLA across motion generation quality (kinematic fidelity and physical plausibility) and real-world vision integration.

- **A. Evaluation on Motion Generation (Kinematic Fidelity):**
 - **Setup:** Evaluated on HumanML3D (basic locomotion) and Humanoid-S (more complex actions, collected by authors with manual annotations), using 15 common humanoid joints.
 - **Metrics:** FID (Fréchet Inception Distance, lower is better for distribution similarity) and Diversity (higher is better for action variation).
 - **Baselines:** MDM (diffusion-based) and T2M-GPT (transformer-based).
 - **Results:** Humanoid-VLA significantly outperformed baselines. On HumanML3D, it achieved an FID of 0.467, a 47.5% improvement over MDM and 12% over T2M-GPT, demonstrating superior capture of real motion distributions. On Humanoid-S, it showed remarkable motion diversity with a score of 4.466, surpassing MDM by 6%. This validates Humanoid-VLA's effectiveness in high-quality text-to-motion synthesis.
 - **Ablation on Data Augmentation:** The ablation study showed that incorporating large-scale video motion data (even "low-quality" recovered motion) with the self-supervised augmentation strategy improved FID from 0.557 to 0.467 (16% improvement). This strongly validates the significance and effectiveness of the self-supervised data augmentation pipeline in strengthening motion-language alignment and addressing data scarcity.

- **B. Evaluation on Motion Generation (Physical Plausibility):**
 - **Setup:** Assessed in the IsaacGym physics simulator, quantifying the humanoid’s tracking accuracy of model-generated kinematic trajectories.
 - **Metrics:** State-related (Global MPJPE, Procrustes-aligned MPJPE) and Transition-related (Acceleration error, Velocity error). Lower values indicate better performance.
 - **Results:** The RL policy integrated with Humanoid-VLA achieved robust motion imitation with consistently low errors. Mean position errors (E_g_mpjpe) were below 40mm (min 31.07mm), pose accuracy (E_pa_mpjpe) at 1.18mm, acceleration (E_accel) at 27.84mm, and velocity (E_vel) at 14.76mm. This demonstrates smooth and physically consistent motion generation across diverse control conditions.

- **C. Evaluation on Vision Integration (Real-World):**
 - **Setup:** Experiments conducted on a real Unitree G1 robot using an RGB camera for first-person view, across four task categories: upper-body, lower-body, full-body, and object interaction (e.g., approaching targets, kicking a ball, obstacle navigation).
 - **Metric:** Success rate (SR) over 10 tests per task.
 - **Results:** Humanoid-VLA demonstrated strong performance in various real-world object interaction tasks. For instance, "Turn to an object," "Wave to people," and "Punch an obstacle" achieved 10/10 success rates. "Hold an object," "Avoid an obstacle," "Jump over an object," and "Kick a ball" achieved 9/10 success rates. "Dance with a partner" achieved 8/10. These results visually and quantitatively confirm that the VLA model effectively leverages visual information to generate appropriate, context-aware motions for autonomous interaction in physical environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Humanoid-VLA marks a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, primarily due to its novel integration of language understanding, egocentric scene perception, and motion control into a unified framework. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering VLA for Humanoids:** By presenting the "first VLA model for humanoid robots," this work bridges a critical gap. While VLA models have been successfully applied to other robot platforms, adapting them to the complex, whole-body dynamics and diverse interaction needs of humanoids is a non-trivial challenge. Humanoid-VLA demonstrates the feasibility and benefits of such an integration, setting a new benchmark for future research.
- **Enabling Autonomous Interaction:** The core contribution of moving beyond reactive control to autonomous perception and interaction is transformative. Humanoids equipped with Humanoid-VLA can perceive their environment, understand context, and make decisions to interact with objects or navigate obstacles without explicit external commands for every action. This moves humanoids closer to truly intelligent and adaptable agents capable of operating in unstructured, dynamic environments, from service industries to disaster relief.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity through Innovation:** The self-supervised data augmentation strategy is a highly impactful methodological contribution. The prohibitive cost and difficulty of collecting large-scale, synchronized egocentric visual, motion, and language data have been a major bottleneck. By generating pseudo-annotations and intelligently expanding existing datasets (25 times larger than previous work), Humanoid-VLA provides a cost-effective and scalable solution that can be adopted by the broader robotics community. This approach is crucial for training foundation models for complex robotic tasks.
- **Enhanced Generalizability and Adaptability:** The framework's ability to learn universal motion patterns from diverse non-egocentric data and then fine-tune with egocentric visual context leads to more generalizable and context-aware motion generation. This means robots can adapt their movements based on real-time visual input, leading to more robust and flexible behaviors than pre-programmed or purely reactive systems.
- **Foundation for Future Humanoid AI:** Humanoid-VLA lays a robust foundation for developing more sophisticated AI capabilities in humanoids. Its unified system for language, vision, and action paves the way for complex loco-manipulation tasks, multimodal reasoning, and perhaps even human-robot collaboration where natural communication and nuanced understanding are paramount. The acknowledged limitations, such as RL policy robustness and the need for higher-quality real-world data, highlight clear avenues for future research, inviting the community to build upon this work.

In essence, Humanoid-VLA significantly advances humanoid robot capabilities by providing a robust, data-efficient framework for intelligent, context-aware, and autonomous interaction, pushing the boundaries of what is possible with humanoid control.
