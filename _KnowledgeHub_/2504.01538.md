# AI-Newton: A Concept-Driven Physical Law Discovery System without Prior Physical Knowledge

> [!summary] Summary
> AI-Newton, developed at Peking University, autonomously discovers general physical laws and concepts from diverse experimental data without prior physical knowledge. The system rediscovered Newton's Second Law, the Conservation of Energy, the Law of Universal Gravitation, and implicitly the weak equivalence principle across 46 simulated classical mechanics experiments.

## Problem

- Existing AI methods excel at deriving empirical models for specific experiments but struggle to abstract these into generalizable, universal physical laws and concepts, unlike human physicists.
- Current AI systems often rely on pre-existing physical frameworks or produce opaque representations, hindering the interpretability and transferability of discovered knowledge.
- The challenge lies in moving beyond problem-specific model fitting to discover knowledge that is inherently generalizable and universally applicable across diverse physical phenomena.

## Method

- **AI-Newton** integrates an 'experiment base', a 'theory base' (for concepts and laws), and '==plausible reasoning==' within an autonomous workflow to continuously distill physical knowledge.
- It employs a physical Domain-Specific Language (DSL) to autonomously define and register explicit, symbolic, and interpretable physical concepts (e.g., velocity, mass) and laws, categorized as specific or general.
- The system utilizes symbolic regression for specific law discovery and a plausible reasoning mechanism to generalize existing laws by iteratively adding new terms or unifying concepts across different experimental contexts.

## Results

- AI-Newton autonomously rediscovered foundational classical mechanics laws, including Newton's Second Law, the Conservation of Energy (with various potential forms), and the Law of Universal Gravitation.
- The system successfully identified approximately 90 physical concepts and 50 general laws from 46 diverse, noisy classical mechanics experiments, providing a complete explanatory framework for all scenarios.
- It implicitly rediscovered the weak equivalence principle by autonomously verifying the numerical equivalence of gravitational and inertial mass derived from distinct experimental pathways, demonstrating conceptual unification.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - General physical laws and concepts can be autonomously discovered from multi-experiment data without prior physical knowledge through a concept-driven and progressively generalizing AI framework.
> - The integration of explicit concept definition via a DSL and plausible reasoning for law generalization allows for human-interpretable knowledge accumulation and refinement.
> - Statistical analysis and identification of conserved quantities, coupled with controlled-variable analysis, enables the system to extract universal concepts and propose new general laws.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of AI-driven scientific discovery is rapidly advancing, with a long-term goal of enabling autonomous systems to uncover fundamental laws of nature. Current AI approaches have shown remarkable success in specific domains, particularly in deriving empirical models or "specific laws" from individual experiments. These methods broadly fall into two categories: neural network (NN)-based methods and symbolic techniques.

NN-based methods (e.g., Hamiltonian neural networks, physics-informed machine learning) excel at identifying latent features or complex relationships within datasets, often leading to accurate predictions for a given experimental setup. However, their representations can be opaque, making it challenging to extract human-interpretable physical concepts or generalizable laws. Symbolic techniques, such as symbolic regression (e.g., AI Feynman), aim to output explicit mathematical expressions that describe observed data. While these methods can produce interpretable formulas, they typically operate on data from a single experiment and struggle to generalize findings across disparate experimental contexts.

The critical limitation of these existing approaches, as highlighted by the authors, is their focus on "problem-specific model fitting." Human physicists, in contrast, excel at discerning common patterns across different experiments, abstracting these patterns into general concepts (like mass, energy, force) and formulating universal laws (like Newton's second law, conservation of energy) that apply across a vast range of phenomena. The current AI systems have not yet achieved this level of conceptual abstraction and generalization.

AI-Newton directly addresses this gap. It aims to move beyond individual experiment-specific model fitting to the discovery of "knowledge that is inherently generalizable and universally applicable." By focusing on the autonomous proposal of interpretable physical concepts and the progressive generalization of laws across broader domains, AI-Newton seeks to emulate the human scientific discovery process more closely. This work positions itself as a significant advancement towards building AI systems that can uncover unifying and interpretable frameworks, a capability essential for contributing to frontier scientific research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind AI-Newton stems from a fundamental challenge in AI-driven scientific discovery: the disparity between AI's capability to generate empirical models for isolated experiments and human physicists' ability to unearth unifying, generalizable physical laws. While current AI systems can fit specific models to data from a single experiment, they typically fail to synthesize these specific insights into broader, universal principles.

The authors articulate the "ultimate goal of fundamental physics research" as describing a wide range of phenomena through a small number of discovered laws. They note that existing AI methods, despite their successes, "have not yet reached a discovery capacity comparable to that of human physicists" in this regard. Human scientists formulate general models by discerning common patterns and defining meaningful physical concepts across diverse experiments, leading to frameworks like Newtonian mechanics.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of AI-Newton are:

- **Autonomous Discovery of General Physical Laws:** To develop a system that can derive fundamental physical laws directly from raw, multi-experiment data without supervision or prior physical knowledge. This distinguishes it from systems that rely on pre-existing physical frameworks or domain expertise.
- **Concept-Driven Discovery:** To integrate a mechanism for proposing and defining interpretable physical concepts (e.g., velocity, mass, potential energy) as building blocks for constructing laws. These concepts are represented explicitly and symbolically, enhancing interpretability and transferability.
- **Progressive Generalization of Laws:** To enable the system to progressively extend and refine discovered laws, making them applicable to increasingly broader domains and more complex experimental configurations. This involves starting with specific laws and generalizing them through plausible reasoning.
- **Bridging the Gap to Human-Like Discovery:** To evolve AI-driven physics discovery beyond problem-specific model fitting towards systems capable of discovering inherently generalizable and universally applicable knowledge, thereby mimicking the inductive and abstractive reasoning processes of human scientists.

In essence, AI-Newton aims to provide a framework for AI to "extract concepts and general laws from problem-specific models," offering a pathway towards truly autonomous scientific discovery.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AI-Newton operates as a "concept-driven discovery system" based on an autonomous workflow integrating an "experiment base," a "theory base," and "plausible reasoning" as its core mechanism. The system is designed to continuously distill knowledge from experimental data.

#### 3.1. Knowledge Base and Representation

The system comprises two main components:
- **Experiment Base:** This component stores details of physical experiments and their corresponding simulated data generators. Crucially, inputs only include basic information such as physical objects involved, geometric data, experimental parameters, and space-time coordinates. No prior physical concepts like mass or energy are provided. Outputs are simulated data, complete with statistical errors, mimicking real-world experimental noise.
- **Theory Base:** This stores discovered physical knowledge in an explicit, interconnected library of symbols, concepts, and laws.
 - **Concepts Layer:** Initially containing only space-time coordinates, new concepts (e.g., velocity, mass) are autonomously defined and registered using a dedicated physical Domain-Specific Language (DSL). This DSL ensures concepts are explicit, symbolic, and interpretable, contrasting with latent features in neural networks. Concepts are categorized into dynamical (e.g., velocity), intrinsic (e.g., mass, spring constant, defined by measurement procedures), and universal constants (e.g., gravitational constant). Explicitly recording measurement procedures for intrinsic concepts is vital for conceptual consistency across experiments.
 - **Laws Layer:** Stores discovered physical laws, including conserved quantities and dynamical equations. Laws are distinguished as *specific* (valid for one experiment) and *general* (valid across diverse experiments), with AI-Newton specifically targeting the latter.

#### 3.2. Autonomous Discovery Workflow

The workflow is driven by "plausible reasoning," a method inspired by human scientific inference, where conclusions are contextually reasonable rather than universally certain.

1. **Trial Initiation and Selection:** Each trial begins by selecting an experiment and a few existing concepts from the theory base. This selection is managed by a **recommendation engine** that combines a UCB-inspired value function with a dynamically adapted neural network, balancing exploration of new knowledge with exploitation of existing successful paths.
2. **Era-Control Strategy:** To ensure foundational knowledge is built before tackling complexity, an "era-control" strategy limits the wall-clock time for trials within an era. If no new knowledge is acquired after sufficient trials, the system advances to a new era with an exponentially increased time limit, progressively focusing on more complex problems.
3. **Law Exploration:**
 - **Specific Law Discovery:** New specific laws are found by searching for relations among selected concepts within an allowed operational space, using **Symbolic Regression (SR)**. AI-Newton's SR combines direct instantiation-verification with PCA-based differential polynomial regression.
 - **General Law Generalization (Plausible Reasoning):** A core innovation involves generalizing existing laws. If a general law applies to many experiments but fails in the current one, AI-Newton attempts to derive a modified, valid law by adding simple terms via SR. For example, kinetic energy conservation (elastic collisions) can be generalized by adding potential energy terms (elastic, gravitational) when applied to spring systems or systems under gravity.
4. **Knowledge Simplification:** To prevent knowledge explosion, AI-Newton simplifies discovered laws into minimal representations using the Rosenfeld Gröbner algorithm from differential algebra.
5. **Controlled-Variable Analysis:** The system numerically identifies dependencies of relations on physical objects and experimental parameters, which informs classification.
6. **Concept and General Law Extraction:**
 - **New Concepts:** A conserved quantity identified in an experiment is extracted as a new, broader concept, suggesting wider utility.
 - **New General Laws:** Specific laws that are found to hold across multiple experiments are proposed as new general laws.
7. **Theory Base Update:** All newly acquired concepts and laws are registered and updated in the theory base, enriching the system's understanding of physics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AI-Newton was rigorously evaluated on a diverse and comprehensive set of **46 predefined classical mechanics experiments**. The dataset systematically varied in complexity, involving different physical objects (balls, springs, inclined planes) and system configurations, from isolated free motion to highly coupled and multi-dimensional problems. These experiments covered the three primary forces in Newtonian mechanics: elastic forces, gravity near Earth's surface, and universal gravitational forces. To simulate realistic conditions, all data were generated by solving differential equations and deliberately incorporated Gaussian-distributed errors, confirming the system's robustness to noise.

**Computational Performance:** The proof-of-concept implementation ran on a powerful computing platform (Intel Xeon Platinum 8370C with NVIDIA A40 GPU, utilizing 64 cores for parallel processing). Over an average runtime of 48 hours and up to 1200 trials, the system demonstrated remarkable knowledge discovery capabilities.

**Key Discoveries:**
On average across test cases, AI-Newton successfully identified approximately **90 physical concepts** and **50 general laws**. Crucially, it autonomously rediscovered foundational and universal laws of classical mechanics, including:
- **Newton's Second Law (F=ma)**
- **Conservation of Energy**, encompassing kinetic, elastic potential, gravitational potential (both near Earth's surface and universal).
- **The Law of Universal Gravitation.**

These discovered laws, along with their associated concepts, provided a complete explanatory framework for all 46 experiments, spanning systems from simple setups to complex, high-degree-of-freedom configurations like coupled oscillations of chained 2-ball-2-spring systems on inclined planes and rotational dynamics of 4-ball-4-spring systems.

**Discovery Progression and Conceptual Unification:**
The statistical analysis of concept discovery timing revealed an incremental progression (illustrated in Fig. 3). AI-Newton first identified simpler concepts before advancing to more complex ones. For instance:
- **Gravitational acceleration (g)** was defined as a constant by analyzing free-fall or projectile motion, where vertical acceleration of a ball remained invariant.
- **Conservation of Kinetic Energy (T)** was initially discovered in elastic collision experiments. Through plausible reasoning, when this law failed in other contexts (e.g., spring systems), AI-Newton progressively introduced new terms—elastic potential energy (V_k), then gravitational potential energy near Earth's surface (V_g), and finally universal gravitational potential energy (V_G)—to restore conservation, ultimately formulating the complete **law of energy conservation**. Newton's second law followed a similar progressive generalization.

**Discovery of the Weak Equivalence Principle:**
A particularly striking finding was AI-Newton's independent discovery and unification of the concept of 'mass'. The system derived 'mass' through two distinct experimental pathways:
1. From the static elongation of a spring under gravity, defining **gravitational mass (m_g)**.
2. From the oscillation of a horizontal spring-mass system, defining **inertial mass (m_i)**.
Remarkably, AI-Newton then autonomously verified the numerical equivalence of m_g and m_i from the raw data. This represents an implicit rediscovery of the **weak equivalence principle**, a cornerstone of general relativity, demonstrating the system's ability to unify concepts from disparate physical contexts without explicit prior knowledge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AI-Newton represents a significant advancement in the field of AI-driven scientific discovery, particularly in its departure from existing methods focused on individual, problem-specific models. Its core contributions and potential impact are multifaceted:

#### 5.1. Advancement Towards Autonomous, Human-Like Scientific Discovery

The primary significance lies in AI-Newton's ability to autonomously derive general physical laws from diverse, noisy multi-experiment data without any prior physical knowledge or supervision. This capability transcends current AI limitations that typically require domain-specific knowledge or are restricted to extracting specific empirical models. By emulating human physicists' capacity for conceptual abstraction and generalization, AI-Newton paves the way for truly autonomous scientific discovery.

#### 5.2. Novelty in Methodology

The paper's key methodological innovations contribute significantly:
- **Concept-Driven Approach:** The system's ability to propose and utilize interpretable physical concepts (e.g., mass, velocity, energy potentials) as explicit, symbolic building blocks for laws is crucial. This makes the discovered knowledge not only accurate but also human-understandable and transferable, addressing the interpretability challenge of many AI models.
- **Plausible Reasoning for Generalization:** The iterative process of extending existing general laws by adding new terms through plausible reasoning allows for a scalable and progressive discovery process. This mechanism mirrors how human scientists refine hypotheses and expand the applicability of theories.
- **Robust Knowledge Representation:** The use of a physical Domain-Specific Language (DSL) ensures conceptual consistency and allows for seamless knowledge transfer across different problem contexts, a critical requirement for discovering universal laws.

#### 5.3. Rediscovery of Foundational Laws and Principles

The successful rediscovery of foundational laws like Newton's second law, the conservation of energy (including its various potential forms), and the law of universal gravitation from raw, simulated experimental data is a strong validation of AI-Newton's capabilities. The autonomous verification of the numerical equivalence of inertial and gravitational mass (the weak equivalence principle) further underscores the system's profound ability to uncover deep physical relationships.

#### 5.4. Potential for Frontier Scientific Research

While AI-Newton was initially applied to rediscover known physics, the framework's inherent design for concept-driven generalization makes it highly promising for future applications in frontier science. The authors envision integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) to augment components like the DSL, recommendation engine, and plausible reasoning. This integration could provide the system with access to vast existing scientific knowledge, enabling more informed and efficient discovery processes for unknown phenomena or in fields where current theoretical understanding is incomplete.

In summary, AI-Newton represents a critical step towards building AI systems that can not only analyze data but also synthesize fundamental knowledge, define abstract concepts, and formulate universal laws—capabilities previously considered exclusive to human intellect. This work has the potential to accelerate scientific discovery and contribute to our understanding of the universe in unprecedented ways.
