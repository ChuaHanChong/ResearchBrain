# AI-Newton: A Concept-Driven Physical Law Discovery System without Prior Physical Knowledge

> [!summary] Summary
> AI-Newton, developed at Peking University, autonomously discovers general physical laws and concepts from diverse experimental data without prior physical knowledge. The system rediscovered Newton's Second Law, the Conservation of Energy, the Law of Universal Gravitation, and implicitly the weak equivalence principle across 46 simulated classical mechanics experiments.

## Problem

- Existing AI methods excel at deriving empirical models for specific experiments but struggle to abstract these into generalizable, universal physical laws and concepts, unlike human physicists.
- Current AI systems often rely on pre-existing physical frameworks or produce opaque representations, hindering the interpretability and transferability of discovered knowledge.
- The challenge lies in moving beyond problem-specific model fitting to discover knowledge that is inherently generalizable and universally applicable across diverse physical phenomena.

## Method

- **AI-Newton** integrates an 'experiment base', a 'theory base' (for concepts and laws), and '==plausible reasoning==' within an autonomous workflow to continuously distill physical knowledge.
- It employs a physical Domain-Specific Language (DSL) to autonomously define and register explicit, symbolic, and interpretable physical concepts (e.g., velocity, mass) and laws, categorized as specific or general.
- The system utilizes symbolic regression for specific law discovery and a plausible reasoning mechanism to generalize existing laws by iteratively adding new terms or unifying concepts across different experimental contexts.

## Results

- AI-Newton autonomously rediscovered foundational classical mechanics laws, including Newton's Second Law, the Conservation of Energy (with various potential forms), and the Law of Universal Gravitation.
- The system successfully identified approximately 90 physical concepts and 50 general laws from 46 diverse, noisy classical mechanics experiments, providing a complete explanatory framework for all scenarios.
- It implicitly rediscovered the weak equivalence principle by autonomously verifying the numerical equivalence of gravitational and inertial mass derived from distinct experimental pathways, demonstrating conceptual unification.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - General physical laws and concepts can be autonomously discovered from multi-experiment data without prior physical knowledge through a concept-driven and progressively generalizing AI framework.
> - The integration of explicit concept definition via a DSL and plausible reasoning for law generalization allows for human-interpretable knowledge accumulation and refinement.
> - Statistical analysis and identification of conserved quantities, coupled with controlled-variable analysis, enables the system to extract universal concepts and propose new general laws.
