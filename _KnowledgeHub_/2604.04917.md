# Vero: An Open RL Recipe for General Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Princeton University researchers introduce Vero, a fully open reinforcement learning recipe and associated VLM family for general visual reasoning, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance among 8B-parameter models on a new 30-benchmark suite. The method consistently improved various base models' performance by 3.6 to 5.3 points across six diverse visual reasoning categories using a curated 600,000-sample dataset and task-routed reward functions.

## Problem

- Lack of transparency in the development of state-of-the-art vision-language models (VLMs) that leverage proprietary RL pipelines, non-public data, and undisclosed reward designs.
- Challenges in applying reinforcement learning (RL) across a diverse range of visual reasoning tasks, including task interference, weak skill transfer, and optimization imbalances.
- Existing open RL efforts for VLMs often specialize in narrow task subsets, limiting their generalizability across broad visual understanding tasks.

## Method

- Developed **Vero**, a fully open-weight VLM family and a transparent ==single-stage RL training recipe== based on ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==.
- Curated **Vero-600K**, a large-scale dataset of 600,000 samples from 59 datasets, covering six broad visual reasoning task categories (STEM, Spatial & Action, Knowledge & Recognition, Chart & OCR, Grounding, Counting & Search, Captioning & Instruction Following).
- Designed a ==multi-route reward system== with ten task-specific functions (string match, numeric tolerance, ==bounding box IoU==, ==LLM-as-judge==) to handle heterogeneous answer formats and evaluation criteria.

## Results

- **Vero-Qwen3I-8B** and **Vero-Qwen3T-8B** achieved average scores of **66.0** and **65.9** on the VeroEval suite, positioning them as top performers among evaluated 8B-parameter VLMs.
- The Vero training recipe consistently improved performance across four different base models, showing average gains of **3.6 to 5.3** points over 30 benchmarks, and outperformed proprietary RL-trained MiMo-VL-7B-RL on **3 out of 6** category averages.
- Demonstrated substantial performance improvements across all six task categories, with notable gains on benchmarks like ChartQA Pro (**+15.9**), MMMU-Pro Vision (**+15.1**), and MMERealWorld (**+10.7**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A uniform mixture weighting across diverse task categories in the training data is crucial for achieving broad general visual reasoning capabilities and preventing negative cross-task transfer.
> - Expressive, task-routed reward functions are necessary for effective reinforcement learning across heterogeneous answer formats in multi-domain visual reasoning tasks.
> - Different visual task categories elicit distinct cognitive behavioral profiles and skill repertoires within VLMs, highlighting the multi-faceted nature of visual reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) is progressing towards models capable of reasoning across a wide spectrum of visual tasks, encompassing domains like chart interpretation, scientific analysis, spatial understanding, and open-ended queries. Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly methods such as Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), has emerged as a significant driver in enhancing multimodal reasoning capabilities. Recent frontier models, including proprietary systems like GPT-5 and Kimi K2.5, as well as leading open-weight models such such as Qwen3-VL, demonstrate substantial improvements attributed to RL.

However, a critical limitation in the current landscape is the proprietary nature of the RL pipelines, training data, and reward function designs employed by many of the most capable visual reasoning models. This lack of transparency impedes systematic study, independent reproduction, and extension of the observed performance gains. While some open-weight models release their weights, their associated RL training methodologies and datasets often remain undisclosed. Existing fully open RL initiatives, such as OpenMMReasoner and VL-Rethinker, typically focus on narrow domains, such as visual math, which limits their generalizability across diverse visual tasks.

This paper addresses this gap by seeking to establish a fully open and transparent RL recipe for training broadly capable visual reasoners. It investigates whether a single-stage RL approach, combined with a diverse and high-quality dataset and carefully designed rewards, can achieve broad visual reasoning capabilities, thus making the advancements in multimodal reasoning more accessible for scientific investigation and community development. The work positions itself as a contribution to open science, aiming to provide a reproducible foundation for understanding and advancing VLM capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and openly release Vero, a family of VLMs trained using a fully transparent Reinforcement Learning (RL) recipe, demonstrating performance that matches or exceeds existing open-weight models across a diverse array of visual reasoning tasks.

The motivation behind this work stems from several key challenges and opportunities in the VLM research domain:

- **Addressing Opacity in Frontier VLM Development:** Many high-performing VLMs leverage proprietary RL pipelines, undisclosed training data, and non-public reward designs. This lack of transparency makes it challenging for the scientific community to study, reproduce, or extend these advancements. The researchers are motivated to provide a "fully open RL recipe" to foster reproducibility, allow for systematic study of design choices, and promote a deeper understanding of what drives multimodal reasoning performance.
- **Overcoming Narrow Task Specialization in Open RL Efforts:** Existing open RL initiatives for VLMs often concentrate on specific domains, such as visual math. The paper explicitly highlights that training on a single task category does not reliably generalize to other visual capabilities, a finding supported by their own experiments on task performance and chain-of-thought behaviors. A key motivation is to build a generalist visual reasoner rather than a specialist, demonstrating that broad capabilities can be achieved within an open framework.
- **Investigating the Role of Data Diversity and Reward Design in Multi-task RL:** Applying RL across heterogeneous visual reasoning tasks presents challenges such as interference, weak transfer, and optimization imbalance. The authors aim to systematically investigate whether these challenges can be mitigated through careful design of the training data distribution and reward functions. This includes exploring dataset selection, sample filtering, task-balanced mixing strategies, and multi-task reward formulations.
- **Demonstrating Efficacy of a Single-Stage, Open Recipe:** The research seeks to establish that a "single-stage RL recipe with diverse and high-quality data suffices" to train a broadly capable visual reasoner. This implicitly challenges the necessity of complex, multi-staged, or proprietary pipelines, demonstrating that fundamental principles of data curation and reward shaping can drive significant gains.
- **Contributing to Open Science:** A foundational motivation is to make all data, code, and models publicly available. This aligns with a broader movement in AI research to democratize access to frontier models and methodologies, enabling wider participation in research and development.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research centers on developing a fully open RL recipe to train VLMs capable of general visual reasoning, anchored by a new dataset, Vero-600K, and a comprehensive evaluation suite, VeroEval.

#### 3.1. RL Algorithm and Training

The RL algorithm builds upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and incorporates advancements from GSPO. Key aspects include:
- **Sequence-level Ratio:** Replacing independent per-token importance ratios with a sequence-level ratio, formed using the sequence-average log-probability difference.
- **Asymmetric Clipping:** Utilizing an asymmetric clipping mechanism (ε_high > ε_low) to manage policy updates.
- **KL Penalty Removal:** Omitting the KL penalty to allow for less-restricted policy updates.
- **Soft Overlong Penalty:** Implementing a linear penalty for excessively long responses within a defined buffer zone.
- **Training Framework:** All models are trained for 2,343 steps using VeRL with FSDP2 on 8 GPUs. Qwen models are trained in fp16 for stability, while MiMo-VL uses bf16.

#### 3.2. Training Data: Vero-600K Curation Pipeline

Vero-600K is a 600,000-sample multi-task RL training set derived from 59 datasets, organized into six task categories, each with 100,000 samples to balance learning. The categories are: Chart & OCR; STEM; Spatial & Action; Knowledge & Recognition; Grounding, Counting & Search; and Captioning & Instruction Following. The curation involves a multi-stage pipeline:

- **Dataset Sourcing and Selection:**
 - **Candidate Pool:** Initiated with over 250 candidate datasets from instruction-tuning and RL collections.
 - **Heuristic Selection:** Filtered out datasets with fewer than 1,000 examples, average image resolution below 200,000 pixels (with five low-res exceptions), or binary questions (to mitigate guessing).
 - **Manual Selection:** 59 datasets were retained after manual inspection for correctness (<5% error), unambiguity (single verifiable answer), and verifiability (compatibility with reward functions). Some questions were rewritten for clarity or high-error subsets were dropped.

- **Data Filtering (Individual Samples):**
 - **Question Filtering:** Utilized Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct to remove ambiguous, image-irrelevant, or unverifiable questions based on five criteria: relevance, ambiguity, language, verifiability, and numeric precision. Any single "true" flag led to sample removal.
 - **Answer Filtering:** Normalized ground-truth answers using text-only Qwen3-235B-A22B-Instruct for stable reward computation. This involved stripping units, converting to decimals for numeric answers, canonicalizing multiple-choice letters, and removing multi-value or non-reducible symbolic expressions. This step was not applied to Spatial & Action or Grounding tasks due to their already standardized answer formats.

- **Data Mixtures:**
 - Investigated four task category weighting schemes for batch sampling: uniform, difficulty-weighted (inverse accuracy), image-size-weighted, and reasoning-length-weighted.
 - Uniform sampling, where each category contributed an equal number of samples per batch, was found to yield the best overall performance and was adopted for Vero-600K.

#### 3.3. Reward Formulation

The total reward for a response `y` is a weighted sum: `R(y, y*) = (1 - α) R_acc(y, y*) + α R_fmt(y) + R_overlong(y)`, with `α = 0.2`.

- **`R_overlong` (Overlong Penalty):** A linear penalty for responses exceeding a maximum token limit, ramping up in a buffer zone (`B = 2048`, `L_max = max_tokens`, `λ = 1.0`).
- **`R_fmt` (Format Reward):** Encourages adherence to the `<think>...</think><answer>...</answer>` format with non-empty thinking content. For discrete symbolic answers, a single `\boxed{...}` in the answer block is required for full `R_fmt = 1`; absence or multiple boxed expressions reduce it to `0.5`.
- **`R_acc` (Multi-task Accuracy Reward):** Ten task-routed reward functions handle diverse answer formats:
 - **Binary:** String match, Multiple choice, Numeric (parsed via MATH-VERIFY with optional tolerance), List string match, Pointing (point-in-box).
 - **Graded:** Ordering (partial reward for correct set/wrong order), Web action (weighted match on JSON fields), Grounding (IoU/F1 with Hungarian matching for bounding boxes), Instruction following (proportion of satisfied programmatic constraints), LLM-as-judge (Qwen3-32B scores response against reference on a 1-10 scale, normalized to, with explicit penalties for reward hacking).

#### 3.4. Evaluation Suite: VeroEval

A comprehensive evaluation suite of 30 benchmarks was curated, spanning all six task categories. Benchmarks were selected based on three criteria: difficulty (room for improvement for frontier models), annotation quality (well-defined protocols, reliable ground-truth), and intra-category diversity (testing complementary sub-skills).

#### 3.5. Ablation Studies and Behavioral Analysis

Systematic ablations were performed to analyze the impact of dataset selection, filtering, mixture strategies, reward design, and different RL algorithms (DAPO, GRPO, GSPO). Additionally, reasoning traces were analyzed at two levels:
- **High-Level Cognitive Behaviors:** Using an augmented framework (Kargupta et al., 2025) of 34 cognitive behaviors (28 text-centric, 6 visual-analysis specific) to assess presence rates across tasks.
- **Low-Level Recurring Skills:** Extracting and clustering fine-grained skills from reasoning traces, and training a logistic regression probe to classify task categories based on skill embeddings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the efficacy of the Vero recipe and the nature of visual reasoning capabilities:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance in Open Models:** Vero models demonstrated strong performance across the 30 benchmarks in VeroEval. Vero-Qwen35-9B achieved an overall average of 74.4, improving by +2.9 points over its initial model and surpassing it on 25 out of 30 benchmarks. Among 8B models, Vero-Qwen3I-8B reached 66.1 overall, outperforming Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking by +3.8 points without additional distillation. Notably, applying RL directly to the pretrained-only Qwen3.5-9B-Base resulted in a +12.9 overall gain, reaching 73.0, positioning it as the second-highest score.
- **Consistent Gains Across Diverse Initial Models:** The Vero training recipe consistently improved the performance of six different initial models, with overall gains ranging from +2.9 to +5.4 points over five post-trained models. These improvements were distributed across all six task categories, indicating that the recipe fosters broad capabilities rather than narrow specialization.
- **Superiority of Vero-600K Dataset:** Under controlled comparisons with identical RL settings, Vero-600K consistently outperformed three prior open RL datasets (ViRL-39k, OpenMMReasoner-74k, LLaVA-OV-1.5-RL-70k) throughout training. It achieved the highest score on five of six categories by the end of training, showing competitive performance on STEM despite its broad focus.
- **Data Diversity Mitigates Negative Transfer:** Ablation studies showed that training on individual task categories often led to neutral or negative transfer on non-target tasks. For example, training on non-captioning tasks severely degraded Captioning & Instruction Following performance. Conversely, diverse task mixing, even with the same computational budget, eliminated negative cross-task transfer and achieved positive gains across all categories on both Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen3-VL base models. Broader exposure to the mixed training distribution showed continued gains over longer training periods.
- **Impact of Reward Design:** The multi-route, task-routed reward design significantly outperformed a simpler, unified `math_verify` reward baseline across all task categories, with a substantial +5.4 point improvement in overall average. This highlights the necessity of expressive reward functions tailored to heterogeneous answer formats in multi-task RL.
- **RL Algorithm Effectiveness:** GSPO demonstrated higher average scores (54.7) and maintained more stable entropy during training compared to GRPO (54.3) and DAPO (54.3), suggesting better exploration capacity and prevention of premature policy collapse.
- **Visual Chat Quality Preservation:** Explicitly including open-ended tasks and LLM-judge rewards was found to be critical for preserving and improving visual chat quality. Without these components, RL led to a collapse in performance on open-ended tasks, producing terse answers. Judge guidelines effectively mitigated reward hacking by penalizing self-evaluative language.
- **Distinct Chain-of-Thought Behaviors:** Analysis of reasoning traces revealed that each task category elicits a distinct cognitive behavioral profile and skill repertoire. For instance, STEM tasks showed elevated backtracking, while grounding tasks suppressed introspective behaviors in favor of directed visual search. Spatial & Action, Chart & OCR, and STEM tasks generally induced much longer reasoning responses compared to Grounding, Counting & Search or Knowledge & Recognition. The skill-level analysis, utilizing a logistic regression probe, confirmed that skill distributions are task-category-specific, with an overall accuracy of 0.77.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on Vero presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of Vision-Language Models and broader AI research:

- **Advancing Open Science and Reproducibility:** By releasing all data, code, and models, Vero addresses a critical challenge in VLM research: the opacity of proprietary RL pipelines. This transparency facilitates independent verification, systematic study of design choices, and open extension of the work, thereby accelerating scientific progress and fostering a more collaborative research environment. It establishes a reproducible foundation for future research in visual reasoning.
- **Redefining Multi-task RL for General Visual Reasoning:** The central finding that a simple, single-stage RL pipeline, when coupled with a diverse, carefully filtered data mixture and task-routed rewards, can achieve broad visual reasoning capabilities is significant. It suggests that data diversity and thoughtful distribution design are as crucial, if not more, than algorithmic complexity alone for multi-task RL. This insight recasts multi-task RL as a problem of effective distribution design, offering new directions for tackling the challenges of interference and negative transfer.
- **Mechanistic Understanding of VLM Reasoning:** The detailed behavioral analyses, at both high-level cognitive behaviors and low-level skills, provide empirical evidence that visual reasoning in VLMs is not monolithic. Instead, it comprises distinct, task-dependent behavioral modes. This deeper mechanistic understanding allows researchers to move beyond black-box evaluations and diagnose why models succeed or fail, which is crucial for developing more robust and reliable AI systems. It suggests that different tasks elicit different "task sets" or control policies within the model, akin to human cognitive processes.
- **Foundation for Next-Generation Generalist VLMs:** The demonstration that broad capability emerges from balanced exposure to distinct behavioral modes provides a blueprint for developing truly generalist VLMs that can dynamically adapt their reasoning strategies across a wide array of tasks. This contrasts with previous efforts that often resulted in specialist models with limited generalization.
- **Practical Implications for VLM Development:** The detailed methodology for data curation (sourcing, multi-stage filtering, and mixing) and reward function design offers practical guidelines for practitioners and researchers building multimodal systems. The emphasis on maintaining visual chat quality during RL training is particularly relevant for developing user-friendly conversational AI assistants.
- **Inspiration for Cognitive Science Parallels:** The observed task-specific differences in reasoning lengths, cognitive profiles, and skill repertoires draw informative parallels to human multi-task reasoning and cognitive control theories. This interdisciplinary connection could inspire further research at the intersection of AI and cognitive science, exploring how AI models learn and execute diverse cognitive functions.
- **Catalyst for Future Research:** The limitations acknowledged by the authors, such as the optimal taxonomy of task categories or the causal mechanisms of behavior improvement, open avenues for future investigation. The work encourages further exploration of larger models, more diverse task sets (e.g., video, multi-turn interactions), and deeper causal analyses of reasoning behaviors.
