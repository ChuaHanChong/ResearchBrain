# ProVision: Programmatically Scaling Vision-centric Instruction Data for Multimodal Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Washington and Salesforce Research developed PROVISION, a programmatic system for generating over 10 million high-quality, vision-centric instruction data points for multimodal language models. This data significantly improved MLM performance on various vision-centric benchmarks, showing up to an 8% increase in specific tasks.

## Problem

- Multimodal Language Models (MLMs) lack sufficient high-quality, vision-centric instruction data for effective training.
- Existing data generation methods using Large Language Models (LLMs) or MLMs are prone to hallucinations, lack interpretability, are expensive, and may have licensing issues.
- MLMs often struggle with fine-grained visual reasoning tasks like object counting, spatial relations, and depth estimation.

## Method

- PROVISION introduces a programmatic pipeline that leverages ==augmented scene graphs==—structured symbolic representations of images including objects, attributes, relations, segmentation, and depth.
- Human-written ==instruction generators==, comprising 24 single-image and 14 multi-image types with hundreds of templates, systematically synthesize diverse question-answer pairs from these scene graphs.
- An automated pipeline integrates state-of-the-art models (e.g., YOLO-world, SAM-2, LLaVA-1.5-13B, Osprey, Depth Anything V2) to generate ==augmented scene graphs== for images lacking manual annotations, enabling large-scale data creation.

## Results

- Instruction tuning LLaVA-1.5-7B with single-image PROVISION data showed up to a **7%** improvement on CVBench's 2D split and **8%** on its 3D split, along with a **3%** increase on QBench2, RealWorldQA, and MMMU.
- Multi-image PROVISION data led to an **8%** improvement in LLaVA-1.5-7B performance on the Mantis-Eval benchmark.
- Incorporating PROVISION data into both pre-training and instruction tuning stages of xGen-MM-4B models resulted in the best overall performance, averaging a **1.6%** improvement across 11 benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Programmatic data generation offers enhanced interpretability, factual accuracy, scalability, and cost-effectiveness compared to purely generative model-based approaches.
> - While data derived from human-annotated scene graphs generally performs better, large quantities of model-generated scene graph data can achieve comparable performance for single-image tasks.
> - Optimal MLM performance is achieved through a synergistic approach, incorporating PROVISION data in both pre-training and instruction tuning stages, and utilizing a mix of multiple-choice and short-answer formats.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Language Models (MLMs): the scarcity and limitations of high-quality, vision-centric instruction data. MLMs like LLaVA and InstructBLIP have demonstrated remarkable capabilities, largely fueled by vast amounts of multimodal data. However, for these models to effectively understand and respond to complex, image-based queries, specialized "visual instruction data" is indispensable.

Existing practices for generating such data primarily rely on powerful Large Language Models (LLMs) or MLMs themselves. While effective to some extent, these methods face several inherent limitations that PROVISION aims to mitigate:
1. **Hallucinations:** LLMs/MLMs are prone to generating factually inaccurate content, which undermines the reliability of the instruction data and is difficult to detect and correct. This is a well-known issue across the LLM domain.
2. **Lack of Interpretability and Control:** The generation process using black-box LLMs/MLMs makes it difficult to understand how outputs are produced or to precisely control and customize the generated data.
3. **Scalability and Cost:** Relying on powerful LLMs/MLMs, especially through API usage, can be prohibitively expensive and slow, hindering large-scale data generation.
4. **Licensing Issues:** Data generated by proprietary LLMs/MLMs might come with restrictive licensing terms, preventing its free use for training other models.

PROVISION proposes a **complementary, programmatic approach** that leverages **scene graphs** as structured, symbolic representations of images, combined with **human-written programs**. This positions the work as a novel alternative to purely generative model-based data synthesis. It builds upon established computer vision techniques (object detection, segmentation, relation extraction, depth estimation) and symbolic AI principles (scene graphs, programmatic rules) to create a more interpretable, controllable, and scalable data generation pipeline.

The paper directly contributes to the growing body of research on **synthetic data for MLMs**, but distinguishes itself by focusing on a procedural, factually grounded method rather than relying solely on the generative capabilities of other large models. It shares common ground with prior programmatic approaches used for visual question answering benchmarks (e.g., GQA, AGQA), but extends this philosophy to *data generation for training*, aiming to produce diverse instruction data adaptable to any image source. By focusing on vision-centric tasks (depth, counting, localization, etc.), it also addresses a known weakness of many MLMs, which often excel at high-level semantic understanding but underperform in fine-grained visual reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **develop a programmatic and scalable system for synthesizing high-quality, vision-centric instruction data for training Multimodal Language Models (MLMs)**. This system aims to overcome the limitations inherent in existing LLM/MLM-driven data generation approaches.

The key motivations driving this work are:
1. **Enhanced Interpretability and Controllability:** By employing scene graphs as explicit symbolic representations of image semantics and utilizing human-written programs, the authors seek to introduce transparency into the data creation process. This allows for clear interpretation of how questions and answers are derived and offers precise control over the type and style of generated instructions, reducing the unpredictability of end-to-end models.
2. **Ensuring Factual Accuracy and Mitigating Hallucinations:** The programmatic approach, grounded in the explicit information within scene graphs, aims to produce instructions and answers that are factually accurate, provided the underlying scene graphs are correct. This directly addresses the prevalent issue of hallucinations in LLM/MLM-generated content.
3. **Achieving Scalability and Cost-Effectiveness:** Shifting the workload from expensive, powerful LLMs/MLMs to programmatic generation enables a more efficient and cost-effective solution for creating vast amounts of instruction data. This is crucial for training increasingly large MLMs that demand enormous datasets.
4. **Avoiding Licensing Constraints:** By not relying on proprietary LLM/MLM outputs, the programmatic method bypasses potential licensing restrictions, allowing the generated data to be freely used for model training and research.
5. **Boosting MLM Performance on Vision-Centric Tasks:** A core motivation is to provide MLMs with diverse training data specifically tailored to improve their performance on fine-grained visual reasoning tasks, such as object counting, attribute identification, spatial relation understanding, segmentation, and depth estimation, where current MLMs often struggle despite strong high-level semantic understanding.
6. **Creating a Versatile and Extensible System:** The design aims for a flexible framework that allows users to easily expand the types of instruction data by adding new programs or customizing existing templates, adapting to evolving research needs.

In essence, PROVISION seeks to provide a reliable, transparent, and resource-efficient method for generating the high-quality instructional "fuel" necessary to power the next generation of capable and factually grounded MLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PROVISION system introduces a novel programmatic pipeline for generating vision-centric instruction data, fundamentally built upon the concept of augmented scene graphs and human-written data generators.

**Core Idea:**
The central tenet is to represent images as **augmented scene graphs** – structured symbolic representations that encode objects, their attributes, relationships, segmentation, and depth information. These scene graphs then serve as input for **human-written programs** (instruction generators) that systematically synthesize diverse question-answer pairs.

**Augmented Scene Graph Definition:**
The paper extends the standard scene graph representation (e.g., from Visual Genome) by incorporating additional spatial information. For an image `x` with `N` objects, the augmented scene graph `G = (V, E)` includes:
- **Nodes (V):** Represent objects, each with a bounding box, label, segmentation mask, list of attributes, and a depth annotation.
- **Edges (E):** Represent relationships between object pairs.

**Instruction Data Generation:**
PROVISION implements two main categories of instruction generators:

1. **Single-Image Instruction Data Generators (24 types):**
 - These generators transform an augmented scene graph of a single image into thousands of high-level perceptual question-answer pairs.
 - They utilize hundreds of pre-defined templates that systematically integrate annotations.
 - The queries cover diverse aspects like object existence ("How many helmets are there?"), attributes ("What are the attributes of the human?"), relations ("How are human and bat related?"), segmentation ("Which point... belong to the same object...?"), and depth ("Determine the closer object...").
 - Examples provided include `ExistsObjectGenerator`, `MostObjectGenerator`, `AttributeBBoxGenerator`, `CloserObjectGenerator`, etc.

2. **Multi-Image Instruction Data Generators (14 types):**
 - These generators take multiple augmented scene graphs as input to create question-answer pairs that span across images.
 - They facilitate more complex cross-image reasoning tasks.
 - Categories include:
 - **Selection:** e.g., "Which image has the most instances of human?"
 - **Comparison:** e.g., "What are the objects common in image 1 and image 2?"
 - **Aggregation:** e.g., "What is the number of human in all images?"

The system is designed to be highly versatile and extensible, allowing users to program additional data generators and customize templates.

**Scene Graph Generation Pipeline (for unannotated images):**
Recognizing that not all datasets come with human-annotated scene graphs, PROVISION includes an automated pipeline to generate augmented scene graphs for any given image. This pipeline integrates state-of-the-art, openly accessible models:
1. **Object Detection:** **YOLO-world** is used to identify bounding boxes and labels for all objects in an image.
2. **Image Segmentation:** **SAM-2** takes the image and detected bounding boxes to generate pixel-wise segmentation masks.
3. **Attribute Detection:** A fine-tuned **LLaVA-1.5-13B** model (trained on the LSA dataset) is used to predict attributes for each cropped object.
4. **Relation Detection:** A fine-tuned **Osprey model** (trained on segmentations) is employed to identify relations between pairs of objects. The generated relations are then grounded by comparing them to a pre-defined relation library.
5. **Depth Estimation:** **Depth Anything V2** generates pixel-wise depth annotations for the entire image, which are then used to infer object depths.

**PROVISION-10M Dataset Construction:**
The methodology culminates in the creation of **PROVISION-10M**, a dataset of over 10 million instruction data points:
- **Visual Genome (VG-S, VG-M):** Manually annotated scene graphs from Visual Genome are augmented with depth and segmentation (using Depth Anything V2 and SAM-2), yielding 1.5 million single-image (VG-S) and 4.2 million multi-image (VG-M) instructions.
- **DataComp (DC-S, DC-M):** For 120,000 high-resolution images from DataComp, the automated scene graph generation pipeline is used to create augmented scene graphs. These then generate 2.3 million single-image (DC-S) and 4.2 million multi-image (DC-M) instructions.
- All instructions are stored in both **multiple-choice and short-answer formats**.

**Experimental Setup:**
The effectiveness of PROVISION-10M is evaluated by training various MLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B, Mantis-SigLIP-8B, xGen-MM-4B) under different conditions:
- **Augmentation vs. Replacement:** Adding PROVISION data to existing datasets or replacing a portion of them.
- **Data Format:** Testing multiple-choice, short-answer, and a half-half mix.
- **Data Scale:** Varying the ratio of PROVISION data.
- **Training Stages:** Incorporating data in pre-training, instruction tuning (fine-tuning), or both.
- **Benchmarks:** Evaluation on a wide array of single-image (CVBench, SEED-Bench, MMBench, MME, QBench2, MMMU, RealWorldQA, MM-Star, MMVet) and multi-image (Mantis-Eval, MMT-Bench) benchmarks.

This systematic approach allows for a thorough investigation of how programmatically generated, vision-centric instruction data impacts MLM capabilities across different tasks and training regimes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with PROVISION-10M yielded several significant findings regarding the effectiveness, scalability, and optimal use of programmatically generated instruction data:

1. **Programmatic Instruction Data Boosts MLM Performance:**
 - **Single-Image Tasks:** Instruction tuning LLaVA-1.5-7B with single-image data (VG-S) showed improvements across various benchmarks. Specifically, it yielded up to a **7% improvement on CVBench's 2D split** and an **8% improvement on its 3D split**. It also led to approximately a **3% increase in performance on QBench2, RealWorldQA, and MMMU**.
 - **Multi-Image Tasks:** Multi-image instruction data (VG-M) resulted in an **8% improvement on the Mantis-Eval benchmark**.
 - These consistent gains across diverse benchmarks affirm that programmatically generated, vision-centric instruction data can indeed enhance MLM capabilities, particularly in low-level vision and reasoning tasks.

2. **Impact of Data Source: Human-Annotated vs. Model-Generated Scene Graphs:**
 - Instruction data derived from **manually annotated scene graphs (VG-S, VG-M)** generally performed better than that from **model-generated scene graphs (DC-S, DC-M)**, especially at lower data scales. This highlights the value of high-quality, human-curated ground truth.
 - However, a crucial finding is that as the **data scale increased (e.g., to a 50% replacement ratio), model-generated data (DC-S) achieved comparable performance to manually annotated data (VG-S)** for single-image tasks. This suggests that large quantities of automatically generated data can mitigate the initial performance gap, underscoring the value of scalability.
 - For multi-image settings (DC-M vs. VG-M), generated scene graphs underperformed compared to human-annotated ones, and performance could decay at very high replacement/augmentation ratios, indicating potential "edge effects" or quality issues with current automated scene graph generation for complex multi-image reasoning.

3. **Importance of Data Format and Scale:**
 - **Data Format:** Experiments explored short-answer, multiple-choice, and a half-half mix.
 - For single-image tasks (VG-S replacement), model performance was positively related to the amount of replaced multiple-choice questions, while negatively related to short answers. However, a "half-half" mix often yielded competitive or superior results, suggesting benefits from diverse answer styles.
 - For multi-image tasks (VG-M replacement), the "half-half" format achieved the highest performance at a 20% replacement ratio, again showing the benefit of mixed formats.
 - **Data Scale:** For both augmentation and replacement scenarios, there was often an optimal data ratio. Augmentation generally provided better performance stability and higher average scores than replacement, especially with increasing data samples. This indicates that adding new, diverse data is often more beneficial than simply swapping out existing data.

4. **Synergistic Effect of Dual-Stage Augmentation (Pre-training and Instruction Tuning):**
 - Incorporating PROVISION data into **both the pre-training and instruction tuning stages** of xGen-MM-4B models led to the best overall performance, with an averaged improvement of **1.6% across 11 benchmarks** for VG-S data, and 1.2% for DC-S data.
 - Augmentation in either stage alone also improved performance over baselines, but the **synergistic effect of dual-stage augmentation** was most pronounced. This suggests that vision-centric knowledge can be effectively instilled and refined throughout the entire model training lifecycle.
 - Further scaling the inclusion of VG-S data during pre-training from 0.75 million to 1.5 million samples yielded additional performance gains (from 59.1% to 60.1% average across benchmarks), emphasizing the benefit of **large-scale data integration**.

In summary, the findings strongly validate PROVISION's programmatic approach as an effective means to generate high-quality, vision-centric instruction data. This data significantly improves MLM performance, particularly when integrated strategically across both pre-training and fine-tuning stages, and when considering the optimal balance of data source, format, and scale.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PROVISION system and the accompanying PROVISION-10M dataset represent a significant contribution to the field of multimodal AI, with several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of LLM/MLM-Generated Data:** PROVISION directly addresses the critical issues of factual inaccuracy (hallucinations), lack of interpretability, high cost, scalability bottlenecks, and licensing concerns associated with current LLM/MLM-driven instruction data generation. By offering a programmatic, rule-based approach, it provides a trustworthy and transparent alternative.
2. **Enhanced Interpretability and Controllability:** The reliance on scene graphs and human-written programs fundamentally makes the data generation process more interpretable. Researchers and developers can understand *why* a particular question is asked or an answer is given, and can precisely control the types of visual reasoning skills the generated data targets. This level of control is invaluable for debugging models, refining training objectives, and custom-tailoring data for specific applications.
3. **Factual Accuracy and Reliability:** By grounding instruction data in explicit, symbolic scene graph representations, PROVISION significantly reduces the risk of hallucinations. This ensures that models trained on PROVISION-generated data learn from factually correct information, fostering greater reliability and trustworthiness in their multimodal understanding.
4. **Scalable and Cost-Effective Data Generation:** The programmatic nature of PROVISION allows for the efficient synthesis of millions of instruction data points without the prohibitive computational costs or API dependencies of LLM/MLM-based methods. This democratizes access to large-scale, high-quality multimodal instruction data, making it more accessible for a broader research community and for resource-constrained organizations.
5. **Boosting MLM Performance on Vision-Centric Tasks:** The explicit focus on generating data for low-level visual reasoning (e.g., counting, spatial relations, depth, segmentation) directly addresses a known weakness in many general-purpose MLMs. By providing targeted training data, PROVISION can significantly improve MLM capabilities in these crucial areas, leading to more robust and versatile multimodal agents.
6. **Paving the Way for More Robust Multimodal Agents:** Models capable of accurate, fine-grained visual reasoning, in addition to high-level semantic understanding, are essential for advanced multimodal applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, detailed image analysis, and assistive technologies. PROVISION contributes foundational data that can accelerate the development of such agents.
7. **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of the PROVISION code and the PROVISION-10M dataset (links provided in the paper) fosters open science and community-driven progress. This allows other researchers to replicate findings, build upon the methodology, and experiment with the generated data, catalyzing further innovation in multimodal learning.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as noted by authors):**
The authors acknowledge limitations, primarily the reliance on the quality and completeness of scene graphs and the dependency on human-written programs. Future work could address these by:
- **Enhancing Scene Graph Generation:** Improving the accuracy and richness of the automated scene graph pipeline would directly lead to higher quality synthetic instruction data.
- **Automated Program Synthesis:** Leveraging LLMs or other AI techniques to automatically synthesize new instruction programs could further scale the diversity and generation process, reducing human effort while maintaining interpretability.

In conclusion, PROVISION offers a compelling and timely solution to the data bottleneck in multimodal AI. By demonstrating a pragmatic and principled approach to scaling vision-centric instruction data, it provides a valuable resource and methodology that can significantly advance the development of more capable, reliable, and interpretable Multimodal Language Models.
