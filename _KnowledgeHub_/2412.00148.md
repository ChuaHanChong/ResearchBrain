# Motion Modes: What Could Happen Next?

> [!summary] Summary
> Motion Modes, developed by researchers from the University of Toronto and Adobe Research, introduces a training-free guidance method for pre-trained image-to-video diffusion models, enabling the automatic discovery and generation of diverse, plausible, and disentangled object motions from static images. User studies found 96% of generated motions plausible and 92% aligned with initial user expectations, while also providing inspirational new possibilities.

## Problem

- Existing video generation models often entangle specific object motion with camera movements, background changes, or other unrelated scene elements, making precise control difficult.
- Computational methods struggle to produce a diverse range of plausible motions for an object from a single static image, often yielding repetitive or irrelevant results.
- Many controlled video generation methods require explicit user input (e.g., motion arrows) or rely on specialized, often synthetic, datasets, limiting their generalizability and ease of use.

## Method

- A training-free inference-time guidance approach is introduced for pre-trained image-to-==video diffusion== models, specifically Motion-I2V, to probe its latent distribution for object motions.
- Four guidance energies—Static Camera, Object Motion, Diversity, and Smoothness—are minimized during the denoising process to disentangle motion, ensure object movement, promote distinct outputs, and enhance temporal coherence.
- An iterative sampling strategy builds a set of diverse motions, where each new motion is repelled from previously generated ones, efficiently overcoming memory limitations of simultaneous sampling.

## Results

- The method significantly outperformed baselines in diversity (average diversity guidance energy ¯E_d = **1.04**) and focus on the selected object (average focus energy ¯E_f = **0.07**).
- User studies confirmed that 96% of generated motions were plausible, 92% met initial user expectations, and 19% provided plausible but unexpected 'inspirational' motions.
- Motion Modes successfully demonstrated an application to complete rough user-provided motion hints (single drag arrows) into detailed, plausible trajectories, enhancing drag-based editing tools.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained image-to-video diffusion models implicitly encode rich distributions of plausible object motions that can be effectively discovered and controlled via inference-time guidance.
> - Disentanglement of object motion from camera and other scene changes can be achieved through a combination of guidance energies that specifically penalize background motion and encourage foreground object movement.
> - Systematic exploration of latent motion space can yield not only expected object behaviors but also plausible, unexpected motions, offering creative inspiration without additional training data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a challenge within the fields of computer vision and generative AI, specifically in predicting object motion from static images. Current video generation models, while capable of producing realistic and temporally consistent videos, often entangle object movement with camera motion and other global scene changes. This entanglement makes it difficult to isolate and control the motion of a specific object.

Existing approaches to animating objects from still images largely fall into a few categories:
- **Prediction of cyclic or small-scale motions**: Methods like those by Li et al. (2024) and Bertiche et al. (2023) learn image-space priors on scene motion or model garment dynamics. These often rely on specialized datasets (e.g., oscillatory dynamics, synthetic garment simulations) and are limited to specific types of motion, making them less generalizable to complex, open-world scenes or non-cyclic movements.
- **Motion-guided video generation**: Techniques such as Dragapart (Li et al., 2024) or Motion-I2V (Shi et al., 2024) allow users to input motion arrows to guide video generation. While offering control, these methods require the user to pre-define the desired motion, which can be challenging for complex scenarios or when exploring diverse possibilities. Furthermore, some of these models are trained on synthetic data, limiting their application to real-world complexity.
- **General image-to-video diffusion models**: Recent advancements in diffusion-based video generators (e.g., Stable Video Diffusion, Movie Gen) can produce high-quality videos from text or image inputs. These models, trained on vast datasets, implicitly encode distributions over plausible animations. However, directly sampling from them often yields random videos with entangled motions of various scene elements.

"Motion Modes" positions itself by leveraging the implicit priors of these advanced pre-trained image-to-video generators. It aims to overcome the limitations of previous methods by offering a *training-free* approach to *probe* the latent distribution of these generators. This enables the discovery of *diverse, distinct, and plausible motions focused on selected objects*, while actively disentangling these motions from camera movement and other scene alterations. The work extends the concept of exploiting generative priors, similar to methods used for handle-based image manipulation (e.g., DragGAN, Diffusion Handles), but specifically for discovering object-centric video motions without requiring additional training data or explicit motion input. It also addresses the computational memory limitations of previous diversity sampling strategies for diffusion models (e.g., Particle Guidance) by proposing an iterative sampling approach for videos.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a method that can automatically discover and generate a diverse set of plausible motions for a selected object within a single static image. This capability is motivated by several key challenges and limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Entanglement of Motion**: Current video generation models, when conditioned on a static image, frequently produce videos where the motion of the target object is intertwined with camera movements, background changes, lighting variations, or motions of other unrelated objects in the scene. This makes it difficult to focus on the dynamics of a specific element. The goal is to achieve clear *disentanglement* of the selected object's motion from all other scene changes.

2. **Lack of Diversity in Predictions**: Humans can readily imagine multiple distinct ways an object might move from a single snapshot (e.g., a drawer opening or closing, but not moving vertically). However, existing computational methods often struggle to produce a *diverse range* of plausible motions. Direct sampling from pre-trained generators is inefficient for discovering varied object-centric motions, as it often yields repetitive or irrelevant results. Therefore, a primary objective is to efficiently explore the latent space to find *multiple, distinct* plausible motions.

3. **Reliance on Explicit Input or Specific Data**: Many control-oriented video generation methods require explicit input, such as motion arrows or text prompts, to guide the animation. This places a burden on the user to accurately specify the desired motion, which can be challenging for complex movements or when the user wants to *discover* possibilities rather than dictate them. Additionally, methods capable of generating complex motions are often trained on synthetic data or specialized datasets for cyclic motions, limiting their generalization to arbitrary objects and real-world scenes. The motivation is to create a *training-free* approach that does not require additional labeled data or explicit motion hints, instead leveraging the broad knowledge embedded in pre-trained, large-scale generators.

4. **Practical Applications**: The ability to automatically generate diverse and plausible object animations has several practical implications. It could assist users in:
 - **Cinematic exploration**: Providing animators with multiple creative options for how an object might move in a scene.
 - **Motion illustration**: Generating dynamic content from static images for various media.
 - **Image/video editing**: Facilitating more intuitive and powerful drag-based image and video manipulation by suggesting detailed motion trajectories from simple user input.

In summary, the key objective is to address the research question: "is it possible to probe such a latent distribution to discover possible motions for a given object in a static image?" by developing a training-free method that disentangles object motion, ensures diversity, and maintains plausibility, thereby overcoming the limitations of previous approaches and enhancing creative workflows.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Motion Modes operates as a training-free inference-time guidance approach for pre-trained image-to-video diffusion models. Its core methodology involves leveraging a diffusion model that separates motion generation from appearance generation and guiding its denoising process with a set of carefully designed energy functions.

**1. Motion Representation and Backbone Model:**
The method takes a static image `y` and a user-provided mask `m` indicating the object of interest. Motions are represented as time-dependent 2D vector fields `x`, which define the trajectory of each pixel across multiple frames relative to its initial position. The underlying image-to-video diffusion model is Motion-I2V (Shi et al., 2024), which is designed to generate motion independently of appearance. This inherent separation is foundational to the approach, as it naturally aids in disentangling object motion from other scene changes like lighting or shadows. The diffusion process typically involves adding Gaussian noise to a motion vector field and then training a denoiser to invert this process. For inference, a noise-free motion `x_0` is iteratively generated by denoising from pure noise `x_T`. This entire process operates in a lower-resolution latent space for efficiency.

**2. Guided Inference with Energy Functions:**
The central technical contribution is the introduction of a set of guidance energies that are minimized during the inference-time denoising process. This steers the generation towards desired properties without requiring any retraining or fine-tuning of the pre-trained diffusion model. The standard denoising step is modified by incorporating a gradient of the combined energy function with respect to the predicted non-noisy motion. This effectively perturbs the denoising trajectory.

The four guidance energies are:
- **Static Camera Guidance (E_c)**: This energy aims to eliminate camera motion. It penalizes the average magnitude of motion observed *outside* the object region defined by the mask `m`. By minimizing this, the method encourages the background and any unmasked scene elements to remain static.
- **Object Motion Guidance (E_o)**: This energy encourages the selected object to move. It penalizes a small difference between the average magnitude of motion *inside* the object mask `m` and *outside* the mask. A soft inverse activation function `ϕ` is used, which assigns higher energies for smaller differences, effectively pushing for non-zero object motion while maintaining a static background (as enforced by `E_c`).
- **Diversity Guidance (E_d)**: To ensure that multiple distinct motions are generated, this energy introduces a repulsion mechanism. Given a set `X` of motions already generated, `E_d` penalizes the similarity of the currently generated motion `x` to any of the previously discovered motions in `X`. The similarity is measured using a distance function `d` that considers both magnitude and angular differences of individual offset vectors, with emphasis on motion direction. This energy is applied only within the object mask.
- **Smoothness Guidance (E_s)**: This acts as a regularization term, promoting temporally coherent and physically plausible object motions. It penalizes large changes in motion across consecutive frames *within* the object mask. The distance function `d` is again used, but with different weights to emphasize minimizing sudden magnitude changes, thus preventing erratic or jerky movements.

These four energies are combined into a single guidance energy `E(x, m, X) = λ_d E_d + λ_c E_c + λ_o E_o + λ_s E_s`, where `λ` are empirically determined weights.

**3. Iterative Sampling and Stopping Criterion:**
The method iteratively samples the motion prior to build the set `X` of diverse motions. For each new motion, the diversity guidance `E_d` uses all motions already in `X` to ensure the new sample is distinct. This iterative approach circumvents the memory limitations faced by other diversity sampling strategies that process multiple samples concurrently.

To prevent the generation of implausible or redundant motions after a sufficient set has been found, a stopping criterion is implemented. If the final guidance energy `E(x_0, m, X)` of a newly generated motion exceeds a predefined threshold `ρ`, the motion is discarded, and the sampling process attempts a new one. The overall sampling stops after two consecutive motions are discarded. Typically, up to six distinct motions are sampled for experiments.

**4. Implementation Details:**
The method employs gradient checkpointing on the U-Net of the diffusion model to manage memory consumption during the backpropagation of guidance gradients in each denoising step. The guidance is applied during the first 20 out of 25 total denoising timesteps. The ControlNet module within Motion-I2V is disconnected to avoid imposing additional constraints that might limit motion diversity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Motion Modes focuses on four key properties: plausibility, diversity, expectedness, and focus (disentanglement from camera/scene motion).

**1. Quantitative Performance:**
- **Diversity**: Motion Modes significantly outperforms all baselines in terms of diversity, as measured by the average diversity guidance energy (¯E_d = 1.04). This indicates that the proposed diversity guidance effectively encourages the generation of largely different motions.
- **Focus**: The method demonstrates superior focus on the selected object, with a very low average focus energy (¯E_f = 0.07). This is attributed to effectively minimizing both camera motion (¯E_c = 0.09) and ensuring object motion (¯E_o = 0.05). While some baselines (e.g., ControlNet, Random Arrows) also show good focus metrics, qualitative analysis reveals this is often due to an overall suppression of motion rather than effective disentanglement.

**2. Qualitative Comparison:**
- **Disentanglement**: Motion Modes consistently generates object motions that are well-disentangled from camera motion and other scene changes. Baselines, especially those relying on random sampling or text prompts, frequently show significant camera motion or animations of secondary objects.
- **Plausibility and Expectedness**: The generated motions are often more natural, physically reasonable, and align better with human expectations compared to baselines. For instance, it generates a natural opening/closing motion for a drawer without deformation, and forward/backward movement for a tank, contrasting with more erratic or implausible motions from other methods. The smoothness energy is identified as a contributor to improved plausibility.
- **Diversity**: Qualitative results visually confirm the generation of multiple distinct ways an object could move (e.g., different directions for a ball, distinct deformations for a wave).

**3. User Studies:**
- **User Study I (Plausibility, Diversity, Expectedness)**: In a forced-choice comparison against multiple baselines, Motion Modes was judged favorably by participants in the majority of comparisons for all three metrics. It was perceived as significantly more plausible, diverse, and expected than motions from prompt generation, ControlNet, random noise, and farthest point sampled noise baselines.
- **User Study II (Expectedness and Inspiration)**: A second study quantified user perception. On average, 96% of Motion Modes' generated motions were deemed plausible. 92% of participants' initial expectations for object movement were covered by the generated motions, suggesting high alignment with human intuition. Notably, 19% of the generated motions were found to be plausible but *outside* participants' initial expectations, highlighting the method's ability to provide creative inspiration and discover novel, yet valid, motion possibilities.

**4. Ablation Study:**
- The ablation study demonstrated the critical contribution of each guidance energy. Removing `E_c` (camera guidance) resulted in pronounced camera motions, while removing `E_o` (object motion guidance) led to minimal or no object movement. Disabling `E_d` (diversity guidance) or replacing it with farthest point sampling for initial noise led to significantly less diverse outputs. Replacing `E_c` and `E_o` with a ControlNet resulted in the object being fixed in place. The smoothness guidance (`E_s`) was also shown to contribute to object focus by steering away from jerky motions that the generator's prior might suppress. The full Motion Modes configuration consistently achieved the best balance between diversity and focus.

**5. Application: Arrow-based Motion Prompting:**
- The research demonstrates an application where Motion Modes can complete rough user-provided motion hints (single drag arrows) into detailed, plausible motion trajectories. This enhanced motion information can then be fed into drag-based image editors (e.g., DragonDiffusion, Drag-A-Part) or motion-to-video generators (e.g., Motion-I2V).
- This application offers two benefits: (i) It simplifies the creation of complex animations or edits, as users only need to provide a simple hint. (ii) It resolves ambiguities inherent in rough hints, preventing implausible interpretations by the conditional generators (e.g., avoiding a "floating train" or "squashed drawer" from simple drag inputs).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Motion Modes introduces a novel and practical approach to a challenging problem in generative AI and computer vision. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

1. **Training-Free Innovation**: The primary contribution is a training-free method that leverages pre-trained, large-scale image-to-video diffusion models. This is significant because it avoids the high computational cost, extensive data requirements, and domain specificity typically associated with fine-tuning or training new models for specific tasks. By applying carefully designed guidance energies at inference time, it efficiently unlocks latent motion priors that are already embedded within these powerful foundation models.

2. **Enhanced Control and Disentanglement**: The method directly addresses the critical issue of entangled motions in video generation. By disentangling object motion from camera motion and other scene changes, it provides a refined level of control, allowing users to focus precisely on the dynamics of a selected object. This precision is a considerable advancement over general video generation techniques.

3. **Discovery of Diverse and Plausible Motions**: Beyond mere generation, Motion Modes excels at *discovering* a diverse set of *plausible* object motions. The user studies highlight its ability not only to meet user expectations but also to provide "inspirational" motions that are plausible yet unexpected. This capability is valuable for creative industries, enabling exploration of possibilities that might not be manually conceived or easily prompted.

4. **Broad Applicability and Generalizability**: Unlike prior methods confined to specific motion types (e.g., cyclic, small-scale) or requiring synthetic training data, Motion Modes' training-free nature and reliance on general-purpose diffusion models suggest broad applicability across various object categories and complex real-world scenes. This versatility expands the scope of automated animation tools.

5. **Facilitating Creative Workflows**: The method has direct implications for creative professionals:
 - **Artists and Animators**: It can accelerate the ideation and production of animations by providing diverse motion options for specific scene elements.
 - **Image and Video Editors**: The arrow-based motion prompting application demonstrates its utility in improving drag-based editing tools. By completing simple user hints into detailed, plausible motion trajectories, it makes complex editing more intuitive and robust against ambiguous inputs, thereby lowering the barrier to entry for high-quality animation.

6. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens avenues for future exploration:
 - **Moving Cameras**: Extending the framework to generate object motion in scenarios with dynamic camera movements (e.g., following shots) could broaden its utility significantly.
 - **3D Motion Generation**: The concept of applying guidance to latent motion representations could be extended from 2D motion fields to 3D dynamics, potentially leading to the generation of animated 4D assets (e.g., mesh sequences), which would have a profound impact on fields like robotics, virtual reality, and 3D content creation.

Despite limitations such as inheriting biases from the underlying generator, discrete sampling of continuous motion spaces, and computational speed, Motion Modes represents a substantial step forward in controllable and versatile object motion generation from static images. It demonstrates the power of intelligently probing pre-trained generative models to unlock sophisticated capabilities without additional training overhead.
