# Do Sparse Autoencoders Capture Concept Manifolds?

> [!summary] Summary
> This research reveals that large language models represent continuous concepts as causally relevant geometric manifolds, and Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) capture these structures primarily through a 'dilution' mechanism where many localized features tile the manifold. The authors developed and applied an Ising model pipeline that successfully recovers these coherent manifold structures from SAE features in an unsupervised manner, identifying known concept groups and uncovering new ones like 'epistemic uncertainty'.

## Problem

- Existing mechanistic interpretability research, particularly with Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs), often relies on the Linear Representation Hypothesis, which assumes concepts correspond to independent linear directions, despite empirical evidence showing many LLM concepts are represented as nonlinear 'manifolds'.
- A formal framework was lacking to define and evaluate how SAEs, designed for linear sparsity, capture complex, continuous geometric concept structures.
- Limitations of current SAE interpretability, such as dictionary instability and ineffective steering via individual features, lacked a unified geometric explanation.

## Method

- Developed a theoretical framework formalizing ==manifold capture==, with concepts like ==subspace capture== and ==tiling regimes== (shattering, dilution).
- Created a synthetic ==Manifold Zoo== benchmark for controlled SAE experimentation; proposed an ==Ising model== to analyze structural co-activation of features.
- Trained various SAE architectures on Llama3.1-8B activations; used ==restricted R²== analysis, tuning curves, and receptive field plots.

## Results

- Llama3.1-8B activations revealed ubiquitous low-dimensional concept manifolds (age, temperature); steering causes smooth, predictable output changes.
- SAEs consistently exhibited **dilution** behavior, requiring significantly more features than a manifold's ambient dimension.
- Ising model pipeline grouped features corresponding to ground-truth manifolds (temperature, colors) and discovered new manifolds like **epistemic uncertainty**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large language models universally encode continuous concepts as low-dimensional, curved geometric objects (manifolds) whose manipulation directly and predictably influences model outputs.
> - Sparse Autoencoders do not achieve compact 'subspace capture'; instead, they primarily 'dilute' manifolds by distributing their geometry across a larger, partially redundant set of localized features that 'tile' different regions.
> - Post-hoc analysis using pairwise Ising couplings effectively groups functionally related SAE features, enabling unsupervised discovery and coherent interpretation of concept manifolds even in diluted representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental question in the mechanistic interpretability of large language models (LLMs): how abstract concepts are represented within their neural networks. Historically, much of this interpretability research, particularly involving Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs), has operated under the "Linear Representation Hypothesis" (LRH). The LRH posits that concepts correspond to independent linear directions in the activation space, and their values can be read out or manipulated via linear maps. SAEs, as a popular tool for unsupervised concept discovery, implicitly align with this hypothesis by attempting to decompose representations into a sparse linear combination of features (directions).

However, a growing body of contemporary research challenges the LRH by suggesting that many concepts are not represented as isolated linear directions but rather as low-dimensional, continuous geometric objects known as "manifolds." Examples include periodic concepts (e.g., days of the week encoded circularly), numerical concepts (e.g., age along a line), and more complex structures like colors organized along a paraboloid with hue and lightness dimensions. These findings indicate that distances and neighborhoods within the representation space often encode semantic similarity, a property inconsistent with the strict direction-focused view of LRH.

This paper bridges the gap between the assumptions of SAEs and the observed manifold-like geometry of LLM representations. It investigates whether SAEs, despite their underlying linear and sparse assumptions, can effectively capture these nonlinear, continuous structures. By formalizing and empirically testing how SAEs interact with manifolds, the research aims to refine the understanding of what constitutes an "interpretable feature" in LLMs, moving beyond individual directions to consider geometric objects as the basic units of meaning. This shift has implications for both the design of future representation learning methods and the post-hoc analysis of existing models. The work also connects to long-standing literature in neuroscience (population coding, tuning curves) and manifold learning/subspace clustering, recontextualizing SAEs within a richer geometric framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether and how Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) capture manifold structures within neural network representations. This objective is motivated by several key observations and questions:

1. **Empirical Evidence of Manifolds in LLMs:** The paper establishes that LLM representations indeed contain ubiquitous, causally relevant manifold structures. Using Llama3.1-8B, PCA projections of activations corresponding to continuous concepts (e.g., age, temperature, days of the week, colors) reveal smooth, low-dimensional geometric forms rather than simple linear directions. Furthermore, steering interventions along these observed manifolds demonstrate smooth and predictable changes in model outputs, indicating that these geometric structures are not mere artifacts but functionally influence downstream behavior. This empirical reality necessitates a re-evaluation of how interpretability tools, particularly SAEs, interact with such representations.

2. **Mismatch between SAE Assumptions and Reality:** Traditional SAEs are often interpreted under the Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH), which assumes concepts map to independent linear directions. This inherent assumption creates a potential mismatch with the observed manifold geometry. The paper is motivated to determine if SAEs, despite this mismatch, can still meaningfully capture these complex structures, or if their current design leads to suboptimal or fragmented representations.

3. **Formalizing Manifold Capture:** To rigorously address the central question, the authors are motivated to develop a theoretical framework that defines what it means for an SAE to "capture" a manifold. This involves extending the LRH to an "Additive Mixture of Manifolds" model, which allows for concepts with multi-dimensional, nonlinearly curved geometries. This formalization enables the derivation of conditions under which SAEs could ideally capture manifolds (subspace capture) and introduces alternative mechanisms like "tiling" to describe less ideal scenarios.

4. **Characterizing Capture Mechanisms:** Beyond theoretical possibility, a key objective is to empirically characterize *how* existing SAE architectures represent manifolds. This involves distinguishing between different modes of representation, such as "compact capture" (where a small, fixed group of atoms spans the entire manifold) versus various forms of "tiling" (where individual features act as localized detectors covering restricted regions of the manifold). Understanding these mechanisms, especially "shattering" (disjoint patches) and "dilution" (redundant, overlapping patches), is crucial for practical interpretability.

5. **Developing Unsupervised Discovery Methods:** Given that SAEs might represent manifolds in fragmented ways, a practical objective is to develop post-hoc unsupervised methods to discover these coherent geometric structures from the sparse codes themselves, even without prior knowledge of the manifold's existence or location. This aims to provide a robust tool for identifying concept groups when individual features offer only partial or localized information.

6. **Explaining Known SAE Limitations:** The research is also motivated by a desire to provide geometric explanations for several documented limitations of SAEs, such as the instability of learned dictionaries, the ineffectiveness of steering through individual features, and the difficulty of automated interpretability. The hypothesis is that these issues stem from a fundamental misalignment between direction-based interpretations and the underlying nonlinear manifold geometry.

In summary, the motivation is to move beyond the linear concept view to a more geometrically informed understanding of LLM representations and to assess the capabilities and limitations of SAEs in this richer landscape, ultimately suggesting new directions for representation learning and interpretability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research integrates theoretical formalization, synthetic experimentation, and empirical analysis on large language models (LLMs) to investigate how Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) interact with manifold structures.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Framework Development

The authors introduce a theoretical framework to formalize manifold capture:
- **Additive Mixture of Manifolds:** This model extends the Linear Representation Hypothesis (LRH) by representing activations as a superposition of compact, smooth submanifolds. Each manifold $M_i$ is immersed into a higher-dimensional space $R^d$, and an observation $x$ is formed by summing contributions from a sparse subset of these manifolds: $x = \sum_{i \in S} f_i(m_i)$.
- **Subspace Capture:** This concept defines an ideal scenario where an SAE captures a manifold $M$ if a small, fixed group of $k_M$ atoms (where $k_M$ is the ambient dimension of $M$) spans a subspace containing $M$, and the encoder consistently activates this group for all inputs from $M$. A theorem is provided (Theorem 1) that specifies conditions (low reconstruction error, aligned sparsity, dictionary incoherence) under which an idealized sparse decoder can achieve subspace recovery.
- **Tiling Regimes:** When the number of atoms allocated to a manifold exceeds its ambient dimension, SAEs may not achieve compact capture. The framework introduces "tiling" as an alternative mechanism, where individual atoms act as localized detectors with receptive fields on the manifold. Two forms of tiling are distinguished: "shattering," where atoms partition the manifold with nearly disjoint active sets, and "dilution," where active sets overlap substantially, but no compact group accounts for the entire manifold.
- **Ising Pairings for Structural Co-activation:** To analyze how atoms interact in these different regimes, a pairwise Ising model is proposed. By binarizing SAE codes and inferring couplings ($J_{ij}$), the model aims to disentangle structural co-activation (atoms jointly representing a manifold) from spurious correlations. The sign and structure of these couplings are theorized to reflect the representation regime: predominantly positive for capture, negative for shattering (mutual exclusion), and a mixture for dilution.

#### 3.2. Synthetic Validation

To validate the theoretical framework in a controlled environment, a synthetic benchmark is constructed:
- **Manifold Zoo:** Eight manifold types (e.g., circles, spheres, tori, Swiss rolls, helices, flat disks, line segments) with varying intrinsic and embedding dimensions are created. Each type has multiple parameter variants.
- **Data Generation:** Manifolds are embedded into a 128-dimensional space ($R^{128}$) using random orthonormal matrices and isotropically rescaled. Observations are generated as sparse mixtures ($L_0=4$) of these embedded manifolds, with a small amount of Gaussian noise.
- **SAE Training:** TopK sparse autoencoders are trained on these synthetic data, with a dictionary size of $c=512$ (expansion factor 4) and varying sparsity budgets ($k \in \{3..., 25\}$) to explore different capture regimes.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Restricted $R^2$ (Subspace Capture Score):** For each manifold, atoms are greedily selected by their ability to explain residual variance of the manifold's true contributions. Reconstruction quality is measured by $R^2$ as a function of the number of selected atoms, assessing how closely it aligns with the manifold's embedding dimension $k_i$.
 - **Support Size and Receptive Field Spread:** These metrics quantify the number of unique atoms active on a manifold and the spatial extent of an atom's activation profile on the manifold, respectively, to characterize tiling.
 - **Ising Model Application:** The Ising model is applied to binarized synthetic SAE codes. Louvain community detection is then used on the absolute coupling matrix ($|J|$) to identify atom groups corresponding to ground-truth manifolds, and the coherence of these groups is analyzed based on their internal coupling structure.

#### 3.3. Empirical Characterization in LLMs

The findings from the synthetic setup are extended to a realistic LLM context:
- **LLM & Activations:** Activations are extracted from Llama3.1-8B (layer 19 residual stream) using 500M tokens from The Pile dataset.
- **Manifold Identification in LLMs:** PCA projections are used to visually identify manifold structures for real-world continuous concepts (e.g., age, temperature, days, colors) in the LLM's activation space.
- **Functional Relevance:** Steering interventions are performed by interpolating between concept centroids in the PCA space of activations and observing their smooth impact on model outputs (e.g., token probabilities), confirming the causal efficacy of these manifolds.
- **SAE Training:** Five different SAE architectures (Standard (ℓ1), JumpReLU, TopK, BatchTopK, Matryoshka) are trained on the LLM activations, with various expansion factors and sparsity levels. Only SAEs achieving variance explained > 0.85 are used for analysis.
- **Assessment of Manifold Capture:**
 - The "restricted $R^2$" protocol is applied to LLM-trained SAEs to measure subspace capture, similar to the synthetic experiments.
 - "Tuning curves" are plotted, showing individual SAE feature activations as a function of position along a manifold (e.g., "years" manifold), to reveal localized activation profiles.
 - "Receptive field plots" visualize the regions in the manifold's ambient PCA space where specific features are maximally active.
- **Unsupervised Manifold Discovery in LLMs:**
 - **Metric Comparison:** Five feature similarity measures (decoder cosine similarity, conditional co-activation probability, Pearson correlation of activation magnitudes, pointwise mutual information, and Ising pairwise couplings) are compared for their ability to reveal ground-truth manifold groups (e.g., for colors, days, temperature).
 - **Ising-Pipeline Application:** The most effective similarity metric (Ising couplings) is used within an "Ising-pipeline" (Ising inference + community detection) to identify atom groups from BatchTopK SAE codes.
 - **Validation:** Discovered groups are validated against known manifolds and then used to identify novel, previously uncharacterized geometric structures in LLMs.

This multi-faceted approach combines theoretical rigor with empirical validation across synthetic and real-world data, providing a comprehensive analysis of manifold representation in SAEs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several findings regarding how Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) represent concept manifolds, challenging existing assumptions and offering new interpretability paradigms.

1. **Ubiquity and Functional Relevance of Manifolds in LLMs:**
 - Analysis of Llama3.1-8B activations (layer 19) confirms the widespread presence of low-dimensional, curved geometric structures (manifolds) corresponding to continuous concepts (e.g., age, temperature, days of the week, colors, political bias). These structures are observable via PCA projections.
 - Crucially, these manifolds are not merely geometric artifacts; they are functionally relevant. Steering interventions that interpolate along these manifold structures result in smooth and predictable changes in the LLM's token probabilities for concept-dependent outputs (e.g., shifting temperature descriptions), demonstrating their causal influence on model behavior.

2. **SAEs Primarily Exhibit "Dilution" Rather Than "Compact Capture":**
 - **Theoretical Possibility of Capture:** The developed theoretical framework shows that SAEs *can*, in principle, achieve "subspace capture" where a small, fixed group of atoms spans the manifold's ambient subspace, given specific conditions like low reconstruction error and dictionary incoherence.
 - **Empirical Observation of Dilution:** However, empirical results from both synthetic benchmarks and actual LLMs contradict this ideal capture scenario.
 - The "restricted R^2" protocol shows that SAEs require a significantly larger number of features than a manifold's ambient dimension to achieve high reconstruction quality. The variance explained plateaus well beyond the necessary minimal support, indicating that a compact group of atoms does not exclusively represent the manifold.
 - This behavior places current SAEs in the "dilution" regime, an intermediate state between ideal compact capture and extreme shattering. In dilution, manifolds are represented by a larger, partially redundant set of localized features, fragmenting the geometry across many atoms.

3. **Manifolds are "Tiled" by Localized Features with Tuning Curves:**
 - Instead of compact capture, SAE features are observed to "tile" manifolds. Individual features act as localized detectors, each capturing a specific region or "patch" of the manifold in a piecewise-linear fashion. The union of these features progressively covers the entire geometry.
 - This localized selectivity is evident in "tuning curves" for concepts like "years." Many SAE features respond selectively to specific ranges or periodic aspects of a continuous variable (e.g., features activating periodically every 10 years for the "ones" digit, alongside features encoding the decade). These patterns are analogous to population coding in neuroscience, where no single neuron encodes the full variable, but the population's joint activity traces out the underlying geometry.
 - Receptive field plots further visualize this tiling, showing how different SAE architectures partition the ambient space of manifolds based on their architectural biases (e.g., angular vs. linear separability).

4. **Ising Couplings Enable Effective Unsupervised Manifold Discovery:**
 - The paper demonstrates that post-hoc analysis can recover manifold structures even in the dilution regime.
 - **Superiority of Ising Model:** A comparison of five feature similarity metrics (decoder cosine similarity, Pearson correlation, conditional co-activation, pointwise mutual information, and Ising pairwise couplings) reveals that Ising couplings and conditional co-activation are most effective. They produce the clearest block-diagonal structures in affinity matrices, accurately grouping atoms associated with known manifolds, unlike decoder similarity or Pearson correlation which fail to capture functional relationships.
 - **Synthetic Validation:** In the synthetic benchmark, the Ising model successfully recovers latent manifold structures across different sparsity regimes, with distinct coupling signatures characterizing capture (positive couplings), shattering (negative couplings/mutual exclusion), and dilution (mixed couplings).
 - **LLM Discovery:** Applying the Ising-pipeline (Ising inference combined with community detection) to LLM SAE codes successfully recovers known manifolds such as "temperature," "colors," and "political bias" as distinct feature communities.
 - **Novel Discoveries:** The pipeline also enables the discovery of previously unidentified manifold structures, such as one encoding "epistemic uncertainty" related to measurement error in scientific contexts. This demonstrates its utility as a hypothesis-generating tool for unsupervised interpretability.

In summary, SAEs do not capture manifolds in the straightforward, compact manner often assumed. Instead, they distribute manifold geometry across many localized, partially redundant features (dilution regime). However, this fragmented representation can still be coherently recovered and interpreted through advanced statistical methods like the Ising model, which identify functional groupings of features rather than relying on individual directional properties.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries significant implications for the field of mechanistic interpretability, representation learning, and the broader understanding of neural network function.

1. **Reframing Interpretability from Directions to Geometric Objects:** The most substantial impact is the compelling argument to shift the fundamental unit of interpretability from isolated linear directions (as assumed by the Linear Representation Hypothesis) to coherent geometric objects, specifically low-dimensional manifolds. By demonstrating the ubiquity and causal efficacy of these nonlinear structures in LLMs, the paper challenges the prevalent paradigm and suggests that understanding neural networks requires moving from a "dictionary of concepts" to a "geometry of representations." This reframing can guide future interpretability efforts to analyze feature *groups* and their emergent topological properties, rather than individual atom semantics.

2. **Explaining Prior SAE Limitations:** The findings provide a unified theoretical and empirical explanation for several previously documented limitations of SAEs, which were often attributed to model failures:
 - **Instability of learned dictionaries:** If manifolds can be tiled in multiple equivalent ways, different SAE training runs may naturally converge to different, yet equally valid, sets of localized features, leading to apparent instability without undermining underlying function.
 - **Ineffectiveness of steering individual features:** If features are localized detectors or patches of a curved manifold, intervening on a single feature may push the representation off the manifold rather than coherently moving along it, explaining brittle steering attempts.
 - **Difficulty of automated interpretability:** Meaning emerges from the collective activity of a group of features that tile a manifold. Inspecting isolated directions will inherently fail to recover this meaning, potentially leading to misinterpretations or "hallucinated" semantics. This work suggests these are not flaws of SAEs themselves, but rather a mismatch between how SAEs represent concepts and how researchers attempt to interpret them.

3. **Guiding Future Representation Learning and SAE Design:** The insights highlight a critical gap: current SAEs are designed around sparsity in an almost-orthogonal dictionary, implicitly favoring linear directions. The paper suggests a need for "featurization methods that explicitly target manifolds as primitives," moving beyond current SAE loss functions that primarily reward reconstruction without explicitly encouraging coherent treatment of geometric objects. This could lead to the development of new SAE architectures or alternative representation learning approaches better aligned with the observed geometric reality.

4. **Advancing Unsupervised Discovery Techniques:** The successful application of the Ising model pipeline for unsupervised manifold discovery in LLMs provides a practical tool for researchers. This method can identify coherent feature communities even in the "dilution" regime, offering a robust way to uncover hidden geometric structures without prior labels. Its ability to surface novel manifolds (like "epistemic uncertainty") demonstrates its utility for scientific hypothesis generation in complex models.

5. **Connecting AI Interpretability to Neuroscience:** By drawing parallels between SAE feature "tuning curves" and population coding in biological neural systems (e.g., place cells, orientation-selective neurons), the research strengthens the interdisciplinary dialogue between AI interpretability and computational neuroscience. This connection suggests that principles from biological brains might offer valuable guidance for understanding and designing artificial intelligence.

In essence, this research significantly advances the understanding of how latent concepts are encoded in modern LLMs and provides a critical re-evaluation of current interpretability tools. By advocating for a geometric perspective, it lays the groundwork for more effective and accurate methods for understanding and manipulating these complex models.
