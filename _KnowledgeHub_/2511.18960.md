# AVA-VLA: Improving Vision-Language-Action models with Active Visual Attention

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from LiAuto Inc. developed the AVA-VLA framework, reformulating Vision-Language-Action models from a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process perspective, which allows for dynamic visual attention based on historical context. The system achieves state-of-the-art success rates on robot manipulation benchmarks and demonstrates robust real-world performance on a dual-arm robot.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models treat robotic tasks as Markov Decision Processes (MDPs), processing visual inputs independently at each timestep.
- This history-agnostic design struggles with dynamic environments and partial observations, leading to inefficient visual attention and poor generalization.
- Prior attempts to incorporate historical information primarily focused on model efficiency (e.g., token pruning) rather than fundamentally enhancing visual processing for improved generalization quality.

## Method

- The framework reformulates the VLA problem as a ==Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP)==, using a ==recurrent state== to approximate the agent's ==belief state== from past observations and actions.
- An ==Active Visual Attention (AVA)== module dynamically modulates visual processing by leveraging the recurrent state to identify and prioritize task-relevant ==visual tokens== through a ==cross-attention mechanism==.
- The recurrent state also initializes the ==learnable action placeholder embedding==, integrating historical context directly into the action generation path.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art overall success rates on the LIBERO and CALVIN simulation benchmarks, demonstrating strong generalization, particularly on challenging sequential tasks.
- Demonstrated robust sim-to-real transferability, achieving the highest average performance across **four** challenging real-world tasks on a dual-arm Mobile ALOHA robot.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the Active Visual Attention (AVA) module and the state-based initialization strategy individually and synergistically contribute to performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating temporal context through a recurrent state and active visual attention fundamentally enhances VLA models' ability to filter redundant information and focus on task-relevant visual regions.
> - A POMDP formulation, approximated via a recurrent state, provides a more principled theoretical basis for VLA models in dynamic, partially observable robotic manipulation tasks.
> - The AVA module not only improves performance but also enables significant visual token reduction (e.g., 70% pruning) while maintaining competitive results, suggesting a pathway for more efficient VLA inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have significantly contributed to progress in embodied tasks, particularly in robotic manipulation. These models typically integrate visual perception, natural language understanding, and action generation within a unified neural architecture, often built upon pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs). They exhibit capabilities such as instruction following and generalization in robotic control.

A prevalent approach within the VLA paradigm involves processing visual observations as isolated temporal frames. This design implicitly formulates robot manipulation as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), where actions are determined solely by the current observation, assuming it completely represents the environment's state. However, real-world robotic control is inherently a partially observable problem. The environment often includes unobservable dynamics, internal states, or occluded information, meaning the current visual frame alone is insufficient for robust decision-making. Disregarding the historical context from past observations and actions renders the MDP-based approach suboptimal for dynamic sequential decision-making in robotics. This history-agnostic design particularly impacts visual processing, as visual attention, guided by static language instructions, must re-evaluate visual information from scratch at each step, preventing effective suppression of redundant information or focus on regions made relevant by previous actions. The visual system thus remains passive.

While some contemporary methods acknowledge the importance of historical information for efficiency, such as visual token pruning or KV-cache reuse, their primary focus remains on efficiency rather than a dynamic, context-aware visual processing paradigm that enhances both visual interpretation and VLA generalization. This paper addresses this gap by reformulating VLA policy learning from a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) perspective, aiming to explicitly incorporate temporal context into the visual processing and action generation pipeline. This work posits itself as a novel approach within the VLA landscape, specifically targeting the limitations of history-agnostic processing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the identified mismatch between the inherent partially observable nature of real-world robotic manipulation and the prevailing Markov Decision Process (MDP) assumption in most Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Current VLA models typically process each visual observation independently, leading to a history-agnostic design for action generation.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing the Limitations of MDP-based VLA Models:** The core problem is that treating robot manipulation as an MDP is suboptimal. Real-world scenarios involve non-observable dynamics and occluded information, requiring agents to reason over past interactions. By discarding this rich temporal context, VLA models struggle with dynamic sequential decision-making. This passive visual processing, where attention is re-evaluated from scratch at each step based only on static language instructions, prevents the model from effectively suppressing redundant information or focusing on regions made important by prior actions.
2. **Reformulating VLA Policy Learning from a POMDP Perspective:** To overcome the limitations of the MDP assumption, the authors propose to re-frame VLA policy learning within the Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) framework. In a POMDP, optimal decision-making requires not only the current observation but also a "belief state" that summarizes all relevant historical context, including past observations and actions. This theoretical foundation motivates the development of a VLA model that explicitly leverages temporal context.
3. **Introducing a Recurrent State for Belief Approximation:** Directly computing or representing the theoretical belief state in a POMDP is generally intractable. Therefore, a key objective is to propose and learn a compressed representation, termed the "recurrent state," which functions as a neural approximation of this belief state. This recurrent state captures historical context from previous timesteps' hidden states, specifically those immediately preceding action generation. This approach naturally transforms the VLA model into a recurrent structure, conditioning action prediction on this learned representation.
4. **Developing an Active Visual Attention (AVA) Module:** Building upon the recurrent state, a central objective is to design an Active Visual Attention (AVA) module. This module is intended to dynamically modulate visual processing of the current frame. The AVA module's goal is to leverage the recurrent state to quantify the importance of visual tokens, allowing the model to filter and focus its attention on regions most relevant given both the language instruction and the execution history, rather than relying solely on static instructions or current perception. This shift from passive to active visual perception is crucial for improved performance.
5. **Demonstrating Performance Improvement and Generalization:** The ultimate objective is to validate the effectiveness of the proposed AVA-VLA framework through extensive experimentation. This involves demonstrating state-of-the-art performance on standard robotic benchmarks and effective transfer to real-world tasks, thereby showing improved VLA performance and generalization capabilities in sequential decision-making.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed AVA-VLA framework is designed to integrate temporal context into Vision-Language-Action models by adopting a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) perspective. It comprises a recurrent state and an Active Visual Attention (AVA) module.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries and Problem Reformulation

Existing VLA models typically operate as: `A_t = Q(M(E(x^I_t), T(x^S_t)))`, where `x^I_t` is the visual input, `x^S_t` is the language instruction, `E` is the vision encoder, `T` is the language tokenizer, `M` is the LLM backbone, and `Q` is the action head. This implicitly models the task as `A_t ~ P_theta(A_t | x_t)`, focusing solely on the current observation.
The authors reformulate this to `A_t ~ P_theta(A_t | x_t, b_{t-1})`, where `b_{t-1}` is a belief state representing historical context. Since `b_{t-1}` is intractable, a neural approximation, `r_{t-1}`, termed the recurrent state, is introduced. This leads to a non-Markovian policy: `A_t ~ P_theta(A_t | x_t, r_{t-1})`.

#### 3.2. Recurrent State (`r_{t-1}`) Formulation

The recurrent state `r_{t-1}` is derived from the action-related hidden state of the previous timestep. Specifically, for a parallel-decoding VLA model that predicts an action chunk of length `L_c * D` (where `D` is action dimensionality), the hidden state output at the last decoder layer `M` at time `t-1` is `h^M_{t-1}`. The recurrent state `r_{t-1}` is computed by:
`r_{t-1} = B(h^M_{t-1})`, where `B` is an MLP module that transforms the hidden state into the recurrent state.
This recurrent state is also used to initialize the learnable action placeholder embedding `p_t`, meaning `p_t = r_{t-1}`, explicitly injecting historical information into the input sequence.

#### 3.3. Active Visual Attention (AVA) Module (`V`)

The AVA module dynamically modulates visual processing of the current frame based on `r_{t-1}` and the language instruction.
The process involves:
1. **Feature Encoding:** Current visual features `z^I_t` from `E(x^I_t)` and language instruction features `z^S_t` from `T(x^S_t)` are first encoded into a lower-dimensional space (`d' < d`) using modality-specific MLPs, resulting in `hat{z}^I_t` and `hat{z}^S_t`.
2. **Language Conditioning:** Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM) is applied to condition the visual features on the language instruction: `hat{z}^I_t = F_gamma(hat{z}^S_t) * hat{z}^I_t + F_beta(hat{z}^S_t)`.
3. **Cross-Attention with Recurrent State:**
 - The language-conditioned visual tokens `hat{z}^I_t` serve as the Query (`Q_t`).
 - The recurrent state `r_{t-1}` (after MLP encoding to `hat{r}_{t-1}`) serves as both Key (`K_t`) and Value (`V_t`).
 - A cross-attention mechanism (`Cross-Att(Q_t, K_t, V_t)`) is computed, followed by a self-attention layer, yielding `O_t`.
4. **Soft Weight Prediction:** The resulting tokens `O_t` are passed through a Feedforward Neural Network (FFN) and a linear layer `W`, followed by a Softmax function along the feature dimension. This predicts logits `rho_t` for enhancing or weakening each visual token.
5. **Soft Weight Generation:** The final soft weights `omega_t` are computed as `omega_t = rho_t * gamma`, where `gamma` is a 2-dimensional vector with scalar scores for enhancing (`gamma_0`) and weakening (`gamma_1`). These `omega_t` values represent importance scores for visual tokens.
6. **Attention Matrix Modulation:** The soft weights `omega_t` are used to modulate the attention matrices across all layers of the LLM backbone. For any layer `m`, the original attention scores `C_{t,m}` are combined with a soft attention mask matrix `U_t` (constructed from `omega_t`) before the Softmax operation. This dynamically reweights the visual tokens' contribution to attention based on their calculated importance.

The overall forward pass for AVA-VLA at timestep `t` is thus formulated as:
`A_t = Q(M_parallel(z^I_t, V(x_t, r_{t-1}), z^S_t, r_{t-1}))`.

#### 3.4. Training and Inference Procedure

- **Training:** Due to computational constraints of modern VLA backbones, a truncated backpropagation through time (TBPTT) strategy is adopted, unrolling the model for a fixed, short horizon (T=4 timesteps). The loss function is Mean Absolute Error (MAE) for action chunk prediction (`L_t,n`). An L2 penalty regularizer (`L_{t,n,omega}`) is added to the mean value of the soft weights `omega_t` to encourage focused attention (`||mu(omega_t) - c||`), preventing overly dispersed weights. The total loss combines prediction and penalty losses. For the first timestep of any sequence, `r_{-1}` is initialized as a zero embedding.
- **Inference:** The model operates in a fully recurrent manner. `r_{-1}` is initialized as a zero embedding at the start of a new episode. For subsequent timesteps, the agent performs a single forward pass, conditioned on `x_t` and `r_{t-1}`, predicting `A_t` and simultaneously extracting `r_t` for the next step.

The framework is built upon the OpenVLA-OFT foundation model, which uses DINOv2 and SigLIP for vision, and LLaMA2-7B for language, with parallel decoding for action chunks. The AVA-related modules introduce a negligible parameter overhead (fewer than 50M parameters, <1% of the full model).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, CALVIN) and real-world dual-arm manipulation tasks demonstrate the effectiveness and superiority of the AVA-VLA framework.

#### 4.1. Performance on LIBERO Benchmark (Table 1)

AVA-VLA achieved state-of-the-art overall performance in both single-task and multi-task learning settings.
- **One policy for all 4 suites:** AVA-VLA achieved an average success rate (SR) of 98.0%, surpassing the OpenVLA-OFT baseline (96.8%) and other state-of-the-art methods. It exhibited particularly strong performance on the challenging LIBERO-Long suite with 97.6% SR.
- **One policy per suite:** AVA-VLA achieved an average SR of 98.2%, also outperforming OpenVLA-OFT (97.1%) and other baselines. It maintained high performance across all suites, including LIBERO-Spatial (99.2%) and LIBERO-Object (99.6%).

#### 4.2. Performance on CALVIN ABC→D Benchmark (Table 2)

AVA-VLA demonstrated strong generalization capabilities on the CALVIN benchmark for language-conditioned, long-horizon manipulation.
- It comprehensively outperformed baseline methods across all tasks, achieving a success rate of 99.6% for 1 task completed in a row and 84.1% for 5 tasks completed in a row.
- The average length of completed tasks was 4.65, which was superior to previous state-of-the-art baselines like FLOWER (4.53) and UnifiedVLA (4.41).

#### 4.3. Performance on Mobile ALOHA Real-Robot Experiments (Figure 3)

The framework exhibited robust semantic understanding and dexterous action capabilities on real-world tasks, even with a small number of demonstrations.
- AVA-VLA achieved the highest average success rate across the four challenging tasks: Pick and Place, Sequenced Instruction Understanding, Flexible Object Folding, and Dexterous Action. This confirms its practical applicability in real-world scenarios.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Model Backbones (Table 3):** AVA-VLA consistently improved performance across different backbone architectures (OpenVLA-7B, LLaMA2-7B, Qwen2.5-0.5B), including those not pre-trained on robotic data, indicating its general applicability. For example, on LIBERO-Long, it improved LLaMA2-7B from 90.0% to 92.6%.
- **AVA Module and State-Based Initialization (Table 4, Table 9):**
 - Both state-based initialization and the AVA module individually contributed to performance gains over OpenVLA-OFT.
 - Their combination yielded the best overall performance, demonstrating complementarity. State-based initialization was particularly beneficial for long-horizon tasks (LIBERO-Long), while the AVA module provided consistent gains by reweighting visual tokens. These findings were consistent across both LIBERO and CALVIN benchmarks.
- **Matched Training Settings (Table 7):** An additional comparison under matched training settings confirmed that the performance gains of AVA-VLA were due to its architectural improvements rather than simply increased training compute. AVA-VLA still outperformed OpenVLA-OFT under identical training conditions.
- **Effect of L_omega Regularizer (Table 8, Figure 12):** The L2 penalty regularizer `L_omega` on soft attention weights was found to be crucial. Removing it reduced the average success rate on LIBERO (e.g., from 98.0% to 97.5%) and led to more dispersed attention, confirming its role in maintaining focused and selective attention on task-relevant regions.

#### 4.5. Analysis

- **Qualitative Visualization (Figure 1, Figure 4, Figures 7-11):** Visualizations of the soft weights `omega_t` revealed that the AVA module consistently concentrated attention on the robotic arm's contact regions and target objects. This demonstrated its ability to dynamically identify and focus on task-relevant visual features. Compared to vanilla baselines, AVA-VLA maintained a more robust and spatially consistent focus by leveraging historical context.
- **Visual Token Reduction (Table 5):** The soft weights computed by the AVA module proved effective for visual token reduction. With pruning ratios of 50%, 60%, and 70%, the model maintained performance comparable to or better than many state-of-the-art baselines, with only negligible drops. Even at 90% token reduction, it still outperformed numerous baseline methods, indicating the module's capability to effectively prioritize critical visual information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AVA-VLA framework introduces a significant contribution to the field of Vision-Language-Action models by explicitly addressing the long-standing limitation of history-agnostic processing in robotic sequential decision-making.

The key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:

1. **Enhanced Temporal Reasoning in Robotics:** By reformulating VLA policy learning from a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) perspective and introducing a recurrent state to approximate the agent's belief, AVA-VLA enables robots to reason over past interactions. This moves beyond the restrictive Markov Decision Process (MDP) assumption, which is often inadequate for complex, partially observable real-world environments. This fundamental shift can lead to more robust and intelligent robotic behaviors.
2. **Active and Context-Aware Visual Perception:** The Active Visual Attention (AVA) module allows VLA models to dynamically modulate visual processing based on both current instructions and historical execution context. This mechanism enables the model to actively focus on task-critical visual features while suppressing irrelevant information. Such adaptive perception is crucial for handling visual clutter, dynamic environments, and long-horizon tasks where object relevance changes over time.
3. **State-of-the-Art Performance and Generalization:** The demonstrated superior performance on established simulation benchmarks (LIBERO, CALVIN) and successful transfer to diverse real-world dual-arm manipulation tasks (Mobile ALOHA) validates the practical utility of the AVA-VLA framework. This indicates improved policy learning, generalization capabilities, and semantic understanding, even with limited real-world demonstrations.
4. **Improved Efficiency Potential:** The inherent property of the AVA module to generate importance scores for visual tokens opens avenues for efficient inference. The ability to prune a significant portion of visual tokens (e.g., 50-70%) with minimal performance degradation suggests that AVA-VLA could be adapted for accelerated and resource-efficient robotic control, which is vital for real-time deployment.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** AVA-VLA establishes a novel POMDP-inspired architecture for VLA models. This provides a theoretical and practical foundation for future research directions, such as developing more sophisticated recurrent state update mechanisms, explicit error-correction strategies to mitigate belief drift over long horizons, and longer-horizon training schemes to better align recurrent states with complex task dynamics. Addressing the identified limitations regarding precision-sensitive manipulation and accumulated perceptual errors could lead to even more capable autonomous systems.
6. **Broader Impact on Embodied AI:** The work underscores the importance of temporally grounded active visual processing for intelligent embodied agents. This principle extends beyond robotic manipulation to other embodied AI tasks requiring sequential decision-making under partial observability, potentially influencing the design of future generalist robot policies and multi-modal models.
