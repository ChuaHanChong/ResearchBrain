# Hierarchical World Models as Visual Whole-Body Humanoid Controllers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC San Diego, New York University, and Meta AI developed "Puppeteer," a hierarchical world model approach that enables whole-body control of a 56-DoF humanoid from visual observations. The method produces natural, human-like motions for complex tasks and demonstrates significant data efficiency, with a user study showing a 97.8% preference for its generated motions over traditional end-to-end baselines.

## Problem

- Learning robust whole-body control for high-dimensional humanoid robots directly from high-dimensional visual observations is challenging due to complex state/action spaces and inherent bipedal instability.
- Prior methods for humanoid control often rely on simplifying assumptions such as low-dimensional/privileged observations, hand-engineered rewards, or pre-defined/learned skill primitives, limiting their generality and naturalness.
- Existing data-driven approaches typically require vast amounts of environment interactions and often fail to produce human-like, natural movements, focusing primarily on task completion.

## Method

- The authors propose **Puppeteer**, a ==hierarchical world-model== reinforcement learning framework built on two algorithmically identical ==TD-MPC2== world models for visual whole-body humanoid control.
- A low-level ==tracking agent== is pretrained once on a large ==MoCap== dataset to translate abstract reference motions (future end-effector positions) into joint-level control actions for a **56-DoF** humanoid.
- A high-level ==puppeteering agent== is trained online using visual observations and task rewards to generate abstract reference motions for the low-level agent.
- The framework extends TD-MPC2 to handle episodic MDPs with termination conditions by adding a ==termination prediction head== to the world model.

## Results

- Puppeteer achieved performance comparable to a direct TD-MPC2 baseline on a new suite of **8** challenging visual and proprioceptive tasks, outperforming strong model-free (SAC) and other model-based (DreamerV3) baselines.
- A human preference study (**51 participants**) showed **97.8%** preference for Puppeteer's motions as more natural vs. end-to-end TD-MPC2, especially evident in tasks like "stairs" where baselines exhibited unnatural behaviors.
- Demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen and harder environment variations (e.g., up to **3×** larger gaps) without additional training.
- Ablations showed combining offline MoCap data with online interaction data for low-level pretraining significantly improved tracking, and planning at both hierarchical levels was essential.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical world model approach effectively decomposes complex visual whole-body control into manageable sub-problems, enabling efficient learning of natural motions.
> - Pretraining a single, general-purpose low-level tracking world model from diverse MoCap data proves highly effective and reusable across tasks, significantly reducing training overhead for new behaviors.
> - Planning at both the high-level (generating abstract commands) and low-level (executing joint actions) is critical for achieving robust and performant whole-body humanoid control from high-dimensional observations.
> - Evaluating "naturalness" via human preferences is crucial for humanoid control, as traditional reward maximization alone can lead to unnatural behaviors like "reward hacking".

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of whole-body control for humanoid robots is a long-standing and highly challenging problem at the intersection of machine learning and robotics. Humanoids are considered versatile platforms for generalist agents, but their high-dimensional observation and action spaces, coupled with inherent bipedal instability, make learning robust controllers exceptionally difficult, especially from high-dimensional visual observations.

Prior research in humanoid control has typically relied on simplifying assumptions to manage this complexity:
- **Low-dimensional or privileged observations/actions:** Many approaches reduce the complexity by providing the agent with simplified state information or by controlling a limited set of joints (e.g., Heess et al., 2017; Peng et al., 2018).
- **Learned skill primitives:** Another common strategy involves decomposing complex behaviors into simpler, reusable skill primitives (e.g., Merel et al., 2017; Hasenclever et al., 2020). These primitives are often learned via imitation learning from motion capture (MoCap) data and then stitched together by a higher-level policy.
- **Adversarial reward terms:** Some methods incorporate human MoCap data through adversarial rewards to encourage human-like motion, but these often suffer from poor sample efficiency (e.g., Peng et al., 2018).

The paper specifically references **MoCapAct (Wagener et al., 2022)**, which learns numerous individual tracking policies and then distills them into a multi-clip policy. While effective for simple tasks, MoCapAct requires a vast number of interactions (approx. 150M steps) and relies on privileged state information, not visual observations. **DeepMimic (Peng et al., 2018)** similarly learns physics-based character skills but also requires extensive interactions and does not typically handle visual inputs.

This work, "Puppeteer," aims to push beyond these limitations. It seeks to achieve **complex, visual whole-body control tasks without simplifying assumptions, reward engineering, or explicit skill primitives**, while being significantly more data-efficient (requiring *several orders of magnitude less interactions* for new tasks compared to prior work). It directly addresses the gap of learning highly performant *and* natural, human-like motions from visual observations in a fully data-driven manner for high-DoF humanoids. Furthermore, the paper introduces a new, challenging task suite for visual whole-body humanoid control, noting the current lack of such a comprehensive benchmark. The emphasis on evaluating the "naturalness" of learned motions via metrics and human preferences also distinguishes it from much of the existing work that focuses solely on task performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a highly data-driven approach to visual whole-body humanoid control using reinforcement learning**, specifically aimed at overcoming the challenges posed by high-dimensional observation and action spaces and the inherent instability of bipedal morphology.

The key motivations and specific objectives are:
- **Achieve Generalist Agents in Physical World:** The long-term goal for the authors is to enable humanoids, as versatile platforms, to perform a wide range of tasks by integrating whole-body control and perception.
- **Avoid Simplifying Assumptions:** To move beyond the limitations of prior work, the authors explicitly aim to learn successful and natural whole-body controllers without relying on low-dimensional (privileged) observations, simplified actions, or pre-designed/learned skill primitives.
- **Synthesize Natural, Human-like Motions:** A critical objective is to produce motions that are not only performant but also "natural" and "human-like," without the need for explicit reward design or domain-specific engineering (e.g., for balancing, gait style). This is a qualitative goal that is often overlooked in RL benchmarks.
- **High Sample Efficiency:** The method seeks to learn new tasks with significantly fewer environment interactions compared to existing approaches, which is crucial for practical robot learning.
- **Develop a Hierarchical World Model:** The core technical objective is to propose and evaluate a hierarchical world model, "Puppeteer," which leverages a low-level agent pretrained on MoCap data and a high-level visual agent.
- **Establish a Challenging Benchmark:** To facilitate future research and fair comparisons, the authors aim to propose a new, challenging task suite for visual whole-body humanoid control with a 56-DoF simulated humanoid.
- **Quantify "Naturalness":** Develop metrics, including a human user study, to quantitatively and qualitatively assess the naturalness of learned policies, moving beyond just task rewards.

In essence, the paper strives for a more complete and holistic solution to visual humanoid control, prioritizing both task success and the realistic quality of generated movements, while maintaining data efficiency and algorithmic simplicity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, dubbed **Puppeteer**, is a hierarchical, data-driven reinforcement learning framework built upon a **JEPA-style world model** for visual whole-body humanoid control. It uses the state-of-the-art model-based RL algorithm **TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al., 2024)** as its learning backbone. The core idea is to decompose the complex control problem into two distinct, independently trained agents, both implemented as TD-MPC2 world models:

1. **Low-Level Tracking Agent (World Model):**
 - **Purpose:** This agent learns to translate abstract reference motions into physically executable joint-level control actions for the 56-DoF humanoid. Its goal is to track a given command, essentially learning the physics and inverse dynamics of the humanoid.
 - **Input:** Robot proprioceptive state (joint positions, velocities, accelerations, gyros, torques, contact sensors) and an abstract reference motion (command).
 - **Output:** A sequence of low-level control actions (joint torques/positions) over a horizon `H`.
 - **Training:**
 - **Pretraining:** It is (pre)trained *once* on a large-scale human MoCap dataset (CMU, 2003), specifically using rollouts from MoCapAct (Wagener et al., 2022) which re-targets MoCap data to the 56-DoF CMU Humanoid embodiment.
 - **Novelty:** Unlike previous work that trains numerous (e.g., 2600) individual low-level policies, Puppeteer trains a *single* tracking world model capable of tracking the entire MoCap dataset. This significantly reduces computational footprint and allows for generalizability.
 - **Data Mixture:** Training combines offline data (noisy, suboptimal MoCapAct rollouts) and online interactions (with newly sampled reference motions) in a 50%/50% ratio. This is crucial for improving state-action coverage and robustness.
 - **Reuse:** Once trained, this tracking agent's weights are *frozen* and it is reused across all downstream tasks.
 - **Command Definition:** For the low-level agent, a command `c_t` is defined as future relative end-effector (head, hands, feet) positions of the sampled reference motion in 3D space. This provides a low-dimensional, geometric abstraction for the higher-level agent.

2. **High-Level Puppeteering Agent (World Model):**
 - **Purpose:** This agent learns to generate the abstract reference motions (commands) for the low-level tracking agent based on visual observations and task rewards. It acts as the "brain" for task-specific behavior.
 - **Input:** Robot proprioceptive state and visual observations (RGB images from a third-person camera).
 - **Output:** A sequence of high-level abstract commands (`c_t`, `c_t+1`..., `c_t+H`) for the low-level tracking agent to execute.
 - **Training:** Trained *online* using task-specific rewards through environment interaction, while the low-level tracking agent remains fixed.
 - **Temporal Abstraction:** The high-level agent can output multiple commands for the low-level agent, allowing for temporal abstraction, though the paper's experiments set this hyperparameter `k=1`, meaning both agents act at the same frequency.
 - **Simplicity:** Both world models are algorithmically identical in their TD-MPC2 implementation, differing only in their inputs and outputs. This "striking simplicity" is highlighted as a key feature.

**TD-MPC2 as Backbone:** Both agents utilize TD-MPC2, which learns a latent, decoder-free world model and performs action selection via Model Predictive Control (MPC) using Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) for local trajectory optimization. It also learns a model-free policy prior for warm-starting the sampling.

**Handling Termination Conditions:** A novel contribution is the extension of TD-MPC2 to handle episodic MDPs with termination conditions (e.g., non-foot contact with the floor for humanoids). A termination prediction head `D(z,a)` is added to the world model, trained with a cross-entropy loss. During planning, a "soft truncation" is applied to model rollouts and value estimates based on predicted termination probabilities to mitigate noise.

**Implementation Details:** The authors use a 56-DoF "CMU Humanoid" model from DMControl. Visual inputs are 64x64 RGB images. The tracking agent is pretrained for 10M steps, and the puppeteering agent for 4M steps on a single RTX 3090 GPU.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling results across a newly proposed suite of 8 challenging tasks for visual whole-body humanoid control (5 visual, 3 proprioceptive-only).

1. **Benchmark Performance:**
 - **Comparability with TD-MPC2:** Puppeteer's performance (episode return) is generally comparable to that of a direct, end-to-end TD-MPC2 baseline across most tasks (e.g., Stand, Walk, Run, Corridor, Hurdles, Walls, Gaps).
 - **Superiority over Model-Free/Other MBRL:** Both Puppeteer and TD-MPC2 significantly outperform strong model-free (SAC) and other model-based RL (DreamerV3) baselines, which achieve minimal or no meaningful performance on these complex tasks within the given computational budget (3M environment steps). Even hierarchical DreamerV3 with a TD-MPC2 low-level agent performs poorly.
 - **Stairs Task Anomaly:** On the "stairs" task, the end-to-end TD-MPC2 baseline achieves a higher asymptotic return than Puppeteer, but as revealed in qualitative evaluations, this is a result of "reward hacking" where TD-MPC2 learns to "roll" up the stairs unnaturally.

2. **"Naturalness" of Humanoid Controllers (Key Qualitative Result):**
 - **Human Preference Study:** A user study with 51 participants showed an overwhelming preference for motions generated by Puppeteer over TD-MPC2. A staggering 97.8% of responses rated Puppeteer's motions as more natural, with only 1.8% rating them as "equally natural" and 0.4% preferring TD-MPC2. This is particularly pronounced in the "stairs" task, where TD-MPC2's "rolling" behavior is contrasted with Puppeteer's human-like walking.
 - **Quantitative Proxies:** Quantitative metrics, such as average torso height (gait), also support these findings. For instance, on the "gaps" task, Puppeteer maintains a higher average torso height (96.0 cm) compared to TD-MPC2 (85.9 cm), indicating a more upright and natural gait. These findings highlight the importance of holistic evaluation beyond just reward maximization.

3. **Ablations and Analysis:**
 - **Pretraining Data Mixture:** Leveraging *both* offline MoCap data and online interactions for pretraining the low-level tracking world model leads to significantly better tracking policies than using either source alone. Offline data helps with difficult motions, while online data improves state-action coverage and robustness.
 - **MoCap Diversity:** Training on a more diverse set of MoCap clips (all 836 available) results in the best tracking performance, suggesting scalability with more data.
 - **Importance of Planning:** Planning at *both* the high-level and low-level is critical for effective whole-body humanoid control. Ablations show substantial performance degradation when planning is replaced by a learned model-free policy at either level or both, supporting the idea that planning is essential for high-dimensional continuous control problems.
 - **High-Level Pretraining:** Pretraining the high-level agent (e.g., on `corridor` and finetuning) can substantially benefit performance on downstream visual tasks, suggesting shared underlying control needs even across different visual environments.
 - **Zero-Shot Generalization:** Puppeteer demonstrates impressive zero-shot generalization to unseen and harder environment variations. In the `gaps` task, the method performs non-trivially on gap lengths up to 3 times larger than the training data, without additional training. This indicates robust learned representations and planning capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper holds significant implications for the fields of reinforcement learning, robotics, and embodied AI:

1. **Advancement in Humanoid Control:** Puppeteer makes a substantial step towards truly generalist humanoid agents. By successfully demonstrating visual whole-body control for a high-DoF humanoid *without* relying on common simplifying assumptions (privileged observations, hand-designed rewards, or discrete skill primitives), it pushes the boundaries of what is achievable with data-driven RL in complex physical embodiments.
2. **Emphasis on "Naturalness":** The explicit focus on and robust evaluation of "naturalness" as a key performance metric is a crucial contribution. Moving beyond mere task reward maximization, the human user study demonstrates that learned policies can synthesize motions that are broadly preferred by humans. This is vital for deploying humanoids in real-world scenarios where intuitive and safe interaction is paramount. It highlights the potential for "reward hacking" in traditional RL and advocates for more holistic evaluation criteria.
3. **Data Efficiency and Simplicity:** The method's ability to learn complex visual control tasks with significantly fewer environment interactions compared to prior work is highly impactful for practical applications. Furthermore, the "striking simplicity" of using algorithmically identical world models for both hierarchical levels reduces engineering complexity and increases reproducibility.
4. **Reusable Low-Level Control:** The concept of pretraining a *single*, general-purpose low-level tracking world model that can be reused across a variety of downstream tasks is a powerful paradigm shift. This drastically reduces the training overhead for new tasks and enables faster adaptation.
5. **New Benchmark and Future Research Directions:** The introduction of a new, challenging task suite for visual whole-body humanoid control fills a critical gap in the research landscape. This benchmark will facilitate standardized evaluation and accelerate algorithmic development in this complex domain. The ablations also provide actionable advice and highlight promising future research directions, such as multi-level pretraining and further investigation into the impressive zero-shot generalization capabilities of hierarchical world models.
6. **Validation of World Models and Planning:** The results strongly validate the efficacy of world models and planning-based RL (specifically TD-MPC2) for high-dimensional continuous control problems, demonstrating their superiority over model-free methods in this challenging domain.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The authors acknowledge limitations, including the current focus on visio-locomotive capabilities and the two-level hierarchy with only the low-level extensively pretrained. They suggest further research into pretraining and transferring the full hierarchical world model across diverse tasks as a promising avenue.

In summary, Puppeteer represents a significant leap forward in learning human-like whole-body control for complex humanoids from visual inputs, emphasizing both performance and naturalness through an elegant, data-efficient hierarchical world model approach.
