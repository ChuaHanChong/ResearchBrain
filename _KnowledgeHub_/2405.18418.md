# Hierarchical World Models as Visual Whole-Body Humanoid Controllers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC San Diego, New York University, and Meta AI developed "Puppeteer," a hierarchical world model approach that enables whole-body control of a 56-DoF humanoid from visual observations. The method produces natural, human-like motions for complex tasks and demonstrates significant data efficiency, with a user study showing a 97.8% preference for its generated motions over traditional end-to-end baselines.

## Problem

- Learning robust whole-body control for high-dimensional humanoid robots directly from high-dimensional visual observations is challenging due to complex state/action spaces and inherent bipedal instability.
- Prior methods for humanoid control often rely on simplifying assumptions such as low-dimensional/privileged observations, hand-engineered rewards, or pre-defined/learned skill primitives, limiting their generality and naturalness.
- Existing data-driven approaches typically require vast amounts of environment interactions and often fail to produce human-like, natural movements, focusing primarily on task completion.

## Method

- The authors propose **Puppeteer**, a ==hierarchical world-model== reinforcement learning framework built on two algorithmically identical ==TD-MPC2== world models for visual whole-body humanoid control.
- A low-level ==tracking agent== is pretrained once on a large ==MoCap== dataset to translate abstract reference motions (future end-effector positions) into joint-level control actions for a **56-DoF** humanoid.
- A high-level ==puppeteering agent== is trained online using visual observations and task rewards to generate abstract reference motions for the low-level agent.
- The framework extends TD-MPC2 to handle episodic MDPs with termination conditions by adding a ==termination prediction head== to the world model.

## Results

- Puppeteer achieved performance comparable to a direct TD-MPC2 baseline on a new suite of **8** challenging visual and proprioceptive tasks, outperforming strong model-free (SAC) and other model-based (DreamerV3) baselines.
- A human preference study (**51 participants**) showed **97.8%** preference for Puppeteer's motions as more natural vs. end-to-end TD-MPC2, especially evident in tasks like "stairs" where baselines exhibited unnatural behaviors.
- Demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen and harder environment variations (e.g., up to **3×** larger gaps) without additional training.
- Ablations showed combining offline MoCap data with online interaction data for low-level pretraining significantly improved tracking, and planning at both hierarchical levels was essential.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical world model approach effectively decomposes complex visual whole-body control into manageable sub-problems, enabling efficient learning of natural motions.
> - Pretraining a single, general-purpose low-level tracking world model from diverse MoCap data proves highly effective and reusable across tasks, significantly reducing training overhead for new behaviors.
> - Planning at both the high-level (generating abstract commands) and low-level (executing joint actions) is critical for achieving robust and performant whole-body humanoid control from high-dimensional observations.
> - Evaluating "naturalness" via human preferences is crucial for humanoid control, as traditional reward maximization alone can lead to unnatural behaviors like "reward hacking".
