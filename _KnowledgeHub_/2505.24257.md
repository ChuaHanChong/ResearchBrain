# Out of Sight, Not Out of Context? Egocentric Spatial Reasoning in VLMs Across Disjoint Frames

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces DISJOINT-3DQA, a benchmark for evaluating Vision-Language Models' ability to perform spatial reasoning between objects that are never co-visible in an egocentric video sequence. Experiments reveal that current VLMs' primary limitation is their inability to construct accurate 3D representations from fragmented visual inputs, rather than a deficiency in spatial reasoning over existing 3D information.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with reasoning about spatial relationships between objects that are never simultaneously visible in input frames.
- Existing VLMs lack the ability to integrate fragmented visual information across temporal sequences to construct and maintain coherent 3D spatial representations.
- Embodied AI systems need to build persistent internal 3D maps from egocentric perspectives to enable sophisticated spatial reasoning.

## Method

- Researchers developed ==DISJOINT-3DQA==, a comprehensive benchmark built on Aria Synthetic Environments (ASE) with a ==strict disjointness constraint for object visibility==.
- The benchmark includes 5,399 question-answer pairs across ==5 spatial reasoning categories==: Relative Direction, Distance, Size, Size and Fit, and Scene Description.
- Evaluation systematically tests VLMs under varying levels of ==3D contextual information: 2D-only, sparse 3D (textual trajectory, BEV), and full 3D (oracle ground truth)==.

## Results

- Human evaluators achieved **93.96%** accuracy, while the best VLM (GPT-4o) reached **65.60%** in the marked condition, showing a substantial performance gap.
- Providing precise ground-truth 3D data (coordinates, metadata) to GPT-4o led to a dramatic **18%** performance increase, reaching **83.2%**, indicating the core bottleneck is 3D representation construction.
- Model accuracy consistently declined with increasing 3D distance between object pairs, especially beyond **20 meters**, suggesting limitations in long-range spatial context accumulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs perform significantly below human levels on disjoint-frame spatial reasoning, with the best model reaching 65.60% compared to human 93.96%.
> - The primary limitation for VLMs in these tasks is not spatial reasoning ability itself, but their incapacity to construct accurate 3D scene representations from fragmented temporal visual observations.
> - Model performance degrades substantially as the 3D distance between objects increases, particularly beyond 20 meters, indicating difficulties with long-range spatial context accumulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) excel at understanding objects within single images or video frames, but struggle with a fundamental capability that humans take for granted: reasoning about spatial relationships between objects that are never simultaneously visible. This research introduces a systematic evaluation of this critical limitation through the DISJOINT-3DQA benchmark, revealing fundamental bottlenecks in how current AI systems construct and maintain spatial understanding from egocentric perspectives.

### 2. The Challenge of Disjoint Frame Spatial Reasoning

When humans navigate environments from a first-person perspective, they continuously build internal 3D maps that persist even when objects move out of view. This capability enables sophisticated spatial reasoning - for instance, remembering where you placed your keys relative to other objects in a room, even after walking away and returning later.

For AI systems to function as effective embodied agents, they must develop similar capabilities. However, current VLMs face significant challenges in this domain because they must:

1. **Integrate fragmented visual information** across temporal sequences where relevant objects appear in different frames
2. **Construct coherent 3D spatial representations** from limited 2D visual observations
3. **Maintain spatial memory** over extended periods with changing viewpoints

The authors term this specific challenge "disjoint-frame spatial reasoning" - the ability to understand spatial relationships between objects that are never co-visible in the input sequence.

### 3. The DISJOINT-3DQA Benchmark

The researchers developed DISJOINT-3DQA, a comprehensive benchmark built on the Aria Synthetic Environments (ASE) dataset, which provides photorealistic indoor scenes with precise 3D geometry. The benchmark's key design principles include:

**Strict Disjointness Constraint**: For any object pair (A, B) referenced in a question, their visibility spans are mutually exclusive ($T_A \cap T_B = \emptyset$), ensuring models cannot rely on co-occurrence.

**Comprehensive Question Categories**: The benchmark covers five spatial reasoning categories:
- **Relative Direction** (20.2% of questions): "Which side is the sofa with respect to the window?"
- **Relative Distance** (21% of questions): "Are the door and window within arm's reach?"
- **Relative Size** (21% of questions): "Which object is larger, the window or the door?"
- **Size and Fit** (20% of questions): "Will the chair fit on the bed?"
- **Scene Description** (17% of questions): "How many chairs are there?"

**Visual Grounding**: Objects are marked with colored circles in relevant frames to minimize referential ambiguity, allowing for both "Marked" and "Unmarked" evaluation conditions.

**Scale**: 5,399 question-answer pairs across 1,668 scenes covering 856 unique object pairs, with high-quality ground truth validated through human evaluation (96% question relevance, 94% answer accuracy).

### 4. Experimental Design and Methodology

The evaluation framework systematically tests VLMs under different levels of 3D contextual information:

**2D-Only Baseline**: Models receive only video frames and questions, representing the standard VLM evaluation setting.

**Sparse 3D Context**: Realistic partial 3D information that might be available in practical applications:
- **Textual Trajectory**: Camera movement described as sequences of (x,y,z) coordinates
- **Bird's-Eye View (BEV)**: Top-down 2D projections showing object layouts and camera paths

**Full 3D Context (Oracle)**: Ground-truth 3D metadata including precise object coordinates, volumes, and complete trajectories - representing an upper bound on spatial reasoning performance.

The researchers evaluated both closed-source models (GPT-4o) and open-source alternatives (LLaVA variants, InternVL, Qwen-VL) using the LLM-Match metric, which assesses semantic similarity between predicted and reference answers on a normalized 0-1 scale.

### 5. Key Findings

The results reveal several critical insights about current VLM capabilities:

#### 5.1. Human vs AI Performance Gap

Human evaluators achieved 93.96% performance on the benchmark, significantly outperforming all AI models. The best-performing VLM (GPT-4o) reached only 65.60% in the marked condition, leaving a substantial 28 percentage point gap.

#### 5.2. Limited Impact of Visual Marking

While visual object marking provided consistent improvements (2.7% for GPT-4o, 7-9% for open-source models), the gains were modest relative to the overall performance gap. This suggests that object identification, while helpful, is not the primary bottleneck.

#### 5.3. Marginal Benefits from Sparse 3D Cues

Providing realistic sparse 3D information (BEV maps or trajectory data) yielded only modest improvements of 2-4%. These gains were most pronounced in "Scene Description" and "Spatial Relationship" categories, suggesting some benefit for global scene understanding.

#### 5.4. Dramatic Impact of Ground-Truth 3D Data

The most striking finding was the 18% performance jump for GPT-4o when provided with precise 3D coordinates and object metadata (reaching 83.2%). This dramatic improvement indicates that **the primary limitation is not reasoning ability over spatial information, but rather the capacity to construct accurate 3D representations from visual observations**.

#### 5.5. Distance-Related Performance Degradation

Model accuracy consistently declined as 3D distance between object pairs increased, with particularly sharp drops beyond 20 meters (typically different rooms). This suggests fundamental limitations in long-range spatial context accumulation.

#### 5.6. Failure Mode Analysis

Qualitative analysis of GPT-4o errors revealed several concerning patterns even when provided with BEV inputs:
- Misinterpretation of spatial relations (18%)
- Complete ignoring of provided spatial cues (17%)
- Hallucination of geometry (24%)
- Misalignment between egocentric views and top-down maps (15%)

These findings suggest that simply providing 3D context is insufficient - models must also develop better capabilities for multimodal integration and spatial reasoning.

### 6. Significance and Future Implications

This research makes several important contributions to the field of embodied AI:

**Diagnostic Benchmark**: DISJOINT-3DQA provides the first systematic evaluation framework specifically targeting disjoint-frame spatial reasoning, establishing a clear benchmark for future progress.

**Identification of Core Bottleneck**: The stark contrast between marginal improvements from sparse 3D cues and dramatic gains from ground-truth metadata definitively identifies the core limitation: current VLMs cannot construct accurate 3D scene representations from temporal visual sequences.

**Research Direction Guidance**: The findings point toward several critical areas for future development:
- Enhanced 3D scene reconstruction capabilities for VLMs
- Better long-horizon spatial memory mechanisms
- Improved integration of geometric priors in model architectures
- More robust multimodal fusion strategies

**Implications for Embodied AI**: The capability for disjoint-frame spatial reasoning is essential for practical embodied agents in applications like augmented reality navigation, assistive robotics, and long-term planning in dynamic environments.

The research demonstrates that while current VLMs have sophisticated language understanding and can process visual information effectively, they lack the fundamental spatial intelligence required for robust embodied interaction. Addressing this limitation will require architectural innovations that go beyond scaling existing attention-based approaches, potentially incorporating explicit 3D reasoning modules or geometric inductive biases.

This work establishes a clear challenge for the community: developing AI systems that can build and reason over persistent 3D spatial representations from egocentric visual experience, bringing us closer to truly intelligent embodied agents capable of sophisticated spatial understanding and interaction.
