# Out of Sight, Not Out of Context? Egocentric Spatial Reasoning in VLMs Across Disjoint Frames

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces DISJOINT-3DQA, a benchmark for evaluating Vision-Language Models' ability to perform spatial reasoning between objects that are never co-visible in an egocentric video sequence. Experiments reveal that current VLMs' primary limitation is their inability to construct accurate 3D representations from fragmented visual inputs, rather than a deficiency in spatial reasoning over existing 3D information.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) struggle with reasoning about spatial relationships between objects that are never simultaneously visible in input frames.
- Existing VLMs lack the ability to integrate fragmented visual information across temporal sequences to construct and maintain coherent 3D spatial representations.
- Embodied AI systems need to build persistent internal 3D maps from egocentric perspectives to enable sophisticated spatial reasoning.

## Method

- Researchers developed ==DISJOINT-3DQA==, a comprehensive benchmark built on Aria Synthetic Environments (ASE) with a ==strict disjointness constraint for object visibility==.
- The benchmark includes 5,399 question-answer pairs across ==5 spatial reasoning categories==: Relative Direction, Distance, Size, Size and Fit, and Scene Description.
- Evaluation systematically tests VLMs under varying levels of ==3D contextual information: 2D-only, sparse 3D (textual trajectory, BEV), and full 3D (oracle ground truth)==.

## Results

- Human evaluators achieved **93.96%** accuracy, while the best VLM (GPT-4o) reached **65.60%** in the marked condition, showing a substantial performance gap.
- Providing precise ground-truth 3D data (coordinates, metadata) to GPT-4o led to a dramatic **18%** performance increase, reaching **83.2%**, indicating the core bottleneck is 3D representation construction.
- Model accuracy consistently declined with increasing 3D distance between object pairs, especially beyond **20 meters**, suggesting limitations in long-range spatial context accumulation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current VLMs perform significantly below human levels on disjoint-frame spatial reasoning, with the best model reaching 65.60% compared to human 93.96%.
> - The primary limitation for VLMs in these tasks is not spatial reasoning ability itself, but their incapacity to construct accurate 3D scene representations from fragmented temporal visual observations.
> - Model performance degrades substantially as the 3D distance between objects increases, particularly beyond 20 meters, indicating difficulties with long-range spatial context accumulation.
