# TangramPuzzle: Evaluating Multimodal Large Language Models with Compositional Spatial Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduced TangramPuzzle, a geometry-grounded benchmark using the Tangram Construction Expression (TCE) to rigorously evaluate compositional spatial reasoning in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). This work revealed that while some advanced models like Gemini3-Pro demonstrated robust geometric understanding, many MLLMs struggled with rigid body constraints, often distorting or overlapping pieces to achieve visual fidelity rather than generating physically valid solutions.

## Problem

- Existing MLLM benchmarks primarily focus on high-level semantic understanding and coarse spatial relations, failing to evaluate precise compositional spatial reasoning under strict geometric and physical constraints.
- Spatial problems in current evaluations are often described ambiguously using natural language or multiple-choice formats, relying more on linguistic reasoning than exact visual-geometric understanding.
- Evaluation metrics are limited, often using text matching or subjective judgment, and lack mathematically rigorous validation for constructive tasks where multiple valid geometric solutions exist.

## Method

- The ==TangramPuzzle benchmark== was developed, leveraging the classic Tangram puzzle, which inherently requires multi-step compositional spatial reasoning from local parts to a global shape.
- A formal symbolic geometry description language, ==Tangram Construction Expression (TCE)==, was introduced to represent tangram configurations with exact algebraic coordinates and structural relations, enabling unambiguous, machine-verifiable evaluation.
- Two complementary tasks were designed: ==Outline Prediction== (inferring a global silhouette from local components) and ==End-to-End Tangram Solution Generation== (solving the inverse geometric assembly problem by outputting a complete TCE in JSON format).

## Results

- Gemini3-Pro consistently outperformed other MLLMs, achieving the highest Validation Pass Rate (VPR) of **22.60%** and a Success rate of **21.56%** in End-to-End Solution Generation, demonstrating robust geometric reasoning.
- Most MLLMs exhibited widespread failures in the End-to-End Solution Generation task, with high rates of Rigid Geometry Errors and Physical Errors, leading to **0%** success rates for several top-tier models despite high Intersection over Union (IoU) scores.
- Human solvers achieved a **72.67%** success rate, rarely violating geometric constraints, which confirms the task's non-trivial difficulty and highlights the current gap in MLLMs' precise spatial intelligence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs often prioritize visual fidelity (matching a target silhouette) over strict geometric constraints, leading to 'cheating' behaviors like distorting pieces or allowing overlaps to force a visual match.
> - Advanced MLLMs heavily rely on explicit textual geometric data for spatial reasoning, showing a significant performance drop when these 'textual crutches' are removed, indicating limited ability to ground precise coordinates directly from visual inputs.
> - In-Context Learning can improve the overall silhouette quality for correctly parsed responses but does not inherently instill a fundamental understanding of rigid body and non-overlap constraints in MLLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated impressive capabilities in a wide array of vision-language tasks, including visual recognition, semantic understanding, document question answering, and chart interpretation. However, the current research landscape reveals a significant gap: while MLLMs excel at high-level semantic understanding and coarse relational concepts (e.g., left/right, front/behind), their ability to perform *precise compositional spatial reasoning* under strict geometric and physical constraints remains largely underdeveloped and underexplored.

Existing MLLM benchmarks often suffer from several limitations in evaluating spatial reasoning:
1. **Coarse Relational Focus**: Many tasks involve only basic relative positioning without demanding rigorous geometric or physical adherence.
2. **Lack of Formal Representation**: Spatial problems are frequently described using natural language or multiple-choice formats, which rely more on linguistic reasoning than precise visual-geometric understanding, leading to ambiguity.
3. **Limited Evaluation Metrics**: Evaluation often relies on text answer matching or subjective human/LLM judgment, struggling with constructive tasks where multiple valid solutions may exist and lacking mathematically rigorous validation.

"TangramPuzzle" directly addresses these limitations by introducing a novel, geometry-grounded benchmark. It leverages the classic Tangram puzzle, a problem inherently requiring multi-step compositional spatial reasoning from local parts to a global shape, subject to non-overlap, full coverage, and topological validity constraints. Unlike previous tangram-related studies (e.g., Tangram, KILOGRAM) that focus on basic recognition or perceptual approximations, TangramPuzzle introduces a *standardized, machine-verifiable geometric representation* and a *strict evaluation protocol* that validates rigidity, physical feasibility, and solution correctness. This positions TangramPuzzle as a crucial step forward in rigorously assessing MLLMs' true spatial intelligence, pushing beyond superficial cues and semantic approximations towards exact, constraint-satisfying geometric understanding. It bridges the gap between high-level visual understanding and precise low-level geometric manipulation, which is vital for developing MLLMs capable of interacting with the physical world in a more grounded and effective manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

1. **Introduce a rigorous, geometry-grounded benchmark**: To specifically evaluate the compositional spatial reasoning abilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), moving beyond current benchmarks that often focus on coarse spatial relations or semantic understanding.
2. **Develop a formal geometric representation**: To mitigate the ambiguity of visual approximation and natural language descriptions, enabling precise, machine-verifiable evaluation. This is achieved through the Tangram Construction Expression (TCE).
3. **Design complementary tasks**: To thoroughly probe different facets of spatial intelligence, ranging from discriminative global-shape inference to constructive inverse assembly generation.
4. **Conduct comprehensive evaluation**: To assess the performance of advanced open-source and proprietary MLLMs on these challenging tasks, thereby identifying their current limitations and systematic failure modes in complex spatial reasoning.
5. **Establish a standardized testbed**: To provide the research community with a robust tool for future MLLM development and comparative analysis in spatial reasoning.

The motivation for this work stems from the observed deficiencies in current MLLMs regarding precise spatial understanding. While MLLMs show strong performance in visual recognition and semantic tasks, they often struggle when confronted with problems demanding exact geometric manipulation, adherence to physical constraints, and compositional reasoning. Such capabilities are fundamental for tasks like visual grounding, GUI agents, and real-world robotics, where structured geometric relationships are paramount for reliable decision-making. The classic Tangram puzzle offers a compact yet expressive domain to test these abilities rigorously. By introducing TCE, the authors aim to eliminate the imprecision of human perception and natural language, enabling a truly objective and fine-grained evaluation of whether MLLMs genuinely "reason compositionally" in space or merely exploit superficial visual patterns.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the TangramPuzzle benchmark lies in its novel **Tangram Construction Expression (TCE)**, a symbolic geometry description language. TCE precisely represents tangram configurations using exact algebraic coordinates (expressed in LaTeX to handle irrational numbers) and structural relations, thus grounding visual inputs in unambiguous mathematical formulations. Each TCE instance comprises: `instance_id`, `target_outline` (vertex coordinates and edge relations), `initial_state` (piece type, vertex coordinates, edge relations, center point for each of the seven tangram pieces), `final_state` (including a `transform_matrix` for rigid motion parameters), and `adjacency_graph`.

Building upon TCE, TangramPuzzle features two complementary tasks:

1. **Outline Prediction (Task 1)**: This task assesses an MLLM's ability to infer a global silhouette from an arrangement of local components. The input consists of a textual TCE specification of the seven pieces and a candidate image showing four distinct silhouettes (one correct, three distractors). The model must select the correct silhouette (A-D).
2. **End-to-End Tangram Solution Generation (Task 2)**: This more challenging task evaluates the model's capacity to solve the inverse geometric assembly problem. Given a visual image of a target outline (with annotated coordinates) and its textual geometric data (vertex coordinates, edge relationships), along with initial state specifications for the seven tangram pieces, the model must output a complete TCE in JSON format. This output must define the final state of all seven pieces, ensuring they perfectly fill the target outline while adhering to all constraints.

**Data Construction Pipeline**: To ensure high-quality geometric data, the authors developed a multi-stage pipeline:
- **Raw Data Collection**: Visual patterns were sourced from the KiloGram dataset, followed by manual filtering to retain only topologically valid configurations (no holes, disconnected pieces).
- **Annotation Tool Development**: A bespoke web-based interactive annotation interface was created. It includes vertex and edge snapping mechanisms to ensure precise alignment and reduce human errors, directly exporting structured JSONL data.
- **Geometric Normalization**: Floating-point annotations were post-processed to convert all geometric quantities into exact symbolic (LaTeX) representations. Each configuration was canonized by translation, and then the global outer contour, edge relations, and piece adjacency were extracted into the TCE format. For Task 1, negative distractors were automatically generated by sampling other valid silhouettes.
- **Human Filtering**: Manual verification through visualization was performed to flag and correct misaligned vertices, gaps, or overlaps, ensuring all instances satisfy TCE's geometric validity constraints.

**Evaluation Metrics**:
- **Task 1 (Outline Prediction)**:
 - **Accuracy (Acc)**: Percentage of correctly identified silhouettes.
 - **Invalid Rate**: Proportion of responses that fail to map to a valid candidate option, indicating instruction-following failures.
- **Task 2 (End-to-End Solution Generation)**: A hierarchical, constraint-based evaluation framework:
 - **Stage 1: Constraint Validation**:
 - **Syntax Error (TSE)**: Checks TCE format, piece count, and type validity.
 - **Rigid Geometry Error (RGE)**: Verifies that each piece preserves its area and perimeter, preventing shape distortion.
 - **Physical Error (PE)**: Detects impermissible piece overlaps or disconnected assemblies.
 - **Validation Pass Rate (VPR)**: Percentage of solutions satisfying all constraints.
 - **Stage 2: Shape Similarity**: Applied only to valid solutions (high VPR).
 - **Intersection over Union (IoU)**: Measures global area overlap between predicted assembly and target silhouette.
 - **Hausdorff Distance (d_H)**: Quantifies fine-grained boundary deviations.
 - **Success**: Solutions with high VPR and good shape similarity.

This comprehensive methodology ensures rigorous, unambiguous, and machine-verifiable evaluation, moving beyond subjective assessments and enabling precise measurement of MLLMs' spatial reasoning capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments, conducted on a diverse set of open-source (Qwen3-VL, InternVL3, DeepSeek-OCR/VL2, GLM-4.6V) and proprietary (GPT-5.2, Gemini3-Pro, Claude-Sonnet-4.5) MLLMs, yielded several key findings:

**Task 1: Outline Prediction**
- **Performance Differences**: There were substantial performance differences. Gemini3-Pro achieved near-ceiling performance (98.65% accuracy), showcasing superior fine-grained visual perception and holistic shape composition. GPT-5.2 also performed well (77.54%).
- **Open-source Challenges**: Open-source models like Qwen3-VL-32B achieved a respectable 73.05%, but smaller or earlier architectures struggled significantly (below 40%).
- **Instruction Following**: Most models exhibited low invalid rates, indicating good instruction following for selecting options. However, DeepSeek-OCR and DeepSeek-VL2 had higher invalid rates, suggesting difficulty in forming decisive conclusions under complex spatial reasoning.

**Task 2: End-to-End Tangram Solution Generation**
- **Widespread Constraint Failures**: This task proved highly challenging for most MLLMs. While many maintained valid JSON syntax (low TSE), Rigid Geometry Errors (RGE) and Physical Errors (PE) were highly prevalent.
- **"Cheating" via Distortion/Overlap**: A critical insight was the "apparent visual fidelity" achieved by some top-tier models (e.g., Claude-Sonnet-4.5, GPT-5.2) which reported high IoU scores but 0% Success rates. Further inspection revealed this was due to impermissibly *distorting rigid pieces* or *overlapping them* to force a visual match with the target silhouette, rather than satisfying geometric constraints.
- **Gemini3-Pro as an Outlier**: Gemini3-Pro was the only model demonstrating robust geometric reasoning, achieving the highest Validation Pass Rate (VPR) of 22.60% and a Success rate of 21.56%, while also maintaining superior silhouette quality (85.93% IoU). This indicates a unique ability to adhere to rigid body and non-overlap constraints.
- **Human Performance Contrast**: Human solvers rarely violated geometric constraints, exhibiting a near-binary success/failure pattern. They averaged a 72.67% success rate, taking about 6.7 minutes per instance, confirming the task's non-trivial difficulty.

**Analytical Experiments**:
- **Effect of In-Context Learning (ICL)**: ICL (providing solved examples) showed a trade-off. It sometimes *increased* syntactic errors (TSE) for models like Qwen3-VL-8B and Gemini3-Pro, suggesting cognitive overload from dense symbolic contexts. However, for successfully parsed responses, ICL generally *improved silhouette quality* (IoU), refining shape approximation but not instilling a fundamental understanding of rigid body constraints.
- **Effect of Textual Geometry (Ablation Study)**: Removing explicit textual descriptions of the target outline caused a significant performance drop for most MLLMs (e.g., GPT-5.2's TSE jumped from 7.19% to 39.67%), confirming their heavy reliance on "textual crutches" for geometric reasoning rather than extracting coordinates directly from visual inputs. Again, Gemini3-Pro was a notable exception, maintaining high performance and demonstrating superior visual grounding capabilities.

**Human Evaluation on MLLMs**: Expert human evaluators confirmed Gemini3-Pro's strength, giving it high scores for Perceptual Silhouette Match (PSM), Piece Plausibility & Canonicality (PPC), and Near-Miss Severity (NMS). GPT-5.2, despite high IoU, received low scores due to frequent perceptual mismatches and implausible constructions.

**Case Studies**: Visualizations reinforced that models often prioritize matching the target silhouette visually, even if it means distorting or overlapping pieces, or hallucinating incorrect piece inventories. Smaller models frequently arranged pieces in simple linear sequences, failing to map symbolic coordinates onto a 2D planar workspace effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TangramPuzzle benchmark represents a significant advancement in the evaluation of Multimodal Large Language Models, with profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Redefining MLLM Evaluation**: By shifting the paradigm from approximate visual matching and semantic understanding to *exact, geometry-grounded, and machine-verifiable constraint satisfaction*, TangramPuzzle provides a more rigorous and objective measure of MLLMs' spatial reasoning abilities. This will compel future MLLM development to focus not just on linguistic or coarse visual understanding, but on precise geometric and physical adherence.
2. **Highlighting Critical Limitations**: The benchmark clearly exposes a fundamental weakness in current MLLMs: their tendency to prioritize visual fidelity (matching the silhouette) over strict geometric constraints (rigid body preservation, non-overlap). This "cheating" behavior, where models distort pieces or allow overlaps to achieve an apparent visual match, reveals a lack of true physical understanding and compositional reasoning, even in advanced models.
3. **Benchmarking for Real-World Applications**: Precise spatial reasoning is crucial for a myriad of real-world AI applications, including robotics, augmented reality, computer-aided design, and autonomous navigation. MLLMs that can reliably solve problems like TangramPuzzle under strict constraints are more likely to succeed in these complex embodied scenarios where geometric accuracy and physical feasibility are non-negotiable. TangramPuzzle provides a standardized testbed to push MLLM capabilities towards these goals.
4. **Guiding Future Research**: The detailed analysis of failure modes (syntax errors, rigid geometry errors, physical errors) and the insights from in-context learning and textual geometry ablation studies offer concrete directions for future MLLM research. Developing models that can robustly ground geometric data directly from vision, better integrate symbolic and visual information, and implicitly understand physical constraints will be key. The distinct performance of Gemini3-Pro suggests that some architectures are already making progress in this area.
5. **Promoting Transparency and Reproducibility**: The commitment to open-sourcing the data (TangramPuzzle dataset and TCE framework) and evaluation code under a permissive license will facilitate widespread adoption, transparency, and reproducibility within the research community, enabling standardized comparisons and accelerated progress.
6. **Ethical Considerations**: The authors proactively addressed ethical concerns, highlighting that the benchmark focuses on abstract geometric reasoning, is derived from publicly available sources, and is free from PII, sensitive biometric data, or offensive content. This ensures the research is ethically benign and socially harmless, contributing positively to the responsible development of AI.

**Limitations and Future Directions**: While a significant step, the authors acknowledge that TangramPuzzle is currently confined to 2D planar geometry with a fixed number of components. Future work aims to extend this evaluation paradigm to three-dimensional settings, incorporating variable object counts and complexities like occlusion, which would bring the evaluation even closer to real-world embodied scenarios. This evolution would further enhance the benchmark's utility in developing truly intelligent and physically grounded MLLMs.
