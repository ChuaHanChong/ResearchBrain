# EWAM: An Enhanced World Action Model for Closed-Loop Online Adaptation in Embodied Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> EWAM introduces a closed-loop online adaptation architecture that enhances the execution quality and robustness of pre-trained World Action Models (WAMs) in zero-shot deployments. It achieves this by adding four specialized neural layers that provide memory-augmented context, real-time anomaly detection, intelligent policy routing, and action refinement, demonstrating substantial reductions in task completion time and execution faults in simulation.

## Problem

- High cost and resource intensity of collecting extensive offline demonstrations for diverse object poses and scene layouts in open environments.
- Vulnerability of static, offline-trained WAMs to execution-level mismatches, such as collisions, empty grasps, and perception-induced hallucinations, during real-time deployment.
- Inefficient utilization of online experience, where unfiltered learning can lead to policy degradation through the indiscriminate incorporation of unsafe or suboptimal trajectories.

## Method

- Developed **EWAM**, a ==closed-loop online adaptation== architecture, by inserting four trainable neural layers into the inference path of a frozen `Cosmos3-Nano--Policy-DROID` WAM backbone.
- Implemented layers for Neural Experience Memory (retrieves relevant past experiences), Neural Anomaly Detection (identifies execution faults), Neural Policy Routing (selects strategy based on anomalies), and Neural Action Correction (refines actions or triggers rollback).
- Incorporated an experience filter that only admits 'qualified' trajectories (meeting safety and success criteria) for memory accumulation and intermittent online parameter updates to the new neural layers, preventing policy degradation.

## Results

- Reduced average task completion time by **63.8%** (from **25.60s** to **9.27s**) and end-effector path length by **54.1%** on the `BananaInBowlTask`, with consistent time reductions across other multi-task scenarios.
- Decreased total execution faults per episode by **83.7%** (from 13.5 to 2.2) on the `BananaInBowlTask`, specifically reducing collisions by **90.6%** and empty grasps by **92.9%**.
- Improved task success rate on the challenging `BlockStackingOrderAgnosticTask` from **87.2%** to **94.4%**, demonstrating enhanced robustness in sequential tasks prone to compounding errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Closed-loop adaptation layers can substantially improve the execution quality of pre-trained WAMs in zero-shot, contact-rich tasks without requiring new task-specific demonstration data.
> - A "memory-first, adapter-second" design, prioritizing immediate memory retrieval for context over frequent parameter updates to lightweight adapters, offers more reliable online adaptation from sparse experience.
> - Integrating real-time anomaly detection with intelligent policy routing and action correction mechanisms (including conservative replanning and rollback recovery) is crucial for effective fault detection and recovery in dynamic environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the field of embodied intelligence, specifically focusing on the deployment and adaptation of robotic policies in dynamic and open environments. It builds upon the established concept of **World Models (WMs)**, which learn predictive representations of environment dynamics. Recent advancements have evolved WMs into **World Action Models (WAMs)**, which integrate future-state prediction directly with robot action generation. Existing WAMs, such as DreamZero, Fast-WAM, GigaWorld-Policy, and Cosmos 3, have explored various aspects of zero-shot policy learning, efficient inference, and action-centered design.

EWAM specifically uses a frozen `Cosmos3-Nano--Policy-DROID` WAM as its backbone, positioning itself within the lineage of models that leverage powerful pre-trained foundational policies. The paper distinguishes EWAM from **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** (e.g., SayCan, RT-1/2, OpenVLA, ABot-M0), which primarily focus on semantic understanding and mapping natural language instructions to actions. While VLA models emphasize language-visual semantic understanding, WAMs like Cosmos3 and EWAM prioritize world-state prediction, physical consistency, and contact-sensitive action generation. EWAM is presented as complementary to VLA models, enhancing the execution quality of a WAM rather than replacing its semantic understanding capabilities.

Furthermore, EWAM engages with research on **action diffusion, asynchronous control, and feedback** (e.g., Diffusion Policy, planning with diffusion, Diffusion Transformers), by linking memory retrieval to the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) reasoning path and connecting filtering, rollback, and action correction to execution diagnostics. The work is evaluated within the context of high-fidelity simulation benchmarks like **RoboLab**, which are designed for analyzing task-generalist policies, and relates to other benchmarks like LIBERO and RoboMimic.

The paper aims to address a deployment-oriented question: whether closed-loop adaptation layers can enhance execution quality for pre-trained WAMs on contact-rich tasks in novel layouts, without requiring new demonstration data for the target tasks (a zero-shot protocol).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a closed-loop online adaptation architecture, the Enhanced World Action Model (EWAM), that improves the execution quality and robustness of pre-trained World Action Models (WAMs) in zero-shot deployment scenarios. The motivation stems from three practical bottlenecks identified in existing WAM pipelines when applied to new environments with limited additional data:

1. **High Cost of Offline Demonstration Collection:** Collecting extensive offline demonstrations and annotated trajectories that cover the diverse range of object poses, scene layouts, and execution contingencies encountered in open environments is expensive and resource-intensive. This cost restricts the scalability of robot deployment to new, varied settings.
2. **Vulnerability to Execution-Level Mismatch:** Many robot failures in real-world deployment arise not from a fundamental misunderstanding of the task, but from discrepancies between predicted and realized states at the execution level. Examples include collisions with container edges, grasping attempts that result in empty hands (empty grasps), and high-confidence perception that does not correspond to physical contact (perception-induced hallucinations). Static, offline-trained WAMs often lack effective mechanisms to detect and recover from these real-time execution divergences.
3. **Inefficient Online Experience Utilization:** Once deployed, static WAMs do not efficiently convert successful local experiences into advantages for future actions. Unfiltered online learning can lead to policy degradation if unsafe or redundant trajectories are indiscriminately incorporated back into the training stream, potentially leading to catastrophic forgetting or accumulation of suboptimal behaviors.

To address these issues, EWAM is designed to operate on a frozen `Cosmos3-Nano--Policy-DROID` WAM backbone, meaning no new demonstration datasets are collected for target tasks in the zero-shot protocol. Instead, adaptation is achieved through four newly introduced neural layers that enable closed-loop inference, memory-augmented context, real-time anomaly detection, intelligent policy routing, and action refinement. The method explicitly targets specific execution problems: prediction-realization mismatch, collision, empty grasp, perception hallucination, and trajectory redundancy, aiming to enhance the robustness, efficiency, and safety of robot manipulation in new, unseen scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EWAM is implemented as a closed-loop online adaptation architecture built upon a frozen `Cosmos3-Nano--Policy-DROID` backbone. The core methodology involves inserting four trainable neural layers into the pre-trained policy's inference path, alongside a rollback manager and an experience filter.

**A. Frozen Backbone and Inserted Neural Layers:**
The `Cosmos3-Nano--Policy-DROID` backbone comprises a Vision-Language Encoder (VLE) for multimodal perception, an Autoregressive (AR) Reasoner for long-horizon task planning, and a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) for action generation. The parameters of this backbone ($\phi$) remain frozen throughout. EWAM adds the following four trainable neural layers ($\theta$):

1. **Neural Experience Memory Layer:**
 - **Placement:** Inserted at layer `l_mem = 6` (an intermediate layer) of the DiT.
 - **Functionality:** Retrieves task-relevant experiences from an experience memory database `M` (using k=5 nearest neighbors) and injects them into the DiT's hidden representations. This conditioning allows the action generation process to leverage historical context, providing task-relevant priors and potential rollback anchors. Retrieval is performed using attention, where a query from the DiT hidden state attends to encoded memory items conditioned by the AR context.

2. **Neural Anomaly Detection Layer:**
 - **Placement:** After the state prediction head of the DiT.
 - **Functionality:** Detects execution anomalies by combining signals such as the previous prediction residual (difference between realized and predicted states), candidate action dynamics inconsistency, and learned risk scores for specific issues like collision probability, empty grasp probability, hallucination probability, and force violation probability. It outputs a 6-dimensional anomaly detection vector `ι_t`. After action execution, the realized state transition residual is used for filtering and online updates.

3. **Neural Policy Routing Layer:**
 - **Placement:** After the Neural Anomaly Detection Layer.
 - **Functionality:** Based on the anomaly vector `ι_t`, the AR context `c_t`, and a summary of the memory `M`, this layer selects an appropriate execution strategy. The routing decision `r_t` can be one of three options:
 - **Direct execution:** When anomaly signals are below a threshold (e.g., aggregate anomaly score < 0.3).
 - **Conservative replanning:** Triggers action correction with conservative constraints for mild anomalies (e.g., aggregate anomaly score between 0.3 and 0.7).
 - **Rollback recovery:** Restores the robot to a nearest stable state from memory when severe anomalies are detected (e.g., aggregate anomaly score $\ge$ 0.7 or unstable contact).

4. **Neural Action Correction Layer:**
 - **Placement:** After the action output head of the DiT.
 - **Functionality:** Refines the raw action chunk `a_0` generated by the backbone. The refinement, `a*`, depends on the routing decision `r_t`, the anomaly signals `ι_t`, and memory context `M`. For conservative replanning, a lightweight `CorrectionNet` predicts bounded action adjustments. For rollback recovery, `RollbackAction` retrieves a conservative action from a stable memory state.

**B. Closed-Loop Online Learning and Experience Filtering:**
EWAM operates in a continuous closed loop:
1. Perception and current state are encoded by the VLE.
2. The AR Reasoner generates a context.
3. The DiT generates raw actions, augmented by memory retrieval via the Neural Experience Memory Layer.
4. The State Head predicts the next state, and the Action Head produces raw actions.
5. The Neural Anomaly Detection Layer assesses risks.
6. The Neural Policy Routing Layer decides the strategy.
7. The Neural Action Correction Layer refines the action.
8. The corrected action is executed in the environment.
9. **Experience Filtering:** Crucially, the execution outcome is evaluated by an experience filter. Only "qualified" trajectories—those meeting specific safety, efficiency, and task success criteria (e.g., SafetyScore $\ge$ 0.75, TaskScore $\ge$ 0.80, low gripper force, good end-effector smoothness, short path, fast time)—are admitted. Low-quality samples are logged for diagnostics but are explicitly prevented from contaminating the memory or contributing to online parameter updates.
10. **Memory and Parameter Updates:** Qualified trajectories are written to the experience memory `M`. Intermittent parameter updates are applied *only* to the four neural layers and lightweight adapters ($\theta$), using replay from admitted memory samples, ensuring the frozen backbone's general capabilities are preserved.

**C. Training and Online Update Objectives:**
Offline preparation for EWAM involves supervised losses for state prediction, action generation, memory retrieval, anomaly detection (binary cross-entropy for risk heads and L2 loss for residuals), routing (cross-entropy), and action correction. During deployment, the memory `M` is updated every time a sample passes filtering, while the neural layer parameters `θ` are updated intermittently based on a scheduled update indicator and sampled from the filtered memory.

**D. Memory-First, Adapter-Second Design:**
This strategy prioritizes immediate memory retrieval for task-specific context at the start of a new task, which is instantaneous and does not modify parameters. Online parameter updates are then applied conservatively and intermittently to lightweight adapters, preserving the general capabilities of the frozen WAM backbone and mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of EWAM primarily focused on zero-shot manipulation tasks within the RoboLab simulation environment, using a frozen `Cosmos3-Nano--Policy-DROID` backbone.

**A. Performance on BananaInBowlTask (Local Test):**
On the `BananaInBowlTask`, EWAM demonstrated improvements in execution efficiency and quality while maintaining task success.
- **Success Rate:** Both `Cosmos3-Nano--Policy-DROID` and EWAM achieved a 100.0% task success rate.
- **Completion Time:** EWAM reduced the average task completion time from 25.60 s to 9.27 s, representing a 63.8% reduction. The 95% confidence intervals (23.29–27.91s for baseline, 8.33–10.21s for EWAM) were non-overlapping.
- **Path Length:** EWAM reduced the end-effector path length from 1.81 m to 0.83 m, a 54.1% reduction. Confidence intervals (1.69–1.93m for baseline, 0.75–0.91m for EWAM) also showed clear separation.
- **Execution Faults:** EWAM substantially reduced the total execution faults per episode from 13.5 to 2.2, an 83.7% reduction. Specific fault reductions included:
 - Collisions: Reduced from 3.2 to 0.3 per episode (90.6% reduction).
 - Empty Grasps: Reduced from 1.4 to 0.1 per episode (92.9% reduction).
 - Over-Force: Reduced from 2.1 to 0.4 per episode (81.0% reduction).
 - Redundancy: Reduced from 4.3 to 0.9 per episode (79.1% reduction).

**B. Inference Latency and Hardware Overhead:**
The computational overhead introduced by the four neural layers was measured on an RTX 5880 Ada platform.
- **Additional Latency:** The four EWAM layers introduced an additional 10.2 ms of latency, increasing the total inference time from the baseline's 66.2 ms to 76.4 ms (+15.4%). The Memory Layer was the largest contributor (3.1 ms).
- **GPU Memory:** An additional 130 MB of GPU memory was required, increasing total usage from 1690 MB to 1820 MB (+7.7%).
- **Throughput:** Throughput decreased from 621 to 520 steps/s (-16.3%).
The authors note that the 76.4 ms inference path exceeds a strict 20 Hz servo loop (50 ms budget), suggesting its use for chunk-level replanning or asynchronous inference.

**C. Ablation Study:**
An ablation study on `BananaInBowlTask` explored the contribution of individual EWAM components, with all variants maintaining 100% success.
- **Impact of Self-Learning:** Removing online self-learning (i.e., filtered online experience accumulation) caused the largest degradation, increasing task time from 9.27s (full EWAM) to 46.53s (+402%), indicating its critical role in deployment-time improvement.
- **Impact of Error Correction:** Removing the Neural Action Correction Layer increased task time to 18.20s (+96.1%), suggesting its importance in mitigating collisions and empty grasps.
- **Impact of Experience Memory:** Removing the Neural Experience Memory Layer increased task time to 10.60s (+14.4%), demonstrating the benefit of retrieved execution context.
- **Impact of Rollback:** Removing rollback had a smaller effect on this specific task, increasing time to 10.27s (+10.8%), consistent with the task's lower fault frequency.
- **Memory-first vs. Update-only:** A "Memory-only" variant (12.67s task time) outperformed an "Update-only" variant (14.67s task time) by 13.6%, supporting the design choice that memory retrieval provides more reliable adaptation from sparse online data than immediate parameter updates.

**D. Multi-Task Generalization:**
EWAM was evaluated on two additional RoboLab tasks: `BananasInBinOneMoreTask` and `BlockStackingOrderAgnosticTask`.
- **Consistent Time Reduction:** EWAM consistently reduced completion time across all tasks: 63.8% on `BananaInBowl`, 39.1% on `BananasInBinOneMoreTask` (from 18.40s to 11.20s), and 32.4% on `BlockStackingOrderAgnosticTask` (from 42.30s to 28.60s).
- **Success Rate Improvement:** On the more challenging `BlockStackingOrderAgnosticTask`, EWAM improved the success rate from 87.2% to 94.4%, suggesting the benefit of anomaly detection and rollback in multi-step sequential tasks prone to compounding errors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EWAM presents a method for enhancing the robustness and efficiency of embodied intelligence systems, particularly those utilizing World Action Models (WAMs), through a closed-loop online adaptation architecture. Its significance lies in several areas:

**A. Enhanced Execution Quality in Zero-Shot Deployment:** EWAM demonstrates that a pre-trained WAM backbone can achieve substantial improvements in execution quality—specifically reducing task completion time, path length, and various execution faults (collisions, empty grasps, over-force, redundancy)—in zero-shot settings without requiring new task-specific demonstration data. This addresses a critical limitation of deploying robots in novel environments, where collecting extensive new demonstrations is often prohibitive.

**B. Practical Online Adaptation with Frozen Backbones:** The architecture introduces four trainable neural layers (Neural Experience Memory, Anomaly Detection, Policy Routing, and Action Correction) that operate around a frozen WAM backbone. This design allows for efficient online adaptation and learning from experience while preserving the broad general capabilities of the pre-trained foundational model, mitigating the risk of catastrophic forgetting from sparse online data.

**C. Robustness through Real-time Anomaly Detection and Recovery:** EWAM integrates real-time anomaly detection, policy routing, and action correction mechanisms. This enables the system to identify and respond to execution-level mismatches—such as prediction errors, collision risks, empty grasps, and perception hallucinations—by adjusting actions, entering conservative replanning, or triggering rollback recovery to a stable state. This capability contributes to safer and more reliable robot operation in dynamic environments.

**D. Structured Online Learning with Experience Filtering:** The inclusion of an experience filtering mechanism is significant. By admitting only high-quality, safe, and efficient trajectories into the experience memory and for parameter updates, EWAM prevents the contamination of its learning processes with erroneous or suboptimal behaviors. This controlled approach to online learning is crucial for maintaining and improving policy quality over time.

**E. Complementary to Existing Robot Learning Paradigms:** EWAM is designed to be complementary to existing advancements in WAMs and VLA models. It focuses on addressing execution-level physical consistency and adaptation rather than fundamental semantic understanding or world modeling, offering a practical layer for improving the deployment readiness of such systems.

**Potential Impact:**
The potential impact of EWAM includes:
- **Reduced Data Collection Costs:** By improving adaptation in zero-shot scenarios, EWAM could significantly reduce the cost and effort associated with collecting new demonstrations for deploying robots to novel environments or tasks.
- **Increased Robot Autonomy and Reliability:** The enhanced ability to detect and recover from execution faults, coupled with online learning from successful experiences, could lead to more autonomous and reliable robotic systems capable of operating robustly in varied and unconstrained settings.
- **Foundation for Future Adaptive Systems:** The "memory-first, adapter-second" design principle and the architecture for closed-loop adaptation could serve as a valuable framework for developing next-generation robot learning systems that can continuously refine their performance in real-world deployments.

**Limitations:**
The study acknowledges several systematic limitations:
- **Simulation-Only Validation:** All experiments were conducted in RoboLab simulation, and sim-to-real transfer has not been established. Real-robot validation for concepts like rollback recovery (which currently uses simulator checkpoints) and force-sensitive primitives is pending.
- **Architecture Specificity:** The EWAM modules and their configurations are currently designed and validated for the `Cosmos3-Nano--Policy-DROID` backbone, limiting immediate generality to other WAMs without further adaptation.
- **Limited Task Coverage:** While showing generalization across three RoboLab tasks, the task family and layout diversity remain limited compared to open-world scenarios.
- **Data Characteristics and Filtering:** The six hard filtering thresholds used for experience admission are scenario-specific to the RoboLab container manipulation tasks and are not universally applicable.
- **Residual Failure Modes:** Current limitations include challenges with extreme occlusion, severe changes in object dynamics, close-proximity multi-object ambiguity, long-horizon compounding errors, and high sensor noise, partly attributed to compact memory representations and simulation-only rollback.

Despite these limitations, EWAM offers a contribution toward practical, efficient, and robust online adaptation for robot manipulation within the framework of pre-trained World Action Models.
