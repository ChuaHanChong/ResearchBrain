# FysicsWorld: A Unified Full-Modality Benchmark for Any-to-Any Understanding, Generation, and Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and Fysics AI developed FysicsWorld, a unified full-modality benchmark for evaluating any-to-any understanding, generation, and reasoning across image, video, audio, and text, utilizing a Cross-Modal Complementarity Screening strategy to necessitate genuine multimodal fusion. Evaluations using this benchmark indicate that current omni-modal models exhibit limitations in fusion-dependent reasoning and robust speech-driven interaction.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal benchmarks offer incomplete modality coverage and lack bidirectional input/output across image, video, audio, and text.
- Many current benchmarks suffer from weak cross-modal dependencies, allowing models to complete tasks using only a single modality without true fusion.
- Evaluation is often restricted to text-centric outputs, overlooking crucial multimodal generation capabilities and speech-driven interaction.

## Method

- Development of **FysicsWorld**, a unified full-modality benchmark enabling any-to-any understanding, generation, and reasoning across image, video, audio, and text.
- Implementation of the ==Cross-Modal Complementarity Screening== (CMCS) strategy to ensure tasks necessitate genuine multimodal fusion by filtering out unimodal shortcuts.
- Inclusion of ==speech-driven image/video understanding== and QA, fusion-dependent cross-modal reasoning, and cross-modal audio generation tasks.

## Results

- **Proprietary MLLMs** consistently achieved superior performance in uni-modal understanding tasks compared to open-source counterparts.
- **Unified understanding-generation models** showed weaker alignment with fine-grained textual constraints in image generation and temporal consistency in video generation.
- All evaluated **OmniLLMs** experienced a **marked performance drop** on fusion-dependent tasks identified by the CMCS strategy, struggling to genuinely integrate heterogeneous information.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proprietary MLLMs generally outperform open-source models in uni-modal understanding, though advanced open-source OmniLLMs are narrowing this gap.
> - Unified understanding-generation models show competitive image synthesis but struggle with fine-grained control and temporal consistency in video generation compared to specialized models.
> - Current omni-modal models exhibit significant performance degradation on fusion-dependent reasoning tasks and speech-driven interactions, indicating a lack of true multimodal fusion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence, particularly large language models (LLMs), has undergone a significant paradigm shift towards multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and, more recently, omni-modal large language models (OmniLLMs). These emerging architectures aim to process and generate information across various modalities such, as text, vision, and audio, moving beyond the text-centric capabilities of traditional LLMs. The ambition is to develop systems that can not only perceive omni-modal content but also integrate understanding and generation capabilities across modalities, enabling synergistic interactions that mirror human perception and action in the physical world.

This research addresses a critical gap in the evaluation of these advanced multimodal systems. While a proliferation of benchmarks has emerged to assess MLLMs and OmniLLMs, existing solutions often exhibit limitations. These include incomplete modality coverage, where interactions between images, videos, audio, and text are not fully integrated or bidirectional. Many current benchmarks rely on shallow modality concatenation, meaning tasks can often be solved by leveraging a single modality, thus failing to enforce genuine multimodal fusion, alignment, or reasoning. Furthermore, a substantial portion of existing omni-modal benchmarks remain text-centric, focusing primarily on cross-modal understanding while neglecting multimodal generation and speech-driven interaction, which are crucial for real-world communication.

FysicsWorld positions itself within this landscape by introducing a unified full-modality benchmark designed to overcome these identified limitations. It aims to provide a platform for comprehensive, any-to-any evaluation that supports bidirectional input and output across image, video, audio, and text. By carefully curating cross-modal dependencies and complementarities, FysicsWorld seeks to ensure that successful task completion necessitates true multimodal fusion, moving beyond tasks solvable via unimodal shortcuts. This work contributes to establishing a more rigorous and comprehensive standard for evaluating the capabilities of next-generation full-modality intelligent systems, thereby guiding future research in omni-modal learning and general-purpose multimodal intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of FysicsWorld is motivated by several identified limitations in existing benchmarks for multimodal and omni-modal large language models (MLLMs and OmniLLMs). The paper highlights a rapid paradigm shift towards OmniLLMs, which are unified architectures capable of jointly processing and generating information across text, vision, audio, and potentially other sensory modalities. The goal of these models is not merely to perceive multimodal content, but to integrate understanding and generation across modalities, enabling synergistic interactions. This ambition is rooted in the complexity of the physical world, where real-world intelligence relies on integrating rich information—visual cues, auditory signals, spatial dynamics—and responding to subtle multimodal interactions.

However, the authors identify several deficiencies in current benchmarks that hinder a comprehensive and realistic evaluation of these advanced models:

1. **Incomplete Modality Coverage:** Many existing benchmarks offer limited combinations of modalities (e.g., images or videos with audio in restricted ways), failing to capture the full spectrum of interactions present in real-world environments involving image, video, audio, and text simultaneously.
2. **Weak Cross-Modal Dependencies:** Current omni-modal data construction methods often rely on superficial modality concatenation. This design can allow models to complete tasks using only a single modality, circumventing the need for genuine multimodal fusion, alignment, or reasoning, and thus obscuring the true multimodal capabilities of the models.
3. **Restricted to Text-Centric Outputs:** Nearly all existing omni-modal benchmarks predominantly evaluate cross-modal understanding through text-based outputs. They largely overlook multimodal generation capabilities and almost entirely omit speech-driven interaction, which is a fundamental interface for real-world human communication.
4. **Fragmented Evaluation Landscape:** Uni-modal benchmarks, despite their proliferation, vary widely in task design, coverage, and difficulty, complicating unified evaluation and systematic analysis of full-modality architectures.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the FysicsWorld project are:

1. **Introduce a Unified Full-Modality Benchmark:** To establish FysicsWorld as the first benchmark that supports bidirectional input–output across image, video, audio, and text.
2. **Enable Comprehensive Any-to-Any Evaluation:** To allow for a wide range of evaluation scenarios covering understanding, generation, and reasoning across any combination of input and output modalities.
3. **Propose a Robust Data Construction Strategy:** To introduce the Cross-Modal Complementarity Screening (CMCS) strategy, integrated into a systematic framework for generating high-quality omni-modal data. This strategy specifically aims to create tasks with strong cross-modal coupling, ensuring that tasks require genuine multimodal fusion and reasoning by preventing single-modality shortcuts.
4. **Establish Baselines and Unify Evaluation:** To conduct a comprehensive evaluation of over 30 state-of-the-art models (including OmniLLMs, MLLMs, modality-specific models, and unified understanding-generation models) on FysicsWorld. This objective aims to reveal performance disparities, expose limitations across different architectures, and establish strong baselines, thereby providing a unified foundation for advancing future full-modality architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FysicsWorld is constructed as a unified full-modality benchmark designed to enable comprehensive any-to-any evaluation across understanding, generation, and reasoning, supporting bidirectional input–output across image, video, audio, and text. The methodology is structured around two complementary subsets: FysicsWorld-Uni for uni-modal tasks and FysicsWorld-Omni for omni-modal tasks, integrating specific data construction strategies and evaluation protocols.

#### 3.1. Overview and Data Characteristics

The benchmark comprises 3,268 curated samples, spanning 16 primary tasks and 226 fine-grained sub-tasks, covering 179 open-domain topics. It aggregates data from over 40 high-quality sources, including public datasets and curated web data. The benchmark is characterized by eight "multi" properties:
- **Multi-dimensional:** Covering understanding, generation, reasoning, and voice interaction.
- **Multi-modal:** Utilizing text, image, video, and audio as both inputs and outputs.
- **Multi-task:** Encompassing 16 primary tasks and 226 sub-tasks.
- **Multi-source:** Aggregating data from over 40 diverse public datasets and web sources.
- **Multi-domain:** Covering 179 open-domain categories.
- **Multi-type:** Including closed-ended questions, open-ended questions, multiple-choice questions, and image/video/audio generation.
- **Multi-target:** Evaluating OmniLLMs, MLLMs, modality-specific models, and unified understanding–generation models.
- **Multi-assurance:** Employing multi-stage quality control strategies during data construction.

#### 3.2. Construction of FysicsWorld-Uni

FysicsWorld-Uni consists of 5 foundational uni-modal tasks designed to assess basic understanding and generation capabilities. The construction process involves a multi-source synthesis and refinement pipeline to address fragmented evaluation and ensure real-world robustness. Data is curated from over 40 uni-modal datasets, with selective integration of complementary, high-quality instances. Low-quality annotations undergo manual correction through human-LLM collaborative review, and coverage is expanded to include real-world interactive scenarios.

- **Image Understanding:** Tasks include general Visual Question Answering (VQA), university-level reasoning, mathematics, OCR/charts, and hallucination detection. Data sources include MME, MMMU, MathVista, MMVP, HallusionBench, and real-world sets like MME-RealWorld and SEED-Bench-H.
- **Video Understanding:** Built from Video-MME and MVBench, supplemented with streaming videos from OmniMMI to emphasize real-world temporal reasoning. Multiple-choice candidate pools are refined to reduce ambiguity and enhance discriminative distractors.
- **Audio Reasoning:** Integrates data from MMAU, MMAR, MMSU, and AIR-Bench to evaluate speech, sound, and music across over 20 sub-tasks, probing both perceptual recognition and semantic reasoning.
- **Image/Video Generation:** Incorporates WISE, GEdit-Bench, VBench, and Video-Bench to assess instruction-conditioned synthesis, controllable editing, and temporal consistency.

#### 3.3. Construction of FysicsWorld-Omni

FysicsWorld-Omni addresses the limitations of prior omni-modal benchmarks by extending evaluation to real-world voice-interactive understanding and generation. It includes 11 omni-modal tasks categorized into: (i) speech-driven image & video understanding and QA interaction, (ii) fusion-dependent cross-modal reasoning, and (iii) cross-modal audio generation.

- **Speech-Driven Image/Video Understanding and QA Interaction:**
 - This pipeline begins with public image/video understanding datasets, filtering tasks unsuitable for speech interaction (e.g., OCR).
 - LLM-based conversational rewriting is applied to textual QA pairs to expand terse answers, reformulate numerals/symbols into spoken-friendly forms, and convert formal phrasing into natural oral expressions, resulting in dialogue-style instructions.
 - Each rewritten sample is synthesized into audio using Text-to-Speech (TTS) with 20 diverse voices.
 - Synthesized audio is validated for semantic alignment with the original text using AlignScore via Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR). Samples below a predefined AlignScore threshold are re-synthesized or discarded.
- **Fusion-Dependent Cross-Modal Reasoning (Cross-Modal Complementarity Screening - CMCS Strategy):**
 - To ensure tasks necessitate genuine multimodal fusion rather than unimodal shortcuts, the CMCS strategy is employed.
 - Advanced MLLMs (GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Pro) first evaluate candidate samples with full multimodal inputs.
 - Subsequently, a single modality is randomly removed from the input stream, and model accuracy is re-evaluated.
 - Samples demonstrating substantial performance degradation upon modality ablation are retained as fusion-dependent cases, confirming that the task requires integrating multiple complementary modalities. Conversely, samples where ablation does not significantly impact performance are filtered out.

#### 3.4. Quality Assurance and Ethical Considerations

A multi-assurance strategy is implemented:
- Data collection involves human screening and sensitive content filtering (e.g., for profanity, violence, PII) on publicly available, high-quality datasets.
- Omni-modal synthesis leverages LLM-based conversational rewriting, TTS–ASR consistency checking, and the CMCS strategy.
- All data is sourced from licensed public origins and excludes personally identifiable information. Synthetic speech does not imitate identifiable voices.

#### 3.5. Experimental Settings and Metrics

The benchmark evaluates a range of models, including OmniLLMs (e.g., Qwen-Omni, VITA, Ming-lite-Omni), MLLMs (e.g., GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Qwen-VL, InternVL), modality-specific models (e.g., Qwen-Audio, SALMONN), and unified understanding–generation models (e.g., BLIP3-o-NEXT, Ovis-U1).

Evaluation metrics are tailored per task:
- **Textual Outputs (Understanding/Reasoning):** Accuracy for closed-ended and multiple-choice questions; BERTScore and LLM judgment (using GPT-5 with a strict protocol, Figure 9) for open-ended tasks.
- **Image Generation/Editing (Task 1-4):** WiScore for generation and VIEScore (semantic consistency, perceptual quality, overall quality) for editing (Figures 7, 10).
- **Video Generation (Task 1-5):** Five-point scale ratings by MLLMs across imaging quality, aesthetic appeal, motion coherence, and temporal consistency (Figures 11, 12, 13).
- **Audio Generation (Tasks 2-3, 3-3):** Speaker Similarity (SIM) for acoustic quality and ASR-BLEU (BLEU score between ASR-transcribed output and textual reference) for semantic accuracy.
- **LLM-based Judgment Validation:** GPT-5 serves as a unified evaluator, showing high Pearson correlation (r > 0.9) with human experts, indicating reliability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation across FysicsWorld-Uni and FysicsWorld-Omni revealed specific performance characteristics and limitations of current multimodal and omni-modal architectures.

#### 4.1. Results on FysicsWorld-Uni

Evaluations of image understanding (Task 1-1), image generation (Task 1-4), video understanding (Task 1-2), video generation (Task 1-5), and audio reasoning (Task 1-3) provided insights into uni-modal capabilities:

- **Understanding Tasks (Image, Video, Audio):** Proprietary and large-scale MLLMs, such as GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5-Pro, consistently exhibited superior performance compared to open-source counterparts. These models demonstrated higher visual-semantic alignment, temporal grounding, and robustness across diverse query types. Among open-source OmniLLMs, Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B performed strongest, narrowing the gap with top proprietary models in image and video understanding, indicating the effectiveness of its training pipelines. However, its performance in higher-level reasoning tasks still trailed leading proprietary models, suggesting limitations in open-source omni-modal training related to data scale, modality diversity, or training efficiency.
- **Generative Tasks (Image, Video):** Unified understanding–generation models showed competitive visual synthesis fidelity in image generation but demonstrated weaker alignment with fine-grained textual constraints compared to modality-specialized generative models. This performance discrepancy became more pronounced in video generation, where issues with temporal coherence and prompt consistency were observed. These findings suggest an architectural tension: while unified models offer flexible I/O pathways, they may lack the precision and control of specialized generative mechanisms.

#### 4.2. Results on FysicsWorld-Omni

The evaluation of 11 omni-modal tasks, designed with strong cross-modal coupling via the CMCS strategy, provided a deeper assessment of cross-modal reasoning and interaction:

- **Speech-Driven Visual Understanding Tasks:** MLLMs, including those with strong performance in text-driven tasks, exhibited a noticeable performance degradation when tasks were driven by speech inputs. This performance gap indicates the compounded difficulty of accurately parsing speech signals, preserving fine-grained semantic cues, and integrating them effectively with visual grounding. Nevertheless, models such as Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B, GPT-5, and Gemini-2.5-Pro showed robustness, suggesting that general-purpose multi-encoder fusion can facilitate the unification of audio and visual semantics to some extent.
- **Fusion-Dependent Reasoning Tasks (CMCS-selected):** All evaluated OmniLLMs demonstrated a marked performance drop on tasks selected by the CMCS strategy, which require integrating heterogeneous information streams (audio cues, visual dynamics, linguistic context) to prevent unimodal shortcuts. Accuracy often lagged significantly behind performance on corresponding uni-modal tasks. This outcome indicates that despite being able to consume multiple modalities, modern MLLMs frequently struggle to interleave modality-specific cues into a coherent reasoning trajectory that genuinely leverages cross-modal dependencies.
- **Cross-Modal Generation Tasks (e.g., Speech Generation from Visual Identity):** These tasks further exposed limitations in multimodal alignment, specifically in the robust mapping of visual identity cues to acoustic characteristics. This capability is considered essential for realistic human-AI interaction.
- **Next-Action Prediction:** This task, which combines temporal reasoning, state tracking, and procedural generation from video sequences and current visual states, presented one of the most challenging evaluation settings. While GPT-5 and Qwen3-Omni achieved the highest accuracies, a substantial gap remained compared to human-level reasoning, indicating that capabilities such as temporal chaining and situational awareness are still developing in current omni-modal systems.

Overall, the evaluations using FysicsWorld revealed that while advanced MLLMs and OmniLLMs have made progress in integrating multiple modalities, significant challenges persist in achieving true fusion-dependent reasoning, robust speech-driven interaction, and precise cross-modal generation, particularly in complex, real-world scenarios that demand deep semantic integration across diverse information streams.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of FysicsWorld is significant for the field of multimodal AI, particularly in the advancement and evaluation of omni-modal large language models (OmniLLMs). Its primary contributions and potential impacts are multi-faceted:

- **Unified and Comprehensive Evaluation Standard:** FysicsWorld establishes the first unified full-modality benchmark that supports bidirectional input–output across image, video, audio, and text, enabling comprehensive "any-to-any" evaluation across understanding, generation, and reasoning tasks. This holistic approach addresses the fragmentation and incompleteness observed in previous benchmarks, providing a more integrated platform for assessing the capabilities of advanced multimodal systems.
- **Enforcement of Genuine Multimodal Fusion:** The proposed Cross-Modal Complementarity Screening (CMCS) strategy is a methodological contribution that significantly enhances the rigor of omni-modal evaluation. By ensuring that retained tasks require strong cross-modal coupling and cannot be solved via single-modality shortcuts, FysicsWorld forces models to engage in genuine multimodal fusion and reasoning, thereby offering a more accurate diagnosis of their true integrative intelligence.
- **Identification of Capability Gaps and Limitations:** Through extensive evaluation of over 30 state-of-the-art models, FysicsWorld has systematically exposed critical performance disparities and fundamental limitations across various architectures. These findings highlight that while models can consume multiple modalities, they often struggle with deep multimodal integration, particularly in speech-driven interaction, fusion-dependent reasoning, and precise cross-modal generation. This diagnostic capability is crucial for guiding targeted research and development efforts.
- **Establishment of Strong Baselines:** The comprehensive evaluation provides a set of robust baselines for current OmniLLMs, MLLMs, and modality-specific models. These baselines serve as critical reference points for future work, allowing researchers to accurately measure progress and compare novel architectures against established performance levels.
- **Roadmap for Next-Generation Architectures:** By clearly identifying the limitations of current systems—such as weaknesses in modality alignment, temporal chaining, situational awareness, and the need for deeper causal inference and structured representations—FysicsWorld implicitly provides a roadmap for the future development of full-modality architectures. It suggests that future OmniLLMs must evolve beyond simple modality concatenation towards more sophisticated mechanisms for multimodal integration and world modeling.
- **Advancing Human-AI Interaction:** The emphasis on speech-driven interaction and cross-modal audio generation directly addresses capabilities essential for realistic human-AI communication. Improvements in these areas, spurred by FysicsWorld's evaluation, could lead to more natural, intuitive, and effective human-AI interfaces for real-world deployment.

In summary, FysicsWorld contributes a foundational benchmark that not only provides a robust framework for evaluating emerging multimodal systems but also serves as a critical tool for understanding their current state and charting a course for the development of truly holistic and general-purpose full-modality intelligence.
