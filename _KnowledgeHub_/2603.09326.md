# OddGridBench: Exposing the Lack of Fine-Grained Visual Discrepancy Sensitivity in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> OddGridBench, a new controllable benchmark, evaluates Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) sensitivity to fine-grained visual discrepancies, revealing they perform well below human levels. The OddGrid-GRPO reinforcement learning framework is introduced, enhancing MLLMs' perceptual accuracy by integrating curriculum learning and a distance-aware reward function, achieving 82.64% total accuracy on the benchmark, a substantial improvement over baselines.

## Problem

- MLLMs show strong high-level visual understanding but their sensitivity to fine-grained visual discrepancies, a low-level perceptual capability essential for human vision, is underexplored.
- Existing MLLM benchmarks primarily focus on high-level semantic reasoning or symbolic interpretation, lacking controlled evaluation frameworks for precise measurement of subtle perceptual discrimination.
- Weaknesses in fine-grained perception could undermine the reliability of MLLMs' higher-level visual grounding and reasoning capabilities, particularly where subtle visual cues are critical.

## Method

- **OddGridBench**, a controllable and scalable benchmark, was developed to systematically evaluate MLLMs' fine-grained visual discrepancy sensitivity using an Odd-One-Out paradigm with parametrically generated grid-based images and varying visual attributes (color, size, rotation, position).
- OddGrid-GRPO, a reinforcement learning framework built on Qwen3-VL-2B, was designed to enhance MLLMs' perceptual sensitivity through curriculum-guided optimization and a distance-aware, smoothly decaying reward function that provides continuous feedback.
- 19 MLLMs (open-source and proprietary) were evaluated on OddGridBench, and OddGrid-GRPO's effectiveness was benchmarked against supervised baselines and standard RL methods, along with generalization tests on cross-dataset and cross-format scenarios.

## Results

- All 19 MLLMs evaluated scored well below human accuracy on OddGridBench, with many models performing near random chance, highlighting a pervasive lack of fine-grained perceptual sensitivity.
- The OddGrid-GRPO framework achieved **82.64%** total accuracy on OddGridBench, outperforming the supervised baseline (**17.14%**), standard GRPO (**70.86%**), and GSPO (**75.93%**).
- OddGrid-GRPO showed improved cross-dataset generalization on real-image anomaly detection benchmarks (MVTec-AD, VisA) and character datasets, indicating better transferability beyond the synthetic training environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs demonstrate significantly limited fine-grained visual discrepancy sensitivity, performing far below human levels (e.g., highest MLLM at 68.07% vs. human 87.47% accuracy).
> - MLLMs exhibit particular weaknesses in detecting geometric discrepancies (rotation and position) and their accuracy relies more on coarse visual differences than fine-grained perception.
> - The OddGrid-GRPO framework, combining curriculum learning and a distance-aware reward, effectively enhances MLLMs' perceptual grounding, improving specific sensitivities like rotation by 13.0% and position by 23.0% over standard GRPO.
