# OddGridBench: Exposing the Lack of Fine-Grained Visual Discrepancy Sensitivity in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> OddGridBench, a new controllable benchmark, evaluates Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) sensitivity to fine-grained visual discrepancies, revealing they perform well below human levels. The OddGrid-GRPO reinforcement learning framework is introduced, enhancing MLLMs' perceptual accuracy by integrating curriculum learning and a distance-aware reward function, achieving 82.64% total accuracy on the benchmark, a substantial improvement over baselines.

## Problem

- MLLMs show strong high-level visual understanding but their sensitivity to fine-grained visual discrepancies, a low-level perceptual capability essential for human vision, is underexplored.
- Existing MLLM benchmarks primarily focus on high-level semantic reasoning or symbolic interpretation, lacking controlled evaluation frameworks for precise measurement of subtle perceptual discrimination.
- Weaknesses in fine-grained perception could undermine the reliability of MLLMs' higher-level visual grounding and reasoning capabilities, particularly where subtle visual cues are critical.

## Method

- **OddGridBench**, a controllable and scalable benchmark, was developed to systematically evaluate MLLMs' fine-grained visual discrepancy sensitivity using an Odd-One-Out paradigm with parametrically generated grid-based images and varying visual attributes (color, size, rotation, position).
- OddGrid-GRPO, a reinforcement learning framework built on Qwen3-VL-2B, was designed to enhance MLLMs' perceptual sensitivity through curriculum-guided optimization and a distance-aware, smoothly decaying reward function that provides continuous feedback.
- 19 MLLMs (open-source and proprietary) were evaluated on OddGridBench, and OddGrid-GRPO's effectiveness was benchmarked against supervised baselines and standard RL methods, along with generalization tests on cross-dataset and cross-format scenarios.

## Results

- All 19 MLLMs evaluated scored well below human accuracy on OddGridBench, with many models performing near random chance, highlighting a pervasive lack of fine-grained perceptual sensitivity.
- The OddGrid-GRPO framework achieved **82.64%** total accuracy on OddGridBench, outperforming the supervised baseline (**17.14%**), standard GRPO (**70.86%**), and GSPO (**75.93%**).
- OddGrid-GRPO showed improved cross-dataset generalization on real-image anomaly detection benchmarks (MVTec-AD, VisA) and character datasets, indicating better transferability beyond the synthetic training environment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs demonstrate significantly limited fine-grained visual discrepancy sensitivity, performing far below human levels (e.g., highest MLLM at 68.07% vs. human 87.47% accuracy).
> - MLLMs exhibit particular weaknesses in detecting geometric discrepancies (rotation and position) and their accuracy relies more on coarse visual differences than fine-grained perception.
> - The OddGrid-GRPO framework, combining curriculum learning and a distance-aware reward, effectively enhances MLLMs' perceptual grounding, improving specific sensitivities like rotation by 13.0% and position by 23.0% over standard GRPO.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated capabilities across a range of vision-language tasks, encompassing high-level reasoning and multimodal understanding. Existing benchmarks for MLLMs primarily evaluate these models on tasks such as image captioning, referring expression comprehension, visual commonsense reasoning, mathematical problem solving, diagram understanding, and structured visual question answering, often focusing on semantic interpretation and high-level abstract reasoning in natural scenes.

Human vision, however, exhibits sensitivity to subtle visual discrepancies, a phenomenon described by concepts like the "just noticeable difference" and the "pop-out effect." This allows the human visual system to rapidly identify elements that deviate from uniformity in a visual field, perceiving fine-grained variations even in complex scenes.

The current research identifies a gap in existing MLLM evaluations: the underexplored ability of these models to detect fine-grained visual discrepancies, a low-level perceptual capability. While prior work in visual recognition and representation learning has utilized Odd-One-Out or anomaly detection paradigms to assess perceptual distinctiveness, these often rely on small-scale datasets or handcrafted stimuli, limiting generalizability. Furthermore, these frameworks are typically designed for visual encoders or recognition networks with dense pixel-level supervision, rendering them unsuitable for MLLM architectures that may not inherently support explicit region-level perception or localization.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has been employed to enhance visual perception and alignment in multimodal models, aligning them with human preferences. Recent RL-based alignment approaches, such as those building on Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), have extended to multimodal domains, improving image reasoning and perceptual grounding. However, these methods often focus on outcome-level rewards that capture semantic correctness or linguistic coherence, rather than directly addressing fine-grained perceptual alignment with human visual judgments.

This work contributes to the landscape by specifically addressing the systematic evaluation of low-level visual perception in MLLMs, particularly their sensitivity to fine-grained visual discrepancies, and by proposing an RL-based framework tailored to improve this specific perceptual grounding, moving beyond purely semantic alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and utilize OddGridBench, a controllable benchmark, to systematically evaluate the fine-grained visual discrepancy sensitivity of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:
- **Fundamental Human Visual Capability:** Human vision possesses a remarkable sensitivity to subtle visual discrepancies, enabling the rapid identification of non-uniform elements in a visual field. This perceptual ability is a foundational aspect of robust spatial reasoning and object understanding.
- **Underexplored MLLM Weakness:** While MLLMs have advanced significantly in high-level visual understanding and reasoning, their capability in low-level visual perception, specifically in detecting fine-grained discrepancies, has remained largely unexamined.
- **Reliability for Higher-Level Tasks:** The authors posit that a weakness in this foundational layer of perceptual sensitivity could undermine the reliability of MLLMs' higher-level capabilities, such as grounding and visual question answering, where subtle visual cues can be critical.
- **Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** Current MLLM benchmarks primarily focus on high-level semantic reasoning or symbolic interpretation, overlooking the crucial aspect of fine-grained perceptual discrimination. There is a lack of systematic analysis and a controlled evaluation framework to precisely measure this specific ability.
- **Need for Controllable Evaluation:** Existing Odd-One-Out or anomaly detection methods often rely on small-scale datasets or handcrafted stimuli, which limit their generalizability and precise control over perceptual variables. A benchmark is needed that can isolate perceptual differences from high-level semantics, allowing for direct and quantitative evaluation.

A secondary objective is to propose a training methodology, OddGrid-GRPO, designed to address the identified limitations by enhancing MLLMs' perceptual sensitivity through a structured reinforcement learning approach. This aims to develop models that can discriminate subtle visual variations more effectively, independent of semantic supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces OddGridBench for evaluation and OddGrid-GRPO for enhancing MLLM capabilities.

#### 3.1. OddGridBench Benchmark

OddGridBench is a controllable and scalable benchmark designed to evaluate MLLMs' fine-grained visual discrepancy sensitivity using an Odd-One-Out paradigm.

- **Design and Structure:** The benchmark comprises over 1,400 grid-based test images, 400 validation images, and 30,000 training images. Each image presents a grid of visually similar icons where one element, the "odd one out," differs from the others in one or more low-level visual attributes. The use of synthetic icons is deliberate to allow for precise, psychophysics-style control over perceptual variables.
- **Icon Source:**
 - **Test and Validation:** 300 Scalable Vector Graphics (SVG) icons were manually selected from IconFont and categorized into three semantic groups: Artificial (man-made objects), Natural (natural elements), and Symbolic (abstract/textual symbols), with 100 icons per group.
 - **Training:** 10,000 SVG icons were collected from Material Design Icons to provide diverse vector representations.
- **Controlled Image Generation:** A parameterized Python program generates each image, ensuring precise control over layout and visual attributes.
 - **Basic Image Layout Parameters:**
 - **Grid Size:** Randomly sampled between 5 to 9 rows and columns to vary spatial density and complexity.
 - **Block Size:** Each icon is rendered within a square region, with size randomly sampled between 60 and 80 pixels.
 - **Visual Discrepancy Parameters:** The perceptual variation of the odd item is controlled along four primary attributes:
 - **Color Discrepancy (∆E):** Color contrast is defined in the CIE–Lab color space, with ∆E sampled from a range of to control perceptual distance.
 - **Size Discrepancy (∆s):** The odd item is resized by 85–95% or 105–115% of its original size.
 - **Rotation Discrepancy (∆θ):** The odd item is rotated clockwise or counterclockwise within a range of −25° to −5° or 5° to 25°.
 - **Position Discrepancy ([∆x, ∆y]):** The odd item is shifted by 5–12% of the block size in horizontal and vertical directions.
 - **Discrepancy Types:** OddGridBench includes both single-attribute discrepancies (Color, Size, Rotation, Position) and multi-attribute combinations (2-Type, 3-Type, 4-Type), where multiple attributes vary simultaneously.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** The evaluation prompt instructs the model to identify the odd element and return its grid position (Row X, Column Y) starting from the top-left corner as (Row 1, Column 1).
- **Human Evaluation:** 350 samples (50 from each category) were independently annotated by three trained human evaluators, with average correctness used for human accuracy.
- **Metrics:** Accuracy (strict, Tolerance Accuracy within 1 cell, Labeled Grid Accuracy with visible indices), and analysis of sensitivity to discrepancy magnitude, grid size, and image resolution.

#### 3.2. OddGrid-GRPO Training Framework

OddGrid-GRPO is a reinforcement learning framework designed to enhance MLLMs' perceptual sensitivity to fine-grained visual discrepancies. It is built upon the Qwen3-VL-2B model using the EasyR1 RL framework.

- **Curriculum-Guided Optimization:**
 - **Difficulty Scoring:** Each sample is assigned a continuous difficulty score based on grid size (larger means harder), odd item attribute count (fewer means harder), and perturbation magnitude (smaller means harder).
 - **Data Partitioning:** The 30,000 training samples are sorted by difficulty and divided into Easy (15K), Medium (10K), and Hard (5K) subsets.
 - **Progressive Training Schedule:** Training proceeds in three stages:
 - **Step 1:** Initial training on 15K easy samples to establish coarse spatial alignment.
 - **Step 2:** Training on a mixed set of 5K easy and 10K medium samples for improved generalization.
 - **Step 3:** Introduction of 5K hard samples along with easier ones to strengthen fine-grained perceptual discrimination.
- **Distance-Aware Reward Formulation:** To provide a more continuous and informative learning signal than the binary reward in standard GRPO, a distance-aware reward is designed.
 - **Euclidean Distance:** For each prediction, the Euclidean distance `d` between the predicted `(r_p, c_p)` and ground-truth `(r_g, c_g)` grid positions is computed.
 - **Decaying Reward:** If the prediction is not an exact match, the reward `r_d` decays smoothly with increasing spatial distance: `r_d = max(exp(-d^2 / (2σ^2)) - β, 0)`, where `σ` is a scale-adaptive parameter (proportional to grid diagonal) and `β` is a bias term.
 - **Overall Reward:** The overall optimization reward `r_overall` combines the distance-aware reward `r_d` with a format reward `r_f` (to ensure structural consistency): `r_overall = (1 - ω)r_d + ωr_f`, with ω set to 0.2.
- **Training Settings:** Qwen3-VL-2B is adopted as the base model, trained with AdamW optimizer, a learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁶, and other standard RL parameters.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Settings

19 MLLMs, including open-source (e.g., Qwen3-VL, InternVL3.5, LLaVA-OneVision) and proprietary systems (e.g., Gemini-2.5-Pro, GPT-5), were evaluated. The base model (Qwen3-VL-2B) was also compared against standard GRPO and a Group Sequence Policy Optimization (GSPO) variant, as well as ablated versions of OddGrid-GRPO. Further generalization tests included cross-dataset evaluation (MVTec-AD, VisA, MNIST, Similar Chinese Characters) and cross-format evaluation (non-grid image sequences), and a real-world anomaly detection setting with multiple anomalies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MLLMs on OddGridBench and the performance of the proposed OddGrid-GRPO framework yielded several findings:

- **MLLMs' Limited Fine-Grained Discrepancy Sensitivity:** All 19 evaluated MLLMs performed significantly below human levels in visual discrepancy detection. Human participants achieved a total accuracy of 87.47%, while the highest-performing MLLM, Qwen3-VL-32B, reached 68.07%. Many models, including Phi-3.5-Vision and LLaVA-v1.6-34B, performed near random chance.
- **Superiority of Select Open-Source Models:** Notably, some open-source models, specifically the Qwen3-VL series (e.g., Qwen3-VL-2B, 4B, 32B), outperformed larger models like InternVL3.5-38B and Molmo-72B, and even proprietary systems like Gemini-2.5-Pro (49.29%) and GPT-5 (28.93%). This suggests that fine-grained perceptual discrimination may depend more on data alignment and perceptual coupling than on parameter scale alone.
- **Specific Weaknesses in Geometric Discrepancies:** MLLMs exhibited particular difficulty with rotation and position perturbations, indicating limited sensitivity to subtle geometric variations. Color discrepancies generally yielded better performance, and accuracy across models was lower for multi-attribute discrepancy types.
- **Sensitivity to Perceptual Discrepancy Magnitude:** Model accuracy increased as the magnitude of visual discrepancy became more pronounced. However, the improvement was slower for geometric attributes (rotation, position) compared to color, suggesting that MLLMs primarily rely on coarse visual differences and lack fine-grained perceptual sensitivity.
- **Localization Accuracy Analysis:** Analysis using relaxed metrics (Tolerance Accuracy, Labeled Grid Accuracy) showed significant improvements for MLLMs. For example, Qwen3-VL-4B's accuracy increased from 52.43% (strict) to 74.14% (Tolerance Accuracy). This indicates that many errors are near the correct grid cell, suggesting a lack of precise spatial calibration rather than complete failure to detect the anomaly.
- **Impact of Grid Size and Resolution:** Model performance consistently declined with increasing grid size (i.e., higher spatial density and visual clutter), highlighting limitations in maintaining spatial attention and object separation in crowded scenes. Higher image resolution provided only modest gains in accuracy. An evaluation using bounding box (IoU-based) localization yielded very low accuracy, reinforcing the choice of row-column output for evaluating perceptual reasoning.
- **Effectiveness of OddGrid-GRPO:** The proposed OddGrid-GRPO framework substantially outperformed the supervised baseline (17.14% total accuracy), standard GRPO (70.86%), and GSPO (75.93%), achieving 82.64% total accuracy. The framework demonstrated notable gains across all categories, with significant improvements in Rotation (+13.0%) and Position (+23.0%) compared to standard GRPO. Ablation studies confirmed that both the distance-aware reward formulation and curriculum-guided optimization contributed to these performance enhancements. Training dynamics also indicated faster convergence and higher final rewards for OddGrid-GRPO.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** OddGrid-GRPO demonstrated improved cross-dataset generalization on real-image industrial anomaly detection benchmarks (MVTec-AD, VisA) and character datasets (MNIST, SCC), surpassing the baseline and standard GRPO. It also showed better performance in a cross-format, non-grid evaluation setting. However, evaluation on industrial anomaly detection with multiple anomalies and revised metrics (Exact Match/F1) revealed that robust real-image discrepancy detection remains challenging even for the enhanced models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds several points of significance and potential impact within the field of multimodal artificial intelligence:

- **Identifies a Critical MLLM Limitation:** The study definitively exposes a fundamental bottleneck in current MLLMs: their limited sensitivity to fine-grained visual discrepancies. This capability, crucial for human visual perception, is demonstrated to be underdeveloped in state-of-the-art MLLMs, hindering their foundational perceptual grounding.
- **Establishes a Novel and Controllable Benchmark:** OddGridBench fills a gap in the existing MLLM evaluation landscape by providing a scalable and highly controllable benchmark specifically designed for fine-grained visual discrepancy detection. Its parameterized generation of stimuli allows for precise quantitative analysis across various perceptual dimensions (color, size, rotation, position), offering a standardized tool for future research.
- **Proposes an Effective Method for Perceptual Enhancement:** OddGrid-GRPO offers a principled and effective reinforcement learning framework to address the identified perceptual limitations. By integrating curriculum learning and a distance-aware reward function, it provides a method to progressively enhance MLLMs' ability to discriminate subtle visual variations and improve spatial alignment, moving beyond binary correctness signals.
- **Advances Multimodal Intelligence Foundations:** The work lays groundwork for advancing perceptual grounding in MLLMs. By focusing on low-level visual perception, it encourages researchers to build more robust and human-aligned multimodal intelligence systems, ensuring that higher-level reasoning is supported by accurate foundational visual understanding.
- **Implications for Real-World Applications:** Improved fine-grained visual discrepancy sensitivity has direct implications for various real-world applications requiring precise visual analysis. These include industrial quality control and inspection (detecting subtle defects), medical imaging (identifying minute anomalies), autonomous navigation (perceiving small environmental changes), and general object interaction and manipulation, where accurate perception of subtle visual cues is paramount for reliable operation.
- **Shifts Research Focus:** The findings encourage a shift in research focus towards fundamental perceptual capabilities within MLLMs, complementing current efforts in high-level semantic and reasoning tasks. This may lead to the development of new architectural components, training paradigms, and datasets specifically designed to foster more granular and human-like visual perception in AI models.
