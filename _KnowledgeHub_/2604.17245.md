# MM-Hand: A 21-DOF Multi-modal Modular Dexterous Robotic Hand with Remote Actuation

> [!summary] Summary
> MM-Hand is a 21-DoF multi-modal modular dexterous robotic hand that utilizes remote tendon-driven actuation to reduce end-effector inertia and integrate extensive sensing. It demonstrates a practical fingertip force of 25 N through 1-meter remote sheaths and maintains joint angle control accuracy below 0.1° despite long-distance compliant transmission challenges.

## Problem

- Existing dexterous robotic hands often suffer from high end-effector mass and inertia due to integrated motors, limiting dynamic manipulation and payload capacity.
- Conventional designs occupy valuable internal volume, restricting the number of DoFs and the integration of comprehensive sensing modules.
- Remote tendon-driven actuation, while reducing hand mass, introduces challenges such as increased friction from long tendon-sheath paths and arm-motion-induced tendon length variations, which can affect control accuracy.

## Method

- Developed **MM-Hand**, a ==21-DoF modular dexterous hand== with a ==remote tendon-driven actuation system== that relocates motors to an external hub.
- Integrated a ==multimodal sensing suite==: joint encoders, tactile sensors, and ==in-palm stereo vision==, leveraging the freed internal volume.
- Established an analytical framework to model ==arm-motion-induced coupling disturbances== and ==long-distance friction loss==, informing design and closed-loop control strategies.

## Results

- MM-Hand achieved a practical fingertip force of **~25 N** through a **1-meter remote tendon sheath**, demonstrating viable load capacity despite long-distance transmission.
- The control system demonstrated a **steady-state joint angle error below 0.1°** in step-response tests and successfully tracked sinusoidal inputs.
- Experiments identified the **metal spring tube** as a suitable sheath material due to its low friction, and the integrated in-palm stereo vision system provided dense depth estimation for environmental perception.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Long tendon-sheath friction and the resulting delay have a more substantial impact on control performance than manipulator-induced disturbances on tendon-driven motion.
> - Modular, 3D-printable finger and palm structures with quick tendon connectors significantly enhance manufacturability, maintainability, and simplify internal routing for tendons and sensor wires.
> - Careful selection of sheath materials and an analytical understanding of transmission losses are crucial for achieving practical load capacity and control accuracy in remote tendon-driven systems.
