# PyVision-RL: Forging Open Agentic Vision Models via RL

> [!summary] Summary
> PyVision-RL introduces an open-weight reinforcement learning framework for agentic multimodal models, enabling dynamic Python-based tool use for images and videos. The framework effectively counters interaction collapse and achieves state-of-the-art performance on image benchmarks, while significantly improving visual token efficiency for video tasks (e.g., 7.3% accuracy increase on VSI-Bench with an order of magnitude fewer visual tokens).

## Problem

- Agentic capabilities with dynamic tooling are largely confined to proprietary systems or image-only tasks, limiting the accessibility and advancement of open-weight multimodal large language models (MLLMs).
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning for agentic MLLMs often suffers from "interaction collapse," where models learn to minimize tool usage and multi-turn reasoning.
- Traditional video MLLMs incur high computational costs due to uniform frame sampling, making efficient and scalable video understanding challenging.

## Method

- A unified agentic scaffold integrates ==Python as a primitive tool==, enabling dynamic, on-the-fly operation synthesis and iterative reasoning for both image and video tasks.
- An ==accumulative tool reward== (only for correct answers) and an oversampling–filtering–ranking strategy for rollouts stabilize RL training and sustain multi-turn tool usage, combating interaction collapse.
- For video understanding, an =="on-demand context construction"== mechanism allows the MLLM to selectively fetch and present task-relevant frames via Python, drastically reducing visual token consumption.

## Results

- PyVision-Image achieved state-of-the-art results on visual search (e.g., **+10.2%** on V* avg@32) and multimodal reasoning benchmarks (e.g., **+9.6%** on WeMath) over previous methods.
- PyVision-Video obtained a **7.3%** absolute accuracy improvement on VSI-Bench while reducing visual token usage from approximately **45K** to **5K** tokens per sample.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the accumulative tool reward is crucial for sustaining tool usage throughout training, and the oversampling–filtering–ranking rollout strategy significantly improves performance and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interaction collapse in agentic RL is not an inherent limitation of multi-turn interaction but rather a solvable issue related to insufficient training incentives and unstable rollout selection.
> - Dynamic tool use, through treating a general-purpose language like Python as a primitive, provides superior expressiveness and compositional reasoning for both image and video understanding in open-weight MLLMs.
> - On-demand visual processing, where an agent intelligently samples and presents only relevant video frames, is crucial for achieving high performance and efficiency in video reasoning, reducing token consumption by an order of magnitude.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in "PyVision-RL" addresses critical challenges and builds upon recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly in the domain of agentic AI.

The broader landscape of AI has seen LLMs evolve from mere chatbots into sophisticated "agents" capable of complex, multi-turn interactions and effective tool utilization. This agentic paradigm has proven transformative for textual tasks requiring interaction with external environments, such as deep research or computer control. A significant frontier in this evolution is extending agentic capabilities to multimodal domains, especially visual understanding. Proprietary systems like OpenAI's o3 have already demonstrated the power of tool-integrated visual reasoning, where models actively manipulate visual inputs (e.g., cropping, zooming) rather than passively consuming them. PyVision-RL aims to bring similar advanced capabilities to *open-weight* models, fostering wider accessibility and research.

Existing approaches to multimodal tool use can be categorized into two paradigms:
1. **Static Toolsets:** This involves manually predefining a fixed set of task-specific tools (e.g., cropping, video clipping). While effective for specific tasks, these methods inherently lack flexibility and require extensive, task-dependent engineering.
2. **Dynamic Tooling:** This paradigm treats general-purpose programming languages like Python as a primitive tool, allowing the model to synthesize novel, task-specific operations on the fly. This offers superior expressiveness and compositional reasoning. However, dynamic tooling has historically been largely confined to image understanding tasks and often relies on proprietary APIs, leaving open-weight multimodal reinforcement learning, particularly for video, largely unexplored.

PyVision-RL directly tackles these limitations. It bridges the gap by:
- **Enabling dynamic tooling for both image and video understanding:** This is a crucial expansion, especially for video, where dynamic interaction is more complex.
- **Focusing on open-weight models:** This democratizes access to advanced agentic capabilities, contrasting with proprietary systems.
- **Addressing interaction collapse:** A prevalent issue in training agentic multimodal models via RL, where models tend to reduce tool usage over training. This "collapse" has led to skepticism about the scalability of interaction-based visual understanding. The authors contend this isn't an inherent flaw of interaction but rather a result of insufficient training incentives and unstable rollout selection in RL.

The paper also situates itself within the broader context of applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) to MLLMs, following successes like DeepSeek-R1. While many existing RL methods for LLMs focus on aspects like improved advantage estimation, modified PPO-style clipping, or addressing training-inference mismatch, PyVision-RL introduces specific innovations tailored to the multimodal agentic setting, particularly regarding rollout quality and sustained multi-turn tool usage.

In essence, PyVision-RL pushes the boundaries of open-weight multimodal agents by developing a robust RL framework that enables flexible, dynamic tool use for both images and videos, while explicitly countering the "interaction collapse" challenge and significantly improving efficiency for video processing.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind PyVision-RL are multifaceted, aiming to advance the state of agentic multimodal models, especially within the open-weight paradigm.

1. **Develop a Robust Reinforcement Learning Framework for Open-Weight Agentic MLLMs:** The primary objective is to create `PyVision-RL`, a unified framework that can endow open-weight multimodal models with dynamic tool-using capabilities. This addresses the gap where powerful dynamic tooling has largely been confined to proprietary systems or image-only tasks.
2. **Combat Interaction Collapse in Agentic RL:** A major motivation stems from the observed phenomenon of "interaction collapse" during RL fine-tuning, where models learn to minimize tool usage and multi-turn reasoning. The authors argue this is not an inherent limitation of agentic interaction but rather a consequence of inadequate training incentives and unstable rollout generation. A key objective is to stabilize RL training and sustain multi-turn interaction.
3. **Enable Dynamic Tooling for Video Understanding:** While dynamic tooling has shown promise for images, its application to video reasoning, particularly for open-weight models, remains underexplored. A specific objective is to extend the flexible, Python-as-primitive-tool paradigm to video understanding tasks, allowing models to synthesize operations dynamically.
4. **Enhance Visual Token Efficiency for Video Reasoning:** Traditional video MLLMs often suffer from high computational costs due to uniform frame sampling, where all frames are passed to the model. PyVision-RL aims to dramatically improve efficiency for video tasks by introducing an "on-demand context construction" mechanism, selectively fetching and presenting only task-relevant frames.
5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance Across Diverse Multimodal Tasks:** Beyond theoretical advancements and efficiency gains, a practical objective is to demonstrate the empirical effectiveness of the PyVision-RL framework. This means developing models (PyVision-Image and PyVision-Video) that achieve strong, competitive, or even state-of-the-art results on a variety of visual search, multimodal reasoning, and agentic reasoning benchmarks for both image and video.
6. **Contribute to the Open-Source AI Community:** By releasing code, data, and models, the authors aim to foster further research and development in agentic multimodal AI, making these advanced capabilities accessible to a broader scientific and engineering community.

In summary, PyVision-RL is motivated by the desire to unlock the full potential of agentic behavior in open-weight multimodal models, specifically by stabilizing RL training, extending dynamic tooling to video, and improving efficiency, all while delivering top-tier performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PyVision-RL is a comprehensive reinforcement learning framework designed to train open-weight multimodal models for dynamic tool use in both image and video understanding. Its methodology revolves around a unified agentic scaffold, novel RL training strategies, and specific optimizations for data handling.

#### 3.1. Agentic Scaffold: Python as a Primitive Tool

The core of PyVision-RL's agentic design is the integration of Python as a primitive tool, enabling dynamic, on-the-fly operation synthesis.

- **Interaction Protocol:** The MLLM operates in an iterative loop, interleaving natural language reasoning with executable Python code blocks (`<code>...</code>`). The external environment executes these code blocks, and the results (`<interpreter>...</interpreter>`), including textual outputs and rendered images, are appended back to the MLLM's context. This interactive process continues until a final answer (`<answer>...</answer>`) is produced.
- **Multimodal Hint Injection:**
 - **For Image Tasks (PyVision-Image):** Images are injected into both the MLLM's context (for direct visual processing) and the Python execution environment (for programmatic manipulation).
 - **For Video Tasks (PyVision-Video):** This introduces a critical innovation: "on-demand context construction." Instead of uniformly sampling and feeding frames directly into the MLLM context, the *full video is loaded only into the Python runtime*. The agent is instructed via the system prompt to *selectively sample and plot task-relevant frames* using Python code during its reasoning process. This "agentic frame fetching" dramatically reduces visual token consumption by only visualizing necessary information.

#### 3.2. Accumulative Tool Reward

To counter the "interaction collapse" observed in prior agentic RL work (where models reduce tool usage), PyVision-RL introduces an explicit incentive for sustained multi-turn tool usage.
- The final reward `R` for a rollout combines answer accuracy (`R_acc ∈ {0, 1}`) with an accumulative tool reward: `R = R_acc + 0.1 * n_tc * 1_{R_acc=1}`.
- `n_tc` is the total number of tool calls during the rollout. The `0.1` coefficient is a hyperparameter.
- Crucially, this tool reward is *only added if the final answer is correct* (`1_{R_acc=1}`), ensuring that tool use is encouraged for productive, correct reasoning, not just arbitrary actions.

#### 3.3. Oversampling–Filtering–Ranking Rollouts

To improve training stability and efficiency, PyVision-RL implements a sophisticated rollout generation strategy:
- **Oversampling:** More rollouts are generated than immediately required for a training batch (e.g., `αB` prompts, each yielding `G` rollouts, where `α > 1`).
- **Filtering:** This step removes problematic rollouts:
 - **Broken Trajectories:** Rollouts that encounter timeouts, runtime failures, or generate invalid Python code are discarded to prevent training crashes.
 - **Zero Reward Variance:** Groups of rollouts where all members receive the same reward (e.g., all correct and same tool calls, or all incorrect) provide limited learning signal. These groups are filtered out.
- **Ranking (Standard Deviation Sorting):** Among the remaining valid rollouts, groups are ranked by their group-level reward standard deviation. This serves as a proxy for "sample difficulty," prioritizing moderately difficult examples that offer the most informative learning signals. Retaining top-ranked groups also mitigates the issue of correct but concise solutions receiving negative advantages due to group normalization, which could otherwise suppress desirable behaviors.

#### 3.4. Optimization and Data Collection

- **Modified GRPO Algorithm:** PyVision-RL adopts GRPO (Group Reinforcement Policy Optimization) as its base algorithm but modifies the advantage computation. It removes the standard deviation normalization term from the intra-group advantage calculation: `A_i,t = R(x, y_i) - mean({R(x, y_i)})`. This modification is empirically found to improve training stability and performance, as it reduces excessive variance in learning dynamics.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Data and Training:**
 - **PyVision-Image-SFT:** Utilizes synthetic data generated by GPT-4.1, spanning diverse domains like multimodal math (MMK12), medical reasoning (GMAI-Reasoning), chart/infographic understanding (ChartQA, InfoVQA), and general VQA (MMPR). Data is filtered for correctness and a minimum of two tool-use turns to emphasize sustained interaction.
 - **PyVision-Video-SFT:** A curated dataset of 44K samples for spatial reasoning (SpaceR) and long-video reasoning (LongVILA), specifically to teach the novel on-demand context construction for videos.
 - Both SFT models are built on the Qwen2.5-VL-7B base model and trained using LLaMA-Factory.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Data and Training:**
 - **PyVision-Image-RL:** Fine-tuned on a mix of visual search (DeepEyes, Mini-o3) and multimodal reasoning data (V-Thinker, WeMath).
 - **PyVision-Video-RL:** Focused on spatial reasoning tasks from SpaceR.
 - Models are trained for 700 RL steps on 8 H100 GPUs with specific hyperparameters (oversampling batch size 32, training batch size 16, group size 8, learning rate 1 × 10⁻⁶). Image resizing is controlled for high-resolution images.

This multi-pronged approach, combining innovative architectural choices with meticulous RL training strategies, allows PyVision-RL to robustly train open-weight multimodal agents with dynamic tool-using capabilities, while addressing key stability and efficiency concerns.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on PyVision-RL demonstrate significant advancements in agentic multimodal reasoning, both in terms of performance and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Strong Performance on Image Benchmarks (PyVision-Image)

PyVision-Image consistently achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) results across a range of visual search, multimodal reasoning, and agentic reasoning benchmarks, establishing the effectiveness of its dynamic tooling and RL framework.

- **Visual Search:** PyVision-Image outperforms all prior methods, including those with static toolsets and other dynamic tooling approaches based on Qwen2.5-VL-7B. It achieves absolute improvements of **+10.2% on V\*** (avg@32), **+6.5% on HRBench-4K**, and **+6.4% on HRBench-8K** compared to the base Qwen2.5-VL-7B model. These gains highlight its enhanced capabilities in fine-grained visual localization and agentic perception.
- **Multimodal Reasoning:** The model sets new SOTA records on challenging multimodal math benchmarks: **+4.4% on DynaMath**, **+3.1% on MathVerse**, and a substantial **+9.6% on WeMath** over the previous best model, DeepEyes-v2. This demonstrates that the benefits of agentic RL extend effectively to complex tasks requiring abstract mathematical reasoning grounded in visual inputs.
- **Agentic Reasoning:** On TIR-Bench, which specifically assesses multi-turn tool usage, PyVision-Image shows a **+7.3% improvement** over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline, validating the efficacy of dynamic tool invocation for long-horizon reasoning.

These results collectively affirm the flexibility and broad applicability of PyVision-RL's dynamic tooling approach across diverse image-based multimodal tasks.

#### 4.2. Token Efficiency and Performance on Video Benchmarks (PyVision-Video)

PyVision-Video showcases the distinct advantages of its "on-demand context construction" mechanism, delivering higher accuracy with significantly improved visual token efficiency.

- **Efficiency:** Compared to conventional MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-7B) that use uniform frame sampling, PyVision-Video uses approximately **5K visual tokens per sample** on VSI-Bench, achieving an accuracy of 44.0%. In stark contrast, Qwen2.5-VL-7B achieves its best performance (38.0%) at a much higher cost of approximately **45K visual tokens per sample** (at 1.0 FPS sampling). This represents an order-of-magnitude reduction in token usage.
- **Performance:** PyVision-Video achieves a **7.3% absolute improvement** in accuracy over the Qwen2.5-VL-7B baseline on VSI-Bench. It also outperforms other video-understanding models like Video-R1 and VITAL (an agentic model with predefined video clipping tools). This demonstrates that selective, agentic frame fetching not only saves tokens but also enhances reasoning performance by focusing on relevant visual information.
- **Favorable Trade-off:** The results clearly show that PyVision-Video achieves the most favorable balance between visual token efficiency and reasoning performance on VSI-Bench, underscoring the critical role of on-demand visual processing for scalable multimodal agents.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Training Dynamics

Detailed ablation studies reveal the individual contributions of PyVision-RL's key components:

- **Max Turn Budget:** A larger maximum turn budget (e.g., 4 turns vs. 2 turns) leads to significantly higher performance gains in the later stages of RL training, suggesting it expands the model's performance ceiling for complex, multi-step tasks.
- **Accumulative Tool Reward:** This reward is crucial for *sustaining* tool usage throughout training. Without it, the average number of tool calls rapidly decreases (interaction collapse). With it, tool usage is encouraged, leading to stronger long-horizon reasoning and improved final performance, especially after sufficient training steps.
- **Standard Deviation Sorting:** Applying this strategy for rollout selection consistently improves performance, particularly in early training stages. It effectively prioritizes "moderately difficult" samples, providing better learning signals, and reduces the incidence of correct samples receiving misleading negative advantages from group-level normalization.
- **Removing Standard Deviation Normalization in GRPO:** The ablation confirms that removing this term from advantage estimation leads to more stable training and improved performance by reducing variance in the learning dynamics.

Analysis of RL training dynamics further supports these findings, showing stable optimization with smooth decreases in entropy loss and gradient norm. Concurrently, validation performance, accuracy reward, response length, and the mean number of tool calls consistently increase, demonstrating that the model successfully learns and generalizes sustained, multi-turn tool-using behaviors.

In conclusion, the findings strongly validate PyVision-RL as an effective framework for developing performant and efficient open-weight agentic multimodal models, addressing crucial challenges in RL stability and token efficiency, particularly for video understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PyVision-RL represents a significant advancement in the field of agentic multimodal AI, offering solutions to critical challenges and paving the way for more capable and accessible intelligent agents. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Overcoming Interaction Collapse:** The introduction of the accumulative tool reward and the oversampling–filtering–ranking rollout strategy directly addresses and effectively mitigates the pervasive problem of "interaction collapse" in agentic reinforcement learning. This is a crucial breakthrough because it validates that sustained, multi-turn interaction and tool use are indeed powerful mechanisms for multimodal reasoning, provided the training incentives and rollout selection are properly designed. This finding can re-energize research into truly interactive multimodal agents.

2. **Democratizing Dynamic Tooling for Multimodal AI:** By providing an open-weight framework for dynamic tooling (using Python as a primitive tool) across *both* images and videos, PyVision-RL democratizes access to advanced agentic capabilities that were previously largely restricted to proprietary systems or image-only applications. This open-source contribution (code, data, and models released) will accelerate research and development in the broader AI community, fostering innovation and enabling more researchers to build upon these foundations.

3. **Pioneering Efficient Video Reasoning with On-Demand Context Construction:** The "on-demand context construction" mechanism for PyVision-Video is a novel and impactful innovation. It drastically improves the visual token efficiency of video understanding models (reducing token usage by an order of magnitude) without sacrificing accuracy. This is critical for scaling multimodal agents to long videos and real-time applications, where processing every frame is computationally prohibitive. This approach can set a new standard for how MLLMs handle temporal data, making video analysis more practical and widespread.

4. **Advancing State-of-the-Art in Multimodal Reasoning:** The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance of PyVision-Image and PyVision-Video across diverse benchmarks (visual search, multimodal math, agentic reasoning, spatial reasoning) is a clear testament to the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed framework. These empirical gains provide strong evidence that the methodological innovations translate into superior real-world reasoning capabilities.

5. **Refining Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** The specific modifications to the GRPO algorithm, such as removing standard deviation normalization and the judicious use of standard deviation sorting for rollout selection, offer valuable insights into stabilizing and optimizing RL for large language models. These findings can influence the design of future RL algorithms tailored for complex, interactive AI systems.

6. **Enabling More Sophisticated AI Agents:** The ability of PyVision-RL models to reason, act, and interact over visual inputs in a dynamic, multi-turn fashion moves closer to human-like intelligence. This can lead to the development of AI agents capable of more complex problem-solving, scientific discovery, robotics control, and human-computer interaction scenarios.

However, the authors responsibly include an impact statement, acknowledging a potential negative impact: since these models use Python as a primitive tool, they may access the host file system and potentially cause damage. This highlights the crucial need for **careful consideration and implementation of robust security measures** when deploying such powerful agentic models in real-world environments. This acknowledgment itself contributes to responsible AI development by forewarning users of inherent risks with agentic systems.

In summary, PyVision-RL significantly advances the frontier of open-weight agentic multimodal AI, providing a robust, efficient, and high-performing framework that addresses key technical challenges and promises to accelerate the development of more intelligent and interactive AI systems.
