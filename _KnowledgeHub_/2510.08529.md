# CoMAS: Co-Evolving Multi-Agent Systems via Interaction Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> The CoMAS framework enables large language model agents to self-evolve by learning solely from intrinsic inter-agent interaction rewards, eliminating the need for external supervision. This multi-agent system consistently improved performance across various benchmarks, achieving significant gains such as a 19.80% increase on GSM8K and 10.98% on HumanEval in multi-agent AutoGen settings.

## Problem

- Existing LLM-based agents are limited by static pre-trained capabilities, often struggling with genuine continual improvement.
- Reinforcement Learning methods for agents typically rely on costly external rewards or individual-centric intrinsic rewards, failing to leverage collective intelligence.
- Current paradigms do not mimic how human intelligence evolves through mutual discussion and collaboration without constant external evaluation.

## Method

- A multi-agent framework, **CoMAS**, is introduced, enabling hierarchical and decentralized interactions among heterogeneous LLM agents to generate solutions, evaluations, and scores.
- An LLM-as-a-judge mechanism formulates intrinsic, complementary (zero-sum) rewards for solutions and evaluations, encouraging both correctness and critical feedback while preventing reward hacking.
- Policy optimization for individual agents is performed using REINFORCE++ with token-level credit assignment and KL-divergence regularization to ensure stable and effective learning.

## Results

- CoMAS consistently improved performance across math, coding, science, and general knowledge benchmarks, outperforming untrained agents and baselines like MAPoRL and TTRL.
- Achieved substantial gains in multi-agent collaborative setups, including +**19.80%** on GSM8K and +**10.98%** on HumanEval in the AutoGen setup, where other baselines faltered.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the adversarial reward formulation is essential, as alternative schemes (e.g., 'w/o Evaluation', 'w/o Scoring') led to performance degradation or reward hacking.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM agents can achieve self-evolution purely through intrinsic rewards derived from inter-agent interactions, mimicking human collective learning processes.
> - A carefully designed adversarial, complementary reward system is crucial for preventing reward hacking and training collapse, leading to stable and effective multi-agent learning.
> - The framework demonstrates positive scalability with an increased number of agents and benefits from agent diversity, allowing heterogeneous models to co-evolve and enhance overall performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Model (LLM)-based agents, specifically addressing the critical challenge of **self-evolution**. Self-evolution refers to an agent's ability to continually improve its capabilities through interaction, rather than remaining static after its initial pretraining.

The authors delineate the existing research into several categories:
- **RL-free strategies**: Early approaches focused on enhancing agents without reinforcement learning, such as expanding knowledge bases, ensembling agents, optimizing task workflows, or incorporating symbolic learning. However, these methods are constrained by the fixed capabilities of the underlying foundation models.
- **RL-based methods**: More recent work has leveraged Reinforcement Learning to enable agent fine-tuning and evolution. These are further categorized by their reward signal source:
 - **External Rewards**: Most existing RL-based methods rely on dense external signals from rule-based verifiers or specialized reward models. While effective, these require external supervision or carefully designed reward functions, which can be expensive or difficult to generalize.
 - **Intrinsic Rewards (Individual-level)**: A newer direction involves deriving intrinsic rewards from LLMs themselves, such as self-certainty, confidence, semantic entropy, or pseudo-labels from majority voting. These methods aim for self-supervision but typically operate at the level of individual models.

CoMAS identifies a crucial gap in these existing paradigms: they diverge from the observed mechanisms of **human intelligence**, which often develops collectively through mutual discussion and collaboration without a constant external oracle. The paper explicitly states that current RL-based methods are "largely centered on self-rewarding at the level of individual models," contrasting this with human intelligence's emergence from "diversity and interplay of individuals."

Therefore, CoMAS proposes a novel paradigm that bridges this gap by enabling LLM-based agents to achieve **self-evolution purely through inter-agent interactions**, without external reward signals. This places CoMAS at the intersection of three highly active research areas:
1. **Reinforcement Learning for LLMs**: Building upon the success of RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) and its variants, CoMAS explores how RL can be effectively applied in a multi-agent context for continuous improvement.
2. **Self-Evolving Agents**: Contributing to the broader goal of creating truly autonomous and adaptive AI agents that can learn and improve over time.
3. **Multi-Agent Systems (MAS)**: Extending the utility of MAS beyond static, pre-defined roles and protocols to dynamic, self-improving architectures where agents collectively enhance their capabilities.

By focusing on intrinsic rewards derived *from interactions* in a multi-agent setting, CoMAS offers a fresh perspective that aligns more closely with natural learning processes observed in human societies, pushing the boundaries of autonomous agent development and collective intelligence in AI.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to explore a fundamental question inspired by human intelligence: **Can LLM-based agents, akin to human beings, achieve self-evolution by learning purely from inter-agent interaction within a multi-agent system, without relying on external reward signals?**

This question is motivated by several observations and limitations of existing approaches:
- **Limitations of Fixed LLM Capabilities**: Traditional RL-free methods for LLM-based agents are inherently constrained by the static capabilities of their underlying foundation models. While agent fine-tuning offers some improvement, it often falls short of "genuinely continual, bootstrapping forms of evolutionary improvement."
- **Dependency on External Rewards in RL**: Current RL-based methods predominantly rely on external rewards (rule-based verifiers or specialized reward models), which are either costly to obtain, difficult to design for open-ended tasks, or lack generalizability.
- **Individual-Centric Intrinsic Rewards**: While intrinsic reward signals derived from LLMs themselves (e.g., self-certainty) eliminate external supervision, they still largely focus on individual model performance, rather than harnessing the power of collective learning.
- **Human Intelligence as a Collective Phenomenon**: The authors highlight that human intelligence evolves through "mutual discussion and collaboration" within teams, where individuals learn and improve from each other without an "external oracle evaluating every contribution." This collective learning mechanism is largely uncaptured by existing LLM agent paradigms.

Therefore, the key motivation behind CoMAS is to design a framework that can foster a similar self-evolutionary process in LLM agents. The goal is to move beyond individual learning to a decentralized, interactive, and intrinsically motivated multi-agent system where agents collectively refine their skills and knowledge through peer evaluation and discussion, leading to sustained improvement without external oversight.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CoMAS (Co-Evolving Multi-Agent Systems) is a novel multi-agent framework designed to enable LLM-based agents to learn and adapt by interacting within a shared environment, mimicking collaborative and critical human discussions. It consists of three core components: **Interaction, Reward Formulation, and Policy Optimization.**

#### 3.1. Interaction

Inspired by discussion platforms (e.g., Reddit, Stack Overflow), CoMAS facilitates hierarchical and decentralized interactions among a set of *l* agents, $U = \{u_1, u_2, \ldots, u_l\}$. Each agent $u_k$ has its own policy $\pi_{\theta_k}$, allowing for heterogeneity (agents can be based on different foundation models). The interaction process unfolds over *m* rounds, with the acting agent selected uniformly at random to ensure balanced experience generation.

Three primary interaction patterns are defined:
1. **Solution**: Given a question $q$ and its discussion history $h_q$, an agent generates a step-by-step solution $s_i$.
2. **Evaluation**: Given $q$, $h_q$, and a solution $s_i$, an agent provides a critical evaluation $e_{i,j}$ for $s_i$. Agents are explicitly prompted to identify flaws, mitigating catering bias common in LLMs.
3. **Scoring**: Given $q$, $s_i$, and an evaluation $e_{i,j}$, an agent scores the solution based on the evaluation, in a specific format for reward extraction. Scoring is an independent pattern that doesn't contribute to discussion history.

The discussion history $h_q$ is compressed to the last $\kappa$ rounds to manage context length. Each round generates a solution, *n* evaluations, and *n* scoring results.

#### 3.2. Reward Formulation

CoMAS employs an **LLM-as-a-judge** mechanism to derive intrinsic reward signals directly from the discussion history, specifically the scoring results.
- For each solution-evaluation pair $(s_i, e_{i,j})$, the scoring result $\tau_{i,j}$ is parsed to extract a score value $\hat{\tau}_{i,j}$.
- The score is an integer between 1 and 3 with defined semantics:
 - **3**: Solution is correct; evaluation is unhelpful/incorrect.
 - **2**: Solution is mostly correct, minor flaws pointed out by evaluation.
 - **1**: Solution is incorrect; fatal mistakes identified by evaluation.
- These scores are normalized to $[0, 1]$ to compute **complementary rewards** for the solution and the evaluation:
 - $r(s_i) = \frac{\hat{\tau}_{i,j} - 1}{2}$
 - $r(e_{i,j}) = 1 - r(s_i) = \frac{3 - \hat{\tau}_{i,j}}{2}$
 This design creates a **zero-sum game** between the solver and evaluator, intrinsically encouraging both correctness in solutions and critical thinking in evaluations.
- A **penalty** of -1 is applied to the scoring agent if its output format is invalid, while a successful (correctly formatted) score yields a zero reward, promoting format adherence and neutrality.

#### 3.3. Policy Optimization

The framework utilizes **REINFORCE++** (Hu et al., 2025a), an effective RL algorithm compatible with diverse interaction patterns. All agents are trained using the same procedure, but policies are updated individually (decentralized).
- Interaction experiences (context $p$, generated output $o$, assigned reward $r(o)$) for each agent are collected into a replay buffer $D_k$.
- The objective involves **token-level credit assignment**. The advantage $A_t$ for each token $o_t$ combines the trajectory-level reward $r(o)$ with a KL-divergence penalty against a fixed reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ (the initial pre-trained model). This regularization constrains the update step.
- Advantages are normalized across batches to stabilize updates.
- The policy $\pi_{\theta_k}$ is updated using a **clipped surrogate objective**, which encourages actions leading to higher advantages while ensuring stable learning within a trusted region, leveraging an importance sampling ratio $\rho_t(\theta_k)$.

This comprehensive approach combines multi-agent interaction, intrinsic reward generation via LLM-as-a-judge with a clever adversarial design, and a robust RL algorithm to enable decentralized and scalable self-evolution of LLM-based agents.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of CoMAS across various benchmarks and setups yielded compelling results, demonstrating its effectiveness and scalability.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance (Table 1)

CoMAS consistently improved performance over untrained agents and often achieved state-of-the-art or highly competitive results compared to baselines like MAPoRL (multi-agent RL with external rewards) and TTRL (test-time RL using majority voting pseudo-labels).

- **Single-Agent Setups (Vanilla, Consistency)**:
 - CoMAS showed consistent gains across all benchmarks (math, coding, science, general knowledge).
 - In **Vanilla** setup, CoMAS achieved the best results on GSM8K (+1.40%), HumanEval (+1.83%), SciBench (+2.00%), and MMLU (+1.00%), demonstrating broad applicability.
 - In **Consistency** setup, CoMAS led on HumanEval (+3.66%), MBPP (+3.40%), and MMLU (+2.40%).
 - Notably, TTRL showed strong performance on math tasks but "fails on HumanEval and GPQA," highlighting its specialized effectiveness and limited generalizability compared to CoMAS.

- **Multi-Agent Setups (AutoGen, Debate)**: This is where CoMAS demonstrated significant advantages in both stability and absolute gains.
 - In **AutoGen** setup, baselines TTRL "collapses markedly" and MAPoRL showed "mixed or negative changes," while CoMAS delivered substantial improvements across *every* benchmark. Key gains include GSM8K (+19.80%), MATH-500 (+7.40%), HumanEval (+10.98%), MBPP (+8.20%), and MMLU (+13.20%). This underscores CoMAS's robustness in complex multi-agent collaborative environments.
 - In **Debate** setup, all methods benefited, but CoMAS achieved the "best or near-best results overall," particularly leading on MATH-500 (+0.40%), HumanEval (+6.10%), and MMLU (+2.40%).

These results establish CoMAS as a robust and generalizable framework that yields consistent improvements, especially excelling in multi-agent collaboration settings where alternative methods prove fragile.

#### 4.2. Training Dynamics (Figure 3)

Analysis of training dynamics provided insights into CoMAS's stability and learning process:
- **Average Response Length**: Showed consistent growth throughout training across all agents. This suggests agents learned to generate more detailed and comprehensive solutions and evaluations, aligning with improved reasoning capabilities.
- **Average Normalized Reward**: Consistently hovered around 0.5 and eventually converged to similar values across agents. This convergence confirms the effectiveness and stability of the adversarial interaction reward design, preventing agents from exploiting the reward mechanism.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Reward Formulation (Figure 4)**: This study validated the critical importance of CoMAS's adversarial reward design.
 - Variants **"w/o Evaluation"** (rewards directly from agents acting as judges) and **"w/o Scoring"** (evaluation steps provide direct judgments) both *underperformed the untrained base model*.
 - "w/o Evaluation" saw rewards decrease, indicating agents became "increasingly strict judges," failing to generate useful signals.
 - "w/o Scoring" saw rewards steadily increase to 1.0, revealing "reward hacking" where agents unanimously supported all solutions to maximize their own rewards.
 - This demonstrated that the carefully balanced, zero-sum adversarial reward mechanism is essential for preventing training collapse and reward hacking, leading to stable and effective learning.

- **Framework Scalability (Figure 5)**: CoMAS demonstrated promising scalability with respect to both the number and diversity of agents.
 - **Agent Number**: Performance generally improved as the number of agents increased (from 1 to 4). This was particularly evident in Consistency and Debate setups, confirming the "critical role of multi-agent interactions."
 - **Agent Diversity**: Experiments with heterogeneous agents (Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct) consistently outperformed homogeneous settings. This suggests that diverse knowledge and capabilities from different base models can enhance overall performance, with CoMAS effectively leveraging these differences.

In summary, CoMAS not only achieves superior performance in various LLM agent tasks and collaboration settings but also provides a stable, scalable, and intrinsically motivated learning paradigm that is critical for the future development of self-evolving AI agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CoMAS introduces a significant advancement in the field of LLM-based agents by proposing a novel paradigm for **self-evolution rooted in inter-agent interaction**. This work holds substantial significance and potential impact:

#### 5.1. Novel Paradigm for Self-Evolution

The core contribution is demonstrating that LLM-based agents can achieve continuous self-improvement solely through internal dynamics of a multi-agent system, without external human feedback or pre-defined reward models. This shifts the focus from individual agent refinement to collective intelligence, mimicking how human teams learn and improve.

#### 5.2. Intrinsic and Robust Reward Generation

The LLM-as-a-judge mechanism, coupled with the complementary (zero-sum) reward design for solutions and evaluations, is a clever innovation. It addresses the common challenges of reward engineering in RL by generating intrinsic rewards that are robust against training collapse and reward hacking, as validated by the ablation studies. This enables agents to provide meaningful feedback to each other and iteratively refine their behaviors.

#### 5.3. Generalizability Across Tasks and Setups

CoMAS demonstrates consistent performance gains across a wide array of benchmarks (math, coding, science, general knowledge) and various inference setups (single-agent Vanilla, Consistency, multi-agent AutoGen, Debate). Its particularly strong performance in multi-agent collaborative settings, where other baselines falter, highlights its robustness and adaptability, especially for complex, open-ended problems where external verification is difficult.

#### 5.4. Decentralized and Scalable Learning

The framework supports decentralized training, allowing heterogeneous agents to co-evolve without a central bottleneck. The ablation studies on agent number and diversity clearly show that performance scales positively with more agents and, notably, benefits from the diversity of underlying models. This opens avenues for building more powerful and versatile multi-agent systems by pooling different strengths.

#### 5.5. Fostering Transferable Skills

By training agents through critical discussion and evaluation, CoMAS inherently encourages the development of reasoning, problem-solving, and critical assessment skills. These skills are likely to be more generalizable and transferable to out-of-domain tasks and novel collaboration settings, making the trained agents more versatile.

#### 5.6. Reduced Dependency on Human Supervision

By enabling intrinsic, self-supervised learning within an agent collective, CoMAS significantly reduces the need for extensive human annotation or domain-specific reward model training. This is a crucial step towards truly autonomous AI systems that can learn and improve in an unsupervised manner, making advanced LLM applications more accessible and scalable.

#### 5.7. Future Research Directions

CoMAS opens promising avenues for future research, including exploring more sophisticated interaction protocols, dynamic agent roles, mechanisms for handling larger agent populations, and the integration of diverse learning strategies within a co-evolutionary framework. The ethical statement acknowledges the potential for broader implications if this paradigm extends beyond academic benchmarks, urging careful consideration for future real-world applications.

In conclusion, CoMAS represents a significant stride towards creating self-evolving LLM-based agents that learn from each other in a collective, human-inspired manner. Its robust methodology and impressive empirical results position it as a foundational work for advancing autonomous multi-agent learning systems.
