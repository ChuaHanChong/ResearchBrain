# Causal-JEPA: Learning World Models through Object-Level Latent Interventions

> [!summary] Summary
> Causal-JEPA presents an object-centric world model integrating Joint Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPAs) with object-level latent interventions, fostering interaction-dependent dynamics learning and efficient planning. It improves counterfactual reasoning accuracy by approximately 20% and accelerates model predictive control planning by over 8 times.

## Problem

- Existing object-centric world models often fail to inherently capture interaction-dependent dynamics, instead relying on learning object self-dynamics or incidental correlations.
- Traditional masked prediction methods (e.g., patch-based) do not explicitly enforce object-level interaction reasoning and can be computationally inefficient for complex dynamic environments.
- World models require a deeper, more robust relational understanding for accurate prediction and control, which is not sufficiently induced by current learning objectives alone.

## Method

- Causal-JEPA leverages a frozen object-centric encoder (e.g., VideoSAUR, SAVi) to transform high-dimensional observations into compact sets of object-level latent representations.
- It employs a ==Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA)==-style masked transformer as the core predictor, operating on these object latents and auxiliary variables (actions, proprioception).
- A central innovation is the application of ==object-level masking== as a latent intervention, replacing masked object states with an 'identity anchor' from an earlier timestep to force inference based on interactions with other objects.

## Results

- Achieved an absolute improvement of approximately **20%** in counterfactual visual question answering accuracy on the CLEVRER dataset, demonstrating enhanced causal understanding.
- Attained comparable task success rates to state-of-the-art patch-based world models on the Push-T robotic manipulation task, while enabling over **8 times faster** planning.
- Operated on only **1.02%** of the input features compared to patch-based models, leading to substantial computational efficiency during Model Predictive Control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Object-level masking, interpreted as a latent intervention, induces a causal inductive bias that compels the model to learn stable, interaction-dependent predictive relationships.
> - The masked history prediction objective formally necessitates reliance on a 'minimal sufficient set of contextual variables' (Influence Neighborhood) for optimal inference, making interaction reasoning functionally essential.
> - Integrating object-centric representations with JEPAs enables efficient world modeling by operating on a significantly reduced latent feature space, without sacrificing performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of artificial intelligence, specifically focusing on the development of world models for understanding, predicting, and reasoning about complex dynamic environments. World models are an abstraction for learning system dynamics, offering a foundation for scalable planning and control in latent space from high-dimensional observations.

Traditional approaches to world modeling often relied on pixel-level representations. However, the research landscape has increasingly moved towards object-centric representations, which abstract scenes into individual entities and their properties. This shift is motivated by the observation that effective world models benefit from representations that expose entities and their interactions, thereby reducing reliance on spurious pixel-level correlations. While object-centric models have been developed for visual dynamics and adopted for world models, existing research indicates that object-centric representations alone are not sufficient to capture interaction-dependent dynamics effectively. Models can still default to object self-dynamics or incidental correlations without explicit mechanisms to guide interaction learning.

Prior work has attempted to address this limitation by explicitly learning interactions through various methods, such as separating temporal dynamics from object interactions via architectural factorization, regularizing attention sparsity, leveraging graph structures, or using downstream-specific techniques. However, a gap remains in how interaction structure can be made functionally necessary directly through the learning objective itself, rather than relying on architectural design choices or auxiliary regularization.

Furthermore, the paper connects to the domain of masked prediction, a self-supervised learning paradigm. While patch-based masked prediction methods have shown success in representation learning, they primarily optimize for local patch correlations and do not inherently enforce object-level interaction reasoning.

This research aims to bridge these gaps by introducing object-level latent masking within a Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) framework. This approach seeks to induce a causal inductive bias that compels the model to learn and rely on object interactions for prediction, moving beyond simple object-centric representations and addressing the limitations of prior masked prediction and object-centric world modeling techniques. It positions itself as a novel method for achieving robust relational understanding through a principled modification of the learning objective.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an object-centric world model, termed Causal-JEPA (C-JEPA), that exhibits robust relational understanding, enabling effective prediction, reasoning, and control in dynamic environments. A central goal is to explicitly induce a causal inductive bias within the learning process, thereby making interaction-dependent prediction a functional necessity for the model.

The motivation for this work stems from several observed limitations in the current landscape of world models:

- **Insufficient Relational Understanding in Object-Centric Models**: While object-centric representations offer a useful abstraction by identifying individual entities, existing models often struggle to capture the complex, interaction-dependent dynamics that govern how these objects behave. Without specific mechanisms to guide interaction learning, models may resort to merely tracking individual object trajectories or exploiting superficial correlations, rather than inferring dynamics based on true inter-object influences. This limits their ability to accurately predict outcomes, especially in scenarios involving multi-object interactions.
- **Lack of Interaction Enforcement in Learning Objectives**: Many current object-centric world models do not embed interaction learning directly into their core objective functions. Instead, interaction awareness is often pursued through architectural choices (e.g., explicit factorization of self-dynamics and interactions) or regularization techniques (e.g., sparse attention), which may not guarantee that interaction reasoning is a fundamental requirement for minimizing the learning objective. The authors aim to design an objective that inherently necessitates such reasoning.
- **Limitations of Patch-Based Masked Prediction**: Existing masked prediction approaches, typically applied to image patches, optimize for local correlations. These methods do not naturally enforce object-level interaction reasoning, as they operate at a granular level below that of coherent entities. The research is motivated to extend the benefits of masked prediction to the object level, where the masked units correspond to meaningful entities.
- **Efficiency and Computational Overhead**: Patch-based world models, while sometimes effective, incur significant computational and memory costs due to the large number of tokens they process. Object-centric representations offer the potential for greater efficiency by operating on a much smaller set of entity-level tokens. The paper aims to leverage this efficiency without compromising performance.
- **Improving Counterfactual Reasoning**: Understanding how changes in one object's state might affect others (counterfactual reasoning) is crucial for robust world models. The current models often exhibit weaknesses in this area. The authors hypothesize that by introducing structured partial observability through object-level masking, the model will be compelled to learn these counterfactual-like dependencies more effectively.

In summary, the key objectives are to design a world model that (1) uses object-level latent masking to impose structured partial observability, (2) compels interaction-dependent prediction as a necessity for minimizing the learning objective, (3) avoids reliance on reconstruction losses, and (4) achieves both strong visual reasoning performance (particularly in counterfactual scenarios) and high efficiency in predictive control tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Causal-JEPA (C-JEPA) framework proposes an object-centric world model that integrates Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) principles with a novel object-level latent masking strategy. The approach is designed to induce a causal inductive bias by making interaction-dependent prediction necessary through its learning objective.

#### 3.1. Architectural Components

The C-JEPA architecture comprises two main components:
- **Frozen Object-Centric Encoder (g)**: This component processes raw pixel-level observations (X_t) from video frames and converts them into a set of object-centric slot representations (S_t = {s_1_t..., s_N_t}). The slots represent distinct entities in the scene. The paper primarily utilizes VideoSAUR, which aggregates features from a frozen DINOv2 backbone into object-centric latents using a Slot Attention-style mechanism. For comparison with some baselines, SAVi is also employed as an encoder. The encoder is frozen during C-JEPA training, meaning its parameters are not updated.
- **Predictor (f)**: This is a Vision Transformer (ViT)-style masked transformer. It takes a sequence of object-centric entity tokens (Z_t = {S_t, U_t}, where U_t includes auxiliary variables like actions and proprioception) as input. The predictor is designed with bidirectional attention, enabling joint inference over masked object tokens across a history window and a future prediction horizon.

#### 3.2. Object-Level Latent Masking

The core innovation of C-JEPA is its object-level latent masking strategy.
- **Masking Mechanism**: For each time step `tau` within a defined history window `T`, a subset of object indices `M_tau` is uniformly sampled. The latent states `s_i_tau` corresponding to these masked objects are then replaced by special mask tokens (`~z_i_tau`).
- **Identity Anchor**: To address the permutation equivariance of slot-based representations and maintain object identity across time, the earliest available latent state `z_i_t0` of a masked object `i` is used as an "identity anchor." This anchor, along with a learnable embedding and temporal positional encoding (`e_tau`), forms the masked token (`~z_i_tau = phi(z_i_t0) + e_tau`). This ensures that the predictor knows *which* object is masked, not just that *an* object is missing.
- **Structured Partial Observability**: This masking scheme imposes structured partial observability during training. By masking an entire object's trajectory (except for its initial identity anchor), the predictor is prevented from inferring the masked object's state through trivial temporal interpolation or reliance on its self-dynamics. Instead, it is forced to infer the masked object's state by leveraging interactions with other *unmasked* objects and auxiliary variables in the scene. This creates "counterfactual-like prediction queries" during training.

#### 3.3. Auxiliary Observable Variables

C-JEPA explicitly incorporates auxiliary observable variables (U_t), such as actions (`a_t`) and proprioceptive signals (`p_t`), as additional conditioning inputs. These variables are treated as external to the object-level state representation, rather than being concatenated directly into the object latents. They are processed by lightweight temporal embedders before being fed to the predictor, allowing the model to capture both object-object dependencies and action-mediated influences.

#### 3.4. Learning Objective

The training of C-JEPA minimizes a masked latent prediction objective, denoted L_mask. The objective requires the predictor to recover all masked object tokens over the full history-future interval (T). This loss can be decomposed into two main components:
- **L_history (Mask Reconstruction)**: This term involves reconstructing masked object states within the history window (τ ≤ t). By requiring the model to infer missing history, it discourages shortcut solutions and compels interaction reasoning.
- **L_future (Forward Prediction)**: This term involves predicting future object states (τ > t), aligning with the standard goal of world modeling.
By optimizing this combined objective, object-level masking functionally necessitates interaction reasoning to minimize predictive error.

#### 3.5. Inference

During inference, the C-JEPA model is used solely for forward latent prediction. The history window is fully observable (no masking applied to past object states), and masking is only applied to future tokens that need to be predicted. This enables direct rollout of future object states, which can then be used for downstream tasks such as planning, control, and reasoning.

#### 3.6. Theoretical Analysis

The paper provides a formal analysis arguing that object-level masked prediction introduces a causal inductive bias. It defines an "influence neighborhood" (N_t(i)) for a masked object `i` at time `t` as the minimal sufficient subset of contextual variables required to predict its state. Theorem 1 formalizes that the optimal predictor, minimizing the masked history prediction loss, must utilize information from this influence neighborhood. This indicates that object-level masking compels the model to rely on interaction-relevant variables, thus inducing a "soft, local relational structure" that captures predictive influence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents empirical evaluations of C-JEPA across visual reasoning and predictive control tasks, supported by qualitative analysis and ablations.

#### 4.1. Visual Reasoning on CLEVRER

- **Improved Performance with Object-Level Masking**: Experiments on the CLEVRER video QA benchmark demonstrate that introducing object-level masking consistently improves C-JEPA's performance compared to an unmasked variant (OC-JEPA). This improvement is attributed to the causal inductive bias induced by the objective.
- **Significant Gains in Counterfactual Reasoning**: The most substantial improvements were observed in counterfactual reasoning questions, with C-JEPA achieving an absolute improvement of approximately 20% over OC-JEPA when masking 4 out of 7 objects using the VideoSAUR encoder. This suggests that the masking strategy directly strengthens the model's ability to reason about "what-if" scenarios, which aligns with the training objective's counterfactual-like queries.
- **Optimal Masking Regime**: The results indicate an optimal range for the number of masked objects; excessive masking can degrade performance by removing too much informative context, highlighting the need for a balance.
- **Outperformance of Reconstruction-Free Baselines**: C-JEPA, without any reconstruction loss, outperforms other object-centric baselines like SlotFormer and OCVP-Seq, especially when these baselines are also evaluated without their reconstruction components. This finding underscores the effectiveness of C-JEPA's decoder-free design and its masking-based learning objective, demonstrating that it can learn robust representations without pixel-level supervision.

#### 4.2. Efficient Predictive Control on Push-T

- **Comparable Performance, Superior Efficiency**: On the Push-T robotic manipulation task, C-JEPA achieves comparable task success rates (e.g., 88.67% vs. 91.33% for DINO-WM) to patch-based world models like DINO-WM. However, C-JEPA accomplishes this while using only 1.02% of the total latent input features.
- **Accelerated Planning**: The significant reduction in token count directly translates to efficiency gains. C-JEPA enables over 8 times faster Model Predictive Control (MPC) planning compared to DINO-WM, requiring 673 seconds for 50 trajectories versus 5,763 seconds.
- **Auxiliary Variable Integration**: The method of treating auxiliary variables (actions and proprioception) as separate conditioning inputs, rather than concatenating them with object latents, was shown to consistently outperform concatenation, indicating a more effective way to integrate control signals.

#### 4.3. Further Analysis

- **Qualitative Plausibility on PHYRE**: Qualitative case studies on the PHYRE benchmark suggest that C-JEPA produces more physically plausible imagined rollouts compared to its unmasked counterpart (OC-JEPA). This indicates that object-level masking encourages more interaction-aware predictive dependencies, preventing reliance on solely object self-dynamics.
- **Interpretable Attention Patterns**: Analysis of the predictor's cross-slot attention patterns on PHYRE shows that C-JEPA concentrates attention more sharply on physically relevant slots during interaction-heavy scenarios, contrasting with the more diffuse attention patterns observed in OC-JEPA. This supports the notion that masking encourages the model to actively seek interaction-relevant context.
- **Masking Strategy Comparison**: Ablations comparing object-level masking with token- and tube-level masking indicate that object-level masking provides a more structured and controllable inductive bias. While other masking strategies can sometimes achieve similar performance, object-level masking leads to more stable training and superior performance on interaction-heavy queries, particularly in control tasks where tube-level masking at higher ratios led to severe performance degradation.

#### 4.4. Theoretical Characterization

The formal analysis presented in the paper describes how object-level masked prediction induces a causal inductive bias. It defines an "influence neighborhood" as a minimal sufficient set of contextual variables for predicting a masked object's state. Theorem 1 demonstrates that the optimal predictor for the masked history completion task must utilize information from this influence neighborhood. This provides a theoretical basis for why the object-level masking approach promotes learning of interaction-dependent dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on Causal-JEPA presents contributions that have implications for the development of advanced artificial intelligence systems, particularly in areas requiring robust understanding and interaction with dynamic environments.

#### 5.1. Scientific Significance

- **Novel Causal Inductive Bias**: This work introduces object-level latent masking as a principled and effective mechanism to embed a causal inductive bias directly into the learning objective of a world model. By posing counterfactual-like prediction queries during training, C-JEPA encourages the model to learn interaction-aware dynamics without relying on reconstruction losses or explicit task-specific supervision. This represents a methodological advance in how interaction reasoning can be enforced.
- **Integration of JEPA with Object-Centric Models**: C-JEPA is presented as the first work to integrate the Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA) paradigm with object-centric world modeling. This combination leverages the benefits of JEPA's decoder-free, prediction-aligned representation learning with the efficiency and abstraction capabilities of object-centric representations.
- **Theoretical Grounding**: The formal analysis of object-level masking, characterizing the emergence of "influence neighborhoods," provides a theoretical foundation for understanding how this masking strategy compels interaction reasoning. This analysis elucidates the predictive sufficiency induced by the masking and how it relates to discovering relevant contextual variables.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities**: The demonstrated substantial gains in counterfactual reasoning on the CLEVRER benchmark suggest that C-JEPA could lead to more intelligent systems capable of deeper "what-if" analysis, crucial for complex decision-making and problem-solving.
- **Improved Efficiency in Robotics and Control**: By achieving comparable performance to patch-based world models with significantly fewer tokens and substantially faster planning times, C-JEPA offers a pathway to more efficient model-predictive control in robotics. This could enable real-time planning for complex manipulation tasks in resource-constrained environments, making advanced robotic applications more practical.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose World Models**: The method's ability to learn interaction-aware dynamics in a task-agnostic manner, without explicit causal graphs or extensive supervision, positions it as a step towards more general and adaptable world models. Such models could generalize across a wider range of tasks and environments.
- **Reduced Computational Cost for Large Models**: The efficiency gains from operating on compact object-centric latents, rather than dense pixel patches, are significant. This could lower the computational and energy costs associated with training and deploying large-scale world models, making advanced AI research and application more accessible.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Directions

The authors identify several limitations, including the dependence of C-JEPA's performance on the quality of the upstream object-centric encoder. Future work could explore jointly refining these encoders using strong pretrained backbones. Another direction involves directly validating the learned "influence neighborhoods" on datasets with explicit temporal causal graphs. Furthermore, evaluating C-JEPA in more complex environments with richer and more diverse interactions is suggested.

Overall, this research establishes object-level masking as a robust and theoretically supported mechanism for imbuing world models with a causal inductive bias, offering advancements in both the performance and efficiency of learning dynamic system behaviors. The societal impacts are anticipated to be primarily foundational, contributing to the broader development of autonomous systems in areas such as robotics and simulation, without immediate specific negative societal consequences highlighted.
