# WAVE-Stereo: Warp-Aligned Volume Encoding for Stereo Matching

> [!summary] Summary
> WAVE-Stereo introduces a unified iterative ConvGRU framework for stereo matching, integrating both correlation-based search and feature-warping alignment via a GeoWarp Correspondence Encoder and efficiently incorporating global context through Periodic Global Context Propagation. The model achieves competitive zero-shot generalization with a 3.18% D1-all error on KITTI 2015 and maintains real-time inference at 66ms per frame, without relying on external foundation models.

## Problem

- Existing iterative stereo matching methods typically rely on either correlation volumes or feature warping, failing to unify these complementary correspondence representations.
- ConvGRU-based iterative updates have limited local context, making disparity estimation challenging in textureless or repetitive regions that require global spatial information.
- Achieving strong zero-shot generalization often necessitates computationally expensive large monocular depth foundation models, preventing real-time deployment.

## Method

- A **GeoWarp Correspondence Encoder (GWCE)** unifies ==correlation features==, disparity priors, and ==cross-view warping alignment== features, encoding them into the ==ConvGRU=='s motion input at each iteration.
- A **Periodic Global Context Propagation (PGCP)** module periodically injects global spatial constraints (e.g., every 3 iterations) at a low resolution using a lightweight ==Transformer==, guiding local updates in ambiguous regions.
- The system employs a lightweight ==MobileNetV2== backbone and an efficient ==ConvGRU==-based iterative refinement framework, along with ==RAFT-style convex upsampling== for real-time operation.

## Results

- WAVE-Stereo achieved a D1-all error of **3.18%** on KITTI 2015 and **3.06%** on KITTI 2012 in zero-shot generalization, along with a Bad-2.0 error of **0.86%** on ETH3D.
- The model demonstrated real-time inference speed of **66ms per frame** for 1280x736 resolution (NVIDIA RTX 3090) with a memory footprint of **980 MB**.
- It attained an End-Point Error (EPE) of **0.43** on the SceneFlow test set, outperforming other real-time methods that do not use external Vision Foundation Models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying complementary correspondence cues, specifically correlation volumes and feature warping, within a single iterative framework leads to more robust and accurate disparity estimation.
> - Global spatial context can be effectively and efficiently integrated into iterative local refinement processes by injecting it periodically at a lower resolution using a lightweight Transformer, without significant computational overhead.
> - Competitive zero-shot generalization and real-time performance in stereo matching can be achieved through thoughtful architectural design, circumventing the need for computationally heavy external foundation models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Stereo matching, the task of estimating dense disparity maps from rectified binocular image pairs to reconstruct 3D scene geometry, is a fundamental visual perception task in fields such as autonomous driving, robotic navigation, and augmented reality. The primary challenge in this domain lies in resolving matching ambiguities that arise in textureless regions, repetitive structures, specular highlights, transparent surfaces, and occluded areas.

Historically, deep learning approaches to stereo matching initially focused on cost volume filtering, employing 3D or 4D convolutional networks to regularize cost volumes derived from image features. While effective, these methods were often constrained by high computational and memory costs, limiting their efficiency and applicability to higher resolutions.

A significant shift occurred with the introduction of iterative optimization paradigms, pioneered by RAFT-Stereo. This approach adapted techniques from optical flow, using recurrent convolutional gated recurrent units (ConvGRUs) to sequentially refine a disparity field based on local matching costs retrieved from a pre-computed all-pairs correlation pyramid. IGEV-Stereo further advanced this by incorporating a geometry encoding volume, applying lightweight 3D CNN regularization to initial correlation volumes to provide the ConvGRU with richer geometric and local matching information, thereby accelerating convergence.

However, the authors identify a prevalent paradigm split within existing iterative stereo matching methods regarding correspondence representation. One class, exemplified by IGEV-Stereo, relies on correlation volumes for explicit matching search, preserving matching ambiguity and the full search space of disparity candidates. The other class, inspired by WAFT and its stereo adaptation WAFT-Stereo, employs feature warping, where the current disparity estimate is used to align one image's features to the other, and iterative refinement focuses on cross-view residuals. The paper notes that correlation-based methods often lack cross-view alignment information under the current estimate, while warping-based methods may discard valuable ambiguity information in matching costs, potentially leading to erroneous alignments in ill-posed regions. A key observation is that these two representations—correlation volumes and feature warping—provide complementary matching cues, yet no existing work had unified them. WAFT-Stereo, for instance, eliminated cost volume dependence but relied on large, pre-trained monocular depth models (e.g., Depth-AnythingV2-Large) as its feature backbone, incurring substantial inference overhead and limiting lightweight deployment.

Another challenge addressed by this work is the inherent limitation of ConvGRU's local context window. In textureless or repetitive areas, local matching signals are ambiguous, requiring global spatial context for robust disparity estimation. Recent attempts to introduce global context, often by integrating Vision Transformer (ViT) backbones or monocular depth foundation models, typically lead to inference times that preclude real-time deployment.

Furthermore, the research landscape for lightweight stereo matching has explored several avenues: reducing the complexity of 3D convolutions, replacing 3D convolutions with purely 2D operations (e.g., LightStereo), or employing hybrid 3D-2D aggregation. Simultaneously, achieving zero-shot generalization—accurate estimation on unseen target domains—is a critical requirement. This has been approached either by integrating large-scale pre-trained monocular depth foundation models to leverage their scene priors (e.g., FoundationStereo, MonSter), which are generally slow, or by improving generalization through specific training strategies like distillation and multi-stage training without external models at inference time (e.g., LiteAnyStereo).

WAVE-Stereo positions itself within this landscape by building on the ConvGRU iterative optimization paradigm, leveraging concepts from geometry encoding volumes, and adopting a lightweight 2D aggregation design inspired by methods like LightStereo. Its distinct contribution is the unification of correlation-based search and feature-warping-based residual alignment within a single iterative framework via the GeoWarp Correspondence Encoder (GWCE) and the periodic injection of global spatial constraints through Periodic Global Context Propagation (PGCP). This approach aims to achieve competitive zero-shot generalization and real-time inference without relying on external foundation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and underlying motivations of the WAVE-Stereo research are multifaceted:

- **To unify complementary correspondence representations:**
 - **Motivation:** Existing iterative stereo matching methods predominantly adopt one of two correspondence representations: explicit matching search via correlation volumes or local residual refinement via warped features. The authors observe that these two paradigms are fundamentally complementary. Correlation volumes preserve explicit matching differences and the ambiguity across disparity candidates, which can be informative in challenging regions. Feature warping, conversely, aligns right-image features to the left-image coordinate system based on the current disparity estimate, transforming the problem into local residual correction, which is amenable to iterative refinement. However, existing methods typically model them separately. The key objective is to integrate both types of information at each iterative update, thereby providing the ConvGRU with a richer and more complete set of matching cues. This unification is hypothesized to lead to more robust and accurate disparity estimation by simultaneously leveraging candidate search information and cross-view alignment signals.

- **To enhance disparity estimation in ambiguous regions by introducing global context efficiently:**
 - **Motivation:** The iterative updates performed by ConvGRUs are inherently limited to a local context window. In textureless surfaces, repetitive patterns, or transparent regions, local matching signals are highly ambiguous, and these ambiguities cannot be resolved solely through local neighborhood context. Long-range spatial constraints are necessary to determine correct correspondences. While large Vision Transformers (ViTs) or monocular depth foundation models can provide global context, they typically incur significant computational overhead, preventing real-time deployment. The objective is to design a mechanism that periodically injects global spatial constraints into the iterative process, with minimal computational cost, to guide local updates in challenging ambiguous regions without sacrificing real-time performance.

- **To achieve competitive zero-shot generalization accuracy without external foundation models:**
 - **Motivation:** For practical deployment in diverse real-world scenarios (e.g., autonomous driving), stereo matching models must demonstrate accurate performance on completely unseen target domains (zero-shot generalization). Many high-performing generalization methods rely on large-scale pre-trained monocular depth foundation models, which contribute significantly to inference latency. The objective is to develop a lightweight iterative stereo matching framework that can achieve strong zero-shot generalization performance, comparable to or better than other lightweight methods, while entirely circumventing the need for any external, computationally intensive foundation models during inference. This would make the solution more self-contained and efficient.

- **To maintain real-time inference speed:**
 - **Motivation:** Real-time operation is a critical requirement for many applications of stereo matching, particularly in embedded systems or those with strict latency budgets. Traditional approaches, especially those involving extensive 3D convolutions or large foundation models, often exceed these speed constraints. The objective is to design an architecture where each component contributes to overall accuracy while maintaining a low computational footprint, ensuring that the final system can process images at a real-time frame rate. This includes designing lightweight convolutional branches in the feature encoder and a compact, periodically invoked global context module.

In summary, WAVE-Stereo seeks to overcome the limitations of existing iterative stereo matching paradigms by intelligently combining complementary matching cues and efficiently incorporating global context, thereby achieving a favorable balance between high zero-shot generalization accuracy and real-time inference speed, without relying on computationally expensive external models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WAVE-Stereo is an iterative optimization framework designed for real-time zero-shot stereo matching. The overall architecture consists of a lightweight feature extraction backbone, an initial disparity estimation module, and a ConvGRU-based iterative refinement process enhanced by two core proposed modules: the GeoWarp Correspondence Encoder (GWCE) and Periodic Global Context Propagation (PGCP).

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

1. **Feature Extraction:** Given a rectified binocular image pair ($I_L, I_R$), a shared-weight MobileNetV2 backbone extracts multi-scale features. These are then fused top-down using an FPN (Feature Pyramid Network) with skip connections to produce features at four scales. The 1/4-resolution features ($f_L, f_R \in R^{C_f \times H/4 \times W/4}$, with $C_f = 24$) are utilized for subsequent operations.
2. **Initial Disparity Estimation:** An all-pairs correlation is computed between $f_L$ and $f_R$ (channel-wise dot product with mean) to construct a 3D cost volume $C \in R^{D \times H/4 \times W/4}$, where $D$ is the number of disparity candidates (D=Max Disparity / 4). A lightweight 2D aggregation module regularizes this cost volume. Soft-argmin regression is then applied to obtain the initial disparity map $d_0 \in R^{1 \times H/4 \times W/4}$.
3. **Iterative Update:** The core refinement loop is driven by a ConvGRU. At each iteration $t$, the ConvGRU receives motion features ($m_t$) and context features to update its hidden state $h_t$. A disparity head then decodes a residual update ($\Delta d_t$), which is added to the previous disparity estimate ($d_t = d_{t-1} + \Delta d_t$).
4. **Upsampling:** A RAFT-style convex upsampling module (Context Upsampling) upsamples the final 1/4-resolution disparity to full resolution. An upsampling mask is generated from the GRU's output mask feature map through a two-stage deconvolution process.

#### 3.2. GeoWarp Correspondence Encoder (GWCE)

GWCE is designed to unify and encode three distinct types of matching cues into the ConvGRU's motion input ($m_t$) at each iteration. It receives correlation features ($c$), the current disparity estimate ($d$), and left/right image features ($f_L, f_R$). These inputs are processed in parallel through three independent branches:
- **Correlation Branch:** This branch processes correlation features ($c$) retrieved from a geometry encoding volume. It consists of a 1x1 convolution to compress channels, followed by a 3x3 convolution to incorporate local spatial context, producing correspondence features $x_c$. This branch explicitly preserves matching ambiguity and candidate search information.
- **Disparity Prior Branch:** This branch encodes the current disparity estimate ($d$). It uses a 7x7 large-kernel convolution to capture wide spatial context, followed by a ReLU activation and a 3x3 convolution, producing geometric prior features $x_d$. This provides the ConvGRU with information about the current geometric state.
- **Cross-View Warping Alignment Branch:** This branch transforms correspondence estimation into local residual correction. The right-image features ($f_R$) are warped to the left-image coordinate system using the current disparity ($d$) via bilinear grid sampling, resulting in $\tilde{f_R}$. This warped feature $\tilde{f_R}$ is then concatenated with $f_L$ along the channel dimension and processed by two 3x3 convolutions interleaved with ReLU activations (encoder $Enc_w$), yielding warped alignment features $x_w$. This branch provides direct observation of cross-view feature differences, which are critical for estimating disparity residuals.
- **Fusion:** The outputs of the three branches ($x_c, x_d, x_w$) are first fused, then concatenated with the current disparity $d$ to form the motion features $m_t$, which are fed into the ConvGRU.

#### 3.3. Periodic Global Context Propagation (PGCP)

PGCP aims to inject global spatial constraints periodically to address the limited receptive field of the ConvGRU's local iterative updates.
- **Mechanism:** Instead of running global self-attention at every iteration, PGCP is activated only at fixed-interval iterations (every K=3 iterations by default) and operates at a significantly lower resolution.
- **Process:** The motion features ($m_t$) are downsampled by 8x average pooling (from H/4 to H/32 resolution). These pooled features are then projected to a Transformer hidden dimension (D_tf = 128) using a 1x1 convolution and flattened into tokens. Three Transformer layers, each with 4 attention heads and an MLP expansion ratio of 3, perform self-attention. The outputs of the first and third Transformer layers are reshaped back to spatial dimensions, forming two feature scales. These are then fed into a simplified DPT (Dense Prediction Transformer) module, which fuses multi-scale features bottom-up (deepest level H/32, then progressively upsampled) to return to H/4 resolution.
- **Integration:** The global feature output ($g_t$) from the DPT module is added to the ConvGRU's hidden state ($h_t$) via a learnable gating parameter $\alpha$.

#### 3.4. Loss Function

The stereo loss ($L_{stereo}$) is computed as a weighted sum of the Smooth L1 loss for the initial disparity ($d_0$) and the L1 loss for all $N$ iterative disparity predictions ($d_i$). Later iterations are assigned higher weights through an exponential decay factor ($\gamma = 0.9$).

#### 3.5. Training Details

WAVE-Stereo is implemented in PyTorch and trained using the AdamW optimizer with a OneCycleLR learning rate scheduler. Training is performed on a diverse collection of 9 public synthetic datasets, including SceneFlow, FSD, FallingThings, and TartanAir, to enhance generalization. Images are randomly cropped to 320x720, and extensive data augmentation techniques (e.g., asymmetric color jitter, random erasing, scaling/stretching, Gaussian blur) are applied. Maximum disparity is set to 192. The model is trained for 12 ConvGRU iterations, with 8 iterations used during inference by default.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative results, along with ablation studies, to support the effectiveness of WAVE-Stereo.

#### 4.1. Zero-Shot Generalization (Table 1)

WAVE-Stereo was evaluated in a zero-shot manner on five real-world benchmarks: Middlebury, ETH3D, KITTI 2012, KITTI 2015, and Booster, none of which were included in the training data.
- The model achieved a D1-all error of 3.18% on KITTI 2015 and 3.06% on KITTI 2012.
- On ETH3D, it reported a Bad-2.0 error of 0.86%.
- On Booster, designed to test robustness against non-Lambertian surfaces, it achieved Bad-2.0 of 4.42% and Bad-3.0 of 2.84%.
- For Middlebury, the Bad-3.0 error was 3.92%.
- Crucially, these results were achieved with an inference speed of 66ms (at 1280x736 resolution on an NVIDIA RTX 3090) and a memory footprint of 980 MB.
- Comparison with other methods indicates that WAVE-Stereo achieved competitive accuracy without relying on external foundation models, distinguishing it from methods like FoundationStereo and MonSter which utilize DepthAnythingV2, or WAFT-Stereo which uses DepthAnythingV2-Large as its backbone. The method demonstrated a favorable balance between accuracy and efficiency.

#### 4.2. SceneFlow In-Domain Comparison (Table 2)

For in-domain performance evaluation, WAVE-Stereo was tested on the SceneFlow test set, achieving an End-Point Error (EPE) of 0.43.
- This performance was the best among methods that do not use external monocular VFM (Vision Foundation Models).
- It significantly outperformed other real-time non-VFM methods, such as LightStereo-H (EPE 0.50), RT-IGEV (EPE 0.50), and RAFT-Stereo (EPE 0.61).
- WAVE-Stereo also surpassed several non-real-time non-VFM methods, including IGEV-Stereo (EPE 0.48), IGEV++ (EPE 0.46), and Selective-IGEV (EPE 0.44), despite these models often employing computationally intensive 3D convolutions.
- The accuracy gap to VFM-dependent methods (e.g., MonSter 0.37, FoundationStereo 0.34) was noted as manageable considering WAVE-Stereo's efficiency advantage.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

A series of ablation experiments were conducted on the SceneFlow test set to validate the contribution of each module and design choice.
- **Component-wise Build-up (Table 3):** Starting from an IGEV-style baseline (0.58 EPE), adding PGCP alone improved EPE to 0.57. Incorporating GWCE alone (without PGCP) resulted in a substantial improvement to 0.46 EPE. The full WAVE-Stereo model (GWCE + PGCP) achieved the optimal 0.43 EPE, demonstrating that both modules contribute positively and complement each other.
- **GWCE Branch Ablation (Table 4):** This study evaluated the individual contributions of the correlation and warping branches within GWCE, always retaining the disparity prior branch. Using only the correlation branch yielded 0.451 EPE, while using only the warping branch achieved 0.447 EPE. The full GWCE, combining both correlation and warping, resulted in 0.434 EPE, demonstrating a synergistic effect where the joint use of both representations surpassed either paradigm alone.
- **PGCP Structural Design (Table 5):** Various configurations of PGCP’s Transformer components (hidden dimension, number of layers, heads, MLP ratio) were tested. The configuration with a 128-dim hidden space and 3 Transformer layers achieved the optimal 0.43 EPE, indicating that a moderate capacity is needed for effective global context propagation. It was also found that overly frequent injection (every iteration, K=1) not only increased computational cost (8.8ms vs. 3.2ms) but also degraded accuracy (0.48 EPE vs. 0.43 EPE), validating the periodic injection strategy. Qualitative visualizations (Fig. 6) demonstrated that PGCP improves disparity continuity in textureless regions.
- **Runtime Decomposition (Table 6):** An analysis of runtime breakdown for an 8-iteration inference process revealed a total GPU forward pass time of 60.32ms, with the entire pipeline (including CPU pre/post-processing) approximately 65ms per frame. The frontend processing accounted for 32.2% of the runtime, while the iterative refinement stage (GWCE + ConvGRU) consumed 57.4%. GWCE was efficient at 2.32ms per call, and PGCP, due to its periodicity and low-resolution operation, only accounted for 4.8% of the total time. This confirms the real-time design objective.

#### 4.4. Limitations

The authors acknowledge two main limitations: (1) The model is primarily trained on synthetic data; incorporating real-world pseudo-label data during training might further improve performance. (2) The maximum disparity is limited to 192 pixels, which may be insufficient for close-range scenes with large baselines and high resolutions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WAVE-Stereo research presents advancements in real-time zero-shot stereo matching with several significant implications:

- **Unified Correspondence Modeling:** The introduction of the GeoWarp Correspondence Encoder (GWCE) represents a notable step in unifying two previously distinct paradigms for correspondence representation in iterative stereo matching: correlation search and feature warping. By demonstrating that these cues are complementary and can be effectively integrated within a single framework, WAVE-Stereo provides a novel approach that leverages a richer set of matching signals. This could inspire future research into more comprehensive and robust ways of encoding and utilizing diverse matching information.

- **Enhanced Generalization without Large Foundation Models:** WAVE-Stereo achieves competitive zero-shot generalization accuracy across multiple real-world benchmarks without relying on external, large-scale monocular depth foundation models. This is a crucial development for practical applications, as it allows for strong performance in unseen environments while circumventing the substantial computational overhead and increased model complexity associated with foundation models. This makes the system more deployable in resource-constrained edge computing environments.

- **Real-time Performance and Efficiency:** The demonstrated real-time inference speed of 66ms, coupled with competitive accuracy, highlights the model's practical utility. This efficiency is critical for time-sensitive applications like autonomous driving, robotic navigation, and augmented reality, where low latency is paramount for responsive and safe operation. The detailed runtime decomposition further validates the efficient design of each component, particularly the lightweight nature of GWCE and the periodic, low-cost operation of PGCP.

- **Improved Robustness in Challenging Scenes:** The Periodic Global Context Propagation (PGCP) module effectively addresses the limitation of local iterative updates by injecting global spatial constraints. This directly contributes to improved disparity estimation quality in textureless regions, repetitive patterns, and transparent surfaces—areas where local matching cues are ambiguous and traditional methods often struggle. This enhanced robustness is valuable for deploying stereo systems in diverse and complex real-world environments.

- **Architectural Innovation:** The research demonstrates that performance and generalization can be significantly improved through thoughtful architectural design, such as the parallel encoding in GWCE and the judicious, periodic injection of global context in PGCP, rather than simply increasing model size or relying on external heavy models. This provides a direction for designing efficient yet powerful deep learning models for stereo vision.

- **Open-Source Contribution:** The availability of the code fosters reproducibility and provides a foundation for other researchers and developers to build upon, potentially accelerating further advancements in the field of stereo matching.

In conclusion, WAVE-Stereo offers a balanced solution to critical challenges in stereo matching, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in terms of accuracy, generalization, and efficiency within a single, self-contained framework. Its impact is likely to be felt in both academic research, by providing new architectural paradigms, and in industrial applications, by enabling more practical and high-performing real-time stereo perception systems.
