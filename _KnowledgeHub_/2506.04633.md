# Unfolding Spatial Cognition: Evaluating Multimodal Models on Visual Simulations

> [!summary] Summary
> STARE, a new benchmark, evaluates multimodal large language models' (MLLMs) spatial reasoning through multi-step visual simulations, demonstrating a substantial performance gap between human abilities and even state-of-the-art MLLMs. The benchmark reveals that current models struggle with complex 3D transformations and inconsistently leverage explicit visual guidance.

## Problem

- Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) largely lack sophisticated spatial reasoning abilities, unlike humans who perform complex mental simulations for spatial tasks.
- Existing AI evaluation benchmarks are insufficient for assessing dynamic, multi-step visual simulation capabilities in MLLMs.
- There is a critical gap in understanding whether MLLMs can perform the kind of multi-step visual simulations characteristic of human spatial intelligence.

## Method

- Introduces **STARE** (==Spatial Thinking and Reasoning Evaluations==), a benchmark designed to assess MLLMs' multi-step visual simulation capabilities for spatial cognition.
- **STARE** comprises approximately 4,000 instances across three levels of complexity: ==Foundational Geometric Transformations==, ==Integrated Spatial Reasoning== (e.g., cube folding, tangrams), and Real-world Spatial Reasoning (e.g., temporal/perspective reasoning).
- Tasks are programmatically generated, allowing for precise control over difficulty and the option to provide explicit intermediate visual states to test performance with and without visual guidance.

## Results

- The top-performing MLLM achieved only **57.2%** overall accuracy on STARE, with GPT-4o reaching **53.9%**, significantly below human performance.
- Models struggled with 3D spatial perception (GPT-4o at **~57%** accuracy) and showed cumulative cognitive load effects, with performance often declining beyond two transformation steps without visual simulation.
- A strong correlation (**r = 0.88**) was observed between performance on STARE's synthetic tasks and real-world spatial reasoning, indicating that improvements on the benchmark tasks could translate to practical gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Human participants achieved near-perfect accuracy (87.5% to 99.0%) across all STARE tasks, while even the best MLLMs struggled.
> - MLLMs performed best on scaling operations (~90% accuracy) but poorly on complex transformations like shearing (~54%), and 3D tasks consistently reduced performance.
> - Providing explicit visual simulations offered inconsistent benefits, and verbal step-by-step guidance sometimes degraded MLLM performance, suggesting limitations in integrating sequential information.
