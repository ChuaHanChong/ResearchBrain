# Direct Preference Optimization for Primitive-Enabled Hierarchical RL: A Bilevel Approach

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed DIPPER, a hierarchical reinforcement learning framework that formulates HRL as a bi-level optimization problem and uses primitive-regularized Direct Preference Optimization to stabilize higher-level policy learning and ensure feasible subgoal generation. This approach achieved up to a 40% improvement over state-of-the-art baselines in complex, sparse-reward robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents often struggle with complex, long-horizon tasks, especially in environments with sparse rewards.
- Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) methods face non-stationarity issues, where the higher-level policy's learning becomes unstable due to the continuous evolution of the lower-level policy.
- The higher-level policy in HRL can generate infeasible subgoals that are beyond the current capabilities of the lower-level policy, leading to inefficient learning and suboptimal overall performance.

## Method

- The work formulates Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning as a ==bi-level optimization== problem to rigorously model the interdependencies between higher-level and lower-level policies.
- **DIPPER** utilizes Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) for the higher-level policy, decoupling its learning from the non-stationary reward signals influenced by the evolving lower-level policy.
- A primitive regularization mechanism is integrated, derived from the bi-level formulation, which grounds subgoal proposals in the lower-level policy's value function to ensure the generation of feasible and achievable subgoals.

## Results

- DIPPER achieved up to a **40%** improvement in success rates compared to state-of-the-art hierarchical and non-hierarchical baselines across challenging robotic tasks with sparse rewards.
- The framework demonstrated effective mitigation of infeasible subgoal generation, evidenced by consistently low values for the 'Subgoal Distance Metric,' indicating that higher-level policies predicted feasible subgoals.
- DIPPER showed robust mitigation of non-stationarity, leading to high values for the 'Lower Q-Function Metric' for predicted subgoals, confirming the generation of high-reward-inducing and achievable subgoals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the higher-level policy's objective from the volatile, non-stationary reward signals generated by an evolving lower-level policy can significantly improve training stability in HRL.
> - Regularizing subgoal generation with respect to the lower-level policy's value function provides a principled way to ensure that proposed subgoals are feasible and achievable, enhancing overall learning efficiency.
> - A rigorous bi-level optimization framework can effectively model and jointly optimize hierarchical policies, enabling a more stable and effective approach to complex, long-horizon tasks.
