# Breaking the Modality Barrier: Universal Embedding Learning with Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> The UniME framework introduces a two-stage approach that enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to learn universal, discriminative representations for diverse vision-language tasks. This method achieves state-of-the-art performance on the MMEB benchmark, showing significant improvements over previous models in short and long caption retrieval, and compositional cross-modal retrieval by overcoming limitations of traditional frameworks like CLIP.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal models like CLIP suffer from text token truncation, isolated image-text processing, and poor compositional understanding.
- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are primarily designed for generative tasks, limiting their capacity to learn transferable, discriminative representations.
- Current MLLM-based embedding methods struggle with data diversity, causal attention constraints, and false negative contamination in contrastive learning.

## Method

- A two-stage framework, ==UniME==, is introduced to enable Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to learn universal, discriminative embeddings.
- Stage 1 employs ==textual discriminative knowledge distillation==, transferring strong textual embedding capabilities from a powerful LLM teacher (NV-Embed V2) to the MLLM's language component using KL divergence.
- Stage 2 refines the embeddings through ==hard negative enhanced instruction tuning==, which mitigates false negative contamination and strategically samples truly challenging negative pairs for robust contrastive learning.

## Results

- UniME achieves state-of-the-art results on the Massive Multimodal Embedding Benchmark (MMEB), with the LLaVA-OneVision backbone reaching an impressive **70.7%** overall average score.
- The framework demonstrates superior performance in long caption cross-modal retrieval, surpassing EVA-CLIP(8B) by **1**4.8%**-18.1%** on the Urban1K dataset due to its ability to handle extended text inputs.
- UniME consistently outperforms baselines on the SugarCrepe compositional benchmark, showing improvements of **3.9%**, **4.2%**, and **9.1%** over VLM2Vec in relation replacement, object swapping, and attribute addition tasks, respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), despite their autoregressive nature, can be effectively transformed into powerful discriminative embedding models.
> - Textual discriminative knowledge distillation is a successful method for injecting robust embedding capabilities into the LLM component of an MLLM, overcoming causal attention limitations.
> - Strategic mitigation of false negatives and selection of hard negative samples are crucial for refining cross-modal alignment and enhancing discriminative power in multimodal contrastive learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Breaking the Modality Barrier: Universal Embedding Learning with Multimodal LLMs" addresses a critical challenge in artificial intelligence: the efficient and effective learning of *universal, discriminative representations* across different data modalities. This work is positioned at the intersection of multimodal learning, large language models (LLMs), and representation learning, aiming to advance the state of the art by overcoming limitations of existing paradigms.

Historically, the **Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP)** framework has been a cornerstone for multimodal representation learning, particularly excelling in image-text retrieval tasks due to its large-scale cross-modal contrastive supervision. However, the authors identify three key limitations of CLIP:
1. **Text token truncation:** CLIP's fixed text length limit (e.g., 77 tokens) hinders its ability to process detailed descriptions and extensive contextual information, crucial for "long caption" retrieval tasks.
2. **Isolated image-text encoding:** Its dual-encoder architecture processes images and text separately, limiting deep cross-modal fusion and making it less effective for complex, instruction-following multimodal tasks.
3. **Deficient compositionality:** CLIP often exhibits "bag-of-words" behavior, struggling with understanding complex compositional semantics where individual elements combine to form a new meaning.

In parallel, the remarkable success of **Large Language Models (LLMs)** has spurred the development of **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs)**. These models, such as LLaVA, Qwen2-VL, and Phi3.5-Vision, integrate vision capabilities into LLM architectures, demonstrating impressive generalized vision-language understanding and reasoning. However, the paper points out that MLLMs are primarily designed for *autoregressive next-token prediction*, which inherently constrains their capacity to learn *transferable multimodal representations* as effectively as contrastive methods like CLIP.

Recent research has begun to explore leveraging MLLMs for unified multimodal representation learning:
- **E5-V** proposed training the language component of MLLMs on sentence pairs to bridge cross-modal representation gaps. Yet, it faced limitations due to data scale/diversity and the inherent causal attention mechanism of LLMs, which restricts complex contextual representation learning.
- **VLM2Vec** introduced a contrastive framework for converting Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into embedding models and established the Massive Multimodal Embedding Benchmark (MMEB). However, VLM2Vec, like many contrastive methods, struggles with "false negative contamination" in batches when using standard InfoNCE loss, complicating the discrimination of truly challenging (hard) negative pairs.

UniME directly builds upon these advancements while explicitly addressing their shortcomings. It aims to bridge the gap between the powerful reasoning abilities of MLLMs and the need for robust, discriminative, and universal multimodal embeddings. By proposing a novel two-stage framework, UniME tackles CLIP's structural limitations and enhances MLLM-based embedding methods by improving their discriminative power, handling false negatives, and strengthening instruction-following capabilities. This positions UniME as a significant step towards achieving truly universal and highly performant multimodal embedding learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop **UniME (Universal Multimodal Embedding)**, a novel two-stage framework that empowers Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to learn **discriminative and universal representations** for a diverse array of downstream vision-language tasks. The authors are motivated by the inherent limitations of existing multimodal representation learning paradigms and the untapped potential of MLLMs in this domain.

The key motivations and specific objectives can be summarized as follows:

1. **Overcome Limitations of Traditional Multimodal Representation Models (e.g., CLIP):** The authors highlight the widely acknowledged shortcomings of CLIP, a seminal model in the field. These include:
 - **Text Token Truncation:** CLIP's fixed limit of 77 tokens for text descriptions restricts its applicability to tasks requiring longer, more nuanced textual inputs, such as long caption retrieval.
 - **Isolated Image-Text Encoding:** The dual-encoder architecture processes modalities separately, hindering deep cross-modal fusion and making it less effective for complex instruction-following scenarios.
 - **Deficient Compositionality:** CLIP often struggles with compositional understanding, exhibiting "bag-of-words" behavior rather than capturing the intricate relationships between concepts. UniME aims to address these limitations by leveraging MLLMs' more advanced language understanding and integrated processing.

2. **Leverage MLLMs for Transferable Representation Learning:** While MLLMs have demonstrated remarkable progress in generalized vision-language understanding and reasoning, their core autoregressive next-token prediction objective limits their direct effectiveness in learning *transferable multimodal representations*. The research is motivated to unlock this potential, transforming MLLMs into powerful encoders for diverse downstream tasks.

3. **Enhance Discriminative Capabilities of MLLM Embeddings:** Previous MLLM-based embedding approaches, such as E5-V, face challenges due to limited training data diversity and the causal attention mechanism of their LLM backbones, which restricts their discriminative capacity. UniME seeks to explicitly improve the discriminative power of MLLM-generated embeddings.

4. **Address False Negative Contamination in Contrastive Learning:** Methods like VLM2Vec, which use contrastive training, often suffer from "false negative contamination" within training batches. This means that some "negative" samples might actually be semantically related to the query, hindering the model's ability to learn from truly challenging (hard) negatives. A key motivation for UniME is to effectively mitigate these false negatives and strategically sample hard negatives to improve learning efficiency and representation quality.

5. **Improve Cross-Modal Alignment and Instruction-Following Ability:** Beyond basic discriminative power, the authors aim to improve how MLLMs align information across modalities and respond to specific instructions. This is crucial for complex multimodal retrieval and question-answering tasks where context and user intent are paramount.

By achieving these objectives, UniME seeks to provide a robust, universal, and highly performant framework for generating multimodal embeddings, pushing the boundaries of what MLLMs can accomplish beyond generation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The UniME (Universal Multimodal Embedding) framework is a novel two-stage approach designed to empower Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with enhanced discriminative representation learning capabilities for diverse downstream tasks. The methodology systematically addresses the limitations of prior approaches by integrating knowledge distillation and a sophisticated negative sampling strategy.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Task Definition and Feature Extraction by MLLM

Unlike dual-tower encoders (e.g., CLIP) that process modalities separately, UniME utilizes MLLMs with their unified structure comprising a vision tower, a projection layer, and an LLM backbone. This allows for flexible processing of unimodal (text or image) and multimodal (interleaved image-text) inputs. Embeddings are extracted from the MLLM using customized prompts, and similarity is computed to rank relevant pairs for tasks like retrieval. Specific prompts are tailored for each stage and task: a generic "summary" prompt for text embedding in Stage 1, and task-specific prompts (from VLM2Vec) for diverse tasks in Stage 2.

#### 3.2. Stage 1: Textual Discriminative Knowledge Distillation

- **Motivation:** The autoregressive decoder architecture of LLMs, with its causal masking, inherently limits its discriminative capacity when used directly for embedding. This stage aims to inject strong discriminative textual knowledge into the MLLM's language component.
- **Approach:**
 - **Decoupling:** The LLM component is decoupled from the MLLM.
 - **Teacher Model:** A powerful, state-of-the-art LLM-based embedding model, NV-Embed V2, is used as the teacher. NV-Embed V2 is known for its strong discriminative performance due to removing the causal attention mask during its own contrastive training on diverse datasets.
 - **Prompt:** Text-only inputs are processed using the prompt: "Summary above sentences in one word: \n" to guide the LLM to compress textual information into a single embedding at the last token.
 - **Loss Function:** Knowledge is transferred by minimizing the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the normalized student (MLLM's LLM) text embeddings and the teacher text embeddings (extracted offline). This aligns the student's embedding distribution with the teacher's discriminative distribution.
- **Inference (Stage 1):** For inference, the original vision encoder and projection layer are re-integrated. Unimodal inputs use modality-specific prompts, while interleaved image-text inputs are processed independently, and their embeddings are aggregated via element-wise summation.

#### 3.3. Stage 2: Hard Negative Enhanced Instruction Tuning

- **Motivation:** After Stage 1, UniME has preliminary discriminative abilities but may still lack visual sensitivity, precise image-text alignment, and robust instruction-following capabilities for complex retrieval tasks due to the generic prompts used initially. This stage refines these aspects.
- **Approach:**
 - **False Negative Contamination Mitigation:** Standard InfoNCE loss can be ineffective if "negative" samples are semantically close to the query (false negatives). To address this, a filtering mechanism is introduced. Negative samples are excluded if their similarity to the query exceeds a dynamic threshold `α = cos(e_q, e_+_c) + β`, where `β` is a hyper-parameter. This step ensures that only truly dissimilar negatives, or genuine hard negatives, remain.
 - **Hard Negative Sampling:** Hard negative samples (distinct in label but closely embedded) provide the most significant gradients for learning. UniME samples `k` such hard negatives per instance within each batch. The model leverages its preliminary discriminative ability (from Stage 1) to rank candidates by similarity to the query and select the top-`k` most similar non-positive, non-false-negative candidates. Existing hard negatives are duplicated if fewer than `k` are found to maintain batch consistency.
 - **Training Objective:** The Noise Contrastive Estimation (InfoNCE) loss is applied over the query, its positive candidate, and the sampled hard negative candidates within the batch.
 - **Instruction Tuning:** Task-specific prompts, similar to those in VLM2Vec, are utilized (e.g., "<Image> Represent the given image with the following question: <Text>" for VQA, "<Image> Find a caption for the news in the given photo." for retrieval) to adapt UniME for diverse downstream tasks and improve instruction-following.

#### 3.4. Training Recipe

- **Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning:** QLoRA (Quantized Low-Rank Adaptation) is used throughout to efficiently fine-tune parameters, minimizing computational overhead. In Stage 1, only the LLM component is tuned; in Stage 2, all MLLM parameters are tuned.
- **Memory Management:** To handle large batch sizes and GPU memory limitations, GradCache (a gradient caching technique) is employed in Stage 2.
- **Datasets:** Stage 1 uses the Natural Language Inference (NLI) dataset (approx. 273k text pairs). Stage 2 uses 20 in-distribution datasets from the MMEB benchmark (approx. 662k multimodal training pairs), covering classification, VQA, multimodal retrieval, and visual grounding.
- **Base Models:** Experiments are conducted using Phi3.5-Vision and LLaVA-1.6 as base MLLMs.

This meticulously designed two-stage methodology allows UniME to systematically acquire strong textual discriminative power and then refine it with visual sensitivity, cross-modal alignment, and robust hard negative handling for universal multimodal embedding learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted demonstrate that UniME consistently achieves state-of-the-art or superior performance across a wide range of multimodal tasks, showcasing its robust discriminative and compositional capabilities.

#### 4.1. Multi-Modal Retrieval (MMEB Benchmark)

- **Overall Superiority:** UniME significantly outperforms existing baselines, including E5-V and VLM2Vec, on the Massive Multimodal Embedding Benchmark (MMEB).
 - After Stage 1 (textual discriminative knowledge distillation), UniME (Phi3.5-V) achieves an average performance improvement of 4.2% over E5-V, and UniME (LLaVA-1.6) shows a 4.1% enhancement. This highlights the effectiveness of knowledge distillation in boosting the discriminative capability of embeddings.
 - After Stage 2 (hard negative enhanced instruction tuning), UniME (Phi3.5-V) improves by 1.3% and UniME (LLaVA-1.6) by 3.3% compared to VLM2Vec, which also uses MLLMs.
 - Notably, UniME with LLaVA-OneVision as the backbone achieves an impressive 70.7% overall average score on MMEB, outperforming all other compared models.
- **Discriminative Power:** Visualizations of cross-modal cosine similarity matrices (Figure 4) clearly show that UniME produces representations with superior distinctiveness, evidenced by the enhanced diagonal clarity compared to E5-V. This indicates a more precise alignment between query and positive samples.

#### 4.2. Short & Long Caption Cross-Modal Retrieval

- **Short Captions (Flickr30K, MS-COCO):** After Stage 1, UniME achieves comparable performance to E5-V. However, Stage 2's hard negative enhanced instruction tuning significantly boosts UniME's performance, yielding 5.2%–11.3% improvement over VLM2Vec.
- **Long Captions (ShareGPT4V, Urban1K):** UniME demonstrates superior performance across all metrics. For instance, UniME (Phi3.5-V) shows a 1.3%-3.8% improvement after Stage 1, which further increases to 2.0%-8.3% over VLM2Vec after Stage 2. Critically, UniME achieves 14.8% and 18.1% performance improvements over EVA-CLIP(8B) on the Urban1K dataset, directly attributing this gain to EVA-CLIP's 77-token length restriction, which UniME (based on MLLMs) overcomes.

#### 4.3. Compositional Cross-Modal Retrieval (SugarCrepe)

- UniME consistently delivers superior performance on the SugarCrepe compositional benchmark, which tests the model's ability to discriminate hard negative samples with fine-grained semantic differences.
 - Post-Stage 1, UniME (Phi3.5-V) outperforms E5-V by 2.0% in relation replacement, 1.0% in object swapping, and a substantial 15.9% in attribute addition tasks.
 - After Stage 2, the compositional understanding capabilities are further enhanced, leading to 3.9%, 4.2%, and 9.1% performance improvements over VLM2Vec across these compositional tasks.
 - UniME also shows improvements of 4.2%, 0.6%, and 6.6% compared to EVA-CLIP(8B), highlighting its robust capability to handle subtle compositional distinctions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Analysis

- **Impact of Training Stages (Table 3):** Both stages are crucial. Stage 1 provides substantial initial gains (15%-24.9% across tasks). Stage 2 further improves performance (14%-38.5% across tasks), especially on MMEB, due to enhanced instruction-following. The combination of both stages yields optimal performance.
- **Hard Negative Effectiveness (Figure 5):** Analysis of training loss and gradient norms shows that hard negatives sustain elevated training losses and significantly higher gradient magnitudes compared to easy or random negatives. This empirically confirms that hard negatives provide critical learning signals for improving discriminative power.
- **False Negative Filtering Threshold (β) (Table 4):** An optimal `β=0.1` ensures effective false negative removal (22.9% of samples) without inadvertently filtering out valuable hard negatives. Deviations from this optimal value (either too low or too high) lead to performance degradation.
- **Number of Hard Negatives (k) (Table 5):** Setting `k=8` is found to be optimal. While increasing `k` initially improves performance by providing more diverse hard negatives, further increases (`k=16, 32, 64`) can introduce easier negatives, causing the model to lose focus and slightly reduce performance.
- **Output Distribution Visualization (Figure 6, 7):** Visualizing the top-k next predicted tokens demonstrates that UniME's embeddings evolve from abstract (base model) to more concrete, semantically aligned, and diverse distributions after training, indicating superior discriminative capability and accurate semantic content expression.

In conclusion, UniME achieves consistent and significant performance improvements across diverse multimodal retrieval, classification, VQA, and grounding tasks. Its strength lies in effectively leveraging MLLMs' capabilities, addressing prior limitations through a principled two-stage approach, and demonstrating robust performance, particularly in compositional and long-caption scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniME framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal representation learning, with broad implications for both research and practical applications. Its core significance lies in effectively harnessing the power of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to produce universal, highly discriminative embeddings.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Breaking Modality Barriers:** UniME directly addresses the long-standing limitations of conventional multimodal models like CLIP, specifically concerning text token truncation, isolated modality processing, and deficient compositional understanding. By leveraging the advanced language understanding and integrated architecture of MLLMs, UniME enables handling of long textual inputs and complex instruction-following tasks, which were previously bottlenecks.

2. **Unlocking MLLM Potential for Embeddings:** While MLLMs have excelled in generative and reasoning tasks, their direct application for learning transferable, discriminative embeddings was constrained by their autoregressive objective. UniME provides a structured and effective framework to transform these powerful MLLMs into robust universal embedding models, expanding their utility beyond generation.

3. **Methodological Innovations:**
 - **Textual Discriminative Knowledge Distillation:** This novel stage efficiently transfers high-quality discriminative knowledge from powerful LLM-based teacher models to the MLLM's language component. This is a clever way to circumvent the limitations of MLLM's causal attention mask for embedding tasks, providing a strong initial foundation.
 - **Hard Negative Enhanced Instruction Tuning:** This stage is crucial for fine-grained discriminative learning. By actively mitigating false negatives and strategically sampling truly challenging hard negatives, UniME forces the model to learn more subtle distinctions, leading to superior performance in difficult retrieval and compositional tasks. The empirical evidence of higher gradient norms for hard negatives underscores its importance.

4. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The consistent performance improvements demonstrated across the MMEB benchmark, short and long caption retrieval, and especially compositional retrieval (SugarCrepe), indicate UniME's effectiveness and robustness. Achieving top performance on MMEB, a benchmark covering 36 datasets across 4 meta-tasks, establishes UniME as a leading solution for universal multimodal embedding.

**Potential Impact:**

1. **Enhanced Multimodal Applications:** UniME's universal and discriminative embeddings can significantly boost the performance of various downstream applications:
 - **Retrieval Systems:** Improve the accuracy and relevance of image-text retrieval, especially for complex queries or long descriptions, and enable more sophisticated compositional retrieval.
 - **Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG):** Provide more precise and contextually rich retrieved documents/images, leading to higher quality and more accurate generative AI outputs.
 - **Visual Question Answering (VQA) and Visual Grounding:** Improve the model's ability to understand specific instructions and ground visual concepts accurately.
 - **Multimodal Search:** Enable more natural and nuanced search experiences across different data types.

2. **Foundation for Future Research:** UniME's success offers valuable insights into effective strategies for multimodal representation learning. It encourages further exploration into:
 - More sophisticated knowledge distillation techniques for MLLMs.
 - Advanced hard negative mining and sampling strategies in multimodal contexts.
 - Designing MLLM architectures that inherently support both generative and discriminative tasks more seamlessly.
 - Investigating the transferability of UniME to even broader ranges of modalities (e.g., video, audio).

3. **Computational Efficiency:** The use of QLoRA for parameter-efficient fine-tuning and GradCache for memory optimization makes the training of UniME feasible with reasonable computational resources, fostering broader adoption and experimentation within the research community. The release of code and project page further supports this.

In essence, UniME pushes the boundaries of multimodal representation learning by effectively leveraging the latest advancements in LLMs. By providing highly discriminative and universal embeddings, it not only improves current multimodal AI applications but also lays a strong foundation for future innovations in general-purpose AI assistants and sophisticated multimodal understanding systems.
