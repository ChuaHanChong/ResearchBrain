# Symbolic Regression with Multimodal Large Language Models and Kolmogorov Arnold Networks

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel symbolic regression framework combines multimodal large language models with Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks to infer mathematical expressions from data plots, achieving higher success rates than Mathematica's FindFormula on randomly generated functions while demonstrating effectiveness with both commercial and open-source LLMs.

## Problem

- Traditional symbolic regression methods often require manual specification of basis functions and struggle with complex expressions
- Existing approaches lack the human-like intuition for inferring functional forms from visual data representations

## Method

- **LLM-LEx** ==algorithm== uses vision-capable LLMs to propose ansatze based on function plots
- **KAN-LEx** ==extension== leverages ==Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks== to handle multivariate functions through ==decomposition into univariate components==

## Results

- Higher success rate than Mathematica's FindFormula in identifying exact expressions for random functions
- Demonstrated resilience to moderate noise levels while maintaining accuracy
- Successfully extended to multivariate functions through KAN decomposition
- Achieved comparable performance using open-source models, making the approach widely accessible

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs can effectively emulate human intuition in suggesting appropriate functional forms from visual data
> - Combining genetic algorithms with LLM-guided proposals enables efficient exploration of the solution space
> - Open-source models like codestral can perform comparably to GPT-4 for this task

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Symbolic Regression:** The paper directly addresses the field of symbolic regression, a long-standing problem in machine learning and applied mathematics. Symbolic regression aims to find mathematical expressions that best fit a given dataset, a task that is fundamentally challenging due to the vast search space of possible functions.
- **Large Language Models (LLMs):** The paper integrates vision-capable LLMs into the symbolic regression process. This is part of a growing trend of using LLMs for scientific tasks, including code generation, mathematical reasoning, and scientific discovery. Prior work has explored using LLMs in symbolic regression, either by relying on raw data or using the LLM as an assistant within a more traditional symbolic regression process. This paper differentiates itself by *exclusively* using images as input, without any raw data.
- **Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks (KANs):** The use of KANs, inspired by the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem, connects the paper to research on neural networks with potentially better approximation properties and interpretability. By combining KANs with LLMs, the paper explores a hybrid approach that leverages the strengths of both methods.
- **Funsearch:** The paper draws inspiration from Google DeepMind's Funsearch, which uses a genetic algorithm to guide LLMs in solving mathematical problems. This places the work within the context of using LLMs for automated mathematical discovery.
- **Comparison to Existing Methods:** The paper explicitly compares its method to traditional symbolic regression techniques, such as Mathematica's `FindFormula` function, highlighting the advantages and disadvantages of the proposed approach. This situates the work within the existing landscape of symbolic regression algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Emulating Human Intuition:** The primary objective is to develop a symbolic regression method that emulates the human ability to infer functional forms from visual representations of data. The authors observe that humans often propose simpler and more accurate ansatze than traditional symbolic regression algorithms.
- **Leveraging LLMs:** The paper aims to explore whether large multimodal language models (LLMs) can capture this human-like intuition and guide the symbolic regression process.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Traditional Methods:** The authors seek to address the limitations of traditional symbolic regression methods, which often require the user to specify a set of basis functions and can struggle with complex functions.
- **Extending to Multivariate Functions:** The paper aims to extend the LLM-based symbolic regression approach to multivariate functions by leveraging the Kolmogorov-Arnold representation theorem and KANs.
- **Demonstrating Viability and Simplicity:** The authors state that their aim is not necessarily to outperform state-of-the-art symbolic regression methods, but rather to demonstrate that this new approach is viable and surprisingly successful given its relatively simple implementation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **LLM-LEx Algorithm:**
 - The core of the method is the LLM-LEx algorithm, which uses a vision-capable LLM to propose ansatze for univariate functions based on their plots.
 - The LLM is prompted with an image of the function and a basic initial ansatz.
 - The LLM returns an improved ansatz in the form of a Python lambda function.
 - The parameters of the ansatz are fitted using numerical optimization routines (SciPy).
 - A genetic algorithm is used to evolve a population of proposed functions.
 - The scoring metric emphasizes capturing the overall shape of the function.
 - The prompts for the genetic algorithm are constructed using two randomly selected functions from the previous generation, weighted by their scores.
- **KAN-LEx for Multivariate Functions:**
 - A Kolmogorov-Arnold Network (KAN) is trained on the multivariate data.
 - LLM-LEx is used to propose symbolic expressions for the univariate functions on each edge of the KAN.
 - The combined expression is simplified using sympy and further processed with an LLM.
 - The free parameters of the simplified expression are refitted using scipy.
- **Benchmarking:**
 - The method is benchmarked against Mathematica's `FindFormula` function on a set of randomly generated functions.
 - The performance is evaluated based on the accuracy of the identified expressions and the resulting scores.
 - The method is tested with open-source language models to evaluate its feasibility with limited computational resources.
 - The method is tested with noisy data to assess its robustness.
- **Prompt Engineering:**
 - The authors explore the use of prompt engineering to influence the LLM's choice of functions, including special functions from `scipy.special`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Viability of LLM-Based Symbolic Regression:** The paper demonstrates that LLMs can be effectively used for symbolic regression, particularly for univariate functions.
- **Superiority to Traditional Methods in Some Cases:** LLM-LEx is shown to find the exact expression more frequently than Mathematica's `FindFormula` function for a set of randomly generated functions.
- **Human-Like Intuition:** The LLM exhibits a degree of human-like intuition in suggesting appropriate functional forms based on visual representations of data.
- **Effectiveness of KAN-LEx for Multivariate Functions:** The KAN-LEx approach is shown to be effective for extending the LLM-based symbolic regression to multivariate functions.
- **Feasibility with Open-Source Models:** The paper demonstrates that LLM-LEx can be used with open-source language models, making it accessible to researchers with limited computational resources. `codestral` performs comparably to `gpt-4o` in some cases.
- **Resilience to Noise:** The method exhibits a degree of resilience to moderate noise, but can overfit if the noise is too large.
- **Importance of Prompt Engineering:** The paper highlights the importance of prompt engineering in influencing the LLM's choice of functions, including special functions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **New Approach to Symbolic Regression:** The paper presents a novel approach to symbolic regression that leverages the power of large language models and Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks.
- **Potential for Automated Scientific Discovery:** The method has the potential to automate the process of scientific discovery by identifying mathematical expressions that describe experimental data.
- **Improved Interpretability:** The LLM-based approach can lead to more interpretable and insightful expressions compared to traditional symbolic regression methods.
- **Accessibility:** The use of open-source language models makes the method accessible to a wider range of researchers.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies several promising directions for future research, including exploring more sophisticated genetic algorithms, training purpose-built vision transformers, and fine-tuning models on symbolic regression data.
- **Educational Tool:** The `LLM-LEx` package can also serve as an educational tool, allowing students to explore the relationship between visual data and mathematical expressions.

**Overall Assessment:**

This paper presents a significant contribution to the field of symbolic regression by demonstrating the viability and potential of using large language models and Kolmogorov-Arnold Networks. The proposed method offers a novel approach to automated scientific discovery and has the potential to improve the interpretability of mathematical models. The paper is well-written, clearly explains the methodology, and provides thorough benchmarking results. The identification of future research directions further enhances the value of this work. The provision of the `LLM-LEx` package on GitHub makes this research accessible and encourages further exploration and development of the proposed techniques.
