# Unified Latents (UL): How to train your latents

> [!summary] Summary
> Google DeepMind Amsterdam's Unified Latents (UL) framework introduces a principled method for learning optimal latent representations for diffusion models. This framework achieves state-of-the-art generation quality and pre-training efficiency on datasets like ImageNet-512 and Kinetics-600, while offering interpretable control over latent information content.

## Problem

- Efficiently scaling diffusion models to high resolutions is challenging without effective latent representations.
- Current latent learning strategies often lack principled regularization, making it difficult to control the information content (bitrate) of the latents.
- A fundamental trade-off exists between the ease of modeling latent representations for generation and their ability to retain high-frequency details for accurate reconstruction.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Unified Latents (UL) framework== that jointly trains an encoder, a diffusion prior, and a diffusion decoder in a two-stage process.
- Employs a fixed amount of Gaussian noise when encoding latents and aligns the diffusion prior with this minimum noise level using an ==unweighted ELBO loss==.
- Utilizes a reweighted ELBO loss with a loss factor for the diffusion decoder, providing explicit and interpretable control over the ==latent bitrate==.

## Results

- Achieves a state-of-the-art FID of **1.4** on ImageNet-512 and an FVD of **1.3** on Kinetics-600 (medium model), demonstrating superior pre-training efficiency.
- Provides interpretable control over latent information content, allowing for optimization of either generation quality (e.g., lower bitrate for smaller models) or reconstruction fidelity (e.g., higher bitrate for larger models).
- Demonstrates robust performance and scalability on large text-to-image datasets, yielding better perceptual quality (gFID **4.1**) compared to baselines while maintaining high reconstruction PSNR.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Co-training a diffusion prior with an unweighted ELBO loss provides principled regularization for latents, preventing information collapse and improving modelability.
> - Encoding latents with a fixed amount of Gaussian noise and aligning the prior's minimum noise level simplifies the VAE's KL term and enhances training stability.
> - Explicit hyperparameters (loss factor and sigmoid bias) in the decoder loss enable interpretable control over the latent bitrate, optimizing the trade-off between reconstruction and generation quality.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge in the field of **generative AI**, particularly within the context of **diffusion models** and their application to high-resolution image and video synthesis. Diffusion models have achieved remarkable success, but scaling them to higher resolutions efficiently often necessitates the use of **latent representations**. The paper "Unified Latents" focuses on optimizing the learning of these latent representations.

Historically, learning effective latent spaces has been a central theme in generative modeling. Early approaches like **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)** (e.g., Kingma & Welling, 2013) aimed to learn compressed, meaningful representations, often regularized by a simple Gaussian prior. With the advent of **Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs)** (Rombach et al., 2022), such as Stable Diffusion, the strategy shifted to using VAE-style autoencoders to compress data into a lower-dimensional latent space, where diffusion processes could operate more efficiently. However, the original LDM framework uses a manually tuned KL penalty and often relies on GAN-trained autoencoders, lacking a principled, interpretable way to control the information content of the latents.

This paper identifies a crucial **trade-off**: easier-to-learn latents (lower information density) often lead to better generation performance, but at the cost of losing high-frequency details and thus worse reconstruction quality (lower PSNR). Conversely, high-information latents enable better reconstruction but are harder for the subsequent diffusion model to learn. Previous attempts to obtain semantic representations, such as those from DINO (Caron et al., 2021) or heavily regularized autoencoders, often fall into the latter category, showing impressive FID scores but poor PSNRs due to information loss.

"Unified Latents" directly aims to bridge this gap by introducing a systematic framework for navigating this trade-off. It builds upon and extends several lines of prior work:
- **Diffusion Decoders**: Works like DiffuseVAE (Pandey et al., 2022), SWYCC (Birodkar et al., 2024), ε-VAE (Zhao et al., 2025), and DiVAE (Shi et al., 2022) have explored using diffusion models as decoders, but often rely on channel bottlenecks for regularization rather than learned priors.
- **Diffusion Priors**: LSGM (Vahdat et al., 2021) jointly trains a diffusion prior within a VAE framework, but faced instability issues with its encoder entropy term.
- **Combined Diffusion Decoder and Prior**: DiffAE (Preechakul et al., 2022) also uses diffusion for both, but its latent typically comes from a pre-trained, "semantically meaningful" encoder rather than being optimized for generation quality.

Unified Latents distinguishes itself by jointly training the encoder, a diffusion prior, and a diffusion decoder from scratch, with an explicit focus on maximizing generation efficiency and providing interpretable control over the latent bitrate. It addresses the "how to train your latents" question by proposing a principled, diffusion-native approach to regularization, directly improving upon the ad-hoc latent regularization of prior LDM-style models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and interpretable framework, dubbed **Unified Latents (UL)**, for learning latent representations that are optimally suited for subsequent modeling by diffusion processes. This framework seeks to overcome the limitations of existing latent learning strategies in generative models.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Lack of Principled Latent Regularization**: Current Latent Diffusion Models (LDMs) often use a VAE-style KL penalty with a standard Gaussian prior. The weight of this KL term is typically set manually, making it challenging to reason about or control the precise information content (bitrate) of the latents. The authors aim to replace this with a more principled, diffusion-native regularization.
- **Trade-off between Information Content and Modelability**: There is an inherent conflict between the amount of information retained in a latent representation (which affects reconstruction quality, e.g., PSNR) and the ease with which a downstream diffusion model can learn to generate from that latent space (which affects generation quality, e.g., FID). The goal is to systematically navigate and control this trade-off.
- **Loss of High-Frequency Details**: While some approaches yield good FID by focusing on semantic representations, they often sacrifice high-frequency details, leading to poorer reconstruction (PSNR) and artifacts in generated images. UL aims to achieve high FID while maintaining strong reconstruction quality.
- **Improve Pre-training Efficiency**: A significant motivation is to enhance the efficiency of pre-training large-scale generative models. By learning better latents, the subsequent "base model" training in the latent space should require fewer computational resources (FLOPs) to achieve a given level of generation quality.
- **Interpretable Control**: To provide researchers and practitioners with simple, interpretable hyperparameters that allow for explicit control over the latent bitrate, thereby directly influencing the reconstruction-modeling trade-off.

In essence, the paper aims to answer the fundamental question: "How should latents be regularized when they will subsequently be modeled by a diffusion model?" Their proposed answer is through co-training a diffusion prior on them, leading to latents that are simultaneously encoded, regularized, and modeled using a unified diffusion framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Unified Latents (UL) proposes a novel framework for learning latent representations by co-training an encoder, a diffusion prior, and a diffusion decoder. The core methodology rests on three key ideas that ensure a principled regularization of the latent space.

#### 3.1. The Three Key Ideas of Unified Latents

1. **Encode Latents with a Fixed Amount of Gaussian Noise**:
 - Unlike traditional VAEs where the encoder predicts a mean and variance, UL's encoder `E_θ` deterministically outputs a `z_clean` latent.
 - This `z_clean` is then *forward-noised* to a fixed, minimum noise level `t=0` to produce `z_0`. This means `z_0` is a slightly noisy version of `z_clean`, specifically `p(z_0 | z_clean) = N(α_0 z_clean, σ_0^2 I)`.
 - This explicit linkage of encoder noise to the prior's minimum noise level (defined by `λ(0) = 5`, implying `σ_0 ≈ 0.08`) is crucial. It simplifies the VAE's KL term and integrates the encoder's output distribution directly into the diffusion prior's process, enhancing training stability.

2. **Align the Prior Diffusion Model with the Minimum Noise Level**:
 - The prior `P_θ` is a diffusion model that learns to model the distribution of `z_0`. It's designed to model the path from pure Gaussian noise `z_1` down to the slightly noisy latent `z_0`.
 - For the prior's KL term (which regularizes the latent), an **unweighted ELBO loss** (`w(λ_z(t)) = 1`) is used. This is critical because it ensures that all noise levels in the latent space are equally penalized. If low noise levels were discounted, the encoder could "abuse" this by encoding information in imperceptible high-frequency details that the prior wouldn't strongly penalize, leading to unmodelable latents.

3. **Use a Reweighted ELBO Loss (Sigmoid Weighting) for the Decoder**:
 - The decoder `D_θ` is also a diffusion model, but it operates in the image space, reconstructing the original image `x` from `x_t` while being *conditioned* on the slightly noisy latent `z_0`.
 - In contrast to the prior, the decoder loss uses a **reweighted ELBO** with a sigmoid weighting (`w_x(λ_x(t)) = sigmoid(λ_x(t) - b)`). This allows the decoder to selectively down-weight the loss contributions of imperceptible high-frequency details, which is often beneficial for image quality.
 - Additionally, a **loss factor (c_lf)** is applied to up-weigh the decoder loss (or equivalently, down-weight the KL term). This actively encourages the decoder to reconstruct more information, counteracting the "posterior collapse" phenomenon where a powerful decoder might ignore the latent.

#### 3.2. Training Procedure: Two Stages

The UL framework employs a two-stage training process:

1. **Stage 1: Autoencoder Training**:
 - The encoder `E_θ`, diffusion prior `P_θ`, and diffusion decoder `D_θ` are jointly trained.
 - The overall loss `L(θ)` is the sum of the prior loss `L_z(θ)` (unweighted ELBO for `z_clean` vs. `z_0` through `z_t`) and the decoder loss `L_x(θ)` (reweighted ELBO for `x` conditioned on `z_0`, with the loss factor).
 - This stage learns the unified latent space, ensuring it is both reconstructible and regularized by the diffusion prior. The hyperparameters `c_lf` (loss factor) and `b` (sigmoid bias) in the decoder loss are crucial here for controlling the latent bitrate.

2. **Stage 2: Base Model Training**:
 - After Stage 1, the encoder and decoder are **frozen**.
 - A *new* diffusion model, referred to as the "base model," is trained exclusively on the `z_0` latents produced by the frozen encoder.
 - This base model typically uses a sigmoid-weighted ELBO loss, similar to standard Latent Diffusion Models, but with its maximum log-SNR (`λ_max`) fixed to the `λ(0)` used to generate `z_0` in Stage 1.
 - This stage is necessary because the Stage 1 prior, trained with an unweighted ELBO to ensure principled regularization, does not necessarily produce high-quality samples. The Stage 2 base model, using a more generation-friendly weighting, is optimized for sample quality.

#### 3.3. Model Architectures

- **Encoder**: A ResNet-based model with 2x2 patching for efficiency.
- **Decoder**: A UVit model (Hoogeboom et al., 2024), also using 2x2 patching and a transformer in the middle.
- **Prior Model**: A single-level Vision Transformer (ViT).
- **Base Model (Stage 2)**: A two-stage ViT, which can be larger than the prior model since it only uses the frozen encoder.

This structured approach allows UL to learn latents with controlled information content, facilitating stable training and superior performance for generative tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on ImageNet-512 and Kinetics-600, along with studies on text-to-image and video generation, demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the Unified Latents (UL) framework.

#### 4.1. Superior Pre-training Efficiency and Generation Quality

- **ImageNet-512**: UL achieves a competitive FID (Fréchet Inception Distance) of **1.4**, outperforming various baselines including DiT-XL/2, EDM2, SiD2, RAE, and even models trained on Stable Diffusion latents (small SD, medium SD) using the same underlying architecture (2-level ViT). Critically, UL shows superior performance in terms of **training cost (zettaflops) versus generation quality (FID)**, establishing itself as the most efficient pre-training approach on this dataset.
- **Kinetics-600 (Video Generation)**: UL sets a new state-of-the-art FVD (Fréchet Video Distance) of **1.3** for the medium model, significantly outperforming MAGVIT-v2, W.A.L.T., RIN, and Video Diffusion models. This underscores its applicability and efficiency beyond static images. The small UL model already achieves 1.7 FVD, demonstrating strong performance even at a smaller scale.
- **High Reconstruction Quality**: Alongside excellent generation metrics, UL maintains high PSNR (Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio), which is crucial for retaining high-frequency details. This contrasts with other semantic latent approaches that often trade PSNR for FID. For example, a loss factor (LF) of 1.5 yields 27.6 PSNR and 0.47 rFID (reconstruction FID).

#### 4.2. Interpretable Control over Latent Bitrate

- **Loss Factor and Sigmoid Bias**: The study confirms that the decoder's loss factor (`c_lf`) and sigmoid bias (`b`) are effective hyperparameters for controlling the latent information content (bits per dimension, bpd).
- **Trade-off Management**:
 - Increasing the loss factor leads to improved reconstruction metrics (lower rFID, higher PSNR) due to higher bitrate in the latents.
 - For **smaller base models**, lower bitrates (e.g., LF=1.3, 0.035 bpd) are optimal for generation (gFID=1.42), even if reconstruction is slightly worse. This suggests that smaller models cannot effectively utilize very high-information latents.
 - **Larger base models** are less sensitive to latent bitrates and can achieve even better gFID with higher bitrates (e.g., LF=1.5, 0.059 bpd yields gFID=1.31, which is better than gFID=1.37 for LF=1.3), as their increased capacity allows them to model more informative latents effectively.

#### 4.3. Latent Shape Insensitivity

- **Channel Count**: UL demonstrates insensitivity to the number of latent channels (from 16 to 64), provided enough channels are available for good reconstruction quality (e.g., 4 and 8 channels were insufficient for reconstruction). This implies flexibility in latent dimensionality design.
- **Spatial Downsampling**: Varying spatial downsampling (8x, 16x, 32x) with fixed channels (32) showed similar rFID for 8x and 16x. The 16x spatial downsampling (32x32 latents from 512x512 images) was found to be slightly easier to model for the decoder, resulting in lower gFID.

#### 4.4. Text-to-Image Generation (Large-Scale Datasets)

- On internal text-to-image datasets, UL achieves superior perceptual quality (gFID of 4.1 with LF=1.5) compared to pixel diffusion (5.0 FID) and Stable Diffusion latents (6.8 FID), while maintaining comparable text alignment (Clip score). This highlights its scalability and practical advantages.
- The trend of lower latent bitrate leading to better gFID for smaller models is also observed in text-to-image settings.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of Diffusion Prior**: Replacing the active diffusion prior with a stop-gradient and a weak KL to N(0,I significantly harms generation quality (gFID of 7.80 vs. 1.54 for baseline), confirming the critical role of the diffusion prior in regularizing the latents.
- **Noisy Latents (Fixed Noise Level)**: Using a very small, fixed amount of noise (high `λ_z(0) = 10`) for `z_0` renders the prior unable to accurately model the bitrate, leading to extremely poor reconstruction (rFID 28.27) and an inability to train a useful base model. This validates the design choice of using a moderate, fixed noise level (`λ_z(0) = 5`).
- **Learned Variance in Encoder**: Allowing the encoder to predict a mean and variance (like in traditional VAEs) leads to instability and worse gFID (1.81 vs. 1.54 for baseline), demonstrating the benefit of the deterministic encoder with fixed added noise.
- **Two-stage vs. Single-stage Training**: The attempts at single-stage training did not yield comparable performance (FID of about 4 after extensive training), affirming the current two-stage approach as more effective for the reported SOTA results.

In summary, UL provides a robust, efficient, and interpretable framework for learning latent representations. It achieves state-of-the-art generation and reconstruction quality while offering explicit control over the fundamental trade-off between latent information content and modelability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Unified Latents (UL) framework represents a significant advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly for diffusion models. Its potential impact spans both theoretical understanding and practical applications:

#### 5.1. Principled and Interpretable Latent Design

- UL provides a novel, principled approach to regularization for latent spaces in diffusion models. By co-training a diffusion prior and linking encoder noise to the prior's minimum noise level, it establishes a tight, interpretable upper bound on the latent bitrate. This moves beyond ad-hoc hyperparameter tuning (like the KL weight in traditional LDMs) and offers a more systematic way to control the information content of the latent space.
- The explicit control via simple hyperparameters (loss factor and sigmoid bias) is a major contribution, allowing researchers to precisely navigate the critical trade-off between reconstruction fidelity and generation modelability.

#### 5.2. Enhanced Pre-training Efficiency and Scalability

- The demonstrated superior pre-training efficiency (lower FLOPs for comparable generation quality) on ImageNet-512 and Kinetics-600 is highly impactful. In an era where foundation models consume vast computational resources, methods that reduce training costs are invaluable. This efficiency makes high-fidelity generative models more accessible and accelerates research and development cycles.
- The framework's ability to scale effectively to large text-to-image datasets and achieve state-of-the-art video generation (FVD 1.3 on Kinetics-600) underscores its robustness for real-world applications.

#### 5.3. High-Fidelity and Detail-Preserving Generation

- By achieving both competitive FID/FVD scores (perceptual quality) and high PSNR (reconstruction fidelity), UL addresses a key limitation of many previous latent approaches that often sacrificed high-frequency details. This means UL-trained models can generate outputs that are not only semantically plausible but also rich in fine-grained visual information.

#### 5.4. Broader Applicability and Future Research Directions

- While primarily demonstrated on images and video, the core principles of jointly regularizing and decoding latents with diffusion models are likely applicable to other data modalities (e.g., audio, 3D data, or even discrete data like text if a suitable diffusion decoder is designed). This opens doors for more unified generative model architectures across domains.
- The work lays the groundwork for future research, such as establishing scaling laws for Unified Latents to predict optimal bitrate given a training budget, or further exploring single-stage training approaches.

#### 5.5. Limitations and Considerations

- **Sampling Cost**: A noted limitation is that diffusion decoders are an order of magnitude more expensive to sample from than GAN-based decoders (like those in original Stable Diffusion). This means that without an additional distillation step for the decoder, UL's end-to-end inference might be slower. This presents a practical challenge for deployment where inference speed is critical.
- **Autoencoder Training Data**: Comparisons with other models can be complicated by differences in autoencoder training data (e.g., ImageNet vs. large web datasets).
- **Comparison with GAN Decoders**: The paper also raises a question about whether the mode-collapsing nature of GAN training might offer certain advantages (e.g., producing "better-looking" images or rFID scores) that a diffusion decoder, designed to model distributions, might not inherently share.

Despite these limitations, Unified Latents offers a compelling and robust solution to a fundamental problem in generative AI. Its principled approach to latent space design, combined with superior performance and efficiency, positions it as an important contribution that will likely influence the development of future high-performance generative models.
