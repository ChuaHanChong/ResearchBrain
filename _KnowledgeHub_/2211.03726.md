# TAP-Vid: A Benchmark for Tracking Any Point in a Video

> [!summary] Summary
> TAP-Vid introduces Tracking Any Point (TAP), the task of estimating long-term trajectories and occlusion for arbitrary physical surface points in video. It provides a benchmark of real (Kinetics, DAVIS) and synthetic (Kubric, RGB-Stacking) videos with precise point tracks, built via a novel flow-assisted crowdsourcing pipeline, plus a baseline model TAP-Net that outperforms all prior methods.

## Problem

- Box, segment, and optical-flow trackers lose deformation/rotation detail or only compare frame pairs, lacking long-term occlusion-aware correspondence for generic surfaces.
- Structure-from-motion keypoint matching assumes rigid scenes and typically ignores occlusion, so it fails on deformable, weakly-textured objects common in real video.
- No dataset or benchmark existed for long-term arbitrary point tracking on real-world footage, since exhaustive manual annotation of non-linear object and camera motion is prohibitively slow.

## Method

- Semi-automatic crowdsourced annotation pipeline where a ==track-assist algorithm== uses ==RAFT optical flow== plus a ==Dijkstra shortest-path formulation== to interpolate sparse annotator clicks into dense, drift-corrected point tracks.
- **TAP-Vid** benchmark combining two real datasets (Kinetics, DAVIS) with human-annotated tracks and two synthetic datasets (Kubric, RGB-Stacking) with simulator ground truth, plus three metrics: Occlusion Accuracy, position accuracy, and Average Jaccard.
- **TAP-Net**, an end-to-end model that builds a per-query ==cost volume== from a ==TSM-ResNet== feature grid, then regresses position via ==spatial softmax soft-argmax== and classifies occlusion via a separate head, trained with ==Huber and cross-entropy loss==.

## Results

- TAP-Net beats COTR, RAFT, Kubric-VFS-Like, and concurrent PIPs on 3 of 4 datasets, reaching **46.6** Average Jaccard on Kinetics and **65.4** on Kubric versus PIPs's **35.1** and **59.1**.
- Annotation quality validated on Kubric ground truth: over **99%** of points accurate within 8 pixels, **96%** within 4 pixels, with flow assistance cutting annotation time **28%** (50 to 36 minutes per 2-second video).
- Human inter-rater agreement reached **95.5%** on occlusion and **92.5%** on location at a 4-pixel threshold, with only **1.46 pixel** average difference between two independent annotators at 256x256 resolution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training on diverse synthetic data (Kubric) transfers better to real-world evaluation than training on narrow, class-specific benchmarks like JHMDB, which actually harms general tracking performance.
> - Occlusion-aware, cost-volume-based end-to-end learning outperforms hand-adapted optical-flow or SFM baselines because it jointly models matching and occlusion rather than bolting occlusion detection onto flow.
> - Assistive algorithms (flow-based interpolation) can make exhaustive fine-grained human annotation tractable, a pattern generalizable to other dense, temporally-continuous labeling tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper introduces and formalizes the problem of "Tracking Any Point (TAP)" in a video, which involves perceiving how generic physical points on surfaces deform and move over longer video clips, including occlusions. This work addresses a critical gap in the existing computer vision landscape, which, despite significant advancements in motion estimation, has largely overlooked this specific problem due to its inherent complexity.

Prior research in motion understanding from video can be broadly categorized, each with distinct limitations:
- **Bounding Box and Segment Tracking:** These methods, while effective for identifying and following objects or regions, provide limited information about the detailed deformation, rotation, or subtle movements of surfaces within those boundaries. They operate at a coarse granularity, insufficient for tasks requiring fine-grained spatial control.
- **Optical Flow:** Optical flow algorithms estimate dense motion between *pairs* of frames. While capable of tracking "any point," they struggle with long-term consistency, accumulation of errors, and effective occlusion estimation over extended video sequences. Their frame-by-frame nature is not designed for continuous, long-horizon tracking.
- **Structure from Motion (SFM) and Keypoint Matching:** SFM techniques typically rely on sparse interest points (keypoints) and are primarily designed for rigid scenes to reconstruct 3D structures. They often ignore occlusion and are brittle when faced with deformable objects or weakly textured surfaces, which are common in real-world videos.
- **Semantic Keypoint Tracking:** This approach involves hand-defining and tracking specific, semantically meaningful keypoints (e.g., human joints, facial landmarks) for a limited set of object categories. While highly useful for their specific domains, these methods are not scalable to arbitrary objects and surfaces, thus failing to provide a generic motion understanding capability.

TAP-Vid distinguishes itself by offering a more fundamental and generic approach. It extends the concept of optical flow to longer timeframes with explicit occlusion estimation and broadens SFM-style keypoint matching to handle non-rigid objects and diverse real-world video conditions. The paper's formulation addresses the need for a general-purpose point tracking capability that is agnostic to object class or semantic meaning, focusing instead on physical surface points. This shift is crucial for applications that require a deep understanding of object physics, deformation, and interaction, such as robotics or realistic 3D content creation. By providing the first dedicated benchmark for this problem, TAP-Vid aims to galvanize research in a previously under-studied but highly important area, bridging the gap between various specialized tracking paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the TAP-Vid project are multifaceted, aiming to advance the fundamental understanding of motion in videos:

1. **Formalize the "Tracking Any Point (TAP)" Problem:** The authors recognized the absence of a clear, standardized definition for tracking arbitrary physical points on surfaces across entire video clips, including explicit handling of occlusions. Their first objective was to formalize this problem, laying down a rigorous framework for input (video and query points at specific spatio-temporal locations) and desired output (trajectories of the same physical point over the entire video, along with a binary occlusion indicator for each frame).

2. **Create a Comprehensive Benchmark Dataset (TAP-Vid):** A major motivation was the lack of a suitable dataset for training and evaluating models capable of solving the TAP problem. Existing datasets were either too specialized (e.g., semantic keypoints), too short-term (e.g., optical flow), or focused on rigid objects (e.g., SFM). TAP-Vid aims to fill this void by providing:
 - **Real-world videos with accurate human annotations:** This component is crucial for evaluating performance in complex, diverse, and realistic scenarios where human perception provides reliable ground truth.
 - **Synthetic videos with perfect ground-truth tracks:** Synthetic data offers the advantage of perfect, unambiguous ground truth for difficult scenarios (e.g., textureless objects) and provides a scalable training source without the overhead and potential errors of human annotation.

3. **Develop an Efficient and Accurate Annotation Pipeline:** Annotating precise, long-term point tracks on arbitrary surfaces in real videos is an extremely labor-intensive and challenging task. A key objective was to design a novel, semi-automatic crowdsourced pipeline that leverages modern optical flow estimates to assist human annotators. This pipeline aimed to reduce the manual effort, compensate for short-term motion like camera shake, and allow annotators to focus on harder sections and precise corrections, thereby making the creation of a large-scale real-world benchmark feasible and accurate.

4. **Establish Strong Baselines and Evaluation Metrics:** To facilitate research and provide a starting point for the community, the authors aimed to:
 - **Propose clear evaluation metrics:** Metrics capable of jointly assessing both positional accuracy for visible points and the correctness of occlusion predictions were needed, as existing metrics often focus on one aspect or are unsuitable for arbitrary points.
 - **Develop a simple yet effective end-to-end model (TAP-Net):** This baseline serves to demonstrate the feasibility of the task and provide a strong initial performance benchmark against which future methods can be compared.
 - **Compare against adapted state-of-the-art methods:** Evaluating how existing approaches (e.g., optical flow, SFM, contrastive learning for correspondence) perform on the TAP task highlights their limitations and the unique challenges addressed by TAP-Vid.

The overarching motivation stems from the belief that generic motion understanding is fundamental for advanced scene understanding, inferring 3D shape, physical properties, and object interactions. This capability is particularly crucial for embodied agents, such as robots, which require precise spatial and temporal control over objects in complex environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of TAP-Vid is characterized by a multi-pronged approach involving problem formalization, novel dataset construction (both real and synthetic), a sophisticated human annotation pipeline, a robust evaluation framework, and the development of a strong baseline model.

#### 3.1. Problem Formalization: Tracking Any Point (TAP)

The problem is defined as taking a video clip and a set of query points (x, y, t coordinates) as input. For each query point, the algorithm must output its precise (x, y) pixel coordinates across all frames and a binary occlusion indicator for each frame (o_t), signifying whether the point is visible. The definition excludes transparent objects, liquids, or gases, focusing on unambiguous physical points on surfaces.

#### 3.2. TAP-Vid Dataset Construction

The benchmark comprises four datasets:
- **TAP-Vid-Kinetics:** 1,189 real-world videos (10s clips, 250 frames at 25 fps) from Kinetics-700, chosen for diverse human actions. Annotations are at ≥720p and downsampled to 256x256 for evaluation.
- **TAP-Vid-DAVIS:** 30 real-world videos from DAVIS 2017 validation set, known for challenging segment tracking. Annotated at 1080p for precision, evaluated at 256x256.
- **TAP-Vid-Kubric:** 38,325 training and 799 validation synthetic videos from MOVi-E (Kubric project), featuring physics simulations and raytraced rendering of diverse objects. Serves as the primary training data.
- **TAP-Vid-RGB-Stacking:** 50 synthetic videos from a simulated robotic stacking environment, challenging due to textureless, rotationally symmetric objects and frequent gripper occlusions.

#### 3.3. Real-World Dataset Construction Pipeline (Kinetics & DAVIS)

A novel semi-automatic crowdsourced annotation pipeline was developed to address the difficulty of labeling:
1. **Stage 1: Object Selection:** Annotators identify up to K salient, moving objects and draw bounding boxes around them every 30 frames, assigning text labels. This ensures diversity beyond just human-centric objects.
2. **Stage 2: Point Annotation:** For each selected object, annotators choose M points and annotate their locations every other frame. A **"track assist" algorithm** is central here: it uses RAFT optical flow to propagate points across frames. When annotators place a second point, the algorithm finds the optimal path between the two manual points by minimizing discrepancy with optical flow, effectively interpolating the track. This helps overcome drift and speeds up annotation, allowing annotators to correct sections rather than drawing every point. Occlusions are marked manually.
3. **Stage 3: Iterative Refinement:** After initial submission, annotated tracks undergo multiple rounds of verification and correction by different annotators to ensure high accuracy. This multi-stage human-in-the-loop process prioritizes quality. On average, a 10-second video with 30 points required 3.3 annotator hours.

#### 3.4. Synthetic Dataset Generation

For Kubric and RGB-Stacking, ground-truth point tracks are derived directly from the simulator:
- Query points in pixel space are back-projected to the object's local 3D coordinate frame.
- 3D locations in world coordinates are computed for all other frames using known object transformations.
- These 3D points are then projected back to the image plane.
- Occlusion is determined by comparing the reprojected point's depth with the simulator's depth map, marking as occluded if significantly behind.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Metrics

The paper defines three metrics to comprehensively evaluate point tracking performance:
1. **Occlusion Accuracy (OA):** Binary classification accuracy for predicting point visibility/occlusion.
2. **<δx:** Position accuracy for *visible* points, measuring the fraction of points within a given pixel threshold (δ) of ground truth. Averaged across thresholds (1, 2, 4, 8, 16 pixels) for <δx_avg.
3. **Average Jaccard (AJ):** A combined metric (Jaccard at δ, averaged over δ=1,2,4,8,16) that accounts for true positives (correctly tracked visible points), false positives (predicted visible but occluded or far), and false negatives (ground truth visible but predicted occluded or far).

#### 3.6. TAP-Net: A Baseline End-to-End Point Tracking Model

TAP-Net is a deep learning model inspired by cost volumes from optical flow:
- **Visual Backbone:** A TSM-ResNet-18 (with time shifting only in the first two layers) extracts dense feature grids (F) for all video frames.
- **Cost Volume Computation:** For a query point (x_q, y_q, t_q), a feature F_q is bilinearly interpolated from F. A cost volume C_q is computed by taking the dot product between F_q and F_ijt for all (i, j, t), indicating feature similarity.
- **Track Prediction:** For each frame, C_q is processed by two branches:
 - **Occlusion Branch:** Convolutions, average pooling, and linear layers output a single occlusion logit.
 - **Position Branch:** Convolutions, spatial softmax, and a soft argmax (weighted average of grid coordinates within a radius around the argmax) output the predicted (x,y) coordinates.
- **Loss Function:** A combined loss consisting of Huber loss for position (applied only to visible ground-truth points) and standard cross-entropy loss for occlusion prediction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling findings regarding the TAP-Vid dataset, human annotation quality, and the performance of the proposed TAP-Net model relative to existing baselines.

#### 4.1. Dataset Statistics and Characteristics

- **TAP-Vid-Kinetics** offers long tracks (most span 250 frames), with substantial motion (trajectory diameters up to 55% of image dimensions) and multiple occlusion segments (>70% trajectories have ≤2 segments). The dataset covers 532 unique Kinetics-700 action categories, ensuring broad diversity. Agglomerative clustering revealed that ≥85% of videos contained ≥5 distinct trajectory clusters, indicating a wide variety of point motions within videos.

#### 4.2. Human Annotation Quality Validation

- **On Synthetic Data (Kubric):** When annotators worked on Kubric videos with known ground truth, the optical flow-assisted pipeline demonstrated high accuracy. Over 99% of annotated points were within 8 pixels of ground truth, 96% within 4 pixels, and 87% within 2 pixels. Crucially, the track assist algorithm significantly improved annotation speed by 28% (from 50 to 36 minutes per 2-second video) while enhancing precision.
- **On Real Data (DAVIS Inter-Rater Agreement):** Independent human annotators showed strong agreement on DAVIS videos. They achieved 95.5% occlusion accuracy and 92.5% location accuracy with a 4-pixel threshold. The average difference between two independent human tracks on the same point was only 1.46 pixels at 256x256 resolution, validating the high quality and consistency of the annotations.

#### 4.3. TAP-Net Performance and Baseline Comparisons

- **Superiority of TAP-Net:** TAP-Net consistently outperformed all adapted prior baseline methods (COTR, Kubric-VFS-Like, RAFT) across all four TAP-Vid datasets (Kinetics, Kubric, DAVIS, RGB-Stacking) by a significant margin in terms of Average Jaccard (AJ), average position accuracy (<δx_avg), and Occlusion Accuracy (OA). For instance, on Kinetics, TAP-Net achieved 46.6 AJ compared to 40.5 for Kubric-VFS-Like and 34.5 for RAFT.
- **Comparison with PIPs:** TAP-Net showed competitive performance with the concurrent work PIPs, an algorithm also designed for video point tracking. TAP-Net performed better on TAP-Vid-Kinetics (46.6 AJ vs. 35.1 AJ) and TAP-Vid-RGB-Stacking (59.9 AJ vs. 37.3 AJ), while PIPs achieved slightly higher AJ on TAP-Vid-DAVIS (42.0 AJ vs. 38.4 AJ). This suggests that PIPs' local search and smoothness biases, while beneficial for continuous, smooth motion (DAVIS), struggle with the more rapid motion, longer occlusions, or video cuts present in Kinetics and the textureless, frequently occluded objects in RGB-Stacking.
- **Limitations of Baselines:** The paper effectively demonstrated the weaknesses of existing methods when applied to the TAP problem:
 - **COTR:** Struggled with deformable objects and lacked inherent occlusion detection.
 - **RAFT:** Suffered from error accumulation over long sequences and inability to handle occlusions.
 - **Kubric-VFS-Like:** Showed poor transfer from synthetic to real data, especially with its cycle-consistency-based occlusion detection.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablations on TAP-Net components showed that the chosen TSM-ResNet-18 backbone (with limited temporal shifting) and the soft argmax operation were important for optimal performance, contributing to better generalization and matching.

#### 4.5. Transferability and Generality (JHMDB Comparison)

- **TAP-Vid's Generality:** Fine-tuning TAP-Net (pretrained on Kubric) on TAP-Vid-Kinetics data led to an improvement in performance on the JHMDB human pose tracking benchmark (from 62.3 to 63.4 PCK@0.1 and 79.8 to 80.1 PCK@0.2). This demonstrates that training on generic, diverse point tracking data like TAP-Vid-Kinetics can yield benefits even for specific semantic keypoint tasks.
- **JHMDB's Limitations:** Conversely, fine-tuning TAP-Net on JHMDB *harmed* its performance on all TAP-Vid datasets, including <δx_avg (which ignores occlusion). This highlights JHMDB's limitations, such as tracking points inside objects or lacking occlusion information, making it unsuitable for learning general-purpose point tracking.

Overall, the findings strongly establish TAP-Vid as a challenging and valuable benchmark, and TAP-Net as a robust starting point, while underscoring the need for dedicated research into generic point tracking.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of TAP-Vid and the formalization of "Tracking Any Point (TAP)" represent a highly significant contribution to the field of computer vision, with the potential for broad and transformative impacts:

1. **Pioneering Benchmark:** TAP-Vid is the first comprehensive, large-scale benchmark specifically designed for long-term tracking of arbitrary physical points on surfaces in videos, including explicit occlusion handling. This fills a critical gap in existing datasets, which previously focused on object-level, semantic keypoint, or short-term optical flow tasks. It will likely become a standard benchmark for researchers working on fundamental motion understanding.

2. **Enabling Fundamental Research:** By providing a well-defined problem, a diverse dataset, and clear evaluation metrics, TAP-Vid will undoubtedly spur new research directions and methodologies in video analysis, tracking, and motion estimation. It shifts the focus from specialized object/semantic tracking to a more generalized understanding of how any visible point on any surface moves, which is a more fundamental visual primitive.

3. **Advancements in Robotics and Embodied AI:** The ability to precisely track arbitrary points on generic objects, even deformable or textureless ones, is paramount for intelligent agents operating in the real world. This work can directly impact:
 - **Robotic Manipulation:** Enabling robots to interact with novel objects, understand their physical properties, monitor deformations, and execute precise actions without requiring object-specific models or pre-defined semantic keypoints. It allows "semantic keypoint-based methods" to be applied to *any* object.
 - **3D Scene Understanding:** Improved point tracking is crucial for building more robust and dynamic 3D reconstructions, especially in scenes with deformable objects or non-rigid motion, which existing Structure from Motion (SfM) techniques often struggle with.

4. **Enhanced Video Content Understanding:** TAP-Vid contributes to a deeper understanding of video content, allowing for more detailed analysis of object interactions, material properties (e.g., how a fabric wrinkles), and fine-grained motion that is not captured by bounding boxes or segmentations.

5. **Methodological Innovation in Data Annotation:** The novel semi-automatic crowdsourced annotation pipeline, which effectively combines optical flow assistance with human refinement, is a significant methodological contribution in itself. It demonstrates a scalable and accurate approach to generate high-quality ground truth for complex, fine-grained tasks, which can be adapted for other challenging annotation problems.

6. **Addressing Data Biases and Ethics:** The authors acknowledge ethical considerations related to using public video sources (Kinetics) concerning Personally Identifiable Information (PII) and potential biases. Their commitment to following Kinetics' policies for video takedowns and their emphasis on using synthetic data for training actively mitigate these concerns, promoting fair and responsible AI development.

While limitations such as handling liquids or transparent objects remain, TAP-Vid lays crucial groundwork. Its broad applicability across fundamental computer vision challenges and practical domains like robotics positions it as a cornerstone for future research in motion understanding.
