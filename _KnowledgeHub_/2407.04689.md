# RAM: Retrieval-Based Affordance Transfer for Generalizable Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RAM, developed by researchers from the University of Southern California, Peking University, and Stanford University, introduces a retrieval-based framework that enables generalizable zero-shot robotic manipulation. It achieves this by transferring 2D affordances from diverse out-of-domain visual data, leading to an average success rate of 52.62% in simulation and 66.7% in real-world tasks, outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Traditional robot learning approaches, primarily imitation learning, suffer from data scarcity due to the high cost of collecting in-domain robot demonstrations.
- Robotic manipulation agents struggle to generalize effectively to novel objects, unseen environments, and different robotic platforms in a zero-shot manner.
- Leveraging the abundance of out-of-domain visual data (e.g., human-object interactions, internet videos) for robotic manipulation is challenging due to inherent domain shifts and the difficulty in extracting actionable 3D information.

## Method

- **RAM** constructs a large-scale, unified ==affordance memory== by semi-automatically extracting ==2D contact points and trajectories== from diverse sources, including robotic datasets, human-object interaction videos, and custom manual annotations.
- It employs a three-step ==hierarchical retrieval== process (task, semantic, and geometrical matching) to identify the most relevant 2D demonstration from memory given a target scene and language instruction.
- The system transfers the 2D affordance from the retrieved demonstration to the target image using ==VFM dense feature maps== and then lifts this 2D information into 3D executable robot actions by ==back-projecting== and estimating 3D post-contact directions using local point cloud normals.

## Results

- RAM achieved an average success rate of **52.62%** in simulation across **13** diverse tasks, outperforming modified baselines like Robo-ABC* (**41.54%**) and Where2Act (**20.50%**).
- In real-world experiments, RAM demonstrated robust performance with a **66.7%** success rate across **6** tasks, showcasing effective sim-to-real transfer and cross-domain capabilities.
- The framework enabled successful downstream applications including policy distillation (boosting performance by **+35.0% SR**), one-shot visual imitation from single examples (including cartoon images), and integration with LLMs/VLMs for open-set long-horizon tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified affordance representation can be extracted from heterogeneous data sources (robot, human, custom) to build a comprehensive memory for generalizable manipulation.
> - A hierarchical retrieval process, particularly geometrical matching using Visual Foundation Model (VFM) features, is crucial for robustly transferring 2D affordances across varying viewpoints and objects.
> - Separating 2D affordance transfer using VFMs from 3D lifting for execution allows the system to leverage powerful 2D visual representations while producing precise 3D robot actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning aims to develop intelligent agents capable of performing diverse manipulation tasks in complex, unstructured environments. A central and persistent challenge is the ability of these agents to generalize to novel objects, unseen environments, and even different robotic platforms (embodiments) without requiring extensive new training data. Traditional approaches, primarily imitation learning (IL) from expert demonstrations, face significant hurdles due to the prohibitive cost and labor involved in collecting sufficient in-domain data. This data scarcity severely limits the generalization capabilities of learned policies.

This paper positions itself directly at the intersection of addressing this data scarcity and enhancing generalization. It recognizes that while in-domain robotic data is scarce, there exists an "abundance of out-of-domain data" rich in actionable knowledge. This includes human-object interaction (HOI) datasets, internet-scale videos, AI-generated content, and even sketches. However, directly leveraging these heterogeneous and often noisy sources for robotic manipulation is challenging due to inherent domain shifts (e.g., human vs. robot hands, 2D videos vs. 3D robot environment).

RAM distinguishes itself from existing literature in several key ways:
- **Affordance Learning:** Previous works on visual affordance, such as point cloud-based methods (e.g., Where2Act), struggle with sim-to-real gaps due to depth sensor noise. Other methods learning from human videos (e.g., HOI-Forecast, VRB) are often hindered by limited data diversity, restricting their generalization. RAM's innovation lies in extracting a *unified affordance representation* from a *diverse array of data sources*, enabling broader generalization.
- **Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation:** Many contemporary zero-shot approaches (e.g., those leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) or Vision-Language Models (VLMs)) often rely on pre-programmed heuristic action primitives for low-level execution, limiting their flexibility. Others require collecting in-domain demonstrations for test-time training. RAM, in contrast, proposes a *training-free* approach that eliminates the need for heuristics or test-time data collection.
- **Learning from Demonstrations & Retrieval:** While retrieval-based imitation learning (IL) has gained traction, most methods focus on retrieving and replaying *in-domain* demonstrations, failing to generalize to "in-the-wild" scenarios without such data. More recent work like Robo-ABC attempts to retrieve and transfer 2D contact points using visual foundation models (VFMs) but is limited to HOI and tabletop grasping, lacking 3D manipulation capabilities. RAM advances this by not only making the most of diverse *out-of-domain* data sources but also by explicitly *lifting 2D affordances to 3D* for direct, generalizable robotic execution across a wide range of tasks and objects. The framework's inspiration from Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) in LLMs further places it within the cutting-edge paradigm of leveraging large datasets and foundation models for enhanced generalization.

In essence, RAM aims to bridge the gap between the vast, unstructured knowledge available in the visual world and the precise, executable actions required for robotic manipulation, all while minimizing the need for expensive robot-specific data collection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a generalist robot agent capable of performing diverse manipulation tasks on common household objects in real-world settings, specifically emphasizing **zero-shot generalization** to unseen objects, environments, and robotic embodiments.

The primary motivations driving this work are:
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** Traditional robot learning heavily relies on costly and labor-intensive in-domain demonstrations collected through teleoperation or scripted policies. This bottleneck significantly hinders the scalability and generalization of robotic manipulation policies. RAM aims to circumvent this by leveraging readily available, large-scale, out-of-domain data.
- **Unlocking Actionable Knowledge in Diverse Data:** Beyond traditional robotic datasets, there is an immense quantity of visual data (e.g., human-object interaction videos, internet images) that contains implicit "actionable knowledge" – information about "where" and "how" to interact with objects. The motivation is to devise a method to effectively extract, unify, and utilize this knowledge despite inherent domain shifts.
- **Leveraging Visual Foundation Models (VFMs):** The rapid advancement of VFMs, trained on massive internet-scale image datasets, offers unprecedented capabilities for understanding and connecting diverse visual domains. This work is motivated to harness the power of these generalizable models to facilitate the distillation of rich affordance information from heterogeneous sources and transfer it to the robotic domain.
- **Enabling Robust and Flexible Manipulation:** The goal is not just to learn specific tasks but to enable versatile manipulation capabilities that are "embodiment-agnostic" (transferable across different robot platforms) and "data-efficient," allowing robots to adapt to new situations without explicit retraining.

The key objective of RAM is therefore to implement a "retrieve-and-transfer" paradigm that:
1. **Extracts unified affordance** at scale from diverse demonstration sources (robotic, HOI, custom data) to build a comprehensive "affordance memory."
2. **Hierarchically retrieves** the most relevant demonstration from this memory given a language instruction and a novel scene observation.
3. **Transfers the 2D affordance** from the retrieved out-of-domain demonstration to the in-domain 3D executable affordance in a zero-shot and embodiment-agnostic manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RAM proposes a novel retrieve-and-transfer framework for zero-shot robotic manipulation, capitalizing on visual affordances. The core insight is to first transfer 2D affordance information from diverse sources and then lift it into 3D for direct execution by a robot.

The methodology comprises four main stages:

1. **Affordance Memory Construction:**
 - **Unified Representation:** The framework defines a unified 2D affordance representation for each entry in the memory, consisting of an object-centric RGB image ($I_S$), a natural language task category ($T$), and a set of 2D waypoints ($C$). The waypoints include the initial contact point ($c_{2D_0}$) and subsequent post-contact trajectories.
 - **Data Sources and Extraction:**
 - **Robotic Data ($M_R$):** For datasets like DROID, 3D end-effector positions during interaction (especially at gripper closure for the contact point) are projected onto the initial 2D image frame ($I_S$) to derive $c_{2D_0}$ and post-contact trajectories. This process is largely automatic given robot proprioception and camera parameters.
 - **HOI Data ($M_H$):** From datasets like HOI4D, video segments are identified, and the initial frame is used as $I_S$. Contact points and trajectories are estimated by averaging hand keypoints within the object mask. This is also semi-automatic.
 - **Custom Data ($M_C$):** For novel or specific manipulation strategies, users can manually annotate demonstrations by selecting start and end points on an RGB image, with automatic interpolation for intermediate points. This provides customization with minimal human effort.
 - This scalable and semi-automatic approach allows for a comprehensive affordance memory ($M = M_R \cup M_H \cup M_C$) spanning diverse objects, environments, and embodiments.

2. **Hierarchical Retrieval:**
 - Given a monocular RGBD image ($I_T, D$) of the target scene and a language instruction ($L$), RAM employs a three-step hierarchical process to find the most suitable demonstration from the affordance memory ($M$).
 - **Task Retrieval:** Uses text encoders (e.g., CLIP) or LLMs (e.g., GPT-4) to extract the task category ($T$) and object name ($N$) from the language instruction ($L$). If the object is unseen, it attempts to reason about similar object geometries or suggest related tasks.
 - **Semantic Filtering:** Calculates a joint similarity score between the source image ($I_S$) from memory and the target observation ($I_T$), incorporating the object name ($N$), using CLIP's visual and text encoders. This filters out irrelevant or low-quality demonstrations.
 - **Geometrical Retrieval:** To address the challenge of viewpoint sensitivity in affordance transfer, this step uses Instance Matching Distance (IMD) computed from Visual Foundation Model (VFM) feature maps (specifically Stable Diffusion). IMD helps select the demonstration whose object is most geometrically similar and oriented to the target object, ensuring robust correspondence.

3. **2D Affordance Transfer:**
 - Once the most similar source demonstration ($I_S$, $C$) is retrieved, RAM leverages dense feature maps from VFMs to establish pixel-aligned correspondences between $I_S$ and $I_T$.
 - Per-point correspondence matching is performed for each waypoint in $C$ to find corresponding points in $I_T$.
 - The RANSAC algorithm is then applied to remove outliers and fit a line in 2D space, resulting in the 2D affordance $A_{2D} = (c_{2D}, \tau_{2D})$, where $c_{2D}$ is the transferred 2D contact point and $\tau_{2D}$ is the post-contact direction.

4. **Sampling-Based Affordance Lifting:**
 - The 2D affordance $A_{2D}$ needs to be converted into 3D executable actions.
 - **3D Contact Point:** The 2D contact point $c_{2D}$ is back-projected to 3D ($c_{3D}$) using the depth map ($D$).
 - **3D Post-Contact Direction:** A local point cloud is cropped around $c_{3D}$. Normal vectors are estimated for points within this local region. K-Means clustering is applied to cluster these normals, and the cluster center whose normal vector has the smallest angle with the projected 2D post-contact direction ($\tau_{2D}$) is selected to derive the 3D post-contact direction ($\tau_{3D}$). This yields the full 3D affordance $A_{3D} = (c_{3D}, \tau_{3D})$.
 - **Action Execution:** Off-the-shelf dense grasp generators (e.g., AnyGrasp, GSNet) are used to sample grasp proposals near $c_{3D}$. The closest valid grasp is chosen. Finally, the robot performs the action by moving its end-effector in position control (or impedance control in real-world for safety) along the direction of $\tau_{3D}$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of RAM was rigorously evaluated through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world environments, demonstrating its superior performance and versatility.

- **Quantitative Performance:**
 - **Simulation:** RAM significantly outperformed all baseline methods (Where2Act, VRB, Robo-ABC) across a diverse set of 13 tasks in "Open," "Close," and "Pickup" categories. RAM achieved an average success rate (SR) of 52.62%, notably higher than Robo-ABC* (41.54%), VRB* (30.77%), and Where2Act (20.50%). The comparison highlights RAM's ability to provide precise contact points and robust manipulation, especially in challenging opening tasks. The * indicates necessary modifications made to baselines for fair 3D adaptation, further underscoring RAM's native 3D capability.
 - **Real-World:** In real-world experiments on 6 different tasks, RAM maintained its superior performance, achieving a 66.7% success rate compared to Robo-ABC*'s 50.0%. This result demonstrates RAM's robust sim-to-real transfer capabilities and cross-domain nature.

- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations of transferred 2D affordances (Fig. 5) indicated that RAM establishes more reliable and accurate affordance transfers, which directly contributes to its robust performance.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Hierarchical Retrieval:** The ablation studies confirmed the critical role of each component within the hierarchical retrieval pipeline. Removing any step (Task Retrieval, Semantic Filtering, Geometrical Retrieval) led to a degradation in both Success Rate (SR) and Distance to Mask (DTM, a measure of contact point accuracy). Geometrical retrieval was found to be particularly crucial, with its removal leading to a significant drop in SR from 38.3% to 26.7%, emphasizing the importance of viewpoint alignment for affordance transfer.
 - **Cross-Task Transfer:** Even when the target task was deliberately excluded from RAM's affordance memory, the framework demonstrated a decent 25.0% SR. This remarkable "cross-task" capability suggests that RAM's learned affordance representation generalizes beyond specific, memorized tasks, hinting at a deeper understanding of object manipulability.
 - **Visual Foundation Models (VFMs):** Comparing different VFMs for affordance transfer, Stable Diffusion (SD) yielded the best overall SR, while SD-DINOv2 achieved the best DTM. The choice of SD for the main pipeline was due to its superior overall success rate, robustness in post-contact correspondence, and faster inference time compared to alternatives like CLIP and DINOv2.
 - **Data Amount:** Experiments showed a positive correlation between the amount of data in the affordance memory and performance. Notably, using only 50% of the original data achieved comparable results to using the full dataset, demonstrating RAM's data efficiency.

- **Downstream Applications:** The research highlighted RAM's versatility through several compelling downstream applications:
 - **Policy Distillation:** RAM's zero-shot capability enables autonomous and efficient collection of high-quality, successful demonstrations for specific tasks. By using these self-collected demonstrations to train an end-to-end policy (e.g., ACT), the distilled policy significantly boosted performance (+35.0% SR) over the zero-shot pipeline, showcasing its potential for scalable data generation for policy learning.
 - **One-Shot Visual Imitation:** RAM is naturally adaptable for one-shot visual imitation, allowing robots to replicate actions from a single, specific in-domain or out-of-domain demonstration. This was showcased by adapting to human preferences (e.g., picking up a tissue paper vs. the box) and even bridging significant domain gaps, such as imitating actions from cartoon images (e.g., Tom and Jerry).
 - **LLM/VLM Integration:** RAM can be seamlessly integrated with LLMs/VLMs (e.g., GPT-4V, Grounded-SAM) for interpreting open-set instructions and executing long-horizon tasks. VLMs can decompose complex instructions into sub-tasks, leveraging RAM for specific manipulation actions and combining them with other basic primitives, enabling more sophisticated and flexible robotic behaviors.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in "RAM: Retrieval-Based Affordance Transfer for Generalizable Zero-Shot Robotic Manipulation" holds significant implications for the future of robot learning and autonomous manipulation.

- **Pioneering Generalizable Zero-Shot Manipulation:** RAM represents a substantial leap forward in achieving truly generalizable zero-shot robotic manipulation. By demonstrating robust performance on unseen objects, environments, and embodiments without task-specific training, it moves robot learning closer to deploying intelligent agents in highly variable real-world scenarios.
- **Effective Leverage of Out-of-Domain Data:** The core contribution of effectively extracting and transferring actionable knowledge from vast, heterogeneous, out-of-domain data sources is groundbreaking. This paradigm shift alleviates the traditional bottleneck of requiring expensive in-domain robotic demonstrations, democratizing robot learning and accelerating skill acquisition.
- **Novel Affordance Transfer Mechanism:** The proposed retrieve-and-transfer pipeline, particularly the 2D affordance transfer combined with the sampling-based 3D lifting, offers a robust and adaptable method for translating high-level visual cues into executable robot actions. This insight into separating 2D perception from 3D execution proves highly effective and generalizable.
- **Embodiment Agnosticism:** The successful deployment of RAM on different robot platforms (Franka Emika arm and Unitree B1 robot dog with Z1 arm) highlights its embodiment-agnostic nature. This greatly enhances the practical applicability of the framework, as learned skills can potentially transfer across various robotic systems without extensive re-engineering.
- **Enabling Downstream Applications:** Beyond its direct manipulation capabilities, RAM's framework opens doors for several transformative downstream applications:
 - **Automated Data Collection:** The ability to perform successful zero-shot manipulation can be leveraged to autonomously collect high-quality, labeled robotic demonstration data. This could revolutionize the way datasets for policy learning are built, making them more efficient, scalable, and less reliant on human teleoperation.
 - **Enhanced Human-Robot Interaction:** The one-shot visual imitation capability, especially when conditioned on human preferences or even bridging real-world to cartoon domains, promises more intuitive and user-friendly robot programming. This could allow non-expert users to teach robots new tasks simply by showing a single example.
 - **Foundation for Long-Horizon and Open-Set Tasks:** The seamless integration with LLMs and VLMs for task decomposition and execution signifies a crucial step towards robots performing complex, multi-stage tasks described by free-form language instructions. This moves beyond predefined primitives, enabling a new level of intelligence and adaptability in robotic systems.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
Despite its compelling successes, the paper acknowledges certain limitations. The current method may require multiple steps for long-horizon tasks, potentially leading to less natural behaviors. Furthermore, complex actions like "screwing" remain challenging. Future work is envisioned to integrate more advanced foundation models for task planning and to develop more sophisticated affordance transfer methods that can directly lift entire 2D trajectories into 3D, potentially enabling even more intricate and nuanced manipulations.

In conclusion, RAM represents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning, offering a powerful and generalizable framework for zero-shot robotic manipulation. Its innovative approach to affordance transfer from diverse data sources, coupled with its demonstrated versatility in downstream applications, positions it as a foundational work that could accelerate the development of truly autonomous and adaptable generalist robots.
