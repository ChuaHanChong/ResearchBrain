# On the Generalization of SFT: A Reinforcement Learning Perspective with Reward Rectification

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT), a method that mathematically reinterprets Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) as an unstable Reinforcement Learning process with an ill-posed implicit reward. By rectifying this reward structure with a single-line code change, DFT consistently enhances SFT's generalization capabilities across various LLM architectures and tasks, often outperforming complex online and offline RL methods while being more resource-efficient.

## Problem

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) often suffers from limited generalization, tending to overfit training data and performing poorly on out-of-distribution examples.
- Traditional SFT's generalization gap compared to Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods, which are computationally expensive and require explicit reward functions or preference data.
- The lack of a precise theoretical understanding of why SFT overfits and how its optimization gradient relates to RL policy gradients.

## Method

- The paper rigorously establishes a mathematical equivalence between the SFT gradient and an offline policy gradient, identifying an ==inverse-probability weighting term== as the root cause of SFT's instability and overfitting.
- ==Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT)== is proposed, which dynamically reweights the SFT loss by multiplying it with the model's token probability, applying a `stop_gradient` to this term.
- DFT's reweighting effectively neutralizes the problematic inverse-probability term, transforming the SFT update into a ==stable, uniformly weighted rule== that directly maximizes token probability rather than log-probability.

## Results

- DFT consistently and substantially outperforms standard SFT and base models across mathematical reasoning, code generation, and multimodal reasoning tasks, achieving gains of up to **+15.66 points** in accuracy.
- On challenging mathematical benchmarks (e.g., Olympiad Bench, AIME 2024), DFT improves performance significantly, while SFT often shows degradation or minimal impact.
- DFT achieves competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art offline RL methods (DPO, RFT) and even online RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO) in offline RL settings, demonstrating its efficiency and effectiveness without complex setups.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SFT's generalization issues stem from an implicit, ill-posed reward structure where an inverse-probability weighting term disproportionately amplifies gradients for rare or hard-to-model expert tokens, causing unstable optimization.
> - By rectifying this implicit reward through dynamic reweighting, DFT enables SFT to generalize more effectively without requiring external reward models, preference data, or negative samples.
> - DFT induces a 'polarizing effect' on token probability distributions, boosting semantically important tokens while suppressing others, which acts as a better regularizer against overfitting compared to SFT's uniform increase of probabilities.
