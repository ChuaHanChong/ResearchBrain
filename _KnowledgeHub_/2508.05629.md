# On the Generalization of SFT: A Reinforcement Learning Perspective with Reward Rectification

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT), a method that mathematically reinterprets Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) as an unstable Reinforcement Learning process with an ill-posed implicit reward. By rectifying this reward structure with a single-line code change, DFT consistently enhances SFT's generalization capabilities across various LLM architectures and tasks, often outperforming complex online and offline RL methods while being more resource-efficient.

## Problem

- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) often suffers from limited generalization, tending to overfit training data and performing poorly on out-of-distribution examples.
- Traditional SFT's generalization gap compared to Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods, which are computationally expensive and require explicit reward functions or preference data.
- The lack of a precise theoretical understanding of why SFT overfits and how its optimization gradient relates to RL policy gradients.

## Method

- The paper rigorously establishes a mathematical equivalence between the SFT gradient and an offline policy gradient, identifying an ==inverse-probability weighting term== as the root cause of SFT's instability and overfitting.
- ==Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT)== is proposed, which dynamically reweights the SFT loss by multiplying it with the model's token probability, applying a `stop_gradient` to this term.
- DFT's reweighting effectively neutralizes the problematic inverse-probability term, transforming the SFT update into a ==stable, uniformly weighted rule== that directly maximizes token probability rather than log-probability.

## Results

- DFT consistently and substantially outperforms standard SFT and base models across mathematical reasoning, code generation, and multimodal reasoning tasks, achieving gains of up to **+15.66 points** in accuracy.
- On challenging mathematical benchmarks (e.g., Olympiad Bench, AIME 2024), DFT improves performance significantly, while SFT often shows degradation or minimal impact.
- DFT achieves competitive or superior performance compared to state-of-the-art offline RL methods (DPO, RFT) and even online RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO) in offline RL settings, demonstrating its efficiency and effectiveness without complex setups.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - SFT's generalization issues stem from an implicit, ill-posed reward structure where an inverse-probability weighting term disproportionately amplifies gradients for rare or hard-to-model expert tokens, causing unstable optimization.
> - By rectifying this implicit reward through dynamic reweighting, DFT enables SFT to generalize more effectively without requiring external reward models, preference data, or negative samples.
> - DFT induces a 'polarizing effect' on token probability distributions, boosting semantically important tokens while suppressing others, which acts as a better regularizer against overfitting compared to SFT's uniform increase of probabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research operates within the critical and rapidly evolving landscape of Large Language Model (LLM) post-training and alignment. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), represent the two dominant paradigms for adapting foundational LLMs to specific tasks and human preferences.

**SFT's Role and Limitations:** SFT, which involves fine-tuning LLMs on expert demonstrations, is widely adopted due to its simplicity, efficiency, and ability to quickly acquire expert-like behaviors. It serves as a foundational step for many advanced LLM capabilities. However, a well-documented limitation of SFT is its tendency towards overfitting and limited generalization, particularly when compared to RL-based approaches. Research, such as that by Chu et al. (2024), concisely summarizes this by stating "SFT memorizes while RL generalizes."

**RL's Strengths and Challenges:** RL methods, especially those leveraging explicit reward signals, are known for their superior generalization capabilities. They allow models to explore diverse strategies beyond the training data. Despite this, RL for LLMs presents significant practical hurdles: it demands substantial computational resources, requires intricate hyperparameter tuning, and relies on the availability of explicit reward signals or well-defined reward models (e.g., in RLHF). These conditions are often impractical in real-world scenarios. Moreover, RL can sometimes struggle to recover the precise expert-like behaviors that SFT efficiently captures.

**Hybrid Approaches:** Recognizing the complementary strengths and weaknesses, much recent research has focused on hybrid methods that combine SFT and RL. Approaches like InstructGPT (Ouyang et al., 2022) involve SFT pretraining followed by RL-based refinement using learned reward models. Other methods interleave SFT and RL updates or bypass explicit reward modeling through direct policy optimization (e.g., DPO, RAFT). While these hybrid approaches have advanced the field, they still typically rely on reward signals, preference pairs, or negative samples.

**The Niche of This Work:** This paper distinguishes itself by taking a different approach. Instead of proposing another hybrid SFT-RL method, it asks a fundamental question: "Can SFT itself be fundamentally improved?" The authors aim to enhance SFT directly, particularly in situations where only positive demonstrations are available, without requiring external feedback mechanisms like negative samples, explicit reward models, or preference pairs. This focus addresses a crucial practical scenario where data might be limited to expert-generated positive examples.

The work also connects to a complementary line of theoretical research that seeks to unify SFT and RL under a common formalism. Previous works have reinterpreted RLHF as reward-weighted SFT or cast SFT as RL with an implicit reward, often suggesting weighting adjustments. This paper builds on this by providing a more formal mathematical equivalence between the SFT gradient and the offline policy gradient, specifically highlighting the problematic role of inverse-probability weighting in standard SFT. This theoretical foundation motivates its proposed solution, making it distinct from purely empirical reweighting schemes. By doing so, this research pushes the boundaries of SFT, attempting to infuse it with the generalization characteristics typically associated with more complex RL setups, while retaining its inherent simplicity and efficiency.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **fundamentally improve the generalization capabilities of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Large Language Models (LLMs)**. This improvement is sought without introducing the computational complexity, hyperparameter tuning burden, or explicit reward signal requirements typically associated with reinforcement learning (RL).

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several key observations and challenges in the current LLM training paradigm:

1. **SFT's Enduring Practicality vs. Generalization Deficiencies:** SFT remains the default and most practical post-training method due to its ease of implementation, computational efficiency, and ability to quickly align models with desired expert behaviors. However, its well-documented limitation is a tendency to overfit training data and exhibit poor generalization to unseen or challenging examples, particularly when compared to RL. This trade-off presents a significant bottleneck for deploying SFT-tuned models in diverse, real-world applications.

2. **The Complementary Strengths of RL and SFT:** While RL offers superior generalization by leveraging explicit reward signals to explore robust policies, it comes with substantial practical hurdles, including high computational cost, sensitivity to hyperparameter tuning, and the necessity of a carefully designed reward function or human preferences. There's a clear need to bridge this "generalization gap" between SFT and RL.

3. **Limitations of Hybrid Approaches:** Many existing methods combine SFT and RL to leverage their complementary strengths. However, these hybrid approaches often introduce additional complexities, requiring either explicit reward models, preference data, or negative samples. A critical gap remains for scenarios where only positive expert demonstrations are available—a common situation in many real-world datasets. The authors are motivated to enhance SFT in its "native setting" without external feedback.

4. **Theoretical Understanding of SFT's Generalization Bottleneck:** The core motivation for the proposed solution arises from a novel theoretical insight. Through mathematical analysis, the authors reveal that standard SFT gradients implicitly encode a problematic reward structure when viewed through an RL lens. Specifically, this implicit reward is (i) sparse (only active for exact matches to expert trajectories) and (ii) *inversely proportional* to the model's probability of generating the expert action (i.e., `1/π_θ`). This inverse proportionality means that when the model assigns a very low probability to an expert action, the gradient signal becomes excessively large. This leads to an ill-posed reward landscape, unstable optimization, and a tendency to overfit rare, low-probability expert examples, severely restricting the model's ability to generalize beyond the training distribution.

The ultimate motivation is to develop a principled, lightweight, and effective method that rectifies this problematic implicit reward structure, thereby stabilizing gradient updates and fostering improved generalization within the simple and efficient SFT framework. This aims to unlock SFT's potential to match or even surpass the generalization of more complex RL methods in various domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in the paper, Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT), is built upon a profound theoretical reinterpretation of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) from a reinforcement learning (RL) perspective.

#### 3.1. Theoretical Foundation: SFT as a Policy Gradient with Problematic Rewards

The authors initiate their approach by mathematically demonstrating that the gradient update in standard SFT can be formally expressed as a policy gradient.
1. **SFT Gradient Expression:** The standard SFT objective minimizes the negative log-probability of expert demonstrations `(x, y*)`: `L_SFT(θ) = E_{(x,y*)~D} [- log π_θ(y* | x)]`. Its gradient is `∇_θ L_SFT(θ) = E_{(x,y*)~D} [- ∇_θ log π_θ(y* | x)]`.
2. **Conversion to On-Policy Expectation:** By employing importance sampling, the authors convert the SFT gradient into an on-policy expectation over responses `y` sampled from the current model `π_θ(· | x)`:
 `E_{(x,y*)~D} [-∇_θ log π_θ(y* | x)] = E_{x~D_x, y~π_θ(·|x)} [ (1[y = y*] / π_θ(y | x)) (-∇_θ log π_θ(y | x)) ]`
3. **Identification of Implicit Reward:** This rewritten form closely resembles the standard policy gradient `∇_θ J(θ) = E_{x~D_x, y~π_θ(·|x)} [ ∇_θ log π_θ(y | x) r(x, y) ]`. Under this interpretation, the SFT gradient corresponds to an RL policy gradient with two key components:
 - **Sparse Reward Function:** `r(x, y) = 1[y = y*]`. This indicator function assigns a reward of 1 only if the model's generated response `y` *exactly matches* the expert demonstration `y*`, and 0 otherwise. This is inherently sparse, providing feedback only for perfect matches.
 - **Importance Weighting Term:** `w(y | x) = 1 / π_θ(y | x)`. This term means the implicit reward is effectively `r(x, y) / π_θ(y | x) = 1[y = y*] / π_θ(y | x)`.

#### 3.2. Identifying the Problem

The authors pinpoint the `1 / π_θ(y | x)` importance weighting term as the root cause of SFT's generalization limitations and instability. When the model assigns a very *low probability* to an expert action `y*`, the corresponding importance weight `1 / π_θ(y* | x)` becomes *excessively large*. This leads to:
- **Ill-posed Reward Landscape:** Disproportionately large gradients are generated for rare or "hard-to-match" expert examples.
- **Optimization Instability:** The large gradients can destabilize training.
- **Overfitting:** The model is incentivized to aggressively fit these low-probability expert actions, potentially memorizing them rather than learning generalizable patterns, thus weakening its ability to generalize beyond the training data.

#### 3.3. Proposed Method: Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT)

To rectify this issue, the authors propose Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT). The core idea is to neutralize the problematic `1 / π_θ` weighting term within the SFT objective.
1. **Reward Rectification:** The proposed solution involves dynamically reweighting the SFT objective by multiplying it with the model's own probability for the expert token, `π_θ(y* | x)`. This effectively cancels out the inverse-probability distortion.
2. **The DFT Loss Function:** The full sequence-level DFT loss is formulated as:
 `L_DFT(θ) = E_{(x,y*)~D} [- sg(π_θ(y* | x)) log π_θ(y* | x)]`
 Crucially, the `sg(·)` (stop-gradient) operator is applied to `π_θ(y* | x)` when it acts as the reweighting term. This ensures that the gradient does not flow through this scaling factor, treating it as a constant during backpropagation. This makes the multiplication a stable reweighting rather than a complex modification of the optimization landscape.
3. **Token-Level Implementation:** In practice, computing importance weights over entire long sequences (`y*`) can be numerically unstable. Following common practices in RL (e.g., PPO), DFT applies this reweighting at the *token level*:
 `L_DFT(θ) = E_{(x,y*)~D} [- Σ_{t=1}^{|y*|} sg(π_θ(y*_t | y*_<t, x)) log π_θ(y*_t | y*_<t, x)]`
 This means each token's cross-entropy loss is scaled by the model's own (detached) probability of generating that specific token given its context.

#### 3.4. Consequences and Comparison

- **Uniform Reward in RL View:** From the RL perspective, this modification effectively transforms the implicit reward function into a uniform `1` for all expert trajectory tokens. This is analogous to "verification-based reward" systems, where all correct samples are treated equally, preventing over-concentration on specific low-probability tokens.
- **Gradient Stability:** The DFT gradient becomes `∇_θ L_DFT = - ∇_θ π_θ(y* | x)`, which directly maximizes the model's probability of the target token. This contrasts with standard SFT, where the gradient is `∇_θ L_CE = - (1 / π_θ(y* | x)) ∇_θ π_θ(y* | x)`. DFT thus avoids the large scaling factor `1/π_θ` for low-probability tokens, leading to more stable and conservative updates.
- **Shift in Weighting Philosophy:** The paper highlights an interesting contrast with Focal Loss (Lin et al., 2017). Focal Loss *downweights* well-classified samples to emphasize hard cases. DFT, conversely, effectively *downweights poorly classified samples* (or low-probability tokens) to encourage generalization. This inversion reflects a fundamental shift in LLM challenges: while underfitting was once central, overfitting and memorization now dominate, requiring a rethinking of objective design to promote robust learning.
- **Simplicity:** A key aspect of DFT is its simplicity—it's described as a "single-line change" to the SFT loss, making it computationally efficient and easy to implement without introducing additional models or complex sampling procedures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments comprehensively evaluate DFT across various LLM architectures, scales, tasks, and training settings, consistently demonstrating its superiority over standard SFT and competitive performance against complex RL methods.

#### 4.1. Mathematical Reasoning (Core Task)

- **Superior Generalization:** DFT consistently yields substantial average performance improvements over base models and standard SFT across a suite of challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks (Math500, Minerva Math, Olympiad Bench, AIME 2024, AMC 2023).
 - For example, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B with DFT achieves an average gain of +15.66 points over the base model, which is nearly six times larger than SFT's +2.09 points. Similar trends hold for LLaMA-3.2-3B (+3.46 vs +2.05), LLaMA-3.1-8B (+10.02 vs +5.33), DeepSeekMath-7B (+15.51 vs +7.18), and Qwen2.5-Math-7B (+15.90 vs +2.37).
- **Robustness on Challenging Benchmarks:** Unlike SFT, which often degrades performance or shows minimal gains on difficult benchmarks (e.g., Olympiad Bench, AIME24, AMC23), DFT consistently improves performance. For Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, SFT dropped accuracy on Olympiad Bench from 15.88 to 12.63, while DFT boosted it to 27.08.
- **Faster Convergence and Sample Efficiency:** Figure 1 illustrates that DFT achieves peak performance faster (within ~120 steps), performs better in early training stages (outperforming SFT's best final accuracy within 10-20 steps), and requires fewer updates for optimal results, indicating more informative gradient updates and efficient acquisition of reasoning patterns.
- **Effectiveness with High-Quality Data and PEFT:** DFT remains effective when trained on higher-quality datasets (OpenR1-Math-220k, yielding +9.03 points over SFT) and in parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) settings using LoRA adapters (+3.44 points over SFT for LLaMA-3.2-3B), showing its broad applicability.

#### 4.2. Offline Reinforcement Learning Setting (Exploratory)

- **Competitive with RL Baselines:** When applied in an offline RL setting (using rejection-sampled rewards for 100,000 math questions, resulting in ~140,000 examples), DFT not only outperforms other offline RL methods like DPO and RFT but also achieves competitive or superior results to online RL methods such as PPO and GRPO.
 - DFT scores an average of 35.43, exceeding RFT by +11.46 points and GRPO (the strongest online method) by +3.43 points. This highlights DFT's strength as a simple yet effective fine-tuning strategy, bypassing the complexities of iterative reward modeling or environment interaction typically required by RL.

#### 4.3. Code Generation (Exploratory)

- DFT demonstrates improvements over base models and SFT on HumanEval, HumanEval+, and MultiPL-E benchmarks across Qwen2.5-3B, Qwen2.5-Coder-3B, and Qwen2.5-Coder-7B models. For Qwen2.5-Coder-7B, DFT surpassed SFT by +12.8 points on HumanEval, +11.0 on HumanEval+, and +4.7 on MultiPL-E average.

#### 4.4. Multi-modal Reasoning (Exploratory)

- DFT achieves consistent improvements over base models and SFT on multi-modal reasoning benchmarks (MathVerse, MathVision, WeMath) using Qwen2.5-VL-3B, demonstrating its effectiveness beyond text-only domains. For instance, on MathVerse, DFT boosted accuracy by +3.71 points over SFT.

#### 4.5. Analysis of Token Probabilities

- **SFT vs. DFT Distribution:** Post-training, SFT tends to uniformly increase token probabilities, shifting the entire distribution towards higher confidence for training set tokens. In contrast, DFT exhibits a "polarizing effect" (Figure 2): it significantly boosts the probabilities of a subset of tokens while actively suppressing others, resulting in a bimodal distribution with more tokens in both the highest and lowest probability bins.
- **Improved Regularization:** The analysis suggests that DFT promotes improved regularization by selectively increasing probabilities for semantically important tokens while downweighting less substantive ones (e.g., grammatical words, punctuation). This is interpreted as focusing on "substantive concepts" rather than "perfecting usage of common connective words."

#### 4.6. Limitations of DFT: A Case Study on Factual Knowledge

- **Domain-Specific Weakness:** While generally powerful, DFT shows a notable limitation in factual knowledge domains. On the Natural Questions dataset, SFT improved performance (31.24% to 36.62%), but DFT unexpectedly reduced it to 30.14%. This suggests that when a model lacks sufficient factual knowledge, DFT's tendency to reinforce existing beliefs (by reweighting based on current confidence) can hinder the acquisition of new, external information.

#### 4.7. Empirical Comparison with Sentence-Level Weighting

- The token-level weighting in DFT proved crucial. Sentence-level weighting variants (using full sequence probability or geometric mean) yielded minimal or no improvements, often suffering from numerical instability or providing weak training signals. This validates the practical design choice of token-level reweighting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work "ON THE GENERALIZATION OF SFT: A REINFORCEMENT LEARNING PERSPECTIVE WITH REWARD RECTIFICATION" offers significant theoretical and practical contributions to the field of Large Language Model (LLM) fine-tuning, with a substantial potential impact on how SFT is understood and applied.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Significance

- **Novel Reinterpretation of SFT:** The paper provides a principled mathematical analysis that reinterprets the standard SFT gradient as a form of policy gradient within the reinforcement learning (RL) framework. This theoretical lens is crucial for understanding SFT's inherent limitations.
- **Identification of SFT's Generalization Bottleneck:** By explicitly revealing the implicit reward structure in SFT as sparse and inversely proportional to model confidence (`1/π_θ`), the authors precisely pinpoint the underlying reason for its instability and limited generalization. This inverse weighting term, causing excessively large gradients for low-probability expert actions, is identified as a core problem. This theoretical clarity is a significant advance in explaining observed empirical phenomena.
- **Bridging SFT and RL:** The work formally connects SFT to RL, showing how a seemingly simple supervised method can be understood through the lens of policy gradients. This unification contributes to a broader theoretical framework for understanding LLM alignment.

#### 5.2. Practical Significance and Potential Impact

- **Simple yet Highly Effective Improvement to SFT:** DFT is a lightweight and remarkably effective method. Described as a "single-line change" to the standard SFT loss function, it offers substantial and consistent improvements in generalization across diverse tasks (mathematical reasoning, code generation, multimodal reasoning) and various LLM architectures and scales. This simplicity makes it highly accessible and easy to adopt for practitioners.
- **Enhanced Generalization without RL Complexity:** DFT addresses the crucial trade-off between SFT's simplicity and RL's superior generalization. It achieves strong generalization comparable to, and often surpassing, more complex and computationally intensive RL-based methods (like DPO, RFT, PPO, GRPO) in specific domains. This means practitioners can achieve better model performance and robustness without the overhead of building reward models, collecting preference data, or performing complex RL training.
- **Efficiency and Resource-Saving:** By leveraging SFT's inherent efficiency while mitigating its generalization weaknesses, DFT offers a more resource-efficient alternative to traditional RL pipelines. This is particularly impactful for researchers and developers with limited computational resources or in settings where reward modeling or online interaction is impractical or expensive.
- **Robustness on Challenging Tasks:** The consistent performance gains on difficult reasoning benchmarks where standard SFT struggles or even degrades highlights DFT's ability to foster deeper, more robust learning rather than mere memorization. This could lead to more capable and reliable LLMs for complex problem-solving.
- **Rethinking Objective Design in the LLM Era:** The contrasting weighting philosophy of DFT (downweighting poorly classified samples) compared to traditional methods like Focal Loss (downweighting well-classified samples) signals a fundamental shift in objective function design for LLMs. As overfitting and memorization become dominant challenges, methods that promote regularization and stable learning from potentially noisy or sparse expert data become increasingly vital.
- **Broader Applicability:** The exploratory experiments on code generation and multimodal reasoning suggest that DFT's benefits extend beyond mathematical reasoning, indicating its potential as a general fine-tuning paradigm for various domains.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper opens avenues for future work, such as exploring non-uniform or quality-aware reward assignments for demonstrations, and investigating DFT's performance on even broader task categories and larger LLM scales. The identified limitation in factual knowledge acquisition also prompts research into hybrid strategies or adaptive weighting mechanisms that can balance generalization with factual learning.

In conclusion, this work significantly advances the state of SFT by providing a clear theoretical understanding of its limitations and offering a practical, elegant solution. DFT's ability to boost generalization while maintaining SFT's efficiency positions it as a highly impactful method for developing more robust and capable LLMs.
