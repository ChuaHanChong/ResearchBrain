# The Markovian Thinker: Architecture-Agnostic Linear Scaling of Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The Markovian Thinker introduces an architecture-agnostic paradigm that enables large language models (LLMs) to perform linearly scalable reasoning by redefining the reinforcement learning environment. This approach allows LLMs to process significantly longer thought sequences without incurring quadratic computational costs, matching or exceeding prior state-of-the-art performance while achieving substantial efficiency gains.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning for Long Chain-of-Thought (LongCoT) reasoning incurs quadratic computational cost and linear memory usage for attention-based LLMs due to treating the entire reasoning sequence as the state.
- The prohibitive cost limits the practical application and further scaling of LLMs for very long reasoning chains, despite the benefits of extensive thinking.
- Existing solutions often constrain reasoning length, optimize inference post-training, or require architectural modifications, which do not fundamentally resolve the quadratic scaling during training for unconstrained thinking.

## Method

- Introduces "Markovian Thinking" through the novel "==Delethink==" RL environment, which ensures the policy always conditions on a constant-sized state regardless of the total reasoning length.
- Delethink structures reasoning into fixed-size chunks (e.g., 8K tokens), where the model's context and KV cache are reset at each chunk boundary.
- A short textual carryover (e.g., the last 4K tokens) from the previous chunk's output is incorporated into the new prompt, effectively creating a textual Markovian state that the policy learns to utilize.

## Results

- Delethink consistently matches or surpasses LongCoT-RL in accuracy on in-distribution math benchmarks (AIME'24, AIME'25, HMMT'25) and out-of-distribution tasks with equivalent thinking budgets.
- Achieves significant empirical computational efficiency, reducing training costs (e.g., 7 H100-months vs. 27 H100-months for **96K** thinking) and maintaining constant token generation throughput regardless of reasoning length.
- Demonstrates superior test-time scalability, improving performance with up to **128K** thinking tokens even when trained with a **24K** budget, while LongCoT-RL plateaus at its trained limit.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Redefining the RL environment to enforce a Markovian state transition enables architecture-agnostic linear scaling of reasoning, decoupling computational cost from total reasoning length.
> - Off-the-shelf reasoning LLMs demonstrate strong "zero-shot Markovian Thinking," adapting to the chunked reasoning paradigm without explicit training, providing a robust initialization for RL.
> - The forced summarization into a constant-size textual state may encourage LLMs to develop more abstract or compressed forms of reasoning, akin to human cognitive processes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a fundamental scalability challenge in the rapidly evolving field of Large Language Models (LLMs) and their application to complex reasoning tasks, specifically "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) reasoning. Recent advances have shown that reinforcement learning (RL) can effectively train LLMs to generate long, step-by-step reasoning chains (LongCoT), which significantly improves their problem-solving capabilities. However, the standard approach to training these models in an RL environment treats the entire sequence of prompt plus all generated reasoning tokens as the "state." This design choice has a critical drawback: for attention-based LLMs (the predominant architecture today), the computational cost grows quadratically with the length of the reasoning chain (O(N^2) for N tokens), and memory usage scales linearly. As reasoning chains extend to tens or hundreds of thousands of tokens, this quadratic scaling becomes prohibitively expensive, limiting the practical applicability and further scaling of LongCoT-RL.

The existing research landscape has tackled this quadratic scaling issue through several avenues:
- **Limiting Reasoning Length:** Many methods constrain how much an LLM can "think" during training or inference, using techniques like multi-stage training, length-regularized objectives, or pruning/early-exit mechanisms. While these reduce cost, they fundamentally restrict the very "long thinking" capability that LongCoT aims to achieve.
- **Inference-Time Efficiency:** Other works focus on making inference faster by shortening traces through distillation, early exits, budget adjustment, or steering activations towards brevity. These are often post-training optimizations and do not address the quadratic scaling during initial RL training.
- **Architectural Innovations:** A distinct line of research explores alternative model architectures that can handle longer sequences more efficiently, such as linear attention mechanisms (e.g., Longformer, Performer) or state-space models (e.g., Mamba). These approaches fundamentally alter the LLM's core attention mechanism to achieve linear or sub-quadratic scaling. While promising, they require significant architectural changes and often involve hybrid systems that still retain some quadratic components.

This paper positions itself uniquely by *not* modifying the LLM's architecture, *not* implicitly or explicitly forcing shorter reasoning, and *not* merely optimizing inference. Instead, it proposes a paradigm shift by **redefining the RL environment itself**. The core innovation is to transform the "thinking environment" into a Markovian process, where the policy always conditions on a constant-sized state, regardless of the total reasoning length. This "architecture-agnostic" approach is a critical distinction, as it allows existing, high-performing attention-based LLMs to benefit from linear scaling without fundamental redesign. It challenges the assumption that the *entire* reasoning history must always be in the model's active context, suggesting that an LLM can learn to effectively maintain a concise "textual state" for seamless long-term reasoning. This work provides empirical evidence that such a redefinition of the RL problem is not only possible but highly effective, opening new avenues for scalable LLM training and deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enable **architecture-agnostic linear scaling of reasoning in Large Language Models**. The authors aim to achieve this by fundamentally redesigning how LLMs "think" within an RL framework, effectively decoupling the total length of the reasoning process from the size of the computational context.

The motivation for this work stems from a critical limitation of current state-of-the-art reasoning LLMs:
- **Prohibitive Computational Costs of LongCoT:** While Reinforcement Learning has proven effective for training LLMs to generate extensive Chains-of-Thought (LongCoT), the standard RL setup leads to an unbounded "state" (the prompt plus all generated tokens so far). For attention-based transformer models, this translates to a quadratic increase in compute and a linear increase in memory usage as the reasoning chain lengthens. This makes training and inference for truly *very* long reasoning (e.g., millions of tokens) economically and technically infeasible.
- **Desire for Unconstrained Thinking:** Current attempts to mitigate these costs often involve constraining the reasoning length, which goes against the goal of enabling LLMs to "think" as long as necessary to solve complex problems. The authors are motivated by the question: "what if the environment never creates quadratic growth to begin with?"
- **Seeking a Fundamental Solution:** Instead of incremental optimizations (like pruning tokens, early exiting, or architectural modifications), the authors seek a more fundamental solution that re-evaluates the basic setup of the RL problem for reasoning. They believe that redesigning the interaction between the LLM and its "thinking environment" can unlock new levels of scalability.

In essence, the research is driven by the vision of enabling next-generation reasoning LLMs to "think for millions of tokens" efficiently, without the quadratic overhead that currently plagues attention-based architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology is the introduction of **Markovian Thinking**, a paradigm instantiated through a novel RL environment called **Delethink**.

#### 3.1. Markovian Thinking Paradigm

The central idea is to constrain the policy to advance reasoning while conditioning only on a *constant-size state*, thereby decoupling total thinking length from context size. This immediately implies linear compute and constant memory with respect to the total reasoning length.

#### 3.2. Delethink Environment

Delethink implements Markovian Thinking by structuring the reasoning process into fixed-size "chunks":
- **Chunked Reasoning:** The model generates reasoning in segments, each up to a predefined "thinking context size" (C, e.g., 8K tokens).
- **Context Reset:** At the boundary of each chunk, the environment *resets* the model's context. This means that all prior reasoning tokens from previous chunks are effectively "deleted" from the active context window.
- **Textual Carryover (Markovian State):** To enable seamless continuation, the new prompt for the next chunk is constructed from the original query plus a *short textual carryover* (m, e.g., the last few tokens) from the *previous* chunk's output. The policy, through RL, learns to intelligently summarize or identify key information to include in this short carryover, effectively creating a "textual Markovian state."
- **KV Cache Management:** Concomitant with the context reset, the KV cache (key and value states) for the transformer's attention mechanism is cleared. The carryover tokens are then re-encoded as part of the new prompt, incurring a small, constant O(m^2) re-encoding cost at each chunk boundary, which does not alter the overall linear scaling.

#### 3.3. Policy Gradient Derivation

The standard RL objective of maximizing expected return is adapted for the chunked Delethink environment. The policy gradient estimator, inspired by GRPO (Generalized Reinforce Policy Optimization) and PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization), is re-derived. The objective essentially sums the PPO-style loss over all chunks within a trace, with the total trace reward guiding learning. This allows for straightforward adaptation of existing RL infrastructure.

#### 3.4. Computational Cost Analysis

The authors provide a theoretical analysis demonstrating the efficiency gains:
- **FLOPs:** For a total thinking length of nS tokens (n = chunk size, S = number of chunks), Delethink achieves O(n^2 S) FLOPs, which is linear in S. This contrasts with LongCoT's quadratic O(n^2 S^2).
- **Memory:** Delethink maintains constant memory O(n) because the context size never exceeds C (the chunk size). LongCoT has linear memory O(nS).
- **Generation Time:** Delethink's generation throughput is constant with respect to total thinking length, leading to linear scaling of generation time. LongCoT's throughput decreases linearly with length, resulting in quadratic generation time.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Model:** R1-Distill 1.5B, a strong open-source reasoning LLM.
- **Training Data:** DeepScaleR dataset (40K competition-level math problems).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:**
 - In-distribution math: AIME'24, AIME'25, HMMT'25.
 - Out-of-distribution (OOD): GPQA-Diamond (PhD-level QA), LiveCodeBench (code generation).
- **Baselines:** LongCoT-RL with equivalent maximum thinking budgets (24K tokens) and a shorter budget (8K tokens).
- **Metrics:** Pass@1 accuracy, estimated by averaging correctness over 128 independently sampled responses (avg@128).
- **Hyperparameters:** Standard PPO-based RL training (e.g., GRPO), with specific tuning of PPO clip ratio to maintain stable entropy. KL penalty (β) set to 0.
- **Delethink Configuration:** C = 8K tokens, Markovian state size m = C/2 = 4K tokens, iteration cap I = 5. This allows a maximum total thinking length of 8K + (5-1) * 4K = 24K tokens, matching the LongCoT baseline.
- **Implementation:** Optimized using `verl`, `SGLang`, and `FSDP` on 8xH100 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results robustly support Delethink's efficacy and efficiency:

- **Performance on Math Benchmarks (24K Budget):** Delethink 24K consistently matches or surpasses LongCoT-RL 24K in accuracy on in-distribution math tasks (AIME'24, AIME'25, HMMT'25). For example, on AIME'25, Delethink achieves higher accuracy than LongCoT-RL with the same thinking budget. The LongCoT-RL 8K baseline significantly underperforms, emphasizing the need for extended reasoning, which Delethink effectively provides.
- **Performance on OOD Tasks:** On out-of-distribution tasks like GPQA-Diamond and LiveCodeBench, Delethink matches or slightly outperforms LongCoT-RL, indicating its reasoning capabilities generalize well despite the math-centric training.
- **Superior Test-Time Scaling:** A critical finding is Delethink's ability to scale *beyond* its training-time thinking budget. While LongCoT-RL 24K plateaus at its trained limit, Delethink continues to improve performance with up to 128K thinking tokens, even though it was trained with only a 24K budget. This demonstrates true test-time scalability enabled by the Markovian Thinking paradigm. Some AIME’25 problems were solved only after 140K thinking tokens.
- **Empirical Computational Efficiency:** The theoretical linear scaling is empirically verified.
 - **Training Cost:** Delethink completes each RL step faster (215s vs. 248.5s for LongCoT-RL 24K). At an average thinking length of 94K tokens, LongCoT-RL is estimated to cost 27 H100-months, compared to just 7 H100-months for Delethink.
 - **Throughput:** Delethink maintains a constant token generation throughput (8,500 tokens/sec/H100) regardless of thinking length, whereas LongCoT-RL's throughput steadily declines as context grows.
 - **FLOPs Crossover:** While Delethink might be slightly slower for very short sequences (below ~30K tokens due to re-encoding overhead), its linear scaling rapidly makes it more efficient than LongCoT's quadratic scaling at longer lengths (e.g., 17x reduction at 1 million tokens).
- **Context Size Ablation:** Delethink demonstrates robustness even with smaller chunk sizes. A C=4K chunk size (with m=2K) performs comparably to C=8K, and even C=2K (m=1K) still shows improvement over the base model, suggesting potential for low-memory environments.
- **Scaling Delethink to 96K:** Expanding the iteration cap to support 96K thinking tokens, Delethink continues to surpass its 24K counterpart and even matches or exceeds a test-time-scaled LongCoT-RL. Average trace lengths reach up to 42K, confirming effective utilization of the larger budget.
- **Zero-Shot Markovian Thinking:** A key insight into Delethink's success is the observation that off-the-shelf reasoning LLMs (R1-Distill 1.5B, GPT-OSS 120B, Qwen3 30B-A3B) already exhibit strong "zero-shot Markovian Thinking." When Delethink Tracing is applied without any specific training, these models can recover or even exceed their standard LongCoT performance. This provides a strong, positive initialization for RL training.
- **Problem Coverage:** Delethink and LongCoT solve largely the same set of problems, indicating that no specific problem-solving ability is lost in the Markovian paradigm.
- **Stress Testing:** Even on tasks like CrossWordBench, which intuitively require "live memory" of previously found words, Delethink remains competitive, suggesting LLMs can adapt to generate Markovian solutions even when the environment is designed to be challenging.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Markovian Thinker" paradigm, instantiated by Delethink, represents a significant leap forward in the scalability and efficiency of reasoning LLMs, with profound implications for future AI development:

- **Unlocking Long-Horizon Reasoning:** The most critical impact is the demonstrated ability to achieve **linear compute and constant memory** for arbitrarily long reasoning chains. This fundamentally breaks the quadratic scaling barrier that has limited LongCoT applications. It opens the door for LLMs to "think" for hundreds of thousands, or even millions, of tokens, enabling them to tackle far more complex, multi-step problems that require extensive deliberation.
- **Redefining the RL Environment as a Lever:** The paper highlights that the RL environment itself, often treated as a fixed component, can be a powerful and under-explored lever for progress. By redesigning the MDP rather than solely focusing on model architecture or training algorithms, new efficiencies and capabilities can be unlocked.
- **Architecture-Agnostic Scalability:** Delethink's architecture-agnostic nature is a major advantage. It means that existing, high-performing attention-based LLMs can immediately benefit from these efficiencies without requiring costly and complex architectural overhauls. This accelerates the deployment of scalable reasoning models.
- **Validation for Efficient Architectures:** By proving that effective reasoning can occur with a bounded context via textual state passing, Delethink provides strong empirical support for the potential of inherently linear-scaling architectures (e.g., Mamba, Longformer, sparse attention) in reasoning tasks. The work suggests that these architectures might find even greater success when integrated with a Markovian thinking approach, as it aligns with their core design principles.
- **Enhanced Practicality and Accessibility:** The drastic reduction in training and inference costs (e.g., 7 H100-months vs. 27 H100-months for 96K thinking) makes advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible to researchers and practitioners with limited compute budgets. This democratizes the development and deployment of sophisticated LLMs.
- **New Research Avenues:**
 - **Abstract Reasoning:** The forced summarization into a constant-size textual state might encourage LLMs to develop more abstract or compressed forms of reasoning, mirroring how humans often remember key insights rather than every detail of a thought process.
 - **Human-like Cognition:** The "chunking" mechanism aligns with cognitive models of human memory and problem-solving, where intermediate thoughts are processed and summarized.
 - **KV-State MDPs:** The paper alludes to future work on defining RL states directly in terms of KV cache entries, which could further optimize Markovian Thinking at the kernel level.

In conclusion, "The Markovian Thinker" paradigm is not merely an incremental improvement; it is a conceptual breakthrough that fundamentally re-imagines how LLMs can perform long-horizon reasoning. By demonstrating that efficient, scalable, and architecture-agnostic reasoning is possible, this work paves the way for a new generation of LLMs capable of tackling problems of unprecedented complexity.
