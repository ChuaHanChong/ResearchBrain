# Cosmos-Transfer1: Conditional World Generation with Adaptive Multimodal Control

> [!summary] Summary
> Cosmos-Transfer1 is a diffusion-based world model that adds per-modality ControlNet branches (blur, edge, depth, segmentation, or HDMap/LiDAR for driving) to Cosmos-Predict1, fused via a spatiotemporal control map that weights each modality per pixel and frame. This adaptive fusion boosts controllability and quality for Sim2Real and AV data generation, and scales to real-time 5-second-video generation on a GB200 NVL72 rack.

## Problem

- Prior spatial-control methods for diffusion video models use fixed, uniform conditioning weights, so they cannot favor different modalities at different spatial regions or time instances within one generation.
- Sim-to-real transfer for robotics and driving still suffers a large domain gap, and existing GAN- or ControlNet-based approaches struggle to preserve task-relevant structure (robot dynamics, lane layout) while diversifying appearance.
- Prior world models are compute-heavy at inference, generating tens of thousands of tokens per video, which blocks real-time or interactive Physical AI applications.

## Method

- ==Adaptive multimodal ControlNet==: a separate control branch (three transformer blocks, zero-initialized linear layers) is trained per modality on top of frozen ==Cosmos-Predict1==, then fused only at inference time.
- ==Spatiotemporal control map==: a per-pixel, per-frame weight tensor over modalities that lets foreground and background regions draw differently on Vis, Edge, Depth, Segmentation, HDMap, or LiDAR signals, derived manually, heuristically, or via a ==SalientObject== VLM-based module.
- Real-time parallel inference: ==data parallelism== on non-attention layers plus ==attention head-parallelism== with all-to-all collectives across GB200 NVL72 GPUs to shard the 56K-token video sequence for low-latency generation, forming **Cosmos-Transfer1**.

## Results

- On the 600-example TransferBench, the uniform-weight multimodal model reaches the best Quality Score (**8.54**) versus single-modality models (**5.48-6.51**), while ranking only second in Vis (**0.87**) and Edge (**0.20**) alignment.
- Spatiotemporal weighting shows strong correlation between per-region modality weight and alignment metric, e.g. foreground Vis weight to Blur SSIM (r=**0.93**) and background Depth weight to si-RMSE (r=**-0.92**).
- Scaling from 1 to 64 B200 GPUs gives about **40x** diffusion speedup, cutting end-to-end 5-second-video generation time from **141.7s** to **4.2s**, reaching real-time throughput.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling modality-specific ControlNet training from inference-time fusion lets each control signal use its own data source and be added or removed without retraining, a reusable pattern for multimodal conditioning.
> - Dense structural modalities (Vis, Edge) trade diversity for fidelity while sparse modalities (Depth, Segmentation) trade fidelity for diversity, so region-adaptive weighting is a general lever for balancing these two objectives.
> - World-generation models are lightweight in parameters but token-heavy per inference call, so scaling laws and parallelism strategies for real-time use differ fundamentally from autoregressive LLM serving.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Cosmos-Transfer1 strategically positions itself at the intersection of several critical research areas:

- **Visual Domain Transfer:** The work builds upon extensive research in visual domain transfer, which aims to convert abstract visual representations (like segmentation maps or sketches) into photorealistic images or videos. Previous efforts have shown success in image synthesis (e.g., generating images from segmentation maps) and, more recently, video synthesis. Cosmos-Transfer1 advances this by integrating multiple diverse modalities (segmentation, depth, edge, blur visual, HDMap, LiDAR) as control signals, enabling more comprehensive and nuanced transformations, particularly in dynamic video contexts.

- **Spatial Control for Diffusion Models:** Diffusion models have revolutionized text-to-image and text-to-video generation, but fine-grained spatial control remains a challenge. This paper directly addresses this by extending the highly successful ControlNet framework (Zhang et al., 2023) to Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architectures, specifically to video generation. The key innovation lies in its *adaptive multimodal control* scheme, which moves beyond simple uniform weighting of control signals. By allowing spatially and temporally varying weights for different modalities, Cosmos-Transfer1 offers a more sophisticated and flexible approach to controlling the generation process, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in guided synthesis.

- **Enhancing Simulation with Generative Models (Physical AI/Sim2Real):** A major application driver for Cosmos-Transfer1 is bridging the "synthetic-to-real" (Sim2Real) domain gap, especially pertinent to Physical AI systems like robots and autonomous vehicles. Traditional simulators provide structured data (e.g., precise 3D geometry, semantic labels) but often struggle to render visually realistic scenes that capture the complexity of the real world. Generative AI, and specifically diffusion models, are increasingly leveraged to augment or enhance simulated environments. Cosmos-Transfer1 exemplifies this trend by taking simulator-native information (depth, segmentation, HD maps, LiDAR) and "transferring" it into diverse, photorealistic videos, thus creating more ecologically valid data for training and testing AI agents. This capability is vital for accelerating development in high-stakes applications where real-world data collection is expensive, dangerous, or impractical.

- **World Models and Foundation Models:** The grounding of Cosmos-Transfer1 within NVIDIA's "Cosmos World Foundation Model Platform" places it within the broader paradigm of developing large-scale, general-purpose AI models that can simulate, predict, and ultimately understand complex dynamic environments. As a *conditional generation* component, Cosmos-Transfer1 allows for the controlled instantiation of different world states or scenarios, making these powerful world models more interactive and applicable to specific tasks. This work represents a significant step towards creating AI systems that can reason about and manipulate virtual worlds with high fidelity and control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of Cosmos-Transfer1 is to establish a **conditional world generation model** that can produce highly controllable and realistic world simulations from diverse, multimodal spatial control inputs.

The motivations behind this objective are multifaceted:

- **Mitigating the Synthetic-to-Real Domain Gap:** A primary driver is to address the significant challenge of the domain gap between synthetic data (from CG-based simulators) and real-world data. While simulators offer easily accessible structured information (depth, segmentation), their visual realism often falls short. Cosmos-Transfer1 aims to leverage these structural cues to generate photorealistic videos, preserving critical scene structure and semantics while enhancing visual fidelity, thereby making simulated data more useful for real-world applications (Sim2Real).

- **Enabling Highly Controllable World Generation:** Existing generative models often lack precise control over the output. Cosmos-Transfer1 seeks to overcome this limitation by introducing a novel *adaptive multimodal control* scheme. This allows users to specify exactly how much influence each modality should have at different spatial locations and time instances, providing unprecedented fine-grained control over the generated content. This level of control is essential for targeted data generation and scenario synthesis.

- **Supporting Physical AI Applications:** The research is strongly motivated by the needs of Physical AI, particularly in robotics and autonomous driving. These fields require vast quantities of diverse and realistic data for training and testing. Cosmos-Transfer1 aims to provide a tool for:
 - **Robotics Sim2Real:** Generating diverse, realistic environments for training robot manipulation policies while preserving critical robot dynamics and object interactions.
 - **Autonomous Vehicle Data Enrichment:** Amplifying real-world edge cases and systematically exploring challenging, rare scenarios (e.g., varying weather, lighting, unexpected events) from structured inputs like HD maps and LiDAR, which are crucial for enhancing the robustness and safety of self-driving systems.

- **Accelerating Research and Development:** By open-sourcing its models and code, NVIDIA aims to foster community engagement and accelerate research in conditional world generation and Physical AI, contributing to collective progress in these critical areas.

- **Demonstrating Practical Scalability:** A practical motivation is to demonstrate the real-time inference capabilities of such a complex generative model. By showcasing deployment on advanced NVIDIA hardware (GB200 NVL72 rack), the paper highlights the feasibility of integrating these powerful AI tools into operational workflows.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Cosmos-Transfer1 is engineered as a **diffusion-based multimodal controllable world generator**, building upon and extending existing powerful architectures.

- **Foundation on Diffusion Transformers (DiT) and ControlNet:** The model's foundation is "Cosmos-Predict1," a Diffusion Transformer-based (DiT-based) world model. To introduce conditional control, Cosmos-Transfer1 integrates a novel ControlNet design, inspired by recent advancements that extend ControlNet from UNet-based models to DiT architectures.
 - **Modular Control Branches:** For each distinct input modality (e.g., blur visual, Canny edge, depth, segmentation, HDMap, LiDAR), a separate "control branch" is added to the base DiT model. These branches consist of transformer blocks, initialized by inheriting weights from the base model, and their outputs are passed through zero-initialized linear layers before being added back to the main generation branch.
 - **Separate Training Strategy:** Crucially, individual control branches are trained *separately* while the weights of the pre-trained Cosmos-Predict1 base model are kept frozen. This "divide and conquer" approach offers several benefits:
 - **Memory Efficiency:** Reduces memory footprint during training, critical for large-scale video data.
 - **Data Flexibility:** Allows training with different datasets for different modalities, accommodating varying data availability or pairing complexities.
 - **Inference Flexibility:** Enables easy addition or removal of modalities at inference time without retraining the entire model.

- **Adaptive Spatiotemporal Multimodal Control:** This is the central technical innovation of Cosmos-Transfer1. It introduces a **spatiotemporal control map** (`w`), a tensor that assigns a unique weight to each modality at every spatial location (pixel) and every time instance (frame) within the video.
 - **Weighted Feature Fusion:** The activations from each control branch are element-wise multiplied by their corresponding spatiotemporal weight from `w` before being channeled back into the main generation branch. A higher weight signifies greater influence of that modality on the generated output at that specific location and time.
 - **Derivation of Control Maps:** The control maps can be: (1) manually designed, (2) heuristically derived based on prior knowledge (e.g., foreground/background distinctions), or (3) learned by a separate neural module. The paper demonstrates a "SalientObject" algorithm that uses a Visual Language Model (VLM) and segmentation masks to create foreground/background control regions, assigning different modalities (e.g., visual/edge for foreground, depth/segmentation for background) with varying weights.
 - **Normalization:** To maintain stability, if the sum of modality weights at any spatiotemporal site exceeds one, they are normalized to ensure they sum up to one.

- **Training Modalities and Datasets:**
 - **General Model (Cosmos-Transfer1-7B):** Post-trained from Cosmos-Predict1-7B-Video2World using a high-quality finetuning dataset. Modalities include: Blur Visual (bilateral blur), Edge (Canny edges), Depth (from DepthAnything2), and Segmentation (GroundingDino + SAM2). Data augmentation techniques are applied (randomizing blur parameters, Canny thresholds).
 - **Autonomous Vehicle Specific Model (Cosmos-Transfer1-7B-Sample-AV):** Post-trained from Cosmos-Predict1-7B-Video2World-Sample-AV (a finetuned version of the general model on dash cam videos) and a newly curated, high-quality "Real Driving Scene HQ (RDS-HQ)" dataset. This dataset includes 360 hours of 360-degree video, 10 Hz LiDAR scans, HD map, and 3D bounding box annotations. Modalities include: HDMap (detailed road/object annotations) and LiDAR (temporally interpolated and projected 3D point clouds).
 - **Upscaling ControlNet (Cosmos-Transfer1-7B-4KUpscaler):** An additional ControlNet trained to upscale generated videos from 720p to 4K resolution, using corrupted high-resolution video crops as input and patch-based generation for seamless output.
 - **Computational Resources:** Training each control branch required 1024 NVIDIA H100 GPUs for 2 to 4 weeks.

- **Real-time Inference Strategy:** To achieve real-time generation, the model leverages the NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 rack (36 Grace CPUs, 72 Blackwell GPUs). A specialized parallelism strategy combines pure data parallelism for non-attention layers with head-parallelism for attention layers. The workload for positive and negative prompt-based denoising (classifier-free guidance) is split across GPU groups, allowing a 5-second 720p video to be generated in 4.2 seconds using 64 B200 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Cosmos-Transfer1 was comprehensive, utilizing a curated "TransferBench" dataset (600 examples across robotics, driving, and everyday life scenes) and a suite of metrics covering adherence to control, generation diversity, and overall quality.

- **Unimodal vs. Multimodal Control (Uniform Weights):**
 - **Specialization of Unimodal Models:** Single-modality control models excel in aligning with their specific input. For instance, Cosmos-Transfer1-7B [Vis] achieved the highest Blur SSIM (0.96), and [Edge] achieved the best Edge F1 (0.28). This confirms that each modality effectively guides its corresponding aspect of generation.
 - **Fidelity-Diversity Trade-off:** Denser structural information from modalities like Blur Visual and Edge tends to yield higher fidelity but lower diversity. Conversely, sparser cues from Depth and Segmentation provide greater generation freedom, leading to higher diversity.
 - **Superiority of Multimodal Fusion:** The full "Cosmos-Transfer1-7B Uniform Weights" model, despite assigning lower individual weights (0.25) to each modality, achieved the *highest overall Quality Score* (8.54) and the best depth reconstruction, while maintaining strong performance across other metrics. This empirically validates the core hypothesis that integrating complementary information from multiple modalities leads to a more balanced, accurate, and high-quality output.

- **Effectiveness of Spatiotemporal Control Maps:**
 - **Granular Control Validation:** Experiments using the "SalientObject" algorithm, which applies different modalities with varying weights to foreground and background regions, demonstrated highly granular control. Increasing the foreground visual weight significantly improved foreground Blur SSIM (Pearson correlation 0.93), and similarly for background depth (Pearson correlation -0.92 for si-RMSE).
 - **Targeted Fidelity vs. Diversity:** By conditioning the foreground with high-fidelity modalities (Edge, Vis) and the background with higher-diversity modalities (Depth, Segmentation), the model successfully maintained close resemblance for salient objects while allowing greater creative freedom and diversity in the background. Swapping these regional controls demonstrated a corresponding shift in fidelity and diversity characteristics.

- **Case Study: Robotics Sim2Real Data Generation:**
 - Cosmos-Transfer1 significantly improved the photorealism and diversity of simulated robotics data from NVIDIA Isaac Lab.
 - **Enhanced Robot Fidelity:** Multimodal control settings, particularly those combining Edge, Segmentation, and Vis with custom weights on the foreground robot region, demonstrably better preserved robot shape, reduced artifacts, and resulted in superior overall video quality compared to single-modal controls. Setting 2 (Edge for foreground, Seg for background) yielded the highest Quality Score (10.42) and strong foreground object consistency (FG Mask mIoU 0.63).
 - This confirms the model's utility in bridging the sim-to-real gap by creating diverse yet physically plausible training data for robot manipulation tasks.

- **Case Study: Autonomous Driving Data Enrichment:**
 - **Improved Scene Realism and Consistency:** Combining Depth and Segmentation inputs restored crucial road markings and generated more plausible vehicle orientations than single modalities. Similarly, fusing HDMap and LiDAR with Cosmos-Transfer1-7B-Sample-AV significantly enhanced the realism of lane layouts and overall scene generation.
 - **Quantitative Advantages:** The combined HDMap+LiDAR model achieved the highest Lane mIoU (51.55) while maintaining competitive 3D-Bbox mAP and reprojection error, highlighting its ability to integrate diverse geometric and semantic cues for superior scene consistency.
 - **Scenario Generation and Diversity:** The model demonstrated the ability to generate diverse driving scenarios by simply altering text prompts (e.g., fog, golden hour, heavy snowfall, fire) while preserving the underlying road layout and object positions dictated by HDMap and LiDAR, showcasing its value for amplifying safety-critical corner cases.
 - **Generalization to Synthetic Data:** The model, trained on real LiDAR data, successfully generalized to simulated LiDAR from NVIDIA Omniverse Sensor RTX, demonstrating its potential to visually enrich physically accurate simulated driving environments.

- **Real-time Inference Performance:**
 - NVIDIA successfully demonstrated real-time inference, generating a 5-second 720p video in just 4.2 seconds using 64 B200 GPUs on an NVIDIA GB200 NVL72 rack. This represents a roughly 40x speedup from a single GPU, showcasing the practical deployability of Cosmos-Transfer1 at scale.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Cosmos-Transfer1 represents a significant leap in controllable video generation, with profound implications for Physical AI and broader generative AI research.

- **Paradigm Shift in Controllable Generation:** The introduction of adaptive multimodal control fundamentally changes how users can interact with and guide generative models. By enabling fine-grained, spatially and temporally varying influence of diverse input modalities, Cosmos-Transfer1 moves beyond coarse-grained control, offering unprecedented precision and flexibility for creative and scientific applications alike. This will likely inspire future research into more sophisticated control mechanisms for generative AI.

- **Transformative for Sim2Real in Physical AI:** This is arguably the most impactful contribution. By providing a robust method to generate highly realistic and diverse data from structured simulator outputs, Cosmos-Transfer1 offers a powerful solution to the perennial "synthetic-to-real" domain gap problem.
 - **Robotics:** It can accelerate robot learning by generating vast, varied, and photorealistic training data for manipulation tasks, greatly reducing the cost and complexity of real-world data collection and improving the robustness of learned policies.
 - **Autonomous Driving:** It addresses the critical challenge of rare and safety-critical scenarios by allowing systematic generation of visual variations (weather, lighting, events) conditioned on precise geometric and semantic data (HD maps, LiDAR). This capability is vital for comprehensive testing, training, and ultimately, safer deployment of autonomous vehicles.

- **Enhanced Data Augmentation and Synthesis:** Beyond Sim2Real, Cosmos-Transfer1 offers a versatile platform for general data augmentation and content creation. Researchers and developers in various fields can leverage its capabilities to generate synthetic datasets with controlled characteristics, addressing data scarcity or biases and enabling new forms of creative expression.

- **Practical Scalability and Deployment:** The demonstration of real-time inference on NVIDIA's advanced hardware (GB200 NVL72) is a crucial step towards practical deployment. It validates that these powerful, large-scale generative models can be operated efficiently in production environments, making them accessible for real-world applications where speed is paramount. This also highlights NVIDIA's holistic approach to AI, integrating cutting-edge research with powerful hardware solutions.

- **Catalyst for Future World Models:** As a component of the "Cosmos World Foundation Model Platform," Cosmos-Transfer1 significantly advances the vision of general-purpose "world models." Its ability to generate controllable, high-fidelity simulations from diverse inputs makes these foundational models more interactive and applicable, paving the way for AI systems that can robustly understand, predict, and interact with complex physical environments.

- **Community Empowerment through Open Source:** NVIDIA's decision to open-source the models and code is a significant contribution to the broader AI community. It lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, fostering innovation, collaboration, and rapid progress in conditional world generation and Physical AI.
