# Unleashing the Potential of All Test Samples: Mean-Shift Guided Test-Time Adaptation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Xi'an Jiaotong University developed Mean-Shift Guided Test-Time Adaptation (MS-TTA), a training-free method that refines Visual-Language Model feature embeddings using Mean-Shift clustering to leverage all test samples. MS-TTA achieved an average accuracy of 69.48% on cross-domain benchmarks with a ViT-B/16 backbone, outperforming previous methods by 0.81%, and demonstrated improved robustness on out-of-distribution datasets.

## Problem

- Visual-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP experience significant performance degradation when encountering distribution shifts at test time, despite strong zero-shot generalization.
- Current training-free Test-Time Adaptation (TTA) methods for VLMs rely on CLIP's original feature space and selectively utilize only high-confidence samples, overlooking the potential of "low-quality" data.
- This selective sample usage and dependence on the original feature space limit adaptation effectiveness and can be sensitive to noisy pseudo-labels under strong distribution shifts.

## Method

- Introduce ==MS-TTA (Mean-Shift Guided Test-Time Adaptation)==, a training-free framework that refines feature embeddings and leverages all test samples for adaptation.
- Employ a single-step ==kNN Mean-Shift operator== to refine initial CLIP embeddings, creating "mean-shifted embeddings" that are more compact and discriminative.
- Utilize a ==dynamic cache== to store high-confidence mean-shifted embeddings and their pseudo-labels, which are then combined with original CLIP logits for final, robust predictions.

## Results

- MS-TTA achieved **69.48%** average accuracy on cross-domain benchmarks (ViT-B/16), surpassing BoostAdapter by **0.81%**, with notable gains of **+3.99%** on EuroSAT.
- The method demonstrated robust performance on out-of-distribution benchmarks, achieving **65.65%** average OOD accuracy (ViT-B/16), outperforming previous approaches.
- t-SNE visualizations showed that mean-shifted embeddings have significantly more compact and distinct clusters, improving class separability and resolving ambiguities between challenging classes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual-Language Models' original feature space is not always optimal for test-time adaptation and can be enhanced for better class separability and compactness.
> - All test samples, including those initially deemed "low-quality" or low-confidence, can be refined and contribute positively to the adaptation process.
> - Mean-Shift clustering provides an efficient, training-free method to refine feature embeddings, improving their discriminability and robustness to distribution shifts.
