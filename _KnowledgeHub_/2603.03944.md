# Spatial Causal Prediction in Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the National University of Singapore and collaborators introduce Spatial Causal Prediction (SCP), a novel task and benchmark, SCP-Bench, designed to evaluate AI models' ability to infer unseen past or predict future spatial states and their causal relationships from video. Evaluations reveal a substantial gap between state-of-the-art multimodal large language models and human performance, highlighting causal reasoning as a primary limitation and showing that model scaling and providing textual causal scaffolds enhance capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing spatial intelligence research predominantly focuses on the perceptual understanding of *observed* static or dynamic scenes.
- Current AI systems lack the ability to infer *unseen* past or anticipate *future* spatial states, and to understand the underlying causal relationships driving these dynamics beyond immediate observations.
- There was no comprehensive benchmark to systematically evaluate models on dynamic, causal, and predictive spatial understanding.

## Method

- Formalized ==Spatial Causal Prediction (SCP)== as a multi-choice question-answering task that requires models to reason beyond directly visible video content.
- Constructed ==SCP-Bench==, a benchmark comprising 2,500 QA pairs across 1,181 video clips, designed with 8 question types, ==2 causal directions (backward/forward)==, 4 perspective settings (single/multi-view), and diverse scene categories.
- Developed a ==semi-automatic pipeline== leveraging GPT-5 for initial content generation (dense captions, QA candidates) followed by rigorous, multi-round human verification and refinement to ensure high-quality and consistent annotations.

## Results

- The top-performing MLLM, GPT-5, achieved **66.24%** accuracy on SCP-Bench, significantly trailing human performance of **89.61%**, demonstrating a considerable **23.37%** gap in unseen spatio-temporal causal prediction.
- Open-source models, such as Qwen3-VL-235B (**61.04%**), exhibited performance comparable to, and occasionally surpassed, proprietary models, while spatially specialized models often underperformed general-purpose MLLMs.
- Models struggled particularly with multi-view prediction and forward prediction tasks, and showed limited temporal extrapolation benefits across short, mid, and long prediction horizons, maintaining an average accuracy of **46.8%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial causal reasoning, rather than pure visual perception, is the primary bottleneck preventing current MLLMs from accurately predicting unseen spatial changes.
> - MLLMs show limited ability to leverage temporal continuity or inherently understand causal dynamics, as indicated by minimal performance changes when processing single frames versus multi-frame video or when input videos are temporally inverted.
> - Model scaling consistently yields performance improvements, and providing 'causal scaffolds'—specifically textual descriptions of future spatial states—offers the most substantial gains for enhancing causal prediction capabilities.
