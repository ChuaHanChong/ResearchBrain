# Spatial Causal Prediction in Video

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the National University of Singapore and collaborators introduce Spatial Causal Prediction (SCP), a novel task and benchmark, SCP-Bench, designed to evaluate AI models' ability to infer unseen past or predict future spatial states and their causal relationships from video. Evaluations reveal a substantial gap between state-of-the-art multimodal large language models and human performance, highlighting causal reasoning as a primary limitation and showing that model scaling and providing textual causal scaffolds enhance capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing spatial intelligence research predominantly focuses on the perceptual understanding of *observed* static or dynamic scenes.
- Current AI systems lack the ability to infer *unseen* past or anticipate *future* spatial states, and to understand the underlying causal relationships driving these dynamics beyond immediate observations.
- There was no comprehensive benchmark to systematically evaluate models on dynamic, causal, and predictive spatial understanding.

## Method

- Formalized ==Spatial Causal Prediction (SCP)== as a multi-choice question-answering task that requires models to reason beyond directly visible video content.
- Constructed ==SCP-Bench==, a benchmark comprising 2,500 QA pairs across 1,181 video clips, designed with 8 question types, ==2 causal directions (backward/forward)==, 4 perspective settings (single/multi-view), and diverse scene categories.
- Developed a ==semi-automatic pipeline== leveraging GPT-5 for initial content generation (dense captions, QA candidates) followed by rigorous, multi-round human verification and refinement to ensure high-quality and consistent annotations.

## Results

- The top-performing MLLM, GPT-5, achieved **66.24%** accuracy on SCP-Bench, significantly trailing human performance of **89.61%**, demonstrating a considerable **23.37%** gap in unseen spatio-temporal causal prediction.
- Open-source models, such as Qwen3-VL-235B (**61.04%**), exhibited performance comparable to, and occasionally surpassed, proprietary models, while spatially specialized models often underperformed general-purpose MLLMs.
- Models struggled particularly with multi-view prediction and forward prediction tasks, and showed limited temporal extrapolation benefits across short, mid, and long prediction horizons, maintaining an average accuracy of **46.8%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Spatial causal reasoning, rather than pure visual perception, is the primary bottleneck preventing current MLLMs from accurately predicting unseen spatial changes.
> - MLLMs show limited ability to leverage temporal continuity or inherently understand causal dynamics, as indicated by minimal performance changes when processing single frames versus multi-frame video or when input videos are temporally inverted.
> - Model scaling consistently yields performance improvements, and providing 'causal scaffolds'—specifically textual descriptions of future spatial states—offers the most substantial gains for enhancing causal prediction capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Spatial reasoning, defined as the ability to comprehend spatial relationships, causality, and dynamic evolution, is a foundational aspect of human intelligence and is critical for real-world artificial intelligence applications such as autonomous driving and robotics. Prior research in this area has made progress by developing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) that integrate the reasoning capabilities of large language models with the perceptual strength of vision encoders.

Existing work primarily focuses on two main areas:
1. **Static Spatial Reasoning:** Early efforts largely concentrated on analyzing object layouts and spatial relationships within static images or from multiple viewpoints (e.g., 3DSRBench, OmniSpatial, Spatial457).
2. **Spatio-Temporal Reasoning:** More recent studies extended this to dynamic sequences or videos (e.g., VLM4D, STI-Bench, DSI-Bench), aiming to understand visual attributes, positions, and relations within observed scenes.

However, a significant limitation of these existing benchmarks and models is their predominant focus on *perceptual understanding of visible spatio-temporal information*. They primarily assess whether models can reason about what is *already observable* in the input. This approach overlooks a crucial aspect of real-world spatial intelligence: the capacity to predict future spatial evolution and infer latent causal relations from dynamic observations, extending reasoning *beyond what is visible*.

This paper introduces **Spatial Causal Prediction (SCP)** as a new task paradigm that specifically addresses this gap. By challenging models to reason about unseen past or future spatial states based on partial observations and underlying causal mechanisms, it aims to advance spatial intelligence from merely interpreting observed scenes to actively predicting and inferring unobserved dynamics. The work positions itself as a critical step towards endowing AI systems with a more complete physical world understanding, analogous to human intuitive physics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to advance the field of spatial reasoning in multimodal AI by moving beyond the interpretation of visible scenes to the prediction and inference of unseen spatial causal outcomes. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and is structured around three specific research questions:

**Key Motivations:**

1. **Bridging the Gap to Human Intelligence:** Human intelligence inherently involves understanding how spatial configurations evolve over time and inferring the causes and consequences of spatial changes, even when not directly observed. Current AI models, despite advancements in visual understanding, largely lack this predictive and causal spatial reasoning capability, limiting their applicability in dynamic, interactive environments.
2. **Enabling Real-World Applications:** Applications like autonomous driving, robotics, and embodied AI agents require systems that can not only perceive their immediate surroundings but also anticipate future states and understand the causal links between actions and spatial outcomes. For instance, an autonomous vehicle needs to predict the future trajectory of other agents, and a robot needs to infer how its actions will spatially affect objects. Existing benchmarks, being largely confined to visible spatio-temporal understanding, do not adequately prepare models for such predictive and causal demands.
3. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** Current benchmarks, while valuable for assessing visual perception and frame-level understanding, often overlook causal dependencies and the ability to extrapolate spatial dynamics over time. They tend to focus on static or perceptual tasks, thereby failing to rigorously evaluate a model's capacity to reason under partial observability about events that have not yet occurred or are no longer visible.

**Specific Objectives (Research Questions - RQs):**

To address these motivations, the paper formulates and investigates three key research questions:

- **RQ1: How do current MLLMs perform on SCP?**
 - This objective aims to conduct a comprehensive evaluation of state-of-the-art MLLMs, including both proprietary and open-source models, as well as those specialized in spatial reasoning. The goal is to establish a baseline understanding of current model capabilities and identify performance gaps relative to human intelligence across diverse task types, scenes, and settings within the SCP paradigm.

- **RQ2: What affects their performance?**
 - This objective seeks to diagnose the underlying reasons for observed model performance. It involves analyzing characteristic error patterns and reasoning behaviors to decompose performance bottlenecks. Specifically, it investigates the relative contributions of visual perception and higher-level causal reasoning, examines whether models truly leverage temporal continuity, assesses their sensitivity to causal inversion, and explores the impact of visible range and the necessity of visual information.

- **RQ3: How to improve their SCP capabilities?**
 - This objective focuses on exploring strategies and approaches to enhance MLLMs' performance on SCP. It investigates the effects of scaling model size, the benefits of enriching spatial perception with intermediate representations (e.g., dense video captions, spatial-interaction graphs), and the efficacy of incorporating prior knowledge or explicit reasoning mechanisms (e.g., chain-of-thought, self-thinking, causal prediction scaffolds). The aim is to provide actionable insights for future research and development of more spatially and causally intelligent AI systems.

To support these objectives, the authors introduce **SCP-Bench**, a new benchmark specifically designed to systematically evaluate spatial causal prediction capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the formulation of the Spatial Causal Prediction (SCP) task and the construction of SCP-Bench, a benchmark designed to evaluate this task.

**SCP Task Formulation:**
SCP is defined as a multi-choice Question-Answering (QA) task. Given a video clip with only partial temporal context (i.e., a "cutpoint" that divides the video into visible and unseen parts), a question, and multiple candidate options, the model must select the option that most accurately reflects the underlying spatial causal reasoning. This requires models to infer unseen past or future spatial states.

**SCP-Bench Design:**
To enable systematic and fine-grained evaluation, SCP-Bench is structured along four complementary dimensions:

1. **Question Type:** Eight distinct task categories are defined to capture variations in spatial causal structure:
 - **Object-invariant identification:** e.g., Relative Size (recognizing entities when object size is constant).
 - **Attribute-dynamic reasoning:** e.g., Appearance Order, Relative Speed, Spatial State (probing changes in object attributes under causal evolution).
 - **Interaction-level inference:** e.g., Counting, Planning, Relation (requiring higher-order reasoning over object interactions).
 - **Relative Distance:** How the spatial distance between objects changes over time.
 - **Planning:** Probing short-horizon action tendencies and anticipated movement choices.
 - **Relation:** Concerns the temporal variation of spatial configurations between objects.

2. **Causal Direction:** The task naturally divides into two directions based on the cutpoint:
 - **Backward inference:** Reconstructing prior states based on subsequent visible evidence.
 - **Forward prediction:** Anticipating subsequent scene evolution based on preceding visible evidence.

3. **Perspective Setting:** To account for how a scene is perceived, the benchmark includes:
 - **Single-view:** Egocentric (first-person) and exocentric (third-person) perspectives.
 - **Multi-view:** Pairing a video with a reference image from another viewpoint (e.g., ego-exo, exo-ego, exo-exo).

4. **Scene Diversity:** SCP-Bench includes a broad range of environments to ensure varied visual dynamics and interaction patterns: Artistic Performances, Animal Related, Sports Related, Life Records, Factory/Machine Related, and Driving-view Related.

**SCP-Bench Construction Process:**
A semi-automatic pipeline, combining model-assisted generation with human verification, was developed to construct the benchmark:

1. **Video Source Selection:** Videos were collected from diverse public datasets and platforms, including Ego-Exo4D, HD-EPIC, YouTube-8M, and ActivityNet, chosen for their varied viewpoints and scenarios.
2. **Automatic Clip Screening:** GPT-5 was utilized to expand segments, generate dense temporally aligned captions, filter for portions exhibiting clear spatial change, and propose candidate video clips with explicit timestamps.
3. **Candidate QA Generation:** A structured prompt template was designed to guide GPT-5 in generating challenging and well-controlled QA pairs. This template explicitly encoded the perspective, the unseen temporal window (time dimension), and the spatial question type, ensuring diverse and well-structured questions.
4. **Filtering and Cutpoint Finding:** All generated QA candidates underwent rigorous manual review. Only unambiguous questions, answerable from the clip and genuinely grounded in spatial reasoning, were retained. Annotators precisely determined a "cutpoint" for each approved QA, separating the visible and invisible parts, after which GPT-5 generated distractor options.
5. **Validation and Optimization:** Multiple rounds of manual review were conducted by annotators to refine and correct items. Unanswerable or invalid samples were removed, and fixable issues (e.g., wording, perspectives, temporal alignments) were revised.

The final benchmark comprises 2,500 question-answer pairs across 1,181 high-quality video clips.

**Experimental Approach:**
The research involved extensive experiments on 23 state-of-the-art MLLMs, encompassing proprietary models (e.g., GPT-5, Gemini), open-source models (e.g., Qwen3-VL, InternVL), and spatially specialized models (e.g., Spatial-MLLM, SpaceR). All models were tested using their officially recommended configurations. Accuracy rate served as the primary evaluation metric. A human performance benchmark was also established for comparison. Further analyses included:
- Probing tasks to analyze error patterns and reasoning behaviors.
- Controlled experiments to decouple perception from reasoning.
- Investigations into temporal extrapolation, sensitivity to temporal inversion, and the impact of visible input range.
- Exploration of improvement strategies such as model scaling, chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, self-think reasoning, perception enhancements (dense captions, spatial-interaction graphs), and causal prediction scaffolds (textual, image, and video).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the performance, limitations, and potential improvements of current MLLMs on the Spatial Causal Prediction (SCP) task.

**RQ1: How do current MLLMs perform on SCP?**

- **Significant Human-Model Performance Gap:** Current MLLMs perform substantially below human levels, with the highest-performing model, GPT-5, achieving 66.24% accuracy compared to human performance of 89.61%. This underscores a notable gap in unseen spatio-temporal causal prediction.
- **Leading Models:** GPT-5 attained the highest overall accuracy, followed by Qwen3-VL-235B (61.04%) and InternVL3.5-241B (56.96%).
- **Open-Source vs. Closed-Source:** Large open-source models demonstrated performance comparable to, and in some specific tasks even surpassed, proprietary models. For instance, open-source models performed comparably to GPT-5 in Counting, Planning, Relative Size, Relative Speed, and Spatial State tasks.
- **Task Difficulty:** "Relative Size" was consistently the easiest task, while "Object Relations," "Planning," and "Counting" proved most challenging, requiring more abstract spatial causal reasoning and higher-order object interaction understanding.
- **Perspective and Causal Direction Effects:** Models exhibited greater difficulty with multi-view prediction compared to single-view reasoning. They also performed better when inferring past (backward) events than when predicting future (forward) ones, suggesting that reasoning from known outcomes is less demanding than anticipating unseen consequences.
- **Temporal Extrapolation Horizon:** Model accuracy remained relatively stable across short (0–2s), mid (2–5s), and long (>5s) temporal prediction ranges, averaging around 46.8%. This indicates that current MLLM architectures offer limited benefits for temporal extrapolation and that dynamic frame sampling may mitigate sensitivity to temporal length differences.
- **Causal Consistency:** A case study showed that models could perceive local motion correctly but often generated explanations that violated fundamental physical and causal constraints, highlighting a deficiency in robust causal understanding.

**RQ2: What affects their performance?**

- **Reasoning as the Primary Bottleneck:** Experiments comparing models provided with the "Gold Video" (unseen part for pure perception) versus "Dense Caption Only" (textual reasoning without video) revealed that spatial causal reasoning, rather than raw visual perception, is the main limitation for SCP performance. Providing the unseen part of the video significantly improved accuracy (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B by 7.44%), while relying solely on captions resulted in performance drops.
- **Limited Use of Temporal Continuity:** When comparing performance with only a single frame (cutpoint image) versus multiple frames (visible part of video), models showed a slight improvement with single-frame input. This counterintuitive result suggests that multi-frame inputs might introduce noise rather than effectively providing useful temporal cues, indicating that models primarily rely on static spatial perception.
- **Weak Vision Causal Perception:** Temporally reversing the input video resulted in only minor accuracy degradation (up to 1.96%). This limited sensitivity to temporal inversion suggests that current models have not developed a robust understanding of spatial causality from dynamic visual inputs.
- **Impact of Visible Range:** Models performed best when the answer was directly observable (Gold Video), followed by the full video, with the baseline (only visible part requiring inference) performing the worst. This indicates that MLLMs perform well when SCP demands are minimal but struggle to infer spatial causality beyond direct observation.
- **Indispensability of Visual Information:** Performance dropped significantly when models relied solely on pure textual input or dense captions without video, confirming that visual information is essential for SCP. Textual priors and captions, while offering some compensation, cannot fully capture the richness and fine-grained cues of visual observations.

**RQ3: How to improve their SCP capabilities?**

- **Model Scale-up:** Increasing model size consistently led to substantial performance gains (e.g., Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B improved by 18% over Qwen3-VL-2B). However, the benefits were more pronounced and stable for substantial increases in parameter scale, with smaller scale models sometimes showing fluctuations.
- **Limited Impact of Explicit Reasoning Strategies:**
 - **Vanilla Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Prompting:** Adding "think step by step" yielded modest improvements for some models (e.g., Qwen3-VL +4%), but was inconsistent across architectures and sometimes led to slight degradation.
 - **Self-Think Reasoning:** Enabling official self-think modes generally resulted in slight performance degradation for most models, suggesting that current self-think mechanisms may introduce noise.
- **Perception Enhancement Strategies:** Enriching spatial perception with intermediate representations like dense video captions or spatial-interaction graphs provided only marginal improvements. This indicates that perception-level augmentations alone are insufficient to substantially boost spatial causal reasoning.
- **Causal Prediction Enhancement Strategies (Scaffolds):**
 - **Textual Scaffolds:** Incorporating textual descriptions of future spatial states (generated by GPT-5) consistently yielded the largest performance improvements across all tasks (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B saw a +13.71% gain). This suggests MLLMs are inherently more adept at processing and reasoning over textual information.
 - **Video Scaffolds:** Generated future causal videos provided limited gains, but outperformed image-based scaffolds in dynamic-related tasks due to richer temporal cues.
 - **Image Scaffolds:** Generated future spatial images provided the weakest benefits.
 - *Overall:* Integrating unseen spatial causal scaffolds, particularly in textual form, was identified as a promising direction for enhancing SCP performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes significantly to the field of multimodal AI and cognitive science by addressing a fundamental limitation in current AI systems' understanding of the physical world.

1. **Novel Task Formulation:** The introduction of the Spatial Causal Prediction (SCP) task establishes a new paradigm for evaluating predictive spatial reasoning that extends beyond directly observable scenes. This moves AI research closer to replicating human-like intuitive physics and causal understanding, which is crucial for real-world autonomy.
2. **Comprehensive Benchmark (SCP-Bench):** The development of SCP-Bench provides a high-quality, systematically designed benchmark comprising 2,500 QA pairs across diverse videos, viewpoints, scenes, causal directions, and spatial question categories. This benchmark serves as a rigorous testbed for evaluating models' capacity to perceive, reason about, and predict spatial causal dynamics under partial observability, offering a standardized platform for future research.
3. **In-depth Evaluation and Diagnostic Analysis:** The extensive evaluation of 23 state-of-the-art MLLMs on SCP-Bench offers critical insights into their current capabilities and inherent limitations. The findings highlight:
 - A substantial performance gap between human and model performance.
 - The observation that reasoning, rather than merely perception, constitutes the major bottleneck for SCP.
 - Limited temporal extrapolation capabilities and weak causal grounding in existing models.
 - The effectiveness of model scaling, while showing limitations in explicit reasoning strategies like vanilla CoT and self-thinking.
 - The promise of integrating causal scaffolds, particularly textual ones, for improving predictive performance.
4. **Guidance for Future Research:** By systematically uncovering *when and why* current models fail, the research provides clear directions for developing more causally intelligent and temporally aware MLLMs. This includes focusing on enhancing reasoning mechanisms, developing more robust temporal understanding, and exploring improved methods for incorporating future causal predictions.
5. **Impact on Real-World Applications:** Advancements in SCP capabilities are directly relevant to critical AI applications such as:
 - **Autonomous Driving:** Enabling vehicles to predict the future movements of pedestrians, other vehicles, and environmental changes.
 - **Robotics:** Allowing robots to anticipate the spatial consequences of their actions and understand dynamic object interactions.
 - **Embodied AI:** Equipping agents with a more robust understanding of their physical environment and the ability to plan actions based on predicted spatial outcomes.

Overall, this work not only introduces a challenging new problem and a benchmark to tackle it, but also provides a foundational analysis that is expected to stimulate significant follow-up research in building AI systems with enhanced spatial causal intelligence.
