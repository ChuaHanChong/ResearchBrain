# A-Evolve-Training: Autonomous Post-Training of a 30B Model

> [!summary] Summary
> A-Evolve-Training is an autonomous post-training system that runs the full research loop with no human in the loop: eight identical memory-free full-stack agents per round fork one immutable audited training substrate, launch real GPU jobs, and a constitutionally bounded meta agent rewrites only the next round's rolling search policy. Over four rounds on a 30B Nemotron base it reached 0.86 leaderboard score versus the top human's 0.87, ranking 8th of roughly 4000.

## Problem

- Public autonomous ML research demonstrations run at roughly GPT-2-class 124M budgets, where cheap retries and free sweeps make the closed loop no evidence it scales.
- At frontier scale each trial is a multi-week H200 run, so seed noise becomes binding and the cheap-repeat assumption underpinning prior loop designs collapses.
- Classical AutoML searches coordinates in a fixed hyperparameter grid, and evolutionary code search scores algorithms, neither evolving the training recipe's code and data.

## Method

- ==Immutable reference substrate==: every round forks the same operator-audited default stack into isolated sandboxes, never overwritten by a winner, keeping recipes comparable across rounds.
- ==Homogeneous memory-free workers==: eight identical full-stack agents per round, one a pure baseline anchor for noise calibration, seven on orthogonal axes, no state carried over.
- ==Round-level aggregation== with ==policy-only promotion==: a one-shot collector emits a fixed five-section evidence record; a meta agent under a frozen constitution rewrites only the rolling search policy and dead-end registry.

## Results

- Scores improved monotonically across rounds, **0.80** substrate to **0.82**, **0.84**, **0.85**, then **0.86**, versus the top human submission's **0.87**, rank **8** of about **4000** entries.
- Round 3 proxy exploitation drove internal dev to **0.93** and lifted the weak equation domain from a **~0.65** ceiling to **0.82**, yet external score stayed in the **0.84-0.85** band.
- A downweighting probe cut that domain's internal score without degrading the external target, and round 4's proxy-aware search recovered progress at dev **0.89**, leaderboard **0.86**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asymmetric freedom transfers: freeze the axes that must stay invariant for comparability, spend all degrees of freedom on axes where semantic variation pays.
> - Mirroring a human research team with specialised handoff agents inverts the right design, because compounding mid-states compounds unobserved variance and corrupts the selection signal.
> - Detecting when a development proxy stops being causal evidence is part of the research problem, not a nuisance, once optimisation pressure concentrates on the easiest dimension.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Post-training a large model usually means researchers spend weeks proposing changes to data and training recipes, launching costly runs, and deciding what to keep. At this scale, a failed or noisy trial is not a cheap experiment to repeat. The authors automated that loop. They kept its starting training stack and evaluation setup fixed, so agents could explore changes without making trials incomparable.

In a reported four-round campaign, the loop post-trained a 30B Nemotron model with no human intervention after the initial substrate was authored. On the NVIDIA Nemotron-Reasoning Challenge public leaderboard, it scored 0.86 versus the top submission's 0.87 and ranked 8th among roughly 4,000 entries. That standing is dated June 1, 2026.

Held-out leaderboard score by round. The autonomous loop rises from 0.80 at round 0, the substrate baseline, to 0.86 at round 4. The dashed line marks human #1 (0.87).

The authors present this as a datapoint for autonomous end-to-end post-training at frontier scale. It is not a claim of matching human researchers or a head-to-head human comparison.

### 2. Keep the expensive trials comparable

Workers explore changes to the training recipe and data pipeline. Every round, however, starts from the same operator-audited workspace: base checkpoint, data construction, training and evaluation code. A winning candidate never overwrites this substrate.

Each round uses eight identical, memory-free full-stack workers. One is a baseline anchor for noise calibration, and seven explore assigned axes. Workers run jobs, evaluate checkpoints and report results. A collector summarizes the evidence in a fixed schema. A meta agent then uses that summary to update only the next round's rolling search policy, under an immutable constitution.

The authors call this principle "asymmetric freedom": freeze what makes trials comparable, and allow exploration of what to change. They report that an earlier design with specialized roles did not work. In their account, mid-state handoffs can compound unobserved variance. The paper does not demonstrate savings in tokens, attempts, elapsed time, or compute. Its argument is that careful management of evidence and policy makes the expensive loop workable.

The proposed workflow. An immutable default workspace feeds isolated candidate sandboxes and parallel workers. A reviewer aggregates their findings, and an orchestrator proposes the next-round policy under a constitution. The next round starts from the same workspace.

### 3. When a better dev score misleads

"Dev" is the internal development score, and "LB" is the external leaderboard score. The paper reports no formula for either.

At first, workers pursued apparently weak reasoning domains and treated dev improvements as promising. In the clearest case, overall dev reached 0.93–0.94. The equation domain rose from a long-standing score near 0.65 to as high as 0.82. External performance, however, stayed in the 0.84–0.85 band.

A separate downweighting intervention then lowered the score on that apparent weak domain without degrading external performance. The authors interpret this to mean the domain was not the causal bottleneck. The policy then asked for changes that could lower the proxy while improving the external target, including domain rebalancing and reweighted checkpoint selection.

Round-by-round scores. Dev and LB rise together through round 2 (0.84 each). In round 3, proxy exploitation, dev reaches 0.93 with LB at 0.85. In round 4, proxy-aware search, dev falls to 0.89 while LB reaches 0.86.

These observations support the authors' interpretation that the proxy had stopped reliably indicating what to improve in this performance band. They do not show that the proxy is generally useless, and they do not identify a single cause of the mismatch. The discovery remains unreplicated.

### 4. What the result establishes

The challenge involves logical-reasoning puzzles that require inferring underlying transformation rules, including bit manipulation and algebraic equations. The result shows the loop completed autonomous post-training at this scale and produced a competitive leaderboard result in this one setting.

The evidence has clear limits: one base model, one task family, one external leaderboard, and one unreplicated campaign. A pre-audited, human-authored substrate bounded the search. The authors also report end-to-end runs at 120B and 550B. Because there is no comparable human baseline, they treat those runs only as infrastructure evidence.

### 5. A loop that changes course

The campaign's key result goes beyond a high score after costly trials. The loop changed its search target when its own proxy stopped being informative.
