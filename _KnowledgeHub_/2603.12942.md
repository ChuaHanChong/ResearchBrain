# ReMem-VLA: Empowering Vision-Language-Action Model with Memory via Dual-Level Recurrent Queries

> [!summary] Summary
> ReMem-VLA is a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architecture that integrates dual-level recurrent memory queries and Past Observation Prediction to enable continuous retention of both short-term and long-term historical context for robotic control. The model achieved a 94.5% average success rate on memory-dependent simulation tasks and an 82.5% average success rate on real-world memory-dependent manipulation tasks, substantially outperforming existing VLA baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often operate under a Markovian assumption, primarily conditioning on current observations, which limits their ability to leverage historical context for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Current memory-augmentation strategies for VLAs, such as retrieval, fixed-horizon extensions, or simple recurrence, struggle to robustly capture multi-dimensional memory (e.g., spatial, sequential, visual, episodic, temporal) across varying time scales.
- VLAs frequently fail in manipulation scenarios requiring understanding of temporal causality, sequential steps, or recall of past object states and visual cues due to a lack of a unified, continuous memory mechanism.

## Method

- **ReMem-VLA** introduces ==dual-level recurrent memory queries==: frame-level for short-term memory using ==dense exponential moving average (EMA)== updates, and chunk-level for long-term memory using ==sparse EMA== updates at chunk boundaries.
- The recurrent update path for memory queries is designed to be ==gradient-free==, allowing for stable, deterministic memory propagation without the limitations of truncated backpropagation through time.
- ==Past Observation Prediction (POP)== is incorporated as an auxiliary task, where a lightweight image head reconstructs a past observation, specifically enhancing the model's visual memory retention.
- A ==slot-based streaming training paradigm== is employed to handle variable-length episodes by maintaining persistent recurrent states for each active episode in a fixed pool of slots, ensuring temporal continuity and isolated training.

## Results

- **ReMem-VLA** achieved an average success rate of **94.5%** across four memory-dependent simulation tasks (MemoryBench and Long Horizon Task), outperforming OpenVLA-OFT (**0.75%**), π0.5 (**8.25%**), and MemoryVLA (**1.5%**) by substantial margins.
- The model demonstrated an average success rate of **82.5%** on four distinct real-world memory-dependent manipulation tasks, significantly exceeding baselines like MemoryVLA (**11%**) and π0.5 (**8%**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that the dual-level recurrent queries, the gradient-free recurrent update, and the Past Observation Prediction objective are critical components contributing to the model's superior memory capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The combination of dual-level recurrent memory queries (frame-level and chunk-level) provides complementary memory capabilities, effectively capturing both immediate and long-range temporal dependencies within an episode.
> - Designing the recurrent memory update path to be gradient-free is crucial for stable and effective long-term memory propagation, preventing the degradation often seen with trainable recurrent units and truncated backpropagation.
> - An auxiliary task like Past Observation Prediction significantly boosts specific memory dimensions, such as visual memory, demonstrating a targeted approach to enhance VLA performance on visually-intensive recall tasks.
> - A slot-based streaming batching strategy efficiently trains recurrent VLA policies on variable-length episodes without data padding or truncation, making the training process scalable and robust.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a prominent research area focused on enabling robots to perform manipulation tasks by leveraging pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) and natural language instructions. Traditionally, these models operate under a Markovian assumption, meaning their decisions are primarily conditioned on the current observation and, at most, a very short history window. This paradigm is efficient for certain tasks but exhibits limitations in scenarios requiring continuous understanding of past events, object states, or overall task progression.

Existing approaches to incorporating memory into VLA or visuomotor policies have explored several strategies. These include retrieving relevant information from a memory bank, extending the observational history window, or using sparse representations of past states (e.g., keyframes or point traces). However, each of these methods presents its own challenges. Retrieval-based systems can be susceptible to distractors or the absence of strong cues. Fixed-horizon methods are inherently limited in their ability to retain long-term context beyond a predefined window. Sparse representations may oversimplify environmental states, potentially losing critical details, and their reliability can depend on external foundation models. While some works have introduced recurrent neural network (RNN)-style recurrence into VLAs, their memory capabilities, particularly for long-term dependencies, have not been thoroughly evaluated on memory-dependent tasks, and simple recurrence often struggles with long temporal credit assignment due to truncated backpropagation through time (TBPTT).

This paper addresses these limitations by proposing ReMem-VLA, a novel VLA architecture designed to provide a more comprehensive and unified memory mechanism. It aims to bridge the gap in existing VLA models by enabling them to effectively retain and utilize both short-term and long-term historical context, moving beyond the Markovian assumption for more robust closed-loop robot control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to empower Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models with robust and continuous memory capabilities for closed-loop robot control. The authors observe that typical VLA models, constrained by a Markovian decision-making paradigm, struggle with tasks that necessitate recalling historical context, leading to systematic failures in complex manipulation scenarios.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations regarding the limitations of current VLA models:
- **Neglect of Temporal Causality:** By primarily conditioning on current or short-term historical observations, existing VLAs fail to understand the temporal sequence of events. This makes them "blind" to prior actions and environmental changes, which is crucial for tasks requiring knowledge of what has already occurred or specific object states over time.
- **Need for Multi-Dimensional Memory:** The authors identify several essential memory capabilities that are inadequately addressed in current VLA research, particularly within a single task episode:
 - **Sequential Memory:** The ability to execute multi-step tasks in a correct order. For example, a robot might need to press buttons in a specific sequence.
 - **Visual Memory:** The capacity to leverage past visual cues to inform current decisions. An example is recalling the original position of an object that needs to be returned.
 - **Spatial Memory:** The retention of object locations, which is critical for navigation or precise manipulation over time.
 - **Episodic Memory:** Tracking previously executed actions or task segments, such as knowing how many items have already been moved.
 - **Temporal Memory:** The representation of durations and elapsed time, for instance, holding a pose for a specific period.
- **Limitations of Existing Memory Solutions:** As detailed in the previous section, current approaches for integrating memory into VLAs suffer from specific drawbacks:
 - **Retrieval-based methods** can be misled by irrelevant information or fail when strong retrieval cues are absent.
 - **Fixed-horizon extensions** inevitably discard long-term history once it falls outside the predefined window.
 - **Sparse history representations** often omit important details and rely on the stability of external models.
 - **Simple recurrent mechanisms**, when trained with truncated backpropagation through time (TBPTT) over short windows, are generally ineffective for learning long-range dependencies, as demonstrated by the authors' ablation studies.

The motivation is to overcome these challenges by developing a unified memory mechanism that can continuously extract and accumulate implicit task-relevant information across both short and long temporal horizons within an episode. This aims to enable VLAs to interpret current states in light of complete historical context, thereby deriving more accurate and contextually appropriate actions for complex, memory-dependent manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReMem-VLA is presented as an end-to-end framework for robotic manipulation, designed to integrate memory capabilities into VLA models. Its architecture comprises several key components and a specialized training paradigm.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The ReMem-VLA architecture consists of five primary components:
1. **Frozen VLM Backbone:** The model utilizes Qwen3-VL-2B, a 2-billion parameter vision-language model, as a frozen backbone. This backbone processes RGB observations from multiple camera views and language instructions, generating hidden states.
2. **Task-Specific Learnable Queries:**
 - **Action Queries (Q_action):** These queries are appended to the input sequence and are designed to extract features from the VLM backbone that are pertinent to action generation and manipulation semantics.
 - **Hindsight Queries (Q_img):** These queries are introduced to extract features for the purpose of reconstructing past visual observations, thereby enhancing visual memory.
3. **Dual-Level Recurrent Memory Queries:** This is a core innovation for capturing multi-scale temporal causality.
 - **Frame-level Recurrent Memory Queries (Q_f):** These queries are designed for short-term memory retention. At each timestep, they attend to the VLM hidden states and other queries to extract current-frame features. These features are then propagated to the next timestep through an exponential moving average (EMA) update: $Q^f_t = \beta_f \cdot \tilde{Q}^f_{t-1} + (1 - \beta_f) \cdot Q^f_{t-1}$. This dense, frame-by-frame update supports tracking recent object states and immediate temporal dependencies.
 - **Chunk-level Recurrent Memory Queries (Q_c):** Intended for long-term memory retention, these queries update at a coarser temporal granularity, specifically at chunk boundaries (every $k$ frames). The update rule is similar to the frame-level queries but applied sparsely: $Q^c_t = \beta_c \cdot \tilde{Q}^c_{t-k} + (1 - \beta_c) \cdot Q^c_{t-k}$ if $t \mod k = 0$, and $Q^c_t = Q^c_{t-1}$ otherwise. This sparse update frequency leads to a slower exponential decay, facilitating stable long-horizon memory for retaining initial configurations or overall task progress.
 - **Gradient-Free Recurrent Update Path:** Crucially, the recurrent update path for both frame-level and chunk-level memory queries (which consists of the VLM backbone and the EMA fusion) is designed to be gradient-free. This approach aims to overcome the limitations of truncated backpropagation through time (TBPTT) by ensuring memory propagation through a deterministic forward update. The learning focuses solely on *what* information the memory queries extract and store, rather than *how* the memory itself is propagated.
4. **Transformer-based Connector:** A 12-layer transformer with bidirectional self-attention acts as a bridge between the VLM backbone and the prediction heads. This connector enables comprehensive interaction among all four query types (Q_action, Q_img, Q_f, Q_c), allowing action and hindsight queries to integrate current-frame features with the accumulated temporal context from the recurrent memory queries.
5. **Prediction Heads:**
 - **Action Head:** A diffusion model predicts continuous action chunks (A_t:t+k) conditioned on the action queries via cross-attention. It is trained using a denoising objective (L_action).
 - **Image Head (Past Observation Prediction - POP):** A lightweight ViT-style patch decoder, conditioned on hindsight queries, reconstructs a past observation (o_t-m). This is supervised by an MSE loss (L_image), designed to strengthen visual memory retention.

The overall training objective combines both losses: L_total = L_action + λ_img * L_image.

#### 3.2. Training and Inference Recipe

- **Streaming Slot-Based Batching:** To address the challenges of training recurrent policies over variable-length episodes, the authors propose a slot-based streaming training paradigm. A fixed pool of `B` concurrent slots is maintained, each tracking a different episode. At each training step, one frame is sampled sequentially from each active slot, forming a batch without padding or truncation. The recurrent state for each slot persists throughout its episode, accumulating context. At episode boundaries, the slot's recurrent state is hard-reset to ensure strict isolation and prevent cross-episode information leakage.
- **Inference:** During inference, both frame-level and chunk-level recurrent query tokens are continuously maintained and updated. They are only reset at the beginning of a new episode. The model achieves an inference frequency of 10 Hz, which can be increased to approximately 20 Hz for effective control by reusing predicted action chunks for intermediate steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of ReMem-VLA was validated through extensive experiments in both simulation and real-world robot settings, comparing it against established VLA baselines.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Benchmark:** The evaluation utilized MemoryBench, comprising three memory-dependent tasks: "Put Block Back," "Rearrange Block," and "Reopen Drawer." To rigorously assess long-term memory, an additional "Long Horizon Task" was introduced, exceeding 600 frames and combining aspects of "Rearrange Block" and "Put Block Back." These tasks collectively require spatial, sequential, and logical reasoning memory.
- **Baselines:** ReMem-VLA was compared against OpenVLA-OFT, π0.5, and MemoryVLA (a retrieval-based memory VLA), with all baselines reproduced under the same data and evaluation protocols.
- **Results:** ReMem-VLA achieved an average success rate of 94.5% across the four tasks. This significantly surpassed the baselines: OpenVLA-OFT (0.75%), π0.5 (8.25%), and MemoryVLA (1.5%). Specifically, ReMem-VLA attained 93% on "Put Block Back," 99% on "Rearrange Block," 100% on "Reopen Drawer," and 86% on the "Long Horizon Task," demonstrating robust memory capabilities.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Task Design:** Four real-world manipulation tasks were designed to probe specific memory dimensions beyond spatial memory:
 1. "Water flower for around six seconds": Assesses temporal memory (maintaining a pose for a duration without visual cues).
 2. "Scoop two spoons of rice into pot": Evaluates episodic memory (tracking the number of completed sub-actions).
 3. "Press buttons in sequence, and each for 3 seconds": Requires sequential and temporal memory (pressing specific buttons in order for specific durations).
 4. "Put fruit back to the plate": Tests visual memory (recalling an initial visual scene, specifically the original position of an object).
- **Baselines:** π0.5 and MemoryVLA served as real-world baselines.
- **Results:** ReMem-VLA achieved an average success rate of 82.5% across these memory-dependent real-world tasks. This significantly outperformed MemoryVLA (11%) and π0.5 (8%), confirming its comprehensive memory capabilities in practical robotic scenarios.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Contribution of Dual-Level Recurrent Queries:**
 - Removing all recurrent queries resulted in a substantial drop in memory capability, with an average success rate of 17.75%.
 - Using only frame-level queries achieved 87.75% average success but struggled with long-term memory tasks (e.g., 70% on Long Horizon Task).
 - Using only chunk-level queries yielded 84.5% average success, improving long-term retention but underperforming on tasks requiring short-term memory (e.g., button pressing).
 - The dual-level configuration consistently outperformed single-level variants, achieving 94.5% average success, indicating that both levels provide complementary memory capabilities.
- **Effectiveness of Past Observation Prediction (POP):**
 - POP demonstrated a substantial improvement on visual memory-intensive tasks, such as "Put fruit back to the plate" (from 34% to 82% success rate).
 - Its benefit was limited on tasks less reliant on detailed visual recall, suggesting its targeted enhancement for visual memory.
- **Gradient-Free Recurrent Update Path:**
 - Ablations showed that introducing trainable parameters into the recurrent update path (e.g., using a trainable VLM backbone, GRU, or simple MLP for updates instead of fixed EMA) significantly degraded the model's memory capability. This supports the design choice of a gradient-free, deterministic propagation path.
- **EMA Hyperparameters (β):**
 - Sweeping the EMA coefficient (β) on the Long Horizon Task showed optimal performance at β = 0.5. This suggests a balance between retaining old information and incorporating new, relevant context.
- **Number of Recurrent Memory Queries:**
 - Varying the number of queries indicated that a moderate count (e.g., 128) performed best on the Long Horizon Task. Too few queries limited memory capacity, while too many could introduce redundancy and optimization challenges.
- **Chunk-Level Recurrent Update Interval:**
 - An update interval equal to 1x the action chunk size (30 frames) yielded the best overall performance across MemoryBench tasks, balancing stability and freshness of long-term memory.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic control by directly addressing the prevalent limitation of a Markovian decision-making framework. The introduction of ReMem-VLA with its dual-level recurrent memory queries offers a novel and effective mechanism for endowing VLA models with robust, multi-scale temporal memory.

The key contributions and potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy:** By enabling robots to retain and leverage complete historical context, ReMem-VLA allows for more sophisticated and robust performance on complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks that require spatial, temporal, episodic, sequential, and visual memory. This moves beyond reactive control towards more intelligent, context-aware robotic systems.
- **Unified Memory Mechanism:** The dual-level recurrent query scheme (frame-level for short-term and chunk-level for long-term memory) provides a unified approach to capture dependencies across different temporal scales, overcoming the fragmentation and limitations of prior memory-augmentation strategies.
- **Improved Visual Memory:** The auxiliary objective of Past Observation Prediction specifically addresses the challenge of visual memory retention, enabling the model to recall critical visual details from the past. This can be crucial for tasks involving returning objects to their original positions or tracking visual cues over time.
- **Efficient Training Paradigm:** The proposed slot-based streaming training method offers a practical solution for batch training recurrent VLA models on variable-length episodes without breaking temporal continuity or mixing information across different tasks. This can facilitate more effective and scalable training of memory-augmented policies.
- **Foundation for Future VLA Development:** The findings regarding the effectiveness of gradient-free recurrent paths and the optimal configuration of memory queries provide valuable insights for the design of future memory-aware VLA architectures. This work suggests a path towards integrating memory into frozen VLM backbones without incurring significant computational overhead or requiring extensive re-training.
- **Broader Applicability:** The demonstrated capabilities in both simulation and real-world settings indicate that ReMem-VLA could be applied to a wider range of challenging robotic tasks, potentially reducing the need for explicit state tracking or complex planning algorithms by implicitly encoding task progress and environmental history.

A noted limitation is the model's current lack of training on large-scale robot datasets, which might impact its generalization ability. However, the work lays a foundation for future research to either pretrain ReMem-VLA on such datasets or integrate its memory mechanism into existing large-scale pretrained VLA models, potentially unlocking even greater capabilities for generalist robot policies.
