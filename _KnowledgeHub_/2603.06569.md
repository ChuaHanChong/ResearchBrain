# Penguin-VL: Exploring the Efficiency Limits of VLM with LLM-based Vision Encoders

> [!summary] Summary
> The Tencent AI Lab's Penguin-VL framework constructs compact Vision-Language Models (2B and 8B parameters) by initializing the vision encoder directly from a text-only Large Language Model, rather than traditional contrastive vision models. This approach achieves leading performance across diverse image and video understanding benchmarks, including document, chart, and temporal video reasoning, while using significantly less pre-training data.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are often very large, hindering deployment on resource-constrained mobile and edge devices.
- Traditional VLM vision encoders, pre-trained with contrastive learning, suppress fine-grained visual cues critical for complex generative VLM tasks.
- Many existing VLMs struggle with consistent performance across both image and video understanding tasks, particularly temporal reasoning.

## Method

- A ==Penguin-Encoder== is introduced, initialized directly from a text-only Large Language Model (Qwen3-0.6B) and trained with generative-aligned objectives, including a ==reconstruction-based relation loss==.
- A ==Temporal Redundancy-Aware (TRA)== token compression mechanism is developed for efficient video encoding, dynamically allocating token budgets based on frame characteristics.
- A comprehensive, ==multi-stage training pipeline== utilizes high-quality, large-scale multimodal recaption and QA datasets for image and video understanding, incorporating image region grounding and multi-granularity video annotations.

## Results

- Penguin-VL **2B** and **8B** models achieve leading performance on numerous image benchmarks, including DocVQA (**96.2** for 8B), ChartQA (**90.5** for 8B), InfoVQA, and MathVista, often surpassing larger baseline models.
- The models demonstrate robust video understanding, excelling in long-form video comprehension (LongVideoBench **67.0** for 8B) and precise temporal reasoning (Charades-STA **61.4** for 8B), significantly outperforming competitors.
- Ablation studies confirm that LLM-initialized vision encoders and the relation loss are critical for performance gains, with the Penguin-Encoder outperforming traditional contrastive encoders using substantially less pre-training data (~**240M** vs. >**40B** samples).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Initializing vision encoders from text-only Large Language Models offers a more synergistic and efficient approach to multimodal learning than using contrastively pre-trained vision models.
> - Incorporating a relation loss in the vision encoder training is crucial for preserving fine-grained visual details and effectively modeling inter-patch relationships, improving generative task performance.
> - Achieving state-of-the-art performance in VLMs is possible with compact model sizes (2B and 8B parameters) by focusing on improved visual representation and aligned training, rather than solely on increasing model scale.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision Language Models (VLMs) has predominantly focused on scaling model size to enhance capabilities. This approach, while effective in achieving impressive general-purpose multimodal understanding, presents challenges for deployment on devices with limited computational resources, such as smartphones and robots. Existing state-of-the-art VLMs, including Qwen3-VL, Intern-VL, and the Molmo series, often rely on vision encoders initialized through extensive contrastive pretraining (e.g., CLIP or SigLIP) and massive parameter counts, which leads to high latency and computational demands. This reliance also results in varying performance across modalities, where a model optimized for image understanding might underperform on video temporal reasoning tasks.

This work by the Penguin-VL team challenges the established practice of using contrastively pre-trained vision encoders. The authors identify an objective mismatch: contrastive learning, primarily designed for discrimination, fosters coarse, category-level invariances. This can suppress the fine-grained visual cues necessary for tasks requiring dense captioning, document understanding, and complex VLM reasoning. Parallel advancements in other modalities, such as speech modeling, have demonstrated the potential of fine-tuning text-only Large Language Models (LLMs) to natively process continuous signals, suggesting a similar avenue for vision.

Penguin-VL positions itself within this landscape by exploring the performance limits of compact VLMs (2B and 8B parameters). It proposes an alternative paradigm where the vision encoder is initialized directly from a text-only LLM. This approach aims to leverage the rich semantic priors and efficient architectural designs already present in LLMs, thereby achieving tighter modality alignment and potentially superior visual fidelity with greater data efficiency. The work contributes to the ongoing effort to develop more efficient, versatile, and deployable multimodal foundation models, aiming to bridge the gap between advanced research prototypes and practical real-world applications under constrained environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to construct a compact, vision-centric multimodal foundation model that exhibits consistently strong capabilities across both image and video modalities. The authors explicitly state a pragmatic goal: to build a model that is efficient enough for deployment while maintaining reliable performance.

The motivation for this work stems from several identified limitations and challenges within the current VLM development paradigm:

1. **Computational Efficiency and Deployment:** Current leading VLMs are characterized by massive parameter counts and computationally intensive training pipelines. This reliance on large-scale models complicates their deployment on resource-constrained mobile and edge devices, where latency and memory footprints are critical factors.
2. **Performance Inconsistencies Across Modalities:** Models optimized for one modality, such as image understanding, frequently exhibit suboptimal performance in other areas, such as video temporal reasoning. The objective is to achieve a model with uniformly strong capabilities across diverse visual tasks.
3. **Mismatched Objective of Traditional Vision Encoders:** The prevailing practice of initializing VLM vision encoders with models pre-trained via large-scale contrastive learning (e.g., CLIP, SigLIP) is questioned. The authors argue that contrastive learning, optimized for discriminative tasks, promotes coarse and category-level invariances. This inductive bias can inadvertently suppress the fine-grained visual information crucial for tasks requiring dense scene understanding, detailed captioning, document analysis, and intricate VLM reasoning.
4. **Leveraging LLM Strengths for Vision:** The authors propose that the bottleneck for efficient multimodal systems might lie in the visual backbone itself. They are motivated by the observation that modern LLMs possess advanced architectural designs and are pre-trained on vast textual corpora, imbuing them with rich semantic and factual knowledge. This suggests an opportunity to leverage this pre-existing linguistic knowledge and efficient architecture for vision encoding, rather than learning visual representations from scratch with contrastive methods.
5. **Achieving Higher Visual Fidelity and Data Efficiency:** By initializing the vision encoder from a text-only LLM, the research aims to inherently align the visual representation space with the downstream LLM decoder, minimizing the modality gap. This is hypothesized to lead to a higher degree of visual fidelity and improved data efficiency for multimodal understanding, as the encoder benefits from existing semantic priors.

In pursuit of these objectives, the research introduces a holistic framework centered on visual representation learning, encompassing a novel vision encoder architecture, a tailored training recipe, and a comprehensive data curation pipeline. The core contributions outlined in the paper directly address these motivations:
- **Penguin-encoder:** A novel vision encoder adapted from a text-only LLM architecture, reusing LLM backbone weights for tighter modality alignment and architectural improvements.
- **Mixed Supervision Encoder Pretraining:** An auxiliary objective tailored for the proposed encoder, enabling efficient utilization of large-scale labeled and unlabeled structured data to improve data efficiency and representation quality.
- **Unified Training Recipe:** A comprehensive pipeline integrating low-to-high resolution curricula, priority-aware video token compression, and a two-stage instruction-tuning strategy to harmonize image and video capabilities.
- **Strong Performance at Compact Scale:** Demonstrating that these innovations can yield competitive performance with compact and computationally efficient model sizes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in Penguin-VL involves a unified architectural design, novel training strategies for the vision encoder, comprehensive data curation, and a multi-stage training pipeline.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The Penguin series, available in 2B and 8B parameter variants, consists of three core modules:
- **LLM-based Vision Encoder (Penguin-Encoder):** This encoder is initialized directly from a text-only LLM, specifically Qwen3-0.6B. Unlike traditional vision encoders, it adapts the LLM's causal self-attention mechanism to bidirectional full attention for symmetric token interactions. It is further equipped with 2D rotary positional embeddings (2D-RoPE) to handle variable-resolution inputs and maintain stable behavior across diverse aspect ratios. This design aims to leverage LLM architectural expressivity, native alignment with the downstream LLM, inherited semantic priors, and scalability.
- **MLP-based Vision-Language Projector:** A lightweight, two-layer MLP with GELU activation is used to align visual features from the encoder with the LLM's hidden size, simplifying integration without complex spatial restructuring.
- **Large Language Model (LLM):** The core LLM backbone is initialized from Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-8B for the respective model scales.

#### 3.2. Penguin-Encoder Training Paradigm

To align the LLM-initialized vision encoder with visual concepts, a training paradigm combining LLM cross-entropy supervision and reconstruction-based objectives is proposed. This involves:
- **Amplitude Loss ($L_A$):** Directly supervises the absolute value of the vision encoder's features ($F_s$) against teacher supervision features ($F_t$).
- **Direction Loss ($L_D$):** Employs cosine similarity to align the feature distribution, mitigating overfitting and representation collapse.
- **Relation Loss ($L_R$):** Explicitly supervises inter-patch relationships using self-correlation similarity, crucial for attention-based models and effective for dense visual data.

#### 3.3. Video Encoding and Compression

For video inputs, a **Temporal Redundancy-Aware (TRA) token compression** strategy is implemented. This dynamically allocates token budgets, prioritizing informative frames. It operates in three cascaded stages:
- **Compression Stage 1 (Resolution Preservation):** If the full-resolution tokenization fits the global budget, frames are tokenized at native resolutions.
- **Compression Stage 2 (Synchronous Downscaling):** If the budget is exceeded, both key and intermediate frames are synchronously downscaled while maintaining a fixed relative ratio (intermediate frames 4× spatially smaller than key frames).
- **Compression Stage 3 (Saturation-Aware Scaling):** Once intermediate frames reach a minimum token count ($T_{min}$), further compression pressure is transferred exclusively to key frames, preventing over-compression of stable contexts.

#### 3.4. Construction of High-Quality Multi-modal Recaption and QA Datasets

A comprehensive data curation pipeline is designed for both images and videos, following a quality- and diversity-first principle:
- **Image Data Curation (Penguin-Recap-I):** Over 57.2 million image-text pairs are sampled from COYO-700M and DataComp-1B. A two-stage pipeline filters low-quality images and promotes semantic diversity using hierarchical k-means clustering. **Detailed Long Captioning** is performed by a proprietary model, synthesizing comprehensive captions from structured annotations covering global semantics, subjects, actions, spatial relationships, scene attributes, notable objects, visible text (OCR), image quality, mood, and knowledge-intensive descriptions.
- **Video Data Curation (Penguin-Recap-V, Penguin-QA):** 3.7 million video-text pairs are curated from 29 public datasets. Filtering includes deduplication (via clustering of video-level features) and removal of static videos (using motion scores from optical flow). Adaptive resampling balances domain distribution. **Multi-granularity video annotation** generates hierarchical descriptions: event-level atomic descriptions, chapter-level narrative syntheses, and holistic video summaries. **Temporal Reasoning QA Construction** derives QA pairs from dense time-level descriptions for temporal ordering and grounding tasks.

#### 3.5. Training Pipeline

The model undergoes a three-stage training process:
- **Stage 1: Penguin-Encoder Training:**
 - **Low-Resolution Pre-training:** Utilizes ~223 million samples (from COYO-700M, DataComp-1B, ChartGalaxy, M-Paper, ChartGen, UniChart) at a resolution capped at 2048 visual tokens. It employs standard image-text loss augmented with the reconstruction-based supervision (Amplitude, Direction, Relation losses) from a teacher encoder, enabling effective use of unlabeled structured data.
 - **High-Resolution Fine-tuning:** Increases resolution to 10240 visual tokens using ~47 million high-quality re-captioned data (from OpenImages, SA-1B, COYO-700M, DataComp-1B, pdfa_eng_wds, and video frames from Ego4D, YouCook2, ShareGPT4Video). The reconstruction branch is removed, focusing on fine-grained alignment. Throughout Stage 1, the language decoder remains frozen.
- **Stage 2: Pre-training (VLM):** All model parameters (LLM, vision encoder, projector) are trainable. A diverse mixture of ~121 million samples is used, comprising general caption data (64%), document, fine-grained, scientific, OCR, code, math, and pure text data. This stage includes **Image Region Grounding and Region Captioning** (7.7 million samples for grounding, 1.5 million for region captioning) where bounding boxes are normalized to integer coordinates for discrete prediction.
- **Stage 3: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Aims to align the model's multimodal capabilities with user intent using high-quality QA datasets. The **Image SFT Mixture** (~39 million samples) covers general, document, chart, OCR, grounding, counting, mathematics, multi-image, and science domains, with ratios balanced for diversity and task difficulty. The **Video SFT Mixture** (~3.7 million samples) focuses on general video understanding, action recognition, temporal grounding, and ego-centric video understanding.

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

Training uses a cosine learning rate decay with a 3% warm-up ratio. The maximum sequence length is 16,384 tokens, with up to 10,240 allocated for visual inputs. Learning rates vary across stages for the encoder and projector. Video inputs are preprocessed by extracting frames at 1 FPS, uniformly subsampling if exceeding 180 frames, and then applying TRA. Visual tokens from videos are spatially downsampled by a factor of 2 after the vision encoder.

#### 3.7. Inference Settings

Inference is deterministic (greedy or near-greedy sampling with temperature 0.0 or 0.1). For videos, max frames are capped at 300, up to 3 FPS, using either the TRA sampling strategy from training or a "TRA-codec" strategy that leverages I-frame information from encoded video files for key frame selection. Baselines included Gemma3n-E2B-it, SmolVLM2, InternVL3.5-2B/8B, Qwen3-VL-2B/8B, and OpenAI GPT-5-nano.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Penguin-VL encompassed extensive ablation studies and benchmark comparisons across various image and video tasks, at both 2B and 8B parameter scales.

#### 4.1. Ablation Study: Initialization and Reconstruction-Loss

An ablation study on a lightweight training pipeline (24M encoder pre-training, 1M VLM pre-training, 665k SFT) yielded two main observations:
- **LLM-based Initialization:** Initializing the Penguin-Encoder with pre-trained LLM weights consistently improved performance. Compared to a random initialization baseline, LLM-based initialization provided a 3.3-point absolute improvement in average score across five benchmarks (AI2D, MathVista, ChartQA, MMMU-Pro, RealworldQA). This suggests that reusing LLM weights offers a well-conditioned starting point that facilitates learning visual representations compatible with the downstream LLM.
- **Relation Loss:** While basic reconstruction objectives provided moderate gains, the inclusion of the relation loss significantly boosted performance. This 1.3-point average score increase highlights its critical role in enabling the encoder to capture inter-patch interactions effectively, which benefits multimodal reasoning.

#### 4.2. Comparisons with Existing Vision Encoders (LMM Integration)

Comparing the full Penguin-encoder (pre-trained on ~240M samples) with existing vision encoders like SigLIP2, VL3-NaViT, and Qwen3-ViT, using Qwen3-1.7B as a unified LLM backbone, revealed:
- **Overall Performance Advantage:** Penguin-encoder achieved an average score of 49.3, outperforming other encoders across all benchmarks, despite using fewer pre-training samples than some baselines (e.g., SigLIP2 which used >40B samples). This advantage is attributed to its generation-aligned design, which directly maps fine-grained visual features into the LLM's semantic space, making representations suitable for autoregressive decoding.
- **Scalability:** Penguin-encoder showed progressively larger gains with increased data scale and task complexity, indicating a higher performance ceiling for LMM integration compared to contrastive encoders.
- **Matched Data Comparison:** Even when trained on the exact same data and recipe as Penguin-encoder (Stage 1), a pre-trained SigLIP2 encoder was outperformed by Penguin-encoder, underscoring the architectural benefits of the generation-aligned encoder initialization.

#### 4.3. Image Benchmarks

- **2B Model (Table 1):** Penguin-VL achieved leading performance on most Chart and Document understanding benchmarks (InfoVQA, ChartQA, DocVQA). While it was competitive on OCRBench, it trailed Qwen3-VL and InternVL3.5. In mathematics tasks, Penguin-VL attained the best performance on MathVista but was outperformed by Qwen3-VL on other math benchmarks, suggesting potential for more focused math SFT data. On general knowledge benchmarks, it secured top rankings on three out of five tasks (AI2D, RealWorldQA, V-star) and was competitive with Qwen3-VL.
- **8B Model (Table 2):** Penguin-VL-8B demonstrated strong command over dense visual information, achieving high scores on DocVQA (96.2) and ChartQA (90.5), frequently surpassing Qwen3-VL 8B. It led in general knowledge and scientific reasoning (AI2D, V-star). For mathematical reasoning, it excelled in foundational tasks like MathVista (77.4) but was outperformed by Qwen3-VL and InternVL-3.5 on abstract logic benchmarks (MathVerse, LogicVista).

#### 4.4. Video Benchmarks

- **2B Model (Table 1):** Penguin-VL demonstrated robust general video understanding, leading on EgoSchema (57.6), ActivityNetQA (61.5), and Perception Test (70.4), and tying for first on MMVU (42.7). It excelled in long-form video comprehension on LongVideoBench (59.5), surpassing Qwen3-VL by 7.4 points. For temporal awareness and reasoning, it achieved state-of-the-art results on NextQA (79.9) and Charades-STA (56.2), with a significant margin over InternVL3.5 on Charades-STA.
- **8B Model (Table 2):** Penguin-VL-8B claimed the top spot in the majority of evaluated video metrics. Its performance on LongVideoBench (67.0) and NextQA (85.4) highlighted its ability for coherence and temporal awareness in extended sequences. It secured state-of-the-art results on ActivityNetQA (65.2) and Perception Test (78.0) and showed competitive performance on MVBench, VideoMME, EgoSchema, and MMVU. It demonstrated proficiency in precise temporal grounding on Charades-STA (61.4), outperforming Qwen3-VL by 5.4 points and InternVL-3.5 by 28.6 points.

#### 4.5. Image and Video Case Study

Qualitative examples illustrated Penguin-VL's capabilities across various tasks:
- **Code Writing:** Generated correct Python code from a competitive programming problem image, demonstrating algorithmic reasoning.
- **OCR and Document Understanding:** Extracted high-fidelity text from historical documents with severe degradation, preserving structural integrity.
- **Chart Image Understanding:** Reliably extracted data points and performed comparative analysis on multivariate line charts.
- **Image Creative Writing:** Generated multi-stanza poetry from a painting, interpreting both concrete elements and abstract atmosphere.
- **Video Understanding and Temporal Grounding:** Processed long video sequences to construct comprehensive narratives and precisely localized events within specific timestamp intervals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in the Penguin-VL technical report contributes to the field of multimodal AI by challenging established practices and demonstrating alternative approaches for VLM development, particularly concerning efficiency.

#### 5.1. Re-evaluation of Vision Encoder Paradigm

The primary significance lies in its direct challenge to the prevalent use of contrastive pretraining for VLM vision encoders. By demonstrating that an encoder initialized from a text-only LLM can achieve superior visual fidelity and data efficiency, Penguin-VL offers an alternative to the resource-intensive reliance on massive contrastive datasets. This suggests that aligning a vision encoder’s initialization with an LLM’s generative objectives is a viable and potentially more effective path than continued scaling of disconnected contrastive pretraining.

#### 5.2. Advancement in Efficiency and Performance for Compact Models

Penguin-VL establishes a new benchmark for parameter-efficient VLMs at 2B and 8B scales. The reported performance, which is competitive with or surpasses leading VLMs across a diverse range of image and video benchmarks, indicates that significant capabilities can be achieved without resort to extremely large model sizes. This efficiency is critical for broadening the applicability of VLMs.

#### 5.3. Enhanced and Consistent Multimodal Capabilities

The model demonstrates consistently strong performance across a wide spectrum of tasks, including fine-grained document OCR, complex mathematical reasoning, general visual knowledge, multi-image analysis, long-context video comprehension, and precise temporal grounding. This holistic capability across image and video modalities addresses a limitation of many existing VLMs that often exhibit uneven performance.

#### 5.4. Architectural and Methodological Innovation

The introduction of the Penguin-Encoder, adapted from an LLM architecture and combined with a novel mixed supervision pretraining strategy (including Amplitude, Direction, and Relation losses), represents an architectural innovation. The Temporal Redundancy-Aware (TRA) token compression mechanism for video processing is also a key methodological contribution, enabling efficient handling of long video sequences while preserving critical temporal dynamics. The comprehensive multi-stage training pipeline and data curation strategies further contribute to the robustness and performance of the model.

#### 5.5. Practical Deployment Implications

The compact nature and strong performance of Penguin-VL directly address the challenges of deploying advanced VLMs on compute-constrained mobile and edge devices such as smartphones and robots. By offering a high-performance, resource-efficient alternative, this work can accelerate the integration of sophisticated multimodal understanding into a wider array of real-world applications where low latency and limited memory are critical factors.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The identified future work directions, including real-time inference optimization, advanced reinforcement learning (RL)-based post-training, and agentic use (especially for GUI agents), indicate potential for further expanding the model's capabilities and practical utility. These areas aim to make VLMs more adaptive, interactive, and autonomous, building upon the efficient foundation established by Penguin-VL.

In conclusion, Penguin-VL represents a significant step towards more accessible, versatile, and efficient multimodal AI systems by demonstrating that architectural alignment and data-efficient training can yield high-performing VLMs without requiring massive parameter scales or the traditional contrastive pretraining paradigm.
