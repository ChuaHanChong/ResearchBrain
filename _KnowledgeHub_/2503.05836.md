# Safe Distributed Learning-Enhanced Predictive Control for Multiple Quadrupedal Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The University of Manchester developed a distributed control framework for multiple quadrupedal robots, integrating Model Predictive Control with Control Lyapunov Functions for formation stability and neural network-based Control Barrier Functions for collision avoidance. The framework, which includes a novel scale-adaptive neighbor encoding and a deadlock resolution mechanism, achieved stable formation control and real-time collision-free navigation for multiple robots in both high-fidelity simulations and real-world heterogeneous robot experiments.

## Problem

- Maintaining stable formation and ensuring collision-free navigation for multiple quadrupedal robots in complex, dynamic environments, while overcoming computational intensity and communication constraints.
- Existing control methods like traditional Model Predictive Control or Control Barrier Functions often lack scalability for multi-agent systems, can be overly conservative, or do not offer formal safety guarantees.
- Handling dynamic changes in team structure, where robot neighbors can vary in count and identity, posed a challenge for robust control policy adaptation.

## Method

- A hierarchical control framework combining Distributed ==Model Predictive Control== (DMPC) with Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) to ensure exponential stability of formation error.
- A neural network-based Control Barrier Function (CBF), enhanced by Scale-Adaptive Permutation-Invariant Encoding (SAPIE), provides decentralized safety guarantees against collisions and adapts to dynamic neighbor sets.
- An event-triggered deadlock resolution mechanism perturbs nominal states to restore motion feasibility without directly altering control inputs or compromising stability.

## Results

- Simulations in an obstacle-dense warehouse environment confirmed stable formation maintenance, effective obstacle avoidance, and autonomous deadlock resolution for multiple quadrupedal robots.
- Real-world experiments with custom XG quadrupedal robots demonstrated stable formation control and real-time dynamic collision avoidance against moving obstacles.
- The framework exhibited robust generalizability across heterogeneous quadrupedal platforms, successfully maintaining formations and avoiding obstacles without requiring model recalibration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formal stability (CLFs) and safety (CBFs) guarantees can be effectively combined within a DMPC framework for complex multi-quadrupedal systems.
> - Scale-Adaptive Permutation-Invariant Encoding (SAPIE) enables control policies to robustly handle dynamically changing numbers and orders of neighboring robots.
> - Deadlocks in CBF-constrained multi-robot systems can be resolved by intelligently perturbing the nominal state, preserving system stability and enabling continuous motion.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of multi-robot systems capable of operating in complex, unstructured, and dynamic environments is a significant area of robotics research. Quadrupedal robots, with their redundant degrees of freedom and ability to adapt gait patterns, are particularly suited for such challenging terrains, making them promising platforms for applications like disaster response, material transport, and warehouse management. However, achieving stable formation control while ensuring collision-free navigation for multiple quadrupedal robots presents substantial technical challenges. These include managing dynamic obstacles, addressing communication constraints, and handling the inherent complexity of legged locomotion.

Existing approaches to multi-robot formation control include traditional methods such as leader-follower and virtual structures. While effective in structured environments, these methods typically struggle in highly dynamic scenarios. Model Predictive Control (MPC) is recognized for its potential in ensuring swarm formation stability and safety, including zero-shot generalizability. Its decentralized variant (DMPC) allows for local decision-making while maintaining global formation stability. However, MPC's high computational cost can limit real-time scalability, especially for systems with complex dynamics like quadrupedal robots. Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) offer formal safety guarantees but can be overly conservative, potentially leading to deadlocks in multi-agent settings. Reinforcement learning (RL)-based controllers provide adaptability but often require extensive training data and may lack formal safety assurances, which can be a barrier for real-world deployment.

Prior work on multi-quadruped robot control has explored aspects like obstacle avoidance, gait synchronization, and flocking. However, these studies often lack theoretical guarantees on formation error convergence or do not fully integrate formation control with collision avoidance mechanisms in a robust, scalable manner. The challenge is further compounded by the need for real-time performance on onboard computational resources and the ability to adapt to dynamically changing team structures, where the number and identity of neighboring robots can fluctuate. This research positions itself by addressing these gaps, proposing a framework that integrates formal stability and safety guarantees with adaptive neighbor encoding and deadlock resolution mechanisms, aiming for a scalable, efficient, and robust solution for multi-quadrupedal robot formation control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a safe distributed learning-enhanced predictive control framework for multiple quadrupedal robots to enable reliable formation control and collision-free navigation in dynamic, unstructured environments.

The specific motivations and objectives driving this work are detailed as follows:

- **Stable Formation Control:** A core objective is to ensure that multiple quadrupedal robots can maintain a desired formation shape and consistent velocity while moving through various environments. This is crucial for collaborative tasks and efficient operation of multi-robot systems. The inherent complexity of quadrupedal locomotion, combined with external disturbances, makes achieving stable formation a non-trivial problem. The paper aims to provide theoretical proof that the formation error can converge to zero, ensuring stability.
- **Collision Avoidance:** Beyond maintaining formation, the framework must guarantee collision-free navigation, both among robots themselves and with static or dynamic obstacles in the environment. Traditional methods often provide insufficient guarantees or suffer from high computational overhead when integrated with formation control. The objective is to enforce safety constraints that prevent collisions effectively.
- **Adaptation to Dynamic Team Structures:** In real-world multi-robot deployments, the set of neighboring robots with which an individual robot communicates can change dynamically. Traditional control methods often assume a fixed number of neighbors, which limits scalability and robustness. A key motivation is to enable robust feature encoding of neighboring robots, irrespective of their number or order, to ensure control policies remain valid and effective under varying team configurations.
- **Real-time Feasibility and Scalability:** Model Predictive Control, while powerful, is computationally intensive. For quadrupedal robots with limited onboard resources, executing complex optimization problems in real-time for multiple agents is a significant challenge. The research aims to reduce the computational burden of DMPC and develop a framework that is efficient enough for real-time deployment, potentially across large-scale systems.
- **Robust Communication and Coordination:** Real-world deployment is often hindered by communication constraints, including latency and efficiency of data distribution. The work aims to develop a low-latency communication protocol to facilitate reliable, real-time exchange of state information among robots, which is critical for distributed control.
- **Deadlock Resolution:** Control Barrier Functions, while providing strong safety guarantees, can be overly conservative and lead to situations where robots become "stuck" or deadlocked in constrained spaces, particularly in dense formations. An important objective is to develop a mechanism that can detect and resolve such deadlocks without compromising overall system stability or safety, ensuring continuous motion.
- **Validation in High-Fidelity Simulations and Real-World:** To validate the practicality and robustness of the proposed framework, the motivation includes rigorous testing in both high-fidelity digital twin simulations that closely replicate physical properties and on custom-designed physical quadrupedal robot platforms. This sim-to-real transfer is crucial for demonstrating the framework's potential for real-world deployment.

These objectives collectively aim to overcome the limitations of existing methods by integrating formal guarantees with adaptive, real-time capabilities, thereby advancing the state-of-the-art in multi-quadrupedal robot control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates a distributed model predictive control (DMPC) framework with Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) for formation stability, Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) for decentralized safety, and a novel encoding mechanism for dynamic neighbor adaptation. A hierarchical control architecture is adopted, where the DMPC acts as the high-level controller, and a pre-trained low-level motor controller, Walk-These-Ways (WTW), handles gait and posture.

**A. Distributed Model Predictive Control (MPC) with CLF Constraints:**
Each quadruped robot independently computes its control input based on local information and communication with its neighbors. The approach considers a fully distributed setup, where agents only communicate with their direct neighbors.
- **Nominal State Definition:** For each robot *i*, a nominal state $\hat{x}_i$ is computed as a weighted average of its neighbors' states, including the leader if applicable, along with predefined formation offsets ($\Delta_{ij}$, $\Delta_{ir}$). The formation error is defined as $e_{N_i} = x_i - \hat{x}_i$.
- **MPC Formulation:** The DMPC problem is formulated as an optimization problem (Eq. 4) that minimizes the square of the control input over a prediction horizon. This cost function is subject to several constraints:
 - The robot's dynamic model ($\dot{x}_i = f(x_i) + g(x_i)u_i$).
 - A Control Lyapunov Function (CLF) constraint ($\dot{V}_i(e_{N_i}, \hat{u}_i) + \beta V_i(e_{N_i}) \leq 0$), which ensures the exponential stability of the formation error, driving it to zero. $V_i(e_{N_i}) = e_{N_i}^T e_{N_i}$ serves as the Lyapunov function.
 - Physical constraints on velocity ($v_{min} \leq v_i \leq v_{max}$) and control input ($u_{min} \leq u_i \leq u_{max}$).
- **Stability Guarantee:** Lemma 1 demonstrates that when the optimal cost function $J_{CLFs}(\hat{u}_i)$ approaches zero, the CLF constraint becomes slack, indicating that the system's inherent dynamics drive the formation error $e_{N_i}$ to zero, thus ensuring formation stability. The proof utilizes KKT conditions to show that a zero cost implies $u_i^* = 0$ and $x_i^* = \hat{x}_i$.

**B. Scale-Adaptive Permutation-Invariant Encoding (SAPIE):**
To address the challenges of varying neighbor counts and arbitrary neighbor ordering in dynamic multi-robot systems, the SAPIE mechanism is introduced.
- **Mechanism:** SAPIE processes the state of each neighbor through a shared nonlinear transformation ($\phi_j = \sigma(W o_i)$). Subsequently, an order-independent pooling operation, specifically row-wise max pooling, aggregates these transformed features into a fixed-length feature vector ($\psi(o_i)$). This ensures that the output representation remains consistent regardless of the number or sequence of neighbors. This approach provides robustness against dynamic changes in the communication topology.

**C. SAPIE-CBF Based on Neural Networks:**
The dynamic nature of multi-agent environments makes traditional, fixed-structure CBFs challenging to apply. Therefore, a deep neural network is employed to approximate the CBF, integrated with the SAPIE mechanism.
- **CBF Training:** The neural network for the CBF (parameterized by $\theta_i$) is trained using an empirical loss function $L_{CBF}$ (Eq. 9). This loss function has three main components:
 - Terms that classify safe and unsafe states (ensuring $h_{\theta_i}(x_i, o_i) \geq 0$ for safe states and $h_{\theta_i}(x_i, o_i) < 0$ for unsafe states).
 - A term that enforces the forward invariance condition of the CBF ($\max(0, \gamma - L_f h_{\theta_i} - L_g h_{\theta_i} \cdot u_i - \alpha(h_{\theta_i}))$, ensuring that if the system starts in a safe state, it remains safe.
- **Control Input Refinement:** The control input $\hat{u}_i$ from the MPC-CLF may not directly ensure safety. The final control input $\check{u}_i$ is obtained by solving a quadratic program that minimizes the deviation from $\hat{u}_i$ while satisfying the CBF constraints. A regularization loss $L_u = ||\check{u}_i - \hat{u}_i||^2$ is added to the total loss function for training the CBF network, balancing safety and control performance.

**D. Deadlock Resolution:**
Deadlocks can occur when CBF constraints become overly restrictive, preventing any motion despite feasible nominal commands.
- **Detection:** An event-triggered deadlock indicator $E_i = \text{sign}(||J_{CLFs} - L_{CBFs}|| + ||v_i||) - \text{sign}(J_{CLFs})$ is used. A deadlock is detected when $E_i < 0$.
- **Resolution Mechanism:** Instead of directly modifying the control input (which could destabilize gait), the mechanism adjusts the nominal state $\hat{x}_i$ to $\tilde{x}_i = T(\hat{x}_i - x_i) + x_i$. The transformation matrix $T$ applies a controlled perturbation to the reference. This adjustment ensures a non-zero control input in the subsequent step, restoring motion feasibility while preserving the stability guarantees of the MPC-CLF framework. The mechanism is designed to trigger only once per deadlock occurrence.

**E. System Design and Validation:**
- **Quadruped Robot (XG):** Custom-built platform with 12 DoF, IMU and LiDAR sensors (Mid-360 LiDAR), dual RK3588-based processing boards, and Wi-Fi connectivity. A Data Distribution Service (DDS) protocol with a Peer-to-Peer architecture is used for low-latency, real-time communication.
- **Digital Twins:** High-fidelity digital twins were created in NVIDIA Omniverse Isaac Sim, replicating physical properties, dimensions, mass distribution, actuator limits, and joint constraints of the XG robots. This environment was used for training locomotion gaits via RL and for multi-robot simulations in various environments (e.g., warehouses, office spaces) with accurate obstacle placements, significantly reducing the sim-to-real gap.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The proposed framework was validated through extensive simulations in NVIDIA Omniverse Isaac Sim and real-world experiments using the custom XG quadrupedal robot system.

**A. Simulated Experiments:**
The simulations were conducted in an NVIDIA warehouse environment (approximately 25 × 35m) using ROS2 Humble, with the XG robots operating with a pre-trained RL locomotion policy. The CBF utilized a convolutional neural network (4 layers, 64, 128, 64, 1) integrated with SAPIE (processing a maximum of 4 neighbors). The MPC problem was solved using IPOPT.

- **Scenario 1: Safe Navigation in Obstacle Dense Environment:**
 - Five quadrupedal robots were deployed in a 2D plane with static obstacles.
 - **Formation Maintenance and Obstacle Avoidance:** The trajectory plots (Fig. 8a) demonstrated that the robots successfully established and maintained their formation while navigating around multiple obstacles. Initially, robots operated without a fixed formation but progressively converged to the desired structure after encountering obstacles.
 - **Formation Tracking Error:** The formation tracking error (Fig. 8b) showed an initial period of error accumulation during obstacle negotiation (peaks around t ≈ 30s), followed by a clear convergence toward minimal values after clearing the obstacle field. This confirmed the controller's ability to prioritize collision avoidance while subsequently re-establishing formation stability. The SAPIE mechanism effectively encoded neighbor information, enabling adaptation to varying neighbor counts.
- **Scenario 2: Deadlock Resolution and Dynamic Collision Avoidance:**
 - Four groups of quadrupedal robots were orchestrated to move simultaneously towards each other, creating conditions prone to collision and deadlock.
 - **Dynamic Collision Avoidance:** The results (Fig. 9) indicated that the framework effectively ensured safe inter-robot interactions, with robots successfully resolving potential deadlocks at intersection points.
 - **Autonomous Deadlock Resolution:** When deadlock conditions arose, the robots autonomously activated the proposed resolution mechanism without external intervention, demonstrating the algorithm's effectiveness in detecting and resolving deadlocks in confined spaces, thus enhancing navigation robustness.
 - **Generalization in Simulation:** Further simulation in a narrow corridor with three robots showed successful navigation while maintaining formation and avoiding static and dynamic obstacles, validating the integrated control framework.

**B. Real-world Experiments:**
- **Formation Control with XG Platform:**
 - A team of two XG quadrupedal robots was used for formation control and dynamic collision avoidance tasks. These robots were equipped with IMU and LiDAR sensors and communicated using a custom DDS-based middleware.
 - **Dynamic Obstacle Avoidance:** The results (Fig. 10) showed that the controller successfully guided the two XG robots around a dynamic obstacle (an office chair) while maintaining their desired formation throughout the experiment. The DDS protocol facilitated real-time state sharing, enhancing coordination.
- **Cross-Platform Validation:**
 - The same control strategy was deployed on a second pair of quadrupedal robots with different physical characteristics (designed for heavy payload transport).
 - **Generalizability:** Despite significant physical differences in materials, structural design, and load-bearing capacity, the formation control framework performed robustly without requiring model recalibration. It successfully maintained desired formations and avoided obstacles, suggesting the generalizability of the approach across heterogeneous quadrupedal platforms with minimal adaptation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a comprehensive distributed control framework that advances the capabilities of multi-quadrupedal robot systems in complex and dynamic environments. The integration of several key technical elements contributes to its significance.

Firstly, the framework provides **formal guarantees for both stability and safety**. By combining Distributed Model Predictive Control (DMPC) with Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs), the system ensures stable formation tracking and proves the theoretical convergence of formation error to zero. Simultaneously, the use of a neural network-based Control Barrier Function (CBF), enhanced by the Scale-Adaptive Permutation-Invariant Encoding (SAPIE), provides decentralized safety guarantees against inter-robot and obstacle collisions, overcoming the limitations of traditional CBFs in dynamic, multi-agent scenarios. This integration addresses the critical need for reliable operation in environments where safety is paramount.

Secondly, the introduction of **SAPIE** is significant for addressing the challenge of dynamically changing team structures. Traditional methods often struggle with varying neighbor counts and orders. SAPIE’s ability to efficiently encode dynamic neighbor information in a permutation-invariant manner enhances the adaptability and scalability of the control policy, making it suitable for practical deployments where robot teams can fluctuate.

Thirdly, the development of a **low-latency Data Distribution Service (DDS)-based communication protocol** is crucial for real-time coordination. This peer-to-peer architecture facilitates efficient and reliable state sharing among robots, which is a fundamental requirement for responsive distributed control. Furthermore, the proposed **event-triggered deadlock resolution mechanism** addresses a practical limitation of CBF-based approaches, preventing motion stagnation in constrained spaces without compromising stability or requiring direct control input modification.

The extensive validation, spanning both high-fidelity simulations in NVIDIA Omniverse Isaac Sim and real-world experiments on custom XG quadrupedal robots, demonstrates the **robustness and real-time feasibility** of the framework. The successful cross-platform validation on different quadrupedal robot variants highlights the **generalizability** of the approach, suggesting its potential for broad applicability across heterogeneous robotic platforms with minimal adaptation.

The potential impact of this work is substantial for various applications requiring autonomous multi-robot coordination in challenging terrains. This includes enhancing capabilities in areas such as:
- **Disaster Response:** Teams of quadrupedal robots could collaboratively navigate collapsed structures or hazardous environments, maintaining formation for coordinated search and rescue operations.
- **Material Transport and Logistics:** In unstructured industrial settings or warehouses, these robots could efficiently transport materials, adapting to dynamic layouts and avoiding obstacles while maintaining precise formations.
- **Environmental Monitoring and Exploration:** Coordinated teams could explore difficult-to-reach areas, collecting data while maintaining relative positions for comprehensive coverage.

By offering a scalable, efficient, and robust solution with formal safety guarantees, this research contributes to the advancement of deployable multi-quadrupedal robot systems, moving them closer to practical, large-scale applications in complex and dynamic real-world environments. Future work aims to extend these capabilities to multi-agent visual-LiDAR SLAM, which could further enhance autonomous operation in unknown environments.
