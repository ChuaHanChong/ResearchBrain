# A Survey of Sim-to-Real Methods in RL: Progress, Prospects and Challenges with Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A survey that frames sim-to-real reinforcement learning through the four MDP elements: observation, action, transition, and reward. It formalizes the gap as the difference between simulated and real evaluation of one policy, organizes classic techniques (domain randomization, domain adaptation, grounded action transformation, reward shaping) alongside foundation-model methods, and compiles 49 simulators and benchmarks plus per-domain metrics and evaluation settings.

## Problem

- Prior sim-to-real surveys organize methods by application or technique family, so no taxonomy ties solutions back to the MDP components that cause each gap.
- Earlier reviews predate foundation models, leaving LLM- and VLM-based perception, action handling, dynamics grounding, and reward design largely uncovered.
- Domain insights stay scattered across robotics, transportation and recommendation, with no formal evaluation protocol, metric set, or consolidated benchmark listing.

## Method

- ==MDP-element taxonomy==: gap causes decomposed into observation (perception completeness, representation mismatch), action (granularity, system latency), transition (simulated versus real dynamics), and reward gaps, each mapped to remedy families.
- Per-element technique review covering ==domain randomization== including ==Automatic Domain Randomization==, ==domain adaptation==, ==sensor fusion==, ==action-delay== and ==action-robust RL==, ==Grounded Action Transformation== variants, ==distributionally robust RL==, ==potential-based== and ==LLM-based reward design==.
- ==Formal evaluation framework==: gap defined as simulated minus real metric for one policy, cumulative reward difference across timesteps, three deployment settings, plus tables of domain metrics and GenAI-based simulators.

## Results

- Comparison against **seven** prior sim-to-real surveys across **eight** criteria shows only this work jointly covers MDP taxonomy, foundation-model methods, formal metrics, and benchmark summary.
- The benchmark table catalogs **49** simulators and sim-to-real benchmarks across robotics, transportation, recommender systems and other domains, separating plain environments from dedicated sim-to-real benchmarks.
- **Three** evaluation settings are ranked on cost, safety and realism: sim-to-real highest realism but lowest safety, sim-to-sim cheapest and safest, scale-down testbeds intermediate; **fifteen** domain metrics tabulated.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diagnosing which MDP element a gap originates in narrows the technique choice; observation randomization cannot repair a dynamics mismatch or action latency.
> - More randomization is not strictly better: wide ranges destabilize training, so curriculum and automatic range scheduling beat static heavy randomization.
> - Foundation models add semantic grounding but import hallucination and per-step inference cost, so transfer claims need uncertainty quantification and model distillation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated remarkable success in solving complex decision-making tasks across diverse domains, from gaming to robotics, transportation, and recommender systems. However, a significant bottleneck to its widespread real-world adoption is the "sim-to-real gap." RL policies are predominantly trained in simulators due to safety concerns, high costs, and the impracticality of collecting vast amounts of real-world data. This training environment, while safe and efficient, often fails to perfectly replicate the complexities and uncertainties of the real world, leading to a substantial performance degradation or even critical failures when policies are deployed in physical environments.

This survey paper addresses this fundamental challenge by providing a comprehensive and structured overview of existing and emerging methods designed to bridge this sim-to-real gap. It is positioned as a critical resource in the evolving landscape of deployable AI, particularly as the field moves towards more autonomous and safety-critical applications. The authors highlight the increasing attention on sim-to-real challenges and, crucially, integrate the rapidly developing field of large foundation models (such as Large Language Models, LLMs, and Vision-Language Models, VLMs) into their analysis.

The distinctive contribution of this survey, as claimed by the authors, is its novel taxonomy. Unlike previous surveys that might categorize methods by technique type or application domain, this paper formally frames sim-to-real techniques based on the four fundamental elements of a Markov Decision Process (MDP): State (Observation), Action, Transition, and Reward. This MDP-centric approach provides a systematic and foundational framework for understanding where and how sim-to-real discrepancies arise and how different techniques attempt to mitigate them. By spanning literature from classic methods to state-of-the-art approaches, including the transformative potential of foundation models, the survey offers a timely and holistic perspective, making it invaluable for researchers aiming to develop robust and reliable RL systems for real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this comprehensive survey paper are clearly articulated by the authors:

1. **Propose a Novel Taxonomy:** To introduce the first formal taxonomy that categorizes sim-to-real techniques based on the key elements of the Markov Decision Process (MDP): Observation (State), Action, Transition, and Reward. This systematic framework aims to unify diverse approaches and provide a clearer understanding of their underlying mechanisms.
2. **Conduct a Comprehensive Literature Review:** To cover an extensive range of sim-to-real literature, encompassing both classic and the most advanced methods. A significant motivation here is to reflect on the root causes of the sim-to-real gap and to specifically introduce how emerging large foundation models can benefit this critical research direction.
3. **Provide Domain-Specific Discussion:** To identify and discuss the unique challenges and opportunities related to sim-to-real problems within different real-world application domains, such as robotics, transportation, and recommender systems. This aims to offer tailored insights beyond generic technical solutions.
4. **Summarize Formal Evaluation Processes:** To detail the formal evaluation processes for sim-to-real performance, including various settings (sim-to-real, sim-to-scale-down-real, sim-to-sim) and relevant metrics, along with accessible code and benchmarks, thereby guiding researchers in assessing policy performance accurately.
5. **Identify Challenges and Opportunities:** To present an up-to-date overview of the persisting challenges in sim-to-real transfer, including traditional issues like simulation fidelity, and new problems introduced by foundation models, such as hallucination and computational scalability. Simultaneously, the authors aim to highlight promising future research directions.

The overarching motivation stems from the recognition that despite RL's theoretical effectiveness, its practical deployment is severely hampered by the sim-to-real gap, leading to degraded performance and safety risks in real-world scenarios. By providing a structured, comprehensive, and forward-looking survey, the authors aim to facilitate future exploration, help researchers easily identify issues, refer to relevant solutions, and accelerate the development of robust and deployable RL systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core methodology of this survey revolves around a novel, **MDP-centric taxonomy** to systematically organize and analyze the vast landscape of sim-to-real techniques in Reinforcement Learning. Instead of merely listing techniques, the authors dissect the sim-to-real problem by examining how discrepancies arise and are addressed within each fundamental component of an MDP: State (Observation), Action, Transition, and Reward.

The approach involves:

1. **Problem Formulation:** The paper begins by formally defining the Reinforcement Learning problem within the MDP framework (S, A, T, R, γ) and then formally defines the "Sim-to-Real gap" as the performance discrepancy between a policy learned in a simulated environment ($M_s$) and its deployment in the real world ($M_r$). They attribute this gap to mismatches in Observation ($\Delta_S$, $\Delta_{perception}$), Action ($\Delta_A$), Transition dynamics ($\Delta_{system}$), and Reward function ($\Delta_{system}$, cascade from $\Delta_A$).
2. **Categorization by MDP Elements:** The main body of the survey is structured around these four MDP elements. For each element, the authors delve into specific categories of techniques that address the corresponding sim-to-real gap:
 - **Observation:** Techniques such as Domain Randomization (varying visual parameters in sim), Domain Adaptation (aligning feature distributions using adversarial training or embedding alignment), Sensor Fusion (combining multiple sensor inputs), and the recent application of Foundation Models (e.g., VLMs for semantic anchoring).
 - **Action:** Methods dealing with Action Space Scale (e.g., discrete-to-continuous conversion, action shielding), Action Delays (constant, random, or control delays), Action Uncertainties (action advising, action robust RL), and how Foundation Models can aid in motion planning and task-level control.
 - **Transition:** Approaches including Domain Randomization (randomizing physical parameters), Domain Adaptation (aligning parameter distributions), Grounding Methods (adjusting simulator dynamics to real-world observations), Distributionally Robust Learning (policies robust to unknown transition shifts), and LLM-Enhanced Approaches (using LLMs for forward model prediction).
 - **Reward:** Strategies like Reward Shaping (modifying reward functions with prior knowledge) and LLM-Based Reward Design (automating reward generation from natural language descriptions).
3. **Integration of Foundation Models:** A key aspect of their approach is the dedicated discussion of how large foundation models are impacting and can further benefit each MDP element's sim-to-real challenges. This reflects the timely nature of the survey and its effort to incorporate cutting-edge AI developments.
4. **Domain-Specific Analysis:** Beyond generic techniques, the survey provides a focused discussion on how sim-to-real challenges and solutions manifest in specific domains (Robotics, Transportation, Recommender Systems), highlighting unique concerns and prevalent research focuses within each.
5. **Resource Compilation:** The authors compile a comprehensive list of simulators and sim-to-real benchmarks, categorized by domain (e.g., Gazebo, MuJoCo for Robotics; SUMO, CARLA for Transportation; RecSim for Recommender Systems). They also dedicate a section to emerging GenAI-based simulations (brute-force scaling, logic-integrated, differentiable/physics-augmented, generative world models), emphasizing new avenues for high-fidelity simulation.
6. **Evaluation Framework:** The survey outlines various evaluation settings (sim-to-real, sim-to-scale-down-real, sim-to-sim) and provides a list of common, domain-relevant metrics (e.g., success rate, delay, CTR) used to quantify the sim-to-real gap.
7. **Future Outlook:** The paper concludes by identifying open challenges (simulation fidelity, safety, hallucination in LLMs, scalability) and opportunities, providing a roadmap for future research.

This structured and comprehensive approach, especially its MDP-based taxonomy and timely inclusion of foundation models, allows the survey to provide a systematic understanding of the sim-to-real problem and its multifaceted solutions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey provides a structured overview of sim-to-real methods in RL, primarily categorized by their impact on the four core elements of a Markov Decision Process (MDP): Observation, Action, Transition, and Reward.

#### 4.1. Techniques Categorized by MDP Elements

- **Observation (State Space) Gap Mitigation:** This gap arises from mismatches in observed information's completeness or feature representation between simulation and reality.
 - **Domain Randomization:** Involves introducing significant variability in simulated visual parameters (textures, lighting, object positions, camera settings) during training. This makes the policy robust to real-world visual variations, even if the exact real-world scenario is unseen. Curriculum-based approaches are noted to stabilize training.
 - **Domain Adaptation:** Focuses on aligning the feature distributions of observational data between simulated and real environments. Techniques include adversarial training and embedding alignment. Some works explore self-supervised frameworks for latent state representation optimization or cross-modality adaptation (e.g., to event-based cameras). Bi-directional adaptation (real2sim and sim2real) is also discussed for efficiency.
 - **Sensor Fusion:** Combines data from multiple sensor modalities (e.g., visual, depth, LiDAR, auditory) to provide a more comprehensive and reliable perception of the environment, compensating for individual sensor limitations.
 - **Foundation Models:** Leverages LLMs and VLMs to interpret and align observational data. VLMs can provide semantic anchors or descriptive annotations for sensory inputs, bridging the visual gap by capturing task-relevant semantics that are consistent across domains.

- **Action Space Gap Mitigation:** This gap relates to differences in action granularity, delays, or uncertainties between simulation and reality.
 - **Action Space Scale:** Addresses the common issue of discrete actions in simulation mapping to continuous actions in reality. Methods include subgoal models and Action Shielding, which uses safety layers to ensure feasible and effective actions in the real world.
 - **Action Delays:** Tackles the discrepancy where simulated actions are instant, but real-world actions experience latency. Solutions include predictive models (Constant-Delayed MDP), randomized delays in training, and specific algorithms like Delay-Correcting Actor-Critic (DCAC) or Prediction model with Arbitrary Delay (PAD) for Random-Delay MDPs.
 - **Action Uncertainties:** Incorporates uncertainty quantification for robust decision-making in unseen real-world scenarios. Techniques include Action Advising (guidance from experienced entities, often uncertainty-aware) and Action Robust RL (designing policies to withstand unexpected disruptions or adversarial actions without external advice).
 - **Foundation Models:** Utilizes LLMs/VLMs for high-level motion planning by sequencing simpler, locally trained policies. LLMs can also improve sample efficiency by generating preliminary rule-based controllers based on their internal knowledge.

- **Transition Dynamics Gap Mitigation:** This gap refers to inaccuracies in the simulated environment's dynamics compared to real-world physics.
 - **Domain Randomization:** Extends to randomizing physical parameters (e.g., friction, motor torque) within the simulator to make policies robust to variations in real-world dynamics. Active Domain Randomization (ADR) focuses on the most challenging variations.
 - **Domain Adaptation:** Aligns parameter distributions between simulated and real dynamics, often using adversarial training, to minimize discrepancies in transition functions.
 - **Grounding Methods:** Adjust simulator dynamics to match real-world dynamics, typically through learning an action transformation. Examples include Grounded Action Transformation (GAT), Stochastic GAT (SGAT), Reinforced GAT (RGAT), and Generative Adversarial Reinforced Action Transformation (GARAT), sometimes with uncertainty quantification.
 - **Distributionally Robust Learning:** Formulates sim-to-real as a robust learning problem, designing policies that generalize to environments with unknown but bounded transition shifts, ensuring theoretical guarantees for off-dynamics RL.
 - **LLM-Enhanced Approaches:** Integrate LLMs' inference capabilities into forward models to predict real-world dynamics more accurately, leading to more effective grounded actions.

- **Reward Function Gap Mitigation:** Discrepancies in reward function design or interpretation.
 - **Reward Shaping:** Modifies the reward function to provide more informative feedback and accelerate learning. Techniques include potential-based reward shaping, automaton-guided shaping, and assistant reward agents. Some methods augment returns based on trajectory distribution matching.
 - **LLM-Based Reward Design:** Leverages LLMs' generative and reasoning capabilities to automate reward function creation from natural language task descriptions (e.g., CARD, Text2Reward), or combine them with evolutionary algorithms for optimization. This approach holds promise for zero-shot RL by dynamically adjusting rewards during inference.

#### 4.2. Research Focus and Simulation Environments

The survey highlights that sim-to-real challenges are domain-specific.
- **Robotics:** Focuses on safety, multi-task capability, and high accuracy, often using simulators like Gazebo, MuJoCo, PyBullet, and benchmarks like RoboSuite, RLBench.
- **Transportation:** Emphasizes multi-agent coordination, smooth transitions, and real-time decision-making, utilizing simulators like SUMO, CARLA, MetaDrive, and benchmarks like SynTraC, LibSignal.
- **Recommender Systems:** Deals with large-scale online learning, off-policy evaluation, and counterfactual sensitivity, with simulators like RecSim, SlateQ, and benchmarks like KuaiSim, S2R-Rec.
- **Other Domains:** Includes finance, healthcare, and energy management, focusing on complex system dynamics, safety/cost constraints, and resource management.

The paper also notes the emerging trend of **GenAI-based Simulations**, including brute-force scaling (billions of samples), logic-integrated simulations (formal logic for scenario generation), differentiable & physics-augmented simulations (integrating physics into neural networks), and generative world models (e.g., NVIDIA COSMOS, Genesis for diverse, physically grounded simulations).

#### 4.3. Evaluation of Sim-to-Real Performance

The survey identifies three main evaluation settings:
- **Sim-to-Real:** Direct deployment on physical systems (highest realism, high cost, low safety).
- **Sim-to-Scale-down-Real:** Deployment on specialized, controlled physical testbeds (moderate realism, balances cost and safety).
- **Sim-to-Sim:** Testing policies trained in one simulator on a different, often more realistic or varied, simulation (lowest cost/risk, but may sacrifice realism).

Performance is quantified using the "Sim-to-Real gap" ($G(\pi) = \psi_s(\pi_{sim}) - \psi_r(\pi_{sim})$), where $\psi$ is a task-specific evaluation metric. Metrics vary by domain (e.g., success rate, execution time, failure rate for robotics; delay, throughput, queue length for transportation; CTR, precision, recall for recommender systems).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey paper holds significant importance for the reinforcement learning community and its journey toward real-world deployment. Its contributions offer a clear roadmap and a systematic understanding of a complex problem.

**Structured Understanding and Navigation:** The most notable impact of this survey lies in its novel MDP-based taxonomy. By framing sim-to-real challenges and solutions around Observation, Action, Transition, and Reward, the paper provides an intuitive and comprehensive mental model. This structure is invaluable for researchers, allowing them to:
- **Diagnose Problems:** Easily pinpoint where a sim-to-real gap might be originating (e.g., "Is it an observation fidelity issue, or are my actions not being executed as expected in the real world?").
- **Identify Solutions:** Quickly navigate to relevant techniques tailored to specific MDP components, saving time and guiding effective solution development.
- **Unify Disparate Research:** Connect seemingly unrelated methods by showing how they all contribute to bridging the gap in different parts of the MDP.

**Timely Integration of Foundation Models:** The survey's forward-looking inclusion of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) is highly significant. These models are rapidly transforming AI, and their potential to enhance sim-to-real transfer is immense. By detailing how foundation models can improve observation alignment, action planning, dynamics prediction, and even reward design, the paper:
- **Highlights New Research Avenues:** Encourages the RL community to explore the synergies between foundation models and sim-to-real challenges.
- **Provides Early Insights:** Offers a preliminary understanding of the opportunities and pitfalls of this integration, serving as a guide for future work.

**Comprehensive Resource Compilation:** The extensive listing of domain-specific simulators, benchmarks, and evaluation metrics, alongside the actively maintained GitHub repository, establishes this survey as a practical, living resource. This facilitates:
- **Standardized Evaluation:** Promotes consistent methodology for assessing sim-to-real performance across different research groups.
- **Reproducibility and Benchmarking:** Provides tools and environments for researchers to build upon existing work and compare new methods effectively.

**Identification of Critical Challenges and Opportunities:** The survey candidly discusses the persistent challenges that still hinder robust sim-to-real transfer, such as simulation fidelity, safety concerns, and the inherent complexity of real-world dynamics. Crucially, it also introduces new challenges brought by foundation models, specifically:
- **Hallucination:** The tendency of LLMs to generate factually incorrect outputs poses a significant safety risk when integrated into decision-making systems for real-world deployment. Addressing this, perhaps through grounding techniques or robust uncertainty quantification, is a critical future research direction.
- **Scalability and Computational Resources:** The immense computational requirements for training and inferencing large foundation models present a practical barrier, especially for real-time RL applications. Research into model distillation, efficient architectures, and optimized inference strategies is essential.

In conclusion, this survey is more than just a review; it serves as a foundational reference and a strategic guide for future research in sim-to-real reinforcement learning. By systematically dissecting the problem, integrating cutting-edge AI paradigms, and clearly outlining the remaining hurdles and opportunities, it is poised to accelerate the development of truly deployable, safe, and effective RL systems, particularly in high-stakes domains where robust transfer from simulation to reality is paramount.
