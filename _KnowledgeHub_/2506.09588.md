# Attention-Based Map Encoding for Learning Generalized Legged Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> An end-to-end learning framework integrates attention mechanisms into deep reinforcement learning to enable precise foothold selection and robust locomotion for legged robots on sparse terrains. The system allowed quadrupedal and humanoid robots to successfully traverse complex obstacle courses, showing higher success rates and better velocity tracking than previous methods.

## Problem

- Achieving simultaneous precision in foothold selection, robustness to uncertainties, and generalization across diverse, sparse terrains for legged robots.
- Model-based controllers offer precision but lack robustness to real-world uncertainties and are computationally expensive.
- Learning-based methods provide robustness but often struggle with precision and generalization on sparse terrains due to overfitting.

## Method

- An ==end-to-end deep reinforcement learning== framework incorporating an ==attention-based map encoding== module.
- The ==attention mechanism== dynamically focuses on relevant terrain features (potential footholds) using ==proprioception as a query== and terrain features as keys/values.
- A ==two-stage training pipeline== progressively develops foundational locomotion skills and then enhances robustness and generalization on challenging, sparse terrains using ==PPO== and ==curriculum learning==.

## Results

- Achieved success rates **26.5%** higher than Deep Tracking Control (DTC) and **77.3%** higher than baseline reinforcement learning in simulations.
- Demonstrated superior velocity tracking accuracy across speed ranges and a **60%** reduction in training time compared to hybrid methods.
- Successfully validated on hardware, with ANYmal-D and Fourier GR-1 robots traversing diverse sparse terrains, including a **100%** success rate for GR-1 on complex obstacle parkour.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Attention mechanisms can implicitly learn optimal foothold selection by dynamically focusing on relevant terrain features, enhancing precision in learning-based control.
> - A progressive two-stage training strategy is crucial for developing robust and generalized legged locomotion policies for sparse terrains.
> - The attention mechanism provides interpretability, revealing the network's focus on specific footholds, which can aid in understanding and trusting autonomous systems.
