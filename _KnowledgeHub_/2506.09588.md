# Attention-Based Map Encoding for Learning Generalized Legged Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> An end-to-end learning framework integrates attention mechanisms into deep reinforcement learning to enable precise foothold selection and robust locomotion for legged robots on sparse terrains. The system allowed quadrupedal and humanoid robots to successfully traverse complex obstacle courses, showing higher success rates and better velocity tracking than previous methods.

## Problem

- Achieving simultaneous precision in foothold selection, robustness to uncertainties, and generalization across diverse, sparse terrains for legged robots.
- Model-based controllers offer precision but lack robustness to real-world uncertainties and are computationally expensive.
- Learning-based methods provide robustness but often struggle with precision and generalization on sparse terrains due to overfitting.

## Method

- An ==end-to-end deep reinforcement learning== framework incorporating an ==attention-based map encoding== module.
- The ==attention mechanism== dynamically focuses on relevant terrain features (potential footholds) using ==proprioception as a query== and terrain features as keys/values.
- A ==two-stage training pipeline== progressively develops foundational locomotion skills and then enhances robustness and generalization on challenging, sparse terrains using ==PPO== and ==curriculum learning==.

## Results

- Achieved success rates **26.5%** higher than Deep Tracking Control (DTC) and **77.3%** higher than baseline reinforcement learning in simulations.
- Demonstrated superior velocity tracking accuracy across speed ranges and a **60%** reduction in training time compared to hybrid methods.
- Successfully validated on hardware, with ANYmal-D and Fourier GR-1 robots traversing diverse sparse terrains, including a **100%** success rate for GR-1 on complex obstacle parkour.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Attention mechanisms can implicitly learn optimal foothold selection by dynamically focusing on relevant terrain features, enhancing precision in learning-based control.
> - A progressive two-stage training strategy is crucial for developing robust and generalized legged locomotion policies for sparse terrains.
> - The attention mechanism provides interpretability, revealing the network's focus on specific footholds, which can aid in understanding and trusting autonomous systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Legged robots face a fundamental challenge when navigating complex environments: they must simultaneously achieve precision in foothold selection, robustness against uncertainties, and generalization across diverse terrains. This paper presents a solution that combines the strengths of deep reinforcement learning with attention mechanisms to enable both quadrupedal and humanoid robots to traverse challenging sparse terrains.

The research addresses a critical gap in legged robotics: while model-based controllers excel at precise foothold planning but struggle with real-world uncertainties, learning-based approaches are robust but often fail on sparse terrains requiring precise stepping. The authors propose an end-to-end learning framework that integrates attention mechanisms to implicitly learn optimal foothold selection while maintaining the robustness benefits of reinforcement learning.

### 2. The Challenge of Legged Locomotion

Legged locomotion on complex terrains requires solving three interconnected problems:

1. **Precision**: Accurately placing feet on small, discrete footholds such as stepping stones or narrow beams
2. **Robustness**: Handling uncertainties in sensing, actuation, and environmental conditions
3. **Generalization**: Adapting to unseen terrain configurations and types

Traditional approaches typically excel at only one or two of these requirements. Model-based controllers using techniques like Model Predictive Control (MPC) can generate precise, dynamically feasible trajectories but are computationally expensive and sensitive to model inaccuracies. Conversely, deep reinforcement learning (DRL) methods achieve impressive robustness through domain randomization but often struggle with precision on sparse terrains, where optimal foothold discovery is challenging.

The authors identify that existing end-to-end learning approaches fail to generalize well to sparse terrains because they tend to overfit to specific terrain types encountered during training. This limitation significantly restricts the operational range of legged robots in real-world scenarios.

### 3. Attention-Based Map Encoding

The core innovation lies in the attention-based map encoding architecture that enables the neural network to dynamically focus on relevant terrain features based on the robot's current state and commanded velocity.

#### 3.1. Architecture Components

The system processes two types of input:

**Map Scan Processing**: Robot-centric terrain height maps (26×16×3 for ANYmal-D, 13×26×3 for GR-1) are processed through a two-layer Convolutional Neural Network (CNN). The CNN extracts local terrain features which are then concatenated with the original 3D coordinates to form point-wise features. These features serve as Keys and Values for the attention mechanism.

**Proprioception Processing**: The robot's internal state (linear/angular velocities, gravity vector, joint positions/velocities, previous actions) is processed through a linear layer to generate a proprioception embedding, which acts as the Query for attention.

**Multi-Head Attention**: The attention mechanism computes a weighted sum of terrain features, where weights are determined by the compatibility between the robot's current state (Query) and each terrain point (Keys). This allows the network to implicitly identify suitable future footholds without explicit planning.

The mathematical formulation follows the standard attention mechanism:

```
Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax(QK^T/√d)V
```

Where Q is the proprioception embedding, K and V are the processed terrain features, and d is the feature dimension.

### 4. Two-Stage Training Pipeline

The training methodology employs a progressive approach to develop both robust locomotion skills and precise foothold selection capabilities.

#### 4.1. Stage 1: Foundation Learning

- **Environment**: Base terrains including stairs, pits, rough ground, basic pallets, gaps, and grid stones
- **Observations**: Privileged information (ground truth without noise)
- **Objective**: Establish fundamental locomotion behaviors and initialize the attention mechanism

#### 4.2. Stage 2: Robustness and Generalization

- **Environment**: Extended terrain set with challenging configurations (pentagon stones, narrow beams, single-column stones, consecutive gaps)
- **Observations**: Realistic sensor data with noise, drift, and disturbances
- **Objective**: Enhance robustness and generalization through domain randomization

The training uses Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with comprehensive reward functions incorporating task completion, motion smoothness, and behavioral style terms. A 10-level curriculum learning system automatically adjusts terrain difficulty based on robot performance.

### 5. Experimental Results and Validation

The experimental validation demonstrates significant improvements across multiple metrics:

#### 5.1. Simulation Performance

The controllers achieved substantial success rate improvements:
- 26.5% higher success rate compared to Deep Tracking Control (DTC)
- 77.3% higher success rate compared to baseline reinforcement learning
- Superior velocity tracking accuracy, particularly at higher speeds

#### 5.2. Hardware Validation

Real-world experiments on both ANYmal-D (quadruped) and Fourier GR-1 (humanoid) robots demonstrated:
- Successful traversal of diverse sparse terrains including stepping stones, narrow beams, and gaps
- 100% success rate for GR-1 on challenging obstacle parkour with disturbances
- Emergent recovery behaviors such as single-leg switch hops and whole-body coordination

### 6. Real-World Deployment

The sim-to-real transfer capabilities represent a significant achievement, particularly for humanoid locomotion on sparse terrains. The GR-1 humanoid robot successfully navigated complex obstacle courses that would challenge even human performers, demonstrating natural arm swinging for balance and adaptive gait modifications based on terrain requirements.

Key deployment characteristics include:
- Real-time execution on onboard computing hardware
- Robust performance despite sensor noise and environmental disturbances
- Adaptive behavior modification based on terrain characteristics
- Emergency recovery capabilities when encountering unexpected situations

### 7. Interpretability Through Attention

One of the most compelling aspects of this work is the interpretability provided by attention visualizations. The attention weights clearly show the network's focus on future footholds, with high-attention regions (visualized as red dots) corresponding to optimal stepping locations. This provides insight into the decision-making process of what would otherwise be a black-box neural network.

The attention mechanism demonstrates intelligent behavior such as:
- Focusing on stable footholds ahead of the robot's current position
- Adapting attention patterns based on commanded velocity directions
- Refusing to follow infeasible commands (e.g., maintaining focus on a narrow beam even when commanded to turn off it)

### 8. Performance Benchmarks

#### 8.1. Benchmark Comparisons

The method significantly outperforms existing approaches:
- **Velocity Tracking**: Lower tracking errors across all speed ranges compared to DTC
- **Success Rates**: Consistently higher success rates across all terrain types
- **Computational Efficiency**: 60% reduction in training time compared to hybrid methods

#### 8.2. Ablation Studies

The ablation studies validate key design choices:
- **Two-Stage Training**: Essential for achieving high performance, with single-stage approaches showing inferior convergence
- **Attention Architecture**: Multi-head attention outperforms alternative architectures including full transformer encoders and CNN-based approaches
- **Progressive Complexity**: The staged introduction of noise and challenging terrains proves crucial for robust generalization

### 9. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge several limitations that provide direction for future research:

1. **Training Efficiency**: Despite improvements, training still requires several days, making hyperparameter optimization challenging
2. **2.5D Representation**: Current height maps may be insufficient for highly complex 3D environments with overhangs or confined spaces
3. **Locomotion Focus**: The framework primarily addresses locomotion, with manipulation integration remaining an open challenge

Future work aims to address these limitations through more efficient training methods, 3D terrain representations, and extension to loco-manipulation tasks involving complex object interaction.

### 10. Conclusion

This research represents a significant advancement in legged robotics by successfully combining precision, robustness, and generalization in a single end-to-end learning framework. The attention-based map encoding provides an elegant solution to the foothold selection problem while maintaining the robustness benefits of reinforcement learning.

The successful deployment on both quadrupedal and humanoid platforms, particularly the achievement of dynamic humanoid locomotion on sparse terrains, demonstrates the broad applicability of the approach. The interpretability provided by attention visualizations offers valuable insights into neural network decision-making, potentially building trust in autonomous systems.

Most importantly, this work expands the operational capabilities of legged robots, enabling them to navigate environments previously accessible only to highly specialized systems or human operators. The combination of theoretical innovation, rigorous experimental validation, and successful real-world deployment positions this research as a significant contribution to the field of autonomous robotics.
