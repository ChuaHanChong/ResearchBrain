# DATT

> [!summary] Summary
> **DATT** (Deep Adaptive Trajectory Tracking) precisely tracks arbitrary, even infeasible, quadrotor trajectories under large disturbances. It uses a ==feedforward-feedback-adaptive control structure== trained in simulation with RL, augmented at deployment with an ==L1 adaptive-control== disturbance estimator in closed loop, with no fine-tuning on hardware.

## Problem

- Precise arbitrary-trajectory tracking for quadrotors is hard due to unknown nonlinear dynamics, trajectory infeasibility, and actuation limits.
- Adaptive nonlinear and model-predictive controllers fail on infeasible trajectories and in unsteady wind, and are often too slow for onboard real-time control.

## Method

- A novel ==feedforward-feedback-adaptive== control structure trained in simulation via ==reinforcement learning==.
- At deployment, augmented with a disturbance estimator using ==L1 adaptive control== in closed loop, requiring no hardware fine-tuning.

## Results

- Significantly outperforms competitive adaptive nonlinear and MPC controllers on both feasible-smooth and infeasible trajectories in unsteady wind, including scenarios where baselines completely fail.
- Runs online with inference time **< 3.2 ms**, less than **1/4** of the adaptive-nonlinear-MPC baseline.

## Takeaways

- Zero-shot sim-to-real quadrotor tracking via RL + L1 adaptive control; a common baseline for learned adaptive control and disturbance rejection on agile aerial robots.
