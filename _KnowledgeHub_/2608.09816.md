# Hierarchical Fast-Slow ReAct Agent for Zero-Shot Object-Goal Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical fast-slow agent for zero-shot object-goal navigation. An always-on reactive vision-language value-map controller moves the robot and writes coordinate-anchored memory: a semantic grid of room types and confirmed object instances plus pose-tagged keyframes. An event-triggered, budget-bounded ReAct layer wakes only at structural impasses, reasons over text first, recalls stored views only when needed, and fails open to the reactive base. Reaches 68.75% SR on HM3D v1 val.

## Problem

- Value-map controllers pick frontiers by argmax over the current map; the evidence behind each score is discarded, leaving no recourse at structural impasses.
- Navigation systems embedding large vision-language models query them on schedules fixed before the robot moves, so rooms traversed minutes earlier can never be re-examined.
- Prior work leaves invocation policy unspecified: when to call the model, how much evidence one call may gather, and where control goes when a call fails.

## Method

- ==Coordinate-anchored two-tier memory==: a coarse semantic grid of room-vote histograms and confirmed object instances (two sightings to become retrievable), plus a bounded per-floor store of pose-tagged keyframes.
- The **Hierarchical Fast-Slow ReAct Agent** runs an ==event-triggered bounded reason-retrieve-act loop== with four structural triggers - contact stalls, distant frontiers, frontier exhaustion, floor changes - each reasoning on text first and retrieving keyframes only when text cannot separate candidates.
- ==Pre-fusion per-detection VLM verification gate== on goal candidates, plus two-level budgets (3 rounds and 2 images per invocation; 20 calls and 10 image-bearing per run), ==watchdog==, ==circuit breaker==, and ==fail-open arbitration==.

## Results

- **68.75%** SR / **33.97%** SPL on HM3D v1 val and **47.29%** SR / **18.01%** SPL on MP3D val, ahead of every zero-shot baseline compared, at **5.00** model calls per episode.
- Greedy argmax over far frontiers loses **3.40** SR points (95% CI [**1.70**, **5.05**], McNemar p<**0.001**); deliberating at every frontier scores **64.80%**, below the greedy arm despite **8.87** calls.
- One-Shot full-context imagery costs **3.70** points and removing the semantic grid costs **3.35** points at identical call budget; the verification gate vetoes **16.7%** of candidate detections.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Gains come from the policy governing when to spend model calls, not from model access; more deliberation at every decision actively degrades success rate.
> - Storing raw pose-anchored views alongside abstract summaries lets an agent re-examine past places without physically retraversing them.
> - Bounded budgets plus a single fail-open terminal controller make model-in-the-loop deliberation deployable: every failure path degrades to the reactive base.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

This work is situated within the field of Zero-Shot Object-Goal Navigation (ZSON), a sub-area of embodied AI that requires a robot to locate a specified object category in an environment it has not previously encountered, without any category-specific training.

The prevailing approaches in ZSON often employ reactive controllers that score frontiers (boundaries between explored and unknown space) using a vision-language value map. Examples include VLFM, ASCENT, ZSON, ESC, and L3MVN, which typically make decisions by selecting the highest-scoring frontier based on the current view. A limitation of these reactive systems is their inability to address "structural impasses," such as when local planners are defeated by clutter, all remaining frontiers are distant, the frontier set is exhausted due to a missed opening, or the goal is on another floor. These systems also tend to discard evidence shortly after it is used, preventing reconsideration of past observations. Furthermore, existing systems that integrate Large Language Models (LLMs) or Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into navigation, such as CogNav and SG-Nav, often query these models on a fixed schedule, providing them with a predefined set of evidence. This means that views from previously visited rooms cannot be re-examined, and the governance mechanisms for VLM consultation (e.g., when to call, how much evidence to gather, what happens on failure) are often unspecified. Memory systems like VLMaps and ConceptGraphs primarily store descriptors rather than raw visual data, and do not dictate when a model should be consulted.

Another relevant area is the development of ReAct agents, which interleave reasoning, tool calls, and observations, allowing the model to dynamically decide what to examine next. This paradigm has been applied in various domains, including software engineering and laboratory automation. In embodied AI, approaches like ORION and TANGO have used ReAct-like loops to drive robot actions through dialogue or program composition, sometimes querying the model at every decision step.

This work addresses the identified limitations by proposing a hierarchical fast-slow agent. It integrates a reactive controller with a deliberative ReAct layer that selectively intervenes at structural impasses, rather than driving every step or querying the VLM on a fixed schedule. This approach aims to leverage memory and deliberative reasoning to overcome challenges that purely reactive or fixed-schedule systems cannot, while maintaining efficiency by keeping the reactive layer as the primary driver. The key distinction lies in its adaptive invocation policy for the VLM, its structured memory, and its explicit failure handling mechanisms.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient robotic agent capable of performing Zero-Shot Object-Goal Navigation (ZSON) in previously unseen indoor environments. This involves enabling a robot to autonomously search for and locate an instance of a named object category (e.g., "bed," "toilet," "plant") without prior training specific to that category or environment.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing ZSON approaches:
1. **Inability to resolve structural impasses:** Current reactive controllers, while efficient for short-horizon navigation, struggle with complex scenarios such as:
 - **Local planner failures:** When the immediate path is blocked by clutter, or an chosen frontier is unreachable.
 - **Distant or ambiguous frontiers:** When all remaining exploration targets are far away, and a simple value-map score is insufficient to make an informed decision.
 - **Frontier exhaustion:** When the robot mistakenly believes it has explored all accessible areas, potentially missing an opening to new space.
 - **Multi-floor navigation:** When the goal object might be on another floor, requiring strategic decision-making about inter-floor transitions.
 These impasses often require reasoning beyond the immediate perception and map.
2. **Lack of persistent and accessible memory:** Existing systems frequently discard past observations, making it impossible to re-examine or reason over previously seen views or semantic information. While some systems maintain summaries (e.g., scene graphs), they typically do not retain raw, pose-tagged visual evidence. This prevents the agent from re-evaluating past observations in light of new information or a changed situation.
3. **Unspecified VLM/LLM consultation policies:** For systems that integrate large language or vision-language models, the decision-making process for when to query these computationally expensive models, how much evidence to provide, and how to handle potential failures (e.g., unparsable replies, service outages, budget exhaustion) is often ad-hoc or fixed. This limits their adaptability and robustness in real-world or complex scenarios.

To address these motivations, the research pursues the following key objectives:
- **Develop a hierarchical fast-slow architecture:** This aims to combine the efficiency of a reactive controller for continuous movement with the strategic reasoning capabilities of a deliberative layer for complex decision points. The reactive layer continuously operates, while the deliberative layer is triggered only when necessary.
- **Establish a coordinate-anchored memory system:** This memory should store both abstract semantic information (room types, confirmed object instances) and concrete pose-tagged keyframes, allowing for retrieval of both textual and visual evidence based on spatial coordinates. This enables the agent to deliberate over what it has already seen.
- **Implement an online verification gate for object detections:** To prevent false positives from permanently corrupting the object map that dictates stopping conditions, a VLM-based gate should screen goal candidates before fusion.
- **Design a bounded and fail-open deliberation policy:** This involves defining clear triggers for deliberative intervention, setting per-invocation and per-run budgets for VLM calls, and ensuring that any deliberative failure gracefully returns control to the reactive layer, preventing the agent from stalling indefinitely.
- **Demonstrate state-of-the-art performance:** The system aims to achieve superior success rates and efficiency compared to existing zero-shot baselines on standard benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed agent employs a hierarchical fast–slow architecture, comprising a continuously running reactive controller and an event-triggered deliberative controller. These two components share a common spatial state, with the reactive layer populating the memory that the deliberative layer reads.

**Reactive Controller (Fast Path):**
The reactive controller operates at every step, managing perception, mapping, and short-horizon motion. It is built upon the ASCENT value-map controller framework and enhanced with an online VLM verification gate and a mechanism to trigger the deliberative layer.
- **Mapping:** For each floor, it maintains four 2D maps projected from depth data into the odometry frame:
 - **Occupancy Map:** Used for collision avoidance and extracting frontier points (boundaries between explored and unknown space).
 - **Value Map:** Stores a target-conditioned score for every free cell, representing the similarity between the view towards that cell and the goal object, using a pretrained image-text matching model (e.g., BLIP-2 encoders).
 - **Semantic Grid:** A coarse grid that aggregates semantic information, primarily for the deliberative controller.
 - **Object Map:** Accumulates goal object detections that have passed a verification gate, serving as the map that authorizes stopping.
- **Perception and Action Primitives:** Goal candidates are generated by a detection ensemble and a lightweight segmenter. Room labels are provided by a scene classifier, and pixel semantics by an RGB-D network. A pretrained point-goal policy executes motion, and a stair finite-state machine handles inter-floor transitions.
- **Online Per-Detection Verification Gate:** Before any goal detection is fused into the object map, it passes through this gate. A VLM examines the current frame cropped to the candidate bounding box along with the proposed category to make an accept/reject decision. Rejected candidates are discarded, preventing false positives from becoming permanent stopping targets.

**Coordinate-Anchored Memory:**
The reactive controller populates two coordinate-indexed memory structures:
- **Semantic Grid:** A coarse grid (0.4m cells) that aggregates 8x8 blocks of occupancy pixels. Each cell stores:
 - A histogram of room-type votes, accumulating evidence over time.
 - A bounded ring of keyframe indices whose view frusta covered the cell.
 - A sparse table of *context* object instances (non-goal categories) with their class, back-projected world position, observation count, and supporting keyframes. An instance becomes "confirmed" and retrievable after being observed at least twice.
- **Keyframe Store:** A bounded collection of low-resolution keyframe thumbnails, sampled based on translation/rotation thresholds. Each keyframe is tagged with its pose, floor, step index, room label, and visible objects. A per-floor capacity ensures that memory of previous floors is not evicted by newly entered ones.
- **Retrieval Interface:** Given a world position query, the memory provides a local room estimate, nearby confirmed instances, coverage statistics, and a ranked set of candidate keyframes. Image recall follows a strict fallback ladder, ensuring that a result is always returned, even if text-only.

**Event-Triggered Deliberation (Slow Path):**
A deliberative controller, implementing a reason–retrieve–act loop (ReAct), wakes only when one of four structural impasses is detected by the reactive controller. Each invocation adheres to per-invocation budgets (e.g., max 3 reasoning rounds, max 2 images per call, wall-clock cap).
- **Contact Stalls:** Triggered by cumulative collision count or lack of net displacement. Recovery is tiered: first a replan (no VLM call), then, if persistent, a VLM query for an escape heading, executing short macro-actions.
- **Distant Frontiers:** Triggered when the nearest frontier is beyond a distance threshold. The loop assembles textual descriptions for multiple candidates from semantic memory and a knowledge graph. The first round is text-only; if ambiguous, it retrieves first-person images for up to two candidates for a second, image-bearing round. The chosen frontier becomes a persistent deliberative target.
- **Frontier Exhaustion:** If the frontier set empties before the goal is found, the controller re-examines stored keyframes on the current floor (sampled into a montage). A VLM is queried to identify overlooked passages (e.g., staircases, doors) that could lead to unexplored space. An identified view's pose becomes a persistent goal.
- **Floor Changes:** When exploration on the current floor stalls and other floors are known, a purely textual per-floor summary is sent to the VLM to decide whether to change floors.

**Arbitration and Budget Control:**
A single arbiter prioritizes commands: recovery macro-actions > stair FSM > detected goal / deliberative target. Deliberative targets are persistent, suppressing reactive preemption until reached or validity expires.
Model calls (VLM service invocations by the deliberative controller) are capped at two levels: per-invocation (as described above) and per-run (max 20 model calls, max 10 image-bearing calls). A watchdog monitors decision time, and a circuit-breaker disables calls after consecutive failures. All failure paths (e.g., empty memory, unparsable reply, budget exhaustion) deterministically return control to the reactive base, ensuring the agent remains operational. The deliberative loops are implemented as declarative skill specifications, allowing for flexible configuration.

### 4. Main findings and results

The agent was evaluated on the ObjectNav val splits of HM3D v1 (2000 episodes, 6 goal categories) and Matterport3D (MP3D, 2195 episodes, 21 categories) using the Habitat simulator. Performance was measured by Success Rate (SR) and Success weighted by Path Length (SPL).

**Main Results:**
The full system (Ours (Full)) achieved a higher success rate and path efficiency compared to all zero-shot baselines:
- **HM3D v1 val:** 68.75% SR and 33.97% SPL.
- **MP3D val:** 47.29% SR and 18.01% SPL.
In comparison, the next best baseline, ASCENT, achieved 65.4% SR and 33.5% SPL on HM3D, and 44.5% SR and 15.5% SPL on MP3D.
The system averaged 5.00 deliberative model calls and 205 environment steps per episode.

**Ablation Studies (on HM3D v1 val):**
Five ablations were conducted to assess the contribution of different components:
- **Greedy Frontier:** This configuration replaced deliberation for far frontiers with a simple argmax selection based on the value map. It resulted in a 3.40 SR point decrease (from 68.75% to 65.35%) compared to the full system, with a 95% confidence interval of [1.70, 5.05] (p < 0.001), indicating the benefit of deliberative reasoning at distant frontiers.
- **Always-Deliberate:** This configuration removed the intelligent trigger and forced deliberation at *every* frontier choice, rather than just far ones. It scored 64.80% SR, which is 3.95 SR points lower than the full system and even below the Greedy Frontier approach (65.35% SR). This occurred despite making more VLM calls per episode (8.87 vs. 5.00 for full system), indicating that *when* deliberation occurs is critical, not just the access to the model.
- **One-Shot:** This ablation kept the trigger policy but used a single, full-context query for images, removing adaptive evidence acquisition. It cost 3.70 SR points (from 68.75% to 65.05%), demonstrating the value of acquiring evidence on demand.
- **w/o Semantic Grid:** This removed the abstract memory tier (room types and confirmed instances), leaving only the keyframe store. It resulted in a 3.35 SR point decrease (from 68.75% to 65.40%), despite spending a similar budget on model calls (4.99 calls, 3.02 image-bearing calls). This highlights the importance of the structured textual memory for deliberation.
- **w/o Rescan:** This removed the functionality to re-examine stored views when frontiers are exhausted. It showed a minor decrease of 0.25 SR points (from 68.75% to 68.50%), suggesting that while rescan is effective when triggered, it contributes less significantly to the overall SR than other components.

**Analysis of Specific Mechanisms:**
- **Rescan Attribution:** The rescan mechanism was activated in 6.1% of episodes, and in 41.4% of these activations, it successfully identified a new goal from remembered views.
- **Verification Gate:** The online per-detection VLM verification gate vetoed 16.7% of candidate goal detections before they were fused into the object map, preventing false positives.
- **Gain Attribution (Failure Classes):** An analysis of failure classes (false positives, false negatives, no-frontier stops, step-outs) showed that deliberation contributes to reducing errors across multiple categories. For instance, deliberation returned 1.35 points from false positives, 1.25 points from no-frontier stops, and 1.00 point from false negatives. The semantic grid primarily reduced no-frontier stops, accounting for 2.55 of its 3.35 point contribution. This suggests that the components address distinct failure modes. The 3.40 points of SR gained by deliberation primarily translated into successes, rather than just delaying failures.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research introduces a hierarchical fast–slow agent for Zero-Shot Object-Goal Navigation, which demonstrates a method for integrating reactive control with deliberative reasoning. The work highlights several aspects of embodied AI:

**Significance:**
- **Improved ZSON Performance:** The agent establishes new state-of-the-art success rates on standard ZSON benchmarks (HM3D v1 and MP3D val), indicating a substantial advancement in the field.
- **Effective Hierarchical Architecture:** The design successfully integrates a continuous, fast reactive controller with an intermittent, slow deliberative reasoning layer. This allows for efficient execution of routine navigation while enabling strategic, memory-augmented reasoning at complex decision points.
- **Importance of Coordinate-Anchored Memory:** The development of a multi-tier, coordinate-anchored memory (semantic grid and keyframe store) demonstrates the value of retaining and efficiently retrieving both abstract semantic information and concrete visual observations. This memory structure enables deliberation over past experiences, which is shown to be crucial for resolving structural impasses.
- **Adaptive and Bounded VLM Consultation:** The research demonstrates that *when* to consult large models (VLMs) and *how* to manage their computational cost (through budgets and adaptive evidence acquisition) are critical for effective performance. The "Always-Deliberate" ablation particularly emphasizes that frequent, untriggered VLM calls can degrade performance.
- **Robustness and Fail-Open Design:** The explicit design of failure paths, where any deliberative failure returns control to the reactive base, ensures the agent's robustness and prevents it from stalling. The online verification gate for object detections also contributes to reliability by filtering out false positives.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** This work provides a framework for building more intelligent and robust embodied AI agents that can navigate complex, unknown environments. The ability to reason over past observations and strategically adapt to structural impasses is a key step towards more general-purpose robot intelligence.
- **Applications in Robotics:** The findings could influence the design of autonomous robots for various applications, including exploration, search and rescue, logistics in unstructured environments, and personal assistance, where adaptability to novel situations is essential.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The hierarchical architecture, memory system, and adaptive model invocation policies presented here can serve as a foundation for further research into integrating large models with robotic control, particularly for tasks requiring long-horizon planning and reasoning under uncertainty.
- **Efficient Resource Utilization:** By demonstrating that strategic and bounded deliberation, rather than continuous and unconstrained model usage, yields better results, the work provides insights into efficiently leveraging computationally intensive large models in resource-constrained robotic systems.
