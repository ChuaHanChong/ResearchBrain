# CLASP: Closed-loop Asynchronous Spatial Perception for Open-vocabulary Desktop Object Grasping

> [!summary] Summary
> CLASP, a novel closed-loop framework, enables robots to perform intelligent, open-vocabulary grasping of desktop objects based on natural language instructions. It achieved an 87.0% overall pick success rate in simulation, surpassing vision-language model baselines, and demonstrated robust performance in real-world pick-and-categorize tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-language models (VLMs) for robotic grasping suffer from a scarcity of high-quality multi-modal demonstration data for training.
- VLMs often exhibit spatial hallucinations due to weak geometric grounding, leading to inaccurate grasp pose selections.
- Traditional open-loop execution in robotic systems is fragile in dynamic environments, lacking mechanisms for error detection and correction.

## Method

- **CLASP** employs a ==Dual-Pathway Hierarchical Perception== module that processes inputs through distinct semantic and geometric pathways to reduce spatial illusions.
- A VLM-Driven Structured Grasp Reasoning module, fine-tuned on a custom dataset, translates multimodal perceptions and natural language into constrained, deterministic grasp parameters.
- An ==Asynchronous Closed-Loop Evaluator==, featuring a Judger module, provides real-time error correction and adaptive re-planning based on execution feedback, coupled with an asynchronous policy for efficiency.

## Results

- CLASP achieved an **87.0**% overall pick success rate in a cluttered simulation environment, outperforming baselines like Qwen3-VL (**72.27**%) and Doubao-Seed (**68.67**%).
- The asynchronous policy reduced total execution time by **39.8**% and average task completion time by **39.9**% while also decreasing execution-time variance by **59.5**%.
- The framework improved categorical placement reliability, increasing classification IoU by **10.0**% (Toys), **20.0**% (Tools), and **26.67**% (Blocks) compared to the Qwen3-VL baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling semantic understanding from fine-grained geometric perception significantly mitigates spatial hallucinations inherent in VLM-based grasping.
> - Implementing an asynchronous closed-loop feedback mechanism with a diagnostic Judger module enhances robustness and allows for dynamic error recovery in complex, unstructured environments.
> - Automated synthesis of high-quality spatial annotations and reasoning templates can effectively address the data scarcity bottleneck for training capable robotic manipulation systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic grasping is progressing towards enabling robots to perform intelligent manipulation based on natural language instructions, particularly for diverse objects in unstructured, desktop-like environments. This capability is crucial for applications in intelligent manufacturing, logistics, agriculture, retail, and service robotics, where objects vary and layouts change frequently.

Existing robotic grasping approaches can be broadly categorized into rule-based and learning-based methods. Rule-based methods, such as GraspNet and AnyGrasp, primarily rely on geometric information. While providing accurate geometric evaluation, they often neglect semantic cues and exhibit limitations in cross-scenario generalization, sometimes requiring complex designs or specialized hardware.

Learning-based methods, particularly those leveraging vision-language models (VLMs) and Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models, have shown promise in incorporating semantic understanding and generalization. Early works in this area, like CLIP and BLIP, focused on multi-modal alignment but lacked capabilities for complex instruction reasoning. More recent VLMs, such as Qwen3-VL and Doubao-Seed, have integrated affordance information into pre-training data, enabling semantic-guided grasping of open-vocabulary objects. However, their deployment in low-level robotic manipulation faces several challenges:
1. **Data Scarcity**: A significant lack of high-quality multi-modal demonstration data is a common bottleneck.
2. **Spatial Hallucination**: VLMs often lack fine-grained geometric grounding, leading to spatial inaccuracies and unreliable grasp selections.
3. **Open-Loop Fragility**: Traditional open-loop execution is vulnerable in dynamic environments, as it lacks mechanisms for detecting and correcting errors.
VLA models, including OpenVLA and RT-2, address some of these issues by directly mapping vision-language inputs to robot actions. However, they typically demand extensive fine-tuning data and large model scales, limiting their practical deployment in resource-constrained or long-tail scenarios. Furthermore, dexterous manipulation approaches, while capable of complex tasks, often incur high hardware costs and suffer from limited compatibility and a lack of standardized datasets.

CLASP positions itself within this landscape by specifically addressing the limitations of VLM deployment for low-level grasping. It aims to mitigate spatial hallucinations and the fragility of open-loop execution by bridging semantic reasoning with physical execution through a novel closed-loop framework. This approach seeks to improve robustness and generalization without requiring the intensive fine-tuning data or specialized hardware typically associated with VLA or dexterous manipulation models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to perform intelligent, open-vocabulary grasping of desktop objects based on natural language instructions, operating effectively in dynamic and unstructured environments. This capability requires robots to integrate geometric perception with complex semantic understanding.

The motivation for developing the CLASP framework stems from critical bottlenecks identified in the deployment of vision-language models (VLMs) for low-level robotic manipulation tasks:

1. **Scarcity of High-Quality Multimodal Demonstrations**: Current learning-based robotic systems, especially those leveraging VLMs, often rely on extensive datasets of human demonstrations or teleoperation for training. Acquiring such high-quality, spatially annotated, and task-specific multimodal data is resource-intensive and not easily scalable. This limitation hinders the generalization capabilities of robots to new objects and environments. CLASP aims to address this by developing a scalable method for automatic data synthesis.

2. **Spatial Hallucination Caused by Weak Geometric Grounding**: Vision-language models excel at high-level semantic understanding but frequently struggle with fine-grained geometric reasoning crucial for precise physical interaction. This can lead to "spatial hallucinations," where a VLM might correctly identify an object or its affordances but propose an unfeasible or unstable grasp pose due to a lack of accurate geometric grounding. The research is motivated by the need to decouple semantic intent from precise geometric information to guide grasp generation and reduce such errors.

3. **Fragility of Open-Loop Execution in Dynamic Environments**: Most robotic systems operate in an open-loop manner, executing pre-planned actions without continuous feedback or error correction. In dynamic, real-world scenarios where object positions might shift, grasps might fail, or unforeseen obstacles appear, open-loop execution is prone to failure and lacks adaptability. This fragility makes real-world deployment challenging. CLASP is motivated to establish a robust error-correction mechanism through closed-loop feedback, allowing the robot to diagnose failures and adapt its actions.

By addressing these three challenges, CLASP aims to develop a robust, generalized, and efficient framework for robotic grasping that can bridge the semantic and geometric gap inherent in current VLM-based approaches, thereby improving reliability and performance in practical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CLASP (Closed-Loop Asynchronous Spatial Perception) is designed as a novel asynchronous closed-loop framework comprising a Dual-Pathway Hierarchical Perception module (P), a Vision-Language Reasoner (R), and an Asynchronous Closed-Loop Evaluator (E). This system maps a natural language instruction (ℓ) and an observation (I_t) to an action (a_t) and a discrete state (S_t), iteratively refining actions until a terminal condition is met.

**Problem Formulation:**
The task is defined as finding an optimal grasp `g*` that minimizes a task-conditioned energy functional `E(g; I)`. This functional combines geometric grasp stability (`E_geo`) and functional affordance preferences (`E_sem`), weighted by `λ_g` and `λ_s`. Parallel-jaw grasps are parameterized in image space by two 2D contact points `g = (p_1, p_2)`, with an implied gripper width `w(g)` constrained by hardware limits. Scene observations are acquired via a calibrated top-down camera, providing RGB images (`I_rgb`), depth maps (D), and semantic bounding boxes (B).

**CLASP Design Overview:**

1. **Dual-Pathway Hierarchical Perception (P)**:
 This module processes the RGB input `I` through two distinct pathways to decouple semantic intent from geometric grounding.
 - **End-to-end Semantic Pathway**: Produces a dense embedding `Z = ψ(I)` for global inference, capturing high-level semantic information.
 - **Geometric-Aware Pathway**: Computes open-vocabulary bounding boxes `B = Det(I, ℓ)`, object masks `M = Seg(I, B)`, and explicit pose/centroid descriptors `G = Geom(M)`. This pathway provides fine-grained spatial and geometric features, reducing uncertainty for downstream reasoning.
 The module guides the inference model's output towards definite action tuples, thereby mitigating spatial illusions.

2. **VLM-Driven Structured Grasp Reasoning (R)**:
 The multimodal outputs from the perception module (`Z`, `G`) along with the natural language instruction (`ℓ`) are fed into the reasoning module. This module, fine-tuned on a large dataset, acts as a constrained predictor, generating a structured JSON output `A = (u, v, θ, c)`. Here, `(u, v)` are pixel coordinates for the grasp center, `θ` is the planar grasp angle, and `c` is the inferred object category. The structured output space and incorporation of spatial and collision constraints as predicates are designed to ensure deterministic control and reduce ambiguity.

3. **Asynchronous Closed-Loop Evaluator (E)**:
 This component manages the execution feedback loop and error correction.
 - **Control & Execution**: Predicted grasp parameters `A` are projected from 2D image coordinates to 3D world coordinates `(X,Y, Z)` using camera intrinsics and depth, and then mapped to a 7-DoF robotic arm command `a_t`.
 - **Task Status Detection**: After execution, the evaluator compares the pre- and post-execution states (`I_t`, `I_{t+1}`) to determine the task status `S_t` (Success, Adjustment Needed, Object Removed).
 - **Judger Module**: If the task state is "Adjustment Needed," the Judger module is activated. It applies a heuristic procedure (Failure→Shift→Execute→Check) and feature processing to compute spatial adjustments, such as shifting grasp points towards the object's center. It then generates text-based diagnostic feedback (`τ_t`) which is stored in the reasoner's memory. This feedback loop dynamically improves subsequent attempts, establishing a robust error-correction mechanism.
 - **Asynchronous Policy**: To enhance efficiency, the Reasoner and Judger modules are decoupled into independent background threads. This allows for asynchronous perception and reasoning to occur concurrently with execution, minimizing blocking overhead and improving overall system responsiveness.

**Data Engine for Multimodal Dataset Construction:**
A scalable multi-modal data engine is developed to automatically synthesize high-quality spatial annotations and reasoning templates without human teleoperation.
- **Data Sources**: Integrates over 200k real-world desktop scenes and over 300k synthetic desktop scenes (with 1000+ high-precision 3D models).
- **Annotation Process**:
 - For real-world scenes, semantic bounding boxes (B) are obtained from annotations, and depth (D) is fused from sensor data and RGB-based depth estimation.
 - For synthetic scenes, B is extracted from rendered labels, and D is directly available.
 - A Spatial Affordance Annotation module `R = f(I_rgb, D, B)` computes feasible graspable regions for each object.
- **Reasoning Template Annotation**: A labeled set `T = {(τ_k, g_k, y_k)}` is constructed, comprising textual templates, grasp point pairs `g_k`, and feasibility indicators `y_k`. Positive examples include valid grasp points, while negative examples include invalid ones. This process enables scalable training for the VLM-driven reasoning module.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments in the Maniskill simulator and demonstrated real-world validation to evaluate the performance of CLASP against several baselines in pick-and-categorize tasks within cluttered scenarios.

**Experimental Setup and Metrics:**
- **Simulation Environment**: Maniskill simulator with a WidowX robot, 500 Hz simulation frequency, 5 Hz control frequency.
- **Objects and Task**: 15 unseen everyday objects (Toys, Blocks, Tools) were used. The task involved picking a specified object from a cluttered scene and placing it into its corresponding category zone.
- **Evaluation Metrics**:
 - **Pick Success Rate**: Proportion of successful grasp-and-lift operations.
 - **Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU)**: Evaluates placement precision and semantic correctness between the placed object and the designated category zone.
- **Baselines**: Qwen3-VL, Doubao-Seed (VLMs outputting end-to-end grasp points); AffordanceNet (segmentation-based affordance masks); AnyGrasp (3D grasp prediction from point clouds). Visual-Language-Action (VLA) models were not fine-tuned for comparison due to their high data dependency, ensuring a fair comparison with pre-trained VLMs.

**Main Findings:**

1. **Overall Pick Task Performance**:
 - CLASP (Ours), after fine-tuning on its custom dataset, achieved an 87.0% overall pick success rate.
 - This performance surpassed all baselines: Qwen3-VL (72.27%), Doubao-Seed (68.67%), AffordanceNet (75.33%), and AnyGrasp (56.53%).
 - Even CLASP (w/o pretrain), demonstrating the framework's inherent design effectiveness, achieved an 80.0% overall success rate, outperforming all baselines.

2. **Component Effectiveness (Ablation Study)**:
 - The full CLASP pipeline (P+R+H+J, including Perception Prior, Reasoner, Heuristic, and Judger) achieved the highest total success rate of 80.0% across various objects.
 - Progressive addition of components showed improvements: R (Reasoner only) yielded 59.3%, R+J (Reasoner + Judger) improved to 61.3%, P+R+J (Perception Prior + Reasoner + Judger) to 64.0%.
 - The Perception Prior (P) module enhanced stability, particularly for geometrically simpler block-like shapes (e.g., cuboid, cylinder).
 - The Heuristic module (H) further boosted overall robustness and cross-category generalization.

3. **Impact of Object Categories and Multiple Attempts**:
 - **Object Difficulty**: Blocks were the easiest category (average 85.5% to 89.0% pick success rate across attempts), followed by Toys (66.0% to 73.0%), with Tools being the most challenging (27.5% to 33.0%). The difficulty for tools was attributed to their complex geometry and strict grasp position requirements.
 - **CLASP's Advantage on Tools**: Despite the overall difficulty, CLASP achieved the highest success rate on Tools (77%) among all methods, demonstrating the framework's effectiveness in utilizing semantic information for challenging geometric categories.
 - **Attempts**: Increasing the number of pick attempts from 1 to 3 provided consistent but limited improvements across all object categories (e.g., +7.0% for Toys, +3.5% for Blocks, +5.5% for Tools).

4. **Simulated System Latency Benchmark**:
 - The proposed Asynchronous Policy significantly improved execution efficiency compared to a Streaming Policy.
 - It achieved a 39.8% reduction in total execution time and a 39.9% reduction in average task completion time.
 - Execution-time variance was reduced by 59.5%, indicating enhanced operational stability.

5. **Categorization and Overall Task Success Rate**:
 - CLASP demonstrated improved categorical placement reliability.
 - It increased classification IoU by 10.0% (Toys), 20.0% (Tools), and 26.67% (Blocks) compared to the Qwen3-VL baseline.
 - The overall task success rate for categorization improved by 20.00% over Qwen3-VL.

6. **Real-World Implementation**:
 - Qualitative validation on a WidowX 250S 6-DoF robotic manipulator confirmed CLASP's effectiveness in physical settings.
 - In a task requiring grasping a wrench, CLASP accurately determined optimal opposing grasp locations on the handle. In contrast, baseline methods exhibited failures, such as selecting incorrect grasp points (Qwen3-VL), failing to produce results (segmentation-based), or identifying unsuitable, non-graspable edge positions (spatial-based). This validates the system's ability to mitigate common pitfalls and operate reliably on real hardware.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The CLASP framework presents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation by directly addressing several critical challenges associated with deploying vision-language models (VLMs) for low-level grasping tasks. Its contributions hold notable significance and potential impact:

1. **Mitigation of VLM Limitations**: By integrating a dual-pathway hierarchical perception module that decouples semantic intent from geometric grounding, CLASP effectively reduces spatial hallucinations that commonly plague VLMs. This is crucial for improving the precision and reliability of grasp point selection, enabling more deterministic control in robotic tasks.

2. **Enhanced Robustness through Closed-Loop Feedback**: The asynchronous closed-loop evaluator, incorporating a Judger module, provides a robust error-correction mechanism. By comparing pre- and post-execution states and generating diagnostic feedback, the system can adapt to dynamic environments and execution failures. This capability moves robotic manipulation beyond fragile open-loop execution, making robots more resilient and autonomous in unstructured settings.

3. **Scalable Data Generation**: The automated multimodal data engine, synthesizing high-quality spatial annotations and reasoning templates without human teleoperation, represents a substantial step towards overcoming the data scarcity bottleneck. This reduces the cost and time associated with dataset creation, facilitating the training of more generalized and capable robotic systems.

4. **Improved Generalization and Performance**: Experimental results demonstrate that CLASP achieves a high overall success rate and exhibits generalization across diverse objects, including geometrically challenging categories like tools. This indicates a pathway towards robots that can handle a wider variety of items in real-world scenarios, a key requirement for intelligent automation.

5. **Increased Efficiency and Stability**: The asynchronous execution policy reduces system latency and operational fluctuation. This improvement in efficiency and temporal stability is vital for real-time robotic applications, where rapid decision-making and smooth operation are necessary.

6. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap**: The framework's demonstrated performance in both simulation and real-world experiments, including qualitative real-world validations, suggests its potential for effective deployment on physical hardware. This is a crucial step for transitioning research findings into practical robotic applications.

**Potential Impact**:
The capabilities offered by CLASP have direct implications for various sectors:
- **Intelligent Manufacturing and Logistics**: Robots equipped with CLASP could more reliably handle diverse components and packages, adapt to production line changes, and manage inventory.
- **Agriculture**: Precision grasping of delicate or irregularly shaped produce could be enhanced, leading to automation in harvesting and sorting.
- **Retail and Service Robotics**: Robots could perform tasks like shelf restocking, item retrieval, and personal assistance with greater accuracy and adaptability in dynamic human environments.
- **Research and Development**: The scalable data engine and robust closed-loop architecture can serve as a foundation for further research into more complex, longer-horizon manipulation tasks and more advanced human-robot interaction.

Future work involves further deploying the framework on physical robots for comprehensive real-world validation and extending its reasoning capabilities to support more intricate, multi-step manipulation tasks, further advancing the state of autonomous robotics.
