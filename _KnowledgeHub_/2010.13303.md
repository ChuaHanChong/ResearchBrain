# Trajectory-wise Multiple Choice Learning for Dynamics Generalization in Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Trajectory-wise Multiple Choice Learning (T-MCL) improves generalization in model-based reinforcement learning by employing a multi-headed dynamics model trained with a trajectory-wise oracle loss. The approach enables robust zero-shot generalization to unseen environment dynamics, achieving up to 3x better performance compared to other methods in environments with varying dynamics like HalfCheetah.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents struggle to generalize to environments with dynamics different from those encountered during training.
- Varying environment dynamics (e.g., damaged robot parts, different terrains) create multi-modal state transition distributions.
- Standard single-headed dynamics models in model-based RL average over these multi-modal distributions, leading to inaccurate predictions and poor generalization.

## Method

- Introduces Trajectory-wise Multiple Choice Learning (T-MCL), which uses a multi-headed dynamics model where each head specializes in different environment dynamics.
- Employs a novel trajectory-wise oracle loss function that updates the most accurate prediction head over entire trajectory segments, rather than individual transitions.
- Implements a context-conditional prediction module with adaptive planning to extract dynamics-specific information from past experiences and select the best head for online adaptation to unseen environments.

## Results

- T-MCL consistently outperforms existing model-based RL approaches and state-of-the-art model-free meta-RL methods in generalization to unseen environments.
- Achieved almost 3x better performance than the next best method in the HalfCheetah environment and showed robust generalization across various MuJoCo tasks including CrippledAnt and SlimHumanoid.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the multi-headed architecture, trajectory-wise loss, adaptive planning, and context learning each contribute significantly to performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Trajectory-wise Multiple Choice Learning naturally encourages prediction heads to specialize in different subsets of environment dynamics in an unsupervised manner.
> - The adaptive planning mechanism, which selects the most accurate prediction head online, is critical for achieving zero-shot generalization to unseen environments.
> - A context encoder helps the model learn meaningful representations of environment dynamics, further enhancing generalization capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated impressive capabilities in solving complex decision-making problems across various domains. However, a major challenge in applying RL to real-world scenarios is ensuring that agents can generalize to environments with dynamics different from those encountered during training. This challenge is particularly significant for model-based RL approaches, which rely on accurate dynamics models to plan and make decisions.

Consider a robot designed to navigate different terrains or function with varied physical configurations (Figure 1). Traditional RL approaches often struggle when encountering unseen dynamics—like a robot with a damaged leg or operating on a new surface. The transition dynamics in such scenarios follow multi-modal distributions, where each mode corresponds to a different environment configuration. Standard single-headed dynamics models fail to capture this multi-modality, resulting in poor generalization.

This paper introduces Trajectory-wise Multiple Choice Learning (T-MCL), a novel approach that significantly enhances the generalization capabilities of model-based RL agents across environments with varying dynamics. T-MCL addresses the multi-modality challenge by learning a multi-headed dynamics model where each head specializes in different environment dynamics.

### 2. Background and Problem Statement

#### 2.1. Model-Based Reinforcement Learning

In model-based RL, agents learn a dynamics model that predicts state transitions based on current states and actions. This model is then used for planning and decision-making. Formally, a dynamics model $f_θ$ approximates the transition function:

$$s_{t+1} = f_θ(s_t, a_t)$$

where $s_t$ is the state at time $t$, $a_t$ is the action, and $s_{t+1}$ is the predicted next state.

#### 2.2. The Dynamics Generalization Problem

When environment dynamics vary (e.g., different masses, friction coefficients, or robot configurations), a standard dynamics model struggles to make accurate predictions. This occurs because:

1. Single-headed models average over different dynamics, leading to poor predictions for any specific environment.
2. Environments with varying dynamics create a multi-modal distribution of possible state transitions.

For example, an ant robot with different crippled legs (Figure 1a) will exhibit distinct movement patterns for each configuration (Figure 1b). Traditional approaches fail to capture these multi-modal behaviors.

### 3. Key Components of T-MCL

T-MCL incorporates three key innovations to address the dynamics generalization problem:

1. **A Multi-Headed Dynamics Model**: Instead of a single prediction head, T-MCL employs multiple heads, each specializing in different environment dynamics.

2. **Trajectory-wise Oracle Loss**: A novel loss function that updates the most accurate prediction head over entire trajectory segments rather than individual transitions.

3. **Context-Conditional Prediction with Adaptive Planning**: A context encoder extracts dynamics-specific information from past experiences, enabling online adaptation to unseen environments.

### 4. Methodology

#### 4.1. Multi-Headed Dynamics Model

T-MCL's model architecture consists of:

1. A backbone network that processes state-action pairs
2. Multiple prediction heads that output different next-state predictions
3. A context encoder that processes past trajectory segments

The multi-headed dynamics model is formalized as:

$$s_{t+1} = f_θ^h(s_t, a_t, c_t)$$

where $h \in \{1, 2..., H\}$ represents the prediction head index, and $c_t$ is the context vector derived from past transitions:

$$c_t = g_ϕ(\{(s_{t-K}, a_{t-K}, s_{t-K+1})..., (s_{t-1}, a_{t-1}, s_t)\})$$

#### 4.2. Trajectory-wise Multiple Choice Learning

Unlike standard Multiple Choice Learning (MCL) that selects the best head for each individual transition, T-MCL applies the selection over entire trajectory segments. This approach encourages heads to specialize in clusters of similar environments.

The training process involves:

1. Sampling a batch of trajectory segments $\{\tau_i\}$ from the replay buffer
2. For each segment, computing prediction errors for all heads
3. Selecting the head with the lowest average error over the entire segment
4. Updating only the selected head's parameters

The loss function for a trajectory segment $\tau$ is defined as:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{T-MCL}}(\theta, \phi) = \min_{h \in \{1...,H\}} \frac{1}{M} \sum_{i=1}^{M} \|s_{t+i} - f_{\theta}^h(s_{t+i-1}, a_{t+i-1}, c_{t+i-1})\|_2^2$$

where $M$ is the trajectory segment length.

#### 4.3. Adaptive Planning

During deployment, T-MCL employs an adaptive planning mechanism that:

1. Evaluates each head's prediction accuracy over recent experiences
2. Selects the most accurate head for planning future actions
3. Periodically updates this selection to adapt to changing dynamics

This adaptive approach enables zero-shot generalization to unseen environments without requiring explicit dynamics identification or online model adaptation.

### 5. Experimental Results

T-MCL was evaluated on a variety of control tasks from OpenAI Gym and MuJoCo, including:

- CartPoleSwingUp with varying pole masses
- Pendulum with varying pendulum lengths
- Hopper with varying body masses
- SlimHumanoid with varying torso masses
- HalfCheetah with varying body masses
- CrippledAnt with different disabled legs

The results demonstrate that T-MCL significantly outperforms existing model-based RL approaches and state-of-the-art model-free meta-RL methods in terms of generalization to unseen environments. For instance, in the HalfCheetah environment (Figure 3e), T-MCL achieves almost 3x better performance than the next best method.

### 6. Ablation Studies

#### 6.1. Effect of Multi-Headed Architecture

The authors compared T-MCL to a standard PETS model and a multi-headed variant without the trajectory-wise loss or context conditioning. Results show that the multi-headed architecture by itself improves generalization, but its benefits are significantly amplified when combined with trajectory-wise learning and context information.

#### 6.2. Impact of Adaptive Planning

Experiments comparing T-MCL with and without adaptive planning reveal that selecting the most appropriate prediction head during deployment is critical for performance. As shown in Figure 4b, T-MCL with adaptive planning achieves significantly higher returns compared to using a fixed planning strategy.

#### 6.3. Role of Context Learning

The context encoder plays a vital role in T-MCL's performance. Ablation studies (Figure 4c) demonstrate that removing the context encoder substantially reduces performance, particularly in more complex environments like HalfCheetah.

Further analysis using t-SNE visualization (Figure 4d) shows that the context encoder learns meaningful representations that effectively cluster environments with similar dynamics.

#### 6.4. Specialization of Prediction Heads

One of the key findings is that prediction heads in T-MCL naturally specialize in different subsets of training environments with similar dynamics. This specialization occurs in an unsupervised manner, allowing the model to effectively cluster environments.

### 7. Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

T-MCL was compared against several state-of-the-art approaches:

1. **PETS**: A standard model-based RL algorithm
2. **CaDM**: Context-aware dynamics model
3. **GrBAL**: Gradient-based adaptive learner
4. **ReBAL**: Recurrence-based adaptive learner
5. **PEARL**: Probabilistic embedding for actor-critic RL

Across all environments, T-MCL consistently outperforms these methods, particularly in complex scenarios like SlimHumanoid, HalfCheetah, and CrippledAnt:

The performance gap is most pronounced in the HalfCheetah and CrippledAnt environments, where the dynamics variations create clear multi-modal transition patterns that T-MCL effectively captures.

### 8. Conclusion and Implications

T-MCL represents a significant advancement in model-based reinforcement learning by addressing the critical challenge of dynamics generalization. By combining multi-headed dynamics models with trajectory-wise multiple choice learning and context-conditional prediction, T-MCL enables robust zero-shot generalization to unseen environment dynamics.

The key contributions and implications of this work include:

1. **Improved Robustness**: T-MCL enhances the robustness of RL agents in scenarios with varying dynamics, a critical requirement for real-world applications.

2. **Zero-Shot Generalization**: Unlike meta-learning approaches that require adaptation steps, T-MCL generalizes immediately to new environments without fine-tuning.

3. **Sample Efficiency**: As a model-based approach, T-MCL maintains good sample efficiency while achieving superior generalization.

4. **Interpretable Specialization**: The specialization of prediction heads provides interpretable insights into how different environment dynamics are clustered.

The trajectory-wise approach to multiple choice learning introduced in this paper could potentially be extended to other domains where multi-modality poses challenges, such as imitation learning, transfer learning, and continual learning.

Future research directions might include extending T-MCL to handle high-dimensional observation spaces like images, combining it with model-free approaches for improved exploration, and applying it to more complex real-world robotics tasks where dynamics variations are prevalent.
