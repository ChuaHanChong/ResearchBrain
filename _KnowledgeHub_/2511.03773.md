# Scaling Agent Learning via Experience Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamGym introduces a framework for scalable online reinforcement learning that enables autonomous LLM agents to train efficiently by synthesizing diverse, causally grounded interaction experiences. This approach leverages a reasoning-based experience model, replay buffer, and curriculum task generator, yielding improved performance and reduced real-world data requirements across interactive environments.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning for LLM agents faces high costs and low sample efficiency when interacting with real-world environments.
- Existing environments offer limited task diversity, requiring costly human expertise for scalable task generation and validation.
- Dynamic interactive settings provide unreliable, sparse, and inconsistent reward signals, hindering stable agent learning.

## Method

- A reasoning-based ==experience model== (Mexp) distills environment dynamics into abstract textual states, predicting transitions and rewards using Chain-of-Thought reasoning, interaction history, and retrieved past experiences.
- An experience replay buffer is continuously enriched with synthetic trajectories generated by Mexp, providing stable context and reducing hallucinations during training.
- A curriculum-based task generator, co-trained with Mexp, adaptively creates new, progressively challenging tasks using a reward entropy heuristic to maximize learning information.

## Results

- Achieved over **30%** improvement in success rates on non-RL-ready environments (e.g., WebArena) compared to baselines.
- Agents trained purely on synthetic rollouts performed on par with traditional RL baselines requiring 80,000 real-world interactions in environments like WebShop and ALFWorld.
- The Sim-to-Real approach (DreamGym-S2R) demonstrated over **40%** performance improvement while needing less than **10%** of external real-world data for fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synthesizing learning-centric interaction data in an abstract, reasoning-driven textual space effectively addresses the data bottleneck for LLM agent RL.
> - Explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning in the experience model enhances causal consistency and factual grounding of generated synthetic experiences.
> - Adaptive curriculum generation, guided by task difficulty and information gain, is crucial for continuous agent improvement and preventing learning plateaus.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper emerges at a critical juncture in the development of artificial intelligence, characterized by the widespread adoption of large language models (LLMs) as foundational components for autonomous agents. These LLM-based agents show immense promise across diverse interactive tasks, including web navigation, embodied control, and multi-turn tool use, leveraging their extensive pre-trained semantic knowledge for reasoning and planning.

However, the paper identifies a significant bottleneck: while LLMs excel in static reasoning, their performance in *interactive settings* remains suboptimal. This is where reinforcement learning (RL) offers a powerful paradigm, enabling agents to improve through self-improvement via environmental interaction. Despite RL's theoretical appeal, its practical application to LLM agents is severely hampered by several challenges, which DreamGym explicitly aims to address:

1. **High Cost and Low Sample Efficiency:** Real-world interactions are expensive, time-consuming, and computationally intensive, yielding sparse rewards and long interaction sequences. This makes collecting sufficient diverse data for modern RL algorithms prohibitively costly.
2. **Limited Task Diversity and Scalability:** Most existing environments offer only a static, limited set of tasks. Scaling task instructions and validating their feasibility typically demands costly human expertise, hindering effective exploration for goal-conditioned RL.
3. **Unreliable Reward Signals:** Dynamic environments like web pages or GUIs often provide noisy, sparse, or inconsistent feedback, which destabilizes learning. Safety concerns and lack of reliable reset mechanisms further complicate real-world exploration.
4. **Infrastructure Complexity:** Developing RL-ready environments is challenging, often relying on heterogeneous, heavyweight backends (ee.g., Docker, virtual machines), which makes large-batch rollout sampling engineering-intensive and costly.

Existing approaches in the broader landscape have attempted to tackle these issues. Traditional RL algorithms, while powerful in games or robotics, struggle with the specific characteristics of LLM agent environments (long horizons, sparse rewards). Offline imitation learning methods (e.g., SFT, DPO) rely on static expert demonstrations, which lack the diversity and adaptivity required for robust RL. More advanced techniques using synthetic data or "world models" (e.g., AlphaGo, Dreamer, WebDreamer, UI-Simulator) aim to generate experiences, but often suffer from limitations: they might be domain-specific, require extensive expert engineering, or are primarily designed for supervised fine-tuning rather than full online RL.

DreamGym differentiates itself by proposing a *unified, scalable RL framework* that synthesizes diverse, informative, and causally grounded experiences online. It reframes the problem by recognizing that perfect realism is not necessary; rather, the goal is to provide *learning-centric interaction data*. By focusing on abstract textual states and integrating adaptive task generation, DreamGym directly tackles the aforementioned practical barriers, bridging the gap between RL's potential and its practical realization for general-purpose LLM agents in complex, open-ended domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enable effective and scalable online reinforcement learning training for autonomous LLM agents**, addressing the significant practical impediments that currently hinder the widespread adoption of RL in this domain.

The core motivations stem from the recognition of RL's transformative potential for LLM agents, allowing them to evolve from static generators into adaptive decision-makers. However, realizing this potential requires overcoming several critical challenges:

- **Cost Reduction:** The prohibitive cost and low sample efficiency of gathering large-scale, diverse, and informative online interaction data from real environments is a major barrier. Traditional RL rollouts are expensive due to long interaction sequences, high computational costs per step, and sparse rewards. DreamGym aims to drastically reduce this cost by generating synthetic experiences.
- **Enhancing Task Diversity:** Current environments typically offer a limited and static set of tasks, insufficient for the broad exploration necessary for effective RL training. Creating diverse, scalable tasks traditionally requires extensive human expertise and validation, which is unscalable. DreamGym seeks to automate and adaptively generate challenging task variations.
- **Stabilizing Reward Signals:** Many interactive settings, such as web interfaces, are highly dynamic, leading to noisy, sparse, or inconsistent reward feedback. This instability hinders robust learning. A key motivation is to provide consistent and informative feedback signals within the synthetic environment.
- **Simplifying Infrastructure:** The heterogeneity and heavy computational demands of existing RL-ready environments (often relying on virtual machines or Docker) complicate large-batch rollout sampling. DreamGym aims to provide a unified and lightweight infrastructure for experience generation.
- **Facilitating Generalization and Transferability:** Beyond merely enabling RL, the project aims to develop agents that can acquire broad, domain-agnostic knowledge in synthetic settings and effectively transfer this knowledge to real-world environments, thereby minimizing reliance on costly real-world interactions. This provides a scalable warm-start strategy for general-purpose RL.

In essence, the motivation is to unlock the "era of experience" for LLM agents by developing a framework that provides an abundant supply of high-quality, relevant, and structured interaction data, which is currently the most fundamental barrier to building more robust and adaptive AI agents via RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamGym is presented as a unified and scalable RL framework built around three synergistic components: a reasoning-based experience model, an experience replay buffer, and a curriculum task generator.

#### 3.1. Reasoning-based Experience Model (Mexp)

At the core of DreamGym is `Mexp`, a Large Language Model (LLM)-based experience model that distills environment dynamics into an abstract, meta-representational textual space. This design choice is critical; instead of simulating raw sensory data (e.g., pixels, HTML), `Mexp` operates on concise textual representations, making it highly sample-efficient to train and effective for agent learning by focusing on salient information.

- **Inference for Rollout Collection:** During agent training, `Mexp` interacts with the agent turn-by-turn. Given the current state and the agent's action, `Mexp` predicts the next state and reward signal. This prediction is enriched by three crucial contexts:
 1. **Interaction History:** The past trajectory `{(s_i, a_i)}` is included in the context window to maintain state consistency across multiple turns.
 2. **Task Instruction:** The current task `τ` conditions `Mexp` to interpret actions relative to the task objective, leading to more accurate state transitions and rewards.
 3. **Past Experiences:** Top-k semantically similar demonstrations `d_j` retrieved from the experience replay buffer guide `Mexp` to reduce hallucinations and improve factual grounding in knowledge-intensive predictions.
 `Mexp` utilizes Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning to derive consistent state transitions and feedback, explicitly explaining the causal effects of the agent's actions. The reward scheme is outcome-based, typically `r=1` for successful task completion and `r=0` otherwise.

- **Training Experience Models to Reason:** `Mexp` is trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on existing offline trajectory datasets (e.g., public benchmarks like WebArena Leaderboard demonstrations). The key is that each transition in the dataset is augmented with an explicit reasoning trace (`R*_t`) generated by a strong teacher LLM. The SFT objective minimizes a joint loss over both reasoning generation and next-state prediction: `L_SFT = E[ -log P(R*_t | ...) - log P(s_t+1 | R*_t...) ]`. This ensures `Mexp` learns to generate faithful causal explanations and leverage them for consistent state prediction, enabling generalization to novel rollouts.

#### 3.2. Experience Replay Buffer

The replay buffer serves as a dynamic memory for `Mexp`.
- **Initialization and Enrichment:** It is initially seeded with offline real-world data to provide essential context. Crucially, it is continuously enriched with synthetic trajectories generated on-the-fly by `Mexp` and the agent.
- **Co-evolution and Support:** This continuous enrichment allows the replay buffer to co-evolve with the agent's updated policy, ensuring that retrieved experiences remain aligned with the agent's current capabilities, thereby supporting stable training and reducing hallucinations in `Mexp`'s predictions.

#### 3.3. Curriculum-based Task Generation

`Mexp` is also repurposed as a curriculum task generator.
- **Adaptive Task Generation:** It actively generates new tasks as variations of a set of `m` seed tasks, sharing parameters with `Mexp`.
- **High-Value Task Selection:** New tasks are selected based on a group-based **reward entropy** heuristic. Tasks where the agent observes a balanced mix of successes and failures (high variance in outcomes) are deemed most challenging yet feasible, providing maximum information gain for credit assignment. This aligns with findings that LLMs learn best from tasks of intermediate difficulty.
- **Progressive Difficulty:** By feeding these high-entropy tasks back to `Mexp`, DreamGym generates progressively more challenging variations, ensuring the agent is continually pushed to improve its capabilities and explore new scenarios effectively. A hyperparameter `λ` bounds the proportion of synthetic tasks per iteration to balance exploration with coverage of the original task distribution.

#### 3.4. Learning from Synthetic Experiences

- **Policy Training in Synthetic Environments:** The agent interacts with `Mexp` to generate multi-turn rollouts. These synthetic rollouts, complete with consistent states and reward signals, are then used with standard RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO) to update the agent's policy. This cycle of interaction, training, and curriculum expansion continues until convergence.
- **Sim-to-Real (S2R) Policy Transfer:** DreamGym supports a hybrid scenario, `DreamGym-S2R`. Agents are first trained purely on synthetic experiences in DreamGym to acquire broad knowledge efficiently. This synthetically pre-trained policy then serves as a strong initialization for further RL fine-tuning in real environments, requiring significantly fewer real-world interactions. State space consistency between synthetic and real environments is ensured through rule-based mapping or lightweight fine-tuned models. A theoretical analysis is provided to demonstrate policy improvement guarantees in real environments when trained with synthetic experiences under trust-region assumptions.

This comprehensive approach unifies interaction, memory, and adaptive task generation, tackling the core challenges of cost, task scarcity, reward instability, and infrastructure demands in LLM agent RL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate DreamGym's significant advantages across diverse environments and LLM agent backbones, validating its effectiveness and generalizability.

- **Performance in Non-RL-Ready Environments (WebArena):**
 - DreamGym provides the **only viable approach for RL-based agent training** in environments like WebArena, which inherently lack scalable RL infrastructure. Agents trained purely within DreamGym achieved over **30% improvement** in success rates across all tested backbones (Llama-3.2-3B, Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen-2.5-7B) compared to traditional baselines. This highlights DreamGym's ability to make RL feasible in domains previously considered intractable due to task and engineering constraints.

- **Performance in RL-Ready Environments (WebShop, ALFWorld):**
 - In environments where RL is supported but costly, such as WebShop and ALFWorld, DreamGym-trained agents (using only synthetic rollouts) achieved performance **on par with traditional GRPO and PPO baselines** that required 80,000 real-world interactions. This confirms that DreamGym produces synthetic transitions and rewards that are sufficiently coherent, meaningful, and effective for stable policy improvement.

- **Sim-to-Real (DreamGym-S2R) Efficiency:**
 - The `DreamGym-S2R` approach, which involves pre-training agents in DreamGym and then fine-tuning with a small amount of real-world data, consistently **outperformed both GRPO and PPO baselines trained from scratch** in real environments. `DreamGym-S2R` yielded over **40% performance improvement** while requiring **less than 10% of the external real-world data**. This validates its role as an efficient warm-start strategy for general-purpose RL.

- **Sample Efficiency and Training Cost Reduction:**
 - DreamGym achieved substantial performance gains on WebArena while reducing total training effort (including rollout sampling and GPU hours) to roughly **one-third or even one-fifth** of traditional RL baselines. This efficiency stems from the dense feedback provided by curriculum-based rollout synthesis and the lightweight, abstract state transitions generated by `Mexp`.

- **Generalization and Cross-Domain Transferability:**
 - Policies trained in DreamGym demonstrated strong generalization. Agents trained on WebShop successfully transferred to WebArena (and vice versa), **surpassing SFT models** directly trained on the target domain. This suggests that DreamGym enables agents to learn domain-agnostic behavioral priors within an abstract meta-representation space. However, transferability showed limits when the domain gap was too large (e.g., web-based to embodied ALFWorld), indicating the scope for further improvement in meta-representations.

- **Training Curve Analysis:**
 - Training curves showed that DreamGym leads to **more rapid performance improvement** and **reduced variance** in learning dynamics. Synthesized trajectories provided more informative gradients and consistent feedback compared to sparse, unstable real-world rewards, mitigating common training instabilities.

- **Ablation Studies on DreamGym Components:**
 - **Curriculum Task Generator:** Removing this component led to significant performance drops (6.6% on WebShop, 6.0% on WebArena) and quicker plateaus in learning, confirming its vital role in providing progressively challenging tasks and preventing replay buffer saturation.
 - **Reasoning Experience Model (Mexp) Components:**
 - Removing access to **interaction history** drastically reduced *causal state consistency*.
 - Removing **explicit reasoning** primarily hurt *informativeness* and increased *hallucinations*, leading to a substantial overall performance drop (Table 2).
 - The full DreamGym `Mexp` achieved the best performance across consistency, diversity, informativeness, and low hallucination, underscoring the complementary benefits of history for temporal coherence and reasoning for factual reliability and depth.
 - **Experience Model Backbones and Offline Data:** `Mexp` was found to be highly **data-efficient**, achieving competitive performance with as few as 2,000-10,000 offline samples. While larger backbones generally performed better, smaller models remained viable and improved with more data. Pretrained world models like WebDreamer offered an early advantage in low-data regimes, but larger, general-purpose LLMs could eventually bridge this gap with sufficient training data.

A case study (Fig. 6) visually illustrated how `Mexp` generates synthetic tasks and progressively predicts states with explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning, demonstrating its ability to ground actions and reflect consequences accurately.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DreamGym represents a significant advancement in addressing the practical challenges of applying reinforcement learning to complex, open-ended tasks for large language model-based agents. Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

- **Democratization of RL for LLM Agents:** By mitigating the prohibitive costs, data scarcity, and infrastructure complexities, DreamGym makes RL training feasible in domains that were previously inaccessible or intractable (e.g., WebArena). This could broaden the applicability of RL to a vast array of real-world agentic problems, including web automation, digital assistants, and complex tool-use scenarios, without requiring specialized, costly, and proprietary RL infrastructure for each environment.
- **Enhanced Scalability and Efficiency:** The framework's ability to synthesize diverse, high-quality experiences on demand and its substantial reduction in training effort (1/3 to 1/5 of traditional methods) signify a major leap in scalability. This allows for training more capable agents faster and with fewer resources, accelerating research and development in agentic AI.
- **Improved Sample Efficiency for Real-World RL:** The `DreamGym-S2R` approach offers a powerful paradigm for warm-starting real-world RL. By leveraging extensive synthetic pre-training, agents can achieve superior performance with significantly less real-world interaction data (less than 10% in experiments). This is crucial for applications where real-world data collection is inherently dangerous, slow, or expensive.
- **Fostering Generalization and Robustness:** Learning within an abstract, reasoning-driven meta-representation space enables agents to acquire domain-agnostic behavioral priors rather than memorizing task-specific patterns. This promotes better generalization across varied tasks and environments, leading to more robust and adaptable agents. The curriculum-based task generation further ensures agents are continuously challenged, improving their ability to handle novel situations.
- **Paradigm Shift in Environment Modeling:** DreamGym advocates for a shift from attempting to perfectly *simulate* external systems to *synthesizing learning-centric experiences*. By focusing on causal consistency, informative feedback, and abstract state representations rather than raw pixel fidelity, it provides a more pragmatic and effective approach for driving agent learning.
- **Foundational Agent Research:** The paper's conclusion points towards an exciting future: extending DreamGym to build universal world models that unify multiple environment models. Such an extension could enable knowledge transfer across environments, paving the way for "foundational agentic models" capable of zero-shot adaptation to new environments, a grand challenge in AI. This could lead to truly general-purpose AI agents that learn continuously and adaptively across an open-ended set of tasks and domains.

In conclusion, DreamGym's contribution is not just an incremental improvement but a fundamental rethinking of how RL can be effectively scaled for LLM agents. By providing a practical and theoretically grounded solution to the interaction data bottleneck, it opens new avenues for building more intelligent, autonomous, and adaptable AI systems.
