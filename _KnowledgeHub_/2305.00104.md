# MMViT: Multiscale Multiview Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> MMViT introduces a novel Transformer architecture that merges multiscale feature hierarchies with parallel multiview encoding and cross-attention for robust representation learning. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on AudioSet (43.0% mAP) and ImageNet1K (83.2% Top-1 accuracy) while also featuring an effective audio-specific CutMix augmentation.

## Problem

- Existing Transformer architectures like Multiscale Vision Transformers (MViT) and Multiview Transformers (MTV) typically focus on either hierarchical scaling or parallel view processing, but not a unified combination.
- The need for a versatile and general-purpose backbone model that can achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse modalities, such as audio and image classification.
- Transformer-based audio models are often susceptible to overfitting, particularly when fine-tuning from models pre-trained on large image datasets.

## Method

- MMViT introduces a novel Transformer architecture that seamlessly integrates ==multiscale feature hierarchies with parallel multiview encoding==, processing distinct views of the input at varying resolutions.
- The model employs strategically placed ==cross-attention blocks== within its multiscale stages to fuse information between different views, enhancing global contextual understanding.
- It utilizes an =='audio-specific CutMix'== data augmentation technique that performs cuts only along the temporal axis to improve regularization and prevent overfitting in audio classification tasks.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on AudioSet with **3**2.2%** mAP** on AudioSet Balanced and **4**3.0%** mAP** on AudioSet Full, outperforming AST (**37.2%** mAP) and MViTv2 (**42.4%** mAP).
- Secured state-of-the-art results on ImageNet1K image classification with a Top-1 accuracy of **83.2%**, surpassing the MViTv2 baseline (**82.7%**).
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the audio-specific CutMix improved AudioSet mAP from **39%** to **43%**, and that using two views was optimal, as three views led to a performance decrease to **82.3%** on ImageNet1K.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining multiscale feature hierarchies with parallel multiview encoding and cross-attention provides a more comprehensive and robust approach to representation learning than either strategy alone.
> - Overlapping patchification and a specialized 'audio-specific CutMix' augmentation are critical for preventing overfitting and enhancing performance, especially when transferring knowledge from image pre-training to audio tasks.
> - The synergistic integration of these architectural elements enables a versatile Transformer backbone capable of achieving state-of-the-art results across distinct modalities like image and audio classification.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of MMViT is situated within the rapidly evolving landscape of deep learning, particularly the shift from convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to transformer-based architectures for representation learning across diverse modalities.

#### 1.1. Rise of Transformers in Representation Learning

Historically, CNNs dominated tasks like image and audio classification due to their effectiveness in capturing local patterns and hierarchical features. However, the introduction of the Transformer architecture in "Attention Is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., 2017) marked a paradigm shift. Initially successful in Natural Language Processing (NLP) for tasks requiring the modeling of long-range dependencies, Transformers, with their self-attention mechanisms, proved exceptionally capable of capturing global contextual information, which CNNs often struggled with inherently. Models like BERT (Devlin et al., 2018) rapidly showcased the power of Transformers in language understanding.

#### 1.2. Vision Transformers and Their Evolution

This success quickly extended to computer vision. The **Vision Transformer (ViT)** (Dosovitskiy et al., 2020) demonstrated that by treating image patches as sequences of "words," Transformers could achieve state-of-the-art performance on image classification tasks, especially with large-scale pre-training data. Following ViT, several advancements emerged to address its limitations and enhance its capabilities:
- **DeiT** (Touvron et al., 2021) focused on data-efficient training strategies for ViT.
- **Swin Transformer** (Liu et al., 2021) introduced a hierarchical architecture with shifted windows to improve efficiency and adapt to varying resolutions, making Transformers more suitable for dense prediction tasks.
- **Multiscale Vision Transformers (MViT)** (Fan et al., 2021) represented a significant step by building multiscale feature hierarchies within Transformer models. MViT progressively changes the spatio-temporal resolution of features, expanding feature complexity while reducing spatial resolution. This approach improved performance and reduced inference costs, especially for video and image recognition. MViT-v2 (Li et al., 2022) further optimized this architecture.
- **Multiview Transformers for Video Recognition (MTV)** (Yan et al., 2022) explored another direction. Instead of a single pyramid-like scaling, MTV models employ separate encoders to process different "views" of the input (e.g., video clips tokenized with varying tubelet sizes) and fuse information across these views using lateral connections. This allowed for capturing diverse spatio-temporal relationships simultaneously.

#### 1.3. Transformers in Audio

The principles of ViT were also adapted for audio processing. The **Audio Spectrogram Transformer (AST)** (Gong et al., 2021) pioneered applying ViT directly to audio by treating log Mel filterbank spectrograms as 2D images. AST demonstrated the effectiveness of cross-modality transfer learning, where models pre-trained on large image datasets (like ImageNet) could be fine-tuned for audio tasks with impressive results. Subsequent works like Patchout (Koutini et al., 2021) and HTS-AT (Chen et al., 2022) further optimized and regularized audio Transformers.

MMViT aims to synthesize the strengths of these two prominent lines of Transformer research: the hierarchical multiscale processing of MViT and the parallel multiview encoding with cross-view fusion seen in MTV. The authors identify a gap where MViT primarily applies self-attention on a single input stream, and they seek to integrate explicit cross-view information merging within a multiscale hierarchy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of MMViT are multi-fold:

1. **Combine Strengths of Multiscale and Multiview Architectures:** The core motivation is to create a novel Transformer-based model that synergistically integrates the benefits of Multiscale Vision Transformers (MViT) and Multiview Transformers (MTV). MViT excels at building hierarchical feature pyramids with varying resolutions, while MTV demonstrates the power of encoding multiple input perspectives. MMViT aims to achieve a more comprehensive representation by processing multiple views in parallel within a multiscale hierarchy.

2. **Enhance Representation Learning Capability:** By introducing multiscale feature maps and multiview encodings, coupled with cross-attention for information fusion, the authors hypothesize that MMViT can acquire more complex, high-dimensional, and robust representations of input signals. This enhanced capability is crucial for tackling challenging classification tasks across different modalities.

3. **Develop a Versatile Backbone Model:** A significant objective is to create a flexible architecture that can serve as a universal backbone for various domains and downstream applications. The paper specifically demonstrates its applicability to audio and image classification, but the design is intended to extend to other modalities like video and text.

4. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** The authors are driven to surpass existing state-of-the-art models in both audio and image classification tasks. This empirical validation is essential to prove the efficacy and superiority of the proposed MMViT architecture.

5. **Address Overfitting in Audio Transformers:** Recognizing the susceptibility of Transformer-based audio models to overfitting, particularly when fine-tuning from image pre-trained models, the authors aimed to develop or adapt regularization techniques. The introduction of an "audio-specific CutMix" method underscores this motivation.

In essence, the motivation stems from the belief that a model simultaneously processing diverse views at multiple resolutions, with explicit mechanisms for cross-view information exchange, can learn richer and more generalizable representations than models focusing solely on one aspect.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MMViT model introduces a novel Transformer architecture that combines multiscale feature processing with multiview encoding and cross-attention.

#### 3.1. Input Representation and Multi-View Patchification

For both image and audio classification, the input is treated as a 2D image (C × H × W). For audio, this involves converting waveforms into log Mel filterbank features (spectrograms), where C=1. For images, C=3.

A key innovation is the generation of *distinct views* of the input at varying resolutions. This is achieved by initially "patchifying" the input images into two views using 2D convolutional operations with different kernel sizes and strides. Specifically:
- **View 1:** Kernel size and stride.
- **View 2:** Kernel size and stride.
This design ensures that View 2 has one-fourth the number of patches compared to View 1. Crucially, unlike traditional ViT, the kernel size is larger than the stride, introducing **overlap** in the patches. This overlapping strategy allows each patch to encompass more contextual information from surrounding pixels, which is vital for robust representation learning.

#### 3.2. Spatio-Temporal Positional Encoding

Following patchification, a learnable spatio-temporal positional encoding is applied to both generated views. This encoding is computed separately for spatial patches and temporal sequences, enabling the model to account for the position of patches and overcome the inherent permutation invariance of Transformers. A [CLS] token, standard in Transformer architectures for classification, is appended *only* to the beginning of the first view. This token's embedding at the final layer is used as the input to the classification head.

#### 3.3. MMViT Scale Stages and Block Structure

The MMViT architecture is structured into **four scale stages**, comprising a total of 16 Transformer blocks. Each stage processes the two views in parallel. The spatial-temporal dimensions and channel sizes are modified only within specific "scaled self-attention blocks," leading to a hierarchical scaling system where channel size increases, and spatial resolution reduces as the network deepens.

The 16 blocks are arranged as follows:
- Cross-attention blocks at layers 1, 3, 14.
- Scaled self-attention blocks at layers 2, 4, 15.
- Regular self-attention blocks at layers 5-13 and 16.

#### 3.3.1. Self-Attention Block

Within each scale stage, multiple self-attention blocks are present. These blocks operate independently on each view and are equivalent to the Multi-Head Pooling Attention (MHPA) mechanism found in MViT. Each input view X_i generates its Query (Q_i), Key (K_i), and Value (V_i) pairs through separate linear layers. A pooling operator (implemented via convolution) is then applied to Q_i, K_i, and V_i before the standard scaled dot-product attention computation.
The "scaled self-attention" blocks, specifically located at the end of each scale stage (except the last), downsample the spatio-temporal dimension of the feature by a factor of four (using a pooling operation with stride) and double the channel size.

#### 3.3.2. Cross-Attention Block

The novel **cross-attention blocks** are strategically placed *before* the scaled self-attention blocks within each stage (to leverage rich, un-downscaled information). Their purpose is to fuse information from the different views. In these blocks, after individual Q_i, K_i, V_i are generated for each view via pooling attention, all Q_i, K_i, V_i are **concatenated** across spatial and temporal dimensions to form global Q, K, V. This concatenated representation then undergoes the standard cross-attention mechanism. After the attention mechanism, the output feature is separated back into individual view representations for subsequent blocks. This mechanism allows the model to extract global context and merge information *between* views.

#### 3.4. Training Details and Data Augmentation

- **Audio Experiments:** Log Mel fbank features (128-dim, 25ms window, 10ms shift) are used. AdamW optimizer (1e-4 weight decay, 1e-5 LR) is employed. Training lasts for 150 epochs (balanced AudioSet) or 100 epochs (full AudioSet), with Mean Average Precision (mAP) as the metric.
- **Image Experiments:** AdamW optimizer (1e-2 weight decay, 5e-5 LR) is used. Training for 400 epochs on ImageNet 1K, evaluated by Top1 accuracy.
- **Data Augmentation:** Critical for preventing overfitting, especially for audio.
 - **Audio:** Mixup, SpecAugment (time and frequency masking), random rolling. A novel **audio-specific CutMix** is introduced, which performs cuts *only along the temporal axis*. The authors argue that cutting along the frequency axis, unlike in images, removes quantitative information that is not invariant. Half of the audio clips are augmented with Mixup, the other half with audio CutMix.
 - **Image:** Standard augmentations including Mixup, Cutmix, Random Erasing, and Randaugment.

#### 3.5. Cross-Modality Pre-training

Following established practices (e.g., AST), MMViT leverages **ImageNet1K pre-training** for audio tasks. The weights from the 3-channel image model are averaged to 1 channel for audio patchification. Learnable positional embeddings and other MMViT weights are interpolated. The [CLS] token's positional embedding is directly reused, and the image classification head is re-initialized for audio. This strategy significantly boosts audio performance by transferring visual knowledge.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The MMViT model demonstrates strong performance across both audio and image classification tasks, achieving state-of-the-art results compared to competitive baselines.

#### 4.1. Audio Classification Performance

On the AudioSet dataset, MMViT consistently outperforms both the Audio Spectrogram Transformer (AST) and the repurposed Multiscale Vision Transformers v2 (MViTv2) baselines.
- **AudioSet Balanced:** MMViT achieved an mAP of **32.2%**, surpassing AST (31.8%) and MViTv2 (32.0%).
- **AudioSet Full:** MMViT achieved an mAP of **43.0%**, significantly outperforming AST (37.2%) and also MViTv2 (42.4%).

These results validate the effectiveness of combining multiscale and multiview processing for complex audio representation learning. The considerable gain on the full AudioSet dataset, which is much larger and more diverse, highlights MMViT's ability to learn robust features at scale.

#### 4.2. Image Classification Performance

The positive trend observed in audio classification extends to image classification on the ImageNet1K dataset.
- **ImageNet1K:** MMViT achieved a Top-1 accuracy of **83.2%**, outperforming the MViTv2 baseline model, which achieved 82.7%.

This indicates that the architectural innovations of MMViT are not modality-specific but provide general improvements in learning visual representations as well.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The paper includes two critical ablation studies that shed light on specific design choices:

1. **Impact of Audio-specific CutMix:** An ablation study was conducted to evaluate the regularization effect of the proposed audio CutMix. Without using audio CutMix (relying only on Mixup for half of the clips), the model's performance on AudioSet dropped significantly to an mAP of **39%** (compared to 43% with audio CutMix). This finding strongly emphasizes that the audio-specific CutMix technique is crucial for regularizing the model and preventing overfitting in audio classification tasks, especially when fine-tuning from image pre-trained models.

2. **Number of Views:** An additional ablation study explored the effect of using three different views from the input instead of two. This experiment resulted in a decrease in Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet1K to **82.3%** (compared to 83.2% with two views). The authors hypothesize that introducing redundant information from a third view might negatively impact performance as the model size increases. They suggest that stronger regularization methods might be necessary to effectively train models with more views, proposing this as future work. This suggests that simply adding more views is not automatically beneficial and that optimal view generation and fusion strategies are important.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MMViT model represents a significant advancement in Transformer-based representation learning, particularly for multimodal applications. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

1. **Unified Multiscale and Multiview Learning:** MMViT successfully integrates two powerful concepts – multiscale feature hierarchies (from MViT) and parallel multiview encoding with cross-view fusion (from MTV). This unification provides a more comprehensive and robust way for Transformers to process complex input signals, enabling them to capture information at various resolutions and perspectives simultaneously. This hybrid approach has proven empirically superior to models employing either strategy alone.

2. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** By achieving new state-of-the-art results in both audio and image classification on widely-used public datasets (AudioSet and ImageNet1K), MMViT demonstrates its effectiveness and sets a new benchmark for multimodal representation learning backbones. This empirical success validates the architectural design choices.

3. **Versatility as a Backbone Model:** The model's ability to perform exceptionally well across distinct modalities (audio and image) underscores its potential as a general-purpose backbone architecture. This versatility is highly desirable in the field of AI, as it reduces the need for modality-specific model designs and facilitates transfer learning across domains. The authors explicitly state its applicability to other modalities like video and text, suggesting a pathway towards more unified multimodal AI systems.

4. **Enhanced Representation Capabilities:** The combination of multiscale and multiview features allows MMViT to generate "high-dimensional complex features," which are inherently richer and potentially more interpretable representations of the input. This could lead to better performance in downstream tasks requiring fine-grained understanding.

5. **Practical Contributions for Audio Processing:** The introduction and validation of the "audio-specific CutMix" augmentation technique is a valuable contribution for the audio AI community. It addresses a known challenge of overfitting in audio Transformers, especially when utilizing cross-modal pre-training, thereby improving training stability and performance.

6. **Future Research Directions:** MMViT opens several avenues for future research, including:
 - Applying MMViT to more complex multimodal tasks such as video recognition (where both multiscale and multiview aspects are highly relevant) and object detection.
 - Exploring more sophisticated regularization techniques to handle a larger number of views effectively.
 - Investigating the optimal strategies for generating and fusing different views for various data types.

In conclusion, MMViT pushes the boundaries of Transformer architectures by effectively merging complementary design principles, leading to superior performance and a highly versatile model architecture with broad implications for future multimodal AI research and applications.
