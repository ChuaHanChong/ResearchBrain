# MMViT: Multiscale Multiview Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> MMViT introduces a novel Transformer architecture that merges multiscale feature hierarchies with parallel multiview encoding and cross-attention for robust representation learning. The model achieves state-of-the-art performance on AudioSet (43.0% mAP) and ImageNet1K (83.2% Top-1 accuracy) while also featuring an effective audio-specific CutMix augmentation.

## Problem

- Existing Transformer architectures like Multiscale Vision Transformers (MViT) and Multiview Transformers (MTV) typically focus on either hierarchical scaling or parallel view processing, but not a unified combination.
- The need for a versatile and general-purpose backbone model that can achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse modalities, such as audio and image classification.
- Transformer-based audio models are often susceptible to overfitting, particularly when fine-tuning from models pre-trained on large image datasets.

## Method

- MMViT introduces a novel Transformer architecture that seamlessly integrates ==multiscale feature hierarchies with parallel multiview encoding==, processing distinct views of the input at varying resolutions.
- The model employs strategically placed ==cross-attention blocks== within its multiscale stages to fuse information between different views, enhancing global contextual understanding.
- It utilizes an =='audio-specific CutMix'== data augmentation technique that performs cuts only along the temporal axis to improve regularization and prevent overfitting in audio classification tasks.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on AudioSet with **3**2.2%** mAP** on AudioSet Balanced and **4**3.0%** mAP** on AudioSet Full, outperforming AST (**37.2%** mAP) and MViTv2 (**42.4%** mAP).
- Secured state-of-the-art results on ImageNet1K image classification with a Top-1 accuracy of **83.2%**, surpassing the MViTv2 baseline (**82.7%**).
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the audio-specific CutMix improved AudioSet mAP from **39%** to **43%**, and that using two views was optimal, as three views led to a performance decrease to **82.3%** on ImageNet1K.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining multiscale feature hierarchies with parallel multiview encoding and cross-attention provides a more comprehensive and robust approach to representation learning than either strategy alone.
> - Overlapping patchification and a specialized 'audio-specific CutMix' augmentation are critical for preventing overfitting and enhancing performance, especially when transferring knowledge from image pre-training to audio tasks.
> - The synergistic integration of these architectural elements enables a versatile Transformer backbone capable of achieving state-of-the-art results across distinct modalities like image and audio classification.
