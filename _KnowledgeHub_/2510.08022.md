# FastUMI-100K: Advancing Data-driven Robotic Manipulation with a Large-scale UMI-style Dataset

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory and collaborators developed FastUMI-100K, a large-scale, multimodal dataset containing over 100,000 human demonstration trajectories for robotic manipulation. This dataset enables robust cross-embodiment transfer and facilitates fine-tuning of Vision-Language-Action models across 54 diverse tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation learning is hindered by data scarcity, high collection costs, and the difficulty of acquiring high-quality, diverse human demonstrations.
- Traditional teleoperation often produces non-smooth robot trajectories and is tightly coupled to specific hardware, impeding policy generalization and cross-robot transfer.
- Existing datasets frequently lack the task complexity, long-horizon sequences, and rich multimodal annotations necessary for training advanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

## Method

- Present FastUMI-100K, a UMI-style multimodal dataset built upon an enhanced FastUMI system, employing RealSense T265 for 6-DoF tracking and GoPro cameras for deployment-view imagery.
- Establish a highly efficient and quality-controlled data collection pipeline, utilizing a dedicated team, standardized protocols, and real-time monitoring to acquire over 100,000 trajectories.
- Integrate a novel dual-level text annotation system (subtask and motion-level) to provide precise semantic descriptions aligned with visual and pose data for robust language-conditioned learning.

## Results

- The FastUMI-100K dataset contains over 100,000 high-quality trajectories, totaling approximately 600 hours, spanning 54 tasks and hundreds of household objects across various environments.
- Baseline models like Diffusion Policy achieved up to 66.67% success rates on single-task imitation learning and demonstrated robust cross-embodiment transfer across Xarm6 and Flexiv Rizon4 robots.
- Fine-tuning the π0-base VLA model with FastUMI-100K yielded high success rates on short-horizon tasks (e.g., 93.33% for "Unplug Charger") and showed compatibility for joint training, although long-horizon tasks revealed challenges in sequential inference.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The UMI-style data collection, with its hardware-decoupled design, enables efficient, scalable data generation and inherently supports cross-embodiment policy transfer.
> - Comprehensive multimodal data, particularly the dual-level textual annotations, is critical for effectively training and fine-tuning Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for complex tasks.
> - Capturing direct human manipulation results in smooth, human-like trajectories that, while demanding robust inverse dynamics, are more suitable for learning dexterous, real-world robotic skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself at the forefront of data-driven robotic manipulation, a field increasingly reliant on extensive, high-quality datasets to train generalized policies. Historically, robotic manipulation has struggled with data scarcity, primarily due to the high cost, labor intensity, and technical challenges associated with collecting real-world robot interaction data. This has led to a bottleneck in scaling up imitation learning, a crucial paradigm for enabling robots to learn complex skills from human demonstrations.

Existing data collection methods typically fall into three categories:
- **Teleoperation-based techniques:** While providing high-fidelity control, these methods are often labor-intensive, time-consuming, hardware-coupled, and can result in jerky, non-smooth trajectories. Examples include GELLO, ALOHA/ALOHA 2, and Bunny-VisionPro.
- **Vision-driven demonstration methods:** These are useful for visual learning but often lack precise robot action labels or fine-grained dynamic data like force feedback or contact states.
- **Sensor-enhanced interfaces:** This category, which FastUMI-100K builds upon, aims to convert human demonstrations into robot-executable data with high authenticity and accuracy, bridging the human-robot adaptation gap.

This work specifically advances the paradigm introduced by the **Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI)** and its predecessor, **FastUMI**. UMI pioneered the concept of a hand-held gripper with a wrist-mounted camera, providing an embodiment-agnostic policy interface that captures dense, dynamic, long-horizon, and bimanual skills from the deployment viewpoint. FastUMI further enhanced UMI by improving throughput and cross-embodiment transfer by decoupling the handheld hardware from robot end-effectors and utilizing a RealSense T265 camera for direct 6-DoF end-effector pose logging, thus avoiding heavy offline SLAM or fixed motion-capture infrastructure. FastUMI-100K significantly scales this approach.

In the context of other large-scale datasets, FastUMI-100K contributes to a growing trend of "large-scale data in policy learning," exemplified by:
- **RoboNet:** Standardized multi-robot visual interaction.
- **BridgeData V2:** Expanded multi-environment, instruction-conditioned behaviors.
- **DROID:** Pushed in-the-wild diversity to 76k demonstrations.
- **Open X-Embodiment:** Aggregated over 1 million trajectories across 22 embodiments, leading to models like RT-X that exhibit cross-embodiment transfer.

FastUMI-100K distinguishes itself within this landscape by offering a **large-scale, multimodal UMI-style corpus** that explicitly leverages the advantages of this specific collection paradigm:
- **Preservation of deployment wrist viewpoint:** Crucial for policies designed to operate from the robot's perspective.
- **Smooth, low-latency end-effector states:** Achieved without expensive lab infrastructure.
- **Inherent hardware decoupling:** Maximizes dataset utility and reusability across diverse robot platforms.
- **Multimodal data:** Incorporates not just visual and pose data, but also hierarchical textual annotations (subtask and motion levels), which are vital for training generalist Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models like RT-1, RT-2, and π0.
- **Focus on complexity:** Addresses long-horizon, hinged, and deformable-object tasks, including single-arm and dual-arm configurations, thereby forming a comprehensive atomic skill library.

By providing such a robust and extensive dataset, FastUMI-100K aims to push the boundaries of generalizable manipulation policies and accelerate the development of more capable, human-like robots that can operate effectively in complex, unstructured real-world environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind FastUMI-100K stems from the critical need for large-scale, high-quality expert demonstration datasets to drive advancements in data-driven robotic manipulation learning. The authors identify several key limitations in existing datasets that FastUMI-100K is designed to overcome:

- **Insufficient Scalability:** Current methods, heavily reliant on human teleoperation, are inherently difficult to scale, leading to relatively small datasets compared to those in computer vision or natural language processing. The objective is to dramatically increase the volume of high-quality data.
- **Poor Trajectory Smoothness:** Teleoperated control often results in trajectories that are not as smooth or efficient as natural human movements, limiting the fidelity of learned policies. A key objective is to provide smoother, more human-like demonstrations.
- **Limited Applicability Across Robotic Embodiments:** Many datasets are collected with specific robotic hardware, making it challenging to transfer learned policies to different robot platforms. The motivation is to create an embodiment-agnostic dataset that fosters broad applicability.
- **Lack of Diversity and Complexity in Tasks:** Existing datasets often focus on simpler tasks like pick-and-place. There is an urgent need for data covering more complex, fine-grained, dual-arm collaborative, and long-horizon tasks that mirror real-world challenges. The objective is to build a comprehensive "atomic skill library" encompassing a wide range of household tasks.
- **High Costs and Time Consumption:** The financial and labor costs associated with current data collection methods act as a significant bottleneck. The project aims to develop a more efficient and cost-effective data collection system.

Based on these motivations, the **key objectives** of FastUMI-100K are:
1. **To present FastUMI-100K:** A large-scale UMI-style multimodal demonstration dataset comprising over 100,000 trajectories.
2. **To provide a scalable, flexible, and adaptable solution:** By leveraging the FastUMI robotic system, featuring a modular, hardware-decoupled mechanical design and an integrated lightweight tracking system.
3. **To ensure comprehensive task and object diversity:** Covering 54 tasks and hundreds of object types across representative household environments.
4. **To integrate rich multimodal data streams:** Including end-effector states, multi-view wrist-mounted fisheye images, and fine-grained textual annotations.
5. **To enable high policy success rates:** Across various baseline algorithms, thereby demonstrating its robustness, adaptability, and real-world applicability for complex manipulation challenges.
6. **To support generalizable policies:** Facilitating single-task imitation learning, cross-embodiment transfer, and fine-tuning of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

Ultimately, the goal is to bridge the gap between human demonstration and autonomous robot execution, providing a foundational resource that propels the development of more intelligent and capable robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in developing FastUMI-100K is comprehensive, encompassing hardware design, a structured data collection pipeline, multi-sensor alignment, and an innovative dual-level annotation system.

#### 3.1. Hardware Design

The core of the data collection system is **FastUMI**, a novel robotic system that builds upon the UMI paradigm.
- **Tracking:** It utilizes a **RealSense T265 camera** for 6-DoF end-effector trajectory tracking. This is a key improvement over the original UMI, which relied on GoPro-based VIO and open-source SLAM, making FastUMI faster and more suitable for real-world scenarios.
- **Vision:** High-resolution, wide-angle **GoPro fisheye cameras** capture RGB images, mounted on the wrist, maintaining the crucial deployment viewpoint.
- **Modularity:** A **standardized, plug-and-play fingertip attachment** is designed for compatibility with common handheld device accessories, ensuring the collected data can be easily adapted to various robotic grippers, making it embodiment-agnostic.
- **Dual-Arm Configuration:** A **dual-arm FastUMI system** was specifically developed, integrating two T265 cameras and two GoPro fisheye cameras onto the end-effectors of both grippers to synchronously record multimodal data.

#### 3.2. Data Collection Pipeline

A highly organized and quality-controlled pipeline ensures the efficiency and reliability of the data collection:
- **Dedicated Team:** A team of 10 data collectors and 3 technical support staff (ages 20-24) was established and divided into five subgroups.
- **Pre-Data Collection:**
 - **Task Design:** Technical support staff designed, distributed, and validated tasks, setting reference times based on difficulty.
 - **Instructional Videos:** Demonstration videos were recorded to standardize protocols.
 - **Scenario Setup:** Diverse task scenarios were carefully arranged to enrich data variety.
- **During Data Collection:**
 - **Initial Positioning:** Two fixed 3D-printed slots secured handheld devices, serving as consistent initial positioning points for T265 cameras.
 - **Guidance:** A voice-guided system provided reminders at various stages.
 - **Real-time Monitoring:** Operators monitored GoPro fisheye images and T265 trajectories in real-time on a computer to detect issues like frame drops or trajectory drift.
- **Post-Data Collection:**
 - **Quality Monitoring System:** Calculated linear and angular velocities between adjacent trajectory points to identify operational anomalies (e.g., sudden jumps, global scale distortion).
 - **Automatic Filtering:** Trajectories identified with quality issues were automatically marked as invalid and deleted, providing immediate feedback to collectors.

#### 3.3. Multi-Device Multi-Sensor Alignment

To address challenges arising from different sampling rates and data patterns in multimodal, multi-device data fusion:
- **Unified ROS Clock:** A unified Robot Operating System (ROS) clock assigned consistent timestamps across all data streams.
- **Extended Alignment:** The single-arm multi-sensor alignment technique from FastUMI was extended to the dual-arm scenario.
- **Synchronization Strategy:** Using ROS’s approximate time synchronizer, the two GoPro (60Hz) and two T265 (200Hz) sensor streams were synchronized. RGB image topics were aligned based on timestamps (max alignment error 1/120 seconds).
- **Downsampling and Matching:** Video frames from each gripper were uniformly downsampled to 20Hz and matched with the closest T265 pose data in time, achieving sub-millisecond precision.

#### 3.4. Data Annotation

A novel dual-level text annotation system was developed to provide precise and detailed semantic information, crucial for training language-conditioned policies:
- **Subtask-Level Annotation:**
 - **GPT-4o Integration:** GPT-4o was initially used to analyze video content and automatically divide long-horizon tasks into distinct subtasks.
 - **Manual Refinement:** A custom GUI allowed manual annotation, extracting and segmenting key frames, and precisely matching them with textual descriptions.
 - **Self-Centered Perspective:** Annotations focused on target positions (e.g., "move towards the cup") rather than relative positions (e.g., "move left towards the cup") due to the first-person view, reducing ambiguity.
 - **Quality Control:** GPT-4o pre-annotations were cross-checked and corrected by multiple human annotators.
- **Motion-Level Annotation:**
 - **RT-H Paradigm:** Inspired by the RT-H annotation paradigm, pose data for each frame and the tenth subsequent frame were analyzed in 3D space.
 - **Detailed Descriptions:** Relative translations (X, Y, Z axes), rotational angle changes, and gripper opening/closing changes were calculated. These were then combined with basic semantic units (e.g., "move forward/backward," "rotate clockwise/counterclockwise," "gripper open/close") to generate fine-grained motion descriptions.
- **Output:** This process yielded a total of 15,000 textual annotations, ensuring a complete semantic chain from high-level task objectives to specific motion details.

This comprehensive methodology ensures that FastUMI-100K is not only large-scale but also high-quality, multimodal, and semantically rich, addressing the core challenges of data-driven robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FastUMI-100K presents a significant advancement in robot learning datasets, validated through extensive data analysis and experimental evaluation.

#### 4.1. Dataset Characteristics

- **Scale and Scope:** FastUMI-100K comprises over **100,000 high-quality demonstration trajectories**, equivalent to approximately 600 hours of interactive data. It was collected using both single-arm and dual-arm configurations of the FastUMI system.
- **Task Diversity:** The dataset covers **54 distinct task scenarios**, categorized into six primary groups: Basic Manipulations (e.g., grasping), Composite Manipulations (combining movement and rotation), Hinged Manipulations (e.g., opening doors), Flexible Object Manipulations (e.g., folding clothes), Fine Manipulations (e.g., pouring water), and Dual-arm Collaborative Manipulations (e.g., transferring a pot). These categories reflect a strong emphasis on real-world relevance and complexity beyond simple pick-and-place tasks.
- **Object Diversity:** It involves **hundreds of real-world household objects** with varying shapes, sizes, and colors, distributed across diverse environments like living rooms, studies, bathrooms, and kitchens. This includes rigid, flexible, and articulated objects, as well as items requiring precise control, enhancing generalization capabilities.
- **Task Horizon:** Trajectory durations range from 6 to 25 seconds (120 to 500 frames at 20 Hz). The data collection scheme is significantly more efficient than teleoperation-based methods, reducing collection time for a given task by up to five times (e.g., 10 seconds for clothes folding compared to 50 seconds in AgiBot), while offering higher information density and smoother, continuous manipulation sequences.
- **Human-like Motion:** Direct human manipulation of the gripper (without VR or complex teleoperation) results in more intuitive, first-person interaction and naturalistic motion patterns. Comparisons to datasets like AgiBot show that FastUMI-100K data exhibits human-like linear and angular velocities, crucial for training robots capable of fine, dexterous manipulations.
- **Multimodal Data:** Each trajectory includes end-effector states, multi-view wrist-mounted fisheye images, and detailed dual-level textual annotations (subtask and motion level), providing a rich, semantically aligned data resource.

#### 4.2. Experimental Evaluation

The effectiveness and versatility of FastUMI-100K were systematically validated across diverse robotic manipulation learning methods:

1. **Single-Task Imitation Learning:**
 - Evaluated using **Diffusion Policy (DP)** and **Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT)** on various tasks (e.g., "Make Sandwich," "Heat Food," "Pour Water," "Wash Clothes") on **Xarm6 and Flexiv Rizon4** platforms.
 - **Results:** Diffusion Policy achieved respectable success rates (e.g., 60% for placing lettuce, 66.67% for opening microwave door, 66.67% for pouring water). ACT also showed strong performance, with TCP (Tool Center Point) pose robustly capturing trajectory dynamics, highlighting the value of UMI-style end-effector data.
 - **Insight:** The human-like nature and complexity of FastUMI-100K motions impose higher demands on robot inverse dynamics and manipulation space, requiring robust algorithms but also better aligning with future real-world robot needs.

2. **Cross-Platform Deployment:**
 - Models trained with Diffusion Policy on FastUMI-100K data were directly transferred to **Xarm6 and Flexiv Rizon4** (robots with different brands and structures) without additional fine-tuning, only adjusting coordinate system mapping.
 - **Results:** Demonstrates strong cross-embodiment transferability of the UMI-style data. For instance, the "Make Sandwich" subtasks achieved 60% success on Flexiv Rizon4 and 53.33-60% on Xarm6. "Open the microwave door" achieved 66.67% on both.
 - **Insight:** A crucial finding was the need for a **workspace-based filtering strategy** during data preparation to ensure trajectories are within the physical reach of the target robot, thus enabling flexible deployment across platforms with varying morphologies.

3. **Fine-tuning on VLA Large Models:**
 - **Compatibility:** FastUMI-100K was used to fine-tune the **π0-base pre-trained model** (a Vision-Language-Action model), initially trained on non-UMI data.
 - **Short-Horizon Tasks (Table III):** High success rates were observed across various short-horizon tasks (e.g., "Open Roaster" 86.67%, "Rearrange Coke" 93.33%, "Unplug Charger" 93.33%, "Pick Series" 80-93.33%). This confirms that the fisheye perspective captures sufficient spatial and contextual information and that UMI-style data is fully compatible for joint training with conventional VLA data.
 - **Long-Horizon Tasks (Table IV):** For complex long-horizon tasks (e.g., "Make Sandwich," "Wash Clothes," "Heat Food"), the first stages showed high success rates (e.g., 100% for "Place lettuce on the plate," 93.33% for "Grasp clothes into washer," 100% for "Open microwave door"). However, accuracy slightly declined in later stages due to accumulated inference errors. The "Heat Food" task highlighted a specific challenge: the model could fail to transition between visually similar observations (e.g., pushing open a microwave door vs. grabbing bread), suggesting limitations in relying solely on a first-person wrist perspective for complex sequential reasoning without richer historical or global state information.

Overall, the findings strongly validate FastUMI-100K as a robust, adaptable, and practical dataset for advancing data-driven robotic manipulation, while also highlighting areas for future model development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FastUMI-100K represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, with several profound implications and potential impacts:

- **Addressing Data Scarcity and Scalability:** By providing over 100,000 high-quality trajectories collected with an efficient, scalable system, FastUMI-100K directly tackles the persistent challenge of data scarcity in robot learning. This large scale is critical for training generalist policies and foundation models in robotics, akin to the large datasets that have propelled advancements in computer vision and natural language processing.
- **Enabling Generalization and Robustness:** The dataset's extensive diversity in tasks (54 distinct scenarios, including complex long-horizon, hinged, flexible object, and dual-arm tasks), objects (hundreds of real-world items), and environments (various household settings) is crucial for developing policies that can generalize beyond specific training scenarios. This improved generalization capability is essential for deploying robots in unstructured, dynamic real-world settings.
- **Promoting Cross-Embodiment Transfer:** The hardware-decoupled, UMI-style design of FastUMI-100K means that learned policies are not tied to a specific robot arm. This significantly enhances the reusability of the dataset across different robotic platforms, reducing the need for costly and time-consuming re-collection for each new robot embodiment. This fosters a more universal approach to robot learning.
- **Advancing Human-like Robotics:** The direct human demonstration method results in trajectories that exhibit natural, human-like motion characteristics. This is vital for training robots that can perform dexterous, fine manipulations with the fluidity and efficiency observed in human actions, paving the way for more intuitive and effective human-robot interaction.
- **Facilitating Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Model Development:** The integration of multimodal data—including end-effector states, multi-view fisheye images, and detailed dual-level textual annotations—makes FastUMI-100K an ideal resource for fine-tuning and evaluating advanced VLA models like π0. This multimodal richness is key to enabling robots to understand and execute natural language commands in complex environments, moving towards truly intelligent embodied AI.
- **Reducing Data Collection Costs and Time:** The FastUMI system and its streamlined data collection pipeline significantly improve efficiency, reducing the time required for data acquisition by up to fivefold compared to traditional teleoperation methods. This cost-effectiveness makes large-scale data collection more accessible and sustainable for research and development.
- **Catalyzing Future Research:** The release of the dataset and source code promotes open science and collaboration, providing a standardized benchmark for researchers worldwide. It will stimulate new research in areas such as:
 - **Dual-arm Collaborative Manipulation:** The inclusion of dual-arm data will accelerate progress in complex coordinated tasks.
 - **Long-horizon Task Reasoning:** The challenges identified in VLA models for long-horizon tasks, particularly regarding state ambiguity from first-person views, will drive innovation in developing more sophisticated perception and reasoning architectures.
 - **Sensor Fusion and Multi-Modality:** The multimodal nature encourages the development of advanced algorithms that can effectively combine and leverage information from diverse sensor streams.

In conclusion, FastUMI-100K is not merely a dataset; it is a foundational resource that addresses critical bottlenecks in robotic manipulation learning. Its scale, diversity, multimodal nature, and embodiment-agnostic design are poised to significantly accelerate the development of more capable, generalized, and human-like robots, bringing embodied AI closer to practical real-world deployment.
