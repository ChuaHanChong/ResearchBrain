# What's Hidden Matters: Identifying Planning-Critical Occluded Agents using Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Honda Research Institute developed a framework that trains Vision-Language Models to identify occluded agents critical to an autonomous vehicle's immediate planning, leveraging a novel dataset curated by Planning KL-Divergence and expert VLM annotations. This approach significantly enhanced VLM performance in discerning high-risk hidden agents, enabling more efficient and safer navigation.

## Problem

- Autonomous vehicles (AVs) struggle with safely navigating environments where potential hazards, specifically other agents, are obscured from sensor view, leading to overly conservative driving or unreliable generic predictions.
- Existing occlusion handling methods often treat all occluded spaces uniformly or generate geometrically detailed but dynamically implausible reconstructions, lacking semantic context for risk differentiation.
- Prior Vision-Language Model (VLM) applications in driving have not explicitly focused on identifying which specific occluded agents are most critical to the ego-vehicle's immediate plan.

## Method

- Introduced a systematic method to identify and rank planning-critical occluded agents by adapting the ==Planning KL-Divergence (PKL)== metric to quantify the impact of individual hidden agents on the ego-vehicle's plan.
- Constructed a new ==benchmark dataset== by selecting high-impact scenarios based on PKL scores and using an ==expert VLM (GPT-5)== to generate structured, detailed annotations for the identified planning-critical occlusions.
- Demonstrated that ==fine-tuning== general-purpose and domain-adapted VLMs on this PKL-guided, VLM-annotated data substantially improves their ability to identify and reason about high-risk occluded agents.

## Results

- Supervised fine-tuning on the PKL-guided dataset resulted in substantial performance improvements across all VLMs, with average accuracy gains up to **+0.45** and smaller fine-tuned models (e.g., Qwen-3B) outperforming larger zero-shot variants (e.g., Qwen-72B).
- Performance improvements were particularly pronounced in critical fields such as visual cue detection (**+950%** gain), priority prediction (**+518%** gain), and action prediction (**+138%** embedding similarity gain), indicating enhanced inference of hidden agent intentions.
- PKL-guided data selection proved crucial for effective training, yielding a **32-34%** increase in average accuracy compared to random data selection, without significantly degrading performance on visible-agent tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Planning KL-Divergence effectively quantifies the planning criticality of individual hidden agents, providing a robust metric for curating datasets that focus on safety-critical autonomous driving scenarios.
> - Expert Vision-Language Models can generate high-quality, structured semantic annotations for complex occlusion reasoning, enriching datasets with nuanced visual cues, action hypotheses, and risk assessments.
> - Task-specific, planning-aware fine-tuning of VLMs on curated data is more impactful for occlusion reasoning than relying solely on raw model scale or general domain adaptation, leading to significant performance gains and enabling smaller models to outperform larger zero-shot counterparts.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous vehicles (AVs) face a fundamental challenge in safely navigating complex environments where potential hazards, specifically other agents (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians), may be obscured from sensor view. This problem, known as occlusion handling, is critical for both safety and efficient traffic flow.

Current approaches to occlusion handling generally fall into two categories:
1. **Uniform Conservatism:** Many traditional methods treat all occluded spaces as potentially occupied, leading to overly cautious driving behaviors such as unnecessary braking or "freezing," which can disrupt traffic. These methods often employ set-based prediction and reachability analysis to guarantee safety under worst-case assumptions, but lack semantic context to differentiate risks.
2. **Generative Scene Completion:** More recent deep learning approaches, particularly those utilizing Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) perception models, attempt to predict occupancy grids or reconstruct 3D scene geometry in unseen areas. While these methods can provide detailed geometric reconstructions, they frequently treat all occlusions uniformly and may generate predictions that are not dynamically plausible or relevant to the AV's immediate planning.

The emergence of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) represents a significant advancement in autonomous driving research. VLMs enable semantic grounding and reasoning beyond raw geometric data, offering capabilities for general scene understanding, BEV question answering, and even closed-loop control. However, prior VLM applications in driving have not explicitly focused on identifying which *specific* occluded agents are most critical to the ego-vehicle's immediate plan.

Concurrently, a new class of "planner-aware" metrics, such as Planning KL-Divergence (PKL), has been introduced to quantify the real-world impact of perception errors on downstream planning. These metrics measure the divergence between a plan made with imperfect perception and an ideal plan based on ground-truth information. While initially developed for evaluating perception systems, their potential for informing data curation and model training has largely been unexplored in the context of occluded agents.

This research bridges a critical gap by integrating the semantic reasoning capabilities of VLMs with planner-aware metrics to address the problem of planning-critical occlusions. It shifts the paradigm from generic occlusion handling or perception evaluation to a focused approach that trains VLMs to actively identify and reason about the most impactful hidden agents, thereby linking perception uncertainty directly to planning safety. By doing so, it aims to enable more semantically grounded and efficient risk assessment for autonomous vehicles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work stems from the limitations of existing autonomous driving systems in handling occluded agents. Autonomous vehicles must operate safely in environments where not all potential hazards are visible. Current strategies, such as uniformly conservative planning or generic scene completion, present trade-offs: the former leads to inefficient and overly cautious driving, while the latter can produce unreliable or contextually irrelevant predictions. The core challenge is that not all occlusions pose an equal risk, and a system needs to discern which hidden agents are "planning-critical"—those whose presence would significantly alter the ego-vehicle's intended trajectory.

This research aims to bridge the gap between perception uncertainty and planning safety by enabling Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to identify and reason about these specific, high-impact occluded agents. The key objectives are structured around developing a systematic framework for this task:

1. **To introduce a systematic method for identifying and ranking planning-critical occluded agents:** The motivation here is to move beyond arbitrary selection of occluded scenarios. By leveraging Planning KL-Divergence (PKL), an information-theoretic metric, the authors aim to quantitatively assess the impact of each hidden agent on the ego vehicle's plan. This allows for the selection of truly "high-impact scenarios" from existing datasets like nuScenes, ensuring that the focus of training is on safety-critical situations rather than routine or low-consequence occlusions.
2. **To construct a new benchmark for occlusion reasoning using expert VLM-generated annotations:** A crucial aspect of training robust models is access to high-quality, semantically rich data. The objective is to utilize a capable VLM (specifically GPT-5) as an "expert annotator" to generate structured, detailed annotations for the planning-critical occlusions identified in the first objective. These annotations are intended to capture not just the presence of a hidden agent, but also the visual evidence, semantic context, likely agent hypotheses (e.g., action, class), and spatial reasoning required for a nuanced risk assessment. This structured data is designed to facilitate the training of other VLMs for similar reasoning tasks.
3. **To demonstrate that fine-tuning VLMs on PKL-guided data significantly improves their ability to identify and reason about high-risk occluded agents:** The ultimate objective is to validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework. By conducting comprehensive experiments on a range of general-purpose and domain-adapted VLMs, the authors aim to show that training with data curated based on planning criticality leads to substantial performance improvements. This objective seeks to prove that task-specific, planning-aware training is more impactful than relying solely on larger, general-purpose models or generic domain adaptation, ultimately enabling VLMs to contribute more effectively to safe autonomous driving by providing interpretable and contextually relevant risk assessments.

In essence, the motivation is to develop a more intelligent and efficient risk assessment mechanism for autonomous vehicles by endowing them with the ability to "see" what is hidden and critically assess its importance for safe navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper outlines a systematic framework for identifying and annotating planning-critical occluded agents, subsequently using this curated data to train Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The approach combines visibility analysis, a planner-aware metric for importance ranking, and expert VLM-driven annotation.

#### 3.1. A. Problem Formulation

The problem is defined as training a VLM to generate a rich, structured description for the most planning-critical hidden agents in a driving scene. Given a set of all agents `A` (visible `V` and hidden `H`) and multi-camera images over a `T`-second window, the VLM's output for a critical hidden agent should include:
- **Agent Identification:** Class (e.g., car, pedestrian) and textual description of visual cues.
- **Contextual Analysis:** Occluding object, occlusion type, and camera view of evidence.
- **Risk Assessment:** Predicted action hypothesis, priority level (high, medium, low), and a Priority Sector on the BEV map (e.g., Ahead-Left). This structured output aims to provide an interpretable assessment of latent risks.

#### 3.2. B. Planning-Based Importance Ranking

To quantify the "planning-criticality" of hidden agents, the authors adapt the **Planning KL-Divergence (PKL)** metric.
- **PKL Definition:** PKL measures the Kullback-Leibler divergence between the ego-vehicle's planned trajectory distribution derived from a full ground-truth scene (`A`) versus a scene with only visible agents (`V`). It quantifies the impact of perception errors (missing `H`) on planning:
 `PKL(H) = Σ D_KL(p_θ(x_{t+Δ} | A_≤t) || p_θ(x_{t+Δ} | V_≤t))`
 where `T` is the planning horizon, `p_θ` is the learned trajectory distribution, `x` are ego-vehicle poses, `A_≤t` is all agents up to time `t`, and `V_≤t` is visible agents up to time `t`.
- **Individual Agent Gain:** To rank individual hidden agents, the "planning gain" for each hidden agent `h_i ∈ H` is calculated. This gain represents the reduction in PKL if `h_i` were to become visible:
 `Gain(h_i) = PKL(V) - PKL(V ∪ {h_i})`
 A higher `Gain(h_i)` indicates a greater impact of `h_i` on the ego-vehicle's planned trajectory, thereby quantifying its importance. This metric forms the basis for ground-truth importance scoring for data curation.

#### 3.3. C. PKL-Guided Dataset Generation Pipeline

The dataset creation process integrates PKL-based ranking with VLM annotation and consists of three main stages:

1. **Hidden Agent Extraction:**
 - The nuScenes dataset is processed using the Occ3D framework to determine visibility scores for all annotated agents.
 - An agent's visibility score `v_i` is calculated as the ratio of its visible voxel area on the camera plane to its total projected 3D bounding box area.
 - Agents with `v_i` below a specified threshold `τ` (set to 0.4 based on ablation studies) are classified as hidden agents.

2. **Planning-Critical Agent Selection:**
 - For each scene containing hidden agents, PKL gains are computed for all identified hidden agents.
 - Scenes containing at least one hidden agent with a "substantial planning impact" (i.e., high PKL gain) are selected. Specifically, the top 1,000 scenes from the nuScenes training set and top 250 from the validation set are chosen, ensuring the dataset focuses on safety-critical scenarios.

3. **Structured Annotation Generation:**
 - GPT-5 is employed as a semantic annotator. For each selected planning-critical hidden agent, GPT-5 is provided with temporal multi-camera panoramas (across `T` seconds), the target hidden agent's class, and its bounding box information.
 - A structured prompt guides GPT-5 to generate detailed JSON annotations, including:
 - **Visual Cue:** Type (e.g., partial visibility, indirect motion, light signatures) and textual description.
 - **Camera:** View where evidence is found (e.g., FRONT, LEFT).
 - **Agent Class:** The hidden agent's category.
 - **Action Hypothesis:** Predicted behavior (e.g., "Preparing to pull out").
 - **Occluder Class:** The primary object causing occlusion.
 - **Occlusion Type:** Pattern (e.g., occluded by agent, landmark, weather) and textual description.
 - **Priority:** Level (High, Medium, Low) and Sector in ego's BEV (e.g., Ahead-Left).
 - **Reasoning:** An explanation for the risk assessment.
 - The annotation process involved iterative refinement of prompts to emphasize indirect cues, standardize coordinate definitions, and provide detailed classification guidelines. A subset of samples was manually audited for quality.

#### 3.4. D. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset:** The final dataset comprises 1,250 scene windows (1,000 training, 250 validation) from nuScenes, each spanning `T = 2` seconds with 3 frames sampled at 1-second intervals from 6 cameras. This results in 3,750 structured JSON annotations.
- **Planner Training:** The planner used for PKL computation was trained on the nuScenes training split for a 4-second horizon, following existing methodology.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Categorical Fields:** Accuracy and F1 scores for Agent Class, Camera, Occluder Class, Occlusion Type, Priority Level, and Sector.
 - **Text Generation:** BLEU, ROUGE-L, and embedding similarity for Action Hypothesis.
 - **Overall:** Average accuracy and F1 across categorical fields, and average action score (mean of BLEU, ROUGE-L, embedding similarity).
- **Implementation:** LoRA (rank 16) fine-tuning was applied to all linear layers of the VLMs (alignment module and language model components) using the `ms-swift` framework. AdamW optimizer (2e-5 learning rate), batch size 4 per GPU, across 4 H100 GPUs, for 3 epochs. Structured JSON output generation was enforced for inference.
- **Baseline Models:** A range of general-purpose VLMs (Qwen2.5-VL, InternVL3.5, Gemma-3, MiniCPM-V 4.5) and domain-adapted models (DriveMM, DriveLM, DriveGPT4) were evaluated in both zero-shot and fine-tuned settings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrated the effectiveness of the PKL-guided data selection and supervised fine-tuning approach for training VLMs to identify planning-critical occluded agents.

#### 4.1. A. Zero-Shot Performance

- **Scale Effects:** Within VLM families, larger models generally exhibited better zero-shot performance. For instance, Qwen-72B achieved the highest average accuracy among open models (0.440).
- **Cross-Family Variations:** Performance varied significantly across different VLM architectures. InternVL3.5-8B, despite being considerably smaller (8B parameters), achieved comparable average accuracy (0.438) to Qwen-72B, suggesting architectural design plays a role.
- **Domain Adaptation Limitations:** Domain-adapted models like DriveMM and DriveGPT4 showed limited zero-shot capabilities for this specific task, with DriveMM performing poorly overall and DriveGPT4 showing no action understanding. DriveLM achieved the highest average action score (0.098) but did not lead in other metrics. This indicates that general driving-related pre-training is insufficient for complex, planning-critical occlusion reasoning without task-specific fine-tuning.

#### 4.2. B. Fine-Tuning Impact

- **Dramatic Performance Improvements:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on the PKL-guided dataset resulted in substantial performance gains across all evaluated models. Average accuracy improvements ranged from +0.09 (Qwen-32B) to +0.45 (InternVL3.5-1B), with smaller models benefiting disproportionately (e.g., InternVL3.5-1B's average accuracy improved by 493%).
- **Efficiency Gains:** Fine-tuned smaller models consistently outperformed larger zero-shot variants. For example, the fine-tuned Qwen-3B (0.478 average accuracy) surpassed the zero-shot Qwen-72B (0.440 average accuracy), despite being 24 times smaller. This highlights the value of task-specific training over raw model scale for this particular challenge.
- **Component-Specific Gains:** The improvements were not uniform across all output fields. Visual cue detection showed the largest gain (+950%), followed by priority prediction (+518%), occluder classification (+390%), camera view (+386%), and action prediction (+138% in embedding similarity). This pattern suggests that fine-tuning is most effective for acquiring skills related to interpreting indirect occlusion cues and inferring hidden agent intentions, which are often absent in general pre-training.

#### 4.3. C. Ablation Studies

1. **PKL and Visibility Threshold Sensitivity:**
 - **PKL Convergence:** Analysis showed that per-scene PKL estimates stabilized after approximately 2 seconds (5 samples), confirming the robustness of the `T=2s` planning window used for data curation.
 - **Visibility Threshold (τ):** A `τ=0.4` was chosen for classifying hidden agents. While PKL recovery ratio remained stable across `τ ∈ [0.1, 0.6]`, this threshold balanced dataset size with occlusion severity, aligning with nuScenes' lowest visibility bin.

2. **Impact of PKL-Guided Selection:**
 - Comparing models fine-tuned on PKL-guided data against those trained on randomly selected hidden agents showed significant advantages.
 - For Qwen-7B, PKL-guided selection improved average accuracy from 0.405 to 0.543 (+34%).
 - For InternVL3.5-8B, average accuracy improved from 0.429 to 0.565 (+32%).
 - These consistent improvements demonstrate that focusing on planning-critical agents during training is crucial for developing accurate occlusion reasoning.

3. **Fine-Tuning Strategy Analysis:**
 - Comparing fine-tuning only the language model (LLM-only) versus the full approach (LLM + alignment module) showed modest but consistent gains for Qwen-7B when both components were fine-tuned, particularly in action score (+0.007).
 - InternVL3.5-8B showed mixed results, with marginal improvements in accuracy and F1 but a slight decrease in precision, suggesting model-dependent benefits of aligner fine-tuning. The authors recommend fine-tuning both components due to minimal overhead and consistent gains for some architectures.

4. **Impact on Visible-Agent Performance:**
 - Evaluation on the MME-RealWorld autonomous driving subset, which predominantly features unoccluded scenarios, showed that fine-tuning on occlusion-focused data did not significantly degrade performance on visible-agent tasks.
 - DriveLM (fine-tuned) showed a minor 1-2% drop in overall and perception scores, while reasoning remained identical. Qwen-3B (fine-tuned) exhibited even smaller degradation, with all scores decreasing by less than 1%. This indicates that catastrophic forgetting of visible-agent capabilities was not a significant issue.

#### 4.4. D. Discussion

The findings indicate that **task-specific, planning-aware training is more critical than raw model scale or general domain adaptation** for this task. The PKL-guided data selection strategy provides substantial performance benefits by prioritizing occlusions that truly impact planning decisions. The improvements in action prediction specifically highlight the models' ability to infer hidden agent intentions from indirect visual evidence. The structured VLM outputs offer clear pathways for downstream integration into ADAS systems, multi-agent collaboration, and cost-map modifications for planners, with some smaller fine-tuned models (e.g., Qwen-3B at 47ms inference latency) approaching real-time feasibility.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a systematic framework for addressing a critical safety challenge in autonomous driving: identifying and reasoning about occluded agents that are most pertinent to the ego-vehicle's trajectory. Its significance and potential impact span several key areas:

- **Bridging the Perception-Planning Gap:** The work introduces the first systematic approach to connect perception uncertainty directly with planning safety by training Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to focus on *planning-critical* occlusions. This is a notable advancement beyond traditional approaches that either treat all occlusions uniformly or attempt generic scene reconstruction.
- **Enhanced Safety and Efficiency in Autonomous Driving:** By enabling AVs to intelligently assess the risk posed by hidden agents, the framework supports a move away from overly conservative driving behaviors (which can hinder efficiency) or reliance on potentially unreliable generative predictions. The ability to identify high-impact scenarios allows AVs to make more informed and context-aware decisions, potentially leading to safer and more fluid navigation.
- **Novel Benchmark for Occlusion Reasoning:** The creation of a new, targeted dataset from nuScenes, curated using Planning KL-Divergence (PKL) and enriched with structured, semantically grounded annotations generated by an expert VLM (GPT-5), provides a valuable resource for future research. This benchmark focuses on scenarios where occluded agents truly affect planning, offering a more challenging and relevant evaluation setting than datasets based on arbitrary occlusions.
- **Validation of Task-Specific Training:** The results underscore that fine-tuning VLMs on carefully curated, task-specific data is more impactful than relying solely on model scale or general domain adaptation. The observation that smaller, fine-tuned models can outperform much larger zero-shot counterparts has implications for deploying efficient and specialized AI systems in resource-constrained environments like autonomous vehicles.
- **Interpretability and Actionable Outputs:** The structured JSON output generated by the VLMs provides not only a classification but also visual evidence, hypothesized actions, occlusion details, and a prioritized risk assessment (priority level and sector). This level of detail offers enhanced interpretability, which is crucial for safety-critical systems, and provides actionable insights that can directly inform downstream planning modules or driver warning systems (ADAS).
- **Facilitating Multi-Agent Collaboration and ADAS Development:** The structured output format has direct utility for multi-agent systems, enabling ego-vehicles to communicate planning-critical occlusion information to other connected agents (V2V, V2X). Furthermore, the priority and sector fields can directly trigger specific warnings or alerts in ADAS, thereby augmenting human driver awareness in complex scenarios. The output can also be converted into dynamic cost-map modifications for motion planners, allowing them to account for predicted hidden threats.

Future work directions identified by the authors include expanding the diversity of training data to address long-tail distributions of safety-critical events, conducting cross-dataset validation, optimizing VLM inference latency for real-time deployment, and mitigating potential biases from single-LLM annotation through ensemble methods or human-in-the-loop verification. A crucial next step involves closed-loop evaluation, integrating these VLM outputs directly into a planner to quantify real-world safety improvements.
