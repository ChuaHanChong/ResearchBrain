# TD-GRPC: Temporal Difference Learning with Group Relative Policy Constraint for Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> TD-GRPC, a new framework for model-based reinforcement learning, integrates Group Relative Policy Optimization and explicit policy constraints to enhance stability and sample efficiency for humanoid locomotion. Evaluated on the 26-degree-of-freedom Unitree H1-2 robot, it demonstrated superior performance and faster convergence on challenging tasks like standing, walking, and running compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid locomotion is challenging due to high-dimensional continuous action spaces, unstable dynamics, and complex contact interactions.
- Existing model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) approaches like TD-MPC suffer from policy instability caused by policy mismatch between data collection and value function training.
- Aggressive policy updates in MBRL lead to distributional shifts, pushing the agent into poorly explored regions and hindering sample efficiency.

## Method

- The **TD-GRPC** framework integrates ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to stabilize policy gradient estimation by comparing actions within sampled groups.
- It employs an explicit policy constraint using KL divergence to limit the deviation of updated policies from previous ones, preventing aggressive changes.
- These two mechanisms are combined within a unified objective function to improve the stability and sample efficiency of model-based reinforcement learning for humanoid control.

## Results

- TD-GRPC consistently outperformed baseline methods (SAC, TD-MPC2) in episode return and convergence speed across several humanoid locomotion tasks, including standing, walking, running, and sitting on the Unitree H1-2 robot.
- The framework demonstrated significant improvements in sample efficiency compared to model-free methods and achieved faster learning compared to other TD-MPC variants.
- Qualitatively, TD-GRPC enabled correct forward locomotion, stable sitting postures, and utilized arm movements for balancing, showcasing more robust and natural behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) uses group-wise action comparisons to provide bounded advantage scores and lower variance policy gradients, leading to more stable policy updates.
> - A KL-divergence-based policy constraint prevents aggressive policy updates and distributional shifts, ensuring policy consistency during training.
> - The combination of GRPO and the explicit policy constraint directly addresses the issues of policy mismatch and instability in model-based reinforcement learning for complex systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Humanoid locomotion represents one of the most challenging problems in robotics and reinforcement learning. The complexity arises from high-dimensional continuous action spaces, unstable dynamics, and intricate contact interactions with the environment. While recent advances in model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) have shown promise, existing methods still struggle with policy instability and poor sample efficiency when applied to complex humanoid robots.

This paper introduces TD-GRPC (Temporal-Difference Group Relative Policy Constraint), a framework that addresses critical limitations in current MBRL approaches by integrating two key mechanisms: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and explicit policy constraints. The work demonstrates significant improvements in stability and sample efficiency for humanoid locomotion control using the 26-degree-of-freedom Unitree H1-2 robot.

### 2. Background and Problem Formulation

#### 2.1. The Challenge of Humanoid Control

Traditional approaches to humanoid control have relied on three main paradigms:

1. **Model Predictive Control (MPC)**: Excels at real-time optimization but requires accurate models and carefully designed cost functions
2. **Model-Free Reinforcement Learning**: Offers flexibility but suffers from poor sample efficiency and unstable training
3. **Model-Based Reinforcement Learning**: Attempts to combine planning efficiency with learning adaptability but faces challenges with model errors and policy instability

#### 2.2. TD-MPC and Its Limitations

Recent hybrid approaches like TD-MPC and TD-MPC2 have emerged as promising solutions. These methods learn latent dynamics models jointly with value functions using temporal difference learning. The framework consists of:

- An encoder `h_θ` that maps high-dimensional states to latent representations
- A latent dynamics model `d_θ` that predicts future states
- Reward and value networks for planning
- Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) for trajectory optimization

However, TD-MPC variants face two critical issues in humanoid locomotion:

1. **Policy Mismatch**: Discrepancy between the policies used for data collection and value function training
2. **Distributional Shifts**: Aggressive policy updates that drive the agent into poorly explored regions

### 3. The TD-GRPC Framework

#### 3.1. Core Innovation

TD-GRPC addresses these limitations through two integrated mechanisms:

#### 3.2. Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

Traditional policy optimization relies on absolute advantage estimates, which can be noisy and lead to high-variance gradients. GRPO instead uses group-wise action comparisons:

1. Sample G actions at each state
2. Compute Q-values for all actions
3. Define softmax-based advantage scores:

```
A_i(q) = exp(q_i/τ) / Σ_j exp(q_j/τ)
```

This approach provides several benefits:
- Bounded advantage scores (0 to 1) reduce the impact of outliers
- Lower variance in policy gradients
- More stable policy updates through relative comparisons

#### 3.3. Policy Constraint (PC)

To prevent aggressive policy updates, TD-GRPC implements a trust-region constraint using KL divergence:

```
L_PC = max{D_KL(π||π_old) - ε, 0}
```

This constraint ensures that policy updates remain within a reasonable distance from the previous policy, preventing destabilizing distributional shifts.

#### 3.4. Unified Objective Function

The complete policy objective combines both mechanisms:

```
L_π(θ) = L_GRPO_π(θ) + β log μ(a|s)
```

where the second term acts as residual-style regularization equivalent to the trust-region constraint.

### 4. Experimental Results and Analysis

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

The authors evaluated TD-GRPC on the HumanoidBench locomotion suite using the Unitree H1-2 robot (26 DoF, 70kg). Baselines included:
- Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)
- TD-MPC2
- TD-M(PC)2

#### 4.2. Quantitative Performance

Key findings include:

**Strong Performance Tasks:**
- **Standing, Walking, Running, Sitting**: TD-GRPC consistently outperformed baselines, achieving stable policies with higher rewards and faster convergence
- **Sample Efficiency**: Significant improvement over model-free methods like SAC
- **Convergence Speed**: Faster learning compared to other TD-MPC variants

**Challenging Tasks:**
- **Crawling**: TD-GRPC struggled compared to SAC and TD-MPC2, highlighting limitations in full-body contact scenarios
- **Balancing and Stair-Climbing**: Extremely difficult for all methods due to the robot's weight (70kg), though TD-GRPC showed slight improvements

#### 4.3. Qualitative Analysis

Critical behavioral improvements:

1. **Correct Locomotion Direction**: TD-GRPC enabled forward walking/running, while baselines often resulted in backward movement
2. **Stable Sitting Postures**: Achieved optimal sitting poses with minimal jerky motions
3. **Arm Balancing**: Experiments revealed the crucial role of arm movements in maintaining stability across all locomotion tasks
4. **Navigation Strategy**: Developed collision-avoidance behaviors, though not always achieving true navigation through obstacles

#### 4.4. Task Completion Summary

TD-GRPC achieved sufficient solutions for 4 out of 10 tasks (Standing, Walking, Running, Sitting) and required only mild refinements for 4 additional tasks, outperforming baseline methods in overall task completion rate.

### 5. Significance and Future Directions

#### 5.1. Key Contributions

1. **Stability Enhancement**: Direct addressing of policy mismatch and distributional shift problems in MBRL
2. **Sample Efficiency**: Demonstrated improved learning speed crucial for practical robotics applications
3. **Methodological Innovation**: Integration of GRPO from language models with trust-region constraints represents cross-domain knowledge transfer
4. **Empirical Validation**: Comprehensive evaluation on challenging humanoid locomotion tasks

#### 5.2. Broader Impact

The TD-GRPC framework's principles extend beyond humanoid locomotion:

- **Robotic Manipulation**: The stability mechanisms could benefit dexterous control tasks
- **Autonomous Systems**: Applications in autonomous driving and other high-dimensional control problems
- **Safety-Critical Domains**: The constraint mechanisms provide foundations for safer RL deployment

#### 5.3. Future Research Directions

1. **Real-World Transfer**: Investigation of sim-to-real transfer capabilities
2. **Advanced Constraints**: Development of safety and energy efficiency constraints
3. **Challenging Environments**: Extension to uneven terrain and human interaction scenarios
4. **Architectural Improvements**: Integration with more sophisticated perception and world models

The work establishes TD-GRPC as a significant advancement in making reinforcement learning more viable for complex humanoid control, providing both theoretical insights and practical improvements that advance the field toward more capable autonomous humanoid robots.
