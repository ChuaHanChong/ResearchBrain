# TD-GRPC: Temporal Difference Learning with Group Relative Policy Constraint for Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> TD-GRPC, a new framework for model-based reinforcement learning, integrates Group Relative Policy Optimization and explicit policy constraints to enhance stability and sample efficiency for humanoid locomotion. Evaluated on the 26-degree-of-freedom Unitree H1-2 robot, it demonstrated superior performance and faster convergence on challenging tasks like standing, walking, and running compared to prior methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid locomotion is challenging due to high-dimensional continuous action spaces, unstable dynamics, and complex contact interactions.
- Existing model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) approaches like TD-MPC suffer from policy instability caused by policy mismatch between data collection and value function training.
- Aggressive policy updates in MBRL lead to distributional shifts, pushing the agent into poorly explored regions and hindering sample efficiency.

## Method

- The **TD-GRPC** framework integrates ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== to stabilize policy gradient estimation by comparing actions within sampled groups.
- It employs an explicit policy constraint using KL divergence to limit the deviation of updated policies from previous ones, preventing aggressive changes.
- These two mechanisms are combined within a unified objective function to improve the stability and sample efficiency of model-based reinforcement learning for humanoid control.

## Results

- TD-GRPC consistently outperformed baseline methods (SAC, TD-MPC2) in episode return and convergence speed across several humanoid locomotion tasks, including standing, walking, running, and sitting on the Unitree H1-2 robot.
- The framework demonstrated significant improvements in sample efficiency compared to model-free methods and achieved faster learning compared to other TD-MPC variants.
- Qualitatively, TD-GRPC enabled correct forward locomotion, stable sitting postures, and utilized arm movements for balancing, showcasing more robust and natural behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) uses group-wise action comparisons to provide bounded advantage scores and lower variance policy gradients, leading to more stable policy updates.
> - A KL-divergence-based policy constraint prevents aggressive policy updates and distributional shifts, ensuring policy consistency during training.
> - The combination of GRPO and the explicit policy constraint directly addresses the issues of policy mismatch and instability in model-based reinforcement learning for complex systems.
