# Hierarchical Soft Actor-Critic for Sparse-Reward Long-Horizon Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> An HRL-SAC framework combines hierarchical reinforcement learning with Soft Actor-Critic at both levels to tackle sparse rewards and long-horizon tasks in continuous control environments. The approach shows superior performance over flat SAC in a search-and-rescue simulation, demonstrating significantly higher extrinsic rewards, success rates, and search coverage with greater sample efficiency.

## Problem

- Standard deep reinforcement learning algorithms struggle with efficient exploration and delayed credit assignment in environments characterized by sparse extrinsic rewards and long operational horizons.
- Existing Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) methods often rely on discrete action spaces, deterministic low-level policies, or task-specific heuristics, limiting their applicability to continuous control problems.
- Combining HRL with continuous control and off-policy learning can lead to training instabilities, such as non-stationary transition distributions and unstable gradients.

## Method

- A two-level Hierarchical Soft Actor-Critic (**HRL-SAC**) framework is proposed, featuring a high-level ==meta-controller== for strategic sub-goal selection and a low-level ==continuous controller== for primitive action execution.
- Both hierarchical levels utilize ==entropy-regularized Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) optimization==, promoting stable learning and diverse exploration within continuous action spaces.
- An ==intrinsic reward==, defined as the negative L2 distance between the achieved state feature and the desired sub-goal, guides the low-level policy, providing dense learning signals for efficient credit assignment.

## Results

- In training, HRL-SAC achieved **3.04** times higher average extrinsic reward, **1.77** times higher success rate, and **2.08** times greater search area coverage compared to a flat SAC baseline.
- HRL-SAC demonstrated significantly higher sample efficiency, requiring only **0.40 million** environment steps for superior performance, while standard SAC needed **4.80 million** steps.
- On a held-out test set, HRL-SAC yielded a positive average reward of **71,440.24** and completed tasks in an average of **879** steps, in contrast to flat SAC's negative average reward of **-365,453.61** and **8,054** average steps.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Applying entropy-regularized SAC optimization at both the meta-controller and low-level provides enhanced learning stability and sample efficiency in continuous control settings.
> - Hierarchical decomposition effectively mitigates the delayed credit assignment problem by enabling strategic long-term planning with the meta-controller and providing dense intrinsic rewards to the low-level agent.
> - Stochastic policies at both levels, encouraged by the maximum-entropy objective, foster diverse exploration and prevent premature convergence to suboptimal or deterministic behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated effectiveness in various sequential decision-making tasks. However, standard deep RL algorithms face significant challenges in environments characterized by sparse extrinsic rewards and long horizons. In such settings, agents struggle to efficiently explore and attribute credit to actions over extended time scales, often leading to stalled convergence or suboptimal policies.

Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) is a paradigm that addresses these limitations by introducing temporal and behavioral abstraction. HRL decomposes complex tasks into a hierarchy of sub-problems, allowing agents to reason over different time scales. A common HRL architecture involves a high-level policy (meta-controller) that selects long-term sub-goals and a low-level policy (worker) that executes primitive actions to achieve these sub-goals. While HRL offers theoretical advantages in exploration and credit assignment, its practical implementation, especially when combined with continuous control and off-policy learning, presents challenges such as non-stationary transition distributions and unstable gradients.

Concurrently, the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm has emerged as an off-policy actor-critic method known for its stability and sample efficiency in continuous control tasks. SAC optimizes a maximum-entropy objective, encouraging stochastic policies that balance exploration and exploitation by incorporating an entropy term into the reward function. Despite its strengths, flat SAC policies often struggle in sparse-reward and long-horizon environments due as they lack temporal abstraction and can exhibit inefficient exploration over extended periods.

This work integrates HRL and SAC, aiming to leverage the benefits of both approaches. It seeks to address the gap where existing HRL methods often rely on discrete action spaces, deterministic low-level policies, or task-specific heuristics, while current SAC extensions typically operate at a single temporal scale, making them less effective for long-horizon sparse rewards. The proposed HRL-SAC framework distinguishes itself by introducing entropy-regularized SAC optimization at both hierarchical levels (meta and low-level controllers) within a continuous control setting. This dual-level entropy regularization and goal-conditioned SAC are presented as a novel approach to enhance sample efficiency and convergence stability in complex sparse-reward, long-horizon tasks, particularly in autonomous search and rescue scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the persistent challenges of exploration and credit assignment in sparse-reward, long-horizon reinforcement learning environments. Standard deep reinforcement learning methods frequently struggle to propagate delayed rewards over extended temporal horizons, resulting in inefficient exploration and unstable convergence.

Specifically, the motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

- **Mitigate Delayed Credit Assignment:** In environments with sparse rewards, agents receive positive feedback infrequently, making it difficult to determine which early actions contributed to a distant reward. This "delayed credit assignment" problem hinders learning. The research aims to overcome this by introducing temporal abstraction, which creates denser, more frequent learning signals at intermediate decision points.
- **Enhance Exploration Efficiency:** Traditional RL exploration strategies often prove insufficient in vast state-action spaces characteristic of long-horizon tasks. Random exploration is exponentially unlikely to discover rewarding states. The proposed framework is motivated by the need for more systematic and directed exploration, achieved through hierarchical decomposition and entropy regularization.
- **Improve Learning Stability and Convergence:** Combining hierarchical structures with continuous control and off-policy learning can lead to training instability due to non-stationary transition distributions and unstable gradients. The study aims to achieve more stable policy optimization and faster convergence by integrating Soft Actor-Critic's maximum-entropy objective at both hierarchical levels. This includes reducing the variance of policy gradient estimates by shortening the effective planning horizon.
- **Develop a Generalizable Framework for Continuous Control:** Many existing HRL approaches are tailored for discrete action spaces or rely on deterministic low-level policies, limiting their applicability to continuous control problems. A key objective is to develop an HRL framework that effectively operates in continuous action spaces using stochastic, entropy-regularized policies at both levels.
- **Address Challenges in Search-and-Rescue Scenarios:** The work is explicitly motivated by the demands of autonomous search-and-rescue tasks, which inherently feature sparse rewards (finding a target), long operational horizons (extensive search areas), and require continuous control (drone navigation). The goal is to develop a robust agent capable of strategic planning and precise execution in such real-world inspired applications.
- **Avoid Handcrafted Rewards and Architectural Tuning:** The research aims to achieve improved performance without relying on auxiliary rewards or complex, task-specific architectural tuning, which can be difficult to design and may limit generalizability. The dual-entropy HRL-SAC formulation is intended to provide a theoretically grounded solution that inherently handles these complexities.

In summary, the core motivation is to create a robust and efficient hierarchical reinforcement learning agent that can effectively learn in continuous-control, sparse-reward, long-horizon settings by leveraging the strengths of both temporal abstraction and maximum-entropy policy optimization, with a specific application focus on search and rescue.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a two-level Hierarchical Reinforcement Learning (HRL) framework named Hierarchical Soft Actor-Critic (HRL-SAC). This architecture decomposes decision-making into a high-level meta-controller and a low-level continuous controller, both utilizing entropy-regularized policy optimization.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Architecture

The HRL-SAC agent consists of two main components:

- **Meta-Controller (High-Level Agent):**
 - **Function:** Responsible for strategic, long-horizon planning. It operates at a coarser temporal scale, selecting a sub-goal ($g \in G$) from a set of predefined abstract goals (e.g., ExploreArea, SearchSpecificLocation, TrackObject, ReturnToBase) every K environment steps.
 - **Policy:** A stochastic meta-policy $\pi_{\phi}(g|s)$ is used, where $\phi$ represents the weights of the high-level policy network. This network maps the global state observation ($s$) to a categorical distribution over possible sub-goals.
 - **Optimization:** The meta-policy is optimized to maximize the expected extrinsic return. A meta-level Q-function, $Q^{\text{meta}}_{\omega}(s, g)$, estimates the cumulative return of selecting a sub-goal $g$ in state $s$. The meta-level Bellman update incorporates aggregated extrinsic rewards, $R^{\text{meta}}_t$, collected over K steps.
 - **Network Architecture:** The Meta Actor (policy network) has an input dimension of 272 (observation without goal encoding) and an output dimension of 4 (for the 4 high-level goals). It consists of two hidden layers with 256 units, using LayerNorm, ReLU, and Dropout. Dual Meta Critic Q-networks, each with two hidden layers of 256 units, estimate scalar meta Q-values.

- **Low-Level Controller:**
 - **Function:** Responsible for fine-grained, short-term continuous control, executing primitive actions to satisfy the sub-goal selected by the meta-controller.
 - **Policy:** A stochastic low-level policy $\pi_{\theta}(a|s, g)$ produces continuous actions ($a$) conditioned on both the current state ($s$) and the generated sub-goal ($g$).
 - **Algorithm:** The low-level controller is trained using the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm, which optimizes a maximum-entropy objective ($J_{LL}$) to encourage exploration and stability.
 - **Intrinsic Reward:** To guide the low-level policy, an intrinsic reward, $r_{\text{int}}(s_t, a_t, g_t) = -\lVert f(s_{t+1}) - g_t \rVert_2$, is defined. This reward encourages minimizing the L2 distance between the achieved state feature (mapped by $f(\cdot)$) and the desired sub-goal, providing dense learning signals even in the absence of extrinsic task rewards.
 - **Network Architecture:** The Low-Level Actor (policy network) takes a 276-dimensional input (state + goal encoding) and outputs 4-dimensional continuous actions (movement X, movement Y, search intensity, camera zoom). It comprises four hidden layers (512, 512, 512, 256 units), using LayerNorm, ReLU, and Dropout, with mean and log-std output heads for a clipped Gaussian policy. Dual Low-Level Critic Q-networks, each with three hidden layers (512, 512, 256 units), estimate scalar Q-values.

#### 3.2. Environment and Data

The framework was evaluated in a simulated continuous-control search task inspired by search-and-rescue (SAR) scenarios.
- **State Space (S):** A 276-dimensional continuous vector comprising agent position, target position, time progress, environmental conditions (sea, weather, time of day, visibility, wind speed), object detection binaries (6 categories), search grid coverage (64D from 32x32 down-sampled), and high-level goal one-hot encoding.
- **Action Space (A):** A 4-dimensional continuous and bounded space representing drone movement (X, Y), search intensity, and camera zoom.
- **Reward Structure:** Sparse rewards are defined (Table 5). A positive reward (+100) is given for reaching the target. Negative penalties are applied for collisions (-50), each step (-0.1), energy inefficiency (-0.01 * ||a||^2), and boundary violations (-10.0).
- **Datasets:**
 - **Synthetic Dataset:** 200 procedurally generated scenarios for initial development and validation.
 - **SAR-2 Dataset:** A real-world dataset (1980 images) sourced from Kaggle and Roboflow, with YOLO-v5-format annotations across 6 object categories. This dataset was used for comprehensive evaluation with a 70%/20%/10% train/validation/test split.

#### 3.3. Training Procedure

Training proceeds by alternating updates between the two hierarchical levels:
1. **Warm-up Phase:** 2,000 random exploration steps to populate replay buffers.
2. **Meta-Policy Update:** The meta-policy selects a sub-goal every K=30 steps. The high-level critic and actor networks are updated using extrinsic returns aggregated over these K steps.
3. **Low-Level Policy Update:** The low-level SAC policy executes continuous actions conditioned on the selected sub-goal. The low-level critic and actor networks are updated using intrinsic rewards at every step.
4. **Replay Buffers:** Separate prioritized replay buffers are used for intrinsic and extrinsic rewards.
5. **Target Networks:** Target networks and Polyak averaging ($\tau = 0.005$) are used for stable updates.
6. **Optimization:** Adam optimizer with a learning rate of $3 \times 10^{-4}$. Entropy coefficient ($\alpha$) is automatically updated.

This decoupled optimization strategy, combined with dual-level entropy regularization and intrinsic rewards, aims to stabilize learning and enable effective long-horizon planning in sparse-reward continuous control environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted a comprehensive evaluation of the HRL-SAC framework against a standard (flat) SAC baseline over 1000 training episodes and on a held-out test set using the SAR-2 dataset. The performance was assessed across several key metrics: cumulative extrinsic reward, target detection success rate, search area coverage ratio, and episode length.

#### 4.1. Training Performance Comparison (HRL-SAC vs. Standard SAC)

The HRL-SAC framework consistently demonstrated superior performance across all measured training metrics compared to the standard SAC baseline.
- **Extrinsic Reward:** HRL-SAC achieved a significantly higher average extrinsic reward, with a 10-episode moving average peaking around 421,330, which is approximately 3.04 times higher than the standard SAC's average of 138,547. The reward curve for HRL-SAC also showed more rapid convergence and greater stability.
- **Success Rate:** HRL-SAC exhibited a higher target detection success rate, averaging 50.9%, which is about 1.77 times greater than the standard SAC's 28.7%. The HRL-SAC success rate curve showed higher consistency and less frequent drops.
- **Coverage Efficiency:** HRL-SAC achieved a substantially higher average search area coverage ratio of 20.0%, indicating 2.08 times more effective exploration compared to the standard SAC's 9.6%. HRL-SAC maintained a higher baseline and more frequent high-coverage episodes.
- **Episode Length:** HRL-SAC completed tasks in an average of 4,253 steps, which is approximately 11% shorter than the standard SAC's average of 4,803 steps, indicating greater efficiency.
- **Sample Efficiency:** The total interaction steps highlight HRL-SAC's superior sample efficiency. HRL-SAC required only 0.40 million environment steps to achieve its performance, whereas standard SAC needed 4.80 million steps for inferior results.

These results indicate that HRL-SAC's hierarchical decomposition, which combines meta-controller goal selection with low-level execution, enables more strategic search patterns, more efficient exploration, and stable convergence, aspects that the flat SAC policy struggled to replicate in the sparse-reward, long-horizon environment. The reduced variance and improved stability observed in HRL-SAC's reward and success rate distributions further support the effectiveness of hierarchical temporal abstraction and intrinsic reward shaping in mitigating credit assignment challenges.

#### 4.2. Test Results on SAR-2 Dataset

Evaluation on the held-out SAR-2 test set (199 images) further corroborated HRL-SAC's advantages:
- **Reward:** HRL-SAC achieved an average reward of 71,440.24, significantly outperforming standard SAC, which yielded a negative average reward of -365,453.61.
- **Success Rate:** HRL-SAC maintained a higher success rate of 40.0% on the test set, compared to standard SAC's 30.0%.
- **Coverage:** Standard SAC showed a slightly higher coverage of 0.123 on the test set compared to HRL-SAC's 0.078. The authors attribute this to the longer trajectory lengths of the flat model.
- **Episode Length:** HRL-SAC completed tasks with a significantly more efficient average trajectory length of 879 steps, whereas Standard SAC required an average of 8,054 steps. This demonstrates HRL-SAC's ability to achieve objectives in considerably fewer actions.

#### 4.3. Performance Insights

The key factors contributing to HRL-SAC's superior performance are identified as:
- **Efficiency and Convergence:** The hierarchical structure allows reasoning over extended horizons (K steps for the meta-controller), reducing gradient variance and leading to more stable learning.
- **Sparse Reward Handling:** The use of intrinsic rewards for the low-level policy provides a dense learning signal, effectively addressing the "delayed credit assignment" problem.
- **Exploration:** Entropy-regularized policies at both levels promote diverse exploration, preventing premature convergence to suboptimal or deterministic behaviors.

Overall, the empirical results consistently demonstrate that HRL-SAC effectively addresses the complexities of sparse-reward, long-horizon reinforcement learning by combining temporal abstraction with stable, entropy-regularized off-policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a Hierarchical Soft Actor-Critic (HRL-SAC) framework that offers a principled approach to tackling significant challenges in reinforcement learning: sparse rewards and long-horizon decision-making in continuous control environments. The work's significance and potential impact can be understood through its methodological advancements and practical implications.

#### 5.1. Methodological Advancements

- **Addressing Core RL Challenges:** The HRL-SAC framework directly confronts the "delayed credit assignment" and inefficient exploration problems that hinder the scalability of conventional deep RL algorithms in complex settings. By decomposing tasks into hierarchical sub-problems and introducing intrinsic rewards, it transforms sparse extrinsic signals into more frequent, structured learning cues.
- **Novel Integration of HRL and SAC:** The paper's primary contribution is the novel integration of entropy-regularized Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) optimization at both the high-level (meta-controller) and low-level controllers within a continuous action space. This dual-level entropy regularization distinguishes it from prior HRL methods and flat SAC extensions, providing a theoretically grounded solution that combines SAC's stability and exploration benefits with the advantages of hierarchical credit assignment without relying on auxiliary rewards or extensive architectural tuning.
- **Improved Learning Stability and Sample Efficiency:** The empirical results demonstrate that HRL-SAC achieves more stable learning curves, faster convergence, and significantly higher sample efficiency compared to the flat SAC baseline. This is attributed to the reduction in effective planning horizons, denser intrinsic reward signals, and the variance reduction offered by hierarchical decomposition.
- **Robustness in Continuous Control:** By leveraging SAC, HRL-SAC maintains robustness in continuous action spaces, an area where many HRL methods have historically struggled due to instability and difficulties with off-policy learning.

#### 5.2. Practical Implications and Potential Impact

- **Autonomous Search and Rescue Operations:** The framework's evaluation in a simulated search-and-rescue (SAR) task highlights its direct applicability to real-world autonomous systems, such as drones for disaster relief. The demonstrated superior performance in target detection, search coverage, and efficiency suggests that HRL-SAC could enhance the capabilities of SAR drones, enabling them to operate more effectively in complex, unstructured, and resource-constrained environments where human intervention is limited.
- **Scalability to Complex Robotic Systems:** The ability to handle long-horizon tasks and continuous control with improved efficiency suggests that HRL-SAC could be a foundational component for developing intelligent agents for other complex robotic applications, including manipulation, navigation in dynamic environments, and multi-robot coordination.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The study lays the groundwork for several promising avenues in RL research.
 - **Adaptive Temporal Abstraction:** Future work could explore dynamic adjustment of sub-goal durations, allowing the meta-policy to adapt its temporal resolution based on task complexity, leading to even more efficient planning.
 - **Multi-Task and Transfer Learning:** The hierarchical structure inherently supports skill reuse, making HRL-SAC a strong candidate for multi-task and continual learning scenarios, where learned sub-goals or low-level policies could be transferred to novel tasks.
 - **Sim-to-Real Deployment:** Addressing the challenges of deploying HRL-SAC in real-world systems, including handling model mismatches, noisy observations, and ensuring robust control, is a crucial next step. This could involve exploring techniques like domain randomization and online adaptation.

In conclusion, this research significantly contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by providing a robust, efficient, and theoretically sound hierarchical framework that effectively overcomes long-standing challenges in sparse-reward, long-horizon continuous control. Its potential impact extends from advancing fundamental RL methodologies to enabling more capable autonomous systems in critical real-world applications.
