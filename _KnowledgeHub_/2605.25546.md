# Safety-Critical Whole-Body Control for Humanoid Robots via Input-to-State Safe Control Barrier Functions

> [!summary] Summary
> A hierarchical control framework utilizes Input-to-State Safe Control Barrier Functions (ISSf-CBFs) to enhance safety-critical whole-body control for humanoid robots by robustly transferring kinematic safety guarantees to full-order dynamics. The approach, validated through simulations with model mismatch and on a physical TOCABI robot, ensures collision avoidance and stability despite unknown disturbances and hardware uncertainties.

## Problem

- Operating humanoid robots safely in complex, human-centered environments requires robust control that handles joint limits, self-collision, and obstacle avoidance.
- Traditional safety-critical control methods like standard Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) often degrade safety guarantees when faced with unknown disturbances, such as model uncertainties or tracking errors.
- Kinematic safety assurances established at higher control levels do not reliably transfer to the full-order robot dynamics, leading to potential safety violations in real-world scenarios.

## Method

![[2605.25546-method.png]]
*The proposed hierarchical safety-critical whole-body control framework, integrating kinematic task prioritization, an ISSf-CBF safety filter, and a dynamic-level controller.*

- A hierarchical safety-critical whole-body control framework was developed, integrating a kinematic-level controller (KinWBC), an ISSf-CBF safety filter, and a dynamic-level controller (DynWBC).
- The ISSf-CBF safety filter minimally modifies nominal joint-velocity references from KinWBC to enforce kinematic safety, treating the discrepancy between a Reduced-Order Model (ROM) and the Full-Order Model (FOM) as a bounded disturbance.
- The DynWBC then computes joint torques based on these filtered references, ensuring full-body dynamic feasibility and contact stability through a Quadratic Program (QP) formulation.

## Results

- Simulations with a **20%** mass mismatch in the humanoid robot model demonstrated that the ISSf-CBF filter consistently maintained collision-free behavior, while standard CBF and eCBF baselines exhibited collision ratios of approximately **50%**.
- Real-robot experiments on the TOCABI humanoid confirmed robust contact stability and enforcement of kinematic workspace-boundary constraints during single-leg balancing (TAICHI motion) under inherent hardware uncertainties.
- During teleoperation, the ISSf-CBF filter successfully prevented robot-obstacle collisions in real-time by modifying hand motions, with the effectiveness being sensitive to the parameter `ε`.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ISSf-CBFs can effectively compensate for bounded disturbances arising from ROM-FOM discrepancies, ensuring forward invariance of an enlarged safe set and thus reliably transferring kinematic safety to full dynamics.
> - Formulating safety constraints on a simplified kinematic ROM and modeling the ROM-FOM mismatch as an input disturbance allows for a practical and robust safety-transfer strategy.
> - Careful, conservative tuning of ISSf-CBF parameters (specifically `ε` and `α`) is crucial for achieving superior safety assurance without introducing excessive jitter or compromising task performance.
