# Affordances from Human Videos as a Versatile Representation for Robotics

> [!summary] Summary
> A new method, Vision-Robotics Bridge (VRB), enables robots to learn object interaction skills by extracting actionable affordance representations (contact points and post-contact trajectories) directly from large-scale human videos. The approach successfully integrates with and enhances various robot learning paradigms, demonstrating improved performance across diverse real-world tasks and robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to acquire an intuitive, human-like understanding of how to interact with novel objects in unstructured environments by observing human behavior.
- Traditional robot learning methods are sample-inefficient and require extensive, costly robot-specific data collection.
- A significant gap exists between advancements in computer vision (understanding human interactions) and their practical, actionable application in real-world robotics.

## Method

- **VRB** (Vision-Robotics Bridge) defines ==affordances== as robot-centric ==contact points== and ==post-contact trajectories==, a representation that directly maps to robot end-effector control and is morphologically agnostic to human-robot differences.
- Develops a ==data-driven pipeline== to learn these affordances from large-scale ==egocentric human video== datasets (e.g., Epic-Kitchens) by extracting contact events and trajectories, using a ==ResNet visual encoder== with ==deconvolutional heatmap heads== and a ==Transformer trajectory predictor== trained under ==L2 loss==.
- Mitigates ==visual domain shift== by processing human video data to create robot-like visual inputs and integrating the learned affordance model into various robot learning paradigms (==imitation learning==, ==reward-free exploration==, ==goal-conditioned learning==, and a ==discretized DQN action space==).

## Results

- VRB achieved an average success rate of **57%** across **8 real-world tasks** for offline imitation learning, significantly outperforming state-of-the-art baselines (best baseline **25%**).
- It led to **3x to 10x** improvements in 'coincidental success' during reward-free exploration, guiding robots to more meaningful interactions.
- The method was successfully deployed and evaluated on both Franka Emika Panda and Hello Stretch mobile manipulator robots, performing diverse manipulation tasks in lab and 'in-the-wild' environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robot-agnostic contact points and post-contact trajectories derived from human videos provide a versatile, actionable representation for robotic manipulation.
> - Large-scale passive human video datasets can serve as a rich and scalable source for learning generalizable interaction skills, circumventing the need for expensive robot-specific data.
> - The learned affordance representation can effectively bootstrap and enhance multiple robot learning paradigms, including offline imitation, reward-free exploration, goal-conditioned learning, and defining structured action spaces.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work operates at the intersection of computer vision, robot learning, and embodied AI, addressing a critical and long-standing challenge: enabling robots to understand and interact with the world by observing human behavior. The concept of "affordances," coined by psychologist J.J. Gibson, refers to the actionable possibilities that an environment offers to an organism. In robotics, understanding affordances means knowing *how* an object can be interacted with (e.g., "this handle affords pulling," "this button affords pressing").

Historically, computer vision research has made significant strides in recognizing objects, segmenting scenes, and even predicting human-object interactions (HOI) from videos. However, much of this progress has been evaluated on static datasets, with limited direct translation to physical robots operating in complex, unstructured environments. The field of robot learning, conversely, often grapples with sample inefficiency and the "tabula rasa" problem, where robots must learn new skills from scratch with minimal prior knowledge, leading to slow and costly training, especially in the real world.

Previous attempts to bridge this gap include:
- **Geometry-based affordance prediction:** Relying on 3D scene understanding, which requires specialized datasets and can be brittle.
- **Semantic interaction segmentation:** Training models to identify interacting objects, often limited to specific categories.
- **End-to-end visuomotor policies:** Learning direct mapping from pixels to actions, which are highly sample-inefficient for continuous control.
- **Learning from demonstrations (LfD):** Human teleoperation or kinesthetic teaching, which is effective but tedious and difficult to scale.
- **Pre-trained visual representations:** Using models like R3M to provide better visual features for robot learning, but these don't inherently provide *actionable* interaction knowledge.

This paper distinguishes itself by specifically leveraging the rich, implicit "how-to" knowledge embedded in large-scale *human videos* (e.g., egocentric datasets like Epic-Kitchens) to learn robot-agnostic, actionable affordances. It focuses on deriving a versatile representation that can bootstrap *multiple* robot learning paradigms, moving beyond simply pre-training visual encoders or directly mimicking human movements (which are morphologically incompatible with robots). By grounding affordances in robot-centric contact points and trajectories, the work seeks to make human observation truly *actionable* for diverse robotic tasks, thereby directly addressing the current gap between vision understanding and robot deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this research is to enable robots to acquire a human-like understanding of how to interact with objects and environments, solely by observing human behavior in videos. The authors argue that this "actionable knowledge" is crucial for robots to operate efficiently and effectively in novel, real-world settings, similar to how humans instinctively understand object affordances in a new kitchen.

The paper articulates three key objectives to achieve this:

1. **Define an actionable representation for affordances:** Moving beyond abstract semantic labels or heatmaps, the goal is to define a representation that a robot can directly convert into physical actions. The challenge lies in identifying a representation that is generalizable across different objects and interaction types, yet specific enough for robot control.

2. **Develop a data-driven and scalable method to learn this representation:** Traditional robot learning requires massive amounts of robot-specific interaction data, which is expensive and time-consuming to collect. The objective is to leverage readily available, large-scale passive human video datasets (e.g., internet videos, egocentric videos) as a rich source of supervisory signals, even in the presence of visual domain shifts (human vs. robot perspectives, human body presence).

3. **Demonstrate seamless integration and deployment across various robot learning paradigms:** The learned affordance model should not be limited to a single robotic task or learning algorithm. Instead, it should act as a versatile prior that can accelerate and enhance different approaches, including offline imitation learning, reward-free exploration, goal-conditioned learning, and defining structured action spaces for reinforcement learning. This objective aims to prove the *generality* and *utility* of the proposed affordance representation.

Ultimately, the paper aims to bridge the significant gap between advancements in computer vision (understanding human interactions) and practical applications in real-world robotics, providing a "Vision-Robotics Bridge" (VRB) that can fundamentally change how robots acquire skills.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach, Vision-Robotics Bridge (VRB), centers on learning a robot-centric affordance representation from human videos and then seamlessly integrating it into various robot learning frameworks.

#### 3.1. Actionable Representation for Affordances

VRB defines affordances using two primary components:
- **Contact points (c):** Where a human's hand makes initial contact with an object.
- **Post-contact trajectories (τ):** The sequence of movements of the hand *after* initial contact, indicating how the object is manipulated.
This representation is chosen because it directly maps to robot end-effector control (reach to a point, perform an action, move along a trajectory), making it morphologically agnostic to human-robot differences. It also inherently captures the multi-modality of interactions (e.g., a cup can be picked up from different points for different purposes).

#### 3.2. Learning Affordances from Egocentric Videos

The core of VRB's learning mechanism involves extracting and processing information from large-scale egocentric human video datasets (specifically, Epic-Kitchens-100).
- **Data Extraction Pipeline:**
 1. **Identify contact events:** Using an off-the-shelf hand-object detection model (e.g., 100 DOH annotations), the system identifies frames where human hands make contact with objects.
 2. **Extract contact points (c):** At the first contact timestep, skin color segmentation and bounding box intersection are used to identify individual contact points around the hand. To capture multi-modality, a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) is fitted to these points, and its means ($\mu_k$) become the target for contact point prediction.
 3. **Extract post-contact trajectories (τ):** The 2D bounding box positions of the human hand from the first contact frame onwards define the post-contact trajectory.
- **Egomotion Compensation:** To account for camera movement (e.g., a person moving closer to an object), homography matrices are used to project extracted contact points and trajectories back into the coordinate system of a reference frame (typically the first human-less frame).
- **Addressing Human-Robot Visual Domain Shift:** A crucial step is to train the affordance model on "robot-like" inputs. This is achieved by mapping the human-derived affordances back to the initial frame *before* the human enters the scene. If a human is always present, they are cropped out, or the frame is discarded. This ensures the robot's visual input (a scene without a human hand) matches the training conditions.
- **Affordance Model Training:**
 - The model (f_θ) uses a ResNet backbone (g_conv_θ) as a visual encoder.
 - For contact points, deconvolutional layers (g_deconv_θ) project the latent representation into K heatmaps, with a spatial softmax yielding the estimated GMM means ($\hat{\mu}_k$).
 - For trajectories, a Transformer encoder (T_θ) predicts relative shifts for post-contact waypoints.
 - Training uses L2 loss for both contact point and trajectory predictions. Local crops around detected interaction regions are used as input to improve generalization and handle uncertainty.

#### 3.3. Robot Learning from Visual Affordances (Deployment Paradigms)

VRB's versatility is demonstrated by integrating its affordance model into four distinct robot learning paradigms:
1. **Imitation Learning from Offline Data Collection:** VRB guides the robot to explore and collect a dataset of "interesting" interactions. This dataset, comprising images and corresponding (c, τ) actions, is then used for standard offline imitation learning via k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN) or Behavior Cloning (BC) to achieve goal-specified tasks.
2. **Reward-Free Exploration:** VRB bootstraps intrinsic exploration by guiding the robot to interact with parts of the scene likely to be of human interest. Trajectories are ranked by "environment change" (how much objects move), and a distribution is fitted to the (c, τ) of the most exploratory trajectories. This elite distribution is then sampled alongside VRB's predictions for subsequent data collection, progressively improving exploration.
3. **Goal-Conditioned Learning:** Similar to exploration, but trajectories are ranked based on their visual similarity to a specified goal image. VRB's guidance helps the robot efficiently discover behaviors that lead to desired goal states.
4. **Affordance as an Action Space:** The continuous (c, τ) output space of VRB is discretized. Multiple queries of VRB on a scene are used to fit GMMs to the predicted contact points and trajectories, creating a finite set of (c, τ) pairs. This transforms the complex continuous control problem into a discrete action space, making it amenable to discrete reinforcement learning algorithms like Deep Q-Networks (DQN).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of VRB is extensive and rigorous, spanning multiple real-world environments, tasks, and robotic platforms, representing hundreds of hours of robot runtime.

- **Robotics Setup:** Experiments were conducted on two platforms: the Franka Emika Panda arm (in two distinct play kitchen environments for tasks like cabinet opening, knife/vegetable manipulation) and the Hello Stretch mobile manipulator (for "in-the-wild" tasks such as opening garbage cans, doors, drawers, and dishwashers). A simulation environment (Franka-Kitchen benchmark) was also used for initial evaluations.
- **Baselines:** VRB was compared against state-of-the-art human hand-object affordance models: Hotspots, Hands as Probes (HAP), and HOI, as well as a "Random" baseline.
- **Performance Across Paradigms:**
 - **Imitation Learning (Offline Data):** VRB significantly outperformed baselines. In k-NN and Behavior Cloning, VRB achieved an average success rate of 57% across 8 real-world tasks, compared to 25% for the best baseline (Hotspots). This indicates that data collected under VRB's guidance is of substantially higher quality and relevance for imitation learning. VRB also showed better performance for grasping held-out "rare" objects.
 - **Reward-Free Exploration:** VRB led to consistent improvements in "coincidental success" (achieving goal configurations without explicit reward) by 3x to 10x across tasks, compared to HAP and Random exploration. This demonstrates VRB's effectiveness in guiding robots toward meaningful interactions in unstructured exploration.
 - **Goal-Conditioned Learning:** VRB enabled faster learning and better final success rates for reaching specified goal configurations across six diverse tasks, outperforming HAP and Random sampling.
 - **Affordance as an Action Space:** When VRB's outputs were used to define a discrete action space for a DQN agent, the robot achieved more successful interactions. This highlights that VRB identifies meaningful actions, improving the efficiency of RL in complex continuous control settings.
- **Analyzing Visual Representations:**
 - **Finetuning:** When VRB's learned visual encoder was frozen and used as a pre-trained representation for behavior cloning, it outperformed R3M on three simulated Franka tasks, suggesting that VRB's representations are highly relevant for robotic control.
 - **Feature Space Distance:** The distance in VRB's feature space between the current and goal image monotonically decreased during successful task execution (e.g., door opening), a strong correlation indicating that the learned representations capture meaningful progress towards a goal, unlike R3M.
- **Failure Mode Analysis:** While all methods failed at times, VRB produced a higher number of successful trajectories and significantly more "partial successes" (e.g., object moved but not fully to goal) compared to baselines, implying that VRB's interactions are more often physically meaningful and productive.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Affordances from Human Videos as a Versatile Representation for Robotics" paper marks a significant step forward in enabling robots to learn complex manipulation skills by leveraging readily available human observational data. Its contributions have several far-reaching implications:

- **Bridging the Vision-Robotics Gap:** The most direct impact is the successful demonstration of translating visual understanding of human interactions into actionable knowledge for real robots. By explicitly defining affordances as robot-centric contact points and post-contact trajectories, the authors provide a practical framework that transcends the traditional limitations of either pure vision or pure robot control approaches.
- **Scalability and Data Efficiency:** This work offers a highly scalable approach to robot skill acquisition. By learning from passive, large-scale human video datasets (like Epic-Kitchens), it bypasses the need for expensive, time-consuming, and robot-specific data collection, which has been a major bottleneck in real-world robotics. This democratization of data sources can accelerate research and development in robot learning.
- **Versatility Across Learning Paradigms:** The demonstration that VRB can bootstrap and enhance four distinct robot learning paradigms (imitation, exploration, goal-conditioned learning, structured action spaces) underscores its versatility and general utility. This suggests that VRB is not a task-specific solution but a foundational representation that can benefit a wide array of robotic applications.
- **Robustness to Real-World Challenges:** The extensive evaluation on two different robotic platforms, across diverse tasks, and critically, "in-the-wild" environments outside controlled lab settings, attests to the robustness and practical applicability of VRB. This is a crucial step towards deploying intelligent robots in unstructured human environments.
- **Improved Robot Autonomy:** By providing robots with an inherent understanding of how to interact with novel objects, VRB reduces the "tabula rasa" problem, enabling robots to explore more efficiently and learn new tasks faster with minimal supervision. This moves closer to truly autonomous and adaptive robotic systems.
- **Foundational for Future Research:** The success of VRB opens avenues for future research into more complex, multi-stage tasks, the integration of other physical concepts (force, tactile feedback), and further development of visual representations that are explicitly optimized for robotic control. It establishes a strong baseline for how passive human observation can directly inform and improve robot behavior.

In conclusion, VRB represents a powerful and practical framework for equipping robots with human-like intuition about object manipulation. By turning passive human videos into active robot knowledge, this research brings us closer to a future where robots can seamlessly integrate into our daily lives, intelligently interacting with the objects around them.
