# Preference Distillation via Value based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Teacher Value-based Knowledge Distillation (TVKD) introduces a method for aligning small language models (SLMs) with human preferences by extracting soft reward labels from a DPO-trained teacher's value function. This approach, which applies potential-based reward shaping, consistently improves SLM performance on preference benchmarks while maintaining computational efficiency in an offline setting.

## Problem

- Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) provides coarse, binary feedback at the sequence level, making it less effective for training small language models (SLMs) with limited capacity.
- Existing preference distillation methods often mimic the teacher's output behavior rather than its underlying reward structure, which can lead to compounding errors and conflict with the primary DPO objective.
- There is a need for a theoretically sound and computationally efficient mechanism to transfer nuanced preference understanding from large teacher models to smaller student models.

## Method

- **TVKD** integrates soft reward labels derived from a ==DPO-trained teacher== model's value function into the standard DPO objective for the student model.
- The teacher's value function is formulated as a state-dependent auxiliary reward, satisfying the conditions for Potential-based Reward Shaping (PBRS) to ensure policy invariance.
- The method operates in a fully offline manner, pre-computing teacher logits to derive the value function, thus avoiding additional student rollouts or online interactions.

## Results

- TVKD consistently outperforms various baseline distillation methods and DPO variants across major evaluation benchmarks, including RM scores, MT-Bench ratings, AlpacaEval win rates, and the Open LLM Leaderboard.
- The method achieves these performance improvements in a fully offline training setting, demonstrating significant computational efficiency compared to online distillation approaches.
- Empirical analysis validates the theoretical principles of PBRS, showing that state-dependent shaping maintains consistency with preference data, while action-dependent shaping leads to performance degradation and lower margin accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A DPO-trained policy can be interpreted as a soft-optimal solution to a token-level Markov Decision Process (MDP), allowing its soft value function to serve as a proxy for the teacher's implicit reward modeling.
> - Potential-based Reward Shaping (PBRS) provides a robust theoretical guarantee that auxiliary reward signals will not alter the optimal policy of the underlying MDP, preventing conflicts with the DPO objective.
> - Distilling the teacher's value function offers fine-grained, soft supervision that transfers a deeper understanding of 'goodness' from the teacher, going beyond simple output behavior imitation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in the rapidly evolving field of language model alignment: effectively transferring human preferences to smaller, more efficient models. The broader landscape is dominated by two primary paradigms for aligning LLMs with human preferences:

- **Reinforcement Learning with Human Feedback (RLHF):** This involves training a reward model to predict human preferences, then using policy gradient methods (e.g., PPO) to fine-tune the LLM based on the reward model's feedback. While effective, RLHF is computationally expensive, complex, and can be unstable.
- **Direct Preference Optimization (DPO):** Introduced as an offline alternative to RLHF, DPO directly optimizes the language model policy using pairwise comparisons of preferred and dispreferred responses, without requiring an explicit reward model or additional rollouts. DPO has gained significant traction due to its efficiency and simplicity.

However, DPO, despite its advantages, faces limitations. Its supervision is based on binary "win-or-lose" signals over entire sequences, offering coarse-grained feedback that doesn't convey the *magnitude* or *quality* of preference. This limitation is particularly acute when training **small language models (SLMs)**, which have limited capacity to infer nuanced preferences from such sparse signals.

To overcome DPO's limitations for SLMs, **preference distillation** methods have emerged. These methods leverage knowledge from larger, well-aligned "teacher" models to guide the training of smaller "student" models. Prior distillation approaches typically fall into two categories:
1. **Behavior Cloning/KL-divergence based:** These methods attempt to mimic the teacher's output distribution (e.g., SeqKD, DCKD) or use KL-divergence as a regularization term. The paper argues that these often focus on imitating current behavior and overlook distilling the teacher's *internal reward modeling*.
2. **Online Rollout based:** Methods like PaLD, DPKD, or ADPA utilize teacher or student rollouts to generate additional feedback, which can be computationally intensive and suffer from stability issues.

The proposed **Teacher Value-based Knowledge Distillation (TVKD)** method distinguishes itself by explicitly aiming to distill the teacher's *reward modeling* rather than just its output behavior. It innovatively bridges the gap between preference optimization and classical reinforcement learning theory by applying **potential-based reward shaping (PBRS)**. PBRS is a well-established technique in RL that allows auxiliary rewards to be added to an environment without altering the optimal policy, thereby ensuring theoretical soundness. By leveraging the teacher model's *value function* as a proxy for its implicit reward model and formulating it as a PBRS term, TVKD provides a fine-grained, soft guidance signal that is theoretically guaranteed not to conflict with the original DPO objective. This approach marks a significant theoretical and practical advancement in preference distillation, offering a robust and efficient way to enhance SLM alignment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind this research are:

1. **Address the limitations of DPO for Small Language Models (SLMs):** DPO's binary, sequence-level preference signals are too coarse for SLMs, which struggle to extract fine-grained guidance from such sparse feedback due to their limited capacity. The authors aim to provide more informative supervision.
2. **Overcome shortcomings of existing preference distillation methods:** Prior methods often focus on mimicking the teacher's *behavior* (e.g., via KL-divergence or behavior cloning) rather than its *underlying reward structure*. This can lead to compounding errors, poor generalization, and potential conflicts with the primary DPO objective of aligning with human preferences. The authors highlight that simply integrating KL-divergence can cause misalignment between following the teacher and optimizing for human preferences.
3. **Introduce a theoretically sound mechanism for teacher guidance:** The authors seek to provide soft, fine-grained reward signals from a large teacher model without interfering with the DPO's fundamental reward structure or altering its global optimal policy. This implies a need for a mechanism with strong theoretical guarantees.
4. **Develop an efficient and practical distillation framework:** The goal is to integrate teacher information into DPO training without requiring expensive additional rollouts or online interactions, thereby maintaining the computational efficiency inherent in DPO.

In essence, the authors are motivated to create a more effective, theoretically grounded, and computationally efficient method for preference distillation, enabling SLMs to achieve better alignment with human preferences by leveraging the deep "understanding" of preferences encoded within a teacher model's value function.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Teacher Value-based Knowledge Distillation (TVKD)**, integrates soft reward labels from a DPO-trained teacher model into the standard DPO objective. The core idea is to leverage the teacher's implicit reward modeling through its value function, formulated in a way that satisfies potential-based reward shaping (PBRS).

The methodology can be broken down as follows:

1. **Token-level Markov Decision Process (MDP) Formulation:** The authors frame language generation as a token-level MDP, where states are sequences of tokens generated so far, actions are the next tokens in the vocabulary, and rewards are associated with state-action pairs. This allows for the application of reinforcement learning concepts. A discount factor $\gamma=1$ is fixed.
2. **Value-based Reinforcement Learning (MaxEnt RL) Context:** The paper briefly reviews MaxEnt RL, where the optimal policy is a Boltzmann distribution over Q-values, and the optimal state value function satisfies a soft Bellman consistency condition ($V^*(s) = \log \sum_a \exp(Q^*(s,a))$).
3. **DPO Revisit:** DPO directly optimizes the log-ratio margin between preferred ($y_w$) and dispreferred ($y_l$) responses for a prompt ($x$), using a reference policy $\pi_{ref}$. This effectively learns an implicit reward model without explicit reward modeling.
4. **Soft Value Function of a DPO-trained Teacher (Lemma 1):** Building on prior work by Rafailov et al., the authors interpret a DPO-trained policy ($\pi_\phi$) as a soft-optimal solution to a token-level MDP. The token-level logits $Q_\phi(s, a)$ are viewed as soft Q-values. The key insight here is that the teacher's soft value function $V_\phi(s)$ can be directly computed from these logits as $V_\phi(s) = \beta \log \sum_{a \in V} \exp(Q_\phi(s, a)/\beta)$. This function serves as a proxy for the teacher's internal reward modeling, indicating the "value" of being in a particular state (sequence prefix).
5. **Reward Shaping with Teacher Value (Lemma 2):** TVKD introduces an auxiliary reward term based on the teacher's value function: $\psi(s, a) = V_\phi(s') - V_\phi(s)$, where $s'$ is the next state after taking action $a$ from state $s$. This term captures the change in the expected future return from the teacher's perspective. Crucially, this specific formulation satisfies the conditions for **Potential-based Reward Shaping (PBRS)**, theoretically guaranteeing that adding this term to the original DPO reward **preserves the optimal policy** of the underlying MDP. This means the shaping guides learning without altering the fundamental goal of DPO.
6. **Failure of Action-dependent Shaping (Corollary 2.1):** The paper explicitly highlights why simpler, action-dependent shaping terms (e.g., using $\log \pi_\phi(a|s)$ or $Q_\phi(s,a)$ directly) would violate policy invariance. These terms do not telescope and cancel out over trajectories, potentially distorting the optimal policy. This rigorous analysis provides a strong theoretical justification for their chosen PBRS formulation.
7. **TVKD Loss Formulation:** The auxiliary reward term is integrated into the DPO objective. The resulting TVKD loss function modifies the log-ratio margin by incorporating the PBRS term. This allows the DPO objective to account not just for a binary win/loss, but also for *how much* better a preferred sequence is, based on the teacher's value function.
 The final loss integrates the $\psi_\phi(s,a)$ term into the log-probability ratios in the DPO objective:
 $L_{TVKD}(\pi_\theta, D; \pi_\phi) = -E_{(\tau_w, \tau_l) \sim D} \left[ \log \sigma \left( \beta \sum_{t} \log \frac{\pi_\theta(a^w_t | s^w_t)}{\exp\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta} \psi_\phi(s^w_t, a^w_t)\right)} - \beta \sum_{t} \log \frac{\pi_\theta(a^l_t | s^l_t)}{\exp\left(\frac{\alpha}{\beta} \psi_\phi(s^l_t, a^l_t)\right)} \right) \right]$.
 Here, $\alpha$ is a hyperparameter controlling distillation strength.
8. **Gradient Analysis:** The gradient of the TVKD loss is shown to encourage increasing log-probabilities of tokens in preferred trajectories and decreasing them in dispreferred ones, modulated by a margin that includes both student likelihoods and teacher-derived shaping. This blend provides a soft, value-aware supervision.
9. **Offline Training:** A key practical advantage is that TVKD requires no additional rollouts. The teacher's logits (from which the value function is derived) can be pre-computed on the existing offline DPO dataset, making it compatible with standard DPO training frameworks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results consistently demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of TVKD across various benchmarks and model configurations.

- **Consistent Performance Improvements:** TVKD consistently outperforms all baseline methods, including other knowledge distillation approaches (VanillaKD, DCKD, SeqKD, DPKD, GKD, DDPO, ADPA) and DPO variants (SimPO, WPO, TDPO), across major evaluation benchmarks:
 - **RM (Fsfair-LLaMA3-RM-V0.1):** TVKD achieves higher reward model scores, indicating better alignment with human preferences.
 - **MT-Bench:** TVKD yields higher average MT-Bench ratings, signifying improved instruction-following and overall response quality evaluated by GPT-4o-mini. It shows particular strengths in "Writing" and "Roleplay" tasks, inheriting the teacher's abilities, and also excels in "STEM" and "Extraction" where DPO traditionally has an advantage.
 - **AlpacaEval:** TVKD achieves the highest win rates against ADPA, a strong baseline, in pairwise comparisons, highlighting its ability to generate superior responses.
 - **Open LLM Leaderboard (OLL):** TVKD demonstrates competitive or superior performance, indicating good generalization across a variety of general language understanding and reasoning tasks.
- **Offline Efficiency:** A significant finding is that TVKD achieves these strong results in a fully offline setting, requiring no additional student rollouts or online evaluation by the teacher. This makes it computationally efficient and more stable than online distillation methods.
- **Robustness Across Model Sizes and Architectures:** TVKD shows strong generalization when distilling from different teacher models (Mistral-7B, LLaMA-3.1-8B) to student models of various sizes (Danube3-500M, LLaMA-3.2-1B, LLaMA-3.2-3B). It even improves performance for very compact models like Danube-500M.
- **Qualitative Evidence of Reward Modeling Distillation:** Visualization of shaping terms shows that TVKD-trained students select conceptually central and semantically meaningful tokens that align with those chosen by the DPO teacher, demonstrating successful inheritance of the teacher's internal reward modeling.
- **Validation of Soft Labels and PBRS:**
 - **Value Function Analysis:** The shaping term derived from the teacher's value function achieves higher accuracy in identifying preferred trajectories compared to raw or length-normalized log-probabilities, suggesting it provides a more reliable sequence-level soft label.
 - **Value Alignment Analysis:** TVKD-trained students exhibit lower KL-divergence with the teacher's value function compared to DPO-trained students, indicating better implicit modeling of the teacher's reward structure.
 - **Reward Conflict Analysis:** Empirical results confirm the theoretical guarantee of PBRS. State-dependent auxiliary reward functions (satisfying PBRS) maintain high "margin accuracy" (consistency with preference data), while action-dependent functions (violating PBRS) lead to lower margin accuracy and inferior performance, validating the importance of policy invariance.
- **Hyperparameter Robustness:** Ablation studies show that TVKD is relatively robust to the distillation strength parameter ($\alpha$) and temperature parameter ($\beta$), with optimal performance achieved within reasonable ranges.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The contributions of "Preference Distillation via Value-based Reinforcement Learning" hold significant theoretical and practical implications for the field of LLM alignment and deployment:

1. **Enabling Effective SLM Alignment:** The most direct impact is providing a highly effective method for aligning small language models (SLMs) with human preferences. As the demand for efficient, deployable LLMs grows, techniques like TVKD are crucial for making advanced alignment capabilities accessible to models with limited capacity, especially in resource-constrained environments or for specialized, domain-specific SLMs.
2. **Novel Integration of RL Theory:** The work innovatively applies the well-established theoretical concept of Potential-based Reward Shaping (PBRS) from classical reinforcement learning to the modern challenge of LLM preference distillation. This demonstrates a powerful way to integrate deep theoretical guarantees into complex deep learning systems, ensuring that auxiliary guidance signals do not inadvertently distort the primary learning objective.
3. **Distilling Reward Modeling, Not Just Behavior:** By focusing on distilling the teacher's *value function* as a proxy for its internal reward model, TVKD moves beyond simple behavior cloning. This approach aims to transfer a deeper understanding of "goodness" and preference from the teacher, which is likely to lead to better generalization and more robust alignment than merely imitating output distributions.
4. **Computational Efficiency:** The fully offline nature of TVKD, requiring no additional rollouts during training, is a major practical advantage. It significantly reduces computational cost and complexity compared to online distillation methods, making preference distillation more scalable and environmentally friendly.
5. **Robustness and Generalizability:** The consistent performance across various benchmarks, student model sizes, and teacher architectures underscores TVKD's robustness and general applicability. This suggests it can be a reliable tool for diverse preference alignment tasks.
6. **Guidance for Future Research:** This paper opens new avenues for research into more sophisticated ways of distilling reward signals and leveraging theoretical RL principles for LLM training. It suggests that a deeper understanding of the implicit reward models within DPO-trained LLMs can be harnessed to unlock more effective alignment strategies. The empirical validation of PBRS principles also highlights the importance of theoretical rigor in designing distillation techniques.

In conclusion, TVKD offers a theoretically sound, highly effective, and computationally efficient method for preference distillation, particularly beneficial for small language models. Its novel application of value-based reward shaping provides a robust pathway to imbue SLMs with nuanced human preferences, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in resource-efficient LLM deployment.
