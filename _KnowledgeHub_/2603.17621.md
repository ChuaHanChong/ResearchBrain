# Complementary Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Complementary Reinforcement Learning introduces a framework where a policy actor and an experience extractor co-evolve to dynamically leverage historical experience in LLM-based agents. This approach yields consistent performance improvements across various tasks, achieving up to a 1.3x performance margin and requiring 1.5x to 2x fewer actions while effectively mitigating sample inefficiency and experience misalignment.

## Problem

- LLM-based reinforcement learning agents face sample inefficiency due to sparse outcome feedback, limiting effective learning from multi-turn interactions.
- Existing methods that leverage historical experience often rely on static or non-adaptive experience, leading to misalignment as the actor's capabilities evolve.
- Valuable procedural information from collected rollouts, such as effective behaviors and failure patterns, remains largely unexploited.

## Method

- A dual-model framework where a policy actor and an experience extractor are jointly optimized in a ==co-evolutionary loop==, with the extractor dynamically distilling and maintaining a structured ==experience bank==.
- An ==asynchronous training framework== coordinates rollout collection, experience distillation, and optimization through a centralized **ExperienceManager** to ensure efficiency and prevent blocking latency.
- The actor is optimized using a modified ==GRPO== objective with ==condition-wise advantage estimation== (for experience-guided and experience-free rollouts) to stabilize training and prevent over-reliance on external guidance.

## Results

- Complementary RL consistently outperformed baselines across MiniHack, WebShop, ALFWorld, and SWE-Bench, achieving up to a **1.3x** performance margin and a **+3.0%** gain on SWE-Bench.
- Tasks were completed more efficiently, with approximately **1.5x** fewer actions on MiniHack Room and **2x** fewer actions on ALFWorld.
- In multi-task settings, the framework consistently outperformed baselines (average **+7%**) and demonstrated robust scalability, showing a **+8.1%** gain in a six-task mixture compared to a three-task mixture.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic co-evolution between the policy actor and an experience extractor is essential to overcome 'stale experience' and sample inefficiency, as static experience offers diminishing returns.
> - Decoupling actor training from experience distillation and management in an asynchronous architecture allows for efficient and scalable integration of experience without significant latency.
> - Normalizing advantages specifically within experience-guided and experience-free rollouts during actor training significantly stabilizes the learning process and promotes robust policy development.
