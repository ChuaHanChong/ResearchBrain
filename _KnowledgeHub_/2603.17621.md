# Complementary Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Complementary Reinforcement Learning introduces a framework where a policy actor and an experience extractor co-evolve to dynamically leverage historical experience in LLM-based agents. This approach yields consistent performance improvements across various tasks, achieving up to a 1.3x performance margin and requiring 1.5x to 2x fewer actions while effectively mitigating sample inefficiency and experience misalignment.

## Problem

- LLM-based reinforcement learning agents face sample inefficiency due to sparse outcome feedback, limiting effective learning from multi-turn interactions.
- Existing methods that leverage historical experience often rely on static or non-adaptive experience, leading to misalignment as the actor's capabilities evolve.
- Valuable procedural information from collected rollouts, such as effective behaviors and failure patterns, remains largely unexploited.

## Method

- A dual-model framework where a policy actor and an experience extractor are jointly optimized in a ==co-evolutionary loop==, with the extractor dynamically distilling and maintaining a structured ==experience bank==.
- An ==asynchronous training framework== coordinates rollout collection, experience distillation, and optimization through a centralized **ExperienceManager** to ensure efficiency and prevent blocking latency.
- The actor is optimized using a modified ==GRPO== objective with ==condition-wise advantage estimation== (for experience-guided and experience-free rollouts) to stabilize training and prevent over-reliance on external guidance.

## Results

- Complementary RL consistently outperformed baselines across MiniHack, WebShop, ALFWorld, and SWE-Bench, achieving up to a **1.3x** performance margin and a **+3.0%** gain on SWE-Bench.
- Tasks were completed more efficiently, with approximately **1.5x** fewer actions on MiniHack Room and **2x** fewer actions on ALFWorld.
- In multi-task settings, the framework consistently outperformed baselines (average **+7%**) and demonstrated robust scalability, showing a **+8.1%** gain in a six-task mixture compared to a three-task mixture.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic co-evolution between the policy actor and an experience extractor is essential to overcome 'stale experience' and sample inefficiency, as static experience offers diminishing returns.
> - Decoupling actor training from experience distillation and management in an asynchronous architecture allows for efficient and scalable integration of experience without significant latency.
> - Normalizing advantages specifically within experience-guided and experience-free rollouts during actor training significantly stabilizes the learning process and promotes robust policy development.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has been applied to enhance the capabilities of Large Language Model (LLM)-based agents. However, a notable limitation in this domain is sample inefficiency, which arises from both sparse outcome feedback and the agents' inability to effectively leverage prior experience across episodes. Traditional outcome-based RL methods primarily rely on reward signals that indicate task success or failure, but do not provide explicit information regarding the intermediate steps or reasons for a trajectory's outcome. This leads to underutilization of procedural information embedded in interaction data, such as effective behaviors, failure patterns, or critical decision points.

Efforts to address this sample inefficiency have involved integrating historical experience to improve data utilization. This includes approaches that distill experience through self-reflection for in-context guidance, maintain auto-optimizing experience banks via specialized data structures, or employ dedicated models to distill and refine structured experience. Some works also focus on designing retrieval heuristics to surface relevant experience from accumulated banks.

A common challenge in these existing approaches is the static nature of the experience leveraged. Experience is often treated as a fixed resource, or extracted by non-adaptive mechanisms that do not evolve alongside the agent's improving policy. This can lead to a progressive misalignment where the distilled experience becomes less relevant and potentially counterproductive as the agent's capabilities advance. The proposed Complementary RL framework aims to address this misalignment by introducing a mechanism for the co-evolution of the experience extractor and the policy actor, drawing inspiration from complementary learning systems observed in neuroscience. This positions the work as an advancement in making experience-driven agent learning more efficient and adaptive, particularly within the context of LLM-based agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an RL framework that enables efficient, experience-driven agent learning by addressing the limitations of static experience utilization in LLM-based agents. The authors aim to design a system where a policy actor and its experience extractor form a closed co-evolutionary loop, with each component continuously adapting and shaping the other.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified weaknesses in current LLM-based RL:

- **Sample Inefficiency:** LLM-based agents, when trained with outcome-based RL, suffer from low sample efficiency due to sparse reward signals. These signals indicate only the final outcome, neglecting the rich procedural information within multi-turn interactions.
- **Underutilization of Procedural Information:** Collected rollouts contain valuable behavioral data (e.g., successful strategies, recoverable failure patterns, critical decision points) that is largely unexploited by existing methods.
- **Static Experience Limitation:** Prior attempts to leverage historical experience often treat it as a static resource or rely on non-adaptive extractors. This results in "stale" or "misaligned" experience as the actor's policy evolves, diminishing its utility and hindering learning efficiency over time. This is empirically demonstrated in a pilot study where both offline and static online experience showed declining benefits compared to a baseline.
- **Inspiration from Neuroscience:** The human brain's Complementary Learning Systems (CLS), which involve the neocortex for slow, structured knowledge acquisition and the hippocampus for fast, episodic memory management, provide a biological analogy. This system consolidates valuable episodes via cortical feedback and replays them to strengthen decision-making, suggesting a model for co-evolutionary learning.

To address these points, the authors outline three core design requirements for an effective RL algorithm for experience-driven agent training:

1. **Actor-Extractor Co-Evolution:** The policy actor and the experience extractor must mutually adapt throughout training, each continuously improving the other's capabilities.
2. **Experience Consolidation:** The system should automatically construct and maintain an experience bank from trajectories, distilling transferable knowledge while resolving conflicts and redundancies.
3. **Training-Distillation Coordination:** Actor training and experience distillation must be efficiently coordinated at scale, without introducing blocking latency to the actor's learning process.

The overarching motivation is to overcome the limitations of static experience management and thereby improve the sample efficiency and overall effectiveness of LLM-based agents in complex, interactive environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Complementary RL is proposed as an algorithm built around two interacting models: a policy actor ($\pi_\theta$) and an experience extractor ($\pi_\phi$). Both models are optimized through RL-based objectives within an asynchronous training framework.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The standard RL objective for an LLM-based actor $\pi_\theta$ in an MDP $\langle S, A, T, R \rangle$ is to maximize the expected success rate (binary outcome reward $R \in \{0, 1\}$). Complementary RL augments this objective by conditioning the actor's policy on distilled experience $m$ retrieved from an experience bank $M$, aiming to maximize $E_{E, g, m \sim M, \tau \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot|g,m)}[R(\tau)]$.

#### 3.2. Co-Evolutionary Paradigm

A pilot study demonstrated that static experience (pre-constructed or dynamically collected by a fixed extractor) provided diminishing returns or marginal gains compared to a baseline. This indicated a distributional misalignment where static experience cannot track the evolving state-action distribution of an improving actor. This observation motivated the co-evolutionary paradigm where $\pi_\phi$ and $\pi_\theta$ are jointly optimized, allowing improved policies to generate higher-quality trajectories that refine $M$, which in turn provides more effective guidance for policy optimization.

#### 3.3. Algorithm Design for Experience Extractor ($\pi_\phi$)

The experience extractor $\pi_\phi$ is responsible for distilling structured experience $m$ from completed episodes (conditioned on task goal $g$ and trajectory $\tau$). Its optimization is based on the utility of its distilled experience in facilitating the actor's success. A binary reward $r(m) \in \{-1, +1\}$ is assigned to each distilled experience $m$ based on the outcome of the trajectory it guided. These experience-reward pairs are accumulated into a training batch $B_\phi$, and $\pi_\phi$ is optimized using the **CISPO (Clipped Importance Sampling Policy Optimization)** objective (Equation 3). CISPO's token-level importance sampling ratio clipping aims to ensure stable co-evolution by preventing abrupt shifts in experience distribution.

#### 3.4. Algorithm Design for Actor ($\pi_\theta$)

The actor $\pi_\theta$ is typically optimized using the **GRPO (Group-Relative Policy Optimization)** objective (Equation 4). However, the authors observed that when all interactions are conditioned on retrieved experience, the actor may over-rely on external guidance and converge prematurely. To counter this, they partition rollouts into two subgroups: experience-guided and experience-free. A critical issue of differing reward scales and variances between these subgroups for advantage estimation is addressed by proposing a **split-GRPO objective** (Equation 5). This objective computes advantages *within* each subgroup, ensuring consistency in relative performance evaluation and stabilizing training.

#### 3.5. Training Framework

A fully asynchronous design is implemented to decouple rollout collection from experience distillation, addressing potential bottlenecks.

- **Primary Training Loop:** The actor $\pi_\theta$ continuously interacts with the environment, collects rollouts, and is optimized via outcome-based rewards.
- **Background Track:** The experience extractor $\pi_\phi$ processes completed trajectories, distills experience, and maintains the experience bank $M$.

A centralized **ExperienceManager ($\mathcal{H}$)** coordinates interactions between these asynchronous tracks, managing concurrency and minimizing latency.

- **Experience Consolidation:**
 - **Producer-Consumer Distillation:** Distillation requests (trajectory $\tau$, goal $g$, outcome $o$, retrieved experience $m$) are submitted to $\mathcal{H}$'s internal queue. A background process forwards these to $\pi_\phi$, which issues structured operations (Add, Update, Return) to $M$. Writes to $M$ occur under a writer lock.
 - **Periodic Merge:** To mitigate redundancy and conflicts from group-based RL, a Merge operation is periodically triggered. Experiences in $M$ are processed in chunks by $\pi_\phi$ to identify, merge, or discard entries, yielding a compact experience bank.
- **Experience Retrieval:**
 - **Query Batching and Parallel Search:** Search requests from environments are accumulated by $\mathcal{H}$ into micro-batches. An embedding cache is used to avoid redundant computation. Batched queries are embedded by $f_\psi$ and distributed to parallel search workers, performing semantic similarity search over $M$ under a reader lock.
 - **Search-and-Ask Tool:** The actor can actively query $M$ at any decision step during interaction using a context-aware query $q'$. If a relevant experience $m$ is found, $\pi_\phi$ refines it for the actor's specific situation. This increases $M$ utilization and provides targeted guidance.

#### 3.6. Co-Evolution Training

$\pi_\theta$ and $\pi_\phi$ are optimized on independent schedules. For $\pi_\phi$, experience entries $m$ from trajectories are assigned binary rewards based on episode success. Unique $m$ entries are accumulated in $B_\phi$ (averaging rewards if retrieved multiple times) and $\pi_\phi$ is optimized once $B_\phi$ reaches the required batch size.

#### 3.7. Implementation Tricks (from Appendix B)

- **Retrieval Diversification:** To stabilize $\pi_\phi$ training and mitigate data redundancy, the system oversamples candidate experiences and re-ranks them using a diversity-aware scoring function. This function penalizes frequently retrieved experiences, exposing $\pi_\phi$ to a broader range of signals.
- **Training-Count-Aware Advantage Reweighting:** To prevent overfitting of $\pi_\phi$ on repeatedly retrieved experiences, the advantage of each experience in $B_\phi$ is reweighted. A cooldown window suppresses recent updates, and a count-based decay progressively down-weights overused experiences.
- **Actor-Critic (Optional):** An Actor-Critic mechanism was explored where $\pi_\theta$ reflects on retrieved experience and assigns a critic score (accept, refine, reject). This score is combined with task reward to form an enriched learning signal for $\pi_\phi$. While showing early performance improvements, it incurred non-trivial latency and was not adopted as a default.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of Complementary RL was conducted across four open-ended environments: MiniHack, WebShop, ALFWorld, and SWE-Bench.

#### 4.1. Single-Task Training

Complementary RL consistently outperformed baselines that do not leverage experience across all four tasks:
- **Performance Improvement:** In MiniHack Room and ALFWorld, Complementary RL achieved a 1.3x performance margin with improved training stability. On SWE-Bench, it demonstrated faster improvement and a +3.0% gain over the baseline.
- **Action Efficiency:** Complementary RL not only achieved higher success rates but also completed tasks more efficiently, requiring 1.5x fewer actions on MiniHack Room and 2x fewer actions on ALFWorld. For SWE-Bench, while actions increased, this correlated with a higher success rate from fully completing tasks rather than premature submission.

#### 4.2. Multi-Task Training

A multi-task setting (MiniHack Room, ALFWorld, WebShop) was used to investigate cross-task experience distillation and disentangle the benefits of co-evolution.
- **Experience Utility:** Integrating experience at test time consistently improved performance (e.g., +5% for both Static Online Exp. and Complementary RL), confirming the value of retrieved experience during inference.
- **Failure of Static Online Experience:** The "Static Online Exp." baseline, where $\pi_\phi$ dynamically maintains $M$ but is not optimized, failed to surpass the "Baseline" (no experience) even with experience at test time (gap > 10%). Its training curves were largely dominated by the baseline, indicating that without parametric updates, a fixed extractor cannot adapt its strategy to the evolving actor, leading to noisy and inconsistent retrieval, particularly with cross-task contamination.
- **Superiority of Complementary RL:** Complementary RL consistently outperformed the baseline both with and without experience at test time (+7% and +2% on average, respectively). This suggests that co-evolutionary training internalizes useful experience into the actor itself, rather than solely relying on external guidance.
- **Limitations of Experience Only:** The "Exp. Only" baseline (optimizing only $\pi_\phi$ while keeping $\pi_\theta$ fixed) yielded marginal actor improvement, indicating that experience quality alone is insufficient when the actor's base capability is limited.

#### 4.3. Analysis

- **Effect of Experience Extractor Capacity:** A larger experience extractor (Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507 vs. Qwen3-4B-Thinking-2507) yielded consistent improvement across tasks (+5% on average). This suggests that greater extractor capacity enables the distillation of more generalizable and informative experience, further benefiting actor learning.
- **Complementary RL with Self-Distillation:** An attempt to integrate self-distillation (internalizing successful experience-guided behaviors) initially showed improvement but later collapsed. The authors hypothesize this might be due to suboptimal hyperparameters or the frequency of applying self-distillation, leaving further investigation for future work.
- **Rollout Latency:** The asynchronous training framework introduced no appreciable latency to rollout collection across varying batch sizes and parallel environment counts, remaining consistently on par with the baseline without experience integration. Average search time remained low, even for large rollout batch sizes.
- **Task Scaling:** Complementary RL consistently outperformed the RL baseline without experience integration as the number of tasks scaled up (3-task vs. 6-task mixtures). It showed performance gains of +6.6% on the 3-task mixture and +8.1% on the 6-task mixture, demonstrating robust scalability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work presents Complementary RL as a framework that addresses a critical limitation in current LLM-based agent learning: the inefficient utilization and progressive misalignment of historical experience. By establishing a co-evolutionary learning loop between a policy actor and an experience extractor, the research offers a mechanism for agents to continuously refine their understanding and application of experience.

The significance of Complementary RL lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency:** The consistent performance improvements and reduced action counts across diverse tasks indicate that Complementary RL enables more efficient learning. This is particularly relevant for LLM-based agents, where interaction with complex environments and fine-tuning can be computationally expensive.
- **Adaptive Experience Management:** The co-evolutionary design ensures that the experience bank remains relevant and useful throughout training, overcoming the "stale experience" problem identified in prior static approaches. The extractor adapts its distillation strategy in lockstep with the actor's evolving capabilities, leading to more targeted and effective guidance.
- **Robustness and Scalability:** The framework's demonstrated effectiveness in multi-task settings and its robust scaling with an increasing number of tasks suggest its applicability to real-world industrial post-training scenarios, which often involve diverse task mixtures.
- **Architectural Innovation:** The asynchronous training framework with a centralized ExperienceManager represents an infrastructure co-design that ensures efficient experience management at scale without introducing blocking latency, which is crucial for practical deployment.
- **Inspiration from Neuroscience:** Drawing inspiration from Complementary Learning Systems in the brain provides a biologically plausible foundation for the proposed co-evolutionary mechanism, potentially opening avenues for further cross-disciplinary research.

The potential impact of Complementary RL extends to the development of more capable and autonomous LLM agents that can learn and adapt effectively in complex, dynamic environments. This could lead to advancements in areas such as code generation and debugging (SWE-Bench), interactive web agents (WebShop), and embodied AI (ALFWorld, MiniHack), where efficient exploration and strategic decision-making are paramount. The findings suggest a paradigm shift from static memory systems to dynamic, self-improving experience management, potentially making LLM agents more robust, generalizable, and adaptable over long training horizons.
