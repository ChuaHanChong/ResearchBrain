# Show, Don't Tell: Evaluating Spatial Cognition in Generative Pixels Rather Than LLM Text

> [!summary] Summary
> The OmniAI Group of ZJU ACES Lab developed ProVisE, a framework for evaluating spatial cognition by allowing image-generation models to provide visual answers, and SpatialGen-Bench, a diagnostic benchmark. Their evaluation revealed that current AI models (top text model 61.04%, top visual model 54.49%) remain significantly below human performance (87.79%), with visual answering excelling in pixel-level spatial states and text-output VLMs showing strengths in abstract spatial reasoning.

## Problem

- An answer-interface mismatch exists in spatial cognition evaluation, where continuous visual judgments are forced into discrete textual or coordinate outputs, which is unnatural for models and incompatible with image-generation models.
- A systematic evaluation framework was lacking for image-generation models to assess their spatial cognition in a way that is compatible with established metrics for text-output Vision-Language Models (VLMs).
- Workarounds using auxiliary VLM judges to interpret generated images introduced unreliability, model dependency, and hindered objective, metric-compatible comparisons with text-output VLMs.

## Method

- Developed **ProVisE** (==Protocolized Visual Evaluation==), a benchmark-agnostic framework that facilitates the evaluation of image-generation models by eliciting protocol-constrained visual answers and parsing them into structured, metric-compatible predictions.
- Introduced an ==Agentic Protocol Builder== to automatically construct and validate task-specific generation-parser protocols, ensuring scalability and fair comparison across various benchmarks without manual engineering.
- Constructed **SpatialGen-Bench**, a diagnostic benchmark comprising 14 spatial subtasks across four capability levels (Perception, Understanding, Reasoning, Interaction) to enable granular assessment of spatial cognition in image-generation models.

## Results

- The top text model (GPT-5.4, **61.04%** Overall) and top visual model (GPT Image 2, **54.49%**) both fall well below the Human reference (**87.79%**) on SpatialGen-Bench; image-generation models excel at 'Depth'/'Relationship' while text-output VLMs lead on abstract tasks like 'Size,' 'Feasibility,' 'Prediction,' and 'Counting.'
- The Agentic builder successfully constructed protocols for **six** external spatial benchmarks, enabling metric-compatible evaluation of image-generation models across diverse tasks and confirming the framework's transferability.
- Analysis of failures in visual answering models revealed that **88.03%** of non-correct outcomes were valid but incorrect spatial predictions, indicating issues in underlying spatial judgment rather than generation or parsing failures.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current AI models, both text-output VLMs and image-generation models, demonstrate a substantial gap in spatial cognitive abilities compared to humans, especially in 'Understanding' tasks like perspective and relational reasoning.
> - Visual and textual answering modes exhibit complementary strengths: image-generation models excel in tasks requiring direct externalization of continuous pixel-level spatial states, while text-output VLMs are superior for abstract reasoning and discrete decision-making tasks.
> - The use of universal black-box VLM parsers for evaluating generated visual outputs introduces significant bias and shifts in model scores and rankings, underscoring the necessity for transparent and metric-aligned parsing mechanisms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Spatial intelligence is a critical capability for artificial agents to transition from abstract semantic understanding to effective interaction within the physical world. Current research in spatial cognition for artificial intelligence has predominantly focused on Vision-Language Models (VLMs) that generate text-based or symbolic outputs. These models are typically evaluated using benchmarks that require answers in discrete formats such as coordinates, option labels, or textual descriptions. This approach has facilitated the measurement of spatial reasoning in VLMs, but it imposes a constraint on how spatial judgments, which are often continuous and visually grounded in real-world scenarios, are expressed.

Concurrently, advancements in generative vision models, particularly image-generation models, have demonstrated capabilities in synthesizing, modifying, or marking visual content directly in pixel space. These models are inherently suited for spatial tasks where answers might be more naturally conveyed through visual means like pointing, drawing, or highlighting regions, rather than precise numerical coordinates or descriptive text. However, a gap exists in the evaluation landscape: existing spatial reasoning benchmarks are tailored for text-output VLMs, and current text-to-image benchmarks primarily assess aspects like prompt fidelity or compositional alignment in generated images. Neither directly addresses the systematic evaluation of image-generation models' spatial cognition in a manner compatible with the established metrics for text-output VLMs.

This work addresses this evaluation gap by proposing a framework that allows image-generation models to express spatial judgments visually and subsequently be evaluated using the same objective metrics as text-output VLMs. It seeks to enable a direct and unified comparison between these two distinct categories of models, thereby enriching the understanding of spatial cognition across different AI modalities. The paper positions itself as establishing a metric-compatible testbed for generative spatial evaluation, which could inform the development of AI agents capable of more natural physical world interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from an identified "answer-interface mismatch" in the evaluation of spatial cognition in AI models. In real-world spatial tasks, information like locations, regions, or paths is often intuitively conveyed through visual means such as pointing, marking, or drawing. However, conventional spatial reasoning benchmarks largely compel models to respond with precise coordinates, discrete options, or textual descriptions. This creates several issues:

- **Unnatural Expression:** For text-output VLMs, translating continuous visual judgments into discrete symbolic outputs (e.g., bounding box coordinates or depth values per pixel) can be an unnatural and burdensome serialization process. It is akin to asking a human to report exact pixel coordinates instead of simply pointing.
- **Incompatibility for Generative Models:** Image-generation models, which operate natively in pixel space and can externalize spatial judgments visually (e.g., by marking an object or drawing a trajectory), cannot be directly assessed by benchmarks expecting textual or coordinate-based answers. Their visual responses are incompatible with existing evaluators.
- **Reliability Concerns with VLM Judges:** A common workaround—using an auxiliary VLM judge to interpret generated images and infer answers—introduces its own set of problems. Such judge-based scores are dependent on the judge model's reliability, may reflect judge uncertainty rather than the generated answer's accuracy, and are challenging to align with the original metrics used for text-output VLMs, thus hindering controlled comparisons.

To address these challenges, the research outlines several key objectives:

1. **Develop ProVisE (Protocolized Visual Evaluation):** Create a benchmark-agnostic framework that facilitates the evaluation of image-generation models by eliciting protocol-constrained visual answers. These visual answers are then parsed into structured predictions that are compatible with the original benchmark evaluation metrics.
2. **Introduce an Agentic Protocol Builder:** Design an automated system within ProVisE capable of constructing and validating task-specific generation-parser protocols for novel or unconfigured benchmarks. This aims to scale the framework beyond manual protocol engineering.
3. **Construct SpatialGen-Bench:** Develop a diagnostic benchmark specifically curated for spatial cognition in image-generation models. This benchmark is designed to cover a diverse range of 14 spatial subtasks across four capability levels (perception, understanding, reasoning, interaction) and various answer forms, enabling a granular assessment of model strengths and weaknesses.
4. **Systematically Compare Model Families:** Evaluate representative text-output VLMs and image-generation models within a unified setting using the proposed framework and benchmark. This objective is to provide a quantitative profile of their respective spatial cognitive abilities.
5. **Reveal Complementary Strengths and Limitations:** Through the comparative analysis, identify the distinct advantages and disadvantages of pixel-space expression versus text-based reasoning in solving different types of spatial tasks.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to establish a robust and metric-compatible evaluation methodology that overcomes the existing interface mismatch, enabling a more direct and equitable assessment of spatial intelligence across diverse AI architectures.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research proposes a multi-faceted approach centered on the ProVisE framework and the SpatialGen-Bench benchmark.

#### 3.1. ProVisE Framework

ProVisE (Protocolized Visual Evaluation) is introduced as a benchmark-agnostic framework designed to bridge the answer-interface mismatch between image-generation models and traditional spatial reasoning benchmarks.

- **Visual Protocols:** The fundamental unit of ProVisE is the visual protocol. Each protocol comprises a "guidance prompt" that instructs an image-generation model on how to visually express its answer in pixel space, paired with a "parser" responsible for converting the generated image into a structured prediction. Protocols also specify the expected answer format, conditions for invalid outputs, and how the parsed output maps to the original task metric. This design ensures that generated visual responses are interpreted objectively. The framework includes a task-aware protocol pool covering formats such as instance/point marking, direction grids, relative-depth maps, region masks, state matching, and trajectory drawing.
- **Protocol Routing:** Before model evaluation, a protocol is selected for each task. If a benchmark has a pre-validated configuration, it is directly used. Otherwise, the "Agentic builder" constructs a task-specific protocol. This separation of protocol design from model evaluation ensures fair comparison, as all models for a given task share the same fixed protocol.
- **Protocol Execution and Parsing:** Image-generation models receive instructions to produce a visual response adhering to the protocol's structural constraints (e.g., marked instances, binary masks, drawn trajectories). A corresponding parser then deterministically converts this visual response into the structured answer format required by the original benchmark. Fallback mechanisms are employed when deterministic parsing is insufficient, utilizing constrained auxiliary parsers that operate solely on the generated response and visual evidence contract, without access to the original source image or question.
- **Metric-Compatible Evaluation:** The final, parsed predictions from image-generation models, alongside the raw normalized responses from text-output VLMs, are evaluated using the *exact same original task metrics*. This ensures a unified and fair comparison of spatial cognition across different model types under shared task semantics.

#### 3.2. Agentic Protocol Construction

For benchmarks without pre-existing ProVisE configurations, an Agentic builder is employed, operating in three stages:

1. **Normalization:** The builder first analyzes benchmark records, input media, task labels, answer formats, and evaluation metrics, normalizing them into standardized task-level contracts. This process preserves the original task semantics and scoring rules.
2. **Construction Route Selection:** The Agent examines representative examples for each task and chooses one of three construction routes:
 - **Reuse:** Selects an existing, compatible protocol from the pool.
 - **Build:** Assembles a new task-specific protocol from registered parsing components (e.g., color differencing, OCR, geometric analysis).
 - **Fallback:** If deterministic components are insufficient, it uses a constrained auxiliary parser (often a VLM acting on the generated image only) to recover the required answer form.
 Each route results in a declarative artifact specifying the generation prompt, parser configuration, answer contract, invalid-output rules, and metric mapping.
3. **Smoke Validation:** Each candidate protocol undergoes validation on a small set of examples. This includes checks for successful visual generation, stable parsing, and compatibility with the original metric. Protocols may be revised once or transitioned to Fallback if initial attempts fail. Accepted protocols are then fixed and applied universally to all target models.

#### 3.3. SpatialGen-Bench Benchmark

SpatialGen-Bench is a curated diagnostic benchmark developed to assess spatial cognition through protocolized visual answers.

- **Capability Hierarchy:** It organizes spatial cognition into four progressive levels:
 - **Perception (145 samples):** Basic spatial attributes (counting, relative depth, orientation, object size).
 - **Understanding (140 samples):** Scene-level representation (relationship verification, perspective judgment, spatial mental modeling, spatial grounding).
 - **Reasoning (90 samples):** Inferring changes or conclusions beyond direct observation (multi-hop reasoning, spatial state prediction, geometric feasibility).
 - **Interaction (95 samples):** Action-oriented decision making (affordance grounding, navigation, trajectory planning).
 This layered design supports diagnostic evaluation from static perception to action-oriented tasks.
- **Benchmark Construction:** Data is sourced from multiple public spatial evaluation benchmarks. A quality-aware sample selection process filters for reliability, removing duplicates, unclear cases, or ambiguous annotations. Each instance is then mapped to a subtask and capability level.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** The benchmark employs a range of metrics: exact-match accuracy for discrete answers (e.g., counting, object size); point-in-mask accuracy for spatial grounding; mean mask precision for affordance; a partial-credit endpoint score for state prediction; and DFD-success@0.4 (based on Discrete Fréchet Distance) for trajectory planning.

The methodology integrates a novel evaluation framework with a purpose-built diagnostic benchmark, facilitating a direct and objective comparison of spatial intelligence across diverse AI model paradigms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 31 model-interface systems (20 text-answering, 11 visual-answering) on SpatialGen-Bench and external benchmarks yielded several key findings:

- **Human-Machine Performance Gap:** Current models, both text-output VLMs and image-generation models, remain significantly below human performance in spatial cognition. The top-performing text model (GPT-5.4) achieved an Overall score of 61.04%, and the top visual model (GPT Image 2) achieved 54.49%, compared to the Human reference of 87.79%. The largest capability-level gap was observed in "Understanding" tasks, particularly in "Perspective," "Relationship," and "Mental Modeling," suggesting that transforming spatial representations across viewpoints and maintaining relation semantics are significant bottlenecks.
- **Uneven Specialization:** Spatially specialized models demonstrated strong performance in specific geometric or action-grounding tasks (e.g., SpaceR for Relationship, RoboBrain2.5-8B for Size and Affordance). However, their performance was inconsistent across the broad range of SpatialGen-Bench tasks, indicating that current specialization improves narrow skills but does not yet confer general spatial competence.
- **Interface Strengths and Complementarity:**
 - **Visual Answering Strengths:** Image-generation models were more competitive and showed gains in tasks where the answer could be directly expressed as a pixel-level spatial state, such as "Depth" and "Relationship." This suggests an advantage when models can externalize continuous spatial structures (e.g., depth fields, grounded overlays) directly in the medium of evidence.
 - **Text Answering Strengths:** Text-output VLMs maintained a clear advantage in tasks requiring abstract transformation or reasoning beyond direct visual evidence, such as "Size," "Feasibility," "Prediction," and "Counting," and generally across the "Reasoning" capability level. These tasks involve operations like comparing object extent, testing geometric compatibility, updating states under dynamics, or aggregating instances into discrete decisions.
 - **Complementarity:** Paired analyses revealed that visual and textual answering modes exhibit complementary capabilities. For instance, GPT Image 2 solved 37.0% of items missed by GPT-5.4, and SenseNova's visual mode solved 30.6% of items missed by its text mode. This indicates that successes in one modality are not merely a subset of the other.
- **Agentic Cross-Benchmark Generalization:** The Agentic builder successfully constructed task-specific protocols for six external spatial benchmarks (EmbSpatial-Bench, OmniSpatial, Q-Spatial+, RoboSpatial-Home, SAT, RoboAfford). This enabled end-to-end, metric-compatible evaluation of GPT Image 2 and text baselines across heterogeneous task contracts, confirming the framework's transferability. The performance patterns largely echoed SpatialGen-Bench, with neither interface uniformly superior.
- **Universal VLM Parser Sensitivity:** Experiments evaluating generated visual outputs with universal VLM parsers (Qwen3-VL-8B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Llama 4 Scout) instead of ProVisE's task-specific parsers demonstrated significant shifts in both model scores and rankings. This highlights that universal black-box VLM parsers introduce a model-dependent interpretation layer, underscoring the importance of transparent, metric-aligned parsing for reliable evaluation.
- **Failure Attribution:** A detailed analysis of failures in visual answering models revealed that the majority of non-correct outcomes (88.03%) were valid but *incorrect spatial predictions*, rather than failures in generation, protocol noncompliance, or parsing. This indicates that while the visual communication channel generally completes, the underlying spatial judgment is often erroneous. Task-localized non-answer failures were observed in areas like "Size" and "Feasibility" (issues with scene preservation or plausible placement) and "Prediction" and "Grounding" (challenges with precise encoding for parsing).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of artificial intelligence by addressing a fundamental challenge in the evaluation of spatial cognition across diverse model architectures. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Unified Evaluation Standard:** ProVisE establishes a benchmark-agnostic framework that enables the systematic and metric-compatible evaluation of image-generation models and text-output VLMs under shared task semantics. This provides a more objective and comprehensive approach to assessing spatial intelligence, moving beyond the previous limitations of interface mismatch and unreliable VLM-as-judge paradigms.
- **Diagnostic Understanding of Model Capabilities:** The introduction of SpatialGen-Bench, with its curated subtasks and hierarchical capability levels, offers a diagnostic tool to pinpoint specific strengths and weaknesses of models in spatial reasoning. This detailed understanding can guide targeted improvements in model architectures and training strategies.
- **Elucidation of Modality Strengths:** The findings delineate the complementary strengths of pixel-space expression and text-based reasoning. They suggest that visual generation is more competitive for directly externalizing continuous spatial states (e.g., depth, relationships), while text-output VLMs excel in tasks requiring abstract compositional reasoning, precise transformations, or discrete decision-making. This insight can inform the design of future multimodal AI systems that optimally leverage different modalities for various stages of spatial problem-solving.
- **Foundation for Embodied AI and World Models:** By making generative spatial cognition measurable, the work lays a foundation for evaluating and advancing AI agents that must interact with the physical world. Understanding how generative models interpret and represent space in pixels is crucial for developing robust embodied agents, robotics, and more sophisticated world models.
- **Agentic Framework for Scalability:** The Agentic protocol builder demonstrates a scalable approach to adapting evaluation frameworks to new benchmarks. Its ability to construct and validate task-specific protocols automatically reduces manual effort and increases the applicability of the ProVisE framework across a broader range of spatial tasks and datasets.
- **Implications for Multimodal Architectures:** The revealed sensitivity of scores and rankings to the choice of parser highlights the critical role of interpretation mechanisms. This underscores the need for transparent and reliable parsing layers in multimodal AI, suggesting that simply generating a visual answer is insufficient; its correct and unambiguous interpretation is equally vital. This can guide research into more integrated "show and tell" systems where visual generation and symbolic reasoning can exchange and cross-check intermediate states.

In summary, this research not only provides a novel and robust evaluation methodology but also offers empirical insights into the current state and complementary nature of spatial cognition in generative and language-based AI models, thereby advancing the development of more capable and intelligent agents.
