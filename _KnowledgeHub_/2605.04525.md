# HDFlow: Hierarchical Diffusion-Flow Planning for Long-horizon Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> HDFlow introduces a hierarchical planning framework for long-horizon robotic tasks that leverages diffusion models for strategic high-level subgoal generation and rectified flow models for fast, low-level trajectory execution. This hybrid approach, coupled with manifold-aware energy-based guidance, improves planning efficiency and achieves higher success rates on diverse manipulation and locomotion benchmarks.

## Problem

- Long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks suffer from error accumulation and sparse reward signals, making planning challenging.
- Purely diffusion-based planners are computationally intensive due to iterative denoising, hindering real-time low-level robotic control.
- Existing conditional diffusion models for planning often lack explicit mechanisms to evaluate plan quality, leading to infeasible or suboptimal trajectories, especially in high-dimensional latent spaces.

## Method

- A hybrid hierarchical architecture combines diffusion models for high-level subgoal generation with rectified flow models for efficient low-level trajectory synthesis.
- An Energy-Based Model (EBM) is trained to provide dense, success-oriented guidance to the high-level diffusion planner.
- Manifold-aware guidance, including a projection step, ensures generated latent subgoals remain on the feasible data manifold.

## Results

- HDFlow achieved higher success rates on FurnitureBench tasks, such as **55%** on `cabinet` (low randomization), outperforming existing imitation learning and diffusion-based planners.
- The hybrid architecture improved computational efficiency while maintaining high success rates (e.g., **68%** success at 88 ms/step on `lamp`), validating the choice of generative models.
- Demonstrated robust real-world performance with a Franka robot and achieved competitive or superior success rates across 18 RLBench and various OGBench tasks, showcasing strong generalization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Different generative models are optimally assigned to hierarchical levels: diffusion for exploratory high-level strategy and rectified flow for efficient low-level execution.
> - An Energy-Based Model providing dense reward signals can effectively guide high-level diffusion planners toward successful strategies in sparse-reward settings.
> - Manifold-aware projection combined with EBM guidance helps maintain the feasibility of generated plans within the latent space, preventing deviation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in robotic manipulation for complex, long-horizon tasks characterized by sparse rewards. Traditional planning methods often encounter difficulties with error accumulation over extended task durations, which can lead to task failure. This has motivated the development of hierarchical planning approaches, which decompose complex goals into a sequence of simpler subgoals. A significant direction within this field involves performing planning within the latent space of learned world models, enabling efficient reasoning by abstracting high-dimensional observations.

Recent advancements in generative models, particularly denoising diffusion models, have demonstrated capabilities in generating behavior plans by framing planning as a conditional generation problem. Prior works such as Diffuser and Decision Diffuser have explored this paradigm, producing diverse and high-quality trajectories. However, the iterative denoising process inherent in diffusion models is computationally intensive, a characteristic that renders them less suitable for the fast, low-level control required for real-time robotic interaction. Applying diffusion models uniformly across all levels of a planning hierarchy, as seen in some hierarchical diffusion planners like Simple Hierarchical Diffuser (SHD), can inherit this computational bottleneck, particularly at the trajectory generation stage. Additionally, standard conditional diffusion models for planning often lack explicit mechanisms to evaluate the quality or long-term viability of generated plans, a limitation that can lead to failures in sparse-reward environments where many seemingly plausible plans may lead to irreversible dead ends. Furthermore, in high-dimensional latent spaces, inexact guidance during diffusion can cause samples to deviate from the feasible data manifold.

HDFlow addresses these limitations by proposing a hybrid hierarchical planning framework. It combines the exploratory power of diffusion models for high-level strategic subgoal generation with the speed and efficiency of rectified flow models for low-level dense trajectory synthesis. This approach is motivated by the hypothesis that different generative modeling paradigms are optimally suited for different levels of a planning hierarchy. The framework also incorporates explicit energy-based guidance and manifold-aware projection to enhance plan quality and maintain feasibility within the latent space, thereby seeking to overcome the computational and robustness issues observed in single-paradigm or purely diffusion-based hierarchical planners.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, efficient, and generalizable hierarchical planning framework capable of solving long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks, particularly those with sparse reward signals. The motivations and specific objectives driving the development of HDFlow are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing Computational Inefficiency of Pure Diffusion Planners:** Current diffusion models, while effective for generating diverse plans, involve an iterative denoising process that is computationally demanding. This makes them unsuitable for real-time, low-level trajectory generation in robotics, where rapid execution is critical. HDFlow aims to mitigate this by employing a faster, ODE-based rectified flow model for low-level planning, thereby improving computational efficiency during inference.
2. **Optimizing Hierarchical Decomposition:** The work posits that a single generative modeling paradigm is not optimal for all levels of a planning hierarchy. High-level planning requires exploratory capabilities to discover viable sequences of subgoals, while low-level planning demands speed and deterministic execution for smooth trajectory generation. The objective is to design a framework that optimally assigns different generative models to these distinct hierarchical needs—diffusion for high-level strategy and rectified flow for low-level execution.
3. **Enhancing Plan Quality in Sparse-Reward Environments:** In complex, long-horizon tasks, many seemingly plausible subgoal sequences can lead to task failure due to sparse rewards providing insufficient feedback. The research aims to improve the quality of generated plans by introducing an Energy-Based Model (EBM) that learns a dense reward signal. This EBM explicitly guides the high-level diffusion planner, steering it towards successful strategies and away from potential dead ends.
4. **Mitigating Manifold Deviation:** Planning in high-dimensional latent spaces, especially with external guidance, carries the risk of generated samples drifting away from the feasible data manifold. This deviation can lead to physically unrealistic or unachievable plans. A specific objective is to implement a manifold-aware guidance process that combines EBM guidance with a projection step onto the local latent manifold, ensuring that generated subgoals remain feasible.
5. **Learning a Structured Latent Space:** Effective planning in latent space relies on a high-quality, semantically structured representation of the environment. The motivation is to train a world model using a combination of observation reconstruction, inverse dynamics modeling, and a contrastive learning objective. This is intended to embed a notion of task progress within the latent space, making it more informative and conducive for downstream hierarchical planning.
6. **Demonstrating Generalizability and Real-World Applicability:** The framework is motivated by the need for solutions that can generalize across diverse long-horizon tasks and operate effectively in both simulated and real-world environments. Comprehensive evaluation on various benchmarks, including contact-rich assembly, diverse manipulation, and goal-conditioned locomotion, is aimed at demonstrating the method's robustness and practical utility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HDFlow is a hierarchical planning framework implemented through a two-stage training process: World Model Learning and Hierarchical Planner Training. The framework operates within a learned latent space to manage long-horizon manipulation tasks.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: World Model Learning

The initial stage focuses on learning a semantically structured latent space from multi-modal, high-dimensional observations (`o`). The core of this stage is a **Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM)** architecture.

- **Observation Processing:** The RSSM's encoder leverages a pretrained DINOv2 model for RGB images, supplemented by a separate Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) for depth images and a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for robot proprioceptive states (end-effector position, orientation, velocities, and gripper width). These are combined to form a high-dimensional observation embedding (`e_t`).
- **Latent Space Learning:** The RSSM learns to encode observations into a latent space `Z` and model environment dynamics. It comprises:
 - A deterministic RNN (`h_t+1 = f_ϕ(h_t, z_t)`) to update its hidden state, summarizing historical information.
 - A dynamics model to predict a prior distribution over the latent state (`_z_t ~ p_ϕ(_z_t|h_t)`).
 - A representation model to infer a posterior distribution over the latent state (`z_t ~ q_ϕ(z_t|h_t, e_t)`) from the observation embedding.
 - A decoder (`_o_t ~ p_ϕ(_o_t|h_t, z_t)`) to reconstruct original observations.
- **Training Objectives:**
 - **World Model Loss (`L_WM`):** The primary objective involves maximizing the Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO), combining observation reconstruction loss and a KL divergence regularization term. This ensures accurate observation reconstruction and compressed, predictive latent states.
 - **Contrastive Learning Objective (`L_contrastive`):** To explicitly structure the latent space with a notion of progress, a contrastive loss is introduced. It pulls latent representations of intermediate states from successful trajectories closer to their final goal representation, while pushing them away from intermediate states of failed trajectories using a modified InfoNCE objective.
 - **Inverse Dynamics Model Loss (`L_IDM`):** An inverse dynamics model is trained to predict the action (`a_t`) taken between two consecutive latent states (`z_t`, `z_t+1`). This objective encourages the latent space to be informative for control.
 - **Total World Model Loss (`L_WM-total`):** A weighted sum of `L_WM`, `L_IDM`, and `L_contrastive`.
- **Output:** After this stage, the world model's weights are frozen, and its encoder is used to generate a static dataset of structured latent representations for subsequent planner training.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Hierarchical Planner Training

This stage frames long-horizon planning as a conditional generative modeling task within the fixed latent space. It decomposes the problem using temporal abstraction. For each full latent trajectory, subgoals are defined by subsampling at a fixed interval `H`, yielding `K = ⌊T/H⌋` subgoals. This creates two datasets: one for sparse subgoal sequences (`z = (z_1..., z_K)`) and another for dense, fixed-length trajectory segments (`τ_k`) between subgoals.

#### 3.2.1. High-Level Planner: Manifold-Aware EBM-Guided Diffusion

- **Model:** A conditional diffusion model (Diffusion Transformer architecture) is trained to generate a sequence of `K` latent subgoals, conditioned on the current latent state (`z_0`) and the goal latent state (`z_G`). It is trained using the standard noise prediction error (`L_HL`).
- **Energy-Based Model (EBM) Guidance:** To ensure plans are successful, an EBM (`E_ϕ(z|z_0, z_G)`) (Transformer architecture) is trained separately to assign low energy to successful subgoal sequences and high energy to poor ones. This EBM provides an explicit dense reward signal via a contrastive loss (`L_EBM`).
- **Manifold-Aware Guidance (Inference):** To mitigate "guidance gap" and prevent deviation from the feasible latent manifold (`M_t`), a two-step process is applied:
 - **Step 1: Guided Sampling:** Standard reverse diffusion samples a temporary latent state (`z_temp_ℓ-1`) guided by the EBM's gradient (`g = ∇_z_ℓ E_ϕ(z_ℓ|c)`).
 - **Step 2: Manifold Projection:** `z_temp_ℓ-1` is projected onto the local tangent space of the latent manifold `M_ℓ-1`. This is achieved by obtaining a denoised estimate using Tweedie's formula, finding `k` nearest neighbors from successful latent subgoal sequences, forward diffusing them to timestep `ℓ-1`, and performing rank-`r` PCA to obtain a projection basis. This ensures generated plans are both successful and feasible (Proposition 4.3).

#### 3.2.2. Low-Level Planner: Rectified Flow for Trajectory Generation

- **Model:** A conditional rectified flow model (`v_θ(τ_u, u, c_k)`) (Rectified Flow Transformer architecture) is used for its speed and efficiency in generating dense, short-horizon latent trajectories (`τ_z`) between consecutive subgoals (`z_k-1, z_k`). It is trained by minimizing the flow-matching objective (`L_LL`).
- **Training Pair Construction:** For each subgoal pair `(z_k-1, z_k)`, a dense `H`-step latent segment ending at `z_k` is used as `τ_1`, and the segment starting at `z_k-1` is used as `τ_0`.

#### 3.2.3. Training and Inference

- **Joint Training:** The entire hierarchical planner is trained jointly with a composite loss function (`L_planner`) that includes `L_HL`, `L_LL`, `L_EBM`, and a `L_projection` term to encourage manifold consistency.
- **Online Deployment (MPC Framework):**
 - The robot's current observation (`o_curr`) and the task goal (`o_G`) are encoded into latent states (`z_curr`, `z_G`).
 - The high-level planner iteratively generates a sequence of subgoals (`z_1..., z_K`) for the remaining task.
 - The low-level planner generates a dense latent trajectory (`τ`) to the first subgoal (`z_1`).
 - The inverse dynamics model (from Stage 1) translates consecutive latent pairs `(z_t, z_t+1)` along `τ` into control actions (`a_t`).
 - The robot executes `a_t`, updates its state, and the process repeats in an MPC loop.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of HDFlow was evaluated across multiple challenging benchmarks in both simulation and real-world settings.

#### 4.1. FurnitureBench Experiments (Simulation)

HDFlow was evaluated on four furniture assembly tasks (one_leg, lamp, round_table, cabinet) under varying levels of initial randomization (Low, Med, High).
- **Performance:** HDFlow consistently achieved higher success rates compared to both imitation learning baselines (BC, Diffusion Policy, JUICER) and other diffusion-based planners (Diffuser, Decision Diffuser, HDMI, SHD). For instance, on the challenging `cabinet` task, HDFlow achieved 55% (Low) and 36% (Med) success rates, significantly outperforming SHD (21% Low, 11% Med).

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Choice of Generative Model (Table 2):** HDFlow's hybrid approach (Diffusion for high-level, Rectified Flow for low-level) demonstrated superior performance and improved computational efficiency compared to single-paradigm or flat architectures on the `lamp` task. While a purely hierarchical flow (HF) was faster (53 ms/step), its success rate (24%) was lower than HDFlow (68% success, 88 ms/step), and a purely hierarchical diffusion (HD) was slower (142 ms/step) and less successful (43%). This supports the claim that the hybrid architecture leverages the strengths of both models effectively.
- **Contribution of Core Components (Table 3):** Each novel component of HDFlow (contrastive world model, manifold-aware EBM guidance, manifold projection) contributed to the overall performance. Removing any of these components led to a measurable decrease in success rates (e.g., removing manifold projection reduced success from 92% to 84% on `one_leg` and 68% to 57% on `lamp`). Removing the manifold-aware EBM or the contrastive world model caused even larger drops in performance.
- **Choice of Vision Encoder (Appendix E.1):** DINOv2 consistently yielded the highest HDFlow success rates compared to other visual backbones (CNN, R3M, VIP, MAE), indicating its importance for learning rich and structured latent representations.
- **Inverse Dynamics Model Loss and RSSM (Appendix E.2, E.3):** Disabling the Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) loss or directly using DINOv2 features without the RSSM world model resulted in significant performance degradation. This indicates that explicitly training the IDM enhances the control-relevance of the latent space, and the RSSM is crucial for capturing temporal dynamics and creating a structured, compact latent representation necessary for effective planning.
- **Total Number of Subgoals (K) (Table 7):** An optimal value for `K` (the total number of subgoals) was identified, with `K=20` consistently yielding the highest success rates across FurnitureBench tasks. Too few subgoals (`K=10`) made low-level planning challenging due to large gaps, while too many subgoals (`K=30`) led to error accumulation in high-level planning.

#### 4.3. FurnitureBench Real-World Experiments

Experiments with a Franka Research 3 robot arm, using 50 real-world demonstrations for finetuning, demonstrated HDFlow's effectiveness in real-world settings.
- **Performance:** HDFlow significantly outperformed baselines such as Vanilla Behavior Cloning (BC) and Implicit Q-Learning (IQL) on `one_leg`, `lamp`, and `round_table` tasks, maintaining robust performance under increased initial randomization (e.g., 8/10 successes on `one_leg` Low vs. 3/10 for IQL).

#### 4.4. RLBench Experiments

Evaluations on 18 diverse 6-DoF manipulation tasks from RLBench demonstrated HDFlow's strong generalization capabilities.
- **Performance:** HDFlow achieved competitive or superior success rates compared to state-of-the-art methods (PerAct, RVT, 3D Diffuser Actor, RVT-2). It achieved 100% success on tasks like `Close Jar` and `Push Buttons`, and significantly outperformed others on `Insert Peg` (93.3% vs. 65.6% for 3D Diffuser Actor) and `Sort Shape` (73.3% vs. 44.0% for 3D Diffuser Actor).

#### 4.5. OGBench Experiments

HDFlow was evaluated on OGBench, a benchmark for offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning, including locomotion (antmaze, humanoidmaze) and manipulation (cube, scene, puzzle) tasks.
- **Performance:** The approach consistently achieved superior performance across all environments and task difficulties. For example, on `antmaze-giant-v0`, HDFlow obtained a 48% success rate compared to 35% for the next best method (DV). On `puzzle-4x6-v0`, where most baselines failed, HDFlow achieved a 14% success rate. These results highlight HDFlow's effectiveness and robustness in diverse and complex long-horizon planning tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces HDFlow, a novel hierarchical planning framework that advances the field of robotic manipulation for long-horizon, sparse-reward tasks. The work is significant for several reasons:

- **Hybrid Generative Architecture:** HDFlow’s core contribution is the principled combination of denoising diffusion models for high-level strategic subgoal generation and rectified flow models for fast, low-level dense trajectory synthesis. This hybrid approach addresses the computational bottleneck of purely diffusion-based planners, demonstrating that different generative paradigms can be optimally leveraged at different levels of a planning hierarchy. This modularity allows for the exploitation of the exploratory strengths of diffusion and the efficiency of ODE-based rectified flow.
- **Enhanced Plan Quality and Feasibility:** The integration of a manifold-aware Energy-Based Model (EBM) guidance mechanism is a key advancement. This EBM learns to differentiate between successful and unsuccessful strategies, providing a dense reward signal that steers the high-level planner towards viable solutions. Furthermore, the manifold projection step mitigates the issue of guidance-induced manifold deviation, ensuring that generated latent subgoals remain within the feasible space, which is critical for robust performance in complex, sparse-reward settings.
- **Structured Latent Space Learning:** The use of a contrastively-trained world model is instrumental in creating a semantically rich and control-relevant latent space. This structured representation, learned through a combination of reconstruction, inverse dynamics, and progress-aware contrastive objectives, provides a solid foundation for the hierarchical planners to operate efficiently and effectively.
- **Demonstrated Robustness and Generalization:** The comprehensive evaluation across challenging benchmarks like FurnitureBench, RLBench, and OGBench, in both simulation and real-world scenarios, underscores HDFlow’s robustness, efficiency, and generalization capabilities. The consistent outperformance of state-of-the-art baselines on diverse manipulation and locomotion tasks highlights its potential for practical application in complex robotic systems.
- **Potential for Real-Time Robotic Applications:** The improved computational efficiency of the low-level rectified flow planner, in contrast to iterative diffusion, makes HDFlow more suitable for real-time robotic control. This could facilitate the deployment of robots for intricate, multi-stage assembly and manipulation tasks in unstructured or dynamically changing environments.

**Limitations:** The paper acknowledges that HDFlow currently relies on resource-intensive datasets comprising both successful and failed demonstrations for training the world model and the EBM. Reducing the data collection requirements represents a potential avenue for future research.

**Potential Impact:** The advancements presented by HDFlow could contribute to the development of more autonomous and capable robotic systems, particularly in domains requiring long-horizon planning and precise manipulation. By offering a more robust and efficient planning framework, it could accelerate research and development in areas such as manufacturing, logistics, and assistive robotics, where complex manipulation tasks are prevalent. The framework’s design also encourages further exploration into hybrid generative models and explicit guidance mechanisms in hierarchical decision-making for artificial intelligence.
