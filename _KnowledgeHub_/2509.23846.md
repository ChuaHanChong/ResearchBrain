# Adversarial Diffusion for Robust Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> AD-RRL (Adversarial Diffusion for Robust Reinforcement Learning) integrates diffusion models with Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) optimization to train policies that are robust to environmental uncertainties. The method generates challenging, low-reward trajectories to train the agent, demonstrating superior robustness and competitive nominal performance across various MuJoCo continuous control tasks while maintaining sample efficiency.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies often exhibit fragility when deployed in real-world environments with even slight deviations in dynamics, limiting their applicability in safety-critical applications.
- Model-based RL (MBRL) approaches, while sample-efficient, traditionally suffer from compounding errors as autoregressive models propagate small prediction inaccuracies across timesteps.
- Existing robust RL methods face challenges with scalability or may not guarantee robustness in worst-case scenarios, and the potential of diffusion models for explicit robustness through targeted generation of challenging scenarios remained largely unexplored.

## Method

- The paper proposes Adversarial Diffusion for Robust Reinforcement Learning (**AD-RRL**), an online ==Dyna-style== RL framework that combines diffusion models for trajectory generation with CVaR-based robustness.
- A novel 'Adversarial Diffusion' (AD) technique is introduced, which uses guided diffusion to generate 'worst-case' trajectories by biasing the generation process towards lower cumulative rewards.
- The method leverages a dual formulation of CVaR to provide theoretical guidance for selecting diffusion constants, ensuring that the generated adversarial trajectories remain within a defined risk envelope and target the lower α-percentile of returns.

## Results

- AD-RRL consistently demonstrated superior robustness and performance compared to various state-of-the-art baselines across MuJoCo continuous control tasks when physics parameters were varied at test time.
- The algorithm achieved the best mean return on four out of five MuJoCo tasks in the nominal training environment, indicating that robustness was gained without sacrificing nominal optimality.
- AD-RRL exhibited competitive or faster convergence and comparable final performance to baselines, even outperforming model-free methods when they were provided with twice the amount of training data, highlighting its sample efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning from adversarially generated, low-reward trajectories, guided by CVaR, is an effective strategy for developing policies that are more resilient to unforeseen dynamics perturbations.
> - Diffusion models can effectively mitigate compounding errors inherent in step-by-step model-based RL by generating entire trajectories 'all at once' through a denoising process.
> - The integration of CVaR's dual interpretation with diffusion model guidance offers a principled approach to systematically generate worst-case scenarios, enabling agents to learn from challenging and critical situations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes across various complex domains, demonstrating super-human performance in games and control tasks. However, a significant challenge in deploying RL agents, especially in safety-critical applications like robotics, finance, or healthcare, is their inherent sensitivity to environmental uncertainties, modeling errors, or discrepancies between simulation and reality (the "Sim2Real gap"). Minor changes in dynamics, sensor noise, or hardware variations can lead to substantial performance degradation.

Model-based RL (MBRL) attempts to improve sample efficiency by learning a model of the environment dynamics and planning within this learned model. Traditional autoregressive MBRL methods, which predict the next state based on the current predicted state, suffer from compounding errors: small prediction inaccuracies accumulate over longer horizons, causing the simulated trajectory to diverge from the true dynamics.

Recently, diffusion models have emerged as a method to mitigate these compounding errors in MBRL. By generating entire trajectories "all at once" rather than step-by-step, they can reduce error accumulation. Furthermore, diffusion models offer flexibility through conditional sampling, allowing them to generate data from specific distributions.

Robust RL (RRL) is an established field that addresses the issue of uncertainty by various means, including adversarial training (introducing worst-case perturbations) and risk-sensitive optimization, such as optimizing Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR). CVaR focuses on the lower tail of the return distribution, aiming to improve performance under adverse outcomes or model inaccuracies.

This work integrates these two distinct lines of research: diffusion models for trajectory generation and CVaR optimization for robustness. It builds upon existing diffusion-based MBRL methods like PolyGRAD, which uses diffusion for dynamics modeling in an online, Dyna-style framework, but pivots its focus from generating high-reward trajectories to generating *challenging* or *adversarial* ones. The paper positions itself as an advancement in developing robust RL agents that can handle modeling mismatches and environmental uncertainty by combining the trajectory-level generation capabilities of diffusion models with the risk-aware objectives of CVaR-based robustness.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an RL algorithm capable of learning policies that are robust to uncertainties and modeling errors in environment dynamics, without sacrificing nominal performance or sample efficiency.

The motivation stems from several limitations observed in current RL paradigms:
1. **Sensitivity to Dynamics Changes:** Standard RL policies, while performing well in their training environment, often exhibit fragility when deployed in environments with even slight deviations in dynamics (e.g., changes in mass, friction, or gravity). This lack of generalization restricts their applicability in real-world scenarios where perfect models are rarely available.
2. **Compounding Errors in Model-Based RL:** Model-based approaches, while sample-efficient, traditionally suffer from compounding errors. Autoregressive models, which predict state transitions sequentially, propagate small errors across timesteps, leading to significant deviations from true trajectories over longer horizons.
3. **Limitations of Existing Robust RL Methods:** While adversarial and risk-sensitive RL methods exist, they often face challenges. Adversarial methods might struggle with scalability in complex environments, while methods like Domain Randomization (DR) aim for average performance over a distribution of dynamics, potentially failing to guarantee robustness in worst-case scenarios.
4. **Underutilization of Diffusion Model Capabilities:** Diffusion models have shown promise in generating full trajectories, thereby mitigating compounding errors. However, their potential for explicitly enhancing policy robustness through targeted generation of challenging scenarios remained an underexplored avenue. Prior work predominantly used conditioning to generate *high-reward* trajectories, whereas the authors hypothesize that learning from *low-reward* or *adversarial* trajectories can lead to more robust policies.

To address these points, the authors propose to leverage the conditional sampling capability of diffusion models to generate "worst-case" trajectories, informed by the Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) framework. By training on these difficult-to-handle scenarios, the RL agent is expected to learn a policy that is inherently more resilient to unforeseen dynamics perturbations. The specific objective is to maximize the CVaR of the cumulative return, which translates to optimizing performance in the lower α-percentile of trajectory returns, thereby explicitly targeting robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Adversarial Diffusion for Robust Reinforcement Learning (AD-RRL), integrates diffusion models with CVaR-based robustness within a Dyna-style online RL framework.

#### 3.1. Background Concepts

- **Markov Decision Process (MDP):** The problem is framed within an MDP defined by state space S, action space A, transition probabilities P, reward function r, and discount factor γ. The goal is to learn an optimal policy π\* maximizing the expected cumulative return.
- **Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR):** CVaR is employed as the robustness metric. For a trajectory return Z(τ) at a confidence level α, CVaR is defined as the expected return on the lower α-portion of its distribution. Its dual formulation states that CVaR can be seen as the expected value of Z under a worst-case perturbed distribution ξ(τ), where ξ acts as an adversarial perturbation to the trajectory distribution, budgeted within a "risk envelope" Uα,πCVaR. This dual perspective directly links CVaR optimization to robustness against model misspecification, as it means maximizing reward under adverse distributions.
- **Diffusion Models:** These generative models learn to reverse a gradual noise corruption process, progressively refining noisy inputs into data samples (in this case, trajectories). They are parameterized to generate data samples τ₀:N. **Guided Diffusion** enhances this process by incorporating a classifier p(y|τ₀), using its gradient to steer generation towards samples aligned with a specific property y (e.g., high optimality).

#### 3.2. Adversarial Diffusion (AD)

The core innovation is "Adversarial Diffusion" (AD), a guided diffusion model designed to generate trajectories that are challenging for the agent and result in low rewards. This process effectively samples from the set of trajectories Cα = {τ : Z(τ) ≤ VaRα(Z)}, representing the worst α-percentile.

- **Sampling Suboptimal Trajectories:** To guide the diffusion process towards Cα, a classifier pθ(τ₀ ∈ Cα | τ₀) is used. The authors approximate this unknown classifier with a smooth function: exp(−c₀ Σγᵗrᵗ), where c₀ > 0. This approximation intuitively biases generation towards trajectories with lower cumulative rewards.
- **Perturbed Diffusion Model:** Lemma 4.1 establishes that this conditional sampling procedure modifies the standard diffusion step. The mean of the Gaussian denoising distribution is perturbed by subtracting cᵢΣᵢgᵢ, where gᵢ = ∇τ Z(μθ(τᵢ,i)) is the gradient of the cumulative reward with respect to the trajectory at the current diffusion step, and Σᵢ is the covariance matrix at step i. This effectively "pushes" the generated trajectory towards lower reward values.
- **Selecting Guidance Constants (cᵢ):** The constants cᵢ for each diffusion step are crucial. Lemma 4.2 shows that the adversarially guided diffusion model ¯pθ(τ₀) can be expressed as a product-form perturbation ξ(τ₀)pθ(τ₀) of the original diffusion model pθ(τ₀). To ensure that this perturbation ξ(τ₀) remains within the CVaR risk envelope (i.e., ξ(τ₀) ≤ 1/α), Proposition 4.3 provides conditions for selecting cᵢ. Specifically, cᵢ is chosen as the minimum of two terms: one related to the magnitude of the reward gradient and the target confidence level α, and another ensuring the generated trajectories remain within a bounded space. For common cosine noise schedules where Σᵢ is diagonal, a simplified condition for cᵢ is derived.

#### 3.3. AD-RRL Algorithm (Algorithm 1)

AD-RRL operates in a Dyna-like iterative loop:
1. **Data Collection:** The current policy πω interacts with the real environment to collect trajectories, which are added to a data buffer D.
2. **Model Improvement (Algorithm 3):** The diffusion model ¯pθ (noise prediction function ϵθ) and the cumulative reward function Zϕ are trained on the collected data D. ¯pθ is trained to reconstruct trajectories, while Zϕ learns to predict the cumulative reward of trajectories.
3. **Adversarial Trajectory Sampling (Algorithm 2):** Using the trained adversarial denoising model ¯pθ and reward model Zϕ, challenging synthetic trajectories {ˆτ} are generated. This involves:
 - Starting with Gaussian noise and a real initial state (inpainting).
 - Iteratively denoising the trajectory. At each step, cᵢ is computed based on Proposition 4.3, and the gradient gᵢ = ∇s Zϕ is evaluated. This gradient guides the diffusion process towards states that minimize cumulative reward.
 - Actions within the generated trajectories are made consistent with the perturbed states using PolyGRAD's policy-guided diffusion method.
4. **Policy Improvement:** The collected synthetic adversarial trajectories {ˆτ} are then used to train the policy πω via an on-policy RL algorithm (Advantage Actor-Critic with GAE in this implementation).

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- Policy πω is a Gaussian policy.
- Diffusion model and reward function Zϕ are MLPs with skip connections and learnable embeddings for diffusion steps.
- Hyperparameters for A2C, diffusion training, and reward model training are provided.
- Specific choices for ρ and ηᵢ(α, N) are detailed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of AD-RRL was conducted on several continuous control tasks from the MuJoCo suite: InvertedPendulum, Reacher, Hopper, HalfCheetah, and Walker. Agents were trained for 1.5M steps in the nominal environment, and their robustness was assessed by varying key physics parameters at test time.

#### 4.1. Baseline Methods

The performance of AD-RRL was compared against a suite of baselines:
- **Robust RL methods:**
 - **Domain Randomization (DR-PPO):** PPO trained with randomized physics parameters, aiming for average robustness.
 - **Max-Min TD3 (M2TD3):** An actor-critic method optimizing a maximin objective under worst-case dynamics.
 - **CVaR-PPO (CPPO):** PPO extended with a CVaR constraint for risk-sensitive optimization.
- **Diffusion-based RL:**
 - **PolyGRAD:** The diffusion-based model-based RL method that AD-RRL builds upon, which uses policy-guided diffusion to generate high-reward trajectories.
- **Model-free RL:**
 - **TRPO:** Trust Region Policy Optimization.
 - **PPO:** Proximal Policy Optimization.

#### 4.2. Robustness Under Varying Physical Parameters

- **Superior Robustness:** Across most environments (Hopper, Walker, HalfCheetah) and varied parameters (body mass, ground friction, gravity, actuator gears), AD-RRL consistently demonstrated superior robustness and performance compared to all baselines. It exhibited greater stability and achieved higher cumulative rewards even under significant deviations from nominal physics.
- **Comparison with PolyGRAD:** A direct comparison with PolyGRAD, the foundational algorithm, revealed that AD-RRL's adversarial modifications substantially improved performance under diverse test-time conditions, highlighting enhanced robustness to large parameter shifts and model misspecifications. While PolyGRAD sometimes showed slightly better nominal performance, it sacrificed robustness.
- **Extreme Changes:** For extreme changes in certain parameters (e.g., very high cart mass in InvertedPendulum), AD-RRL's performance did degrade, though it often remained superior to other algorithms. This is hypothesized to be due to the model generating plausible, yet challenging, trajectories that might not encompass dynamics outside a certain realistic range.

#### 4.3. Performance on the Nominal (Training) Environments

- **No Trade-off for Robustness:** AD-RRL achieved the best mean return on four out of five MuJoCo tasks in the nominal training environment. This indicates that the risk-sensitive training approach did not necessitate a trade-off between robustness and nominal optimality. On Hopper, PolyGRAD had a slightly higher mean, but within overlapping confidence intervals. For InvertedPendulum, multiple methods including AD-RRL reached the maximum score.

#### 4.4. Sample Efficiency

- **Matching or Faster Convergence:** The learning curves demonstrated that AD-RRL reached comparable or higher final performance with the same number of samples as the baselines and, in some cases (e.g., Cheetah and Walker), converged faster.
- **Ablation Study (Model-Free Baselines with 2x Samples):** An ablation study compared AD-RRL (1.5M samples) against model-free baselines (3M samples). Even with twice the training data, AD-RRL generally maintained its superior or on-par performance. This suggests that the adversarial guidance mechanism provides efficient learning, avoiding the need for extensive data collection that might lead to overfitting in nominal environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents AD-RRL, a novel approach that effectively addresses the critical challenge of robustness in reinforcement learning. By integrating the trajectory-generation capabilities of diffusion models with the risk-averse framework of Conditional Value at Risk, the method provides a systematic way to train RL agents that are resilient to environmental uncertainties and modeling errors.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Robustness without Sacrificing Performance:** The empirical results demonstrate that AD-RRL achieves superior robustness across a range of environments and physics parameter variations, while maintaining or even improving nominal performance and sample efficiency compared to state-of-the-art robust and non-robust baselines. This addresses a common challenge in robust RL, where robustness often comes at the cost of performance in ideal conditions.
- **Mitigation of Compounding Errors:** By leveraging diffusion models for "all at once" trajectory generation, AD-RRL inherently mitigates the compounding errors typical of step-by-step transition models, which is a known problem in model-based RL.
- **Novel Application of Conditional Value at Risk:** The integration of CVaR through "adversarial guidance" for diffusion models offers a principled way to generate worst-case trajectories, enabling agents to learn from challenging scenarios that might be rare or unexplored during standard interaction. This dual interpretation of CVaR provides a strong theoretical foundation for the adversarial sampling process.
- **Potential for Safety-Critical Applications:** The improved robustness of AD-RRL policies makes them potentially more suitable for deployment in safety-critical domains such as robotics, autonomous driving, healthcare, and finance, where real-world deviations from training conditions are inevitable and performance stability is paramount. Reduced sensitivity to Sim2Real gaps is a key implication.

**Potential Impact:**
The methodology could lead to more reliable and deployable RL systems in practical settings. By focusing on training with adversarial examples generated by the model itself, AD-RRL offers a data-efficient alternative to purely empirical methods like extensive domain randomization.

**Future Work:**
The authors identify several avenues for future research, including exploring alternative guidance methods for the diffusion process beyond the current CVaR framework, enhancing diffusion model architectures or algorithms to reduce computational overhead (as diffusion models can be slower than model-free baselines), and conducting Sim2Real studies to validate the approach in real-world hardware deployments. Addressing the assumption of smooth dynamics for gradient computations in more complex, non-smooth environments is also highlighted.
