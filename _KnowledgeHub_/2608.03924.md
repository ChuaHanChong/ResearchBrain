# ETA: A New Agentic Paradigm for Embodied Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenMOSS: ETA, a new agentic paradigm for embodied tasks, introduces a planner-centered, closed-loop architecture for general-purpose embodied AI. It integrates large language models as Planners with specialized robot capabilities as Tools, achieving up to 90.0% task success on 130 LIBERO manipulation tasks using a lightweight 3-Tool interface and GPT-5.6 Sol.

## Problem

- Current embodied AI systems, such as Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and World Action Models (WAM), struggle with generalization due to heavy reliance on training data and lack controllability over long task horizons.
- End-to-end observation-to-action learning approaches often lack transparency, making it difficult to inspect behavior, debug, and ensure robust adaptation to novel situations.
- There is a persistent gap between the advanced reasoning and planning capabilities of Large Language Model (LLM) agents in digital environments and their reliable application to physical interaction in embodied systems.

## Method

- The **Embodied Task Agent (ETA)** paradigm proposes a ==planner-centered, closed-loop architecture==, featuring an Agent (Planner), Interface, and World, operating on a strict ==runtime invariant== of fresh observation after every world-changing action.
- **OpenETA**, an open-source implementation, integrates foundation models as Planners with a registry of 44 Tools and Skills, enforcing stable contracts, ==execution gates, and trust boundaries== to orchestrate physical interactions.
- The system abstracts robot control into a modular Tool-based interface, enabling high-level LLM Planners to make sequential decisions based on verified physical outcomes rather than generating full programs upfront.

## Results

- OpenETA, utilizing GPT-5.6 Sol and a lightweight three-Tool interface, achieved a PASS@5 rate of **90.0%** on **130** LIBERO manipulation tasks, demonstrating improved performance with stronger Planner models.
- The full OpenETA configuration, leveraging **44** Tools and GPT-5.6 Luna, attained an overall success rate of **14.0%** across **40** LIBERO manipulation tasks, with dominant failure modes related to long-horizon planning and perceptual rechecking.
- Qualitative hardware demonstrations on a UR5e arm confirmed the practical feasibility of deploying the closed-loop Tool pipeline on real robots, while also highlighting sim-to-real bottlenecks in depth perception and trajectory control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A structured, planner-centered architecture with explicit verification and fresh observations after each physical interaction can significantly enhance the controllability and generalizability of embodied agents.
> - Powerful general-purpose LLM Planners, such as GPT-5.6 Sol, can achieve high success rates on complex robot manipulation tasks using a minimal and stable set of physical interaction primitives (e.g., `observe`, `mark_point`, `move_to`).
> - Establishing robust promotion gates for self-evolution, while not yet yielding consistent performance gains, is crucial for validating experience-based updates and ensuring reliability and safety in physical robot learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI is actively pursuing the development of general-purpose robotic systems capable of performing diverse tasks in varied environments. Current approaches in embodied intelligence primarily rely on end-to-end observation-to-action learning, exemplified by Vision-Language-Action models (VLAs) like PaLM-E, RT-2, and OpenVLA, and World Action Models (WAMs) such as DreamZero and DreamDojo. These models translate visual observations and language instructions directly into robot actions or predict future states and actions.

Despite advancements, these end-to-end systems exhibit limitations, including heavy dependence on the breadth of robot training data for generalization and challenges in controlling and inspecting behavior over long task horizons. Their performance can be sensitive to data distribution, making it difficult to adapt to unfamiliar tasks or environments reliably.

Concurrently, large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated emergent capabilities in digital environments, encompassing reasoning, planning, tool use, and error correction. Coding agents, built upon LLMs, can decompose goals, navigate digital interfaces, execute actions, evaluate results, and learn from experience by storing successful procedures. Approaches like ReAct, Reflexion, ExpeL, and Voyager have explored iterative interaction and experience-based improvement in digital agents, with some recent demonstrations extending these concepts to robot operation.

This work addresses the gap between the advanced reasoning capabilities of digital LLM agents and the practical demands of reliable physical interaction for embodied systems. While existing VLM coding models for robot tasks have connected LLMs to robot APIs, often generating executable programs or using 3D scene graphs for planning, many operate by generating a full program that is then refined, rather than making high-level decisions after every physical action. The Embodied Task Agent (ETA) paradigm positions itself within this landscape by introducing a structured, planner-centered, closed-loop approach that treats specialist VLA or WAM models as Tools, emphasizing explicit verification and fresh observation after each physical interaction, thereby aiming for enhanced controllability and generalizability in physical agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the development of general-purpose robots towards a "ChatGPT moment" in embodied intelligence. This aspirational goal is characterized by three core capabilities for an embodied system:

1. **Generality**: The system should be capable of understanding and executing new tasks by reusing or composing its perception, planning, and control capabilities across a range of objects, environments, and robot embodiments. This contrasts with traditional approaches that often require a dedicated policy for each specific task.
2. **Controllability**: The system needs to maintain an explicit and interpretable task state throughout long-duration interactions. Each physical action must be exposed through a bounded interface, and subsequent decisions must be explicitly driven by the actual, verified outcomes of physical executions. This design prioritizes transparency and the ability to inspect the robot's decision-making process.
3. **Self-improvement**: The system should possess mechanisms to learn from its interactions, converting both successful and failed experiences into reusable knowledge and "Skills." A critical aspect of this is the validation of any learned updates, ensuring that new experiences demonstrably improve future behavior without introducing regressions before they are integrated.

The motivation for this work stems from the limitations observed in contemporary embodied intelligence systems. Current end-to-end observation-to-action learning paradigms, including Vision-Language-Action models, typically rely heavily on the diversity and coverage of their training data for effective generalization. Furthermore, controlling and inspecting the behavior of these systems over long and complex tasks remains challenging, hindering debugging, safety assurances, and robust adaptation to novel situations.

To address these challenges, the authors propose the Embodied Task Agent (ETA) paradigm. This paradigm is motivated by the success of digital agents in reasoning and tool use, seeking to extend these principles to the physical domain. By introducing a task-level Planner that orchestrates specialized models and Tools within a structured observe-decide-act-verify loop, ETA aims to enable greater generality, explicit control, and a systematic framework for self-improvement in physical agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces the Embodied Task Agent (ETA) paradigm, an architecture designed for extending digital agents into the physical world, and its open-source implementation, OpenETA. The core of ETA is a planner-centered information flow operating on a strict runtime invariant and clear trust boundaries.

**Embodied Task Agent (ETA) Paradigm:**
ETA operates on an iterative loop involving three primary roles:
1. **Agent (Planner)**: This component understands the natural-language goal, maintains a working memory, and proposes the next structured command (Tool call) based on the current observation and task state. The Planner's decisions are task-level and do not involve direct, unrestricted control of the physical world.
2. **Interface**: This intermediary layer receives commands from the Agent. It validates the command's structure, verifies the Tool contract, checks provenance and authority, and confirms any prerequisite evidence before dispatching an atomic action. The Interface acts as a gate, preventing the Agent from directly manipulating the World.
3. **World**: This layer executes the atomic action. It represents either a simulator or a real robot. After execution, the World returns a Tool result, an environment receipt (trusted evidence of what changed), and a fresh observation of the updated state to the Agent. Planning then resumes only after these records are integrated into working memory.

**Runtime Invariant and Trust Boundary:**
A fundamental principle of ETA is the runtime invariant: "Execute only one world-changing action at a time. Afterward, obtain a fresh observation before executing the next state-dependent action." This rule prevents unobserved state changes and ensures that subsequent decisions are based on verified, up-to-date physical evidence. The trust boundary explicitly separates the Agent's intelligence (decision-making) from the Interface's execution authority (validation and dispatch) and the World's physical execution and evidence provision.

**OpenETA System Implementation:**
OpenETA implements the ETA paradigm as an open-source runtime and experimental framework, integrating foundation models, Tools, Skills, an Interface, World backends, and evaluation mechanisms within its observe–decide–act–verify loop.

- **Agent Components:**
 - **Tools**: Host-registered atomic capabilities with stable parameter and return contracts. OpenETA's default registry contains 44 Tools categorized into:
 - *Perception and localization*: e.g., `observe`, `molmopoint`, `sam3`.
 - *Geometry and manipulation planning*: e.g., `grasp_pose_estimate`, `anygrasp`, `anyplace`.
 - *Safety and physical execution*: e.g., `ik_preview_check`, `move_to`, `gripper_control`.
 - *Environment and evidence*: e.g., `create_simulator_env`, `materialize_mcp_images`.
 - *Agent support*: e.g., `save_memory`, `web_search`, `register_skill`.
 Each Tool declares a side-effect class (e.g., `read_only`, `world_mutating`) which determines whether fresh observations are required.
 - **Skills**: Editable textual guidance that informs the model's reasoning, checking, and recovery processes. Skills do not directly execute actions; the Agent must still explicitly select atomic Tools.
 - **AtomAction**: A subset of `world_mutating` Tools that physically change the World (e.g., end-effector motion). Skill text cannot bypass `AtomActions`.
- **Interface Components:**
 - **Stable Contracts and Normalized Results**: The Planner submits `tool_call` or `response` commands. The host runtime defines Tool names, parameters, and side-effect declarations, which the Agent cannot alter. All Tool returns are normalized into `ToolResult` objects containing outputs, artifacts, state changes, and diagnostics.
 - **Execution Gates and Obligations**: The Interface blocks execution if safety checks fail or prerequisite evidence is missing. Explicit obligations (e.g., `fresh_observation_obligation` after a world-changing action, `explicit_selection_obligation` for ambiguous targets) constrain subsequent Tool calls until resolved.
 - **Trusted Receipts and Backend Isolation**: The Interface connects Tools to simulators or robots via adapter boundaries (Model Context Protocol, MCP), abstracting low-level details from the Planner. Reward and termination are only accepted if host-attested and matching current execution IDs.
- **World Layer**: Implemented by Simulator MCP and Real-robot MCP services. These provide environment lifecycle management, normalized observations, atomic motion/gripper execution, reward, and termination. The Interface maps these services to common planner-facing Tool contracts, maintaining a security boundary that prevents the Agent from accessing privileged ground-truth state.

**Experimental Configurations:**
OpenETA was evaluated in two main configurations:
1. **Full OpenETA Configuration**: Uses the broad registry of 44 Tools. The Planner was GPT-5.6 Luna. Evaluation was conducted on 40 LIBERO manipulation tasks (4 suites, 10 tasks each), with 10 seeds per task, totaling 400 episodes. No additional task-specific policy training was used.
2. **OpenETA for Codex Configuration**: Designed as a lightweight plugin, exposing only three embodied Tools: `observe`, `mark_point`, and `move_to`.
 - `observe`: Requests live images (third-person, wrist, orthographic views).
 - `mark_point`: Converts 2D pixel selections (via multi-view or single-view modes) into 3D World coordinates.
 - `move_to`: Moves the gripper to a specified target position and orientation, offering a preview before execution.
 This configuration was evaluated on 130 LIBERO tasks (including LIBERO-90) using GPT-5.6 Luna, Terra, and Sol as Planners, each with medium reasoning effort, across 5 ordered seeds per task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented results from extensive simulation studies using the LIBERO benchmark, experiments on constrained self-evolution, and qualitative real-robot demonstrations.

**Full OpenETA Baseline (40 LIBERO tasks, 10 seeds each):**
Using GPT-5.6 Luna with the full 44-Tool registry, OpenETA achieved an overall success rate of 14.0% (56 successful episodes out of 400).
- **Suite-level success rates**: Object (26.0%), Goal (21.0%), Spatial (8.0%), and Long / LIBERO-10 (1.0%).
- **Task coverage**: 18 out of 40 tasks succeeded at least once across the 10 seeds.
- **Resource utilization**: The average successful episode required 30.7 turns and Tool calls, with a mean wall time of 1045.0 seconds. Failed episodes had higher average turns (45.5) and wall time (1866.6 seconds).
- **Failure modes**: The dominant terminal failure label was `episode_timeout` (62.5% of failures), followed by `unattended_ask_human` (19.2%) and `max_turns` (10.2%). This indicated challenges with long-horizon subgoal tracking, perceptual rechecking, placement relations, and effective budget management. Three episodes were affected by simulator handle expiration, classified as infrastructure contamination.

**OpenETA for Codex Results (130 LIBERO tasks, 5 seeds each):**
With the lightweight three-Tool interface (`observe`, `mark_point`, `move_to`), performance showed a clear improvement with stronger Planner models.
- **PASS@1 (tasks solved on the first seed)**: GPT-5.6 Luna solved 21 tasks, Terra solved 58 tasks, and Sol solved 92 tasks.
- **PASS@5 (tasks solved within the first five seeds)**:
 - GPT-5.6 Luna: 62/130 tasks (47.7%).
 - GPT-5.6 Terra: 83/130 tasks (63.8%).
 - GPT-5.6 Sol: 117/130 tasks (90.0%).
- **Qualitative observations**: Sol demonstrated improved performance on tasks requiring precise grasp-point localization, exhibiting fewer empty grasps compared to Terra and Luna. It also made more frequent use of the `move_to` Tool's pose preview feature before execution.

**Constrained Agent Self-Evolution:**
Experiments explored self-improvement through three forms of updates: task-local Skills, exact-task playbooks, and stage-local deltas. The core finding was that no self-evolution candidate successfully passed all the defined promotion gates (deterministic contract check, isolated review, same-task replay, and paired held-out evaluation).
- Broad Skill edits sometimes altered single-run outcomes but without stable or persistent gains.
- Exact-task playbooks performed worse than their paired baselines, consuming more turns and leading to more timeouts.
- Stage-local updates failed to reproduce the source success, preventing their evaluation on held-out tasks.
This indicates that while the promotion gates effectively prevented unsupported or regressive updates from being integrated, the current self-evolution methods did not yield reproducible improvements in task success. Candidates often introduced additional visual checks or recovery rules, increasing Planner turns and timeouts without addressing underlying failure causes.

**From Simulation Closed Loops to Real Robots:**
Qualitative hardware demonstrations were conducted on a UR5e arm with a Robotiq gripper.
- **Demonstrations**: Recordings visually covered a complete sponge-to-tray sequence (contact approach, lift probe/attachment check, multi-waypoint transport, final placement) and a bell-pepper grasp and transport.
- **Sim2Real bottlenecks identified**: Depth perception from RealSense cameras was less complete and reliable than in simulation, impacting pose estimation. Some lateral-grasp target poses led to acceleration-limit protective stops, suggesting potential issues with controller tuning or trajectory shaping.
- **Status**: The hardware integration is at an interface-level, demonstrating that the closed-loop Tool pipeline can drive the robot through manipulation stages. However, this does not constitute claims of primitive execution success, task success, or safety certification.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces the Embodied Task Agent (ETA) paradigm, representing a structured approach for integrating advanced AI models into physical robot systems. Its significance lies in proposing a methodology that aims to enhance the generality, controllability, and self-improvement capabilities of embodied agents, addressing known limitations of purely end-to-end learning systems.

**Key Contributions and Advancements:**
- **Structured Embodied Intelligence**: ETA offers a planner-centered, closed-loop architecture that explicitly separates the Agent's task-level decision-making from the Interface's execution authority and the World's physical interaction. This design fosters greater transparency and inspectability of robot behavior.
- **Modularity and Extensibility**: The OpenETA framework, as an open-source implementation, provides modular Planners, composable Tools and Skills, auditable memory, and replayable trajectories. This modularity facilitates the integration of diverse specialist models (e.g., VLA/WAMs as Tools) and supports iterative development.
- **Enhanced Controllability**: The runtime invariant, which mandates a fresh observation after every world-changing action, coupled with execution gates and explicit obligations, ensures that all physical actions are verified and decisions are based on current, trusted evidence. This approach contributes to safer and more predictable robot operation.
- **Demonstrated Capability with Minimal Interface**: The OpenETA for Codex results highlight that powerful general-purpose Planners can achieve high success rates (90.0% PASS@5 with GPT-5.6 Sol) on complex manipulation tasks using a remarkably small and stable set of physical interaction primitives (`observe`, `mark_point`, `move_to`). This suggests a pathway for abstracting complex robotic control into a manageable, universal interface.
- **Framework for Self-Improvement**: While current self-evolution experiments did not yield performance gains, the robust promotion gates are significant. They demonstrate a principled mechanism for validating experience-based updates before deployment, prioritizing reliability and preventing regressions in physical systems. This establishes a foundational framework for future research into robot learning and adaptation.
- **Bridging Sim-to-Real**: OpenETA provides common interfaces for both simulation and real robots, facilitating the transfer of task-level intelligence. The qualitative real-robot demonstrations, while acknowledging current bottlenecks, indicate the practical feasibility of deploying the closed-loop Tool pipeline on physical hardware.

**Potential Impact:**
The ETA paradigm and OpenETA framework have the potential to impact the development of more robust, flexible, and inspectable general-purpose robots. By providing a structured way for AI models to interact with the physical world, it could accelerate progress towards robots that can adapt to new tasks and environments with minimal human intervention. The emphasis on verifiable evidence and constrained self-improvement is critical for building trust and ensuring safety in real-world robotic deployments. Future work, including addressing current limitations in long-horizon planning, perception, and expanding the Tool set, will be crucial for realizing the full potential of this paradigm in diverse and complex embodied AI applications.
