# DETRs Beat YOLOs on Real-time Object Detection

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Baidu Inc. developed RT-DETR, an end-to-end and NMS-free object detector that leverages optimized Transformer architecture to achieve state-of-the-art real-time performance. RT-DETR-R50 achieved 53.1% AP on COCO val2017 at 108 FPS on a T4 GPU, outperforming leading YOLO models while demonstrating a 21x FPS increase over prior strong DETR variants.

## Problem

- The reliance of current real-time object detectors (e.g., YOLO series) on Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) introduces computational overhead, inference latency, and instability due to sensitive hyperparameters.
- End-to-end Transformer-based detectors (DETRs), while NMS-free, historically suffer from very high computational costs and slow training convergence, making them impractical for real-time applications.
- A lack of a true end-to-end speed benchmark that accounts for NMS time prevents accurate comparisons of real-time object detectors.

## Method

- An **Efficient Hybrid Encoder** is introduced, which decouples ==intra-scale attention== (performed only on the highest-level feature map, S5) from ==CNN-based cross-scale feature fusion== for improved computational efficiency.
- **Uncertainty-Minimal Query Selection** is proposed to optimize initial object queries by minimizing the ==epistemic uncertainty== between predicted localization and classification distributions.
- The model supports ==flexible speed tuning== by adjusting the number of ==decoder layers== at inference time, allowing adaptation to various scenarios without retraining.

## Results

- RT-DETR-R50 achieves **53.1% AP** on COCO val2017 at **108 FPS** on a T4 GPU (TensorRT FP16), outperforming YOLOv8-L (**52.9% AP**, **71 FPS**) in both accuracy and speed.
- Compared to prior DETRs, RT-DETR-R50 demonstrates a **2.2% AP** accuracy improvement and an astounding **21 times** increase in FPS over DINO-Deformable-DETR-R50 (**50.9% AP**, **5 FPS**).
- Ablation studies confirm the Efficient Hybrid Encoder's role in latency reduction and AP improvement, and Uncertainty-Minimal Query Selection boosts accuracy by **0.8% AP** by selecting higher quality initial queries.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Transformer-based detectors can achieve and surpass the real-time performance of CNN-based models like YOLO by optimizing the encoder and query initialization.
> - Decoupling intra-scale attention to high-level features and using CNN-based cross-scale feature fusion in an 'Efficient Hybrid Encoder' drastically improves speed while maintaining accuracy.
> - Optimizing initial object queries by minimizing uncertainty (discrepancy between predicted localization and classification distributions) leads to higher quality queries and improved detection accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Object detection is a cornerstone of computer vision, with real-time performance being critical for numerous applications such as autonomous driving, video surveillance, and robotic control. Historically, **CNN-based architectures**, particularly the **YOLO (You Only Look Once) series**, have dominated the real-time object detection landscape. YOLOs are renowned for their impressive trade-off between speed and accuracy, achieving high inference speeds by processing an entire image in a single pass. However, a fundamental characteristic of YOLOs, and many other CNN-based detectors, is their reliance on **Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS)** as a post-processing step. NMS is crucial for filtering out redundant and overlapping bounding box predictions, but it has significant drawbacks: it is computationally intensive, adds to inference latency, introduces hyperparameters (confidence and IoU thresholds) that are sensitive to scenario variations, and can lead to instability in both speed and accuracy. The paper rigorously quantifies the negative impact of NMS, showing how its execution time is influenced by hyperparameters and the number of predicted boxes.

More recently, the advent of the Transformer architecture in natural language processing spurred its adoption in computer vision, leading to the development of **end-to-end Transformer-based detectors (DETRs)**. Pioneered by the original DETR, these models offer a streamlined, NMS-free pipeline by formulating object detection as a direct set prediction problem, leveraging bipartite matching to directly output a fixed number of unique detections. This eliminates the complexities and drawbacks associated with NMS, offering a more elegant and end-to-end approach. However, early DETRs suffered from significant limitations: extremely high computational costs (especially due to the self-attention mechanism on long sequences of image features) and slow training convergence, making them impractical for real-time applications. Subsequent variants like Deformable-DETR, DAB-DETR, DN-DETR, and DINO addressed some of these issues, particularly convergence speed and computational efficiency, but largely remained too slow for real-time deployment. For instance, DINO-Deformable-DETR-R50, a strong DETR variant, still operates at a mere 5 FPS on a T4 GPU, rendering it non-real-time.

This paper positions **RT-DETR** as a groundbreaking development that bridges the gap between these two paradigms. It aims to achieve the best of both worlds: the real-time inference speed characteristic of YOLOs while retaining the end-to-end, NMS-free simplicity and potentially higher accuracy of DETRs. By specifically addressing the computational bottlenecks of Transformer encoders and the quality of object query initialization in DETRs, RT-DETR pushes the boundaries of what is possible in real-time object detection. It directly challenges the long-held dominance of YOLO by demonstrating that a Transformer-based model can not only match but *outperform* YOLOs in their traditional stronghold of speed, while also offering the inherent advantages of an end-to-end architecture. This work represents a significant step forward in making Transformer-based models a viable and superior alternative for real-time vision tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of RT-DETR stem from the inherent limitations observed in the prevailing real-time object detection frameworks:

- **Addressing NMS Bottlenecks in YOLO:** The YOLO series, despite its popularity for real-time detection, is fundamentally hampered by Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS). The authors identify NMS as a "negative impact" that "not only slows down the inference speed but also introduces hyperparameters that cause instability in both the speed and accuracy." Furthermore, the need to "carefully select the appropriate NMS thresholds" for different scenarios (e.g., recall vs. accuracy emphasis) hinders practical deployment. The core motivation is to eliminate this dependency.
- **Overcoming Computational Costs in DETRs:** While end-to-end Transformer-based detectors (DETRs) offer an attractive NMS-free alternative, their "high computational cost prevents them from meeting real-time detection requirements." This means that the architectural advantage of being NMS-free hasn't translated into an inference speed advantage for DETRs. The authors are thus motivated to make DETRs computationally efficient enough for real-time scenarios.
- **Achieving State-of-the-Art Real-time Performance:** The overarching goal is to explore "whether DETRs can be extended to real-time scenarios and outperform the advanced YOLO detectors in both speed and accuracy." This ambition implies a desire to establish a new state-of-the-art for real-time object detection by leveraging the strengths of DETRs while mitigating their weaknesses.
- **Improving End-to-End Speed Benchmark:** The paper also explicitly motivates the need for a fair "end-to-end speed benchmark" that includes NMS time, as traditional YOLO speed reports often exclude this crucial post-processing step. This objective aims to provide a more accurate comparison metric for real-time detectors.
- **Practical Applicability and Flexibility:** The authors aim for a detector that supports "flexible speed tuning by adjusting the number of decoder layers to adapt to various scenarios without retraining," enhancing its practical utility in diverse real-world applications where computational resources or latency requirements might vary.

In essence, the motivation is to synthesize the best attributes of both YOLO and DETR architectures into a novel framework that is fast, accurate, NMS-free, and adaptable for widespread real-time deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RT-DETR is built upon the advanced DETR framework, focusing on a two-step optimization process: first, maintaining accuracy while improving speed, and second, maintaining speed while improving accuracy. Its architecture comprises a backbone, an efficient hybrid encoder, and a Transformer decoder with auxiliary prediction heads.

#### 3.1. Efficient Hybrid Encoder

The core of RT-DETR's speed improvement lies in redesigning the Transformer encoder, identified as a computational bottleneck in previous DETRs due to the high cost of processing multi-scale features. The paper argues that simultaneously performing intra-scale and cross-scale feature interaction on concatenated multi-scale features is inefficient and redundant. The proposed **Efficient Hybrid Encoder** decouples these interactions into two main modules:

- **Attention-based Intra-scale Feature Interaction (AIFI):** This module aims to reduce computational cost by performing intra-scale interaction *only* on the highest-level feature map (S5, from the last stage of the backbone). The rationale is that high-level features possess richer semantic information, making attention operations more beneficial for capturing conceptual entity connections crucial for object localization and recognition. Lower-level features, lacking sufficient semantic concepts, are deemed unnecessary for such expensive interactions, reducing computational redundancy. AIFI employs a single Transformer layer for this purpose.
- **CNN-based Cross-scale Feature Fusion (CCFF):** This module is designed for efficient cross-scale feature fusion. Unlike prior Transformer-based approaches that might use attention for cross-scale interaction, CCFF leverages a PANet-style structure enhanced with convolutional layers. It incorporates "fusion blocks" composed of RepBlocks (from RepVGG) to effectively fuse two adjacent scale features into a new feature. This CNN-based fusion is computationally more efficient than Transformer attention for cross-scale interactions, especially given the typically less semantically rich nature of lower-level features.

By decoupling and optimizing these interactions, the hybrid encoder significantly reduces latency while maintaining or even improving accuracy.

#### 3.2. Uncertainty-Minimal Query Selection

To address the "hard-to-optimize object queries" and "considerable level of uncertainty" observed in selected encoder features in previous DETRs, RT-DETR proposes the **Uncertainty-Minimal Query Selection** scheme. Prior query selection methods typically rely solely on classification scores to select top-K features as initial queries. However, the authors argue that object quality depends on both classification and localization.

The new scheme explicitly models and optimizes the "epistemic uncertainty" of encoder features. It defines feature uncertainty `U(X)` as the discrepancy between the predicted distributions of localization `P(X)` and classification `C(X)` (`U(X) = ||P(X) - C(X)||`). This uncertainty is then integrated into the loss function for gradient-based optimization. By minimizing this uncertainty, the model is encouraged to select initial queries that have both high classification confidence and high localization accuracy, leading to "high-quality initial queries to the decoder" and thereby improving overall detection accuracy.

#### 3.3. Flexible Speed Tuning

Leveraging the multi-layer decoder architecture common in DETRs, RT-DETR supports flexible speed tuning without retraining. The authors observe that removing a few decoder layers at the end has minimal impact on accuracy but significantly boosts inference speed. This allows users to adjust the number of decoder layers at inference time to adapt the model's speed-accuracy trade-off to various real-time scenarios and hardware constraints.

Overall, the methodology combines careful architectural design for efficiency (hybrid encoder) with a novel optimization strategy for improved accuracy (uncertainty-minimal query selection), all within an end-to-end, NMS-free framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results presented in the paper demonstrate that RT-DETR achieves state-of-the-art performance in real-time object detection, significantly outperforming both advanced YOLO detectors and existing DETR models in terms of speed and accuracy.

- **Superior Performance against YOLO Series:**
 - **RT-DETR-R50** achieves **53.1% AP** on COCO val2017 and **108 FPS** on a T4 GPU (TensorRT FP16). This beats larger YOLO models like YOLOv5-L (49.0% AP, 54 FPS), PP-YOLOE-L (51.4% AP, 94 FPS), YOLOv6-L (52.8% AP, 99 FPS), YOLOv7-L (51.2% AP, 55 FPS), and YOLOv8-L (52.9% AP, 71 FPS).
 - **RT-DETR-R101** reaches **54.3% AP** and **74 FPS**, surpassing YOLOv5-X (50.7% AP, 43 FPS), PP-YOLOE-X (52.3% AP, 60 FPS), YOLOv7-X (52.9% AP, 45 FPS), and YOLOv8-X (53.9% AP, 50 FPS).
 - RT-DETR consistently shows **higher AP and higher FPS** compared to YOLO models of similar scale, often with fewer parameters. For example, RT-DETR-R50 improves accuracy by 0.2% AP over YOLOv8-L while being 52.1% faster. It also significantly reduces parameters compared to many YOLO counterparts.
 - Scaled RT-DETRs (R18, R34, lighter R50 variants) also outperform lighter YOLO models (S and M models) in both speed and accuracy.

- **Significant Improvement over Other DETRs:**
 - RT-DETR exhibits a dramatic leap in performance compared to other DETR variants. For instance, **RT-DETR-R50** achieves **53.1% AP** and **108 FPS**, while **DINO-Deformable-DETR-R50** (a strong prior DETR) only reaches **50.9% AP** at **5 FPS** (on an 800x1333 input, whereas RT-DETR uses 640x640). This represents a **2.2% AP accuracy improvement** and an astounding **21 times increase in FPS**. This key result underscores RT-DETR's success in making DETRs real-time without sacrificing accuracy.

- **Impact of Key Innovations (Ablation Studies):**
 - **Efficient Hybrid Encoder:** Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of the hybrid encoder. Decoupling intra-scale interaction and cross-scale fusion (Variant D) not only improves accuracy but also reduces latency. Specifically, performing intra-scale interaction only on S5 (D_S5) leads to a 35% latency reduction and 0.4% AP improvement over interaction on all scales. The final hybrid encoder (Variant E) provides a 1.5% AP gain over decoupled approach (D) while further reducing latency by 24%, demonstrating a better speed-accuracy trade-off.
 - **Uncertainty-Minimal Query Selection:** This module boosts accuracy by 0.8% AP (from 47.9% to 48.7%). Visualization and quantitative analysis show that it selects significantly more high-quality encoder features (with both high classification and IoU scores), demonstrating its effectiveness in providing better initial queries for the decoder.

- **Flexible Speed Tuning:**
 - The ablation on the decoder layers shows that adjusting the number of layers at inference time allows for flexible speed-accuracy trade-offs *without retraining*. For example, reducing the decoder from 6 layers to 5 layers in RT-DETR-R50 only results in a minor 0.1% AP drop (53.1% to 53.0%) but improves latency by 0.5 ms (9.3 ms to 8.8 ms).

- **Large-scale Pre-training:**
 - Pre-training RT-DETR on the Objects365 dataset followed by fine-tuning on COCO further boosts performance. RT-DETR-R50 achieves 55.3% AP (a 2.2% AP improvement), and RT-DETR-R101 reaches 56.2% AP (a 1.9% AP improvement), establishing new benchmarks for real-time detectors.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that RT-DETR is the first real-time end-to-end object detector that can outperform the widely-used YOLO series across various scales, marking a significant milestone in the field.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of RT-DETR carries substantial significance and potential impact for the field of real-time object detection and broader computer vision applications:

- **Shifting Paradigm in Real-time Detection:** For years, YOLOs have been synonymous with real-time object detection. RT-DETR fundamentally challenges this dominance by demonstrating that Transformer-based models can not only achieve but surpass YOLO's performance in both speed and accuracy in real-time scenarios. This could lead to a paradigm shift, encouraging more research and adoption of end-to-end, NMS-free architectures for high-speed tasks.
- **Elimination of NMS and its Benefits:** The end-to-end nature of RT-DETR completely eliminates the need for NMS. This has several profound benefits:
 - **Simplified Pipeline:** Removes a complex, hand-crafted post-processing step, making the detection pipeline cleaner and more streamlined.
 - **Improved Robustness and Consistency:** Eliminates the need for manual tuning of NMS hyperparameters (confidence and IoU thresholds), which are sensitive to different scenarios and inputs. This leads to more stable and consistent performance across diverse environments.
 - **True End-to-End Latency:** Provides a true measure of inference speed without the hidden cost of NMS, which is often excluded from reported YOLO speeds. This allows for more accurate system design and deployment in latency-critical applications.
- **Enhanced Practicality and Deployment:**
 - **Flexible Speed Tuning:** The ability to adjust decoder layers without retraining is a highly practical feature. It allows the same model to be adapted to different deployment scenarios with varying computational resources (e.g., edge devices vs. powerful GPUs) or latency requirements, without the overhead of maintaining multiple specialized models.
 - **Industry Adoption:** Backed by Baidu Inc., the research is likely to be quickly integrated into real-world applications, accelerating the deployment of advanced, efficient object detection systems in areas like autonomous driving, smart cities, and robotics.
- **Advancing Transformer-based Vision:** RT-DETR's success in optimizing Transformer-based architectures for real-time performance will spur further research into efficient attention mechanisms, encoder-decoder designs, and query initialization strategies for various real-time vision tasks beyond object detection. It demonstrates that the computational challenges of Transformers in vision are surmountable.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper identifies its limitation in small object detection, an area where some YOLOs still perform slightly better. This clearly points to a crucial direction for future research in DETRs. Moreover, the homogeneous decoder architecture opens up possibilities for knowledge distillation from larger, higher-accuracy DETR models, potentially achieving even higher performance for lightweight RT-DETR variants.

In conclusion, RT-DETR is not just an incremental improvement; it represents a significant leap forward by making end-to-end, Transformer-based object detection a practical reality for real-time applications. Its ability to outperform YOLOs while eliminating NMS positions it as a strong contender to reshape the landscape of real-time computer vision.
