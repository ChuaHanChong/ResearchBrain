# Real2Render2Real: Scaling Robot Data Without Dynamics Simulation or Robot Hardware

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Berkeley and Toyota Research Institute introduce Real2Render2Real (R2R2R), a framework that generates diverse robot training data from smartphone videos without requiring dynamics simulation or robot hardware, achieving 51 demonstrations per minute compared to 1.7 via human teleoperation while matching teleoperation-trained policy performance.

## Problem

- Scaling robot learning datasets is bottlenecked by slow, costly human teleoperation and inaccurate physics simulations
- Current robot datasets are orders of magnitude smaller than those used for language and vision models
- Physics-based simulation struggles with inaccurate dynamics modeling and complex object interactions, requiring labor-intensive creation of high-quality assets with accurate collision models

## Method

- Convert smartphone scans and demonstration videos into 3D assets and trajectories using ==Gaussian Splatting==
- Generate diverse robot executions through trajectory interpolation and parallelized rendering
- Synthesize physically grounded observation-action pairs without dynamics simulation

## Results

- Generates demonstrations **30x** faster than human teleoperation (51 vs **1.7** demos/minute)
- Matches or exceeds performance of policies trained on human teleoperation data
- Success rates scale predictably with dataset size across diverse task types, including object picking, multi-object interactions, and articulated manipulation

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Treating objects as kinematic rather than dynamic bodies avoids complex contact modeling challenges
> - Trajectory interpolation enables adaptation to new start/end poses while preserving semantic intent
> - Visual diversity in rendering improves policy learning but excessive appearance perturbation can be harmful

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical bottleneck in robot learning: the limited availability of large and diverse datasets. It fits into a growing body of work aimed at scaling robot data acquisition, with a specific focus on avoiding the limitations of traditional methods like human teleoperation and physics-based simulation.

**How it relates to existing work:**

- **Challenges of Teleoperation:** The paper directly acknowledges the constraints of human teleoperation, which, despite offering task and visual diversity, is costly, slow, and embodiment-specific.
- **Limitations of Simulation:** The authors highlight the problems associated with physics-based simulation, including inaccurate dynamics modeling, difficulties in modeling complex object interactions, and the labor-intensive process of generating high-quality assets for simulation with accurate collision models.
- **Comparison to Other Approaches:** The paper meticulously compares Real2Render2Real (R2R2R) to existing robot data generation paradigms like industrial robot logs, procedural data generation, real2synthetic data generation, and real2sim2real data generation. Table 1 provides a clear comparative overview of these methods.
- **Building on Existing Techniques:** The approach leverages recent advancements in computer vision, specifically 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) and differentiable rendering techniques, to achieve high visual fidelity and accurate object tracking.

**The gap R2R2R fills:**

R2R2R distinguishes itself by:

1. **Eliminating Reliance on Teleoperation:** Unlike methods that require initial human demonstrations, R2R2R only needs a single human demonstration video and a smartphone scan.
2. **Avoiding Dynamics Simulation:** By sidestepping physics simulation, R2R2R avoids the challenges of accurate contact modeling and expensive parameter tuning.
3. **Enabling Scalable Trajectory Diversity:** R2R2R generates multiple, diverse robot trajectory renderings and action rollouts from a single human demonstration, addressing the limitation of methods that produce only one trajectory per video.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

**Objectives:**

- To develop a novel framework (R2R2R) for synthesizing diverse, physically grounded observation-action pairs for robot learning using only smartphone-captured videos.
- To demonstrate the compatibility of R2R2R-generated data with modern vision-language-action (VLA) and imitation learning policies, including both transformer-based and diffusion-based architectures.
- To show that policies trained on R2R2R-generated data can match the performance of those trained on human teleoperation demonstrations, with significantly less data generation time.

**Motivation:**

- The primary motivation stems from the need to scale robot learning to tackle complex, open-world manipulation tasks.
- The authors are inspired by the success of large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), which have demonstrated remarkable capabilities due to their training on massive datasets.
- They recognize that the current scale of robot datasets is orders of magnitude smaller than those used to train LLMs and VLMs, hindering the development of generalist robot policies.
- The underlying question driving this research is: "Can we computationally scale robot vision-action data – while not requiring dynamics simulation or human teleoperation – to train robot learning models?"

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The R2R2R pipeline comprises three main stages:

1. **Real-to-Sim Asset and Trajectory Extraction:**
 - **3D Object Reconstruction:** Smartphone scans are used to reconstruct 3D object geometry and appearance using 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). This method enables flexible asset generation and high-fidelity rendering.
 - **Part Segmentation:** GARField is employed to segment the scene into semantically meaningful parts, enabling part-level decomposition for both rigid and articulated objects.
 - **6-DoF Trajectory Extraction:** 4D Differentiable Part Modeling (4D-DPM) is used to extract the 6-DoF motion of object parts from a human demonstration video.

2. **Augmentation:**
 - **Randomized Object Initialization:** Object initializations are randomized to increase trajectory diversity.
 - **Trajectory Interpolation:** Trajectory interpolation and resampling techniques adapt the original trajectory to new start and end poses, preserving its semantic intent. This is particularly important for multi-object interactions.
 - **Grasp Pose Sampling:** 3D hand keypoints are estimated from the demonstration video, and an antipodal grasp sampler is used to determine candidate grasp axes.

3. **Parallelized Rendering:**
 - IsaacLab is used as a photorealistic, parallelized rendering engine to generate diverse robot executions under varied lighting, camera pose, and object placement.
 - Objects are treated as kinematic rather than dynamic bodies, avoiding the complexities of contact modeling.
 - Differential inverse kinematics is used to generate corresponding robot executions based on the extracted object trajectories.

**Policy Learning:**
The authors train two modern imitation learning architectures using R2R2R-generated data: Diffusion Policy and π0-FAST. These models take RGB image observations and robot proprioceptive states as inputs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Performance Scaling:** R2R2R-generated data scales predictably with dataset size, with success rates increasing monotonically as the number of demonstrations grows.
- **Comparable Performance:** Policies trained on R2R2R-generated data can match or surpass the performance of those trained on human teleoperation data, particularly at larger dataset sizes (e.g., 1000 demonstrations).
- **Data Generation Efficiency:** R2R2R achieves a significant throughput advantage over human teleoperation, rendering complete robot demonstrations at an average rate of 51 demonstrations per minute on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090, compared to 1.7 demonstrations per minute via human teleoperation.
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablation studies demonstrate the importance of trajectory interpolation for adapting object motion to varied contexts and that while visual diversity is generally beneficial, overly strong appearance perturbations may harm policy learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The R2R2R framework has significant implications for robot learning:

- **Scalable Data Generation:** It offers a practical and scalable alternative to human teleoperation and physics-based simulation for generating robot training data.
- **Accessible Data Collection:** By relying on readily available tools like smartphones, R2R2R democratizes robot data collection, enabling anyone to contribute.
- **Reduced Development Costs:** By eliminating the need for expensive robot hardware and time-consuming manual effort, R2R2R has the potential to significantly reduce the cost of developing robot skills.
- **Accelerated Research Progress:** By enabling the creation of large and diverse datasets, R2R2R can accelerate research progress in areas like generalist robot policies, imitation learning, and vision-language-action models.
- **Impact on Various Tasks:** As proved in the paper, the task can be applied to several object picking tasks, multi-object interactions, and articulated object manipulation.
