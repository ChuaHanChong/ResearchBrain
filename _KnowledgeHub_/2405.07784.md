# Generating Human Motion in 3D Scenes from Text Descriptions

> [!summary] Summary
> A two-stage pipeline generates human motions in 3D indoor scenes from text: an LLM (ChatGPT) grounds the target object via scene-graph prompting, then an object-centric diffusion model synthesizes trajectory and pose. Outperforms baselines on the HUMANISE benchmark and generalizes zero-shot to PROX scenes.

## Problem

- Prior text-driven motion generators ignore scene context and human-object interaction, limiting physical and visual realism of synthesized motions.
- HUMANISE regresses object centers only as an implicit auxiliary task, so visual-grounding inductive bias is never explicitly exploited by the motion generator.
- Encoding an entire 3D scene with one point-transformer is noisy and complex, making it hard for the model to locate and focus on the relevant target object.

## Method

- Two-stage ==ChatGPT== grounding: converts 3D scenes into spatial ==scene-graph== text, then prompts ChatGPT twice (narrow category, infer target) to localize the target object as visual grounding via question answering.
- Object-centric ==volumetric sensors== (Environment, Target, Trajectory sensors) recenter scene point clouds around the located object, shrinking scale/complexity versus whole-scene encoding.
- Two-stage ==diffusion== pipeline: a transformer-decoder diffusion model first generates trajectory conditioned on text and sensors, then a second diffusion model completes local pose using an added trajectory sensor.

## Results

- On HUMANISE, method cuts goal-distance to **0.384** versus HUMANISE's **0.995** and GMD*'s **1.229**, and lifts scene score to **3.54** versus **2.50** for the next-best baseline.
- Achieves **97.1%** action-recognition accuracy and FID of **0.12**, versus HUMANISE's **89.7%** accuracy and FID of **1.11**, with human quality score of **3.50** versus **2.81**.
- Ablations show removing localization raises goal-dist to **0.592** and drops accuracy to **74.5%**; ChatGPT localization reaches **75.6%** accuracy versus **62.5%** for BUTD-DETR baseline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing a multimodal generation problem into explicit grounding-then-generation stages can outperform end-to-end joint modeling on interaction-heavy tasks.
> - LLMs can serve as zero-shot 3D visual grounding modules by converting geometric scene graphs into text, sidestepping task-specific grounding training.
> - Object-centric, scale-normalized representations reduce input complexity and transfer better across datasets than raw whole-scene point-cloud encodings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic human motion from high-level descriptions is a long-standing challenge in computer graphics and artificial intelligence, with wide applications in game development, virtual reality, film production, and robotics. This paper addresses a significant sub-area within this field: **text-driven human motion generation in 3D scenes that explicitly considers human-scene interactions.**

Initially, motion synthesis relied heavily on explicit user input or motion capture data for avatar animation. The field has seen a paradigm shift towards learning-based methods, where models learn to generate motions from high-level language prompts, such as text descriptions. Early works in text-driven motion synthesis, like those building upon benchmarks such as KIT-ML, primarily focused on generating generic human movements (e.g., "walk," "jump") without considering a specific environmental context. These methods often learned a shared latent space for text and motion or employed generative models like Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) and, more recently, diffusion models to produce diverse and natural-looking motions.

However, a critical limitation of many text-driven approaches is their neglect of the surrounding 3D environment and the physical constraints it imposes. Human actions are inherently scene-aware; we interact with objects, navigate spaces, and adapt our movements to obstacles and surfaces. Without considering these interactions, generated motions often lack visual and physical realism, leading to unnatural or impossible poses and trajectories.

This paper explicitly tackles this gap by focusing on generating motions that are consistent with both textual descriptions *and* the geometry of a 3D indoor scene. Previous scene-aware motion generation efforts existed, often using volumetric sensors or hierarchical frameworks to synthesize motions like walking, sitting, or grasping with specific objects. A few pioneering works, notably HUMANISE, started to combine text and scene conditions. HUMANISE used a transformer VAE and auxiliary tasks for object localization. This paper distinguishes itself from HUMANISE by proposing a more explicit and robust two-stage pipeline, particularly in its approach to language grounding and object-centric motion generation, aiming to overcome the limitations of implicit spatial reasoning and scene complexity handling in prior art. The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) for spatial reasoning represents a significant trend in multimodal AI, leveraging their powerful understanding capabilities to bridge the gap between natural language descriptions and complex 3D environments. By decomposing the problem and focusing on target objects, the authors aim to improve both the accuracy of interaction and the overall quality of generated motion, thus pushing the boundaries of realistic and contextually appropriate human animation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **generate natural and physically plausible human motions within 3D indoor scenes, guided by textual descriptions that specify human-scene interactions.** This goes beyond merely generating actions to ensuring these actions correctly interact with specific objects in the scene.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in this task:

1. **Multi-modality:** The problem involves integrating information from three disparate modalities: natural language text, complex 3D scene geometry (point clouds), and dynamic human motion. Effectively combining and reasoning across these modalities is computationally and conceptually challenging.
2. **Spatial Reasoning:** Text descriptions often include spatial relations (e.g., "sit on the chair near the desk"). Models must possess robust spatial reasoning abilities to correctly interpret these descriptions, locate the target object within a cluttered 3D scene, and plan a motion trajectory accordingly. Previous methods often struggled with explicit spatial grounding or relied on implicit learning, which could be less precise.
3. **Scene Complexity:** 3D indoor scenes are inherently complex, featuring numerous objects, varied geometries, and potential occlusions. Directly modeling human motion within an entire, noisy point cloud can overwhelm generative models, making it difficult to focus on relevant scene elements.
4. **Fidelity and Realism:** Generated motions must not only align with text and scene but also be visually natural, physically realistic, and free from artifacts or penetrations. This requires high-quality generative modeling capable of synthesizing diverse and coherent motion sequences.

To address these challenges, the authors propose a novel approach that decomposes the complex problem into two more manageable sub-problems:
 a. **Language grounding of the target object:** Accurately identifying which specific object in the 3D scene the text description refers to.
 b. **Object-centric motion generation:** Generating human motion by focusing on the geometry and context of the identified target object, simplifying the scene representation for the generative model.

This decomposition is motivated by the observation that not every part of a complex scene is equally relevant to a specific human-object interaction. By explicitly grounding the target object first, the generative model can then concentrate its computational resources on the most pertinent scene information, thereby improving both accuracy and quality.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is a novel two-stage pipeline designed to tackle the multi-modality and complexity challenges of text-driven scene-aware motion generation.

**Stage 1: Language Grounding of Objects in 3D Scenes**
This stage focuses on accurately identifying the target object within the 3D scene based on the text description. Instead of learning a direct text-object mapping, the authors leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) for their advanced natural language understanding and reasoning capabilities.

1. **Spatial Scene Graph Extraction:** The 3D scene, initially represented as a point cloud, is first processed to extract a symbolic representation. A pre-trained 3D object detection model identifies bounding boxes for all objects in the scene. Subsequently, spatial relationships between these detected objects (e.g., horizontal proximity, vertical proximity, support, in-between) are inferred from their bounding boxes, forming a "scene graph" where objects are nodes and relationships are edges. This 3D scene graph is then converted into a textual description.
2. **Leveraging ChatGPT for Object Localization:** To manage the potential complexity of large scene graphs, a two-prompt strategy is employed with ChatGPT (specifically, gpt-3.5-turbo):
 - **Prompt 1 (Narrowing Search Space):** Given the full textual scene description and the input text instruction, ChatGPT is asked to identify the *categories* of the target object and any anchor objects mentioned in the instruction. This filters out irrelevant objects, simplifying the scene graph.
 - **Prompt 2 (Inferring Target Object):** With the simplified scene graph (containing only relevant object categories) and the original instruction, object relations are detailed in text. ChatGPT is then prompted to infer the *specific instance* of the target object, providing its bounding box. This explicit reasoning process, guided by LLMs, is crucial for accurate 3D visual grounding.

**Stage 2: Object-Centric Diffusion-Based Motion Generation**
Once the target object is precisely localized, the second stage generates human motion in two sub-steps: trajectory generation and motion completion, both utilizing diffusion models.

1. **Object-Centric Scene Representation:** To help the generative model focus on the target object and reduce scene complexity, the authors design volumetric sensors:
 - **Target Sensor (T):** Captures detailed geometry of the localized target object. It's an 8x8x8 cubic volume covering the target object's bounding box, encoding occupancy and normal directions.
 - **Environment Sensor (E):** Provides coarse spatial information around the target object. It's a larger 4x4x4 m³ cubic volume, also 8x8x8 voxels, centered at the target object, encoding similar information. These sensors are transformed into an object-centered coordinate system.
2. **Diffusion-based Trajectory Generation:** A transformer-based conditional diffusion model (G_r) is used to synthesize the character's global translation and orientation (trajectory). The conditions for this model are the CLIP-encoded text feature (L) from the input text, and the Environment Sensor (E) and Target Sensor (T). This model is trained to denoise the noisy trajectory data conditioned on these multimodal inputs.
3. **Diffusion-based Motion Completion:** A second transformer-based conditional diffusion model (G_m) is employed to complete the full human motion, generating global orientation and local pose parameters (SMPL-X body model parameters). This model takes additional information:
 - **Trajectory Sensor (O):** An ego-centric volumetric sensor positioned around the character in each frame, capturing scene occupancy relative to the human's current pose. This explicitly models the immediate interaction space.
 - The conditions for G_m are the text feature (L), Environment Sensor (E), Target Sensor (T), and the sequence of Trajectory Sensors (O_1...O_N) derived from the generated trajectory.

**Implementation Details:** The models are pre-trained on the large AMASS dataset (pure motion) and then fine-tuned on the HUMANISE dataset (scene-aware text-driven motion). Separate diffusion models are used for trajectory and motion generation, and the AdamW optimizer is employed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents a comprehensive evaluation of its proposed method, demonstrating significant improvements over existing baselines across multiple metrics and scenarios.

**Quantitative Results on HUMANISE Dataset (Table 1):**
The method was benchmarked against several baselines, including MDM* (a text-driven diffusion model), GMD* (an enhanced MDM), GMD_HC (GMD guided by HUMANISE's predicted object centers), and HUMANISE (the most relevant prior work).
- **Scene-conditional Motion Quality:**
 - **Goal Distance (↓):** Our method achieved the lowest goal distance (0.384), significantly outperforming HUMANISE (0.995) and other baselines, indicating more accurate human-object interactions.
 - **Scene Score (↑):** A human perceptual study yielded a scene score of 3.54 for our method, the highest among all, suggesting that generated motions are perceived as highly consistent with the scene and text.
- **Action-conditional Motion Quality:**
 - **Accuracy (↑):** Achieved 97.1% accuracy in action recognition, competitive with or slightly better than MDM* (96.0%) and GMD* (96.6%), and markedly better than HUMANISE (89.7%).
 - **Diversity (→) and Multimodality (→):** Our method showed competitive results (4.78 and 2.31 respectively), indicating the ability to generate a variety of motions that align with the text.
- **Pure Motion Quality:**
 - **FID (↓):** Recorded the lowest FID (0.12), significantly better than HUMANISE (1.11) and competitive with MDM* (0.16), implying generated motions are more realistic and closer to ground truth.
 - **Quality Score (↑):** Another human perceptual study scored 3.50, the highest, affirming the high realism and naturalness of the generated motions.

**Qualitative Results (Figure 5 & 6):**
Visual comparisons confirmed that the proposed method synthesizes motions that precisely interact with the specified objects, closely matching ground truth. Baselines often failed to accurately position the human relative to the object or produced less natural interactions. The paper also showed successful generalization to the unseen PROX dataset without fine-tuning (Figure 6), demonstrating robustness across different scene types and objects.

**Ablation Studies (Table 2 & 3):**
- **Main Components (Table 2):**
 - Removing the localization module ("w/o localization") led to a significant drop in goal distance and action accuracy, confirming the criticality of accurate object grounding.
 - Removing object-centric representation ("w/o object-centric") negatively impacted motion quality (higher FID, lower accuracy), validating the benefits of focusing on relevant scene parts.
 - Removing the two-stage generation ("w/o two-stage") showed competitive metrics but was prone to lacking gradual transitions, highlighting the importance of the two-stage approach for motion realism.
 - Using cVAE instead of diffusion models ("w/o diffusion") drastically reduced accuracy and increased FID, underscoring the superiority of diffusion models for this task.
 - Skipping pre-training on AMASS ("w/o pretrain") also led to worse performance, confirming the benefits of leveraging large-scale motion data.
- **Object Localization Design Choices (Table 3):**
 - The proposed ChatGPT-based localization achieved the highest accuracy (75.6% with predicted detection, 90.5% with GT detection) and lowest center distance (0.61, 0.37), surpassing HUMANISE and BUTD-DETR.
 - Using an open-source LLM like Mistral 7B yielded only slightly lower performance (72.4% acc., 0.74 center dist.) compared to ChatGPT, suggesting a viable alternative for reproducibility.
 - Ablations showed that the two-stage prompt design ("ours w/o two-stage"), few-shot examples ("ours w/o few-shot"), and ChatGPT itself (compared to text matching or LLaMA 2) were crucial for optimal localization performance.

In summary, the main findings clearly demonstrate that the proposed two-stage approach, combining LLM-driven object localization with object-centric diffusion models for motion generation, significantly improves the quality, accuracy, and realism of text-driven scene-aware human motion synthesis.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research represents a notable advancement in the field of human motion generation, particularly in its ability to produce highly realistic and contextually appropriate motions within complex 3D environments guided by natural language. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced Realism and Fidelity:** By explicitly considering human-scene interactions and achieving precise object grounding, the generated motions are far more visually and physically realistic than prior methods that often ignored scene context or struggled with accurate interaction. This improves the believability of virtual characters.
2. **Bridging Language and 3D Environments:** The novel application of Large Language Models (LLMs) for 3D visual grounding is a significant contribution. It showcases a powerful way to leverage the advanced reasoning capabilities of LLMs to interpret spatial descriptions in 3D scenes, a traditionally challenging task for neural networks alone. This could inspire similar integrations in other multimodal AI applications.
3. **Decomposition as a Solution Strategy:** Decomposing the complex problem into object localization and object-centric motion generation offers a robust and effective strategy for handling multimodal inputs and high-dimensional outputs. This modular approach could be adapted to other challenging generative tasks.
4. **Applications in Virtual Worlds and Robotics:** The ability to generate human motions that accurately interact with 3D scenes from text has immense potential in:
 - **Game Development and Virtual Reality:** Populating virtual worlds with intelligent, context-aware NPCs (Non-Player Characters) and avatars, enabling more immersive and interactive experiences.
 - **Film Production and Animation:** Automating character animation based on script descriptions, reducing manual effort and improving consistency.
 - **Robotics:** Providing high-level instructions to robots for complex human-robot interaction tasks, where robots need to understand spatial context and perform human-like movements.
 - **Architectural Visualization:** Simulating human movement patterns and interactions within proposed building designs.
5. **Generalization Capabilities:** The demonstrated ability to generalize to unseen datasets (PROX) without fine-tuning is a strong indicator of the method's robustness and potential for broader applicability in diverse virtual environments.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, which also hint at future research avenues:
- **Motion Duration:** The current dataset primarily consists of short motions, limiting the complexity of generated actions. Extending to long-term, temporally complex action sequences is a future goal.
- **Static Scenes:** The method currently handles only static scenes. Extending it to interactions with dynamic or moving objects (e.g., catching a ball, manipulating tools) would be a significant next step.
- **LLM Dependency:** While powerful, the reliance on LLMs (like ChatGPT) has drawbacks such as potential failures with missing detector objects, sensitivity to prompts, and less efficiency during inference compared to classical parsing. Exploring more robust or efficient LLM integration, potentially with fine-tuned open-source models, or hybrid approaches, is a viable direction. The authors' results with Mistral 7B are a promising step in this regard.

In conclusion, this paper presents a highly impactful contribution by effectively integrating large language models with diffusion-based motion generation for contextually rich human-scene interactions. Its innovations lay a strong foundation for more intelligent and realistic character behaviors in a wide array of virtual and real-world applications.
