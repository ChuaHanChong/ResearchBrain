# BoxTwin: Learning Elastoplastic Articulated Object Dynamics from Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> BoxTwin is an interactive digital-twin framework that learns elastoplastic articulated object dynamics from video. It reconstructs the scene, then fits per-hinge constitutive models coupling nonlinear elastic torque, a yield-threshold plastic flow rule that shifts the rest angle, and a damage variable driven by accumulated plastic slip. Folding and dual-arm Aloha replays in MuJoCo track joint trajectories and post-contact plastic behavior over long horizons.

## Problem

- Prior digital twins target rigid bodies or purely elastic deformation, so they cannot represent objects whose creases yield permanently once critical loads are exceeded.
- Linear spring models miss the strongly nonlinear stress-strain response of creases, losing energy dissipation and coupling between degrees of freedom during contact.
- Existing methods keep material parameters static, ignoring damage that drifts stiffness and permanent-set capacity across repeated loading cycles, so long-horizon predictions degrade.

## Method

- ==Articulated representation==: rigid links joined by ideal hinges in a rooted kinematic tree, with ==joint-space Lagrangian dynamics== (inertia, Coriolis, gravity, contact torques) over the hinge-angle vector.
- ==Per-crease elastoplasticity==: a smooth differentiable elastic torque map on the gap between hinge and rest angle, plus a ==yield condition== and ==flow rule== evolving that rest angle.
- ==Damage law==: a scalar per hinge multiplicatively attenuates both elastic torque and plastic flow, its rate a monotone function of accumulated plastic slip; parameters identified from video and simulated in MuJoCo as **BoxTwin**.

## Results

- Folding test on a two-panel, single-joint object with one panel anchored: color markers give ground-truth joint angle, and the simulated trajectory tracks the real one across the long sequence.
- Prediction test replays logged Trossen Aloha dual-arm follower trajectories in a rebuilt virtual scene; simulated joint angles closely match hardware for both single-joint and multi-joint box assemblies.
- Tracking holds despite accumulated hysteresis and wear over repeated actuation cycles, though the paper reports qualitative trajectory overlays and image comparisons rather than quantitative error metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Permanent set needs a moving rest configuration, not a stiffer elastic model; letting the rest angle evolve is the cheapest way to encode plasticity.
> - Multiplicative damage on both elastic torque and plastic flow preserves sign and monotonicity, a safe way to add time-varying degradation to an existing constitutive law.
> - Claims of long-horizon fidelity rest on visual overlays; without error metrics or baselines, the modeling recipe's appeal outruns the evidence supporting it.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of digital twins into robotic platforms is an area of active research, aiming to enhance robot adaptability and interaction capabilities within complex physical environments. Existing digital twin frameworks typically focus on rigid bodies or objects exhibiting purely linear elastic deformation. This limitation restricts their applicability in scenarios involving objects that undergo more complex mechanical responses.

Previous research in deformable object modeling for robotics has explored various aspects, including general deformable objects, elastic deformations, and specific material properties. However, a gap exists in comprehensively modeling objects that combine multiple intricate mechanical behaviors, specifically elastoplastic articulated objects (EAOs). EAOs are characterized by nonlinear elasticity, plastic yielding (permanent deformation), and damage accumulation (degradation of material properties over time), alongside articulated joints. These properties make their dynamics highly nonlinear and evolving, posing challenges for accurate simulation and control.

Current techniques often struggle to capture the full spectrum of mechanical responses, particularly the intertwined phenomena of plasticity, nonlinear elasticity, and progressive damage. This deficiency leads to robots failing in tasks requiring precise manipulation or prediction of post-interaction states involving such objects, thereby impacting safety and reliability in diverse applications. BoxTwin aims to address this gap by developing a digital twin framework specifically designed to capture the full, time-evolving dynamics of EAOs, thereby extending the capabilities of digital twins beyond rigid or linearly elastic material assumptions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an interactive digital-twin framework, named BoxTwin, capable of learning and reproducing the full dynamics of elastoplastic articulated objects (EAOs) from video data. This framework aims to enable robots to anticipate and adapt to physical interactions with these complex objects, which exhibit a combination of nonlinear elasticity, plastic yielding, and cumulative damage.

The motivation for developing BoxTwin stems from several identified limitations in current robotic manipulation and digital twin technologies:
- **Challenges with Deformable Objects**: Modeling deformable objects remains a significant obstacle in robotic manipulation. Existing techniques often fail to encompass the complete range of mechanical responses observed during contact with materials that deform significantly and irreversibly.
- **Complexity of Elastoplastic Articulated Objects (EAOs)**: EAOs, such as boxes, doors, hinges, or sheet-metal assemblies, present a distinct challenge. They combine reversible elastic deformations with irreversible plastic changes, and feature articulated joints, leading to highly nonlinear dynamics that evolve throughout interaction.
- **Three Coupled Physical Effects**: The difficulty in modeling EAOs arises from three interconnected physical phenomena:
 1. **Non-linear Elasticity**: Stress-strain relationships deviate from Hookean behavior, leading to energy dissipation and inter-degree-of-freedom coupling not captured by linear models.
 2. **Plastic Deformation**: Permanent shape alterations occur once critical loads are surpassed, establishing new rest configurations that elastic models alone cannot predict.
 3. **Damage Evolution**: Repeated loading cycles cause material parameters (e.g., stiffness) to drift due to micro-fractures or delamination, requiring time-dependent material descriptions.
- **Inadequacy of Conventional Digital Twins**: Traditional digital twin frameworks are typically designed for rigid bodies or linearly elastic materials. They do not accommodate the triad of nonlinear elasticity, plasticity, and progressive damage inherent to EAOs. This prevents robots from robustly performing tasks like grasping, folding, or accurately forecasting post-interaction states with such objects.
- **Need for Physics-Aware Models**: Instead of relying on hand-crafted physics models with static parameters, there is a need for physics-aware architectures that can capture the full, evolving dynamics of EAOs through calibrated constitutive models, with continuous updates to material properties.

BoxTwin's objectives are therefore to overcome these limitations by providing a robust, high-fidelity digital twin that accounts for these complex material behaviors, thereby laying the groundwork for interactive digital twins that support safe, adaptive robotic manipulation in real-world, unstructured environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

BoxTwin's methodology involves a multi-stage process that reconstructs the scene from input video, identifies a physics-aware constitutive model for each EAO, and subsequently predicts future states. The core of the approach lies in its detailed modeling of articulated bodies and the complex elastoplastic-damage behavior of their creases.

**A. Articulated Body Representation:**
An EAO is conceptualized as a collection of rigid links connected by ideal hinge joints, which model the flexible creases of the structure.
- **Kinematic Tree**: The object's topology is represented by a rooted kinematic tree $G = (V, E)$, where each vertex $i \in V$ corresponds to a link $L_i$ and each edge $e = (p, i) \in E$ signifies a hinge joint.
- **Joints**: Each hinge joint permits a single relative rotation about a specific axis $a_e$. The joint angle $q_e \in \mathbb{R}$ is the sole configuration variable for that edge.
- **Transforms**: Homogeneous transforms are used to map the world frame to each link, composing transforms along the unique path from the root. This involves rotation $R(a_e, q_e)$ and translation vectors $t_{p \to e}$ and $t_{e \to i}$.
- **Configuration**: All joint angles are collected into a vector $q = [q_{e_1}, \ldots, q_{e_{|E|}}]^\top$ for a compact configuration description.
- **Dynamics**: Joint-space dynamics are formulated using the standard Lagrange approach: $M(q)\ddot{q} + C(q, \dot{q})\dot{q} + g(q) = \tau$, where $M(q)$ is the inertia matrix, $C(q, \dot{q})$ accounts for Coriolis and centrifugal effects, $g(q)$ represents generalized gravity forces, and $\tau$ comprises generalized forces from external contacts.

**B. Elastic Response of the Creases:**
The model introduces a nonlinear elastic response for each crease.
- **Deformation Gap**: The elastic response is driven by the scalar gap $\Delta_e = q_e - \bar{q}_e$, where $q_e$ is the instantaneous hinge angle and $\bar{q}_e$ is the associated rest angle.
- **Nonlinear Torque Function**: The elastic torque $\tau_{el_e}$ is defined as a smooth, differentiable mapping $f_{\theta_e}(\Delta_e)$, parameterized by coefficients $\theta_e$ specific to each edge. This formulation is designed to capture strong nonlinear behavior while allowing for analytical differentiation required for identification and optimization.

**C. Plastic Deformation Modeling:**
Permanent deformation of a crease is modeled by allowing the rest angle $\bar{q}_e$ to evolve.
- **Rest Angle Evolution**: $\bar{q}_e = q_{0_e} + \alpha_e$, where $q_{0_e}$ is the nominal angle and $\alpha_e$ is an internal scalar variable recording accumulated plastic rotation.
- **Yield Condition**: Plastic flow is initiated when the elastic gap exceeds a material-specific yield threshold $\Delta_{y_e}$. The yield condition $\Phi_e(\Delta_e) = |\Delta_e| - \Delta_{y_e} \leq 0$ defines the elastic regime.
- **Flow Rule**: When $\Phi_e > 0$, the internal variable $\alpha_e$ evolves according to a smooth flow rule $\dot{\alpha}_e = g_{\psi_e}(\Delta_e)$, parameterized by coefficients $\psi_e$.

**D. Damage Evolution Modeling:**
To account for material degradation (e.g., micro-fracture, fiber delamination) due to repeated folding, a scalar damage variable $d_e \in$ is introduced for each hinge.
- **Damage Effect**: $d_e = 0$ signifies an undamaged crease, while $d_e = 1$ indicates a mechanically inactive joint. Damage attenuates both the elastic torque and plastic flow multiplicatively: $\tau_{el_e} = (1 - d_e) f_{\theta_e}(q_e - \bar{q}_e)$ and $\dot{\alpha}_e = (1 - d_e) g_{\psi_e}(q_e - \bar{q}_e)$. This form preserves the sign and monotonicity of the underlying maps while physically reducing the joint's load-bearing capacity.
- **Damage Rate Law**: The evolution of $d_e$ is driven by the cumulative plastic slip $P_e(t) = \int_0^t |\dot{\alpha}_e(\tau)| d\tau$, which records the total absolute plastic rotation. The damage-rate law is a smooth, monotone function $\dot{d}_e = h_{\xi_e}(P_e)$, parameterized by coefficients $\xi_e$.

This comprehensive modeling approach allows BoxTwin to identify and continuously update material properties, including nonlinear elasticity, plastic yield thresholds, and damage progression, directly from video data. The identified parameters are then used within a physics-based simulator (MuJoCo) to predict and reproduce the complex dynamics of EAOs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the BoxTwin model was evaluated through two experimental setups: a manual folding test and a dual-arm manipulation prediction test, both implemented using the MuJoCo simulator.

**A. Folding Test:**
- **Setup**: In this experiment, a simple EAO consisting of two panels connected by a single joint was used. One panel was anchored to a table, and the other was manually deformed. Color markers affixed to the side of the moving panel were continuously monitored to derive the joint angle.
- **Procedure**: The actuation applied to the physical object was transferred to the simulation, and the resulting joint angles were recorded over the motion sequence.
- **Results**: The simulated joint angle trajectory was compared against the real-world measurements. The findings indicated that the BoxTwin model accurately captured the actual behavior of the object over a long sequence. This included faithfully reproducing instantaneous kinematic responses and the gradual evolution of material properties. Specifically, the model tracked joint-angle trajectories despite the accumulation of hysteresis and wear. It also preserved the interplay between elastic, plastic, and damage mechanisms throughout repeated actuation cycles. This suggests that the model provides a digital twin capable of remaining reliable even when the system is subjected to prolonged, complex loading patterns.

**B. Prediction Test:**
- **Setup**: This experiment utilized a Trossen Aloha dual-arm robotic platform to interact with various EAOs, including simple single-revolute-joint objects and more complex multi-joint assemblies. The system operated in a leader-follower configuration, where follower arms executed physical actions on the objects.
- **Procedure**: After each manipulation trial, the full follower-arm trajectory data (joint positions, velocities, torques, end-effector pose) were logged. This data was used to reconstruct an identical virtual scene within a physics-based simulator. The simulated environment included Trossen Aloha dual-arms with matched kinematic and dynamic parameters (link lengths, masses, joint limits, friction), and the EAOs were modeled using experimentally identified BoxTwin parameters. The recorded control signals from the physical robot were then replayed on the simulated follower arms, ensuring precise timing and magnitude of commands.
- **Results**: A close correspondence was observed between the simulated joint angles and overall object configurations and the measurements obtained from the physical hardware across all tested cases (single-joint to multi-joint assemblies). Figure 3 in the paper illustrates this correspondence for a box with multiple joints, showing real-world video frames alongside their simulated counterparts. This validation demonstrated the reliability of the simulated environment for tasks such as algorithm development, predictive analysis, and transfer-learning studies, without requiring repetitive physical trials. The ability to reproduce post-contact plastic behavior, which prior EAO models struggled with, was a specific observation from these tests.

Overall, the experiments indicated that BoxTwin can accurately learn the complex, time-evolving dynamics of EAOs from video data and use this learned model to predict future states and behaviors in a physics-based simulation environment, matching real-world observations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

BoxTwin represents an advancement in the field of digital twins and robotic manipulation by introducing a framework specifically designed for elastoplastic articulated objects (EAOs). Its significance lies in addressing a previously underserved category of objects whose complex mechanical behaviors (nonlinear elasticity, plastic deformation, and damage evolution) have limited the applicability of existing digital twin models.

The potential impacts of this research include:
- **Enhanced Robotic Manipulation**: By providing digital twins that accurately capture the full, evolving dynamics of EAOs, BoxTwin can enable robots to perform more precise, adaptive, and robust manipulation tasks involving objects like foldable packaging, textiles with creases, or aging mechanical components. This capability moves beyond static simulations and rigid-body assumptions, which often fail in unstructured environments with deformable objects.
- **Improved Predictive Control**: The ability of BoxTwin to predict post-contact plastic behavior and track joint trajectories over long horizons, despite accumulating hysteresis and wear, can lead to the development of more sophisticated predictive control strategies for robots. This could allow robots to anticipate permanent changes in object geometry and material properties, adapting their control policies accordingly.
- **Safer Human-Robot Collaboration**: In scenarios involving human-robot collaboration, the improved predictive capabilities regarding deformable and potentially fragile objects can contribute to safer interactions. Robots equipped with BoxTwin could better understand and respond to the evolving state of objects, reducing the risk of damage to objects or injury to humans.
- **Accelerated Algorithm Development and Transfer Learning**: The validated correspondence between simulated and real-world EAO behavior means that the BoxTwin-enabled simulation environment can be reliably used for developing and testing robotic algorithms. This can significantly reduce the need for repetitive and time-consuming physical trials, thereby accelerating research and development in robotics and potentially facilitating more effective transfer learning from simulation to real-world robots.
- **Foundation for Interactive Digital Twins**: BoxTwin lays groundwork for interactive digital twins that integrate video-driven reconstruction with sophisticated elastoplastic-damage modeling. This approach suggests a path toward more generalizable and autonomous robotic systems capable of operating in diverse and dynamic real-world settings where object properties are not perfectly known or static.
