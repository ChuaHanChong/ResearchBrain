# Causally Debiased Latent Action Model for Embodied Action Conditioned World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Causally Debiased Latent Action Model (CD-LAM) framework addresses action-irrelevant bias in latent action representations, enhancing the controllability of Action-Conditioned World Models (ACWMs). CD-LAM significantly improves latent-action controllability, visual fidelity, and robot action following, while reducing robot action adaptation data requirements by over 12x.

## Problem

- Existing Latent Action Models (LAMs) for Action-Conditioned World Models (ACWMs) often entangle action-relevant dynamics with action-irrelevant visual factors (e.g., background, non-interacted objects) due to reconstruction-only training.
- This entanglement biases ACWMs, causing them to generate visually plausible but action-inconsistent rollouts that do not reliably follow specified actions and are sensitive to minor perturbations.
- The resulting contaminated latent action spaces lead to weak robot action-following performance and inefficient adaptation when fine-tuning ACWMs with limited labeled robot data.

## Method

- Introduced **CD-LAM**, a framework that applies a ==causally debiased fine-tuning== approach to LAMs using three novel objectives.
- Developed an ==embodiment-centric reconstruction loss==, an ==action-centric contrastive learning loss==, and a ==latent space calibration loss== (including ==free-bit KL== and ==zero-transition calibration==) to encourage action-focused latent representations.
- Proposed a ==multi-stage training pipeline== involving LAM debiased fine-tuning, ACWM debiased fine-tuning, and efficient robot action adaptation using a lightweight ==MLP bridge==.

## Results

- CD-LAM reduced action-irrelevant bias, with zero-transition responses dropping from **0.527** to **0.043** and camera-shift responses reducing by **3.6x-8.3x** compared to baselines.
- Improved action following, reducing Foreground Displacement Chamfer Error (FDCE) by **42%** at 2B and **26%** at 14B scales during latent-action conditioned rollouts, and by **35%** (2B) and **30%** (14B) for robot action rollouts.
- Achieved comparable performance to baselines with over **12x** fewer robot action adaptation updates, demonstrating significant data efficiency, and halved residual FDCE under zero-action interventions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action-irrelevant visual factors like static backgrounds and camera shifts can significantly confound latent action representations, hindering ACWM controllability.
> - Explicitly debiasing latent action models through objectives that prioritize embodiment, structure by action semantics, and calibrate the 'zero-action' state is crucial for achieving reliable action-conditioned world models.
> - Causal debiasing allows ACWMs to learn more effectively from unlabeled human video data, making them more robust and significantly reducing the data and computational resources needed for adaptation to real robot actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Action-Conditioned World Models (ACWMs) represent a paradigm for simulating future observations based on current visual input and an action trajectory. These models are considered foundational for applications in robot planning, policy evaluation, and data augmentation, as they enable the prediction of environmental and embodiment evolution under various interventions.

A significant challenge in developing controllable ACWMs is the requirement for extensive, action-labeled data, which is costly and labor-intensive to collect in real-world robotic environments. In contrast, human interaction videos are more readily available and contain rich physical interaction information, but they typically lack executable action annotations. Latent Action Models (LAMs) have emerged as a method to bridge this data gap by inferring compact latent action representations from unlabeled video transitions. By pre-training ACWMs on these inferred latent actions, researchers aim to warm-start action-controllable dynamics, which can then be fine-tuned with limited labeled robot data to improve action-following performance.

However, a limitation of existing LAMs is their predominant reliance on reconstruction-only objectives during training. This approach can lead to the entanglement of action-relevant dynamics with action-irrelevant visual factors, such as background elements or non-interacted objects. When these action-irrelevant confounders are encoded into the latent action representation, they can bias the ACWM, potentially causing it to generate visually plausible but action-inconsistent rollouts that do not reliably follow specified actions and are sensitive to minor perturbations. This issue hinders the effective use of latent action spaces for real robot control, as robot action adaptation processes inherit this contaminated latent space, potentially resulting in suboptimal robot action following. This paper directly addresses this identified issue of action-irrelevant bias in LAMs, aiming to improve the causal grounding of latent action representations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the controllability and reliability of Action-Conditioned World Models (ACWMs) by addressing the issue of action-irrelevant bias in Latent Action Models (LAMs).

The motivation stems from a recognized limitation in current LAM-based ACWMs:
- **Confounding in Latent Actions:** Existing LAMs are typically trained with reconstruction-only objectives. While effective for video reconstruction, this objective does not inherently prevent action-irrelevant visual factors (e.g., background scenes, non-interacted objects, camera motion, appearance continuity) from being encoded into the latent action representations. These factors, while predictive for general video continuation, are not relevant to embodied action dynamics.
- **Impact on ACWM Controllability:** When these action-irrelevant factors are embedded in the latent action, they confound the ACWM. The model may generate visually coherent future frames, but it struggles to reliably follow the *intended action* specified by the latent condition. This can manifest as generated embodiments continuing to move despite a "zero-action" input, or failing to reproduce a target action's dynamics when that action is transferred to a different visual context. Such behavior indicates that the ACWM is partly conditioned on non-actionable visual properties rather than pure embodiment dynamics.
- **Inefficient Robot Action Adaptation:** This confounding issue complicates the subsequent adaptation of ACWMs to real-world executable robot actions. A robot action, by definition, grounds embodied dynamics. When forced to align with a latent space contaminated by non-actionable visual information, the robot control signals become less effective, leading to weak action-following performance and requiring extensive adaptation data and computational resources.

To mitigate these issues, the paper sets out several specific objectives:
- To empirically analyze and identify the presence and effects of action-irrelevant bias in representative LAM-based ACWMs.
- To introduce specific evaluation metrics designed to quantify latent-action bias, action-following accuracy, and robustness, thereby providing a clearer assessment of controllability.
- To propose a causally debiased framework, CD-LAM, for LAM-based ACWMs.
- To develop and integrate three debiasing objectives—embodiment-centric reconstruction, action-centric contrastive learning, and latent space calibration—to encourage latent action representations that are focused on embodiment, aware of actions, and well-calibrated without collapsing.
- To demonstrate that CD-LAM substantially improves latent-action controllability, robot action following, visual fidelity, and adaptation efficiency across different model scales, particularly in scenarios with limited robot action data.
- To contribute the debiased LAMs, ACWMs, evaluation protocols, and training code to the research community to foster further advancements in controllable embodied world models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Causally Debiased Latent Action Model (CD-LAM) proposes a framework for debiasing latent action representations to improve the controllability of ACWMs. The methodology consists of specific design principles embodied in three novel objectives and an efficient multi-stage fine-tuning pipeline.

**A. Design Principles for Debiased Latent Actions:**
The approach is guided by three principles aimed at ensuring latent actions primarily capture embodiment dynamics while disentangling action-irrelevant factors:
1. **Embodiment-centric Reconstruction:** Reconstruction signals should prioritize regions associated with embodiment dynamics over static background elements.
2. **Action-centric Structure:** The latent action space should organize transitions based on action similarity, not merely visual context similarity.
3. **Calibrated, Non-collapsed Latent Space:** Inputs representing no change (e.g., duplicated frames) should map to a defined zero-transition reference, while ordinary transitions maintain sufficient variation.

**B. CD-LAM Objective:**
The LAM is fine-tuned using a combined objective function $L_{CD} = L_{emb} + \lambda_{ctr}(k)L_{ctr} + \lambda_{cal}L_{cal}$.

1. **Embodiment-centric Reconstruction Loss ($L_{emb}$):** This objective uses a weighted Mean Squared Error (MSE) for next-frame prediction, $\hat{o}_{t+1} = D_{\psi}(o_t, z^{CD}_t)$. A spatial weight map, $W_t$, is derived from an embodiment–object foreground mask (obtained using SAM3). $W_t = \alpha_{fg}M_t + \alpha_{bg}(1 - M_t)$, where $\alpha_{fg} > \alpha_{bg}$. This emphasizes reconstruction accuracy in foreground regions (embodiment and interacted objects) while maintaining a lower weight for global visual consistency in the background.

2. **Action-centric Contrastive Learning ($L_{ctr}$):** This loss encourages latent actions representing similar coarse manipulation primitives to be close in the latent space, while pushing apart those representing different primitives. It operates on normalized latent action vectors, $v_i$, and uses a softplus contrastive loss function. Coarse primitive labels (e.g., pick–place, pour, open) are obtained by clustering verb-level categories extracted from video caption annotations. This objective structures the latent space based on abstract action semantics without relying on executable robot actions.

3. **Latent Space Calibration ($L_{cal}$):** This objective comprises two components:
 - **Free-bit KL Term ($L_{KL-fb}$):** This is a standard KL penalty applied only when a per-dimension free-bits floor is exceeded. Its role is to regulate latent space capacity, preventing the latent actions from encoding excessive, arbitrary transition information, thereby maintaining variation among ordinary transitions.
 - **Zero-transition Calibration Term ($L_{zero}$):** This term anchors the latent representation of duplicated-frame inputs ($z^0_t = \mu_{\phi}(o_t, o_t)$) to a zero-transition reference. It pushes the norm of these "static" latents below a small margin ($m_{zero}$) relative to the running RMS norm of ordinary transition latents. This calibrates the 'zero-action' point in the latent space without forcing all ordinary action latents to collapse.

**C. Multi-stage Training Pipeline:**
CD-LAM employs a three-stage fine-tuning process:

1. **Stage 1 (LAM Debiased Fine-tuning):** The LAM is fine-tuned using the $L_{CD}$ objective (Eq. 7) on action-unlabeled videos for 1,000 optimizer steps. This stage is responsible for creating the debiased latent action space.

2. **Stage 2 (ACWM Debiased Fine-tuning):** Debiased latent actions ($z^{CD}_t$) are extracted from unlabeled video transitions using the fine-tuned LAM. The ACWM is then further trained, conditioned on these $z^{CD}_t$ for 2,000 optimizer steps. This step adapts the world model to operate effectively with the new, debiased latent action space.

3. **Stage 3 (Robot Action Adaptation):** For data with paired robot actions ($o_t, u_t, o_{t+1}$), a lightweight MLP bridge ($g_{\eta}$) is trained. This bridge maps executable robot actions ($u_t$) into the debiased latent action space by regressing to the gradient-stopped CD-LAM latent, $g_{\eta}(u_t) \approx \text{sg}(\mu_{\phi}(o_t, o_{t+1}))$. An auxiliary cycle-consistency objective encourages the mapped latent to retain information about $u_t$. During this stage, the ACWM receives $\hat{z}_t = g_{\eta}(u_t)$, effectively integrating executable robot controls into the debiased latent space.

**D. Evaluation Protocol and Metrics:**
The evaluation encompasses:
- **Latent Action Understanding Audit:** Direct evaluation of the LAM's latent space for confounding using metrics such as zero-transition response (norm of latent from static frames), camera-shift robustness (norm of latent from synthetically shifted frames), and action-neighbor structure (shortcut leakage, cosine similarity between same/different primitive pairs).
- **Action Following and Visual Fidelity:**
 - **Visual Fidelity Metrics:** PSNR, Foreground PSNR (FG-PSNR), SSIM, and LPIPS are used to quantify visual quality of generated rollouts.
 - **Action Following Metric:** Foreground Displacement Chamfer Error (FDCE) is introduced. This metric measures the symmetric Chamfer distance between foreground displacement tracks in generated and reference videos. Foreground masks are obtained via SAM3, and point tracks are computed using CoWTracker within these regions. Lower FDCE indicates better action following.
- **Intervention Protocol:** The ACWM's response to direct action interventions is tested, including:
 - **Zero-action intervention:** Inputting a zero action, $do(u_t = 0)$, to assess if embodiment motion is suppressed.
 - **Target-action transfer:** Replacing the conditioning action with one from a different target video, $do(u_t = u^{tar}_t)$, to evaluate if the rollout reproduces the target embodiment dynamics under a fixed source context.

**E. Experimental Setup:**
Experiments are conducted on 2B and 14B ACWM backbones, with DreamDojo serving as the baseline. CD-LAM integrates its debiased LAM while keeping the ACWM architecture and conditioning format consistent. Evaluation data includes EgoDex for latent action rollouts and AgiBot for robot action rollouts and interventions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides empirical evidence supporting the existence of action-irrelevant bias in standard LAMs and demonstrates the effectiveness of CD-LAM in addressing this issue, leading to improved ACWM controllability and efficiency.

**A. Latent Action Understanding Audit (Stage 1):**
Initial analysis of the DreamDojo LAM revealed substantial action-irrelevant confounding.
- The median zero-transition response (latent norm from duplicated frames) was 0.527.
- Synthetic camera shifts induced a mean relative latent response of 0.555 (horizontal) and 0.545 (vertical).
- Shortcut leakage (cosine gap between different-action same-episode and same-action different-episode pairs) was 0.151.
CD-LAM significantly reduced these responses:
- Median zero-transition response dropped to 0.043.
- Camera-shift responses reduced by 3.6x-8.3x, to 0.156/0.096.
- Shortcut leakage fell to 0.014.
This indicates that CD-LAM effectively debiased the latent action space towards embodied transition semantics while preserving local action structure.

**B. ACWM Debiased Fine-tuning Rollouts (Stage 2):**
After ACWM debiased fine-tuning, latent-action conditioned rollouts showed consistent improvements:
- **Action Following (FDCE):** CD-LAM reduced FDCE by 42% at 2B (from 34.00 to 19.63) and 26% at 14B (from 40.29 to 29.87). Under target-action transfer, FDCE dropped by 21% (2B) and 34% (14B).
- **Visual Fidelity:** PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS also improved.
These gains, observed before robot action adaptation, indicate that the debiasing of the LAM successfully propagated to the world model. Notably, scaling the baseline DreamDojo from 2B to 14B sometimes worsened FDCE, suggesting that model scale alone does not mitigate confounding, whereas CD-LAM improved action following at both scales.

**C. Robot Action Adaptation Rollouts (Stage 3):**
After robot action adaptation, CD-LAM demonstrated strengthened embodiment action following and improved visual fidelity:
- **Action Following (FDCE):** FDCE_mean decreased by 35% at 2B (from 12.63 to 8.24) and 30% at 14B (from 11.11 to 7.73). FDCE_med also improved.
- **Visual Fidelity:** PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS consistently improved at both scales. The 14B CD-LAM model achieved the best performance across all metrics.
- **Interventions:**
 - **Zero-action ($do(u_t = 0)$):** Residual FDCE was halved at 2B (from 10.71 to 5.03) and reduced to less than a quarter at 14B (from 9.36 to 2.18), indicating effective suppression of unintended motion.
 - **Target-action transfer ($do(u_t = u^{tar}_t)$):** FDCE improved from 24.36 to 22.55 (2B) and from 24.82 to 21.11 (14B), demonstrating better transferability of commanded motions.
Qualitative rollouts (Fig. 5) illustrated that CD-LAM tracked commanded motions more reliably than DreamDojo, which often exhibited arm pose drift. The improvements in FDCE were observed across various action categories (Fig. 7a) and shifted the overall rollout distribution towards lower FDCE and higher PSNR (Fig. 7b).

**D. Robot Action Adaptation Efficiency and Debiasing Data Scaling:**
CD-LAM demonstrated significant efficiency gains in robot action adaptation:
- At 14B, CD-LAM achieved performance comparable to the DreamDojo reference within approximately 3k-4k updates for FDCE and PSNR, respectively. This represents a more than 12x reduction compared to DreamDojo's 50k update budget. CD-LAM surpassed DreamDojo's performance by the 6k final checkpoint.
- Regarding debiasing data scale, even the 1-hour CD-LAM tier yielded about 80% of the FDCE improvement achieved by the 1000-hour tier, suggesting that the primary benefit is derived from the targeted debiasing mechanism rather than solely from the quantity of debiasing data.

**E. Objective Ablation:**
An ablation study confirmed the complementary roles of the three CD-LAM objectives:
- **Embodiment-centric weighting:** Important for foreground fidelity (0.31 dB FG-PSNR gain) and action following (1.17 px FDCE improvement).
- **Action-centric contrast:** Primarily influences action-consistent transition neighborhoods (1.84 px FDCE improvement), with less impact on pixel-level fidelity or camera-shift response.
- **Zero-transition calibration:** Crucial for suppressing action-irrelevant camera-shift responses, reducing them by factors of 4.8x-6.3x. This term calibrates the "no-change" state in the latent space.

These findings collectively indicate that CD-LAM successfully addresses the identified confounding problem in LAMs, leading to more controllable and efficient ACWMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework, CD-LAM, that addresses a critical limitation in the development of controllable embodied action-conditioned world models (ACWMs). The significance and potential impact of this work span both theoretical understanding and practical applications:

**A. Theoretical Contributions:**
- **Identification of Causal Confounding:** The work rigorously identifies and empirically analyzes action-irrelevant bias as a key obstacle in existing latent action models (LAMs) that rely on reconstruction-only objectives. This highlights a fundamental limitation in defining "action" purely through predictive sufficiency for video generation.
- **Causal Debiasing Framework:** By introducing the concept of causally debiased latent actions, the paper shifts the focus from merely predicting video transitions to ensuring that the latent space explicitly encodes controllable embodiment dynamics, disentangled from spurious visual correlations.
- **Novel Metrics:** The introduction of specific metrics (e.g., FDCE, zero-transition response, camera-shift robustness, shortcut leakage) provides a more comprehensive and causally informed way to evaluate latent action representations and downstream ACWM controllability, beyond traditional pixel-level fidelity metrics.

**B. Practical Impact:**
- **Improved Controllability and Robustness:** CD-LAM substantially enhances the ability of ACWMs to reliably follow commanded latent and robot actions. This is crucial for applications like robot planning and policy evaluation, where precise control over simulated outcomes is necessary. The improved robustness to action-irrelevant factors reduces fragility and increases the practical utility of these models.
- **Increased Data Efficiency:** A key practical benefit is the significant reduction in the amount of robot action-labeled data and computational effort required for robot action adaptation. By pre-debiasing the latent action space, CD-LAM achieves comparable or superior performance with more than 12 times fewer robot action adaptation updates compared to baselines. This addresses a major bottleneck in scaling robot learning to diverse tasks and environments.
- **Enhanced Visual Fidelity:** Beyond improved action following, CD-LAM also leads to better visual fidelity in generated rollouts (higher PSNR, SSIM), demonstrating that causal debiasing can simultaneously improve both the control aspects and the visual realism of world models.
- **Leveraging Unlabeled Data More Effectively:** The framework enables more effective utilization of large-scale, unlabeled human video data for pre-training, transforming it into a more causally grounded basis for robot control signals. This paves the way for generalist robot world models that learn rich interaction dynamics from readily available human videos.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The release of debiased LAMs, ACWMs, evaluation protocols, and training code provides valuable resources for the research community, facilitating further exploration and development of controllable embodied AI systems.

In conclusion, CD-LAM represents an advancement in designing more controllable and efficient action-conditioned world models. By systematically addressing the problem of action-irrelevant confounding, this work contributes to building more reliable simulation environments and accelerating the development of intelligent embodied agents capable of complex real-world interactions.
