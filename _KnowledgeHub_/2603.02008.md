# Temporal Representations for Exploration: Learning Complex Exploratory Behavior without Extrinsic Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces C-TeC (Curiosity-Driven Exploration via Temporal Contrastive Learning), an intrinsic exploration method that leverages temporal contrastive representations to generate an intrinsic reward based on the unpredictability of future state outcomes. The method enables agents to learn complex exploratory behaviors in environments lacking extrinsic rewards, demonstrating superior state coverage and achievement unlocking in robotic and open-world game settings.

## Problem

- Reinforcement learning agents struggle with inefficient exploration in high-dimensional state spaces, requiring strategies to distinguish between useful and trivial states.
- Many representation learning approaches for exploration incur high computational costs due to demands of full state reconstruction or world modeling.
- Current intrinsic motivation methods suffer from limitations, such as impracticality in continuous spaces (count-based) or susceptibility to irrelevant noise (prediction-error methods like the 'Noisy TV problem').

## Method

- **C-TeC** trains a contrastive model using ==InfoNCE== loss to learn representations encoding discounted future state occupancy, specifically for current state-action pairs and future states.
- An intrinsic reward is formulated directly from the negative similarity score of the trained contrastive model, incentivizing exploration into states whose future outcomes are less predictable according to the learned temporal representations.
- The agent's policy is optimized using standard reinforcement learning algorithms (PPO/SAC) to maximize this intrinsic reward, with the process alternating between trajectory collection, contrastive model updates, and policy updates.

## Results

- C-TeC achieves consistently higher state coverage compared to RND, ICM, APT, and random policies across diverse robotic locomotion (Ant, Humanoid mazes) and manipulation (Arm Binpick Hard) environments.
- In the complex open-world survival game Craftax-Classic, C-TeC substantially outperforms baselines by unlocking significantly more achievements, showcasing its ability to foster complex, long-horizon exploratory behaviors.
- The method demonstrates emergent behaviors, such as a humanoid agent learning to jump over maze walls, and exhibits robustness to irrelevant perceptual distractors (Noisy TV problem) while maintaining stable representations throughout training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal contrastive representations that capture the temporal structure of the environment can effectively guide exploration without requiring full state reconstruction or explicit episodic memory mechanisms.
> - An intrinsic reward derived from the reverse KL-divergence between conditional and marginal future-state distributions drives mode-seeking behavior, encouraging agents to explore familiar yet temporally unpredictable regions.
> - The use of a factorized representation architecture (separate encoders for current state-action and future state) and proper representation normalization are crucial for the method's effectiveness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Exploration is a fundamental challenge in reinforcement learning (RL), particularly in environments characterized by high-dimensional observations or requirements for long-horizon reasoning. Traditional approaches often struggle with the vastness of state spaces, leading to inefficient learning. A prominent direction in addressing this challenge involves leveraging representation learning to compress raw observations into a more manageable and informative feature space. This allows agents to quantify "usefulness" or "novelty" to guide their exploration.

This paper positions itself within the domain of intrinsic motivation, where agents are incentivized to explore without relying on explicit extrinsic rewards. Prior work in this area includes count-based methods, effective in discrete or low-dimensional settings, and prediction-error-based approaches, which have shown utility in high-dimensional and continuous control tasks. Furthermore, representation learning has been applied in various contexts within RL, such as goal-conditioned learning, skill discovery, and curiosity-driven exploration, often utilizing self-supervised methods like contrastive learning.

The presented method, C-TeC (Curiosity-Driven Exploration via Temporal Contrastive Learning), focuses specifically on temporal representations. It argues that representations reflecting the temporal structure of the RL problem are more advantageous than those requiring full state reconstruction. Theoretically, such representations are considered sufficient statistics for Q-functions, akin to kernelized successor representations, and computationally, they offer efficiency advantages by avoiding the demands of full world models. Previous research has demonstrated the utility of temporal contrastive representations for learning policies and value functions.

C-TeC is closely related to methods that employ contrastive learning for curiosity-driven exploration and state coverage. It distinguishes itself from approaches that learn explicit distance metrics via quasimetric learning (e.g., Jiang et al., 2025) and those that rely on episodic memory mechanisms. By avoiding these components, C-TeC aims for a simpler algorithmic structure, which may make it more compatible with off-policy RL algorithms. Unlike some contrastive methods that focus on learning visual representations or generic prediction error, C-TeC specifically leverages temporal contrastive features to capture how an agent perceives and represents the world's temporal dynamics. The work implicitly seeks to overcome issues like the "Noisy TV problem" by rewarding meaningful unpredictability rather than arbitrary noise.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an effective and efficient method for intrinsic exploration in reinforcement learning environments that lack extrinsic rewards. The authors are motivated by several key challenges in current exploration paradigms:

1. **Ineffective Exploration in High-Dimensional Spaces**: In complex environments with vast state spaces, agents often struggle to distinguish between useful and trivial states. Visiting every state is computationally infeasible, necessitating strategies that guide agents to informative regions.
2. **Computational Cost of Full State Reconstruction**: Many representation learning approaches in RL, particularly those based on world models, incur significant computational overhead due to the requirement of reconstructing high-dimensional inputs. The authors aim to find a more computationally efficient alternative.
3. **Necessity of Extrinsic Rewards for Complex Behaviors**: Learning complex exploratory behaviors in domains like locomotion, manipulation, or open-world games typically requires carefully designed extrinsic reward functions. This work seeks to enable such complex behaviors solely through intrinsic motivation, thus reducing the need for human reward engineering.
4. **Limitations of Existing Intrinsic Motivation Methods**:
 - **Count-based methods**: These are often impractical in continuous or high-dimensional state spaces.
 - **Prediction-error methods**: While more suitable for continuous spaces, they can be susceptible to "noisy TV" problems, where agents are drawn to states with unpredictable but meaningless outcomes (e.g., random visual noise). The motivation here is to identify unpredictability that is meaningful in a temporal sense.
 - **Quasimetric/Episodic memory methods**: Recent contrastive exploration techniques often rely on learning explicit similarity metrics or maintaining episodic memories, which can add complexity or limit applicability (e.g., to off-policy settings). The paper aims to simplify this by directly leveraging temporal similarities without these additional components.

To address these motivations, the core objective is to:
- **Learn Powerful Temporal Representations**: Develop representations that inherently capture the temporal structure of the environment and agent interactions, sufficient for various potential tasks without needing to reconstruct full states.
- **Formulate an Intrinsic Reward from Temporal Unpredictability**: Design a reward signal that guides exploration by prioritizing states where the agent's learned temporal representations exhibit high prediction error or low predictability of future outcomes. This reward should encourage the agent to visit states that contribute to its knowledge of environmental dynamics over time.
- **Demonstrate Effectiveness across Diverse Tasks**: Evaluate the proposed method on a range of challenging tasks, including locomotion, manipulation, and open-world survival games, to show its capacity for learning complex exploratory behaviors comparable to, or exceeding, methods that use extrinsic rewards or more complex intrinsic motivations.
- **Achieve Simplicity and Efficiency**: Develop an exploration strategy that is algorithmically simpler, avoids episodic memory, and is amenable to standard RL algorithms (on-policy and off-policy).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The C-TeC method proposes an exploration strategy that leverages temporal contrastive learning to generate an intrinsic reward signal. The core idea is to learn representations that encode an agent's future state occupancy and then reward the agent for transitioning into states whose future outcomes are unpredictable given the current state-action.

**Discounted State Occupancy Measure:**
The method begins by defining a $\gamma$-discounted state occupancy measure, $p_T(s_f | s_t, a_t)$, derived from a trajectory buffer $T$. This buffer collects trajectories from a history of policies. To sample a future state $s_f$ for a given $(s_t, a_t)$ pair, an offset $\Delta$ is drawn from a geometric distribution $GEOM(1-\gamma)$, and $s_f$ is set to $s_{t+\Delta}$. This formulation means that states further in the future are less likely to be sampled, reflecting a temporal discount.

**Contrastive Model Training:**
A contrastive model, $C_\theta(s_t, a_t, s_f)$, is trained to estimate state occupancy. This model uses two parameterized encoders: $\phi_\theta(s_t, a_t)$ for the current state-action pair and $\psi_\theta(s_f)$ for the future state. The outputs of these encoders are normalized to have unit norm. The similarity between representations is computed using either the negative $\ell_1$ or $\ell_2$ norm, depending on the environment.

The model is trained using the InfoNCE loss (Equation 2). For each positive pair $((s_t, a_t), s_f)$, negative examples are drawn from the product of marginal distributions, $p_T(s_t, a_t)p_T(s_f)$, meaning $s_f$ is sampled independently of $(s_t, a_t)$. In practice, the positive examples from other samples in the batch serve as negatives. The InfoNCE loss aims to maximize the similarity between positive pairs while minimizing it for negative pairs. An additional LogSumExp regularizer is incorporated, consistent with prior work on contrastive reinforcement learning. The temperature parameter $\tau$ in the InfoNCE loss is learned during training. The optimal critic $C^*((s_t, a_t), s_f)$ is shown to approximate the log probability ratio $\log p_T(s_f | s_t, a_t) - \log p_T(s_f)$.

**Intrinsic Reward Formulation:**
The intrinsic reward, $r_{intr}(s_t, a_t)$, is constructed directly from the trained contrastive model. It is defined as the expectation of the negative similarity score over future states sampled from $p_T(s_f | s_t, a_t)$:
$E[r_{intr}(s_t, a_t)] = E_{p_T(s_f | s_t, a_t)}[-C_\theta((s_t, a_t), s_f)] = E_{p_T(s_f | s_t, a_t)}[||\phi_\theta(s_t, a_t) - \psi_\theta(s_f)||]$.
This reward incentivizes the agent to visit states that are less predictable in terms of their future outcomes according to the learned representations. A higher reward is given when the representation of the current state-action pair is "far" from the representation of its future state, indicating unpredictability or a temporal distance that the model has not yet fully captured. For policy training, a single sample future state is typically used to approximate this expectation, though Monte Carlo estimates are used in more complex environments like Craftax-Classic.

**Policy Optimization:**
The agent's policy is trained to maximize this intrinsic reward using standard reinforcement learning algorithms, specifically PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) for on-policy settings and SAC (Soft Actor-Critic) for off-policy settings. The overall process alternates between collecting trajectories, updating the contrastive model, computing intrinsic rewards, and updating the policy.

**Theoretical Interpretation (Information-Theoretic):**
In an idealized scenario where representations perfectly capture point-wise mutual information, the intrinsic reward can be interpreted as the negative KL-divergence between the conditional future-state distribution and the marginal future-state distribution: $E[r_{intr}(s_t, a_t)] = -D_{KL}[p_T(s_f | s_t, a_t) || p_T(s_f)]$. This is a "mode-seeking" objective, which encourages the policy to find $(s_t, a_t)$ pairs that lead to future states $s_f$ which are minimally informative about $s_t, a_t$, or states that are "familiar" (present in $p_T(s_f)$) but temporally distant or hard to predict from the current context. The method focuses on states that have been visited but are temporally distant, rather than solely unvisited states.

**Importance of Learned Representations:**
The authors emphasize that the learned contrastive representations are crucial. They not only approximate an information-theoretic signal but also capture a form of representation prediction error. The representations learn features relevant for temporal classification and ignore irrelevant perturbations (e.g., noise in the "Noisy TV" problem), making the intrinsic reward robust. Ablation studies support the necessity of the factorized representation architecture (separate encoders for $(s_t, a_t)$ and $s_f$) over a monolithic critic.

**Distinction from ETD (Jiang et al., 2025):**
C-TeC avoids the explicit quasimetric learning and episodic memory mechanisms used in ETD. While ETD encourages novelty by maximizing minimum temporal distance from past states (a backward-looking approach), C-TeC prioritizes states that can lead to a larger set of possible future states (a forward-looking perspective). This forward-looking view is hypothesized to capture long-term exploratory value more effectively.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates C-TeC across a range of environments, including locomotion (Ant and Humanoid mazes), manipulation (Arm Binpick Hard), and open-world survival games (Craftax-Classic). The evaluation focuses on state coverage and the emergence of complex exploratory behaviors, comparing C-TeC against several baselines: Random Network Distillation (RND), Intrinsic Curiosity Module (ICM), Active Pre-training (APT), and a uniform random policy. A direct comparison with Exploration via Temporal Distance (ETD) and a model-based RL (MBRL) method is also provided. All experiments are conducted in an intrinsic exploration setting, without extrinsic task rewards.

**Q1: Comparison to ETD (Jiang et al., 2025)**
- **Robotic Environments**: C-TeC demonstrates competitive performance with ETD in `ant_hardest_maze` and shows higher state coverage in `humanoid_u_maze`, albeit with increased variance. ETD exhibits better performance in `arm_binpick_hard`.
- **Crafter (a variant of Craftax)**: C-TeC significantly outperforms ETD in terms of achievement scores, suggesting its forward-looking exploration strategy is more beneficial in open-world settings that require discovering long-term action sequences.
- **Algorithmic Complexity**: The findings indicate that C-TeC achieves comparable or superior performance while requiring less algorithmic complexity, notably by avoiding quasimetric parameterization and episodic memory.

**Q2, Q3: Leveraging the Future State Distribution for Exploration and Effectiveness**
- **Intrinsic Reward Behavior**: Visualizations in `ant_hardest_maze` illustrate that the C-TeC reward captures the agent’s future state density, rewarding visits to less-occupied or temporally distant future states. The reward consistently encourages deeper exploration as training progresses.
- **State Coverage (Full State Vector)**: C-TeC consistently achieves higher state coverage than APT, RND, ICM, and random policies in `ant_large_maze`, `humanoid_u_maze`, and `arm_binpick_hard` environments. This indicates its effectiveness in diverse continuous control and manipulation tasks.
- **State Coverage (Prior Knowledge/Subspace Exploration)**: When leveraging prior knowledge by restricting the "future state" to specific components (e.g., ant's (x,y) position, humanoid's (x,y,z) position, cube position in manipulation), C-TeC maintains its superior performance compared to baselines. This demonstrates the method's flexibility in guiding exploration within specific subspaces of the state.
- **Emergent Behaviors**: In `humanoid_u_maze`, the C-TeC agent learns a complex emergent behavior: jumping over maze walls to escape, a capability not observed with other exploration methods. This suggests C-TeC can facilitate the discovery of non-trivial motor skills.
- **Reward Properties**: Unlike RND and ICM, whose rewards tend to zero as states become familiar, C-TeC's reward does not diminish to zero, which may contribute to its sustained exploration capabilities.

**Learning Complex Behavior in Craftax-Classic**
- C-TeC substantially outperforms RND, ICM, E3B, and a random policy in the complex open-world survival game Craftax-Classic, measured by the sum of achievements unlocked. This highlights its ability to foster complex, long-horizon behaviors in challenging environments without any task-specific rewards.

**Ablation Studies**
- **Representation Normalization**: Normalizing contrastive representations is shown to be crucial for effective exploration; unnormalized representations significantly degrade performance.
- **Contrastive Losses**: C-TeC demonstrates reasonable robustness to the choice of contrastive loss function (InfoNCE, Symmetric InfoNCE, FlatNCE, FB), though NCE (Binary) performed less effectively in Craftax.
- **Contrastive Critic Functions**: The choice of similarity function matters: L1 distance yields best performance in robotic environments, while L2 distance performs better in Craftax.
- **Contrastive Critic Architecture**: The factorized representation parameterization (separate encoders $\phi_\theta$ and $\psi_\theta$) is necessary for effective exploration; a monolithic critic architecture performs poorly.
- **Forward vs. Reverse KL**: Using the reverse KL-divergence, which promotes mode-seeking behavior, is found to be essential for C-TeC's exploration success, outperforming the forward KL.
- **Future State Sampling Strategy**: C-TeC is robust to different future state sampling strategies (geometric, uniform, or a scheduled increase in $\gamma$), with all variants outperforming baselines.
- **Effect of Discount Factor ($\gamma_{cl}$)**: The optimal discount factor for sampling future states varies across environments, suggesting it can be a tunable hyperparameter.
- **Effect of Temperature Parameter ($\tau$)**: C-TeC's performance is sensitive to $\tau$, with a value of 1 generally yielding strong state coverage, implying the importance of balancing alignment and uniformity in representations.
- **In-episode vs. Across-episode Negative Sampling**: In-episode negative sampling can provide an additional performance boost in some environments.

**Noisy TV Setting**
- In a modified Xland-Minigrid environment with a noisy TV region, C-TeC exhibits effective state coverage, demonstrating its robustness to irrelevant perceptual distractors and its ability to learn meaningful temporal features.

**Sample Efficiency and Representation Stability**
- C-TeC is shown to explore effectively with significantly fewer environment steps (e.g., 10x or 16x less) while also demonstrating scalability with more interactions.
- The variance of the contrastive representations remains stable throughout training, indicating that the model does not suffer from mode collapse.

**Comparison to Model-Based Baselines**
- C-TeC outperforms a model-based RL baseline (Stadie et al., 2015) in the tested robotics environments, suggesting its intrinsic motivation strategy is more effective for exploration in these settings.

**Didactic Toy Examples**
- Toy MDPs illustrate the core differences: C-TeC's mode-seeking behavior favors branches with fast, deterministic dynamics (where $p(s_f|s,a)$ aligns with $p(s_f)$), while ETD's backward-looking approach (maximizing temporal distance) pushes into slow, sticky dynamics. These examples clarify how C-TeC's forward-looking rewards can be beneficial in environments with varied transition times.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by introducing C-TeC, a method for intrinsic exploration that leverages temporal contrastive representations. The significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Advancement in Unsupervised Reinforcement Learning**: C-TeC demonstrates that complex exploratory behaviors can be learned effectively without the need for extrinsic reward functions or human-engineered task specifications. This capability is crucial for developing autonomous agents that can learn in open-ended or unknown environments.
2. **Simplicity and Efficiency**: By avoiding explicit distance learning mechanisms (like quasimetrics) and episodic memory, C-TeC offers a simpler algorithmic framework compared to some contemporary methods. Its reliance on temporal contrastive representations also sidesteps the computational expense associated with learning full world models or performing state reconstruction. This simplicity may facilitate broader adoption and integration into existing RL pipelines, including off-policy algorithms.
3. **Robustness to Distractors**: The method's demonstrated resilience to "Noisy TV" environments indicates that its intrinsic reward mechanism is capable of distinguishing meaningful unpredictability from irrelevant perceptual noise. This is a critical property for practical application in visually rich and complex environments.
4. **Discovery of Complex Emergent Behaviors**: The ability of C-TeC agents to discover non-trivial motor skills, such as jumping over walls in a maze, highlights its potential to uncover sophisticated control strategies that might be difficult to engineer or learn via traditional reward shaping. This could lead to more versatile and intelligent agents.
5. **Generalizability Across Domains**: The consistent performance across diverse environments—ranging from robotic locomotion and manipulation to complex open-world games—suggests that C-TeC's principles are broadly applicable. This broad applicability increases its potential utility in various real-world and simulated tasks.
6. **Theoretical Insights into Exploration**: The information-theoretic interpretation of C-TeC as a mode-seeking reward, which minimizes the reverse KL-divergence, offers a conceptual understanding of why it encourages exploration into states that are "familiar" yet "unpredictable" from a temporal perspective. This contributes to the theoretical foundation of intrinsic motivation.
7. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens avenues for further investigation into the role of temporal representations in RL. Future work could involve integrating C-TeC with extrinsic task rewards to accelerate learning, adapting the method to pixel-based observations and partially observed settings, and exploring dynamic adjustments to its internal parameters (e.g., the discount factor $\gamma_{cl}$) based on environmental characteristics.

In summary, C-TeC provides a principled, effective, and relatively simple approach to intrinsic exploration, offering a significant step towards developing more autonomous and capable reinforcement learning agents in environments where external rewards are sparse or absent.
