# MiniMax-M1: Scaling Test-Time Compute Efficiently with Lightning Attention

> [!summary] Summary
> MiniMax developed MiniMax-M1, a large reasoning model leveraging a hybrid Mixture-of-Experts architecture with Lightning Attention to enable efficient scaling of test-time compute for extremely long contexts. It supports up to 1 million input tokens and 80,000 generation tokens while achieving substantial FLOPs reduction and competitive performance across various reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- The quadratic computational complexity of traditional softmax attention limits the ability to continuously extend reasoning processes and support very long context lengths in large language models (LLMs).
- Existing large reasoning models (LRMs) struggle with high computational costs during inference for extended generations, making them prohibitively expensive for complex real-world applications.
- There was a lack of open-weight, large-scale reasoning models that effectively leverage and validate computationally efficient attention mechanisms for long-context tasks.

## Method

- The model employs a ==hybrid Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture== with 456 billion total parameters, integrating ==Lightning Attention== (an I/O-aware linear attention variant) in most blocks, interspersed with standard softmax attention blocks.
- A multi-stage training pipeline was used, including continual pre-training on a reasoning-intensive corpus, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with long Chain-of-Thought examples, and extensive reinforcement learning (RL).
- A novel RL algorithm, ==ClippedIS-weight Policy Optimization (CISPO)==, was introduced to clip importance sampling weights rather than token updates, enhancing training stability and achieving a ==2x speedup over DAPO==.

## Results

- MiniMax-M1 achieved a significant FLOPs reduction during inference, consuming less than **50%** of FLOPs compared to DeepSeek R1 at **64K** generation length and approximately **25%** at **100K** tokens.
- The model natively supports input contexts up to **1 million tokens** and generation lengths of **80,000 tokens**, demonstrating an **8x** context size increase over DeepSeek R1.
- MiniMax-M1-80k outperformed other open-weight models and even some closed-weight models (like OpenAI o3 and Claude 4 Opus) on long-context understanding benchmarks (e.g., OpenAI-MRCR at 128k and 1M tokens), while performing comparably on general coding and agentic tool use benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Efficient linear attention mechanisms, when integrated into a hybrid architecture, can achieve competitive performance at frontier model scales while providing substantial efficiency gains for long-context reasoning.
> - The novel CISPO reinforcement learning algorithm, which clips importance sampling weights instead of token updates, significantly improves training efficiency and stability for complex reasoning behaviors.
> - Scaling context lengths during training for hybrid attention models requires a gradual, multi-staged approach and careful management of precision mismatches and pathological generation patterns to ensure stability.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current landscape of large language models (LLMs) is heavily influenced by the success of "large reasoning models (LRMs)," exemplified by models such as OpenAI o1, DeepSeek-R1, Anthropic's Claude, and Google DeepMind's Gemini. A key trend identified in their success is the concept of "test-time compute scaling," where dedicating more computational resources to extended reasoning processes during generation leads to consistent performance improvements, particularly for complex real-world applications.

However, the paper highlights a significant challenge: the inherent quadratic computational complexity of the traditional softmax attention mechanism within the prevailing transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017). This quadratic scaling poses a formidable barrier to continuously extending the reasoning process and supporting very long context lengths, making it prohibitively expensive. Researchers have proposed various techniques to mitigate this issue, including sparse attention, linear attention variants (e.g., with delta decay), state-space models (SSMs) like Mamba, and linear RNNs. Despite these advancements, the authors note that most competitive LRMs to date still rely on traditional attention designs, with few large-scale models fully validating or leveraging these more efficient architectures. The Hunyuan-T1 model by Tencent AI Lab is mentioned as an exception for its use of the Mamba architecture, but its closed-source nature limits transparency and broader validation.

MiniMax-M1 directly addresses this critical gap. It aims to build and open-source a large reasoning model that not only efficiently scales test-time compute but also competes effectively with state-of-the-art reasoning models, both open-weight and closed-weight. By adopting a *hybrid* Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture combined with Lightning Attention (an I/O-aware linear attention variant), MiniMax-M1 seeks to demonstrate the practical viability and performance competitiveness of computationally efficient attention mechanisms at a frontier scale. This positions MiniMax-M1 as a significant contribution to the field, pushing the boundaries of what is possible with open-source, long-context LLMs and offering a clear path forward for efficient, scalable reasoning. It directly challenges leading open-weight models like DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3-235B by offering superior efficiency and context handling, and even demonstrates competitive performance against top commercial models in specific challenging domains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop and openly release MiniMax-M1, a large-scale reasoning model capable of efficiently scaling test-time compute to handle extremely long contexts and complex reasoning tasks. This core objective is driven by several key motivations:

- **Overcoming Computational Bottlenecks:** The primary motivation stems from the recognition that while extended reasoning processes significantly enhance LRM performance, the quadratic computational complexity of traditional softmax attention makes continuous scaling of reasoning lengths economically and computationally challenging. The team aims to circumvent this bottleneck by integrating a more efficient attention mechanism.
- **Enabling True Long-Context Reasoning:** The ambition is to support context lengths far beyond what is commonly available in current LLMs, particularly open-weight models. The goal is to natively support context lengths up to 1 million tokens and generation lengths of 80,000 tokens, which is crucial for tackling real-world problems that require processing vast amounts of information and extensive "thinking."
- **Achieving Cost-Efficiency at Scale:** A crucial objective is to significantly reduce the computational cost (FLOPs) during inference, especially for long generations. By making reasoning more efficient, the model becomes more practical for widespread deployment and use in agents that might perform numerous reasoning steps.
- **Advancing RL Training Efficiency:** Developing and implementing novel techniques to make large-scale reinforcement learning (RL) training more efficient and stable for such complex, hybrid architectures is a key goal. This includes both architectural advantages and algorithmic innovations.
- **Demonstrating State-of-the-Art Performance:** The motivation is not merely to achieve efficiency but to ensure that this efficiency does not compromise performance. The team aims for MiniMax-M1 to be comparable or superior to leading open-weight and even some closed-weight models across a diverse range of challenging reasoning and long-context tasks.
- **Fostering Open-Source Collaboration:** By open-sourcing MiniMax-M1, the authors aim to facilitate broader research, development, and adoption of efficient large reasoning models, aligning with their previous contributions and promoting community-driven progress.
- **Paving the Way for Next-Generation Agents:** Ultimately, the work is motivated by the vision of building a strong foundation for future language model agents that can robustly reason, interact with environments and tools, and tackle complex, multi-turn, real-world challenges.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of MiniMax-M1 involved a multi-stage training pipeline, architectural innovations, and novel algorithmic contributions, all focused on enabling efficient and scalable reinforcement learning for reasoning.

#### 3.1. Architectural Design: Hybrid Mixture-of-Experts with Lightning Attention

MiniMax-M1 is built upon a hybrid Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture, a common approach for scaling LLMs that allows for a vast total parameter count (456 billion) while activating a smaller, efficient subset (45.9 billion parameters activated per token) via 32 experts. The distinguishing feature is its integration of **Lightning Attention** (Qin et al., 2024b), an I/O-aware implementation of a linear attention variant (Qin et al., 2022a). This marks a departure from traditional softmax attention. The hybrid nature means that a standard transformer block with softmax attention is included after every seven "transnormer" blocks (Qin et al., 2022a) that utilize lightning attention. This design is theoretically crucial for achieving efficient scaling of reasoning lengths to hundreds of thousands of tokens, addressing the quadratic complexity bottleneck. The model natively supports an impressive context length of up to 1 million tokens for input and 80,000 tokens for generation, leveraging the efficiency of lightning attention.

#### 3.2. Training Pipeline

The training of MiniMax-M1 is divided into three key stages:

1. **Continual Pre-training:**
 - **Foundation Model:** The process began by continuing the pre-training of their existing MiniMax-Text-01 model.
 - **Data Curation:** An additional 7.5 trillion tokens were used, sourced from a carefully curated, reasoning-intensive corpus. This involved refining Web and PDF parsing, enhancing heuristic cleaning rules for high recall of mathematical and code data, prioritizing natural Question-Answer (QA) pairs (strictly avoiding synthetic data), and semantic deduplication. The proportion of STEM, code, book, and reasoning-related data was significantly increased to 70%.
 - **Training Recipe:** The MoE auxiliary loss coefficient was decreased, and parallel training strategies were adjusted to support larger micro-batch sizes. A constant learning rate of 8e-5 was used for the first 2.5T tokens, followed by a decay schedule over 5T tokens down to 8e-6.
 - **Long Context Extension:** A crucial innovation for the hybrid-lightning architecture was a smoother, staged extension of the context length across four phases, starting from 32K and gradually reaching 1M tokens. This was implemented to prevent gradient explosions observed with aggressive extensions, attributed to the differing decay rates and local information focus of earlier and later layers in lightning attention.

2. **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):**
 - After continual pre-training, SFT was performed to inject specific reasoning patterns, particularly Chain-of-Thought (CoT) behaviors. High-quality examples with long CoT responses were curated, covering diverse domains like math, coding, STEM, writing, QA, and multi-turn chat, with math and coding accounting for about 60% of the data. This stage provided a robust starting point for subsequent RL.

3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - RL was the core stage for M1 development, aimed at enhancing reasoning capabilities. This stage involved significant algorithmic and practical innovations:
 - **Novel RL Algorithm: CISPO (ClippedIS-weight Policy Optimization):** The authors found that standard PPO/GRPO's "token clipping" operation, which discards tokens with high importance sampling (IS) weights, adversely affected training, especially for rare, reflective tokens crucial for reasoning paths. CISPO addresses this by clipping the IS weights (Eq. 5) rather than the token updates. This ensures all tokens contribute to gradient computations, enhancing efficiency and stabilizing training by preserving entropy. Empirically, CISPO achieved a 2x speedup compared to DAPO on a controlled study.
 - **Challenges and Solutions for Hybrid Attention RL:**
 - *Computational Precision Mismatch:* A significant discrepancy in rolled-out token probabilities between training and inference modes was observed due to precision mismatches in kernels. The solution involved increasing the precision of the LM output head to FP32, which significantly improved the correlation between training and inference probabilities (from ~0.9x to 0.99x) and enabled reward growth.
 - *Optimizer Hyperparameter Sensitivity:* AdamW's default settings led to non-convergence due to wide-ranging gradient magnitudes. The team tuned AdamW hyperparameters to β1=0.9, β2=0.95, and eps=1e-15 based on empirical observations.
 - *Early Truncation via Repetition Detection:* Long, repetitive responses, especially from complex prompts, caused model instability and large gradients. A heuristic was developed to halt generation if 3,000 consecutive tokens had a probability above 0.99, preventing pathological loops and improving throughput.
 - **Diverse RL Training Data and Reward Mechanisms:**
 - *Rule-based Verification:* For tasks with verifiable answers, rule-based correctness rewards were used:
 - **Mathematical Reasoning:** ~50K competition-level problems, filtered for quality and difficulty (pass@10 between 0 and 0.9).
 - **Logical Reasoning:** ~53K synthesized samples across 41 tasks using the SynLogic framework, with difficulty parameters controlled to ensure learnability.
 - **Competitive Programming:** 30K problems, some with LLM-generated test cases, filtered for moderate challenge.
 - **Software Engineering (SWE):** Real-world GitHub issues (bug localization, code repair, test synthesis) leveraged within a sophisticated containerized sandbox for execution-based, verifiable rewards.
 - *Model-based Feedback (Generative Reward Models - GenRM):* For general domain tasks without easy rule-based verification:
 - **Tasks with Ground Truth (STEM, factual):** GenRM used as a verifier with a five-grade reward scale, trained on human-annotated data.
 - **Tasks without Ground Truth (Instruction-following, creative writing):** ~25K complex samples. Pairwise comparison (-1, 0, 1) against reference answers. Addressed "length bias" in GenRMs (preference for longer outputs) through continuous online monitoring and recalibration of GenRMs, combined with RL-side techniques like reward shaping and value clipping.
 - **Curriculum Learning:** RL training initiated with reasoning-intensive tasks (rule-based rewards), gradually incorporating general domain tasks (model-based feedback). This curriculum aimed to preserve specialized skills while fostering generalization.

#### 3.3. Extending RL Scaling to Longer Thinking

Two models were trained: MiniMax-M1-40k and MiniMax-M1-80k. The 80K model extended the maximum generation length during RL training.
- **Data Adaptation:** For 80K training, the 40K model guided data filtering, prioritizing challenging examples and downsampling synthetic reasoning data due to its tendency to induce repetitive and homogenous outputs in long contexts.
- **Staged Length Scaling:** A gradual window expansion strategy was employed (40K -> 48K -> ... -> 80K) to ensure training stability, with transitions based on perplexity convergence and output length approaching the current limit.
- **Addressing Instability:** During long-context scaling, "pattern collapse" (incoherent text in later parts of generations) occurred. This was linked to negative samples growing disproportionately in length, causing large negative gradients. Solutions included: (1) Early stopping for repetitive patterns, (2) Combined sample-level loss and token-level normalization, and (3) Decreased gradient clipping threshold and IS weight upper bound (εIS_high).

The entire RL training for MiniMax-M1 completed in three weeks using 512 H800 GPUs, at an estimated rental cost of approximately $534,700, showcasing remarkable efficiency for a model of its scale.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MiniMax-M1 demonstrates its strengths in efficiency, long-context handling, and performance across diverse reasoning tasks, particularly in complex, real-world scenarios.

#### 4.1. Efficiency and Scalability

- **Computational Cost Reduction:** MiniMax-M1 achieves significant FLOPs reduction during inference for long generations. Compared to DeepSeek R1, it consumes less than 50% of FLOPs at a generation length of 64K tokens and approximately 25% at 100K tokens. This validates the effectiveness of its hybrid attention design.
- **Context Length Capabilities:** The model natively supports input contexts up to 1 million tokens and generation lengths of 80,000 tokens. This is 8x the context size of DeepSeek R1 and an order of magnitude greater than other open-weight LRMs available at the time of the paper.
- **RL Training Efficiency:** The novel CISPO algorithm proved highly effective, achieving a 2x speedup compared to DAPO in controlled studies (e.g., on AIME 2024 using Qwen2.5-32B-base). This algorithmic efficiency, combined with the architectural advantages of lightning attention, enabled the complete RL training of MiniMax-M1 on 512 H800 GPUs in just three weeks, with a relatively low rental cost of approximately $0.53M USD.

#### 4.2. Benchmark Performance (MiniMax-M1-80k)

MiniMax-M1-80k (the larger model with 80K thinking budget) generally performs comparably or superior to strong open-weight models such as the original DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3-235B, and even shows competitive results against leading closed-weight models in certain areas.

- **Mathematics:** Achieves 86.0% on AIME 2024, placing it second among open-weight models, trailing only the latest DeepSeek-R1-0528 model. On MATH-500, it scored 96.8%.
- **General Coding:** Matches Qwen3-235B on LiveCodeBench and outperforms it on FullStackBench, demonstrating robust capabilities.
- **Software Engineering (SWE-bench Verified):** Achieves a strong score of 56.0%, which is slightly inferior to DeepSeek-R1-0528 (57.6%) but significantly surpasses other open-weight models. This highlights the effectiveness of their execution-based RL training environments.
- **Long Context Understanding:** This is a particular strength. MiniMax-M1-80k significantly outperforms all other open-weight models on OpenAI-MRCR (128k context) and LongBench-v2. Crucially, it even surpasses OpenAI o3 and Claude 4 Opus on these benchmarks, trailing only Gemini 2.5 Pro by a small margin. For the 1M token MRCR evaluation, M1-80k scores 56.2%, only slightly behind Gemini 2.5 Pro's 58.8%.
- **Agentic Tool Use (TAU-bench):** MiniMax-M1-40k (the intermediate model) remarkably surpasses all open-weight models and even Gemini 2.5 Pro. MiniMax-M1-80k consistently outperforms MiniMax-M1-40k on most benchmarks, further validating the benefits of scaling test-time compute.
- **Reasoning & Knowledge (GPQA Diamond, HLE, ZebraLogic, MMLU-Pro):** While generally competitive with other top open-weight models, it typically trails DeepSeek-R1-0528 in these categories.
- **Factuality (SimpleQA):** Outperforms most other open-weight models and Seed-Thinking-v1.5, though it underperforms DeepSeek-R1 in this specific area.
- **General Assistant (MultiChallenge):** Both MiniMax-M1 models perform comparably to DeepSeek-R1-0528 and Claude 4 Opus, with superior results only from OpenAI o3 and Gemini 2.5 Pro.

#### 4.3. Effect of RL Scaling

Tracking performance and response length during training clearly showed consistent improvements in both model accuracy and average response length. For instance, on AIME 2024, accuracy increased substantially from 68% to 80%, with average response lengths exceeding 20,000 tokens. This strong correlation underscores that extending RL scaling effectively facilitates more extensive reasoning processes, validating the underlying hypothesis of the work.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MiniMax-M1 model represents a significant stride in the development of large reasoning models, particularly for the open-source community. Its core significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Pioneering Open-Weight Efficient LRMs:** MiniMax-M1 is introduced as the world’s first open-weight, large-scale reasoning model featuring a hybrid-attention mechanism (Lightning Attention). This is a crucial step towards bridging the gap between theoretical advancements in efficient attention and their practical, large-scale deployment, making such capabilities accessible to a wider research and development community.
- **Unlocking Extremely Long Context and Reasoning:** By natively supporting input contexts up to 1 million tokens and generation lengths of 80,000 tokens, MiniMax-M1 pushes the boundaries of what is feasible for LLMs in terms of context handling. This unprecedented capacity (8x that of DeepSeek R1 and an order of magnitude more than other open-weight models) is vital for complex, real-world tasks that necessitate processing vast amounts of information or generating extensive, multi-step reasoning.
- **Demonstrating Cost-Effective Scaling of Test-Time Compute:** The significant reduction in FLOPs for long generations (e.g., 25% of DeepSeek R1's FLOPs at 100K tokens) demonstrates that high-performance, long-context reasoning can be achieved with substantially lower inference costs. This efficiency is critical for the practical adoption of LRMs in diverse applications, especially for agentic systems requiring numerous reasoning steps.
- **Algorithmic Innovation for RL:** The introduction of CISPO, a novel RL algorithm that clips importance sampling weights instead of token updates, provides a more efficient and stable training mechanism. This addresses a common issue in RL that can hinder the development of complex reasoning behaviors, particularly those involving low-probability, reflective tokens. CISPO's demonstrated speedup (2x over DAPO) can accelerate future RL-based LRM development.
- **Foundation for Next-Generation Agentic AI:** The paper explicitly positions MiniMax-M1 as a "strong foundation for next-generation language model agents." Real-world applications, such as automating company workflows or conducting scientific research, often demand agents that can interact with environments, tools, and other agents, requiring multi-turn reasoning and the integration of long-context information. MiniMax-M1's unique combination of long context, efficient scaling, and strong reasoning capabilities makes it exceptionally well-suited for these complex agentic scenarios.
- **Accelerating Community Research and Development:** The public release of MiniMax-M1, with support for popular frameworks like vLLM and Transformers, along with a commercial API, significantly lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and developers. This open approach will undoubtedly stimulate further innovation in efficient LLM architectures, long-context reasoning, and the development of more capable AI agents.

In conclusion, MiniMax-M1 represents a critical advancement in the field of large language models, proving that efficient, hybrid attention mechanisms can scale to competitive levels in reasoning tasks while offering substantial cost and context advantages. Its open-weight release and demonstrated capabilities set a new benchmark for what is achievable in open-source LLMs and lay a robust foundation for the future of AI agents tackling real-world challenges.
