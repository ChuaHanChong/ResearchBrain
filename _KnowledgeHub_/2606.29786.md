# OP3DSG: Open-Vocabulary Part-Aware 3D Scene Graph Generation for Real-World Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> The Gwangju Institute of Science and Technology developed OP3DSG, an open-vocabulary framework for generating unified 3D Scene Graphs that jointly model objects, interactive parts, spatial relations, functional relations, and affordances. This system significantly improves part-level recognition and multi-level relational reasoning, providing a more comprehensive scene understanding for embodied AI agents.

## Problem

- Existing 3D Scene Graphs (3DSGs) offer an incomplete view for embodied AI, often focusing solely on objects or functions, lacking fine-grained interactive part details or comprehensive spatial context.
- Robust open-vocabulary part-level perception in 3D is challenging due to parts being small, ambiguous, viewpoint-dependent, and under-represented in typical foundation model training data.
- Naively using Large Language Models (LLMs) for unified 3DSG generation leads to computational inefficiency, spurious predictions, and hallucinations when reasoning over a large space of objects, parts, and relations.

## Method

- **OP3DSG** employs a ==knowledge-guided entity space control== strategy for 2D detection, using an object-part knowledge base to explicitly prompt ==open-vocabulary detectors== for interactive parts.
- A ==part-aware multi-view 3D fusion== module incrementally builds a global map by associating objects and parts across frames, utilizing geometric, semantic, and color-distribution features for robust tracking.
- ==LLM-based reasoning== refines a ==geometry-initialized prior graph== using multiple specialized LLM agents, each with a ==geometry-anchored verification gate==, to infer spatial, functional relations, and affordances while ensuring physical consistency and scalability.

## Results

- OP3DSG achieved substantial improvements in part-level node recognition, outperforming baselines by **+31.2 points (R@3)** and **+32.1 points (R@5)** for part nodes on the new UniGraph3D benchmark.
- The framework demonstrated significant gains in edge prediction, improving functional relations by **9.2%p (R@3)** and spatial relations by **7.9%p (R@3)** over the strongest baseline, largely due to structural constraints.
- Real-world robotic experiments with a Stretch3 robot showed OP3DSG serving as an effective perception backbone for embodied AI tasks, including language-guided question answering, task planning with part-aware subgoals, and text-conditioned navigation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Guiding open-vocabulary 2D detectors with domain-specific object-part knowledge significantly enhances the accuracy of part-level grounding, which is crucial for detailed scene understanding.
> - Integrating color-distribution features alongside geometric and semantic cues improves the robustness of part association and tracking during multi-view 3D reconstruction, especially for small components.
> - Decomposing complex relational reasoning into specialized, geometry-constrained LLM agents effectively mitigates issues of hallucination and computational overload, leading to more accurate and reliable graph generation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses the domain of 3D Scene Graph (3DSG) generation, a field focused on creating compact, structured semantic representations of 3D environments. 3DSGs connect geometric data with semantic information and relationships, facilitating efficient querying and reasoning, particularly for embodied artificial intelligence (AI) agents in tasks such as navigation and task planning.

Historically, 3DSG research has largely bifurcated into two main categories:
- **Spatial 3DSGs**: These approaches primarily represent objects as nodes and model spatial relationships (e.g., "on," "next to") between them. Early works focused on constructing graphs from point clouds or RGB-D inputs based on geometric consistency and object detection. More recent efforts have extended spatial 3DSG generation to open-vocabulary settings by integrating 2D vision-language foundation models (VLMs) and open-set detectors. However, these methods are predominantly object-centric and often struggle with recognizing small or thin structures, thus overlooking fine-grained, interactive parts critical for detailed interaction.
- **Functional 3DSGs**: This line of research emphasizes modeling functional relationships between objects or their parts, leveraging VLMs and Large Language Models (LLMs) to encode interaction semantics. While useful for understanding object functionality, existing functional 3DSGs often exhibit limited part-level perception and typically do not explicitly encode spatial structure or object-part hierarchies. This can lead to incomplete scene representations for general-purpose reasoning.

A separate but related area is **Part-level Perception**, which aims for fine-grained understanding by recognizing the constituent components of objects. While significant progress has been made in 2D and 3D part segmentation, often supported by large-scale part-centric datasets, integrating robust part grounding into unconstrained 3D reconstruction pipelines and combining it with diverse relational reasoning remains a challenge. Existing foundation models are often trained on object-centric data, leading to difficulties in reliably detecting and localizing small, ambiguous, or viewpoint-dependent parts, even with part-specific prompts.

The current work, OP3DSG, proposes a "Unified 3D Scene Graph" framework designed to bridge the gap between these paradigms. It seeks to integrate the strengths of both spatial and functional 3DSGs by jointly modeling objects, interactive parts, spatial relations, functional relations, and affordances within a single, coherent representation. This unification aims to provide a more comprehensive scene understanding that is essential for complex embodied AI tasks requiring fine-grained interaction and reasoning over both global scene structure and local actionable components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an open-vocabulary, part-aware 3D Scene Graph generation framework that can construct unified graphs for real-world environments. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific limitations of prior work:

1. **Addressing Incomplete Scene Understanding for Embodied Agents**: Current 3DSGs, whether object-centric spatial graphs or function-centric graphs, provide an incomplete view of the world for embodied AI. Object-level graphs can guide an agent to a "microwave" but lack the granularity to support sub-goals like "press start button." Function-centric graphs, while modeling interactions, often miss the broader spatial context, object-part hierarchy, or affordance information necessary for high-level planning. The motivation is to provide a compositional world model that specifies not only what objects are present, but also which parts are actionable and how they enable functions, thus enabling more sophisticated task planning and interaction.

2. **Overcoming Challenges in Part-Level Open-Vocabulary Grounding**: Recognizing and localizing parts in an open-vocabulary setting is inherently more difficult than for objects. Parts are generally smaller, more ambiguous, highly viewpoint-dependent, and under-represented in the training data of many foundation models. This often leads to object-level localizations even when part-centric prompts are used, or low confidence scores for detected parts. A key motivation is to develop robust methods for fine-grained part detection and tracking in 3D, ensuring that interactive components are accurately identified and preserved throughout the 3D reconstruction process.

3. **Managing the Complexity of Unified Graph Generation**: Unifying objects, parts, spatial relations, functional relations, and affordances significantly expands the reasoning space. Naive approaches, such as exhaustive relation prediction using unconstrained LLMs over all candidate pairs, can lead to computational inefficiency, spurious predictions, and missed dependencies. The motivation is to develop a scalable and accurate reasoning mechanism that can infer complex multi-level relationships and affordances while mitigating issues like hallucination and computational overload. This requires leveraging structural and geometric priors to constrain the reasoning process.

4. **Establishing a Standardized Evaluation Benchmark**: The absence of a consolidated benchmark for systematic evaluation of unified 3DSG construction hinders progress. Existing datasets often focus on specific aspects (e.g., spatial, functional) and may contain inconsistencies. A motivation is to create a comprehensive benchmark that supports the evaluation of part-aware perception and multi-level relational reasoning, facilitating fair comparison and future research.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
- Formulate and implement a **Unified 3D Scene Graph** representation that jointly models objects, interactive parts, spatial relations, functional relations, and affordances.
- Develop a framework, **OP3DSG**, that addresses the challenges of open-vocabulary part grounding and scalable, accurate multi-level relational reasoning, primarily by integrating knowledge-guided detection, part-aware 3D fusion, and geometry-anchored LLM-based refinement.
- Introduce **UniGraph3D**, a benchmark designed for systematic evaluation of unified 3DSG generation, covering part-aware perception and multi-level relational reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, OP3DSG, for Open-vocabulary Part-aware 3D Scene Graph generation, is structured into three main components: object-part 2D detection, multi-view 3D fusion, and LLM-based reasoning.

#### 3.1. Object-Part 2D Detection

This stage aims to robustly detect both objects and their interactive parts from an RGB-D input stream.
- **Object/Part Candidates**: To address the difficulty of detecting small or functionally important parts, the framework employs a knowledge-guided control strategy. An open-vocabulary image tagging model provides initial object candidates (`C_o^t`) for an RGB frame. A pre-built object-part knowledge base `K`, curated for common indoor object-part relations, then retrieves plausible interactive part candidates (`K(c)`) for each detected object `c`. This forms the part candidate set (`C_p^t`), and the final prompt list (`C^t`) combines both object and part candidates. This mechanism explicitly guides part-level prompting without restricting open-vocabulary object recognition, ensuring comprehensive perception coverage.
- **Detector**: An open-vocabulary detection model, pre-trained on large-scale part-centric datasets, is used. When prompted with `C^t`, it outputs semantic labels and masks for detected instances. Each mask is then back-projected to 3D to yield per-instance point clouds.
- **Feature Extraction**: For downstream 3D fusion, two types of features are extracted:
 - **Semantic Features (`f_s`)**: Region-level semantic features are extracted from the detector's recognition head, aligned with CLIP text embeddings.
 - **Color-Distribution Features (`f_c`)**: Recognizing that parts often have consistent material properties, color and texture cues are hypothesized to be stable for part association. Classical approaches are used to compute color-distribution features. For each part mask, white balancing reduces illumination bias, and a valid pixel set (`Ω_t,i`) is defined by excluding specular highlights (pixels simultaneously over-exposed and weakly chromatic in HSV color space). The `f_c` feature concatenates three normalized histogram vectors from complementary color spaces: Color Names, Opponent color space, and CIELAB `a*b*` space.

#### 3.2. Multi-view 3D Fusion

This stage incrementally integrates 2D detection results from multiple frames into a global 3D map, handling both objects and parts.
- **Object Association**: For each newly detected object, both geometric similarity (Nearest Neighbor Ratio, NNR) and semantic similarity (cosine similarity of L2-normalized semantic descriptors) are computed against existing objects in the map. An aggregated similarity (`S_o`) combines these two. A greedy online association strategy merges new instances with existing map objects based on the highest similarity above a threshold; otherwise, a new object node is initialized.
- **Part Association**: For newly detected parts, in addition to geometric and semantic similarities, color-distribution similarities (`s_c`) are computed. `s_c` is derived from a weighted sum of chi-square distances between the color histograms (Color Names, Opponent, CIELAB `a*b*`) of the new part and existing parts. An aggregated similarity (`S_p`) combines geometric/semantic and color-distribution similarities. A greedy assignment similar to object association is used.
- **Feature Update**: For associated nodes, semantic features are updated by averaging previous and current representations. Geometric information is updated by taking the union of point clouds, followed by down-sampling. Color-distribution histograms are updated using an exponential moving average (EMA) rule to ensure numerical stability.

#### 3.3. LLM-based Reasoning

This stage generates the final Unified 3DSG by refining a geometry-initialized prior graph using multiple LLM agents.
- **Prior 3D Scene Graph Generation**: To mitigate LLMs' limited spatial perception, a prior graph is constructed without directly feeding raw 3D coordinates. Nodes (objects and parts) are derived from the fused 3D map, with labels determined by voting. 3D center coordinates and bounding box sizes are computed from point clouds. Edges are initially formed between nodes whose 3D centers are within a 1-meter radius. For object-part connections, a semantic proximity matching is applied: an edge is retained if the object label text is contained within the part label text (e.g., "cabinet handle" for "cabinet"), promoting coherent object-part hierarchies.
- **Geometry-anchored LLM-based Reasoning**: Multiple LLM agents operate in parallel to decompose the reasoning space, reduce spurious predictions, and separate short-range structural reasoning from long-range semantic reasoning. Each agent employs a Chain-of-Thought-inspired step-wise prompt. Crucially, before each reasoning task, agents independently refine spatial relations in the prior graph by verifying consistency with node labels, bounding box extents, and 3D center coordinates. This "geometry-anchored verification gate" filters spatially implausible relations, providing a physically consistent context.
 - **Local Relation Reasoning Agent**: Infers functional relations for physically connected object-part pairs (e.g., "knob – pull to open – cabinet"). It conditions on the prior 3DSG and reasons over spatially associated pairs, ensuring scalability.
 - **Remote Relation Reasoning Agent**: Handles functionally related but spatially distant entity pairs (e.g., "TV – turn on/off – remote control"). It selects "remotable" candidates from the prior graph to reduce the LLM's burden, then predicts relation labels among them using pretrained world knowledge.
 - **Affordance Reasoning Agent**: Predicts feasible physical interactions for each node (e.g., "handle – pinch pull"). For part nodes, affordance inference additionally considers the connected object label and geometric attributes for context-aware and physically grounded predictions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of OP3DSG was conducted using a newly introduced benchmark, UniGraph3D, and assessed through quantitative and qualitative analyses, including ablation studies and real-world robot applications.

#### 4.1. UniGraph3D Benchmark

The paper introduces UniGraph3D, a benchmark for evaluating unified 3D scene graph generation. It consolidates and extends existing datasets (FunGraph3D and SceneFun3D) by:
- **Re-examination and Consolidation**: Human annotators re-examined existing annotations to resolve inconsistencies and ambiguities, consolidating labels into canonical forms for fair comparison.
- **Expanded Annotations**: UniGraph3D significantly augments the original datasets with explicit object-part spatial relations and comprehensive affordance annotations (nine categories) for each node, considering geometric characteristics.
As a result, UniGraph3D provides a testbed with 251 object nodes, 414 part nodes, 357 spatial edges, 425 functional edges, and 554 affordances, enabling systematic assessment of part-aware perception and multi-level relational reasoning.

#### 4.2. Metrics

- **Node Evaluation**: A predicted node is considered correct if it has a non-zero 3D Intersection over Union (IoU) with a ground-truth node, and its label ranks within the top-K candidates based on cosine similarity between CLIP embeddings of predicted and ground-truth labels. Node recall (R@K) is reported.
- **Edge Evaluation**: A correct triplet (<node-edge-node>) is counted if all its components are individually retrieved within top-K, and the relation is matched via cosine similarity of Sentence-BERT embeddings of phrase descriptions.
- **Affordance Evaluation**: Recall is computed solely based on Sentence-BERT embedding similarity of affordance phrases, independent of node label. An "associated node" metric also measures recall for node pairs without a relation label.

#### 4.3. Quantitative Results

OP3DSG was compared against representative open-vocabulary 3DSG generation models (ConceptGraph, OpenFunGraph, FunGraph, KeySG), with baselines extended to support unified 3DSGs where necessary and updated with recent LLMs (GPT-5).

- **Node Evaluation (Table 2)**: OP3DSG substantially improves overall node performance, with significant gains in part-level recognition. It surpassed the strongest baseline by +31.2 points (R@3) and +32.1 points (R@5) for part nodes. The authors attribute this to explicit handling of part grounding at perception and fusion levels, unlike baselines where scaling foundation models alone did not resolve the intrinsic difficulty of open-vocabulary part grounding (often leading to object-level localization for part-centric prompts). For affordance predictions, OP3DSG achieved 76.7% (R@3) and 83.2% (R@5), outperforming all baselines. This suggests that accurate part-node recognition and stable object-part attachment improve language-based affordance reasoning.

- **Edge Evaluation (Table 3)**: OP3DSG also demonstrated substantial improvements in edge performance, achieving gains of 9.2%p (R@3) for functional relations and 7.9%p (R@3) for spatial relations over the strongest baseline. These improvements were maintained even when baselines used newer LLMs, indicating that OP3DSG's advantage stems from effective structural constraints. The geometry-initialized prior graph and geometry-anchored, verification-gated multi-agent LLM refinement are credited for restricting reasoning to plausible neighborhoods and enforcing geometric consistency, thereby reducing spurious edges and missed dependencies.

- **Node Localization Sensitivity (Table 4)**: Under stricter overlap constraints (IoU ≥ 0.10 and IoP ≥ 0.25), OP3DSG showed strong overall performance for object nodes. However, it still exhibited limited geometric localization accuracy for part nodes, reflecting the inherent challenge of precisely localizing small components in surface-based fusion methods.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Table 5)

The ablation study confirmed the importance of OP3DSG's key components:
- **Without knowledge-guided control**: A significant drop in part retrieval and subsequent degradation in functional/spatial triplets and affordances was observed, highlighting the propagation of incomplete part grounding to higher-order reasoning.
- **Without color-distribution features**: A modest decrease in part recall occurred, but triplet and affordance performance declined more noticeably, suggesting that even minor part-association instability is amplified at the relational level.
- **Without prior graph**: Replacing the geometry-initialized prior graph with a flat list of node attributes reduced triplet and affordance recall, demonstrating that structured spatial context aids reliable LLM-based inference.
- **Without split LLM agents**: Assigning all reasoning roles to a single LLM led to a sharp decline in relation and affordance performance, attributed to increased hallucinations and context overload.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results (Figure 4)

Qualitative visualizations of the unified 3DSG showed representations of objects, their parts (e.g., oven, knob, handle), and various spatial (e.g., "on," "near") and functional (e.g., "provide power," "pull to open") relations, illustrating the fine-grained structure encoded.

#### 4.6. Real-world Robot Applications (Figure 5)

OP3DSG was demonstrated as a perception backbone for diverse real-world embodied tasks on a Stretch3 robot. The incrementally reconstructed 3DSG supported:
- **Question Answering**: Language-guided reasoning over structured scene entities, including counting, localization, relational inference, and part-level distinctions.
- **Task Planning**: Grounding high-level language instructions into executable, part-aware subgoals ordered by spatial dependencies.
- **Text Query & Navigation**: Language-conditioned spatial retrieval for open-ended user intents, facilitating navigation to target objects or parts based on functional needs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for Unified 3D Scene Graph generation, contributing to the field of 3D scene understanding and embodied AI. The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

1. **Comprehensive Scene Representation**: By introducing the Unified 3DSG, the work moves beyond isolated object-centric or function-centric representations. This integrated graph, jointly modeling objects, interactive parts, spatial relations, functional relations, and affordances, offers a more complete and actionable understanding of real-world environments. This is particularly impactful for embodied agents that require fine-grained reasoning for complex interactions and task execution.

2. **Addressing Part-Level Grounding Challenges**: The proposed OP3DSG framework directly tackles the intrinsic difficulties of open-vocabulary part-level perception. The integration of knowledge-guided entity space control and part-aware multi-view 3D fusion contributes to more robust detection and tracking of small, interaction-relevant components. This improved part grounding is crucial for enabling agents to understand and interact with the detailed mechanics of objects.

3. **Scalable and Constrained Relational Reasoning**: The methodology employs a geometry-initialized prior graph and a geometry-anchored, verification-gated multi-agent LLM reasoning approach. This design addresses the computational complexity and error-proneness of unconstrained LLM inference in large reasoning spaces. By turning LLMs into "constraint-aware refiners" rather than free-form generators, the framework enhances both the precision (reducing spurious relations) and recall (reducing missed dependencies) of relation and affordance prediction, while maintaining scalability.

4. **Standardized Evaluation Benchmark**: The introduction of UniGraph3D provides a consolidated and enriched benchmark. This dataset, with its refined annotations and expanded coverage of object-part structure, spatial and functional relations, and affordances, offers a standardized platform for systematic evaluation. This can accelerate future research by enabling fair comparisons and guiding the development of more effective methods for fine-grained 3D scene understanding.

5. **Direct Applicability in Embodied AI**: The demonstration of OP3DSG as a perception backbone for real-world robotic applications (question answering, task planning, language-conditioned navigation) highlights its practical utility. The ability to ground high-level language instructions into part-aware subgoals and retrieve objects based on functional needs represents a step towards more capable and autonomous embodied systems operating in complex human environments.

In conclusion, this research advances the state of 3D scene understanding by offering a unified, fine-grained, and open-vocabulary representation. The proposed methods for robust part grounding and constrained relational reasoning, along with the new benchmark, are expected to facilitate further progress in developing intelligent agents capable of sophisticated interaction and reasoning in dynamic real-world settings.
