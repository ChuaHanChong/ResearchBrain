# PerchRL: Vision-Based Agile Perching on Inclined Platforms under Rapid and Irregular Motion

> [!summary] Summary
> PerchRL, developed by researchers from SUSTech, HITSZ, and PCL, enables quadrotors to perform vision-based agile perching on rapidly and irregularly moving inclined platforms. The framework achieves zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and maintains high perching accuracy and success rates by robustly handling intermittent visual perception loss.

## Problem

- Existing model-based perching methods struggle with computational overhead and limited replanning frequency under rapid, irregular platform motion, and are susceptible to inaccurate motion prediction.
- Prior RL-based perching policies overfit to fixed or highly structured platform trajectories, leading to poor generalization to diverse, unseen motion patterns.
- Both model-based and RL approaches lack robust mechanisms for handling intermittent visual perception loss, which is common during agile flight due to limited camera Field of View (FOV).

## Method

- A two-stage learning strategy involving state-based pre-training and vision-based fine-tuning using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) in a large-scale parallel simulation environment.
- Highly randomized kinematically constrained B-spline trajectories, a Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN), and an asymmetric actor-critic architecture with privileged future states are used to enhance generalization to diverse platform motions.
- A hybrid learning framework with visibility-aware input augmentation (Extended ==Kalman== Filter and a visual reliability indicator `ρ_t`) and explicit active perception rewards (`r_est`, `r_perc`) to ensure robust perception recovery under intermittent visual loss.

## Results

- PerchRL achieved higher success rates across diverse platform velocities and inclinations in simulation compared to Fast-Perching and InclineLander, demonstrating improved generalization to complex, randomized trajectories.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both visibility-aware input augmentation and active perception rewards are crucial, with their combined use leading to the best success rates and visual perception stability.
- Real-world experiments with a custom quadrotor showed successful vision-based perching on a 70° inclined platform moving at 2.0 m/s, and demonstrated zero-shot generalization to challenging scenarios with periodically varied speeds and distinct motion patterns outside the training distribution, maintaining high perching accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generalization to diverse, irregular platform motion is significantly improved through extensive trajectory randomization and temporal modeling using TCNs and privileged information for the critic.
> - Robustness to intermittent visual loss is achieved by explicitly incorporating a visual reliability indicator into the policy's observations and employing active perception rewards to guide re-establishing stable visual contact.
> - A decoupled two-stage learning strategy and domain randomization facilitate effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and adaptability to various quadrotor platforms and real-world uncertainties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The autonomous perching of quadrotors on moving inclined platforms is a capability relevant to air-ground collaboration applications, such as mobile recharging and emergency response. Vision-based approaches are a common perception modality due to their characteristics of being lightweight, low-cost, and effective for relative pose estimation in outdoor environments where external infrastructure may be unreliable.

Traditional vision-based perching methods typically involve explicit platform motion prediction and trajectory planning through multi-constrained optimization. These methods often exhibit high computational overhead, limited replanning frequencies, and reduced responsiveness. Furthermore, their performance is closely tied to the accuracy of motion prediction, which can become unreliable under complex platform movements, leading to error accumulation and performance degradation. Prior efforts in this area have addressed stationary inclined platforms and later extended to moving platforms by explicitly modeling their dynamics. Strategies have been developed to maintain stable visual visibility, but these still rely on accurate motion prediction and computational resources for visibility analysis.

In response to these limitations, end-to-end reinforcement learning (RL) based approaches have emerged, offering potential for high-frequency control and reduced system coupling. However, existing RL-based methods for aerial perching have primarily focused on mapping instantaneous observations to actions. They frequently rely on fixed or highly structured platform trajectories during both training and evaluation, which can lead to policies that overfit to specific movement patterns and consequently exhibit poor generalization to diverse or unseen platform motions. Another challenge in existing RL approaches is their limited robustness to intermittent visual loss, which is an inherent issue in agile quadrotor flight due to underactuated dynamics, aggressive maneuvers, and limited camera field of view (FOV). While some methods incorporate active-perception rewards to encourage visibility maintenance, they often lack predictive compensation or explicit recovery mechanisms for when visual perception is lost. Some prior work has attempted perception recovery using keypoint-based perception modules and state estimators, but these were typically evaluated under less complex conditions, such as horizontally moving platforms with larger tolerances.

PerchRL addresses these identified gaps by proposing an RL framework specifically designed for vision-based agile perching on inclined platforms subject to rapid and irregular motion. It aims to improve generalization by exposing the policy to highly randomized platform trajectories and incorporating temporal augmentation. Additionally, it targets robustness to intermittent visual loss through a hybrid learning framework that combines implicit state augmentation and explicit active perception rewards, providing predictive compensation and recovery capabilities. This work seeks to advance the state of autonomous aerial perching by enabling more generalized and robust operation in dynamic and visually challenging environments compared to existing model-based and RL-based methodologies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and generalized flight policy, named PerchRL, for vision-based agile perching of quadrotors on inclined platforms that exhibit rapid and irregular motion, with a capability for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

This objective is motivated by several critical challenges inherent in autonomous aerial perching:

1. **Limited Camera Field of View (FOV) and Unstable Visibility:** Agile flight maneuvers often cause the target platform to leave the camera's FOV, leading to intermittent visual loss. This issue is exacerbated by the underactuated dynamics of quadrotors and the need for aggressive maneuvers during perching. Maintaining stable visibility is challenging, and current methods often fail to recover from perception loss, rendering successful perching difficult.

2. **Limitations of Traditional Model-Based Methods:** Existing model-based approaches for perching typically involve explicit motion prediction and multi-constrained trajectory optimization. These methods are computationally intensive, resulting in low replanning frequencies and limited responsiveness. This reliance on accurate motion prediction makes them susceptible to performance degradation when confronted with rapid and unpredictable platform movements, leading to accumulated closed-loop errors.

3. **Lack of Generalization in Existing RL-Based Methods:** While RL-based methods offer potential for high-frequency control, current implementations often train and evaluate policies on fixed or highly structured platform trajectories (e.g., linear or circular paths). This practice can lead to policies that overfit to specific patterns, exhibiting poor generalization capabilities when faced with unseen or diverse platform motions. Such methods tend to be reactive rather than predictive, limiting their utility in dynamic, real-world scenarios.

4. **Insufficient Robustness to Intermittent Visual Loss in RL-Based Methods:** Current RL approaches primarily focus on maintaining visibility, often through simple active-perception rewards. However, they generally lack sophisticated predictive compensation or recovery mechanisms to handle inevitable visual loss during aggressive maneuvers. Once the perception loop is broken, these systems frequently fail to recover, limiting their practical robustness.

To address these motivations, PerchRL aims to:
- **Improve Generalization:** By employing highly randomized platform trajectories during training, including B-spline curves with velocity perturbations, to prevent overfitting and expose the policy to a wide range of motion patterns. This is further enhanced by temporal augmentation methods to extract latent motion patterns from historical observations.
- **Enhance Robustness to Intermittent Visual Loss:** Through a hybrid learning framework. This framework incorporates visibility-aware input augmentation (using an Extended Kalman Filter for state estimation and a perceptual reliability indicator) to maintain continuous state estimates and inform the policy about the reliability of visual perception. It also includes explicit active perception rewards to guide the policy in actively maintaining visibility and recovering from loss.
- **Enable Agile and Efficient Perching:** By decoupling the learning process into state-based pre-training and vision-based fine-tuning, thereby reducing overall training difficulty while still achieving high-frequency, agile control.
- **Demonstrate Adaptability:** By validating the policy's performance across distinct quadrotor platforms in real-world environments, confirming its transferability and practical utility.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PerchRL employs a two-stage learning strategy designed to manage complexity and improve training efficiency.

#### 3.1. Algorithm Overview

The overall framework comprises a training pipeline and a deployment architecture. The policy receives platform observations in different forms across its development stages:
1. **State-based Pre-training:** The policy accesses ground-truth platform states directly from the simulator.
2. **Vision-based Fine-tuning:** The policy receives noisy visual observations from a simulated visibility-gated observation model.
3. **Real-world Deployment:** A plug-and-play visual detection module provides real-time visual observations from onboard camera images.

#### 3.2. State-Based Policy Pre-Training

This initial stage formulates the perching task as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) to learn an optimal policy that maximizes cumulative reward.

- **Platform Motion:** To ensure policy generalization and prevent overfitting, a kinematically constrained randomized trajectory generation method is utilized. This method constructs geometrically diverse closed-loop reference trajectories for the moving platform using periodic uniform cubic B-spline curves. The control points of these B-splines are randomized, and a global curvature checking and optimization procedure is applied to ensure kinematic feasibility by suppressing excessively sharp turns. Additionally, a discretized Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process generates perturbed traversal speeds around a nominal speed, introducing dynamic variability.
- **Observations and Actions:** The policy observation ($s_t$) includes the quadrotor's velocity and vectorized rotation matrix, a history of 30 past platform observations (relative position, normal vector, velocity, and relative timestamp), a visual reliability indicator ($\rho_t$, fixed to 1 in this stage), and the previous action. For agile maneuvers, mass-normalized collective thrust and body rates (CTBR) are used as actions, mapped to actual control commands within predefined thrust and body-rate limits. This action space is intended to improve robustness to sim-to-real discrepancies.
- **Policy Architecture:** A Temporal Convolutional Network (TCN) with three 1D convolutional layers encodes the historical platform observations, capturing latent temporal representations of motion. These temporal features are concatenated with other instantaneous inputs and fed into a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) head to output actions. An asymmetric actor-critic architecture is employed, where the critic's input is augmented with a sequence of 15 future platform states sampled from its reference trajectory. This privileged future information aids in more accurate value estimation, enhancing actor training.
- **Reward Function:** The immediate reward ($r_t$) consists of a dense shaping term ($r_{shaping}$) and a sparse terminal term ($r_{terminal}$).
 - $r_{shaping}$ comprises several components: $r_{guide}$ (encourages approach and reduction of relative pose error), $r_{align}$ (enhances state alignment during terminal phase, matching velocity and suppressing spinning), $r_{act}$ (penalizes excessive or abrupt control actions), $r_{aggr}$ (penalizes aggressive motions exceeding velocity limits), and $r_{time}$ (a constant penalty to encourage efficient task completion). These components use shaping functions to transform raw state errors into bounded and smooth reward signals.
 - $r_{terminal}$ provides an explicit reward for successful perching. Successful perching is defined by satisfying tolerance thresholds for relative tangential distance, relative tangential velocity, and normal angular difference upon contact. An accuracy-dependent bonus is incorporated to encourage more precise terminal state alignment.

#### 3.3. Vision-Based Policy Fine-Tuning

This stage addresses the task as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) where the platform state is only partially observable.

- **Visibility-Gated Observation Model:** Instead of direct raw image input, the policy assumes an upstream visual detector providing intermittent 6-DoF pose estimates. A measurement is triggered based on a predefined detection frequency. Visibility is then evaluated using a simulated downward-facing pinhole camera model. If visible, a visual observation noise model is applied to the ground-truth pose to generate a noisy observation. This noise model incorporates continuous perturbations (anisotropic, geometry-dependent Gaussian noise based on relative distance, optical axis alignment, incidence angle, and relative velocity) and systematic biases (constant position and orientation biases sampled per episode). This approach aims to make the policy robust to real-world perception degradations and uncertainties.
- **Visibility-Aware Policy Learning with Hybrid Guidance:** To achieve robust perception recovery under intermittent visual loss, a hybrid training scheme is proposed.
 - **Visibility-Aware Input Augmentation:** An Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) based on the Constant Turn Rate and Velocity (CTRV) model is used as a state estimation module. This filter performs state prediction at each step, with measurement updates (gated by Mahalanobis distance) applied only when visual observations are available. The policy receives smoothed platform motion features from this EKF. Additionally, an internal indicator $\rho_t$ represents the current visual perception reliability, decreasing exponentially with the duration of visual loss. This indicator informs the policy about the reliability of the estimated state, implicitly encouraging adaptive behavior for visibility maintenance.
 - **Active Perception Rewards:** An explicit auxiliary reward ($r_{active}$) is introduced to provide structured guidance for active perception. It consists of:
 - $r_{est}$: Penalizes estimation error and prolonged perception loss, with a weight that increases with visual loss duration.
 - $r_{perc}$: Promotes perception awareness by encouraging alignment between the camera's optical axis and the target direction.
 The final immediate reward for fine-tuning is $r_t = r_{shaping} + r_{terminal} + r_{active}$.

#### 3.4. Simulation and Training

The training uses Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) implemented with Omnidrones, a parallel simulation platform. Training is conducted on 8192 environments simultaneously. Initial states of the quadrotor and platform are randomized. Domain randomization is applied to parameters like mass, inertia, thrust-to-weight ratio (±30%, ±10%, ±15% respectively), camera tilt angle (±5°), and detector update frequency ( Hz) to reduce the sim-to-real gap.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive simulation and real-world experiments to validate PerchRL's performance.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

**State-based Perching Evaluation:**
- PerchRL was compared against two baselines: Fast-Perching (model-based) and InclineLander (RL-based), as well as an ablated variant of PerchRL without temporal augmentation (PerchRL w/o TA).
- PerchRL and its variant consistently outperformed the baselines across fixed (reciprocating linear, racetrack, figure-8) and randomized B-spline trajectories, particularly under challenging conditions (higher velocities, inclinations).
- Fast-Perching's performance degraded under complex motion due to its constant-velocity assumption. InclineLander showed limitations under challenging conditions due to reliance on sparse terminal rewards.
- PerchRL w/o TA performed comparably on structured trajectories but struggled with the randomized B-spline trajectories, indicating limited adaptability and generalization to rapid and irregular motion without temporal augmentation. PerchRL, with its temporal augmentation, demonstrated strong generalization and the highest success rate across diverse scenarios.

**Vision-based Perching Ablation Studies:**
- Ablation studies investigated the contributions of the input augmentation and active perception rewards.
- A variant without any auxiliary mechanisms showed the poorest performance, failing to explore effectively.
- Removing the input augmentation significantly degraded performance, highlighting its substantial contribution to vision-based training robustness. Independently, the input augmentation strategy was sufficient for competitive performance.
- Active perception rewards improved training efficiency and enhanced final performance, but were not sufficient on their own for robust perching under intermittent visual loss.
- Removing the $\rho_t$ indicator, which provides explicit awareness of visual loss, led to degraded performance as the policy over-relied on potentially outdated state estimates.
- PerchRL, with the combined input augmentation and active perception rewards, achieved the best performance, faster convergence, and superior visual perception stability.

**Overall Vision-based Performance Evaluation:**
- Systematic evaluation across the speed-inclination space showed that the vision-based policy achieved lower overall success rates compared to the state-based policy (ideal observations), particularly under high-speed and large-inclination conditions where maintaining reliable perception is challenging.
- Under moderate conditions (platform speed below 2.5 m/s and inclination angle below 60°), the vision-based policy showed only a small performance degradation, approaching the state-based policy's upper bound.
- Detailed metrics (task duration, position error, normal angle error, tangential velocity) confirmed that while the vision-based policy had an average success-rate degradation of 8.29% compared to the state-based policy, most perching accuracy metrics remained unaffected, except for relative tangential velocity, which increased. This indicates accurate pose alignment but increased difficulty in terminal velocity matching under visual constraints.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

**State-Based Perching on Crazyflie:**
- Preliminary experiments on a lightweight Bitcraze Crazyflie 2.1 demonstrated the feasibility of the proposed state-based framework under aggressive flight conditions, including perching on a 90° inclined static surface. This validation was performed using motion capture data and an offboard controller.

**Vision-Based Perching on Custom Quadrotor:**
- Experiments with a custom-built quadrotor equipped with an NVIDIA Orin NX processor and a monocular global-shutter camera demonstrated real-time, closed-loop vision-based control.
- Six scenarios across three difficulty levels (simple, normal, hard) were designed, involving stationary, reciprocating linear, and various randomized spline trajectories, some with periodically varying speeds or changing motion patterns.
- **Normal-I Experiment:** Demonstrated successful perching on a 70° inclined platform following a spline trajectory at approximately 2.0 m/s. The drone successfully navigated repeated visual perception loss and recovery cycles, eventually perching with precise state alignment.
- **Hard-I Experiment:** Showcased strong zero-shot generalization to a challenging scenario where the platform's desired velocity periodically varied (1.0-2.5 m/s), a motion pattern outside the specific training distribution. The policy achieved successful perching despite additional velocity fluctuations from the ground vehicle controller.
- **General Performance:** PerchRL consistently enabled vision-based quadrotor perching under distinct platform motion patterns, with perching accuracy metrics (terminal position error, normal alignment error) generally falling within training tolerance thresholds. The method maintained strong generalization in challenging, unseen scenarios like Hard-I and Hard-II.

Overall, the experiments collectively demonstrate the feasibility, stability, real-time performance, and adaptability of PerchRL across distinct quadrotor platforms and diverse, irregular platform motions, even under intermittent visual loss.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PerchRL framework contributes to the field of autonomous aerial robotics by addressing significant challenges in vision-based perching on moving inclined platforms. Its primary significance lies in enhancing the robustness and generalization capabilities of quadrotor perching systems, particularly under dynamic and visually constrained conditions.

The methodological contributions, including the two-stage learning strategy, randomized platform motion generation, temporal augmentation, and the hybrid learning framework for visibility-aware perception, provide a comprehensive approach to an complex problem. The use of randomized B-spline trajectories with velocity perturbations during training helps in developing policies that are less prone to overfitting and can generalize to a wider array of unseen platform movements, a common limitation in prior RL-based methods. The temporal augmentation (TCN and asymmetric actor-critic with privileged information) further refines the policy's ability to interpret historical motion patterns, moving beyond purely reactive behaviors.

A key aspect of PerchRL's significance is its approach to managing intermittent visual loss. By integrating an EKF-based state estimator with a perceptual reliability indicator ($\rho_t$) and active perception rewards, the system can maintain a continuous understanding of the platform's state even when direct visual input is unavailable. This mechanism allows the quadrotor to adaptively balance aggressive perching maneuvers with the need to re-establish visual perception, thereby improving robustness in real-world scenarios where visual occlusion is common.

The empirical validation, both in extensive simulations and real-world experiments on distinct quadrotor platforms (Crazyflie and a custom-built quadrotor), demonstrates the practical viability and adaptability of PerchRL. The achievement of zero-shot sim-to-real transfer and successful perching under rapid, irregular, and even un-trained platform motion patterns highlights the policy's effectiveness and potential for deployment in various applications.

The potential impact of this work extends to enhancing air-ground collaboration in domains such as mobile recharging for extended drone operations, emergency response where dynamic platforms might be involved, and various logistical or inspection tasks requiring precise aerial docking. By enabling reliable and agile perching on non-cooperative moving inclined platforms, PerchRL moves autonomous systems closer to operating effectively in unstructured and dynamic environments. The reported real-time performance and generalization capabilities suggest that this technology could be integrated into future autonomous systems to improve their operational envelope and reliability.

The identified limitation regarding perception-control coupling due to rigid camera mounting and the proposed future work on incorporating an active gimbal indicate a recognition of ongoing challenges and pathways for further enhancement, suggesting a foundational contribution upon which more advanced perching systems can be built.
