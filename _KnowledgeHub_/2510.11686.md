# Representation-Based Exploration for Language Models: From Test-Time to Post-Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers introduce a representation-based exploration strategy that leverages pre-trained language model hidden states to identify and incentivize novel behaviors, improving the efficiency of finding correct solutions during inference and preventing diversity collapse during reinforcement learning post-training. This approach, evaluated across various models and reasoning tasks, notably reduced the samples needed to find correct answers and preserved diverse solution paths.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) techniques for language models (LLMs) primarily 'sharpen' existing behaviors rather than enabling the discovery of truly novel capabilities, leading to 'diversity collapse'.
- Scalably quantifying novelty and diversity in the combinatorially vast space of language model outputs is a significant challenge.
- Realizing the full potential of RL for LLMs requires moving beyond mere refinement to robustly discover new reasoning strategies and solution paths.

## Method

- The paper introduces '**RepExp**,' a representation-based exploration strategy that uses elliptical bonuses derived from an LLM's averaged last-layer hidden states to quantify and incentivize novelty.
- A two-pronged evaluation approach is employed: first, at inference-time (Test-Time Exploration) to select a diverse set of responses, and then, by integrating the diversity bonus into a standard RL post-training pipeline (GRPO).
- For inference, RepExp iteratively selects responses that maximize the ==elliptical bonus== relative to previously chosen ones, promoting diversity; for RL, the bonus is added to the extrinsic verifiable reward.

## Results

- RepExp consistently improved verifier efficiency across models and tasks, reducing the average samples needed to find a correct answer by over **50%** on Qwen-2.5-14b-Instruct across four reasoning tasks.
- The benefits of RepExp were more pronounced for stronger base models and for harder questions within datasets, showing up to a **3x** improvement for the hardest Game-of-24 questions.
- When integrated into RL post-training, RepExp completely eliminated 'diversity collapse', maintaining or improving pass@k rates for large k, and matched GRPO's pass@256 rate with only pass@80 on AIME 2024, indicating a **3x** improvement in test-time sample efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained LLM representations, specifically averaged last-layer hidden states, provide a robust and scalable basis for quantifying novelty and guiding exploration.
> - Deliberate, representation-based exploration can effectively move LLMs beyond merely sharpening existing low-probability behaviors to genuinely discovering novel and diverse solutions.
> - A staged evaluation, starting with controlled inference-time testing before full RL integration, offers a principled methodology for developing and validating LLM exploration techniques.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the burgeoning field of applying Reinforcement Learning to enhance Language Models, particularly for reasoning tasks like mathematical problem-solving and code generation. While RL has enabled significant breakthroughs in these areas, the authors highlight a critical limitation: **"sharpening versus discovery."**

Existing RL techniques for LLMs, such as PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization) and GRPO (Gradient Regularized Policy Optimization), are increasingly observed to primarily "amplify or sharpen" behaviors that the base pre-trained model can already execute with low probability. This means they improve the likelihood of already known correct answers rather than enabling the discovery of entirely new solution paths or reasoning strategies. This phenomenon, often referred to as "diversity collapse" or "reward hacking," is a significant bottleneck, especially as data curation becomes increasingly challenging and expensive in complex, open-ended domains. The paper explicitly cites prior work (Yue et al., Gandhi et al., Huang et al., Dang et al., Wu et al.) that identifies and discusses this "sharpening" limitation.

The authors argue that **deliberate exploration**—explicitly incentivizing the model to discover novel and diverse behaviors—is essential to overcome this limitation and realize the full potential of RL for LLMs. They draw parallels to exploration techniques in deep RL for embodied decision-making (e.g., game playing, robotics), where algorithms learn complex behaviors from scratch without pre-training. However, a major challenge for LLMs is the scalable quantification of novelty and diversity in the combinatorially large space of language. Moreover, LLMs possess "tremendous prior knowledge" through pre-training, which could potentially guide exploration more efficiently than traditional "from scratch" RL.

This work contributes to the landscape by:
- **Proposing a scalable and principled exploration technique** tailored for LLMs that leverages their pre-trained representations.
- **Introducing a novel two-pronged evaluation methodology** that first tests exploration in a simplified, controlled inference-time setting to isolate its effects from other complex RL mechanisms, before integrating it into full RL post-training. This methodological contribution provides a clearer path for understanding and validating exploration strategies.
- **Addressing the "diversity collapse" problem** directly in RL post-training for LLMs, demonstrating that their method can preserve or improve diversity (pass@k for large k) where standard RL often degrades it.

The paper differentiates its approach from other concurrent efforts in LLM exploration, which include count-based exploration, random network distillation, posterior sampling, entropy bonuses, and methods that learn diversity metrics. Their representation-based objective and inference-time validation distinguish their work in this rapidly evolving field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this research is to advance the capabilities of Language Models beyond merely refining existing knowledge, towards autonomously discovering genuinely novel and valuable behaviors. The authors articulate two key research questions that guide their investigation:

1. **Can the knowledge in pre-trained representations guide the search for novel behaviors?** This question probes the effectiveness of leveraging the inherent understanding embedded within an LLM's hidden states to inform and direct its exploratory actions. It seeks to ascertain if the rich features learned during pre-training can serve as a robust and scalable basis for quantifying novelty and diversity in textual outputs.
2. **Does deliberate exploration have the potential to move beyond sharpening the base model?** This is the core challenge addressed by the paper. It aims to determine if explicit mechanisms for incentivizing novelty can enable LLMs to learn capabilities or generate solutions that were not merely low-probability occurrences in the base model, but truly new and emergent. The motivation here is to unlock the "full promise of reinforcement learning" for LLMs, allowing them to solve complex problems more robustly and creatively, without the prohibitive costs of extensive human data curation for every new capability.

In essence, the authors are motivated by the desire to empower LLMs with a more "discovery-oriented" form of learning, moving away from a "refinement-only" paradigm. They believe that if LLMs can be equipped with effective exploration strategies, they can significantly advance reasoning capabilities and reduce dependency on costly, manual data interventions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopt a novel and principled two-pronged evaluation approach to investigate deliberate exploration:

**a) Inference-Time Selection (Test-Time Exploration):**
This initial setting aims to isolate the effectiveness of exploration by disentangling it from the complexities of optimization and generalization inherent in full RL training.
- **Problem:** Given a prompt `x` and a fixed pre-trained LLM `π`, the goal is to select a small, diverse set of `k` responses from a larger pool of `N` candidates (`y_1..., y_N`) generated by `π`. This selected set should maximize the probability of containing a correct response (as judged by a verifier), effectively improving "verifier efficiency."
- **Diversity Bonus:** The core of their method is a "representation-based bonus" derived from the LLM's hidden states. For a given prompt `x` and response `y_i`, a feature vector `h_θ(x, y_i)` is formed by averaging the last-layer hidden states of the model for each token in the response. This `d`-dimensional vector is then reduced to 512 dimensions using a sparse random projection.
- **Elliptical Bonuses (RepExp Algorithm 1):** The method employs an adaptation of elliptical bonuses, a standard technique in linear bandits and active learning. The bonus `div(h | h_1:i-1)` for a new feature vector `h` is defined using an inverse covariance matrix `Σ_i` that accumulates information from previously selected features. This bonus increases for features that are poorly represented by prior selections, thereby promoting novelty.
- **Selection Process (RepExp - Figure 3):** The `RepExp` algorithm iteratively selects responses. It starts with a randomly chosen response, then in each subsequent step, it selects the response from the candidate pool that maximizes the elliptical bonus with respect to the already selected responses. The inverse covariance matrix is efficiently updated using the Woodbury identity. This "history-aware" selection penalizes redundancy in the representation space.

**b) Reinforcement Learning Post-Training Integration:**
After validating the diversity bonus in the inference-time setting, the authors integrate it into a standard RL post-training pipeline (specifically, GRPO).
- **Reward Augmentation:** Instead of coreset selection, the diversity bonus `b(x, y_i)` is directly added to the extrinsic verifiable reward `r*(x, y_i)`. The augmented reward becomes `r_i = r*(x, y_i) + β * b(x, y_i)`, where `β` is a bonus parameter.
- **Bonus Calculation:** The bonus `b(x, y_i)` for a response `y_i` in a batch of `k` rollouts for prompt `x` is calculated as a leverage-score-like elliptical bonus, `h_θ(x, y_i)^T Σ^(-1) h_θ(x, y_i)`, where `Σ` is the covariance matrix computed from all `k` rollouts in the current batch. This ensures the bonus measures novelty *within* the current batch of samples. The covariance matrix is re-initialized for each RL training batch, and a new random projection of `h_θ` is drawn at each optimization step for better exploration.
- **Representations:** The same representation extraction (`¯h_θ(x, y_i)`) as in the inference-time setting is used.

**Why Representation-Based Elliptical Bonuses?** The authors emphasize several advantages:
- **Leverages LLM Prior Knowledge:** Uses hidden states to capture rich information and the model's understanding.
- **History-Aware:** Covariance matrix summarizes past selections, penalizing redundancy.
- **Simple and Scalable:** No additional learning machinery, efficient rank-one updates for inverse covariance.

The experimental setup involves a variety of models (Phi, Llama, Qwen, Mistral) and reasoning tasks (MATH, GSM8K, MBPP+, Game-of-24, AIME). Baselines include random sampling for inference-time and GRPO/Unlikeliness for post-training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several significant findings, demonstrating the efficacy of representation-based exploration across different settings:

**Inference-Time Exploration (Section 4):**

- **RF1: RepExp improves verifier efficiency across models and tasks.** Figure 1 illustrates that RepExp consistently reduces the "samples-to-correct" (average number of samples needed to find a correct answer) compared to random sampling. For example, on Qwen-2.5-14b-Instruct, it achieved over 50% improvement in verifier efficiency on GSM8K, MATH, MBPP+, and Game-of-24.
- **RF2: The benefits of RepExp grow with model strength.** Figure 5 (Left) shows a strong correlation between the base model's strength (measured by pass@1 rate) and the performance gain from RepExp. Stronger models (e.g., Qwen-2.5-32B) almost uniformly benefited, while weaker models (e.g., Qwen-2.5-0.5B) saw no benefit or even degradation. This suggests that the quality of internal representations is crucial for effective exploration.
- **RF3: RepExp provides more improvement for harder questions.** Figure 5 (Right) reveals that RepExp yields the largest improvements in verifier efficiency for the hardest questions within a dataset (e.g., the hardest 20% of MATH questions), where the base model struggles the most. For instance, a 3x improvement was observed for the hardest Game-of-24 questions with Phi-4.
- **RF4: RepExp improves verifier efficiency over standard generation modifications.** When compared to other sampling strategies (low temperature, min-p, nucleus sampling), RepExp consistently improved verifier efficiency. The only exception was high-temperature sampling, possibly because it generates incoherent responses that appear novel in representation space but lack correctness (Figure 6).
- **RF5: RepExp for autoregressive generation improves solve rate (Extension).** A preliminary investigation into token-level representation-based exploration (modifying logits during autoregressive generation) showed that for Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct on the hardest MATH questions, this approach improved pass@k rates for large `k` (exceeding 512-640 samples) compared to naive sampling (Figure 7). This suggests a promising direction for reducing the computational burden of generating large candidate pools.

**RL Post-Training (Section 5):**

- **RF6: RepExp improves pass@k and mitigates "diversity collapse."** Figure 2 (and Figure 9 in the Appendix) demonstrates that integrating RepExp into GRPO yields competitive pass@k performance across all values of `k`. Crucially, RepExp "completely eliminates the ‘diversity collapse’ phenomenon" where standard GRPO and even Unlikeliness (a related exploration method) degrade pass@k for larger values of `k` relative to the base model. For example, on AIME 2024, RepExp matched GRPO's pass@256 rate with only pass@80, indicating a 3x improvement in test-time sample efficiency. This is a strong indicator that RepExp enables the discovery of diverse, correct behaviors beyond mere sharpening.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes a significant contribution to the field of large language model development and reinforcement learning by demonstrating that **deliberate, representation-based exploration is a practical and effective strategy for expanding the reasoning capabilities of LLMs.**

**Key Significance Points:**

- **Moving Beyond Sharpening:** The most impactful finding is that RepExp helps LLMs discover genuinely novel and diverse behaviors, effectively moving beyond the "sharpening" limitation observed in many contemporary RL approaches. This is crucial for unlocking advanced reasoning in complex, open-ended domains where simply amplifying existing probabilities is insufficient.
- **Enhanced Verifier Efficiency:** For inference-time applications, RepExp significantly improves the efficiency of finding correct answers, especially for stronger models and harder questions. This has direct practical implications for scenarios where querying a verifier (e.g., human expert, costly API) is expensive, enabling more cost-effective data curation and selection of high-quality, diverse responses.
- **Mitigation of Diversity Collapse in RL:** In RL post-training, RepExp successfully counters the "diversity collapse" problem, where standard RL methods often lead to a degradation of pass@k at higher k-values. By preserving and even enhancing pass@k for larger k, RepExp allows the model to maintain a broader range of correct solution strategies, leading to more robust and reliable performance.
- **Principled and Scalable Approach:** The use of elliptical bonuses applied to internal LLM representations provides a theoretically grounded, yet simple and scalable method for quantifying and incentivizing novelty. It avoids the need for additional auxiliary networks, reducing computational and implementation complexity often associated with other exploration techniques.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Improved LLM Capabilities:** By fostering the discovery of novel reasoning paths, this work can lead to LLMs that are more capable of tackling truly challenging problems in mathematics, science, and engineering, where creative problem-solving is required.
- **Reduced Data Curation Costs:** The ability to discover behaviors autonomously can reduce the reliance on vast, meticulously curated datasets for complex tasks, alleviating a growing bottleneck in LLM development.
- **Foundation for Future Exploration Research:** The two-pronged methodology (inference-time validation before post-training integration) offers a robust framework for evaluating and comparing future exploration techniques, enabling a deeper understanding of their benefits and tradeoffs.
- **Practical Applications in Best-of-N Sampling:** For applications relying on best-of-N sampling, where multiple generations are evaluated to find the best one, RepExp can dramatically improve the success rate and efficiency, leading to higher-quality outputs with fewer samples.

The authors acknowledge that the algorithm design space for exploration is vast and suggest future work in scaling up RL compute, combining exploration with other performance-enhancing techniques, optimizing token-level exploration, and extending exploration to domains without verifiable rewards. This paper lays a strong foundation for these future directions, highlighting deliberate exploration as a critical ingredient for the next generation of intelligent language models.
