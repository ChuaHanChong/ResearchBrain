# $AutoDrivext{-}P^3$: Unified Chain of Perception-Prediction-Planning Thought via Reinforcement Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers developed AutoDrive-P3^33, a Vision-Language Model-based framework that unifies perception, prediction, and planning through structured Chain-of-Thought reasoning for autonomous driving. This system employs a novel P3^33-GRPO reinforcement fine-tuning algorithm for hierarchical supervision and achieves state-of-the-art performance on NAVSIM benchmarks, alongside a reduced collision rate of 0.06% on nuScenes.

## Problem

- Current VLM-based autonomous driving systems often lack explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, leading to direct planning output without foundational perception and prediction steps.
- Existing VLM-AD approaches treat perception, prediction, and planning as fragmented tasks, hindering synergistic interaction and overall planning performance.
- Reinforcement learning applications in VLM-AD primarily optimize only for final planning metrics, neglecting direct reward supervision for perception and prediction modules.

## Method

- **AutoDrive-P3**, a VLM-based framework, unifies perception, prediction, and planning (P3) through a structured ==P3-CoT reasoning== process.
- A specialized ==P3-CoT dataset== was constructed with comprehensive, integrated ==Chain-of-Thought annotations== for all three stages to guide VLM training.
- The ==P3-GRPO algorithm==, a novel ==hierarchical and progressive reinforcement fine-tuning== method, provides explicit, multi-component ==reward supervision== across all P3 modules for synergistic optimization.

## Results

- **AutoDrive-P3** achieved state-of-the-art performance on the closed-loop NAVSIMv1 (**90.6** PDMS) and NAVSIMv2 (**89.9** EPDMS) benchmarks.
- On the nuScenes benchmark, the framework attained an average L2 displacement error of **0.33m** and a significantly reduced collision rate of **0.06%**, outperforming other VLM-based methods in safety metrics.
- Ablation studies confirmed that P3-GRPO's joint reinforcement of perception, prediction, and planning capabilities leads to substantial improvements, and dual thinking modes enabled a balance between inference efficiency (**1.0 Hz** fast, **0.5 Hz** detailed) and performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating perception and prediction as intermediate Chain-of-Thought reasoning steps within a VLM significantly enhances the coherence and interpretability of autonomous driving decisions.
> - Hierarchical reinforcement learning that provides direct, progressive supervision to perception, prediction, and planning modules concurrently leads to superior overall planning performance compared to optimizing planning alone.
> - A carefully constructed multimodal dataset with unified Chain-of-Thought annotations is essential for effectively training VLMs to perform complex, multi-stage autonomous driving tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous driving research has historically evolved from modular pipelines to end-to-end learning systems. Traditional approaches decouple the driving process into distinct perception, prediction, and planning stages. While this modularity offers interpretability, it often leads to error accumulation across stages, degrading overall trajectory quality. In response, end-to-end models have emerged, integrating these stages into a single learning framework to mitigate error propagation and improve planning performance. However, these smaller-scale end-to-end models are typically limited by their dataset size and model capacity, which can restrict their world knowledge and performance in diverse or "long-tail" scenarios.

The recent integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into autonomous driving systems represents a significant shift. VLMs, leveraging large-scale pre-training, offer enhanced world knowledge and reasoning capabilities, making them adaptable to a wider range of driving situations, particularly long-tail events. Despite these advancements, current VLM-based end-to-end systems exhibit several limitations that this work addresses. Some VLM-based approaches directly output planning decisions without engaging in explicit chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning through perception and prediction stages. This direct output bypasses critical intermediate cognitive steps, creating a domain gap between VLM capabilities and the sequential reasoning required for robust autonomous driving, thereby potentially compromising decision-making. Other VLM-based systems can generate outputs for perception, prediction, and planning tasks but treat these modules in a fragmented manner. This separate operation inhibits synergistic interactions between the stages, thereby undermining the full potential of planning performance. Furthermore, existing applications of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for VLM-based autonomous driving primarily focus on optimizing final planning metrics, such as L2 distance or closed-loop performance, without providing direct reward supervision to the perception and prediction modules. This narrow optimization focus can lead to superficial performance gains, limit interpretability, and result in unreliable overall planning.

The AutoDrive-P$^3$ framework positions itself within this landscape by proposing a unified and synergistically supervised VLM-based system. It aims to bridge the gaps identified in current VLM-AD systems by explicitly integrating perception, prediction, and planning through structured CoT reasoning and by extending reinforcement learning (GRPO) to provide hierarchical and progressive supervision across all three modules. This approach seeks to combine the benefits of end-to-end learning (reduced error accumulation) with the interpretability and robustness of modular, human-like reasoning, while leveraging the world knowledge of VLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel VLM-based framework, AutoDrive-P$^3$, that integrates Perception, Prediction, and Planning (P$^3$) into a cohesive architecture, facilitating structured reasoning and synergistic operation for autonomous driving.

This overarching goal is motivated by three identified limitations in current VLM-based autonomous driving systems:

1. **Lack of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning in Planning:** Many existing VLM-based systems directly generate planning results without explicitly performing perception and prediction as intermediate reasoning steps. This bypasses crucial cognitive stages that human drivers naturally execute. The motivation is that this omission creates a substantial "domain gap" between the VLM's capabilities and the complex, multi-stage nature of autonomous driving decision-making. Consequently, decisions may lack the necessary contextual grounding, potentially leading to compromised safety and interpretability. The objective is to introduce a structured P$^3$-CoT reasoning process within the VLM to ensure that planning is informed by explicit perception and prediction outcomes.

2. **Fragmented Multi-Task Synergy:** While some VLMs can generate outputs for perception, prediction, and planning tasks, they often treat these tasks as isolated components. This fragmented approach neglects the inherent interdependencies among these stages; accurate perception is a prerequisite for reliable prediction, and both are fundamental for robust planning. The motivation is that this lack of synergy prevents the full leverage of information flow between modules, ultimately limiting the model's ability to achieve optimal planning performance. The objective is to design a framework that ensures these modules operate collaboratively, where perception provides essential information for subsequent prediction and planning, and both perception and prediction collectively contribute to the final planning decisions.

3. **Limited Reinforcement Learning Supervision (Planning-Only GRPO):** Current applications of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) in VLM-based autonomous driving primarily optimize solely for planning metrics (e.g., L2 distance, closed-loop performance). This narrow focus means that the perception and prediction modules do not receive direct, explicit reinforcement supervision through the reward mechanism. The motivation is that this leads to superficial improvements in planning without addressing potential weaknesses in the foundational perception and prediction capabilities. This can result in limited interpretability and unreliable planning outcomes, as the model may arrive at correct plans for the wrong reasons or fail in scenarios where intermediate reasoning is critical. The objective is to introduce P$^3$-GRPO, a hierarchical and progressive reinforcement learning algorithm that provides explicit supervision and fosters coordinated optimization across all three P$^3$ modules, thereby enhancing accuracy in perception and prediction, and consequently improving planning capabilities.

Additionally, a practical objective is to balance inference efficiency with performance by introducing dual thinking modes: "detailed thinking" (with full CoT reasoning) and "fast thinking" (with only answers) to cater to different operational requirements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AutoDrive-P$^3$ framework is designed to integrate Perception, Prediction, and Planning into a cohesive architecture. The methodology involves three main stages: dataset creation, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) for cold-start initialization, and reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) using a novel P$^3$-GRPO algorithm.

#### 3.1. P$^3$-CoT Dataset Construction

To facilitate coherent P$^3$ reasoning within VLMs, a specialized dataset, P$^3$-CoT, was developed. This dataset addresses limitations of existing datasets, which often lack comprehensive key object annotations, unified CoT sequences across tasks, or suitable CoT formats for VLM training.

The construction process for the P$^3$-CoT dataset is as follows:
1. **Data Sampling:** Key frames are sampled from nuScenes (at 2Hz) and NAVSIM datasets. Each sample includes a 3-second historical video as input and a 3-second future trajectory as ground truth for planning. All object bounding boxes are projected into the image view.
2. **Key Object Identification and Annotation:** Critical objects, defined as those significantly impacting driving decisions, are identified. Objects deemed too distant are filtered out. Bounding boxes for these critical objects are manually checked and annotated as perception labels.
3. **Prediction Label Generation:** Future trajectories of these critical objects are used to derive their predicted behaviors (e.g., "go straight," "turn left"). Manual additions are made based on video context if trajectory data is insufficient.
4. **Planning Label Generation:** The ego vehicle's planned trajectory (waypoints) is extracted and converted to ego vehicle coordinates, forming the planning labels.
5. **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Synthesis:** Utilizing the Qwen2.5-VL-72B model, coherent CoT data is generated. A crucial aspect is ensuring that the CoT *seamlessly connects all three stages*: perception reasoning informs prediction reasoning, which in turn informs planning reasoning. Manual verification is performed to ensure the correctness and logical integrity of these synthesized reasoning chains.
The P$^3$-CoT dataset includes 25,303 frames from 850 nuScenes and 115,434 frames from 1,382 NAVSIM scenes. The dataset's structure emphasizes a sequential reasoning flow, where information from earlier stages is explicitly used in later stages, and it provides a CoT format rather than fragmented question-answer pairs.

#### 3.2. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for Cold-Start

The AutoDrive-P$^3$ base model is initialized through SFT using the P$^3$-CoT dataset. This stage aims to imbue the VLM with fundamental autonomous driving knowledge and the ability to produce structured P$^3$-CoT outputs.
- **Multimodal Input:** The model processes combined inputs including ego state, video frames, driving commands, and a prompt.
- **Structured Output:** The model is trained to generate outputs structured into perception, prediction, and planning modules. Each module’s output includes both a `<thinking>` tag for the CoT reasoning process and an `<answer>` tag for the final structured output (e.g., object bounding boxes, predicted actions, planned trajectory points).
- **Training Objective:** The training minimizes the negative log-likelihood of the target sequence, ensuring the model learns to produce coherent reasoning traces followed by concrete answers across all three stages.

#### 3.3. P$^3$-GRPO Algorithm (Reinforcement Fine-Tuning)

Following SFT, the P$^3$-GRPO algorithm is applied to further enhance the VLM's reasoning across all P$^3$ stages, treating them as a unified chain. This is a hierarchical and progressive optimization method based on the GRPO algorithm.
- **Multi-Component Reward Function:** A weighted sum of individual reward components guides the policy model. The total reward `R(q, a)` is composed of:
 - `R_format`: Rewards for adherence to the specified output format.
 - `R_perc` (Perception Reward): Measures object detection quality using average Intersection over Union (IoU), precision (P), and recall (R). It rewards comprehensive scene understanding, spatial accuracy, and provides interpretable feedback on visual understanding.
 - `R_pred` (Prediction Reward): Evaluates forecasting accuracy by combining behavior label correctness (indicator function) weighted by IoU, and overall detection quality. This component links perceptual accuracy to semantic correctness, fostering robust prediction of object behaviors.
 - `R_plan` (Planning Reward): Quantifies trajectory quality via an exponential transformation of the L2 distance between the predicted and ground-truth trajectories. For NAVSIM, the Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) is also incorporated. This reward serves as the ultimate performance metric, ensuring that improvements in earlier stages translate into better planning.
- **Coordinated Learning:** The reward structure explicitly encodes the causal relationships between P$^3$ modules, ensuring that improvements in planning are grounded in enhanced perceptual understanding and predictive capabilities. Reward weights `(λ_format, λ_perc, λ_pred, λ_plan)` are set to a ratio of 1:2:2:5, emphasizing perception, prediction, and planning.
- **GRPO Optimization:** During RFT, for each query, the model generates a group of responses. The normalized advantage for each response is computed based on its reward relative to the group's mean and standard deviation. The policy is updated using a clipped surrogate loss combined with a KL penalty term, which helps prevent the policy from diverging too much from the SFT-initialized base model, ensuring stable learning.
- **Dual Thinking Modes:** To manage inference efficiency, the framework implements:
 - **Detailed Thinking:** Generates the complete CoT reasoning and answers for all P$^3$ modules.
 - **Fast Thinking:** Generates only the final answers from each module, bypassing the explicit reasoning steps for quicker inference.

**Experimental Setup:** Experiments are conducted on nuScenes (open-loop) and NAVSIMv1/v2 (closed-loop). Qwen2.5-VL-3B is used as the base model, trained with AdamW optimizer on 8 A100 GPUs. Group size for GRPO is 8, and KL weight is 0.01.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive experiments on the open-loop nuScenes dataset and the closed-loop NAVSIMv1/v2 benchmarks to evaluate the performance of AutoDrive-P$^3$.

#### 4.1. Performance Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods

- **nuScenes Benchmark (Open-Loop Planning):**
 - AutoDrive-P$^3$ (Detailed Thinking) achieved an average L2 displacement error of 0.33m and an average collision rate of 0.06%. This L2 performance is comparable to or slightly better than state-of-the-art VLM-based methods like OmniDrive (0.33m) and OpenDriveVLA (0.33m).
 - Crucially, AutoDrive-P$^3$ demonstrated a significantly lower collision rate (0.06%) compared to OmniDrive (0.11%) and OpenDriveVLA (0.10%).
 - The "Fast Thinking" mode showed slightly degraded performance but remained competitive (L2: 0.34m, Collision: 0.08%).
 - The model achieved these results using a smaller base VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-3B) and less training data (20k samples) compared to some baselines (e.g., OmniDrive used LLaVa-7B, OpenDriveVLA used 1000k samples).

- **NAVSIMv1 Benchmark (Closed-Loop Planning):**
 - AutoDrive-P$^3$ (Detailed Thinking) achieved a Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of 90.6, which represents the state-of-the-art performance with vision-only input on this benchmark. This surpassed previous leading methods like WoTE (88.3 PDMS).
 - AutoDrive-P$^3$ (Fast Thinking) also demonstrated strong performance with a PDMS of 90.2.

- **NAVSIMv2 Benchmark (Closed-Loop Planning):**
 - AutoDrive-P$^3$ (Detailed Thinking) achieved an Extended Predictive Driver Model Score (EPDMS) of 89.9, establishing state-of-the-art performance. This outperformed methods such as WoTE (87.7 EPDMS).
 - AutoDrive-P$^3$ (Fast Thinking) yielded an EPDMS of 88.7.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Impact of Joint RFT (P$^3$-GRPO):**
 - Comparing different training settings on nuScenes:
 - **Only SFT:** Achieved an average 3s collision rate of 0.17%, indicating a foundational capability.
 - **SFT + Only Planning GRPO:** Improved the 3s collision rate to 0.12%, matching some baselines, but without direct supervision on perception and prediction.
 - **SFT + P$^3$-GRPO:** Achieved significant improvements across the board, with perception score of 0.64, prediction score of 0.54, and a 3s collision rate of 0.15% (average 0.06%). This demonstrated that jointly reinforcing perception and prediction capabilities through P$^3$-GRPO leads to substantial gains in planning performance, validating the synergistic approach.
- **Training Setting Configurations:**
 - Increasing the GRPO group size from 4 to 8 resulted in improved performance, attributed to more diverse reasoning samples.
 - Incorporating historical trajectories enhanced contextual understanding and performance.
 - Using video sensors (multiple frames) outperformed image-based inputs (single frames) by capturing temporal dynamics. The full configuration (group size 8, historical trajectories, video input) yielded optimal results.
- **Reward Weighting:** Ablation studies on reward weights demonstrated that an unbalanced emphasis on planning reward (e.g., a ratio of 1:1:1:7) could hinder the optimization of perception and prediction modules, ultimately leading to inferior overall planning performance. The optimal ratio identified was 1:2:2:5 for format, perception, prediction, and planning rewards, respectively.
- **KL Divergence Term:** Removing the KL divergence term from the GRPO objective led to significant performance degradation and model collapse during training, highlighting its role in maintaining stability by preventing excessive deviation from the base policy.

#### 4.3. Runtime and Dual Thinking Modes

- The dual thinking modes successfully balanced inference efficiency and performance. Using vLLM 0.8.0 on an H100 GPU, AutoDrive-P$^3$ (Fast Thinking) achieved near real-time performance at 1.0 Hz, while AutoDrive-P$^3$ (Detailed Thinking) operated at 0.5 Hz.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Visualization

- Visualizations confirmed the model's ability to accurately perceive pedestrian locations and predict safe passage opportunities, resulting in less conservative planning compared to other methods.
- In complex scenarios with multiple vehicles, the approach successfully identified key objects and their behaviors, generating trajectories that closely aligned with human driving habits.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AutoDrive-P$^3$ framework represents a notable advancement in the field of autonomous driving, particularly in the application of Vision-Language Models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Addressing Core Limitations of VLM-AD:** The work directly tackles identified limitations in current VLM-based autonomous driving systems, specifically the fragmentation of perception, prediction, and planning, and the lack of comprehensive Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. By unifying these stages and ensuring progressive information flow, AutoDrive-P$^3$ provides a more integrated and coherent decision-making process.

2. **Enhanced Safety and Interpretability:** The explicit generation of CoT reasoning across perception, prediction, and planning modules offers a degree of transparency that is often absent in black-box end-to-end systems. This interpretability can be crucial for debugging, validation, and building trust in autonomous systems, potentially leading to safer deployments. The progressive supervision ensures that planning decisions are grounded in verifiable perceptual and predictive understanding.

3. **State-of-the-Art Performance:** AutoDrive-P$^3$ demonstrates competitive or superior performance on established autonomous driving benchmarks, including both open-loop (nuScenes) and challenging closed-loop (NAVSIMv1/v2) settings. Achieving state-of-the-art results, especially with a comparatively smaller VLM backbone and less training data, highlights the efficiency and effectiveness of the proposed methodology.

4. **Novel Dataset for VLM Training:** The introduction of the P$^3$-CoT dataset is a significant contribution. This dataset, with its high-quality, comprehensive, and explicitly connected CoT annotations for perception, prediction, and planning, provides a valuable resource for future research in training VLMs for autonomous driving tasks. It facilitates both domain adaptation and the development of structured reasoning capabilities.

5. **Innovative Reinforcement Learning Algorithm (P$^3$-GRPO):** By extending Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to provide hierarchical and progressive supervision across all three P$^3$ tasks, the P$^3$-GRPO algorithm offers a novel approach to reinforcement fine-tuning. This mechanism promotes synergistic learning, ensuring that improvements in foundational perception and prediction capabilities directly contribute to more robust and reliable planning.

6. **Practical Deployment Flexibility:** The implementation of dual thinking modes (detailed and fast) offers practical benefits for real-world deployment. Developers can choose between high-interpretability/performance (detailed) and high-efficiency (fast) modes, adapting the system to varying computational resources and operational requirements.

7. **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper identifies ongoing challenges, such as mitigating hallucinatory phenomena in reasoning and transitioning reinforcement learning from offline simulators to real-world interaction. These points offer clear directions for future work, indicating that the AutoDrive-P$^3$ framework serves as a strong foundation for continued advancements in VLM-based autonomous driving.

In summary, AutoDrive-P$^3$ significantly contributes to the field by proposing a principled, unified framework that enhances the reasoning, interpretability, and performance of VLM-based autonomous driving systems, pushing towards more robust and transparent intelligent agents.
