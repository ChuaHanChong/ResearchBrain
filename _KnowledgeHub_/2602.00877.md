# Learning When to Jump for Off-road Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University at Buffalo developed Motion-aware Traversability (MAT), a framework that models terrain cost as a truncated Gaussian function of velocity, enabling robots to reason about dynamic maneuvers like jumps for off-road navigation. This approach reduced detour distance by an average of 75% in simulations and 68.2% in real-world ditch traversals compared to static traversability methods.

## Problem

- Existing off-road navigation systems treat terrain traversability as a static property, leading to conservative or suboptimal planning and an inability to adapt to speed-dependent risks.
- Motion-aware traversability methods are often computationally intensive, making real-time cost updates infeasible for online planning frameworks.
- Autonomous robots lack a robust framework to understand when and where agile maneuvers, such as jumping, are appropriate based on terrain geometry and dynamic conditions.

## Method

- Introduced **Motion-aware Traversability (MAT)**, a novel representation modeling terrain cost as a ==truncated Gaussian function== of velocity, parameterized by peak penalty, velocity at peak, and sensitivity to deviations.
- Developed a two-stage computational approach: a ==U-Net== predicts Gaussian parameters from an elevation map in a single pass, followed by rapid, analytical evaluation of terrain costs during planning.
- Integrated MAT into a ==Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI)== planner to jointly optimize robot positions and velocities, enabling adaptive maneuvers like jumping over ditches or decelerating for bumps.

## Results

- Achieved an average **75% reduction** in detour distance in various simulated off-road scenarios and a **68.2% reduction** in real-world ditch traversal compared to position-only traversability baselines.
- Demonstrated significant reductions in traversal time and energy consumption in both simulated and real-world experiments, indicating improved navigation efficiency.
- Enabled the autonomous execution of agile maneuvers, such as accelerating to jump over ditches and decelerating for bumps, effectively adapting to terrain and speed dynamics in diverse environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Terrain traversability is often non-monotonic with velocity, meaning certain speeds can be more or less hazardous, and a static cost map cannot capture this dynamic relationship.
> - Representing motion-dependent terrain costs with analytical functions (like Gaussians) allows for computationally efficient, real-time evaluation during path planning without re-running deep neural networks.
> - A self-supervised learning framework, utilizing vehicle-terrain interaction data (rollover risk, time, energy) to derive Gaussian parameters, enables robust generalization of traversability to unobserved velocities and diverse environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous off-road navigation presents challenges where robot safety and efficiency are intrinsically linked to both terrain characteristics and the robot's motion dynamics. Conventional approaches to traversability estimation frequently simplify this relationship by treating terrain traversability as a static property, independent of the robot's intended motion. These methods typically infer position-dependent cost maps, assigning a single scalar value to each location.

Existing position-only traversability methods include analytical approaches that compute geometric features like roughness, slope, and step height from LiDAR data, and learning-based models trained on various supervision signals such as semantic labels, terrain elevation, past vehicle-terrain interactions (e.g., vertical forces, pose/velocity discrepancies), or expert trajectories. While these methods are effective for tasks like obstacle avoidance or following smooth trails, they are limited in scenarios where the same terrain may be safe or unsafe depending on the robot's speed or motion profile. This limitation often leads to conservative or suboptimal path planning, such as always avoiding a ditch that could be safely traversed with a specific, higher-speed maneuver.

Recent efforts have attempted to incorporate robot motion into traversability cost computations, but these also face significant limitations. Analytical formulations, such as those that estimate vehicle orientations from elevation maps to calculate traversability constraints, often struggle with generalization due to the difficulty of deriving precise physical models for diverse terrains and dynamic maneuvers like jumps. Such models typically rely on simplifying assumptions, for instance, that the vehicle always maintains ground contact, which precludes agile strategies.

Learning-based approaches to motion-enhanced traversability, such as conditioning neural network predictions on velocity by fusing speed signals with visual features (e.g., HDIF), encounter computational feasibility issues. Any change in velocity necessitates a new network inference, rendering real-time cost updates during online planning infeasible. Consequently, these methods often resort to planning with a fixed velocity. Furthermore, learning a robust traversability-velocity relationship in an end-to-end manner is challenging without dense labels across the full velocity spectrum, leading predictions to be dominated by visual features rather than dynamic properties. Other learning-based works, such as Velociraptor and Salon, learn speed maps to encourage adherence to expert velocities but primarily for regions already deemed traversable by a motion-independent traversability map, thus not addressing terrains requiring specific dynamic strategies.

Beyond traversability estimation, other research focuses on advancing agile motion by improving vehicle kinodynamics or in-air attitude control (e.g., PHLI). These works enable maneuvers like reorientation during airborne phases but do not provide a framework for reasoning about *when* or *where* such maneuvers are feasible based on terrain geometry and dynamic conditions.

This paper addresses the identified gap by introducing a novel traversability representation called Motion-aware Traversability (MAT). MAT explicitly models terrain cost as a function of both perception and actual robot motion. By representing terrain traversability as a truncated Gaussian function of velocity, the authors aim to capture non-monotonic relationships between speed and safety, enable efficient online cost evaluation, and support complex, agile maneuvers, thereby overcoming the limitations of previous motion-agnostic or computationally intensive motion-aware approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research is the observation that low speed does not consistently guarantee safety or efficiency in off-road driving. The traversability of challenging terrains is often highly dependent on the robot's motion dynamics, particularly its velocity. For example, traversing a ditch might be risky at low speeds due to the possibility of getting stuck, yet safe and efficient at a higher, controlled speed through a jump. Conversely, navigating bumpy terrain typically necessitates reduced speeds to maintain stability. Current autonomous navigation systems frequently overlook this coupling between terrain properties and robot motion, leading to suboptimal or overly conservative planning behaviors.

The authors identify several specific limitations in existing methodologies that motivate their work:
- **Neglect of Motion Dynamics:** Most traversability estimation methods provide a static, position-dependent cost map, assigning a uniform scalar value to a terrain region regardless of the robot's velocity. This prevents the robot from distinguishing between situations where the same terrain can be safe or unsafe based on its speed or motion profile.
- **Computational Inefficiency for Real-time Planning:** While some methods attempt to incorporate velocity into traversability, they often require repeated, computationally expensive neural network inferences for every change in velocity. This makes real-time cost updates during path optimization infeasible, forcing planners to operate under fixed-velocity assumptions.
- **Difficulty in Generalization and Extrapolation:** Analytical models struggle to generalize across diverse terrains and complex physics. Learning-based models, when directly regressing traversability from terrain-velocity inputs, often fail on out-of-distribution velocities due to sparse training data across the full velocity spectrum, and their predictions can be overly dominated by visual features.
- **Suboptimal and Conservative Planning:** The inability to accurately model motion-dependent traversability leads to navigation policies that are either excessively cautious (e.g., taking long detours around a traversable obstacle) or potentially unsafe if a crucial dynamic maneuver is not considered.

To address these limitations, the key objectives of this research are:
1. **Introduce a Motion-aware Traversability (MAT) Representation:** Develop a novel representation that explicitly conditions terrain cost on both terrain perception and robot motion dynamics. This representation models each terrain region's cost as a function of velocity, specifically a truncated Gaussian, parameterized by attributes such as peak difficulty, the velocity at which it occurs, and sensitivity to deviations.
2. **Enable Efficient Online Terrain Cost Updates:** Design a system that can predict these Gaussian parameters from perception in a single, efficient forward pass. Subsequently, allow for rapid, analytical updates of terrain costs for any new velocity inferred during online planning, without requiring repeated neural network inferences.
3. **Facilitate Agile Off-road Navigation:** Integrate the MAT representation into a complete navigation system to enable an autonomous vehicle to reason about and execute adaptive motion strategies, such as accelerating to jump over ditches, decelerating to crawl over bumps, or steering to avoid impassable obstacles, based on real-time motion conditions and terrain characteristics.
4. **Demonstrate Enhanced Performance and Safety:** Validate the proposed system in both simulated and real-world environments with various challenging obstacles, demonstrating improvements in navigation efficiency (reduced detours, traversal time, and energy consumption) while maintaining safety.

By achieving these objectives, the authors aim to provide a more robust and adaptive solution for autonomous off-road navigation, enabling vehicles to perform more complex and efficient maneuvers in unstructured and challenging environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a Motion-aware Traversability (MAT) representation and integrates it into a complete navigation system.

**A. Motion-aware Traversability Learning**

The core of MAT is to model terrain traversability as a truncated Gaussian function of velocity, defined over a fixed velocity range. This approach allows for capturing diverse terrain profiles, including non-monotonic risks where intermediate velocities might be more hazardous than either very low or very high speeds. Each pixel in a traversability map is associated with a Gaussian function, parameterized by three terrain-dependent attributes:
- **Peak Penalty (A(x)):** The maximum difficulty for terrain at position `x`.
- **Velocity at Peak Penalty (μ(x)):** The velocity at which the peak penalty occurs.
- **Sensitivity to Velocity Deviations (σ(x)):** How quickly the penalty decreases as velocity deviates from `μ(x)`.

These parameters, `ψ(x) = [A(x), μ(x), σ(x)]^T`, define the velocity-conditioned terrain cost function `T(x, v) = A(x) * exp(-(v - μ(x))^2 / (2 * σ(x)^2))`.

The learning process for MAT involves two main stages: self-supervised label construction and neural network training.

1. **Self-supervised Label Construction:**
 - **Data Collection:** To generate ground-truth labels, the system performs multiple runs over the same terrain with varying commanded speeds (`v_min` to `v_max`). For each run `r` and at each resampled position `p_i`, the velocity `v_r,i` and vehicle response are recorded.
 - **Traversability Cost Derivation:** The traversability cost `t_r,i` at position `p_i` and velocity `v_r,i` is derived from vehicle-terrain interaction data, combining safety and effort metrics:
 - **Safety (Rollover Risk):** This is quantified by the amplification of vehicle instability, specifically deviations of the actual roll and pitch angles (`θ_r,i`) from a defined safe orientation bound (`θ`). An accumulated, exponentially discounted sum of future instantaneous risks is used to account for delayed effects. The raw risk is normalized to a range.
 - **Effort (Time and Energy):** These are normalized metrics. Travel time measures the duration to reach the goal, and control energy is the time integral of the throttle command from `p_i` to the goal. Normalization ensures comparability across different positions and speeds.
 - **Combined Score:** The final traversability score `t_r,i` is a weighted sum of normalized rollover risk (`t_roll_r,i`), travel time (`t_time_r,i`), and energy consumption (`t_energy_r,i`), with weights `w_r = 0.8`, `w_t = 0.1`, and `w_e = 0.1` respectively.
 - **Gaussian Parameter Estimation:** For each position `p_i`, a set of velocity-traversability samples `S_i = {(v_r,i, t_r,i)}` is used to fit the Gaussian model (Eq. 1) via a least-squares minimization, yielding the ground-truth Gaussian parameters `ψ(p_i)`. These parameters are then assigned to the corresponding pixel and its surroundings on a height map, forming a partial ground-truth MAT map `Ψ` and a binary mask `M` indicating labeled pixels.

2. **Neural Network Training:**
 - A U-Net-based neural network (`g_θ`) is employed to predict the MAT map.
 - **Input:** A LiDAR-based 2D elevation map (`H`).
 - **Output:** A three-channel MAT map `ˆΨ` where each channel corresponds to `A(x)`, `μ(x)`, and `σ(x)`.
 - **Activation:** Sigmoid activations followed by affine scaling are applied to ensure predicted parameters fall within valid physical ranges.
 - **Loss Function:** A masked Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is calculated over the labeled pixels specified by `M`, penalizing deviations between predicted and ground-truth Gaussian parameters.
 - **Augmentation:** Random spatial augmentations are used during training to enhance robustness.

**B. Motion-aware Navigation System**

The MAT representation is integrated into a complete ROS-based navigation system for real-time off-road driving. The system operates in a pipeline:

1. **Perception:**
 - **LiDAR-Inertial Odometry:** FAST-LIO provides pose estimates at 10 Hz.
 - **Terrain Mapping:** A dedicated node fuses recent LiDAR scans to maintain a dense local point cloud, which is then converted into a 2D elevation map `H` (0.1m x 0.1m grid, 40m sensing range).

2. **MAT Estimation:** The trained neural network (`g_θ`) takes the elevation map `H` as input and performs a single forward pass to predict the three-channel MAT map (`A`, `μ`, `σ`) for the entire local environment. This one-shot inference is crucial for computational efficiency.

3. **Local Planner (MPPI):** A Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) planner is used to jointly optimize robot positions and velocities.
 - **Trajectory Sampling:** At each iteration, `N` control sequences are sampled and propagated through a vehicle dynamics model (including position, heading, speed, and curvature) to generate candidate trajectories.
 - **Cost Evaluation:** Each trajectory is evaluated using a cost function that includes control effort, a terminal cost, auxiliary costs for goal tracking and stability, and the terrain cost `T(x_t, v_t)`.
 - **Motion-conditioned Terrain Cost:** The key innovation is the real-time calculation of `T(x_t, v_t)`. Since the MAT map provides the Gaussian parameters `ψ(x_t)` for all positions, the terrain cost for any sampled position `x_t` and velocity `v_t` can be analytically evaluated using the closed-form Gaussian function without re-running the neural network. This allows for rapid cost updates as velocities change during the iterative MPPI optimization.
 - **Optimization:** The MPPI algorithm then updates the nominal control sequence based on a weighted average of sampled trajectories, effectively selecting the optimal path and velocity profile.

4. **Control:** A low-level PID controller translates the optimized path's steering and throttle/brake commands into Pulse-Width Modulation (PWM) values for vehicle actuation.

The two-stage computation—one-shot neural inference for Gaussian parameters, followed by rapid analytical evaluation of terrain costs—enables the system to perform real-time, motion-conditioned terrain cost optimization on resource-constrained platforms, a significant departure from prior motion-aware approaches that faced computational bottlenecks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the MAT system in both simulated (BeamNG.tech) and real-world environments, demonstrating its efficiency and effectiveness in off-road navigation with challenging obstacles.

**A. Real-time Efficiency**
The MAT system demonstrates real-time efficiency on an edge device. The proposed two-stage computation, where Gaussian parameters are predicted in a single neural network pass and then analytically evaluated during planning, enables rapid updates of motion-conditioned terrain costs, which is crucial for online optimization.

**B. Simulation: Obstacle Traversal**
Experiments were conducted with various terrain geometries (short ditch, long ditch, bump) and at different operating velocities (slow, moderate, fast, induced by MPPI time horizons T1, T2, T3).

1. **Quantitative Comparison (Table I):**
 - **Detour Distance:** MAT reduced detour distance by an average of 75% across all obstacles compared to the Position-Only Traversability (PO-Trav) baseline.
 - **Traversal Time & Energy Consumption:** MAT also achieved lower traversal time and energy consumption compared to PO-Trav, AnyNav, and PhysORD. The gains were particularly notable in scenarios where agile maneuvers were possible.

2. **Qualitative Comparison (Fig. 5):**
 - **Short Ditch:** PO-Trav consistently avoided the ditch due to high static cost. MAT, in contrast, avoided the ditch at slow speeds but accelerated to jump across it when sufficient speed was achievable, demonstrating velocity-adaptive behavior.
 - **Long Ditch:** MAT exhibited further adaptability, crawling over the ditch at low speed, avoiding it at medium speed, and accelerating to jump at high speed, based on the specific Gaussian profiles (A, μ, σ) predicted for the terrain.
 - **Bump:** For bumps traversable only at low velocities, MAT planned to decelerate before crossing.
 - These behaviors highlight MAT's ability to reason about how speed affects risk and to generate dynamic, terrain-adaptive motion plans, unlike the uniform avoidance strategies of PO-Trav.

**C. Simulation: Long-range Navigation**
Experiments were performed on modified BeamNG maps (Forest, Utah Canyon, Desert) with emergent, unstructured obstacles.

1. **Navigation with Emerging Obstacles (Fig. 6 - Forest):**
 - MAT enabled agile maneuvers, such as jumping a ditch.
 - The system adapted strategies based on vehicle dynamics and terrain context; for instance, avoiding an uphill ditch where momentum was insufficient but accelerating to jump a downhill ditch when descent provided favorable momentum.

2. **Generalization to Unseen Maps (Fig. 6 - Utah Canyon, Desert):**
 - MAT maintained its agile and adaptive behavior in novel environments with new terrain geometries (boulders, bedrocks) and surface interactions (sand).
 - It reproduced ditch-handling strategies and appropriately responded to new obstacles by avoiding untraversable boulders/rocks and decelerating for flatter bedrock surfaces.

**D. Real-world: Ditch Traversal**
The system was deployed on a 1/5-scale Racer car in real-world scenarios involving a manmade ditch with a fence and a natural snow ditch.

1. **Quantitative Comparison (Table II):**
 - **Manmade Ditch & Fence:** MAT reduced detour distance by 68.2% and traversal time by 12.3% compared to PO-Trav.
 - **Natural Snow Ditch:** MAT completed traversal in 40.1% of the time required by PO-Trav and significantly reduced detour distance (from 1.58m to 0.09m).
 - Lower energy consumption was observed for MAT in both scenarios due to more efficient motion plans.

2. **Qualitative Comparison (Fig. 7):**
 - **Manmade Ditch & Fence:** PO-Trav avoided both obstacles. MAT identified the ditch as traversable at high speed and executed a jump, while correctly recognizing the fence as universally untraversable. When configured for a slow vehicle ("Ours-Slow"), MAT correctly avoided the ditch, demonstrating robust reasoning under varying system dynamics.
 - **Natural Snow Ditch:** PO-Trav avoided the ditch. MAT utilized higher speeds to traverse it directly but avoided it under low-speed conditions.

**E. Real-world: Outdoor Navigation with Obstacles (Fig. 8)**
The system demonstrated effectiveness in autonomous navigation among diverse natural obstacles (trees, curbs, rocks) in an outdoor environment. MAT successfully captured distinct traversability-velocity characteristics for each obstacle type (e.g., high cost for trees/rocks at all speeds, low cost for curbs at low speed but high cost at high speed) through varying Gaussian parameters. This enabled the MPPI planner to generate appropriate adaptive maneuvers, such as avoiding trees and rocks while decelerating to crawl over curbs.

Overall, the findings indicate that MAT significantly enhances the adaptability, efficiency, and safety of off-road navigation by explicitly modeling and utilizing motion-conditioned terrain costs, leading to more intelligent and agile decision-making by autonomous vehicles.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces Motion-aware Traversability (MAT), a novel representation that conditions terrain cost on both terrain perception and robot motion, representing a development in off-road navigation for autonomous systems.

**Significance:**
- **Addressing a Fundamental Limitation:** The work directly addresses the fundamental limitation of many existing traversability methods, which treat terrain traversability as a static property. By modeling traversability as a velocity-dependent function (a truncated Gaussian), MAT allows for the explicit capture of non-monotonic relationships between speed and safety, where certain terrains may be traversable only within specific speed ranges or via dynamic maneuvers.
- **Enabling Agile Maneuvers:** MAT empowers autonomous vehicles to reason about and execute agile, dynamic maneuvers such as controlled jumps over ditches, or precise speed adjustments for bumps and curbs. These behaviors are often necessary for efficient and safe traversal of challenging unstructured environments but are largely beyond the capabilities of systems relying on static cost maps.
- **Computational Efficiency for Real-time Applications:** The proposed two-stage computational approach (one-shot neural inference for Gaussian parameters followed by rapid analytical evaluation during planning) ensures that the richer, motion-aware traversability information can be integrated into real-time model predictive control frameworks without significant computational overhead. This is a crucial practical advancement for deploying such capabilities on resource-constrained platforms.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** The self-supervised learning framework for Gaussian parameters, derived from vehicle-terrain interactions, allows the model to extrapolate traversability to unobserved velocities, enhancing its generalization capabilities to out-of-distribution conditions and unseen environments.
- **Quantitative Performance Improvements:** The demonstrated reductions in detour distance (average 75% reduction in simulation), traversal time, and energy consumption in both simulation and real-world scenarios highlight the practical benefits and improved efficiency of MAT-enabled navigation strategies.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Off-road Autonomy:** This work can contribute to the development of more robust, efficient, and versatile autonomous ground vehicles capable of operating in highly unstructured and dynamic off-road environments. This is relevant for applications in defense, exploration, agriculture, and logistics where vehicles frequently encounter varied and challenging terrains.
- **Decision-Making for Dynamic Environments:** The ability to "learn when to jump" or to adapt speed for specific obstacles can lead to more intelligent and human-like decision-making in complex scenarios, allowing robots to navigate paths that would otherwise be deemed impassable or excessively long by conservative planning methods.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** MAT provides a foundational framework for further advancements in motion-aware traversability. Future work could explore more complex functional representations beyond the Gaussian model, incorporate additional motion factors (e.g., acceleration, slip), or integrate these concepts with higher-level mission planning to enable even more sophisticated behaviors.
- **Safety and Efficiency in Critical Missions:** For time-critical missions or operations in remote, rugged terrain, the ability to rapidly and safely traverse difficult obstacles without extensive detours can significantly improve mission success rates and operational efficiency, while also reducing wear and tear on robotic platforms.

In summary, this research offers a practical and effective method for integrating motion dynamics into traversability analysis, leading to more adaptive, efficient, and agile autonomous navigation capabilities in challenging off-road settings.
