# Flow-Based Single-Step Completion for Efficient and Expressive Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Iowa State University proposes the Single-Step Completion Policy (SSCP), a flow-based generative model that enables efficient, one-shot action generation for reinforcement learning. The approach significantly reduces inference and training times compared to iterative generative policies while achieving competitive performance on D4RL benchmarks and outperforming diffusion-based methods in online settings and behavior cloning.

## Problem

- Traditional unimodal Gaussian policies in reinforcement learning often lack the expressiveness to model complex, multimodal action distributions.
- Existing iterative generative policies (e.g., diffusion models) incur high computational costs during inference due to multi-step sampling and introduce training instability in off-policy RL frameworks.
- Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) typically relies on complex hierarchical methods, and distilling their benefits into efficient, flat policies remains an open challenge.

## Method

- A Single-Step Completion Policy (**SSCP**) is developed using flow-matching to predict a "==completion vector==" that directly maps an initial noise sample to the final action in one step, bypassing iterative generation.
- SSCP is integrated into an offline reinforcement learning algorithm, SSCQL, combining flow-matching, a direct completion loss, and a Q-guided policy loss, making it compatible with off-policy gradient methods without requiring multi-step backpropagation.
- For Goal-Conditioned RL, GC-SSCP extends this principle by training a unified flat policy to output both actions and subgoals, using a self-supervised loss to distill hierarchical reasoning for direct, single-step inference.

## Results

- SSCQL achieved competitive performance on D4RL MuJoCo benchmarks, matching or exceeding several state-of-the-art baselines with an average normalized return of 87.9 across tasks.
- The method demonstrated substantial efficiency gains, with up to **64x** faster training than DQL and an order-of-magnitude faster inference (**0.27** ± **0.36 ms** per action) compared to diffusion-based models.
- GC-SSCP outperformed the average of the hierarchical state-of-the-art (HIQL) on OGBench GCRL tasks (**54.44%** average success rate vs. **50.94%**) and showed superior stability during offline-to-online finetuning and fully online RL compared to diffusion-based policies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generative policies can achieve both high expressiveness and computational efficiency by formulating action generation as a single-step completion problem, making them viable for real-time control.
> - Directly supervising the model to predict a 'completion vector' to the target action, rather than relying solely on instantaneous velocity fields, significantly improves the stability and effectiveness of one-shot action generation.
> - The benefits of hierarchical reasoning for long-horizon tasks can be effectively distilled into a flat, single-network policy architecture, simplifying design and achieving competitive performance against explicit hierarchical methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of reinforcement learning (RL) is continuously evolving, with a particular focus on learning effective policies from fixed datasets, a challenge known as offline RL. Traditionally, unimodal Gaussian policies have been widely adopted due to their simplicity and compatibility with gradient-based learning. However, these policies often exhibit limited expressiveness, which can hinder performance in environments characterized by multimodal or highly non-linear behavior distributions.

Recent advancements in generative modeling, specifically diffusion and flow-based methods, have introduced more expressive alternatives for policy representation. These models are capable of capturing complex, multimodal action distributions, demonstrating notable performance in imitation learning, particularly when learning from expert demonstrations. Despite these capabilities, practical limitations have restricted their widespread adoption as policy classes in RL. A significant challenge is computational inefficiency during inference, as diffusion models typically necessitate numerous denoising steps (tens to hundreds) to generate a single action, which is often impractical for real-time control applications. Furthermore, integrating iterative generative structures into conventional off-policy value-based learning frameworks presents difficulties. The requirement for long generation horizons can make backpropagation through time (BPTT) either intractable or highly inefficient, thereby impeding scalable off-policy gradient-based optimization in offline RL.

Existing efforts to mitigate these issues include reducing the number of generative steps or distilling policies into simpler networks. However, these strategies often introduce trade-offs, such as reduced empirical performance, increased sensitivity to hyperparameters, or greater architectural complexity.

In parallel, Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL) focuses on learning policies that can achieve diverse target states. Hierarchical methods in GCRL have demonstrated effectiveness by decomposing tasks into subgoal selection and low-level action execution. This raises a research question regarding whether the benefits of hierarchical subgoal exploitation can be distilled into a single, flat decision-making policy.

This work addresses the aforementioned limitations by proposing a flow-based policy that aims to combine the expressiveness of generative models with the inference and training efficiency typically associated with simpler policy representations. By developing a single-step action generation mechanism, the authors seek to overcome the computational and stability challenges observed in multi-step generative policies, thereby positioning their method as a more practical and scalable solution for various RL and robotic decision-making tasks, including an extension to GCRL to emulate hierarchical reasoning with a flat policy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and expressive generative policy for offline reinforcement learning that addresses the computational inefficiencies and training instabilities prevalent in existing iterative generative models. The authors are motivated by several specific challenges:

- **Limited Expressiveness of Traditional Policies:** Unimodal Gaussian policies, while simple, often fail to adequately represent complex, multimodal action distributions encountered in realistic environments. This limitation can lead to suboptimal performance, particularly in tasks requiring diverse or nuanced behaviors. The motivation is to leverage the expressiveness of generative models to capture these distributions more effectively.
- **High Inference Costs of Iterative Generative Models:** Existing generative policies, such as those based on diffusion, require a large number of iterative sampling steps to produce an action. This iterative process results in significant computational overhead during inference, rendering these policies impractical for real-time control applications where low latency is critical. The objective is to enable one-shot, direct action generation to achieve inference efficiency comparable to unimodal policies.
- **Training Instability and Inefficiency in Off-Policy RL:** Integrating iterative generative models into standard off-policy, value-based RL frameworks (e.g., DDPG+BC) is challenging. The need to backpropagate gradients through multiple sampling steps leads to prohibitively long computational graphs, increasing memory requirements and training time, and potentially causing gradient instability. The motivation is to design a generative policy class that is compatible with off-policy gradient methods without necessitating long backpropagation chains.
- **Trade-offs in Existing Mitigation Strategies:** Previous attempts to enhance the efficiency of generative policies, such as reducing the number of generative steps or distilling policies, often involve compromises in performance, increased hyperparameter sensitivity, or greater architectural complexity. The authors aim to avoid these trade-offs by fundamentally re-thinking the generative process to be single-step from the outset.

A secondary objective is to extend this concept of efficient, single-step generative policies to Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GCRL). The motivation here is to investigate whether the benefits of hierarchical decomposition, which typically involves explicit multi-level policies for subgoal selection and action execution, can be distilled into a single, flat policy architecture. This would simplify both training and inference in GCRL while retaining the ability to exploit subgoal structures for long-horizon tasks, potentially surpassing flat baselines and rivaling hierarchical state-of-the-art methods.

The key contributions outlined by the authors reflect these objectives:
- A novel policy method based on single-step completion using flow-matching generative models, designed for direct, iterative-free action sampling.
- Demonstration of this policy's compatibility with off-policy policy gradient methods (e.g., DDPG+BC), enabling strong empirical results without BPTT issues and facilitating offline-to-online finetuning.
- A framework for distilling hierarchical behavior into flat policies for long-horizon, sparse-reward, multi-goal conditioned environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology centers on the Single-Step Completion Policy (SSCP), which leverages flow-matching generative models to predict actions directly, bypassing iterative generation steps. This approach is then integrated into an offline reinforcement learning algorithm (SSCQL) and extended to goal-conditioned RL (GC-SSCP).

#### 3.1. Single-Step Completion Model

The core idea is to augment the standard flow-matching objective with an additional component that trains the model to predict *completion vectors*. Unlike conventional flow-based models that learn instantaneous velocity fields, the completion vector provides a direct "shortcut" to the target action from any intermediate point in the generative flow.

- **Background on Shortcut Models:** The authors contextualize their approach by referencing prior work on shortcut models, which learn normalized directions *h(x_τ, τ, d)* to predict future states *x'_(τ+d)* from *x_τ* by scaling the direction by a step size *d*. These models typically use a self-consistency loss (*L_shortcut*) where targets are bootstrapped from the model itself. However, the authors note that relying on self-generated targets can be problematic in offline RL, especially during early training, due to potential model drift and out-of-distribution issues.

- **Single-Step Completion Vector (Proposed):** To address the limitations of bootstrap targets, the authors propose training the model to predict a "completion vector" that points directly from an intermediate sample *x_τ* to the final target sample *x_1*. This is achieved by setting the step size *d* to *(1-τ)*, the remaining time to reach the final state. The model then predicts *h_θ(x_τ, τ, 1-τ)*, and the predicted final point is *x̂_1 = x_τ + h_θ(x_τ, τ, 1-τ) ⋅ (1-τ)*. The training objective, *L_completion* (Equation 12), minimizes the squared error between *x̂_1* and the true *x_1* (drawn from the training data). This formulation allows for direct supervision from ground-truth data, circumventing the need for internally generated pseudo-targets.

- **Policy Representation:** The completion model is adapted for policy learning by conditioning it on the environment state *s*. The policy *π_θ(s)* is defined as a one-step jump from an initial noise sample *a_τ* (typically *a_0* from a noise distribution) to the final action: *π_θ(s) = a_τ + h_θ(a_τ, s, τ, 1-τ) ⋅ (1-τ)*. The paper clarifies that while the model defines a transport between distributions, the policy output for a fixed input *a_τ* is deterministic.

#### 3.2. SSCQL: Offline RL with Single-Step Completion Policy

The SSCP is integrated into an off-policy actor-critic framework called SSCQL (Single-Step Completion Q-Learning). The actor training objective combines three loss components:

- **Flow Matching Loss (*L_flow*, Eq. 15):** This term ensures local flow consistency by training the model to predict the instantaneous velocity field *v = a - z* (where *a* is the target action and *z* is the initial noise) from an interpolated sample *a_τ*. The model predicts *h_θ(a_τ, s, τ, 0)*, corresponding to instantaneous velocity.
- **Completion Loss (*L_completion*, Eq. 16):** This is the proposed single-step completion loss, directly supervising the model to regress to the terminal action *a* from *a_τ* in one step, as described above.
- **Q-Guided Policy Loss (*L_πQ*, Eq. 13):** This is a standard deterministic policy gradient loss, steering the policy towards actions that maximize the estimated Q-value: *E_[s~D] [-Q_ϕ(s, π_θ(s))]* (where *π_θ(s)* is the action generated by the single-step completion model).

The total actor loss (*L_π(θ)*, Eq. 17) is a weighted sum of these three components, with *α_1* and *α_2* as hyperparameters balancing the flow and completion regularization, respectively. The critic is trained using a standard Bellman backup loss (*L_Q(ϕ)*, Eq. 18) with a min-operator over two Q-functions and target networks, similar to TD3.

A key advantage highlighted is efficiency: the single-step nature of the policy eliminates the need for multi-step flow rollouts during actor and critic training, as well as at test time, thus avoiding expensive backpropagation chains and iterative inference.

#### 3.3. GC-SSCP: Goal-Conditioned RL with Completion Modeling

For Goal-Conditioned RL (GCRL), the authors extend the single-step completion principle to enable flat policies to exploit subgoal structures.

- **Hierarchy Distillation:** The approach proposes a single unified policy model *π_θ* that outputs both an immediate action *a_t* and an intermediate subgoal *s_(t+k)*. This policy is conditioned on the current state *s_t*, the final goal *g*, and an abstract hierarchy level indicator *k ∈ {0, 1}*. The variable *k* allows shared parameterization across coarse-grained (goal-level, *k=0*) and fine-grained (subgoal-level, *k=1*) reasoning.

- **Training Losses:** The model is trained using advantage-weighted regression (AWR) losses:
 - *L_0(θ)* (Equation from text, first AWR loss): For high-level reasoning, penalizing the policy's prediction of *a_t* and *s_(t+k)* based on high-level advantage *A_h(s_t, s_(t+k), g)*.
 - *L_1(θ)* (Equation from text, second AWR loss): For low-level reasoning, penalizing the policy's prediction of *a_t* and *s_(t+1)* based on low-level advantage *A_ℓ(s_t, a_t, s_(t+k))*.
 The advantage estimates (*A_h* and *A_ℓ*) are derived from a GCIVL-trained value function (similar to HIQL).

- **Shortcut-Based Flat Policy & Distillation:** To enable *direct flat inference*, an additional variable *d* is introduced to the policy input, specifying the number of forward steps in the hierarchy. This allows the model to simulate multi-step reasoning. A self-supervised loss *L_s(θ)* (Eq. 36) is incorporated to distill the composed hierarchical rollout (π_d=1_k=0 ◦ π_d=1_k=1) into a single flat prediction (π_θ(⋅ | s_t, g, k=0, d=2)). This means the model trained for a *d=2, k=0* setting learns to match the output of a two-step hierarchical inference process, with gradients stopped through the target.

- **Inference:** During test time, GC-SSCP performs direct inference by sampling from *π_θ(⋅ | s_t, g, 0, 2)*, effectively bypassing recursive hierarchical rollouts.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details (from Appendix)

- **Hyperparameters:** Common hyperparameters include a discount factor (γ) of 0.99, batch size of 1024, learning rates of 3x10^-4, and actor/critic networks with four layers of 512 hidden units. For online RL, reduced batch sizes and network sizes were used, with a smaller learning rate and gradual exploration probability.
- **Time and Step Size Encoding:** Learnable Fourier feature projections encode scalar time inputs (τ) and step sizes (d), passed through two-layer MLPs.
- **Time Distribution and Sampling:** A non-uniform sampling scheme (τ = t^2, resulting in a Beta(0.5, 1) distribution) is used to emphasize early stages of the flow process during training.
- **Actor Q Loss Normalization:** An adaptive scaling normalizes the Q-based policy update to stabilize gradient magnitudes.
- **Goal-Conditioned RL Specifics:** Simplified advantage estimates from previous works are used. The policy predicts an *encoded representation* of the subgoal, and discrete hierarchy indicators (*k, d*) are encoded using Fourier features, similar to time encoding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of SSCP and its variants spanned several reinforcement learning settings.

#### 4.1. Offline RL Performance (SSCQL)

- **D4RL Benchmarks:** SSCQL was evaluated on continuous control tasks from the D4RL MuJoCo suite (HalfCheetah, Hopper, Walker2d) across medium, medium-replay, and medium-expert datasets.
- **Competitive Results:** SSCQL achieved strong performance, frequently matching or exceeding various unimodal Gaussian policy baselines (BC, TD3+BC, CQL, IQL) and state-of-the-art generative policy approaches (Diffuser, FQL, DQL, CAC, IDQL). For example, on average across all D4RL tasks, SSCQL achieved a normalized return of 87.9, matching the top baseline (FQL) and surpassing others. Notably, SSCQL demonstrated top performance in several Hopper and Walker2d tasks.
- **Impact of Completion Loss:** Ablation studies (Figure 7) indicated that omitting the completion loss (setting *α2*=0) generally degraded performance, affirming the contribution of the shortcut-completion objective to one-shot action generation. Conversely, relying heavily on self-supervised bootstrap shortcut losses led to instability and performance degradation (Figure 8).

#### 4.2. Efficiency Characteristics

- **Training and Inference Speed:** A key finding was SSCQL's significant advantage in training and inference efficiency compared to generative-policy baselines. SSCQL was reported to be up to 64 times faster in training than DQL and offered over an order-of-magnitude speedup in inference time (0.27 ± 0.36 ms per action versus 1.27-2.01 ms for diffusion models). This efficiency is attributed to its single-step generative process.

#### 4.3. Offline-to-Online Finetuning and Online Training

- **Finetuning Stability:** SSCQL demonstrated stable improvements during offline-to-online finetuning, where policies pretrained offline were further trained with online interaction. In contrast, diffusion-based baselines (DQL, CAC) often exhibited performance degradation, suggesting brittleness in adapting to online data.
- **Online RL Performance:** In fully online RL, SSCQL significantly outperformed DQL and CAC across all Gym locomotion tasks. The paper hypothesizes that SSCQL's single-step generative actor, which avoids backpropagation through time (BPTT), contributes to more stable gradients and efficient learning dynamics compared to multi-step denoising chains.

#### 4.4. Behavior Cloning

- **RoboMimic and Push-T:** SSCP was evaluated on state-based human demonstration datasets from RoboMimic and the Push-T task.
- **Competitive Performance:** Despite its single-step generation, SSCP achieved competitive or superior performance compared to Diffusion Policy (DP), which uses 100 iterative denoising steps. SSCP matched DP's average success rate of 0.88 across tasks while offering substantially reduced computational overhead. It also outperformed other baselines such as LSTM-GMM, Implicit Behavior Cloning, and Behavior Transformer.

#### 4.5. Offline Goal-Conditioned RL (GC-SSCP)

- **OGBench Benchmarks:** GC-SSCP was evaluated on PointMaze and AntMaze tasks from OGBench, which involve long-horizon and hierarchical reasoning.
- **Strong Performance:** GC-SSCP achieved strong performance across tasks, outperforming other flat baselines (GCBC, GCIVL, GCIQL, QRL, CRL) and, on average, the hierarchical state-of-the-art method HIQL. GC-SSCP achieved an average success rate of 54.44, surpassing HIQL's 50.94.
- **Hierarchy Distillation Effectiveness:** Ablation studies on inference type for GCRL (Figure 11a) showed no significant performance gap between the proposed flat inference and a standard hierarchical inference strategy, indicating that the hierarchy distillation mechanism effectively flattens the policy without sacrificing performance.
- **Robustness to Subgoal Choice:** Performance was found to be relatively stable across a range of subgoal step intervals (5 to 500 steps), with a peak around 25-100 steps, indicating robustness to this hyperparameter.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a policy class and associated algorithms that address a notable trade-off in current reinforcement learning methodologies: the balance between policy expressiveness and computational efficiency.

The **Single-Step Completion Policy (SSCP)** offers a method for generative policies to capture complex, multimodal action distributions while maintaining inference efficiency. This is a significant advancement for applications requiring real-time decision-making, such as robotics and autonomous systems, where the latency introduced by iterative generative processes (e.g., in diffusion models) has been a limiting factor. The demonstrated reduction in training and inference time, sometimes by orders of magnitude, suggests that expressive generative policies could become more broadly deployable in practical settings.

The **SSCQL algorithm** demonstrates that this efficient generative policy can be effectively integrated into standard off-policy actor-critic frameworks. Its compatibility with DDPG+BC methods, without requiring complex backpropagation through time, contributes to training stability and efficiency in offline RL. The observed robustness during offline-to-online finetuning and superior performance in fully online RL scenarios indicate its potential for broader applicability beyond static dataset learning, enabling agents to leverage offline data and adapt efficiently to online environments.

The extension to **Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning (GC-SSCP)**, through the distillation of hierarchical reasoning into a flat policy, presents an alternative to explicit hierarchical architectures. By allowing a single policy network to implicitly handle multi-level decision-making, GC-SSCP simplifies policy design and inference while achieving strong performance, even outperforming some hierarchical state-of-the-art methods. This approach could simplify the development of general-purpose agents for long-horizon, complex tasks by reducing architectural complexity and potentially improving generalization across different levels of abstraction.

Potential impacts include:
- **Enhanced Real-time Control:** The efficiency gains make sophisticated generative policies more viable for real-time robotic control and other applications where immediate action generation is critical.
- **Broader Adoption of Generative Policies:** By addressing key computational limitations, this work may encourage wider adoption of expressive generative models in various RL domains, moving beyond the current dominance of unimodal policies.
- **Simplified Hierarchical Learning:** The GC-SSCP framework could offer a simpler, yet effective, paradigm for goal-conditioned learning, reducing the overhead of designing and training explicit hierarchical controllers.

Despite these advancements, the authors acknowledge limitations, including potential expressiveness constraints for extremely high-dimensional or highly multimodal behaviors compared to multi-step generative policies, sensitivity to the new regularization hyperparameters (α1, α2), and the current restriction to continuous action spaces. Addressing these limitations could further expand the applicability and robustness of single-step completion policies.
