# Ground-level Viewpoint Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper presents Ground-level Viewpoint Navigation (GVNav), an approach that enables ground-level robots to follow natural language instructions by addressing the viewpoint disparity between human-height VLN research and real-robot perspectives. GVNav enhances waypoint prediction and incorporates adaptive information gathering, leading to improved navigation accuracy and robustness for low-height robots in both simulated and real-world environments.

## Problem

- A significant disparity exists between the human-height (approx. 1.5m) viewpoints used in most Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) research and the much lower viewpoints (approx. 0.3m) of practical robotic platforms.
- This viewpoint difference leads to challenges such as restricted field of view, increased visual obstructions, and mismatches between human instruction reference points and the robot's perception.
- Existing VLN models experience substantial performance degradation when deployed with low line-of-sight cameras, hindering their real-world applicability.

## Method

- Introducing **Ground-level** ==Viewpoint Navigation (GVNav)==, a framework designed to bridge the viewpoint discrepancy for low-height robots in continuous environments.
- Implementing an enhanced waypoint prediction method that uses data augmentation from low-height perspectives and depth-aware training to improve path planning.
- Developing an adaptive information gathering module that integrates weighted historical observations and uses an attention mechanism to create richer spatiotemporal context, compensating for limited instantaneous field of view.

## Results

- GVNav achieved superior performance compared to baseline methods across standard VLN metrics in both simulation and real-world experiments on a Xiaomi Cyberdog.
- The enhanced waypoint prediction module led to substantial improvements in navigation accuracy and generalization, particularly in handling ground-level depth information.
- The adaptive information gathering module demonstrated significant benefits in environments with visual obstructions, enabling the robot to maintain situational awareness despite its limited viewpoint, and real-world deployment confirmed robust navigation and accurate instruction following.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The disparity between human-height viewpoints used in most VLN research and the ground-level perspectives of many real robots creates a significant information gap.
> - Accurate waypoint prediction is particularly critical for effective navigation from low-height viewpoints, where visibility is often limited.
> - Integrating historical observations and adaptively selecting informative viewpoints can mitigate the impact of restricted field of view and visual obstructions common in ground-level navigation.
