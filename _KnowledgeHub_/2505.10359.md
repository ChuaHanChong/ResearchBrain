# NVSPolicy: Adaptive Novel-View Synthesis for Generalizable Language-Conditioned Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> NVSPolicy, a framework from researchers at National University of Defense Technology, enhances robot generalization in language-conditioned manipulation by integrating adaptive novel-view synthesis and disentangled latent representations. It achieves improved success rates on the CALVIN benchmark for long-horizon tasks, with an average successful sequence length of 2.93, and demonstrates a 44% average success rate on real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Robots struggle to generalize across diverse objects and scenes in unstructured environments due to partial visual observations and limited context.
- Integrating deep generative models with robot policy learning is challenging due to potential visual artifacts in synthesized images and the need for efficient multi-modal feature integration.
- Language-conditioned robotic manipulation requires robust methods for long-horizon tasks and effective utilization of imperfect visual information.

## Method

- Employs adaptive novel-view synthesis using a pre-trained generative model to dynamically provide context-enhanced observations from adaptively selected viewpoints.
- Introduces a cycle-consistent ==Variational Autoencoder (VAE)== to disentangle visual features into semantic and remaining components, mitigating the impact of imperfections in synthesized images.
- Develops a hierarchical policy network that integrates these disentangled features with language instructions, augmented by keyframe selection and policy distillation for efficiency and robustness.

## Results

- NVSPolicy achieved improved task success rates on the CALVIN benchmark for long-horizon tasks, with a **56.3%** success rate for three consecutive tasks and an average successful sequence length of **2.93**, surpassing previous state-of-the-art methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed the positive contributions of novel-view synthesis, disentangled feature representation, policy distillation, and adaptive viewpoint selection to overall performance.
- The framework demonstrated robustness in real-world environments, achieving an average success rate of **44%** across five representative tasks on a physical robotic platform, significantly outperforming SPIL (**8%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically synthesizing novel views can effectively enhance visual context for robot policy learning, especially when an adaptive viewpoint selection strategy is used to balance information gain and synthesis reliability.
> - Disentangling visual features into semantic and 'remaining' components allows for the robust utilization of potentially imperfect synthesized images within a hierarchical policy architecture.
> - Hierarchical policy designs, combined with improved visual representations and language conditioning, contribute to better generalizability for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.
> - Computational efficiency for generative models in robotics can be improved through mechanisms like keyframe selection and policy distillation, making them more practical for real-world deployment.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous robotic systems capable of performing complex manipulation tasks in unstructured, open-world environments is a significant area of research. A key challenge in this domain is enabling robots to generalize across diverse objects and scenes without requiring extensive task-specific training data. Recent advancements in deep generative models, which can synthesize novel content across various modalities (text, images, video), have been explored for their potential to provide robots with open-world knowledge and enhance generalization capabilities. These models can, for instance, generate unseen regions of a scene from partial observations, thereby enriching the visual context available to a robot.

However, integrating deep generative models with robot policy learning presents several challenges. One notable issue is the presence of visual artifacts or imperfections in generated images, which can degrade the robot's performance if relied upon directly. Another challenge involves the effective and efficient integration of multi-modal features, such as vision and language, within policy learning frameworks.

This work addresses these challenges by specifically focusing on language-conditioned robotic manipulation, where robots execute tasks based on natural language instructions. The paper positions itself within the landscape of:
- **Language-conditioned Robotic Manipulation:** Prior work in this area has evolved from cross-modal attention mechanisms for object grounding to large vision-language-action models for zero-shot generalization. Hierarchical skill learning methods, such as GCBC, HULC, and SPIL, aim to improve scalability by reusing low-level primitives. This paper contributes to improving generalization in these systems, particularly for long-horizon tasks and unstructured environments.
- **Generative Models for Robot Manipulation:** Existing research explores visual-motor learning and vision-language-action integration using generative models like diffusion models. While these approaches can generate diverse action distributions or integrate web-scale knowledge (e.g., RT-2, RoboFlamingo), they often require extensive data or face computational efficiency issues. This paper leverages generative models for contextual scene enrichment rather than direct action generation.
- **Novel-View Synthesis (NVS):** NVS methods, including implicit radiance fields (NeRF) and explicit representations (3D Gaussian Splatting), aim to create photorealistic views. Generative image models like PIGAN and NVS-GAN also contribute to NVS. The paper builds upon NVS techniques, specifically GenWarp, which leverages semantic-preserving diffusion-based warping, but acknowledges its limitations with large viewpoint shifts. NVSPolicy integrates NVS as a means to enhance visual context for policy learning, coupled with strategies to manage synthesis imperfections and select informative viewpoints.

By coupling adaptive novel-view synthesis with a hierarchical policy network and introducing mechanisms to mitigate synthesis imperfections and improve efficiency, NVSPolicy aims to advance the generalization capabilities of language-conditioned robot manipulation in complex scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a generalizable language-conditioned policy learning method for robot manipulation that can operate effectively in unstructured environments. The authors identify several motivations and specific problems that their proposed NVSPolicy aims to address:

1. **Enhancing Visual Context from Partial Observations:** Robots often receive only partial observations of a scene, which can limit their ability to understand the environment and generalize to unseen situations. The paper is motivated by the potential of deep generative models to synthesize unseen regions, thereby providing additional context. The goal is to leverage novel-view synthesis to dynamically generate informative visual data from adaptively selected viewpoints, thereby enriching the robot's perception.

2. **Mitigating the Impact of Imperfect Synthesized Images:** While generative models can produce novel content, the synthesized images are not always perfect and may contain visual artifacts or geometric distortions. Directly feeding these imperfect images into a policy network could degrade performance. A key objective is to develop a mechanism to manage these imperfections and extract reliable features for downstream policy learning.

3. **Efficient Integration of Multi-Modal Features for Hierarchical Policy Learning:** Robotic manipulation tasks, especially long-horizon ones, often benefit from hierarchical control, separating high-level skill selection from low-level action estimation. The motivation is to design a policy network that can effectively integrate visual features (from real and synthesized images) and language instructions in a hierarchical manner. Specifically, the aim is to ensure that relevant visual information (e.g., semantic content for meta-skill selection, finer details for precise action) is appropriately utilized.

4. **Improving Computational Efficiency for Practical Application:** Novel-view synthesis can be computationally intensive, making it impractical to perform at every timestep in real-time robotic control. The authors are motivated to introduce practical mechanisms that optimize the computational cost without significantly compromising policy performance, thereby enhancing the method's applicability on real robotic platforms.

In summary, the work seeks to develop a robust and generalizable robotic policy that uses intelligently generated visual context to overcome limitations of partial observations and imperfect generative models, all while maintaining computational feasibility for practical deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

NVSPolicy integrates adaptive novel-view synthesis with a hierarchical policy network, incorporating mechanisms for disentangling visual features and ensuring efficient inference. The system operates on inputs including a language instruction (`l`), an eye-in-hand camera image (`I_in-hand`), and a fixed eye-to-hand camera image (`I_to-hand`), producing a 7-DoF action vector (`a`).

The methodology consists of four main components:

**A. Adaptive Novel-view Synthesis**
To provide context-enhanced observations, NVSPolicy leverages a pre-trained generative model, GenWarp, to synthesize novel-view images (`I_synthesis`). GenWarp is selected for its ability to warp visible content and hallucinate unseen regions from a single input image, preserving semantic structure under viewpoint shifts.
- **Viewpoint Selection Strategy:** Recognizing that the quality of synthesized images is sensitive to viewpoint changes, an adaptive strategy is employed. A local spherical coordinate system is established with its origin at the primary ray of the camera. The distance from the camera to the origin (`d_cam-ori`) is estimated as the average depth of the input image. The angular offset (`θ`) for the novel viewpoint is determined dynamically based on `d_cam-ori` using the formula `θ = -w1 * d_cam-ori + w2`, where `w1` and `w2` are hyperparameters. A small random perturbation is added to the sampled viewpoint. This approach aims to maximize information gain while maintaining fidelity in the synthesized view.

**B. Disentangled Latent Representation**
To mitigate the impact of imperfections (geometric distortions, unrealistic appearances) in the synthesized images, a cycle-consistent Variational Autoencoder (VAE) mechanism is used to disentangle visual features into a semantic feature (`s`) and a remaining feature (`r`).
- **Training Protocol:** The VAE is trained on a dataset of image pairs (`Ψ`) captured from the same scene but different viewpoints, with added random local geometric distortions and appearance changes to simulate synthesized image imperfections.
 - **Forward Process:** For an image pair `(I1, I2)`, a VAE encoder `fϕ` extracts `(s1, r1)` and `(s2, r2)`. A VAE decoder `fω` reconstructs images (`Î1`, `Î2`) by exchanging semantic latent variables while retaining original remaining latent variables: `Î1 = fω(s2, r1)` and `Î2 = fω(s1, r2)`. The forward loss (`L_forward`) combines reconstruction errors (expected log likelihood) and KL divergence terms to regularize `s` and `r` towards standard Gaussian priors.
 - **Reverse Process:** Two images are randomly sampled from `Ψ` (without scene consistency). Their remaining latent variables (`r1`, `r2`) are extracted. A semantic latent variable `s*` is sampled from a standard Gaussian. The decoder `fω` reconstructs images `Î1 = fω(s*, r1)` and `Î2 = fω(s*, r2)`. These reconstructed images are re-encoded by `fϕ` to obtain `ŝ1` and `ŝ2`. The reverse loss (`L_reverse`) enforces consistency: `E_s*~p(s) (||ŝ1 - ŝ2||1)`, encouraging `ŝ1` and `ŝ2` to be identical due to sharing the same `s*`.
- **Inference:** Once trained, the `fϕ` encoder disentangles visual features from input images into semantic and remaining components for the policy network.

**C. Hierarchical Policy Network**
The disentangled latent representations are utilized within a hierarchical policy network.
- **Input Features:** The network takes semantic (`s_to-hand`, `s_in-hand`, `s_synthesis`) and remaining (`r_to-hand`, `r_in-hand`, `r_synthesis`) features extracted from the three camera views, along with a language instruction embedding (`z_language`) generated by a MiniLM model. A rolling buffer of semantic features from previous timesteps (length `T=15`) is also used as a temporal state.
- **Meta-skill Selector:** A meta-skill selector `qξ(o|·)` aggregates the semantic features (`s_to-hand`, `s_in-hand`, `s_synthesis`), the language feature (`z_language`), and the temporal state. It outputs a categorical distribution (`o`) over predefined meta-skills (e.g., rotation, transportation, grasping). Rule-based labeling from prior work is used to acquire meta-skill ground truth for training.
- **Action Estimator:** Given the selected optimal meta-skill (`o_optimal`), an action estimator `fα` generates the robot's 7-DoF action. It takes `o_optimal`, remaining features (`r_to-hand`, `r_in-hand`), and `z_language` as input. `r_synthesis` is excluded due to its potentially lower reliability. The action estimator is a two-layer Transformer with eight attention heads.
- **Loss Function (`L_hierarchical`):** Training involves imitation learning for action estimation (KL divergence between demonstrated and predicted actions), a cross-entropy loss for meta-skill prediction (supervised by rule-based labels), and a regularization term (KL divergence) to encourage balanced skill selection by `qξ(o|·)` towards a uniform prior.

**D. Practical Considerations**
Two mechanisms are introduced to enhance efficiency and robustness:
- **Keyframe Selection:** To reduce the computational cost of novel-view synthesis, it is not performed at every timestep. Keyframes are selected when the viewpoint change from the previous keyframe exceeds a predefined threshold. For non-keyframes, the synthesized image is replaced with a blank placeholder.
- **Policy Distillation:** A student model is introduced for efficient inference. This student model does not explicitly generate novel-view images. Instead, it learns to estimate the semantic feature of the novel-view image (`s_synthesis`) directly from the input views, distilling knowledge from the teacher model (NVSPolicy with full NVS). This is optimized via a KL divergence loss (`L_PD`) between the student's predicted semantic feature (`s_student`) and the teacher's extracted semantic feature (`s_teacher`). This allows for semantic estimation even on non-keyframes, promoting smoother policy behavior.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of NVSPolicy was evaluated on the CALVIN benchmark, a dataset designed for language-conditioned, long-horizon robot manipulation tasks.

**A. Evaluation Metrics**
Two metrics were used:
1. **Task Success Rates:** Measured for sequences of up to five consecutive subtasks.
2. **Average Successful Sequence Length:** The mean number of consecutive successful tasks completed.

**B. Comparison to Recent Methods**
NVSPolicy was compared against several state-of-the-art methods that do not involve pre-trained large-scale language or vision-language foundation models: GCBC, MCIL, HULC, SPIL, and LCD.

- **Quantitative Results:** NVSPolicy demonstrated performance improvements across all metrics on long-horizon tasks. For instance, the success rate for completing three consecutive tasks increased from 51.5% (LCD) to 56.3%. The average successful sequence length was 2.93, compared to 2.88 for LCD, indicating greater robustness in executing multi-step tasks.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visual comparisons in challenging scenarios, including scenes with occlusion, multiple similar objects, and long-horizon tasks, showed NVSPolicy accomplishing manipulation tasks where other methods exhibited failures.

**C. Ablation Studies**
Ablation studies were conducted to assess the contribution of key components:

- **Novel-view Synthesis (w/o NS):** Replacing the synthesized novel-view image with an all-zero placeholder resulted in a noticeable performance degradation, particularly for long-horizon tasks. This indicates the contribution of novel-view synthesis in providing additional context.
- **Disentangled Feature Representation (w/o FD):** Removing the feature disentanglement module and using a simple convolutional encoder to extract visual features directly led to a reduction in the average successful sequence length from 2.93 to 2.74. This suggests the utility of disentangled features for hierarchical policy learning.
- **Policy Distillation (w/o PD):** Removing the policy distillation process and reverting to GenWarp for novel-view image generation at every instance resulted in a slight performance reduction. This indicates that policy distillation not only contributes to computational efficiency but also to policy robustness.
- **Adaptive Viewpoint Selection:** Comparing the adaptive viewpoint selection strategy with several fixed angular offsets (0°, 10°, 20°, 30°, 40°) showed that the adaptive strategy consistently achieved higher success rates and average horizon lengths across all task horizons. This demonstrates its effectiveness in balancing information gain and synthesis reliability.

**D. Performance on Real-world Robotic Platform**
NVSPolicy was evaluated on a real-world robotic platform across five representative tasks and compared against SPIL. Both methods used an adaptation module to reduce the sim-to-real gap.

- **Quantitative Results:** NVSPolicy achieved an average success rate of 44% across the real-world tasks, compared to 8% for SPIL. Specific task success rates for NVSPolicy ranged from 20% ("push the pink block right") to 60% ("close the drawer"). This suggests a degree of sim-to-real robustness.
- **Qualitative Results:** Illustrations of NVSPolicy performing tasks on the real robot platform indicated satisfactory performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The NVSPolicy framework presents a method for enhancing generalization in language-conditioned robotic manipulation. The work integrates several components to address known challenges in this field.

The technical contributions include:
- **Adaptive Novel-View Synthesis:** The system dynamically selects informative viewpoints and synthesizes new visual information, which can provide additional context beyond the robot's immediate camera views. This addresses the limitation of partial observations.
- **Disentangled Latent Representation:** The use of a cycle-consistent VAE mechanism to separate semantic features from remaining features offers a strategy to process potentially imperfect synthesized images. This disentanglement facilitates hierarchical policy learning by providing distinct feature types for high-level skill selection and low-level action estimation.
- **Hierarchical Policy Network Design:** The architecture aligns the disentangled features with a hierarchical control structure, which is generally suitable for complex, long-horizon manipulation tasks.
- **Practical Considerations for Efficiency:** The introduction of keyframe selection and policy distillation mechanisms aims to improve the computational efficiency of the system, making it more feasible for real-world deployment on robotic platforms by reducing the overhead of generative models during inference.

The experimental results demonstrate that NVSPolicy achieves performance improvements on a challenging benchmark (CALVIN) for long-horizon, language-conditioned tasks, compared to several contemporary methods. The successful application on a real-world robotic platform further suggests its practical applicability and sim-to-real robustness.

The potential impact of this research lies in its capacity to facilitate the development of more adaptable and generalizable robotic systems. By improving a robot's ability to understand its environment through enhanced visual context and to process complex instructions, NVSPolicy could contribute to expanding the range of tasks robots can perform autonomously in less structured and more dynamic environments. The efficiency considerations also contribute to bridging the gap between research prototypes and deployable robotic solutions.
