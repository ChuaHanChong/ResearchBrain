# Seeing Across Views: Benchmarking Spatial Reasoning of Vision-Language Models in Robotic Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from multiple universities and Microsoft Research Asia introduced MV-RoboBench, a benchmark designed to evaluate multi-view spatial reasoning in Vision-Language Models for robotic manipulation. The benchmark revealed that even state-of-the-art models perform significantly below human accuracy (91.04% vs. 56.41% for GPT-5), highlighting challenges in integrating multi-view information for embodied tasks.

## Problem

- Existing VLM evaluations primarily focus on single-view perception and general spatial understanding, neglecting their ability to integrate multi-view information.
- There is an absence of benchmarks that combine multi-view spatial reasoning with embodied robotic execution tasks.
- Current VLM evaluations often lack direct connection to the action-oriented requirements of robotics, failing to assess 3D structure interpretation and cross-view consistency.

## Method

- Introduced **MV-RoboBench**, a benchmark with 1,708 manually curated ==multiple-choice QA== items derived from real robotic demonstrations.
- The benchmark is structured into eight subtasks covering multi-view spatial understanding (e.g., 3D Spatial Consistency, Cross-View Object Matching) and robotic execution (e.g., Action Planning, Affordance Recognition).
- Utilizes a human-in-the-loop pipeline for data collection, QA generation, and quality review, incorporating synchronized multi-view image pairs from robotic datasets.
- Investigated Chain-of-Thought (CoT) inspired enhancements including textual, visual (synthetic views), and structural (depth priors) augmentations to aid reasoning.

## Results

- The best performing VLM, GPT-5, achieved **56.41%** accuracy on MV-RoboBench, significantly below the human baseline of **91.04%**.
- CoT-style augmentations yielded mixed results; synthetic novel views often degraded performance, while depth priors were only beneficial for models with sufficient capacity.
- A strong positive correlation exists between spatial reasoning and robotic execution within multi-view scenarios, particularly for proprietary reasoning models.
- Models like GPT-5 showed significant performance drops (e.g., -**18.73%** overall) when confronted with vertically flipped image orientations, indicating reliance on canonical viewpoints.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate a substantial performance gap compared to humans in multi-view robotic reasoning.
> - Multi-view inputs are critical for resolving spatial ambiguity in robotic scenes, but their benefits are positively correlated with the model's overall capability.
> - Strong performance on existing single-view spatial understanding benchmarks does not reliably transfer to multi-view embodied robotic tasks, indicating unique challenges in cross-view integration.
> - VLMs exhibit an 'Over-Compliance' phenomenon, struggling to reject plausible but incorrect options and relying on canonical image orientations for spatial reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have emerged as foundational components for Embodied AI and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling robots to interpret complex environments and execute physical actions. Existing evaluations of VLMs primarily focus on single-view perception and general-purpose spatial understanding tasks, often lacking a direct connection to the embodied, action-oriented requirements of robotics. While multi-camera systems are becoming standard in robotic platforms to mitigate issues like occlusion and depth ambiguity, the capacity of VLMs to effectively integrate information from these multiple views for robotic reasoning remains an underexplored area.

Prior benchmarks, such as EmbSpatial-Bench (Du et al., 2024), Visual Spatial (Liu et al., 2023a), RoboSpatial (Song et al., 2025a), Spatial-MM (Shiri et al., 2024), and SpatialVLM (Chen et al., 2024), predominantly assess single-view spatial relations or abstract reasoning. Some datasets, like ERQA (Team et al., 2025a) and MMSI-Bench (Yang et al., 2025c), include limited multi-view data but often focus on basic perception rather than embodied reasoning for manipulation. ShareRobot (Ji et al., 2025) extends evaluation to embodied robotic tasks but without multi-view perception. Benchmarks like All-Angles Bench (Yeh et al., 2025) and Ego3D-Bench (Gholami et al., 2025) incorporate multi-view inputs but concentrate on photographic alignment or navigation, not manipulation. OmniSpatial (Jia et al., 2025) offers broad spatial evaluation but remains single-view.

This work addresses the gap by introducing a benchmark designed specifically for multi-view spatial reasoning in robotic manipulation scenarios, thereby combining embodiment with multi-view perception. It aims to evaluate how VLMs leverage complementary multi-view information to support decision-making for robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to evaluate the multi-view spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) within the context of robotic manipulation. The authors motivate this objective by highlighting several critical needs:

1. **Bridging the Gap in VLM Evaluation:** Current VLM evaluations predominantly focus on single-view settings, leaving the ability of these models to integrate multi-view information largely unexplored. As multi-camera setups are increasingly common in robotic platforms, providing complementary perspectives to overcome occlusion and depth ambiguity, it is crucial to understand if VLMs can effectively leverage such inputs.
2. **Addressing Embodied Spatial Intelligence:** Unlike abstract 2D tasks, robotic operation in physical environments inherently demands robust spatial intelligence, including the interpretation of 3D structures, reasoning about geometric relationships, and maintaining consistency across viewpoints. Single-view inputs are inherently limited in this regard.
3. **Developing a Comprehensive Benchmark:** There is an identified absence of benchmarks that combine multi-view spatial reasoning with robotic execution tasks. Existing benchmarks either focus on single-view data, general spatial intelligence, or embodied tasks without multi-view components.
4. **Assessing Performance Against Human Baseline:** The research aims to quantify the performance of state-of-the-art VLMs on multi-view robotic perception and reasoning, comparing it against human performance to establish the current capabilities and identify remaining challenges.
5. **Investigating Correlations and Transferability:** The study seeks to determine: (i) whether improved spatial intelligence positively correlates with better robotic task execution in multi-view scenarios, and (ii) if strong performance on existing general-purpose single-view spatial understanding benchmarks reliably translates to success in multi-view robotic tasks.

To achieve these objectives, the authors introduce **MV-RoboBench**, a benchmark specifically designed to provide a standardized evaluation protocol and data for multi-view embodied reasoning. The key contributions include:
- Establishing the first benchmark integrating spatial and robotic reasoning with synchronized multi-view inputs in robotic manipulation scenarios.
- Evaluating a diverse set of open-source and closed-source VLMs, including Chain-of-Thought (CoT) enhanced versions, demonstrating that current state-of-the-art models perform significantly below human levels.
- Conducting a correlation analysis that shows a positive correlation between spatial intelligence and robotic execution in multi-view scenarios, but also reveals that strong single-view spatial understanding does not reliably transfer to multi-view robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces **MV-RoboBench**, a benchmark comprising 1,708 manually curated multiple-choice QA items derived from real robotic demonstrations. It is designed to evaluate multi-view spatial reasoning capabilities of VLMs in robotic manipulation scenarios.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Structure

MV-RoboBench is divided into two primary categories:
- **Spatial Understanding:** Focuses on perception and reasoning across multiple camera views to build a coherent 3D scene representation. It includes four subtasks:
 - **Cross-View Object Matching:** Identifying the same object across different camera viewpoints, using a reference view with a highlighted object and distractors in other views.
 - **Distance Judgement:** Reasoning about relative distances to a specified gripper, requiring multi-view integration to resolve depth ambiguities.
 - **Viewpoint Identification:** Recognizing and reasoning about different viewpoints, specifically identifying a gripper camera's view given a head camera observation. Distractors are designed with varying temporal and episodic gaps.
 - **3D Spatial Consistency:** Reasoning about object locations within a structured 3D coordinate system (a 5x5x5 cubic grid), assessing consistency across views. A right-handed orthogonal coordinate system is defined relative to the camera frame and gravity vector.
- **Robotic Execution:** Extends spatial reasoning to embodied decision-making for manipulation tasks. It also includes four subtasks:
 - **Action Planning:** Selecting a multi-step sequence of normalized directional actions (e.g., leftward, forward, downward) to achieve a manipulation goal with minimal collisions.
 - **Step Execution:** Focusing on primitive, single-step actions (e.g., picking, placing), expressed as short sequences of directional terms in a defined coordinate system.
 - **Trajectory Selection:** Identifying the most feasible motion trajectory among candidates, overlaid on reference views, by integrating cross-view evidence to account for occlusions and perspective distortions.
 - **Affordance Recognition:** Identifying the most likely successful grasp candidate among visually plausible alternatives, utilizing multi-view observations to overcome occlusions and reveal critical contact geometry.

#### 3.2. Benchmark Construction Pipeline (Figure 2)

The benchmark construction follows a three-stage, human-in-the-loop pipeline:
1. **Data Collection:** Synchronized multi-view image pairs from AgiWorld (Bu et al., 2025) and BridgeV2 (Walke et al., 2023) datasets are initially filtered using rule-based criteria for temporal separation, scene diversity, and visual clarity. GPT-4.1 assists as an auxiliary filter to check if pairs satisfy task definitions (without generating content), followed by manual human validation for clarity and genuine multi-view reasoning requirements. A custom Qt-based annotation tool (Figures 11, 13 in Appendix E) was developed to facilitate multi-view image pairing and annotation.
2. **QA Generation:** Trained annotators create five-choice QA pairs for each subtask using task-specific templates. Distractors are designed to be plausible yet distinguishable from the correct option, preventing ambiguity. A standardized right-handed coordinate system (Appendix E.3) is used for consistency in spatial and action descriptions.
3. **Human-in-the-loop Quality Review:** Iterative review by trained annotators ensures quality. Items not aligned with benchmark objectives are discarded or revised. Content errors are manually corrected, while minor grammar/structural issues are refined with GPT-4.1. Accepted items are balanced for randomized answer distribution to prevent bias.

#### 3.3. CoT-inspired Enhancements

To explore enhanced reasoning, three Chain-of-Thought (CoT)-style extensions are investigated:
1. **Textual CoT (Descriptive Augmentation):** GPT-4.1 generates holistic scene descriptions from multi-view images, verbalizing spatial context that is prepended to the user prompt.
2. **Visual CoT (Synthetic View Augmentation):** Novel view synthesis methods are used to generate intermediate viewpoints, providing extra visual evidence for cross-view alignment. VGGT (Wang et al., 2025a) is adopted as the synthesis baseline, after evaluating its robustness over other methods in cluttered robotic scenes (Appendix C.3).
3. **Structural CoT (Depth Prior Augmentation):** MoGe-2 (Wang et al., 2025b) is used to generate per-pixel depth maps for each view. This depth information is normalized and smoothed, then appended as textual input (e.g., "red areas indicate closer objects") to provide explicit geometric constraints (Appendix C.4).

#### 3.4. Correlation Analysis

The study defines two axes for analysis:
- **Internal Correlation Axis:** Examines the relationship between spatial reasoning and robotic execution within multi-view manipulation tasks.
- **External Generalization Axis:** Assesses whether spatial intelligence measured by existing single-view benchmarks (e.g., OmniSpatial) transfers to embodied multi-view tasks.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Setup

A broad spectrum of VLMs is evaluated in a zero-shot, multiple-choice setting using answer accuracy. Models are categorized into:
- **Blind Evaluation:** Text-only LLMs without visual grounding (e.g., Random, GPT-3.5-turbo, GPT-4-turbo).
- **Proprietary Models:** Multimodal systems from major providers (e.g., GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, Claude-3.5/3.7, Gemini-2.x flash).
- **Proprietary Reasoning Models:** Architectures optimized for multi-step reasoning (e.g., o4-mini, GPT-5 family, Claude-3.7-think, Gemini-2.5-pro).
- **Open-Source Models:** Community-developed VLMs (e.g., Gemma-3 family, InternVL3 series, Qwen2.5-vl series).
- **Open-Source MoE Models:** Mixture-of-Experts models (e.g., Llama-4-Scout, Llama-4-Maverick).
Human participants with computer science backgrounds also completed the benchmark to provide a reference performance baseline (Appendix B.5).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Overall Model Performance on MV-RoboBench (Table 2, Figure 4)

- **Significant Gap to Human Performance:** All evaluated models, including state-of-the-art proprietary reasoning systems, remain substantially below human accuracy (91.04%). GPT-5 achieves the highest VLM score at 56.41%.
- **Proprietary Reasoning Models Lead:** Models optimized for multi-step reasoning (e.g., GPT-5, Gemini-2.5-pro, o4-mini) achieve the highest scores (46.47% to 56.41%), significantly outperforming general proprietary multimodal models (20.85% to 30.90%) and open-source models (18.93% to 26.11%).
- **Challenging Subtasks:** 3D Spatial Consistency proves particularly difficult, with many non-reasoning models performing near or below random-choice accuracy (19.07%), indicating an inability to leverage multi-view information for spatial integration. Robotic subtasks (Action Planning, Step Execution, Trajectory Selection, Affordance Recognition) also show substantial improvements with reasoning-based architectures.

#### 4.2. Necessity of Multi-View Inputs (Appendix G, Table 7)

- An ablation study comparing multi-view versus single-view performance on selected tasks (Distance Judgement, 3D Spatial Consistency, Action Planning, Step Execution) demonstrates that multi-view inputs are critical for resolving spatial ambiguity.
- GPT-5 shows substantial gains (e.g., +18.90% in Distance Judgement) with multi-view inputs, confirming their role in mitigating depth ambiguity and occlusion.
- The benefit from multi-view information positively correlates with model capability; smaller models show negligible or even negative changes, suggesting they may be overwhelmed by increased visual context without sufficient reasoning capacity.

#### 4.3. Evaluation of CoT-inspired Enhancements (Table 3)

- CoT-style augmentations yield mixed and non-uniform effects across models.
- For Qwen2.5-vl-7B, auxiliary cues offer negligible or negative changes, except for a slight gain from depth priors.
- Gemma-3-12B benefits substantially from CoT prompting.
- GPT-4.1 gains most noticeably from depth priors, with textual augmentation providing marginal improvements.
- Overall, synthetic novel views often degrade performance, depth priors are beneficial only for models with sufficient capacity to exploit geometric cues, and CoT is most effective for mid-capacity open-source models.

#### 4.4. Internal Correlation: Spatial vs. Robotic Intelligence (Figure 5)

- A positive correlation exists between spatial and robotic accuracy in multi-view manipulation tasks, but this relationship is model-dependent.
- Proprietary and reasoning-optimized systems exhibit a monotonic trend where improved spatial perception leads to gains in robotic execution.
- Most open-source VLMs cluster near random-choice accuracy, indicating that without explicit multi-view fusion, perception does not translate into actionable understanding.

#### 4.5. External Transferability: Single-View to Multi-View (Figure 6, Table 4)

- Strong performance on general single-view benchmarks (e.g., OmniSpatial) does not reliably transfer to multi-view embodied reasoning tasks.
- Many models performing well on OmniSpatial remain close to random on MV-RoboBench. Even for high-performing reasoning models, single-view competence only partially translates, with multi-view accuracy lagging.
- This suggests that multi-view robotic reasoning introduces unique demands (viewpoint integration, occlusion resolution, spatial fusion) not addressed by existing single-view benchmarks.

#### 4.6. Impact of Image Orientation (Appendix H, Table 8)

- Stress testing with vertically flipped camera views (except gripper-mounted) reveals that strong models like GPT-5 suffer significant performance drops (e.g., -18.73% overall, -55.88% in 3D Spatial Consistency). This indicates that their spatial reasoning relies heavily on canonical "upright" orientations.
- Weaker models show negligible changes, suggesting their performance is already dominated by pattern matching or random guessing.

#### 4.7. Evaluation of Model Rejection Capability (Appendix I, Table 9)

- An experiment where the correct answer was "None of the above" across 7 subtasks revealed a severe "Over-Compliance" phenomenon.
- GPT-5's accuracy drops from 56% to 13%, and most other models collapse to near 0%. This indicates a strong tendency to select a "visually approximate" option rather than acknowledging the absence of a correct answer, highlighting a limitation in absolute correctness verification.

#### 4.8. Error Analysis (Appendix J)

Qualitative error analysis on representative models (GPT-5, Qwen2.5-VL-72B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B) reveals recurring failure modes:
- **Single-view bias and weak multi-view fusion:** Over-reliance on a single view, ignoring contradictory evidence from others.
- **Depth, occlusion, and 3D geometry confusion:** Frequent substitution of 2D heuristics for true 3D reasoning, leading to incorrect estimations of distance and spatial relationships.
- **Frame-of-reference errors and instance-level correspondence:** Difficulty in handling camera-centric coordinate systems and tracking specific object instances across views.
- **Affordance and physical-feasibility misunderstandings:** Inability to robustly model robotic affordances and physical constraints, often preferring visually "neat" but physically unstable or collision-prone actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces MV-RoboBench, a benchmark designed to evaluate multi-view spatial reasoning in robotic manipulation scenarios. This addresses a significant gap in current VLM evaluation, which predominantly focuses on single-view or abstract reasoning tasks. The benchmark's grounding in real robotic demonstrations and its comprehensive suite of eight subtasks, divided into spatial understanding and robotic execution, provides a realistic and rigorous testbed for embodied multi-view intelligence.

The findings demonstrate that state-of-the-art VLMs, including advanced proprietary reasoning models, perform substantially below human levels in multi-view robotic reasoning. This highlights that current models, despite their progress in general visual-linguistic competence, still possess fundamental limitations in integrated multi-view fusion, robust 3D geometric understanding, frame-of-reference handling, and grounded physical affordance reasoning. The specific identification of these failure modes—such as single-view bias, 3D spatial consistency challenges, and over-compliance in rejecting incorrect options—serves as a diagnostic tool for the community.

The study's correlation analysis indicates a positive relationship between spatial reasoning and robotic execution in multi-view settings, affirming the importance of spatial intelligence for embodied AI. However, the finding that strong performance on single-view spatial benchmarks does not reliably transfer to multi-view robotic tasks underscores the unique demands of multi-view perception and the need for specialized evaluation.

The exploration of CoT-inspired enhancements yielding mixed results further suggests that generic prompting strategies alone may be insufficient. Future progress may require tighter coupling between explicit geometric understanding, robust 3D scene representations, and structured reasoning mechanisms, rather than relying solely on superficial prompt-level augmentations.

MV-RoboBench is positioned to serve as an open resource, providing data and a standardized evaluation protocol. Its release is intended to foster progress in spatially grounded VLMs and VLAs, guiding the development of architectures and training pipelines that can explicitly encode geometric priors, enforce cross-view consistency, and align perception with action grounding in complex, multi-camera robotic environments. This work exposes bottlenecks that, if addressed, could lead to more robust and capable embodied AI systems.
