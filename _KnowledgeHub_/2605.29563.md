# Planning with the Views via Scene Self-Exploration

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces VI E W SU I T E, a new benchmark for 6-DoF multi-turn view planning in real 3D scenes, revealing a "planning gap" where frontier Vision-Language Models struggle to compose single-step view-action understanding into multi-turn plans. An iterative self-exploration and view graph distillation framework is proposed, which improves a VLM's interactive view planning success rate from 2.5% to 47.8%, surpassing leading proprietary models.

## Problem

- Current visual reasoning benchmarks treat VLMs as passive observers, lacking interactive control for active view planning in 3D environments.
- Existing embodied navigation benchmarks focus on physical affordances and arrival, not isolating 6-DoF viewpoint control and multi-turn view composition.
- Frontier Vision-Language Models exhibit a significant "planning gap" in multi-step view planning in complex 3D environments, struggling to compose single-step view-action knowledge into multi-turn plans.

## Method

- Introduces VI E W SU I T E, a benchmark featuring 6-DoF viewpoint control in real ScanNet 3D scenes, and three diagnostic tasks: Path-to-View, View-to-Path, and Interactive View Planning.
- Proposes an iterative training framework that alternates between self-exploration using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and "view graph distillation."
- All self-generated exploration trajectories, including failed ones, are converted into a view graph, from which paths are sampled and reformulated into dense supervised training instances.

## Results

- Frontier VLMs achieved approximately **50%** accuracy on single-turn tasks (P2V/V2P) but only below **10%** average success on multi-turn Interactive View Planning (IVP), with the best proprietary model reaching **21.4%**.
- The proposed iterative framework improved the Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct model's IVP success rate from a baseline of **2.5%** to **47.8%**, outperforming all evaluated frontier models, including GPT-5.4 Pro (**18.5%**) and Gemini 3.1 Pro (**21.4%**).
- The trained model developed a more effective two-phase exploration strategy and demonstrated transferable spatial priors, leading to 8-12 percentage point gains on P2V/V2P and ~10 percentage point gains on the external MindCube benchmark.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Frontier VLMs exhibit a "planning gap," performing reasonably on single-turn view-action understanding but significantly worse on multi-turn planning, particularly for 3D translation.
> - Direct reinforcement learning with sparse rewards is ineffective for teaching multi-turn view planning to VLMs.
> - Distilling knowledge from all self-generated trajectories, even unsuccessful ones, through a view graph provides dense and effective supervision for view planning.
> - Interactive view planning, enabled by the iterative framework, fosters transferable spatial priors, improving VLM performance on related view-understanding tasks and external benchmarks.
