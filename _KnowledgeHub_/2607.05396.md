# From Fixed to Free Cameras: Calibration-Free View-Robust Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> CamVLA introduces a Vision-Language-Action model that achieves calibration-free, view-robust robot control by predicting actions in a camera-centric frame and simultaneously regressing camera geometry from visual input. This approach improved average success rates on unseen camera configurations by 18.2% in simulation and demonstrated robust generalization to uncalibrated camera repositioning in real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models are highly susceptible to camera viewpoint changes and typically require accurate, known camera extrinsics for deployment.
- This dependency on explicit camera calibration limits VLA model generalization and practical application in dynamic, unconstrained real-world robotic environments.
- Conventional VLA policies implicitly couple manipulation control with camera geometry, causing performance to degrade significantly with even minor camera shifts.

## Method

- **CamVLA** decomposes the policy into an ==Action Head==, which predicts end-effector actions in a ==camera-centric frame==, and a ==Geometric Head==, which regresses the ==6-DoF hand-eye matrix== directly from visual features.
- The model deterministically combines these camera-centric actions with the learned hand-eye matrix to produce base-frame actions, effectively decoupling manipulation control from camera geometry.
- During deployment, the model operates in a ==calibration-free== manner, relying solely on its self-predicted camera geometry without requiring external extrinsic information.

## Results

- CamVLA improved the average success rate on unseen camera configurations by **18.2%** in RLBench simulations when integrated with "pi_0", demonstrating enhanced zero-shot viewpoint generalization.
- In real-world tasks, the model showed robust performance against uncalibrated camera repositioning, maintaining success rates significantly higher than baselines under **15°** viewpoint offsets.
- The proposed architecture introduced minimal computational overhead, increasing parameters by **0.19%** and FLOPs by **0.15%**, ensuring efficient real-time deployment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Factorizing robot control into camera-centric actions and explicitly predicted camera-to-robot geometry allows policies to generalize across varied viewpoints.
> - By learning the hand-eye transformation directly from visual input, the model can infer camera positioning itself, eliminating the need for external calibration at inference time.
> - Predicting relative (delta) actions in the camera frame and transforming them using the learned rotation matrix minimizes the impact of potential errors in estimated hand-eye translation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represents a significant progression toward creating generalist robot policies capable of grounding broad semantic knowledge into executable manipulation tasks. These models leverage large-scale vision-language data and robotic demonstrations to infer actions from visual observations and natural language instructions.

However, a critical limitation of existing VLA models, even state-of-the-art architectures such as $\pi_0$ and GR00T N1.7, is their susceptibility to camera viewpoint shifts. Robots deployed in real-world environments often experience variations in camera positioning, such as repositioning, remounting, or continuous drift from mobile bases, which rarely match the controlled camera setups used during training. This spatial brittleness can lead to substantial performance degradation; for instance, a 15-degree camera rotation can significantly reduce a policy's success rate. While collecting large-scale multi-view training data could address this, it is often prohibitively expensive and difficult to scale.

Previous efforts to enhance viewpoint robustness in robotics have explored several avenues:
- **Explicit 3D Structure:** Some approaches, like Perceiver-Actor, incorporate 3D scene representations (e.g., voxelized RGB-D, point clouds, depth priors) to improve geometric consistency. However, these methods often introduce computational overhead and depend on depth sensing, multi-view observations, or pre-calibrated camera geometry.
- **Novel-View Synthesis and Augmentation:** Other works mitigate viewpoint shifts through generating novel views or applying image-level augmentations to training data.
- **View-Invariant Representations:** Some research focuses on learning representations or latent actions that are inherently invariant to viewpoint changes.
- **Camera-Aware Policy Conditioning:** A more direct line of recent work addresses viewpoint shifts by explicitly conditioning policies on camera information. This includes grounding actions in camera space, using ray embeddings derived from known camera parameters, back-projecting pixels into the robot frame using known intrinsics and extrinsics, or synthesizing canonical views based on relative camera poses.

The work presented in this paper directly addresses a shared limitation of most existing "view-robust" methods, particularly those in the camera-aware conditioning category: their reliance on *known and accurate camera extrinsics at deployment*. This assumption renders these methods fragile in the very scenarios that motivate view robustness—environments with uncalibrated, hand-held, or drifting cameras. This paper argues for a paradigm shift, proposing that the policy should infer camera geometry itself rather than requiring external calibration information. This self-localizing capability aims to close the deployment gap and enable more adaptable robotic systems in dynamic, unstructured settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a critical practical challenge in robot deployment: the assumption of a fixed, known camera setup. Real-world robotic applications frequently encounter scenarios where cameras are repositioned, remounted, or experience continuous pose drift. Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, despite their advancements in semantic understanding and task execution, exhibit a pronounced brittleness to these camera viewpoint variations. This vulnerability persists even in methods designed for "view-robustness," as they often predicate their functionality on the explicit provision of camera extrinsics during deployment. The requirement for known and accurate camera calibration renders these systems impractical and fragile in dynamic, unconstrained environments where such information is frequently unavailable or changes unexpectedly.

The authors identify a structural origin for this brittleness: standard VLA models rigidly predict actions in the robot base frame directly from camera-perspective visual observations. This inherent misalignment between the camera-centric input and the robot base-frame output forces the network to implicitly resolve the spatial transformation (hand-eye transformation) from the camera to the robot base. Without explicit geometric constraints, this transformation becomes a hidden variable, leading to a coupling of manipulation control with camera geometry. This entanglement causes policies to memorize view-dependent coordinate mappings, which readily collapse under even minor viewpoint shifts.

Inspired by human cognition, which factorizes egocentric (how to move) and allocentric (where I am looking from) spatial understanding, the key objective of this work is to introduce a novel VLA model named Camera-Centric VLA (CamVLA). The primary motivation is to overcome the aforementioned dependency on external camera calibration by enabling the policy to *figure out where the camera is by itself*.

Specifically, the detailed objectives are:
1. **Decouple Manipulation Control from Camera Geometry:** To develop a VLA architecture that disentangles the process of generating manipulation controls (pose-independent) from the spatial context of the camera's perspective (viewpoint-dependent).
2. **Achieve Calibration-Free Operation:** To eliminate the need for known and accurate camera extrinsics at deployment, making the policy robust to uncalibrated camera repositioning or remounting.
3. **Enable Depth-Free and Single-View Functionality:** To ensure that the proposed model operates solely on a single monocular RGB image as the visual observation, without requiring depth sensors, multi-view inputs, or complex 3D reconstruction techniques.
4. **Improve View-Robustness and Generalization:** To significantly enhance the success rates of VLA policies across diverse and unseen camera viewpoints in both simulated and real-world robotic tasks.

By fulfilling these objectives, the research aims to address a fundamental barrier to the widespread deployment of generalist robot policies in real-world, less-controlled settings.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CamVLA is designed to achieve viewpoint robustness by decomposing the policy into two distinct and parallel subproblems, whose outputs are then deterministically combined. This factorization aims to isolate viewpoint variations from the core visual-action mapping. The model receives a single monocular RGB image ($I_t$), a proprioceptive state ($s_t$), and a natural language goal ($L$) at each timestep $t$.

#### 3.1. Standard VLA Action Formulation (Context)

Conventional VLA models predict a base-frame delta action $\Delta A_{b,t} = [\Delta p_{b,t}, \Delta r_{b,t}, g_t]$, where $\Delta p_{b,t}$ and $\Delta r_{b,t}$ are translational and rotational displacements in the robot base frame, and $g_t$ is the gripper state. This formulation requires the network to implicitly learn a complex cross-frame mapping from camera-perspective visual observations to base-frame actions, where the hand-eye transformation ($T_t$) between camera and robot base frames is implicitly entangled within the network weights.

#### 3.2. Camera-Centric Action Generation (Action Head)

To circumvent the implicit entanglement of camera geometry in action prediction, CamVLA introduces an **Action Head** that predicts end-effector actions natively in the local camera coordinate frame. This is defined as the camera-centric delta action $\Delta A_{c,t} = [\Delta p_{c,t}, \Delta r_{c,t}, g_t]$, where $\Delta p_{c,t}$ and $\Delta r_{c,t}$ represent relative translation and axis-angle rotation expressed in the camera frame. The rationale is that by aligning visual observations and actions within the same egocentric reference frame, the visual flows become naturally consistent with physical movement (e.g., a leftward visual shift corresponds to a negative X-axis displacement in the camera frame). This consistent spatial relationship aims to prevent visual-action confusion and enhance generalization to unseen viewpoints.

#### 3.3. Camera-Perspective Geometric Grounding (Geometric Head)

Since robot arms operate based on kinematics defined in their base frame, the camera-centric action $\Delta A_{c,t}$ cannot be directly executed. To bridge this gap, CamVLA incorporates an auxiliary **Geometric Head**. This head is designed to regress the 6-DoF hand-eye matrix $T_t \in SE(3)$ (Special Euclidean group 3) directly from the visual features extracted by the network. The hand-eye matrix is parameterized by a translation vector $\tau_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$ and an axis-angle rotation vector $\omega_t \in \mathbb{R}^3$. By making $T_t$ an explicit network output, the model explicitly learns the relative spatial geometry, thereby isolating viewpoint-dependent variation. The overall network $f_\theta$ is thus redefined to jointly output $(\Delta A_{c,t}, T_t)$.

#### 3.4. Deterministic Geometric Transformation

The predictions from the Action Head and the Geometric Head are combined using a deterministic geometric transformation to produce the final robot base-frame delta action $\Delta A_{b,t}$. The relative translation $\Delta p_{c,t}$ and axis-angle rotation $\Delta r_{c,t}$ (free vectors) transform linearly under the predicted rotation matrix $R_t \in SO(3)$ (Special Orthogonal group 3), which is derived from $\omega_t$:
- $\Delta p_{b,t} = R_t \Delta p_{c,t}$
- $\Delta r_{b,t} = R_t \Delta r_{c,t}$

The full base-frame delta action is then $\Delta A_{b,t} = [\Delta p_{b,t}, \Delta r_{b,t}, g_t]$. A key aspect of this formulation is that the transformation of delta actions is independent of the translation vector $\tau_t$ of the hand-eye matrix. This implies that any test-time drift or error in the predicted $\tau_t$ has no physical impact on the execution of relative actions, confining viewpoint errors primarily to $R_t$. The translation $\tau_t$ is still regressed to enhance the geometric grounding of the visual backbone and to support potential future absolute-action variants.

#### 3.5. Implementation and Training Details

- **Backbone Integration:** CamVLA is instantiated on established VLA architectures, specifically $\pi_0$ and GR00T N1.7, leveraging their existing training recipes and configurations.
- **Visual Input:** Only single third-person monocular RGB images (resized to 224x224 pixels) are used, excluding wrist cameras to prevent reliance on unaffected views and simplify hardware.
- **Action Parameterization:** Predicted robot actions are 6-DoF end-effector poses relative to the current state, with rotation as a 3D axis-angle vector.
- **Geometric Head Architecture:** A lightweight three-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) with GELU activations and a hidden dimension of 1024. It processes high-level semantic features from the visual tokens of the backbone, aggregated via mean pooling.
- **Optimization:** The model is optimized end-to-end using a joint objective function $L = L_{act} + \lambda L_{ext}$. $L_{act}$ is the action prediction loss of the underlying baseline, and $L_{ext}$ is a mean squared error for the geometric grounding: $L_{ext} = \sum_t (\|\tau_t - \hat{\tau}_t\|_2^2 + \|\omega_t - \hat{\omega}_t\|_2^2)$, where $\hat{\tau}_t$ and $\hat{\omega}_t$ are ground-truth translation and axis-angle rotation vectors. A weight $\lambda = 0.1$ is used.
- **Calibration-Free Deployment:** Crucially, ground-truth hand-eye matrices are used *only* as supervision during training; at deployment, the model receives no external extrinsic information.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CamVLA was conducted in both simulation (RLBench) and real-world robot environments, comparing its performance against strong VLA baselines.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (RLBench)

- **Experimental Setup:** The model was trained on a discrete subset of camera viewpoints (0°, ±15°, ±30°..., ±90°) and evaluated for zero-shot generalization on unseen viewpoints sampled at 5° intervals. Six representative manipulation tasks were used.
- **Zero-Shot Viewpoint Generalization (Table 1):**
 - **$\pi_0$ Integration:** CamVLA, when integrated with $\pi_0$, improved the average success rate on unseen camera configurations from 33.2% to 51.4% (an absolute gain of 18.2%). For challenging tasks like "Slide Block" and "Lamp Off," success rates increased from 18.3% to 44.5% and 29.8% to 58.0%, respectively.
 - **GR00T N1.7 Integration:** Similarly, CamVLA enhanced GR00T N1.7's average success rate on unseen configurations by 10.0% (from 28.4% to 38.4%).
 - These results indicate that CamVLA consistently generalizes to novel viewpoints without requiring explicit retraining or calibration for each new view.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Experimental Setup:** A Franka Research 3 robot arm was used with third-person Intel RealSense D435i cameras. Training data was collected from five distinct camera perspectives. Evaluation involved three testing cameras, each rotated by 5°, 10°, and 15° horizontally from their default positions, simulating typical camera perturbations. Five common household manipulation tasks were performed.
- **Generalization to Repositioned Cameras (Table 2):**
 - **Canonical View (0°):** CamVLA achieved higher average success rates even under the canonical view compared to baselines (79.0% vs. 63.3% for $\pi_0$, and 80.7% vs. 64.7% for GR00T N1.7).
 - **Viewpoint Offsets:** Under an extreme 15° offset, baseline success rates substantially decreased (16.0% for $\pi_0$ and 14.7% for GR00T), whereas CamVLA maintained robust performance (29.3% and 33.0%, respectively). This demonstrates generalization to uncalibrated viewpoint shifts in physical environments.
- **Hand-Eye Matrix Estimation Errors (Table 3):** The Geometric Head maintained stable geometric grounding, with rotation errors remaining within a tolerable range (e.g., 9.39° at 15° offset), despite increasing translation errors at larger offsets. The relative-action formulation minimizes the impact of translation errors.

#### 4.3. Computational Efficiency (Table 4)

- CamVLA introduces minimal computational overhead. Compared to $\pi_0$, it adds 6.30 million parameters (0.19% increase), 1.0 G FLOPs (0.15% increase), and increases inference latency by only 1 ms (from 61 ms to 62 ms) on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. This efficiency supports real-time deployment.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (RLBench with $\pi_0$ baseline)

- **Density of Training Viewpoints (Table 5):** Denser training viewpoints (e.g., 15° intervals) led to better generalization. Even with sparse training (e.g., 45° intervals), CamVLA consistently outperformed the baseline. At 15° intervals, CamVLA's self-predicted extrinsics achieved performance close to using ground-truth extrinsics (51.4% vs. 52.3%, with a 1.41° rotation error), validating the accuracy of the Geometric Head.
- **Viewpoint Intervals (Table 6):** CamVLA exhibited a lower average relative performance drop (4.1% vs. 8.9% for $\pi_0$), indicating superior viewpoint invariance. In specific challenging intervals like [30°, 45°], CamVLA's drop was 0.9% compared to the baseline's 20.8%.
- **State and Action Representations (Table 7):**
 - Shifting the action output from the robot base frame to the camera frame provided a substantial performance gain (33.2% to 51.4%).
 - The use of self-predicted extrinsics resulted in a marginal performance decrease compared to ground-truth extrinsics (e.g., 0.9% difference).
 - Using base-frame proprioceptive state, despite a minor performance drop (0.3%), was a deliberate choice to achieve true calibration-free deployment by avoiding camera calibration requirements for state observation.
- **Extrinsic Noise Robustness (Supplementary Figure 5):** Experiments with artificially injected rotation noise demonstrated high robustness. CamVLA's performance decreased only moderately with small noise (e.g., 63.3% at 1° vs. 64.0% no-noise reference) and outperformed the noise-free baseline even with up to 12° of injected noise. This suggests that the policy can tolerate estimation errors from the Geometric Head.
- **Hand-Eye Pose Representation (Supplementary Table 8):** Predicting only the rotation component of the hand-eye matrix yielded comparable success rates (51.3% vs. 51.4% for full 6-DoF), confirming the derivation that delta action execution is independent of hand-eye translation. Predicting full 6-DoF enhances versatility.
- **Visual Feature Sources for Hand-Eye Prediction (Supplementary Table 9):** Features from the Image Encoder provided the most precise geometric localization and stable performance, while deeper VLM Backbone features, though slightly boosting task success, led to worse geometric estimation and stability, making them less suitable for real-world deployment. Detaching gradients consistently reduced both localization accuracy and control performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces CamVLA, a framework that addresses a fundamental limitation in the deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models: their brittleness to varying camera viewpoints and their reliance on known camera extrinsics. By developing a calibration-free, depth-free, and single-view approach, CamVLA offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Enhanced Real-World Deployability:** The primary impact is enabling VLA policies to operate robustly in unstructured and dynamic environments where camera positions are subject to change, drift, or are entirely uncalibrated. This removes a significant barrier to transferring robot learning from controlled laboratory settings to practical real-world applications such as industrial automation, logistics, or home robotics, where sensors may be repositioned or hand-held.
2. **Simplified System Integration:** By eliminating the need for explicit camera calibration at deployment, CamVLA reduces the setup complexity and maintenance burden for robotic systems. This can accelerate deployment times and lower operational costs.
3. **Novel Architectural Decoupling:** The proposed decoupling of camera-centric action generation from camera-perspective geometric grounding offers a biologically inspired and structurally sound approach to viewpoint robustness. This factorization isolates viewpoint-dependent variations within a dedicated geometric head, simplifying the core visual-action mapping task. This methodological insight could influence the design of future VLA models.
4. **Computational Efficiency:** The demonstrated minimal computational overhead ensures that CamVLA remains practical for real-time robotic control, making it suitable for deployment on existing hardware platforms without requiring significant upgrades.
5. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a baseline for self-localizing VLA policies. It opens avenues for further research into improving geometric regression robustness under more extreme viewpoint shifts, integrating wrist-mounted cameras in multi-camera systems without requiring their explicit calibration, and exploring the combination of learned geometry with occasional online calibration for improved long-term accuracy.

**Limitations:**
Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges certain limitations:
- The current model primarily focuses on viewpoint perturbations of a single third-person camera and does not yet account for wrist-mounted cameras in multi-camera systems, which are common in advanced robotic setups.
- While robust to typical camera shifts, CamVLA's performance can still degrade under extreme viewpoint changes. This is attributed to out-of-distribution visual features and increasing hand-eye regression errors at very large angular displacements, particularly for high-precision tasks.

Overall, CamVLA represents a step towards more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems by addressing the fundamental challenge of camera calibration in dynamic operational environments. By predicting camera geometry directly from visual input, it moves VLA policies closer to truly robust and flexible real-world manipulation.
