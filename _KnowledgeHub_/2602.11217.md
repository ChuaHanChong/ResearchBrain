# The Magic Correlations: Understanding Knowledge Transfer from Pretraining to Supervised Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Google DeepMind and EPFL systematically analyzed how accuracy and confidence transfer from pretraining to supervised fine-tuning in large language models using correlation protocols. Their findings reveal category-dependent transfer reliability and an inverse scaling dynamic where larger models exhibit stronger accuracy transfer but weaker confidence transfer, indicating significant calibration reorganization.

## Problem

- LLM development often relies on the untested assumption that pretraining performance reliably predicts post-fine-tuning performance.
- Decisions about data curation and resource allocation are frequently based on early-stage evaluations with smaller proxy models, which may lead to suboptimal outcomes if transfer is not stable.
- A lack of systematic understanding of how model capabilities, specifically accuracy and confidence, evolve and transfer across pretraining and fine-tuning stages.

## Method

- Trained ==decoder-only transformer models== (240M and 1B parameters) on 9 distinct pretraining data mixtures, varying proportions of general web, code, and curated knowledge.
- Fine-tuned all pretrained checkpoints on the ==Tulu-v2-mix instruction-following dataset== for 5 epochs.
- Evaluated models on **20** benchmarks across four capability categories (Commonsense, Scientific, NLI, Semantic) using both accuracy and confidence metrics.
- Employed ==five correlation protocols== to quantify the preservation and reorganization of model capabilities across training stages and data mixtures.

## Results

- Science and Commonsense benchmarks show high cross-stage accuracy correlation (mean r > **0.5**), indicating pretraining performance reliably predicts SFT outcomes, unlike NLI and Semantic tasks.
- **1B** models achieve stronger accuracy transfer (r = **0.60**) but substantially weaker confidence transfer (r = **0.39**) compared to **240M** models, which maintain higher confidence transfer (r = **0.68**).
- Persistent miscalibration is observed in Commonsense and Semantic tasks, exhibiting weak or negative accuracy-confidence alignment (r_align ≈ **-0.1** to **-0.2**) even after SFT.
- Benchmarks like WiC and MultiRC show weak cross-stage accuracy correlations (r < **0.3**), making them unreliable early-stage predictors of post-SFT success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Knowledge transfer from pretraining to supervised fine-tuning is not uniform; its reliability varies significantly by capability category and model scale.
> - Larger models exhibit stronger accuracy transfer from pretraining but weaker confidence transfer, suggesting extensive reorganization of calibration patterns during fine-tuning.
> - Pretraining data choices have lasting, scale-dependent effects on model calibration and task performance that supervised fine-tuning does not easily overwrite.
> - Specific benchmarks can be unreliable early-stage predictors of downstream SFT performance, highlighting the need for careful benchmark selection.
