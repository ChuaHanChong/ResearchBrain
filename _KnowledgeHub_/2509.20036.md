# MARG: MAstering Risky Gap Terrains for Legged Robots with Elevation Mapping

> [!summary] Summary
> MARG is a deep reinforcement learning controller that enables quadrupedal robots to traverse challenging risky gap terrains, such as narrow bridges and wide gaps, using only a single LiDAR sensor for terrain perception. The system achieves robust zero-shot transfer from simulation to physical Unitree Go1 and Go2 robots, demonstrating improved safety and stability compared to multi-sensor approaches.

## Problem

- Legged robots struggle to safely and efficiently traverse risky gap terrains like narrow bridges, balance beams, and wide gaps, where missteps can cause severe instability or falls.
- Existing perception-based deep reinforcement learning (DRL) controllers typically require complex, multi-sensor setups (e.g., multiple depth cameras or LiDARs), increasing hardware complexity, cost, and computational burden.
- Current LiDAR-based terrain mapping methods often suffer from height drift and computational inefficiency, hindering precise interaction with complex environments requiring accurate vertical position awareness.

## Method

- A Deep ==Reinforcement Learning== (DRL) controller, **MARG**, integrates proprioception and terrain map information via an asymmetric actor-critic architecture with specialized Elevation and Estimator networks.
- Introduces novel foot-related reward functions (Feet air time, Feet stumble, Feet center) to explicitly encourage safe foothold selection and dynamic stability on precarious terrains.
- Develops an efficient Terrain Map Generation (TMG) model that uses only a single LiDAR and contact-aware state estimation to provide accurate, drift-minimized, ego-centric elevation maps.

## Results

- MARG achieved superior learning performance and generalizability on risky gap terrains, outperforming other DRL algorithms while consuming significantly less GPU memory (approx. 12 GB vs. 15-45 GB) during training.
- The learned policy demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to physical Unitree Go1 and Go2 robots, enabling them to traverse large gaps (up to 65 cm, **1.6x** robot length), narrow bridges (18 cm wide), and complex balance beams in diverse real-world indoor and outdoor environments.
- The Terrain Map Generation (TMG) model provided highly accurate pose estimation with the lowest Absolute Pose Error (APE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE), and minimal height drift across various challenging real-world datasets, using only a single LiDAR sensor.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate, low-drift terrain mapping from a single LiDAR is critical for legged robots to master risky gap terrains and enables efficient, generalizable policy learning with reduced hardware complexity.
> - Specialized reward functions focusing on feet interaction, such as encouraging air time, preventing stumbling, and promoting central foothold placement, are crucial for training DRL policies that ensure safe and stable locomotion on complex terrains.
> - An asymmetric actor-critic DRL framework, combined with a concurrently trained estimator network that predicts unmeasurable states, effectively leverages privileged information during simulation for robust real-world policy execution.
