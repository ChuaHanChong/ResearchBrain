# MARG: MAstering Risky Gap Terrains for Legged Robots with Elevation Mapping

> [!summary] Summary
> MARG is a deep reinforcement learning controller that enables quadrupedal robots to traverse challenging risky gap terrains, such as narrow bridges and wide gaps, using only a single LiDAR sensor for terrain perception. The system achieves robust zero-shot transfer from simulation to physical Unitree Go1 and Go2 robots, demonstrating improved safety and stability compared to multi-sensor approaches.

## Problem

- Legged robots struggle to safely and efficiently traverse risky gap terrains like narrow bridges, balance beams, and wide gaps, where missteps can cause severe instability or falls.
- Existing perception-based deep reinforcement learning (DRL) controllers typically require complex, multi-sensor setups (e.g., multiple depth cameras or LiDARs), increasing hardware complexity, cost, and computational burden.
- Current LiDAR-based terrain mapping methods often suffer from height drift and computational inefficiency, hindering precise interaction with complex environments requiring accurate vertical position awareness.

## Method

- A Deep ==Reinforcement Learning== (DRL) controller, **MARG**, integrates proprioception and terrain map information via an asymmetric actor-critic architecture with specialized Elevation and Estimator networks.
- Introduces novel foot-related reward functions (Feet air time, Feet stumble, Feet center) to explicitly encourage safe foothold selection and dynamic stability on precarious terrains.
- Develops an efficient Terrain Map Generation (TMG) model that uses only a single LiDAR and contact-aware state estimation to provide accurate, drift-minimized, ego-centric elevation maps.

## Results

- MARG achieved superior learning performance and generalizability on risky gap terrains, outperforming other DRL algorithms while consuming significantly less GPU memory (approx. 12 GB vs. 15-45 GB) during training.
- The learned policy demonstrated successful zero-shot transfer to physical Unitree Go1 and Go2 robots, enabling them to traverse large gaps (up to 65 cm, **1.6x** robot length), narrow bridges (18 cm wide), and complex balance beams in diverse real-world indoor and outdoor environments.
- The Terrain Map Generation (TMG) model provided highly accurate pose estimation with the lowest Absolute Pose Error (APE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE), and minimal height drift across various challenging real-world datasets, using only a single LiDAR sensor.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurate, low-drift terrain mapping from a single LiDAR is critical for legged robots to master risky gap terrains and enables efficient, generalizable policy learning with reduced hardware complexity.
> - Specialized reward functions focusing on feet interaction, such as encouraging air time, preventing stumbling, and promoting central foothold placement, are crucial for training DRL policies that ensure safe and stable locomotion on complex terrains.
> - An asymmetric actor-critic DRL framework, combined with a concurrently trained estimator network that predicts unmeasurable states, effectively leverages privileged information during simulation for robust real-world policy execution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robotics has seen significant advancements in recent years, with quadrupedal robots demonstrating impressive capabilities in various locomotion tasks such as climbing, running, parkour, and even backflipping. These achievements largely stem from sophisticated control strategies, particularly those leveraging Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL). However, a persistent challenge remains in enabling these robots to safely and efficiently traverse highly complex and "risky gap terrains," characterized by narrow bridges, balance beams, and wide gaps where missteps can lead to severe instability or falls.

Historically, legged robot controllers have been categorized into model-based and model-free approaches. Model-based controllers, relying on precise dynamic models, excel in ideal conditions but struggle with real-world uncertainties like irregular terrain, varying friction, and external disturbances. The computational complexity of Nonlinear Model Predictive Control (NMPC) further limits their real-time applicability in dynamic environments. While some model-based methods incorporate multiple sensors to enhance accuracy, this significantly increases hardware complexity, data synchronization challenges, and computational load.

Model-free methods, especially DRL, have emerged as powerful alternatives, allowing robots to learn optimal policies through trial and error, thus inherently managing uncertainties without explicit dynamic models. Early DRL controllers, often termed "blind" controllers, achieved impressive locomotion on continuous challenging terrains but lacked environmental perception, rendering them ineffective in risky terrains requiring precise foothold selection.

The integration of exteroceptive sensors (e.g., cameras, LiDARs) with DRL has become a crucial area of research, enabling robots to "see" and adapt to their environment. However, existing perception-based DRL controllers face their own set of limitations:
1. **Sensor Dependency:** Many require complex multi-sensor deployments (e.g., multiple depth cameras or LiDARs) to achieve sufficient terrain mapping accuracy, increasing hardware complexity, cost, and computational requirements.
2. **Mapping Accuracy:** Traditional LiDAR-based localization methods often suffer from height drift, which can lead to inaccurate terrain maps, especially critical for tasks requiring precise vertical position awareness.
3. **Data Challenges:** Processing high-dimensional image or point cloud data for DRL can be computationally intensive, and issues like lighting changes, occlusion, and the need for ground-truth information (e.g., from motion capture systems) can hinder performance and practical applicability.

MARG directly addresses these limitations. It positions itself as an advancement in perception-aware DRL for legged robots by:
- **Simplifying Hardware:** Utilizing *only one LiDAR* for terrain mapping, drastically reducing the complexity and cost of hardware deployment compared to multi-sensor setups.
- **Improving Mapping Robustness:** Proposing a novel Terrain Map Generation (TMG) model that minimizes drift, particularly height drift, a common issue in LiDAR-based localization, and provides accurate robot-centered elevation maps.
- **Enhancing Policy Learning:** Integrating terrain maps with proprioception and selective privileged information within an asymmetric actor-critic DRL framework, enabling robust decision-making for risky terrains.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** Designing a system capable of zero-shot transfer from simulation to the real world, a major goal in robotics research.

By tackling these core challenges, MARG contributes significantly to bridging the gap between highly capable simulated robot locomotion and robust, practical deployment in complex, unstructured real-world environments, particularly those involving high-consequence risky terrains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind the MARG research stems from the inherent limitations of existing legged robot locomotion controllers when confronted with **risky gap terrains**. These terrains, exemplified by narrow single-plank bridges, balance beams with varying heights and inclinations, and large gaps, demand highly accurate perception, precise foothold selection, and dynamic balance capabilities. The paper highlights several specific shortcomings of current approaches:

- **Failure of Blind Controllers:** Traditional DRL controllers, which do not incorporate external sensory perception, are fundamentally incapable of navigating such terrains safely and efficiently, often leading to falls or collisions.
- **Complexity of Perception-Based Systems:** While some perception-enabled controllers exist, they typically rely on complex multi-sensor setups (e.g., multiple depth cameras and LiDARs). This increases hardware deployment complexity, system cost, and computational burden, making them less practical for widespread application.
- **Mapping Inaccuracies:** Even with sensors, existing localization technologies often suffer from "map drift," particularly in height estimation, which is detrimental for precise interaction with gap terrains. Furthermore, maintaining and updating maps can be computationally intensive.

Given these challenges, the overarching objective of this work is to develop a **safe, robust, and deployable Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) controller** that empowers quadrupedal robots to master risky gap terrains using a simplified sensing setup.

To achieve this, the specific key objectives are:
1. **Develop a DRL Controller (MARG):** Design a DRL-based locomotion controller that integrates terrain map information with robot proprioception to enable dynamic action adjustments and enhance stability specifically for risky gap terrains.
2. **Optimize Policy Learning with Privileged Information:** Accelerate the policy optimization process during training by selectively incorporating privileged information (e.g., center of mass, friction coefficients) that is available in simulation but not directly measurable in the real world. This leverages the benefits of simulation while maintaining real-world deployability.
3. **Encourage Safe Foothold Selection:** Design novel, foot-related reward functions that explicitly encourage the robot to explore and select safe footholds, thereby improving safety and preventing missteps on precarious terrains.
4. **Propose an Efficient and Accurate Terrain Map Generation (TMG) Model:** Create a new mapping model that utilizes *only one LiDAR* sensor to generate accurate, robot-centered height maps. This model aims to minimize mapping drift compared to traditional localization approaches and ensure computational efficiency, providing a robust foundation for zero-shot transfer of the learned policy to real robots.
5. **Validate Zero-Shot Transfer and Real-World Robustness:** Demonstrate that the learned MARG policy can be directly transferred to physical quadrupedal robots (Unitree Go1 and Go2) without additional fine-tuning, and effectively navigate a variety of challenging risky gap terrains in real-world indoor and outdoor environments.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to push the boundaries of legged robot autonomy, making them more capable and practical for navigating complex and hazardous environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MARG controller's methodology integrates Deep Reinforcement Learning with a novel, efficient terrain mapping system. The approach can be broken down into three main components: problem formulation and neural network design, specialized reward functions for footholds, and the Terrain Map Generation (TMG) model.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Neural Network Design

The locomotion task is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) due to the inaccessibility of certain "privileged information" in real-world deployments (e.g., true linear velocity, contact forces, friction coefficients).

- **Observation Space:** The robot's observations include:
 - **Proprioceptive observations ($e_o_t$):** Body angular velocity, projected gravity, linear velocity command, joint angles, joint angular velocities, and the previous action.
 - **Temporal observations ($e_o_H_t$):** A history of proprioceptive observations over the past H time steps.
 - **Exteroceptive observations ($h_t$):** An egocentric terrain map representing the height variations around the robot.
 - **Privileged state ($e_s_t$):** Ground-truth information available only in simulation, such as real linear velocity, foot contact boolean, critical link masses, body friction coefficient, center of mass, and disturbance forces. This information is crucial for the critic network during training.

- **Action Space:** The actions ($a_t$) are desired increments of joint angles, which are then tracked by a joint-level Proportional-Derivative (PD) controller on the robot's motors.

- **Neural Network Architecture (Fig. 2a):** MARG employs four interconnected sub-networks:
 - **Elevation Net ($E_{\theta_1}$):** A Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) that processes the relative height map ($z_t - h_t$) to extract compact elevation features ($e_h_t \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$). This allows the policy to understand terrain contours and identify safe zones.
 - **Estimator Net ($E_{\theta_2}$):** Another MLP that takes the history of proprioceptive observations ($e_o_H_t$) and predicts critical *privileged features* ($e_o_t$), specifically the estimated linear velocity ($\hat{v}_t$) and estimated foot contact boolean ($\hat{c}_t$). This bridges the reality gap by allowing the actor to use estimates of unmeasurable quantities.
 - **Asymmetric Actor-Critic:**
 - **Actor Net:** Receives proprioceptive observations ($e_o_t$), the *estimated* privileged features ($e_o_t$), and the elevation features ($e_h_t$) to output the desired actions ($a_t$).
 - **Critic Net:** Benefits from *ground-truth* privileged states ($e_s_t$), along with proprioceptive ($e_o_t$) and elevation features ($e_h_t$), to accurately assess the value of the current state and provide rich feedback for policy improvement.
 - **Concurrent Training:** All four networks are trained simultaneously using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The policy gradient loss updates the actor, estimator, and elevation nets, while a value function loss updates the critic. The estimator net also has an additional regression loss (Mean Squared Error) to minimize the difference between its predictions and the ground-truth privileged information.

#### 3.2. Rewards for Footholds

Beyond standard DRL rewards (velocity tracking, smoothness, collision avoidance, joint limits), MARG introduces three novel foot-related rewards specifically designed for risky gap terrains:
1. **Feet Air Time:** Encourages longer leg lifting durations, essential for crossing larger gaps or stepping over obstacles. It rewards $\sum (t_{air,f} - 0.5)$, where $t_{air,f}$ is the time a foot is in the air.
2. **Feet Stumble:** Penalizes feet hitting vertical surfaces, preventing tripping or getting stuck. It triggers a penalty if the horizontal contact force magnitude ($||f_{x,y}||$) significantly exceeds the vertical contact force ($|f_z|$).
3. **Feet Center:** Penalizes stepping on terrain edges, promoting safer foothold placement. This is achieved by classifying 9 points around each foot into three types (contact, within $d_1$, within $d_2$) and penalizing steps where points within the outer radii are significantly below the foot's contact height, indicating an edge.

#### 3.3. Terrain Map Generation (TMG) Model

The TMG model is central to MARG's real-world deployability and efficiency, designed to provide accurate elevation maps using only a single LiDAR sensor.

- **Contact-aware State Estimation:** To overcome height drift in LiDAR-based localization, an Error State Kalman Filter (ESKF) is employed. Crucially, the state vector is augmented with **contact point positions** ($p_f_i$), incorporating kinematic measurements. This allows for more robust pose estimation, especially when feet are in contact with the ground, mitigating drift. The ESKF combines IMU measurements, LiDAR point cloud data, and kinematic constraints (zero foot velocity during contact) to refine state estimates.
- **Ego-centric Elevation Mapping:** Instead of a global sliding window map, which is computationally expensive and susceptible to global odometry errors, MARG uses an ego-centric (robot-centered) hashmap-based approach. This maintains a local elevation map (1.6m x 1.0m) around the robot, updating only the relevant cells. Global indices are updated based on the refined local state estimate, and local indices are normalized to stay within the map dimensions.
- **Local Map Updating:**
 - **Noise Filtering:** Statistical Outlier Removal (SOR) is applied to LiDAR point clouds to reduce measurement noise.
 - **Probabilistic Occupancy:** Ray casting is used to update the occupancy probability of each grid cell using a logarithmic odds formulation, considering both "hit" and "miss" points.
 - **Bounded Probability:** Probability bounds are applied to manage dynamic and static objects effectively, ensuring robust occupancy estimation.
- **Priori Ray Interpolation:** To handle regions with missing data (e.g., due to sparse LiDAR scans or beam divergence), a bidirectional ray-based interpolation strategy is used. Empty columns in the elevation grid are assigned values from the farthest detected occupied cell (forward traversal) or the nearest occupied cell (reverse traversal), maintaining terrain continuity and structural features.

This comprehensive methodology, combining a sophisticated DRL framework with an innovative and efficient single-LiDAR mapping system, enables MARG to achieve stable, safe, and adaptive locomotion over challenging risky gap terrains.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate MARG's superior performance, robustness, and practicality across various aspects:

#### 4.1. Algorithm Comparison

- **Superior Learning Performance:** In risky gap terrains, MARG consistently achieved the highest average rewards and exhibited minimal fluctuations in later training stages compared to other DRL algorithms (MorAL, Vanilla PPO, Concurrent, RMA, DreamWaQ). This highlights its robust overall performance attributed to effective environmental perception.
- **Generalizability:** MARG's strong performance extended to conventional challenging terrains, proving its generalizability beyond just risky gaps.
- **Training Efficiency and Deployment Advantages:** Compared to other parkour controllers (Anymal Parkour, PIE, Extreme Parkour), MARG demonstrated:
 - Significantly lower GPU memory consumption (≈12 GB vs. >15 GB to >45 GB), enabling large-scale training with 4096 parallel agents.
 - Elimination of exteroceptive sensors during simulation training, simplifying the setup.
 - Single-stage, end-to-end training (like PIE).
 - Reliance on only *one LiDAR* for deployment, drastically reducing hardware complexity and cost compared to multi-camera/LiDAR setups.

#### 4.2. Explicit Estimation Comparison

- **Accurate State Estimation:** MARG's Estimator net demonstrated consistently lower squared errors in linear velocity estimation across various gap sizes compared to NMN, MorAL, and DreamWaQ.
- **Precise Foot Contact Prediction:** MARG accurately predicted foot contact states, closely matching real states, showing similar trot gait patterns across different feet. NMN also performed well but relied on time-consuming offline data collection.
- **Robustness in Gaps:** MARG's estimator proved exceptionally precise and robust during gap traversal, outperforming other algorithms in average squared linear velocity error.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies of Observation Space

- **Critical Role of Terrain Map:** The terrain map (exteroception) was identified as the most crucial factor for successful locomotion on risky terrains, followed by body velocity and contact state (privileged information). Policies lacking terrain maps or body velocity showed near-zero success rates and significantly shorter travel distances.
- **Elevation Net's Efficiency:** Using the Elevation Net to extract features ($e_h_t$) was more efficient and stable than directly inputting the raw relative height map.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies of Footholds Rewards

- **Enhanced Estimator Accuracy:** The combination of all three novel foothold rewards (Feet air time, Feet stumble, Feet center) led to the lowest average foot contact loss during training, significantly enhancing the accuracy of the Estimator network.
- **Improved Traversal Performance:** Controllers with all foothold rewards achieved the highest success rates and longest travel distances on risky gap terrains.
- **Individual Reward Impact:** "Feet stumble" was the most effective individual reward, promoting stable gait; "Feet air time" helped optimize leg movements for uneven terrain; and "Feet center" subtly guided footholds away from precarious edges.

#### 4.5. Dynamics Analysis

- Detailed analysis in Gazebo simulation demonstrated MARG's intricate coordination of gait, joint angles, and torques, enabling stable and efficient locomotion across discontinuous gaps.

#### 4.6. Analysis of the TMG Model

- **Superior Localization Accuracy:** MARG's TMG model consistently achieved the lowest Absolute Pose Error (APE) and Relative Pose Error (RPE) across various challenging real-world datasets (Park, Running, Indoor, Corridor), outperforming EKF, FAST-LIO2, LIO-SAM, and A-LOAM.
- **Minimal Height Drift:** The TMG model maintained accurate height estimation with minimal z-axis drift, a critical factor for legged locomotion, unlike other methods that showed significant fluctuations.
- **High-Resolution Mapping:** In real-world comparisons, MARG's 100 Hz TMG provided significantly clearer and more detailed elevation maps than a 20 Hz conventional elevation mapping method, closely aligning with ground truth.
- **Importance of Components:** Ablation studies showed that both the kinematic state (preventing deviation from IMU saturation) and the noise filter (managing LiDAR beam divergence) were crucial for the TMG model's mapping quality.

#### 4.7. Real-world Transfer

- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** The policy trained in simulation successfully demonstrated zero-shot transfer to physical Unitree Go1 and Go2 robots.
- **Versatile Risky Terrain Traversal:** The robots adeptly navigated:
 - Large gaps (up to 65 cm), approximately 1.6 times the robot's length, using a trot gait.
 - Narrow single-plank bridges (18 cm wide, 0.7 times the robot's width), demonstrating excellent balance.
 - Complex balance beams with varying heights, widths, and inclinations, requiring precise foothold planning and dynamic adjustments.
 - Mixed uneven gap terrains.
- **Robust Outdoor Performance:** Extensive tests on the University of Hong Kong campus and a hiking route in Lung Fu Shan Country Park confirmed MARG's robustness in diverse outdoor environments (gardens, slopes, stairs) under varying lighting conditions. The TMG model dynamically updated terrain maps, enabling the robot to adapt effectively.

#### 4.8. Limitations

- **Sensor Dependency:** Performance remains critically dependent on the accuracy of LiDAR point cloud data, which can be affected by noise, dust, calibration errors, and limited vertical field of view.
- **Limited Gait Diversity:** The current MARG controller primarily achieves a trot gait, suggesting unexplored potential for adapting to terrains with other gaits.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MARG research presents a significant advancement in the field of legged robotics, particularly for enabling robust and safe autonomous locomotion in challenging, risky terrains. Its potential impact spans both academic research and practical applications:

#### 5.1. Advancing Legged Robot Autonomy

- **Mastery of Risky Terrains:** The most immediate impact is empowering quadrupedal robots to confidently and safely navigate terrains that have traditionally posed substantial challenges, such as wide gaps, narrow bridges, and unstable balance beams. This expands the operational envelope of legged robots into more complex and hazardous environments.
- **Enhanced Safety and Stability:** By dynamically adjusting actions based on accurate terrain perception and explicitly rewarding safe foothold selection, MARG significantly enhances the robot's stability and reduces the risk of falls or collisions, which are critical for deployment in real-world scenarios.

#### 5.2. Practicality and Deployment Efficiency

- **Simplified Hardware:** The groundbreaking achievement of relying on only a *single LiDAR* for accurate terrain mapping drastically reduces the hardware complexity, cost, and weight associated with perception systems on legged robots. This makes advanced robotic capabilities more accessible and practical for a wider range of applications.
- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The robust zero-shot transfer capability from simulation to the real world is a major breakthrough. It eliminates the tedious and resource-intensive process of real-world fine-tuning, accelerating development cycles and enabling faster deployment of new locomotion skills.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The proposed Terrain Map Generation (TMG) model, with its ego-centric, hashmap-based approach and contact-aware state estimation, offers superior computational efficiency and minimizes mapping drift. This ensures real-time performance on onboard computing platforms, even with limited resources.

#### 5.3. Contributions to Deep Reinforcement Learning

- **Effective Integration of Perception and Privileged Information:** MARG demonstrates a highly effective method for integrating exteroceptive terrain maps with proprioceptive feedback and leveraging selective privileged information in an asymmetric actor-critic DRL framework. This provides a blueprint for future DRL approaches aiming for robust sim-to-real transfer in complex physical systems.
- **Novel Reward Engineering:** The specialized foot-related reward functions (Feet air time, Feet stumble, Feet center) highlight the importance of task-specific reward engineering for achieving fine-grained control and safety on challenging terrains. These insights can inform reward design in other complex locomotion tasks.
- **Robust State Estimation:** The concurrently trained Estimator net accurately predicts unmeasurable states (e.g., linear velocity, foot contact), which is crucial for robust policy execution in real-world scenarios where full state observability is impossible.

#### 5.4. Broader Application Potential

- **Search and Rescue:** Robots equipped with MARG could be deployed in disaster zones, navigating rubble and precarious structures to locate survivors or assess damage.
- **Industrial Inspection:** Facilitating inspection of hazardous industrial sites or construction areas with uneven terrain, where human access is difficult or dangerous.
- **Environmental Monitoring:** Enabling autonomous exploration of rough outdoor environments for data collection or monitoring.
- **Logistics and Delivery:** Improving the capability of legged robots for last-mile delivery in complex urban or off-road settings.

While the current limitation of gait diversity and reliance on sensor accuracy are noted, the MARG controller represents a significant step forward in making legged robots truly robust and autonomous in challenging, dynamic environments. Future work on expanding gait capabilities and addressing sensor robustness issues will further amplify its impact. Overall, MARG provides a foundation for more intelligent, safer, and adaptable legged robots in a diverse range of real-world applications.
