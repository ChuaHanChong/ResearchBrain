# Analyzing Hierarchical Structure in Vision Models with Sparse Autoencoders

> [!summary] Summary
> This research investigates whether the DINOv2 vision foundation model implicitly learns hierarchical visual concepts by applying Sparse Autoencoders to its class token representations. The study reveals a progressive development of taxonomic features across model layers, with late layers forming rich, spatially grounded hierarchical representations.

## Problem

- Deep learning models, especially large vision models, operate as 'black boxes,' making their internal decision-making processes opaque.
- A lack of understanding exists regarding whether vision models implicitly learn hierarchical structures that mirror human cognitive organization.
- There was a need to investigate if state-of-the-art unsupervised vision foundation models spontaneously organize visual concepts in taxonomic hierarchies without explicit supervision.

## Method

- The research employs ==Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs)==, adapted from Large Language Model interpretability techniques, to extract and analyze hierarchical features from DINOv2.
- ==Forty separate SAEs== are trained, one for each intermediate layer of DINOv2's class token, allowing for a granular analysis of representation evolution through the network.
- Two novel metrics, ==Lowest Common Hypernym (LCH) Height== and ==Ontological Coverage==, are introduced to quantify how well SAE-extracted features align with ImageNet's hierarchical structure.

## Results

- Early layers of DINOv2's class token show very low reconstruction error and poor classification performance, indicating minimal class-specific information.
- Layer **36** of DINOv2's class token demonstrates remarkable hierarchical organization, with **90** Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) heads achieving perfect ontological coverage for broad categories such as 'elasmobranch', 'whales', and 'woodwind instruments'.
- Relevancy maps confirmed that SAE-extracted hierarchical features are spatially grounded, consistently activating on semantically relevant regions across different images (e.g., a 'whale' concept activating on whale-like forms).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - DINOv2's class token exhibits late information emergence, with early layers containing minimal class-specific data and semantic information gradually accumulating.
> - A clear progression in hierarchical feature development occurs, with features evolving from single-class or dissimilar activations in early layers (e.g., Layer 24) to broad, high-level taxonomic concepts in later layers (e.g., Layer 36).
> - Extracted hierarchical features are spatially grounded, consistently activating on relevant image regions for specific concepts (e.g., a 'whale' concept activating on whale-like shapes across different species).

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Deep learning models, particularly large vision models, have achieved remarkable performance across various tasks. However, their internal decision-making processes remain largely opaque, creating a "black box" problem that limits our understanding of how these models work. This paper addresses this challenge by investigating whether vision models implicitly learn hierarchical structures that mirror human cognitive organization.

The research focuses on DINOv2, a state-of-the-art unsupervised vision foundation model, and employs Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) - a technique originally developed for interpreting Large Language Models - to extract and analyze hierarchical features. The core question is whether these models spontaneously learn to organize visual concepts in taxonomic hierarchies similar to the ImageNet/WordNet structure, without explicit hierarchical supervision.

### 2. Understanding Sparse Autoencoders

Sparse Autoencoders represent a powerful interpretability tool that has shown success in extracting meaningful features from complex neural network representations. The authors employ ReLU SAEs with a specific architecture designed to encourage "monosemantic" representations - where each neuron corresponds to a single, interpretable concept.

The SAE architecture consists of:
- **Encoder**: `ReLU(W_enc * x + b_enc)`
- **Decoder**: `W_dec * z + b_dec`

Where `x` is the input model representation and `z` is the sparse latent representation.

The training objective combines reconstruction accuracy with sparsity:
```
Loss = MSE(x, x_hat) + λ * ||z||_1
```

The L1 penalty (with λ = 10) encourages most latent units to remain inactive, forcing the model to learn sparse, disentangled features. The hidden layer uses an expansion factor of 8, resulting in 12,288 hidden units, allowing the SAE to potentially discover more fine-grained concepts than the original representation dimensionality would suggest.

### 3. Methodology

The research employs a systematic approach to analyze hierarchical structure across the depth of DINOv2. The authors train 40 separate SAEs, one for each intermediate layer of the model's class token, enabling a granular analysis of how representations evolve through the network.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Metrics

Two novel metrics quantify how well SAE-extracted features align with ImageNet's hierarchical structure:

1. **Lowest Common Hypernym (LCH) Height**: For a set of ImageNet classes activated by an SAE head, this measures the average distance from those classes to their lowest common ancestor in the WordNet taxonomy. Higher values indicate more abstract concepts.

2. **Ontological Coverage**: The ratio of classes activated by an SAE head to the total number of classes under their common hypernym. High coverage suggests the SAE head captures a complete hierarchical concept.

The combination of high LCH Height and high Ontological Coverage indicates that an SAE head effectively captures a broad, high-level hierarchical concept.

#### 3.2. Experimental Design

The study analyzes the class token representations from DINOv2 layers, training SAEs for 3 epochs with careful hyperparameter selection. Linear probe classifiers are trained on each layer as a baseline to assess the information content available for classification tasks. Additionally, relevancy maps provide qualitative visualization of which image regions contribute to specific hierarchical features.

### 4. Key Findings

The research reveals several significant insights about how DINOv2 processes and represents hierarchical information:

#### 4.1. Late Information Emergence

A counterintuitive finding is that early layers of DINOv2's class token contain minimal class-specific information. The SAEs trained on these layers achieve very low reconstruction error and require few active units, while linear probes perform poorly on classification tasks. This suggests that the class token gradually accumulates semantic information as data flows through the network, rather than capturing class-specific features from the beginning.

#### 4.2. Progressive Hierarchical Development

The analysis reveals a clear progression in hierarchical feature development:

- **Layer 24**: SAE heads predominantly activate on single classes or many dissimilar classes, showing limited hierarchical organization
- **Layers 28-32**: Increasing emergence of multi-class features with higher ontological coverage
- **Layer 36**: Remarkable hierarchical organization with 90 heads achieving perfect ontological coverage, capturing broad categories like "elasmobranch" (sharks and rays), "whales," and "woodwind instruments"

#### 4.3. Spatial Grounding

Relevancy maps confirm that hierarchical features are spatially grounded in meaningful image regions. For example, an SAE head capturing the "whale" concept consistently activates on whale-like shapes across different species, regardless of specific taxonomic labels.

### 5. Significance and Impact

This research makes several important contributions to the field of AI interpretability:

#### 5.1. Methodological Advancement

The successful extension of SAEs from language models to vision represents a significant methodological breakthrough. The paper demonstrates that interpretability techniques can transfer across modalities, potentially enabling a unified approach to understanding different types of neural networks.

#### 5.2. Foundation Model Understanding

The findings provide crucial insights into how unsupervised vision models learn. The discovery that DINOv2 implicitly develops hierarchical representations without explicit supervision reveals the powerful emergent properties of self-supervised learning on large-scale visual data.

#### 5.3. Practical Applications

The ability to identify and analyze hierarchical features has immediate practical implications:
- **Model debugging**: Researchers can diagnose specific failures in hierarchical reasoning
- **Concept-based editing**: Potential for targeted modifications to model behavior
- **Educational tools**: Visual demonstrations of how AI models organize knowledge

#### 5.4. Theoretical Implications

The research contributes to our understanding of the relationship between artificial and human cognition. The emergence of hierarchical organization in neural networks, mirroring human taxonomic thinking, suggests fundamental principles of efficient knowledge representation.

### 6. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge several limitations that present opportunities for future research:

#### 6.1. Scope Limitations

The study focuses specifically on DINOv2 and ImageNet's hierarchy. Future work could expand to other vision models, different taxonomic structures, and alternative datasets to validate the generalizability of findings.

#### 6.2. SAE Challenges

Sparse Autoencoders face inherent challenges in feature disentanglement and interpretation. While the results are promising, continued development of SAE techniques could improve the reliability and interpretability of extracted features.

#### 6.3. Intervention Studies

The current work is primarily observational. Future research could incorporate causal interventions to test whether identified hierarchical features actually drive model behavior, moving beyond correlation to establish causation.

The paper concludes by suggesting that SAE-based analysis could be extended to other aspects of model behavior, such as robustness analysis and bias detection, potentially creating a comprehensive framework for understanding complex AI systems.

This research represents a significant step toward making powerful vision models more interpretable and controllable, addressing fundamental questions about how artificial intelligence systems organize and represent knowledge.
