# DeWorldSG: Depth-Aware 3D Semantic Scene Graph Generation via World-Model Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> DeWorldSG builds spatio-temporally robust 3D semantic scene graphs from RGB-D video. Each object becomes a probabilistic 3D Gaussian estimated from SAM-masked, dual-domain-filtered depth rather than a lifted 2D box, and ambiguous relations are refined by fusing temporally aggregated visual logits with V-JEPA 2 video world-model predicate priors, lifting 3DSSG triplet recall 77.4% over prior state of the art.

## Problem

- Geometry-first 3D scene graph methods tie graph quality to dense point-cloud reconstruction, so pose drift, incomplete geometry, and compute overhead degrade relational reasoning.
- Lifting-based pipelines approximate object geometry from projected 2D bounding boxes, so boundary depth distortion and flying pixels fragment single instances or wrongly merge distinct ones.
- Frame-wise relation inference drops edges under occlusion or low resolution, yielding graphs that reflect local geometric proximity instead of coherent scene-level context.

## Method

- ==Depth-aware 3D Gaussian estimation==: box-conditioned segmentation masks sample instance depth, ambiguous pixels are discarded, and sample mean plus covariance of back-projected inliers define each probabilistic node.
- ==Dual-Domain Depth Refinement==: a 3x3 median spatial-consistency filter removes flying pixels, then adaptive-threshold 1D clustering along the depth axis retains the dominant self-consistent depth layer per instance.
- ==World-model predicate refinement==: a frozen ==V-JEPA 2== backbone encodes 16-frame union-crop clips through an MLP probe, whose class-pair prior is additively fused into ==entropy-gated==, temporally aggregated visual logits into **DeWorldSG**'s final relation distribution.

## Results

- On 3DSSG it reaches **50.2%** relationship, **75.0%** object, and **57.3%** predicate recall, relative gains of **77.4%**, **20.2%**, and **23.2%** over prior best.
- On ReplicaSSG it gains **71.3%** relationship, **35.6%** object, and **62.9%** predicate recall, and keeps **87.6%** of ground-truth-pose relationship recall using ORB-SLAM3 estimated poses.
- Ablations raise relationship recall from **28.5%** to **40.3%** with masking, **47.8%** with refinement, **50.2%** with the video prior, beating a DINOv2 static-feature variant at **49.5%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modeling lifted objects as depth-sampled probabilistic Gaussians instead of single representative depths is what stabilizes cross-frame association; geometric uncertainty matters more than dense reconstruction.
> - Temporal video priors beat static image features for relation inference: the world-model probe outperformed a DINOv2 probe, so motion context carries genuine relational evidence.
> - Gate priors by uncertainty: injecting predictive beliefs only on high-entropy edges adds missing relations without overriding strong visual evidence. Upstream detector label flips still fragment nodes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of 3D Semantic Scene Graphs (3D SSGs) is a fundamental task for spatial perception in embodied AI and robotic systems. These graphs provide a compact, structural representation of a 3D environment, capturing both object instances (nodes) and their semantic and spatial relationships (edges). This abstraction is crucial for higher-level cognitive tasks such as agent planning, 3D content synthesis, and object augmentation in Augmented Reality (AR).

Existing approaches to 3D SSG generation broadly fall into two categories:
1. **Geometry-Explicit Methods:** These methods typically leverage dense 3D structures such as point clouds or SLAM-estimated 3D reconstructions. They benefit from direct geometric grounding of relations but are often sensitive to reconstruction quality, pose inaccuracies, and incur significant computational overhead. Examples include methods that regress scene graphs directly from reconstructed point clouds or use GNNs on SLAM-derived 3D structures.
2. **2D-to-3D Lifted Methods:** These approaches first extract 2D scene graphs from RGB(-D) sequences and then project and merge them into a global 3D scene graph. This strategy aims to reduce dependency on full 3D reconstruction and offer robustness to geometric noise and camera pose errors, making them more suitable for real-world interactive applications. Recent advancements in this area, such as FROSS, have introduced probabilistic Gaussian representations for object merging to enhance robustness during incremental graph construction.

Despite advancements, both categories face challenges. Geometry-explicit methods are hampered by the computational cost and fragility of dense 3D reconstruction. Lifting-based methods, while more robust to certain noise, still struggle with unstable 3D object representations due to unreliable depth estimates near object boundaries and fragmented object instances. A significant limitation across many existing methods is their reliance on frame-wise inference for relationships, leading to relational sparsity, inconsistencies, and a tendency to predict relations based purely on local geometric proximity rather than broader scene context. This results in missing or unreliable relations, especially under occlusion or limited observations.

DeWorldSG positions itself within the 2D-to-3D lifted paradigm but addresses its specific shortcomings. It aims to overcome unstable 3D object representations by estimating instance-level geometric 3D Gaussian distributions with depth-guided filtering, thus representing objects as probabilistic 3D nodes rather than single projected points. Furthermore, it tackles relational sparsity and inconsistency by aggregating spatiotemporal evidence across object pairs and refining relations using contextual priors derived from a video world model (V-JEPA 2). This integration of probabilistic geometric modeling with predictive world-model priors represents a departure from previous methods, aiming for more robust, complete, and spatio-temporally consistent 3D SSG generation suitable for real-world robotic and AR applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of DeWorldSG is to generate spatio-temporally robust 3D Semantic Scene Graphs from RGB-D sequences. This overarching goal is motivated by several identified limitations in current 3D SSG generation frameworks and the increasing demands of robotic intelligence and Augmented Reality (AR) systems.

The specific motivations and objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Address Unstable 3D Object Representations:** Existing 2D-to-3D lifting methods often approximate 3D object geometry from projected 2D bounding boxes or rely on single representative depth values. This can lead to distorted lifted object geometry, unstable object-level 3D representations, and issues such as erroneous merging of distinct objects or fragmentation of a single object during global graph construction. DeWorldSG aims to overcome this by:
 - Estimating instance-level geometric 3D Gaussian distributions directly from depth observations, rather than relying on weak 2D bounding box projections.
 - Employing depth-guided filtering to account for and mitigate uncertainty in lifted object geometry.
 - Representing each object as a probabilistic 3D node (3D Gaussian) to better capture its position, spatial extent, and inherent uncertainty.

2. **Mitigate Relational Sparsity and Inconsistency from Frame-wise Inference:** Many current approaches infer object relationships independently for each video frame. This frame-wise inference is susceptible to limited observations due to occlusion, low-resolution input, or transient noise, often resulting in missing essential relational edges or inconsistent predictions over time. The generated scene graphs thus tend to reflect only local geometric proximity rather than coherent scene-level context. DeWorldSG aims to address this by:
 - Aggregating spatiotemporal evidence of relations across multiple frames and object pairs.
 - Refining relations using contextual priors derived from a video world model (specifically V-JEPA 2), which can capture structural regularities and physical consistency beyond instantaneous visual evidence.
 - Implementing confidence-based probabilistic fusion to balance these priors against direct visual and geometric cues, thereby suppressing hallucinated relations and enhancing relational completeness and consistency.

3. **Enable Robust Incremental 3D SSG Construction:** For practical applications in robotics and AR, the system needs to operate online and incrementally update the scene graph as new observations become available. The proposed framework is designed to maintain stable object representations and refine inter-object relations over time, making it suitable for continuous operation in dynamic real-world environments.

4. **Provide a Reliable Structural Representation for Downstream Applications:** The ultimate motivation is to provide a fundamental basis for higher-level cognitive tasks. By generating a more robust and consistent 3D SSG, DeWorldSG aims to improve the performance of applications such as robotic manipulation, embodied AI agent planning, 3D content synthesis, and context-aware AR anchoring, which demand high-fidelity relational reasoning and stable spatial understanding.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeWorldSG is presented as a novel framework for context-grounded 3D Semantic Scene Graph generation from RGB-D sequences. Its methodology integrates depth-aware probabilistic object modeling with spatiotemporal relation reasoning through a three-stage process: 2D scene graph generation, depth-aware 3D lifting, and world-model-based relation enhancement, followed by incremental merging.

#### 3.1. Depth-aware Local 3D Gaussian Representation

This initial stage focuses on generating robust per-frame 3D object representations.
- **2D Scene Graph Generation:** For each input RGB-D frame (I_t, D_t), a 2D scene graph (G^2D_t) is extracted from the RGB image (I_t). An object detector (e.g., RT-DETR) identifies instances and provides bounding boxes (b_i,t) and class probabilities (p(c_i,t)). A 2D relation inference model (e.g., EGTR) then predicts relation logits (p(r_i→j,t)) for each ordered object pair.
- **Box-Conditioned Instance Extraction:** Given the estimated bounding box for an instance (b_i,t), an object-wise binary mask (M_i,t) is generated using a semantic segmentation model (e.g., SAM). To prevent depth mixing, pixels within bounding boxes assigned to multiple masks are considered ambiguous and discarded. Instances with fewer than 10 valid pixels after masking are excluded due to potential instability in covariance estimation. This mask-based conditioning aims to produce cleaner depth samples for subsequent processing.
- **Dual-Domain Depth Refinement (DR):** To address outliers and noise in the depth map (D_t) within instance masks, a two-step filtering algorithm is applied:
 - **Spatial Domain Filtering:** For each pixel p within M_i,t, its depth D_t(p) is compared to the median depth of its 3x3 neighborhood. Pixels deviating significantly from this local median (based on a threshold τ) are filtered out, creating M_spatial. This step aims to remove discontinuous boundary noise and "flying pixels," thus enforcing local depth consistency.
 - **Depth-Distribution Domain Refinement:** From the spatially refined depth values (Z_spatial), a 1D clustering along the depth axis is performed. A dynamic threshold (Δ_thr), considering base separation, local depth variation, and global depth scale, is used to partition the sorted depth sequence. The cluster whose mean depth is closest to the reference depth (median of Z_spatial) is retained as the final inlier set (P_final). This process isolates the dominant, self-consistent depth layer for each instance.
- **Depth-guided 3D Gaussian Estimation:** The refined inlier pixels in P_final are back-projected into 3D camera coordinates using their depth values and then transformed into the world frame using camera intrinsics (K) and poses (T_t). This generates an instance-specific 3D point set (P_i,t). A 3D Gaussian distribution, characterized by its mean (μ^3D_i,t) and covariance (Σ^3D_i,t), is then estimated from P_i,t using sample mean and covariance, with a regularization term (ϵI) for numerical stability. This probabilistic representation directly models geometric uncertainty and surface spread. These 3D Gaussians, along with frame-wise relation predictions, form the local 3D scene graph (G^3D_t).

#### 3.2. Incremental Merging into the Global 3D SSG

Local 3D graphs (G^3D_t) are incrementally integrated into a global 3D scene graph (G^3D).
- **Object Association:** For each new local object node (v^t_i), a correspondence in the global graph (V^3D) is sought. This involves comparing:
 - **Geometric Similarity:** Hellinger distance between 3D Gaussians is used, with a threshold (δ_g) to determine compatible spatial location and dispersion.
 - **Semantic Similarity:** An inner-product-based class distance (D_c) is computed between class probability distributions, with a threshold (δ_c) for semantic compatibility.
 - Nodes are merged only if both geometric and semantic criteria are satisfied.
- **Gaussian Fusion:** When nodes are merged, their 3D Gaussians are fused using weighted averaging, considering their respective observation frequencies. The mean and covariance of the merged Gaussian are updated using a standard Gaussian fusion formula that also accounts for the difference between the means.
- **Relation Aggregation:** All relation edges connected to merged nodes are reassigned. Relation prediction distributions for each object pair are aggregated across multiple frames to produce a stabilized, temporally consistent final relation, selected as the most probable.

#### 3.3. 3D Semantic Scene Graph Meets World Models

This stage refines relational edges by integrating geometric constraints with spatiotemporal visual context from a world model.
- **Geometric Neighbor Pairing with Temporal Context:** To avoid hallucinated relations, candidate edges are restricted to geometrically proximal object pairs. A geometric proximity cost, combining normalized 2D center distance and depth disparity, is used to filter candidate pairs. For each retained pair, a union bounding box is created, and the corresponding region from a sliding 16-frame RGB-D buffer is extracted to form a short video clip. This encourages predicates to stabilize over time.
- **Spatiotemporal Predicate Refinement with World-Model:**
 - A frozen V-JEPA 2 backbone, pre-trained as a video world model, encodes the 16-frame union-crop clips into temporal representations.
 - A lightweight MLP probe network, trained on the specific dataset, takes these spatiotemporal tokens to predict clip-level predicate probabilities (p_WM(r_i→j)).
 - These world-model-derived relation distributions (P_WM(r | c_s, c_o)) are aggregated over time at the class-pair level, forming a stabilized relational prior. This prior is updated solely from model predictions, reinforcing consistent predicates.
 - Concurrently, a visually aggregated relation distribution (P_vis(r | i, j)) is computed by summing per-frame relation logits.
 - **Uncertainty-aware Additive Fusion:** The world-model prior is injected into the visual prediction only when the entropy of P_vis indicates high relational ambiguity (exceeding a threshold). The final relation distribution (P_new) is obtained via additive fusion: P_new ∝ P_vis + α_ij * P_WM, where α_ij activates the prior under high visual uncertainty. This mechanism complements missing relation probabilities without overriding strong visual or geometric evidence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DeWorldSG was evaluated on the 3DSSG and ReplicaSSG datasets, demonstrating its performance in object and predicate prediction, spatio-temporal consistency, and robustness.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **3DSSG Dataset:** DeWorldSG exhibited improvements across all evaluated metrics compared to prior state-of-the-art methods.
 - Relationship Recall improved by 77.4%.
 - Object Recall improved by 20.2%.
 - Predicate Recall improved by 23.2%.
 - The method also demonstrated robust performance under class-balanced mRecall, indicating effectiveness despite class imbalance.
 - It surpassed point cloud-based methods (e.g., 3DSSG, SGFN, MonoSSG) in relational reasoning, relying on depth-guided lifting and temporal relation accumulation rather than dense scene-level point cloud inference.
- **ReplicaSSG Dataset:** On this dataset, DeWorldSG substantially outperformed the baseline (FROSS).
 - Relationship Recall gained 71.3%.
 - Object Recall gained 35.6%.
 - Predicate Recall gained 62.9%.
- **Robustness to Camera Pose Errors:** When evaluated on ReplicaSSG using predicted camera trajectories from ORB-SLAM3 instead of ground-truth poses, DeWorldSG maintained strong performance (89.2% of GT-pose Object Recall and 87.6% Relationship Recall). This indicates stability under typical pose noise encountered in online AR/robotics systems.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- Qualitative comparisons on ReplicaSSG revealed that DeWorldSG produced more consistent object nodes and spatial extents, attributed to depth-refined 3D Gaussians. It also generated semantically coherent relation graphs through temporal evidence accumulation from world models.
- In contrast, a prior state-of-the-art method (FROSS) frequently exhibited failure cases, including redundant instantiation of the same object or inaccurately projected object locations, leading to overly dense or incorrectly connected relation edges.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study on the 3DSSG benchmark assessed the contribution of DeWorldSG's key components:
- **(a) Baseline (2D SG lifting only):** Achieved the lowest performance across all metrics (e.g., 28.5% Rel. Recall, 33.4% Pred. Recall).
- **(b) + Instance Mask:** Adding box-conditioned instance masking led to overall performance improvements (e.g., 40.3% Rel. Recall, 46.7% Pred. Recall), validating the role of mask-guided depth sampling in stabilizing 3D Gaussian estimation.
- **(c) + Dual-Domain Depth Refinement (DR):** Further boosted performance (e.g., 47.8% Rel. Recall, 52.9% Pred. Recall) by effectively removing depth noise and flying pixels, confirming the reliability of spatial and depth filtering.
- **(d) + DINOv2-based Refinement:** Replacing the V-JEPA 2 world model with DINOv2 (a generic pretrained visual representation) while maintaining the refinement pipeline yielded modest predicate gains (49.5% Rel. Recall, 56.6% Pred. Recall).
- **(e) DeWorldSG (full model, + World Model):** The full model with the V-JEPA 2 world-model prior achieved the highest performance (50.2% Rel. Recall, 57.3% Pred. Recall). This demonstrated that spatiotemporal evidence collection via a world model provides more informative cues for relation inference than static image-level representations.

#### 4.4. Runtime

The runtime analysis, averaged over 14,400 frames from ReplicaSSG, showed the following:
- Object detection and instance processing: 4.42 ms/frame
- Relation inference: 7.28 ms/frame
- Global 3D SSG merging: 2.23 ms/frame
- SAM-based mask segmentation: 25.26 ms/frame
- Relation refinement (world model): 69.39 ms/frame
- **Overall latency:** 108.53 ms/frame. This latency suggests practical feasibility for incremental 3D scene graph updates in interactive settings.

#### 4.5. Limitations

A notable limitation identified is the reliance on a 2D object detector for object class prediction. Inconsistent 2D object classifications across frames (e.g., labeling the same physical object as "table" in one frame and "bench" in another) can lead to errors in the constructed 3D scene graph. Such errors may result in duplicated nodes and fragmented relational structures, especially under partial occlusion or significant viewpoint changes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DeWorldSG offers a notable contribution to the field of 3D semantic scene graph generation by addressing critical limitations of prior work and improving the robustness and consistency of scene understanding from RGB-D sequences.

The primary significance lies in its **depth-aware probabilistic object modeling** and **spatiotemporal relation reasoning via world models**. By moving beyond simple 2D-to-3D lifting of bounding boxes and instead estimating instance-level 3D Gaussian distributions from refined depth observations, DeWorldSG provides a more geometrically grounded and stable representation of objects. This approach explicitly accounts for spatial uncertainty, which is crucial for distinguishing objects and merging observations consistently over time.

Furthermore, the introduction of a **spatiotemporal relation reasoning scheme** that aggregates evidence across frames and incorporates priors from a video world model (V-JEPA 2) is significant. This mechanism effectively mitigates the problem of relational sparsity and inconsistency often caused by frame-wise inference, especially in dynamic or partially observed environments. The uncertainty-aware fusion of world-model priors with visual and geometric cues enables the system to infer more complete and contextually appropriate relationships, thereby moving beyond mere local geometric proximity.

The demonstrated **state-of-the-art performance** on established benchmarks (3DSSG, ReplicaSSG) and the **robustness to estimated camera poses** (as opposed to ground-truth) suggest that DeWorldSG is a more practical framework for real-world applications. The reported runtime, while not real-time for all components, demonstrates feasibility for interactive, incremental updates, which is essential for continuous operation in embodied systems.

The potential impact of DeWorldSG extends to several domains:
- **Robotic Perception and Manipulation:** A reliable 3D SSG provides a rich, structured understanding of the environment, enabling robots to reason about objects, their locations, and their functional relationships. This can lead to more intelligent planning for manipulation tasks, navigation, and human-robot interaction.
- **Embodied AI Agents:** For AI agents operating in complex 3D environments, a robust scene graph can facilitate better decision-making, object recognition, and interaction with the physical world by providing a cognitive map that encapsulates semantic and spatial knowledge.
- **Augmented Reality (AR) Systems:** Stable object representations and consistent relational understanding are critical for context-aware AR applications, such as anchoring virtual content to real-world objects, understanding user intent in AR, and synthesizing interactive 3D content.

In conclusion, DeWorldSG represents a step toward more scalable and interactive 3D scene understanding in real-world environments. By integrating probabilistic geometric modeling with predictive world-model priors, it offers a foundation for developing more intelligent and adaptive embodied systems that can robustly perceive and reason about complex 3D scenes. Future work could focus on addressing limitations such as inconsistent 2D object classification to further enhance the reliability of the generated scene graphs.
