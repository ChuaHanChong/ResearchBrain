# ExpertGen: Scalable Sim-to-Real Expert Policy Learning from Imperfect Behavior Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> EXPERTGEN introduces a three-phase framework for learning robust, generalizable sim-to-real expert policies for robotics, initiating from imperfect behavior priors and distilling them for real-world deployment. The system achieved high success rates, including an average of 90.5% on industrial assembly tasks, and demonstrated robust transfer to physical robots via visuomotor policies.

## Problem

- Scarcity of high-quality, expert-level robotics data, especially for closed-loop control, long-horizon skills, and failure recovery.
- Limitations of existing simulation-based data generation methods (e.g., scripted policies lack diversity, conventional RL requires labor-intensive reward engineering).
- Challenges in bridging the sim-to-real gap for policies trained with privileged state information to real robots relying on limited sensory observations.

## Method

- Generative Behavior Prior Modeling: Trains a state-based diffusion policy on imperfect, but feasible, demonstrations to capture structured motions.
- Expert Policy Acquisition: Refines the pretrained diffusion policy using Diffusion Steering Reinforcement Learning (DSRL) with FastTD3, optimizing only the initial noise input to preserve the motion manifold while maximizing sparse task-level rewards in parallel simulation.
- Visuomotor Policy Distillation: Employs DAgger with extensive visual domain randomization to convert state-based expert policies into robust observation-based (RGB or point cloud) policies for real-world deployment.

## Results

- Achieved highest success rates across ANYTASK tabletop manipulation tasks and an average 90.5% success rate on eight AutoMate industrial assembly tasks, outperforming all baseline methods.
- Demonstrated strong failure recovery, with only minimal performance degradation (e.g., 0.5% for gripper, 28.6% for external force) under targeted perturbations on ANYTASK.
- Successfully transferred visuomotor policies to a Franka robot, achieving 80% real-world success on RGB-based Lift Banana and significantly outperforming baselines on contact-rich tasks like Push Pear (65.0% vs 16.7%) and Open Drawer (85.0% vs 42.5%).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion Steering Reinforcement Learning (DSRL) effectively refines imperfect behavior priors, achieving high task success and robustness without significantly altering the learned motion manifold.
> - Massively parallel RL, leveraging algorithms like FastTD3, is crucial for scaling expert policy acquisition across diverse scene configurations and long-horizon tasks using only sparse, task-level rewards.
> - Iterative distillation via DAgger combined with visual domain randomization provides an efficient and robust mechanism for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of complex visuomotor policies.
