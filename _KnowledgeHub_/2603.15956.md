While human demonstrations (e.g., through teleoperation) serve as the standard source for expert behaviors, acquiring such data at scale in the real world is prohibitively expensive. This paper introduces ExpertGen, a framework that automates expert policy learning in simulation to enable scalable sim-to-real transfer. ExpertGen first initializes a behavior prior using a diffusion policy trained on imperfect demonstrations, which may be synthesized by large language models or provided by humans. Reinforcement learning is then used to steer this prior toward high task success by optimizing the diffusion model's initial noise while keep original policy frozen. By keeping the pretrained diffusion policy frozen, ExpertGen regularizes exploration to remain within safe, human-like behavior manifolds, while also enabling effective learning with only sparse rewards. Empirical evaluations on challenging manipulation benchmarks demonstrate that ExpertGen reliably produces high-quality expert policies with no reward engineering. On industrial assembly tasks, ExpertGen achieves a 90.5% overall success rate, while on long-horizon manipulation tasks it attains 85% overall success, outperforming all baseline methods. The resulting policies exhibit dexterous control and remain robust across diverse initial configurations and failure states. To validate sim-to-real transfer, the learned state-based expert policies are further distilled into visuomotor policies via DAgger and successfully deployed on real robotic hardware.

## Problem

- Acquiring sufficient high-quality, expert-level robotics data in real-world environments is costly and time-consuming, hindering the development of robust policies.
- Current simulation-based data generation methods often produce limited behavioral diversity, lack robustness to out-of-distribution states, or require extensive manual reward engineering.
- The data scarcity limits the reliable physical execution capabilities of advanced Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotics.

## Method

- A three-phase framework is proposed, starting with training an imperfect state-based Diffusion Policy from various demonstration sources.
- This policy is refined into an expert policy using Diffusion Steering Reinforcement Learning (DSRL) with FastTD3, leveraging sparse task-level rewards in massively parallel simulation.
- The state-based expert is then distilled into a visuomotor policy for real-world deployment via iterative DAgger, incorporating extensive domain randomization.

## Results

- EXPERTGEN achieved state-of-the-art success rates on manipulation tasks (up to 100% on short-horizon ANYTASK, 90.5% on AutoMate industrial tasks), consistently outperforming other methods.
- Expert policies demonstrated robust failure recovery, showing significantly less performance degradation under random perturbations compared to the base diffusion policy.
- Distilled visuomotor policies successfully transferred to real robots, achieving 80% real-world success for RGB-based lifting and outperforming behavior cloning baselines on contact-rich interactions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Diffusion Steering Reinforcement Learning can effectively refine imperfect behavior priors into robust, generalizable policies using sparse rewards, maintaining natural motion manifolds.
> - Scalable, off-policy reinforcement learning in massively parallel simulation environments significantly reduces the reliance on dense reward shaping for complex tasks.
> - Iterative visuomotor policy distillation via DAgger, coupled with extensive domain randomization, proves crucial for robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a central challenge in robotics: the scarcity and high cost of collecting large volumes of high-quality, expert-level robotics data in real-world environments. This data scarcity is identified as a bottleneck for the reliable physical execution capabilities of modern Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which have otherwise demonstrated strong semantic generalization. Unlike static visual or linguistic data, robotics data requires specific properties such as temporal alignment, support for closed-loop control, long-horizon skill chaining, and recovery from failures.

Simulation environments offer a scalable alternative for data generation. However, existing simulation-based strategies have limitations. Teleoperation still requires significant human effort. Pre-defined skill libraries or scripted policies, while efficient for data generation, typically produce limited behavioral diversity and may be brittle when confronted with distribution shifts or failure states. Reinforcement Learning (RL) is a promising avenue for directly optimizing closed-loop task success and generating generalizable, robust policies. However, scaling RL-based data generation is often hindered by the need for meticulous reward engineering, which demands extensive domain expertise and limits scalability across diverse tasks.

Within this landscape, EXPERTGEN positions itself by integrating diffusion policies as imperfect behavior priors with diffusion steering reinforcement learning (DSRL) in massively parallel simulation. This approach aims to circumvent the need for manual reward engineering while enabling the refinement of imperfect demonstrations into robust, generalizable policies. It specifically builds upon prior work in offline-to-online reinforcement learning, particularly DSRL, which refines policies by steering the initial diffusion noise rather than directly modifying policy weights. It also distinguishes itself from synthetic data generation methods that primarily focus on augmenting successful trajectories or generating scripted behaviors, neither of which reliably covers failure modes or ensures robust recovery. By combining these elements, EXPERTGEN seeks to provide a scalable and practical method for generating high-quality synthetic robotics data for sim-to-real learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind EXPERTGEN is to overcome the data bottleneck in robotics that impedes the development of robust and generalizable robotic policies, particularly for complex manipulation tasks and for improving the physical execution capabilities of VLA models. The cost and logistical challenges associated with acquiring large datasets of expert human demonstrations in the real world make this a significant impediment. While simulation offers a path to scalability, existing methods, such as scripted policies or traditional reinforcement learning, present their own challenges, including limited behavioral diversity, fragility to out-of-distribution states, and the extensive effort required for reward engineering.

To address these limitations, EXPERTGEN outlines four specific objectives for the learned sim-to-real expert policies:

1. **Spatial Generalization:** The policies should exhibit robust performance beyond the specific trajectories or configurations present in the initial demonstrations. This implies an ability to adapt to varying initial object placements and scene layouts.
2. **Robust Failure Recovery:** Policies must possess the capacity to recover from off-nominal conditions, perturbations, or task failures without requiring re-initialization or external intervention. This is critical for reliable operation in unstructured real-world environments.
3. **Transferability to Real-World Systems:** Policies learned in simulation, often relying on privileged state information, must be effectively transferable to physical robotic hardware where only sensory observations (e.g., images, point clouds) are available. This necessitates a robust sim-to-real transfer mechanism.
4. **Learning under Sparse Task-Level Rewards without Reward Engineering:** The framework aims to learn high-performing policies using only simple binary success signals, rather than requiring meticulously shaped dense reward functions. This objective is crucial for scaling policy learning across a wide range of tasks and reducing the reliance on domain expertise.

By achieving these objectives, EXPERTGEN aims to provide a scalable and automated pipeline for generating expert robotic behaviors that are both effective in simulation and robustly deployable in the real world, thereby mitigating a significant bottleneck in current robotics research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EXPERTGEN is structured as a three-phase framework designed to learn scalable sim-to-real expert policies from imperfect behavior priors.

**Phase 1: Generative Behavior Prior Modeling**
The process begins with the acquisition of an imperfect behavior prior. This prior is derived from a collection of demonstration trajectories, which may be generated by Large Language Models (LLMs) invoking skill libraries or provided by human teleoperation. These demonstrations are considered "imperfect" because they may exhibit:
- **Incomplete state or geometry coverage:** The demonstrations might only cover a subset of possible object poses, contact configurations, or scene layouts.
- **Limited recovery behaviors:** Demonstrations might only succeed under nominal conditions, lacking coverage for off-nominal or failure states.
- **Dynamics or embodiment mismatch:** Demonstrations could be generated under different physical parameters, simplified dynamics, or approximate controllers (e.g., reversed disassembly trajectories used for assembly).

Despite these imperfections, the prior policy is assumed to generate state-action pairs that are feasible for real-world deployment and achieve non-trivial task performance (positive expected return). A lightweight state-based Diffusion Policy (DP) is trained on these imperfect demonstrations. The DP models the conditional distribution of an action chunk (a sequence of future actions) given the current state, learning to predict the noise injected into perturbed action chunks during a forward diffusion process. During inference, Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) sampling is used to generate action trajectories from Gaussian noise, providing a deterministic and efficient method.

**Phase 2: Expert Policy Acquisition**
This phase refines the pretrained diffusion policy into a task-specialized expert policy using reinforcement learning. EXPERTGEN employs Diffusion Steering Reinforcement Learning (DSRL) combined with a massively parallel, off-policy RL algorithm, FastTD3.
- **Diffusion Steering Reinforcement Learning (DSRL):** Unlike methods that fine-tune policy parameters or learn residual actions, DSRL operates by learning a steering policy that manipulates only the initial noise input to the frozen pretrained diffusion model. This mechanism allows for optimization of task performance while preserving the underlying structure and natural motion manifold of behaviors captured by the diffusion prior. By not modifying the diffusion model's weights, exploration is regularized to remain within safe, human-like behavior manifolds.
- **Off-Policy RL with FastTD3:** The steering policy (π_ϕ) is optimized using FastTD3, a scalable variant of TD3. FastTD3 is designed for high-throughput robotic simulation environments like IsaacLab. It incorporates modifications such as large-batch training, distributional critics, and mixed exploration noise to stabilize training and improve efficiency, particularly in sparse reward settings. This parallel training paradigm is critical for scaling ExpertGen across diverse scene configurations and long-horizon tasks without relying on sophisticated reward shaping. The reward function for RL is defined as a sparse binary task success signal.

**Phase 3: Visuomotor Policy Distillation through DAgger**
The state-based expert policies acquired in Phase 2 often rely on privileged information unavailable on real hardware. To enable real-world deployment, these policies are distilled into visuomotor policies that operate solely on observations (e.g., RGB images, point clouds).
- **DAgger (Dataset Aggregation):** This iterative imitation learning approach addresses the distribution shift problem common in standard behavior cloning. The process involves:
 1. Executing the current student policy (π_k) in simulation to generate states (s_t).
 2. Mapping these states to observations (o_t) using an observation function (f).
 3. Querying the state-based expert policy (π_E) to obtain corrective actions (a_t) for these observed states.
 4. Aggregating these (o_t, a_t) pairs into a dataset (D_k).
 5. Updating the student policy (π_{k+1}) by minimizing the imitation loss over the aggregated dataset.
- **Domain Randomization:** During distillation, extensive visual domain randomization is applied. This includes variations in textures, lighting, camera poses, object appearances, and object scaling (for RGB-based policies). This strategy enhances the generalization capabilities of the student policy and facilitates robust sim-to-real transfer by training the policy to be invariant to variations between simulation and reality. This combination of DAgger and aggressive visual randomization aims to produce visuomotor policies that accurately reproduce expert behaviors and are robust to real-world visual uncertainty.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluations of EXPERTGEN were conducted on two robotics manipulation benchmarks: ANYTASK and AutoMate, and included real-world sim-to-real transfer experiments.

**Expert Policy Acquisition:**
- **ANYTASK Benchmark (Table I):** EXPERTGEN demonstrated the highest success rates across all eight evaluated tasks, encompassing both short-horizon (e.g., Lift, Open Drawer, Push) and long-horizon (e.g., Stack Banana on Can, Put Object In Closed Drawer) interactions. For short-horizon tasks, success rates approached 100%. Even on inherently challenging long-horizon tasks, EXPERTGEN consistently surpassed all baseline methods, including scripted policies, pure diffusion policies, residual RL, Score-Matching Motion Priors (SMP), and EXPERTGEN-PPO. The FastTD3 baseline, without behavior priors, exhibited minimal success.
- **AutoMate Benchmark (Figure 3):** On eight high-precision industrial assembly tasks, EXPERTGEN achieved an average success rate of 90.5%. This indicated its capacity to solve dexterous manipulation tasks, even when initialized with behavior priors characterized by inaccurate contact dynamics (e.g., reversed disassembly trajectories).

**Failure Recovery Capacity (Table III):**
- EXPERTGEN policies demonstrated robustness to targeted perturbations, experiencing minor performance degradation. Under random gripper opening events, the average success rate drop was 0.5% across three tasks. With random external forces applied to the end-effector, the average drop was 28.6%.
- In contrast, the base diffusion policy was highly sensitive to such disturbances, with its success rates decreasing to nearly zero when subjected to random external forces. These results suggest that EXPERTGEN policies integrate failure recovery capabilities.

**Smoothness and Feasibility (Table II & Figure 12):**
- The quality of learned behaviors was assessed using open-ended Dynamic Time Warping (DTW) to measure similarity to feasible imperfect behavior priors and joint-space jerk cost to quantify motion smoothness.
- The base diffusion policy achieved the lowest DTW values, indicating strong adherence to the demonstrated motion manifold.
- Residual RL policies, particularly with larger action scales (0.1), exhibited substantially higher jerk costs (35.3) and increased DTW distances (0.313), suggesting jittery and less feasible actions.
- EXPERTGEN achieved a balance between these metrics: while slightly increasing DTW relative to the diffusion policy, it notably reduced jerk on several tasks and avoided the large distributional shifts observed with residual RL. This indicated that the learned policies produced both effective and physically plausible motions.

**Sim-to-Real Transfer of Visuomotor Policies:**
- **Point-cloud-based policies (Table IV):** EXPERTGEN teacher policies, distilled into point-cloud-based student policies via DAgger, achieved comparable or superior real-world performance compared to standard behavior cloning (BC) policies trained on scripted demonstrations. For instance, on "Push Pear to Center," EXPERTGEN achieved 65.0% success compared to 16.7% for the ANYTASK BC baseline. This suggests the utility of RL-based teachers for discovering robust strategies for contact-rich interactions.
- **DAgger Necessity (Figure 5 & Table V):** Ablation studies demonstrated that DAgger consistently led to faster convergence and higher success rates in simulation compared to direct BC distillation. Real-world experiments investigating the teacher rollout ratio (α) in DAgger identified that a low, non-zero α (e.g., 0.2) yielded the highest real-world success rates, implying that retaining a minimal level of teacher correction can stabilize the policy against real-world noise.
- **RGB-based policies (Table IV):** For the "Lift Banana" task, an RGB-based policy distilled from EXPERTGEN achieved an 80% real-world task success rate, demonstrating robustness to visual distractors. A standard BC policy trained directly on scripted demonstrations failed to achieve any successful trials, highlighting the stronger training signals provided by an expert teacher for robust visuomotor behaviors.

**Additional Analyses:**
- **Imperfect Behavior Priors (Figure 6):** The initial scripted policies provided incomplete state coverage, which limited the base diffusion policy's success rates for certain initial configurations. EXPERTGEN consistently improved performance by refining these priors.
- **Number of Imperfect Demonstrations (Figure 7):** EXPERTGEN's performance remained largely consistent when initialized with 200 to 1000 imperfect demonstrations. A noticeable performance drop occurred only when the dataset was reduced to 50 demonstrations.
- **Choice of RL Algorithms (Table I):** FastTD3, the off-policy RL algorithm, consistently outperformed PPO when integrated into EXPERTGEN, attributed to its efficiency in reusing action-chunked rollouts and more stable optimization under sparse rewards.
- **Human Motions as Behavior Priors (Figure 8 & Table XV):** Using human demonstrations (augmented with SkillMimicGen) as behavior priors significantly improved the performance of both the base diffusion policy and EXPERTGEN on tasks like "Stack Banana on Can," suggesting that human priors can offer richer diversity and adaptability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EXPERTGEN represents a contribution to the field of robotic learning by offering a scalable framework for acquiring expert policies and transferring them to real-world robots. Its significance is reflected in several aspects:

- **Scalability without Reward Engineering:** The framework addresses a long-standing challenge in reinforcement learning by demonstrating effective policy acquisition with sparse, task-level rewards, eliminating the need for complex and time-consuming reward engineering. This contributes to making RL-based robotics solutions more accessible and scalable across diverse tasks and domains.
- **Robustness and Generalization:** EXPERTGEN produces policies that exhibit spatial generalization and robust recovery from failure states. These capabilities are critical for deploying robots in dynamic and uncertain real-world environments, where deviations from nominal conditions are common.
- **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The integration of diffusion steering RL for expert policy refinement and DAgger with aggressive domain randomization for visuomotor policy distillation enables robust zero-shot transfer from simulation to physical hardware. This is a crucial step towards bridging the sim-to-real gap, allowing for efficient policy development in controlled virtual environments before real-world deployment.
- **Leveraging Imperfect Data:** The ability of EXPERTGEN to initiate policy learning from imperfect behavior priors, whether generated by LLMs or synthesized from reversed demonstrations, is practical. It acknowledges that perfectly expert or comprehensive datasets are often unavailable, thereby expanding the utility of existing or easily acquirable sub-optimal data.
- **Potential Impact on VLA Models:** By providing a scalable source of high-quality, robust, and generalizable robotics data, EXPERTGEN has the potential to enhance the physical execution component of vision-language-action (VLA) models. This could contribute to realizing more capable and versatile robots that can translate semantic understanding into reliable physical interactions.

The paper identifies a limitation in that the learned expert policies remain constrained by the coverage of the initial imperfect behavior priors. If a prior lacks specific behaviors, performance may degrade in scenarios requiring qualitatively novel skills not represented in the initial data. Future work could explore more flexible generative priors, such as classifier-free guidance, to synthesize novel motions beyond the demonstration distribution. This could potentially enable more effective behavior transfer across tasks and reduce the reliance on task-specific demonstrations, further enhancing the framework's versatility.
