# Humanoid-DART: Humanoid Loco-Manipulation using Diffusion-guided Augmentation through Relabeling and Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> The Humanoid-DART framework presents a self-supervised, iterative approach for humanoid robots to acquire diverse, goal-conditioned loco-manipulation skills from a sparse set of initial demonstrations. This system successfully deployed learned behaviors on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid, demonstrating robust task-space coverage up to 96.6% and generalization beyond the initial demonstration range.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid loco-manipulation methods require extensive human demonstrations, which are costly and difficult to scale for contact-rich and diverse tasks.
- The combinatorial complexity of task spaces involving robot body motion, object states, and environmental interactions limits the generality of current imitation learning approaches.
- Generating dynamically feasible and goal-conditioned motions from minimal data for complex humanoid behaviors remains a significant challenge.

## Method

- **Humanoid-DART** employs an iterative curriculum that bootstraps a ==goal-conditioned Diffusion Transformer== (motion generator) with a physics-based Reinforcement Learning policy (motion tracker).
- Kinematic trajectories generated for sampled goals are evaluated by the motion tracker in simulation, and successful (re-labeled) rollouts are added to an 'elite archive' to progressively expand the skill set.
- The motion generator incorporates a dual-branch backbone and structured partial unmasking for enhanced expressivity, while the motion tracker uses PPO with domain randomization for robust policy learning and sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- Learned loco-manipulation trajectories were successfully deployed on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid for push, kick, and pick-and-place tasks, confirming dynamic feasibility and hardware transferability.
- The framework achieved near-complete task-space coverage (e.g., **96.6%** for pick-and-place) from as few as four initial demonstrations, demonstrating generalization to goals 4-5 times further than the base data.
- Humanoid-DART outperformed baseline methods in task-space coverage across all evaluated tasks, showcasing its superior ability to expand the repertoire of achievable behaviors, despite sometimes having lower average fitness due to broader task-space exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An iterative feedback loop between a kinematic motion generator and a physics-based tracking policy effectively enables structured exploration and learning of complex loco-manipulation skills from sparse initial data.
> - Decomposition of the problem into motion generation and motion tracking provides dense, task-agnostic supervision and reduces the computational burden on each component, leading to efficient skill acquisition.
> - Specific architectural enhancements in the diffusion model, such as the dual-branch backbone and structured partial unmasking, are crucial for generating kinematically consistent, goal-conditioned motions with improved coordination between locomotion and manipulation.
