# EmbodiedEval: Evaluate Multimodal LLMs as Embodied Agents

> [!summary] Summary
> EMBODIEDEVAL is a comprehensive benchmark from Tsinghua University designed to evaluate Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as embodied agents in interactive 3D environments. Evaluations reveal a substantial performance gap between state-of-the-art MLLMs and humans, particularly in long-horizon tasks, complex interactions, and spatial reasoning.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) largely focus on non-interactive scenarios or lack the diversity needed to comprehensively test embodied intelligence.
- There is a significant gap between MLLMs' perceptual capabilities (understanding static visual inputs) and the requirements for active interaction, decision-making, and action execution in dynamic 3D environments.
- Current evaluation methods do not adequately assess complex embodied capabilities such as ego-centric perception, visual grounding, spatial reasoning, episodic memory, and long-horizon planning in MLLMs.

## Method

- **EMBODIEDEVAL** introduces a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating MLLMs as embodied agents within interactive 3D simulations, leveraging the ==LEGENT platform== developed by Tsinghua University.
- It features a unified evaluation framework where MLLMs receive ==egocentric visual observations== and ==discrete action options==, designed to assess diverse interactions, tasks, and scenes.
- The benchmark includes **328 meticulously constructed tasks** across ==five categories==: Navigation, Object Interaction, Social Interaction, Attribute Question Answering, and Spatial Question Answering, built from diverse scenes and expert-annotated.

## Results

- GPT-4o, the best-performing MLLM, achieved an overall success rate of only **25.00%** and a Goal-condition Success (GcS) score of **32.42%**, significantly trailing non-expert human agents (**97.26%** success rate).
- MLLM performance dropped notably for interaction tasks, with GPT-4o achieving only **10.11%** success for Object Interaction and **11.76%** for Social Interaction.
- Providing more historical images as input often decreased performance for models like Gemini-Flash, suggesting difficulty processing and integrating extensive sequential egocentric visual inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current MLLMs struggle significantly with long-horizon tasks, showing a decline in success as task length increases and difficulty integrating extensive sequential egocentric visual inputs.
> - Interaction tasks (object and social) are a major bottleneck for MLLMs, indicating a weak understanding of object affordances and proper visual grounding.
> - Common errors include hallucination in grounding, insufficient exploration strategies, poor spatial reasoning, and ineffective action planning, highlighting core limitations in embodied intelligence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen remarkable advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) and, more recently, Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which integrate capabilities across different modalities like vision and language. Early MLLMs, exemplified by models like GPT-4V and LLaVA, demonstrated strong abilities in understanding and reasoning over static visual inputs, such as images and videos, primarily for tasks like captioning, visual question answering, and logical inference.

However, a significant gap exists between these "perceptual" capabilities and the requirements for truly intelligent embodied agents. An embodied agent needs to interpret multimodal inputs not just to answer questions about a scene, but to actively interact with it, make decisions, and execute actions in a dynamic 3D environment. This necessitates complex capabilities such as ego-centric perception (understanding the world from the agent's viewpoint), visual grounding (linking language to specific objects and locations in the visual scene), spatial reasoning (understanding relationships, distances, and layouts), episodic memory (recalling past observations and actions), and long-horizon planning (devising multi-step action sequences to achieve goals).

Existing benchmarks for evaluating MLLMs have largely focused on non-interactive scenarios or lacked the diversity required to comprehensively test embodied intelligence. While some benchmarks like ALFRED or BEHAVIOR provide interactive environments, they are often task-specific (e.g., "pick and place") and might impose rigid input/output formats that are not conducive to evaluating general-purpose MLLMs. Other approaches, which represent environments purely with textual descriptions, downplay crucial visual grounding and spatial reasoning skills.

EMBODIEDEVAL directly addresses this critical void. It positions itself as the "first comprehensive benchmark for evaluating MLLMs' embodied capabilities in interactive environments." The work contributes to the burgeoning field of embodied AI, where the goal is to develop intelligent agents that can perceive, act, and learn in physical (or simulated physical) spaces. By creating a unified simulation and evaluation framework specifically tailored for MLLMs, EMBODIEDEVAL aims to bridge the gap between powerful perception/reasoning models and practical embodied agents. Its emphasis on diverse interactions, tasks, and scenes, coupled with an MLLM-friendly discrete action space and egocentric visual input, represents a significant step forward in establishing a robust evaluation standard for this rapidly evolving research area. The paper's Table 1 clearly illustrates how EMBODIEDEVAL surpasses previous benchmarks in terms of scene diversity, task diversity, discrete action space compatibility, egocentric vision, navigation, object interaction, social interaction, and question-answering capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind EMBODIEDEVAL stems from the recognized limitations of existing benchmarks in adequately assessing the full spectrum of capabilities required for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to function as effective embodied agents. While MLLMs have demonstrated impressive advancements in understanding and reasoning over static visual and textual information, their ability to interact dynamically with complex 3D environments remains largely unevaluated comprehensively.

The key objectives of the EMBODIEDEVAL project are:

1. **Develop a Comprehensive and Challenging Benchmark:** To create a novel benchmark, EMBODIEDEVAL, that is robust enough to push the boundaries of current MLLMs in embodied tasks. This involves moving beyond static image/video analysis to interactive, dynamic environments.
2. **Facilitate Evaluation of Interactive Capabilities:** To enable the assessment of MLLMs' ability to interpret multimodal inputs (specifically egocentric visual information) and translate them into a sequence of meaningful actions within an interactive 3D simulation environment. This is crucial for evaluating real-world applicability.
3. **Ensure Diversity in Interactions, Tasks, and Scenes:**
 - **Diverse Interactions:** To provide a simulation framework that supports a broad range of interactions with both objects and human avatars in realistic 3D settings, requiring agents to gather information or modify the environment.
 - **Diverse Tasks:** To move beyond predefined task templates found in older benchmarks by systematically generating and carefully selecting novel, high-quality tasks that assess a wide spectrum of abilities, including complex reasoning, memory, and planning.
 - **Diverse Scenes:** To incorporate a wide variety of scenes, from typical household rooms to larger residences and public areas like gyms, stores, and offices, to minimize biases towards specific scene types and promote generalization.
4. **Provide a Unified Simulation and Evaluation Framework:** To offer a standardized, efficient, and user-friendly platform tailored for MLLMs, featuring a unified input-output space that emphasizes visual information as the primary connection between the agent and the environment, and utilizing a discrete action space compatible with current MLLM architectures.
5. **Identify Limitations and Guide Future Development:** Through evaluating state-of-the-art MLLMs on this benchmark, to reveal their significant shortcomings in embodied capabilities compared to humans, thereby providing crucial insights and guiding directions for future research and development in MLLMs. The goal is to pinpoint specific areas where MLLMs need improvement, such as grounding, spatial reasoning, planning, and exploration.

In essence, EMBODIEDEVAL aims to be the definitive testbed for measuring how well MLLMs can "think and act" in a simulated physical world, moving beyond passive observation to active engagement.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EMBODIEDEVAL employs a carefully designed methodology to ensure a comprehensive and challenging evaluation of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) as embodied agents.

#### 3.1. Evaluation Formulation and Framework

The core of EMBODIEDEVAL is formulated as a **decision-making problem** within an interactive 3D simulation.
- **Simulator:** The benchmark leverages the **LEGENT platform** (Cheng et al., 2024c), a self-developed open platform designed for embodied agents, providing rich and interactive 3D environments.
- **Input Space:** For each step of an evaluation episode, the MLLM agent receives:
 - A textual task description (`g`).
 - A list of available action options (`C`).
 - An ego-centric visual observation history (`M_o`), consisting of multiple first-person images or a video capturing the interaction process. Crucially, the system design emphasizes visual information as the most accessible and general medium, deliberately **excluding additional environmental state information**.
- **Output Space:** The MLLM is required to choose a single discrete action (`a`) from the provided `C`. The action space is intentionally high-level and discrete to be feasible for current MLLMs. It encompasses:
 - **Movement:** Constrained to a pre-constructed navigation graph, allowing agents to make directional decisions (move forward, turn left/right, look up/down) between navigation points, rather than continuous 3D positions.
 - **Interaction:** Uses an open-vocabulary, discrete interaction space. Actions like "pick up," "wash," or "hand over" are defined with brief text, operable objects, and success conditions. This abstracts away the complexity of continuous manipulation.
 - **Answering:** For question-answering tasks, the agent selects an answer from a set of pre-annotated, challenging textual responses (typically eight options).
- **Evaluation Process:** An episode unfolds iteratively: the MLLM predicts an action based on the current context, the environment executes the action (updating state and providing new observations/feedback), and this cycle continues until success criteria are met or maximum steps are exceeded (set to 24 steps per task).
- **Success Criteria:** Task success is determined by a set of predefined **predicate functions** (e.g., `agent_at`, `grab`, `special_action_success`) that map the simulator's state to a boolean value, allowing for both final state and process-based evaluation (e.g., `agent_pass` checks if a point was visited).

#### 3.2. Task Categories

EMBODIEDEVAL classifies tasks into five categories to comprehensively assess different facets of embodied intelligence:
1. **Navigation:** Agent must navigate to target locations or objects based on coarse-grained or fine-grained natural language instructions.
2. **Object Interaction:** Agent modifies the environment by interacting directly with objects (e.g., moving, opening, operating devices).
3. **Social Interaction:** Involves human-agent interactions like item delivery, perspective-taking, or interpreting human feedback.
4. **Attribute Question Answering:** Agent explores the environment to answer questions about object and scene attributes (e.g., color, material, function, quantity).
5. **Spatial Question Answering:** Agent answers questions requiring spatial reasoning about size, position, distance, layout, and relationships through actions and observations.

#### 3.3. Benchmark Construction

The dataset construction is a meticulous, multi-stage process involving significant manual effort and quality control:
1. **Scene Collection:**
 - **Diverse Sources:** Scenes are sourced from Objaverse, AI2THOR, Habitat Synthetic Scenes Dataset (HSSD), and Sketchfab.
 - **Procedural Generation & Refinement:** For Objaverse, 3D assets are curated and then procedurally placed to generate indoor scenes. These procedurally generated scenes are then refined manually using a self-developed runtime scene editor to ensure realism and organization. Public spaces from HSSD and Sketchfab are also integrated, and all scenes are formatted consistently.
2. **Task Collection:**
 - **Seed Tasks:** Over 30 existing datasets provide initial "seed tasks" for each of the five categories.
 - **LLM-Assisted Generation:** Large Language Models (Claude, ChatGPT) are prompted to generate diverse task examples based on these seeds, specifically incorporating requirements for complex capabilities like grounding, episodic memory, spatial reasoning, quantitative reasoning, common sense, and planning.
 - **Manual Selection:** From this large pool, over 300 distinct, high-quality candidate tasks are manually selected to ensure diversity and avoid repetition.
3. **Task Annotation:**
 - **Expert Annotators:** Eight expert annotators are systematically trained and employed for the manual annotation process, ensuring precision and consistency.
 - **Annotation Workflow:** For each task, annotators:
 - Align a suitable scene from the collected pool.
 - Configure the output space: adjusting navigation points, defining discrete interaction actions (including distractors), and writing challenging multi-choice answer options (8 per QA task).
 - Define precise success criteria using predicate functions.
 - Set initial agent/person positions and orientations.
 - Crucially, they "run the annotated tasks in the simulator" to confirm feasibility and ensure tasks can be successfully completed.
 - **Quality Control:** Each annotated task undergoes independent review by at least three evaluators. Expert demonstrations are created, and performance with non-expert human participants is assessed to validate feasibility and difficulty.

This rigorous construction process results in a dataset of 328 tasks across 125 unique scenes, involving 575 predicate instances and 1533 varied options, with an average task requiring 10.72 steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of state-of-the-art Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) on EMBODIEDEVAL reveals significant insights into their current capabilities and profound limitations as embodied agents.

#### 4.1. Performance Gap between Current MLLMs and Humans

- **Significant Shortfall:** The most striking finding is the substantial performance disparity between MLLMs and human agents. The best-performing MLLM, **GPT-4o, achieves an overall success rate of only 25.00%** and a Goal-condition Success (GcS) score of 32.42%. In stark contrast, **non-expert human agents achieve a near-perfect 97.26% success rate**. This highlights that tasks considered trivial for humans pose immense challenges for current AI models in interactive 3D environments.
- **Efficiency Issues (SPL):** The lower Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) scores for MLLMs further emphasize their inefficiency in task execution, indicating struggles in finding optimal and direct solutions compared to human demonstrations.
- **Open-Source vs. Proprietary Models:** Open-source MLLMs demonstrate an even larger performance gap, with the top open-source model, LLaVA-OneVision-72B, achieving only 12.80% overall success, barely competitive with proprietary models.

#### 4.2. Model Performance Across Different Task Types

- **Varying Strengths:** MLLM performance varies considerably across the five task categories. Proprietary models like GPT-4o show relatively stronger performance in Attribute Question Answering (35.79% success) and Navigation tasks (31.03% success).
- **Challenges in Reasoning-Intensive Tasks:** Performance drops notably for tasks requiring deeper spatial reasoning and interaction. For example, success rates for Spatial Question Answering (32.69% GcS for GPT-4o, but much lower for others) are generally lower than Attribute QA, requiring more complex interpretation of spatial relationships.
- **Interaction Tasks as a Major Bottleneck:** Performance on **Object Interaction (10.11% for GPT-4o)** and **Social Interaction (11.76% for GPT-4o)** tasks is consistently and significantly lower across all evaluated models. This underscores the difficulty MLLMs face in scenarios demanding a nuanced understanding of object affordances and social cues, as well as multi-step manipulation.

#### 4.3. Performance Analysis - Specific Challenges

- **Long-Horizon Tasks:**
 - **Success Rate Decline with Steps:** Models perform relatively better on tasks requiring fewer steps, but their success rates decline significantly as the task length (number of required steps) increases.
 - **Context Handling Issues:** While increasing the maximum allowed steps initially improves success, the gain diminishes rapidly, suggesting that current MLLMs struggle to effectively manage and leverage longer historical contexts.
 - **Multimodal Context Limitations:** Counter-intuitively, providing more historical images as input (e.g., 24 images vs. 1 image) often decreases performance for Gemini-Flash across most task categories, except for Spatial Question Answering where additional visual context can be beneficial. This indicates that current models have difficulty processing and integrating extensive sequential egocentric visual inputs.
- **Interaction Tasks (Further Analysis):**
 - **Low Interaction Success Rate:** Despite varying interaction frequencies (proportion of interaction actions), MLLMs exhibit a consistently low interaction success rate. This means they frequently attempt interactions under incorrect conditions, indicating a weak understanding of affordances (what actions are possible with which objects) and proper grounding. Humans, in contrast, have a very high interaction success rate with lower interaction frequency, demonstrating precise affordance judgment.

#### 4.4. Error Case Analysis

The paper categorizes common MLLM errors into four main types, providing valuable insights into their current shortcomings:
1. **Hallucination in Grounding:** Models misperceive the environment, either "seeing" non-existent objects or failing to detect present ones, leading to incorrect answers or failed interactions.
2. **Insufficient Exploration:** Agents often employ suboptimal exploration strategies, getting stuck in local areas, failing to gather enough information, or answering prematurely due to overconfidence.
3. **Lack of Spatial Reasoning:** Models struggle with fundamental spatial understanding, misinterpreting directional instructions (e.g., "to my left") and having difficulty navigating based on spatial relationships.
4. **Wrong Planning:** Agents exhibit poor state estimation and action planning, resulting in random, repetitive actions (e.g., aimless circling) and an inability to adapt or recover from failed attempts.

In summary, EMBODIEDEVAL rigorously demonstrates that while MLLMs possess impressive perceptual and language capabilities, they are far from achieving human-level performance in complex, interactive, embodied tasks. Their limitations in long-horizon planning, robust multimodal context understanding, precise grounding, spatial reasoning, and dynamic interaction abilities are significant bottlenecks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EMBODIEDEVAL represents a significant contribution to the fields of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), embodied AI, and robotics. Its significance and potential impact can be understood across several dimensions:

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Pioneering Comprehensive Benchmark:** EMBODIEDEVAL is the first interactive benchmark specifically designed for MLLMs that offers comprehensive embodied tasks. This fills a critical void in the evaluation landscape, moving beyond static image/video understanding to dynamic, interactive scenarios.
- **Revealing Fundamental Limitations:** By rigorously evaluating state-of-the-art MLLMs, the benchmark starkly highlights their profound shortcomings in embodied intelligence compared to humans. It pinpoints specific weaknesses in areas like visual grounding, spatial reasoning, long-horizon planning, and object/social interaction, which are crucial for real-world agency.
- **Standardized Evaluation Framework:** The provision of a unified simulation and evaluation framework, tailored with a discrete action space and egocentric visual input, offers a standardized and efficient platform for the community. This allows for fair comparison and systematic tracking of progress in MLLM development for embodied applications.
- **Driving Research Direction:** The detailed error analysis (hallucinations, insufficient exploration, poor spatial reasoning, flawed planning) provides concrete, actionable insights that can directly guide future research efforts. It identifies the most pressing challenges that need to be addressed to advance MLLMs towards true embodied intelligence.
- **Diverse and High-Quality Dataset:** The meticulous, multi-source scene collection and LLM-assisted, expert-annotated task construction process ensure a diverse, challenging, and high-quality dataset. This dataset's variety in scenes, tasks, and interactions makes it a robust testbed for model generalization.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Accelerating Embodied AI Development:** By clearly defining the current performance ceiling and highlighting specific weaknesses, EMBODIEDEVAL will spur concentrated research on improving MLLMs' embodied capabilities. This could lead to faster innovation in areas like robust perception from egocentric views, complex action planning, and adaptive behavior in dynamic environments.
- **Bridging to Real-World Robotics:** While conducted in simulation, the benchmark's focus on realistic 3D environments, diverse interactions, and human-like tasks provides a critical stepping stone for real-world robotic applications. Models that perform well on EMBODIEDEVAL are more likely to exhibit generalizable embodied skills relevant to physical robots.
- **Informing New Training Paradigms:** The identified challenges (e.g., handling long multimodal sequences, spatial reasoning) suggest a need for novel training data and methodologies. This could encourage the creation of more embodied trajectory data, egocentric video datasets, and advanced synthetic data generation techniques that explicitly teach interaction, affordances, and complex planning.
- **Advancing Human-AI Collaboration:** As MLLMs become more capable embodied agents, this benchmark will contribute to developing AI that can understand and respond to human instructions in shared physical spaces, enhancing potential for human-robot collaboration in various settings.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose Agents:** The ultimate goal of AI is often considered to be the development of general-purpose intelligent agents. EMBODIEDEVAL is a crucial step in evaluating MLLMs' potential in this direction, moving them beyond mere knowledge systems to active participants in the world.

In conclusion, EMBODIEDEVAL serves as a foundational benchmark that not only quantifies the significant gap between current MLLMs and human embodied intelligence but also provides a clear roadmap for future research, ultimately pushing the boundaries of what MLLMs can achieve in interactive, real-world scenarios.
