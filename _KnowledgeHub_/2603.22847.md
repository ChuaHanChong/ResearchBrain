# Rethinking Token-Level Policy Optimization for Multimodal Chain-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> Perception-Exploration Policy Optimization (PEPO) introduces a token-level framework for multimodal Chain-of-Thought reasoning in Large Vision-Language Models. This method refines policy updates by integrating a supervision-free perception prior with token-level entropy through a smooth gating mechanism. PEPO improved average accuracy by +3.67 points over GRPO on geometry/logic reasoning for Qwen2.5-VL-3B, showed up to +5.32 points gain on few-shot classification, and operated with less than 1% computational overhead.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods optimize Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) at a coarse, sequence-level, applying uniform advantage scores to all tokens.
- Token-level methods for LLMs primarily capture textual uncertainty (entropy) but lack mechanisms to account for visual semantics or the specific relevance of tokens to visual input in LVLMs.
- Prior perception-aware RL methods for LVLMs often incur significant computational overhead from auxiliary branches, hindering efficiency and compatibility with acceleration frameworks.

## Method

- PEPO develops a ==token-level RL framework== that derives a ==perception prior== (visual similarity) from hidden states and an 'exploration score' (token entropy) from model logits.
- It fuses these scores using a ==smooth gating mechanism==, where entropy's modulation is conditioned on visually grounded tokens to create adaptive token-level weights.
- The framework modulates sequence-level advantages with these fused token-level weights, generating precise ==token-level advantages== to direct policy gradient updates towards visually relevant and explorative tokens.

## Results

- PEPO consistently improved average accuracy across diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, achieving **+3.67** points over GRPO on geometry/logic reasoning for Qwen2.5-VL-3B and up to **+5.32** points on few-shot classification for FGVC Aircraft.
- The framework demonstrated superior performance and stability compared to GRPO, DAPO, and High-Entropy RL baselines, particularly in challenging visual grounding and out-of-domain puzzle reasoning tasks.
- PEPO operates with marginal computational overhead, adding less than **1%** to the total RL update step time, while scaling effectively with larger datasets like ViRL39K.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Successful multimodal reasoning involves a dynamic interplay between perceptual grounding (tokens aligned with visual evidence) and exploratory inference (tokens considering alternative reasoning paths).
> - Visual similarity (from hidden-state correlation with vision tokens) and token entropy (from output logits) capture complementary aspects of multimodal CoT: perceptual grounding and reasoning uncertainty.
> - Balancing perception and exploration via token-level advantage modulation leads to more stable and effective policy optimization than uniform sequence-level or purely text-based token-level approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) has significantly advanced capabilities across various vision-language tasks, including question answering, visual reasoning, and reasoning grounding. A notable area of focus for enhancing these models is Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which involves constructing multi-step inference trajectories that interleave perceptual grounding with linguistic reasoning.

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) frameworks, such such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and DeepSeek-R1 (DAPO), have been employed to refine CoT reasoning in both Large Language Models (LLMs) and LVLMs. However, current RLVR methods primarily operate at a coarse, sequence-level granularity. This means that an advantage score, derived from an outcome-based reward (e.g., final answer correctness), is applied uniformly across all tokens in a generated CoT sequence. This approach overlooks the varying contributions of individual intermediate steps and fails to distinguish between tokens that are critically grounded in visual information versus those that represent general textual exploration.

In text-only LLMs, some approaches have introduced token-level entropy advantages to encourage exploration at uncertain CoT steps. However, these entropy-based methods mainly capture textual uncertainty and do not inherently account for visual semantics or the specific relevance of tokens to visual input. While perception-aware RL methods for LVLMs have emerged to incorporate visual signals, they often introduce additional computational overhead through auxiliary branches (e.g., masking) or attention-based measures, which may not be compatible with efficient acceleration frameworks.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a fine-grained, token-level policy optimization approach specifically designed for multimodal CoT. It integrates perceptual grounding with exploratory dynamics, aiming to provide more precise optimization signals than previous sequence-level or purely text-based token-level methods, and without incurring the overhead of auxiliary perception branches. It positions itself as a refinement to existing RLVR pipelines, offering a method to more effectively align reasoning with visual evidence while also promoting beneficial exploration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from an observed inefficiency in current Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods when applied to Multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). The authors identified several specific issues:

1. **Coarse Granularity of Optimization:** Existing RLVR methods, such as GRPO, typically optimize CoT at a sequence level. A single advantage score, often based on the correctness of the final answer, is uniformly applied to all tokens within a reasoning trajectory. This approach fails to differentiate the contributions of individual tokens or intermediate reasoning steps, treating all parts of a CoT as equally important, regardless of their visual grounding or their role in exploratory inference. This can lead to an "optimization imbalance" where irrelevant textual reasoning receives as much gradient signal as crucial perceptual grounding.
2. **Insufficient Multimodal Discrimination in Token-Level Methods:** While some prior work in Large Language Models (LLMs) introduced token-level entropy advantages to encourage exploration in uncertain steps, these methods primarily capture textual uncertainty. They exhibit limited correspondence to visual semantics and are insufficient in distinguishing tokens based on their visual relevance in an LVLM context. Multimodal reasoning requires more than just language-based uncertainty; it demands an understanding of how tokens relate to the visual input.
3. **Computational Overhead of Existing Perception-Aware Methods:** Previous attempts to incorporate visual signals into RL for LVLMs often relied on auxiliary components, such as masking branches or explicit attention-based measures. These additions can increase computational overhead, making them less efficient or incompatible with optimized model acceleration frameworks. The authors aimed for a solution that leverages existing model states without requiring architectural modifications.
4. **Complementary Nature of Perception and Exploration:** The authors' preliminary token-level analysis revealed that successful multimodal reasoning is characterized by a dynamic interplay between perceptual grounding (where tokens align strongly with visual evidence) and exploratory inference (where the model considers alternative reasoning paths, often marked by higher uncertainty). Current RLVR frameworks, by treating CoT uniformly or focusing on only one aspect, overlook this fine-grained coupling.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:

1. **Develop a Token-Level Policy Optimization Framework:** To create a novel framework, Perception-Exploration Policy Optimization (PEPO), that refines policy updates at the token level, moving beyond sequence-level supervision.
2. **Derive a Supervision-Free Perception Prior:** To extract a "perception prior" that quantifies the degree of visual grounding for each token. This prior should be derived from the model's hidden states (specifically, hidden-state similarity between response and visual tokens), avoiding the need for additional supervision or auxiliary model branches.
3. **Integrate Perception and Exploration:** To unify the perception prior with token-level entropy (representing reasoning uncertainty) through a smooth gating mechanism. This integration aims to create adaptive token-level weights that reflect both visual relevance and exploratory potential.
4. **Produce Fine-Grained Token-Level Advantages:** To use these fused weights to modulate sequence-level advantages, thereby generating token-level advantages that precisely reweight policy gradient updates toward visually grounded and explorative reasoning tokens.
5. **Ensure Seamless Integration and Efficiency:** To design PEPO such that it integrates seamlessly with existing RLVR frameworks (like GRPO and DAPO) with marginal computational overhead, maintaining training stability, and without requiring architectural changes.
6. **Demonstrate Broad Efficacy:** To validate PEPO's effectiveness and robustness across diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks, including geometry, visual puzzles, visual grounding, and few-shot classification.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research begins with an analysis of token-level dynamics in multimodal reasoning, identifies limitations in existing RLVR frameworks, and then introduces Perception-Exploration Policy Optimization (PEPO) as a solution.

**Background: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**
The paper first contextualizes its work by describing GRPO, a common RL algorithm for LVLMs. GRPO operates by sampling a group of `G` candidate responses for each query. Verifiable rewards `R^(i)` are computed for these responses, and a sequence-level advantage `A^(i)` is derived by comparing `R^(i)` to the mean and standard deviation of rewards within the group. This `A^(i)` is then uniformly applied to all tokens of the `i`-th response in a PPO-style objective. The core limitation highlighted is this uniform application, which fails to capture the heterogeneous contributions of individual tokens.

**Token-Level Analysis of Multimodal Reasoning**
To address this, the authors conducted an empirical analysis on the Geometry3K dataset using Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, focusing on visual similarity and entropy as key indicators.

1. **Visual Similarity Analysis:**
 - **Definition:** For each response token `t`, its visual similarity (`VS_t`) is defined as the mean cosine similarity between its hidden states and the hidden states of all vision tokens, averaged across all model layers. This metric quantifies the perceptual grounding of a token.
 - **Correlation with Correctness:** The analysis evaluated three aggregate metrics: global mean visual similarity (`M_glob`), top-K highest visual similarity (`M_high`), and bottom-K lowest visual similarity (`M_low`). Distributions showed that correct responses were associated with a clear rightward shift (higher scores) in `M_glob` and `M_high`, but negligible shift in `M_low`. This indicated that successful reasoning is characterized by a subset of visually-grounded tokens, rather than a uniform distribution of visual relevance.

2. **Visual-Entropy Complementarity:**
 - **Representation Shift:** A controlled experiment compared hidden-state shifts under image removal for tokens ranked by visual similarity versus those ranked by entropy. High visual similarity tokens exhibited substantially larger hidden-state shifts when the image was removed, confirming their strong dependence on visual evidence. High-entropy tokens showed more stable representations, suggesting entropy primarily reflects linguistic uncertainty.
 - **Semantic Patterns:** Word clouds revealed that high-entropy tokens were enriched with "reasoning-transition" expressions (e.g., verification, correction, analysis), indicating decision points. High visual similarity tokens, conversely, concentrated on "perceptually-grounded" concepts (e.g., geometric entities, spatial attributes). This analysis established that visual similarity and entropy capture complementary aspects: perceptual grounding and reasoning uncertainty, respectively.

**Perception-Exploration Policy Optimization (PEPO)**
Building on this analysis, PEPO was designed as a token-level RL framework with the following components:

1. **Perception Modeling:**
 - The `VS_t` (visual similarity score) computed during the token-level analysis is directly adopted as the perception prior. It is derived from hidden-state correlations between response and vision tokens across layers, making it lightweight and supervision-free. Higher `VS_t` indicates stronger perceptual alignment and greater importance.

2. **Exploration Modeling:**
 - Token-level entropy (`H_t`) is used as the exploration score. It is calculated from the output logits of the policy model for each token. Higher `H_t` signifies greater uncertainty or potential transition points in the reasoning process, where exploration of alternative paths could be beneficial.

3. **Perception-Exploration Fusion:**
 - To combine `VS_t` and `H_t`, both scores are min-max normalized within each response to `ˆVS_t` and `ˆH_t` to ensure comparability.
 - A smooth gating mechanism is then employed:
 - An intermediate joint score `ˆg_t` is calculated as `ˆVS_t + ˆH_t` minus its mean across tokens in the response. This mean-centering helps prevent global scaling bias.
 - This `ˆg_t` is passed through a `tanh` activation and linearly scaled by `(1 + α tanh(ˆg_t))`, where `α` is a gate strength hyperparameter.
 - Crucially, this gate is then multiplied by `VS_t` to produce the final token-level weight `w_t`. This multiplication ensures that the entropy-driven modulation is conditioned on visually grounded tokens, preventing the amplification of high-entropy but visually irrelevant tokens.
 - Finally, `w_t` is scaled such that its sum over a response equals the response length `T` (i.e., `E[w_t] = 1`), preserving the overall advantage scale.

4. **Token-Level Advantage:**
 - The fused weight `w_t` modulates the sequence-level GRPO advantage `A^(i)`. The token-level advantage `A^(i)_t` is defined as `((1 - λ) + λw^(i)_t) * A^(i)`.
 - `λ` is a linearly increasing annealing coefficient that controls the strength of token-level modulation, gradually increasing from 0 to 1 over training steps. This gradual introduction helps stabilize training.
 - This `A^(i)_t` replaces the uniform `A^(i)` in the policy gradient update, directing optimization towards visually relevant and explorative tokens.

**Integration and Efficiency:**
PEPO is designed for seamless integration with existing RLVR frameworks like GRPO (resulting in PEPO_G) and DAPO (resulting in PEPO_D). The computation of visual similarity and entropy leverages existing hidden states and logits, incurring marginal computational overhead (reported as less than 1% of total step time).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across diverse multimodal reasoning benchmarks consistently demonstrate that Perception-Exploration Policy Optimization (PEPO) enhances the performance of Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) while maintaining stable training dynamics. The key findings are summarized below:

**Overall Performance Improvement:**
Across all evaluated benchmarks and two different LVLM architectures (Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct and InternVL3-2B-Instruct), PEPO consistently outperformed established RL baselines, including Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), DeepSeek-R1 (DAPO), and a High-Entropy RL variant. The High-Entropy RL baseline often exhibited unstable optimization and weaker multimodal reasoning, indicating that visual grounding is a crucial complement to entropy-based exploration in the multimodal context.

**Geometry and Logic Reasoning (Table 1):**
- On Qwen2.5-VL-3B, PEPO improved the average accuracy across Geometry3K, MathVista-mini, MathVerse-mini, and LogicVista by +3.67 points over GRPO and +0.45 points over DAPO.
- On InternVL3-2B, the improvements were more substantial, with PEPO achieving +3.51 points over GRPO and +5.15 points over DAPO on average.
- These gains were particularly evident on MathVerse and LogicVista, benchmarks that necessitate integrated visual and symbolic reasoning.

**Visual Grounding (Table 2):**
- For in-domain performance on RefCOCO, PEPO showed comparable or slightly higher IoU@50 scores than GRPO and DAPO.
- More significant improvements were observed on the out-of-domain LISA-Grounding dataset, where PEPO_G achieved a +0.86 IoU@50 improvement over GRPO. This suggests that the visual similarity weighting mechanism aids in aligning textual and visual representations, particularly under domain shift. The High-Entropy RL model reportedly collapsed in multiple runs, yielding no valid results.

**Few-shot Classification (Table 3):**
- PEPO consistently outperformed GRPO across 1-shot, 2-shot, and 4-shot settings on FGVC Aircraft and Flower102 datasets.
- PEPO_G achieved average gains of +5.32 points on FGVC Aircraft and +1.46 points on Flower102 compared to GRPO. These results indicate that fine-grained advantage modulation enhances training effectiveness in data-limited scenarios, leading to improved generalization.

**Visual Puzzle Reasoning (Table 4):**
- Similar to geometry reasoning, PEPO achieved consistent improvements on both in-domain (PuzzleVQA) and out-of-domain (AlgoPuzzleVQA) puzzle reasoning benchmarks.
- On both Qwen2.5-VL-3B and InternVL3-2B backbones, PEPO variants yielded higher accuracy than GRPO and DAPO. The gains on AlgoPuzzleVQA suggest that PEPO's visual similarity weighting facilitates visuospatial reasoning and transfer to novel puzzle configurations.

**Scalability Analysis (Table 5):**
- When trained on the large-scale ViRL39K dataset and evaluated across multiple reasoning benchmarks, PEPO_G achieved higher average performance than GRPO and PAPO_G.
- Notable gains were observed on perception-intensive datasets like MathVista and Counting, suggesting that PEPO scales effectively with larger datasets and complex reasoning tasks, where visual grounding is critical.

**Efficiency and Computational Overhead (Table 6):**
- PEPO demonstrated a negligible computational overhead, with the additional time for weight computation (`ρ`) remaining below 1% of the total RL update step across all benchmarks.
- Throughput (iterations per second) was comparable to or slightly higher than GRPO. The observation that PEPO tends to produce shorter responses during training partially offset the minor overhead, contributing to improved effective throughput.

**Ablation Study (Tables 7, 8, 9, 10):**
- **Component Analysis:** The full PEPO framework (integrating both visual similarity for perception and token entropy for exploration) consistently achieved the best results, outperforming variants that relied solely on perception or exploration. This confirmed the complementary roles of both components in stable and robust multimodal reasoning.
- **Sensitivity to α:** Performance remained robust across a range of `α` values (gate strength), indicating limited sensitivity to precise tuning of this hyperparameter. Small positive `α` values generally led to the strongest gains.
- **Weighting Design:** Removing key elements of the weighting strategy, such as gradual scheduling, per-sample min-max normalization, or the gated fusion mechanism (e.g., replacing it with simple additive fusion), consistently degraded performance. This highlights the importance of these design choices for stable and effective optimization.
- **Layer Selection for Visual Similarity:** Aggregating hidden states across all transformer layers for visual similarity computation yielded the highest accuracy, outperforming approaches that restricted feature extraction to shallow, intermediate, or deep layers only. This indicates that visual relevance signals are distributed throughout the model's hierarchy.

**Qualitative Comparisons and Training Dynamics (Figure 5 & 6):**
- Qualitative examples demonstrated that PEPO-trained models generated more coherent, visually grounded reasoning chains and produced correct answers where GRPO-trained models exhibited perception failures or inconsistent reasoning.
- Training curves showed that PEPO_G achieved consistently higher training rewards and converged to a stronger plateau. It exhibited a more gradual decrease in mean response length, a progressively increasing mean visual similarity, and a more moderate entropy level compared to GRPO, indicating more stable optimization dynamics and improved visual alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Perception-Exploration Policy Optimization (PEPO), a token-level policy optimization framework for multimodal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning in Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be understood in several key areas:

**Advancement in Multimodal RL:** PEPO represents a methodological advancement in applying reinforcement learning to LVLMs. By introducing token-level advantage estimation that considers both perceptual grounding (via hidden-state similarity) and reasoning exploration (via token entropy), it moves beyond the limitations of coarse, sequence-level reward propagation prevalent in existing RLVR methods. This fine-grained optimization allows for more precise credit assignment during learning, aligning gradient updates with the specific contributions of individual tokens to multimodal reasoning.

**Principled Integration of Modalities:** The work highlights and leverages the complementary roles of visual perception and linguistic exploration in multimodal reasoning. Unlike prior approaches that might treat these aspects separately or introduce them with significant overhead, PEPO integrates them within a unified framework without requiring auxiliary branches or additional supervision. The design of the smooth gating mechanism, which conditions entropy modulation on visually grounded tokens, provides a principled way to balance these two crucial factors.

**Enhanced LVLM Performance and Robustness:** Extensive experiments across diverse benchmarks—including geometry reasoning, visual grounding, visual puzzle solving, and few-shot classification—demonstrate consistent and robust performance improvements over strong RL baselines. This indicates that PEPO can generalize well across different types of multimodal tasks, enhancing both reasoning accuracy and the ability to transfer knowledge to out-of-domain scenarios. The observed stability in training dynamics, as evidenced by more consistent reward curves and controlled entropy levels, further underscores its robustness.

**Efficiency and Scalability:** A notable aspect of PEPO is its efficiency. It achieves performance gains with only marginal computational overhead, integrating seamlessly into existing RLVR pipelines like GRPO and DAPO. This efficiency makes it practical for deployment and scalability, as demonstrated by its performance on larger datasets like ViRL39K. This suggests that more sophisticated token-level optimization does not necessarily imply prohibitive computational costs.

**Implications for LVLM Development:**
- **Improved Interpretability:** By assigning differentiated importance to tokens based on their visual grounding and exploratory value, PEPO could implicitly lead to more interpretable reasoning chains, where critical visual anchors and decision points are more explicitly reinforced.
- **Data Efficiency:** The consistent gains in few-shot classification tasks suggest that PEPO can help LVLMs learn more effectively from limited data, potentially reducing the reliance on massive, meticulously curated datasets for specialized tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a foundation for future research into fine-grained multimodal policy optimization. It could inspire further exploration of other token-level signals (e.g., semantic relevance, causal dependencies) and more sophisticated fusion mechanisms. The methodology could be extended to other multimodal tasks, such as video understanding, tool-augmented reasoning, or embodied AI, where dynamic interaction with visual environments and nuanced decision-making are paramount.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
While effective on tested models (2B/3B LVLMs) and benchmarks, the authors note that the framework's application to significantly larger model backbones (e.g., 7B or above) or to a broader range of complex, long-context multimodal tasks (like video understanding or advanced tool-use scenarios) remains an area for future exploration due to current computational and memory constraints. Addressing these limitations would further solidify PEPO's position as a generalizable approach.
