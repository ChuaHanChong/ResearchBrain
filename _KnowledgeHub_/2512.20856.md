# NVIDIA Nemotron 3: Efficient and Open Intelligence

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA introduces Nemotron 3, a family of hybrid Mamba-Transformer Mixture-of-Experts LLMs, achieving up to 3.3x higher inference throughput and 1 million token context length with state-of-the-art accuracy for agentic AI. The project openly releases model weights, training software, recipes, and over 10 trillion tokens of training data.

## Problem

- There is a growing demand for AI models capable of complex reasoning, planning, and multi-step tool use (agentic AI) that are both efficient and robust.
- Traditional Transformer LLMs face significant challenges regarding high inference costs, latency, and memory consumption, particularly with increasing context lengths.
- Many advanced AI applications require processing extremely long input sequences, often beyond what current models can efficiently handle.

## Method

- Developed **Nemotron 3**, a hybrid ==Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Mamba-Transformer== architecture, predominantly interleaving MoE layers with efficient ==Mamba-2 layers== to optimize inference throughput.
- Introduced ==LatentMoE==, a hardware-aware expert design for larger models to improve accuracy per byte, and utilized ==NVFP4== for stable 4-bit floating-point pretraining.
- Implemented ==multi-environment reinforcement learning post-training== on diverse tasks simultaneously to imbue strong agentic capabilities, and integrated ==Multi-Token Prediction== for enhanced reasoning and speculative decoding.

## Results

- Achieved **3.3x** higher inference throughput for common reasoning workloads compared to a similarly sized Transformer MoE model (Qwen3-30B-A3B).
- Demonstrated state-of-the-art accuracy across leading reasoning benchmarks (e.g., Arena-Hard-v2-Avg, AIME25, SWE-Bench) while effectively leveraging up to **1 million token context length**.
- **LatentMoE** showed consistent accuracy improvements across downstream tasks, and **NVFP4** pretraining maintained a train/validation loss difference of **<1%** compared to BF16, confirming stability and accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining Mamba, Transformer, and MoE architectures, with Mamba layers for efficiency, effectively pushes the accuracy-to-inference-throughput frontier for LLMs.
> - Hardware-aware architectural designs like LatentMoE and ultra-low precision training with NVFP4 can yield substantial efficiency gains without compromising model quality or stability.
> - Simultaneous multi-environment reinforcement learning and specialized long-context training can robustly develop agentic capabilities and support unprecedented context lengths, fostering open AI innovation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has been a central theme in AI research and industry for several years, with a continuous drive to enhance capabilities such as reasoning, agency, and conversational fluency, while simultaneously addressing critical challenges related to efficiency, scalability, and accessibility. NVIDIA Nemotron 3 directly addresses these intertwined objectives, aiming to push the "accuracy-to-inference-throughput frontier" in the domain of open-source LLMs.

**Current Landscape and Challenges:**
The prevailing architecture in LLMs has been the Transformer, known for its powerful self-attention mechanism but also its quadratic scaling of computational and memory requirements with sequence length, leading to high inference costs and limitations in long-context processing. Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures have emerged as a way to scale model capacity (total parameters) while keeping active parameters (and thus compute) manageable, offering improved accuracy for a given compute budget. However, MoE layers can introduce communication bottlenecks and memory bandwidth challenges. State Space Models (SSMs) like Mamba have recently gained attention for their linear scaling with sequence length, offering superior efficiency for long contexts and fixed inference-time memory. Furthermore, the increasing complexity of AI applications, especially "agentic AI" that involves multi-step reasoning and tool use, demands models with robust reasoning capabilities and the ability to operate within practical computational budgets.

**NVIDIA Nemotron 3's Contribution to the Landscape:**
Nemotron 3 positions itself at the forefront of this evolving landscape by integrating and innovating upon these advanced architectural and training paradigms:

- **Hybrid Mamba-Transformer MoE:** This work is a significant step towards combining the strengths of different architectures. By predominantly interleaving MoE layers with efficient Mamba-2 layers, Nemotron 3 mitigates the computational bottlenecks of dense Transformers and traditional MoE-Transformer designs, particularly for long sequences. This addresses the core efficiency challenge while maintaining the expressive power needed for complex tasks.
- **Hardware-Aware Design (LatentMoE, NVFP4):** NVIDIA's deep expertise in hardware is evident in LatentMoE and NVFP4 training. LatentMoE is a novel approach to MoE design that explicitly considers hardware bottlenecks (memory bandwidth, communication volume) to improve accuracy per byte without compromising inference performance. NVFP4 training, utilizing NVIDIA's custom 4-bit floating-point format, pushes the boundaries of extreme quantization for pretraining, directly leveraging NVIDIA's specialized hardware capabilities (e.g., Blackwell Ultra's FP4 throughput) to drastically improve training efficiency. This represents a trend towards deeper integration of model design with underlying hardware.
- **Long-Context Capabilities:** The support for 1M token context length, achieved by leveraging Mamba's inherent positional encoding properties and specific pre-training strategies, positions Nemotron 3 favorably against models struggling with RoPE limitations in context extension. This is crucial for applications requiring extensive document analysis or comprehensive conversational histories.
- **Agentic AI Focus:** The emphasis on multi-environment reinforcement learning (RL) post-training for agentic capabilities (tool use, competitive coding, math, instruction following) reflects a broader industry shift towards more autonomous and capable AI systems. The simultaneous training approach contrasts with previous staged methods, suggesting improved stability and comprehensive skill development.
- **Openness and Reproducibility:** By committing to openly release model weights, training software (NeMo-RL, NeMo-Gym), recipes, and a substantial portion of training data (over 10 trillion tokens), Nemotron 3 contributes to the open-source movement in LLMs. This helps democratize access to advanced AI research and allows for broader scrutiny, reproducibility, and further innovation, potentially accelerating the development of agentic AI applications.

In essence, Nemotron 3 aims to set a new standard for efficient, high-performing, and openly accessible LLMs, particularly for the burgeoning field of agentic AI, by strategically combining and optimizing various cutting-edge techniques with NVIDIA's hardware-software co-design philosophy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the "NVIDIA Nemotron 3: Efficient and Open Intelligence" paper is to **introduce a new family of large language models (LLMs)—Nano, Super, and Ultra—designed to deliver leading accuracy and efficiency for agentic AI applications, while also fostering an open ecosystem for AI development.**

The core motivations driving this ambitious project are multifaceted:

- **Addressing the Demand for Agentic AI:** There is a rapidly growing need for AI models capable of complex reasoning, planning, and multi-step tool use, collectively known as agentic AI. Current LLMs often struggle with the efficiency and robustness required for such applications at scale. Nemotron 3 aims to provide foundational models that excel in these capabilities.
- **Pushing the Efficiency Frontier:** Traditional Transformer models and even earlier MoE designs face significant challenges regarding inference throughput, latency, and memory consumption, especially with increasing context lengths. A key motivation is to overcome these bottlenecks by developing novel architectures and training techniques that drastically improve efficiency without compromising model quality. This is crucial for deploying LLMs in high-volume, real-time, or resource-constrained environments.
- **Enabling Ultra-Long Context Processing:** Many advanced AI applications, such as code analysis, extensive RAG pipelines, and detailed document summarization, require LLMs to process extremely long input sequences. Motivated by this need, Nemotron 3 aims to support context lengths up to an unprecedented 1 million tokens efficiently.
- **Leveraging Hardware-Software Co-Design:** As a leading hardware manufacturer, NVIDIA is motivated to demonstrate the power of deeply integrated hardware and software solutions. The development of techniques like LatentMoE and NVFP4 training is driven by the desire to exploit the unique capabilities of NVIDIA's GPU architecture (e.g., higher FP4 throughput on Blackwell Ultra) for maximal performance gains.
- **Promoting Open Science and Community Engagement:** In an AI landscape increasingly dominated by closed, proprietary models, NVIDIA is motivated to contribute to the open-source community. By releasing model weights, software, training recipes, and data, they aim to lower the barrier to entry for advanced AI development, foster transparency, encourage research, and accelerate innovation across the broader AI ecosystem. This also positions NVIDIA as a key enabler for developers building on open platforms.
- **Scalability for Multi-Agent Environments:** The efficiency gains and agentic capabilities are specifically designed to enable developers to build and scale complex multi-agent environments, which are becoming central to advanced AI systems.
- **Granular Control over Reasoning:** Providing users with inference-time control over reasoning budget offers flexibility and optimization, motivated by the practical need to balance performance and computational cost in diverse real-world applications.

In summary, the Nemotron 3 project is motivated by a vision for accessible, highly efficient, and exceptionally capable AI models that can serve as the backbone for the next generation of agentic applications, all while capitalizing on NVIDIA's integrated hardware-software expertise and contributing significantly to the open-source community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Nemotron 3 family of models employs a sophisticated, multi-pronged approach that integrates architectural innovation, novel training techniques, and hardware-aware optimizations to achieve its objectives of efficiency, accuracy, and long-context capabilities for agentic AI.

#### 3.1. Hybrid MoE Mamba-Transformer Architecture

The core architectural innovation is a **hybrid Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) Mamba-Transformer design**. This architecture is explicitly chosen for inference efficiency, especially in reasoning workloads. Instead of interleaving MoE layers solely with computationally expensive self-attention layers (which incur linearly increasing KV Cache costs during generation), Nemotron 3 predominantly interleaves MoE layers with **Mamba-2 layers**. Mamba-2 layers are a type of Structured State Space Model (SSM) known for their linear scaling with sequence length and constant state memory requirements during generation, making them highly efficient for long contexts. Only a "select few" self-attention layers are retained to provide high-fidelity all-to-all information routing where necessary (e.g., Nemotron Nano 3's pattern in Figure 1 shows more Mamba-2 blocks than attention blocks). This strategic combination aims to balance the parallelization strengths of attention, the sequential modeling efficiency of Mamba, and the sparse parameter scaling of MoE.

#### 3.2. LatentMoE: Hardware-Aware Expert Design

For the larger Super and Ultra models, Nemotron 3 introduces **LatentMoE**, a novel hardware-aware expert design. This approach addresses the memory bandwidth and communication bottlenecks inherent in standard MoE layers. In LatentMoE, token embeddings are first projected from the model's hidden dimension (d) to a smaller **latent dimension (ℓ)**. Expert routing and computation then occur entirely within this latent space. This reduces per-expert weight loads and all-to-all communication payloads by a factor of *d/ℓ*. The key innovation is to **reinvest these saved computational and memory capacities** by increasing both the total number of experts (N) and the number of active experts per token (K) by the same factor *d/ℓ*. This strategy enhances the "nonlinear budget" and expert diversity, thereby improving accuracy per byte without increasing overall inference cost. Non-routed computations (routing gate, shared experts, non-expert layers) remain in the original hidden dimension to preserve quality.

#### 3.3. Multi-Token Prediction (MTP)

Also incorporated into Super and Ultra models, **MTP** is a technique that involves predicting multiple future tokens simultaneously during training. This provides richer training signals, encouraging the model to plan several steps ahead and enhancing its multi-step reasoning capabilities. From an inference perspective, MTP generates high-quality draft tokens that can be used for **speculative decoding**, significantly accelerating text generation without needing a separate draft model. MTP is designed as a lightweight module, incurring minimal additional FLOPs.

#### 3.4. NVFP4 Training

Super and Ultra models are trained using **NVIDIA's custom NVFP4 (4-bit floating-point) number format**. This is a cutting-edge quantization technique applied to weight, activation, and gradient tensors to enable the use of NVFP4 GEMMs, which offer significantly higher throughput (e.g., 3× higher than FP8 on GB300) on compatible NVIDIA hardware. The methodology involves:
- **Fine-grained (16-element) micro-block scaling** for numerical stability.
- **2D block scaling** for weight quantization.
- **Random Hadamard Transforms (RHTs)** on inputs to wgrad.
- **Stochastic rounding** on gradients.
- Keeping the **last 15% of the network in high precision**, and specifically maintaining QKV and attention projections, Mamba output projection layers, and LatentMoE/MTP projections in BF16 or MXFP8 to preserve fidelity and stability, especially in sensitive layers prone to "flushes to zero."

#### 3.5. Long Context Support

Nemotron 3 models are designed for context lengths up to **1 million tokens**. The approach leverages Mamba layers, which provide implicit positional information, thus avoiding the out-of-distribution issues commonly encountered with Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) when extending context beyond training lengths in Transformer-only models. The training regimen includes:
- A **continued pre-training (CPT) stage** at 512k sequence length.
- **Supervised fine-tuning (SFT)** at 256k sequence length.
- A **long-context environment** in the reinforcement learning stage with inputs up to 32k tokens.
- Integration of **synthetic data** specifically designed for long-range retrieval, multi-hop reasoning, and multi-document aggregation across all three stages.

#### 3.6. Multi-environment Reinforcement Learning Post-training

To imbue the models with strong agentic capabilities, Nemotron 3 undergoes extensive **multi-environment Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training**. Unlike prior approaches that used separate, staged training for different tasks, Nemotron 3 models are trained on a **diverse set of RL environments simultaneously**. These environments cover mathematical/scientific reasoning, competitive coding, instruction following, software engineering, search, chat, and general agentic tool use. This simultaneous training is found to be more stable and less prone to "reward hacking" compared to staged approaches. The RL architecture is asynchronous, decoupling training from inference, and employs Multi-Token Prediction for efficient rollout generation. For stable learning, **GRPO (Generalized Proximal Policy Optimization)** with masked importance sampling is utilized. NVIDIA open-sources its post-training software stack, including NeMo-RL for scalable training and NeMo-Gym for RL environments.

#### 3.7. Granular Reasoning Budget Control

The models are specifically trained to support **inference-time reasoning budget control**. This allows users to specify a maximum number of tokens for a "thinking trace." If the model reaches this budget, it can be prompted to generate the final response based on the partial thinking trace, offering a knob to trade off accuracy for efficiency.

This comprehensive methodological framework highlights NVIDIA's holistic approach, from low-level hardware-aware numerical formats to high-level multi-agent learning paradigms, all integrated into a unified training and deployment strategy for Nemotron 3.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Nemotron 3 family of models presents a compelling suite of findings demonstrating significant advancements in efficiency, accuracy, and long-context capabilities for agentic AI.

- **Superior Throughput with Hybrid Architecture:** The hybrid Mamba-Transformer MoE architecture, exemplified by Nemotron 3 Nano 30B-A3B, achieves a remarkable **3.3× higher inference throughput** compared to a similarly sized Transformer MoE model (Qwen3-30B-A3B) for common reasoning workloads (8k input, 16k output tokens). The paper indicates further speedups at longer sequences, underscoring the efficiency gains of minimizing expensive self-attention layers.

- **State-of-the-Art Accuracy:** Despite the throughput improvements, Nemotron 3 models achieve state-of-the-art accuracy across a diverse range of leading reasoning benchmarks. Figure 2 showcases strong performance on tasks like Arena-Hard-v2-Avg (Chat), AIME25 (Math), IFBench (Instruction Following), 2-Bench (Tool Use), SWE-Bench (Coding), LCB v6 (Coding), and RULER @ 1M (Long Context). This demonstrates that the architectural innovations do not compromise model quality.

- **LatentMoE for Enhanced Accuracy per Byte:** LatentMoE consistently outperforms a standard MoE baseline with comparable active and total parameters across all evaluated downstream tasks (Table 1). This includes MMLU-Pro (52.87% vs 48.30%), MMLU (72.11% vs 70.10%), Code (55.14% vs 51.95%), Math (80.19% vs 78.32%), and Commonsense Understanding (82.10% vs 81.73%). This validates LatentMoE's ability to improve model quality by strategically reinvesting efficiency savings into increased expert diversity and nonlinear budget.

- **MTP for Accuracy Gains and Speculative Decoding:** Integrating Multi-Token Prediction (MTP) leads to consistent improvements in model quality, with an average performance gain of approximately **2.4% across various benchmarks** (Table 2), including general knowledge, code generation, common-sense understanding, reading comprehension, and math. Furthermore, MTP provides a significant practical benefit for inference efficiency by enabling high-quality speculative decoding, with the module achieving around **97% acceptance on the first two predicted tokens** in an 8B active MoE model ablation.

- **Stable and Accurate NVFP4 Training:** Pretraining with the NVFP4 format is demonstrated to be stable and accurate for up to 25T tokens. The relative difference in train and validation loss between NVFP4 and BF16 training is remarkably small: **<1% for Nemotron 3 Nano (A3B)** and **<0.6% for a larger MoE model (A8B)** (Figure 4). Crucially, downstream task evaluations show that NVFP4 accuracy closely follows BF16 trajectories throughout training (Figure 5), confirming that the small loss gap does not lead to degraded evaluation accuracy. Specific recipe modifications, such as keeping sensitive layers in higher precision, were key to achieving this stability.

- **Robust 1M Token Long Context:** Nemotron 3 models effectively leverage ultra-long contexts. On the RULER benchmark, Nemotron 3 Nano (MoE hybrid) exhibits graceful degradation at a 1M context length (54.19%), significantly outperforming Nemotron 2 Nano (dense hybrid), which experienced an abrupt drop-off (23.43%) at the same length (Table 3). Analysis of negative log-likelihood (NLL) on repository-level code sequences larger than 1M tokens shows that NLL decreases with sequence length (Figure 6), indicating the model's ability to use long input context effectively up to the tested range.

- **Effective Multi-environment Reinforcement Learning:** The simultaneous multi-environment RL post-training approach results in a steady increase in performance across a wide array of agentic and reasoning benchmarks (Figure 7), including AALCR, AIME25, GPQA, IFBench Prompt, LiveCodeBench, MMLU Pro, SciCode, and 2-Bench. This confirms the efficacy of this stable training paradigm over previous staged approaches.

- **Granular Reasoning Budget Control:** The models are shown to support inference-time budget control, allowing users to fine-tune the accuracy-efficiency trade-off (Figure 8). This provides practical utility for applications requiring flexible performance optimization.

In essence, the Nemotron 3 family of models successfully demonstrates that it is possible to achieve leading accuracy and robust agentic capabilities while simultaneously pushing the boundaries of inference efficiency and context length, thanks to its innovative hybrid architecture and advanced training methodologies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The NVIDIA Nemotron 3 family of models represents a significant leap forward in the landscape of efficient, open, and capable large language models, with profound implications across research and industry.

- **New Paradigm for LLM Efficiency:** The introduction of the hybrid MoE Mamba-Transformer architecture, coupled with innovations like LatentMoE and NVFP4 training, sets a new benchmark for **accuracy-to-inference-throughput**. This is critically important for the widespread adoption and scaling of LLMs, as computational cost remains a major barrier. By drastically improving throughput and memory efficiency, especially for long contexts, Nemotron 3 makes advanced AI more economically viable for a broader range of applications and users. This efficiency is a direct outcome of NVIDIA's integrated hardware-software co-design philosophy, further solidifying its leadership in AI infrastructure.

- **Acceleration of Agentic AI Development:** The strong emphasis on agentic capabilities, honed through multi-environment reinforcement learning, directly addresses one of the most exciting and challenging frontiers in AI. Models capable of complex reasoning, multi-step tool use, competitive coding, and instruction following are foundational for building intelligent agents that can automate tasks, assist in complex workflows, and interact more naturally with dynamic environments. The granular reasoning budget control further empowers developers to deploy these agents with tailored performance-cost profiles. Nemotron 3 could significantly accelerate the development of autonomous systems, intelligent assistants, and sophisticated automation solutions.

- **Empowering Long-Context Applications:** The ability to handle context lengths up to 1 million tokens efficiently is transformative. This capability unlocks new possibilities for applications requiring deep analysis of extensive documents (legal, medical, scientific papers), understanding large codebases, processing entire conversation histories for customer service or therapy, and enhancing Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines with unprecedented contextual understanding. This effectively removes a significant bottleneck that limited the scope of many real-world LLM applications.

- **Driving Open AI Innovation and Democratization:** NVIDIA's commitment to openly releasing model weights, over 10 trillion tokens of datasets, pre- and post-training software (NeMo-RL, NeMo-Gym), and training recipes is a monumental contribution to the open-source AI community. In an era where many cutting-edge models are proprietary, this move fosters transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative research. It democratizes access to state-of-the-art AI technology, allowing researchers, startups, and developers worldwide to build upon, scrutinize, and innovate with highly capable models without incurring exorbitant development costs. This could lead to an explosion of new applications and research directions, further cementing NVIDIA's role as a foundational provider in the AI ecosystem.

- **Validation of Advanced Quantization:** The successful and stable pretraining with NVFP4 demonstrates the viability of ultra-low-precision training, pushing the boundaries of what is considered achievable in terms of numerical formats for large-scale model training. This research validates that significant efficiency gains can be realized at the fundamental level of data representation, paving the way for even more efficient future AI hardware and software designs.

- **Competitive Landscape Shift:** By offering a family of open models that challenge the performance and efficiency of existing proprietary and open-source models, Nemotron 3 raises the bar for the entire LLM community. Its specialized tiers (Nano, Super, Ultra) cater to diverse needs, from cost-efficient inference to state-of-the-art reasoning, making it a compelling alternative for various deployment scenarios.

In conclusion, NVIDIA Nemotron 3 is not just a collection of new models; it represents a holistic approach to building the next generation of AI. Its blend of architectural ingenuity, training innovations, hardware-aware optimizations, and commitment to openness positions it to profoundly impact the development and deployment of intelligent agents, enabling more efficient, capable, and accessible AI for everyone.
