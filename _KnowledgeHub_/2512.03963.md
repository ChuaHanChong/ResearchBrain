# TempR1: Improving Temporal Understanding of MLLMs via Temporal-Aware Multi-Task Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanjing University, ByteDance Inc., and Shanghai AI Lab introduce TempR1, a time-aware multi-task reinforcement learning framework that significantly enhances Multimodal Large Language Models' (MLLMs) temporal understanding across various video comprehension tasks. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance on multiple benchmarks by jointly optimizing over diverse temporal event structures and utilizing tailored reward functions.

## Problem

- Traditional Vision-Language Pretraining (VLP) pipelines require separate expert models for each specific temporal understanding task, limiting scalability, flexibility, and cross-task generalization.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of MLLMs for temporal tasks often leads to overfitting on limited datasets and can reduce the model's general reasoning capabilities due to rigid token-level supervision.
- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) methods for temporal understanding in MLLMs are largely restricted to single tasks, such as temporal grounding, limiting their applicability to more complex and diverse temporal reasoning scenarios.

## Method

- **TempR1** introduces a ==temporal-aware multi-task== reinforcement learning framework, built upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, to jointly optimize MLLMs across various temporal understanding tasks.
- A curated multi-task corpus of approximately 60,000 video samples, covering five diverse temporal understanding tasks (Temporal Grounding, Dense Temporal Grounding, Video Highlight Detection, Grounded Video QA, Temporal Action Localization), is used for training.
- The framework employs a multi-component reward system with task-specific, time-aware localization rewards designed for one-to-one, many-to-one, and many-to-many temporal correspondence structures, in addition to format and classification rewards.

## Results

- TempR1 achieved state-of-the-art performance on several benchmarks, including 61.4 mIoU on Charades-STA for Temporal Grounding and 71.1 mIoU on QVHighlights for Video Highlight Detection, surpassing previous MLLM and VLP expert models.
- The framework demonstrated robust performance gains across other tasks, such as achieving **70.1%** QA accuracy and 39.2 mIoU for visual evidence localization on NExT-GQA (3.1-point gain in localization over VideoChat-R1), and 71.0 mF1 on ActivityNet-v1.3 for Temporal Action Localization, outperforming MUSEG by 13.0 points.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical importance of the proposed time-aware localization reward components, such as the instance number reward and DP-based matching for TAL, and validated the synergistic effects of multi-task training for performance improvement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint optimization over a diverse set of complementary temporal understanding tasks via reinforcement learning leads to synergistic improvements, enhancing both multi-task generalization and performance on individual tasks for MLLMs.
> - Tailored time-aware reward functions, particularly those utilizing dynamic programming for optimal interval matching in complex many-to-many scenarios, are crucial for effectively capturing fine-grained temporal dependencies.
> - Reinforcement fine-tuning can improve the temporal comprehension capabilities of MLLMs without compromising their broader general video understanding abilities, addressing a limitation of some supervised fine-tuning approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Video temporal understanding, encompassing tasks such as temporal grounding, action detection, and video highlight detection, is a critical area for advancing comprehensive video comprehension and its real-world applications. Historically, this field has been dominated by traditional methods that employ Vision-Language Pretraining (VLP) models for feature extraction followed by task-specific prediction modules. While these approaches have achieved performance on specific benchmarks, they often encounter limitations related to feature misalignment, cumulative error propagation, and a lack of generalization across diverse tasks and datasets, necessitating distinct models for each specific scenario.

The recent development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has introduced a paradigm shift, enabling end-to-end multimodal reasoning within a single architecture. This unified approach facilitates knowledge transfer across tasks and datasets, offering potential for improved multi-task and cross-domain temporal understanding. Within the MLLM context, two primary strategies have emerged for enhancing temporal comprehension: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL). SFT-based methods, while leveraging large-scale instruction tuning, frequently exhibit overfitting to limited temporal datasets and can lead to a reduction in general reasoning capabilities due to rigid token-level supervision.

Reinforcement learning approaches, in contrast, directly optimize task-specific objectives by rewarding temporally and semantically accurate predictions, often resulting in greater data efficiency and improved generalization. However, most existing RL-based methods for temporal understanding have been confined to a narrow range of tasks, predominantly temporal grounding. This narrow scope restricts their applicability to more complex and diverse temporal reasoning scenarios, such as dense grounding, action localization, and time-sensitive question answering, and limits their exposure to the varied temporal structures inherent in real-world videos.

TempR1 addresses these limitations by introducing a temporal-aware multi-task reinforcement learning framework. It expands the scope of RL for temporal understanding beyond single tasks by integrating a diverse set of temporal understanding tasks within a unified optimization framework. This approach aims to leverage the shared underlying temporal reasoning requirements across tasks while accommodating their specific nuances, thereby enhancing both generalization and task-specific performance of MLLMs in video temporal understanding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically enhance the temporal understanding capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) across a diverse range of video comprehension tasks. This is achieved through the development of a novel temporal-aware multi-task reinforcement learning framework, TempR1.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in existing approaches to temporal video understanding:

1. **Limited Generalization of Traditional Methods**: Traditional VLP-based pipelines typically require separate expert models for each dataset or task, leading to issues of scalability, flexibility, and limited cross-task/cross-domain generalization. This makes it challenging to deploy models capable of handling the full spectrum of temporal understanding tasks.
2. **Overfitting and Loss of Generalization in SFT-based MLLMs**: While MLLMs offer architectural unification, supervised fine-tuning (SFT) often leads to overfitting on the relatively small temporal datasets available. The rigid, token-level supervision in SFT can also compromise the MLLM's broader reasoning abilities, hindering its effectiveness in more open-ended scenarios.
3. **Narrow Scope of Existing RL-based MLLMs**: Reinforcement learning has shown promise in improving data efficiency and generalization for MLLMs by directly optimizing task-specific objectives. However, current RL methods for temporal understanding are largely restricted to single tasks, primarily temporal grounding. This limits their ability to capture the hierarchical and compositional nature of diverse temporal dependencies required for tasks like dense grounding, action localization, or grounded video question answering.
4. **Need for Unified and Scalable Temporal Reasoning**: The field lacks a unified framework that can train MLLMs to simultaneously address a variety of temporal understanding tasks, which differ in query semantics and prediction objectives. Such a framework could leverage common requirements (e.g., accurate timestamp prediction, video-text alignment) while adapting to task-specific patterns, thereby fostering stronger and more generalizable temporal reasoning abilities.

To address these motivations, TempR1 aims to:
- Curate a multi-task corpus covering diverse temporal event structures to expose the model to a rich set of temporal understanding scenarios.
- Develop a stable and effective cross-task optimization strategy based on reinforcement learning.
- Design tailored reward functions that account for varying temporal properties and correspondence structures across different tasks, enabling fine-grained temporal dependency capture.
- Demonstrate that joint optimization over complementary tasks yields a synergistic effect, improving both multi-task generalization and performance on individual tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TempR1 is a temporal-aware multi-task reinforcement learning framework designed to enhance MLLMs' temporal comprehension. Its methodology is structured around three core components: the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, a curated multi-task corpus, and a set of task-specific, time-aware reward functions.

#### 3.1. Foundation: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)

TempR1 builds upon the GRPO algorithm, which extends Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) by eliminating the need for a separate critic network. Instead of learning an absolute value function, GRPO estimates relative advantages by normalizing rewards within a group of sampled responses. For an input prompt, the policy model samples multiple candidate responses. Each response is assigned a reward score, and these scores are then normalized to compute relative advantages, indicating the comparative quality of each response within the group. The policy is optimized to increase the likelihood of higher-quality responses, with a KL-divergence term used to regularize the policy update and ensure stability.

#### 3.2. Multi-Task Corpus and Task Formulation

The framework incorporates a multi-task corpus comprising approximately 60,000 video samples drawn from various datasets, exposing the MLLM to diverse temporal event structures. Five representative temporal understanding tasks are included:
- **Temporal Grounding (TG)**: Locates a single temporal interval corresponding to a query. Output format: `<answer>t_s to t_e</answer>`.
- **Dense Temporal Grounding (DTG)**: Locates multiple sequential intervals for events described by a query. Output format: `<answer>t_s1 to t_e1, t_s2 to t_e2...</answer>`.
- **Video Highlight Detection (VHD)**: Identifies an uncertain number of highlight segments for a single event, sharing the DTG output format.
- **Grounded Video QA (GVQA)**: Predicts an answer to a question and provides supporting temporal evidence. Output format: `<answer>A</answer><glue>t_1s to t_1e...</glue>`.
- **Temporal Action Localization (TAL)**: Locates all instances of a predefined action category, using the DTG format.

#### 3.3. Reward Functions

To guide the MLLM toward accurate temporal predictions and ensure consistent supervision across diverse tasks, TempR1 employs a multi-component reward system:

- **Format Reward ($R_{format}$)**: A binary reward (1 if the output matches the required template, 0 otherwise) is used to encourage machine-parsable and temporally grounded outputs.

- **Temporal Localization Rewards ($R_{loc}$)**: These rewards are designed based on the correspondence structure between predicted and ground-truth temporal intervals, categorized into three types:
 - **Type 1: One-to-One Correspondence (TG, DTG)**: Each predicted interval is expected to correspond to a unique ground-truth event, with the same number of predicted and ground-truth intervals. The reward is the mean temporal Intersection-over-Union (IoU) across all matched pairs.
 - **Type 2: Many-to-One Correspondence (VHD, GVQA)**: Multiple predicted intervals may correspond to a single ground-truth instance (e.g., several highlight segments for one event, or multiple evidence segments for one answer). For these tasks, all predicted intervals are merged into a single union region, and the IoU is computed between this aggregated prediction and the union of ground-truth intervals.
 - **Type 3: Many-to-Many Correspondence (TAL)**: The model predicts multiple instances of an action, and the number of predictions may differ from the ground truth. This type employs two complementary components:
 - **Instance Number Reward ($R_{num}$)**: Penalizes mismatches in the count of predicted ($N_{pred}$) and ground-truth ($N_{gt}$) instances using an exponential decay function: $exp(-|N_{pred} - N_{gt}| / (min(N_{gt}, 3) \cdot \sigma))$.
 - **Matching Reward ($R_{match}$)**: Evaluates the accuracy of temporal boundary prediction. It uses a dynamic programming algorithm (Algorithm 1) to optimally match predicted intervals with ground-truth instances, maximizing the total summed IoU (sIoU). Precision ($P$), Recall ($R$), and F1 score are calculated based on this sIoU, and the F1 score serves as the matching reward.
 The final localization reward for TAL combines both components: $R_{loc}^{(TAL)} = R_{num} + R_{match}$.

- **Classification Reward ($R_{cls}$)**: For the GVQA task, an additional binary reward (1 for correct answer option, 0 otherwise) evaluates the accuracy of the question's answer.

#### 3.4. Multi-Task Training Integration

During training, data from all five tasks are mixed and randomly sampled. For each sample, the task type dictates the combination of reward functions used. The overall reward combines the format reward, the task-specific localization reward, and, for GVQA, the classification reward. The base model for reinforcement fine-tuning is Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Video frames are sampled and scaled to manage visual token limits. The model is trained for one epoch on the multi-task corpus, and during inference, outputs are parsed using regular expressions to extract structured results.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on TempR1 demonstrate its performance across various temporal understanding benchmarks and validate its design choices through ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Comparison with State-of-the-art Methods

TempR1 was evaluated against both VLP-based expert models and MLLM-based methods across five temporal understanding tasks.

- **Temporal Grounding (TG)**: On Charades-STA and ActivityNet-Caption, TempR1 achieved state-of-the-art performance. It attained 61.4 mIoU on Charades-STA, surpassing VideoChat-R1 by 0.6 points and MUSEG by 1.7 points. With additional dataset-specific fine-tuning (TempR1\*), it further improved to 62.5 mIoU on Charades-STA, outperforming TAR-TVG by 1.4 points.
- **Video Highlight Detection (VHD)**: On the QVHighlights dataset, TempR1 obtained 71.1 mIoU, a gain of 5.2 mIoU over the second-best TAR-TVG. TempR1\* reached 71.6 mIoU.
- **Dense Temporal Grounding (DTG)**: For DTG on ActivityNet-Caption, TempR1 achieved 59.8 mIoU, demonstrating performance comparable to the VLP expert HSCNet.
- **Grounded Video Question Answering (GVQA)**: On NExT-GQA, TempR1 achieved 70.1% question-answering accuracy and 39.2 mIoU for visual evidence localization. This represents a 3.1-point improvement in temporal localization over VideoChat-R1 while maintaining similar answer accuracy.
- **Temporal Action Localization (TAL)**: On ActivityNet-v1.3, TempR1 achieved 71.0 mF1, outperforming MUSEG by 13.0 points.

#### 4.2. Performance on General Video Understanding Benchmarks

Evaluation on general video understanding benchmarks (VideoMME, Perception Test, TempCompass, MVBench) indicated that while supervised fine-tuning (SFT) could weaken the general reasoning ability of the base model, TempR1's reinforcement fine-tuning notably enhanced it. This suggests that the approach improves temporal comprehension without compromising broader video understanding capabilities.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Localization Reward for TAL (Type-3)**: An ablation study on ActivityNet-v1.3 for TAL showed the importance of both components in the Type-3 reward. Removing the instance number reward led to a drop in mF1 (from 70.6 to 60.8), indicating its role in estimating action counts. Replacing the dynamic programming (DP)-based matching with a sequential strategy severely degraded performance to 45.4 mF1, highlighting the necessity of optimal alignment between predicted and ground-truth intervals.
- **Influence of RL Algorithm**: Comparison of DAPO, GSPO, and GRPO demonstrated that GSPO and GRPO generally achieved better performance than DAPO across most tasks. DAPO's token-level policy gradient loss was noted to potentially bias optimization toward samples with more output instances. GRPO was selected as the default due to its alignment with prior work and robust performance.
- **Effect of Multi-Task Training**: Incrementally adding tasks to the training regimen consistently improved performance across benchmarks. Training on all five tasks yielded the best results on most metrics, confirming a synergistic effect where the model benefits from shared abilities (e.g., timestamp prediction, video-text alignment) learned from diverse temporal understanding tasks.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons illustrated that the DP-based matching strategy for TAL produced more accurate and consistent temporal localization in multi-instance scenarios. For GVQA, TempR1 demonstrated more complete and precise temporal evidence localization compared to VideoChat-R1, and an improved consistency between visual evidence and textual answers.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TempR1 framework represents a notable advancement in enhancing the temporal understanding capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models. By systematically integrating diverse temporal understanding tasks within a multi-task reinforcement learning paradigm, this work addresses several limitations of prior approaches.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that joint optimization over a range of complementary temporal tasks via reinforcement learning leads to substantial improvements in MLLM performance. This multi-task approach not only enhances generalization across different temporal scenarios but also concurrently boosts performance on individual tasks, indicating a strong synergistic effect. The introduction of time-aware reward functions, tailored to distinct temporal correspondence types (one-to-one, many-to-one, many-to-many), is a key methodological contribution. This structured reward design enables the model to capture fine-grained temporal dependencies more effectively and adapt to varied temporal patterns, particularly evidenced by the performance gains in complex tasks like Temporal Action Localization.

The research establishes a scalable and principled paradigm for reinforcement fine-tuning in video temporal understanding. By utilizing the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, TempR1 offers a robust and efficient optimization strategy that mitigates the training overhead typically associated with RL, making it more practical for real-world MLLM applications. The empirical results, showing state-of-the-art performance across multiple established benchmarks, validate the effectiveness of this unified design.

The potential impact of TempR1 is broad. Stronger and more generalizable temporal reasoning in MLLMs can significantly advance applications such as:
- **Intelligent Video Retrieval**: More precise temporal grounding can lead to highly accurate event or moment retrieval from long-form videos based on natural language queries.
- **Human-Computer Interaction**: Improved understanding of temporal actions and events can enable more natural and effective interaction with video content, such as answering complex temporal questions or summarizing key events.
- **Long-form Video Analysis**: The ability to analyze diverse temporal structures and semantics will be crucial for automated content moderation, summarization, and understanding complex narratives in lengthy videos.
- **Foundation Models for Video**: This work contributes towards developing more robust and versatile foundation models for video comprehension, reducing the need for task-specific architectures and facilitating broader adoption of MLLMs in video analytics.

Overall, TempR1 provides a robust methodology for equipping MLLMs with a more comprehensive and nuanced understanding of temporal dynamics in videos, paving the way for more intelligent and capable video AI systems.
