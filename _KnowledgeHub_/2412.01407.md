# HoloDrive: Holistic 2D-3D Multi-Modal Street Scene Generation for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> HoloDrive jointly generates multi-view camera video and LiDAR point clouds for driving scenes by wiring bidirectional BEV-to-Camera and Camera-to-BEV transform modules with depth supervision between a pretrained 2D diffusion generator and a 3D VQ-VAE/MaskGIT LiDAR generator, extended to temporal prediction via progressive multi-task training, achieving state-of-the-art FID/FVD/Chamfer over single-modality and prior joint baselines.

## Problem

- Existing driving-scene generators handle camera images and LiDAR point clouds separately, so generated outputs cover only 2D or only 3D information, not both.
- The one prior joint 2D-3D attempt trains a new unified BEV latent autoencoder from scratch, so its image quality lags far behind SOTA models fine-tuned from large pretrained 2D generators.
- LiDAR-only generation methods rely solely on point-cloud priors, lacking the rich semantic and perceptual cues that camera images provide.

## Method

- ==3D-to-2D and 2D-to-3D cross-modal transform modules== built via camera-LiDAR frustum projection and voxel pooling, enabling bidirectional feature exchange between a ==diffusion== image generator and a ==MaskGIT==-based LiDAR generator.
- ==Depth prediction branch== inside the 2D U-Net, supervised by projected LiDAR points, disambiguating the image-to-BEV unprojection needed for cross-modal alignment.
- ==Progressive multi-task training== recipe with ==conditional dropping== and ==joint-interaction dropping== that extends the pipeline from single-frame to temporal video/LiDAR world-model prediction.

## Results

- HoloDrive reaches FID **10.64** versus **12.99** for Drive-WM and **16.20** for MagicDrive on multi-view image generation, the best realism among all compared baselines.
- On video generation HoloDrive gets FID **13.6** and FVD **103.0**, beating Drive-WM's FID **15.8** and matching-range FVD versus DriveDreamer-2's **74.9**.
- Ablations show joint 2D-3D interaction with **30%** condition and **30%** joint dropping improves FID from **11.6** to **9.4** and FVD from **117** to **83** over the non-dropping variant.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reusing large pretrained single-modality generators as frozen-ish backbones and connecting them via lightweight projection modules preserves per-modality quality better than training a new shared multi-modal latent space from scratch.
> - Depth prediction supervised by the complementary modality is a cheap, generalizable bridge for aligning 2D and 3D generative spaces without redesigning either backbone.
> - Naively adding cross-modal interaction to pretrained unimodal models barely helps; explicit conditional and joint-interaction dropout is needed to force genuine multi-task cross-modal learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative models has seen advancements in creating realistic content, including images, videos, and 3D objects. This capability is applied in simulations for training and evaluating safety-critical systems like autonomous vehicles (AVs), reducing the need for extensive manual modeling and facilitating scenario testing. Predictive world models, which learn to understand and forecast dynamic real-world behaviors, are also gaining traction for generating simulation scenes and videos.

Existing generative approaches for autonomous driving data primarily focus on single modalities. For instance, methods have been developed for 2D camera image and video generation, or separately for 3D LiDAR point cloud generation. However, a functional autonomous driving system integrates multiple sensor types, typically cameras and LiDARs, which provide complementary information. Cameras offer rich texture and semantic details, while LiDARs supply precise 3D geometric data. The combination of these modalities enhances perception accuracy.

Despite the recognized benefits of multimodal input for AV perception, the exploration of joint multimodal generation remains in a preliminary stage. Prior attempts, such as BEVWorld, have explored joint camera and LiDAR generation using a unified latent representation, but the generation quality and controllability have not consistently matched state-of-the-art methods in single-modality generation. Furthermore, while many predictive world models generate multi-view videos, it has been noted that it is often unclear whether these models genuinely account for the underlying 3D world structure.

HoloDrive aims to address this gap by proposing a framework for holistic 2D-3D multi-modal generation, integrating the strengths of established single-modality generation models and enabling consistent joint generation of camera images and LiDAR point clouds. It seeks to unify 2D and 3D generation for street-view autonomous driving data within a single, effective framework, thereby pushing the boundaries of multimodal world modeling for autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind HoloDrive stems from the observation that real-world autonomous driving systems rely on multiple sensor modalities (e.g., cameras and LiDARs) that provide complementary information. Current generative models, however, typically operate on a single modality, producing outputs that only cover separate 2D or 3D information. This disparity creates a gap where the generated data, despite individual quality improvements, lacks the multi-modal consistency and richness required to fully support the development and testing of robust autonomous driving systems.

The key objectives of this work are:

- **To enable joint generation of multi-view camera images and LiDAR point clouds:** The central goal is to create a framework capable of simultaneously generating realistic street scene videos from surround-view cameras and corresponding LiDAR point clouds, ensuring consistency between the 2D and 3D outputs.
- **To establish effective information exchange between 2D and 3D generative models:** This involves designing mechanisms that allow heterogeneous 2D and 3D generative models to interact and leverage each other's complementary information during the generation process. This includes projecting 3D features into 2D space to inform image generation and aggregating 2D semantic priors into 3D space to enrich LiDAR generation.
- **To integrate depth perception within the 2D generative model:** A specific objective is to introduce a depth prediction branch in the 2D model, supervised by 3D LiDAR data. This aims to resolve ambiguities when un-projecting information from image space to Bird's-Eye-View (BEV) space, a critical step for 2D-3D alignment.
- **To extend the joint generation framework to video prediction (world modeling):** The framework is designed to not only generate single frames but also to predict future sequences of consistent multi-view camera images and LiDAR point clouds, incorporating temporal dynamics.
- **To achieve state-of-the-art performance in both single-frame and video generation:** The aim is to demonstrate that this joint multimodal approach can yield generated outputs that surpass existing single-modality and preliminary joint-modality methods in terms of realism, fidelity to conditions, and cross-modal consistency.
- **To incorporate textual and layout conditions for controllable generation:** The system is motivated to allow conditioning on high-level scene descriptions (text) and geometric layouts (bounding boxes, HD maps) to enable controlled and diverse scene generation.

By addressing these objectives, HoloDrive seeks to provide a more comprehensive and realistic data generation tool for autonomous driving research, facilitating more effective training and evaluation of perception and planning systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HoloDrive proposes a holistic 2D-3D generation framework that jointly predicts multi-view video and future LiDAR points. The pipeline integrates separate 2D and 3D generative models with novel cross-modal interaction structures (2D-to-3D and 3D-to-2D) and a depth supervision module to facilitate information exchange and ensure consistency.

#### 3.1. Multi-view Image Generation

The 2D generation component is built upon a Latent Diffusion Model (LDM) architecture, specifically SD 2.1.
- **Image Latent Representation:** Raw multi-view camera images are encoded into latent representations using a VAE encoder. The generative model then iteratively denoises random Gaussian noise in this latent space to produce a clean image latent.
- **Cross-view Attention:** To ensure consistency across different camera views, cross-view attention blocks are integrated into the diffusion U-Net after each spatial attention block. These blocks apply self-attention across views and merge outputs.
- **Conditions:** The model is conditioned on textual descriptions and geometric layout information. This includes projected 3D bounding boxes and projected HD map conditions, concatenated along the channel dimension. These conditions are injected into the model using a T2I-Adapter-like mechanism.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained using a standard denoising objective, minimizing the L2 distance between the predicted noise and the actual noise added to the latent representation.

#### 3.2. LiDAR Generation

The 3D generation component focuses on producing LiDAR point clouds using discrete representations.
- **VQ-VAE-like Tokenizer:** A VQ-VAE-like tokenizer, similar to UltraLiDAR, is employed. It uses a PointNet-based encoder followed by Swin Transformer blocks to convert LiDAR point clouds into BEV latent features. These features are then quantized into discrete tokens. A decoder reconstructs the point clouds from tokens, incorporating a differentiable depth rendering branch for voxel reconstruction.
- **Generative Model:** A U-Net-like transformer, `G^(l)`, is trained to generate diverse LiDAR point clouds. This model is conditioned on multi-channel BEV features, which can be derived from 3D box and HD map annotations or cross-modal features from the 2D network.
- **Masked Generation:** The training paradigm is similar to MaskGIT, where a portion of input tokens are masked during training, and the model is tasked with reconstructing the original tokens. A cosine mask scheduler controls the masking ratio.
- **Training Objective:** The model is optimized using a cross-entropy loss to reconstruct the original LiDAR tokens from their masked versions and conditions.
- **Inference:** During inference, a sampling algorithm similar to MaskGIT is used to iteratively refine and generate tokens, which are then decoded into point clouds.

#### 3.3. Joint Generation of Camera and LiDAR

Two main structures facilitate interaction and consistency between the 2D and 3D models:
- **Depth Supervision:** A depth prediction branch is added to the 2D generative model's U-Net. This branch estimates depth using intermediate image features, which are resized and concatenated. The output, representing depth bins, is supervised by projected point clouds from the 3D LiDAR using a Cross Entropy loss (`L^(d)`). This helps the 2D model perceive 3D structure.
- **3D → 2D Transformation:** This module projects 3D features onto the 2D perspective view, enriching the 2D generation with 3D context.
 - It creates a frustum-shaped point cloud for each camera view, mapping 2D image coordinates to 3D points based on depth bins.
 - LiDAR generation model's hidden states from its down-blocks are sampled using these 3D points.
 - The sampled features are summed along the depth dimension, weighted by the 2D model's depth prediction.
 - These projected features (`e^(proj)`) are then injected into the 2D generation model alongside existing 2D conditions via a lightweight adapter, augmenting the 2D condition features.
- **2D → 3D Transformation:** This module aggregates 2D prior semantic knowledge from the multi-view 2D model into the 3D space.
 - Multi-view intermediate features from the 2D model (noisy latent features) are converted to BEV space.
 - This conversion uses voxel pooling, similar to BEVDepth, where the 2D depth prediction (`F^(c)_d`) serves as weights for aggregating image-space features into 3D BEV embeddings (`e^(l)`). These embeddings serve as conditions for the 3D LiDAR generation model.
- **Joint Training:** The overall training objective `L` is a weighted sum of the 2D image generation loss (`L^(c)`), the 3D LiDAR generation loss (`L^(l)`), and the depth prediction loss (`L^(d)`), balanced by hyperparameters.

#### 3.4. Temporal Modeling

To extend to video prediction (world model), temporal structures are introduced.
- **Temporal Generator Architecture:** Temporal attention layers are inserted after spatial attention layers in both 2D and 3D models to process sequential data. A causal mask is applied to the 3D video generator.
- **Joint World Model:** Given `S` past observations for both camera and LiDAR, the model is trained to predict `T` future frames. The joint training loss is averaged over all `S + T` frames. For 2D, the input generator uses a concatenation of ground truth and noisy image latents, with a binary mask for future frames. For 3D, mask tokens are directly replaced with ground truth for the prediction task.
- **Multi-task Training Policy:** A progressive training strategy is employed.
 1. Pre-training of unimodal models (2D and 3D separately).
 2. Fine-tuning on the joint training task.
 3. During joint training, conditional dropping (randomly inhibiting layout conditions on only one modality) and joint dropping (randomly reverting to unimodal training) are used. These policies encourage cross-modal learning and manage the transition from unimodal to joint learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of HoloDrive was evaluated through a series of experiments on single-frame generation and world model benchmarks using the NuScenes dataset.

#### 4.1. Experimental Settings

- **Dataset:** NuScenes, which provides paired multi-view camera images, LiDAR point clouds, text descriptions, and map layouts (700 training videos, 150 validation videos, each ~20 seconds with ~40 key frames).
- **Baselines:** For image generation, existing multi-view AD image generation methods were used. For LiDAR, UltraLiDAR (conditional and unconditional versions) served as the baseline.
- **Training Scheme:** A three-stage progressive training approach:
 1. Cross-view camera generation model (from SD 2.1 checkpoint, with added modules).
 2. LiDAR generation model (from scratch).
 3. Joint generation model (fine-tuned from stages 1 and 2, with cross-modal interactions).
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - Images/Videos: Frechet Inception Distance (FID) for realism, Frechet Video Distance (FVD) for video quality, and mAP (Mean Average Precision) using BEVFusion or BEVFormer for detection accuracy.
 - LiDAR: Chamfer distance, L1 error (Mean/Median), and relative L1 error (AbsRel Mean/Median).

#### 4.2. Quantitative Results

- **Multi-view Image Generation (Table 1):** HoloDrive achieved an FID of 10.64, outperforming all baseline methods, including MagicDrive (16.20) and Drive-WM (12.99), indicating higher realism. It also showed competitive mAP scores for object detection (BEVFusion mAP: 14.06, BEVFormer mAP: 19.98), demonstrating the structural fidelity of the generated scenes.
- **Single-frame LiDAR Generation (Table 3):** HoloDrive demonstrated superior LiDAR generation quality. It achieved a Chamfer distance of 7.61, L1 Mean of 2.37, and AbsRel L1 Mean of 16.89%, outperforming UltraLiDAR (conditional version: Chamfer 8.45, L1 Mean 3.06, AbsRel 27.31%). This indicates more accurate and detailed point cloud generation. The ablation studies (Table 7 in supplementary material) confirmed that incorporating 2D features significantly enhanced LiDAR quality.
- **LiDAR Prediction (Table 5):** For future LiDAR prediction, HoloDrive (Joint) achieved a Chamfer distance of 0.83, surpassing Copilot4D (1.40) and other baselines, indicating improved future point cloud forecasting.
- **Predictive World Model (Table 2):** In multi-view video generation, HoloDrive achieved an FID of 13.6, which was lower (better) than Drive-WM (15.8) and DriveDreamer-2 (18.4), suggesting higher realism in generated videos. Its FVD of 103.0 was competitive, though slightly higher than DriveDreamer-2 (74.9), which benefited from SVD pre-training.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

- **Depth Estimation:** Figure 6 (main paper) and supplementary materials illustrate the depth estimation capabilities of the Diffusion U-Net, which is crucial for cross-modal transformation.
- **Cross-modal Consistency:** Figure 3 (main paper), Figure 10, and Figure 11 (supplementary materials) visually demonstrate the high consistency between the jointly generated 2D multi-view camera images and 3D LiDAR point clouds. Objects and scene elements align across modalities, a key advantage of the joint approach.
- **Realism:** Qualitative comparisons (Figures 4, 5, 7, 10, 11) show that HoloDrive generates realistic street scenes with diverse elements, including vehicles, pedestrians, and infrastructure, across both image and point cloud modalities.
- **Generalizability:** Experiments on the Argoverse dataset (Figure 7 in supplementary material) indicated the method's ability to generalize to other datasets.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Temporal Joint Training (Table 4):**
 - Depth supervision alone improved image generation (FID 11.6, FVD 140 vs. 2D baseline FID 12.7, FVD 136), indicating its value in scene structure perception.
 - Cross-modal interaction without "dropping policies" showed only minor improvements.
 - Introducing conditional dropping (30% on condition, 0% on joint) significantly improved FID (10.7) and slightly improved FVD (120).
 - Combining both conditional dropping (30%) and joint dropping (30%) yielded the best performance across all metrics (FID 9.4, FVD 83, Chamfer 0.838, L1 Mean 1.469), validating the multi-task learning hypothesis.
- **Image and LiDAR Ablations (Tables 6 & 7 in supplementary material):** These studies further confirmed the benefits of 3D-to-2D feature projection for image realism and accuracy, and the significant improvements in LiDAR quality from integrating 2D features and enabling bi-directional interaction.

#### 4.5. Limitations and Failure Cases

The paper notes some limitations, including distorted pedestrians and occasional unreasonable elements in the generated scenes (Figures 8 and 9 in supplementary material), suggesting areas for future refinement.

Overall, HoloDrive demonstrates a significant advancement in multi-modal generative modeling for autonomous driving, achieving state-of-the-art performance in both single-frame and video generation of consistent 2D-3D street scenes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HoloDrive represents a notable advancement in the field of generative models for autonomous driving by addressing the crucial need for joint 2D-3D multi-modal data generation. The significance of this work can be understood through its contributions and potential impact:

- **Bridging the Modality Gap:** The primary significance lies in effectively unifying 2D camera image and 3D LiDAR point cloud generation within a single framework. Existing approaches largely focused on single modalities, limiting their utility for systems that inherently rely on multimodal input. HoloDrive provides a method for generating inherently consistent data across these disparate sensor types, which is a more accurate representation of real-world autonomous driving scenarios.
- **Enhanced Realism and Consistency:** By fostering information exchange between modalities through BEV-to-Camera and Camera-to-BEV transformation modules, and incorporating depth supervision, the framework generates outputs that exhibit high cross-modal consistency. This consistency is critical for producing synthetic data that can faithfully replicate the complementary information fusion observed in real autonomous driving sensor suites, leading to more realistic and usable simulated environments.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** HoloDrive achieves competitive or superior performance compared to previous state-of-the-art methods in both single-frame and video generation benchmarks for multi-view camera images and LiDAR point clouds. This suggests that the proposed joint modeling approach does not compromise individual modality quality but rather enhances it.
- **Foundation for Advanced World Models:** The extension of the joint generation to temporal modeling, enabling the prediction of future frames, positions HoloDrive as a foundational step towards more comprehensive and holistic world models for autonomous driving. Such models can predict dynamic changes in the environment, which is vital for planning and decision-making for autonomous vehicles.
- **Improved Autonomous Driving Simulation and Training:** The ability to generate large volumes of diverse, realistic, and consistent multimodal street scene data can significantly impact the development cycle of autonomous driving systems. It can:
 - **Reduce dependence on real-world data collection:** Generating synthetic data can mitigate the high costs and logistical challenges associated with acquiring and annotating real-world driving data, especially for rare or dangerous scenarios.
 - **Facilitate extensive scenario testing:** Developers can generate an infinite variety of scenarios, including edge cases, to rigorously test perception algorithms, planning modules, and safety protocols in a controlled environment.
 - **Enable closed-loop training:** The generated data can be used for training end-to-end autonomous driving agents within simulation, allowing for iterative refinement and evaluation.
- **Advancement in Generative AI:** The novel architectural designs, including cross-modal transformation modules, depth-supervised 2D generation, and progressive multi-task training strategies, contribute to the broader field of generative artificial intelligence, particularly in complex, multi-modal synthesis tasks.

In summary, HoloDrive represents a significant stride towards generating more complete and realistic synthetic environments for autonomous driving, with the potential to accelerate research, development, and validation efforts in this critical domain.
