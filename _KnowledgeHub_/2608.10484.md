# Lost in Reconstruction: Aligning Action Representations with Language in Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SALT is a semantically aligned action tokenizer for vision-language-action models. It augments a residual VQ-VAE over action chunks with an alignment loss: quantized latents become soft prefix embeddings that a frozen language model must use to regenerate the episode instruction. The tokenizer is then frozen for policy training, lifting SimplerEnv success from 42.7% to 71.9%.

## Problem

- Action tokenizers are trained with L1/L2 reconstruction in raw action space, where numerical proximity need not match linguistically meaningful distinctions between verbs.
- Across Bin, FAST and VQ-VAE families, discretization loses verb information relative to continuous trajectories, and the gap widens as compression increases.
- Prior grounding work assumes vision carries verb semantics, overlooking that motion trajectories hold unique verb information visual start-and-end state changes cannot supply.

## Method

- **SALT** adds a ==language-alignment== term to the ==residual VQ-VAE== objective, L = Lrecon + LVQ + lambda*Lalign, shaping codebooks during tokenizer training only, leaving the VLA architecture untouched.
- Quantized chunk latents map to ==soft prefix embeddings== via a learned scalar gain plus ==positional encoding==, prompting a frozen language model to generate the episode instruction.
- Gradients flow through the frozen model's input embeddings and the ==straight-through quantizer== into encoder and codebooks; the language model is discarded afterwards and the tokenizer frozen for policy training.

## Results

- On SimplerEnv WidowX (**96** rollouts), miniVLA with SALT reaches **71.9%** mean success versus **42.7%** for reconstruction-only VQ-VAE and **31.2%** for FAST, winning every task.
- Verb probes on token IDs give SALT **39.1** macro-F1 versus **37.3** for VQ-VAE and **30.3** for FAST, at near-equal reconstruction L1 (**0.088** vs **0.080**).
- Probe-free code-verb majority-vote lookup gives SALT **46.3%** held-out episode accuracy from two residual groups versus **43.6%** for VQ-VAE (McNemar p=**.011**) and **35.0%** for FAST.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action trajectories carry verb information beyond visual state change (0.059 bits unique); the action modality is itself a grounding signal, not just an output.
> - Reconstruction error is the wrong proxy for semantic fidelity: slightly worse L1 with better language alignment still yielded large closed-loop gains.
> - Regenerating free-form instructions with a frozen language model supervises representations without verb inventories or contrastive pairs, but only where latents are learnable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and embodied AI, specifically addressing how language is grounded in robotic actions. Existing VLA models typically adapt pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) to robot control by conditioning action prediction on visual observations and natural language instructions. This paradigm allows policies to leverage representations already aligned across vision and language, primarily focusing on language as a means to specify goals, identify objects, or describe desired physical states. Consequently, alignment research in VLAs has largely concentrated on connecting referring expressions to objects and scenes or shaping visual representations through language supervision.

A common approach in VLAs is to represent continuous robot trajectories as discrete action tokens, which enables the reuse of autoregressive next-token prediction interfaces from pre-trained VLMs. Current discrete action tokenization methods include per-dimension binning (used by RT-1, RT-2, OpenVLA), learned VQ-VAE-style tokenizers optimized for trajectory reconstruction, and FAST, which compresses frequency coefficients of trajectories. These tokenization approaches are generally optimized for reconstruction fidelity in the raw action space or for data compression. A notable omission in these methods is the explicit design to align action representations with linguistic abstractions. The implicit assumption has been that achieving low action-space loss during trajectory prediction would inherently preserve the language grounding signals relevant to the instruction. This paper investigates whether this assumption holds and proposes an alternative approach.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate whether the action modality provides additional grounding signals for verbs, distinct from visual input, and to develop an action representation method that explicitly preserves linguistically meaningful distinctions. The authors hypothesize that verb meanings reflect not only the physical outcomes of actions but also the specific ways those actions are performed, involving patterns of motion, contact, and gripper timing.

The motivation for this work stems from the observation that action representations in current VLA models are not explicitly designed for alignment with language abstractions. These models primarily optimize action representations based on Euclidean physical control space metrics, leaving the relationship to language for downstream policy training to infer. The authors identify this as a potential bottleneck at the action interface of a VLA, particularly when continuous actions are converted into discrete tokens.

Specific objectives include:
- **Demonstrating the unique role of action trajectories in verb grounding:** To establish that action trajectories contain information relevant to verb meaning that visual state changes alone do not capture. This aims to show that grounding language for control requires alignment with both visual and action modalities.
- **Diagnosing semantic loss in reconstruction-only tokenization:** To empirically demonstrate that common reconstruction-only discrete action tokenization methods systematically erode verb-grounding information, especially under stronger compression. This would highlight a limitation of existing approaches where numerical proximity in raw action space does not necessarily reflect linguistically meaningful distinctions.
- **Developing a semantically aligned action tokenizer:** To introduce a novel action tokenizer that augments traditional reconstruction objectives with an auxiliary loss designed to align action representations with natural language instructions. The goal is for this tokenizer to preserve linguistically meaningful distinctions while maintaining reconstruction fidelity.
- **Evaluating the impact on policy performance:** To empirically assess whether policies trained with semantically aligned action representations achieve higher task success rates in language-conditioned control tasks compared to those using reconstruction-only tokenizers.
- **Analyzing the learned action vocabulary:** To investigate if the proposed tokenization method leads to the development of more linguistically meaningful and verb-specialized codes within the action vocabulary.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employed a multi-faceted approach involving diagnostic analyses and the development of a novel tokenization method, SALT. All experiments were conducted using the BridgeV2 dataset.

#### 3.1. Dataset

- **BridgeV2:** A dataset comprising teleoperated real-robot manipulation trajectories paired with free-form natural language instructions. It features a WidowX robot arm with 7-Degrees-of-Freedom (DoF) control. The dataset includes 27,271 episodes spanning 17 verb classes, obtained by extracting and lemmatizing instruction verbs with a minimum count filter.

#### 3.2. Diagnostic 1: Verbs Describe Both Action Goals and Motion Dynamics

- **Operationalization:** Action goal was defined as the visual change between an episode's first and last frames, represented by frozen DINOv2-S embeddings. Motion dynamics were defined as the 7-DoF action trajectory.
- **Information Estimation:** Mutual information I(Y; X) was estimated, where Y represents the verb and X represents motion dynamics, action goals, or both. This was achieved by training matched Transformer classifiers in a 5-fold stratified cross-validation setup. The classifiers predicted the verb given: (a) the 7-DoF action sequence (motion condition), (b) initial and final frame DINOv2-S representations (goal condition), or (c) both (combined condition). The architecture used a Transformer encoder consuming vision patch tokens and action tokens, with a classifier head on the CLS token.

#### 3.3. Diagnostic 2: Semantic Loss in Discrete Tokenization

- **Tokenizer Comparison:** The verb-decoding analysis from Diagnostic 1 was applied to token sequences generated by three representative tokenizer families: Bin (per-dimension discretization), FAST (frequency coefficient transformation with byte-pair encoding), and VQ-VAE (learned encoder, codebook, and decoder optimized for reconstruction).
- **Analysis Metric:** Mutual information between verbs and tokenized actions was measured as a function of bitrate (bits per timestep) across various compression levels, with the continuous trajectory serving as a reference.

#### 3.4. Semantically ALigned action Tokenizer (SALT)

- **Architecture:** SALT augments a VQ-VAE-style tokenizer, specifically a residual VQ-VAE, which encodes an 8-step, 7-DoF action chunk into a sequence of K codebook indices (e.g., 7 residual groups of 256 codes).
- **Objective Function:** The standard VQ-VAE objective (L_recon for reconstruction, L_VQ for codebook and commitment losses) is augmented with a language-alignment loss (λL_align).
 - L = L_recon + L_VQ + λL_align
- **Language Alignment Mechanism:** For an episode, the quantized latents (q) from all action chunks are converted into soft prefix embeddings (p) using a learned scalar gain and positional encoding. This prefix sequence, along with a textual prompt, is fed to a frozen, pre-trained Vision-Language Model (VLM). The VLM is then tasked with generating the episode's natural language instruction, and L_align is the language model's cross-entropy loss for this generation task.
- **Gradient Flow:** The language model remains frozen, and gradients from L_align propagate through the VLM's input embeddings and a straight-through quantizer back into the tokenizer's encoder and codebooks. This process shapes the tokenizer's latent space to preserve information predictive of language without altering the VLM itself.
- **Deployment:** After tokenizer training, the VLM used for alignment is discarded, and the SALT tokenizer is frozen. Downstream VLA training proceeds normally, with the policy predicting discrete codebook indices that are decoded by SALT into continuous actions.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup for Policy Evaluation

- **VLA Model:** miniVLA, a Prismatic-style VLA with a Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone, was used as the policy.
- **Training:** All policies were trained on BridgeV2 for 15,000 gradient steps with a global batch size of 128, starting from a base vision-language checkpoint.
- **Comparison:** SALT was compared against a reconstruction-only VQ-VAE tokenizer (same architecture, capacity, and training data as SALT, but without L_align) and FAST. Hyperparameters were matched to ensure comparable compute and compression budgets (approximately 7-8.6 bits per timestep).
- **Evaluation Environment:** Rollout success was measured in SimplerEnv's visual-matching WidowX suite, consisting of four tabletop manipulation tasks (24 episodes each, 96 total rollouts per policy) with open-loop execution of 8-step action chunks.
- **Metrics:** Policy success rate, verb decodability (using Macro-F1 on token IDs and policy's action-token input embeddings), reconstruction fidelity (L1 error), and qualitative analysis of code-verb co-occurrence distributions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several key findings regarding the semantic content of action trajectories and the impact of linguistically-aligned action tokenization.

- **Action Trajectories Contain Unique Verb-Grounding Information:** Diagnostic 1 demonstrated that action goals and motion dynamics provide complementary information about verbs. The combined visual and motion representation contained more verb information than either modality alone. Motion dynamics contributed an estimated 0.059 bits of unique verb information beyond the visual goal representation, while action goals contributed 0.282 bits beyond motion. This indicates that verbs are grounded in both the outcome of an action and the manner in which it is performed. Verbs such as "move" and "put" showed significant motion-unique contributions, while "fold" was strongly associated with visual state changes.

- **Reconstruction-Only Tokenization Erodes Verb-Grounding Signals:** Diagnostic 2 revealed that discrete action representations produced by reconstruction-only tokenizers (Bin, VQ-VAE, FAST) retained less verb information than continuous actions. This semantic loss generally increased with stronger compression levels. This finding suggests that optimizing solely for reconstruction in Euclidean action space does not guarantee the preservation of linguistically meaningful distinctions, indicating a bottleneck at the discrete action interface.

- **SALT Improves Downstream Task Performance:** Policies trained with SALT achieved a 71.9% average task success rate in SimplerEnv, which represents a substantial improvement over the 42.7% achieved by policies using a reconstruction-only VQ-VAE tokenizer and 31.2% for FAST. SALT demonstrated superior performance across all individual tasks, with the largest margins observed on more complex tasks such as "stack" and "put eggplant in basket." The performance gap between SALT and VQ-VAE, which shared architectural and training specifics except for the alignment loss, attributes the improvement directly to the semantically aligned action vocabulary.

- **Semantic Alignment Transfers to Policy Representations:** Although the alignment objective directly supervises only the tokenizer's latent representations, its effects propagated to both the discrete token IDs generated by the tokenizer and the action-token embeddings learned by the downstream VLA. SALT achieved the highest verb decodability at both stages, with 39.1% Macro-F1 on token IDs (compared to 37.3% for VQ-VAE) and 43.7% on learned action embeddings (compared to 38.3% for VQ-VAE). This indicates that the semantic structure introduced during tokenizer training is preserved and utilized by the subsequent policy.

- **Reconstruction Fidelity is Maintained:** The improved semantic structure did not compromise the ability to reconstruct continuous actions. At comparable compression rates, SALT's held-out reconstruction error (L1 of 0.088) remained close to that of VQ-VAE (0.080). Furthermore, characteristic motion signatures were largely preserved after reconstruction, suggesting that SALT primarily reorganizes how action trajectories are partitioned rather than their faithfulness.

- **Emergence of Verb-Specialized Codes:** Analysis of code-verb co-occurrence demonstrated that SALT developed highly selective action codes, with individual codes becoming specialized for particular verb classes such as "flip" or "turn." In contrast, VQ-VAE distributed similar trajectories across more generic, semantically mixed codes, and FAST's codes largely reflected the corpus verb distribution. Quantitatively, a probe-free majority-vote lookup based on code-verb co-occurrence achieved higher held-out accuracy for SALT across all residual groups and cumulative group prefixes compared to VQ-VAE and FAST. This indicates that semantic alignment effectively reorganizes the action vocabulary into linguistically meaningful categories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work provides contributions to the field of embodied AI and VLA model development, particularly concerning the often-overlooked aspect of action representation.

- **Reconceptualization of Language Grounding:** The research establishes that language grounding for robotic control is not solely reliant on visual input but significantly benefits from information embedded within the dynamics of action trajectories. This reinforces a deeper understanding of verb semantics in an embodied context, where "how" an action is performed is as crucial as "what" is achieved.
- **Addressing a VLA Bottleneck:** By identifying and providing a solution for the semantic loss introduced by reconstruction-only discrete action tokenization, the paper addresses a critical bottleneck in VLA architectures. This is particularly relevant for models that leverage pre-trained VLMs and discrete action interfaces.
- **Improved Robotic Policy Learning:** The substantial increase in task success rates achieved by policies trained with SALT demonstrates that explicitly aligning action representations with language can lead to more robust and effective robot control. This suggests a pathway for improving the generalizability and performance of language-conditioned robotic systems.
- **Enhanced Interpretability of Action Vocabularies:** The finding that SALT produces verb-specialized codes indicates a move towards more interpretable action representations. Such structured vocabularies could facilitate debugging, analysis, and potentially human understanding of learned robotic behaviors, which is valuable for complex autonomous systems.
- **A General Design Principle:** The work proposes a design principle for language-conditioned control: action representations should be learned not only to enable the execution of trajectories but also to preserve the linguistic abstractions through which humans describe and categorize actions. This principle could guide future research and development in robot learning.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper opens several avenues for future work, including exploring the benefits of semantic tokenization with richer linguistic datasets, extending similar semantic supervision to other tokenizer architectures (e.g., fixed binning), and evaluating the method's efficacy in large-scale multi-embodiment pretraining and real-world robot deployments. It also suggests further investigation into the causal mechanisms linking semantically organized action codes to improved policy learning.
