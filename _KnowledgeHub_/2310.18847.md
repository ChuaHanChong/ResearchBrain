# Bird's Eye View Based Pretrained World model for Visual Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> A visual navigation system trained entirely in CARLA that zero-shot transfers to a real differential-drive robot. A ResNet-50 perception model maps first-person RGB to embeddings aligned with Bird's Eye View maps via a joint reconstruction plus time-contrastive loss, while a Mixture-Density LSTM memory module with anchor and temporal state checking interpolates missing observations, yielding higher out-of-domain accuracy and real-world navigation success than a classifier baseline.

## Problem

- Policies trained end-to-end in simulators overfit to the training task and fail to learn generalizable representations that survive the synthetic-to-real domain gap.
- Style-transfer and domain-adaptation approaches to closing the sim-to-real gap generate realistic data but neglect learning representations optimal for the downstream navigation task.
- BEV map prediction from front-view images is evaluated only on vision metrics, with no evidence such representations serve robotic control, and reconstruction-only encoders rival random ones.

## Method

- Perception model: ImageNet-initialized ==ResNet-50== trained with a combined loss adding binary ==BEV== reconstruction to an ==InfoNCE== time-contrastive term, giving temporally smooth embeddings that decode to occupancy maps.
- Memory model: an ==LSTM== with a ==Mixture Density Network== head predicts the next latent from previous action, state and hidden context, trained by negative log-likelihood over Gaussian mixtures.
- Robustness modules: **Anchor State Checking** snaps embeddings to their nearest BEV anchor, and **Temporal State Checking** discards low-confidence percepts, substituting the LSTM's prior prediction.

## Results

- On a 6-class Google Street View out-of-domain set and an unseen simulator set, the method beat a ResNet-50 classifier baseline trained on **140,213** labelled first-person images in per-class accuracy.
- Frozen-encoder ==PPO== policy training in CARLA gave higher cumulative reward than VAE, time-contrastive, random and CLIP-RN50 encoders, and higher planner success rate using decoded occupancy maps.
- Ablations on five real-robot ROSBag sequences showed adding the LSTM lowers reconstruction mean-squared-error and cross-entropy while raising accuracy, with anchor snapping drawn from **1,439** BEV anchors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pairing a reconstruction objective with a contrastive one fixes the discontinuous embeddings contrastive-only training produces, giving representations useful for both control and planning.
> - Choosing a compact, texture-invariant intermediate target like binary BEV maps, roughly 0.5KB versus 1MB per frame, transfers across domains better than pixel-level realism tricks.
> - A learned dynamics model doubles as a runtime safety layer: rejecting low-confidence percepts and interpolating covers sensor rates slower than control loops, useful for low-frequency large models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a central challenge in robotics and artificial intelligence: the Sim2Real gap. This gap refers to the performance degradation observed when models trained in simulated environments are deployed in the real world, primarily due to discrepancies between synthetic and real-world data. Reinforcement Learning (RL), a popular paradigm for training autonomous agents, largely relies on simulators to circumvent the prohibitive costs and safety concerns associated with real-world trial-and-error.

Traditional approaches to Sim2Real transfer include optimizing simulators to closely mimic reality, utilizing domain randomization to make models robust to variations, or employing domain adaptation techniques. Some existing works focus on deploying models trained in simulators to real-world applications like self-driving cars. However, these often struggle to generalize to complex, cluttered, or diverse real-world scenarios due to overfitting to specific training tasks or environments. Other methods, such as style transfer, attempt to bridge the domain gap by generating data that blends synthetic and real-world characteristics but may not inherently focus on learning optimal, generalizable representations for robotic tasks. The paper notes that end-to-end models trained solely in simulators tend to overfit, failing to learn representations that generalize effectively.

This research positions itself within the context of learning generalizable representations for visual navigation, particularly those that are lightweight and robust for mobile platforms. It leverages the concept of pretraining encoders on large unlabelled datasets, a method shown to yield promising results across various tasks by allowing smaller networks to be trained on top of frozen encoders. A key intermediary representation explored is the Bird's Eye View (BEV) image, recognized for its compact, texture-invariant, and efficient nature, which can be beneficial for point-goal navigation. While previous work has explored estimating BEV maps from First-Person View (FPV) RGB images, their utility for actual robotic tasks beyond visual reconstruction has often lacked conclusive evidence. Furthermore, the paper acknowledges the utility of recurrent world models in RL for capturing sensory data representation and temporal dynamics, which improve agent performance and allow for freezing certain modules after learning environment representations.

The present work contributes to this landscape by proposing a novel system for zero-shot Sim2Real transfer in visual navigation that does not rely on maps. It aims to overcome the Sim2Real gap by training a system entirely in a simulator, using BEV as an intermediary representation, and enhancing its robustness for real-world deployment through specific state-checking modules.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust visual navigation system that can achieve zero-shot Sim2Real transfer, meaning it is trained entirely in a simulator and directly deployed in the real world without further real-world training. This objective is driven by several motivations:

1. **Addressing the Sim2Real Gap:** The high costs and risks associated with real-world robot training necessitate simulator-based development. However, the inherent domain gap between simulated and real data leads to significant performance degradation upon real-world deployment. The research seeks to mitigate this by designing a system specifically for zero-shot transfer, focusing on the perception pipeline.

2. **Learning Generalizable and Optimal Representations:** Many existing models, particularly those trained end-to-end in simulators, are prone to overfitting to specific tasks and fail to learn generalizable visual representations. The paper aims to learn compact, intermediate embeddings from FPV RGB images that are optimal for downstream robotic tasks, specifically navigation. These embeddings should be consistent with BEV reconstructions and exhibit smooth temporal transitions.

3. **Enhancing Robustness in Real-World Scenarios:** Real-world environments introduce uncertainties such as noise, missing observations, and out-of-domain data, which can severely impact a robot's navigation performance. A key motivation is to enhance the system's robustness against these real-world challenges, particularly by interpolating uncertain or missing observations and ensuring consistency of representations.

4. **Creating a Lightweight and Flexible System:** Mobile robot platforms often have limited computational resources. The research is motivated to design a model that is efficient and lightweight, achieved by decoupling the perception and control models and utilizing pretrained encoders. This also allows for the pretraining of the encoder using diverse observation sequences, independent of specific robot dynamics.

5. **Facilitating Research and Development:** By releasing a comprehensive codebase, dataset, and models, the authors aim to contribute to the open-source community, lowering the barrier for other researchers to build upon and further develop robust visual navigation systems.

In summary, the core motivation is to enable mobile robots to efficiently and robustly function in diverse real-world scenarios, entirely leveraging simulator-trained models, by focusing on learning effective visual representations and enhancing system reliability through architectural and methodological innovations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed system for visual navigation consists of two main components: a perception model and a control model. The perception model processes FPV RGB observations to output an embedding, which is then fed into the control model (policy) to generate actions. The entire system is trained in a simulator and then directly deployed without retraining.

**A. Perception Model:**
The perception model's objective is to learn compact intermediate representations that are compatible with downstream policy learning. It takes an FPV RGB image (o_t) as input and outputs an embedding (z_t) and a reconstructed Bird's Eye View (BEV) image (x_t).

1. **Architecture:** A ResNet-50 encoder, initialized with ImageNet pretrained weights, processes the RGB image. The embedding z_t is then decoded through a BEV decoder to reconstruct the BEV image.
2. **Training Principles:**
 - The embedding z_t should consistently correspond to the BEV reconstruction.
 - BEV images should be represented in a continuous latent space with smooth temporal transitions.
 - The model should efficiently utilize unlabelled sequences of images to learn optimal behavior representations.
3. **Loss Functions:**
 - **Reconstruction Loss (L_R):** This is a binary cross-entropy loss that measures the difference between the reconstructed BEV image (x) and the ground-truth BEV label (y). It guides the encoder to embed FPV observations in a way that allows their corresponding BEV images to be reconstructed.
 - **Time-Contrastive Network Loss (L_InfoNCE):** This loss encourages embeddings of observations that are temporally close to be closer in the embedding space (positives) and observations that are temporally distant to be farther apart (negatives). This aims to capture temporal understanding within the representations.
 - **Combined Contrastive with Reconstruction Loss (L_CR):** The perception model is optimized using a weighted sum of the reconstruction loss and the InfoNCE loss: L_CR = L_R + β · L_InfoNCE. The hyperparameter β balances the two loss components. This combined loss is designed to learn more temporally continuous and smoother embeddings by constraining proximity based on both contrastive learning and BEV reconstructions, aiming to alleviate the discontinuity issues observed in prior contrastive methods.

**B. Temporal Model with Robustness Modules:**
To enhance robustness and stability, particularly for real-world deployment, an LSTM-based Memory model is integrated into the pipeline.

1. **Architecture:** An LSTM takes the current latent representation z_t and previous action a_(t-1) as input, along with historical context (previous state prediction ˆz_(t-1) and hidden state h_(t-1)), to refine the state into a candidate ˆz_t. A Mixture Density Network (MDN) is placed on top of the LSTM output layer to model uncertainty in future states.
2. **Loss Function (L_M):** The memory model is optimized using the negative log-likelihood of the ground truth state z_t under a Gaussian Mixture Model predicted by the MDN. This helps the model predict future states while accounting for uncertainty.
3. **Robustness Modules:**
 - **Anchor State Checking (ASC):** The system maintains a dataset R of pre-collected BEV-based latent embeddings (called BEV anchors). Upon obtaining an embedding z_t from the ResNet-50, it is replaced with the closest matching anchor embedding (¯z_t) from R. This ensures that the LSTM and the policy consistently operate within a predefined BEV data distribution, mitigating the impact of slight domain shifts when transferring to the real world.
 - **Temporal State Checking (TSC):** This module rejects erroneous predictions from the ResNet-50. If the confidence score (τ_t, derived from cosine-similarity or MSE) of the perception model's processed prediction (¯z_t) falls below a predefined threshold (ρ), ¯z_t is discarded. Instead, the output of the LSTM from the previous time step (ˆz_(t-1)) is used as the current state, effectively interpolating the robot's state during unreliable observations.
 - **Interpolation for Delayed Observations:** The Memory model inherently addresses discrepancies in update frequencies between control signals (e.g., 50Hz) and camera frames (e.g., 15Hz) by providing continuous state predictions, thus ensuring system continuity even when actual observations are delayed.

**C. Experimental Platform and Data Collection:**
1. **Platform:** A non-holonomic, differential-drive robot (Beobotv3) running ROS is used for real-world evaluation. A Coral EdgeTPU is employed for running CNN models. The CARLA simulator (version 0.9.13) was modified to support differential-drive robots, ensuring a consistent control pipeline between simulation and reality.
2. **Data:**
 - **Train Dataset:** A large FPV-BEV image dataset was collected from the CARLA simulator, incorporating weather randomization and non-player traffic for diversity.
 - **Validation Dataset:** Panoramic images from Google Street View (USC campus) were segmented and manually labeled into 6 distinct classes, serving as an out-of-domain dataset to evaluate the perception model's generalization without temporal sequencing.
 - **Test Dataset:** Five ROSBag video sequences were recorded from the Beobotv3 robot on the USC campus. These temporally continuous sequences were labeled and used to evaluate the complete navigation system, including the robustness modules.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the effectiveness of the proposed method in various aspects of visual navigation, including representation learning, planning, robustness, and real-world transfer.

1. **Policy Learning with Pretrained Representations:**
 - The study compared the performance of a 1-layer policy, trained using the PPO algorithm in the CARLA simulator, when fed embeddings from different frozen encoders. Baselines included VAE (reconstructing only BEV), TCN (trained with InfoNCE loss), a randomly initialized encoder, and CLIP-RN50.
 - **Result:** The proposed method, trained with the combined contrastive and reconstruction loss (L_CR), demonstrated higher cumulative rewards and faster learning convergence compared to all baselines (Fig. 6). This indicates that the representations learned by the proposed method are effective for enabling a policy to learn navigation tasks efficiently. VAE showed the next best performance.

2. **Planning Using BEV Reconstructions:**
 - The effectiveness of BEV reconstructions for planning was evaluated using the TEB planner. This involved reconstructing occupancy maps from RGB inputs, a capability inherent to methods with decoders (ours and VAE). For other baselines (Random, CLIP, TCN), decoders were trained to upsample embeddings for BEV reconstruction.
 - **Result:** The proposed method achieved a higher success rate (SR) in the planning task compared to VAE and other baselines (Fig. 6, dotted lines). This suggests that the quality of BEV reconstructions from the proposed perception model is beneficial for navigation planning.

3. **Quantitative Analysis of Perception Model (Generalization):**
 - The ResNet-50 perception model was quantitatively evaluated on the validation dataset (unseen simulation and real-world Google Street View data). Its performance (identifying the closest matching class for the output embedding) was compared against a 6-way ResNet-50 classifier baseline trained on a large labeled dataset.
 - **Result:** The proposed perception model significantly outperformed the baseline ResNet-50 classifier in accuracy on both unseen simulator data and the real-world validation dataset (Table 5). This finding indicates that contrastive learning combined with BEV prediction facilitates better generalization to out-of-domain visual data.

4. **Ablation Experiments for State Checking Modules:**
 - Ablation studies were conducted on the test dataset (temporal sequences from simulator and real-world) to assess the impact of the LSTM memory model and its robustness modules (ASC and TSC). Metrics included accuracy (ACC), Mean Squared Error (MSE), and Cross-Entropy (CE) for BEV reconstruction quality.
 - **Result:** The full system incorporating the LSTM with both ASC and TSC modules demonstrated superior performance across ACC, MSE, and CE metrics (Table 7), particularly for real-world data sequences. The results showed that these modules enhance the robustness of the navigation pipeline, yielding more granular BEV representations and improved handling of abnormal observations and ambiguities within time-series data. TSC and ASC individually contributed to performance gains, and their combined effect further improved the system's resilience.

5. **Evaluation on a Real-world System:**
 - The pretrained ResNet encoder and simulator-trained policy were deployed on a real-world differential-drive robot (Beobotv3) for visual navigation to a designated destination.
 - **Result:** The system achieved a success rate (SR) of 85% for real-world planning experiments (Fig. 5). This demonstrates the feasibility and effectiveness of zero-shot Sim2Real transfer for visual navigation using the proposed architecture.
 - **Runtime Efficiency:** The computational costs of each module were measured (Table 8). The perception model operated at approximately 20ms, the memory model at 5ms, and the policy at 5ms. These low latencies indicate that the system is computationally efficient and suitable for real-time deployment on edge computing devices like the Coral EdgeTPU.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of visual navigation and robotics by demonstrating a method for achieving zero-shot Sim2Real transfer, which holds significant implications for the development and deployment of autonomous systems.

1. **Bridging the Sim2Real Gap Effectively:** The primary significance lies in providing a robust framework that allows models trained entirely in a simulator to be directly deployed in complex real-world environments without requiring costly and time-consuming real-world data collection or fine-tuning. This reduces development cycles and operational costs associated with robotic systems.

2. **Learning Robust and Generalizable Representations:** The approach of translating FPV RGB images to BEV representations combined with a contrastive learning objective is shown to produce compact, continuous, and generalizable embeddings. These representations are less susceptible to the domain gap and are effective for various downstream tasks like policy learning and planning, enhancing the reliability of visual navigation.

3. **Enhanced System Robustness:** The integration of Anchor State Checking (ASC) and Temporal State Checking (TSC) into a memory-based recurrent model significantly improves the system's ability to cope with real-world challenges such as noisy, uncertain, or missing observations and out-of-domain data. This architectural innovation provides a mechanism for state interpolation and consistency, which is crucial for stable robot operation.

4. **Efficient and Lightweight Deployment:** By decoupling the perception and control models and utilizing pretrained encoders, the system maintains a low computational footprint, making it suitable for deployment on mobile platforms with limited processing power, such as those relying on edge AI accelerators. This addresses a practical constraint for many real-world robotic applications.

5. **Open-Source Contribution:** The release of the codebase, dataset, and models provides a valuable resource for the research community. This initiative can accelerate further research and development in zero-shot Sim2Real transfer, visual navigation, and robust robot learning, fostering collaboration and advancements in the field.

Looking forward, the methodology's flexibility to incorporate additional FPV-BEV datasets (simulated or real-world) suggests potential for continuous improvement and adaptation to new environments or tasks. The authors also highlight the possibility of unsupervised training or fine-tuning using real-world expert videos, which could further enhance the system's capabilities and autonomy. The work represents a step towards developing more practical, efficient, and resilient autonomous navigation systems.
