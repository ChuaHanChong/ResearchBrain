# Aligning Cyber Space with Physical World: A Comprehensive Survey on Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper meticulously reviews the current state and future trajectories of Embodied Artificial Intelligence, specifically focusing on the integration of Multi-modal Large Models and World Models. It systematically categorizes advancements across robots, simulators, perception, interaction, agent architectures, and sim-to-real adaptation, while introducing the ARIO (All Robots In One) dataset standard to foster the development of general-purpose embodied agents.

## Problem

- Existing surveys on Embodied AI were largely outdated, predating the significant advancements brought by Multi-modal Large Models (MLMs) and World Models (WMs) around 2023.
- A lack of comprehensive, up-to-date resources made it challenging to understand the rapidly evolving landscape of Embodied AI, particularly regarding the alignment of cyberspace and the physical world.
- The heterogeneity and scarcity of comprehensive, multi-modal robotic datasets hindered the development and generalization capabilities of robust embodied agents.

## Method

- The authors conduct an extensive, structured literature review, systematically categorizing recent works on embodied robots, simulators, perception, interaction, agent architectures, and ==sim-to-real adaptation==.
- The survey integrates the transformative impact of ==Multi-modal Large Models (MLMs)== and ==World Models (WMs)== into its analysis, offering a contemporary perspective on agent capabilities and future directions.
- A novel ==ARIO (All Robots In One)== dataset standard and a unified large-scale ARIO dataset are proposed to address data heterogeneity, volume, and cross-platform incompatibility.

## Results

- The survey provides a detailed taxonomy of embodied robots (e.g., humanoid, quadruped) and simulators (general-purpose, real-scene based), outlining their characteristics and applications.
- It synthesizes state-of-the-art methods and datasets across key research targets, including active visual perception, visual language navigation (VLN), embodied question answering (EQA), and language-guided grasping.
- The authors propose the ARIO dataset standard and a unified large-scale ARIO dataset (**3 million episodes**, **321,064 tasks**) designed to facilitate training of versatile and general-purpose embodied agents across diverse robot types and environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Embodied AI is deemed crucial for achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), requiring agents to actively perceive, interact with, and reason within the physical world.
> - Multi-modal Large Models (MLMs) and World Models (WMs) are identified as foundational for enhancing embodied agents' perception, interaction, and hierarchical planning capabilities.
> - Bridging the 'sim-to-real gap' remains a central challenge, necessitating robust adaptation strategies and unified, high-quality datasets to transfer learned behaviors from simulation to physical robots effectively.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied Artificial Intelligence (Embodied AI) is a rapidly evolving and increasingly critical field within the broader landscape of Artificial Intelligence, considered by many as fundamental for achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). The core premise of Embodied AI is to develop agents that can not only process information in virtual environments (cyberspace) but also actively perceive, interact with, and reason within the complexities and unpredictability of the physical world through physical embodiments like robots. This paradigm seeks to overcome the limitations of disembodied AI (e.g., conversational agents like ChatGPT) by integrating cognition with physical interaction, thereby grounding AI in real-world experiences.

This survey paper enters the research landscape at a crucial juncture, specifically in the "era of Multi-modal Large Models (MLMs) and World Models (WMs)." The recent emergence and remarkable capabilities of MLMs (e.g., in perception, interaction, and reasoning) have infused new potential into embodied agents, enabling them to better understand linguistic instructions, interpret visual information, and perform complex tasks. Similarly, World Models, with their ability to simulate environments and comprehend physical laws, offer a promising architecture for enhancing the predictive and planning capacities of embodied agents.

The authors explicitly state that while several surveys on Embodied AI exist, most are "outdated as they were published before the era of MLMs, which started around 2023." They highlight a gap in the literature for a truly comprehensive survey that fully considers the impact and integration of MLMs, WMs, and recent developments in embodied robots and simulators. This paper positions itself to fill this void, providing an up-to-date and holistic reference that captures the state-of-the-art in this fast-moving field.

By focusing on the alignment of cyberspace (virtual environments, abstract planning) with the physical world (real robots, tangible interactions), the survey directly addresses one of the grand challenges in AI: bridging the simulation-to-reality gap and enabling intelligent systems to operate effectively beyond controlled digital spaces. Its scope encompasses fundamental components (robots, simulators), key research tasks (perception, interaction, agent development), and crucial adaptation mechanisms (sim-to-real), thereby offering a panoramic view that can guide future research towards more robust, versatile, and general-purpose embodied agents for applications ranging from intelligent mechatronics and smart manufacturing to healthcare and autonomous vehicles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this paper is to provide a comprehensive and up-to-date exploration of the latest advancements in Embodied Artificial Intelligence (Embodied AI), particularly in the context of emerging Multi-modal Large Models (MLMs) and World Models (WMs). The authors aim for this survey to serve as a foundational reference for the research community, offering a clear big picture of current achievements and future potential in the field.

The motivation behind this comprehensive review stems from several critical factors:
1. **Foundational Role of Embodied AI for AGI:** The authors firmly believe that Embodied AI is "crucial for achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)." Unlike disembodied AI, Embodied AI focuses on agents that interact with both simulated and physical environments, which is seen as essential for developing true intelligence capable of navigating the complexities and unpredictability of the real world.
2. **Bridging Cyberspace and the Physical World:** Embodied AI acts as a "foundation for various applications (e.g., intelligent mechatronics systems, smart manufacturing) that bridge cyberspace and the physical world." This highlights the practical and industrial significance of the field, enabling systems to learn, perceive, and execute autonomously in real-world scenarios.
3. **Impact of Multi-modal Large Models (MLMs) and World Models (WMs):** The recent emergence of MLMs and WMs has revolutionized the capabilities of AI, offering "remarkable perception, interaction, and reasoning capabilities." These models are identified as "promising architecture for embodied agents," injecting strong potential for developing general-purpose agents that interact actively with diverse environments. This transformative impact necessitates a reassessment of the Embodied AI landscape.
4. **Addressing Gaps in Existing Surveys:** The authors note that previous surveys on Embodied AI are largely "outdated" or too narrowly focused, predating the significant advancements brought by MLMs (which gained prominence around 2023). Specifically, they state that "MLMs, WMs and embodied agents are not fully considered in previous surveys," nor are "recent developments in embodied robots and simulators." This survey is explicitly motivated to fill this critical gap, providing a much-needed contemporary perspective.
5. **Understanding the Evolving Landscape:** Given the rapid pace of innovation, it is "imperative to capture the evolving landscape of embodied AI" to unravel its complexities, evaluate its current developmental stage, and contemplate future trajectories. This survey aims to provide a systematic overview to help researchers navigate this dynamic field.
6. **Developing General-Purpose Embodied Agents:** The pursuit of general-purpose embodied agents that can actively interact with virtual and physical environments is a central theme. The survey intends to consolidate knowledge that facilitates the development of such agents, especially by leveraging the powerful capabilities of MLMs.
7. **Proposing a New Dataset Standard:** Beyond surveying existing work, a key motivation and contribution is to propose "a new dataset standard ARIO (All Robots In One) and a unified large-scale ARIO dataset," directly addressing the need for better data infrastructure to train robust, general-purpose embodied agents and overcome current data heterogeneity and scalability issues.

In summary, the paper is driven by the ambition to chart the course of Embodied AI towards AGI, leveraging the power of modern MLMs and WMs, while providing an indispensable, comprehensive, and forward-looking resource for the global research community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

As a comprehensive survey paper, the methodology and approach employed by the authors involve a systematic and structured review, synthesis, and analysis of existing literature and recent advancements in Embodied AI. The paper's organization reflects this structured approach, designed to provide a holistic understanding of the field.

The methodology can be broken down into the following key steps and structural elements:

1. **Initial Scope Definition:** The survey begins by establishing the importance of Embodied AI for AGI and its role in bridging cyberspace and the physical world, emphasizing the recent impact of Multi-modal Large Models (MLMs) and World Models (WMs). This sets the context for the literature review.

2. **Categorization of Core Components and Research Targets:** The authors adopt a layered approach, first examining the foundational elements and then delving into specific research areas:
 - **Foundational Hardware/Software:** They start by exploring "representative works of embodied robots and simulators." This includes categorizing various robot types (fixed-base, wheeled, tracked, quadruped, humanoid, biomimetic) and differentiating between general-purpose simulators (e.g., Isaac Sim, Gazebo) and real-scene based simulators (e.g., SAPIEN, AI2-THOR). This initial analysis helps understand the physical and virtual platforms available for Embodied AI research and their respective limitations.
 - **Main Research Targets:** They then divide the core research challenges of Embodied AI into four main targets:
 1. **Embodied Perception:** How agents sense and interpret their environment.
 2. **Embodied Interaction:** How agents manipulate objects and communicate with humans.
 3. **Embodied Agent:** The architecture and planning mechanisms of the agents themselves.
 4. **Sim-to-Real Adaptation:** The crucial process of transferring learned behaviors from simulation to the physical world.

3. **In-depth Analysis within Each Research Target:** For each of the four main research targets, the authors consistently provide:
 - **State-of-the-art Methods:** A review of the most recent and impactful algorithms and models.
 - **Essential Paradigms:** Underlying theoretical frameworks or recurring approaches (e.g., graph-based learning, reinforcement learning, hierarchical planning).
 - **Comprehensive Datasets:** A discussion and comparison of benchmark datasets pertinent to the task, highlighting their features, environments, and challenges (e.g., R2R, ALFRED for VLN; EQA v1, OpenEQA for EQA).
 - **Focus on MLMs and WMs:** Throughout the discussion, the authors highlight the role and implications of MLMs and WMs, emphasizing how these models are transforming capabilities in perception, interaction, and planning, and how they facilitate interactions in both digital and physical environments.

4. **Structured Comparison and Summarization:** The survey frequently employs tables (e.g., Table I for Disembodied vs. Embodied AI, Table II for General Simulators, Tables III-VII for methods and datasets) to provide clear, concise comparisons of different approaches, datasets, and their characteristics, features, and limitations. This structured comparison enhances readability and helps readers quickly grasp key distinctions and advancements.

5. **Identification of Challenges and Future Directions:** A critical component of the methodology involves synthesizing the limitations of current approaches and identifying overarching challenges facing the field. Based on this analysis, the authors then propose promising future research directions, covering aspects like high-quality robotic datasets, long-horizon task execution, causal reasoning, unified evaluation benchmarks, and security and privacy.

6. **Introduction of Novel Contributions:** Beyond reviewing the existing literature, the authors introduce a novel contribution: the **ARIO (All Robots In One) dataset standard and a unified large-scale ARIO dataset**. This demonstrates a proactive approach to addressing current data-related limitations in the field, aiming to optimize existing datasets and facilitate the development of more versatile and general-purpose embodied agents. This concrete proposal is integrated within the "Sim-to-Real Adaptation" section, showcasing how a unified data standard can bridge gaps.

7. **Referencing and Project Link:** The inclusion of an extensive list of references (over 200) underscores the thoroughness of the review. The associated project link (https://github.com/HCPLab-SYSU/Embodied_AI_Paper_List) further provides an active resource for the community, reflecting the authors' commitment to facilitating ongoing research.

In essence, the methodology of this survey is an exhaustive, multi-faceted literature analysis structured around key conceptual and technical pillars of Embodied AI, with a particular emphasis on recent paradigm shifts driven by large models and a forward-looking perspective on open problems and practical solutions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

As a comprehensive survey, this paper's "findings" are a systematic synthesis of the state-of-the-art, identifying key trends, achievements, limitations, and future directions across various facets of Embodied AI. The main findings and results are presented through a structured review of robots, simulators, and four critical research targets: embodied perception, embodied interaction, embodied agents, and sim-to-real adaptation.

#### 4.1. Embodied Robots and Simulators

- **Robot Categorization:** The survey presents a taxonomy of embodied robots, including fixed-base, wheeled, tracked, quadruped, humanoid, and biomimetic robots, highlighting their diverse applications (e.g., industrial automation, logistics, exploration, personalized services) and unique challenges.
- **Simulator Landscape:** It categorizes simulators into "General Simulators" (e.g., Isaac Sim, Gazebo, PyBullet, MuJoCo, and the newly launched Genesis) and "Real-Scene Based Simulators" (e.g., SAPIEN, VirtualHome, AI2-THOR, iGibson, Habitat, InfiniteWorld). General simulators are noted for their cost-effectiveness and scalability, while real-scene based simulators offer high realism and interactivity crucial for household tasks. Automated simulation scene construction (e.g., RoboGen, ProcTHOR) is identified as a vital advancement for generating high-quality data.

#### 4.2. Embodied Perception

- **Active Visual Perception:** Perception in Embodied AI requires more than passive observation; it demands active exploration. Key components include:
 - **Visual SLAM (vSLAM):** Advances from traditional (MonoSLAM, ORB-SLAM) to semantic vSLAM (SLAM++, QuadricSLAM), integrating semantic information for better environmental interpretation.
 - **3D Scene Understanding:** Methods are categorized into projection-based, voxel-based, and point-based, with recent emphasis on scalable Transformer-based (PointTransformer, Swin3d, PQ3D) and Mamba-based (PointMamba) architectures for processing sparse, disordered 3D data.
 - **Active Exploration:** Agents are learning to interact with the environment (Pinto et al.) or change viewing directions (Jayaraman et al., NeU-NBV) to acquire more informative visual observations and reduce uncertainty.
- **Visual Language Navigation (VLN):** This is a crucial task requiring agents to navigate unseen environments based on linguistic instructions.
 - **Diverse Datasets:** A wide array of datasets is reviewed (R2R, ALFRED, BEHAVIOR-1K, etc.), characterized by different instruction granularities (step-by-step, goal-described, demand-driven) and interaction levels (non-interactive, interactive, dialogue-based).
 - **Methodologies:** VLN methods are broadly classified into Memory-Understanding Based (e.g., graph learning for historical trajectories, semantic mapping, LLM integration like NaviLLM) and Future-Prediction Based (e.g., waypoint predictors, environmental encoding, reinforcement learning, LLM 'imagination' like MiC). The trend shows LLMs significantly enhancing instruction understanding and historical context leveraging.

#### 4.3. Embodied Interaction

- **Embodied Question Answering (EQA):** Agents explore environments to answer questions.
 - **Dataset Variety:** Datasets like EQA v1, IQUAD V1, K-EQA, OpenEQA, HM-EQA, and EXPRESS-Bench offer challenges ranging from simple object location to complex, knowledge-intensive, and interactive queries.
 - **Methodological Shift:** Early EQA relied on neural networks (CNNs, RNNs) for modular vision, language, and navigation. More recently, LLMs and VLMs are leveraged to perform complex reasoning, utilize external knowledge, and guide exploration (Majumdar et al., Patel et al.), often employing frontier-based exploration.
 - **Limitations:** Dataset construction challenges and a significant performance gap between models and human levels remain, particularly in storing environmental information and ensuring interpretability.
- **Embodied Grasping:** This involves grasping and placing objects based on human instructions.
 - **Dataset Evolution:** Traditional grasping datasets (Cornell, GraspNet) are being augmented or re-structured to include linguistic text, leading to semantic-grasping datasets (OCID-VLG, ReasoningGrasp, CapGrasp), crucial for language-guided manipulation.
 - **Language-Guided Grasping:** With MLMs, grasping now incorporates semantic scene reasoning, enabling agents to execute grasps based on explicit (object category) and implicit (spatial, logical reasoning) instructions.
 - **Approaches:** Both end-to-end (e.g., CLIPORT, CROG) and modular (e.g., F3RM, GaussianGrasper) methods are emerging, integrating vision-language models with grasp pose generation.

#### 4.4. Embodied Agent

- **Multimodal Integration:** Modern embodied agents exhibit strong multimodal perception, interaction, and planning capabilities, crucial for operating in complex real-world environments.
- **Hierarchical Planning:** Agent operation involves:
 - **High-Level Task Planning:** Decomposing abstract tasks into subtasks. Traditionally rule-based (PDDL, MCTS), this now heavily leverages LLMs' emergent capabilities (Translated LM, Inner Monologue) for reasoning and plan generation, often refined with visual information (SayPlan, ConceptGraphs) or VLMs (EmbodiedGPT, LEO).
 - **Low-Level Action Planning:** Executing these subtasks. This can be achieved by utilizing pre-trained policy models via APIs (Reflexion, DEPS) or by leveraging VLA models directly (RT-series, PaLM-E) for tightly integrated perception-decision-execution.
- **Cyber-Physical Alignment Challenge:** A significant finding is that while task planning accuracy can be high, actual task completion rates are often limited by low-level planner performance and the agent's ability to translate cyber-space plans into physical-world interactions.

#### 4.5. Sim-to-Real Adaptation

- **Bridging the Reality Gap:** This is vital for transferring capabilities learned in simulation to real-world scenarios.
 - **Embodied World Models:** These models predict future states, learn physical laws, and can be Generation-based (World Models, Sora, Pandora), Prediction-based (I-JEPA, MuDreamer), or Knowledge-driven (ElastoGen, PLoT) by injecting artificial knowledge or common sense. They aim to provide robots with physical intuition.
 - **Data Collection and Training:** Real-world data (Open X-Embodiment, UMI, Mobile ALOHA) offers high capacity but is costly. Simulated data (CLIPORT, GAPartNet) is efficient but faces sim-to-real gaps.
 - **Sim-to-Real Paradigms:** Key methods include Real2Sim2Real (reconstructing real scenes in simulation), TRANSIC (human-in-the-loop correction), Domain Randomization (varying simulation parameters), System Identification (accurate simulation matching), and Lang4sim2real (language descriptions for invariant representations).
- **ARIO Dataset:** A significant practical contribution is the proposal of the ARIO (All Robots In One) dataset standard and a unified large-scale ARIO dataset (3 million episodes from 258 series, 321,064 tasks). This aims to address the absence of comprehensive sensory modalities, lack of unified format, incompatibility across robot platforms, insufficient data volume, and scarcity of combined simulated/real data in existing datasets.

#### 4.6. Overall Challenges and Future Directions

- **Challenges Identified:** High-quality robotic datasets (cost, diversity), long-horizon task execution (planning complexity, real-time adaptation), causal reasoning (beyond correlation, understanding affordances), unified evaluation benchmarks (diverse skills, realistic simulators, end-to-end assessment), and security/privacy (LLM vulnerabilities, backdoor attacks).
- **Future Directions:** Solutions include developing more realistic simulators, close inter-institutional collaboration for data, efficient planners with robust perception, integrating common sense and causal reasoning, creating unified benchmarks for long-horizon tasks, and developing robust security mechanisms for LLM-driven agents.

In summary, the survey highlights the tremendous progress in Embodied AI, especially with the integration of MLMs and WMs, while meticulously detailing the remaining scientific and engineering hurdles that need to be overcome to achieve truly general-purpose intelligent agents that seamlessly operate between cyber and physical realms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This comprehensive survey on Embodied AI holds significant implications and potential impact across the artificial intelligence research landscape, robotics, and various application domains. Its significance stems from its timely and detailed synthesis of a rapidly evolving field, coupled with concrete proposals for future advancement.

1. **Foundational Reference for the Research Community:** By providing a detailed and up-to-date overview of Embodied AI, encompassing robots, simulators, key research tasks (perception, interaction, agent development), and sim-to-real adaptation, the survey serves as an invaluable "foundational reference." It consolidates dispersed knowledge, clarifies terminology, and categorizes methodologies, making it easier for new and experienced researchers alike to grasp the field's complexities and identify promising avenues. The accompanying GitHub project further enhances its utility as a living resource.

2. **Advancing Artificial General Intelligence (AGI):** The paper explicitly positions Embodied AI as "crucial for achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)." By outlining the advancements and challenges in enabling AI agents to interact meaningfully with the physical world, it underscores the importance of grounding intelligence in physical experience. This deepens the understanding of how AGI might be realized, moving beyond purely abstract problem-solving to robust, real-world capabilities.

3. **Bridging the Cyber-Physical Gap:** A core theme of the survey is "Aligning Cyber Space with Physical World." This is critical for practical applications like "intelligent mechatronics systems, smart manufacturing," healthcare, and autonomous driving. The survey's detailed review of sim-to-real adaptation strategies directly contributes to overcoming one of the most persistent hurdles in robotics—transferring knowledge from controlled simulations to unpredictable real environments. Success in this area will unlock unprecedented levels of autonomy and efficiency in physical systems.

4. **Catalyzing Research in the Era of Large Models:** The survey's focus on Multi-modal Large Models (MLMs) and World Models (WMs) highlights their transformative impact on Embodied AI. By dissecting how these models enhance perception, interaction, and reasoning, it encourages researchers to fully leverage the power of foundation models. This provides a roadmap for integrating the latest advancements in language and vision processing into embodied systems, accelerating progress in areas like natural language instruction following and complex task planning.

5. **Standardizing Data for General-Purpose Agents:** The introduction of the **ARIO (All Robots In One) dataset standard** and a large-scale unified ARIO dataset is a major practical contribution. The current heterogeneity and scarcity of comprehensive, multi-modal robotic datasets hinder the development of general-purpose agents. ARIO aims to solve this by providing a unified format for diverse robot types and sensory modalities. This standardization has the potential to:
 - **Reduce friction** in data processing and model training across different research groups.
 - **Facilitate large-scale pre-training**, analogous to the success seen in natural language processing and computer vision with large datasets.
 - **Accelerate the development** of more versatile and robust embodied AI models that can generalize across different robots, tasks, and environments, significantly contributing to the long-term goal of generalist robots.

6. **Guiding Future Research and Investment:** By meticulously summarizing current limitations and identifying "Challenges and Future Directions" (e.g., high-quality datasets, long-horizon tasks, causal reasoning, unified benchmarks, security), the survey provides clear guidance for future research agendas and funding priorities. It highlights critical areas where concentrated effort is needed, such as developing more realistic simulators, fostering inter-institutional collaboration for data collection, and enhancing the interpretability and robustness of AI decisions. Addressing security and privacy concerns, particularly with LLM-driven agents, is crucial for public trust and safe deployment.

In conclusion, this survey is not merely a summary; it is a strategic document that maps the present and future of Embodied AI. Its comprehensive scope, emphasis on transformative technologies like MLMs, and concrete proposals like the ARIO dataset standard, position it to significantly impact the trajectory of AI research, bringing us closer to a future where intelligent agents seamlessly and safely navigate and interact with our physical world.
