# Continual Learning Mechanisms Compose for Long-Horizon Memorization

> [!summary] Summary
> Long-horizon memorization asks a language model to absorb 100 query-answer tasks via sequential LoRA fine-tuning on Qwen3-4B-Base, with no stored examples and no task identifiers. The work composes three retention anchors (generative replay, self-distillation, importance regularization) with merged LoRA, searched by task-level successive halving and a factorial design, lifting average final retention from 1.2% to 34.9%.

## Problem

- Existing continual-language benchmarks measure transfer or downstream task performance across heterogeneous corpora, so they never isolate whether specific associations survive many later parameter updates.
- Prior mechanism combinations, such as rehearsal plus distillation, are single points rather than a systematic study of how complementary anchors interact across a large composition space.
- Sequential model-editing work studies targeted edits with growing external storage, leaving ordinary task-wise supervised fine-tuning over a hundred task boundaries unexamined.

## Method

- Three ==anchors== define what each update preserves: a **data anchor** using unconditional ==generative replay== from a frozen prior model, a **function anchor** using ==self-distillation==, and a **weight anchor** using ==Synaptic Intelligence== or ==online EWC==.
- ==Low-rank allocation rules== define where updates land: **shared LoRA** keeps one adapter across all tasks, while **merged LoRA** folds each task's update into the dense weights and reinitializes fresh factors.
- **Task-level successive halving** prunes 90 candidate compositions over lengthening task horizons, then a ==2^4 factorial== isolates main effects and interactions across three purpose-built 100-task datasets.

## Results

- The best composition, all three anchors with merged LoRA, reaches **34.9%** average final retention versus **1.2%** for naive fine-tuning, a **28-fold** gain, and is the only top-3 method on every dataset.
- Replay and merged LoRA dominate main effects (**9.5-19.3** and **5.9-20.5** points) and interact super-additively: standalone gains summing to **3.9/7.7/13.9** points become **15.6/31.0/46.9** points combined.
- Memory half-life grows from **1-2** tasks under naive fine-tuning to **4/6/11** for the best single mechanism and **19/32/44** for the best composition, though retention still decays with age.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Continual-learning mechanisms attacking different forgetting sources compose super-additively; treating replay, distillation and weight penalties as competing baselines understates what their combination achieves.
> - Periodically merging low-rank updates into dense weights and restarting fresh adapters beats reusing one adapter, and beats allocation rules whose stored state grows with task count.
> - Better memorization does not imply preserved general ability: all methods still degrade on held-out reasoning and knowledge benchmarks, and recall was tested only on training-time query phrasings.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses the problem of catastrophic forgetting in continual learning (CL) within the context of language models (LMs), specifically focusing on "long-horizon memorization." Catastrophic forgetting, where a neural network loses previously acquired knowledge upon learning new tasks, has been a central challenge in continual learning since its early documentation in connectionist networks. Prior work in CL has broadly categorized mitigation strategies into regularization (e.g., EWC, SI, Learning without Forgetting), replay (e.g., Deep Generative Replay, LAMOL), and parameter isolation (e.g., PackNet, Progressive Neural Networks). Some approaches combine these signals, such as Dark Experience Replay or Momentum Knowledge Distillation.

For language models, continual learning research has explored sequential language modeling, instruction tuning, and adapting models to evolving corpora (continual pretraining). More recently, Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) has emerged as an efficient fine-tuning method that can help preserve prior knowledge. LoRA-based CL methods, such as O-LoRA and OSRM, have aimed to reduce interference between tasks by managing low-rank updates. Separately, sequential model editing addresses the problem of retaining targeted corrections in LMs, often revealing challenges in preserving earlier edits and general capabilities.

This work distinguishes itself by focusing on the systematic composition of complementary CL mechanisms. While some prior studies combined mechanisms, they did not extensively investigate a broad design space of compositions. The paper introduces a specific problem setting—long-horizon memorization—involving 100 sequential query-answer tasks where the model must internalize information over time without retaining raw examples or receiving task identifiers during inference. This setting differs from existing public CL benchmarks for LMs, which often measure transfer or performance across heterogeneous downstream tasks or changing corpora. By systematically exploring compositions of "anchors" (data, function, weight preservation) and "low-rank allocation rules" (how LoRA updates are managed), this research aims to provide a more comprehensive understanding of how to achieve robust knowledge retention in LMs over many successive updates. Furthermore, it contributes novel datasets and an evaluation methodology tailored to this specific long-horizon memorization challenge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate how language models can internalize and retain new information over extended periods, specifically across a "long horizon" of many sequential updates, without succumbing to catastrophic forgetting. This problem is framed as "long-horizon memorization," where a model learns 100 query-answer tasks through continual supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and is subsequently evaluated on its ability to recall associations from all previously learned tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations and hypotheses:
1. **Challenge of Retention in LMs**: Language models are expected to accumulate knowledge over time. However, sequential updates for new information often overwrite previously stored knowledge, leading to catastrophic forgetting. Existing methods, while effective in some settings, may not scale to the demanding long-horizon scenario of 100 tasks.
2. **Limitations of Individual Mechanisms**: The authors hypothesize that no single continual learning mechanism alone can effectively maintain strong retention across a long horizon because different mechanisms address complementary sources of forgetting. For instance, some mechanisms might prevent parameter drift, others might maintain model behavior, and yet others might re-expose the model to past data.
3. **Potential of Composition**: Building on the previous point, a core motivation is to test the hypothesis that mechanisms addressing *complementary* sources of forgetting will be more effective when composed. This implies that a synergistic combination of diverse CL strategies could lead to substantially better long-term retention than any single strategy in isolation.
4. **Lack of Suitable Benchmarks**: Existing public benchmarks for continual language learning primarily focus on transfer learning or performance across diverse tasks or evolving text corpora. They are not specifically designed to isolate and measure the recall of associations over a long sequence of identical-format memorization tasks without task identifiers at inference. This necessitates the development of new datasets and evaluation protocols.
5. **Systematic Exploration of Design Space**: To rigorously test the composition hypothesis, the researchers aimed to organize and systematically explore the combinatorial design space of CL mechanisms. This involves categorizing mechanisms along two key dimensions: "anchors" (what prior information an update should preserve—data, function, or weights) and "low-rank allocation rules" (how successive LoRA updates are retained).

In summary, the key objectives are to formalize and study long-horizon continual memorization, empirically demonstrate the benefits of composing complementary CL mechanisms, identify the most effective combinations, and develop specialized tools (datasets, search procedures) for this research domain.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research investigates long-horizon memorization in language models through continual supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a sequence of 100 query-answer tasks.

#### 3.1. Experimental Setting and Base Model

The setting is domain-incremental, meaning the model processes one task at a time without retaining raw examples from earlier tasks, and no task identifier is provided during inference. The objective is to learn each new task while retaining knowledge from previous ones. The base model used is Qwen3-4B-Base, an autoregressive language model.

#### 3.2. Evaluation Protocol and Metrics

- **Temporal Accuracy Matrix (M_i,j)**: After learning task *i*, the model is evaluated on all tasks *j* ≤ *i*. M_i,j represents the accuracy on task *j* after task *i* has been learned.
- **Final Retention**: The mean accuracy over all 100 tasks after the final (100th) update.
- **Immediate Acquisition (Diag)**: The mean accuracy on each task immediately after it is learned (M_j,j).
- **Forgetting**: The average drop from each task's best observed accuracy to its final accuracy after 100 tasks.
- **Memory Half-life**: The number of tasks (memory age) at which retention falls below half of the immediate acquisition rate.

#### 3.3. Continual Learning Mechanisms and Compositions

Mechanisms are categorized into two dimensions:
- **Anchors (Preservation Signals)**: Specify what prior information to preserve.
 - **Data Anchor (Generative Replay)**: Uses a frozen copy of the model from the previous task to generate pseudo-sequences from a single task-agnostic replay token. The current model is trained to match the previous model's soft next-token targets on these generated sequences, mixed with current-task data.
 - **Function Anchor (Self-Distillation)**: Employs the previous model as a teacher to constrain the current model's predictions on *current-task inputs* via forward KL divergence, limiting behavioral changes.
 - **Weight Anchor (Importance-based Regularization)**: Constrains updates to model parameters deemed important for previously learned behavior.
 - **Synaptic Intelligence (SI)**: Estimates parameter importance from their contribution to reducing the fit loss along the optimization path.
 - **Online EWC**: Estimates importance using a running diagonal Fisher information matrix, scaled by the first task's mean Fisher.
- **Low-Rank Allocation Rules (LoRA Strategies)**: Determine how LoRA parameters are updated and retained across tasks. LoRA rank (r) is 32, and α_LoRA is 64.
 - **Shared LoRA**: Optimizes a single set of LoRA matrices across all tasks.
 - **Merged LoRA**: After each task, the learned LoRA update is folded into the dense model weights, and a new, re-initialized LoRA adapter is used for the next task. This allows for a higher effective rank over time while keeping the LoRA adapter size constant.
 - **O-LoRA**: Assigns a new LoRA adapter for each task, with regularization terms discouraging overlap between the current task's input-side factors and those accumulated from earlier tasks. State size grows linearly with tasks.
 - **Sequential OSRM Adaptation**: Initializes new LoRA adapters based on feature vectors collected from *completed* tasks, aiming to find update subspaces that reduce interference. State size grows linearly with tasks.

#### 3.4. Datasets for Long-Horizon Memorization

Three distinct 100-task datasets were constructed, increasing in semantic realism:
- **Symbol-QA**: 10,000 random 6-character key-value associations (100 examples/task). Pure rote memorization, no underlying semantic structure.
- **LLM-QA**: 10,000 LLM-generated query-answer pairs about fictional topics (100 examples/task). Natural language format but with invented facts.
- **Real-QA**: 5,000 natural query-answer pairs from ten public QA datasets (50 examples/task), filtered to exclude items the base model already knew. Natural language and real-world facts.

#### 3.5. Combinatorial Design Space Exploration

- **Task-Level Successive Halving (TSH)**: An iterative search procedure was introduced to navigate the large combinatorial design space efficiently. It progressively evaluates 90 initial configurations (combinations of anchors and LoRA rules with varying hyperparameters) over increasing task horizons (10, 20, 50 tasks). Configurations are ranked by mean final retention across three random seeds, and only the top performers are promoted to the next stage (45 after 10 tasks, 23 after 20, 10 after 50, which continue to 100 tasks). This prunes less promising methods early, saving computational resources.
- **Factorial Experiment (2^4)**: After TSH identified promising directions, a full factorial experiment was conducted. This involved evaluating all 16 combinations of the three anchors (SI, SD, Replay) and Merged LoRA, using the hyperparameters selected by TSH. This allowed for measuring the individual and interaction effects of each mechanism on final retention. Naive sequential fine-tuning served as a baseline.
- **Comparison of Low-Rank Allocation Rules**: O-LoRA and sequential OSRM, which involve task-growing state, were evaluated separately against the best compositions identified by TSH.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research produced several findings regarding long-horizon memorization in language models:

- **Superiority of Mechanism Compositions**: No single continual learning mechanism evaluated maintained strong retention over 100 tasks. Naive sequential fine-tuning achieved an average final retention of 1.2%, while the best individual mechanism reached 8.1%. In contrast, mechanism compositions substantially outperformed standalone methods. The best performing method, which combined all three anchors (SI, SD, Replay) with Merged LoRA, achieved 34.9% average final retention across the three datasets, representing a 28-fold improvement over naive fine-tuning. This specific composition was also the only one to consistently rank among the top 3 methods across all three datasets.

- **Memory Half-Life Extension**: Compositions significantly extended the memory half-life. Naive fine-tuning showed a half-life of only 1-2 tasks. The strongest standalone mechanism extended this to 4-11 tasks. The most effective compositions, however, prolonged memory half-lives to 19-44 tasks, indicating a substantial delay in forgetting. Despite these improvements, all retention curves eventually declined with memory age, suggesting that even with compositions, older memories are gradually lost.

- **Key Contributors and Synergies (Factorial Analysis)**:
 - **Replay (Data Anchor) and Merged LoRA** were identified as the largest sources of improvement. Their main effects consistently showed the greatest gains in final retention across all datasets (Replay: 9.5 to 19.3 percentage points; Merged LoRA: 5.9 to 20.5 percentage points).
 - A **super-additive interaction** between Replay and Merged LoRA was observed on all three datasets. Their combined effect on retention significantly exceeded the sum of their individual contributions, highlighting a strong synergy between data-level replay and parameter-level update management.
 - The contributions of Synaptic Intelligence (SI) and Self-Distillation (SD) were more context-dependent, varying by dataset and the presence of other mechanisms. For example, SI showed positive main effects on natural-language datasets but not on Symbol-QA, while SD had a positive main effect but a negative interaction with Replay on some datasets.

- **Task-Growing Low-Rank Allocation Rules**: Low-rank allocation rules with state that grows linearly with the number of tasks (O-LoRA and Sequential OSRM) did not consistently improve retention compared to Merged LoRA (which maintains constant state size). O-LoRA showed minor gains on natural-language datasets but a decrease on Symbol-QA, while Sequential OSRM lowered retention across all datasets relative to matched Merged LoRA compositions.

- **General Capability Preservation vs. Memorization**: While memorization was substantially improved, all evaluated methods, including the best compositions, still exhibited catastrophic forgetting on held-out general capability benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, MGSM, MMLU-Redux). This indicates a trade-off, where strong memorization of specific tasks does not automatically preserve broader, general abilities. Notably, O-LoRA preserved substantially more general capability than Merged LoRA on natural-language datasets, despite similar memorization performance, suggesting that the parameter isolation strategy of O-LoRA might be more conducive to preserving general skills.

- **Acquisition Failures**: Certain compositions, particularly those combining weight anchors with Merged LoRA, occasionally resulted in a loss of plasticity, leading to a failure to acquire new tasks (low immediate acquisition) on some seeds. This suggests that the coordinate resetting inherent in Merged LoRA can interfere with the importance estimates of weight anchors if not handled appropriately.

- **Effectiveness of Task-Level Successive Halving**: The TSH method proved effective in identifying promising configurations, as 10-task search rankings showed strong agreement with 100-task final rankings for comparable compositions, even across different task orders.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of continual learning and language model development by formalizing and systematically investigating the challenge of long-horizon memorization. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Formalization of a Key Challenge**: The explicit formulation of "long-horizon continual memorization" as a distinct problem for language models highlights a critical capability required for adaptive AI systems. This new problem setting, along with the introduction of three dedicated 100-task query-answer datasets (Symbol-QA, LLM-QA, Real-QA), provides a valuable framework and resources for future research.

- **Empirical Validation of Composition Hypothesis**: The study provides strong empirical evidence that composing complementary continual learning mechanisms—specifically, data, function, and weight anchors with appropriate low-rank allocation rules—is substantially more effective for long-horizon memorization than relying on individual mechanisms. This shifts the research paradigm from seeking monolithic solutions to understanding the synergistic effects of mechanism combinations.

- **Identification of Core Mechanisms**: The factorial analysis specifically identifies generative replay (data anchor) and merged LoRA as the largest and most consistently beneficial components, demonstrating a powerful super-additive interaction between them. This insight offers concrete guidance for designing more effective continual learning systems for language models. It suggests that replay, which re-exposes the model to representations of past data, coupled with a flexible parameter update strategy like merged LoRA, is fundamental.

- **Novel Methodological Contributions**: The introduction of task-level successive halving (TSH) offers an efficient and principled approach to explore large combinatorial design spaces in continual learning, reducing computational costs while maintaining reliability. This methodology can be adopted for hyperparameter optimization and mechanism selection in other complex CL settings.

- **Implications for Adaptive Language Models**: The findings suggest that language models can be engineered to internalize and retain new information over many updates, moving beyond static models or models that suffer rapid forgetting. This has significant implications for:
 - **Personalized AI**: Creating models that can continuously learn from individual user interactions.
 - **Autonomous Agents**: Developing agents that can accumulate knowledge and adapt over long periods in dynamic environments.
 - **Knowledge Base Updates**: Enabling LMs to stay current with evolving information without complete retraining.

- **Highlighting Open Challenges**: The research also clarifies persistent challenges. While memorization of specific facts improved, general capabilities on held-out benchmarks still suffered catastrophic forgetting. This indicates that a comprehensive solution must address both specific memory retention and the preservation of broad reasoning and language understanding skills. Furthermore, the observed loss of plasticity when certain weight anchors are combined with LoRA parameter resets points to the need for more sophisticated integration strategies that align importance estimates with dynamic parameter representations.

In conclusion, this work not only offers practical recipes for improving long-horizon memorization in LMs but also deepens the understanding of how different continual learning mechanisms interact, paving the way for more robust and adaptive language intelligence.
