# Reliability-Adjusted Prioritized Experience Replay

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Technical University Munich and University St. Gallen developed Reliability-adjusted Prioritized Experience Replay (ReaPER), an enhanced experience replay strategy that accounts for the reliability of target Q-values. ReaPER achieved faster convergence and up to 34.98% higher median peak performance on continuous control and Atari benchmarks compared to standard Prioritized Experience Replay, especially in challenging partial observability settings.

## Problem

- Prioritized Experience Replay (PER) prioritizes transitions based on Temporal Difference Error (TDE), which can be biased if target Q-values are inaccurate, potentially leading to suboptimal Q-function updates.
- Unreliable target Q-values, particularly in early learning stages or complex environments, can misdirect the learning process and hinder policy convergence.
- The hierarchical nature of value learning suggests that targets for states closer to episode termination are generally more reliable, implying a need to prioritize learning from more trustworthy information first.

## Method

- Introduces a ==reliability score== (RtR_tRt​) for each transition, inversely proportional to the sum of absolute TDEs in subsequent states of the same episode, quantifying target Q-value trustworthiness.
- Proposes a new sampling criterion (Ψt=Rt⋅δt+\Psi_t = R_t \cdot \delta_t^+Ψt​=Rt​⋅δt+​) that prioritizes transitions exhibiting both a significant learning signal (large TDE) and a reliable target value.
- Integrates this reliability-adjusted TDE into a Deep Q-Network (DQN) framework, incorporating priority updates, regularization exponents, and importance sampling to mitigate bias.

## Results

- ReaPER demonstrated faster convergence in continuous control tasks, reaching score thresholds **16.6%** to **32.6%** faster than PER and **25.0%** to **41.4%** faster than uniform replay.
- On the ATARI-10 benchmark, ReaPER achieved a **22.97%** higher median peak performance compared to PER and a **229.78%** higher median peak performance compared to uniform experience replay.
- Under partial observability (single-frame Atari observations), ReaPER's performance advantage over PER widened to a **34.98%** median improvement in peak score, indicating increased robustness in challenging settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The reliability of a target Q-value for a given state is strongly influenced by the unresolved uncertainties (TDEs) in the Q-value estimates of subsequent states within its trajectory.
> - By accounting for target reliability, the proposed method reduces the detrimental "bias-error interaction" term in Q-value updates, leading to more stable and accurate learning.
> - A formal analysis demonstrates that ReaPER is theoretically expected to yield lower Q-value error compared to standard PER and uniform sampling, establishing a convergence hierarchy.
