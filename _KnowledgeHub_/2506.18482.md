# Reliability-Adjusted Prioritized Experience Replay

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Technical University Munich and University St. Gallen developed Reliability-adjusted Prioritized Experience Replay (ReaPER), an enhanced experience replay strategy that accounts for the reliability of target Q-values. ReaPER achieved faster convergence and up to 34.98% higher median peak performance on continuous control and Atari benchmarks compared to standard Prioritized Experience Replay, especially in challenging partial observability settings.

## Problem

- Prioritized Experience Replay (PER) prioritizes transitions based on Temporal Difference Error (TDE), which can be biased if target Q-values are inaccurate, potentially leading to suboptimal Q-function updates.
- Unreliable target Q-values, particularly in early learning stages or complex environments, can misdirect the learning process and hinder policy convergence.
- The hierarchical nature of value learning suggests that targets for states closer to episode termination are generally more reliable, implying a need to prioritize learning from more trustworthy information first.

## Method

- Introduces a ==reliability score== (RtR_tRt) for each transition, inversely proportional to the sum of absolute TDEs in subsequent states of the same episode, quantifying target Q-value trustworthiness.
- Proposes a new sampling criterion (Ψt=Rt⋅δt+\Psi_t = R_t · \delta_t^+Ψt=Rt⋅δt+) that prioritizes transitions exhibiting both a significant learning signal (large TDE) and a reliable target value.
- Integrates this reliability-adjusted TDE into a Deep Q-Network (DQN) framework, incorporating priority updates, regularization exponents, and importance sampling to mitigate bias.

## Results

- ReaPER demonstrated faster convergence in continuous control tasks, reaching score thresholds **16.6%** to **32.6%** faster than PER and **25.0%** to **41.4%** faster than uniform replay.
- On the ATARI-10 benchmark, ReaPER achieved a **22.97%** higher median peak performance compared to PER and a **229.78%** higher median peak performance compared to uniform experience replay.
- Under partial observability (single-frame Atari observations), ReaPER's performance advantage over PER widened to a **34.98%** median improvement in peak score, indicating increased robustness in challenging settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The reliability of a target Q-value for a given state is strongly influenced by the unresolved uncertainties (TDEs) in the Q-value estimates of subsequent states within its trajectory.
> - By accounting for target reliability, the proposed method reduces the detrimental "bias-error interaction" term in Q-value updates, leading to more stable and accurate learning.
> - A formal analysis demonstrates that ReaPER is theoretically expected to yield lower Q-value error compared to standard PER and uniform sampling, establishing a convergence hierarchy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents learn through interactions with their environment, often storing these interactions as "transitions" in a replay buffer. This mechanism, known as experience replay, is employed to enhance sample efficiency and stabilize the learning process. Historically, transitions were sampled uniformly from the buffer. However, this approach does not account for differences in the learning potential of individual experiences.

The introduction of Prioritized Experience Replay (PER) by Schaul et al. (2015) marked a significant advancement in this area. PER samples transitions based on their absolute Temporal Difference Error (TDE), with the rationale that transitions exhibiting larger TDEs possess higher learning potential. This method has become a widely adopted component in many state-of-the-art RL algorithms, as detailed in the paper's literature review (Appendix C), which highlights that most leading algorithms utilizing experience replay also incorporate PER.

Despite its advantages, PER relies on TDEs as a proxy for learning potential. TDEs are derived from the difference between predicted and target Q-values, both of which are approximations. When the target Q-value is inaccurate, the TDE can be a biased indicator, potentially leading to suboptimal or even detrimental updates to the Q-function. This can hinder convergence and degrade policy performance.

This work, ReaPER, aims to refine prioritized experience replay by addressing the inherent bias in TDEs. It builds directly upon PER by introducing a measure of target Q-value reliability, thereby seeking to enhance the efficiency and stability of learning. Other researchers have explored alternative prioritization criteria such as entropy-based sampling (Ramicic and Bonarini, 2017) or reward-based prioritization (Gao et al., 2021), as well as more complex dynamic or learning-based selection mechanisms (Zha et al., 2019; Oh et al., 2021). However, these alternatives have not achieved the same widespread adoption as PER due to various factors, including limited evaluation on complex benchmarks, lack of public code, or significant computational overhead, positioning PER as the current practical benchmark for prioritized sampling. ReaPER seeks to improve upon this established baseline directly.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an enhanced experience replay strategy that improves upon the sample efficiency and stability of Prioritized Experience Replay (PER) by accounting for the reliability of target Q-values. The motivation for this objective stems from a critical limitation identified in PER:

1. **Bias in Temporal Difference Error (TDE) as a Priority Metric**: PER prioritizes transitions based on the absolute magnitude of their TDE. The underlying assumption is that a large TDE indicates a high learning potential because it represents a significant discrepancy between the agent's current Q-value estimate and the target Q-value. However, both the predicted and target Q-values are often approximations learned by a neural network. If the target Q-value itself is inaccurate or unreliable, a large TDE might not signify true learning potential but rather a misleading error. Prioritizing such transitions can misdirect the learning process, potentially leading to degraded value estimates and hindering policy convergence.
2. **Impact of Unreliable Target Q-values**: The target Q-value for a given state-action pair is bootstrapped from the estimated Q-values of subsequent states. Consequently, its accuracy depends on the agent's understanding of future dynamics. In early stages of learning, or in complex states, these future estimates can be highly inaccurate, rendering the current target Q-value unreliable. When unreliable targets are used to update Q-values, the updates may not move the agent towards the optimal policy. In the worst case, they can move the Q-value estimates further away from the true values, causing instability.
3. **Hierarchical Nature of Value Learning**: The authors present an intuitive argument, particularly relevant in game environments, that the reliability of Q-value estimates exhibits a temporal hierarchy. States closer to terminal outcomes are generally easier to evaluate accurately because they involve fewer future steps and less uncertainty. As the agent learns to accurately assess later-game states, the reliability of target Q-values for earlier states in the trajectory improves. This suggests that learning should ideally prioritize transitions whose targets are more reliable, often those occurring later in an episode, before using them to update earlier transitions.

To address these motivations, the research aims to:
- Propose a formal concept of "target Q-value reliability" and introduce a quantifiable reliability score based on the sum of absolute TDEs in subsequent states within the same trajectory.
- Theoretically demonstrate that incorporating this reliability score into the sampling criterion can lead to more efficient learning by mitigating the negative impact of biased TDEs.
- Develop a practical sampling algorithm, ReaPER, that leverages this reliability-adjusted TDE to select transitions from the replay buffer. This algorithm is designed to be plug-and-play with existing off-policy RL algorithms.
- Empirically validate ReaPER's performance against uniform experience replay and standard PER across a diverse set of RL environments, including classic control tasks and a representative Atari benchmark.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of ReaPER centers on the concept of "reliability-adjusted TDE" to guide transition sampling. This involves defining a reliability score, formally analyzing its properties, and integrating it into an experience replay algorithm with specific technical refinements.

#### 3.1. Reliability Score Formulation

The core idea is that the reliability of a target Q-value, $Q_{target}(S_t)$, is inversely related to the magnitude of unresolved TDEs in subsequent states ($S_{t+1}, S_{t+2}, \ldots, S_n$) within the same episode. If future Q-values are still uncertain (indicated by high TDEs), then the bootstrapped target for the current state $S_t$ is also likely to be unreliable. The reliability score, $R_t$, for a transition $C_t$ is defined as:

$R_t = 1 - \frac{\sum_{i=t+1}^n \delta_i^+}{\sum_{i=1}^n \delta_i^+}$

where $\delta_i^+$ represents the absolute TDE of transition $C_i$, and the summation is over all transitions in the episode. A higher $R_t$ indicates greater reliability, signifying that a larger fraction of the total episodic TDE has already been resolved for subsequent states.

The sampling criterion, $\Psi_t$, for selecting transitions is then proposed as the product of this reliability score and the absolute TDE of the current transition:

$\Psi_t = R_t \cdot \delta_t^+$

Transitions with high $\Psi_t$ are prioritized, meaning they offer both a significant learning signal (large $\delta_t^+$) and a reliable target value (high $R_t$).

#### 3.2. Formal Analysis

The theoretical foundation for ReaPER is built upon analyzing the convergence behavior of Q-learning with different sampling strategies.
- **Decomposition of Error Update**: The expected change in squared true value estimation error due to an update is decomposed into three components (Lemma D.2): TDE variance, true squared error, and a bias-error interaction term. This third term, $E[e_t \epsilon_t]$, where $e_t$ is the true value error and $\epsilon_t$ is the target bias ($Q_{target}(S_t) - Q^\ast(S_t, A_t)$), is critical. A positive bias-error interaction indicates that the update direction is misaligned with the true error, potentially degrading the Q-value estimate (Lemma D.1).
- **Assumption on Target Bias**: A key Assumption 3.4 is introduced, postulating that the absolute target bias $|\epsilon_t|$ for a transition $C_t$ is bounded by a constant $\lambda$ times the sum of absolute TDEs of subsequent transitions within the trajectory: $|\epsilon_t| \le \lambda \sum_{i=t+1}^n \delta_i^+$. This formalizes the intuition that target bias primarily arises from unresolved inaccuracies in future predictions. Empirical validation (Appendix E) supports this assumption.
- **Reliability Bounds Target Bias**: Under Assumption 3.4, Lemma 3.5 demonstrates that the reliability score $R_t$ bounds the normalized target bias: $|\epsilon_t| \le \lambda (1 - R_t) \sum_{i=1}^n \delta_i^+$. This implies that transitions with higher reliability scores have lower target bias, making their TDEs more trustworthy.
- **Convergence Hierarchy**: Proposition 3.6 formally proves that, under Assumption 3.4, ReaPER (sampling based on $R_t \delta_t^+$) is expected to yield lower Q-value error than standard PER (sampling based on $\delta_t^+$), which in turn outperforms uniform sampling. This is attributed to ReaPER's ability to reduce the bias-error interaction term while preserving the true error reduction advantages of PER.
- **Variance Reduction**: Proposition 3.8 demonstrates that the optimal sampling distribution to minimize update variance is proportional to $\delta_t^+ / \sigma_t^2$, where $\sigma_t^2$ is the variance of the bootstrapped target. The authors argue that if reliability $R_t$ is inversely proportional to this variance ($R_t \propto 1/\sigma_t^2$), then ReaPER acts as a reasonable proxy for this variance-reducing strategy.

#### 3.3. Algorithmic Implementation (ReaPER)

ReaPER integrates the reliability-adjusted TDE into a Deep Q-Network (DQN) framework. The algorithm (Algorithm 1 and 2 in Appendix A) incorporates several technical refinements:
- **Priority Updates**: TDEs and reliabilities for stored transitions are tracked. When a transition $C_t$ is sampled and used for a Q-function update, its $\delta_t^+$ is updated. Additionally, its $R_t$ (and that of other transitions in the same episode) is recomputed. New transitions added to the buffer are initially assigned maximum priority.
- **Priority Regularization**: To temper the impact of extreme TDEs or reliabilities, regularization exponents $\alpha \in (0, 1]$ and $\omega \in (0, 1]$ are introduced, modifying the sampling criterion to $\Psi_t = R_t^\omega \cdot (\delta_t^+)^\alpha$.
- **Reliability for Ongoing Episodes**: For episodes that have not yet terminated, the sum of future TDEs required for $R_t$ is undefined. In such cases, a conservative estimate is used by assuming the maximum sum of TDEs observed from any *terminated* episode in the buffer.
- **Weighted Importance Sampling**: As a non-uniform sampling method, ReaPER introduces bias. To mitigate this, importance sampling weights $w_t$ are applied to scale the loss during Q-learning updates, similar to how it's done in standard PER. These weights are computed as $w_t = (N \cdot p_t)^{-\beta} / \max_i w_i$, where $p_t$ is the sampling probability and $\beta$ is an exponent that increases with training time.

#### 3.4. Numerical Study Setup

ReaPER was evaluated against PER and uniform experience replay using a Double Deep Q-Network (DDQN) agent.
- **Environments**: A diverse set including continuous control environments (CARTPOLE, ACROBOT, LUNARLANDER) and the ATARI-10 benchmark. ATARI-10 is used as a computationally efficient proxy for the full Atari-57 benchmark.
- **Control for Randomness**: All algorithms used identical neural architectures, model hyperparameters, and fixed seeds across trials, ensuring that observed performance differences were attributable solely to the experience replay mechanism.
- **Partial Observability**: An additional experiment was conducted on the ATARI-10 benchmark under partial observability (single-frame observations) to evaluate ReaPER's robustness in more challenging settings.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The numerical studies indicate that ReaPER consistently outperformed both uniform experience replay and standard PER across the evaluated environments.

#### 4.1. Continuous Control Environments (CARTPOLE, ACROBOT, LUNARLANDER)

- ReaPER demonstrated faster convergence to pre-defined score thresholds compared to both uniform replay and PER.
- In **ACROBOT**, ReaPER reached the threshold 16.6% faster than PER and 25.0% faster than uniform replay.
- In **CARTPOLE**, ReaPER achieved the threshold 32.6% faster than PER and 41.4% faster than uniform replay.
- In **LUNARLANDER**, ReaPER converged 21.1% faster than PER and 37.1% faster than uniform replay.
- These results suggest that ReaPER's approach of reliability-adjusted prioritization leads to more efficient learning in environments of lower to moderate complexity.

#### 4.2. Atari-10 Benchmark

- ReaPER showed superior performance on the ATARI-10 benchmark, outperforming PER and uniform experience replay in 8 out of 10 games, with ties in the remaining two.
- Across all games, ReaPER achieved a **22.97% higher median peak performance** compared to PER.
- Compared to uniform experience replay, ReaPER achieved a 229.78% higher median peak performance.
- These findings suggest ReaPER's ability to scale to more complex, high-dimensional environments and maintain its performance advantage.

#### 4.3. Partial Observability (Atari-10 single-frame observations)

- Under a modified, more challenging partial observability setting (using single-frame observations instead of stacked frames), ReaPER's performance advantage over PER widened.
- ReaPER achieved a **34.98% median improvement** in peak score over PER in this setting, compared to the 22.97% improvement in the standard setting. This indicates that the benefits of reliability adjustment are amplified when the agent operates with incomplete information, where target Q-values might be inherently more uncertain.

#### 4.4. Discussion of Performance and Limitations

- The reported performance gains were achieved with minimal hyperparameter tuning for ReaPER. The authors suggest that further optimization of regularization exponents ($\alpha, \omega$), importance sampling exponent ($\beta$), and learning rate ($\eta$) could lead to additional improvements.
- A identified limitation of ReaPER is its dependence on terminal states for calculating meaningful TDE reliabilities, as the episodic sum of TDEs is only finalized upon episode completion.
- The computational overhead for tracking and re-calculating episodic cumulative sums of TDEs can be $O(N)$ for a naive implementation but can be reduced to $O(n-t)$ by optimizing updates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Reliability-adjusted Prioritized Experience Replay (ReaPER), offering a novel approach to enhance the efficiency and stability of off-policy deep reinforcement learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Mitigation of Unreliable Targets**: ReaPER addresses a fundamental limitation of traditional prioritized experience replay methods like PER: the reliance on potentially biased Temporal Difference Errors (TDEs) when target Q-values are inaccurate. By introducing a principled reliability score based on downstream TDEs, ReaPER directly confronts the issue of target value unreliability, leading to more robust updates.
- **Improved Learning Efficiency and Stability**: The theoretical analysis, supported by empirical evidence, demonstrates that ReaPER improves both convergence speed and reduces variance in Q-function updates compared to uniform sampling and standard PER. This suggests that agents using ReaPER can learn optimal policies faster and with greater stability, especially in complex environments where accurate Q-value estimation is challenging.
- **Enhancement of State-of-the-Art Algorithms**: Given that PER is a widely adopted component in many advanced RL algorithms, ReaPER's direct improvement upon PER positions it as a potential enhancement for a broad spectrum of existing and future off-policy methods. Its algorithm-agnostic design allows for straightforward integration into various RL frameworks.
- **Robustness in Challenging Environments**: The enhanced performance of ReaPER under partial observability (using single-frame Atari observations) highlights its potential utility in real-world scenarios where agents often operate with incomplete or noisy information. In such contexts, where target Q-value reliability is likely lower, ReaPER's benefits are further amplified.
- **New Avenues for Research**: ReaPER's concept of explicitly accounting for target reliability opens several new research directions:
 - **Adaptive Reliability Estimation**: Future work could explore more dynamic or adaptive methods for estimating reliability, potentially moving beyond fixed episode-centric calculations.
 - **Uncertainty Quantification**: Integrating more sophisticated uncertainty estimates, beyond just TDE-based reliability, into replay buffers could lead to more nuanced prioritization schemes.
 - **Extension to Other RL Paradigms**: Adapting ReaPER to actor-critic methods or infinite-horizon settings, where the concept of a terminal state is less straightforward, presents an interesting challenge.
 - **Integration with Representation Learning**: Exploring how reliability information can be coupled with representation learning to improve the quality of state representations for value estimation.

Overall, ReaPER represents a step towards more informed and robust experience replay, contributing to the development of more efficient and reliable deep reinforcement learning agents.
