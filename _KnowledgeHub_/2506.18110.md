# RL for Reasoning by Adaptively Revealing Rationales

> [!summary] Summary
> Adaptive Backtracking (AdaBack) introduces per-sample adaptive partial supervision during reinforcement learning for large language models, allowing them to learn problem-solving strategies otherwise inaccessible to standard supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning. The approach consistently outperformed baseline methods on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, including novel GSM8k variants, and facilitated the discovery of new solution modes.

## Problem

- Acquiring extensive, high-quality reasoning traces for specialized LLM tasks is challenging and does not scale with sequence length.
- Standard reinforcement learning (RL) struggles with exploration in long reasoning tasks due to exponentially sparse, binary rewards and combinatorially large output spaces.
- Current RL fine-tuning methods for reasoning often reinforce behaviors already present in the pretrained model, failing to discover genuinely novel solution strategies.

## Method

- Propose ==Adaptive Backtracking== (**AdaBack**), a per-sample adaptive curriculum learning algorithm that dynamically reveals partial prefixes of target reasoning sequences during reinforcement learning.
- The algorithm uses a stochastic binary search over a sample-specific supervision ratio, reducing revealed rationale as the model's performance improves to maintain useful reward signals.
- This approach aims to transform problems with exponentially sparse rewards into a series of easier sub-searches with more frequent positive feedback.

## Results

- AdaBack successfully solved a synthetic L=**16** chain-of-parities task with **1024** samples, a scenario intractable for standard RL or SFT.
- AdaBack consistently outperformed Base+RL and SFT+RL across DeepScaleR, MATH, and GSM8k benchmarks, with base models often matching or exceeding SFT-initialized standard RL performance.
- The method significantly improved pass@k scores on GSM8k for base models, indicating an expanded solution space and the discovery of novel reasoning paths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adaptive partial supervision can bridge the gap between supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and standard reinforcement learning (RL), enabling models to learn tasks intractable for either method alone.
> - Progressively reducing the revealed portion of a reasoning trace transforms a complex, multi-step search problem into a series of simpler, localized learning tasks.
> - AdaBack's per-sample adaptive curriculum manages learning without manual design, making the process more robust and efficient.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The broader research landscape for this work centers on enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs), particularly Transformers, in complex tasks like mathematics, algorithmic reasoning, and code generation. A significant body of research has demonstrated that incorporating explicit reasoning traces, often termed "scratchpads" or "chain-of-thought" (CoT), can substantially improve model performance in these domains.

However, a core challenge lies in acquiring extensive, high-quality reasoning traces for specialized areas. Reinforcement Learning (RL)-inspired methods have emerged as a strategy to overcome this data scarcity. These methods treat the language model as an RL policy, leveraging reward functions or verifiers (e.g., checking final answers to math problems) to generate and refine reasoning traces. Examples include approaches based on REINFORCE (e.g., STaR), Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

Despite advancements, existing RL-based approaches for structured reasoning tasks face limitations, primarily due to exploration challenges. As reasoning chains lengthen, the output space expands combinatorially, leading to sparse and often binary reward signals. This sparsity makes it difficult for standard RL algorithms to sample correct solutions through random exploration. Consequently, prior studies suggest that current RL fine-tuning methods tend to reinforce behaviors already present in the pretrained model's distribution rather than discovering novel solution trajectories or acquiring genuinely new reasoning capabilities.

This paper addresses these limitations by exploring an intermediate regime between traditional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and pure Reinforcement Learning. It investigates whether partial supervision, specifically by adaptively revealing prefixes of target outputs, can make exploration more tractable and enable models to learn problem-solving strategies that are otherwise inaccessible to SFT or conventional RL. The work positions itself as a response to the observed inability of standard RL to substantially expand the model's effective reasoning capacity beyond existing knowledge.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to determine whether reinforcement learning, when guided by adaptive partial supervision, can endow language models with genuinely new reasoning capabilities. Specifically, the authors aim to ascertain if this approach can enable the discovery of solutions for variable-length sequence learning tasks that were previously exponentially unlikely under the model’s initial probability distribution.

The motivation for this objective stems from the inherent difficulties encountered by existing training paradigms:
1. **Challenges in Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Providing expert demonstrations for long and complex reasoning chains does not scale effectively with sequence length. High-quality, exhaustive reasoning traces are often expensive or impossible to obtain for all problem types.
2. **Limitations of Standard Reinforcement Learning (RL):**
 - **Combinatorial Output Space:** Reasoning tasks often involve generating sequences of tokens, where the space of possible outputs is combinatorially large.
 - **Sparse Rewards:** In many reasoning tasks, rewards are binary (correct/incorrect final answer) and only awarded upon completing a full, correct reasoning chain. For a task requiring *n* sequential steps, each with probability *p* of being correct, the overall success probability is *p^n*. This exponential decay in success probability with increasing sequence length makes positive feedback exponentially infrequent, rendering standard RL exploration impractical.
 - **Lack of Novelty:** Empirical observations suggest that standard RL fine-tuning predominantly amplifies pre-existing behaviors and strengthens already probable reasoning paths, rather than enabling the discovery of entirely new solution strategies or expanding the model's core reasoning capacity. Models struggle to explore beyond the support of their initial distribution.

To mitigate these issues, the authors propose exploring "partial supervision" – an intermediate regime between dense SFT demonstrations and the absence of demonstrations in RL. The core intuition is illustrated by considering a task with *n* steps: instead of attempting to learn all *n* steps simultaneously with exponentially sparse rewards, the model can initially be guided by revealing *n-1* steps and learning only the final step. This transforms an *O(p^n)* success probability problem into an *O(p)* problem. As the model masters the final step, more steps are progressively concealed, gradually training the model to complete longer segments while maintaining a frequent positive feedback signal. This incremental learning aims to decompose a complex search space into a series of simpler sub-searches.

The specific contributions of this work are:
- Introducing AdaBack, a per-sample adaptive curriculum learning algorithm that adjusts supervision based on reward feedback, eliminating the need for manual curriculum design.
- Demonstrating that AdaBack bridges the gap between SFT and RL, enabling learning in regimes where both otherwise fail, exemplified by a synthetic parity task.
- Showing that AdaBack improves performance over standard RL and SFT+RL pipelines on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (DeepScaleR, MATH, GSM8k) and can equip base models with reasoning capabilities matching or exceeding SFT-initialized counterparts.
- Introducing two new variants of GSM8k (Base-7 and Tensor-2) to assess generalization to symbolic shifts and longer reasoning horizons, where AdaBack exhibits strong performance.
- Identifying a limitation: AdaBack provides no significant benefits for instruct-tuned models or tasks where models have already been sufficiently exposed during pretraining, suggesting its utility in scenarios with high uncertainty or sparse rewards.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is **Adaptive Backtracking (AdaBack)**, a per-sample curriculum learning algorithm designed to dynamically adjust the level of supervision during reinforcement learning (RL) training. The method aims to expose a partial prefix of the target sequence (rationale) and gradually reduce this supervision as the model’s performance improves, allowing each training sample to progress at its own pace.

**Problem Setup:**
An input sample `X` is a sequence of tokens representing a prompt or problem. The desired output `Y` is a chain-of-thought (CoT) style response. The existence of a dataset of `(X, Y)` pairs is assumed. Correctness of a generated output `Ŷ` is verifiable via a reward model `R(X, Ŷ)`, which assigns `r = 1` for a correct output and `r = 0` otherwise, with an optional `r_format ∈ [0, 1)` for syntactically valid but incorrect outputs.

**Method (AdaBack):**
1. **Partial Supervision:** For each training sample `i`, with input `X^(i)` and target `Y^(i)`, a prefix `Y^(i)_1:k` is revealed. The length `k` is determined by `k = ⌊ρ^(i) ⋅ m_i⌋`, where `m_i` is the full length of `Y^(i)` and `ρ^(i) ∈` is a sample-specific supervision portion. The model is then trained to generate the continuation, `Ŷ^(i)_k+1:m'`, conditioned on `X^(i)` and `Y^(i)_1:k`.
2. **Adaptive Update Rule:** For each sample `i`, an interval `[ρ^(i)_min, ρ^(i)_max]` (initialized as ``) is maintained. At each epoch `t`, `ρ^(i)_t` is uniformly sampled from this interval, `U(ρ^(i)_min, ρ^(i)_max)`. After the model generates predictions and an average reward `r^(i)_t` is obtained (e.g., from multiple rollouts in GRPO), this interval is updated based on a fixed reward threshold `τ`:
 - If `r^(i)_t < τ` (model struggles): The lower bound of the interval is increased, `ρ^(i)_min ← ρ^(i)_t`. This increases the expected supervision in the next epoch.
 - If `r^(i)_t ≥ τ` (model performs well): The upper bound of the interval is decreased, `ρ^(i)_max ← ρ^(i)_t`, and the lower bound is reset, `ρ^(i)_min ← 0.0`. This reduces the expected supervision, making the task harder.
 The next supervision level `ρ^(i)_t+1` is then sampled uniformly from this updated range `U(ρ^(i)_min, ρ^(i)_max)`. This mechanism effectively performs a stochastic binary search over the supervision ratio, aiming to minimize revealed rationale while maintaining useful reward signals.
3. **Training Details:**
 - **ρ = 0 Injection:** To mitigate train-test distribution mismatch and accelerate convergence, with a small probability (10%), `ρ^(i)_t = 0` is directly sampled, exposing the model to fully unsupervised completions.
 - **Bootstrapping:** For samples without prior reward history, `ρ^(i)` is initialized from global moving averages `¯ρ_min` and `¯ρ_max`, which are updated using exponential moving averages of `ρ^(i)_min` and `ρ^(i)_max`.
 - **Reward Threshold (τ):** `τ` is the sole hyperparameter for AdaBack, controlling the target average reward. The authors found the method robust to its choice, using `τ = 0.5` in experiments to maximize reward variance and avoid vanishing gradients.

**Chain-of-Parities: A Synthetic Environment:**
To study learning dynamics in a controlled environment, a synthetic "chain-of-parities" task was introduced. Given a binary input sequence `X ∈ {0, 1}^L`, the model must generate `Y_1, Z_1..., Y_L, Z_L`. `Y_i` are unconstrained "scratchpad" values, and `Z_i` is the parity of `X_i`, `Y_i`, and `Z_{i-1}` (with `Z_0 = 0`). This task enforces a latent, step-wise dependency where `Z_i`'s correctness depends on `Z_{i-1}` and previous CoT steps. Randomly generating a correct output has a probability of `2^(-L)`.
- **Why SFT and RL fail:** For small training sets, SFT cannot learn the task due to the high sample complexity required for learning degree-three parity functions (Ω(L^(k-1)) samples for degree-k parity). Sparse rewards prevent standard RL from effective exploration.
- **Why AdaBack succeeds:** By revealing prefixes up to `Z_L-1`, AdaBack reduces the problem to learning `Y_L, Z_L`. This effectively transforms the degree-three parity learning problem for `Z_L` into a degree-two parity problem (as `Y_L` acts as a revealed input), which has lower sample complexity (Ω(L) samples) and is learnable. This iterative "backtracking" allows the model to learn the full sequence step-by-step.

**Experimental Setup for Real-World Tasks:**
- **Models:** Llama-3 family (1B and 3B parameters) base models.
- **RL Algorithm:** GRPO (Group Relative Policy Optimization) with 8 rollouts per input sample.
- **Comparison Baselines:**
 - `Base+RL`: GRPO on a base model (no SFT).
 - `SFT+RL`: GRPO on an SFT-initialized model (standard pipeline).
 - `AdaBack`: AdaBack-GRPO on a base model.
 - `SFT+AdaBack`: AdaBack-GRPO on an SFT-initialized model.
- **Benchmarks:** DeepScaleR, MATH, and GSM8k.
- **Novel Variants of GSM8k:**
 - `Base-7 GSM8k`: Numeric quantities and computations in base-7 to test generalization to symbolic shifts.
 - `Tensor-2 GSM8k`: Concatenated pairs of GSM8k problems to increase reasoning depth and sequence length.
- **Training Protocol:** Models trained for at least 10,000 iterations. Final test accuracy/reward averaged over the last 5 checkpoints. A format reward of 0.1 was applied for syntactically valid outputs, especially for tasks with non-trivial output formats or base models without SFT.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding AdaBack's efficacy compared to standard SFT and RL approaches.

1. **Separation Result on Synthetic Chain-of-Parities Task:**
 - AdaBack successfully solved the `L=16` chain-of-parities task with 1024 training samples, a regime where the probability of a random correct solution is `2^(-16)`.
 - AdaBack achieved substantial training rewards early on and progressively reduced the revealed portion of the solution, demonstrating seamless learning.
 - In contrast, standard RL (even with SFT pre-training) failed to obtain meaningful rewards, plateauing at a minimal value (0.1), indicating it only learned the output format but not the underlying task logic.
 - R3, another curriculum learning method, also struggled, reaching only 0.8 reward after over 16,000 iterations, significantly slower than AdaBack's performance. This supports the hypothesis that AdaBack can solve problems intractable for SFT, RL, and their naive combinations.

2. **Generalization on Natural Language Reasoning Tasks:**
 - **Consistent Outperformance:** AdaBack consistently outperformed both `Base+RL` and `SFT+RL` across DeepScaleR, MATH, GSM8k, and its novel variants (Base-7, Tensor-2 GSM8k) for both 1B and 3B Llama-3 models.
 - **Base Model Efficacy:** AdaBack applied directly to base models often matched or exceeded the performance of standard RL pipelines that were initialized with SFT checkpoints. This suggests that adaptive partial supervision acts as an effective learning prior, potentially making an SFT phase less critical for establishing initial reasoning capability.
 - **Out-of-Distribution Tasks:** AdaBack demonstrated particular strength on out-of-distribution tasks like `Tensor-2 GSM8k`, which require longer reasoning chains, and `Base-7 GSM8k`, which involves a symbolic shift. For `Tensor-2 GSM8k` (1B model), AdaBack on the base model even outperformed `SFT+AdaBack`, suggesting SFT might sometimes prematurely constrain the search space.

3. **Automatic Per-Sample Curriculum:**
 - AdaBack effectively manages curriculum learning without requiring manual staging, heuristics, or extensive hyperparameter tuning. The average supervision portion naturally decreased over training as the model's reward increased, demonstrating self-adaptation.
 - Sensitivity analysis showed AdaBack to be robust to the choice of the reward threshold `τ`, with learning dynamics and final performance converging to similar values across a wide range of `τ` values.

4. **Expansion of Solution Space (Pass@k):**
 - AdaBack significantly improved `pass@k` scores over standard RL on GSM8k for both SFT-initialized and base models, particularly at higher `k` values (e.g., `2^8`).
 - These `pass@k` gains were more pronounced when AdaBack was applied to the base model, suggesting that it facilitates the discovery of novel solution modes rather than merely reweighting existing ones, thus expanding the model's effective solution distribution.

5. **Limitations of AdaBack:**
 - AdaBack provided limited or no benefit on tasks where the base model (especially instruct-tuned variants like Llama 3.2 3B-Instruct on MATH) already achieved high performance very early in training. This saturation indicates that such models may have been heavily exposed to similar problem types during pretraining, leaving minimal room for RL or AdaBack to assist in exploration or capability acquisition.

6. **Comparison with R3:**
 - AdaBack generally outperformed R3 (another curriculum method) on more structurally complex and heterogeneous datasets like DeepScaleR (for both 1B and 3B models) and MATH (for 1B). This was attributed to AdaBack's per-sample adaptivity and independence from heuristic-based segmentation, which can be brittle for irregular reasoning traces.
 - On GSM8k, where reasoning steps are more cleanly separated by newlines, R3 showed slightly better performance. However, AdaBack's efficiency gain (over 20x) on the synthetic chain-of-parities task, which has non-trivial reasoning steps, suggested its potential for longer-horizon tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field by demonstrating that adaptive partial supervision, implemented through the AdaBack algorithm, can enable language models to acquire reasoning capabilities that are otherwise inaccessible to traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or standard reinforcement learning (RL). This finding provides a constructive separation result, particularly evident in the synthetic chain-of-parities task, where AdaBack succeeded while other methods failed.

The significance of this work lies in several aspects:
- **Addressing the Exploration Challenge:** AdaBack offers a principled approach to mitigate the sparse reward problem inherent in long reasoning chains. By transforming exponentially rare positive feedback into more frequent, localized feedback, it makes exploration tractable in combinatorially large output spaces. This is crucial for training models on complex, multi-step reasoning tasks.
- **Expanding Model Capabilities:** The results, particularly the consistent improvements in `pass@k` scores for base models, suggest that AdaBack does not merely reweight existing solution paths but facilitates the discovery of genuinely new solution modes. This implies that adaptive partial supervision can genuinely expand a model's reasoning capacity, moving beyond merely refining pre-existing behaviors.
- **Streamlined Curriculum Learning:** AdaBack simplifies the practical application of curriculum learning by providing an automatic, per-sample adaptation mechanism. This eliminates the need for manual curriculum staging, heuristic schedules, or extensive hyperparameter tuning often associated with traditional curriculum design, making the approach more robust and easier to implement across diverse tasks.
- **Improved Generalization:** The superior performance of AdaBack on tasks requiring generalization, such as the Base-7 GSM8k (symbolic shift) and Tensor-2 GSM8k (longer reasoning horizons), indicates its potential to build more robust and adaptable reasoning agents.
- **Foundational Training Enhancement:** AdaBack is not presented as an alternative to state-of-the-art methods that use larger models, extensive pretraining, or complex inference-time search strategies. Instead, it is positioned as a complementary, foundational training component that can be integrated into existing pipelines to strengthen models from the ground up, potentially enhancing their ability to benefit from subsequent advanced techniques.

A recognized limitation is its reduced benefit when the base model has already "memorized" or been extensively exposed to problem types during pretraining, underscoring its primary utility in scenarios with significant learning barriers such as sparse rewards or symbolic mismatches. Future work could focus on improving scalability for very large datasets, possibly by adapting supervision schedules per region in embedding space rather than per individual sample. This could maintain the benefits of adaptation while being more efficient for high-data regimes. Overall, this research provides a novel and effective method for developing more capable and generalizable reasoning systems based on LLMs.
