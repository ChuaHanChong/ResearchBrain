# Unlabeled Data vs. Pre-trained Knowledge: Rethinking SSL in the Era of Large Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Nanjing University researchers introduce "Few-shot SSL," a unified framework for comparing semi-supervised learning (SSL) and pretrain-finetuning with Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Their comparative analysis indicates that VLMs generally achieve superior performance and efficiency across diverse low-labeled-data scenarios, while SSL remains more effective for low-resolution inputs or semantically coarse tasks.

## Problem

- Deep learning applications face a significant bottleneck due to the high cost and laboriousness of acquiring large, meticulously labeled datasets.
- Practitioners and researchers lack clear guidance on whether to leverage additional unlabeled data (SSL) or pre-trained knowledge (VLMs) when labeled data is scarce.
- A systematic and fair comparison framework was missing to evaluate the comparative efficacy and optimal application scenarios of SSL and pretrain-finetuning paradigms.

## Method

- Proposed =="Few-shot SSL,"== a unified evaluation framework designed for a fair comparison between SSL methods and pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) under various limited labeled data conditions.
- Conducted extensive comparative experiments across four semi-supervised settings: ==standard, open-set, open-world, and long-tailed==, to investigate the performance of both paradigms.
- Utilized prominent SSL methods (e.g., FixMatch, DWD) and VLM fine-tuning techniques (e.g., CLIP, CoOp, PromptSRC) as representative baselines.

## Results

- VLMs generally outperformed SSL methods across most semi-supervised settings (e.g., over **73%** accuracy on ImageNet with **2%** labeled data vs. FixMatch with **10%** labels), particularly on datasets with resolutions higher than 32x32 pixels.
- SSL methods demonstrated better performance on lower-resolution datasets like CIFAR-10/100, where VLM performance was degraded by upsampling distortions.
- VLMs exhibited strong robustness against out-of-distribution unlabeled data and effectively handled long-tailed distributions, showing balanced performance across known and unseen classes in open-world settings on high-resolution data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Pre-trained VLMs generally exhibit stronger generalization capabilities and data efficiency compared to SSL, especially when dealing with higher image resolutions and clear semantic categories.
> - SSL methods maintain a performance advantage in specific niches, particularly with low-resolution inputs or when semantic structures are ambiguous among categories.
> - The presence of out-of-distribution (OOD) data in the unlabeled pool and severe class imbalance in long-tailed distributions disproportionately affect SSL performance, making VLMs a more robust alternative in these cases.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of deep learning has achieved notable performance in various tasks, such as image classification, primarily through the use of extensive labeled datasets. However, the acquisition of large-scale labeled data is often resource-intensive, posing a significant challenge for real-world applications. To mitigate this dependency, two primary research paradigms have evolved:

1. **Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL):** This approach aims to reduce annotation costs by leveraging abundant unlabeled data in conjunction with a limited amount of labeled data to improve model performance. Traditional SSL methods focus on tasks where both labeled and unlabeled samples belong to a predefined set of known classes, often relying on techniques like pseudo-labeling and consistency regularization.

2. **Pre-trained Models:** With the advent of large foundation models, such as Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP, and Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs) like Qwen-VL and GPT-4o-mini, a new paradigm has emerged. These models are pre-trained on vast datasets (e.g., image-text pairs) to acquire rich, transferable knowledge. This pre-trained knowledge can then be adapted to downstream tasks with minimal labeled data through various parameter-efficient fine-tuning techniques (e.g., prompt tuning).

Both SSL and pre-trained models address the problem of label scarcity, but they do so through distinct mechanisms: SSL by exploiting unlabeled data, and pre-trained models by leveraging prior knowledge. Despite their individual advancements, a systematic and fair comparison between these two paradigms, particularly in the context of large foundation models, has been largely absent from the literature. Existing comparisons between pre-trained models and self-training algorithms in areas like object detection did not specifically account for VLMs, MLLMs, or diverse SSL settings. This research endeavors to bridge this gap by conducting a controlled comparative study, thereby re-evaluating the efficacy of SSL in an era dominated by large pre-trained models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this study stems from the parallel development and growing prominence of both Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL) methods and large pre-trained models (VLMs and MLLMs) as solutions to the challenge of limited labeled data. This dual advancement prompts a critical re-evaluation of strategies for label-scarce scenarios. The paper articulates three interconnected objectives:

1. **Prioritization under Label Scarcity:** The primary objective is to investigate whether, when labeled data is scarce, researchers should prioritize the utilization of unlabeled data (via SSL methods) or the exploitation of knowledge embedded in pre-trained models (via fine-tuning). This addresses a fundamental decision point for practitioners and researchers.

2. **Comparative Analysis of Strengths and Limitations:** The study aims to delineate the specific strengths and limitations inherent in each approach. This involves a detailed examination of how well each paradigm performs across various challenging image classification scenarios, considering factors such as data resolution, distribution shifts (e.g., open-set, open-world), and class imbalance (long-tailed distributions). The motivation is to provide a clear empirical understanding of when one approach might be more advantageous than the other.

3. **Pathways for Methodological Integration:** Beyond a direct comparison, the research is motivated by the potential for synergistic integration. It seeks to explore how new methods could be designed to mitigate the individual shortcomings of both SSL and pre-trained models while maximizing their complementary benefits. This objective aims to foster future research into hybrid algorithms that can potentially surpass the performance of either approach in isolation.

By addressing these objectives, the authors intend to offer guidance on selecting appropriate strategies for image classification tasks under limited supervision, thereby contributing to the development of more effective and efficient learning paradigms in the era of large models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research establishes a controlled and fair evaluation framework to compare Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL) methods with pre-trained models across various image classification tasks. The methodology is structured to ensure consistent supervision budgets and experimental conditions.

**Evaluation Framework and Standardization:**
The authors designed an evaluation framework inspired by 50 recent top-tier publications, ensuring consistent experimental conditions. A core aspect of this framework is the standardization of the amount of labeled data used across different experimental setups. For pre-trained models, which do not typically utilize unlabeled data for training in the same manner as SSL, the settings are reformulated to represent equivalent few-shot scenarios using only the labeled data.

**Evaluated SSL Settings:**
The study focuses on four common and challenging SSL settings:

1. **Standard Semi-Supervised Learning:**
 - **Definition:** Labeled and unlabeled samples belong to the same known set of classes ($Y_l = Y_u$).
 - **VLM Reformulation:** For pre-trained models, this reduces to a standard few-shot learning scenario, where training is conducted solely on the labeled subset.
 - **Datasets:** CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, STL-10, ImageNet.

2. **Open-Set Semi-Supervised Learning:**
 - **Definition:** The unlabeled dataset ($D_u$) may contain instances from classes not present in the labeled set ($Y_l \subset Y_u$). The test set only contains classes seen during training.
 - **VLM Reformulation:** Since VLMs do not use unlabeled data for training in this context, this also reduces to a few-shot learning problem on the labeled data.
 - **Datasets:** CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-30, ImageNet-100.

3. **Open-World Semi-Supervised Learning:**
 - **Definition:** Extends the open-set scenario by requiring the model to classify both known (base) and novel (unseen) classes at test time.
 - **VLM Reformulation:** Pre-trained models naturally support this via zero-shot classification, making it analogous to the base-to-novel setting.
 - **Datasets:** CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet-100, Tiny ImageNet.

4. **Long-Tailed Semi-Supervised Learning:**
 - **Definition:** Features a highly imbalanced class distribution, with a few "head" classes having many samples and "tail" classes having few.
 - **VLM Reformulation:** The few-shot setting for VLMs is aligned by matching the shot count with the number of samples in the smallest labeled class (tail classes).
 - **Datasets:** CIFAR-10-LT, CIFAR-100-LT, STL-10-LT, ImageNet-127.

**Baseline Models:**

- **SSL Methods:**
 - **Standard SSL:** FixMatch (representative), MixMatch, UDA, ReMixMatch.
 - **Open-Set SSL:** DWD.
 - **Open-World SSL:** OwMatch.
 - **Long-Tailed SSL:** CCL, DARP, CReST+, DASO, ACR, CPE.
- **Pre-trained Models:**
 - **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** CLIP (ViT-B/16 architecture for most experiments), CoOp (prompt tuning), PromptSRC (state-of-the-art prompt tuning).
 - **Multi-Modal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct, GPT-4o-mini.

**Experimental Setup:**
For SSL methods, results are often directly reported from recognized articles to maintain consistency. For VLMs, experiments simulate few-shot learning by training exclusively on the labeled subset of data without accessing unlabeled samples. Prompt tuning methods are primarily used for fine-tuning VLMs. Training duration and GPU resources (single NVIDIA A800 GPU for VLM experiments) are also considered for efficiency comparison. The impact of "adaptation bias" in VLMs is analyzed by testing different visual encoder backbones and varying input resolutions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study systematically compared SSL methods and pre-trained models across diverse image classification settings, yielding several findings regarding their performance, efficiency, and limitations.

#### 4.1. Comparisons in Standard SSL Setting (Table 1, 2, Figure 2, 3)

- **Performance:** SSL methods (e.g., FixMatch) generally perform well on low-resolution datasets like CIFAR-10/100, often surpassing zero-shot CLIP and its fine-tuned variants. This is attributed to the "adaptation bias" of VLMs, which struggle to extract meaningful features from low-resolution inputs due to discrepancies with their high-resolution pre-training data. Conversely, pre-trained VLMs excel on higher-resolution datasets like STL-10 and ImageNet, where zero-shot CLIP often outperforms the best SSL baselines. On ImageNet, VLMs achieved over 73% accuracy using 2% of labeled samples, while FixMatch, using 10% labeled data, lagged behind.
- **Efficiency:** Prompt tuning approaches for VLMs demonstrate significantly higher training efficiency, completing tasks in GPU minutes compared to GPU hours for SSL models on CIFAR datasets.
- **Adaptation Bias Analysis:** This bias in VLMs is primarily caused by resolution mismatches and distribution shifts between pre-training and downstream tasks, rather than limitations in visual encoder capacity. Performance of CLIP models improves with higher input resolution. Fine-tuning techniques (e.g., PromptSRC) and model distillation can partially mitigate this bias.
- **Takeaway 1:** Pre-trained models exhibit strong generalization and high data efficiency, often outperforming traditional SSL with minimal labeled data. Adaptation bias is present in pre-trained models but can be mitigated through downstream adaptation techniques, leading to performance gains and improved efficiency.

#### 4.2. Comparisons in Open-Set SSL Setting (Table 3)

- **Performance:** Pre-trained VLMs (Zero-shot CLIP, CoOp, PromptSRC) consistently outperform SSL methods (e.g., DWD-SL) across all datasets, particularly on ImageNet-30 and ImageNet-100.
- **Robustness to OOD data:** VLMs demonstrate an advantage because they do not rely on unlabeled data for training, thus being less affected by out-of-distribution (OOD) samples in low-quality datasets. SSL methods, conversely, are more sensitive to such data and require specialized mechanisms for OOD handling, which impacts efficiency and performance.
- **Stability:** The extensive pre-training and targeted fine-tuning of VLMs contribute to lower performance variance and a higher performance ceiling across different data distributions.
- **Takeaway 2:** When unlabeled data contains out-of-distribution classes, pre-trained models, by leveraging their pre-trained knowledge and rapid adaptation, are better suited to avoid the complexities of unlabeled training. They offer robust performance and stability.

#### 4.3. Comparisons in Open-World SSL Setting (Table 4)

- **Performance:** SSL methods (e.g., OwMatch+) achieve superior performance on low-resolution datasets (CIFAR-10/100) for both base and new classes. This suggests that specially designed SSL losses can effectively handle novel classes despite the adaptation bias affecting pre-trained models. However, this often requires substantial GPU training time.
- **Generalization to Novel Classes:** On higher-resolution datasets like ImageNet-100, zero-shot CLIP approaches or exceeds the performance of OwMatch on base classes and significantly outperforms it on novel classes. Fine-tuned VLMs exhibit a smaller performance gap between base and novel classes, highlighting strong generalization.
- **Takeaway 3:** In open-world settings, SSL methods perform better on low-resolution datasets. Pre-trained VLMs demonstrate balanced performance on both base and new classes in high-resolution datasets. Overall, pre-trained models are deemed more suitable for scenarios where unlabeled data may contain unseen classes.

#### 4.4. Comparisons in Long-Tailed SSL Setting (Table 5, 8)

- **SSL Vulnerability:** While SSL methods are designed for long-tailed distributions, their performance often declines compared to standard SSL settings on the same datasets, indicating continued vulnerability to class imbalance challenges.
- **VLM Efficiency:** Pre-trained models demonstrate efficiency and good performance with minimal labeled data even on low-resolution datasets.
- **Semantic Ambiguity:** Pre-trained models perform well on highly imbalanced datasets like STL-10 but struggle with semantically coarse datasets such as ImageNet-127, where fine-grained classes are grouped into ambiguous higher-level categories. This creates an "adaptation bias" from unclear semantic boundaries. SSL, learning from neural representations, is less vulnerable to such ambiguity.
- **Takeaway 4:** The low data requirements of pre-trained models allow them to address long-tailed distributions effectively. However, their adaptability can decline when downstream task categories lack clear semantic boundaries, a fundamental issue impacting VLMs.

#### 4.5. Comparisons Between VLMs and MLLMs (Table 6, Appendix D)

- **Performance Scaling:** Increasing parameter scale in MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL) does not consistently translate to improved classification accuracy compared to CLIP-based VLMs. In some cases, larger MLLMs even show performance degradation.
- **GPT-4o-mini:** GPT-4o-mini demonstrates strong performance, suggesting explicit enhancements to its visual encoder capabilities.
- **MLLM Limitations:** Case studies reveal that MLLMs frequently misclassify by failing to focus on the primary subject in an image, instead being distracted by secondary cues. This is attributed to their autoregressive training paradigm, which may hinder effective vision-language alignment by making it difficult to align image tokens with relevant text tokens.
- **Takeaway 5:** Despite stronger semantic understanding, MLLMs' advantages in purely visual classification tasks remain limited. Future research should prioritize effective visual feature extraction and leveraging for both VLMs and MLLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a comprehensive and comparative analysis of Semi-Supervised Learning (SSL) methods and pre-trained models in addressing label scarcity, particularly within the context of image classification. The findings offer practical guidance and highlight critical directions for future research.

#### 5.1. Conclusions from the Comparative Analysis

The study concludes that:
- **SSL Methods** incur high training costs and often exhibit lower performance. They are particularly susceptible to severe performance degradation in open environments characterized by changes in class space or distribution between labeled and unlabeled data.
- **Pre-trained Models** demonstrate strong generalization capabilities due to their pre-trained knowledge, making them robust in open environments. However, they can suffer from "adaptation bias" when the downstream task data differs significantly from their pre-training data distribution, such as performance degradation on low-resolution images for models pre-trained on high-resolution data (e.g., CLIP on CIFAR-10).

#### 5.2. Discussion on Future Directions

1. **Potential for Combining SSL and Pre-trained Models:** The study suggests that integrating SSL techniques with pre-trained models is a promising direction. This fusion can leverage the generalization and zero-shot capabilities of pre-trained models for more reliable pseudo-label generation, while SSL mechanisms can effectively mine domain-specific knowledge from unlabeled data. Examples like GRIP, CPL, and PromptKD have demonstrated significant performance improvements by adopting such hybrid strategies, showcasing enhanced model performance and generalization.

2. **Effects of Training with Long-Tailed Data on Pre-trained Models:** The research indicates that aggressive fine-tuning of pre-trained models on long-tailed datasets can be detrimental, especially to the performance of tail classes. This is because fine-tuning can disrupt the pre-trained class-conditional structure and weaken representations of rare categories. Current approaches often reduce fine-tuning intensity to mitigate this, which can, however, compromise performance on head classes. This highlights an urgent need for new strategies that can adapt to imbalanced data without introducing learning bias.

#### 5.3. Recommendations for Future Research

Based on the empirical findings, the authors provide practical recommendations for researchers facing label scarcity:

- **Initial Approach:** Researchers should first attempt to address the problem using pre-trained models combined with fine-tuning strategies.
- **Addressing Adaptation Bias:** If "adaptation bias" is observed, incorporating additional pseudo-labeling techniques (possibly generated by pre-trained models) to facilitate adaptation is advisable.
- **Task-Specific SSL:** If these approaches prove ineffective and the discrepancy between training and testing classes is relatively small, designing a task-specific SSL method—either traditional or one that leverages pre-trained models for pseudo-label generation—might be beneficial.

#### 5.4. Specific Research Avenues

The study also outlines five specific directions for advancing research:

1. **Datasets:** Incorporating higher-resolution and more challenging benchmarks to better assess and compare SSL methods and pre-trained models, aligning with advancements in image resolution.
2. **SSL Methods:** Emphasizing the necessity for future SSL research in image classification to include systematic, head-to-head comparisons with strong pre-trained baselines under matched conditions (e.g., identical data, resolution, augmentation, compute).
3. **Pre-trained Models:** Focusing research on robust adaptation strategies and evaluation protocols to mitigate the sensitivity of current pre-trained models to distribution shifts (e.g., resolution mismatches between pre-training and testing data).
4. **MLLMs:** Investigating and addressing the limitations of MLLMs' visual encoders, particularly their challenges in recognizing low-resolution images, despite strong semantic understanding.
5. **General Directions:** Exploring principled integrations of SSL with pre-trained model adaptation to enhance performance in label-scarce settings.

#### 5.5. Limitations and Future Work

A stated limitation of the study is its relatively narrow focus on image classification tasks, as most SSL methods are primarily designed for this domain. Future work will aim to explore broader multimodal scenarios and the deeper integration of SSL with pre-trained models, such as leveraging the zero-shot classification capabilities of VLMs for pseudo-labeling and iterative training.

The significance of this work lies in its systematic re-evaluation of established and emerging paradigms for label-efficient learning, providing a clearer empirical foundation for strategic choices in machine learning research and application.
