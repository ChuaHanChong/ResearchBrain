# Tri-Info: Generalizable, Interpretable Failure Prediction for VLA Models via Information Theory

> [!summary] Summary
> Tri-Info treats a VLA-controlled robot as a closed-loop information pipeline and derives eight entropy/mutual-information metrics, reduced to three complementary signals: action entropy, action-action mutual information, and state-transition-action coupling, each fed to its own GRU and fused under a functional conformal threshold. Across six VLA models and three benchmarks it matches strong in-domain baselines and reaches 83% balanced accuracy under sim-to-real transfer where baselines collapse to chance.

## Problem

- Embedding-based failure detectors train classifiers on architecture-specific VLA latents, so they cannot transfer to another model without retraining, and their scalar score explains nothing.
- Score-based monitors such as temporal-consistency distances read off the same embedding geometry, so a single consistency number cannot say which failure mode is occurring.
- Prior information-theoretic robotics work applies entropy and mutual information to training, data curation or representation learning, never to deployment-time failure prediction for VLA policies.

## Method

- A closed-loop ==information pipeline== over current and next state-action embeddings yields eight metrics in four categories: ==marginal statistics==, ==policy coupling==, ==dynamics (empowerment)== and ==temporal coherence==.
- Correlation pruning plus an action-centric preference reduces those eight to the **Tri-Info** triplet: ==action entropy==, ==action-action mutual information==, and ==state-transition-action mutual information==, estimated by ==k-NN== and ==Kozachenko-Leonenko== estimators in a sliding window.
- One ==GRU== plus MLP head per signal, trained with per-timestep ==binary cross-entropy== on trajectory labels, late mean-probability fusion, and a ==functional conformal-prediction== time-varying threshold for online alarms.

## Results

- All eight metrics exceed **0.70** pooled logistic-regression AUC, with action-action mutual information strongest at **0.895**; GRU temporal modeling lifts every metric to **0.97-0.98** pooled AUC.
- In-domain Tri-Info peaks at **0.92** balanced accuracy versus SAFE's **0.91** while alarming within **10-20%** of the rollout; under sim-to-real transfer it reaches **0.83** where all baselines sit at chance.
- Ablation: the three-signal set alone tops both in-domain (**0.88**) and OOD (**0.87**); all eight features drop OOD to **0.74**, and single signals fall to **0.60**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Distributional functionals like entropy and mutual information survive changes of embedding geometry, so monitors built on them buy cross-architecture transfer that geometry-dependent scores cannot.
> - More features are not better under shift: adding five redundant metrics kept in-domain accuracy flat but cut out-of-distribution accuracy, so taxonomy-driven selection beats brute-force fusion.
> - In-domain detection is saturated, any single signal suffices once temporally modeled, so progress should be measured on cross-model and sim-to-real transfer, not in-domain AUC.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, built upon large-scale vision-language pretraining, are increasingly employed in diverse robotic tasks by mapping multimodal inputs directly to low-level robot actions through methods like behavioral cloning. While effective within their training distribution, these models tend to fail under distributional shifts, such as changes in object appearance, lighting, or task specifications. Unlike software errors that can often be reversed, robotic failures are immediate and can lead to irreversible physical damage or unsafe situations, making deployment-time failure anticipation a critical concern for safe embodied AI.

The broader research landscape for VLA models is moving towards deployment in heterogeneous and opaque environments, necessitating failure detectors that are both generalizable and interpretable. Generalizability implies the ability to transfer across different robot architectures, environmental conditions, and the sim-to-real gap without extensive retraining. Interpretability means not only predicting an impending failure but also providing a diagnosis of the underlying failure mode, enabling more targeted intervention or rectification.

Existing VLA failure detection methods generally fall into two categories:
1. **Embedding-based methods:** These approaches train classifiers on a model's internal representations (latents). While achieving strong in-domain accuracy, they are architecture-specific, meaning a detector trained for one VLA model typically cannot transfer to another without retraining due to differing embedding spaces. Furthermore, their scalar scores offer limited diagnostic information about the cause of failure. Examples include SAFE, Fail-Detect, and FIPER.
2. **Score-based methods:** These methods compute a scalar signal, often related to temporal action consistency, to identify anomalies. An example is STAC, which measures temporal action consistency. While more portable than embedding-based methods, a single consistency score also provides limited diagnostic insight into specific failure modes.

This research aims to address the limitations of current methods by proposing an information-theoretic framework for failure prediction. Previous work has utilized information theory in robotic control for concepts like empowerment, exploration, skill discovery, and filtering training data or representation learning. This paper extends the application of information theory to *deployment-time failure prediction* for VLA models, creating a taxonomy of information-theoretic metrics organized around the perception-action loop. This approach seeks to provide architecture-agnostic and interpretable signals, thereby offering mechanistic insights into failure modes that are often unavailable through existing black-box detection methods. The work distinguishes itself by providing both strong cross-domain generalization and diagnostic interpretability, which existing methods meet at most individually.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a failure prediction method for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that is simultaneously generalizable and interpretable, addressing significant limitations of current approaches in real-world robotic deployment.

The motivation for pursuing these two key properties is detailed as follows:

1. **Interpretability for Diagnostic Insight:**
 - VLA models are considered "black boxes," making it difficult to understand why they fail. In robotics, failures can be instantaneous and irreversible, causing harm or damage. Therefore, a useful warning system must not only predict a failure but also explain what is going wrong.
 - The authors observe that successful and failed robotic rollouts exhibit systematically different information-theoretic signatures. This suggests that analyzing information flow within the perception-action loop could provide diagnostic insights.
 - Specific examples illustrate this:
 - A "drift" failure, where the robot moves erratically, is characterized by a surge in action entropy (H(A$_t$)).
 - A "freeze" failure, where the robot stalls, is associated with a collapse in action entropy.
 - A "phantom grasp," where the robot acts as if holding an object it has not grasped, is linked to a drop in state-action mutual information (I(S$_t$, S$_{t+1}$; A$_t$)).
 - These distinct information-theoretic signatures, shifting sharply at the onset of failure, motivate the development of complementary diagnostic signals that can mechanistically account for why failures occur.

2. **Generalizability for Broad Deployment:**
 - Robotic deployments are diverse, implying that a failure detector needs to function across various VLA architectures and environments without requiring extensive retraining.
 - Existing embedding-based detectors are not generalizable because their underlying classifiers are fitted to architecture-specific embedding spaces. A classifier trained on one model's internal representations rarely transfers effectively to another. Score-based detectors, while sometimes more portable, often derive their scores from similar underlying geometric properties that also suffer from architectural specificity.
 - The authors hypothesize that entropy and mutual information, as functionals of the embedding distribution rather than its coordinate geometry, are substrate-independent. This property is expected to enable transfer across different VLA architectures, diverse task environments, and importantly, the sim-to-real gap, without requiring model-specific retraining. This architectural agnosticism is crucial for practical, scalable deployment in varied real-world scenarios.

By formalizing VLA control as a closed-loop information pipeline, the research aims to derive a set of information-theoretic signals that not only effectively predict failures but also inherently provide interpretable diagnostics, addressing the dual challenge of interpretability and generalizability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on formalizing VLA control as an information pipeline, deriving information-theoretic metrics, and constructing an online detection framework.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

A VLA-controlled system is modeled as a tuple (S, A, T, p, $\pi_w$), where S is the observation space ($s_t$), A is the low-level control space ($a_t$), and T is the language instruction space ($\tau$). At each step $t$, the policy $\pi_w(a_t | s_t, \tau)$ outputs an action, and the environment transitions via $p(s_{t+1} | s_t, a_t)$. A trajectory $\xi = \{(s_1, a_1), \dots, (s_T, a_T)\}$ is generated, with an associated failure label $f_\xi \in \{0, 1\}$.
State embeddings $s'_t = \phi_w(s_t)$ are extracted from the visual encoder, and action embeddings $a'_t = \psi_w(s_t, \tau)$ from the layer preceding the action decoder (or velocity field for flow-matching models). $S_t, A_t, S_{t+1}, A_{t+1}$ denote the random variables of these embeddings.
The objective is online detection: at each step $t$, a detector reads a scalar information-theoretic statistic $x_t$ and outputs a failure probability $P_\xi^t(\text{Failure} | x_t) \in$. The goal is for this score to remain below a time-varying threshold $\theta(t)$ for successful trajectories and to exceed it as early as possible for failed ones.

#### 3.2. Information-Theoretic Metrics

The core hypothesis is that successful and failed rollouts lead to systematically different information flows. By treating the VLA system as a closed-loop pipeline of policy and dynamics, eight initial metrics are derived from entropy (H) and mutual information (I) quantities on $S_t, A_t, S_{t+1}, A_{t+1}$. These fall into four diagnostic categories:
- **Marginal statistics:** $H(S_t)$ (state diversity), $H(A_t)$ (action diversity).
- **Policy coupling:** $I(S_t; A_t)$ (action response to visual input).
- **Dynamics:** $I(A_t; S_{t+1})$ (empowerment/controllability), $I(A_t, S_t; S_{t+1})$ (forward predictability), $I(S_t, S_{t+1}; A_t)$ (how actions are explained by state transitions).
- **Temporal coherence:** $I(S_t; S_{t+1})$ (state smoothness), $I(A_t; A_{t+1})$ (action consistency).

**Reduction to Three Complementary Signals (Tri-Info):**
A correlation analysis revealed high redundancy among the eight metrics. To obtain a compact yet comprehensive set, the authors applied two criteria:
1. **Eliminate redundancy:** The four state-action coupling metrics were highly correlated ($r \ge 0.95$), so $I(S_t, S_{t+1}; A_t)$ was chosen as their representative.
2. **Prefer action-centric metrics:** Actions are considered the direct locus of failure and reflect policy behavior more directly. Thus, $H(A_t)$ and $I(A_t; A_{t+1})$, which were not highly correlated with the coupling cluster, were added.
This process yielded the three Triple Information-theoretic (Tri-Info) metrics: $\{H(A_t), I(A_t; A_{t+1}), I(S_t, S_{t+1}; A_t)\}$.
These metrics are semantically complementary:
- $H(A_t)$ captures action diversity anomalies (collapse for "freeze," surge for "drift").
- $I(A_t; A_{t+1})$ captures temporal incoherence common in failing rollouts.
- $I(S_t, S_{t+1}; A_t)$ flags action-state decoupling (e.g., "phantom grasp").
An exhaustive search over all subsets of the eight metrics independently confirmed this triplet as the optimal set of size 3 in terms of AUC, with no further performance gain from additional metrics.

**Estimation and Normalization:**
Because entropy and MI are distributional, they are estimated non-parametrically within a sliding window of $W$ consecutive executed state-action pairs $W_t$.
- For continuous, high-dimensional embeddings, the k-NN MI estimator (Kraskov et al., 2004) and the Kozachenko–Leonenko entropy estimator (Kozachenko, 1987) are used.
- To mitigate issues with high dimensionality for k-NN estimators, embeddings are first projected to a matched 7-dimensional latent space using per-model Variational Autoencoders (VAEs).
- Each estimated metric is then z-normalized using training-set statistics for cross-domain consistency.

#### 3.3. Online Detection Framework

The metrics are transformed into a real-time detector with two main components:
1. **Per-metric GRU detector:** Since failure signatures often span multiple timesteps, a single-input Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) is trained for each of the three normalized Tri-Info sequences $\{z_t\}$. This is followed by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) classification head to output a failure probability. The GRUs are supervised at every timestep using the trajectory-level failure label via binary cross-entropy, designed to push the score towards 1 early on failures.
2. **Late mean-probability fusion:** The three distinct GRU detectors (one for each Tri-Info signal) are trained independently. Their outputs are aggregated only at the final stage by taking the mean of their predicted failure probabilities: $\bar{P}_\xi^t = \frac{1}{3} \sum_{m \in M} P_\xi^t(\text{Failure} | x_t^{(m)})$. This independent training allows each signal to be tuned appropriately and permits flexible inclusion/exclusion of signals without retraining.
3. **Time-varying threshold via Functional Conformal Prediction (CP):** A fixed threshold is insufficient due to systematic shifts in success-score distribution over trajectory execution. Functional CP is used to construct a dynamic threshold $\theta(t)$. This threshold is set as the finite-sample-corrected $(1-\alpha)$ quantile of successful-trajectory scores, interpolated onto a normalized time grid. A failure is flagged when the fused score $\bar{P}_\xi^t$ exceeds $\theta(t)$. The parameter $\alpha$ allows a trade-off between detection timeliness and accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study validated the Tri-Info method across six VLA models and three benchmark environments, including simulated and real-world robot tasks.

#### 4.1. Predictive Power of Information-Theoretic Metrics

- **Individual Metric Performance:** When individual information-theoretic metrics were used as sole inputs to a memoryless Logistic Regression (LogReg) classifier, all eight metrics showed predictive signal, achieving pooled in-domain AUCs ranging from 0.70 to 0.90. The temporal action consistency metric, $I(A_t; A_{t+1})$, was the strongest individual predictor for LogReg (0.90 AUC).
- **Impact of Temporal Modeling:** When a GRU was used to model the temporal evolution of each metric, the performance of every single metric significantly improved, reaching near-ceiling pooled AUCs of 0.97–0.98. This indicates that while the metrics carry inherent predictive signal, modeling their temporal dynamics is crucial for maximizing in-domain detection accuracy.
- **Qualitative Behavior:** The Tri-Info detector demonstrated early warning capabilities. For example, on real-world ALOHA tasks, the detector raised a high probability of failure just before events where the robot nearly caused an object to tip or fall. On simulated LIBERO tasks, successful trajectories consistently remained below the dynamic CP threshold, while failed ones crossed it.

#### 4.2. Baseline Comparison

The Tri-Info detector was compared against embedding-based (SAFE, Fail-Detect, FIPER) and score-based (STAC, GRU-STAC) baselines across three settings.

- **In-domain Performance:** Tri-Info achieved high balanced accuracy (BA) early in rollouts. On PI$_0$-LIBERO, it reached 0.91 BA by mid-trajectory. On PI$_{0.5}$-LIBERO, it achieved 0.85 BA by 10% progress and peaked at 0.92. While some strong baselines (SAFE, STAC) eventually matched or slightly exceeded Tri-Info's peak accuracy (e.g., SAFE peaked at 0.96 on PI$_0$), Tri-Info consistently flagged failures substantially earlier. SAFE often peaked past 70% progress, and STAC remained near chance until mid-trajectory. Fail-Detect and FIPER generally plateaued below 0.74.
- **Cross-domain Generalization:** This is where Tri-Info demonstrated its primary advantage:
 - **Cross-model transfer (PI$_0$ → PI$_{0.5}$):** Tri-Info reached 0.91 BA by 20% progress and maintained 0.92, while GRU-STAC collapsed to chance after 10% progress, and STAC showed only spurious spikes.
 - **Sim-to-real transfer (PI$_{0.5}$-LIBERO → PI$_{0.5}$-ALOHA-real):** All baselines performed at chance level ($\approx 0.50$), whereas Tri-Info steadily rose to 0.83 BA.
 - **Harder sim-to-real transfer (PI$_{0.5}$-LIBERO → ACT-ALOHA-real):** This involved shifts in robot morphology, action dimensionality, and visual appearance. Tri-Info rose rapidly, exceeding 0.90 BA by 30% progress and peaking near 0.95 BA at 40%. In contrast, STAC anti-correlated with failure, and GRU-STAC remained flat at 0.76 regardless of progress.
In summary, Tri-Info matched or surpassed baselines in-domain and was the only method to maintain strong performance under various distribution shifts, whereas baselines largely collapsed. Comparisons against other action-continuity metrics (variance, KNN distance, cosine dissimilarity, temporal gradient) also showed Tri-Info's superior generalizability.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Metric Ablation:** Comparing the three-metric Tri-Info detector against subsets and the full 8-metric fusion revealed that the Tri-Info triplet achieved the best performance in both in-domain (0.88 mean BA) and out-of-distribution (0.87 mean BA) settings. No smaller subset matched its performance on both. Expanding to all eight metrics slightly reduced in-domain accuracy (0.87) and significantly degraded OOD accuracy (0.74), indicating that the additional metrics overfit in-distribution patterns and failed to generalize. This confirmed the utility of the taxonomy-driven signal reduction.
- **Threshold Calibration (α):** The significance level $\alpha$ for Functional Conformal Prediction directly influenced the trade-off between detection timeliness and the true-negative rate (TNR). A smaller $\alpha$ (e.g., 0.05) increased the TNR (0.870), meaning fewer successful trajectories were falsely flagged, but resulted in slightly later detection (0.481 trajectory progress). A larger $\alpha$ (e.g., 0.20) led to earlier alarms (0.385 progress) but with a lower TNR (0.704). This demonstrates that $\alpha$ functions as a tunable knob for practitioners to balance risk tolerance and timeliness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a systematic information-theoretic framework for generalizable and interpretable failure prediction in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, offering several contributions and potential impacts.

**Significance:**
1. **Novel Information-Theoretic Framework:** The work formalizes VLA control as a closed-loop information pipeline, systematically deriving and reducing eight potential information-theoretic metrics to a set of three core Tri-Info signals: action diversity ($H(A_t)$), temporal action consistency ($I(A_t; A_{t+1})$), and action-state coupling ($I(S_t, S_{t+1}; A_t)$). This constitutes a novel application of information theory for real-time robotic failure detection.
2. **Unprecedented Generalizability:** A primary significance is the demonstrated ability of Tri-Info to transfer across diverse VLA models, benchmark environments, and crucially, the sim-to-real gap, without requiring retraining. This capability is notable, especially as prior embedding-based and score-based baselines often collapse to chance performance under distribution shifts, while Tri-Info maintains high balanced accuracy (e.g., 83% in real-world sim-to-real transfer). This substrate-independence, stemming from the information-theoretic nature of the signals, addresses a critical challenge in deploying VLA models in varied real-world scenarios.
3. **Interpretable Diagnostics:** Beyond mere failure prediction, Tri-Info provides interpretable diagnostics. Each of the three signals corresponds to distinct failure modes (e.g., freeze/drift via action entropy, phantom grasp via action-state coupling, temporal incoherence). This allows the detector to function as a "failure-mode dashboard," offering mechanistic insights into *why* a failure is occurring, which is valuable for debugging, system improvement, and guiding interventions.
4. **Early and Robust Detection:** The method consistently achieves high accuracy early in robotic rollouts and provides early warnings ahead of visually identifiable dangerous events, enabling more timely intervention compared to several baselines.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Enhanced Safety in Embodied AI:** By providing generalizable and interpretable failure prediction, Tri-Info can contribute significantly to the safer deployment of VLA models in real-world robotic applications. Early and diagnostic warnings can mitigate irreversible harm to people or objects, which is a paramount concern for embodied AI systems.
2. **Improved Debugging and Development:** The diagnostic nature of Tri-Info's signals can aid developers and operators in understanding and addressing the root causes of VLA model failures. This could accelerate debugging cycles and lead to the development of more robust and reliable robotic policies.
3. **Foundation for Adaptive and Recoverable Systems:** While the current work focuses on detection, the interpretable failure modes identified by Tri-Info could serve as a foundation for future work on automated recovery actions. Knowing *what* is failing (e.g., loss of action diversity vs. state-action decoupling) could guide mode-specific interventions, such as re-injecting exploration, performing rollbacks, or re-grounding perception, thus moving towards more autonomous and resilient robotic systems.
4. **Broader Applicability:** The architecture-agnostic nature of the information-theoretic signals suggests that this methodology could be adapted to a wider range of AI-controlled systems beyond just VLA robotics, wherever understanding the internal information flow of a "black box" system is critical.

**Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge that while Tri-Info detects and diagnoses failure modes, it does not currently provide recovery actions. Closing this loop, by connecting specific diagnostic signals to automated interventions, is indicated as an area for future research.
