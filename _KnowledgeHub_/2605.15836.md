# GAP: Geometric Anchor Pre-training for Data-Efficient Visuomotor Learning of Manipulation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Polytechnic University of Turin developed Geometric Anchor Pre-training (GAP), a method that enhances data-efficient visuomotor learning for robotic manipulation by regularizing spatial adapters with object-centric geometric priors. This approach mitigates issues like "bottleneck collapse" and yields improved policy robustness and success rates, especially in low-data scenarios.

## Problem

- Visuomotor policies for robotic manipulation struggle with data scarcity, requiring effective extraction of task-relevant geometric information from high-dimensional observations without overfitting.
- Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) often lack the fine-grained geometric precision needed for contact-rich manipulation, as their semantic invariance objectives can suppress critical spatial details.
- In low-data regimes, lightweight spatial bottlenecks in VFM-based pipelines are prone to "collapse," latching onto task-irrelevant visual shortcuts and producing poorly localized, brittle keypoints.

## Method

- **GAP** is an action-free warm-up stage pre-training the visual spatial adapter using a simple simulated proxy task, decoupling geometric feature learning from action-mapping.
- The pre-training phase leverages ground-truth object segmentation masks and a multi-objective spatial loss (LGAPL_{GAP}LGAP) comprising centroid alignment, geometric spread, and keypoint diversity.
- After this geometric pre-training, the regularized adapter and frozen VFM encoder are transferred and fine-tuned on downstream manipulation tasks with limited expert demonstrations.

## Results

- GAP consistently achieved higher success rates across diverse manipulation tasks and data scarcity levels, outperforming baselines such as AFA and end-to-end fine-tuning. For example, it reached **62%** success on `Can` with 15 demonstrations, significantly higher than AFA's **46%**.
- The geometric priors learned by GAP demonstrated invariance to the proxy task or simulator, indicating their generalizability, with average success rates of 56-**59%** on `StackCube` across different pre-training conditions.
- GAP effectively enhanced performance for all tested Vision Foundation Model backbones (R3M, DINOv2, VC-1), illustrating its broad compatibility as a spatial regularizer in data-scarce settings.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision Foundation Models, while semantically rich, benefit from explicit geometric regularization to achieve fine-grained precision necessary for contact-rich manipulation, especially with scarce data.
> - A lightweight, action-free pre-training strategy can effectively prevent "bottleneck collapse" by injecting robust, object-centric geometric priors into the visual representation.
> - The combination of centroid alignment, geometric spread, and keypoint diversity losses synergistically guides the spatial adapter to learn stable, well-distributed, and non-redundant keypoints.
