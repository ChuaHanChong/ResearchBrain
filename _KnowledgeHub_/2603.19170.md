# ADMM-Based Distributed MPC with Control Barrier Functions for Safe Multi-Robot Quadrupedal Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> A new distributed model predictive control framework integrates Control Barrier Functions with an ADMM-based node-edge splitting technique to enable safe, decentralized locomotion for multi-robot quadrupedal teams. This approach achieved a 51% reduction in per-cycle planning time for four robots compared to centralized methods, while maintaining robust collision avoidance and trajectory performance.

## Problem

- Managing high-dimensional dynamics and complex contact behaviors in multi-robot quadrupedal locomotion while ensuring safety.
- Explicit inter-agent coupling in existing Control Barrier Function (CBF)-constrained Distributed Model Predictive Control (DMPC) frameworks limits true parallelization and symmetric computational load.
- The necessity for robust inter-agent and agent-obstacle collision avoidance in dynamic and uncertain environments.

## Method

- Developed a hierarchical control architecture, integrating a high-level distributed ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) with mid-level nonlinear MPC and low-level whole-body control.
- Utilized the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) with a node-edge splitting technique and consensus constraints to reformulate the centralized MPC problem.
- Decomposed the global problem into independent node-local and edge-local quadratic programs (QPs) solvable in parallel with neighbor-to-neighbor communication.

## Results

- Hardware experiments with Unitree Go2 robots showed successful navigation in cluttered environments with static obstacles, maintaining safety thresholds and demonstrating robustness to external disturbances and rough terrain.
- Numerical simulations with up to four robots confirmed scalability, satisfying safety constraints and preserving decentralized computation.
- The ADMM-based DMPC reduced the average per-cycle planning time by approximately **51%** for four agents compared to a centralized CBF-MPC, while yielding comparable trajectory quality and safety.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Applying ADMM with a novel node-edge splitting technique effectively decouples the inter-agent coupling introduced by Control Barrier Function constraints in multi-robot DMPC.
> - This decomposition allows for fully parallelized computation of local optimization problems, leading to a symmetrically distributed computational load across agents.
> - Safety guarantees provided by CBFs can be preserved within a decentralized ADMM framework, ensuring collision avoidance in complex multi-robot scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multi-robot systems, particularly those involving legged robots like quadrupeds, is experiencing advancements due to their robust locomotion capabilities in challenging terrains. Extending these capabilities to multi-robot teams offers benefits in efficiency and robustness for applications such as inspection, search and rescue, and collaborative transportation. However, this extension introduces complexities related to high-dimensional dynamics, hybrid contact behaviors, and the need for inter-agent safety guarantees.

Model Predictive Control (MPC) has emerged as a prominent framework for trajectory generation and control in legged robotics, allowing for systematic handling of system dynamics and constraints while optimizing performance. Both reduced-order models (e.g., linear inverted pendulum, single rigid body) and full-order robot dynamics have been utilized in MPC for quadrupedal locomotion. For multi-robot systems, Distributed MPC (DMPC) is often preferred over centralized approaches due to its scalability and robustness, as it allows agents to solve local optimization problems with local communication.

Ensuring safety is a critical aspect in multi-robot operations, especially in environments with static obstacles and close-proximity interactions between agents. Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) provide a theoretical framework for encoding safety specifications by ensuring the forward invariance of safe sets. Integrating CBFs into MPC formulations enables safety-critical trajectory planning by explicitly incorporating safety constraints into the optimal control problem. This approach has been applied to both single and multi-robot quadrupedal systems.

A challenge in integrating CBFs into DMPC for multi-robot legged systems is the explicit inter-agent coupling introduced by safety conditions, which depend on the states and inputs of neighboring agents. Existing DMPC formulations with embedded CBF constraints often maintain this coupling within the local nonlinear programming problems, preventing a direct and explicit decomposition suitable for fully parallelized and symmetrically loaded computations. This limitation motivates the development of methods that can achieve a truly decentralized optimization structure.

The Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) is a recognized algorithm for distributed optimization, known for its decomposability and convergence properties, even for certain classes of nonconvex problems. This paper positions itself within this landscape by proposing an ADMM-based framework to address the challenge of explicit inter-agent coupling in CBF-constrained DMPC for multi-robot legged systems, aiming to enable a fully decentralized and computationally efficient solution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent challenges of deploying multi-robot legged systems in safety-critical applications. These challenges include managing the high-dimensional dynamics and complex contact behaviors of quadrupedal robots, compounded by the need for robust inter-agent and agent-obstacle collision avoidance in uncertain environments.

Specifically, the authors identify a limitation in existing distributed MPC (DMPC) formulations that incorporate Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) for safety. While CBFs are effective for ensuring safety, their integration into multi-robot MPC problems introduces explicit coupling between the states and control inputs of neighboring agents. This coupling prevents the direct decomposition of the resulting optimal control problems into fully independent subproblems for each robot. Consequently, the optimization problems solved by individual agents remain implicitly coupled, hindering the ability to fully exploit parallel computation and achieve a truly decentralized implementation with a symmetric computational load across all agents.

To address these limitations, the overarching goal of this paper is to develop a fully decentralized Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework that guarantees safety using CBFs for teams of quadrupedal robots.

The key objectives are articulated as follows:
- **Reformulate the centralized safety-critical MPC problem:** This involves transforming the problem, which inherently contains inter-agent coupling due to CBF constraints, into a structured distributed optimization format.
- **Utilize the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM):** The aim is to leverage ADMM by introducing a novel node-edge splitting technique combined with consensus constraints. This specific reformulation is intended to expose an underlying separable structure that enables decomposition.
- **Decompose the global problem:** The reformulation should break down the centralized problem into independent node-local and edge-local quadratic programs (QPs).
- **Enable parallel and local computation:** These decomposed subproblems must be solvable in parallel, requiring only neighbor-to-neighbor communication, thereby allowing for fully decentralized trajectory optimization.
- **Achieve symmetric computational load:** The decomposition strategy should distribute computational burden evenly across agents.
- **Preserve safety and dynamic feasibility:** The decentralized approach must maintain the safety guarantees provided by CBFs and ensure that the generated trajectories are dynamically feasible for the robots.
- **Integrate into a hierarchical control architecture:** The proposed high-level distributed planner should be seamlessly integrated with mid-level nonlinear MPC and low-level whole-body control layers for robust quadrupedal locomotion.
- **Demonstrate real-time efficiency and performance:** The framework's effectiveness should be validated through hardware experiments and numerical simulations, showcasing improved computational efficiency compared to centralized approaches while achieving comparable trajectory quality and safety.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to overcome a significant hurdle in scaling safety-critical multi-robot legged systems by providing a computationally efficient and truly decentralized control solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper involves a hierarchical control architecture, with the core contribution being an ADMM-based Distributed Model Predictive Control (DMPC) framework incorporating Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) at the high-level planning layer.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The work considers a network of $n_V$ quadrupedal robots, where each robot $i$ has discrete-time nonlinear dynamics $x_i(t+1) = f_i(x_i(t), u_i(t))$. The Cartesian center-of-mass (CoM) position of agent $i$ is given by $g(x_i)$. The objective is to navigate agents to goal configurations while avoiding static obstacles ($o_\ell$) and inter-agent collisions. Safety is formally defined through an inter-agent safe set $S_V$ and an agent-obstacle safe set $S_O$, both ensuring a minimum distance $d_{th}$.

Discrete-time CBFs are employed to enforce safety. For each agent $i$ and obstacle $\ell$, an obstacle-avoidance CBF $h_{i,\ell}(x_i)$ is defined. For each pair of neighboring agents $\{i,j\}$, an inter-agent CBF $h_{i,j}(x_i, x_j)$ is defined. The CBF conditions require that $\Delta h \ge -\alpha(h)$, where $\alpha$ is a class K function. The obstacle avoidance CBF depends only on local agent state and input, whereas the inter-agent CBF explicitly couples the states and inputs of agents $i$ and $j$.

#### 3.2. Centralized MPC Problem

A centralized CBF-based MPC problem is initially formulated over a prediction horizon $N$. This problem minimizes a sum of individual agent costs $J_i(\xi_i)$ (penalizing deviations from reference trajectories) subject to:
1. Local dynamics for each agent.
2. Local CBF conditions for obstacle avoidance for each agent.
3. Coupled CBF conditions for inter-agent collision avoidance for each pair of neighbors.
4. Initial state conditions.
This centralized formulation highlights the inter-agent coupling that the distributed approach aims to resolve.

#### 3.3. ADMM-Based DMPC with CBF Constraints

To achieve a fully decentralized solution, the centralized problem is reformulated using ADMM.
1. **Separation of Local and Coupled Constraints:** The centralized MPC problem is rewritten as minimizing a sum of local costs $J_i(\xi_i)$ subject to local feasibility constraints $\xi_i \in \Xi_i$ (encompassing local dynamics and obstacle avoidance CBFs) and coupled inequality constraints $\psi_{ij}(\xi_i, \xi_j) \le 0$ (representing inter-agent CBF conditions).
2. **Conversion to Coupled Equality Constraints:** To fit the standard ADMM form, slack variables $s_{ij} \ge 0$ are introduced for each coupled inequality constraint, transforming $\psi_{ij}(\xi_i, \xi_j) \le 0$ into $\psi_{ij}(\xi_i, \xi_j) + s_{ij} = 0$. A convex quadratic penalty $\varphi(s_{ij})$ is added to the objective to encourage $s_{ij}$ to be near zero, maintaining the original constraint's intent.
3. **Node-Edge Splitting Technique:** To further decompose the problem, edge-local copies of the decision variables are created for each coupled constraint. For each edge $\{i,j\} \in E$, auxiliary variables $z_{ij,i} \approx \xi_i$ and $z_{ij,j} \approx \xi_j$ are introduced, along with $s_{ij}$. Consensus constraints are then imposed: $z_{ij,i} = \xi_i$ and $z_{ij,j} = \xi_j$. The original edge feasibility (i.e., $\psi_{ij}(z_{ij,i}, z_{ij,j}) + s_{ij} = 0, s_{ij} \ge 0$) is enforced locally at each edge. This reformulation yields a standard ADMM structure with a sum of node-local terms, edge-local terms, and consensus constraints.

#### 3.4. Scaled ADMM Iterations

The ADMM algorithm proceeds iteratively through three steps:
1. **Node Update:** Each agent $i$ solves an independent local optimization problem (a QP after linearization) to update its trajectory $\xi_i^{p+1}$. This problem minimizes $J_i(\xi_i)$ plus augmented Lagrangian terms that penalize deviations from its neighbors' previous iteration's edge variables ($z_{ij,i}^p$) and dual variables ($\lambda_{ij,i}^p$). This step is performed in parallel across all agents.
2. **Edge Update:** For each edge $\{i,j\}$, a separate optimization problem (a QP) is solved to update the edge variables ($z_{ij,i}^{p+1}, z_{ij,j}^{p+1}, s_{ij}^{p+1}$). This minimizes the slack penalty $\varphi(s_{ij})$ plus augmented Lagrangian terms, using the newly updated node trajectories ($\xi_i^{p+1}, \xi_j^{p+1}$) and previous dual variables. This step requires communication between neighboring agents $i$ and $j$.
3. **Dual Updates:** The Lagrange multipliers ($\lambda_{ij,i}, \lambda_{ij,j}$) associated with the consensus constraints are updated using the primal residual.

#### 3.5. Communication Protocol and Computational Load

The ADMM process involves only neighbor-to-neighbor communication. Each agent sends its updated local trajectory to its neighbors, and after edge subproblems are solved, updated edge variables are exchanged. This decomposition results in $n_V$ node-local QPs and $n_E$ edge-local QPs per ADMM iteration, all solvable in parallel, leading to a symmetrically distributed computational load.

#### 3.6. Layered Control Architecture

The ADMM-based DMPC forms the high-level planning layer of a hierarchical control scheme for quadrupedal robots:
1. **High-Level (ADMM-based CBF-DMPC):** Operates at 10 Hz. It models each agent using a kinematic car model (position and yaw) and its dynamics are successively linearized around the current operating point. The CBF conditions are also linearized. This transforms the node and edge subproblems into quadratic programs (QPs), which are solved efficiently using the OSQP solver. The maximum number of ADMM iterations is capped at 15 for real-time performance.
2. **Mid-Level (Nonlinear MPC - NMPC):** Operates at 50 Hz. This NMPC enforces the reduced-order single rigid body (SRB) dynamics of each quadruped. It tracks the reference trajectories generated by the high-level DMPC and computes optimal ground reaction forces (GRFs) to ensure dynamic feasibility, stability, and friction-cone compliance. It uses the CasADi framework with the FATROP solver.
3. **Low-Level (Whole-Body Controller - WBC):** Operates at 500 Hz. This nonlinear WBC enforces the full-order robot dynamics, tracking the desired SRB states and GRFs from the NMPC layer. It is formulated as a real-time QP, respecting full-body dynamics and actuation constraints.

#### 3.7. Experimental Setup

The framework is validated using Unitree Go2 quadrupedal robots (mass 15 kg, 18 DoFs) for both numerical simulations (in RaiSim physics engine) and hardware experiments. A multi-threaded offboard desktop computer handles the parallel execution of the layered control. The DMPC parameters include a prediction horizon $N=50$, sampling time $T_s=0.1$ s, safety margin $d_{th}=0.5$ m, and a class K function $\alpha(s)=0.3s$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of the proposed ADMM-based CBF-DMPC framework, integrated into a hierarchical locomotion control architecture, was demonstrated through both hardware experiments and numerical simulations.

#### 4.1. Hardware Experiments (Two Unitree Go2 Robots)

1. **Cluttered Environment with Eight Obstacles:** The two Go2 robots successfully navigated an environment with eight static obstacles while tracking individual goal configurations. The distributed CBF-DMPC generated collision-free trajectories in real-time, maintaining the inter-robot distance above the prescribed safety threshold ($d_{th} = 0.5$ m) and avoiding all obstacles. The robots autonomously adjusted their trajectories through local edge updates without requiring centralized coordination.
2. **Robustness to External Disturbances:** During locomotion, one of the robots was subjected to multiple physical perturbations. The layered control architecture demonstrated robustness by maintaining safety and convergence towards the assigned goals. While external pushes temporarily reduced CBF values due to model discrepancies, the ADMM-based CBF-DMPC rapidly replanned trajectories, restoring CBF values to the non-negative region and thereby recovering safety.
3. **Navigation on Rough Terrain with Ten Obstacles:** The robots successfully navigated an environment containing ten static obstacles and rough terrain created by wooden blocks. This experiment validated the effective interaction between the high-level distributed planning and the mid/low-level dynamic locomotion controllers. Safe inter-agent distances and obstacle avoidance were maintained by the high-level planner, while the NMPC and WBC layers ensured stable locomotion over uneven surfaces.

#### 4.2. Numerical Simulations (Up to Four Robots)

Numerical simulations involving four quadrupedal robots in RaiSim, navigating environments with ten obstacles, were conducted. This extended validation confirmed that the structured ADMM-based CBF-DMPC formulation scales beyond two agents while preserving decentralized computation and satisfying safety constraints. In the four-agent scenario, the system involved 4 node-update QPs and 6 edge-update QPs per ADMM iteration, all solvable in parallel.

#### 4.3. Computational Performance and Comparison Analysis

A direct comparison was performed between the proposed ADMM-based CBF-DMPC and a centralized CBF-MPC (solving problem 13) in terms of per-cycle computation time. Both methods used the same successive linearization procedure, horizon length, cost weights, safety parameters, and solver configurations.
- **Two Agents:** The ADMM-based DMPC reduced the average per-cycle planning time by approximately 16% compared to the centralized CBF-MPC (average 8.06 ms vs. 9.61 ms).
- **Four Agents:** The computational benefits increased significantly with more agents. The ADMM-based DMPC achieved an average per-cycle planning time reduction of approximately 51% compared to the centralized CBF-MPC (average 14.58 ms vs. 29.94 ms).
- **QP Computation Times:** The node-update QPs had average solution times of 0.51 ms (two agents) and 0.92 ms (four agents), while edge-update QPs were substantially faster (0.002 ms for two agents, 0.009 ms for four agents).
- **Solution Quality:** The trajectories generated by the distributed formulation closely matched those of the centralized optimal solution. Negligible differences were observed in terms of trajectory quality, objective values, and CBF constraint satisfaction (e.g., collision avoidance distances), indicating that the computational efficiency gain did not compromise performance or safety.

These results indicate that the structured ADMM decomposition significantly improves computational efficiency, particularly as the number of agents increases, while maintaining the quality and safety guarantees of a centralized approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of multi-robot locomotion control, specifically for safety-critical operations involving quadrupedal robots. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Enabling Fully Decentralized Safety-Critical Control:** The core contribution is the development of a fully decentralized MPC framework that integrates CBF-based safety guarantees. This is significant because it directly addresses the challenge of explicit inter-agent coupling in prior distributed MPC-CBF formulations, which often limited true parallelization. By using a novel node-edge splitting technique with ADMM, the paper demonstrates a method to truly decouple the optimization problems.

2. **Improved Computational Efficiency and Real-Time Performance:** The proposed ADMM-based decomposition leads to a substantial reduction in per-cycle planning time compared to centralized approaches. A 51% reduction in planning time for the four-agent case is a significant improvement, demonstrating its suitability for real-time implementation in computationally constrained robotic systems. This efficiency is critical for complex tasks and dynamic environments where rapid replanning is necessary.

3. **Symmetric Computational Load Distribution:** The methodology ensures that the computational burden is symmetrically distributed across agents. This enhances the scalability and robustness of multi-robot systems, as it avoids bottlenecks that might arise in centralized or unevenly distributed computational schemes.

4. **Preservation of Safety and Trajectory Quality:** Despite the shift to a decentralized and more efficient computation paradigm, the framework maintains the safety guarantees provided by CBFs (collision avoidance for both inter-agent and agent-obstacle scenarios). The findings indicate that the distributed solution produces trajectories comparable in quality to those from a centralized optimal solution, without compromising safety margins.

5. **Robustness to Disturbances and Complex Environments:** The validation through hardware experiments and simulations across various challenging scenarios – cluttered environments, external disturbances, and rough terrain – demonstrates the practical robustness and reliability of the integrated hierarchical control architecture. This showcases its potential for deployment in real-world, unpredictable settings.

6. **Integrated Hierarchical Control:** The successful integration of the high-level ADMM-based DMPC with mid-level nonlinear MPC and low-level whole-body control layers provides a comprehensive and effective solution for complex robotic locomotion, bridging the gap from abstract trajectory planning to detailed physical execution.

7. **Scalability Potential:** The demonstrated performance increase with a higher number of agents (from two to four) suggests that the ADMM-based approach offers increasing computational benefits as team sizes grow. This is crucial for applications requiring large-scale multi-robot coordination.

The potential impact of this research is broad, extending to various applications where teams of legged robots can operate autonomously and safely. This includes industrial inspection in hazardous or inaccessible areas, search and rescue missions where coordination and resilience are paramount, and collaborative transportation tasks that demand both efficiency and safety in dynamic environments. Future work on scaling to larger teams and integrating fully decentralized onboard computing architectures could further enhance the impact of this approach, making multi-robot quadrupedal locomotion more practical and widespread.
