# Think Small, Act Big: Primitive Prompt Learning for Lifelong Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Primitive Prompt Learning (PPL) enables lifelong robot manipulation by learning reusable primitive prompts. A motion-aware query fuses CLIP text embeddings with RAFT optical flow to weight prompt components, which are prepended to keys and values of a diffusion transformer policy. New skills append fresh prompts optimized alongside frozen pretrained ones, reaching 0.83 average forward transfer on MimicGen versus 0.65 sequential, without replay data.

## Problem

- Experience replay retains knowledge but scales poorly in deployment because memory demands grow with tasks and stored demonstrations raise privacy concerns.
- Regularization and dynamic-architecture methods lack explicit transfer mechanisms, so they underperform on complex vision-based manipulation and only passively preserve old weights.
- Task-specific adapter methods isolate each task's parameters, preventing interference but also blocking reuse of shared motion primitives to accelerate new skill acquisition.

## Method

- **Primitive prompts** split into key and value halves and prepended via ==prefix-tuning== to every ==multi-head self-attention== layer of a ==diffusion transformer== policy, with per-layer parameters.
- **Motion-Aware Prompting (MAP)** builds a text-flow query from ==CLIP== instruction embeddings plus ==RAFT optical-flow== features, then ==cosine similarity==-matches it against prompt keys to weight-sum prompt components.
- Lifelong stage expands prompt components, keys and attention vectors for each new skill while freezing pretrained prompts, so old weighting stays intact and no prior data is needed.

## Results

- On MimicGen lifelong sequences PPL averaged **0.83** forward transfer versus **0.65** sequential, **0.61** task-specific LoRA, with sequential and LoRA suffering backward transfer of **-0.56** and **-0.46**.
- Multi-skill pretraining beat a mixture-of-experts sparse diffusion policy by **17%** on LIBERO-GOAL (**0.84** versus **0.82** and **0.77**), and real-world Franka averaged **0.84** versus **0.73** MoE and **0.42** diffusion transformer, **0.68** lifelong versus **0.58** replay, over **15** runs per skill.
- Ablations show performance drops when pretraining prompts are omitted or unused for new skills, and prompt count has a non-monotone optimum.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optical flow supplies appearance-invariant motion signatures, letting semantically unrelated skills share latent primitives that text-only prompt queries cannot connect.
> - Growing a frozen prompt pool gives replay-free transfer at LoRA-level training cost, beating mixture-of-experts accuracy at roughly half its training time.
> - More prompts is not better: unmatched components inject noise, so capacity must be tuned against the number of underlying primitives.
> - Flow-based queries degrade under strong illumination shifts where text-only queries stay stable, so RAFT inherits brightness-constancy fragility.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a foundational challenge in artificial intelligence and robotics: **lifelong learning** for continuous skill acquisition without **catastrophic forgetting**. Lifelong learning aims to enable intelligent agents, particularly robots, to incrementally learn new tasks and skills over time, leveraging previously acquired knowledge while retaining proficiency in older tasks. This capability is inspired by human learning, where past experiences accelerate new skill acquisition and prevent knowledge erosion.

The existing research landscape for lifelong robot learning broadly includes several approaches:
- **Experience Replay (ER):** Methods like store and re-train on samples from previous tasks. While effective at mitigating forgetting, ER is memory-intensive and faces practical limitations in real-world robotics due to data storage, privacy concerns, and computational overhead as the number of tasks grows.
- **Regularization-based Methods:** Approaches such as add constraints to the learning process to prevent significant changes to parameters critical for old tasks. These methods can be less effective for complex, high-dimensional tasks like vision-based manipulation, as they indirectly preserve knowledge and may struggle to facilitate positive knowledge transfer.
- **Dynamic Architectures:** Methods like expand the model's capacity for new tasks. While avoiding catastrophic forgetting, they can lead to ever-growing models and may not actively transfer knowledge.
- **Parameter-Efficient Methods:** More recent advancements, particularly from Natural Language Processing (NLP) and Computer Vision (CV), involve adapting large pre-trained models with minimal task-specific parameters. **Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA)** is a prominent example, as explored in robotics by TAIL. LoRA trains small, low-rank matrices for each task, preventing interference. However, the paper argues that LoRA-based methods often require specific parameters per task and may not explicitly leverage shared knowledge to boost *new* skill acquisition, making them less efficient for true lifelong learning. Other prompt-based methods like Dual-Prompt, Coda-Prompt, and MP2 also exist but often struggle with temporal dependencies inherent in robotics or rely on task IDs, limiting generalization across diverse motions.

Crucially, a common limitation across these existing works, as highlighted by the authors, is their **failure to effectively leverage shared knowledge and common underlying "primitives" between skills**. Robotic tasks often involve sequences of fundamental motion patterns (e.g., "grasp," "reach," "place"). While previous works have explored learning these **robotic primitives** using pre-defined libraries or extraction from demonstrations via statistical models or deep neural networks, they have not adequately focused on how to *leverage these primitives specifically for lifelong learning challenges*. These prior primitive-learning methods confirm the benefits of reusability and modularity but lack a framework for continuous knowledge transfer in a lifelong context.

This paper's contribution, **Primitive Prompt Learning (PPL)**, directly addresses this gap. It integrates the concept of reusable primitives with parameter-efficient prompt learning in a two-stage framework. By learning "primitive prompts" that capture shared motion and semantic information, PPL aims to enable efficient knowledge transfer for continuous skill acquisition, distinguishing itself from methods that merely prevent forgetting or adapt task-specifically without explicit primitive-level knowledge sharing. It proposes a novel "motion-aware prompting" mechanism, which is a key differentiator, to identify and transfer these shared primitives even between semantically distinct tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and efficient framework for **lifelong robot manipulation** that can continuously acquire new skills while effectively leveraging prior knowledge and mitigating catastrophic forgetting.

The motivation stems from several core challenges in achieving truly generalist robotics:
- **Bridging the gap between human and robot learning:** Humans effortlessly transfer skills (e.g., using chopsticks to tweezers) by recognizing and adapting underlying common motions. Robots struggle with this "knowledge transfer" in diverse, complex tasks.
- **Limitations of current lifelong learning methods:** Existing techniques like experience replay are memory-intensive, while regularization or dynamic architectures often lack explicit mechanisms for beneficial knowledge transfer, leading to suboptimal performance in complex vision-based manipulation. Parameter-efficient methods like LoRA prevent task interference but don't actively leverage shared knowledge for *new* skill acquisition.
- **Underutilization of shared primitives:** Robotic tasks, despite superficial differences, often share fundamental motion patterns or "primitives" (e.g., a "grasp" action applies to various objects). Current methods often fail to explicitly recognize and leverage these shared building blocks for knowledge transfer across tasks, thus limiting true lifelong learning.

To address these motivations, the authors propose **Primitive Prompt Learning (PPL)**, with the following key objectives:
- To learn a set of **reusable and extensible primitive prompts** that can represent shared underlying actions and semantic elements across different robotic skills.
- To design a **two-stage learning scheme** that first pre-trains these primitive prompts on a diverse set of initial skills and then efficiently expands upon them for new skill acquisition.
- To introduce **motion-aware prompting** that leverages both semantic (language instruction) and kinematic (optical flow) information to robustly identify and capture shared primitives, even between semantically dissimilar but motion-related tasks.
- To demonstrate that by freezing pre-trained primitive prompts and optimizing new "lifelong prompts," the framework can achieve **positive knowledge transfer** from old to new skills without requiring access to previous task data, thereby boosting learning efficiency and performance.
- To evaluate PPL extensively in both **simulation and real-world scenarios** using a newly constructed large-scale skill dataset to validate its superior performance compared to state-of-the-art lifelong learning methods.

In essence, the paper aims to "think small" by decomposing complex tasks into shared "primitive" motion patterns, and "act big" by enabling truly lifelong learning and robust knowledge transfer across a vast range of robotic manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Primitive Prompt Learning (PPL) framework operates in a novel two-stage learning scheme: a multi-skill pre-training stage and a lifelong learning stage. The core idea is to leverage "primitive prompts" that capture shared knowledge between skills, facilitated by a unique "motion-aware prompting" mechanism. The underlying policy architecture is a diffusion transformer.

#### 3.1. Multi-Skill Pre-Training Stage

In this initial stage, the goal is to learn a foundational set of reusable primitive prompts from a diverse collection of existing robotic skills.
- **Policy Architecture:** The policy is based on a diffusion transformer. This architecture is well-suited for sequence prediction problems like robotic imitation learning, where future actions are predicted conditioned on historical states. Behavioral cloning loss is used to optimize the policy.
- **Prompt Integration:** PPL integrates primitive prompts into the Multi-Head Self-Attention (MSA) layers of the diffusion transformer. Unlike typical prompt learning that prepends learnable tokens to inputs, PPL splits each prompt `p` into `p_K` (key prompt) and `p_V` (value prompt) and prepends them to the keys and values, respectively, of the MSA layers. This allows the prompts to influence the attention mechanism directly, guiding the model's focus on relevant features.
- **Learning Shared Primitives:** The challenge here is to make prompts capture *shared* primitives, not just task-specific information. Traditional prompt learning, often relying solely on task identifiers or language instructions, struggles to identify commonalities between semantically distinct tasks (e.g., "grasp banana" and "place pot" might share a "reach and close gripper" primitive).

#### 3.2. Motion-Aware Prompting (MAP)

To overcome the limitations of semantic-only prompting and capture both semantic and *motion-shared* primitives, PPL introduces Motion-Aware Prompting (MAP).
- **Optical Flow for Motion Information:** MAP integrates optical flow, which mathematically represents the apparent motion of objects between consecutive images (Eq. 4). This captures low-level kinematic properties and movement patterns, providing invariance to appearance changes. RAFT, a recurrent all-pairs field transforms model, is used to estimate optical flow iteratively (Eq. 5, 6).
- **Semantic Integration:** While optical flow provides rich motion, it might overlook task-specific nuances. Therefore, MAP combines optical flow features with task-specific semantic embeddings derived from pre-trained CLIP models (Eq. 7). CLIP provides a high-level understanding of the language instruction.
- **Prompt Query Generation:** The MAP function `f_prompt` combines these semantic and motion features to generate a "motion-aware prompt query." This query is then used to dynamically weight and combine different primitive prompt components.
- **Dynamic Prompt Weighting:** PPL maintains a set of prompt components `P`, each associated with attention vectors `A` and keys `K`. The motion-aware query `MAP(T, F)` (where `T` is task description, `F` is optical flow) is element-wise multiplied with `A` to form an "Attention Query" (Eq. 8). This Attention Query is then used to compute a weight vector `α` based on cosine similarity with the keys `K` of the prompt components (Eq. 9). Finally, a weighted sum of the prompt components, using `α`, forms the final prompt `p` that is prepended to the transformer's keys and values (Eq. 10). This dynamic weighting allows the model to activate only the most relevant primitive prompts for a given state and task, even if the task is semantically different but shares a common motion primitive.

#### 3.3. Lifelong Skill Acquisition Stage

After multi-skill pre-training, the learned primitive prompts are highly effective for new skill acquisition.
- **Freezing Pre-trained Prompts:** A crucial aspect of lifelong learning is preventing catastrophic forgetting. PPL achieves this by *freezing* the primitive prompts obtained from the multi-skill pre-training stage. This ensures that the foundational knowledge encoded in these prompts is preserved.
- **Appending Lifelong Prompts:** When a robot encounters a new skill (Task `t`), new "lifelong prompts" are initialized and *appended* to the existing set of frozen pre-trained prompts.
- **Optimization:** Only the parameters of these *new* lifelong prompts (and the main policy head) are updated during training for the current task. However, when calculating the dynamic weights `α`, the model considers *all* frozen pre-trained prompts and all previously learned lifelong prompt components. This mechanism allows for knowledge transfer: the new skill benefits from the shared primitives learned earlier, as the frozen prompts contribute to the weighted sum, influencing the policy without being directly modified. This prevents the need for replaying old data.
- **Incremental Expansion:** The set of prompt components `P`, keys `K`, and attention vectors `A` incrementally expands as new skills are acquired, facilitating continuous learning.

In summary, PPL's methodology ingeniously combines the efficiency of prompt learning with the concept of motion primitives, using a sophisticated attention mechanism to dynamically retrieve and apply relevant shared knowledge, thereby enabling robust and efficient lifelong robot manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper evaluates PPL's performance in both simulated and real-world environments, demonstrating its superiority over state-of-the-art methods in multi-skill pre-training and lifelong learning capabilities.

#### 4.1. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Tasks:** A large-scale skill dataset was constructed, combining tasks from MimicGen and LIBERO benchmarks. This dataset includes diverse skills (e.g., "Grasp the mug," "Open the drawer") with language instructions and broad initial state distributions, ensuring comprehensive evaluation of generalization.
- **Real-world Experiments:** Experiments were conducted on a Franka Panda robotic arm. The setup involved pre-training on four distinct skills (200 human demonstrations each) and evaluating lifelong learning on four additional skill tasks with randomly placed objects to assess position generalization.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Standard lifelong learning metrics were used:
 - **Forward Transfer Weight (FWT):** Measures the success rate achieved when adapting to a *new* task, indicating how well prior knowledge aids new learning. Higher FWT is better.
 - **Backward Transfer Weight (BWT):** Measures the retention of performance on *previous* tasks after learning a new one, quantifying resistance to catastrophic forgetting. Higher BWT (less negative) is better.

#### 4.2. Multi-Skill Pre-Training Results

- **LIBERO-GOAL Environment (Simulation):** Table 2 shows PPL achieves the highest average success rate (0.84 ± 0.05) across all pre-training tasks, outperforming a baseline Diffusion Transformer (0.42 ± 0.09) and a MoE-based policy (0.73 ± 0.08). PPL shows a significant 17% improvement over MoE.
- **Real-World Scenarios:** Table 2 also presents real-world results, where PPL consistently outperforms existing approaches in multi-skill pre-training, achieving 0.84 ± 0.05 average success rate compared to Diffusion Transformer (0.42 ± 0.09) and MoE (0.73 ± 0.08). This validates PPL's effectiveness in practical settings.

#### 4.3. Lifelong Learning Results

- **Simulation (MimicGen):** Table 1 presents a comparative analysis against traditional sequential learning, experience replay (ER), and task-specific LoRA. PPL demonstrates superior performance:
 - **FWT:** PPL (0.78 ± 0.09 average) significantly outperforms Sequential ER (0.65 ± 0.06) and LoRA (0.61 ± 0.09), indicating its strong ability to leverage prior knowledge for new task acquisition.
 - **BWT:** PPL (0.83 ± 0.03 average, which implies minimal forgetting as FWT values are high) also shows superior resistance to catastrophic forgetting compared to LoRA (-0.46 ± 0.18) and Sequential ER (-0.56 ± 0.16), which exhibit negative BWT (forgetting). This highlights PPL's ability to retain proficiency in old skills while learning new ones, without requiring replay data.
- **Real-World Scenarios:** Table 2 shows similar trends in real-world lifelong learning, with PPL achieving a higher average success rate (0.68 ± 0.05) than Sequential ER (0.55 ± 0.09) and other methods. This confirms PPL's ability to effectively transfer pre-trained primitives to new skill acquisition in challenging real-world conditions.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Motion-Aware Prompting (MAP):** Figure 6 visualizes the prompt weight distributions. When only text embeddings are used for queries, weights show similarity only for semantically related tasks. In contrast, the text-flow query of MAP allows similar weight responses even for semantically different skills (e.g., "Grasp Red Cube" and "Place Coffee Pod"), demonstrating its success in identifying and leveraging latent shared motion primitives across diverse tasks.
- **Effect of Primitive Prompt Count:** Figure 8 illustrates that there's an optimal number of primitive prompts. Too few might not capture all primitives, while too many can introduce noise and degrade performance. This underscores the need for a balanced prompt capacity.
- **Effect of Primitive Prompt Mechanism:** Figure 8 shows significant performance degradation when (1) primitive prompt learning is omitted during pre-training, or (2) pre-trained prompts are not utilized (frozen) in the lifelong learning stage. This strongly validates the effectiveness of PPL's proposed mechanism for extracting and repurposing common knowledge.

#### 4.5. Robustness Analysis under Complex Lighting Conditions

- Figure 9 and Table 3 present results under extreme lighting variations (warm→cold→dark). While performance generally degrades for all methods under such conditions, PPL *with only text as prompt query* shows better robustness (0.80 success rate at L3) compared to PPL *with flow+text* (0.61 at L3) and a Diffusion Transformer baseline (0.75 at L3). This suggests that while optical flow is beneficial for identifying motion primitives, its reliance on pixel intensity changes makes it vulnerable to extreme lighting shifts. The authors propose incorporating depth or 3D scene flow as future work to mitigate this.

#### 4.6. Discussion on PPL vs. LoRA and MoE

- Figure 8 and the text note that while MoE excels in average success rate, its training speed is about twice that of LoRA and PPL due to its gating network and multiple expert networks. LoRA is fastest but has lower overall performance. PPL achieves performance superior to MoE while maintaining comparable training speed to LoRA, balancing efficiency and efficacy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Think Small, Act Big" paper, through its Primitive Prompt Learning (PPL) framework, represents a significant advancement in the field of lifelong robot manipulation. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Enabling True Lifelong Learning for Robotics:** PPL directly addresses the persistent challenge of catastrophic forgetting while simultaneously facilitating positive knowledge transfer. By explicitly learning and leveraging shared "primitive prompts," it moves beyond merely preventing forgetting to actively accelerating the acquisition of new skills by building upon a foundation of reusable knowledge. This capability is crucial for developing general-purpose robots that can adapt and learn continuously in dynamic, real-world environments, much like humans.

- **Novelty of Motion-Aware Prompting:** The introduction of Motion-Aware Prompting (MAP) is a methodological breakthrough. By combining high-level semantic understanding (from language instructions via CLIP) with low-level motion dynamics (from optical flow), MAP enables the robot to identify and transfer common movement patterns even between semantically disparate tasks. This allows for a richer and more robust representation of primitives, expanding the scope of knowledge transfer beyond what purely semantic or task-ID based methods can achieve.

- **Efficiency and Scalability:** PPL leverages parameter-efficient prompt learning, which means that new skills can be acquired by only optimizing a small set of "lifelong prompts" while keeping the majority of the model (and especially the pre-trained primitive prompts) frozen. This design significantly reduces computational overhead and memory requirements compared to experience replay methods or continuously expanding architectures. The comparison with LoRA and MoE highlights PPL's strong balance between performance and training efficiency, making it more practical for large-scale robot deployment.

- **Robustness and Generalization:** The extensive experiments in both large-scale simulations (MimicGen, LIBERO) and real-world Franka Panda tasks demonstrate PPL's superior performance and generalization capabilities. The ability to perform well across diverse tasks, objects, and initial conditions, coupled with its performance metrics (high FWT and BWT), indicates its potential for real-world applications where adaptability is key.

- **Contribution to the Robotic Learning Community:** The construction of a large-scale skill dataset based on MimicGen and LIBERO is itself a valuable contribution, providing a comprehensive benchmark for future research in lifelong robot manipulation. This facilitates standardized evaluation and accelerates progress in the field.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robot Deployment:** Robots equipped with PPL could be deployed in complex industrial or service environments and continually learn new tasks on-the-job without needing extensive re-training or data collection for every new scenario.
- **Enhanced Human-Robot Collaboration:** As robots become more adept at understanding and executing diverse manipulation skills, they can more effectively assist humans in various tasks, from manufacturing to healthcare.
- **Foundation for Generalist AI in Robotics:** PPL pushes the boundaries towards creating more intelligent, autonomous robots that can truly learn and evolve their capabilities over their lifespan, moving closer to the vision of generalist AI agents.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The paper acknowledges a limitation regarding robustness to extreme lighting conditions, where optical flow-based MAP showed degradation. This suggests that while optical flow is powerful, it might need complementary modalities like depth information or 3D scene flow for scenarios with significant visual distortions. Future work will likely explore these avenues to further enhance PPL's robustness and applicability in highly variable environments. Overall, PPL offers a compelling blueprint for how robots can "think small" by decomposing tasks into shared primitives and "act big" by continuously expanding their skill repertoire.
