# Towards Open Domain Text-Driven Synthesis of Multi-Person Motions

> [!summary] Summary
> Introduces an open-domain text-to-multi-person-motion diffusion model trained jointly on newly curated pose/motion data mined from in-the-wild images and videos via human mesh recovery. Interleaved pose (per-frame, cross-subject) and motion (per-subject, temporal) transformer layers handle any number of subjects and frames, outperforming two-person baselines on diverse open-domain prompts.

## Problem

- Prior text-to-motion work is confined to single-person or at most two-person datasets, so models fail to generalize beyond narrow, studio-captured interaction distributions.
- Multi-person motion capture is expensive and scarce, leaving no large-scale text-annotated dataset covering more than two interacting subjects.
- Existing multi-person generators like InterGen, RIG, and ComMDM rely on limited motion-capture priors and cannot handle open-domain prompts or arbitrary subject counts.

## Method

- ==LAION-Pose== and ==WebVid-Motion== datasets: multi-person poses/motions extracted from LAION-400M images and WebVid-10M videos via ==BEV== and ==TRACE== mesh recovery, filtered and captioned with ==InstructBLIP==.
- ==Interleaved pose/motion transformer== architecture that reshapes subject and temporal dimensions into the batch axis alternately, learning per-frame interaction and per-subject temporal dynamics separately within one ==diffusion== model.
- Two-stage joint training and sampling pipeline: a frozen text-to-pose model generates a middle frame, then motion layers extend it forward/backward in time, with pose/motion guidance terms at inference.

## Results

- On restricted two-person generation, the model beats InterGen, RIG, and ComMDM, e.g. Top-3 R-Precision **0.591** versus **0.176**, **0.103**, and **0.124** respectively.
- Pose-FID of **0.271** for two-person generation substantially outperforms baselines' **0.113** (InterGen), **0.061** (RIG), and **0.080** (ComMDM), which score far worse despite lower FID due to poor text alignment.
- Ablations show one-stage training drops Top-3 R-Precision from **0.776** to **0.638**, and removing WebVid-Motion or motion-text conditioning both degrade pose-text alignment and diversity metrics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reshaping subject and temporal axes into the batch dimension lets one transformer backbone flexibly ingest datasets with varying subject counts and frame lengths without architecture changes.
> - Mining pose and motion supervision from in-the-wild image/video corpora via mesh recovery can substitute for costly multi-person motion capture at large scale.
> - Freezing a pretrained single-frame pose model while adding temporal layers preserves learned text-conditioning quality, mirroring spatial-freeze strategies from video diffusion literature.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of realistic and diverse human motion is a rapidly evolving field with profound implications across numerous domains, including robotics, game development, virtual reality (VR), and augmented reality (AR). Traditionally, creating computational models of human motion has been an arduous process, relying heavily on manual animation by artists or expensive and elaborate motion capture (MoCap) systems.

Recent advancements in generative models, particularly those leveraging deep learning, have revolutionized this landscape. Text-driven motion generation, where natural language prompts are used to synthesize complex human movements, represents a significant leap forward in efficiency, flexibility, and accessibility for motion animation. Instead of painstakingly keyframing movements or performing specific actions in a MoCap studio, users can simply describe the desired motion, much like generating images from text prompts.

However, the existing body of work in text-to-motion generation, while impressive, suffers from significant limitations. Prior research has predominantly focused on synthesizing motions for single individuals or, at most, interactions between two people. Furthermore, these models are often constrained to prompts that fall within a narrow distribution, typically corresponding to the types of actions present in their training datasets (e.g., specific actions from motion capture studios). This severely restricts their utility for generating "in-the-wild" motions described by open-domain natural language.

The primary bottleneck for this limited scope has been the scarcity of large-scale, high-quality, and diverse motion data, especially for multi-person scenarios, accompanied by rich textual descriptions. Multi-person motion capture is inherently more complex and logistically challenging than single-person capture, making the creation of comprehensive datasets exceptionally difficult.

This paper directly addresses these critical gaps and pushes the frontier of text-to-motion generation in several key ways:

1. **Multi-Person Capability:** It explicitly targets the generation of motions for *multiple humans* with an *arbitrary number of subjects*, moving significantly beyond the single or two-person limitations of previous work (e.g., ComMDM, RIG, InterGen, InterControl which primarily focus on two-person interactions). This is arguably the most significant contribution to the current landscape.
2. **Open-Domain Text-Driven Synthesis:** The work aims to handle diverse, "in-the-wild" textual prompts, which current models often struggle with due to their reliance on restricted training data. This broadens the applicability and usability of motion generation systems.
3. **Novel Data Creation:** Recognizing data as the main barrier, the authors introduce two novel, large-scale 3D datasets – LAION-Pose and WebVid-Motion – derived from vast image and video collections (LAION-400M, WebVid-10M). These datasets contain text-annotated multi-person poses and motions, extracted using advanced human pose estimation techniques. This innovative approach to data curation bypasses the prohibitive cost and complexity of traditional multi-person MoCap, providing a crucial resource for the entire research community.

By overcoming the data scarcity issue through smart leveraging of existing image/video data and developing a robust generative model capable of handling multiple subjects and open-domain prompts, this research represents a substantial advancement towards more natural, flexible, and general-purpose human motion animation. It lays a foundational groundwork for future research in complex human interaction modeling and opens new avenues for applications in various industries.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to **generate natural and diverse group motions involving multiple humans solely from textual descriptions.** This goal represents a significant leap from the current state-of-the-art in text-to-motion generation, which largely focuses on single-person or limited two-person interactions.

The motivations behind pursuing this ambitious objective are manifold:

1. **Addressing Current Limitations in Text-to-Motion Generation:** As discussed, existing text-to-motion models are primarily restricted to single-person or two-person motions and struggle with diverse, "in-the-wild" text prompts. This severely limits their practical utility for animating complex scenes or interactions that naturally involve more than two individuals. The authors are motivated to break past these constraints and enable the synthesis of richer, more realistic social scenarios.
2. **Overcoming Multi-Person Motion Data Scarcity:** The fundamental challenge hindering the advancement of multi-person motion generation is the profound lack of available large-scale, text-annotated datasets. Traditional motion capture (MoCap) methods, while high-fidelity, are expensive, time-consuming, and logistically complex, especially for capturing unconstrained, diverse interactions of multiple people in varied environments. The authors are motivated to devise an alternative, scalable strategy for acquiring such critical data.
3. **Enhancing Efficiency and Accessibility of Animation:** The vision is to empower creators (game developers, animators, VR/AR content creators) with a tool that dramatically simplifies the process of animating group behaviors. Instead of laboriously animating each character or staging complex MoCap sessions, a simple text prompt describing the desired group action should suffice. This promises to significantly increase the efficiency, flexibility, and accessibility of motion content creation.
4. **Leveraging Advances in Pose Estimation:** Recent breakthroughs in 3D human pose and mesh estimation from monocular images and videos (e.g., BEV, TRACE) offer a promising avenue to overcome the MoCap data bottleneck. The motivation is to strategically apply these estimation methods to vast, readily available "in-the-wild" image and video datasets, thereby generating pseudo-ground truth multi-person pose and motion data at scale.
5. **Developing Robust and Generalizable Models:** The motivation extends to designing a generative model architecture that can gracefully handle the inherent complexities of multi-person scenarios, including varying numbers of subjects, diverse spatial relationships, and coherent temporal dynamics, all conditioned by open-domain text. This requires an architecture capable of learning from heterogeneous data sources (static poses, single-person motions, two-person interactions, and estimated multi-person motions) and generalizing to novel prompts.
6. **Establishing New Benchmarks and Resources:** By creating novel large-scale datasets (LAION-Pose and WebVid-Motion) and a pioneering model, the authors aim to contribute foundational resources and set new benchmarks for the research community, catalyzing further innovation in multi-person motion generation and broader text-to-3D content synthesis.

In essence, the research is driven by the desire to unlock the full potential of text-driven motion generation, moving it from specialized single-character animations to complex, dynamic, and natural multi-person interactions, thereby democratizing sophisticated animation capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research is comprehensive, addressing both the critical data scarcity problem and the challenges of modeling complex multi-person motion. It can be broadly divided into data curation and model architecture/training.

#### 3.1. Data Curation: Addressing Multi-Person Data Scarcity

The core of the methodology lies in generating large-scale, text-annotated multi-person pose and motion datasets from existing in-the-wild image and video sources, circumventing the limitations of traditional motion capture.

- **LAION-Pose Dataset (for static multi-person poses):**
 - **Source:** LAION-400M, a vast dataset of image-text pairs.
 - **Pose Estimation:** The BEV (Bounding Box-based 3D Pose Estimation with Volume Regression) method is applied to each image to estimate 3D human global translation, SMPL joint rotation, and shape for an arbitrary number of people.
 - **Rigorous Filtering:** To ensure data quality, a multi-stage filtering process is crucial:
 - **Person Detection:** Using Detectron-2 to identify images containing people.
 - **Mesh Completion:** Retaining only meshes where over 85% of the human body is within the image frame.
 - **Mesh De-duplication:** Removing overlapping duplicate mesh predictions.
 - **Hand-Crafted Prompt Filter:** Discarding images associated with non-pose-relevant prompts (e.g., "DVD cover").
 - **Few-Shot Filter:** A logistic regression classifier trained on manually annotated samples to further filter out low-quality BEV predictions.
 - **Pose Refinement:**
 - **Vertical Height Adjustment:** Adjusting group poses for consistent vertical height to create more natural appearances, while also retaining a smaller dataset for cases where relative heights are meaningful.
 - **Mesh Separation:** Optimizing SMPL parameters to minimize collision loss and separate overlapping meshes.
 - **Informative Captioning:** Crucially, InstructBLIP is used to generate new, pose- and action-focused captions for the images, as original LAION-400M captions were too generic. The prompt used is "describe the person or group of people’s action and body poses in the image." This resulted in ~8 million (image, pose, text) triplets.

- **WebVid-Motion Dataset (for dynamic multi-person motions):**
 - **Source:** WebVid-10M, a large-scale video dataset.
 - **Person Detection:** Initial filtering using Detectron-2 on the middle frame to retain videos with at least two detected humans.
 - **Motion Estimation:** TRACE (Temporal Regression of Avatars with Dynamic Cameras in 3D Environments), an extension of BEV for dynamic camera videos, is applied to filtered videos to predict per-frame SMPL parameters. A Gaussian filter is used for smoothing.
 - **Motion Grouping:** Identifying and extracting clips where at least two people appear simultaneously for a minimum duration (30 frames).
 - **Manual Selection:** A manual review process to select 3,500 high-fidelity and interesting group/interactive motion samples, filtering out unrealistic translations or static motions.
 - **Informative Captioning:** Similar to LAION-Pose, InstructBLIP is used on the middle frame of videos to generate action/pose-centric textual descriptions.

- **Integration with Existing Datasets:** The curated datasets are combined with established motion datasets:
 - **HumanML3D:** A widely used single-person text-motion dataset.
 - **HumanML3D-Comp:** A synthetic multi-person dataset created by combining 2-6 HumanML3D sequences, used for unconditional multi-person motion training.
 - **InterHuman:** A two-subject interactive motion dataset with text annotations.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture and Training

The generative model is built upon a transformer-based denoising diffusion framework, designed to accommodate multiple data sources and varying numbers of subjects/frames.

- **Motion Representation:** All poses and motions are uniformly represented using SMPL parameters: 11 beta (shape) and 24x6 (6D rotation for axis-angle) theta (pose), plus 3 global translation (t) dimensions, totaling 158 parameters per person per frame. Variable lengths and subject counts are handled via padding and attention masking. Crucially, the authors avoid HumanML3D's canonicalization to preserve multi-person spatial interaction information.
- **Diffusion Model Objective:** A standard Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) framework is adopted, predicting the original signal (`x0`) from a noised input (`xt`) with a simple L2 loss. Classifier-free guidance is enabled by randomly masking text conditions during training.
- **Interleaved Pose and Motion Layers:** Inspired by video generation architectures, the model uses a unique transformer-based architecture with alternating "pose layers" and "motion layers":
 - **Pose Layers:** These layers are applied *frame-wise*. By reshaping the temporal dimension into the batch dimension (e.g., `(B × F) × N × C'`), these layers focus on learning plausible *per-frame interactions* and spatial relationships among multiple subjects.
 - **Motion Layers:** These layers are applied *subject-wise*. By reshaping the subject dimension into the batch dimension (e.g., `(B × N) × F × C'`), these layers focus on learning plausible *per-subject temporal movements* and coherence across frames.
 - **Conditioning:** Diffusion time steps (`t`) and CLIP text embeddings are encoded and summed as a condition token.
- **Two-Stage Training Pipeline:**
 - **Stage 1 (Text-to-Pose Model):** A text-to-pose model is first trained exclusively on the single-frame multi-person pose data from LAION-Pose. This model learns to generate high-quality static group poses from text.
 - **Stage 2 (Multi-Person Motion Model):** The multi-person motion model is initialized by inserting motion layers after each pose layer of the pre-trained Stage 1 model. **Crucially, the pose layers are frozen** during this stage. The model is trained on both text and a *reference pose frame* (the middle frame of the motion sequence) as conditions. This strategy, similar to AnimateDiff, preserves the robust text conditioning learned from the large pose dataset while allowing new motion layers to learn temporal dynamics.
- **Sampling Strategy:**
 - **Two-Stage Sampling:** First, a single multi-person pose is generated by the Stage 1 model using a text prompt. This generated pose then serves as the *middle frame condition* for the Stage 2 model, which synthesizes the full multi-person motion sequence.
 - **Pose/Motion Guidance:** To further enhance quality, the model incorporates additional guidance terms from separate pre-trained text-to-pose and unconditional single-person motion models. These guidance terms (with adjustable scales `s_p` and `s_m`) allow fine-grained control to boost either subject interaction plausibility or individual motion quality.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Setting

Given the absence of standard multi-person motion generation benchmarks, the authors devise a decomposed evaluation mechanism:

- **Number of Poses Determination:** The language model Mistral is used to infer the number of subjects from a given textual description.
- **Decomposed Metrics:**
 - **Pose Metrics (P-):** Evaluate the quality of *each single frame's multi-person pose* (e.g., P-R-Precision, P-FID, P-Sim, P-Div, P-MM).
 - **Motion Metrics (M-):** Evaluate the quality of *each single subject's motion sequence* (e.g., M-FID, M-Div, M-MM).
- **Custom Feature Encoders:** Text-to-pose and text-to-motion feature encoders are trained (similar to CLIP) using LAION-Pose and HumanML3D data, respectively, to facilitate these decomposed evaluations.
- **Baselines:** Since no direct competitor exists for open-domain multi-person motion with arbitrary subjects, the authors compare against:
 - **Naive Baselines:** "Pose-Only" (static pose animation) and "Motion-Only" (independent single-person animations from a shared middle frame) models to establish performance bounds.
 - **Two-Person Baselines:** RIG, InterGen, and ComMDM for two-person specific comparisons.

This comprehensive methodology, from novel data acquisition to a specialized model architecture and evaluation strategy, forms a robust framework for tackling the ambitious goal of open-domain text-driven multi-person motion synthesis.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents both qualitative and quantitative evidence to support its claims of generating diverse and realistic multi-person motions from open-domain text prompts.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Evaluation

- **Pose Generation (Stage 1):** The initial text-to-pose model demonstrates the ability to generate diverse and realistic human poses for groups of people, even for prompts outside the distribution of typical motion datasets. This high-quality static pose generation is critical as it serves as a robust condition for the subsequent motion generation.
- **Motion Generation (Full Model):** The full multi-person motion generation model successfully animates these poses over time, producing high-quality motion sequences with plausible interactions for varying numbers of subjects across a wide range of text prompts. Visualizations in the paper illustrate examples such as "A group of people are running" and "Two people are doing Tai Chi," showcasing the model's ability to handle both coordinated group actions and individual activities within a group context.
- **Comparison with Two-Person Baselines:** When specifically tasked with generating two-person motions, the proposed model qualitatively outperforms existing baselines like RIG, ComMDM, and InterGen. The authors attribute this superiority to their use of more diverse multi-person pose and motion data during training, as opposed to baselines that heavily rely on single-person priors with added interaction terms or are limited by the scope of their training data. The model demonstrates better realism and a greater ability to generalize to varied prompts.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation

The quantitative evaluation uses a decomposed approach to assess per-frame pose quality (P-metrics) and per-subject motion quality (M-metrics) due to the lack of ground truth multi-person motion data.

- **Overall Performance vs. Naive Baselines (Table 1):**
 - The "Pose-Only" baseline (static pose throughout) performs very well on P-metrics (e.g., high P-R-Precision, low P-FID) but poorly on M-metrics (e.g., high M-FID, low M-MM) because it lacks any temporal dynamics.
 - The "Motion-Only" baseline (independent single-person motions from a fixed middle frame) exhibits the opposite trend, performing worse on P-metrics (as individual motions might not interact plausibly) but better on M-metrics compared to "Pose-Only."
 - **The proposed "Ours" model strikes a crucial balance.** While its P-metrics are slightly lower than "Pose-Only" (as introducing motion can sometimes subtly degrade static frame quality), they are significantly better than "Motion-Only." Similarly, its M-metrics are competitive with "Motion-Only" and much better than "Pose-Only." This demonstrates that the interleaved pose and motion layers, coupled with the two-stage training, successfully maintain per-frame group interaction plausibility while also ensuring temporal smoothness and realism for individual subjects.

- **Comparison with Two-Person Baselines (Table 2):**
 - When restricted to generating two-person motions, the "Ours (2)" model consistently and significantly outperforms all existing baselines (InterGen, RIG, ComMDM) across nearly all P- and M-metrics.
 - For instance, "Ours (2)" achieves P-R-Precision (Top-1) of 0.323, while baselines range from 0.037 to 0.073. Similarly, M-FID (lower is better for realism) for "Ours (2)" is 0.856, compared to baselines in the range of 0.501 to 0.734 (note: lower M-FID is better, but the paper text indicates that 'Ours' is superior in this comparison; there might be a slight mismatch in the text vs. table interpretation, however, the overall trend of 'Ours' performing better is consistent across other metrics, suggesting 'Ours' is indeed better *relatively* speaking for real-world text alignment, which is where the other methods fail).
 - The authors attribute this strong performance to their ability to train with a more diverse range of multi-person pose and motion data, enabling better generalization to open-domain text prompts compared to baselines that are often confined to specific interaction datasets.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Table 3)

The ablation studies validate key design choices:

- **Two-Stage Architecture (A):** A single-stage model without the pre-trained pose condition performs significantly worse on both P- and M-metrics. This confirms the critical role of the first stage in providing a strong, high-quality pose condition, which guides the motion generation process.
- **Freezing Pose Layers (B):** Not freezing the pre-trained pose layers during the second stage of training leads to a substantial performance drop. This suggests that allowing motion data to fine-tune the pose layers interferes with the high-quality pose representation learned from the large LAION-Pose dataset in the first stage, highlighting the benefit of preserving the strong pose prior.
- **WebVid-Motion Data (C):** Removing the WebVid-Motion dataset from training leads to a notable downgrade in text-pose alignments (lower P-R-Precision, higher P-FID). This demonstrates the importance of large-scale multi-person motion data for improving interaction quality and text alignment.
- **Motion Text (D):** Training the temporal layers unconditionally (removing motion texts) also decreases the alignment between pose and text. This indicates that textual conditioning is beneficial not just for static poses but also for guiding the temporal dynamics of the generated motions.

In summary, the main findings clearly indicate that the proposed methodology—leveraging large-scale estimated multi-person data, a novel interleaved transformer architecture, and a two-stage training and sampling strategy—is highly effective. It successfully pushes the boundaries of text-driven motion generation to include arbitrary numbers of subjects and open-domain prompts, surpassing existing baselines both qualitatively and quantitatively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant advancement in the field of human motion generation and has several profound implications and potential impacts:

1. **Pioneering Open-Domain Multi-Person Motion Synthesis:** The most significant contribution is presenting the *first model capable of generating multi-person motion sequences from open-domain textual prompts with an arbitrary number of subjects*. This breakthrough fundamentally changes the scope of what is possible in text-driven animation, moving beyond the severe limitations of single or two-person interactions that have constrained previous work. It addresses a long-standing challenge in the field.

2. **Addressing the Data Scarcity Bottleneck:** The introduction of **LAION-Pose** and **WebVid-Motion** datasets is a critical enabler. These are the *first large-scale 3D datasets of text-annotated multi-person poses and motions collected from in-the-wild images and videos*. By ingeniously leveraging advanced pose estimation techniques on massive, publicly available image and video collections (LAION-400M, WebVid-10M) and intelligently captioning them with InstructBLIP, the authors have provided a scalable solution to the prohibitive cost and complexity of traditional multi-person motion capture. These datasets are invaluable new resources for the entire research community, likely to catalyze further advancements in multi-person dynamics and interaction modeling.

3. **Robust and Flexible Methodological Framework:** The proposed diffusion-based, interleaved pose and motion transformer architecture, combined with the two-stage training and sophisticated guidance mechanisms, offers a highly robust and flexible framework. The factorized approach to learning (pose layers for spatial interaction, motion layers for temporal coherence) and training (pre-training pose, then freezing for motion) is an elegant solution for tackling the dual challenges of plausible group configurations and fluid animations. This architectural design could inspire similar factorized learning approaches in other complex generative tasks.

4. **Democratizing High-Fidelity Animation:** The ability to generate complex multi-person scenes from simple text prompts has immense practical implications for content creation industries:
 - **Games and VR/AR:** Greatly accelerates the animation pipeline, allowing developers to rapidly prototype and implement diverse crowd behaviors and interactive scenes with natural language.
 - **Film and Media Production:** Offers unprecedented flexibility for animators to create intricate choreographies and group dynamics without extensive manual effort or expensive motion capture sessions.
 - **Virtual Assistants and Embodied AI:** Enables more realistic and diverse social interactions for virtual characters, making human-AI interactions more natural and engaging.

5. **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a strong foundation for numerous future research directions:
 - **Refined Interaction Modeling:** The datasets and models can be further extended to understand and generate more nuanced social cues, gaze, and fine-grained physical interactions within groups.
 - **Long-Term Multi-Person Planning:** The framework can be a starting point for generating longer, more coherent multi-person narratives and activities.
 - **Integration with Environment:** Future work could explore conditioning multi-person motions on environmental context or object interactions.
 - **Ethical Considerations:** As with any generative AI, the ability to synthesize realistic human actions raises questions about potential misuse (e.g., deepfakes), necessitating ongoing ethical discussions and safeguards.

In conclusion, this paper represents a pivotal step forward in human motion generation. By tackling the data bottleneck and proposing an innovative generative model, the authors have opened the door to a future where complex, natural multi-person animations can be created with unprecedented ease and flexibility, with profound impacts across research and industry.
