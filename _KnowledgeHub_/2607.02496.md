# Controllable Sim Agents with Behavior Latents

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed Controllable Neural Variational Agents (CNeVA), an offline simulation framework that learns explicit, steerable behavioral latents from logged replay data. This framework enables fine-grained, interpretable control over simulated agent behavior while maintaining high realism, demonstrating physically valid steering responses across safety, map, speed, and acceleration.

## Problem

- Existing data-driven traffic simulators often lack fine-grained controllability, making it difficult to adapt generated scenarios for specific user-defined objectives or adversarial evaluations of autonomous vehicles.
- Methods based on VAEs or Diffusion models struggle with deliberate steering of behaviors, with VAEs offering limited control and diffusion models requiring non-trivial guidance tuning that can compromise realism.
- Reinforcement learning approaches, while controllable, typically require computationally expensive online training, necessitate retraining for changing reward specifications, and may sacrifice realism for control.

## Method

- Introduces **Controllable Neural Variational Agents (CNeVA)**, an offline sim-agent framework that learns explicit, steerable ==behavioral latents== from logged replay data using ==variational inference== with a closed-form ==conjugate Gaussian posterior==.
- Employs a conditional generative model trained with ==conditional flow matching (CFM)== and a ==mixed channel-mask curriculum== (enabling ==classifier-free guidance==), using the inferred latents as conditioning variables for fine-grained control.
- Integrates novel components like ==context-residual return labeling== to decorrelate behavioral signals from scenario context and ==soft eligibility gates== to provide smooth supervision for sparse rewards such as safety and map conformity.

## Results

- CNeVA demonstrated competitive realism, achieving a Realism meta-metric of **0.7145** and a minADE of **1.80 m** on the Waymo Open Sim-Agents Challenge leaderboard.
- The framework exhibited a clear hierarchy of controllability, with dense kinematic channels (speed, acceleration) showing strong steering responses (e.g., ΔR_speed = **+8.15**) and sparse semantic channels (safety, map) demonstrating moderate but substantial control, especially with classifier-free guidance.
- Physically valid steering was achieved for safety, speed, and acceleration, demonstrating genuine behavioral changes (e.g., defensive driving) while avoiding common pitfalls like reward hacking and maintaining ground-truth kinematic characteristics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Controllable behavior generation can be effectively formulated as variational inference over a Gaussian behavior latent, which yields a closed-form conjugate posterior and allows for interpretable control.
> - The "tilt-vs-regression" interpretation reveals that kinematic channels (e.g., speed) with high signal-to-noise ratios are inherently easier to steer than sparse, context-dependent channels (e.g., safety).
> - Soft eligibility gates are vital for the successful learning and controllability of sparse reward channels by maintaining continuous gradient signals for agents near critical thresholds, which hard thresholds fail to provide.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of autonomous vehicles (AVs) necessitates extensive validation to ensure safety and reliability. Traditional real-world testing is cost-prohibitive and time-consuming, while conventional traffic simulators often lack the fidelity and scalability required for comprehensive AV evaluation. This has led to a growing interest in data-driven traffic simulation, which offers the potential to generate diverse and realistic traffic scenarios for testing.

Current data-driven simulation methods primarily fall into a few categories, each with limitations:
- **Imitation Learning:** Models like those based on tokenized approaches (e.g., TrajTok, SMART-R1) can reproduce diverse and realistic behaviors. However, they generally lack fine-grained controllability, making it difficult to adapt generated scenarios to specific user-defined objectives or adversarial evaluations.
- **Generative Models (VAEs, Diffusion Models):**
 - **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs)** (e.g., TrafficBots V1.5) can produce behavioral variation through latent-space resampling but do not allow for deliberate steering of behaviors towards specific driving preferences.
 - **Diffusion-based models** (e.g., SceneDiffuser) improve controllability by injecting guidance during the generation process. However, achieving desired stylistic trajectories often involves non-trivial guidance tuning, and excessive guidance can compromise the realism of generated trajectories.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Self-play reinforcement learning approaches encourage desired behaviors through hand-crafted reward functions in closed-loop interaction. While these methods offer controllability, they typically require computationally expensive online training and often necessitate retraining when reward specifications change. Some RL methods may also sacrifice realism for controllability.

A practical, controllable simulator is required to offer flexible behavioral control while preserving the realism of generated trajectories and providing interpretable control axes. Existing studies also exhibit a lack of comparable representations of preference profiles across agent populations, which are essential for compositional-style behaviors and adapting to scenario variations.

This work addresses these limitations by introducing Controllable Neural Variational Agents (CNeVA), a framework that aims to provide interpretable, fine-grained control over simulated agent behavior while maintaining high realism. It seeks to bridge the gap between realism achieved by advanced imitation models and the controllability sought for AV testing by learning explicit, steerable behavioral latents from offline data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a framework for realistic traffic simulation that enables agents to imitate logged behavior and be actively steered along interpretable axes. This capability is motivated by several engineering and research needs:

1. **Isolation of Variables and Edge Case Reproduction:** Engineers require the ability to isolate specific behavioral variables within a simulation and reproduce particular edge cases. This fine-grained control is crucial for systematically testing autonomous systems and understanding their performance boundaries without the risks and costs associated with real-world scenarios.
2. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Generative Models:**
 - **Lack of Fine-grained Control:** Existing imitation learning models struggle to offer precise control over agent behavior, limiting their utility for targeted AV validation.
 - **Ineffective Steering Mechanisms:** VAE-based models lack mechanisms for deliberately steering behavior. Diffusion models offer guidance, but tuning can be challenging, and excessive guidance can lead to unrealistic outputs.
 - **Computational Cost and Realism Trade-offs:** Reinforcement learning methods, while controllable, are often computationally intensive and may compromise behavioral realism.
3. **Preserving Realism with Controllability:** A core motivation is to achieve flexible behavioral control without sacrificing the fidelity and realism of the generated trajectories, a balance often difficult to strike in prior work.
4. **Comparable Preference Profiles:** The authors aim to establish comparable representations of preference profiles across different agent populations and scenarios, which is important for understanding and generalizing driving styles.

To address these motivations, the specific objectives of the CNeVA framework are detailed as follows:

- **C1: Controllable Behavior Generation as Variational Inference:** To cast controllable behavior generation as variational inference over a Gaussian behavior latent ($\lambda_n$) with a closed-form conjugate posterior. This involves developing a reward-tilted preference factor and providing an explanatory framework (tilt-vs-regression) to predict channel identifiability from the return-shrinkage factor.
- **C2: Enhancing Safety Controllability and Realism:** To introduce novel components, including context-residual return labeling, soft eligibility gates, and contrastive conditioning. These extensions aim to improve the effectiveness of safety controllability, ensuring a substantial response above ablations, while simultaneously retaining near-ground-truth kinematic characteristics like speed.
- **C3: Systematic Evaluation of Framework Limits:** To provide a systematic account of the framework's limitations through the analysis of drift-paired channel-steering matrices (CSMs) across various return measures. This objective includes demonstrating that map controllability can be coordinate-specific and emphasizing the necessity of interpreting CSM values alongside physical-plausibility guardrails to avoid confounds such as reward hacking.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CNeVA is designed as an offline sim-agent framework that learns from logged replay data. Its core methodology involves two coupled objectives: variational inference of per-trajectory behavior latents and a conditional generative model that uses these latents for guidance.

#### 3.1. Hierarchical Graphical Models for Simulated Agents

The framework models multi-agent driving scenarios using a Hidden-Markov Process, extending the standard formulation by augmenting each agent with a per-agent behavior profile $\lambda_n \in \mathbb{R}^K$. This profile captures how each agent weights a shared set of reward signals (e.g., safety, map compliance, speed, acceleration). A per-step preference factor $\psi_t(o_{t,n}, \lambda_n) \triangleq \exp(\gamma^{t-1} \lambda_n^\top r(o_{t,n}))$ is defined, where $r(o_{t,n})$ is a K-channel per-step reward decomposition. Belief propagation is applied to simplify the trajectory product into $\psi(\tau_n, \lambda_n) \triangleq \exp(\lambda_n^\top G_n)$, where $G_n$ is the per-channel discounted return. This formulation frames controllability as an inference problem: how to infer the latent preference profile governing an observed agent's behavior.

#### 3.2. Variational Behavior Labeling

To enable conditional generation, the latent $\lambda_n$ for each trajectory $\tau_n$ is inferred using variational inference. Given a Gaussian prior $p(\lambda_n) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_0, \Sigma_0)$ and the trajectory-level factor $\exp(\lambda_n^\top G_n)$, the framework derives an analytic, closed-form conjugate Gaussian posterior $q^\star(\lambda_n) = \mathcal{N}(\mu_0 + \Sigma_0 G_n, \Sigma_0)$. This posterior identifies the most probable behavior profile that explains the observed returns, regularized by the population prior.
- **Tilt-vs-Regression Interpretation:** This formulation provides an interpretation where channels with high signal-to-noise ratios (e.g., dense, trajectory-level penalties like speed) experience strong shrinkage towards the observed return and are predicted to be easier to steer. Conversely, channels with low signal-to-noise ratios (e.g., sparse, context-dependent events like collisions) are more prior-dominated and harder to control through $\lambda$ alone.
- **Per-channel Standardization:** Raw returns often have vastly different scales across channels. To ensure that all four reward channels (safety, map, speed, acceleration) contribute equally to the posterior update and the conditional generator, CNeVA standardizes the per-channel returns $G_n$ using empirical mean ($\mu_G$) and standard deviation ($\sigma_G$) derived from a calibration split. With a standard normal prior ($\mu_0=0, \Sigma_0=I$), this brings per-channel posterior means to an order-unity scale.

#### 3.3. Conditional Behavior Generation

A conditional generator $p(\tau | m, \lambda; \theta)$ is trained using conditional flow matching (CFM). This method involves regressing a neural velocity field $v_\theta(x_s, s, o_{<t,n}, m, \tilde{\lambda}_n)$ onto a rectified-flow target. The behavior latent $\lambda_n$ serves as a conditioning variable, integrated as an extra cross-attention token in the Transformer decoder architecture.
- **Mixed Channel-Mask Curriculum:** To enable flexible classifier-free guidance (CFG) beyond binary on/off conditioning, a mixed channel-mask curriculum is employed during training. This curriculum exposes the model to arbitrary subsets of channels being masked out (null, single-channel, two-channel, and full conditioning), with specific probabilities. The input to the projection layer is a concatenation of the value-zeroed label and a binary mask indicator $\tilde{\lambda}_n = [(1-b_n) \odot \lambda_n; b_n]$. For fully masked agents ($b_n = \mathbf{1}^K$), a learned null embedding $e_\emptyset$ is used, recovering the unconditional path required for CFG.

#### 3.4. Inference and Controllable Generation

During deployment, an operator supplies a per-agent desired latent $\lambda^{\text{op}}_n$. Trajectories are generated by numerically integrating the rectified-flow ODE in reverse time using an Euler sampler. Classifier-free guidance is applied by combining conditional and unconditional velocity fields: $\tilde{v}_w^\theta(x_s, \lambda^{\text{op}}_n) = (1+w) v_\theta(\ldots, \lambda^{\text{op}}_n) - w v_\theta(\ldots, e_\emptyset)$, where $w \ge 0$ is the guidance scale. This allows for population-marginal, user-controlled, or prior-sampled rollouts. A receding-horizon patch scheme is used to re-evaluate conditioning at regular intervals.

#### 3.5. Return Labeling and Eligibility

- **Context-Residual Returns:** To separate driving style from scenario difficulty, per-channel returns $G_{n,k}$ are residualized against a scene-context baseline $\bar{G}_k(m_n)$, yielding $G^{\text{cr}}_{n,k} = G_{n,k} - \bar{G}_k(m_n)$. This formulation isolates the behavioral signal and is the primary return measure for CNeVA.
- **Soft Eligibility Gates:** For sparse channels like safety and map conformity, traditional hard binary eligibility thresholds (e.g., fixed clearance or time-to-collision limits) exclude most agents from supervision and introduce discontinuities. CNeVA replaces these with smooth exponential decay functions applied to per-step pairwise risk. This ensures all valid agents receive labels, while agents far from hazards contribute negligibly, preserving gradient signals for near-threshold agents.
- **Contrastive Conditioning:** Training is further augmented with a contrastive conditioning step where both steered and null forward passes are computed over the same flow-time noise. This encourages the velocity field to learn the difference between steered and unsteered trajectories, improving robustness against closed-loop drift.

#### 3.6. Reward Channels and Setup

The framework uses K=4 reward channels: safety (collision count, proximity, inverse TTC), map (off-road indicator, road-edge distance, context-residual), speed (linear and angular velocity magnitudes), and acceleration (linear and angular acceleration magnitudes). These are defined as negative sums of penalties. Experiments are conducted on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset (WOMD) under the WOSAC protocol.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CNeVA on the Waymo Open Motion Dataset focuses on realism and controllability, alongside ablations concerning eligibility gating and reward hacking.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Realism

- CNeVA achieved a Realism meta-metric of 0.7145 and a minADE (minimum Average Displacement Error) of 1.80 m on the official 2025 Waymo Open Sim-Agents Challenge test server.
- This places CNeVA competitively in the mid-spectrum of benchmark models. The observed realism is attained using inference-time guidance and best-of-128 selection on a frozen model.
- The paper notes that top-ranked leaderboard models are primarily non-controllable closed-loop and tokenized imitation models. CNeVA's performance gap, particularly in collision and off-road components, is attributed to open-loop error accumulation over the 8-second rollout horizon.
- A key finding is that CNeVA achieves this level of realism without requiring closed-loop fine-tuning or autoregressive token-prediction objectives, while simultaneously providing a per-channel, reward-conditioned controllability interface that higher-realism imitation models typically lack.

#### 4.2. Controllability (Per-Channel Response)

- **Channel Steering Matrix (CSM):** Evaluation using drift-paired CSMs (comparing steered trajectories with a null path under identical random seeds) demonstrated CNeVA's ability to steer behavior. The CSM diagonal $\Delta R_k$ measures the per-channel response.
- **Hierarchy of Controllability:** A clear hierarchy of response was observed:
 - **Speed and Acceleration:** These dense kinematic channels exhibited the strongest response, consistent with the tilt-vs-regression interpretation that high signal-to-noise ratio channels are more easily identifiable and steerable. For example, $\Delta R_{\text{speed}}$ was +8.15 and $\Delta R_{\text{accel}}$ was +8.76 with guidance ($w=1.5$).
 - **Safety and Map Conformity:** These sparse semantic channels showed moderate, but substantial, responses. $\Delta R_{\text{safety}}$ was +0.66 and $\Delta R_{\text{map}}$ was +0.61 (with guidance).
- **Impact of Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG):** The latent-only (w=0) setting showed strong steerability for kinematic channels, while the sparse safety and map channels roughly doubled their response under CFG (w=1.5). This indicates that high-SNR channels are identifiable from the latent alone, whereas sparse channels benefit significantly from CFG.
- The unconditional baseline produced near-zero diagonals, confirming no inherent steering bias in the null path.
- Qualitative rollouts (Figure 3) visually confirmed shifts in behavior for safety and speed steering, while map steering appeared more subtle.

#### 4.3. Eligibility Gating and Reward Hacking

- **Reward Hacking:** The study highlights that CSM diagonals must be interpreted in conjunction with physical-plausibility guardrails. An early-stage ablation demonstrated an inflated speed CSM (+51.3) achieved by reward hacking: 75.9% of steered agents stalled, and their average speed was only 61% of the ground-truth. In contrast, CNeVA's main model (with soft eligibility) achieved a physically valid speed response ($\Delta R_{\text{speed}}$ = +8.15) while maintaining 94.7% of ground-truth speed and a 65.1% stall fraction, indicating genuine speed control.
- **Safety Erosion Under Hard Eligibility:** A hard-eligibility ablation, retaining fixed binary thresholds for safety, resulted in a safety CSM of +0.21 $\pm$ 0.22, which was statistically indistinguishable from zero. This occurred because most safe agents received no safety label, diminishing the signal for the generator.
- **Effectiveness of Soft Eligibility:** Replacing hard thresholds with smooth exponential decay (soft eligibility gates) preserved gradient signals for near-threshold agents, leading to a statistically significant safety CSM of +0.66 $\pm$ 0.10.
- **Physical Validity of Safety Steering:** Under safety-channel steering, the main model exhibited only a minor increase in stall fraction (+0.9 percentage points) and retained 97.9% of ground-truth speed. This indicates that the safety CSM reflects genuine defensive driving behaviors (e.g., spacing, yielding) rather than a simple slow-equals-safe confound.

#### 4.4. Map Controllability Across Reward Measures (Limitations)

- The study found that map controllability is highly sensitive to the choice of return measure.
- Using the context-residual return, the map channel showed a positive response ($\Delta R_{\text{map}}$ = +0.61).
- However, when evaluated with a raw physical-offroad return, the response was slightly negative (-0.12).
- With a lane-centerline return (based on lateral offset), the response was effectively zero (-0.002).
- This suggests that while the context-residual measure captures a controllable shift relative to scene-contextual expectations, coordinate-level map control (e.g., precise lane-keeping) remains a structural limitation of the current reward basis and requires richer, more explicit reward decompositions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Controllable Neural Variational Agents (CNeVA), an offline simulation framework that advances the field of data-driven traffic simulation by offering fine-grained, interpretable control over agent behaviors while maintaining realism. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced AV Validation:** CNeVA provides a practical tool for AV engineers to isolate behavioral variables, reproduce specific edge cases, and perform targeted adversarial evaluations. This capability enables more thorough and safer validation of autonomous systems, reducing reliance on costly and risky real-world testing.
2. **Addressing Key Limitations of Existing Methods:** The framework effectively bridges the gap between the realism offered by advanced imitation learning models and the controllable nature of reinforcement learning-based approaches, which often suffer from computational overhead or realism trade-offs. By inferring behavior latents from offline data, CNeVA avoids expensive online training and offers a flexible control interface.
3. **Novel Methodological Contributions:**
 - The casting of controllable behavior generation as variational inference over a Gaussian behavior latent with a closed-form conjugate posterior is a novel approach for learning interpretable behavior profiles.
 - The introduction of a mixed channel-mask curriculum for classifier-free guidance allows for granular control over arbitrary subsets of behavioral axes, which is more sophisticated than binary on/off conditioning.
 - The development of soft eligibility gates significantly improves the learning of sparse, context-dependent rewards like safety and map compliance, preserving gradient signals for critical near-threshold events.
 - Context-residual return labeling successfully decorrelates behavioral signals from scenario-specific difficulties, leading to more informative and controllable latents.
4. **Demonstrated Physically Valid Controllability:** The research rigorously demonstrates that CNeVA can produce physically plausible and monotonic steering responses across interpretable axes such as speed, acceleration, and safety. This includes showing that the framework can avoid common pitfalls like reward hacking (e.g., stalling to maximize speed return) through careful design and evaluation with physical guardrails.
5. **Insights into Reward Design and Evaluation:** The findings underscore the critical importance of reward definition and evaluation metrics. The sensitivity of map controllability to different reward measures and the necessity of interpreting channel steering matrices alongside physical plausibility guardrails provide valuable methodological insights for future research in controllable agent design.

In summary, CNeVA offers a robust and flexible framework for creating simulated traffic agents that are both realistic and steerable along interpretable behavioral dimensions. Its contributions are expected to accelerate the development, testing, and deployment of safer and more reliable autonomous vehicles by providing engineers with precise control over simulated environments.
