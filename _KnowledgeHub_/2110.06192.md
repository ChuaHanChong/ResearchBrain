# Beyond Pick-and-Place: Tackling Robotic Stacking of Diverse Shapes

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepMind researchers developed a vision-based robotic stacking policy capable of handling diverse, complex geometries, demonstrated on a new RGB-Stacking benchmark. The policy, trained through a three-stage simulation-to-real framework, achieved up to 81.6% real-world stacking success for challenging object combinations, significantly outperforming baselines and exhibiting emergent manipulation skills.

## Problem

- Prior robotic manipulation research often focused on simpler tasks like grasping, typically avoiding the complex inter-object contact dynamics inherent in stacking.
- Existing work on robotic stacking was largely confined to objects with simple geometries (e.g., cuboids) or a very limited, known set of irregular objects, often reporting low success rates.
- A notable challenge remained in developing generalizable stacking strategies for a wide array of novel object shapes and effectively handling intricate contact physics using learned vision-based policies.

## Method

- Introduced RGB-Stacking, a novel benchmark featuring 152 diverse, procedurally-generated geometric objects that necessitate strategies beyond simple pick-and-place.
- Implemented a three-stage learning framework: training state-based expert policies in simulation, distilling them into vision-based policies via interactive imitation learning (IIL) with extensive domain randomization, and refining performance using offline ==reinforcement learning== on real-world data.
- Utilized a 4-DoF Cartesian velocity control with a hybrid action space, combined with ResNet vision encoders and Transformer models, to enable robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The vision-based policies achieved a **67.9%** average success rate on five specific Skill Mastery test triplets and **51.9%** on Skill Generalization test triplets in real-world zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.
- Offline policy improvement using real-world data from sim-to-real agents significantly enhanced performance, with CRR-IMP boosting Skill Mastery success to **81.6%** (from **67.9%**) and Skill Generalization to **55.6%** (from **32.6%**).
- Learned policies exhibited diverse emergent stacking skills for non-cuboid objects, including precise grasping, object reorientation through pushing, and balancing, surpassing the capabilities of a ground-truth-aware scripted baseline (which achieved **51.2%** in real-world).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Interactive Imitation Learning (IIL) significantly outperformed standard Behavior Cloning for distilling vision-based policies from state-based experts, demonstrating its importance for robust sim-to-real transfer by mitigating covariate shift.
> - The source and quality of real-world data are critical for offline policy improvement; data collected by autonomously executing sim-to-real agents led to substantial performance boosts, whereas data from scripted agents did not.
> - A hybrid action space with binary gripper actions and the use of Transformer architectures for temporal information were identified as key factors for successful sim-to-real transfer and improved generalization in complex stacking tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen substantial advancements through the application of learning algorithms. However, a significant portion of existing work has concentrated on tasks such as grasping, which typically do not involve complex inter-object contact dynamics. Manipulation tasks that necessitate interactions between diverse objects, such as constructing simple structures, demand control strategies that are qualitatively different from and more complex than simple grasping and placing.

Previous research on vision-based real-robot stacking has largely focused on objects with simpler geometries, predominantly cuboids. While some work has addressed stacking a limited set of known irregular objects, these studies often involved objects with wide support and high friction, requiring comparable pick-and-place strategies. Furthermore, prior efforts often reported low task success rates, indicating the inherent difficulty of real-world stacking, even for less complex objects. The ability to generalize stacking strategies to a wide array of novel object shapes and to handle intricate contact dynamics remains a notable challenge.

This work contributes to the landscape by addressing the problem of robotic stacking with objects of complex and diverse geometries. It aims to develop learned policies that can execute strategies beyond basic pick-and-place, reasoning about factors such as object orientation and stack stability. By introducing a new benchmark with a large variety of procedurally-generated objects and conducting extensive real-world evaluations, this research seeks to push the boundaries of generalizable vision-based robotic manipulation, particularly in tasks involving complex inter-object physics. It builds upon prior work in large-scale deep reinforcement learning and sim-to-real transfer, adapting these methodologies to a more challenging and diverse stacking scenario without relying on human demonstrations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the feasibility and methodology for robotic stacking of diverse objects in real-world environments using learned policies. This objective is pursued by leveraging information solely from RGB cameras and robot proprioception, aiming to develop agents that can exhibit a variety of stacking skills beyond simple pick-and-place.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in the existing research landscape:
1. **Limited Object Diversity in Stacking Tasks:** Prior studies on robotic stacking have predominantly focused on objects with simple geometries, such as cubes, or a very restricted set of irregular, known objects. These objects often possess properties (e.g., wide support, high friction) that simplify the task and do not necessitate complex reasoning about shape, orientation, or stack stability. The authors highlight a gap in addressing stacking scenarios involving a large and varied set of objects that introduce substantial geometric and dynamic challenges.
2. **Complexity of Inter-Object Dynamics:** Many manipulation tasks, including stacking, involve intricate interactions and contact dynamics between objects. Existing general manipulation work, particularly in grasping, typically does not require accounting for these complexities. The authors are motivated to explore whether learning algorithms can effectively acquire policies that manage such nuanced inter-object physics.
3. **Need for Generalizable Skills:** The goal is not merely to stack specific objects but to learn general stacking strategies that can transfer to novel and unseen object combinations. This addresses the broader challenge of developing robust and adaptable robotic agents for diverse real-world scenarios.
4. **Efficiency in Policy Learning and Transfer:** The authors aim to develop an efficient framework for acquiring these complex stacking skills, specifically focusing on methods that reduce the need for extensive real-world data collection and human intervention. This includes leveraging simulation for initial policy learning and employing simulation-to-reality transfer techniques.
5. **Standardized and Reproducible Benchmark:** To facilitate principled research and comparison of methods, there is a motivation to propose and release a standardized benchmark. This benchmark includes a diverse set of objects specifically designed to challenge existing methods and to enable quantifiable evaluation of generalization capabilities.

To address these motivations, the study articulates several key objectives:
- To present and release "RGB-Stacking," a novel benchmark featuring 152 diverse geometric objects, designed to require strategies beyond pick-and-place and offering tens of thousands of possible stacking combinations.
- To demonstrate the possibility of learning a single vision-based policy capable of stacking multiple object combinations in the real world, exhibiting emergent diverse stacking strategies.
- To introduce and evaluate a learning framework that combines reinforcement learning (RL) in simulation with vision-based interactive policy distillation and simulation-to-reality transfer, without requiring human demonstrations.
- To conduct an extensive experimental study to identify crucial factors influencing the learning of general vision-based agents in simulation and their optimal transfer to real robots.
- To investigate whether offline reinforcement learning (RL), utilizing data collected by sim-to-real policies in the real world, can further enhance policy performance. This objective also includes analyzing the impact of data distribution on offline RL effectiveness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research follows a three-stage approach designed to efficiently develop generalizable vision-based robotic stacking policies and transfer them to the real world. This approach decouples skill learning from perception mapping and real-world refinement.

#### 3.1. The RGB-Stacking Benchmark

The core of the research involves a new benchmark, RGB-Stacking, featuring 152 procedurally-generated and 3D-printed rigid geometric objects. These objects are derived from a parametric family by applying deformations to a seed cube along four major axes: Polygon, Trapezoid, Parallelogram, and Rectangle. These deformations, and their pairwise combinations, alter the grasping and stacking affordances, requiring diverse manipulation strategies. Objects are colored Red, Green, and Blue to signal their roles (top, distractor, bottom).
The benchmark defines two tasks:
- **Skill Mastery:** Focuses on mastering stacking for 5 specific, pre-selected object triplets, each designed to present unique challenges (e.g., precise grasp, reorienting a slanted object, balancing, precise alignment). The goal is to learn a single vision-based policy that can reliably stack all five.
- **Skill Generalization:** Aims to assess transferability. Policies are trained on a set of 103 objects from combined deformation axes and then tested on 40 held-out objects from single deformation axes, including the 5 triplets from the Skill Mastery task.

#### 3.2. Robotic Setup and Control

Experiments are conducted using a Sawyer robot arm equipped with a Robotiq 2F-85 parallel gripper, outfitted with custom nylon fingertips. Sensing capabilities include the robot's inherent joint torque and position sensors, a Robotiq FT 300 force-torque sensor at the wrist, and three Basler ace RGB cameras providing full view of the robot's workspace (a basket, or "playground").
The robot is controlled using a 4-degrees-of-freedom (DoF) Cartesian velocity control (3D translational velocity and 1D angular velocity around the vertical axis). A P-controller maintains the gripper's vertical orientation. Joint velocities are computed via a constrained least-squares problem, biasing towards a nominal joint configuration and respecting joint limits and collision avoidance. The control rate is 20 Hz.

#### 3.3. Environment Observations

Agents receive observations at 20 Hz, consisting of robot proprioception (joint angles, velocities, torques, wrist/pinch poses, gripper state, wrist force/torque) and cropped 128x128 RGB images from the front-left and front-right cameras. Observations are stacked over several time steps (except camera images) to address actuation delays. Crucially, learned vision-based policies do not have access to object pose information; this is reserved for reward computation, environment resets, and scripted baselines.

#### 3.4. Task Evaluation and Reward

A sparse, binary reward is defined for successful stacking in both simulation and real-world evaluations. A stack is successful if the top object's centroid is within a 3 cm horizontal radius of the bottom object's centroid and at least 2.5 cm above it, and the gripper is fully open. Evaluations involve randomizing object initial positions (and orientations in sim) and robot TCP pose. Each episode lasts 20 seconds, with early termination on excessive force. Evaluation protocols are standardized across 1000-5000 episodes in simulation and 200 episodes per triplet (1000 total) in the real world.

#### 3.5. Three-Stage Learning Framework

**Stage 1: Training Expert Policies from State Features in Simulation**
- **Objective:** Learn high-performing policies in a fast manner.
- **Algorithm:** Maximum a Posteriori Policy Optimization (MPO), an off-policy actor-critic method.
- **Input:** Full state information, including ground-truth 6-DoF object poses from the simulator.
- **Reward:** A shaped reward function that progressively rewards reaching, grasping, lifting, hovering, stacking, and leaving the objects. This shaped reward is only used in simulation.
- **Output:** State-based expert policies ($\pi_e$). For Skill Mastery, 5 specialized experts are trained; for Skill Generalization, a single generalist expert conditioned on object parameters is trained.

**Stage 2: Interactive Imitation Learning (IIL) of a Vision-Based Policy for Sim-to-Real Transfer**
- **Objective:** Distill the state-based expert(s) into a single vision-based "student" policy ($\pi_{vis}$) that can operate in the real world without object pose information.
- **Method:** DAgger-style interactive imitation learning. The student policy is continuously executed in a heavily domain-randomized simulation environment, collecting trajectories. The student is then trained to imitate the actions of the expert(s) on states encountered during its own execution. This approach addresses covariate shift better than simple Behavior Cloning (BC).
- **Sim-to-Real Transfer:**
 - **Domain Randomization (DR):** Extensive randomization of physical properties (mass, friction, damping, armature) and visual properties (object colors/textures, lighting, camera poses) in simulation. Special care is taken to preserve the hue ranges of RGB objects.
 - **Image Augmentation:** Application of random brightness, hue, saturation, contrast, and translation to image observations, both in simulation and real-world data.
 - **Network Architecture:** A ResNet vision encoder processes camera images, followed by a Transformer model to incorporate temporal information. The action space is hybrid: continuous for Cartesian/angular velocities and Bernoulli for gripper actions.

**Stage 3: One-Step Offline Policy Improvement for Sim-to-Real Policies**
- **Objective:** Enhance the performance of the sim-to-real policy using real-world data.
- **Procedure:**
 1. Deploy the $\pi_{vis}$ policy on real robots to collect a dataset $D_{real}$.
 2. Train a new policy $\pi_{imp}$ offline using a filtered behavior cloning loss (L_IMP).
- **Offline Learning Algorithms:**
 - **BC-IMP:** Behavior Cloning on only successful trajectories ($f(s,a,\tau) = r(s_T)$).
 - **CRR-IMP:** Critic Regularized Regression, using an exponential advantage filter ($f(s,a,\tau) = exp(A_{\pi_{imp}}(o(s),a)/\alpha)$). This requires learning a distributional action-value function alongside the policy from the collected real data.
- **Reward:** A sparse, binary success reward is used for offline training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from extensive evaluations in both simulated and real-world environments, addressing the proposed RGB-Stacking tasks.

#### 4.1. Task Difficulty Assessment

- **Baseline Performance:** Human teleoperators achieved an average simulation success rate of 46.6% across the 5 test triplets. A carefully tuned scripted agent, utilizing ground-truth object positions, achieved 45.0% on the training set and 43.1% on the test triplets in simulation. In the real world, the scripted agent achieved an average of 51.2% across the test triplets, demonstrating the inherent difficulty of the task even with privileged information and hand-engineered strategies.
- **Triplet-Specific Challenges:** The 5 specific triplets in the Skill Mastery task posed varied difficulties. Triplet 1 (precise grasp, rolling bottom object), Triplet 2 (reorienting slanted bottom object), and Triplet 3 (balancing an elongated object on a slanted base) were significantly more challenging for the scripted agent and showed larger performance gaps compared to learned policies. Triplets 4 and 5 (rectangular prisms, cylindrical top object) were comparatively easier.

#### 4.2. Simulation Performance of Learned Agents

- **State-Based Expert Policies:** MPO-trained state-based agents achieved 79.3% success on the Skill Mastery test triplets and 68.8% on the Skill Generalization training objects. These agents learned approximately 10 times faster than vision-based agents trained from scratch.
- **Interactive Imitation Learning (IIL) vs. Behavior Cloning (BC):** For vision-based policies, IIL significantly outperformed standard BC when distilling from the state-based experts.
 - Skill Mastery (test triplets): IIL-s2r achieved 74.2% compared to BC's 52.4%.
 - Skill Generalization (test triplets): IIL-s2r achieved 56.0% compared to BC's 41.2%. This indicates the importance of interactive data collection for addressing covariate shift.
- **Generalization in Simulation:** While state-based agents showed limited generalization from training to test objects (47.8% for Skill Generalization), IIL-s2r vision agents achieved better generalization (56.0% on test triplets), suggesting that visual cues aid in inferring object properties and adapting strategies.

#### 4.3. Real-World Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer

- **High Performance:** The vision-based policies trained with IIL and domain randomization in simulation demonstrated effective zero-shot transfer to the real robot.
 - Skill Mastery: Achieved 67.9% average success on the 5 test triplets.
 - Skill Generalization: Achieved 51.9% average success on the 5 test triplets.
- **Emergent Skills:** Qualitatively, the learned policies exhibited diverse stacking skills necessary for the non-cuboid objects, such as precise grasping, reorienting objects by pushing them, and balancing.
- **Ablation Study on Sim-to-Real Components:**
 - **Binary Gripper Actions:** The choice of using a hybrid action space with binary gripper actions (versus continuous) was identified as the most impactful factor for successful sim-to-real transfer.
 - **Deterministic Policy Execution:** Executing the deterministic mode of the stochastic policy on real robots generally resulted in marginally higher success rates compared to stochastic execution.
 - **Transformer Architecture:** The Transformer model, used to incorporate temporal information, showed a more pronounced positive effect on Skill Generalization performance, both in simulation and real-world, compared to Skill Mastery.
 - **Image Augmentation and Action Delays:** Random image augmentations and random action delays (0-2 timesteps) did not significantly impact overall transfer success but were retained for increased robustness.
 - **Object Parameters (Skill Generalization):** Not explicitly conditioning the vision policy on object parameters, requiring inference from images, resulted in reduced performance.

#### 4.4. Offline Policy Improvement with Real-World Data

- **Impact of Data Source:** The source of real-world data for offline RL was critical.
 - Using data collected by a *scripted agent* for offline RL (BC-IMP and CRR-IMP) did not yield performance improvements over zero-shot sim-to-real; in some cases, performance was lower, potentially due to the "robotic" and limited movement variety in the scripted data.
 - In contrast, using data collected by the *sim-to-real agents* themselves for offline RL led to significant performance boosts.
 - **Skill Mastery:** The sim-to-real policy's 67.9% average success improved to 74.6% with BC-IMP and 81.6% with CRR-IMP (using 85,213 episodes, 58,979 successful).
 - **Skill Generalization:** An earlier, suboptimal sim-to-real agent's 32.6% success improved to 49.0% with BC-IMP and 55.6% with CRR-IMP (using 38,446 episodes from the training set, 14,381 successful).
- **CRR-IMP vs. BC-IMP:** CRR-IMP consistently achieved higher success rates than BC-IMP, demonstrating the effectiveness of critic-regularized approaches for leveraging diverse real-world experience, including unsuccessful trajectories.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing the challenge of stacking objects with diverse and complex geometries, moving beyond traditional pick-and-place paradigms.

#### 5.1. Novel Benchmark for Complex Manipulation

The introduction and release of the RGB-Stacking benchmark represent a significant contribution. It provides a standardized, diverse, and challenging testbed for developing and evaluating robotic manipulation policies that must contend with intricate contact dynamics, object orientation, and stack stability. This benchmark can accelerate research by providing a common platform for comparing methods and fostering the development of more generalizable manipulation skills.

#### 5.2. Demonstrated Capability for Diverse Stacking

The study demonstrates that a single vision-based policy, predominantly trained in simulation, can achieve high real-world stacking performance across a variety of complex objects. The emergence of diverse stacking strategies, such as reorienting objects or precise balancing, indicates that reinforcement learning, when combined with effective sim-to-real transfer, can acquire sophisticated motor skills for challenging manipulation tasks. This capability moves beyond simple object handling and towards more adaptable interaction with unstructured environments.

#### 5.3. Validation of a Robust Learning Framework

The proposed three-stage learning framework (state-based RL in simulation, vision-based interactive distillation with domain randomization, and offline RL for real-world refinement) is validated as an effective strategy for acquiring complex robotic skills without relying on human demonstrations. This framework offers a pathway for developing real-world robot agents by systematically bridging the simulation-to-reality gap and leveraging real-world experience for continuous improvement.

#### 5.4. Insights into Sim-to-Real Transfer and Offline RL

The comprehensive experimental study provides specific insights into factors crucial for successful sim-to-real transfer, such as the utility of hybrid action spaces (e.g., binary gripper actions) and the benefits of a Transformer architecture for handling temporal visual information. Furthermore, the findings highlight that the source and quality of real-world data are critical for the effectiveness of offline reinforcement learning, with data from autonomously collected sim-to-real policies proving more beneficial than data from scripted agents. CRR-IMP is shown to be a robust method for leveraging mixed-quality real-world data for policy improvement.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

Despite the demonstrated success, the observed performance gap between the Skill Mastery and Skill Generalization tasks indicates open challenges in achieving truly generalized manipulation skills. This suggests avenues for future research in areas such as lifelong learning, continuous adaptation at deployment time, and more robust methods for inferring object properties from visual inputs. The release of the benchmark materials, including object designs and simulation environments, promotes reproducibility and encourages further community engagement in these research directions.
