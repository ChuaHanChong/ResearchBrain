# LEGS: Fine-Tuning Teleop-Free VLAs for Humanoid Loco-manipulation in an Embodied Gaussian Splatting World

> [!summary] Summary
> Stanford University researchers developed LEGS, a photorealistic simulator that uses 3D Gaussian Splatting and procedural motion generation to create large-scale, teleop-free training data for humanoid loco-manipulation. Policies fine-tuned exclusively on LEGS data matched or surpassed policies trained on human teleoperation, achieving 2-6/10 success rates on the most complex task where teleoperation-trained policies consistently failed.

## Problem

- Training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for humanoid loco-manipulation traditionally relies on costly and labor-intensive human teleoperation demonstrations.
- Conventional mesh-based simulators suffer from a significant "visual sim-to-real gap," hindering effective transfer of policies to real robots due to visual discrepancies.
- Adapting pre-trained VLA foundation models to specific humanoid platforms for complex tasks requires extensive fine-tuning with platform-specific data, which is difficult to scale.

## Method

- **LEGS** employs a hybrid simulator that decouples ==MuJoCo== for robot physics from ==3D Gaussian Splatting== for photorealistic scene rendering, compositing dynamic foreground meshes with static 3DGS backgrounds.
- A ==procedural motion-primitive generator== synthesizes labeled humanoid loco-manipulation demonstrations at scale, eliminating the need for human teleoperation or per-task supervision.
- A ==two-stage color calibration== pipeline deterministically aligns rendered images with the robot's deployment camera color space, bridging the visual sim-to-real gap.

## Results

- VLA policies fine-tuned on LEGS (200) data consistently performed at or above policies trained on Teleop (50) data, achieving **2-6/10** task success rates on the most difficult loco-manipulation task, where Teleop (50) policies had **0/10** success.
- The photorealistic rendering pipeline of LEGS (3DGS background + color calibration) was critical, with LEGS (200) achieving an average **67%** end task success rate compared to **33%** for the mesh-only SAM3D (200) baseline.
- Re-rendering existing motion data to generate augmented datasets (LEGS-AUG) enabled effective adaptation to new object and scene appearances at significantly lower cost (**0.1** GPU-hours for re-rendering vs. **1.5** operator-hours for new teleoperation data).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Procedural generation within a photorealistic simulation environment can effectively replace human teleoperation for generating high-quality, large-scale humanoid loco-manipulation datasets.
> - The combination of 3D Gaussian Splatting for backgrounds and a precise color calibration pipeline is crucial for achieving high visual fidelity, enabling robust sim-to-real transfer, especially for close-range manipulation.
> - Decoupling motion data from scene appearance allows for efficient re-rendering of existing trajectories under new backgrounds and objects, transforming data adaptation from an operator-bound to a compute-bound process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The development of generalist humanoid robots capable of operating in human environments is a significant objective in robotics. These robots possess a shared morphology with humans, enabling them to utilize existing human-built infrastructure and tools without extensive retrofitting. A key challenge in this domain is the development of robust Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies that allow humanoids to perform complex loco-manipulation tasks based on visual inputs and language instructions.

Traditionally, training such VLA policies for humanoid loco-manipulation has relied heavily on human teleoperation demonstrations. While effective, this approach is characterized by high costs, logistical complexity, and dependence on operator labor and robot uptime. Data collection through teleoperation is also typically bound to specific scenes, requiring new collection for any appearance shift at deployment, leading to escalating costs due to operator and hardware fatigue. An alternative, human-demonstration retargeting, removes the robot from the data collection loop but still necessitates per-scene human capture, which remains expensive to scale.

Simulators offer a potential solution to mitigate human labor and costs. However, conventional mesh-based simulators introduce a significant "visual sim-to-real gap." Images rendered in these environments often diverge visually from real camera footage due to issues such as material and shading mismatches, simplified lighting, and aliasing. These discrepancies can bias visual encoders and hinder the effective transfer of policies trained in simulation to real-world robots. Even advanced mesh-synthetic pipelines built on SAM3D reconstructions can exhibit these limitations.

Recent advancements in vision-language-action foundation models, such as π0.5, ψ0, and GR00T N1.6, have demonstrated impressive zero-shot manipulation capabilities. However, adapting these large pre-trained models to specific humanoid platforms for complex tasks involving both manipulation and locomotion still necessitates extensive fine-tuning with platform-specific demonstrations.

Concurrent research efforts to address teleoperation-free humanoid loco-manipulation include:
- Sim-augmented behavior cloning from a single seed demonstration, which still requires a hand-collected seed per task.
- Distillation of a privileged Reinforcement Learning (RL) teacher into a vision-based student, necessitating hand-engineered rewards.
- Reference-tracking RL from optimization-derived trajectories, which relies on hand-designed trajectories.
Each of these approaches, while removing the operator, still requires some form of per-task human supervision.

In the realm of photorealistic simulation for robot learning, techniques like domain randomization attempt to bridge the sim-to-real gap by perturbing non-photorealistic renderers. More recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has emerged as a method that can minimize rendering error against real photographs, thus closing the visual sim-to-real gap. 3DGS has been applied in various robotic contexts, including fixed-base manipulation, high-throughput RL for locomotion and tabletop tasks, visuomotor drone navigation, and aerial manipulation. However, its application to fine-tuning pre-trained VLA backbones for complex humanoid loco-manipulation tasks, specifically quantifying the impact of the rendering backend on real-robot success, has not been extensively explored prior to this work.

The present work addresses the gap in scalable, low-cost, and photorealistic data generation for humanoid VLA fine-tuning, aiming to overcome both the human labor constraints of teleoperation and the visual fidelity limitations of conventional simulators.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, photorealistic simulator named LEGS (Loco-manipulation via Embodied Gaussian Splatting) capable of generating large-scale, labeled humanoid loco-manipulation training data entirely in simulation, without requiring any human teleoperation demonstrations. This simulator aims to enable the effective fine-tuning of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies that transfer reliably to real humanoid robots.

The specific objectives that underpin this overall aim include:

1. **Develop a Hybrid Simulator:** To create a simulator that integrates the physical fidelity of a robust physics engine (MuJoCo) for robot and object dynamics with the visual fidelity of photorealistic 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for scene backgrounds. This hybrid approach seeks to provide a visually accurate yet physically plausible simulation environment.
2. **Enable Teleop-Free Data Generation:** To design and implement a procedural motion-primitive generator that can synthesize labeled humanoid loco-manipulation demonstrations at scale. This generator is intended to eliminate the need for human teleoperation, seed demonstrations, human video, or per-task rewards, thereby significantly reducing the cost and complexity of data collection.
3. **Bridge the Visual Sim-to-Real Gap:** To implement a deterministic two-stage color calibration pipeline that aligns the rendered 3DGS-composited images with the color space of the robot's deployment camera. This calibration is crucial for ensuring that policies trained on synthetic data perceive visual information consistently with the real robot.
4. **Demonstrate Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To experimentally validate that VLA policies fine-tuned exclusively on data generated by LEGS can achieve task success on a real humanoid robot, matching or exceeding the performance of policies trained on human teleoperation demonstrations.
5. **Quantify the Impact of Photorealistic Rendering:** To empirically demonstrate that the photorealistic 3DGS background, combined with color calibration, is a critical component for effective synthetic data transfer, outperforming mesh-only simulation baselines.
6. **Facilitate Low-Cost Adaptation to New Scenes:** To establish a workflow where recorded motion data can be efficiently re-rendered under new backgrounds and object meshes, allowing for rapid and low-cost augmentation of training data to improve robustness against scene and object variations.
7. **Validate Across Diverse VLAs and Tasks:** To evaluate the LEGS framework across multiple state-of-the-art VLA backbones (ψ0, π0.5, GR00T N1.6) and a range of pick-and-place tasks with varying degrees of whole-body difficulty, demonstrating the general applicability and robustness of the data pipeline.

The motivation for this work stems from the inherent difficulties and resource intensiveness associated with collecting real-robot data for humanoid loco-manipulation. The inability of previous simulator-trained policies to transfer effectively to the real world, primarily due to visual discrepancies, further compounds this challenge. By addressing these limitations, LEGS aims to unlock the potential of humanoid robots to operate seamlessly in human environments by providing a scalable, high-fidelity, and cost-effective data generation solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The LEGS framework is predicated on two fundamental decouplings: the separation of physics from rendering, and the segregation of static background from dynamic foreground elements.

#### 3.1. LEGS Framework Architecture

The system uses a MuJoCo backend to handle dynamics on mesh geometry at 500 Hz, while a distinct rendering frontend generates image observations. The static background is composed of a 3DGS field, which is solely responsible for visual appearance. The dynamic foreground, comprising the robot, manipulable objects, and props, consists of rasterized meshes that interact physically within the MuJoCo environment. These layers are composited per frame using a depth test. This design allows for the motion recorded against the foreground meshes to be independent of the scene's appearance, facilitating the re-rendering of a single motion dataset with various backgrounds and object meshes.

#### 3.2. Visual Frontend

- **Background and Foreground Reconstruction:** Each scene is initially captured with a short handheld video (1-2 minutes) and reconstructed into a 3DGS field using COLMAP for camera pose estimation followed by 3DGS optimization. This process aims to generate photorealistic novel views, thereby minimizing the visual sim-to-real gap. The foreground meshes are sourced from the robot's URDF and SAM3D reconstructions of objects and props, derived from single handheld images. In each frame, the foreground is rasterized and depth-composited with the 3DGS background.
- **Color Calibration:** To address the disparate color spaces of the scanning device's ISP (for 3DGS), SAM3D's regressed albedo (for meshes), and the on-robot deployment camera, a deterministic two-stage color calibration is applied. Auto-exposure and auto-white-balance are disabled on both the capture and deployment cameras to ensure a stationary mapping.
 1. **Stage 1: Mesh-to-Deployment Albedo Match (R):** For each object mesh, a per-channel diagonal scale in linear RGB is applied. This scale matches the rendered mean color of the mesh to the mean color of the corresponding object within its SAM2 mask in the original handheld scan, correcting for SAM3D's tendency to produce muted albedo values.
 2. **Stage 2: iPhone-to-RealSense Color-Correction Matrix (M):** A global 3x3 color-correction matrix is derived by fitting a least-squares solution from a 24-patch ColorChecker. This chart is photographed by both the iPhone (used for 3DGS capture) and the robot's deployment camera (Intel RealSense D435) under controlled illumination. This matrix then maps the composited frames from iPhone space to the RealSense camera's color space.

#### 3.3. Physics Backend and Controller Interface

The physics backend operates in MuJoCo, resolving dynamics for the robot and objects. Collision geometry for objects is decomposed using CoACD. A collision mesh for the static background is also derived from a SAM3D reconstruction and aligned to the 3DGS field. The low-level whole-body controller used is SONIC, an RL-trained policy that manages whole-body coordination. SONIC accepts an 18-dimensional command, which includes a 6-D wrist SE(3) pose and a continuous grip scalar per arm (14-D upper-body), concatenated with a 4-D base command (vx, vy, ωz, h). This identical SONIC binary is utilized in both simulation and on the real robot, ensuring consistency during deployment.

#### 3.4. Procedural Episode Generation

LEGS employs a procedural generator to create labeled episodes without human intervention. This is achieved by composing parameterized motion primitives (e.g., Walk, Pick, Place) whose arguments are scene-level (e.g., target pose, object identity, arm choice) rather than joint-level. This allows the same task plan to generate varied, valid trajectories under randomized initial conditions (e.g., robot position/heading, object pose). Each high-level motion is composed of lower-level primitives, ending with verification checks to ensure success before an episode is saved. The recorded 18-D command stream is decoupled from the rendering inputs, enabling the dataset to be re-rendered with different backgrounds and object meshes at minimal computational cost.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Robot Platform:** Experiments were conducted on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, featuring a 29-DoF body, 7-DoF Dex3 hands, and a head-mounted Intel RealSense D435 camera streaming RGB at 30 Hz.
- **Tasks:** Three pick-and-place tasks of increasing whole-body difficulty were defined: manipulation-only, easy loco-manipulation, and hard loco-manipulation. The common goal was to pick an orange and place it on a white plate.
- **Evaluation Protocol:** For each data condition, VLA backbone, and task, 10 trials were conducted. Initial conditions were perturbed by randomizing object and plate positions (±5 cm), robot heading (±10°), and robot base position (±10 cm for loco-manipulation tasks). Task success rate (TSR) was the primary metric.
- **Data Conditions:**
 - **Teleop (50):** 50 real demonstrations per task, collected by a non-expert operator using SONIC’s VR 3-point teleoperation interface.
 - **SAM3D (200):** A mesh-only synthetic baseline, identical to LEGS (200) except that the 3DGS background was replaced by SAM3D meshes, and color calibration was disabled.
 - **LEGS (200):** The main dataset, generated procedurally with the 3DGS background and color calibration.
 - **LEGS (50):** A 50-episode subsample of LEGS (200) for a budget-matched comparison with Teleop (50).
 - **LEGS-AUG (200) / SAM3D-AUG (200):** Re-rendered datasets used for appearance randomization experiments, covering varied objects and scenes.
- **VLA Backbones:** Three pre-trained VLA backbones (ψ0, π0.5, GR00T N1.6) were fine-tuned from their public checkpoints. The fine-tuning recipe was fixed per backbone across all data conditions, isolating the impact of the data source.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated LEGS across three pick-and-place tasks and three VLA backbones, comparing it against human teleoperation and a mesh-only synthetic baseline.

#### 4.1. Performance Across VLA Backbones

- **LEGS Data Matches or Exceeds Teleoperation:** Policies trained purely on LEGS (200) data consistently performed at or above policies trained on Teleop (50) data across all nine (backbone, task) experiments. The performance gap in favor of LEGS (200) widened with increasing task difficulty. For Task 3 (the most complex loco-manipulation task), Teleop (50) policies consistently failed (0/10 success rate across all backbones), whereas LEGS (200) achieved success rates of 2-6/10. This trend was observed consistently across ψ0, π0.5, and GR00T N1.6 backbones, suggesting the benefits are inherent to the data pipeline rather than a specific VLA model.
- **Not Solely a Budget Artifact:** A budget-matched comparison, LEGS (50) vs. Teleop (50), showed that LEGS (50) matched or outperformed Teleop (50) on every experiment, including Task 3. This indicates that the superior performance of LEGS is not solely due to a larger dataset size. The additional performance gain from LEGS (50) to LEGS (200) was consistent with typical imitation learning scaling, particularly noticeable during the close-range pick and place stages where more demonstrations are beneficial.

#### 4.2. Effect of Photorealistic Rendering

- **Photorealistic Rendering Improves Transfer:** The SAM3D (200) condition, which was identical to LEGS (200) except for the absence of the 3DGS background and color calibration, consistently performed worse than LEGS (200). SAM3D (200) averaged a 33% end task success rate compared to LEGS (200)'s 67%, representing a substantial performance drop. While both methods showed comparable success at the walking stage, SAM3D's performance diverged significantly from LEGS during the pick and place stages across all tasks and backbones. This finding indicates that the combined photorealistic rendering pipeline (3DGS background and color calibration) is crucial for achieving high success rates, especially during close-range manipulation where visual fidelity is critical.

#### 4.3. Low-Labor Adaptation to New Scene Appearance

- **Cost Reduction through Re-rendering:** Generating 50 Task 3 demonstrations cost 1.5 operator-hours for teleoperation. In contrast, LEGS required approximately 0.5 GPU-hours on a single RTX 4090, with no operator involvement, for the initial 50-episode dataset. Critically, for each additional appearance condition, teleoperation incurred a cost exceeding 1.5 operator-hours (due to re-collection), whereas LEGS only required about 0.1 GPU-hours for re-rendering existing motion. This demonstrates a shift from an operator-bound to a compute-bound problem for scene adaptation.
- **Re-rendering Enables Adaptation; Photorealism Improves Accuracy:** Without re-rendering, policies trained on default data (Teleop (50), SAM3D (50), LEGS (50)) largely failed (0-1/10 TSR) under combined object-and-scene appearance shifts. However, policies trained on re-rendered data (LEGS-AUG (200) and SAM3D-AUG (200)) maintained high success rates across these appearance-randomized conditions. LEGS-AUG (200) consistently outperformed SAM3D-AUG (200) in these scenarios. For instance, on the "objects + scene" shift, LEGS-AUG (200) achieved 100%, 80%, and 40% success on Tasks 1-3, compared to SAM3D-AUG (200)'s 60%, 50%, and 20%. This indicates that while re-rendering enables adaptation, the photorealistic rendering of LEGS enhances its accuracy. Teleoperation, being a physically captured dataset, cannot benefit from this re-rendering capability.
- **Augmentation Outperforms Scale for Distribution Shifts:** When facing object-and-prompt shifts, LEGS-AUG (50) (a smaller, augmented dataset) surpassed LEGS (200) (a larger, un-augmented dataset). For the "objects + scene" shift, LEGS-AUG (50) achieved 50%, 40%, and 30% success on Tasks 1-3, while LEGS (200) remained at 0%, 0%, and 10%. This finding suggests that re-rendering into the new object and prompt distribution is more effective than simply increasing the size of a default-only dataset when the policy needs to recognize new objects or respond to new prompts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LEGS framework presents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in addressing the challenges associated with training Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies for complex loco-manipulation tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Overcoming Data Collection Bottlenecks:** LEGS effectively mitigates the high cost, complexity, and labor intensity traditionally associated with collecting humanoid robot demonstrations via teleoperation. By enabling procedural generation of labeled data entirely in simulation, it offers a scalable and cost-effective alternative, which is crucial for the advancement of generalist humanoid robots.
- **Enhanced Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The research demonstrates that combining high-fidelity physics simulation with photorealistic rendering (3DGS backgrounds) and a robust color calibration pipeline can significantly bridge the visual sim-to-real gap. Policies fine-tuned on LEGS-generated synthetic data achieved performance levels comparable to or exceeding those trained on real-world teleoperation, a critical breakthrough for deploying simulation-trained policies on physical robots.
- **Scalability and Efficiency in Data Augmentation:** The decoupling of motion from appearance within LEGS allows for the efficient re-rendering of existing motion datasets under new scene backgrounds and object meshes. This capability drastically reduces the marginal cost of covering new appearance conditions, transforming data adaptation from an operator-bound process to a massively parallelizable, compute-bound task. This scalability is vital for generating the diverse and extensive datasets required for robust robot learning.
- **Facilitating Foundation Model Development:** By providing a reliable and high-fidelity data pipeline, LEGS directly supports the fine-tuning of pre-trained VLA foundation models (e.g., ψ0, π0.5, GR00T N1.6) for specific humanoid platforms and tasks. This accelerates the adaptation and deployment of these powerful models in complex, real-world scenarios.
- **Increased Robustness to Environmental Variations:** The ability to generate augmented datasets with varied object and scene appearances enhances the robustness of trained policies to real-world environmental shifts. The results show that explicit augmentation through re-rendering is more effective than simply increasing the size of a non-augmented dataset when the policy encounters new objects or prompts.
- **Shifting Paradigms in Robot Learning:** This work suggests that photorealistic rendering and procedural motion generation can serve as sufficient substitutes for human teleoperation at the data-collection stage for humanoid loco-manipulation. This paradigm shift could accelerate research and development in humanoid robotics by making the data acquisition process more accessible, efficient, and reproducible.

In conclusion, LEGS represents a substantial step towards realizing general-purpose humanoid robots by providing a robust, scalable, and photorealistic simulation framework that effectively addresses the critical data bottleneck in robot learning. Its contributions have the potential to significantly impact the trajectory of humanoid robot development and deployment in human-centric environments.
