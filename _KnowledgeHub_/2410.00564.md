# Scaling Offline Model-Based RL via Jointly-Optimized World-Action Model Pretraining

> [!summary] Summary
> JOWA, an offline model-based reinforcement learning agent, scales to diverse visual multi-task environments by jointly optimizing a world model and a Q-value critic via a shared transformer backbone. It achieved an IQM human-normalized score of 78.9% on 15 Atari games and demonstrated sample-efficient transfer to new tasks with minimal data.

## Problem

- Scaling trends observed in large-scale models for NLP and multi-modality have not consistently transferred to reinforcement learning, particularly for TD-based offline RL.
- Existing multi-task offline RL methods often depend on extensive expert trajectories and struggle to generalize effectively to diverse, unseen tasks.
- Scaling temporal difference (TD) learning to larger models can lead to instability or performance degradation, hindering the development of generalist RL agents.

## Method

- Introduces **JOWA** (Jointly-Optimized World-Action model), an offline model-based RL agent utilizing a ==shared transformer backbone== for both ==world dynamics modeling== and ==Q-value estimation==.
- Jointly optimizes the world-part (next observation, reward, termination prediction) and action-part (==Q-value estimation== via ==distributional CQL==) modules using a combined loss, leveraging ==world modeling== as a regularizer for ==TD-learning== stability.
- Incorporates an efficient, ==parallelizable planning algorithm== at inference time to mitigate Q-value estimation errors and identify more optimal policies.

## Results

- JOWA-150M achieved an Inter-Quartile Mean (IQM) human-normalized score of **78.9%** on 15 Atari games, outperforming leading offline RL baselines by an average of **71.4%**.
- Performance consistently scaled with increased model capacity, with JOWA demonstrating the steepest scaling curve among evaluated algorithms from **40M** to **150M** parameters.
- Achieved an IQM DQN-normalized score of **64.7%** on 5 novel Atari games with only **5k** fine-tuning transitions, significantly outperforming baselines and models trained from scratch by **69.9%** on average.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint optimization of a world model and Q-value critic with a shared transformer backbone effectively scales TD-based offline RL, with the world modeling loss stabilizing training for larger models.
> - A provably efficient, parallelizable planning algorithm applied during inference can compensate for Q-value estimation errors, leading to improved policy quality and decision-making.
> - Multi-task pre-training in a low-data regime, combined with few-shot fine-tuning, enables strong generalization and sample-efficient adaptation to novel, unseen tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large-scale generalist models has significantly advanced fields such as natural language processing (NLP) and multi-modality, driven by scaling laws that demonstrate performance improvements with increased model size and data. However, similar scaling trends have not been consistently observed in reinforcement learning (RL). Traditional RL research has often favored smaller models tailored to specific tasks or environments, with larger model capacities sometimes leading to instability or performance degradation.

In offline reinforcement learning (RL), which focuses on learning policies from static datasets without online interaction, prior efforts to scale across multiple tasks have largely relied on supervised learning (SL) approaches, such as conditional behavior cloning. These methods frequently necessitate substantial amounts of expert trajectories. For instance, approaches like Multi-Game Decision Transformers (MGDT) and Elastic Decision Transformers (EDT) fall into this category. Other attempts, such as Scaled-QL, scale offline Q-learning by employing a shared representation network but use separate Q-heads for each game, which still requires significant data and gradient steps for adaptation to new tasks due to Q-head resets during fine-tuning. Model-based RL (MBRL) has also been explored for offline settings, typically focusing on single-task environments or continuous control tasks with low-dimensional proprioceptive states, which limits their generalization across diverse task domains.

Concurrently, the concept of world models has evolved into a distinct research area within computer vision and multi-modality, primarily focusing on conditional video generation. Recent advancements, such as SORA, have demonstrated robust generalization capabilities as world simulators through large-scale training on time-series image data. This success suggests a potential pathway for scaling RL by leveraging the representational power of generative models trained on rich visual data.

This research aims to bridge these areas by investigating whether image observation-based world models can effectively scale offline RL across multiple tasks while concurrently enhancing generalization to diverse, unseen tasks. The paper seeks to address the challenge of scaling temporal difference (TD)-based offline RL to simultaneously learn general-purpose representations and decision-making abilities, a critical hurdle that existing methods have not fully overcome. By integrating world model pretraining with TD-learning in a multi-task offline setting, this work attempts to move beyond reliance on expert data and enhance generalization capabilities, positioning itself at the intersection of large-scale generative models and robust offline reinforcement learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a generalist agent in offline reinforcement learning that can acquire high capabilities from large and heterogeneous datasets. This primary goal is motivated by several identified limitations in the current RL landscape.

Specifically, the paper identifies two key challenges with existing approaches to scaling offline RL:
1. **Reliance on Expert Trajectories:** Many prior methods for scaling offline RL heavily depend on access to extensive expert trajectories, which may not always be available in real-world scenarios.
2. **Limited Generalization to Unseen Tasks:** Even when scaling is attempted, existing methods often struggle to generalize effectively to diverse tasks not encountered during initial training. This issue is particularly pronounced in temporal difference (TD) learning, which can exhibit instabilities when scaled to larger models. Previous work on multi-task offline Q-learning, for instance, might learn generalizable representations but still require significant adaptation for new tasks due to task-specific Q-heads. Efforts in model-based RL for continuous control have also faced generalization limitations due to heterogeneous sensory inputs across different task domains.

The motivation for the proposed approach, JOWA, stems from the observed success of large-scale generative models, particularly world models in conditional video generation (e.g., SORA). These models have demonstrated exceptional generalization capabilities when trained on vast amounts of time-series image data, effectively learning to simulate complex visual environments. This inspires a central research question: "Can image observation-based world model scale offline RL across multiple tasks while enhancing generalization to diverse unseen tasks?"

To address this question and overcome the identified limitations, the key objectives for JOWA are:
- **Unlock Scaling Trends:** To demonstrate that increasing model capacity and data can lead to improved performance in offline RL, akin to scaling laws in NLP and vision.
- **Achieve Sample-Efficient Adaptation:** To enable rapid and efficient transfer of learned policies to novel, unseen games using minimal fine-tuning data.
- **Jointly Learn Representations and Decision-Making:** To develop an architecture that leverages a shared transformer backbone to learn both generalizable environmental representations (world model) and effective decision-making skills (Q-value criticism) simultaneously.
- **Stabilize Large-Scale TD-Learning:** To mitigate the instability issues typically associated with scaling TD-learning to larger models by using the world modeling loss as a regularizer.
- **Improve Policy Quality through Planning:** To propose and implement an efficient planning algorithm that compensates for inherent Q-value estimation errors, thereby facilitating the identification of more optimal policies at inference time.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to advance the state of multi-task offline RL, making it more scalable, generalizable, and data-efficient.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, JOWA (Jointly-Optimized World-Action model), is an offline model-based RL agent designed for multi-task learning across visual games. It leverages a transformer architecture for both world dynamics modeling and Q-value estimation.

#### 3.1. World-Action Model Architecture

JOWA utilizes a shared transformer backbone, a variant of GPT-2, to simultaneously learn environmental dynamics and Q-values. The transformer's output embeddings are processed by distinct prediction heads:
- **World Dynamics Heads (World-Part):** These include a next observation token predictor ($p_o$), a reward predictor ($p_r$), and a termination predictor ($p_d$).
- **Q-values Head (Action-Part):** A Q-function head ($h_Q$) learns Q-values through temporal difference (TD) learning based on trajectory representations.

#### 3.1.1. Image Tokenization

High-dimensional image observations are first converted into a sequence of discrete tokens using a VQ-VAE (Vector Quantized Variational AutoEncoder) tokenizer. This VQ-VAE is trained independently using a combination of L1 reconstruction loss, commitment loss, and perceptual loss. Once trained, the VQ-VAE is frozen for subsequent stages. The transformer operates on interleaved observation and action tokens, where the sequence length $L$ is set to 8. For multi-task learning, learnable task embeddings are incorporated for both observation and action tokens, providing task-specific contextual information to the transformer.

#### 3.1.2. Training of World-Part Module

The world-part module models the distributions of the next observation, reward, and termination condition at each timestep $t$. The dynamics predictor ($\hat{z}_k^t \sim p_o$), reward predictor ($\hat{r}_t \sim p_r$), and termination predictor ($\hat{d}_t \sim p_d$) are all optimized as classification problems using cross-entropy loss. Scalar rewards are converted into ternary quantities $\{-1, 0, 1\}$ using a sign function to unify the loss category and facilitate balanced training. The overall loss for the world-part module ($L_{world}$) is the sum of these cross-entropy losses over an L-timestep segment.

#### 3.1.3. Training of Action-Part Module

The action-part module estimates the return distribution for all actions given an observation and historical information. It employs Conservative Q-Learning (CQL) for offline TD-learning, which incorporates a regularizer to prevent overestimation of Q-values for out-of-distribution actions. To enhance training stability and scaling performance, JOWA utilizes distributional TD-error (based on the C51 algorithm) instead of mean-square TD-error. The loss function for the action-part module ($L_{action}$) combines the CQL regularization term with the KL divergence between the target return distribution and the predicted return distribution. The Q-value is the mean of the learned return distribution.

#### 3.1.4. Joint Optimization Objective

The world-action model is jointly optimized using a combined loss function: $L(\theta, \phi) = \beta L_{world}(\theta) + L_{action}(\phi)$, where $\beta$ is a coefficient (set to 0.1). This joint optimization allows gradients from both world modeling and TD losses to propagate to the shared transformer backbone. The $L_{world}$ loss serves as a regularizer, contributing to the stabilization of TD-learning, particularly in large-scale models, a known challenge in previous works.

#### 3.2. Parallelizable Planning at Inference Time

To mitigate Q-value estimation errors and identify optimal policies, JOWA incorporates a planning algorithm during inference. This process is framed as a tree search problem. Given an estimated optimal Q-value ($\hat{Q}^*$), a learned dynamic predictor ($\hat{P}$), and a reward predictor ($\hat{r}$), the objective is to find the initial action that maximizes the ground-truth optimal Q-value.
- **Search Objective:** The planning algorithm maximizes a sum of predicted rewards (income-to-date) over a horizon H and the discounted maximum estimated Q-value at the end of the horizon (income-to-go). The policy space for search is constrained to actions with the top-K highest Q-values ($\Pi_{\hat{Q}^*}$).
- **Implementation:** Beam search is used for efficiency. At each timestep, only K states with the highest total estimated income are retained. The algorithm involves $K^2(H-1) + K$ calls to the world model, but due to parallelization across K beams, its runtime is proportional to H times the forward propagation of the world model.
- **Theoretical Guarantee:** The paper provides a theorem demonstrating an upper error bound for the search-based optimal Q-function, indicating that it can be tighter than the estimated optimal Q-function under certain conditions, thereby provably improving Q-value estimates.

#### 3.3. Training Pipeline

The multi-task offline RL pretraining proceeds in two stages:
1. **Stage 1:** Training of the VQ-VAE tokenizer on image observations, followed by training of the world-part module using $L_{world}$ for $M_1$ steps (250k steps).
2. **Stage 2:** The VQ-VAE is frozen. The world-action model is jointly optimized using the combined loss $L(\theta, \phi)$ for $M_2$ steps (1.5M steps).

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Dataset:** A subset of 20 Atari games from the DQN-Replay dataset is used, including 15 for pretraining and 5 for out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization. The experiments use a "low-data regime" with 10% uniformly subsampled data (10M transitions per game).
- **Model Variants:** JOWA-40M, JOWA-70M, and JOWA-150M are trained.
- **Pretraining:** 1.75M gradient steps.
- **Fine-tuning:** 5k transitions (approximately 4 expert trajectories) per game for 50k gradient steps.
- **Evaluation:** Human-normalized scores (HNS) and inter-quartile mean (IQM) for pretrained games; DQN-normalized scores (DNS) for fine-tuning. Planning is enabled during evaluation (horizon H=2, beam width K adjusted per game).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to assess JOWA's performance in a low-data multi-game regime, its scaling capabilities with model capacity, and its ability to transfer to novel games efficiently.

#### 4.1. Performance on Multi-Games in Low-Data Regime

JOWA demonstrated performance on a set of 15 Atari games using a 10% subsampled dataset (10M transitions per game).
- JOWA-150M achieved an Inter-Quartile Mean (IQM) human-normalized score (HNS) of 78.9%.
- This performance surpassed several state-of-the-art large-scale offline RL baselines, including MGDT-200M (49.8%), EDT-200M (50.2%), Scaled-QL-80M (42.0%), FICC-85M (43.3%), and MTBC-120M (32.9%) by an average of 71.4%.
- JOWA-70M (47.6% IQM HNS) also outperformed Scaled-QL-80M and FICC-85M.
- These results indicate JOWA's sample efficiency in learning from heterogeneous offline data.

#### 4.2. Scaling with Model Size

The study investigated the relationship between JOWA's model capacity and performance.
- JOWA's performance reliably increased as the model size grew from 40M to 150M parameters.
- A comparison with MTBC and MGDT showed that JOWA exhibited the steepest scaling curve among the evaluated algorithms.
- This scaling trend was attributed to the architecture's ability to stabilize TD-learning in large models, partly via the auxiliary world modeling loss ($L_{world}$) acting as a regularizer.

#### 4.3. Sample-Efficient Transfer to New Games

JOWA's generalization capability was assessed by fine-tuning pre-trained agents on 5 held-out Atari games using a minimal amount of data (5k transitions, equivalent to approximately 4 expert trajectories) per game.
- The fine-tuned JOWA-150M achieved an IQM DQN-normalized score (DNS) of 64.7% across the 5 novel games.
- This significantly outperformed baselines by an average of 69.9%.
- The scaling trend observed in pre-training also held for novel games after fine-tuning, with larger JOWA variants demonstrating better transfer performance.
- JOWA-150M significantly outperformed a model trained from scratch on the held-out games (JOWA-150M (scratch)), which achieved a mean DNS of 19.6%. These results highlight JOWA's capacity for rapid and sample-efficient transfer.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments were conducted to evaluate the impact of key design choices.
- **Effects of Planning at Decision Time:** The inclusion of the proposed planning algorithm at inference time consistently improved performance across all JOWA variants. JOWA-150M experienced a notable +28.1% increase in IQM HNS. Even without planning, JOWA maintained scaling trends but at a reduced rate.
- **Comparison with MCTS:** The proposed parallelizable planning algorithm demonstrated superior efficiency and performance compared to a MuZero-style MCTS implementation, being 10 times faster and exceeding MCTS by 71.0% in Mean HNS on certain games. It also exhibited less sensitivity to hyperparameters.
- **Different Training Losses:**
 - Removing the CQL regularization term from $L_{action}$ or entirely omitting $L_{world}$ from the joint objective led to significant performance degradation. This indicates the necessity of both conservative constraints and the world model regularization.
 - Disabling gradient propagation from $L_{action}$ to the transformer backbone (separate optimization) or replacing distributional TD-loss with mean square error (MSE) resulted in failure to train effective multi-game agents. This underscores the importance of joint optimization and distributional TD-learning.
- **Effects of Synthetic Data in Pretraining:** Surprisingly, incorporating synthetic data during pretraining yielded negative gains. This was hypothesized to be due to an accumulation of inference errors and potential over-conservatism from the COMBO loss used.
- **Ensemble of Q-value heads:** Using an ensemble of Q-heads did not result in significant improvements over a single Q-head.
- **Task Embedding:** The inclusion of learnable task embeddings improved mean and median HNS by 21.9% and 35.7%, respectively.
- **Fine-tuning with Non-Expert Data:** Fine-tuning performance was observed to correlate directly with the quality of the fine-tuning data, with expert-level data yielding the best results, followed by suboptimal and highly-suboptimal data.
- **Discussion of Emergent Behaviors:** Observed performance spikes on certain games (e.g., Zaxxon) were attributed to non-linear reward functions rather than genuine emergent capabilities, aligning with prior research suggesting metrics can influence perceived emergence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by demonstrating a methodology for effectively scaling offline model-based RL agents to diverse, visually complex multi-task environments.

**Key areas of significance include:**
- **Advancement in Scalable Offline RL:** JOWA offers a novel approach to scaling TD-based offline RL. By jointly optimizing a world model and a Q-value critic via a shared transformer backbone, it addresses the traditional instability of large models in TD-learning, a significant hurdle that has limited the scalability of RL compared to other AI domains.
- **Development of Generalist Agents:** The work makes progress towards building generalist RL agents capable of learning a wide range of skills from heterogeneous, static datasets. This capability reduces the need for task-specific architectures or extensive online interaction for each new task.
- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency:** The method achieves high performance in a low-data pretraining regime and demonstrates significant sample efficiency for adaptation to novel tasks with minimal fine-tuning data (few-shot transfer). This has practical implications for real-world applications where data collection or expert trajectories are limited.
- **Robustness through Joint Optimization:** The strategy of using world modeling loss as a regularizer for TD-learning provides a mechanism for stabilizing training, thereby enabling the effective use of larger, more expressive models.
- **Improved Decision-Making with Planning:** The introduction of a provably efficient and parallelizable planning algorithm at inference time allows the agent to actively compensate for Q-value estimation errors, leading to the identification of more optimal policies. This enhances the reliability and performance of the learned agent, especially in complex environments.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The ablation studies offer insights into the importance of specific design choices, such as joint optimization and distributional TD-loss. The unexpected negative results with synthetic data in multi-task pretraining highlight an open research problem, suggesting avenues for developing more effective methods for utilizing synthetic experience in this context. The observations on "emergent" behaviors and their correlation with non-linear reward structures also contribute to a deeper understanding of model capabilities.

The potential impact of this research extends to various domains requiring intelligent agents that can learn and adapt from observational data, such as robotics, autonomous driving, and game AI. By pushing the boundaries of scalable and generalizable offline RL, JOWA represents a step towards creating more capable and autonomous AI systems that can operate effectively in complex, dynamic environments with limited interactive data.
