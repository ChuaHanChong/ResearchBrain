# THE COLOSSEUM: A Benchmark for Evaluating Generalization for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> THE COLOSSEUM introduces a simulation benchmark for evaluating robotic manipulation policies against 14 environmental and physical perturbations across 20 tasks. It quantifies that state-of-the-art models experience significant performance degradation under these conditions, with 3D models showing improved robustness, and validates a strong correlation between simulated and real-world generalization.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation policies, despite high performance in controlled settings, struggle to generalize to novel environmental conditions (e.g., varying lighting, object appearance, or physical properties).
- The "sim-to-real gap" and lack of robustness to domain shifts impede the deployment of robots in unpredictable real-world scenarios.
- Existing generalization benchmarks are limited in scope, often lacking a systematic and unified framework to stress-test policies against a wide range of environmental perturbations.

## Method

- Developed THE COLOSSEUM, a simulation benchmark extending RLBench, featuring 20 diverse manipulation tasks in CoppeliaSim.
- Introduced 14 distinct perturbation factors across four categories (Manipulation Object, Receiver Object, Background, Physical), including novel physical property perturbations (friction, mass).
- Established a structured evaluation protocol to quantify performance degradation and provided open-sourced 3D-printable models for real-world validation experiments using a Franka Panda robot.

## Results

- All evaluated baseline models exhibited substantial drops in success rates, with individual perturbations leading to a 30-50% degradation and combined perturbations causing ≥75% degradation.
- A strong correlation (average R² = 0.614, up to R² = 0.94 for some factors) was found between performance degradation observed in simulation and that in real-world experiments.
- VoxPoser, a zero-shot model, showed minimal performance degradation (3.21%) on tasks it could accomplish, suggesting inherent robustness when leveraging large pre-trained world models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current state-of-the-art robotic manipulation models suffer severe performance degradation (30-50% for individual factors, ≥75% for combined) when exposed to environmental perturbations.
> - Factors like distractor objects, manipulation object color, and light color are most detrimental to model performance, while object size changes have a comparatively lesser impact.
> - 3D-based models generally demonstrate superior overall task performance and greater robustness to environmental perturbations, particularly camera pose changes, compared to 2D-based models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has made significant strides, particularly with advancements in deep reinforcement learning (RL) and behavior cloning (BC). However, a persistent challenge remains: **generalization**. While models can achieve high performance in environments identical or very similar to their training setups, their success rates drastically drop when faced with novel environmental conditions. This "sim-to-real gap" and the broader issue of robustness to domain shifts impede the deployment of robots in unpredictable real-world scenarios.

This paper directly addresses this critical gap by providing a **systematic and comprehensive benchmark for evaluating generalization capabilities**. Prior works have explored aspects of generalization through data augmentation, pre-training on large datasets, and incorporating 3D priors. While these techniques show promise, existing evaluation benchmarks are often limited in scope, either focusing on task generalization (adapting to new tasks) or specific, curated environmental changes. They rarely provide a unified, large-scale framework to stress-test policies against a *wide range of systematic environmental perturbations*.

The authors critically compare THE COLOSSEUM to existing benchmarks like FactorWorld and KitchenShift, highlighting its superior scale and diversity:
- **Scale**: THE COLOSSEUM features 14 perturbation factors across 20 tasks, surpassing FactorWorld's 11 factors/19 tasks and KitchenShift's 7 factors/3 tasks.
- **Novelty**: It is the first benchmark to incorporate *physical property perturbations* (e.g., object friction and mass), which are crucial for real-world robotic interaction but often overlooked in visual-centric generalization studies.
- **Scope**: Unlike FactorWorld, which focuses only on 2D visual-motor policies, THE COLOSSEUM supports evaluation of both 2D and 3D models, reflecting the diverse landscape of contemporary robot learning architectures.
- **Reproducibility**: Crucially, THE COLOSSEUM emphasizes real-world reproducibility by open-sourcing 3D-printable object models for its real-world mirror experiments, a feature akin to GROOT but integrated into a broader generalization benchmark.

By offering a structured way to quantify robustness against systematic variations, THE COLOSSEUM positions itself as a foundational tool. It aims to serve a role similar to ImageNet in computer vision—providing a standardized, challenging platform that can accelerate the development of truly generalizable and robust robotic manipulation systems, ultimately bridging the gap between laboratory success and widespread real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind THE COLOSSEUM is to address the critical need for robots to operate effectively and reliably in diverse, unstructured real-world environments. The authors argue that current robotic systems, despite impressive performance in controlled settings, inherently lack the ability to adapt to changes in ambient conditions, object appearances, or physical properties. The example of a robot needing to operate a stove regardless of knob size, kitchen backdrop, counter texture, or lighting conditions succinctly captures this core challenge.

The key objectives of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Systematic Evaluation of Generalization**: To introduce a novel simulation benchmark that enables a systematic and comprehensive evaluation of robot policies against a wide array of environmental perturbations. This moves beyond ad-hoc testing to a structured methodology.
2. **Quantify Generalization Degradation**: To use this benchmark to quantitatively assess the performance degradation of state-of-the-art robotic manipulation models when exposed to various environmental changes.
3. **Identify Impactful Factors**: To pinpoint which specific perturbation factors (e.g., object color, lighting, distractors) have the most significant negative impact on model success rates, providing crucial insights for future model design.
4. **Establish Ecological Validity**: To verify that the insights gained from simulation generalize to real-world scenarios by conducting parallel physical experiments and demonstrating a strong correlation between simulated and real-world performance degradation.
5. **Foster Community Research**: To open-source all necessary code, simulation assets, and 3D-printable models to ensure reproducibility and encourage the broader research community to develop and evaluate new methods using THE COLOSSEUM.
6. **Drive Future Development**: To establish a public challenge and leaderboard that incentivizes the creation of more robust and generalizable robotic manipulation models, ultimately aiming to identify fundamental modeling decisions that consistently improve generalization.

In essence, the authors aim to shift the paradigm of robot learning evaluation from focusing solely on average task success in clean environments to robust performance under diverse, challenging, and systematically varied conditions, thereby accelerating the path towards ubiquitous robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

THE COLOSSEUM is meticulously designed as a comprehensive simulation benchmark, extending the existing **RLBench framework**, known for its variety of realistically useful tasks and scripted demonstration generation. The underlying simulation engine is **CoppeliaSim**, controlled via the **PyRep** API.

**Task Selection**: The benchmark includes 20 diverse manipulation tasks carefully curated from RLBench's suite of 100 tasks. These tasks span a range of complexities, from simple actions requiring fewer than 100 steps (e.g., "open drawer") to intricate challenges exceeding 1000 steps (e.g., "empty dishwasher"), ensuring a broad test of primitive actions (pick, place, open, close, turn, slide). Tasks are categorized into three tiers of difficulty based on their "task horizon" (number of waypoints), acknowledging the compounding error challenge in behavior cloning (BC).

**Perturbation Factors**: The core innovation lies in introducing 14 distinct axes of environmental perturbations, grouped into four categories:
- **Manipulation Object (MO) Perturbations**: Affect objects directly handled by the robot (MO_Color, MO_Texture, MO_Size).
- **Receiver Object (RO) Perturbations**: Affect task-relevant but not directly manipulated objects (RO_Color, RO_Texture, RO_Size).
- **Background Perturbations**: Alter non-task-relevant scene characteristics (Light_Color, Table_Color, Table_Texture, Distractor objects, Background_Texture, Camera_Pose).
- **Physical Perturbations**: Modify physical properties of objects (Object_Friction, Object_Mass) – a novel inclusion for such benchmarks.

**Implementation of Perturbations**:
- Perturbations are configured via YAML files, allowing for single or combinatorial application.
- **Visual changes** (color, texture) are achieved by randomly sampling from curated sets of 20 colors and 213 textures.
- **Size changes** (MO_Size, RO_Size) involve scaling objects within task-dependent continuous ranges, with waypoints automatically re-scaled.
- **Distractor objects** are sampled from the YCB Object Dataset and strategically placed within predefined boundaries.
- **Lighting conditions** are varied by adjusting the RGB values of three directional lights.
- **Camera pose** is perturbed by altering the position and orientation (Euler angles) of three cameras (front, left/right shoulder).
- **Physical properties** (friction, mass) are adjusted by sampling from specified ranges, requiring the gripper to adapt.

**Real-World Extension**: To ensure ecological validity, the authors created a physical counterpart for 4 of the 20 simulation tasks using a **Franka Panda robot arm** and a **Kinect-2 RGB-D camera**. Critically, all task objects are **3D-printed with open-sourced models**, allowing exact replication of varying sizes, colors, and textures from simulation. Real-world perturbations mirror simulation counterparts: alternative filament colors/textures, different table mats/wallpapers, varied camera positions, dynamic spotlight colors, and additional tabletop objects as distractors.

**Evaluation Protocol (THE COLOSSEUM Challenge)**: The benchmark proposes a structured challenge:
1. **Standardized Training Data**: Participants generate 100 demonstrations per task without COLOSSEUM perturbations (though with standard RLBench variations).
2. **Model Training**: Train BC models on this dataset.
3. **Perturbed Evaluation**: Evaluate models over 25 episodes for each of the 14 perturbation factors, plus "No Perturbation" and "All Perturbations" scenarios, leading to 235 unique test sets.
4. **Leaderboard Ranking**: Models are ranked based on the *percentage change in success rate* under perturbations compared to the "No Perturbation" baseline, emphasizing generalization robustness.

**Baseline Models**: Five state-of-the-art robotic manipulation models were evaluated:
- **Zero-Shot**: **VoxPoser**, an LLM-guided model that composes 3D value maps, evaluated without training.
- **2D Learning**: **R3M-MLP** and **MVP-MLP**, which use frozen, task-agnostic pretrained visual encoders (ResNet-50 and ViT-Base, respectively) with an MLP action prediction head, operating on RGB inputs.
- **3D Learning**: **PerAct** and **RVT**, both transformer-based models that process RGBD inputs into 3D representations (voxel grids for PerAct, re-rendered views/point clouds for RVT) to predict discretized keypoint actions (translation, rotation, gripper state).

All models were trained with 100 demonstrations per task, using CLIP for language encoding, and evaluated on task-averaged success rates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted with THE COLOSSEUM revealed critical insights into the generalization capabilities of current state-of-the-art robotic manipulation policies:

1. **Significant Performance Degradation**: All evaluated baseline models exhibited substantial drops in success rates under perturbed conditions. Individual perturbation factors led to a **30-50% degradation** in success rate. When multiple perturbations were applied simultaneously ("All Perturbations"), the degradation was even more severe, reaching **≥75%**. This underscores the severe limitations of current models in handling environmental variations.

2. **Most Impactful Perturbation Factors**:
 - The factors that most significantly reduced model performance were **Distractor objects**, **Manipulation Object Color (MO_Color)**, and **Light Color**. These changes often caused policies to either misidentify the target object, struggle with visual recognition, or fail to infer correct poses due to altered appearance.
 - Conversely, **Object Size (MO_Size, RO_Size)** perturbations had a comparatively lesser impact on performance, suggesting that models might be more robust to scaling changes or that the underlying task geometry is less sensitive to them within the tested ranges.

3. **2D vs. 3D Learning Models**:
 - **3D-based models (PerAct, RVT)** generally demonstrated **superior overall task performance and greater robustness to environmental perturbations** compared to 2D-based models (R3M-MLP, MVP-MLP). This suggests that incorporating 3D scene understanding is beneficial for generalization.
 - Notably, 3D models were **more robust to Camera_Pose changes** because they preprocess RGBD views into canonical 3D representations (voxel grids or re-rendered views), making them less sensitive to input view variations.
 - However, 3D models without real-world pretraining (like PerAct) were still significantly affected by **Distractors and color-related perturbations**, indicating that even 3D reasoning benefits from diverse visual training or explicit handling of clutter.
 - **MVP-MLP**, which benefits from large-scale *real-world* visual pretraining, showed better resilience to **Distractors** compared to R3M-MLP and the 3D models without similar pretraining, highlighting the value of pre-training on diverse, cluttered real-world data.

4. **Zero-Shot Generalization (VoxPoser)**: While its overall success rate was low across tasks, for the specific tasks where VoxPoser succeeded, it demonstrated remarkable consistency. Its performance degradation from "No Perturbation" to "All Perturbations" was only 3.21% for a successful task, suggesting that leveraging large pre-trained world models and language instructions can provide inherent robustness to environmental and object perturbations *when the underlying task is well within its capabilities*.

5. **Limited Gains from Training on Perturbed Data**: An ablation study showed that training PerAct directly on data with "All Perturbations" enabled resulted in only a 21.1% increase in success rate compared to a model trained without perturbations but tested with them. This indicates that merely exposing models to perturbed scenes during training, while helpful, does not fully solve the generalization problem, and compounding perturbations can inherently increase task difficulty even with ground truth data.

6. **Strong Real-World Alignment**: A cornerstone finding is the **strong correlation (average R² = 0.614)** between the performance degradation observed in simulation and that in real-world experiments across 4 mirrored tasks. For 7 out of 14 perturbation factors (e.g., Table_Color, Distractor, Light_Color), the R² value ranged from 0.74 to 0.94, with Table_Color being the most significant. This crucial validation confirms the ecological validity of THE COLOSSEUM, demonstrating that evaluations in simulation reliably predict real-world generalization behavior. Real-world experiments reaffirmed MO_Color and Light_Color as highly detrimental to performance, while MO_Size had minimal impact. The authors also showed a strong correlation (R² = 0.75 to 0.83) between benchmark-generated compounded perturbations and realistic real-world scenes (e.g., workbench, dining table).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

THE COLOSSEUM represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, with far-reaching implications for future research and real-world robot deployment.

1. **Standardized, Comprehensive Benchmark**: By introducing the most comprehensive and diverse benchmark for evaluating generalization, THE COLOSSEUM fills a critical gap in robotic learning. It provides a much-needed standardized platform for researchers to systematically quantify and compare the robustness of their policies against a wide range of environmental shifts, moving beyond qualitative assessments or limited evaluations.

2. **Accelerating Generalization Research**: The establishment of "THE COLOSSEUM Challenge" and its associated leaderboard is a powerful mechanism to galvanize the research community. By creating a competitive yet collaborative environment, it will undoubtedly accelerate the development of innovative techniques and model architectures that prioritize generalization as a core performance metric.

3. **Guiding Model Design**: The empirical findings offer crucial insights into the weaknesses and strengths of current state-of-the-art models. Identifying specific factors like object color, lighting, and distractors as major failure modes provides actionable guidance for future model design, encouraging researchers to focus on developing representations and learning strategies that are inherently robust to these pervasive real-world variations. The observed benefits of 3D-based models and real-world pre-training for handling certain perturbations also point towards promising architectural directions.

4. **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap**: The strong correlation between simulation and real-world results is perhaps the most impactful validation of THE COLOSSEUM. This finding suggests that researchers can reliably use the simulation environment to predict and improve real-world generalization, drastically reducing the cost, time, and safety concerns associated with real-robot experimentation. This capability democratizes robotics research, making advanced generalization studies more accessible.

5. **Enhanced Reproducibility**: The commitment to open-sourcing the code, simulation assets, and particularly the 3D-printable object models, is invaluable. High reproducibility is fundamental for scientific progress, allowing other researchers to replicate experiments, build upon existing work, and validate findings consistently across different labs.

6. **Towards Ubiquitous Robotics**: Ultimately, THE COLOSSEUM lays foundational groundwork for enabling the promise of truly ubiquitous robotics. By pushing the boundaries of generalization, it helps to identify and overcome the practical barriers that currently limit robots to controlled factory settings or specific, unchanging environments. A robot that can robustly operate despite varying lighting, cluttered scenes, or different object appearances is a robot that can transition from laboratories to homes, hospitals, and dynamic industrial environments, impacting a multitude of applications. The ongoing maintenance and expansion plans for the benchmark indicate a long-term vision for its role in shaping the future of robotic intelligence.
