# Physics-Informed Neural Networks with Unscented Kalman Filter for Sensorless Joint Torque Estimation in Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia developed a framework combining Physics-Informed Neural Networks with an Unscented Kalman Filter for sensorless joint torque estimation in humanoid robots. This approach enables compliant whole-body torque control without direct torque sensors, achieving superior torque tracking with an RMSE of 1.08 Nm and enhanced stability under disturbances compared to traditional model-based methods.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots often rely on expensive, bulky, and fragile joint torque sensors for compliant interaction, limiting widespread adoption.
- Sensorless torque control methods struggle with unmodeled dynamics, especially complex friction and gearbox elasticity, leading to inaccurate torque estimation.
- Traditional model-based approaches, like RNEA, are highly susceptible to inaccuracies from unmodeled effects, compromising performance in dynamic, real-world scenarios.

## Method

- An integrated framework combines a **Physics-Informed** ==Neural Network (PINN)== for advanced friction modeling with an Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) for robust joint torque estimation.
- The PINN is enhanced with motor and joint velocity buffers as inputs to accurately capture static friction effects and is trained using a hybrid data-driven and physics-based loss function.
- The UKF is specifically adapted for floating-base systems and incorporates the PINN-predicted friction torques as a direct measurement input, rather than an unmodeled disturbance.

## Results

- The UKF-PINN configuration achieved superior torque tracking performance, with an RMSE of **1.08** Nm and MAE of **0.70** Nm, outperforming other configurations.
- UKF-PINN maintained robust Center-of-Mass tracking and stability even under multiple random external disturbances, showcasing enhanced adaptability compared to RNEA-based methods.
- Torque control with UKF-PINN exhibited significantly lower required joint torques (improving energy efficiency) and successfully maintained balance during sudden support loss, unlike rigid position control. The framework generalized successfully across two different ergoCub robots without re-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Accurately modeling complex, non-linear friction via PINNs, using rich input features (velocity buffers) and a hybrid loss, is critical for precise torque estimation.
> - Integrating PINN-predicted friction as a direct measurement into a floating-base UKF significantly enhances the filter's ability to estimate true joint torques and compensates for unmodeled dynamics.
> - Compliant torque control, enabled by accurate sensorless estimation, allows robots to maintain stability and adapt to sudden disturbances, unlike rigid position control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The ongoing evolution of humanoid robotics necessitates robots that can operate safely, adaptively, and precisely in complex, human-centric environments. A key enabler for these capabilities is **compliant interaction**, which is often facilitated through **joint torque control**. However, direct measurement of joint torques typically relies on expensive, bulky, and fragile joint torque sensors, making their widespread adoption impractical for many humanoid robot designs, particularly those with high-ratio harmonic drives and electric motors. This limitation has led many sensorless humanoid robots to rely on rigid position control strategies, which, while effective in controlled settings, struggle with unpredicted interactions and dynamic real-world scenarios.

The broader research landscape in sensorless torque control primarily focuses on estimating joint torques from available sensor data like motor currents, joint positions, and velocities.
- **Model-based approaches**, such as the **Recursive Newton-Euler Algorithm (RNEA)**, calculate torques based on rigid-body dynamics. However, RNEA is highly susceptible to inaccuracies caused by unmodeled dynamics, including complex friction, gearbox elasticity, sensor noise, and unmeasured external contacts. These unmodeled effects can lead to significant torque estimation errors and compromise robot performance.
- **Motor current-based methods** offer fast response times but often assume rigid gear transmission, overlooking crucial elastic deformations and backlash prevalent in high-ratio gears.
- **Sensorless variable impedance control** dynamically adjusts joint stiffness and damping but still heavily depends on precise robot dynamics models, which are challenging to obtain for complex systems with unmodeled phenomena.

A significant challenge across all sensorless torque control frameworks is the accurate characterization and compensation of **friction dynamics**. While simplistic friction models (e.g., Coulomb and viscous) offer limited accuracy, more advanced models (e.g., LuGre, Stribeck) can capture intricate friction effects but often require complex identification procedures. Recent trends involve integrating physics-based modeling with data-driven techniques to estimate friction, leveraging physical insights to enhance data-driven models.

**Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs)** have emerged as a powerful tool in various scientific and engineering domains, including robotics. They enable the embedding of physical laws directly into neural network architectures, allowing models to learn from data while adhering to fundamental physical principles. While PINNs have seen applications in dynamic modeling, parameter identification, and hybrid modeling for robots, their application for *real-time compensation of complex friction dynamics in floating-base humanoid robots* has remained largely unexplored. Existing PINN studies in robotics often focus on offline modeling or static parameter identification, rather than dynamic, real-time compensation in highly complex, underactuated systems.

This paper directly addresses these limitations by offering a novel, integrated framework that synergistically combines an advanced **PINN-based friction model** with a robust **Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF)** for joint torque estimation. By leveraging the PINN’s ability to learn complex, non-linear friction from observed data and the UKF’s strength in robust state estimation in nonlinear systems, the authors aim to overcome the inaccuracies inherent in traditional sensorless methods, particularly concerning friction and unmodeled disturbances in floating-base systems. This work sets a new benchmark for sensorless torque control, paving the way for more compliant, robust, and energy-efficient humanoid robots in dynamic, unpredictable environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel, real-time framework for whole-body torque control of humanoid robots that do not possess direct joint torque sensors, specifically designed for systems with electric motors and high-ratio harmonic drives. The core motivation stems from the inherent limitations of current sensorless torque control methods, which often struggle with unmodeled dynamics, particularly complex friction, leading to compromised accuracy, stability, and adaptability in dynamic, real-world scenarios.

The authors identify several specific advancements as key objectives:

1. **Enhanced PINN-based Friction Modeling:** To significantly improve the accuracy of friction estimation by providing the PINN with more informative inputs, specifically motor and joint velocity buffers, to effectively capture static friction effects (i.e., when the motor is actuated but the joint remains stationary). This also includes extending the model to cover all leg joints of a humanoid robot.
2. **Extended UKF for Floating-Base Systems with Friction Integration:** To reformulate their previous UKF approach from fixed-base to floating-base systems, allowing for real-time adaptation to dynamic conditions. A critical innovation here is the introduction of PINN-predicted friction torques as *direct measurement inputs* within the UKF, rather than treating them as unmodeled disturbances or state variables, thereby enhancing estimation accuracy and robustness.
3. **Synergistic Real-Time Control Architecture:** To integrate these advanced estimation techniques (PINN and UKF) within a unified real-time control architecture, enabling effective and compliant whole-body torque control.
4. **Demonstrate Scalability and Practicality:** To show that the proposed framework can achieve consistent performance across similar robotic platforms with varying friction characteristics without requiring re-identification or extensive re-tuning, thus proving its practical applicability and generalizability.

Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to bridge critical gaps in sensorless torque control, enabling humanoid robots to achieve compliant, robust, and energy-efficient operation for tasks demanding safe adaptability and precision in unpredictable surroundings, moving beyond rigid control strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework integrates two key estimation techniques: Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) for advanced friction modeling and an Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) for robust joint torque estimation. These are combined within a multi-layer real-time torque control architecture.

#### 3.1. A. PINN Architecture for Friction Modeling

The PINN component is tasked with predicting the nonlinear static and dynamic friction torque (τ_F). Building upon previous work, this iteration introduces several key improvements:
- **Input Enhancement:** Unlike prior approaches that relied on noisy joint velocity estimates and position differences, this PINN utilizes **motor and joint velocity buffers** as inputs. This allows the network to accurately capture static friction, a critical phenomenon where the motor might be actuated but the joint remains stationary due to stiction.
- **Smoother Velocity Estimates:** A custom **Kalman Filter (KF)** is employed to provide smoother and more accurate velocity estimates from encoder data. The KF's dynamics model minimizes jerk and acceleration while aligning estimated positions with measured data, significantly improving the precision of the PINN's inputs.
- **Hybrid Loss Function:** The PINN is trained using a hybrid loss function (L), combining both data-driven (L_data) and physics-based (L_physics) components:
 - `L = (1 - λ) * L_data + λ * L_physics`
 - `L_data` represents the squared difference between the PINN's predicted friction torque (τ_F,pred) and the true friction torque (τ_F,true) obtained during training.
 - `L_physics` represents the squared difference between τ_F,pred and a physics-derived friction torque (τ_F,physics) obtained from a **Stribeck-Coulomb-Viscous (SCV) friction model**. The hyperparameter `λ` balances the influence of the data and physics components.
- **Architecture and Optimization:** The PINN features two hidden layers with ReLU activation functions, dropout regularization to prevent overfitting, and a linear output layer. Hyperparameter optimization is performed using a Random Search algorithm via the Weights and Biases platform, ensuring an efficient exploration of the parameter space.

#### 3.2. B. UKF for Joint Torque Estimation

The Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF) is central to the framework, providing real-time estimates of joint torques. The UKF is chosen for its superior ability to handle nonlinear system dynamics and its robustness to sensor noise and uncertainties compared to linear filters. Key innovations in this UKF formulation include:
- **Floating-Base System Adaptation:** The UKF is specifically adapted for **floating-base systems**, a non-trivial modification from previous fixed-base implementations. This involves incorporating accelerometer and gyroscope measurements from the robot's Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) to accurately estimate the robot's base state (linear acceleration and angular velocity).
- **PINN-based Friction as Direct Measurement:** A crucial departure from previous approaches is the integration of PINN-predicted friction estimates (τ_F) as a **direct measurement input** to the UKF. Instead of treating friction as an unmodeled disturbance or an additional state to be estimated, the UKF leverages the PINN's explicit predictions during its measurement update step. This allows the filter to accurately account for and compensate for friction effects.
- **Extended State and Measurement Vectors:** The state vector (x_k) now includes estimated joint velocities (ṡ), motor torques (τ_m), friction torques (τ_F), external wrenches from force/torque (FT) sensors (f_FT), unknown external wrenches (f_ext), base accelerations (α_acc), and base angular velocities (ω_gyro). The measurement vector (y_k) includes observed joint velocities, motor currents, PINN-predicted friction torques, FT sensor readings, and IMU linear accelerations and angular velocities.
- **Refined Process Model:** The UKF's process model is derived from the robot's floating-base rigid-body dynamics (Equation 1), specifically focusing on joint acceleration dynamics. It incorporates the estimated motor torques, friction torques, Coriolis/centrifugal/gravitational effects, and known/unknown external wrenches. The dynamics of motor torques, friction torques, external wrenches, and IMU measurements are generally assumed constant over short time intervals (Equation 8). The base acceleration and angular velocity measurements are explicitly used to define the base state in the system model.
- **KF Gain Optimization:** The Kalman Filter gains are optimized offline using a Genetic Algorithm (GA) with PyGAD, utilizing a 6-minute training trajectory. This optimization process tunes the filter for optimal performance.

#### 3.3. C. Control Architecture

The overall control architecture is multi-layered (Figure 2):
- **High-Level Controller:** A momentum-based controller operates at 100 Hz, computing desired joint torques (τ_d) to regulate the robot's centroidal momentum.
- **Low-Level Joint Torque Controller:** This PI controller operates at 1 kHz, taking the desired torques from the high-level controller, torque feedback from either RNEA or the proposed UKF-based estimator, and friction compensation from the PINN-based estimator.
- **Motor Control:** The desired motor currents (i_d) are derived from the motor and gearbox dynamics, assuming negligible motor shaft acceleration, and are regulated at 20 kHz.

The methodology explicitly contrasts this torque control approach with a traditional position control scheme (Figure 3) to highlight the advantages of compliance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation was conducted on the ergoCub humanoid robot during a dynamic balancing task. Six control configurations were evaluated, systematically comparing the effects of feedforward control, RNEA-based feedback, UKF-based feedback, and PINN-based friction compensation.

#### 4.1. A. Center-of-Mass and Torque Tracking

- **Torque Tracking Performance:** The **UKF-PINN** configuration consistently demonstrated superior torque tracking performance. It achieved the lowest RMSE (1.08 Nm) and MAE (0.70 Nm) among all configurations (Table II), significantly outperforming RNEA-PINN (RMSE: 7.03 Nm, MAE: 4.12 Nm) and configurations without friction compensation.
- **CoM Tracking under Disturbances:** While RNEA-PINN achieved slightly lower CoM errors in nominal conditions, it showed significant CoM divergence after repeated external contacts (Figure 4a), attributed to RNEA's inability to detect unmeasured interactions. In contrast, **UKF-PINN** maintained robust CoM tracking (Figure 4b) and stability even under multiple random external disturbances. The UKF's sensor fusion capabilities allowed it to effectively detect changes in joint torques caused by external contacts, leading to enhanced system responsiveness and adaptability in dynamic environments (Figure 5b).

#### 4.2. B. Stability and Compliance

- **Disturbance Rejection:** **UKF-PINN** proved to be significantly more stable under strong and varied external disturbances compared to RNEA-PINN. RNEA-PINN exhibited instability and often required early termination of experiments due to uncontrolled leg rotations, highlighting its deterministic nature's struggle with unmodeled dynamics. UKF-PINN's robustness stems from its probabilistic estimation and sensor fusion, enabling dynamic adaptation and recovery.
- **Compliance vs. Rigidity:** A crucial comparison was made between the proposed torque control approach (UKF-PINN) and traditional position control in a dynamic balancing task involving the abrupt removal of an object from under the robot's foot.
 - Under **position control**, the robot exhibited a rigid response, failing to adapt to the environmental change and immediately losing balance. Furthermore, position control required significantly higher joint torques (Figure 8a), leading to increased energy consumption and greater mechanical stress on motors.
 - Under **torque control (UKF-PINN)**, the robot successfully adapted to the sudden loss of support, maintaining balance through compliant behavior (Figure 8b). The required torques were significantly lower, demonstrating improved energy efficiency and reduced mechanical stress. This highlights the framework's ability to provide the necessary compliance for safe and effective operation in unpredictable environments.

#### 4.3. C. Energy Efficiency

The **UKF-PINN** configuration achieved the lowest average torque magnitudes among all tested configurations (Figure 7), indicating smoother and more controlled torque profiles. This translates directly to improved energy efficiency and reduced wear on the robot's mechanical components compared to other configurations, especially feedforward approaches that exhibit higher torque efforts.

#### 4.4. D. Scalability and Reproducibility

The framework's generalizability was demonstrated by successfully replicating the balancing task on **two different ergoCub robots** with varying mechanical wear and frictional properties, without any re-tuning. This remarkable result underscores the robustness and portability of the UKF-PINN approach, suggesting its potential for broad deployment across similar robotic platforms without extensive re-identification efforts.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, particularly in enabling robust and compliant behavior in challenging, unstructured environments. The synergistic integration of Physics-Informed Neural Networks for detailed friction modeling and an Unscented Kalman Filter for robust, floating-base torque estimation addresses long-standing challenges in sensorless torque control.

The key contributions and their potential impact are:

1. **Enabling Compliant Interaction without Torque Sensors:** By providing accurate real-time joint torque estimates, the framework allows humanoid robots to adopt compliant control strategies essential for safe human-robot interaction, manipulation of unknown objects, and robust locomotion over uneven terrain. This is achieved without the cost, complexity, and fragility associated with direct joint torque sensors, making advanced torque control more accessible for a wider range of robotic platforms.
2. **Enhanced Robustness to Unmodeled Dynamics:** The framework's ability to accurately model and compensate for complex nonlinear friction, combined with the UKF's robustness to noise and unmeasured external disturbances, significantly improves the reliability and stability of humanoid robots in dynamic settings. This directly addresses a critical limitation of traditional model-based approaches like RNEA.
3. **Improved Energy Efficiency and Mechanical Longevity:** The demonstrated reduction in required joint torques leads to more energy-efficient operation and reduces mechanical stress on motors and gearboxes, potentially extending the lifespan of robotic hardware.
4. **Demonstrated Scalability and Practicality:** The successful application of the framework across two physically distinct ergoCub robots without re-identification highlights its practical utility and generalizability. This reduces the burden of system identification and calibration for each robot instance, facilitating faster deployment and wider adoption.
5. **Paving the Way for Advanced Humanoid Locomotion:** The ability to precisely estimate and control joint torques in a floating-base system is a foundational requirement for complex locomotion tasks like dynamic walking, running, and navigating complex terrains. This work lays crucial groundwork for developing more advanced and stable walking pipelines for humanoid robots, as explicitly mentioned in the authors' future work.

While the computational cost of the UKF is noted as a potential limitation, the presented results establish this method as a scalable, practical, and highly effective solution. Future work will focus on optimizing computational efficiency and validating the framework in more complex scenarios, including full locomotion tasks and application to other humanoid platforms. Overall, this research represents a crucial step towards deploying highly capable, adaptable, and safe humanoid robots into real-world applications.
