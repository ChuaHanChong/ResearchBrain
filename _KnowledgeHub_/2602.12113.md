# Stop Unnecessary Reflection: Training LRMs for Efficient Reasoning with Adaptive Reflection and Length Coordinated Penalty

> [!summary] Summary
> ARLCP, an Adaptive Reflection and Length Coordinated Penalty framework, optimizes Large Reasoning Models by dynamically penalizing excessive reflection and overall response length. The approach achieved a 53.1% reduction in response length and a 5.8% accuracy improvement for a 1.5B LRM, and a 35.0% length reduction with a 2.7% accuracy improvement for a 7B LRM on mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) generate extensive, verbose reasoning chains, leading to high token consumption, significant computational overhead, and increased latency.
- Existing training-free inference optimizations provide limited efficiency gains, while training-stage length penalties often compromise reasoning quality and answer accuracy.
- LRMs frequently exhibit 'over-reflection,' including repetitive self-questioning and unproductive hesitations, which increases with problem complexity and does not consistently improve accuracy.

## Method

- **ARLCP** is a reinforcement learning framework that dynamically estimates ==problem complexity== based on the model's reflection token count.
- It applies an adaptive reflection penalty, dynamically adjusted by estimated complexity, to curb unnecessary reflective steps and a coordinated length penalty for overall conciseness.
- A composite reward function, combining accuracy with these penalties, guides Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) using the REINFORCE Leave-One-Out (RLOO) estimator.

## Results

- ARLCP reduced average response length by **53.1%** and improved accuracy by **5.8%** on mathematical reasoning benchmarks for DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-**1.5B**.
- For DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-**7B**, the method achieved a **35.0%** reduction in response length alongside a **2.7%** accuracy improvement.
- The framework demonstrated robust generalizability, reducing length by **40%** and improving accuracy by **0.7%** on the MMLU benchmark, and consistently enhancing performance across different model series like Qwen3 and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLama.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LRMs often generate redundant or circular 'over-reflection' that escalates with problem complexity and frequently correlates with incorrect solutions rather than improved accuracy.
> - The count of reflection tokens within an LRM's output can serve as a dynamic indicator of its perceived problem difficulty, allowing for adaptive intervention.
> - A nuanced, dynamic penalty that distinguishes between necessary and unnecessary reflection is crucial, as a one-size-fits-all length penalty risks suppressing essential reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities in complex reasoning tasks, often by employing test-time scaling through extensive Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and self-reflective mechanisms. Models such as OpenAI's o1, QwQ, and DeepSeek-R1 exemplify this trend, utilizing explicit `<think>` and `</think>` tags to structure their multi-step reasoning processes. While these long reasoning chains can enhance performance, they introduce significant efficiency challenges, including high token consumption, substantial computational overhead, and increased latency. These limitations restrict the practical deployment of LRMs in resource-constrained or real-time applications.

Existing research efforts to address this efficiency-accuracy trade-off primarily fall into two categories. The first involves training-free inference-stage optimizations, such as Early Exit mechanisms and Model Switching techniques. These methods aim to optimize the generation process by stopping early or pruning responses without altering the model's core inference capabilities. However, their efficiency gains can be limited, particularly for models that inherently produce redundant reasoning or for highly complex tasks. The second direction focuses on training LRMs under length-penalty guidance, often through Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or Reinforcement Learning (RL). While these approaches offer greater control over reasoning length, they frequently encounter a compromise in reasoning quality, potentially by suppressing necessary reflection or discarding valuable parts of the thinking process, thereby negatively impacting answer accuracy.

This work positions itself within the latter category, specifically utilizing reinforcement learning. It addresses a recognized gap in existing methods: the inability to dynamically adapt response length to a problem’s intrinsic complexity or to effectively mitigate specific "over-reflection" tendencies of LRMs without sacrificing accuracy. The paper observes that LRMs often engage in repetitive self-questioning, unproductive hesitations, and circular reasoning, particularly as problem complexity increases. This "over-reflection" leads to inefficiency and, critically, does not consistently improve accuracy, especially in smaller models. By proposing a dynamic, coordinated penalty mechanism, this research aims to provide a more nuanced solution to the efficiency problem, allowing LRMs to generate more concise and effective reasoning paths while preserving or enhancing solution accuracy.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a reinforcement learning framework that dynamically balances reasoning efficiency and solution accuracy in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) by specifically addressing the phenomenon of "over-reflection." The motivation for this work stems from detailed observations and analyses of LRM behavior during complex reasoning tasks.

**Motivation based on observations:**

1. **Redundant Reasoning Steps:** The authors observe that LRMs, particularly smaller ones, frequently generate redundant reasoning steps. These include repetitive self-questioning loops (e.g., "wait"), unproductive hesitations (e.g., "hmm"), and circular reflections that do not advance task resolution. Such behaviors are indicative of inefficient exploration of the solution space.

2. **Correlation between Over-reflection and Problem Complexity:** An in-depth analysis revealed that as problem complexity increases, the phenomenon of over-reflection becomes more pronounced. Quantitative studies on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and 7B models demonstrated a positive correlation between the number of reflection tokens and dataset complexity. This suggests that LRMs expend more tokens on reflective processes when faced with challenging problems.

3. **Over-reflection Leading to Inefficiency and Incorrectness:** Further investigation into the relationship between reflection tokens and model performance on AIME 2024-2025 datasets showed that correct responses exhibited significantly shorter average token lengths and fewer reflection tokens compared to incorrect ones. More critically, an increasing number of reflection tokens correlated with a decline in model accuracy. This indicates that excessive or unproductive reflection traps models in inefficient and often incorrect solution pathways, consuming substantial inference time and computational resources without yielding correct answers.

**Key Insights derived from these observations:**

- **Reflection as a Complexity Indicator:** The number of reflective tokens generated by an LRM can serve as a dynamic indicator of perceived problem difficulty. This insight suggests that models can adapt their reasoning strategy based on this self-generated signal.
- **Risks of Over-reflection:** Excessive reflection is identified as a detrimental behavior that leads to inefficient exploration and often results in incorrect solutions. It represents a key area for optimization.
- **Need for Balanced Intervention:** The research posits that a one-size-fits-all penalty for reasoning length is insufficient. Low-complexity tasks might only require general length penalties, while complex tasks demand specific strategies to curtail unnecessary reflections without suppressing essential reasoning steps.

**Specific Objectives of ARLCP:**

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives of the Adaptive Reflection and Length Coordinated Penalty (ARLCP) framework are:

1. To dynamically reduce unnecessary reflection behaviors in LRMs during reasoning processes.
2. To maintain or improve solution accuracy while significantly reducing token consumption and improving overall reasoning efficiency.
3. To introduce an adaptive reflection penalty that dynamically curtails unnecessary reflective steps while preserving essential reasoning required for problem-solving.
4. To complement the reflection penalty with a length penalty that is calibrated to the estimated complexity of the problem, ensuring overall conciseness.
5. To develop a reinforcement learning framework capable of coordinating these penalties to encourage the generation of more concise and effective reasoning paths.

Ultimately, the goal is to enable LRMs to achieve a superior efficiency-accuracy trade-off, making them more suitable for practical deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Adaptive Reflection and Length Coordinated Penalty (ARLCP) is a reinforcement learning framework designed to optimize LRM efficiency and accuracy by adaptively managing reflection and response length. The methodology is structured around estimating problem complexity, applying dynamic penalties, and optimizing the model through a customized reward function within an RL framework.

#### 3.1. Preliminary Concepts

The method operates on an LRM, `M`, which takes an input prompt `x` (problem context and `<think>` token) and generates a response `y`. The response `y` consists of a thinking phase `[y_1..., y_l]`, an explicit `</think>` token, and a final solution segment `[y_{l+2}..., y_m]`. The generation is auto-regressive, terminating with an End-Of-Sequence (EOS) token.

#### 3.2. Adaptive Reflection and Length Coordinated Penalty (ARLCP) Framework

The ARLCP algorithm consists of three main components: complexity estimation, dynamic penalty mechanisms (reflection and length), and a composite reward function, all integrated within a reinforcement learning loop.

#### 3.2.1. Estimating Problem Complexity (λ)

The complexity of each problem `λ` is estimated dynamically based on the model's own Reflection Token Counts (RTC).
- For each input prompt `p_i` in a training batch, the LRM generates `m` candidate rollouts `o_i = [o^1_i..., o^m_i]` using standard sampling.
- For each rollout `o^k_i`, two metrics are extracted: `LEN(o^k_i)` (total response tokens) and `RTC(o^k_i)` (number of reflection tokens). Reflection tokens are identified by matching specific keywords (e.g., "wait", "hmm", "alternatively") and their variants.
- Problem complexity is then categorized into three levels—simple, moderate, and hard—based on `RTC(o^k_i)` using two predefined thresholds, `n_1` and `n_2`.

#### 3.2.2. Adaptive Reflection Penalty

This component aims to curb unnecessary reflective steps while preserving critical reasoning.
- A reflection penalty coefficient `α_1` is dynamically assigned based on the estimated complexity level:
 - `λ_1` for `RTC(o^k_i) ≤ n_1` (simple problems)
 - `λ_2` for `n_1 < RTC(o^k_i) ≤ n_2` (moderate problems)
 - `λ_3` for `RTC(o^k_i) > n_2` (hard problems)
- The reflection penalty `f(RTC(o^k_i))` is calculated using a sigmoid function:
 `f(RTC(o^k_i)) = σ((RTC(o^k_i) - mean(RTC(o_i))_correct) / std(RTC(o_i))_correct)`.
 Here, `mean(RTC(o_i))_correct` and `std(RTC(o_i))_correct` are the mean and standard deviation of reflection tokens from *correct* responses within the current batch. This normalization ensures the penalty is relative to the model's typical successful reflection behavior.

#### 3.2.3. Length Penalty

To address general verbosity not directly linked to specific reflection tokens, a length penalty is introduced.
- The length penalty coefficient `α_2` is defined as `α - α_1`, where `α` is an overall penalty coefficient. This allows `α_1` and `α_2` to dynamically distribute the total penalty weight based on problem complexity.
- The length penalty `f(LEN(o^k_i))` is calculated similarly to the reflection penalty:
 `f(LEN(o^k_i)) = σ((LEN(o^k_i) - mean(LEN(o_i))_correct) / std(LEN(o_i))_correct)`.
 `mean(LEN(o_i))_correct` and `std(LEN(o_i))_correct` are the mean and standard deviation of response lengths from *correct* responses in the batch.

#### 3.2.4. Composite Reward Function and RL Optimization

The framework uses a composite reward function `r(o^k_i)` to jointly optimize for accuracy and efficiency:
- `C(o^k_i)` is a binary accuracy reward (1 if `ANS(o^k_i)` matches `o*(p_i)`, 0 otherwise).
- The final reward is `r(o^k_i) = C(o^k_i) * (1 - α_1 * f(RTC(o^k_i)) - α_2 * f(LEN(o^k_i)))`.
This structure ensures that incorrect responses receive zero reward, preventing the model from learning to be concise but inaccurate. For correct responses, the reward is reduced proportionally to the reflection and length penalties.

The model is optimized using Reinforcement Learning.
- **Policy Optimization**: Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is the underlying policy gradient method, maximizing the expected reward while controlling policy updates.
- **Advantage Estimation**: Instead of a value network, the REINFORCE Leave-One-Out (RLOO) estimator is employed to compute advantages via Monte Carlo sampling. For a prompt `P` with `m` responses `O = [O^1..., O^m]`, the advantage for `O_i` is `A(O_i, P) = R(O_i, P) - (1 / (m-1)) * Σ_{j!=i} R(O_j, P)`. The sequence-level advantage is approximated for token-level updates. RLOO is chosen for its robustness in the presence of length-based rewards, where other methods like GRPO have shown instability.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup

- **LRMs**: DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and 7B.
- **Training Dataset**: DeepScaleR (40K math problem-answer pairs).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks**: Five mathematical reasoning datasets of increasing complexity: GSM8K, MATH-500, AMC2023, AIME 2024, and AIME 2025. MMLU was used for cross-domain generalization.
- **Metrics**: Pass@1 accuracy (Acc) and response length (Length).
- **Parameters**: `m = 16`, `λ_1 = 0.05`, `λ_2 = 0.1`, `λ_3 = 0.15`, `n_1 = 40`, `n_2 = 80`, `α = 0.2`.

This comprehensive approach allows ARLCP to dynamically tailor reasoning efficiency based on problem characteristics, aiming to yield both accurate and concise solutions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of ARLCP consistently demonstrated its effectiveness in achieving a balanced trade-off between reasoning efficiency and solution accuracy across various mathematical reasoning benchmarks and model scales.

#### 4.1. Core Performance on Mathematical Reasoning Benchmarks (Table 1)

ARLCP was evaluated against several baselines (Vanilla, Nothinking, SFT_Shortest, DPO_Shortest, O1-Pruner, TLMRE, AdaptThink, LASER) on five math benchmarks: AMC 2023, AIME 2024, AIME 2025, GSM8K, and MATH-500, using DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B and 7B models.

- **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B Model:**
 - ARLCP reduced the average response length by 53.1% while simultaneously improving accuracy by 5.8% compared to the vanilla model.
 - It achieved the highest overall accuracy gain (∆Acc = 5.81) and a substantial reduction in average response length (∆Length = -53.05%) across all baselines.
 - For complex competition-level benchmarks (AMC 2023, AIME 2024, AIME 2025), ARLCP achieved the best accuracy results (73.28%, 34.17%, and 26.46% respectively).

- **DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B Model:**
 - ARLCP achieved a 35.0% reduction in response length with a 2.7% accuracy improvement.
 - It maintained a significant accuracy gain (∆Acc = 2.69) and the greatest reduction in response length (∆Length = -34.96%) among the compared methods.

These results indicate that ARLCP enables significant efficiency gains, primarily by reducing unnecessary verbosity and reflection, without compromising or even improving accuracy, especially for more challenging tasks where LRMs tend to over-reflect.

#### 4.2. Training Dynamics (Figure 4)

Throughout the training process, the analysis of accuracy, response length, and reflection token counts on the five benchmarks showed a consistent trend. As training steps progressed, both the average count of reflection words and the overall output length gradually decreased. Concurrently, model accuracy improved. This indicates that ARLCP's adaptive reflection penalty successfully incentivizes the LRM to prune unnecessary reflective behaviors and generate more concise outputs, leading to reduced token consumption and inference costs.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Reflection Behavior (Figure 5)

Direct comparison between vanilla models and ARLCP-trained models revealed that ARLCP significantly reduced the average reflection token count across all evaluated math datasets for both 1.5B and 7B models. This finding substantiates the effectiveness of ARLCP's reflection penalty in mitigating unnecessary reflective behaviors.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on Penalty Components (Table 2)

An ablation study on the 1.5B LRM confirmed the cooperative roles of the reflection penalty and length penalty.
- Removing either the reflection penalty or the length penalty led to a degradation in performance (lower accuracy gain or less length reduction), highlighting that both components contribute synergistically to optimizing reasoning efficiency.
- When fixed coefficients were used instead of adaptive ones, model performance also degraded, demonstrating the importance of the dynamic adjustment mechanism in ARLCP.

#### 4.5. Generalizability to Different Domains (MMLU) (Table 4)

To assess cross-domain generalization, ARLCP (on DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) was evaluated on the MMLU benchmark.
- ARLCP reduced response length by 40% while achieving a 0.7% improvement in accuracy compared to the vanilla model.
- This indicates that ARLCP's approach is not confined to mathematical reasoning but can effectively transfer to a broad range of open-domain tasks, consistently enhancing reasoning efficiency while maintaining or improving accuracy.

#### 4.6. Generalizability to Different Model Series (Table 5)

The method's generalizability was further tested on Qwen3-1.7B and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLama-8B models, outside the DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen family.
- ARLCP consistently improved accuracy and substantially reduced output length across nearly all benchmarks for these different model series.
- This demonstrates strong transferability across various model architectures and families, suggesting its broad applicability.

#### 4.7. Case Study (Figure 7)

A qualitative case study on a MATH-500 problem illustrated that while the vanilla DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B generated a verbose response with numerous repetitive reflections (9713 tokens, 115 reflection tokens), ARLCP-7B provided a concise and accurate solution (1479 tokens, 8 reflection tokens) by dynamically stopping unnecessary reflections.

In summary, the results collectively demonstrate that ARLCP effectively addresses the LRM efficiency challenge by intelligently curbing over-reflection and excessive verbosity through its adaptive, coordinated penalty mechanism, leading to a superior efficiency-accuracy trade-off across diverse tasks and models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, introducing Adaptive Reflection and Length Coordinated Penalty (ARLCP), addresses a critical challenge in the field of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) and carries several significant implications and potential impacts.

**Addressing LRM Efficiency Challenges:** The work directly confronts the fundamental trade-off between performance and efficiency that plagues current LRMs. By identifying and targeting "over-reflection" as a specific source of inefficiency, ARLCP provides a granular and effective mechanism to reduce token consumption and computational overhead. This is crucial for the sustainable and scalable deployment of powerful LRMs in real-world applications where latency and cost are significant concerns.

**Enhancing Accuracy Alongside Efficiency:** A notable achievement of ARLCP is its ability to improve reasoning efficiency without sacrificing, and often enhancing, solution accuracy. Many prior attempts to reduce LRM verbosity have resulted in accuracy degradation. ARLCP's adaptive penalty, which intelligently distinguishes between necessary and unnecessary reflection based on problem complexity, allows for more concise outputs that are also more accurate, particularly for complex tasks. This represents a substantial step forward in optimizing LRM performance holistically.

**Novel Adaptive Penalty Mechanism:** The introduction of an adaptive reflection penalty coordinated with a dynamic length penalty is a methodological innovation. By linking penalty strength to a model-aware estimate of problem complexity, ARLCP moves beyond static or naive truncation methods. This dynamic allocation of penalty weights allows the model to flexibly adjust its reasoning depth and verbosity, enabling tailored responses that are efficient without being prematurely curtailed. This approach offers a valuable paradigm for controlling generative AI outputs.

**Demonstrated Generalizability:** The robust experimental validation across different LRM scales (1.5B, 7B), diverse mathematical reasoning benchmarks of varying complexity (GSM8K to AIME), other problem domains (MMLU), and even different model families (Qwen3, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-LLama series) highlights the broad applicability and robustness of ARLCP. This strong generalizability suggests that the method's core principles—identifying and penalizing inefficient reflective behaviors—are relevant across a wide spectrum of reasoning models and tasks, making it a broadly useful technique.

**Insights into LRM Reasoning Processes:** The paper's initial observations, which identify "over-reflection" as a recurring, detrimental pattern correlating with problem complexity and reduced accuracy, provide valuable insights into the internal workings and failure modes of LRMs. This deeper understanding of LRM behavior can inform future research into more intrinsically efficient model architectures or training methodologies.

**Practical Deployment Facilitation:** By significantly reducing the average response length (e.g., 53.1% for the 1.5B model and 35.0% for the 7B model in math tasks), ARLCP directly contributes to lower inference costs (fewer tokens to process) and reduced latency. These are critical factors for the practical deployment of LRMs in commercial products, real-time interactive systems, and resource-constrained environments (e.g., edge devices), making advanced reasoning capabilities more accessible and economically viable.

In summary, ARLCP represents a meaningful contribution to the field of efficient large language models. Its adaptive, coordinated penalty mechanism offers a promising direction for developing LRMs that are not only powerful in their reasoning capabilities but also practical and efficient in their operation, thereby accelerating their adoption in diverse real-world applications.
