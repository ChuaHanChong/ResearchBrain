# 3D Dynamics-Aware Manipulation: Endowing Manipulation Policies with 3D Foresight

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology researchers developed "3D Dynamics-Aware Manipulation," a framework providing robotic policies with "3D foresight" by integrating 3D world modeling through current depth, future RGB-D, and 3D flow predictions. This approach enhances policies' spatial awareness and robustness, achieving higher success rates in depth-critical manipulation tasks across diverse simulations and real-world experiments with minimal inference latency.

## Problem

- Existing robotic manipulation policies primarily rely on 2D visual world models, leading to an insufficient representation of crucial depth information.
- Robust manipulation tasks often involve significant depth-wise movement, precise distance guidance, and intricate obstacle avoidance, for which 2D-centric models are inadequate.
- Prior flow-enhanced manipulation methods largely confine optical flow to the 2D frame or use 3D flow only for reconstruction, not end-to-end policy integration.

## Method

- The **3D Foresight Manipulation** framework utilizes a ==causal transformer architecture== to integrate multimodal inputs and jointly model language, vision, proprioception, 3D world dynamics, and robotic actions.
- It employs three complementary ==self-supervised learning== tasks: ==current depth estimation==, ==future RGB-D prediction==, and ==3D flow prediction== to explicitly teach 3D dynamics.
- A ==preprocessing pipeline== automatically annotates depth and 3D flow using state-of-the-art ==monocular depth estimators== and ==3D trackers== for datasets lacking ground-truth 3D information.

## Results

- "3D Foresight" improved task completion on CALVIN, achieving **4.08** (D0D) and **4.23** (ABC0D) average solved tasks with pretraining, and increased the average success rate on LIBERO to **95.3%**.
- It outperformed 2D foresight baselines by larger margins (e.g., **+0.11** in CALVIN D0D and **+0.17** in LIBERO) and showed superior performance in real-world depth-critical tasks, like "stack two cups" (**70%** success vs. **35%** for 2D foresight).
- The integration of 3D foresight incurred a negligible increase in inference latency, adding only **6 ms** compared to a 2D world-modeling baseline (**112 ms** vs. **106 ms**), demonstrating practical efficiency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly learning 3D dynamics provides policies with "3D foresight," significantly enhancing spatial awareness and enabling more accurate action planning in 3D space beyond superficial 2D pixel dynamics.
> - The proposed self-supervised learning objectives (current depth, future RGB-D, and 3D flow prediction) are complementary and mutually beneficial, leading to a more calibrated and effective world model.
> - Shared 3D dynamics between scene transformation and low-level robotic actions can be effectively leveraged to improve policy understanding of physical interactions and achieve robust behaviors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements through the integration of world modeling into policy learning. This approach typically involves pretraining policy models on large-scale video data to predict future visual observations, primarily in 2D. Methods like GR-1, SeeR, and UP-VLA exemplify this trend by focusing on future RGB prediction to make policies aware of desired future states, thereby facilitating action prediction. This paradigm offers an interpretable alternative to large vision-language model (VLM) based methods, which, while powerful, can present challenges in efficiency and interpretability.

However, a significant limitation of these 2D-centric world models is their lossy representation of depth information. While neural networks can implicitly infer depth from monocular images, relying solely on 2D visual dynamics is often insufficient for robust manipulation tasks that involve substantial depth-wise movement, precise distance guidance, or intricate obstacle avoidance. This gap highlights a need for explicit 3D understanding.

Concurrently, other lines of research have explored language-conditioned manipulation, using frozen language embeddings (e.g., CLIPort) or leveraging large VLMs (e.g., RT-2, OpenVLA, Pi0). Another area involves flow-enhanced manipulation policies, where motion information is decoupled from visual appearance. While some methods incorporate 2D optical flow to guide actions (e.g., ATM, Track2Act), and a few recent works (e.g., General Flow, G3Flow) have explored 3D flow, the general trend has been to confine flow to the 2D frame space or use 3D flow for specific purposes like 3D reconstruction rather than end-to-end policy integration.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of world modeling and 3D perception. It addresses the limitations of 2D world modeling by explicitly integrating 3D dynamics into manipulation policy learning. By moving beyond 2D visual dynamics, the authors aim to provide policies with a more comprehensive understanding of the physical world, particularly its depth and movement, to enhance manipulation robustness, especially for tasks with prominent depth-wise requirements. This work seeks to unify 3D world modeling with policy learning, offering a framework that leverages self-supervised learning on 3D data representations to improve robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a 3D dynamics-aware manipulation framework that endows manipulation policies with "3D foresight." This capability aims to improve the robustness and performance of robotic manipulation, particularly in tasks that necessitate precise depth perception and navigation through 3D space.

The motivation for this objective stems from two primary insights:

1. **Limitations of 2D World Modeling:** Existing world modeling approaches for manipulation policy learning predominantly rely on predicting 2D visual dynamics, such as future RGB observations. While these methods have shown promising results, the monocular 2D representation of the world inherently lacks comprehensive depth information. Depth is critical for tasks requiring accurate distance estimation, preventing collisions, and executing movements along the Z-axis (depth-wise movements). Although it is theoretically possible for models to implicitly learn depth from 2D images, as humans with monocular vision or monocular depth estimation networks demonstrate, the authors posit that explicitly teaching models about depth is a more practical and direct approach to acquire this ability.
2. **Shared 3D Dynamics between Scene Transformation and Robotic Actions:** The paper highlights that both the transformation of objects within a 3D scene and the low-level SE(3) robotic actions (translation and rotation in 3D space) fundamentally operate in the same 3D coordinate system and share similar underlying dynamics. Understanding how objects move in 3D space, driven by a language command, is closely correlated with how a robot should move its end effector to interact with those objects. By enabling policy models to explicitly capture these 3D dynamics, they can develop a more intuitive and accurate understanding of the physical interactions required to achieve a task, leading to more appropriate and robust behaviors.

To achieve 3D foresight, the research proposes a framework built upon three complementary self-supervised learning tasks:
- **Current Depth Estimation:** To directly provide the model with an understanding of the immediate 3D structure of the environment.
- **Future RGB-D Prediction:** To enable the model to foresee both visual and depth changes in the environment as a result of actions, thereby predicting future 3D states.
- **3D Flow Prediction:** To capture the precise 3D motion of points within the scene, bridging the gap between current and future 3D states and offering a dynamics-centric view of scene evolution.

These objectives are pursued with the aim of seamlessly integrating 3D world modeling into policy learning without compromising inference speed, leveraging cross-embodiment pretraining, and enhancing the model's spatial reasoning capabilities across various manipulation tasks and environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology integrates 3D world modeling with policy learning into a unified framework designed to endow manipulation policies with 3D foresight. The framework leverages a causal transformer architecture to process multimodal inputs and queries in an end-to-end manner.

**A. 3D World Representation and Self-Supervised Learning Tasks:**
The approach represents the 3D world using RGB-D images, which combines standard RGB channels with a depth channel. This choice is made to balance between detailed 3D information and computational feasibility, avoiding the complexities of large-scale 3D point cloud reconstruction for diverse real-world data. To facilitate learning of 3D dynamics, three self-supervised learning tasks are introduced:
1. **Current Depth Estimation:** The model is trained to predict the depth map (`d_t`) for the current main and wrist camera RGB images. This provides an immediate 3D understanding of the scene.
2. **Future RGB-D Prediction:** The model predicts the future RGB-D frame (`rgbd_{t+S}`) at a future timestep `S` from current observations. This allows the policy to anticipate how the 3D environment will appear and change.
3. **3D Flow Prediction:** The model predicts 3D flow (`τ_{t:t+L-1}`), which tracks the 3D trajectories of points over a sequence of `L` frames. 3D flow serves as a bridge between current and future RGB-D frames, explicitly modeling the transformation of 3D scene points over time. The three dimensions of 3D flow correspond to x, y pixel coordinates, and metric depth values.

**B. Causal Transformer Architecture:**
A GPT-style causal transformer forms the core of the framework, jointly modeling language, vision, proprioception, 3D world dynamics, and robotic actions.
- **Input Encoding:** Language commands are encoded using a CLIP text encoder and a linear projection. RGB images (main and wrist views) are encoded into (1+r) vectors using MAE and a perceiver resampler. Robot proprioception states (6D end-effector pose, gripper status) are embedded into a vector via linear layers.
- **Query Construction:**
 - **Flow Query:** Initialized with learnable vectors, combined with encoded starting pixel coordinates of sampled 3D points to specify which points to track.
 - **Future Query:** Learnable vectors equal in number to image embedding vectors, designed to reconstruct future RGB-D.
 - **Action Query:** A single learnable vector responsible for querying action-related information.
- **Causal Attention Mechanism:** All input sequences from `t-T+1` to `t` are concatenated and fed into the transformer. A carefully designed self-attention mask ensures causal relationships, allowing tokens to attend only to current and historical language, vision, and proprioception tokens. The action query specifically attends to current and historical proprioception tokens and the 3D flow query, reflecting their close correlation.

**C. Decoders and Loss Functions:**
Auxiliary decoding heads are attached to the transformer for the self-supervised tasks. These heads are used during training and can be removed or offloaded during inference to minimize latency.
- **Current Depth Decoder:** A bidirectional self-attention-based decoder takes projected final hidden states of current image tokens (context) and 2D masked patch tokens (positional embeddings + learnable mask vector) to predict current depth.
- **Future RGB-D Decoder:** The final representation of the future query feeds into an image decoder (structurally similar to the depth decoder, but with 3 output channels) and a depth decoder to predict future RGB and depth.
- **3D Flow Decoder:** Similar to the depth decoder, it uses masked patch tokens from starting pixel coordinates, updated by the flow query's representation, to predict `L`-length 3D flow trajectories.
- **Action Decoder:** For policy learning, a CVAE encoder compresses the ground-truth action chunk into a latent vector. This, along with the action query's representation and mask tokens, feeds into a transformer decoder to predict the action chunk (`a_{t:t+K-1}`). The action space is SE(3), including 6D end-effector pose delta and binary gripper status.

The overall learning objective `L` is a weighted sum of Mean Squared Error (MSE) for current depth (`L_depth`), future RGB-D (`L_future`), and 3D flow (`L_flow`), combined with Smooth L1 and Binary Cross Entropy for the action chunk (`L_act`).

**D. Automatic Annotation of Depth and 3D Flow:**
For datasets lacking ground-truth depth, a preprocessing pipeline is employed:
- **Depth Estimation:** State-of-the-art monocular metric depth estimators, Depth-Anything-V2 and Video-Depth-Anything, are used to generate temporally consistent metric depth estimates.
- **3D Flow Tracking:** DELTA is utilized to track the 3D positions of points within RGB-D videos. A sampling strategy ensures a mix of moving and static points (approximately 1250 total points, with 1/4 moving), balancing between learning 3D dynamics and maintaining inference-time compatibility (uniform grid sampling).

**E. Cross-embodiment Pretraining and Fine-tuning:**
The model undergoes pretraining on a large-scale dataset of 44K trajectories from 5 diverse datasets (RH20T, Bridge, Berkeley UR5, Mutex, LIBERO) covering various embodiments (humans, Franka, UR5, WidowX robots). During pretraining, modalities that are often heterogeneous or missing across different data sources (proprioception states, actions, wrist-view depth/images) are excluded. The pretrained GR-MG checkpoint, a 2D world-modeling policy, serves as the base for initialization. After pretraining, the model is fine-tuned and evaluated on downstream manipulation tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted across simulation benchmarks (CALVIN, LIBERO) and real-world settings to evaluate the efficacy of 3D foresight.

**A. Benefit of 3D Foresight (Q1):**
- **CALVIN Benchmark:** Without pretraining, "3D Foresight (scratch)" improved the average number of completed tasks (Avg. Len.) from 3.84 (GR-MG) to 4.01 in the in-domain setting (D→D) and from 4.04 to 4.15 in the zero-shot scene transfer setting (ABC→D). With cross-embodiment pretraining, the "3D Foresight" model further increased performance to 4.08 (D→D) and 4.23 (ABC→D), demonstrating sustained benefits.
- **LIBERO Benchmark:** Despite using pseudo-depth labels, "3D Foresight (scratch)" consistently increased performance across all four task suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long), raising the average success rate from 91.7% (GR-MG) to 94.3%. With pretraining, the "3D Foresight" model achieved an average success rate of 95.3%. This indicates robustness to noisy depth labels and versatility across diverse tasks.
- **Inference Speed:** The integration of 3D foresight resulted in a negligible increase in inference latency, adding only 6 ms compared to the GR-MG baseline (112 ms vs. 106 ms). This is attributed to the removal or offloading of auxiliary decoding heads during inference.

**B. 2D Foresight vs. 3D Foresight (Q2):**
- A direct comparison with a "2D Foresight (scratch)" baseline (GR-MG with 2D flow prediction, but no 3D depth dimension) revealed the superiority of 3D foresight. "2D Foresight (scratch)" showed smaller improvements over GR-MG: +0.06 in CALVIN D→D, +0.04 in CALVIN ABC→D, and +0.09 in LIBERO. However, "3D Foresight (scratch)" surpassed "2D Foresight (scratch)" by larger margins: +0.11 in CALVIN D→D, +0.07 in CALVIN ABC→D, and +0.17 in LIBERO.
- Task-wise analysis on CALVIN D→D highlighted that tasks involving prominent depth-wise movement, such as "lift block from drawer" and "lift block from slider," benefited most significantly from 3D foresight, showing a greater increment in success rates compared to 2D foresight.

**C. Ablation Study (Q3):**
An ablation study on CALVIN D→D investigated the contribution of each self-supervised learning objective:
- **Removing Future RGB-D Prediction:** Reduced the average number of solved tasks by 0.07.
- **Removing 3D Flow Prediction:** Reduced the average number of solved tasks by 0.06.
- **Removing Current Depth Prediction:** Led to a smaller decrease of 0.04 in average solved tasks.
This indicates that dynamics-related objectives (future RGB-D and 3D flow prediction) contributed more profoundly to performance than current depth estimation.
- **Complementarity:** Removing any of the three learning objectives consistently led to an increase in the losses of the remaining two objectives on validation sets for both CALVIN and real-world data. This suggests that the proposed objectives are complementary and mutually beneficial, aiding in model calibration during training.

**D. Real-world Evaluation (Q4):**
Real-world experiments on a JAKA K-1 7DoF robotic arm involved two tasks requiring strong spatial awareness: "stack two cups" and "get a tape from the middle drawer."
- **"Stack two cups":** Under a "longitudinal" cup placement (requiring more depth-wise perception), "3D Foresight" achieved a 70% success rate compared to 35% for "2D Foresight (scratch)" and 40% for GR-MG.
- **"Get a tape":** "3D Foresight" achieved a 75% success rate, outperforming "2D Foresight (scratch)" (60%) and GR-MG (55%).
- **Qualitative Analysis:** Case studies revealed that "3D Foresight" policies accurately located target objects or determined gripping timing based on distance, thanks to enhanced depth perception from both main and wrist views. In contrast, "2D Foresight" policies sometimes failed due to misjudging distances or relying on occluded views, confirming the hypothesis that 3D foresight provides stronger spatial awareness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by addressing a limitation in existing world modeling approaches: the insufficient representation of 3D dynamics. By explicitly integrating 3D world modeling into policy learning, the paper demonstrates a method to enhance the robustness and performance of manipulation policies, particularly for tasks that inherently involve significant depth-wise movement, precise distance perception, and intricate 3D spatial reasoning.

The development of the "3D Dynamics-Aware Manipulation" framework offers several significant aspects:
- **Enhanced Spatial Awareness:** The framework provides manipulation policies with "3D foresight" through self-supervised learning on current depth, future RGB-D, and 3D flow predictions. This explicit learning of 3D dynamics moves beyond superficial 2D pixel dynamics, leading to a more comprehensive understanding of the physical environment and enabling more accurate action planning in 3D space.
- **Robustness to Depth-Critical Tasks:** The experimental results consistently show that policies equipped with 3D foresight achieve higher success rates on tasks requiring strong spatial awareness and depth perception, both in diverse simulation environments (CALVIN, LIBERO) and real-world scenarios. This is particularly evident in tasks where traditional 2D-focused methods struggle with depth ambiguities or occlusions.
- **Practical Integration:** The proposed end-to-end integration within a causal transformer architecture allows for parameter sharing and knowledge transfer across different modalities. Furthermore, the design ensures minimal overhead during inference by removing or offloading auxiliary decoding heads, making the approach practical for real-time robotic control.
- **Leveraging Existing Data and Tools:** The method for automatic annotation of depth and 3D flow using state-of-the-art monocular depth estimators and 3D trackers (Depth-Anything-V2, Video-Depth-Anything, DELTA) allows the framework to leverage large-scale, potentially unlabeled, RGB video datasets by synthesizing the necessary 3D labels. This mitigates the common challenge of obtaining comprehensive 3D data.
- **Complementary Learning Objectives:** The ablation studies confirm the complementary nature of the three self-supervised learning objectives, demonstrating that they mutually benefit each other during the learning process, leading to a more calibrated and effective world model.
- **Generalizability through Cross-embodiment Pretraining:** The benefits of pretraining on a diverse set of cross-embodiment manipulation videos suggest that the learned 3D dynamics representations are generalizable, allowing for improved performance even in novel or zero-shot scenarios.

The potential impact of this work is in advancing the capabilities of general-purpose robotic manipulation systems. Policies with enhanced 3D foresight could lead to more reliable and adaptable robots capable of performing complex manipulation tasks in unstructured and dynamic environments, such as household robotics, industrial assembly, and assistive robotics. Future research directions could involve exploring even more advanced 3D scene representations beyond RGB-D, such as point clouds or neural radiance fields, to further enhance the model's spatial reasoning abilities.
