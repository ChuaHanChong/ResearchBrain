# Q-Guided Stein Variational Model Predictive Control via RL-informed Policy Prior

> [!summary] Summary
> Q-Guided Stein Variational Model Predictive Control (Q-SVMPC) unifies reinforcement learning with model predictive control by using learned soft Q-values as optimality likelihoods and an RL-informed policy prior within a Stein Variational Gradient Descent planner. This approach enables robust and sample-efficient robotic control, mitigating mode collapse and achieving superior performance in complex manipulation tasks and real-world fruit picking.

## Problem

- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) heavily relies on accurate dynamics models and meticulously hand-designed cost functions, which are challenging to formulate for complex robotic tasks.
- Existing learning-based MPC methods, including gradient-based and parametric sampling approaches, often suffer from mode collapse, converging to a single trajectory and failing to explore or preserve diverse optimal solutions.
- Prior Stein Variational MPC methods still required manual specification of appropriate prior distributions and task-dependent cost functions, limiting their general applicability.

## Method

- Q-SVMPC frames learning-guided ==MPC== as a Q-guided posterior inference problem, conditioned on a learned policy prior.
- It leverages learned soft Q-values from a critic network to define an optimality likelihood for trajectory inference, replacing hand-designed cost functions.
- The approach integrates an RL-informed policy prior for effective warm-starting of trajectory optimization and employs Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) for non-parametric, mode-preserving particle refinement.

## Results

- Q-SVMPC consistently achieved higher returns and success rates across 2D navigation and complex Kinova manipulation tasks (e.g., Reach with Obstacles and Pick and Place), outperforming model-free and model-based RL baselines.
- The method demonstrated strong sample efficiency and training stability, reliably converging to higher cumulative returns, while also exhibiting a favorable trade-off between high performance and low collision rates (e.g., **2.42%** collision rate in Reach with Obstacles).
- It successfully transferred to a real Kinova arm for a fruit-picking task with obstacle avoidance, achieving superior success rates (**93.3%** obstacle avoidance, **80.0%** target reaching) compared to SAC and S2AC, validating its real-world applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The framework establishes a principled connection between Bayesian MPC, the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) framework, and SVGD for trajectory-level inference.
> - Utilizing soft Q-values as an optimality likelihood enables data-driven guidance for particle-based trajectory optimization, effectively moving beyond hand-crafted costs and implicitly handling safety.
> - Non-parametric SVGD particle refinement is crucial for explicitly preserving solution diversity across the trajectory distribution, which overcomes the mode collapse issue prevalent in many control methods.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Model Predictive Control (MPC) is a well-established method for trajectory optimization, known for its ability to handle dynamics constraints. However, its effectiveness has traditionally relied on the availability of accurate dynamics models and meticulously designed cost functions, which can be challenging to formulate for complex robotic tasks.

In response to these limitations, the research landscape has seen the emergence of *learning-based MPC* methods. These approaches integrate data-driven learning, often from reinforcement learning (RL), to alleviate the burdens of model and cost design. Existing learning-based MPC techniques generally fall into several categories:
1. **Learning Dynamics Models:** Methods that learn predictive models of system dynamics to improve planning under uncertainty.
2. **Learning Value/Cost Functions:** Approaches that learn cost-related or value functions to provide informative optimization signals within the MPC framework.
3. **Learning Priors/Initialization:** Techniques that learn better initializations or prior distributions to warm-start trajectory optimization.

Despite advancements, many existing learning-based MPC methods still employ optimization strategies with inherent limitations. These include:
- **Deterministic Gradient-Based Solvers:** Such as differentiable MPC, which typically optimize for a single dominant trajectory, potentially leading to *mode collapse* where alternative high-return solutions are ignored.
- **Parametric Sampling-Based Planners:** Like the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) or Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), which iteratively refit a parametric distribution (e.g., Gaussian) and may also converge to a single mode.

Another significant development in control theory is the *control-as-inference* framework, which reformulates optimal control as a probabilistic inference problem. Under this paradigm, finding an optimal policy is akin to performing approximate inference over a trajectory posterior defined by system dynamics and optimality criteria. Methods like Stein Variational MPC (SVMPC) and DuSt-MPC have adopted this perspective, leveraging Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) for particle-based posterior approximation. However, these methods still face challenges related to the manual specification of appropriate prior distributions and task-dependent cost functions.

This work, Q-SVMPC, positions itself at the intersection of learning-based MPC and the control-as-inference framework. It seeks to address the aforementioned limitations by:
- Overcoming mode collapse in trajectory optimization by explicitly preserving solution diversity through non-parametric SVGD.
- Replacing hand-designed cost functions with learned soft Q-values, reducing the need for manual engineering.
- Integrating an RL-informed policy prior to provide a more effective initialization for trajectory inference, thereby improving sample efficiency and stability compared to methods relying on manually specified priors.
- Establishing a theoretical connection between Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and SVGD for trajectory-level inference, building upon single-step SVGD applications in RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust and sample-efficient learning-guided Model Predictive Control (MPC) method that addresses the limitations of existing approaches, specifically regarding the reliance on accurate models and hand-designed costs, as well as the issue of mode collapse in trajectory optimization.

The key motivations driving the development of Q-SVMPC are detailed as follows:

- **Reducing Model and Cost Design Burdens:** Classical MPC heavily relies on accurate dynamics models and carefully hand-designed cost functions. For complex robotic tasks, obtaining such precise models or crafting effective cost functions can be arduous or impractical. The authors aim to mitigate these burdens by leveraging learned components, particularly soft Q-values, to guide trajectory optimization.
- **Addressing Mode Collapse in Trajectory Optimization:** Many existing learning-based MPC methods, whether based on deterministic gradient solvers or parametric sampling (e.g., CEM, MPPI), tend to converge to a single dominant solution. This can lead to mode collapse, where the planner fails to explore and preserve diverse, potentially optimal or safer, feasible trajectories. A core motivation is to develop a method that explicitly preserves a distribution of diverse solutions, thereby enhancing robustness and flexibility.
- **Integrating Reinforcement Learning Guidance into MPC:** The paper aims to bridge the gap between learning-based methods and MPC by incorporating strong learning signals from Reinforcement Learning (RL). Specifically, learned soft Q-values are proposed to define an "optimality likelihood" for trajectory inference, providing a data-driven, rather than hand-engineered, measure of trajectory quality.
- **Leveraging Probabilistic Inference for Control:** Building on the "control as inference" paradigm, the authors are motivated to cast learning-based MPC as a trajectory-level posterior inference problem. This perspective allows for a principled framework to approximate optimal control sequences by drawing samples from a posterior distribution.
- **Improving Sample Efficiency and Stability:** By using an RL-informed policy prior, the method aims to provide a more effective warm-start for trajectory optimization, reducing the number of online planning steps required. Furthermore, the non-parametric nature of Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) is intended to provide lower-variance targets for critic learning and more stable policy updates, contributing to overall improved sample efficiency and training stability.
- **Enhancing Robustness to Model Errors and Complex Task Environments:** The proposed approach seeks to improve the adaptability and robustness of MPC in environments with imperfect dynamics models, geometric constraints, and contact-intensive interactions. By learning from experience and preserving diverse solutions, the method is designed to be more resilient to uncertainties and unmodeled effects.
- **Establishing Theoretical Connections:** The research is also motivated by the desire to establish a theoretical connection between the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) framework and SVGD, extending existing single-step SVGD applications in RL to multi-step, trajectory-level inference within MPC.

In summary, Q-SVMPC is motivated by the need for a learning-guided MPC framework that is less reliant on manual engineering, avoids mode collapse, and exhibits improved performance and robustness in complex robotic control tasks through a principled integration of RL and probabilistic inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Q-SVMPC (Q-Guided Stein Variational Model Predictive Control via RL-informed Policy Prior) frames learning-guided Model Predictive Control (MPC) as a Q-guided posterior inference problem, conditioned on a learned policy prior. The methodology involves several interconnected components: an actor network for prior sampling, a dynamics model for trajectory rollouts, a critic network for Q-value estimation, and a Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) planner for non-parametric trajectory refinement.

**A. Overview of the Algorithmic Flow**
The Q-SVMPC algorithm operates in a receding-horizon manner, alternating between online inference and offline learning:
1. **Prior Generation:** At each timestep $t$, an actor network (parameterized by $\phi$) generates an RL-informed policy prior, typically a sequence of Gaussian distributions (mean and covariance) over a finite control horizon $H$, conditioned on the current state $s_t$.
2. **Particle Sampling:** A set of $M$ action-trajectory particles is sampled from this learned Gaussian prior distribution. These particles represent initial guesses for control sequences.
3. **Trajectory Rollouts:** Each sampled particle (control sequence) is rolled out through a dynamics model (either analytical $f$ or learned $f_\psi$) to generate corresponding state trajectories over the horizon.
4. **Q-Value-Guided Likelihood:** The critic network (parameterized by $\theta$) computes soft Q-values for these rolled-out trajectories. These Q-values are then used to define an optimality likelihood within a Bayesian MPC formulation, where higher Q-values correspond to higher likelihoods of optimality.
5. **SVGD-Based Refinement:** Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) iteratively refines the initial particle set. The SVGD update direction is derived from the log-posterior, which combines the Q-value-based optimality likelihood and the learned policy prior. This process pushes particles towards high-value regions while maintaining particle diversity through a repulsive kernel term.
6. **Action Execution:** After a few SVGD refinement steps, the control sequence with the highest trajectory value from the refined particle set is selected. Its first action is executed in the real or simulated environment.
7. **Experience Collection and Learning:** The resulting state transition ($s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1}$) is stored in a replay buffer. This experience is then used to update both the actor (policy prior) and critic (soft Q-function) networks using minibatches, following a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) like objective. A control sequence sampled from the refined particle set (rather than the highest-value one) can be used for training to encourage exploration.

**B. Learned Policy Distribution as Prior**
The paper defines the prior distribution over control sequences $A_t = (a_t, \dots, a_{t+H-1})$ as a sequence of Gaussian distributions: $N(\mu_{t:t+H-1}, \Sigma_{t:t+H-1})$. The mean $\mu$ and covariance $\Sigma$ for each action in the sequence are predicted by the actor neural network $\pi_\phi(s_t)$. This learned prior serves as an informative initialization for the trajectory optimization, bringing the initial particles closer to the true posterior distribution and thus reducing the required number of SVGD refinement steps.

**C. Stein Variational Inference Guided by Soft Q-values**
This is a central component of Q-SVMPC. The approach extends the single-step Q-value concept from SAC to trajectory-level inference:
1. **Soft Trajectory Value Function:** For a trajectory particle $\tau^i_t = (s^i_t, a^i_t, \dots, s^i_{t+H})$, a soft trajectory value function $Q(\tau^i_t)$ is defined as the sum of soft Q-values for each state-action pair along the trajectory: $Q(\tau^i_t) = \sum_{h=0}^{H-1} Q(s^i_{t+h}, a^i_{t+h})$.
2. **Learned Energy Function and Optimality Likelihood:** Instead of a hand-crafted cost function $C(\tau)$, a learned energy function $C_Q(\tau_t) \triangleq -\frac{1}{\alpha} Q(\tau_t)$ is introduced, where $\alpha$ is the entropy temperature from SAC. This energy function defines the optimality likelihood as $p(O_\tau | A_t, s_t) \propto \exp(-\frac{C_Q(\tau_t)}{\beta}) = \exp(\frac{1}{\alpha \beta} Q(\tau_t))$, (where $\beta$ is a temperature parameter implicitly absorbed into $\alpha$ here). This likelihood assigns higher probability to trajectories with higher soft Q-values.
3. **Trajectory Posterior:** Combining this Q-shaped likelihood with the learned Gaussian prior $q_0(A_t; s_t)$ yields the trajectory posterior $\pi^*_\phi(\tau^i_t | s_t) \propto \exp(\frac{1}{\alpha} Q(\tau^i_t)) q_0(A^i_t; s_t)$.
4. **SVGD Update:** The SVGD update rule (Eq. 7 from preliminaries) is then applied to refine the particles. The logarithm of the posterior (Eq. 12) provides the gradient signal for SVGD:
 $\hat{\phi}^*(A^i, s_t) = \frac{1}{M} \sum_{j=1}^M [k(A^j, A^i)\nabla_{A^j}(\frac{1}{\alpha} Q(\tau^j_t) + \log q_0(A^j_t; s_t)) + \nabla_{A^j} k(A^j, A^i)]$.
 This formulation leverages the soft Q-value to guide particle evolution towards high-value trajectories while the kernel function $k(\cdot, \cdot)$ introduces repulsion to maintain diversity among particles. This establishes a connection between Bayesian MPC and SAC, where Q-values provide the gradient for SVGD.

**D. Entropy Computation**
Q-SVMPC computes policy entropy following S2AC, extending it to the trajectory level. A closed-form approximation of the trajectory-level entropy is derived (Eq. 14), which includes a term related to the initial prior and a trace term that approximates the log-density change induced by SVGD updates. This entropy term is used in the SAC-like objective for policy updates.

**E. Dynamics Model**
The system dynamics can be either an analytical model $f(s, a)$ or a learned model $f_\psi(s, a)$. For 2D navigation, exact dynamics are used. For Kinova tasks, an approximate analytical model based on differentiable forward kinematics with noisy propagation is employed, which is acknowledged to be imperfect due to unmodeled contact effects.

**F. Adaptive Action Integration for Robot Control (Sim-to-Real)**
For real-world deployment, an adaptive action integration strategy is adopted to mitigate the reality gap and achieve smoother control. The high-level planner's output (target joint velocity $a_t$) is up-sampled into $n$ intermediate updates. A per-segment increment $\delta a^d_t = \frac{a_t - u_t}{n}$ is computed, where $u_t$ is the previously executed command. These increments are then integrated to produce a sequence of smooth commands $u_{t,k}$ sent to the robot's low-level controller at a higher frequency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Q-SVMPC involved comparisons against several baselines (SAC, S2AC, MBPO, PETS, SVMPC) across 2D navigation, Kinova robotic manipulation tasks (Reach, Reach with Obstacles, Pick and Place), and a real-world fruit-picking scenario. The findings cover comparative performance, constraint satisfaction, ablation studies, computational cost, and sim-to-real transfer.

**A. Comparative Evaluation**
- **Overall Performance:** Q-SVMPC demonstrates robust performance across all benchmarks. On 2D Navigation, it outperforms both variants of SVMPC and converges to higher returns than other learning-based baselines.
- **Robotic Manipulation Tasks:**
 - On the **Reach** task, Q-SVMPC achieves performance comparable to SVMPC with a larger budget (SVMPC*), and surpasses budget-matched SVMPC†. SVMPC* attains the highest return, suggesting that with a sufficient computational budget, planning-based control can be effective in obstacle-free environments.
 - On more challenging tasks like **Reach (Obstacles)** and **Pick and Place**, Q-SVMPC maintains a clear performance margin over all baselines. This indicates its adaptability to complex problems involving geometric constraints and contact.
- **Sample Efficiency and Stability:** Training curves (Fig. 3) illustrate that Q-SVMPC exhibits strong sample efficiency and training stability, converging reliably to higher cumulative returns compared to model-free (SAC, S2AC) and model-based (MBPO, PETS) RL baselines.
- **Success Rates:** (Table I) In Kinova manipulation tasks, Q-SVMPC achieves high and reliable success rates, particularly excelling in Reach (Obstacles) and Pick-and-Place. For example, on Pick-and-Place, Q-SVMPC is reported as the only method achieving high success (95.3% at 100% training progress), while planning-based and model-based baselines fail under the same protocol. SVMPC achieves 100% success on the simpler Reach task but its performance significantly degrades with increasing task complexity.

**B. Qualitative Analysis: Constraint Satisfaction vs. Performance**
- **Safety and Rewards:** (Table II) On 2D Navigation, Q-SVMPC achieves low collision rates (5.49%) while securing high returns (-206.60), suggesting that its improved performance is achieved through careful exploration rather than unsafe shortcuts. In contrast, S2AC shows a higher collision rate (10.1%) with lower returns, while SVMPC* has a 0% collision rate but also higher negative returns, indicating overly timid behavior.
- **Reach (Obstacles):** Q-SVMPC achieves higher returns (74.9) with a low collision rate (2.42%), demonstrating its ability to find feasible and effective solutions. SVMPC† maintains a very low collision rate (2.87%) but also very low returns (7.06), suggesting conservative behavior. SVMPC* becomes more aggressive with a larger budget, resulting in higher collisions (20.9%) and slightly improved returns (21.4) compared to SVMPC†.
- **Trajectory Visualization:** (Fig. 4) Visualizations on 2D Navigation illustrate that Q-SVMPC's sampled trajectory particles explore high-risk, high-return regions, guided by Q-values, while SVMPC* tends to avoid obstacles in a more conservative manner, potentially missing high-value paths.

**C. Ablation Studies**
- **Prior Type:** (Fig. 5, left) The SAC-learned policy prior is crucial for performance. Using a "Random" prior (sampling from a fixed Gaussian) fails to converge, while a "Mean" prior (sampling from the policy's mean action) offers only limited early-stage improvement before collapsing. This validates the importance of an informative, learned prior.
- **Horizon Length:** (Fig. 5, left) Performance is sensitive to horizon length. Short horizons (e.g., <3) limit the Q-value guidance, while long horizons (e.g., >7) lead to reduced performance due to accumulating model errors. An intermediate horizon (e.g., 5 in the experiments) appears optimal.
- **Dynamics Model:** Q-SVMPC maintains consistently high final performance under both analytical and learned dynamics models (for 2D Navigation and Reach (Obstacles)). This suggests reduced sensitivity to dynamics model inaccuracies compared to model-based baselines.

**D. Computational Cost**
- (Table III) Q-SVMPC has an inference time of 24.6 ms per control step, corresponding to an equivalent control frequency of 40.7 Hz. This is practical for online control. While slower than model-free RL (SAC: 0.296 ms, S2AC: 13.6 ms) and budget-matched SVMPC† (15.5 ms), it is considerably faster than PETS (578 ms) and SVMPC* (79.9 ms), which uses a larger planning budget for potentially better performance on simple tasks. The overhead is attributed to model rollouts and trajectory-level prior inference.

**E. Sim-to-Real**
- (Fig. 6, Table IV) Q-SVMPC demonstrates successful transfer to a real Kinova arm for a fruit-picking task with obstacle avoidance. It achieves the highest success rates in both obstacle avoidance (93.3%) and target reaching (80.0%) compared to SAC (20.0%, 40.0%) and S2AC (86.7%, 60.0%). This real-world validation suggests that Q-guided particle optimization yields robust, geometry-aware actions resilient to real-world perturbations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Q-SVMPC framework represents a contribution to the field of robotic control, particularly at the intersection of Model Predictive Control (MPC) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging MPC and Soft RL with Probabilistic Inference:** The work establishes a principled connection between Bayesian MPC and the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) framework by leveraging soft Q-values to define an optimality likelihood within a trajectory-level posterior inference problem. This formulation allows for the benefits of both MPC (constraint handling, receding horizon) and SAC (maximum entropy exploration, stable learning) to be combined.
- **Addressing Mode Collapse in Trajectory Optimization:** By employing Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) for non-parametric trajectory refinement, Q-SVMPC explicitly addresses the problem of mode collapse prevalent in many existing gradient-based or parametric sampling-based MPC methods. SVGD allows the maintenance of a diverse set of high-value trajectories, which can be crucial for robustness and adaptability in complex, uncertain, or multi-modal environments.
- **Reduced Reliance on Hand-Designed Costs and Priors:** A key challenge in traditional MPC and even some learning-based MPC methods is the need for meticulously hand-designed cost functions and prior distributions. Q-SVMPC mitigates this by utilizing learned soft Q-values as the optimality signal and an RL-informed policy prior. This makes the approach more generally applicable to tasks where explicit cost engineering or prior specification is difficult.
- **Improved Performance and Robustness in Complex Tasks:** The empirical results demonstrate that Q-SVMPC achieves improved sample efficiency, training stability, and robustness across a range of tasks, from 2D navigation to complex robotic manipulation (obstacle avoidance, pick-and-place). It consistently outperforms several baselines, including model-free RL, model-based RL, and other Stein variational MPC variants, particularly in scenarios involving geometric constraints and contact interactions.
- **Implicit Safety and Favorable Trade-offs:** The method exhibits a favorable trade-off between performance and safety, achieving high returns while maintaining low collision rates. The Q-guided optimization implicitly discourages unsafe trajectories, as they would typically yield lower Q-values, without requiring explicit, hand-crafted safety constraints in the planning objective.
- **Demonstrated Sim-to-Real Transferability:** The successful deployment of Q-SVMPC on a real Kinova arm for a fruit-picking task with obstacle avoidance underscores its practical relevance. The ability to transfer learned policies from simulation to hardware, even with real-world perturbations, highlights the robustness of the approach.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework opens avenues for future research, such as extending Q-SVMPC to vision-based settings by incorporating visual observations into value learning and dynamics modeling. This could enable planning under partial observability and in more complex, unstructured environments, further expanding its applicability in real-world robotics.

In essence, Q-SVMPC offers a robust, data-driven, and theoretically grounded approach to robotic control that moves beyond the limitations of purely model-based or purely model-free methods, paving the way for more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems.
