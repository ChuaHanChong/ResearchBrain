# Q-Guided Stein Variational Model Predictive Control via RL-informed Policy Prior

> [!summary] Summary
> Q-Guided Stein Variational Model Predictive Control (Q-SVMPC) unifies reinforcement learning with model predictive control by using learned soft Q-values as optimality likelihoods and an RL-informed policy prior within a Stein Variational Gradient Descent planner. This approach enables robust and sample-efficient robotic control, mitigating mode collapse and achieving superior performance in complex manipulation tasks and real-world fruit picking.

## Problem

- Traditional Model Predictive Control (MPC) heavily relies on accurate dynamics models and meticulously hand-designed cost functions, which are challenging to formulate for complex robotic tasks.
- Existing learning-based MPC methods, including gradient-based and parametric sampling approaches, often suffer from mode collapse, converging to a single trajectory and failing to explore or preserve diverse optimal solutions.
- Prior Stein Variational MPC methods still required manual specification of appropriate prior distributions and task-dependent cost functions, limiting their general applicability.

## Method

- Q-SVMPC frames learning-guided ==MPC== as a Q-guided posterior inference problem, conditioned on a learned policy prior.
- It leverages learned soft Q-values from a critic network to define an optimality likelihood for trajectory inference, replacing hand-designed cost functions.
- The approach integrates an RL-informed policy prior for effective warm-starting of trajectory optimization and employs Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) for non-parametric, mode-preserving particle refinement.

## Results

- Q-SVMPC consistently achieved higher returns and success rates across 2D navigation and complex Kinova manipulation tasks (e.g., Reach with Obstacles and Pick and Place), outperforming model-free and model-based RL baselines.
- The method demonstrated strong sample efficiency and training stability, reliably converging to higher cumulative returns, while also exhibiting a favorable trade-off between high performance and low collision rates (e.g., **2.42%** collision rate in Reach with Obstacles).
- It successfully transferred to a real Kinova arm for a fruit-picking task with obstacle avoidance, achieving superior success rates (**93.3%** obstacle avoidance, **80.0%** target reaching) compared to SAC and S2AC, validating its real-world applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The framework establishes a principled connection between Bayesian MPC, the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) framework, and SVGD for trajectory-level inference.
> - Utilizing soft Q-values as an optimality likelihood enables data-driven guidance for particle-based trajectory optimization, effectively moving beyond hand-crafted costs and implicitly handling safety.
> - Non-parametric SVGD particle refinement is crucial for explicitly preserving solution diversity across the trajectory distribution, which overcomes the mode collapse issue prevalent in many control methods.
