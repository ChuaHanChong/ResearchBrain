# Prior Availability in Industrial Visual Sim-to-Real: A Review of CAD-Guided and CAD-Unavailable Regimes

> [!summary] Summary
> A review reframing industrial visual sim-to-real as a domain-gap problem organized by prior availability: CAD-available, boundary-prior, and CAD-unavailable regimes, made operational by a rubric over source generation, correspondence, test-time checking, and calibration. Empirical anchors on T-LESS/BOP, MVTec AD, and VisA show render count alone fails, while domain randomization, detector capacity, and tiny real calibration sets dominate transfer.

## Problem

- Sim-to-real is read narrowly as synthetic-to-real image transfer, ignoring that industrial systems meet mismatched sensors, lighting, materials, fixtures, calibration, and rare defect modes at deployment.
- CAD-based detection and 6D pose literature is surveyed separately from industrial anomaly and surface-inspection work, hiding that both confront the same deployment gap through different priors.
- Cross-task leaderboards rank methods without stating what evidence each prior carries: geometric verification, weak correspondence, or only thresholded appearance statistics and normality assumptions.

## Method

- ==Prior-availability== taxonomy splits industrial vision into CAD-available regimes with renderable geometry, CAD-unavailable regimes using normality or feature substitutes, and boundary priors keeping partial geometric authority.
- A four-channel ==prior-strength rubric== scores each method as absent, weak, or strong on source generation, correspondence, test-time checking, and calibration or decision support, plus a practitioner reporting checklist.
- An empirical anchor suite: ==YOLOv8== source-recipe sweep on T-LESS PBR renders, ==MegaPose== render-and-compare with depth-fusion diagnostics, and ==PatchCore==/==EfficientAD==/==WinCLIP==/==AnomalyDINO== with normal-reference budget curves.

## Results

- Scaling synthetic PBR from **5k** to **50k** lowered real T-LESS mAP50:95 from **0.1521** to **0.1287**; domain randomization reached **0.4041**, larger detector plus **50** real images reached **0.7424**.
- Ground-truth-pose CAD rendering hit **0.8553** visible-mask IoU and **0.9963** oracle proposal AUROC, practical MegaPose gave **0.7322** full-mask IoU, and depth-plus-confidence fusion gave **0.8804** AUROC.
- PatchCore led MVTec AD at **0.9820** image and **0.9801** pixel AUROC, while zero-shot WinCLIP collapsed to **0.6204** MVTec and **0.5900** VisA pixel AUROC, **0.0194** pixel F1.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Synthetic volume is not synthetic coverage: source-distribution design, model capacity, and a 5% real calibration slice outweigh an order-of-magnitude more renders.
> - Geometry's durable value is a test-time verification channel, not just cheap labels; without a mesh, decisions rest on calibrated normality and feature deviation.
> - Normal references are data-efficient for pixel ranking but not for thresholded masks, so report the prior, evidence channel, and operating point explicitly.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

Industrial visual recognition systems are increasingly deployed in environments characterized by limited labeled real data, the rarity of certain conditions (e.g., defects), and dynamic factory conditions (e.g., changes in sensors, lighting, materials, production variation). This context frequently necessitates the use of sim-to-real transfer or source-to-target domain adaptation techniques.

The term "sim-to-real" is often interpreted narrowly as the transfer from synthetic rendered images to real camera images. However, the industrial context presents a broader domain-gap problem, where the source information for system development (e.g., CAD renderings, simulated RGB-D, synthetic defects, pretrained features, language prompts) may differ significantly from the target deployment conditions (e.g., real sensors, varying illumination, material properties, production tolerances, rare defect modes). Existing literature in robotics and industrial vision acknowledges the challenges of transferring models across domains, with reviews covering aspects such as randomized simulations, image-based quality control, object detection, and 6D pose estimation.

This paper positions its work within this landscape by arguing that the most useful organizational principle for industrial visual sim-to-real is "prior availability." It bridges two distinct literatures that are typically reviewed separately: CAD-available industrial vision (e.g., 6D pose estimation, robotic guidance, render-and-compare verification, with datasets like T-LESS and BOP) and CAD-unavailable industrial inspection (e.g., surface defect detection, anomaly detection, with datasets like MVTec AD and VisA). By introducing prior availability as a unifying framework, the paper seeks to provide a more structured understanding of how different types of information affect the design, training, and deployment of industrial vision systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this review is to reframe industrial visual sim-to-real as a domain-gap problem characterized by the nature of "prior availability." The authors contend that the traditional, narrow definition of "sim-to-real" as solely synthetic-to-real image transfer is insufficient for the complexities of industrial deployments. Instead, they propose that the critical distinction lies in whether explicit object geometry, such as CAD models, is available to guide the vision system.

The motivation for this reframing stems from several practical challenges in industrial settings:
- **Data Scarcity and Cost:** Obtaining large, fully labeled real datasets is often prohibitive due to new product lines, the intentional rarity of defects, and the expense of annotating pixel-level masks or 6D poses.
- **Dynamic Deployment Conditions:** Factory environments are subject to constant changes in hardware (cameras, lighting, tooling), materials, backgrounds, and production variability, leading to mismatches between development and deployment conditions.
- **Controllability vs. Completeness of Simulation:** While simulation offers control over data generation, it often incompletely approximates real-world physics, material properties, and sensor behaviors. CAD models may also omit manufacturing tolerances or wear.
- **Sensitivity to Errors:** Industrial tasks frequently demand high precision in localization, alignment, or segmentation, meaning even small domain shifts can lead to operational failures.

To address these issues, the paper establishes a detailed distinction based on prior availability:
- **CAD-available settings:** These environments feature explicit object geometry (e.g., CAD models, renderable meshes). This prior supports the generation of synthetic views, precise camera calibration, 6D pose estimation, segmentation, and geometric verification at test time (e.g., comparing rendered masks or depth with real observations).
- **CAD-unavailable settings:** In these scenarios, explicit object geometry is absent or unusable. The system must instead rely on alternative priors such as normal-reference appearance, feature distributions, reconstruction residuals, synthetic anomaly assumptions, pretrained foundation model representations, or vision-language prompts to infer normality or abnormality.
- **Boundary-prior settings:** These represent intermediate cases where approximate models, templates, sparse reference views, or semantic correspondences provide partial geometric or structural information, blurring the line between full CAD availability and its complete absence.

The authors operationalize this distinction through a rubric examining four channels: source generation, correspondence, test-time checking, and calibration/decision support. This framework is intended to connect disparate research literatures—such as 6D object pose estimation (often CAD-centric) and industrial anomaly detection (often appearance-centric)—which are traditionally reviewed separately. The core motivation is to move beyond a single "cross-task leaderboard" for sim-to-real performance and instead ask what specific prior grounds the deployment decision, thereby guiding the selection of appropriate methods, datasets, and evaluation metrics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this paper primarily involves a comprehensive literature review and a conceptual reframing of industrial visual sim-to-real. This reframing is then anchored by empirical diagnostics designed to illustrate the proposed taxonomy rather than to conduct an exhaustive benchmark.

**Conceptual Framework:**
The central approach is to organize industrial visual sim-to-real along an axis of "prior availability," distinguishing between settings where explicit object geometry (CAD) is available, completely unavailable, or present in a boundary-prior form.
- **Operational Rubric:** A four-channel rubric defines prior strength:
 1. **Source Generation:** How labeled training data are produced (e.g., renderable geometry for synthetic views vs. normal images/synthetic anomalies).
 2. **Correspondence:** How real observations are mapped to underlying structures (e.g., pose/mask/depth to object geometry vs. patch similarity/feature memory).
 3. **Test-Time Checking:** The mechanisms for verifying hypotheses during deployment (e.g., geometric rendering/projection vs. anomaly scores/thresholds).
 4. **Calibration and Decision Support:** How real data or external knowledge tunes system behavior (e.g., detector proposals/geometric rules vs. false-alarm budgets/normality coverage).

**Review of Method Families:**
The paper reviews established and emerging method families within its proposed framework:
- **CAD-Guided Sim-to-Real Methods:** These leverage explicit object geometry.
 - **CAD as Source:** Methods utilizing CAD for synthetic data generation, PBR rendering, and domain randomization (e.g., BlenderProc2, T-LESS/BOP benchmarks).
 - **Model-Based Recognition and Learned 6D Pose:** Approaches that match model evidence to real observations for identity, mask, or 6D pose estimation (e.g., LINEMOD, SSD-6D, PoseCNN, DenseFusion).
 - **Render-and-Compare Verification:** Techniques that keep CAD active at inference by rendering hypotheses for geometric comparison (e.g., MegaPose, FoundationPose).
 - **Foundation-Assisted CAD Matching:** Integration of foundation model features (e.g., SAM, DINOv2) to enhance CAD correspondence (e.g., FoundPose, SAM-6D).
 - **Boundary-Prior Variants:** Methods using approximate meshes, partial scans, or reference views.
- **CAD-Unavailable Industrial Inspection Methods:** These rely on "replacement priors."
 - **Normal-Reference Memory:** Storing or modeling dense normal features to detect deviations (e.g., PatchCore, PaDiM).
 - **Teacher-Student Residuals:** Detecting anomalies via discrepancies between teacher and student models trained on normal data (e.g., EfficientAD, Reverse Distillation).
 - **Synthetic-Anomaly Self-Supervision:** Generating artificial defects on normal images to create supervisory signals (e.g., DRAEM, CutPaste, SimpleNet).
 - **Vision-Language Weak Priors:** Using language-aligned features or prompts for semantic comparison (e.g., WinCLIP, AnomalyCLIP).
 - **Dense Foundation Features:** Employing self-supervised visual features for transferable similarity priors (e.g., AnomalyDINO with DINOv2).
 - **Robustness and Logical Anomalies:** Discussion of benchmarks and methods addressing more complex industrial scenarios (e.g., MVTec LOCO AD, MVTec 3D-AD).
 - **Generative and LVLM Boundary Directions:** Emerging approaches using diffusion models for defect synthesis or large vision-language models for reasoning (e.g., AnomalyDiffusion, AnomalyGPT).

**Empirical Anchors:**
To concretize the taxonomy, the paper uses empirical anchors on specific datasets and methods. These are not intended as a new benchmark but as diagnostics for the review's arguments.
- **CAD-Guided Anchor (T-LESS/BOP dataset):**
 - **CAD as Synthetic Renderer:** YOLOv8 detectors are trained on synthetic T-LESS PBR images and evaluated on real Primesense images. The experiments vary synthetic source size, domain randomization, real background negatives, detector capacity, and small real fine-tuning (50 images). Metrics include mAP@0.5 and mAP@0.5:0.95.
 - **CAD at Test Time:** MegaPose is used to instantiate render-and-compare verification. Diagnostics include an "oracle geometry check" (CAD render from ground truth pose vs. masks), detector ROI recall as a bridge to CAD inference, and "oracle CAD verification" (separating good/bad proposals with ideal geometric scores). Practical MegaPose runs estimate 6D pose from YOLOv8 proposals, rendering predicted poses to compute full-mask IoU, visible coverage, and depth-fusion AUROC.
- **CAD-Unavailable Anchor (MVTec AD and VisA datasets):**
 - **Normality and Foundation Priors:** Representative methods from the CAD-unavailable families are evaluated: PatchCore (normal-reference memory), EfficientAD-S (teacher-student residuals), WinCLIP (zero-shot vision-language), and AnomalyDINO (dense foundation features based on DINOv2). These are trained only on normal images and evaluated on anomalous test images. Metrics include image AUROC, pixel AUROC, image F1, and pixel F1, macro-averaged over categories.
 - **Normal-Reference Budget Diagnostic:** PatchCore and AnomalyDINO-S are evaluated on selected MVTec AD and VisA categories using reduced percentages (5%, 10%, 25%, 100%) of normal training images to assess data efficiency for defining normality.

The methodology emphasizes comparing the *mechanisms* by which different priors affect transfer, calibration, and verification, rather than providing a single performance ranking across disparate industrial tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical anchors and literature review collectively support the paper's central thesis: the efficacy and strategy of industrial sim-to-real depend fundamentally on the nature of the available prior information.

**Findings for CAD-Guided Sim-to-Real (T-LESS/BOP Anchors):**
- **Synthetic Data Quantity vs. Quality:** Increasing the volume of plain PBR-rendered synthetic images (from 5k to 50k for YOLOv8n) did not improve real-domain detection accuracy (mAP@0.5:0.95 decreased from 0.1521 to 0.1287). This indicates that simply more synthetic data is insufficient to close the sim-to-real gap.
- **Importance of Source Distribution Design:** Incorporating strong domain randomization during synthetic training significantly improved performance (mAP@0.5:0.95 to 0.4041 for YOLOv8n). This suggests that designing the synthetic distribution to cover deployment variability is more critical than raw image count.
- **Impact of Real Calibration:** A small amount of real labeled data proved highly effective for calibration. Fine-tuning a domain-randomized model with only 50 labeled real images boosted mAP@0.5:0.95 from 0.4041 to 0.6265. Combining a larger detector (YOLOv8s) with domain randomization and this small real calibration set yielded the highest performance (mAP@0.5:0.95 of 0.7424).
- **Detector Capacity:** Using a larger detector backbone (YOLOv8s) with domain randomization, even without real labels, achieved strong performance (mAP@0.5:0.95 of 0.6077), indicating that model capacity contributes to absorbing domain mismatch.
- **CAD at Test Time for Verification:** CAD provides a distinct geometric verification channel. Oracle diagnostics showed high internal consistency of CAD models and ground-truth poses (0.8553 mean visible-mask IoU). A practical render-and-compare pipeline (MegaPose) on detector proposals achieved 0.7322 mean full-mask IoU and 0.9218 mean ground-truth visible coverage for valid proposals, demonstrating that geometry can explain object regions. Fusing rendered depth consistency with detector confidence improved good-pose AUROC to 0.8804, indicating that metric geometry adds information beyond appearance-based detection, despite practical challenges like proposal errors.

**Findings for CAD-Unavailable Industrial Inspection (MVTec AD/VisA Anchors):**
- **Effectiveness of Normal-Reference Memory:** Methods relying on normal-reference memory (PatchCore) consistently performed well, achieving high image and pixel AUROC on both MVTec AD (0.9820 image AUROC, 0.9801 pixel AUROC) and VisA (0.9080 image AUROC, 0.9807 pixel AUROC). This highlights the strength of defining normality from real examples when geometry is absent.
- **Teacher-Student Residuals for Dense Signals:** Teacher-student models (EfficientAD-S) also demonstrated strong performance, particularly in pixel-level metrics (0.9512 pixel AUROC on MVTec AD, 0.9766 on VisA), indicating their efficacy in generating dense anomaly signals.
- **Limitations of Zero-Shot Vision-Language Priors for Localization:** While zero-shot vision-language models (WinCLIP) showed moderate image-level anomaly classification (0.8812 image AUROC on MVTec AD), their pixel-level localization performance was notably lower (0.6204 pixel AUROC on MVTec AD, 0.5900 on VisA). This suggests that broad semantic priors may struggle with the fine-grained, texture-level industrial defects.
- **Strength of Dense Visual Foundation Features:** Methods leveraging dense self-supervised visual foundation features (AnomalyDINO-S with DINOv2-Small) achieved performance comparable to normal-reference methods (0.9793 image AUROC, 0.9700 pixel AUROC on MVTec AD; 0.9109 image AUROC, 0.9568 pixel AUROC on VisA). A larger backbone (DINOv2-Large) further improved VisA image AUROC (0.9330), but did not monotonically enhance pixel-level localization. This indicates foundation features can provide strong transferable spatial priors.
- **Data Efficiency of Normal References:** The normal-reference budget diagnostic showed that CAD-unavailable methods can maintain high pixel AUROC even with limited normal training images (e.g., PatchCore at 5% of normal data yielded 0.9819 pixel AUROC on selected MVTec AD categories). Image-level discrimination and thresholded F1 scores, however, improved more gradually with increased reference coverage.

**Cross-Regime Synthesis:**
The empirical results reinforce that industrial sim-to-real is not a monolithic problem. CAD availability enables geometric verification, transforming the domain gap into a geometrically addressable problem. In contrast, CAD-unavailable scenarios necessitate reliance on appearance-based or statistical priors, where calibration and the definition of "normality" become central.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a refined framework for understanding and addressing industrial visual sim-to-real challenges, moving beyond a simplistic "synthetic vs. real" dichotomy to a more nuanced view based on "prior availability." The significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Conceptual Clarity and Unification:** The paper's primary contribution is the introduction of "prior availability" as an organizing axis. This framework offers conceptual clarity by categorizing industrial vision problems into CAD-available, CAD-unavailable, and boundary-prior regimes. It unifies previously disparate literatures—such as 6D object pose estimation and industrial anomaly detection—under a common theoretical lens, explaining why different strategies are effective in different contexts.
- **Guidance for Method Selection and Design:** For researchers and practitioners, the framework provides explicit guidance on selecting appropriate methodologies. When CAD is available, the emphasis shifts towards effective synthetic data distribution design, smart integration of small real calibration sets, and leveraging CAD at test time for geometric verification (e.g., render-and-compare). When CAD is unavailable, the focus is on developing robust "replacement priors" such as normal-reference memories, teacher-student models, or dense foundation features, and on calibrating anomaly scores for reliable decision-making.
- **Informed Evaluation and Benchmarking:** The paper argues against a single, cross-task leaderboard, advocating instead for evaluations that explicitly state the available prior. This promotes more meaningful comparisons by aligning metrics and test conditions with the type of evidence a method can leverage. For CAD-available systems, evaluation should consider both detection performance and geometric verification capabilities. For CAD-unavailable systems, metrics should account for image-level ranking, pixel-level localization, and the stability of decision thresholds.
- **Practical Implications for Deployment:** The findings highlight that even small amounts of real data play distinct but crucial roles in both regimes: calibrating synthetic transfer in CAD-available settings and defining normality in CAD-unavailable inspection. This informs strategies for managing data collection budgets in industrial deployments. The persistent challenges, such as proposal errors and pose ambiguity in CAD-guided systems, and threshold stability and dense mask quality in CAD-unavailable systems, are explicitly identified, guiding future research toward robust deployment.
- **Structured Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies "boundary-prior" methods (e.g., approximate models, foundation-assisted matching, generative anomalies, LVLMs) as key areas where the distinction between CAD-available and CAD-unavailable regimes blurs. This encourages investigation into how partial or weak priors can be effectively utilized, pushing the frontier of industrial vision. It also points to the importance of test-time adaptation and uncertainty estimation within this framework.
- **Reporting Standards:** The proposed "practitioner reporting checklist" (Table 10) offers a concrete tool to enhance transparency and reproducibility in industrial sim-to-real studies by requiring explicit reporting of the available prior, evidence channels, calibration budgets, operating points, and stress audit results.

In conclusion, this review significantly advances the discourse on industrial visual sim-to-real by providing a robust, prior-centric framework. Its impact lies in offering a more coherent understanding of the field, guiding methodological choices, and informing the development of more robust and deployable industrial vision systems.
