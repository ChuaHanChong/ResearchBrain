# Learning from the Self-future: On-policy Self-distillation for dLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> d-OPSD introduces the first on-policy self-distillation framework tailored for diffusion large language models, leveraging 'self future-experience' and step-level divergence supervision to enable self-improvement. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance in reasoning tasks while demonstrating superior sample efficiency, converging in approximately 10% of the optimization steps required by RLVR methods.

## Problem

- Existing on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) frameworks are designed for autoregressive (AR) LLMs, relying on prefix conditioning and token-level supervision, making them incompatible with dLLMs' arbitrary-order, iterative denoising process.
- Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs) lacked an on-policy self-distillation method, limiting their self-improvement capabilities compared to AR LLMs which benefit from such dense supervision techniques.
- Teacher construction in AR-OPSD relies on static prefix conditioning, which does not fully leverage dLLMs' bidirectional generation capabilities and may offer limited 'new knowledge' for distillation.

## Method

- Developed **d-OPSD**, an OPSD framework for dLLMs, incorporating a novel self-teacher construction based on '==self future-experience==' via suffix conditioning from the student model's own generated answers.
- Introduced step-level divergence supervision, which aligns with the iterative denoising process of dLLMs by computing KL divergence over currently masked tokens predicted at each denoising step.
- Implemented practical considerations such as pointwise KL clipping, LoRA, and computing loss only on correctly generated trajectories to stabilize and optimize the training process.

## Results

- d-OPSD consistently outperformed or matched state-of-the-art SFT and RLVR baselines across various mathematical and planning reasoning tasks (GSM8K, MATH500, Countdown, Sudoku).
- The framework demonstrated vastly superior sample efficiency, achieving strong performance in approximately **10%** of the optimization steps required by RLVR methods like diffu-GRPO.
- Analysis revealed that d-OPSD's 'self future-experience' teacher generated significantly more 'new knowledge' for transfer compared to AR-style prefix-conditioned teachers, indicated by suitable overlap in Top-K vocabulary distributions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging the student's 'self future-experience' through suffix conditioning provides a richer and more effective learning signal than static prefix conditioning for dLLMs, enabling the teacher to impart more novel 'thinking patterns'.
> - Step-level divergence supervision is crucial for dLLMs, as it provides dense, appropriate-granularity feedback aligned with their parallel, iterative denoising mechanism, unlike token-level supervision.
> - Careful design choices, including using reverse KL divergence, selecting active tokens for divergence from the teacher distribution, and especially pointwise KL clipping, are essential for d-OPSD's stability and performance gains.
