# Learning from the Self-future: On-policy Self-distillation for dLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> d-OPSD introduces the first on-policy self-distillation framework tailored for diffusion large language models, leveraging 'self future-experience' and step-level divergence supervision to enable self-improvement. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance in reasoning tasks while demonstrating superior sample efficiency, converging in approximately 10% of the optimization steps required by RLVR methods.

## Problem

- Existing on-policy self-distillation (OPSD) frameworks are designed for autoregressive (AR) LLMs, relying on prefix conditioning and token-level supervision, making them incompatible with dLLMs' arbitrary-order, iterative denoising process.
- Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs) lacked an on-policy self-distillation method, limiting their self-improvement capabilities compared to AR LLMs which benefit from such dense supervision techniques.
- Teacher construction in AR-OPSD relies on static prefix conditioning, which does not fully leverage dLLMs' bidirectional generation capabilities and may offer limited 'new knowledge' for distillation.

## Method

- Developed **d-OPSD**, an OPSD framework for dLLMs, incorporating a novel self-teacher construction based on '==self future-experience==' via suffix conditioning from the student model's own generated answers.
- Introduced step-level divergence supervision, which aligns with the iterative denoising process of dLLMs by computing KL divergence over currently masked tokens predicted at each denoising step.
- Implemented practical considerations such as pointwise KL clipping, LoRA, and computing loss only on correctly generated trajectories to stabilize and optimize the training process.

## Results

- d-OPSD consistently outperformed or matched state-of-the-art SFT and RLVR baselines across various mathematical and planning reasoning tasks (GSM8K, MATH500, Countdown, Sudoku).
- The framework demonstrated vastly superior sample efficiency, achieving strong performance in approximately **10%** of the optimization steps required by RLVR methods like diffu-GRPO.
- Analysis revealed that d-OPSD's 'self future-experience' teacher generated significantly more 'new knowledge' for transfer compared to AR-style prefix-conditioned teachers, indicated by suitable overlap in Top-K vocabulary distributions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging the student's 'self future-experience' through suffix conditioning provides a richer and more effective learning signal than static prefix conditioning for dLLMs, enabling the teacher to impart more novel 'thinking patterns'.
> - Step-level divergence supervision is crucial for dLLMs, as it provides dense, appropriate-granularity feedback aligned with their parallel, iterative denoising mechanism, unlike token-level supervision.
> - Careful design choices, including using reverse KL divergence, selecting active tokens for divergence from the teacher distribution, and especially pointwise KL clipping, are essential for d-OPSD's stability and performance gains.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has seen significant advancements, broadly categorized into autoregressive (AR) models and more recently, diffusion LLMs (dLLMs). Post-training techniques are crucial for enhancing the capabilities of these models, particularly for complex reasoning tasks.

**On-policy Distillation (OPD) and Self-Distillation (OPSD) for AR LLMs:**
On-policy distillation (OPD) is a paradigm that has emerged for post-training AR LLMs. Unlike Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), which uses static datasets and can suffer from exposure bias, OPD involves a student model sampling its own trajectories. A stronger teacher model then provides dense, token-level supervision, addressing the issue of sparse outcome rewards common in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). Recent extensions, known as On-policy Self-Distillation (OPSD), remove the need for an external stronger teacher by having a single model serve as its own teacher, albeit with the injection of privileged information. OPSD has been shown to be a powerful framework for self-improvement in AR LLMs.

**Diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs):**
Concurrently, dLLMs have gained attention as an alternative to AR LLMs. These models frame language generation as an iterative denoising process, deviating from the strict left-to-right dependency of AR models. This architectural difference provides dLLMs with unique advantages, such as arbitrary-order generation and potentially faster inference speeds. Recent research has successfully applied RLVR methods to dLLMs to improve their reasoning abilities through post-training.

**The Research Gap:**
Despite the effectiveness of OPSD for AR LLMs and the emerging potential of dLLMs, the application of OPSD specifically to dLLMs has remained largely unexplored. Existing OPSD methods are fundamentally designed for the autoregressive nature of AR LLMs, relying on left-to-right prefix conditioning and token-level divergence supervision. These design choices are inherently incompatible with the arbitrary-order, iterative denoising process of dLLMs. This paper aims to bridge this gap by developing an OPSD framework specifically tailored for dLLMs, thus extending the benefits of self-improvement through dense on-policy supervision to this new class of language models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce and validate the first On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) framework specifically tailored for diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs), termed d-OPSD. The research is motivated by the observation that while OPSD has proven effective for autoregressive (AR) LLMs, its application to dLLMs is unexplored, mainly due to fundamental conflicts between existing OPSD formulations and the operational characteristics of dLLMs.

The authors identify two core questions:
1. **Is there a better OPSD formulation tailored specifically for dLLMs?** The paper argues for an affirmative answer, proposing that both the self-teacher construction and the level of divergence supervision can be optimized for dLLMs.
2. **Does OPSD outperform Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in enhancing the reasoning ability of dLLMs?** The paper aims to demonstrate that a dLLM-specific OPSD can achieve superior results in both reasoning performance and sample efficiency compared to RLVR.

The motivation for these objectives stems from several key insights:

- **Suboptimality of Existing OPSD for dLLMs:** Current OPSD approaches for AR models typically construct a self-teacher by appending privileged information (e.g., reference solutions) as a prefix to the prompt. This design is intrinsically linked to AR models' left-to-right generation, where only `p(suffix|prefix)` conditioning is available. dLLMs, however, generate sequences non-autoregressively, offering bidirectional modeling capabilities, including `p(prefix|suffix)`. This presents an opportunity to incorporate privileged information in a more aligned manner.
- **Leveraging "Self Future-Experience":** The bidirectional nature of dLLMs enables a novel approach to self-teacher construction. Instead of using static reference solutions as privileged information, dLLMs can utilize their *own self-generated answers* as suffix conditioning. This allows the student model to learn from its "self future-experience," akin to reflecting on past outcomes to improve future actions. This is posited to provide richer, more relevant knowledge (thinking patterns) for transfer compared to static references.
- **Incompatibility of Token-level Supervision:** AR models naturally use token-level divergence supervision, as they predict the next token sequentially. dLLMs, conversely, predict all masked tokens simultaneously at each denoising step, selectively revealing a subset and re-masking others. Consequently, token-level supervision is incompatible with the iterative denoising process of dLLMs.
- **Necessity of Step-level Supervision:** Each denoising step in a dLLM can be conceptualized as an independent Markov transition. This suggests that step-level divergence supervision is a more natural and compatible choice for dLLMs, aligning the OPSD objective with the model's core generative mechanism.

By addressing these fundamental mismatches, the authors aim to unlock the full potential of OPSD for dLLMs, leading to a more effective and sample-efficient post-training paradigm for enhancing their reasoning capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, d-OPSD, is an On-Policy Self-Distillation framework specifically designed for dLLMs. It operates by having a single dLLM act as both student and teacher, leveraging novel approaches to teacher construction and divergence supervision.

#### 3.1. Self-Teacher Construction: Learning from the Self-future

The core innovation in teacher construction lies in utilizing the student model's own self-generated answers as privileged information. This contrasts with AR-centric OPSD methods that use prefix conditioning with static reference solutions.
- **On-policy Trajectory Generation:** Given a prompt `x`, the student model `p_θ` first samples a complete `T`-step denoising trajectory `Y = {y_T..., y_0}`, where `y_T` is a fully masked sequence and `y_0` is the final generated response.
- **Student Input:** At each denoising step `t`, the student's input is simply the current noisy sequence `y_student,t = y_t`.
- **Teacher Input with Suffix Conditioning:** For the teacher, the input `y_teacher,t` is constructed by partially revealing tokens from the *final self-generated response `y_0`*. Specifically, for each token position `i`, `y_i_teacher,t` is `y_i_0` if `i` belongs to a selected revealing subset `S_t`, and `y_i_t` otherwise. `S_t` is a subset of currently masked positions, randomly selected with a fixed retaining ratio `ρ_teacher`. This leverages the dLLM's ability to model `p(prefix|suffix)`.
- **"Self Future-Experience":** This approach allows the teacher to benefit from the model's own "future" knowledge (the complete self-generated answer), providing dense supervision for the student to learn from its on-policy trajectory.
- **Pass@k Sampling:** To ensure the quality of the "self future-experience," the student samples multiple trajectories for a given prompt until a correct final answer `y_0` is generated, or a maximum iteration threshold (default `k=8`) is met. This ensures the teacher is provided with a beneficial signal.

#### 3.2. Step-level Divergence Supervision

Given the iterative denoising process of dLLMs, token-level supervision (common in AR models) is deemed unsuitable.
- **Incompatibility of Token-level:** dLLMs predict all masked tokens simultaneously at each denoising step, only revealing a subset and re-masking others. This makes token-level factorization inappropriate.
- **Step-level Approach:** Each denoising step `t` is treated as an independent Markov transition. The model computes full-sequence distributions `P_i_student,t = p_θ(y_i|y_student,t, x)` and `P_i_teacher,t = p_θ(y_i|y_teacher,t, x)` for all tokens `i`.
- **Focused Supervision:** Supervision is applied only to a "top-k subset" `K_t` of tokens. `K_t` comprises the `k` most confident predictions among the currently masked positions, as only these tokens are chosen for revelation in that specific step. The sum of sizes of these subsets across all steps equals the total sequence length (`Sum |K_t| = L`).
- **Objective Function:** The overall learning objective for d-OPSD is to minimize the step-level KL divergence over these `K_t` subsets, averaged across the trajectory:
 `L_OPSD(θ) = E_x [ (1/T) Sum_{t=1 to T} (1/|K_t|) Sum_{i∈K_t} D_KL(P_i_student,t || P_i_teacher,t) ]`.
 The default divergence measure is reverse KL.
- **K_t Selection:** Ablation studies indicate that deriving `K_t` from the teacher's distribution rather than the student's yields better performance, as it forces the student to align with the teacher's most confident predictions.

#### 3.3. Additional Implementation Details

- **Pointwise KL Clipping:** Pointwise clipping is applied to vocabulary-level divergence contributions to stabilize training, as token-level divergence can be highly skewed. The clipping threshold is typically set to 0.05.
- **Fixed Teacher Policy:** The teacher policy is fixed to the initial policy throughout training to enhance stability.
- **Inputs Concatenation:** To mitigate Out-of-Memory (OOM) issues resulting from storing full attention gradients across all denoising steps, all step-level inputs for a trajectory are concatenated into a single batch. This allows for shared model computations without requiring separate gradient storage for each step.
- **Loss Computation on Correct Generations:** By default, the loss objective is computed only on trajectories that result in a correct final answer (`y_0`). For tasks without binary right/wrong answers (e.g., Sudoku scores), a score threshold (e.g., 0.25) is used.

This methodology constructs a novel OPSD framework that aligns with the unique generation mechanism of dLLMs, offering dense, on-policy supervision derived from the model's self-generated "future" experiences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across four reasoning benchmarks—GSM8K, MATH500 (mathematical reasoning), Countdown, and Sudoku (planning)—demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of d-OPSD.

#### 4.1. Toy Verification: Self-Teacher Strength

A preliminary verification confirmed that the self-teacher is sufficiently strong to guide distillation. By conditioning on self-teacher inputs (constructed with Pass@8 and varying `ρ_teacher`), the model could resume correct answers, significantly outperforming the unconditioned student model (Table 3). Even with a moderate `ρ_teacher = 0.10`, the self-teacher demonstrated improved performance, and at higher `ρ_teacher`, its performance closely matched its origin (Pass@8). This validated the premise that the self-teacher could provide high-quality distillation guidance.

#### 4.2. Reasoning Performance Comparison

d-OPSD consistently outperformed or matched existing baselines across the four reasoning tasks and various generation lengths (Table 1).
- **Superiority over SFT:** d-OPSD achieved better performance than both the SFT Variant [Zhao et al., 2025] and d3LLM [Qian et al., 2026], an existing off-policy self-distillation approach. For instance, on GSM8K (256 length), d-OPSD scored 81.0 compared to SFT Variant's 78.8 and d3LLM's 72.7.
- **Superiority over RLVR:** d-OPSD also demonstrated superior or comparable performance against RLVR baselines, including diffu-GRPO [Zhao et al., 2025] and VRPO [Zhu et al., 2025]. For example, on Countdown (128 length), d-OPSD achieved 37.9, surpassing diffu-GRPO's 33.2 and VRPO's 21.9. In Sudoku (256 length), d-OPSD reached 20.6, significantly higher than diffu-GRPO's 12.9 and VRPO's 9.6.

#### 4.3. Sample Efficiency Comparison

d-OPSD exhibited vastly superior sample efficiency compared to the RLVR baseline (diffu-GRPO) (Table 2 and Figure 1).
- **Reduced Optimization Steps:** d-OPSD converged to its peak performance using only approximately 10% of the optimization steps required by diffu-GRPO across all tasks. For example, on GSM8K, d-OPSD required 425 steps compared to diffu-GRPO's 7700 steps. This efficiency is attributed to the dense, token-level supervision provided by the teacher distribution.

#### 4.4. Comparison with AR-style OPSD: Unlocking New Knowledge

The study directly compared d-OPSD's self-teacher construction with an AR-style counterpart (prefix conditioning with a reference solution).
- **Performance Gap:** d-OPSD's tailored self-teacher construction significantly outperformed the AR-style OPSD (Table 4). For instance, on GSM8K (256 length), d-OPSD achieved 81.0, while AR-style OPSD reached 78.4.
- **Mechanism (Overlap Top-K_t):** An analysis using "Overlap Top-K_t" (proportion of shared top-K vocabulary tokens between student and teacher distributions) revealed the reason. AR-style OPSD exhibited an extremely high overlap (near 1.0), indicating that simply appending a reference solution as a prefix did not introduce substantial new "knowledge" or "thinking patterns" for the student to learn from the teacher. In contrast, d-OPSD maintained a suitable `Overlap Top-K_t` (Figure 3), suggesting it provided more novel information to facilitate learning.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Divergence Objective (Table 5):** Reverse KL consistently outperformed Forward KL, which is attributed to reverse KL's model-seeking behavior, offering greater robustness.
- **Retaining Ratio (ρ_teacher) (Table 6):** Different `ρ_teacher` values had moderate impacts. `ρ_teacher = 0.25` (default) yielded the best results, but even `ρ_teacher = 0.10` performed better than `ρ_teacher = 0.50`, suggesting that the distillation effectiveness is not solely dependent on the teacher's absolute accuracy.
- **Top-k Subset (K_t) Selection (Table 7):** Selecting `K_t` from the teacher's distribution resulted in higher performance than from the student's, as it provided a stronger learning signal by guiding the student towards the teacher's most confident predictions.
- **Pass@k Sampling (Table 8):** While `k=8` (default) yielded slightly better reasoning performance, `k=1` still surpassed the RLVR baseline and offered even greater sample efficiency.
- **Per-token Pointwise Clipping (Table 9):** Pointwise clipping significantly improved performance and, more critically, stabilized training. Without clipping, the model experienced policy collapse around step 150, with performance substantially degrading.
- **Teacher Fixing (Table 10):** Fixing the teacher model to the initial policy during training led to greater performance gains compared to an unfixed teacher, though even the unfixed variant nearly matched RLVR baselines.
- **Compute Only on Correct Generations (Table 11):** Computing the loss only on correct generations yielded superior results compared to computing on all trajectories, although the latter still outperformed RLVR.

#### 4.6. Failure Modes

Similar to RLVR, d-OPSD was observed to be susceptible to policy collapse in certain settings after reaching peak performance (Figure 12). This phenomenon is hypothesized to stem from the model-seeking behavior of the objective becoming overly narrow, hindering further learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the post-training of diffusion Large Language Models (dLLMs) and has several significant implications:

- **Pioneering OPSD for dLLMs:** d-OPSD is the first on-policy self-distillation framework tailored specifically for dLLMs. This addresses a previously unexplored area, extending the proven benefits of OPSD (dense supervision, exposure bias mitigation) to the dLLM architecture.
- **Architectural Alignment for Non-Autoregressive Models:** The work highlights the critical importance of aligning post-training methodologies with the underlying model architecture. By redesigning self-teacher construction to leverage dLLM's bidirectional generation (suffix conditioning from self-generated "future") and adapting supervision from token-level to step-level, the paper demonstrates how to effectively harness the unique properties of dLLMs for self-improvement. This provides a blueprint for future work in non-autoregressive language model training.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Performance:** The consistent outperformance or matching of d-OPSD against strong SFT and RLVR baselines across diverse reasoning tasks (mathematical reasoning and planning) indicates its efficacy in improving dLLM capabilities. This suggests that d-OPSD can enable dLLMs to tackle complex problems with higher accuracy.
- **Superior Sample Efficiency:** A significant finding is d-OPSD's vastly superior sample efficiency, requiring only approximately 10% of the optimization steps compared to RLVR. This reduction in training time and computational resources makes the post-training process more accessible and scalable, facilitating faster development and deployment of capable dLLMs.
- **New Knowledge Transfer Mechanism:** The analysis demonstrating that d-OPSD's teacher construction introduces more "new knowledge" or "thinking patterns" compared to AR-style prefix conditioning is crucial. It underscores the potential for innovative ways to transfer information within a self-distillation loop, especially for models with flexible generation orders.
- **Robustness and Stability Insights:** The ablation studies provide valuable insights into crucial training elements such as divergence objectives, teacher fixing strategies, and the necessity of pointwise clipping for training stability. These findings can guide future research and practical implementations of OPSD for dLLMs, helping to prevent issues like policy collapse.
- **Promising Pathway for dLLM Post-training:** Overall, d-OPSD opens a promising new pathway for dLLM post-training. Its ability to achieve high performance with remarkable sample efficiency positions it as a competitive and potentially preferred method for fine-tuning dLLMs for reasoning and other complex tasks. Future work can build upon these foundations to further stabilize and enhance the OPSD framework for diffusion models.
