# InternSpatial: A Comprehensive Dataset for Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Shanghai AI Laboratory introduces InternSpatial, a comprehensive open-source dataset with 12 million QA pairs designed to enhance spatial reasoning in Vision-Language Models. Training with InternSpatial led to InternVL-Spatial-8B achieving an average 12.1% accuracy improvement on a new spatial benchmark and outperforming proprietary models like GPT-4o on spatial tasks.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) consistently struggle with spatial reasoning in both single-view and multi-view contexts, limiting their applicability in robotics, autonomous navigation, and augmented/virtual reality.
- Existing spatial reasoning datasets suffer from limited scene diversity, restricted instruction formats, a strong single-view bias, and are often not openly accessible.
- Prior spatial training data predominantly focuses on single-view contexts, neglecting the complex multi-view or temporal spatial relationships crucial for dynamic environments.

## Method

- Developed **InternSpatial**, a comprehensive 12-million QA pair dataset, using a fully automated and modular ==data engine== to consolidate annotation extraction and QA synthesis from heterogeneous data sources.
- Generated diverse QA pairs at scale using an ==LLM-guided template-based== approach, expanding each into up to 19 distinct instruction formats combining textual and visual cues (e.g., ==bounding boxes==, masks, numbered regions).
- Introduced a substantial multi-view component for training and evaluation, including a novel ==rotation angle prediction== task, along with **InternSpatial-Bench**, a manually verified diagnostic benchmark for spatial reasoning.

## Results

- InternVL-Spatial-8B, trained on InternSpatial, achieved an average **12.1%** accuracy improvement on InternSpatial-Bench, with notable gains of **25.0%** in Position Comparison and **20.9%** in Size Comparison.
- The model surpassed advanced proprietary models such as GPT-4o-2024-11-20 and Claude 3.5 Sonnet on InternSpatial-Bench, and achieved the highest overall score (**52.3%**) among evaluated models on VSI-Bench for multi-view tasks.
- Training with InternSpatial's diverse instruction formats significantly narrowed performance gaps across instruction styles, improving robustness and overall spatial reasoning without compromising general VQA performance on standard benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training VLMs on large-scale, diverse spatial data, including multi-view scenarios and varied instruction formats, substantially improves their spatial reasoning capabilities.
> - A template-based QA generation approach, complemented by robust annotation extraction and alignment to a canonical view space, allows for efficient creation of high-quality, large-scale spatial datasets.
> - Enhancing a VLM's spatial reasoning does not degrade its general multimodal performance, enabling the development of more versatile and capable general-purpose VLMs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have made remarkable strides in various multimodal tasks, including Visual Question Answering (VQA), image captioning, and grounding. However, despite these advancements, VLMs consistently exhibit significant limitations in **spatial reasoning**. This struggle is evident in both single-view scenarios (e.g., precise object position or size from a static image) and multi-view/temporal contexts (e.g., estimating distances or tracking object appearance across video frames). The inability to robustly understand spatial relationships severely constrains the applicability of VLMs in critical real-world domains such as robotics, autonomous navigation, and augmented/virtual reality, where accurate spatial comprehension is paramount for effective interaction with physical environments.

The existing research landscape has seen several efforts to enhance spatial reasoning in VLMs through specialized datasets and benchmarks, such as SpatialVLM, SpatialQA, OSD, and VSI-Bench. While these works have contributed to the field by focusing on 2D and 3D spatial relationships, they suffer from notable shortcomings that InternSpatial aims to address:
- **Limited Scene Diversity:** Many existing datasets are confined to narrow visual environments, primarily indoor or outdoor scenes, lacking the breadth required for generalizable spatial understanding.
- **Restricted Instruction Formats:** Datasets often rely on a single input modality (e.g., natural language or region masks), failing to capture the rich diversity of human-expressed spatial queries, which can include various visual cues (bounding boxes, masks, numbered regions) alongside text.
- **Narrow Training Scope (Single-View Bias):** Prior spatial training data largely concentrate on single-view contexts, neglecting the complex multi-view or temporal spatial relationships crucial for dynamic environments.
- **Proprietary or Limited Access:** Some valuable datasets are not publicly available, hindering broader research and development.

InternSpatial directly positions itself as a solution to these limitations. It contributes to the landscape by providing the largest *open-source* dataset specifically designed for spatial reasoning, offering unprecedented scale, a wider array of visual environments, and a rich diversity of instruction formats. Furthermore, it explicitly tackles multi-view reasoning, including a novel rotation angle prediction task, which extends beyond the scope of previous benchmarks. By addressing these gaps, InternSpatial aims to accelerate the development of more capable and deployable VLMs, bridging the current performance deficit in spatial understanding for practical applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the creation of InternSpatial and InternSpatial-Bench are directly derived from the identified limitations of existing resources for spatial reasoning in VLMs:

- **Overcome Limitations in Scale and Diversity:** A core motivation is to provide a significantly larger and more visually diverse dataset than currently available. Existing datasets are often limited in size and confined to specific scene types (e.g., only indoor or only outdoor). InternSpatial seeks to aggregate data from a wide spectrum of visual environments, including in-the-wild, structured indoor spaces, urban streetscapes, object-centric scenes, and embodied navigation contexts, to foster robust and generalizable spatial understanding in VLMs.
- **Enhance Instruction Expressiveness:** The paper aims to address the restricted instruction formats prevalent in current datasets. Real-world spatial queries are multimodal and varied, incorporating visual cues (like bounding boxes, segmentation masks, numerical indicators) alongside natural language. InternSpatial is designed to support 19 distinct instruction formats, enabling VLMs to learn to interpret diverse query styles, thereby improving their utility in practical applications.
- **Advance Multi-View Spatial Reasoning:** A significant objective is to move beyond the predominant single-view focus of existing spatial datasets. Multi-view understanding is crucial for applications like robotics and embodied AI, which interact with dynamic 3D environments. InternSpatial introduces a substantial multi-view component, including a novel rotation angle prediction task, to specifically train and evaluate VLMs on complex spatial relationships across different viewpoints or temporal sequences.
- **Provide a Comprehensive Open-Source Resource:** The authors explicitly aim to release InternSpatial as the *largest open-source* dataset for spatial reasoning, coupled with a comprehensive evaluation benchmark (InternSpatial-Bench). This open availability is crucial for fostering community-wide research, enabling researchers to fine-tune and evaluate VLMs on a challenging and diverse spatial reasoning task set.
- **Support Practical AI Applications:** Ultimately, the overarching motivation is to support the development of spatially capable VLMs that can be effectively deployed in real-world applications. By improving spatial understanding, InternSpatial seeks to facilitate advancements in robotics, autonomous navigation, augmented reality, and other domains where accurate environmental interaction is critical.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The creation of InternSpatial and InternSpatial-Bench involved a sophisticated, multi-stage methodology designed for scalability, diversity, and quality.

#### 3.1. Data Engine for InternSpatial

The core of the dataset generation is a "fully automated and modular data engine" capable of consolidating annotation extraction and QA synthesis across heterogeneous data sources. This engine operates in three main steps:

1. **Generating Image-level Annotation:**
 - For image datasets that already provide 2D bounding boxes, region descriptions, or segmentation masks, these existing labels are directly utilized.
 - For datasets lacking such annotations, pretrained models are employed:
 - Open-source VLMs are used to extract object-level 2D boxes and associated textual descriptions.
 - SAM2 (Segment Anything Model 2) is applied to generate segmentation masks within these detected boxes.
 - These 2D masks are then lifted into 3D space to construct 3D bounding boxes, enabling 3D spatial reasoning.
 - For 3D datasets, which include global 3D annotations and per-view camera parameters, 3D information is projected onto the image plane to obtain corresponding 2D annotations, essential for supporting visual reference forms in prompts (e.g., bounding boxes, masks).

2. **Aligning to View Space:**
 - To establish consistent spatial relationships, all object positions and dimensions are transformed into a canonical view space—a 3D Cartesian coordinate system centered at the camera's optical center. The y-axis aligns with the viewing direction, and the z-axis points upward.
 - For 3D datasets, this transformation is straightforward given global annotations and camera parameters.
 - For image-only datasets, camera parameters and depth maps must be estimated. This is achieved using specialized models: WildCamera for intrinsic parameters, PerspectiveFields for extrinsic parameters, and Metric3Dv2 for dense depth maps. Their combined outputs allow for lifting 2D annotations into the canonical 3D space for accurate spatial reasoning.

3. **Template-based QA Generation:**
 - To generate 12 million QA pairs efficiently and at scale, a template-based approach is adopted instead of expensive direct LLM prompting.
 - An LLM is first prompted to generate diverse question-answer templates for various task types and answer formats. These templates include placeholders for object references and other variable content.
 - During generation, a subset of tasks and object instances are randomly selected for each image. The answers are derived from the pre-constructed annotations, and the templates are instantiated accordingly.
 - Low-quality or ambiguous QA pairs (e.g., due to occlusion) are filtered, and positive/negative examples are balanced. Templates are generated for 4 single-view tasks (Position Comparison, Size Comparison, Existence Estimation, Object Counting).
 - **Extending Instruction Formats:** A key novelty is the expansion of each QA pair into up to 19 distinct instruction formats (5 textual, 4 image, leading to combinations). Image formats include original, bounding boxes, segmentation masks, and numbered regions. Textual formats include natural language, text with `<ref>` captions, text with `<box>` coordinates, and combinations thereof. This significantly enhances instruction diversity.
 - **Generating Multi-view QA Pairs:** Multi-view data is systematically collected from datasets like ScanNet, MultiScan, R2R, and Objaverse. Scene-level geometric priors (room dimensions, centroids) are established using point clouds and algorithms like Alpha Shape. Object annotations are standardized to OrientedBoundingBox format. Temporally-agnostic training samples are formulated to capture inter-object relational attributes (properties, scale, distances) and cross-view relationships (rotation). A novel rotation angle prediction task is introduced for multi-view reasoning.

#### 3.2. InternSpatial-Bench

InternSpatial-Bench serves as a novel diagnostic benchmark for evaluating VLM spatial reasoning, addressing limitations of existing benchmarks like SpatialRGPT-Bench and SpatialBench.

- **Refining and Expanding Existing Benchmarks:**
 - For SpatialRGPT-Bench, the focus is on expanding question format diversity (not size), augmenting instruction styles following the format extension strategy used for InternSpatial, excluding ambiguous formats.
 - For SpatialBench (originally natural language only), reference phrases are manually extracted, questions converted to templates, and VLMs/SAM2 are used for object grounding and segmentation. Then, the format extension method is applied to generate diverse instruction variants, and all generated QAs are manually verified and corrected.
- **Extending the Benchmark with Curated QA Pairs:**
 - A dedicated pipeline is implemented for generating high-quality QA pairs for the benchmark, independent of pre-annotated information.
 - VLMs are prompted to directly generate questions to encourage diversity and expressiveness.
 - Crucially, all automatically constructed questions and answers undergo a **manual verification step** to ensure correctness and quality, resulting in 6,008 high-quality QA pairs across 5 tasks (Position Estimation, Size Estimation, Rotation Estimation, Existence Estimation, Object Counting).
 - The benchmark incorporates images from a broad range of domains (COCO, Flickr30K, Objaverse, ScanNet, Cityscapes, plus those from existing benchmarks) for robustness. Instruction format expansion is applied, with minor adaptations for tasks like Rotation Estimation (where only single objects are involved).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the substantial effectiveness of InternSpatial in enhancing the spatial reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), without compromising general multimodal performance.

#### 4.1. Evaluation on InternSpatial-Bench

- **Significant Spatial Reasoning Improvement:** The model trained on InternSpatial, named **InternVL-Spatial-8B**, achieved an average accuracy improvement of **12.1%** on InternSpatial-Bench compared to its baseline, InternVL2.5-8B.
- **Task-Specific Gains:**
 - Most notably, InternVL-Spatial-8B showed a remarkable **25.0% improvement** in the **Position Comparison** task and a **20.9% improvement** in the **Size Comparison** task.
 - It also demonstrated improvements in Rotation Estimation (+5.1%), Object Counting (+3.5%), and Existence Estimation (+6.0%).
- **Superiority over Proprietary Models:** InternVL-Spatial-8B surpassed advanced proprietary models such as GPT-4o-2024-11-20 and Claude 3.5 Sonnet across all tasks on InternSpatial-Bench, positioning it as a leading model for single-view spatial reasoning.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on VSI-Bench

- **Enhanced Multi-View Capabilities:** InternVL-Spatial-8B exhibited notable improvements over the InternVL2.5-8B baseline across all multi-view tasks on VSI-Bench.
- **Substantial Gains in Specific Multi-View Tasks:** It outperformed the baseline by more than **10%** in Object Counting (+17.0%), Object Size Estimation (+18.0%), and Appearance Order (+10.0%). Improvements were also seen in Absolute Distance (+8.0%), Room Size (+12.0%), and Route Plan (+2.1%).
- **Top-Tier Overall Performance:** InternVL-Spatial-8B achieved the highest average score (52.3%) among all evaluated models on VSI-Bench, including strong contenders like GPT-4o and Gemini 1.5 Pro. It ranked first in Object Counting, Absolute Distance Estimation, Object Size Estimation, and Appearance Order, and second in the remaining tasks. This confirms its superior multi-view spatial reasoning abilities.

#### 4.3. Effect of Various Question Formats (Ablation Study)

- The ablation study on InternSpatial-Bench revealed that the baseline InternVL2.5-8B performed best on natural language and original image formats but struggled significantly with less common formats (e.g., those involving bounding boxes `<box>`).
- **InternVL-Spatial-8B significantly narrowed this performance gap across diverse instruction styles**, indicating improved robustness.
- Even without explicit instruction format expansion (InternVL-Spatial-Raw-8B), training on InternSpatial data (with its inherent diversity) still consistently outperformed the baseline, suggesting a degree of generalization and cross-format transferability.
- Ultimately, InternVL-Spatial-8B achieved the best performance across *all* instruction formats, demonstrating that **instruction format expansion not only improves robustness but also enhances overall spatial reasoning capability.**

#### 4.4. General VQA Performance

- Training with InternSpatial data did not compromise the model's general reasoning capabilities. InternVL-Spatial-8B maintained comparable performance to InternVL2.5-8B on a suite of standard general-purpose benchmarks, including MathVision, OCRBench, TextVQA, ChartQA, and MMStar. Minor fluctuations were observed (e.g., +1.8% on MathVista, -1.6% on ChartQA), but overall performance remained strong, indicating that specialized spatial reasoning training can be integrated without negative side effects on broad VLM tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of InternSpatial and InternSpatial-Bench represents a significant advancement in the field of Vision-Language Models, particularly concerning their spatial reasoning capabilities.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Unprecedented Scale and Open-Source Availability:** InternSpatial, with its 12 million QA pairs, is the largest open-source dataset for spatial reasoning. This scale, combined with its open availability, provides an invaluable resource for the research community, facilitating broader access and accelerating innovation in VLM development.
- **Comprehensive Diversity:** The dataset addresses critical limitations of prior work by incorporating diverse visual environments (in-the-wild, indoor, urban, object-centric, embodied) and, crucially, **19 distinct instruction formats**. This rich expressiveness allows VLMs to learn to interpret spatial queries in ways that more closely resemble real-world human interaction.
- **Novel Multi-View Focus:** The inclusion of a substantial multi-view component, particularly the novel rotation angle prediction task, pushes the boundaries of spatial reasoning beyond static single-image analysis. This is a crucial step towards developing VLMs capable of understanding and navigating dynamic 3D environments.
- **Demonstrated Performance Gains:** The empirical results unequivocally show that training on InternSpatial substantially improves spatial reasoning performance on both single-view (InternSpatial-Bench) and multi-view (VSI-Bench) benchmarks. The model even surpasses leading proprietary models, highlighting the dataset's efficacy.
- **No Degradation of General Capabilities:** The fact that training with InternSpatial does not negatively impact a VLM's general multimodal reasoning abilities is highly significant. It implies that these specialized spatial skills can be integrated into general-purpose VLMs without the need for trade-offs, making them more versatile.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

The enhanced spatial reasoning abilities fostered by InternSpatial have profound implications for a wide range of downstream applications:

- **Robotics:** More spatially aware VLMs can significantly improve robot navigation, manipulation, and interaction with objects in complex, unstructured environments. This could lead to more autonomous and versatile robots for manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and domestic use.
- **Embodied AI:** For agents operating in simulated or real-world environments, accurate spatial understanding is fundamental for tasks like goal-oriented navigation, object search, and human-agent interaction. InternSpatial can help develop AI agents that reason more effectively about their surroundings.
- **Augmented Reality (AR) / Virtual Reality (VR):** Immersive AR/VR experiences rely heavily on accurate understanding of spatial relationships between virtual content and the real world. VLMs with superior spatial reasoning can enable more seamless object placement, scene understanding, and interactive experiences.
- **Autonomous Navigation:** For self-driving cars, drones, and other autonomous systems, precise spatial reasoning about obstacles, distances, trajectories, and object relationships is critical for safety and efficiency.
- **General-Purpose Multimodal AI:** The dataset's scale and diversity can serve as a foundation for pre-training or fine-tuning future generations of VLMs, making them inherently more robust and capable in spatial tasks, thus broadening their overall applicability.

While the template-based generation pipeline might inherently limit the full richness of natural language expression compared to human-curated data, the sheer scale and systematic diversity provided by InternSpatial offer an unparalleled resource. The authors acknowledge this limitation and suggest future work in exploring more expressive QA generation and open-ended spatial reasoning in interactive environments. Overall, InternSpatial is poised to become a cornerstone resource for advancing spatial intelligence in Vision-Language Models, paving the way for more intelligent and capable AI systems in practical, real-world scenarios.
