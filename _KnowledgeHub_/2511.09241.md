# Unveiling the Impact of Data and Model Scaling on High-Level Control for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Shanghai AI Laboratory developed Humanoid-Union, a large-scale, robot-executable motion dataset, and SCHUR, a scalable learning framework, to advance high-level control for humanoid robots. The framework demonstrates that data and model scaling enable the generation of diverse and stable text-conditioned motions on a physical humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Robot learning is bottlenecked by the difficulty, expense, and time-consumption of collecting large-scale, real-world robot data for humanoids.
- Existing methods using human motion as a prior for robots suffer from distribution mismatch, executability issues, and limited data scale utilization.
- Prior explorations into data scaling for robot control lacked comprehensive investigation into robust motion representations, effective data filtering, and advanced model scaling strategies.

## Method

- ==Developed Humanoid-Union==, a large-scale (over 260 hours), high-quality, semantically annotated, and robot-executable motion dataset, via an autonomous video mining and tracker-based filtering pipeline.
- Proposed SCHUR, a two-stage scalable learning framework, featuring an enriched robot motion representation and Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) for robust tokenization.
- Implemented an autoregressive, LLaMA-based Transformer for text-to-motion generation, leveraging prefix-bidirectional attention and RMSNorm for stability.

## Results

- SCHUR's tokenizer, utilizing FSQ, achieved superior reconstruction quality (e.g., MPJPE of **0.0326**) and robust scaling with large codebooks, outperforming VQ-VAE which exhibited codebook collapse.
- The LLaMA-based generator demonstrated consistent performance improvements in text-motion alignment (e.g., FID of **12.6**) with increased model size and surpassed diffusion-based methods like MDM.
- Generated motions from SCHUR were successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, enabling diverse, precise, and stable whole-body control for complex, long-horizon action sequences in the real world.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale, robot-executable datasets, carefully filtered using whole-body trackers, are crucial for achieving high-fidelity and physically compliant robot motion generation.
> - An enriched robot motion representation, incorporating virtual keypoints that mimic human skeletal topology, substantially improves the quality of motion tokenization and generation.
> - Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) effectively overcomes 'codebook collapse' in tokenization, enabling robust scaling with larger codebooks and continuous performance improvement for large motion datasets.
