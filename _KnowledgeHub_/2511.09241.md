# Unveiling the Impact of Data and Model Scaling on High-Level Control for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and Shanghai AI Laboratory developed Humanoid-Union, a large-scale, robot-executable motion dataset, and SCHUR, a scalable learning framework, to advance high-level control for humanoid robots. The framework demonstrates that data and model scaling enable the generation of diverse and stable text-conditioned motions on a physical humanoid robot.

## Problem

- Robot learning is bottlenecked by the difficulty, expense, and time-consumption of collecting large-scale, real-world robot data for humanoids.
- Existing methods using human motion as a prior for robots suffer from distribution mismatch, executability issues, and limited data scale utilization.
- Prior explorations into data scaling for robot control lacked comprehensive investigation into robust motion representations, effective data filtering, and advanced model scaling strategies.

## Method

- ==Developed Humanoid-Union==, a large-scale (over 260 hours), high-quality, semantically annotated, and robot-executable motion dataset, via an autonomous video mining and tracker-based filtering pipeline.
- Proposed SCHUR, a two-stage scalable learning framework, featuring an enriched robot motion representation and Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) for robust tokenization.
- Implemented an autoregressive, LLaMA-based Transformer for text-to-motion generation, leveraging prefix-bidirectional attention and RMSNorm for stability.

## Results

- SCHUR's tokenizer, utilizing FSQ, achieved superior reconstruction quality (e.g., MPJPE of **0.0326**) and robust scaling with large codebooks, outperforming VQ-VAE which exhibited codebook collapse.
- The LLaMA-based generator demonstrated consistent performance improvements in text-motion alignment (e.g., FID of **12.6**) with increased model size and surpassed diffusion-based methods like MDM.
- Generated motions from SCHUR were successfully deployed on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot, enabling diverse, precise, and stable whole-body control for complex, long-horizon action sequences in the real world.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale, robot-executable datasets, carefully filtered using whole-body trackers, are crucial for achieving high-fidelity and physically compliant robot motion generation.
> - An enriched robot motion representation, incorporating virtual keypoints that mimic human skeletal topology, substantially improves the quality of motion tokenization and generation.
> - Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) effectively overcomes 'codebook collapse' in tokenization, enabling robust scaling with larger codebooks and continuous performance improvement for large motion datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper positions itself within the rapidly evolving landscape of scalable learning, a paradigm that has driven significant breakthroughs in fields like natural language processing (NLP), computer vision (CV), and image/video generation. The core idea is that scaling up data and model size can lead to emergent capabilities and superior performance. This work extends this paradigm to robot learning, specifically for humanoid robots, an area where scaling faces unique and significant challenges.

Historically, robot learning has been bottlenecked by the difficulty, expense, and time-consumption of collecting real-world robot data. This contrasts sharply with the abundance of data available in other AI domains. For humanoid robots, human motion data offers a compelling alternative: it is relatively easy to acquire at scale, and its inherent semantic information can be leveraged for high-level control.

Prior research has explored using human motion as a prior for humanoid robot control. Approaches like those in, and have attempted to transfer human movements to robots. However, these methods suffer from several shortcomings:
1. **Distribution Mismatch:** Human motion, even after retargeting, often has a different distribution than what is physically executable or optimal for a robot, leading to reduced modality alignment and control quality.
2. **Executability Issues:** A significant portion of raw human motion data may not be directly executable by a robot's low-level controllers due to kinematic constraints, physical limitations, or interactions with scenes/objects not present in the robot's environment. This necessitates complex and often imperfect retargeting, filtering, or refinement processes.
3. **Limited Data Scale:** Many existing robot motion datasets, or those derived from human motion, are relatively small (e.g., HumanML3D with less than 30 hours of data), preventing researchers from fully exploring the benefits of large-scale data and model scaling that have transformed other AI fields.
4. **Shallow Scaling Exploration:** Even preliminary explorations into scaling for robot control, such as, have been noted to lack effective investigation into robot motion representation, neglect the advantages of directly generating refined robot motion, and offer shallow analysis of model scaling strategies.

This paper directly addresses these limitations by proposing a novel approach that aims to overcome the "data scaling bottleneck" in robot learning. It bridges the gap between abundant human motion data and the specific requirements of robot control by:
- Creating a **large-scale, high-quality robot motion dataset (Humanoid-Union)** that is *directly executable* by robots and includes semantic annotations, moving beyond merely retargeting human motion.
- Developing a **scalable learning framework (SCHUR)** that can effectively leverage this large dataset, incorporating improved robot motion representations and robust quantization techniques to support significant model scaling.

By focusing on generating high-quality *robot-specific* data and developing a scalable learning architecture, this work pushes the boundaries of high-level control for humanoid robots, facilitating more generalizable, diverse, and robust behaviors. It aims to demonstrate that, similar to other AI domains, data and model scaling can unlock unprecedented capabilities in robotic control, moving towards more intelligent and versatile humanoid systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the recognized success of data and model scaling in various AI domains and the pressing need to extend these benefits to robot learning, particularly for humanoid robots. While human videos and motion data are abundant, translating this raw, large-scale source into robot-learnable representations and leveraging it effectively for scalable learning remains an open problem.

The paper identifies several critical limitations in existing approaches that motivate their work:
1. **Inadequate Data Quality and Distribution Mismatch:** Current methods often rely on human motion as a direct prior, which inherently suffers from distributional discrepancies with robot motion. Retargeting, while useful, can complicate usage and alter the data distribution, limiting effective modality alignment. Moreover, portions of human motion data may not be executable by robot low-level executors, degrading data quality and execution effectiveness.
2. **Limited Data Scale Utilization:** Existing datasets and methods fail to fully exploit the vast amount of human motion data available, operating at relatively small scales. This hinders the development of more capable and generalized humanoid control policies.
3. **Lack of Effective Scalable Learning Strategies:** Prior explorations into data scaling for robots have been shallow, lacking comprehensive investigation into robust robot motion representations, effective data filtering, and advanced model scaling techniques. Specifically, they often overlook refined robot motion data and strategies for model size increases.
4. **Insufficient High-Level Control and Semantic Alignment:** Achieving robust semantic alignment (e.g., text-to-motion) under scaling is crucial for diverse high-level control paradigms, including future vision-language-action (VLA) models. Existing methods often struggle with this, especially when relying on human motion.

To address these motivations, the authors set out with two main objectives:

1. **To construct a large-scale, high-quality, and executable robot motion dataset:** This involves developing an autonomous pipeline to mine raw human videos and motion, extract robot-learnable representations, and meticulously filter and process them using a whole-body tracker to ensure physical and kinematic compliance for real-world robot deployment. The dataset, named **Humanoid-Union**, is designed to be expandable and semantically annotated to enable high-level control.
2. **To propose a scalable learning framework (SCHUR) for high-level control of humanoid robots:** SCHUR is designed to effectively leverage the Humanoid-Union dataset, exploring the impact of data and model scaling. Its specific objectives include:
 - Optimizing robot motion representation to enhance generation quality.
 - Employing an effective quantization method (FSQ) and a robust training strategy to support large codebook sizes and prevent model collapse, enabling true scalable learning.
 - Achieving high robot motion generation quality and strong text-motion alignment under data and model scaling.
 - Validating the framework's effectiveness through deployment on real-world humanoid robots.

In essence, the research aims to unlock the potential of large-scale data for humanoid robots by providing both the necessary high-quality data resource and a robust learning architecture to effectively utilize it, thereby pushing the boundaries of high-level, semantic-driven robot control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a two-pronged approach: the creation of a novel, large-scale dataset called Humanoid-Union, and a scalable learning framework named SCHUR to leverage this data for high-level humanoid robot control.

#### 3.1. Humanoid-Union Dataset Construction

The Humanoid-Union dataset comprises over 260 hours of diverse, high-quality robot motion data with semantic annotations. Its creation involves an autonomous pipeline with three key steps:

1. **Video MoCap and Description:**
 - Human motion is extracted from a vast array of human-centric videos (e.g., from) using standard pose estimation and refinement techniques, resulting in SMPL (Skinned Multi-Person Linear) model representation (θ_human, r_pos_human).
 - Existing high-quality human motion data with semantic annotations is also integrated.
 - Textual descriptions for each motion sequence are automatically generated using a vision-language model (VLM), specifically GPT-4V, to create paired multi-modality data essential for semantic alignment training.

2. **Motion Retargeting:**
 - Human SMPL motion data is transformed into robot states. A crucial innovation here is the binding of **virtual keypoints** to the robot. These keypoints mimic the topology of the human skeleton, improving alignment between human and robot structures and preserving human-like motion style.
 - The retargeting process involves: i) defining virtual keypoints with fixed topological relationships to robot body parts; ii) recalibrating body part scales; iii) solving for robot joint positions (j_robot) using inverse kinematics (IK); and iv) computing virtual keypoints' positions (k_pos) and rotations (k_ori) via forward kinematics (FK).
 - Robot motion height is corrected (similar to PHC), followed by smoothing.
 - The final robot motion representation includes root position (r_pos_robot), root orientation (r_ori_robot), joint positions for all DoFs (j_robot), and the positions and rotations of the virtual keypoints (k_pos, k_ori).

3. **Whole Body Tracker Filtering and Postprocessing:**
 - To ensure high-quality, executable robot motion, a **pretrained universal whole-body tracker** (trained on AMASS akin to ExBody2) is utilized for post-processing.
 - Motions derived from raw human data, which might contain infeasible actions (e.g., due to scene interactions, extreme amplitudes, or noise), are simulated with this tracker.
 - Approximately 10% of the motion sequences that fail during tracker execution (indicating infeasibility or physical inconsistency for the robot) are filtered out.
 - The motions successfully processed by the tracker are used to complete the final post-processing, thereby introducing physics-informed priors and ensuring kinematic and physical constraints are met. This step is vital for producing data that is directly amenable to real-world robot deployment.

#### 3.2. SCHUR Scalable Learning Framework

SCHUR (Scalable Humanoid Robot model) is designed as a two-stage learning framework, adopting a discrete tokenization and autoregressive generation paradigm:

1. **Robot Motion Tokenization (Stage 1):**
 - **Enriched Robot Motion Representation:** While root position, orientation, and DoFs can represent a robot's state, the authors found this insufficient for effective tokenization. They incorporate the positions (k_pos) and orientations (k_ori) of the virtual keypoints (derived during retargeting) into the representation: `e = concat(r_pos_robot, r_ori_robot, j_robot, k_pos, k_ori)`. This redundant information, akin to human motion style, significantly enhances generation quality.
 - **Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) for Tokenization:** Instead of conventional VQ-VAE, FSQ is employed. FSQ directly quantizes each element of the latent vector into a set of unique integers using a `round(f(z))` operation, where `f(z)` is a bounding function (e.g., sigmoid). This bypasses the `arg min` operation of VQ-VAE, mitigating the "codebook collapse" problem, allowing for significantly larger codebook sizes, and improving scaling capabilities. The loss function focuses directly on reconstruction error.
 - The encoder and decoder for this stage utilize convolutional residual blocks.

2. **Autoregressive Text-Based Generation (Stage 2):**
 - **Architecture:** A decoder-only Transformer architecture, structurally similar to LLaMA, is used.
 - **Input and Attention:** Text tokens, derived from a pre-trained T5-XL language model, serve as a prefix. The model then autoregressively generates robot motion tokens (obtained from Stage 1). A **prefix-bidirectional attention mask** is used, allowing bidirectional attention over the text prefix while maintaining causal (autoregressive) attention for motion token generation.
 - **Training Stability:** RMSNorm is applied within the transformer blocks to improve training stability.
 - **Loss Function:** The model is optimized using a cross-entropy loss to predict the next motion token given previous motion tokens and the text prefix.

#### 3.3. Real-World Deployment

The same universal whole-body tracker used for data filtering is also employed for real-world deployment. The motions generated by SCHUR (as sequences of robot states) are fed into this tracker, which then translates them into low-level control signals executable by a physical humanoid robot (Unitree G1).

This comprehensive methodology, encompassing a carefully curated dataset and a scalable, two-stage learning framework with architectural innovations, represents a significant step towards practical and high-fidelity high-level control for humanoid robots.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted validate the effectiveness of both the Humanoid-Union dataset and the SCHUR learning framework across various metrics, including reconstruction quality, text-motion alignment, tracking performance, and real-world deployment.

#### 4.1. Impact of Humanoid-Union Dataset

- **Data Quality and Executability:** The filtering and post-processing steps using the universal whole-body tracker are crucial. The paper demonstrates that training SCHUR on raw, unfiltered robot motion data (without tracker-based processing) or directly generating human motion and then retargeting it, leads to a significant drop in the tracker's success rate and an increase in tracking errors (e.g., success rate drops from 0.907 to 0.762 with raw data). This highlights that the Humanoid-Union dataset, by discarding infeasible motions and incorporating physics-informed priors, provides higher-quality data that is more compatible with robot execution.
- **Ablation Studies:** Training with only 20% of the full Humanoid-Union dataset significantly degrades performance across all metrics (e.g., MPJPE for tokenizer increases from 0.0326 to 0.0483), underscoring the importance of large-scale data for effective robot motion generation and control.

#### 4.2. SCHUR's Tokenization Performance (Stage 1)

- **Superior Reconstruction Quality:** SCHUR's tokenizer, utilizing FSQ, consistently outperforms a conventional VQ-VAE baseline across all reconstruction metrics (MPJPE, MPKPE, and L1 loss). For instance, with the largest codebook size, SCHUR achieves an MPJPE of 0.0326, significantly better than the VQ-VAE baseline.
- **Robust Scaling Capabilities:** As the codebook size increases, SCHUR (with FSQ) demonstrates continuous improvement in reconstruction quality. In contrast, conventional VQ-VAE exhibits a clear "collapse effect" with larger codebooks, failing to improve and showing decreased codebook usage (dropping below 90% for VQ-VAE vs. consistently above 99% for FSQ). This validates FSQ's robustness against collapse and its superior scalability.
- **Effectiveness of Enriched Representation:** An ablation study confirms that incorporating virtual keypoints (k_pos, k_ori) into the robot motion representation substantially improves the tokenizer's reconstruction quality (MPJPE improved from 0.0406 to 0.0326), demonstrating the value of this redundant yet human-like structural information.

#### 4.3. SCHUR's Text-Based Generation Performance (Stage 2)

- **Strong Scaling for Text-Motion Alignment:** Experiments show that as the model size of the LLaMA-based generator increases, all evaluation metrics (FID, R@1, R@2, R@3) consistently improve. This indicates that larger models better leverage the large-scale data from Humanoid-Union, achieving stronger alignment between text and generated motion.
- **Impact of Codebook Size:** A larger codebook size from the tokenization stage (65,536 vs. 256) generally leads to better generation results due to superior reconstruction quality. However, with very small generator model parameters, a larger codebook can sometimes lead to poorer performance, as the limited model capacity struggles to effectively learn from the more granular representation.
- **Comparative Performance:** SCHUR demonstrates superior generative performance compared to alternative methods:
 - **Generating human motion + retargeting:** Similar to the tokenizer ablation, this approach is constrained by distributional discrepancies and yields suboptimal results (e.g., FID of 16.9 compared to SCHUR's 12.6).
 - **MDM (diffusion-based approach):** SCHUR outperforms MDM, achieving better FID (12.6 vs. 20.8) and higher R@K scores, further demonstrating the effectiveness of the proposed autoregressive approach with high-quality discrete tokens.

#### 4.4. Real-World Deployment

- **Reliable Execution on Physical Robot:** The generated motions from SCHUR, when combined with the universal whole-body tracker, are successfully deployed on a real-world Unitree G1 humanoid robot.
- **Diverse and Complex Behaviors:** SCHUR is capable of generating a wide range of motions, including upper- and lower-limb actions, locomotion behaviors, abstract semantic controls, and complex long-horizon action sequences (e.g., "Walking forward and squatting to pick something up, then keeping walking").
- **High Precision and Stability:** The deployment achieves highly precise and stable control, maintaining balance and motion stability even under whole-body tracking, affirming the practical utility and robustness of the entire pipeline.

In summary, the results conclusively demonstrate that the Humanoid-Union dataset provides high-quality, executable robot motion data, and SCHUR effectively leverages this data through scalable tokenization and autoregressive generation. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance in text-to-robot motion generation, enabling reliable and diverse behaviors on physical humanoid robots, validating the profound impact of data and model scaling in this domain.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of humanoid robot learning by directly addressing critical bottlenecks related to data availability, quality, and scalable learning strategies. Its impact can be multifaceted:

1. **Breaking the Data Bottleneck for Humanoid Robots:** The introduction of **Humanoid-Union** is a monumental contribution. By providing a large-scale (over 260 hours), diverse, semantically annotated, and crucially, *robot-executable* motion dataset, the authors overcome a long-standing challenge in robot learning. This dataset, generated through an autonomous and expandable pipeline, significantly reduces the prohibitive costs and difficulties associated with direct robot data collection. Its focus on robot-compliant motion (via tracker-based filtering) ensures that learned policies are directly applicable and reliable for real-world deployment, differentiating it from prior human-centric datasets.

2. **Enabling True Scalable Learning in Robotics:** **SCHUR** demonstrates that the successful paradigm of data and model scaling, prevalent in other AI domains, can effectively be applied to humanoid robotics. By showing continuous performance improvements with increased data and model parameters, the work provides strong evidence for the potential of large-scale foundation models in robotics. This paves the way for developing more generalist and capable humanoid agents that can learn from vast amounts of experience.

3. **Advancing High-Level Semantic Control:** The ability to generate high-quality, diverse whole-body robot motions from natural language text represents a significant leap in high-level control. This "text-to-robot-motion" capability simplifies human-robot interaction, allowing users to command complex behaviors intuitively. It also lays foundational groundwork for more sophisticated **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** for robots, where robots can interpret complex visual scenes and language instructions to perform intricate tasks.

4. **Methodological Innovations:**
 - **Enriched Robot Motion Representation:** The inclusion of virtual keypoints in the robot motion representation is a subtle yet powerful innovation. By mimicking human skeletal topology, it provides crucial information for generating more human-like and natural motions, enhancing both tokenization and generation quality.
 - **Robust Quantization with FSQ:** The adoption of Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) effectively tackles the codebook collapse issue common in VQ-VAE, enabling the use of much larger codebooks. This is critical for capturing the complexity and diversity of large-scale motion data and supporting stable model scaling.
 - **LLaMA-based Autoregressive Generation:** Adapting successful large language model architectures (like LLaMA) with prefix-bidirectional attention for motion generation demonstrates a promising direction for future robotic learning systems, leveraging established techniques from other fields.

5. **Direct Real-World Applicability:** The successful deployment on a physical Unitree G1 humanoid robot validates the entire framework's practical utility. The demonstration of generating and executing diverse and complex locomotion and manipulation tasks, while maintaining balance and stability, is a strong indicator of the research's readiness for real-world applications and its potential to accelerate the development of agile and versatile humanoid robots.

In the long term, this research has the potential to:
- **Democratize Robot Learning:** By providing an automated pipeline for dataset generation, it can reduce reliance on expensive, domain-specific human expertise, making advanced robot learning more accessible.
- **Accelerate Humanoid Development:** It provides tools and methodologies to rapidly iterate on and improve humanoid robot capabilities, potentially leading to more advanced robots for various applications, from logistics and manufacturing to assistance in daily life.
- **Inspire Future Research:** The paper identifies limitations such as direct robot state/action control without a low-level tracker as future work, setting clear directions for further research in developing end-to-end, balance-aware control policies from large models.

Overall, by demonstrating the profound impact of data and model scaling on high-level humanoid control, this work contributes significantly to moving robotics closer to the vision of truly intelligent and general-purpose humanoid agents.
