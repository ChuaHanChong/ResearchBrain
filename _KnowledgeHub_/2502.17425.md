# Introducing Visual Perception Token into Multimodal Large Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the National University of Singapore introduce Visual Perception Tokens, a mechanism enabling Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to autonomously control their visual perception process. This approach allows MLLMs to dynamically select regions for re-processing and incorporate enriched features from specialized encoders, leading to a 2B parameter MLLM outperforming a 7B parameter MLLM in visual understanding tasks.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with precise visual details, fine-grained attribute understanding, and accurate spatial reasoning due to static visual perception processes.
- MLLMs lack autonomous control over their visual perception, unable to decide when or how to refine their visual understanding based on query context, leading to incomplete or inaccurate outputs.
- Visual hallucination and misinformation in MLLMs often stem from insufficient or ambiguous visual information, as models cannot actively seek clarifying visual data.

## Method

- Introduces ==Visual Perception Tokens== into the MLLM's vocabulary, allowing the model to autonomously generate these non-natural language tokens to trigger specific visual perception actions.
- Proposes a ==Region Selection Token== mechanism that enables the MLLM to identify and re-encode specific image regions using grid-based coordinates, effectively increasing resolution for relevant areas.
- Develops a ==Vision Re-Encoding Token== that prompts the MLLM to re-encode the entire image with an additional, specialized vision encoder, with the token's hidden state acting as a control signal to a dedicated projector for feature integration.

## Results

- A **2B** parameter MLLM equipped with Visual Perception Tokens (2B+VPT) achieved an average performance score of **0.749**, a **30.9%** improvement over the base 2B model and outperforming a larger **7B** base model by **20.0%**.
- VPT substantially improved performance on fine-grained VQA (0.207 improvement over 7B baseline) and visual reasoning tasks, demonstrating enhanced capability in handling complex visual relationships and minute details.
- The approach maintained or surpassed the performance of larger models in zero-shot settings and across different image resolutions (224 and 512), indicating robust generalization and adaptability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Introducing Visual Perception Tokens allows MLLMs to autonomously control their visual perception, shifting from passive processing to active, iterative visual information extraction.
> - The use of grid-based Region Selection Tokens provides a robust and learnable method for MLLMs to focus on specific image regions, outperforming direct pixel-level bounding box generation.
> - Leveraging the hidden state of a 'Re-Encoding Control' token enables fine-grained influence over how enriched visual features from specialized encoders are integrated, enhancing feature utility for the LLM.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), a rapidly evolving field aimed at integrating visual and linguistic understanding within a unified model architecture. Current MLLMs, while powerful in general visual information understanding and generation, primarily rely on a fixed visual perception process determined by their pre-trained vision encoders. They lack the intrinsic ability to autonomously control or refine their visual perception based on the context of a query or an ongoing dialogue. This limitation manifests in several challenges: difficulty with fine-grained understanding (e.g., small objects, detailed attributes), imprecise spatial reasoning, and tendencies towards visual hallucination where the model generates plausible but incorrect responses not grounded in the image.

The broader research landscape has explored several avenues to enhance MLLM visual perception:
- **Visual Prompting:** Earlier techniques often involved manual annotations (points, masks) to guide segmentation models. More recently, MLLMs have been trained to interpret visual cues directly, or utilize masks generated by segmentation models like SAM as visual prompts. Some methods generate query-specific visual prompts via auxiliary VLMs. This paper distinguishes itself from these by introducing a novel *token-based* mechanism that allows the MLLM to *autonomously generate* these prompts, rather than relying on external VLM generation or fixed attention mechanisms. Crucially, its "Region Selection Token" design avoids natural language bounding box specifications, which MLLMs often struggle with, opting for a more native, grid-based token representation.
- **Improving Visual Inputs:** Another line of research focuses on providing richer visual inputs to MLLMs. This includes incorporating features from more robust vision encoders like DINOv2, adding image captions for better grounding, providing depth maps for spatial reasoning, or integrating 3D scene information. While effective, these approaches generally involve pre-processing or fixed augmentations of the input, rather than enabling the MLLM to *iteratively and adaptively request* specific types of visual information or re-perception.
- **Reasoning and Planning Tokens in LLMs:** In the realm of pure Large Language Models, "reasoning tokens" or "planning tokens" have been introduced to guide the model's internal thought processes, enabling complex reasoning chains or multi-step problem-solving. This paper extends this conceptual paradigm to the *visual domain* of MLLMs. Unlike general reasoning tokens that primarily control textual generation or high-level planning, the Visual Perception Tokens are specifically designed to trigger and convey fine-grained *control information* for visual perception processes, moving beyond simple action triggers to allow for nuanced guidance.

This work fills a critical gap by proposing a mechanism that grants MLLMs *autonomous control* over their own visual perception. Instead of relying on pre-defined pipelines or external modules for visual enhancement, the MLLM itself decides when and how to refine its visual understanding by generating special "Visual Perception Tokens." This approach represents a significant step towards more cognitively flexible and efficient MLLMs that can adaptively "focus" their attention and "re-examine" visual information as needed, much like humans do.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **empower Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with the autonomous capability to control their own visual perception processes**. This means moving beyond static, pre-determined visual encoding pipelines and enabling the MLLM to actively engage in an iterative and adaptive process of visual understanding.

The core motivations driving this objective stem from critical limitations observed in current MLLMs:
- **Incomplete and Inaccurate Visual Perception:** MLLMs often struggle with tasks requiring precise visual details, such as distinguishing small objects, inferring fine-grained attributes, or accurately understanding spatial relationships. The completeness and accuracy of the visual features provided by the vision encoder significantly impact the model's performance on these tasks.
- **Lack of Autonomous Control:** Current MLLMs typically process visual information through a single, pre-defined pass through their vision encoder. They lack the ability to autonomously decide to "look closer" at a specific region, or to "re-examine" the image with a different or specialized visual processing module, based on the query or the unfolding conversational context. This often necessitates manual intervention or reliance on pre-engineered, inflexible pipelines.
- **Hallucination and Misinformation:** When visual information is ambiguous or insufficient, MLLMs are prone to generating "hallucinations"—textual responses that are plausible but not grounded in the visual content. Enhancing the model's control over its perception process is hypothesized to mitigate such issues by allowing it to seek clarifying visual information.
- **Efficiency and Interpretability of Control Signals:** While some MLLM-based agents can control external tools via natural language outputs, this approach can be inefficient due to natural language redundancy and constrained by specific formats. The authors seek to explore control signals *beyond* natural language, which are naturally compatible with the LLM's next-token prediction paradigm, aiming for greater efficiency and flexibility in guiding visual processing.

To achieve its primary objective, the paper sets out to:
- **Design and introduce the concept of "Visual Perception Tokens,"** which MLLMs can generate just like text to trigger and control additional visual perception actions.
- **Propose two specific types of these tokens:** the Region Selection Token for focused attention on specific image regions, and the Vision Re-Encoding Token for incorporating enriched visual features from specialized encoders with fine-grained control.
- **Develop a training methodology and dataset** that enables MLLMs to learn to effectively use these new tokens.
- **Demonstrate through extensive experimentation** that these Visual Perception Tokens significantly enhance MLLM performance on tasks requiring detailed visual understanding, spatial reasoning, and reduced hallucination, even allowing smaller models to outperform larger ones.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work lies in introducing "Visual Perception Tokens" into the vocabulary of an MLLM, allowing the model to autonomously generate these tokens during its response generation process. These tokens do not correspond to natural language words but rather serve as direct commands to trigger and control additional visual perception actions.

The authors design two distinct types of Visual Perception Tokens:

#### 3.1. Region Selection Token

- **Purpose:** To enable the MLLM to explicitly identify and re-process specific regions of an image that are highly relevant to the query. This "crop and re-input" approach effectively increases the resolution of query-relevant areas, benefiting tasks like OCR and fine-grained object recognition.
- **Mechanism:** A group of six consecutive tokens is used: `<RegionSelectionStart>`, two tokens for the top-left cell index (`<x_min>`, `<y_min>`), two tokens for the bottom-right cell index (`<x_max>`, `<y_max>`), and `<RegionSelectionEnd>`.
- **Coordinate Representation:** Instead of precise pixel coordinates, which MLLMs struggle to generate reliably, the image is divided into a `k x k` grid of cells (with `k=8` in experiments). The tokens represent the row and column indices of the cells containing the top-left and bottom-right corners of the desired region. This approximate representation simplifies training and improves robustness to varying input resolutions.
- **Action:** When these tokens are generated, the specified region is cropped from the original image, re-encoded using the MLLM's *original vision encoder*, and the resulting new visual embeddings are concatenated to the ongoing input sequence for the LLM to continue generation.
- **Semantic Meaning:** These tokens have clear and interpretable semantics, directly indicating a spatial location.

#### 3.2. Vision Re-Encoding Token

- **Purpose:** To signal the MLLM to re-encode the *entire original image* using an *additional, potentially specialized, vision encoder* (e.g., DINOv2, SAM, or even the MLLM's original vision branch/CLIP). The goal is to obtain richer or different types of visual features to supplement the initial perception.
- **Mechanism:** A set of three tokens: `<Re-EncodeStart>`, `<Re-EncodingControl>`, and `<Re-EncodeEnd>`.
- **Fine-Grained Control:** Crucially, the hidden state of the central `<Re-EncodingControl>` token is leveraged. Along with the features from the additional vision encoder, this hidden state is fed into a *dedicated cross-attention projector*. This design allows the hidden state of the control token to act as a *control signal*, influencing how the additional visual features are processed and aligned before being concatenated to the LLM's input. The loss is *not* calculated for this token during training, allowing its hidden state to freely encode complex control information without being forced into a specific natural language representation.
- **Mask Modeling:** To enhance the informativeness of the `<Re-EncodingControl>` token, a training approach similar to Masked Language Modeling is employed. For 50% of relevant samples, the attention mask is modified such that answer tokens can *only* access the `<Re-EncodingControl>` token's hidden state, forcing the model to embed critical information within it for accurate answering.
- **Action:** When these tokens are generated, the original image is re-encoded by the chosen additional vision model, processed by the new projector (conditioned on the control token's hidden state), and the resulting embeddings are appended to the LLM's input.

#### 3.3. Architecture and Training

- **Base MLLM:** The Qwen2-VL-2B and Qwen2-VL-7B models serve as the foundational MLLMs.
- **Modified Forward Pass:** If Visual Perception Tokens are generated, the described re-perception processes are triggered, generating new visual embeddings that are dynamically concatenated to the existing sequence of image and text embeddings. The LLM then continues its next-token prediction based on this enriched input.
- **Training Data Construction:** A comprehensive dataset of 829k samples was constructed, drawing from existing VQA, OCR, and spatial reasoning datasets (DocVQA, TextVQA, TextCaps, VSR, GQA, OpenImage, CUB-200-2011, LLaVA Instruction Tuning). For Region Selection Tokens, existing bounding box annotations were converted into the proposed grid-based token sequences. For Vision Re-Encoding Tokens, these tokens were inserted between the question and answer in relevant dialogue pairs.
- **Training Phases:**
 1. **Alignment Stage:** Aligns features from the *additional* vision encoders (DINOv2/SAM) with the LLM embedding space. Only the projector connecting these are trained, while other components are frozen.
 2. **Finetuning Stage:** The full MLLM (with new tokens and projectors) is fine-tuned on the constructed dataset. All components except the original and additional vision encoders are unfrozen, allowing the model to learn to generate and utilize the Visual Perception Tokens effectively.

This methodology represents a significant departure from traditional MLLM architectures by introducing a dynamic, feedback-driven loop for visual perception, controlled internally by the MLLM itself.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation demonstrates that the introduction of Visual Perception Tokens (VPT) significantly enhances the performance of MLLMs across a range of visual understanding tasks. The core findings are summarized below:

- **Overall Performance Enhancement:** The 2B parameter model equipped with Visual Perception Tokens (2B+VPT, specifically with DINO for re-encoding and "Free Choice" mechanism) achieved an average performance score of **0.749**, a substantial **30.9% improvement** over the base 2B model's 0.572. Remarkably, this 2B+VPT model even **outperformed the much larger 7B parameter base model** (score of 0.624) by **20.0%**. This highlights the efficiency and effectiveness of the VPT approach in leveraging visual information.

- **Task-Specific Advantages:**
 - **Visual Reasoning and Fine-Grained VQA:** VPT demonstrated clear and substantial advantages. For instance, on Visual Reasoning tasks, the 2B+VPT (DINO) model showed an improvement of 0.161 over the 7B baseline model. For Fine-Grained VQA, the improvement was even more pronounced at 0.207 over the 7B baseline. This indicates VPT's strong capability in handling complex visual relationships and minute details.
 - **General VQA and Text/OCR-Related VQA:** While the gains were less dramatic compared to the 7B model in some specific datasets for these categories, the 2B+VPT model still consistently performed comparably or better, demonstrating its broad applicability. Notably, in Text/OCR-related VQA, the 2B+VPT (DINO) model at 512 resolution achieved 0.573 on DocVQA, surpassing the 7B model's 0.360.

- **Generalization to Unseen Data (Zero-Shot):** The effectiveness of VPT extends to zero-shot settings. The 2B-VPT model maintained or surpassed the performance of the 7B model on datasets (Flickr, DUDE, POPE) that were not included in its training, underscoring its robust generalization capabilities.

- **Resolution Adaptability:** The VPT approach proved effective across different image resolutions. At lower resolutions (224), the 2B+VPT model improved by 0.094 over the 7B model, and at higher resolutions (512), it showed an improvement of 0.084, indicating its versatility.

- **"Free Choice" Mechanism:** Allowing the MLLM to autonomously decide *whether* and *which type* of Perception Token to use yielded even better average performance (0.749) compared to explicitly enforcing token usage (0.738), suggesting that the model learns to adaptively apply the most suitable perception strategy.

- **Ablation Study on Region Selection Token:**
 - **Superiority over Bounding Boxes:** Region Selection Tokens, using grid-based approximate coordinates, were demonstrably more effective than directly generating pixel-level bounding boxes (as in). Models using VPT achieved significantly higher scores, and the authors noted that direct bounding box prediction led to many invalid outputs.
 - **Optimal Granularity (k):** Experiments showed an optimal `k` value of 8 for grid division. Smaller `k` (e.g., 4) resulted in overly large cropped regions, while larger `k` (e.g., 16, 32) introduced increased learning difficulty due to more tokens, leading to performance degradation.

- **Ablation Study on Vision Re-Encoding Token:**
 - **Value of Control Information:** The hidden state of the `<Re-EncodingControl>` token significantly boosted performance (e.g., from 0.7459 to 0.8943 on CUB Bird), confirming its critical role in conveying valuable control information to the projector that refines the visual features.
 - **Number of Control Tokens:** Increasing the number of `<Re-EncodingControl>` tokens from 1 to 2 provided limited gains, and more (e.g., 4) led to a decline, possibly due to the projector's lightweight design being susceptible to overfitting.
 - **Mask Modeling:** Introducing mask modeling during training consistently improved performance. Further fine-tuning the projector's linear layer reinforced the importance of the control token's hidden states.

- **Qualitative Examples:** Illustrated the effectiveness of VPT in scenarios such as locating small text in documents, mitigating hallucination by grounding responses (e.g., correctly identifying "teddy bear" vs. "person" next to a dog), and accurately counting objects in complex scenes due to enhanced segmentation.

- **General Benchmark Performance:** Supplementary experiments on MME and MM-Bench benchmarks demonstrated that the fine-tuning process for VPT did not lead to performance degeneration on general tasks, and in some cases, even showed slight improvements, indicating the approach's stability.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that Visual Perception Tokens provide MLLMs with a novel and powerful mechanism for autonomous, fine-grained control over visual perception, leading to substantial performance gains and enabling smaller models to achieve or surpass the capabilities of larger baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Visual Perception Tokens represents a significant conceptual and practical advancement in the field of Multimodal Large Language Models. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Paradigm Shift in MLLM Perception:** This work fundamentally shifts MLLM visual perception from a largely passive and fixed process to an active, iterative, and controlled one. Instead of simply consuming pre-processed visual features, MLLMs can now dynamically "decide" where and how to focus their visual attention, and what type of visual information to extract, based on the evolving context of a query. This mimics more closely how humans process visual information, by selectively focusing on details as needed.

- **Enhanced Precision and Robustness:** By enabling MLLMs to refine their visual understanding, the approach significantly boosts performance on tasks that demand high visual precision, such as fine-grained visual question answering, accurate spatial reasoning, and robust OCR. The ability to crop and re-encode specific regions directly addresses the challenge of small objects or text, while the Vision Re-Encoding Token can leverage specialized encoders to glean richer features. This also directly contributes to reducing visual hallucination, as the model can actively seek grounding information.

- **Improved Efficiency and Scalability:** A particularly impactful finding is that a 2B parameter model equipped with Visual Perception Tokens can outperform a 7B parameter model that lacks this capability. This demonstrates a clear path towards developing more efficient yet highly capable MLLMs. By allowing models to dynamically allocate computational resources for perception only when needed, and by making smaller models more effective, this research contributes to democratizing access to powerful multimodal AI systems by lowering computational barriers.

- **Novel Control Mechanism beyond Natural Language:** The design of Visual Perception Tokens as non-natural language control signals, seamlessly integrated into the next-token prediction paradigm, opens new avenues for MLLM control. This approach offers advantages in efficiency and flexibility compared to relying solely on natural language commands or fixed pipelines, setting a precedent for future innovations in MLLM interaction and autonomy. The explicit use of a control token's hidden state to condition feature projection is a clever way to embed nuanced control information without requiring explicit decoding.

- **Foundation for More Complex Visual Reasoning:** The ability for an MLLM to iteratively refine its visual input lays a foundation for more sophisticated visual reasoning chains. Future work could explore MLLMs performing multi-step visual analysis, where each step of perception informs the next, or even prompting other visual tools or human feedback through the generation of these tokens, similar to agentic behaviors.

- **Broad Applicability:** While demonstrated on VQA tasks, the concept of Visual Perception Tokens is adaptable. It could be extended to other multimodal tasks, different types of visual prompts, or even other modalities beyond vision, fostering a more generally intelligent and adaptive AI system. The lack of degeneration on general benchmarks (MME, MM-Bench) further suggests its practical viability without compromising existing capabilities.

In conclusion, this research marks a significant step towards more intelligent and autonomous MLLMs. By granting these models an internal mechanism for controlling and refining their visual perception, it not only pushes the boundaries of current model capabilities but also lays crucial groundwork for future developments in more efficient, robust, and perceptually aware artificial intelligence.
