# Introducing Visual Perception Token into Multimodal Large Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the National University of Singapore introduce Visual Perception Tokens, a mechanism enabling Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to autonomously control their visual perception process. This approach allows MLLMs to dynamically select regions for re-processing and incorporate enriched features from specialized encoders, leading to a 2B parameter MLLM outperforming a 7B parameter MLLM in visual understanding tasks.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) struggle with precise visual details, fine-grained attribute understanding, and accurate spatial reasoning due to static visual perception processes.
- MLLMs lack autonomous control over their visual perception, unable to decide when or how to refine their visual understanding based on query context, leading to incomplete or inaccurate outputs.
- Visual hallucination and misinformation in MLLMs often stem from insufficient or ambiguous visual information, as models cannot actively seek clarifying visual data.

## Method

- Introduces ==Visual Perception Tokens== into the MLLM's vocabulary, allowing the model to autonomously generate these non-natural language tokens to trigger specific visual perception actions.
- Proposes a ==Region Selection Token== mechanism that enables the MLLM to identify and re-encode specific image regions using grid-based coordinates, effectively increasing resolution for relevant areas.
- Develops a ==Vision Re-Encoding Token== that prompts the MLLM to re-encode the entire image with an additional, specialized vision encoder, with the token's hidden state acting as a control signal to a dedicated projector for feature integration.

## Results

- A **2B** parameter MLLM equipped with Visual Perception Tokens (2B+VPT) achieved an average performance score of **0.749**, a **30.9%** improvement over the base 2B model and outperforming a larger **7B** base model by **20.0%**.
- VPT substantially improved performance on fine-grained VQA (0.207 improvement over 7B baseline) and visual reasoning tasks, demonstrating enhanced capability in handling complex visual relationships and minute details.
- The approach maintained or surpassed the performance of larger models in zero-shot settings and across different image resolutions (224 and 512), indicating robust generalization and adaptability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Introducing Visual Perception Tokens allows MLLMs to autonomously control their visual perception, shifting from passive processing to active, iterative visual information extraction.
> - The use of grid-based Region Selection Tokens provides a robust and learnable method for MLLMs to focus on specific image regions, outperforming direct pixel-level bounding box generation.
> - Leveraging the hidden state of a 'Re-Encoding Control' token enables fine-grained influence over how enriched visual features from specialized encoders are integrated, enhancing feature utility for the LLM.
