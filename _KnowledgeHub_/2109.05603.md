# Learning to Navigate Sidewalks in Outdoor Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> A learning framework empowers quadrupedal robots to autonomously navigate complex urban sidewalks, following waypoints over kilometers in real outdoor environments. This is achieved through a two-staged simulation-based training process, employing intermediate semantic features and specialized sim-to-real transfer techniques for robust performance.

## Problem

- Enabling quadrupedal robots to autonomously navigate urban sidewalks for applications like last-mile delivery and patrol.
- Existing navigation systems often rely on extensive manual engineering or struggle with unstructured, dynamic outdoor environments and complex behaviors such as pedestrian avoidance.
- Learning-based methods for outdoor navigation face a significant sim-to-real gap, hindering the transfer of policies from simulation to complex real-world scenarios.

## Method

- A two-staged 'learning by cheating' framework trains an expert teacher with privileged information in an abstract PyBullet environment, then transfers this policy to a student in a high-fidelity CARLA simulator.
- The student policy leverages realistic sensors, specifically using intermediate feature layers from a re-trained semantic segmentation network (BiSeNet) as visual input (RFEAT).
- Targeted data augmentation and retraining of the semantic network for sidewalk perspectives, combined with LiDAR and GPS for localization, mitigate the sim-to-real gap.

## Results

- The quadruped robot successfully navigated **3.2** kilometers across six diverse urban routes in Atlanta, demonstrating robust long-distance sidewalk traversal with minimal human intervention.
- The 'INF-SEM-FEAT' agent achieved an **83.3%** success rate in real-world obstacle avoidance tests, effectively navigating past 17 out of 19 naturally occurring static and dynamic obstacles.
- The two-staged learning framework converged in approximately 30 hours, significantly reducing training time compared to an estimated 300+ hours for direct end-to-end learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intermediate feature representations from semantic networks are more robust for sim-to-real transfer in outdoor navigation than raw RGB images or final semantic classifications.
> - Adapting semantic network training objectives to account for sidewalk perspectives and the robot's current occupancy significantly improves real-world performance and generalizability.
> - A two-staged teacher-student learning framework enables efficient policy training, reducing the required simulation time tenfold compared to single-stage end-to-end approaches.
