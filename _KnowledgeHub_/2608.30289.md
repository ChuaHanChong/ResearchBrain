# CometVLA: Co-Training on an Embodied Data Pyramid towards Physical Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> CometVLA is a vision-language-action model co-trained across the full embodied data pyramid: teleoperation, simulation, egocentric video, and VQA. It contributes CometData/CometBench, physical-commonsense VQA harvested from the robot's own trajectories, plus Global Action Prior tokens, a detached bottleneck conditioning a flow-matching action expert. It reaches 89.24% and 88.38% success on RoboTwin 2.0 easy and hard splits.

## Problem

- Existing physical-commonsense VQA corpora are disembodied, drawn from web imagery or third-person clips, so they mismatch the robot's action distribution and embodiment.
- Prior work decouples perception from action or reduces control to discrete primitives, leaving unproven whether better VLM physical understanding improves real action generation.
- Egocentric human video is treated only as auxiliary pre-training blend or world-model fodder, never curated into in-domain physical reasoning supervision for policies.

## Method

- **CometData** pipeline: a ==physics-aware middleware== scores keyframes by Hamiltonian jump, gripper step, and power anomaly, then 16 generators across 5 domains synthesize 1M grounded QA pairs.
- **Global Action Prior** token, a single learnable bottleneck inserted in the ==Qwen3-VL== backbone that crosses a ==stop-gradient barrier== to condition the ==DiT== action expert via ==cross-attention==.
- Single-stage co-training combining ==autoregressive language loss==, ==FAST discrete action tokens==, and ==flow-matching== continuous actions, using ==knowledge insulation== to protect pre-trained multimodal representations.

## Results

- On RoboTwin 2.0, CometVLA scores **89.24%** easy and **88.38%** hard success, exceeding Motus, LingBot-VLA, HALO, and flow-matching baselines, whose hard-split scores collapse as low as **13.72%**.
- Ablations show swapping embodied physical VQA for generic VQA causes the largest drop (to **83.54/83.46**), while removing the GAP token costs roughly **3 points** on both splits.
- GAP linear probing beats mean-pooled VLM features by **7.0 points** on gripper state and **3.9** on motion magnitude; CometBench correlates with policy success (Pearson r up to **0.721**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Physical-reasoning data helps policies most when harvested from the same embodiment and visual domain used for action training, not from generic web sources.
> - A narrow detached latent interface can carry semantics into a control head while blocking gradients, avoiding interference between language modeling and low-level control.
> - Spatial reasoning benchmarks predict manipulation success better than task understanding, but the correlation rests on checkpoints clustered near 85%, limiting statistical strength.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is progressively integrating Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage large visuo-linguistic backbones to endow robots with capabilities such as grounding, spatial reasoning, and instruction following. This integration aims to enable robots to perform complex tasks in diverse environments. Despite advancements, VLA models often exhibit brittleness in tasks requiring physical commonsense, a critical aspect for robust real-world manipulation.

Existing approaches to incorporating physical knowledge into Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently employ Visual Question Answering (VQA) datasets for fine-tuning or alignment. However, a prevalent limitation is that these VQA datasets are often disembodied, deriving visual input from generic web images or simulation renderings rather than direct robot-centric observations. This creates a domain gap, where the VQA scenarios and data distributions do not consistently align with the robot's action data or specific embodiment during policy training. Consequently, it remains largely unclear whether improvements in VLM physical understanding genuinely translate into enhanced action generation performance in practical robot manipulation settings.

Furthermore, egocentric human videos, a scalable source of natural interaction data, are typically utilized in existing robot foundation models as auxiliary pre-training data or for learning world models and latent representations. Their potential for curating specific physical-understanding VQA pairs for co-training with VLA models has not been fully explored. Previous works have also often decoupled perception from action or simplified control problems to high-level planning, which limits the applicability of their conclusions to real-world continuous control.

CometVLA directly addresses these identified gaps by proposing an end-to-end framework that systematically integrates physical understanding into VLA models. It focuses on bridging the misalignment between VLM representations and the dynamic physical realities of robotic tasks, aligning VQA data directly with robot embodiment and action data, and demonstrating a quantifiable correlation between VLM physical understanding and downstream action generation. This work contributes to the development of more physically intelligent and reliable robot manipulation policies by proposing an architecture and training paradigm designed for explicit consumption of physical commonsense.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness and effectiveness of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in manipulation tasks that necessitate physical commonsense. The authors observed that existing VLA models often lack the necessary physical understanding, leading to brittle performance in real-world scenarios. This brittleness is largely attributed to several underlying issues, which form the core motivations and specific objectives of the CometVLA project.

**Motivation:**
1. **Misalignment of Representations:** Current VLM backbones, while capable of grounding and instruction following, are fundamentally misaligned with the dynamic, contact-rich physical interactions inherent in robotic manipulation. Their pre-training often does not adequately prepare them for the continuous, temporally evolving physical dynamics crucial for robust robot control.
2. **Disembodied and Mismatched VQA Data:** Existing physical VQA datasets, intended to inject physical priors, are typically disembodied. They are often sourced from internet images or simulation renderings that do not match the visual domain, data distribution, or specific embodiment of robot action data. This domain gap hinders the transfer of learned physical reasoning to actual robot control.
3. **Unclear Translation to Action Generation:** A fundamental question in the field has been whether improved physical understanding in VLMs actually translates into better action generation in realistic manipulation scenarios. Prior efforts often decouple perception from action or simplify the control problem, leaving this question largely unanswered in the context of continuous robot control.
4. **Underutilization of Embodied Data:** Egocentric human videos, a rich source of fine-grained manipulation behaviors, are frequently treated as merely auxiliary pre-training data or used for learning world models, rather than being explicitly leveraged for curating physical-understanding VQA pairs to directly benefit action generation.

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Construct an Embodied Physical VQA Corpus and Benchmark:** To address the issue of mismatched data, a primary objective was to create `CometData` and `CometBench`. These resources are designed as an embodied physical commonsense VQA corpus and benchmark, respectively, strictly aligned with the robot’s action data and embodiment. The goal is to provide in-domain semantic enhancement for physical understanding.
2. **Develop an Architectural Mechanism for Physical Commonsense Consumption:** To facilitate the effective integration of physical commonsense into the action generation process, the authors aimed to introduce `Global Action Prior (GAP) tokens`. These tokens are conceived as a compact, learnable information bottleneck designed to isolate task-agnostic motion regularities and allow the action head to consume physical commonsense without corrupting the pre-trained VLM backbone.
3. **Implement a Co-training Paradigm across the Embodied Data Pyramid:** The research sought to establish a systematic training approach, `CometVLA`, that leverages all layers of the embodied data pyramid—spanning teleoperation, simulation, egocentric trajectories, and VQA data. This co-training paradigm aims to jointly train the VLM backbone and action expert, structurally coupling them to achieve end-to-end action generation with enhanced physical understanding.
4. **Validate the Correlation between VLM Performance and VLA Success:** A crucial objective was to empirically demonstrate that stronger VLM performance on the `CometBench` benchmark directly correlates with higher VLA success rates in downstream manipulation tasks, thereby proving that physical understanding pre-training genuinely benefits robot manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and approach

CometVLA is presented as an end-to-end framework designed to infuse physical commonsense into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its methodology spans data construction, model architecture design, and a multi-stage training paradigm.

#### 3.1. Data Level: CometData and CometBench Construction

The foundation of CometVLA's physical understanding capabilities is `CometData`, a corpus of 1 million embodied physical VQA pairs, incorporating over 1.8 million images. This data is created through a physics-aware embodied data generation pipeline:
1. **Multi-source Embodied Trajectories:** Trajectories are collected from diverse sources, forming an embodied data pyramid. These sources include teleoperation data, robot simulation data, and egocentric videos, ensuring a hierarchical structure with increasing physical grounding.
2. **Physics Middleware:** A key component is a physics-aware middleware that processes synchronized proprioceptive states ($s_t$) and commanded actions ($a_t$). Instead of uniform sampling, this middleware identifies manipulation-critical keyframes using dynamics-based criteria:
 - **Hamiltonian jump ($C_A(t)$):** Detects abrupt energy changes during contact, impact, or release.
 - **Gripper step ($C_B(t)$):** Captures grasp/release boundaries by monitoring gripper aperture changes.
 - **Power anomaly ($C_C(t)$):** Flags phases of high command effort with disproportionately small Hamiltonian changes.
 A saliency score ($S_t$) aggregates these indicators to prioritize physically salient frames. Each selected keyframe is paired with a structured descriptor summarizing the triggered criteria, energy changes, aperture changes, command effort, response ratio, and inferred task phase.
3. **Category-Specific QA Synthesis:** Sixteen generators, organized across five domains (Spatial Reasoning, Task Understanding, Physics & Dynamics, Error & Tool, Affordance), transform these middleware descriptors into question-answer pairs. These generators fill category templates with descriptors, render selected keyframes, and prompt a teacher VLM to produce grounded answers. This process ensures the generated VQA data is aligned with the robot data recipe for in-domain semantic enhancement.

A balanced held-out subset of CometData, named `CometBench`, consists of 2,000 question-answer pairs. It serves as a benchmark for evaluating embodied physical reasoning in VLM-VLA models. Evaluation involves an LLM judge comparing candidate answers against reference answers, assessing correctness, logical coherence, relevance, completeness, and physical reasoning on a 100-score scale.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture Level: CometVLA Architecture

CometVLA integrates auto-regressive vision-language modeling with diffusion-based continuous action generation within a unified framework:
1. **VLM Backbone:** A pre-trained Qwen3-VL backbone is utilized. It processes multi-view visual observations, proprioceptive states, language instructions, and discrete FAST action tokens. The backbone is trained under a standard auto-regressive objective ($L_{AR}$) for language generation and $L_{fast}$ for supervising discretized action tokens.
2. **Action Expert:** A lightweight diffusion-based action expert is attached to enable high-frequency continuous control. This expert is trained with a flow-matching objective ($L_{fm}$), predicting continuous flow-matching (FM) actions. Continuous actions are noised using a Beta-sampled timestep and optimized via velocity regression, conditioned on VLM embeddings, GAP tokens, and state tokens. A stop-gradient barrier isolates the action expert from the backbone during training, ensuring $L_{fm}$ only updates the action head and GAP token parameters, thus preserving the VLM's pre-trained multimodal reasoning capabilities.
3. **Global Action Prior (GAP) Tokens:** To bridge the auto-regressive VLM domain and the bidirectional diffusion policy domain, learnable `GAP tokens` are introduced. These tokens (1024-dimensional) are inserted into the backbone's token sequence and transferred to the action expert through a detached latent pathway. They function as a global action prior, aggregating high-level semantic and temporal context from the VLM backbone, and conditioning action generation within the DiT-based action expert via cross-attention. The GAP token is specifically permitted to traverse the stop-gradient boundary, enabling controlled semantic information flow without gradient leakage into the backbone, thereby decoupling language modeling from continuous control. The overall training objective is $L_{total} = L_{AR} + L_{fast} + L_{fm}$.

#### 3.3. Training Level: Co-training Paradigm

CometVLA's training is conducted in two stages:
1. **Co-training Stage (Pre-training):** The Qwen3-VL-4B backbone, FAST action tokenizer, and DiT-B flow-matching action head with learnable GAP tokens are jointly trained. The training data mixture includes AgiBot-World-Beta, InternData-A1, EgoLive, EgoDex, common VQA datasets, and CometData. Robot datasets are retargeted to a bi-manual format with actions padded to 32 dimensions. This stage runs for 100,000 steps using AdamW optimizer, a cosine learning rate scheduler, gradient checkpointing, and mixed precision.
2. **Post-training Stage (Fine-tuning):** The model, initialized from the pre-trained checkpoint, is further fine-tuned on task-specific datasets for real-world deployment or RoboTwin simulation benchmarks. No modules are frozen during this stage.

### 4. Main findings and results

The evaluation of CometVLA involved both real-world robot experiments and simulation benchmarks, alongside detailed correlation and ablation analyses.

#### 4.1. Real-Robot Experiments

Experiments were conducted on a G1 dual-arm robot (AgiBot Robotics) within a dressing table organization scenario. CometVLA was fine-tuned on task-specific datasets for five distinct manipulation tasks, with 16 trials performed for each.
- **Success Rates:** The success rates varied across tasks: Makeup Sponge (93.75%), BB Cream (75.00%), Serum (43.75%), Lotion (37.50%), and Liquid Foundation (31.25%).
- **Observations:** The variations in success rate were noted to be influenced by factors such as object size and texture. Failure case analysis, exemplified by an instance where the robot prematurely opened the gripper, indicates challenges with precise temporal control in some scenarios.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on RoboTwin 2.0

CometVLA was compared against several VLA baselines (including discrete-token, diffusion, and flow-matching action heads) on the RoboTwin 2.0 simulation benchmark.
- **Performance:** CometVLA achieved a success rate of 89.24% on the RoboTwin Easy (clean) split and 88.38% on the Hard (randomized) split. These results indicated an outperformance of prior methods, including $\pi^{0.5}$.
- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Impact of Embodied Physical VQA:** Replacing the curated embodied physical VQA corpus (CometData) with an equivalent amount of generic VQA datasets during pre-training led to the most significant performance degradation ("w/o phys. VQA"). This finding suggests that physical understanding derived from embodied data substantially contributes to downstream manipulation performance.
 - **Impact of GAP Tokens:** Removing the Global Action Prior (GAP) token ("w/o GAP") also resulted in a consistent decline in performance, validating its contribution to the model's capabilities.

#### 4.3. VLM-VLA Performance Correlation Analysis

A correlation analysis was performed between VLM performance on CometBench and VLA success rates on the RoboTwin 2.0 easy benchmark, utilizing models with identical VLM backbone and action expert configurations but varied training mixtures or configurations.
- **Overall Trend:** A consistent positive correlation was observed across all semantic domains, indicating that stronger physical understanding in the VLM is predictive of higher success rates in downstream robotic manipulation.
- **Per-Domain Correlation (Pearson r):**
 - Spatial Reasoning: Exhibited the strongest correlation (0.721), suggesting that geometric and spatial priors are highly effective in transferring to embodied control.
 - Physics & Dynamics: Showed the second strongest correlation (0.645), directly supporting the claim that physical understanding pre-training benefits manipulation.
 - Error & Tool: 0.624
 - Task Understanding: 0.586
 - Affordance: 0.552

#### 4.4. Analysis on GAP Tokens

Two complementary analyses were conducted on a held-out subset of AgiBot-World-Beta to understand the role of the 1024-dimensional GAP token compared to a 2560-dimensional mean-pooled final-layer VLM representation.
- **Qualitative Semantic Preservation (t-SNE):** t-SNE visualization of GAP token features revealed continuous global regions for broad embodiment concepts (e.g., "arm," "left") and localized clusters for interaction-centric semantics (e.g., "bottle," "door"). This indicates that the GAP token learns a semantic-level representation of actions despite its bottleneck design.
- **Quantitative Action Information Content (Linear Probing):** Linear probing on action-relevant labels demonstrated that the GAP token consistently outperformed the VLM-mean baseline. Specifically, it achieved a +7.0% higher accuracy on gripper state, +0.7% on motion direction, and +3.9% on motion magnitude. These results suggest that the GAP token, despite its smaller dimensionality, effectively preserves the backbone’s semantic structure while selectively distilling control-relevant information into a compact latent interface for the action expert.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

This research makes several contributions to the field of robotic manipulation and embodied AI, with potential impacts on the development of more capable and robust robotic systems.

Firstly, the work provides empirical evidence demonstrating a positive correlation between improved VLM physical understanding and higher success rates in downstream VLA action generation. This addresses a critical, previously underexplored question, establishing that investment in physical commonsense pre-training for VLMs genuinely translates into enhanced performance in realistic robot manipulation scenarios. This insight can guide future research towards more focused development of physically grounded VLM capabilities.

Secondly, the introduction of `CometData` and `CometBench` provides a novel and valuable resource for the research community. As the first embodied physical VQA corpus and benchmark explicitly aligned with robot action data and embodiment, it addresses the pervasive issue of domain mismatch in existing datasets. This alignment is designed to foster in-domain semantic enhancement, offering a more relevant training and evaluation environment for developing physically intelligent robot policies. Such curated datasets can accelerate progress in embodied reasoning and manipulation by providing high-quality, targeted data.

Thirdly, the architectural innovation of `Global Action Prior (GAP) tokens` presents a modular and effective mechanism for integrating physical commonsense into VLA models. By creating a compact information bottleneck that aggregates high-level semantic and temporal context from the VLM backbone and conditions the action expert without corrupting the pre-trained backbone, GAP tokens improve optimization stability and preserve multimodal reasoning capabilities. This architectural design could inspire similar approaches in future VLA models seeking to decouple reasoning and control more effectively.

Finally, `CometVLA`'s demonstrated superior performance on both simulation benchmarks (RoboTwin 2.0) and real-world dual-arm manipulation tasks, when compared to existing strong VLA baselines, highlights its effectiveness. The co-training paradigm, leveraging the full embodied data pyramid (teleoperation, simulation, egocentric video, and VQA), offers a scalable and comprehensive approach to developing physically intelligent agents. The systematic correlation and ablation analyses further validate the proposed methods, providing actionable insights into the contributions of each component. This research represents a step towards developing more reliable, adaptable, and physically intelligent robots capable of performing complex manipulation tasks in diverse real-world environments.
