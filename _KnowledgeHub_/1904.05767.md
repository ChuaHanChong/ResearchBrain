# Learning joint reconstruction of hands and manipulated objects

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces an end-to-end learnable model for joint 3D reconstruction of hands and manipulated objects from single RGB images. It leverages a novel differentiable contact loss to enforce physical plausibility and presents the large-scale synthetic ObMan dataset, demonstrating improved reconstruction accuracy and transferability to real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Reconstructing accurate 3D meshes of both hands and manipulated objects simultaneously from a single monocular RGB image is challenging due to severe mutual occlusions.
- Existing methods for hand or object reconstruction often do not account for physical interaction constraints, leading to unrealistic interpenetration or lack of contact.
- A lack of large-scale datasets with precise 3D ground truth for interacting hands and objects hinders the development and training of robust deep learning models.

## Method

- An ==end-to-end neural network== architecture that integrates differentiable parametric models (==MANO== for hands, ==AtlasNet==-based for objects) is used to predict 3D meshes from a single RGB input.
- A novel ==differentiable contact loss== is developed to enforce physical plausibility, penalizing interpenetration and encouraging contact between the reconstructed hand and object meshes.
- A large-scale synthetic dataset, **ObMan**, is generated with diverse hand-object manipulations, providing ground truth for 3D shapes, poses, and interactions to facilitate model training.

## Results

- Training on images with occlusions reduced hand reconstruction errors by **12%** and object errors by **25%** compared to training on unoccluded hand and object images.
- The balanced contact loss (equal weighting of repulsion and attraction) simultaneously improved maximum penetration depth and grasp stability in simulations without compromising reconstruction metrics.
- The model, trained solely on ObMan, demonstrated generalization to real-world scenarios and novel objects (e.g., CORe50) without fine-tuning, outperforming models trained on limited real data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inclusion of a balanced differentiable contact loss significantly improves the physical quality of hand-object reconstructions by reducing interpenetration and enhancing grasp stability.
> - Training models with images depicting hand-object occlusions is critical for robust reconstruction performance, yielding substantial improvements over training on isolated or unoccluded data.
> - Pre-training on large-scale synthetic data like ObMan enables effective transfer to real-world datasets, particularly in low-data regimes, and benefits from matching the synthetic data's distribution to the target real-world distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper contributes to the field of 3D computer vision, specifically focusing on the interpretation of human actions involving hand-object interactions. Understanding these interactions is a foundational problem with applications in virtual and augmented reality, human-computer interaction, action recognition, and robotic learning.

Historically, research in hand analysis began with estimating and tracking hand poses using articulated or statistical models. The introduction of RGB-D sensors advanced hand pose estimation from depth images. More recent efforts have shifted towards monocular RGB images, leveraging convolutional neural networks (CNNs) for 2D and 3D hand pose estimation. However, much of this work has focused on sparse keypoint estimation, which is insufficient for detailed reasoning about hand-object contact. While some methods estimate full 3D hand meshes, they often rely on fitting to 2D predictions or tracking with good initialization. Reconstructing full 3D hand and object meshes from monocular RGB images, especially during manipulation, presents challenges due to significant occlusions of both the hand and the object.

Object reconstruction has also evolved, exploring various 3D representations such as voxels, point clouds, and mesh surfaces within CNN frameworks. Methods like AtlasNet and Pixel2Mesh aim to produce accurate object shapes, typically from simple renderings of CAD models. When hands are involved, previous work on joint hand-object reconstruction has primarily utilized multi-view RGB or RGB-D inputs, often relying on optimization-based approaches to enforce contact and penetration constraints. These methods have commonly employed proximity metrics or contact measurements. A key limitation has been the lack of large-scale datasets providing ground truth 3D shapes for interacting hands and objects, hindering the training of deep learning models. Synthetic datasets have emerged as an alternative for hand pose estimation but have generally lacked hand-object interaction scenarios.

This research positions itself by addressing the problem of joint hand and object reconstruction from monocular RGB images, explicitly exploiting the physical constraints of manipulation. It aims to bridge the gap between isolated hand or object reconstruction and comprehensive hand-object interaction modeling, particularly for dense mesh outputs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop an end-to-end learnable model for the joint 3D reconstruction of human hands and manipulated objects from a single monocular RGB image. This endeavor is motivated by both the inherent challenges and potential opportunities within the domain of hand-object interaction analysis.

The main motivations are:
- **Addressing Occlusion Challenges:** During manipulation, hands often severely occlude objects, and objects can similarly occlude parts of the hand. This mutual occlusion significantly complicates accurate 3D reconstruction from a single 2D image. Previous methods have made progress on hand or object reconstruction in isolation but struggle with the combined, occluded scenario.
- **Leveraging Physical Constraints:** While occlusions present difficulties, the physical nature of hand-object manipulations introduces inherent constraints that can simplify the problem space. For instance, hands and objects must be in contact during a grasp but cannot physically interpenetrate. The authors hypothesize that integrating these physical plausibility constraints into the learning process can regularize the reconstruction and lead to more accurate and realistic outputs.
- **Bridging the Data Gap:** A significant barrier to developing robust deep learning models for hand-object interaction is the scarcity of large-scale datasets with precise 3D ground truth annotations for both hands and manipulated objects. Existing datasets are often too small, provide only partial annotations, or lack the diversity required for training complex neural networks. This necessitates the creation of a new, comprehensive dataset.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives articulated by the authors are:
1. **Develop an end-to-end learnable model:** This model should integrate differentiable components for hand shape, object shape, and their relative pose, allowing for direct training from monocular RGB input to 3D mesh outputs.
2. **Propose a novel contact loss:** This loss function should encode the physical constraints of manipulation, penalizing interpenetration between hand and object meshes while encouraging contact in plausible regions.
3. **Create a new large-scale synthetic dataset (ObMan):** This dataset should feature diverse hand-object manipulations, providing ground truth for 3D hand and object shapes, poses, and interactions to facilitate model training and evaluation.
4. **Demonstrate transferability to real data:** The developed model, primarily trained on synthetic data, should exhibit the ability to generalize and perform effectively on real-world images, addressing the practical limitations of synthetic-only training.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves an end-to-end learnable neural network architecture designed for joint 3D reconstruction of hands and objects from a single RGB image. The system leverages parametric models, differentiable layers, and a novel contact-aware loss function, supported by a newly generated synthetic dataset.

#### 3.1. Network Architecture

The network architecture is composed of two main branches, each starting with a ResNet18 encoder pre-trained on ImageNet. One branch processes hand-related information, and the other handles object-related information. The output consists of hand and object meshes, along with their relative scale and translation. The model operates in a single forward pass.

#### 3.2. Hand Mesh Estimation

- **Differentiable MANO Model:** The core of hand reconstruction is the integration of the MANO parametric hand model as a differentiable layer within the network. MANO maps low-dimensional pose (θ) and shape (β) parameters to a 3D hand mesh.
- **Parameter Prediction:** The hand encoder extracts an image encoding (Φ_Hand), which is then fed into a fully connected network to regress the MANO pose (30 PCA components of the 45-dimensional pose space) and shape (10-dimensional β) parameters.
- **Supervision:**
 - **Vertex and Joint Positions (L_V_Hand, L_J):** L2 distance is used to supervise the predicted hand vertices and 16 MANO joints, augmented with 5 fingertip vertices to form 21 keypoints. Root-relative 3D positions are used.
 - **Shape Regularization (L_β):** An L2 regularization (‖β‖²) is applied to the shape parameters to constrain them towards the average hand shape (β=0), preventing extreme deformations.
- **Combined Hand Loss:** L_Hand = L_V_Hand + L_J + L_β.

#### 3.3. Object Mesh Estimation

- **AtlasNet for Object Shape:** The object branch employs an AtlasNet-based approach to reconstruct object meshes, focusing on genus 0 topologies. AtlasNet inputs sampled point coordinates (from a sphere) concatenated with image features (Φ_Obj) and outputs deformed mesh coordinates. The reconstruction is performed in view-centered coordinates.
- **Supervision:**
 - **Vertex Positions (L_V_Obj, L_n_V_Obj):** The primary loss is a symmetric Chamfer distance between predicted object vertices and points sampled on the ground truth object surface. An initial normalized object loss (L_n_V_Obj) is used during an initial training phase.
 - **Shape Regularization (L_E, L_L):**
 - **Edge Regularization (L_E):** Penalizes edges with lengths significantly different from the average edge length, promoting uniform triangulation.
 - **Laplacian Smoothness (L_L):** Encourages smoothness by minimizing the mean curvature of the mesh, based on the discrete Laplace-Beltrami operator.
- **Hand-Relative Coordinate System (L_S, L_T):** Beyond normalized object reconstruction, the model predicts the object's translation (T) and scale (S) relative to the hand. L2 losses (L_T = ‖T - ˆT‖², L_S = ‖S - ˆS‖²) supervise these predictions.
- **Combined Object Loss:** L_Object = L_T + L_S + L_V_Obj (for hand-relative training) and L_n_Object = L_n_V_Obj + μ_L*L_L + μ_E*L_E (for normalized training).

#### 3.4. Contact Loss (L_Contact)

A novel, differentiable contact loss is introduced to enforce physical constraints on hand-object interactions:
- **Penalization Function:** A common penalization function l_α(x) = α tanh(x/α) is used, where α is a characteristic distance.
- **Repulsion (L_R):** Penalizes interpenetration by summing l_r(d(v, V_Obj)) for all hand vertices (v) detected inside the object (Int(Obj)). Ray casting is used to determine if a vertex is inside the object. The repulsion characteristic distance (r) is set to 2cm.
- **Attraction (L_A):** Encourages contact between the hand and object. It is applied to specific hand vertices (C_i) that are statistically identified as frequent contact regions (5 fingertips and part of the palm, clustered into 6 regions from ObMan data). The loss is l_a(d(C_i ∩ Ext(Obj), V_Obj)) for each region. The attraction characteristic distance (a) is set to 1cm.
- **Final Contact Loss:** L_Contact = λ_R * L_R + (1 - λ_R) * L_A, where λ_R balances repulsion and attraction (empirically set to 0.5 for optimal balance). The overall contact loss is added to the total loss with a weight μ_C.

#### 3.5. Training Strategy

The network is initially trained with L_Hand + L_Object. Subsequently, fine-tuning is performed with the addition of the contact loss: L_Hand + L_Object + μ_C * L_Contact. Batch normalization layers are frozen during training on synthetic datasets.

#### 3.6. ObMan Dataset Generation

To address the data scarcity, a large-scale synthetic dataset called ObMan (Object Manipulation) was created:
- **Objects:** 2,772 diverse 3D object models from 8 everyday categories in ShapeNet.
- **Grasps:** 21,000 plausible grasps generated using the GraspIt software, adapted for the MANO hand model, and sorted by a heuristic quality metric.
- **Body Pose:** Hand poses are transferred to SMPL+H body models, with body poses and shapes sampled from the SURREAL dataset (CMU MoCap, CAESAR) for realism and varied occlusions.
- **Textures:** Object textures from ShapeNet, body textures from SURREAL, combined with high-resolution hand textures from 20 subjects, with skin tone matching.
- **Rendering:** Images rendered using Blender with varying backgrounds (LSUN, ImageNet). Each configuration yields hand-object, hand-only, object-only images, along with segmentation and depth maps. Filters are applied to ensure visibility of hand and object.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several key findings related to the impact of occlusions, the effectiveness of the contact loss, and the transferability from synthetic to real data.

#### 4.1. Effect of Occlusions

Controlled experiments on the ObMan dataset demonstrated the significant impact of occlusions:
- Training a model on images depicting hand-object interactions (HO-img) led to a substantial improvement in reconstruction accuracy for both hands and objects when tested on occluded images, compared to models trained on isolated, unoccluded hands (H-img) or objects (O-img).
- Specifically, reconstruction errors for hands decreased by 12%, and for objects by 25% when both training and testing involved occluded hand-object images.
- The most challenging scenario was observed when training on unoccluded images and testing on occluded ones, underscoring the critical importance of including occlusions in training data for accurate hand-object configuration reconstruction.

#### 4.2. Effect of Contact Loss

The introduction and balancing of the novel contact loss significantly improved the physical plausibility of the reconstructions:
- Without an explicit contact loss, the models produced reconstructions with considerable interpenetration (average of 9mm for ObMan and 19mm for FHB_C), indicating a lack of implicit learning of physical rules.
- **Repulsion (L_R) in isolation:** Effectively minimized maximum penetration depth (reducing it by 33% on ObMan and 68% on FHB_C). However, this often occurred at the expense of grasp stability in physics simulations.
- **Attraction (L_A) in isolation:** Stabilized grasps in simulation but increased collisions between hands and objects.
- **Balanced Contact Loss (λ_R = 0.5):** An equal weighting of repulsion and attraction terms (L_R = 0.5) simultaneously improved both maximum penetration depth and simulation displacement, indicating enhanced physical quality. This improvement was observed without negatively impacting the reconstruction metrics for either the synthetic ObMan or the real FHB_C datasets.
- Qualitative results on FHB_C showed notable improvements in contact and reduced penetration with the balanced contact loss.

#### 4.3. Synthetic to Real Transfer

The study investigated the advantages of pre-training on the synthetic ObMan dataset for real-world applications:
- **Low-Data Regimes:** Pre-training on ObMan proved beneficial for fine-tuning on real datasets (FHB, HIC), particularly when the amount of available real training data was limited (e.g., less than 1000 images). In such scenarios, pre-training on synthetic data was critical for both hand and object reconstruction accuracy.
- **Target Distribution Matching:** Experiments on the HIC dataset revealed that the similarity between the synthetic training data's hand-object pose distribution and the target real data distribution was crucial. The best performance was achieved when synthetic data was generated to match the object and pose distributions of the real HIC dataset, outperforming training solely on real images, even before fine-tuning for object performance.
- For hand pose, performance saturated after pre-training when the synthetic data matched the target distribution, indicating that the real data provided little additional improvement.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results on CORe50

The model, trained exclusively on ObMan, demonstrated generalization capabilities to diverse real-world scenarios without any fine-tuning on real data:
- The model showed robustness across different object categories, including novel objects not present in the ObMan training set (e.g., lightbulbs).
- Global outlines of hands and objects were generally well-recovered.
- Limitations included coarse object shapes, missed fine details (e.g., phone antennas), and occasional errors in the relative positioning of the hand and object, sometimes leading to hand-object interpenetration. The model also occasionally produced anatomically implausible hand poses.

#### 4.5. Supplementary Findings (from Appendix)

- **Hand Loss Ablation:** Combining L2 losses for joint positions (L_J) and shape regularization (L_β) improved mean end-point error (EPE) for keypoints. Dense vertex supervision (L_V_Hand) provided a minor additional benefit when available.
- **MANO Pose Representation:** Using 30 PCA components for MANO pose parameters was found to be optimal, as performance saturated beyond this dimensionality.
- **State-of-the-Art Hand Pose Comparison:** The hand estimation branch alone demonstrated competitive performance on the StereoHands benchmark, achieving 3D PCK comparable to state-of-the-art methods that focus solely on sparse keypoint prediction, while also outputting a dense hand mesh.
- **Object Reconstruction Viewpoint:** AtlasNet performed similarly whether reconstructing objects in a canonical view or a camera-centered view, indicating no degradation in accuracy for the latter.
- **Object Mesh Regularization:** Edge regularization (L_E) and Laplacian smoothness (L_L) were shown to improve the perceptual quality and triangulation of predicted object meshes, reducing irregularities, even though they might slightly increase the Chamfer distance metric.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions that have implications for the field of 3D computer vision and its applications.

Firstly, the paper introduces an **end-to-end learnable model for joint 3D reconstruction of hands and objects from single RGB images.** This represents an advancement over previous approaches that often treated hand and object reconstruction in isolation or relied on multi-view or depth inputs. The ability to reconstruct both entities and their interaction from a single monocular image broadens the applicability of such systems to common image and video sources.

Secondly, the development of a **novel differentiable contact loss** is a significant methodological contribution. By explicitly incorporating physical constraints—penalizing interpenetration and encouraging contact—the model is guided towards physically plausible hand-object configurations. This enhances the realism and utility of the reconstructions, moving beyond purely geometric fidelity to incorporate an understanding of interaction dynamics. The empirical validation of this loss demonstrates its effectiveness in improving grasp quality metrics, such as reduced penetration and increased stability in simulation.

Thirdly, the creation of the **large-scale synthetic ObMan dataset** addresses a critical data scarcity issue in this domain. Annotating real-world 3D hand-object interactions with dense ground truth is prohibitively difficult. ObMan provides a diverse and comprehensive resource for training deep neural networks, covering various objects, grasps, body poses, and rendering conditions. This dataset is made publicly available, which can facilitate further research in the community.

Finally, the study demonstrates the **transferability of models trained on synthetic data to real-world scenarios.** This finding is crucial, as it validates the practical utility of synthetic datasets like ObMan. The effectiveness of pre-training on ObMan, particularly in low-data regimes for real datasets, indicates a viable pathway for developing robust models even when real-world annotated data is limited. The qualitative results on the CORe50 dataset further support the model's generalization capabilities to unseen objects and diverse backgrounds.

The potential impact of this work extends to several areas:
- **Robotics:** The ability to accurately reconstruct hand-object manipulations can inform imitation learning for robots, allowing them to learn complex manipulation skills from human demonstrations.
- **Virtual and Augmented Reality:** More realistic and physically consistent hand-object interactions can enhance immersion and interactivity in VR/AR applications.
- **Human-Computer Interaction:** Improved understanding of hand gestures and object manipulations can lead to more intuitive and natural interfaces.
- **Action Recognition and Analysis:** Detailed 3D reconstructions can provide richer input for understanding complex human actions, particularly those involving object interaction.

Future directions for research, as suggested by the authors, include learning grasping affordances from large-scale visual data and recognizing complex and dynamic hand actions, building upon the foundational capabilities established in this work.
