# Ascending the Infinite Ladder: Benchmarking Spatial Deformation Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces Inf-Bench, the first comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate Vision-Language Models' capabilities in spatial deformation reasoning across 2D, 2.5D, and 3D contexts. The study reveals that current VLMs exhibit significant limitations in understanding and executing multi-step spatial transformations, particularly in three dimensions, where their performance lags far behind human capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Model (VLM) benchmarks primarily assess static spatial relationships, navigation, or general scene understanding, largely overlooking complex spatial deformation reasoning.
- A critical gap exists in evaluating how VLMs understand, predict, and execute dynamic transformations that alter an object's intrinsic form and topological properties.
- Traditional static benchmarks quickly become obsolete as VLMs rapidly improve, failing to continuously challenge and differentiate model capabilities over time.

## Method

- Introduces ==Inf-Bench==, a novel benchmark evaluating ==spatial deformation reasoning== across 2D, 2.5D (layered 2D), and 3D (Rubik's Cube) tasks, assessing both forward (predicting outcome) and inverse (inferring operations) reasoning.
- Develops an ==automated shape generation engine== to produce an unlimited supply of unique problem pairs, ensuring data quality, diversity, and preventing data leakage.
- Proposes an 'Infinite Ladder Competition' framework that dynamically adjusts task difficulty based on model performance (number of deformation steps), enabling continuous assessment and preventing performance saturation.

## Results

- Human evaluators consistently and significantly outperform all **18** tested VLMs across all spatial dimensions (2D, 2.5D, 3D) and reasoning types, with the largest gap in 3D tasks.
- Models achieve competitive performance in 2D and 2.5D deformation but show severe limitations in 3D, with most models failing even basic single-step deformations, though select models like OpenAI O3/O4-mini and Gemini-2.5-pro show limited ability.
- Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) significantly boosts reasoning depth in 2D and 2.5D tasks, but 3D forward reasoning performance remains limited (rarely exceeding five steps), and general reasoning enhancement methods offer only marginal improvements.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-language models exhibit two main reasoning strategies: 'direct pairing' and 'step-by-step reasoning execution', with stronger models favoring the latter.
> - Perceptual encoding is a significant bottleneck; providing pre-encoded textual input substantially improves VLM performance in 2D/2.5D spatial deformation tasks.
> - Current VLMs fundamentally struggle with 3D spatial deformation reasoning due to complex coupled shape states and cumulative errors, indicating a need for new architectural approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has seen significant progress, enabling models to perform impressive feats in language understanding and image recognition. However, a critical gap exists in their ability to perform complex *spatial deformation reasoning*. The broader research landscape for VLMs largely focuses on tasks such as static scene understanding, identifying spatial relationships between objects, navigation, and planning in dynamic environments. Existing benchmarks, as highlighted by the authors (e.g., iVISPAR, SPGym, LEGO-Puzzles, VisuLogic, VSI-Bench, Sparkle), predominantly assess capabilities like determining relative positions ("which is higher"), finding paths ("find the shortest path"), or understanding static object configurations. While these are vital aspects of spatial intelligence, they do not deeply evaluate how models handle the *dynamic transformation of objects' shapes in space*.

This paper directly addresses this unexplored frontier. It identifies that no existing benchmark comprehensively evaluates VLMs' capacity for *spatial deformation*, which involves understanding, predicting, and executing complex manipulations that alter an object's intrinsic form and topological relationships. Such abilities are fundamental for real-world tasks like sculpting, engineering design, or even simple acts like folding clothes. By introducing "Inf-Bench," the authors position their work as a pioneering effort to fill this specific void. The paper argues that current benchmarks, often with a fixed number of questions or a static level of difficulty, quickly become obsolete as VLMs rapidly evolve and achieve near-perfect scores. Inf-Bench proposes a novel "Infinite Ladder Competition" format that offers gradually increasing difficulty and an unlimited supply of unique problems, thereby ensuring continuous capability exploration and preventing performance saturation, unlike traditional, static evaluation sets. This innovative approach to benchmarking aims to push the boundaries of VLM spatial reasoning far beyond current limitations, particularly in multi-step, dynamic deformation tasks across varying dimensions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to comprehensively assess the spatial deformation reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The authors aim to determine whether these models genuinely understand and can manipulate spatial objects through transformation, or if their apparent spatial abilities are superficial.

The motivation for this study stems from the observation that while humans naturally excel at tasks involving spatial deformation, such as molding clay or crafting sculptures, the extent to which advanced VLMs possess analogous abilities remains unclear. Current VLMs have demonstrated remarkable proficiency in interpreting visual data and understanding natural language, yet their capacity to predict and plan sequential physical transformations of objects is largely unexplored in existing benchmarks. This gap is significant because spatial deformation is a common and essential aspect of daily life and many practical applications.

Specifically, the key objectives and motivations include:
- **Filling a Benchmarking Gap:** To create the first effective benchmark specifically designed to evaluate spatial *deformation* reasoning, distinguishing it from general spatial relationship or navigation tasks.
- **Assessing True Understanding:** To move beyond simple visual recognition or static spatial queries and investigate whether VLMs can genuinely reason about dynamic changes to object shapes and their underlying topological properties.
- **Exploring Reasoning Directions:** To examine both forward reasoning (predicting the outcome of given operations) and inverse reasoning (inferring the operations needed to achieve a target state), as both are crucial for complete spatial understanding.
- **Developing a Scalable Evaluation:** To overcome the limitations of static benchmarks that quickly become obsolete. The "Infinite Ladder Competition" is motivated by the need for a continuously challenging and adaptable evaluation framework that can push models to their performance limits without saturation.
- **Identifying Performance Bottlenecks:** Through systematic evaluation, to pinpoint the specific weaknesses and underlying mechanisms (e.g., "direct pairing" vs. "step-by-step reasoning") that limit current VLMs in spatial deformation tasks, particularly in higher dimensions (3D).
- **Guiding Future Research:** To provide clear directions for future model development by highlighting critical areas where VLMs fall short, such as managing high-dimensional coupled relationships and complex multi-step transformations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces **Inf-Bench**, a novel evaluation framework designed to rigorously test Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on spatial deformation reasoning. The methodology encompasses benchmark design, automated data generation, a unique evaluation paradigm, and a series of experimental settings.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Design (Inf-Bench)

Inf-Bench categorizes spatial deformation tasks across three distinct levels of dimensionality:
- **2D Tasks:** Inspired by the game *Shapez*, these tasks involve single-plane deformations. Shapes are divided into four quadrants, each containing a predefined pattern or being empty. Operations include cutting (removing quadrants), 90-degree rotations (clockwise/counter-clockwise), filling empty quadrants, mirroring (horizontal flip), and coloring.
- **2.5D Tasks:** An extension of 2D, these tasks introduce a vertical stacking dimension, with shapes comprising up to four layers. Models must perform intra-layer transformations while also reasoning about alignment and composition across layers, adhering to specific physical constraints (e.g., a shape in an upper layer must be supported by a shape in the layer below). Operations expand upon 2D tasks to include stacking.
- **3D Tasks:** Based on the *Rubik's Cube*, these tasks challenge models with manipulation and reasoning in three dimensions. A 3x3x3 cube (27 units, 54 visible faces) can be transformed via 54 basic rotations (face rotations, wide layer rotations, middle layer rotations). This requires complex 3D spatial reasoning, action planning, and hierarchical understanding.

For each spatial level, two types of reasoning tasks are evaluated:
- **Forward Reasoning:** Given an initial state and a sequence of deformation operations, the model must predict the final state of the object. This assesses the model's understanding and execution of spatial operations.
- **Inverse Reasoning:** Given an initial state and a target final state, the model must infer the sequence of operations required to achieve the transformation. This assesses the model's reverse-engineering and problem-solving abilities. All tasks are presented as multiple-choice questions.

#### 3.2. Dataset Construction

To ensure data quality, diversity, and prevent data leakage, an automated **shape generation engine (S)** was developed. This rule-based system, implemented in Python functions, generates an unlimited supply of unique problem pairs. The process is deterministic, guaranteeing consistent results for identical initial shapes and operation lists. The data generation pipeline involves five steps:
1. **Initial Shape Generation:** Randomly generating an initial shape (`s_ini`).
2. **Target Action List Creation:** Randomly selecting `n` actions from the defined action space to form a target action list (`o_tar`).
3. **Distractor Action List Generation:** Creating `k` distractor action lists (`o_dis`) by modifying the target list (e.g., replacing selected operations).
4. **Shape Generation:** Applying `o_tar` to `s_ini` to produce the target shape (`s_tar`), and applying `o_dis` to `s_ini` to generate distractor shapes (`s_dis`).
5. **Question Assembly:** Constructing multiple-choice questions for both forward (initial shape, target actions, options are shapes) and inverse tasks (initial shape, target shape, options are action lists), along with the ground truth.

#### 3.3. Ladder Competition Framework

The evaluation employs an "Infinite Ladder Competition" framework to progressively increase task difficulty and explore model boundaries. Difficulty is defined by `R`, the number of deformation steps.
- Models start at `R = 1` (one-step deformation).
- Each level consists of five questions.
- **Advancement:** If a model correctly answers at least three questions at a given level, it advances to `R + 1`.
- **Downgrade:** If it answers fewer than three questions correctly, it is downgraded to `R - 1`.
- **Termination:** The competition ends if the model fails at the same level (`R`) twice consecutively or reaches `R = 0`.
- The **final value of R** (reasoning depth) serves as the primary performance metric. This dynamic system ensures continuous challenge, preventing models from saturating the benchmark.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Settings and Additional Experiments

- **Model Evaluation:** 18 mainstream VLMs were evaluated, including open-source models (Qwen, Llama, InternVLM), proprietary models (GPT-4.1, Claude 3.7, Gemini 2.5, Doubao 1.5), and reasoning-focused models (OpenAI O3/O4 Mini, Grok 3 Reasoning).
- **Human Baseline:** 100 human volunteers were recruited to provide a comparative performance baseline.
- **Test Protocol:** All evaluations were conducted in a zero-shot setting, with 10 independent runs per model to ensure statistical validity. Greedy decoding (temperature=0) was used for reproducibility.
- **Additional Analyses:**
 - **Internal Reasoning Processes:** A qualitative analysis of how VLMs attempt to solve problems, identifying "direct pairing" and "step-by-step reasoning."
 - **Encoded Input Evaluation:** To isolate perceptual errors from reasoning errors, models were tested on tasks where visual inputs were pre-encoded into pure text representations (e.g., symbolic descriptions of shapes and colors).
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Two open-source models (Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 72B-Instruct) were fine-tuned using LoRA and DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 on datasets of varying difficulty (`S_max` represents the highest step count in the training data) to assess the impact of targeted training.
 - **Reasoning Enhancement Methods:** The effectiveness of common reasoning prompts and frameworks (Chain-of-Thought (COT), Few-shot learning, Self-reflection, Tool Invocation, ReAct) was explored on selected models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The comprehensive evaluation on Inf-Bench reveals significant insights into the current spatial deformation reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models.

#### 4.1. Human vs. Model Performance

- **Overall Dominance by Humans:** As expected, human evaluators consistently and significantly outperform all tested models across all spatial dimensions (2D, 2.5D, and 3D) and reasoning types (forward and inverse). Humans generally demonstrate stronger performance in forward reasoning tasks compared to inverse reasoning.
- **Performance Gap in 3D:** The gap between human and model performance is most pronounced in 3D tasks, where many models struggle to complete even a single deformation step.

#### 4.2. Model Performance Across Dimensions

- **2D and 2.5D Performance:** Models show competitive, though still sub-human, performance in 2D and 2.5D tasks. Proprietary and "reasoning-focused" models generally perform better than open-source models in these simpler dimensions.
- **Severe Limitations in 3D:** A significant limitation is exposed in 3D spatial transformation reasoning. Most open-source and proprietary models effectively fail, unable to perform basic single-step deformations. Only the most powerful reasoning models, such as OpenAI's O3 and O4-mini, and Google's Gemini-2.5-pro-03-25, exhibit some limited 3D deformation abilities, but their performance remains far behind human capabilities.

#### 4.3. VLM Reasoning Strategies and Error Types

- **Two Primary Approaches:** Analysis of model responses identifies two main strategies: "direct pairing" (extracting direct information from prompts and matching options) and "step-by-step reasoning execution" (encoding inputs, abstracting operations into rules, and executing them sequentially). Stronger models tend to utilize step-by-step reasoning more frequently.
- **Error Categories:**
 - **Abstracting Errors (42.43%):** The most common error, occurring when models misinterpret operations or their effects, particularly in 3D cube tasks where coupled shape states increase complexity.
 - **Pairing Errors (30.28%):** Incorrectly matching based on misleading information from the problem statement.
 - **Encoding Errors (16.33%):** Confusion in encoding shapes, especially with multi-layered (2.5D) structures.
 - **Execution Errors (10.96%):** Mistakes in multi-step reasoning where one step is incorrectly executed, more prevalent in non-reasoning models.

#### 4.4. Impact of Encoded Input

- **Significant Improvement in 2D/2.5D:** Providing pre-encoded text input instead of images leads to substantial performance gains in 2D and 2.5D tasks. This suggests that perceptual encoding (visual recognition and interpretation) is a significant bottleneck for VLMs, and that they can abstract operational patterns effectively once this bottleneck is removed. Reasoning models show even greater improvements, with O3 achieving a forward reasoning depth of over 671 steps in 2D.
- **3D Challenges Persist:** Despite improvements in lower dimensions, most models still struggle with 3D tasks even with encoded input. The inherent complexity of coupled shape states and multi-dimensional dependencies in 3D operations remains a fundamental barrier.
- **Human Performance Disparity:** Interestingly, human performance on text-based (encoded) tasks is lower than with visual input, occasionally lagging behind models. This highlights a difference in processing strategies between humans and current VLMs.

#### 4.5. Impact of Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)

- **Enhanced Depth and Generalization (2D/2.5D):** SFT significantly boosts reasoning depth in 2D and 2.5D tasks, enabling models to perform multi-step reasoning beyond the complexity seen in their training examples. This indicates the models' ability to learn a reusable "deformation operator."
- **Limited 3D Forward Task Improvement:** In 3D tasks, even with SFT using high-difficulty data, the forward reasoning performance remains limited, rarely exceeding five steps. This reveals a fundamental limitation of traditional training paradigms in capturing complex 3D deformation sequences and managing error accumulation over many steps.
- **Inverse Task Robustness:** The inverse task benefits more from SFT, often achieving deeper inference than the forward task. This is attributed to the ability to iteratively verify against the target state, which helps reduce error accumulation.
- **Forward-to-Inverse Generalization:** Models trained on forward tasks show better generalization to inverse tasks than vice versa, suggesting that learning the step-wise application of operations (forward reasoning) provides a more robust foundation.

#### 4.6. Impact of Reasoning Enhancement Methods

- **Marginal Overall Impact:** Mainstream reasoning enhancement methods (Chain-of-Thought, Few-shot learning, Self-reflection, Tool Invocation, ReAct) yield only slight improvements in specific tasks and dimensions.
- **Limited Effect in 3D and Inverse Tasks:** Their positive effects are minimal or even detrimental in 3D tasks and inverse reasoning tasks. This suggests that these methods, primarily designed to guide language model reasoning, do not fundamentally address the core challenges of spatial deformation understanding in VLMs.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The paper "Ascending the Infinite Ladder: Benchmarking Spatial Deformation Reasoning in Vision-Language Models" makes several significant contributions to the field of artificial intelligence and computer vision, with far-reaching potential impacts:

#### 5.1. Pioneering a New Benchmark Area

- **First Comprehensive Benchmark for Spatial Deformation:** The most significant contribution is the introduction of Inf-Bench, the first benchmark explicitly designed to evaluate VLMs' ability to reason about *spatial deformation*. This fills a critical void in the existing landscape of spatial reasoning benchmarks, which largely overlooks the dynamic transformation of object shapes.
- **Focus on Core Capabilities:** By defining and testing spatial recognition, abstraction of operational law principles, and stable reasoning execution, Inf-Bench provides a structured framework to probe the fundamental abilities required for true spatial imagination.

#### 5.2. Introducing an Infinitely Scalable Evaluation Paradigm

- **The "Infinite Ladder Competition":** The proposed ladder competition system is a novel and powerful paradigm for VLM evaluation. Unlike static benchmarks that quickly become obsolete as models improve, this dynamic system offers infinitely scalable difficulty by adjusting the number of deformation steps. This ensures continuous, meaningful assessment of model capabilities and prevents performance saturation, effectively "future-proofing" the benchmark.
- **Automated Data Generation:** The development of a rule-based, deterministic data engine that can generate unlimited, diverse problem pairs is crucial for the scalability of the ladder competition and ensures no data leakage, enhancing the reliability and reproducibility of evaluations.

#### 5.3. Identifying Critical VLM Limitations

- **Highlighting the Human-AI Gap:** The stark contrast between human performance and even the most advanced VLMs, particularly in 3D spatial deformation, underscores a fundamental limitation in current AI. It reveals that despite progress in general visual-language tasks, models still lack genuine spatial imagination and multi-step abstract reasoning in complex environments.
- **Pinpointing Bottlenecks:** The detailed analysis of reasoning strategies and error types (especially "Abstracting Errors" in 3D due to coupled states and error accumulation) provides invaluable diagnostic insights. It clarifies that simply fine-tuning or applying generic reasoning prompts is insufficient to overcome these inherent architectural or representational challenges.
- **The Perceptual vs. Reasoning Divide:** The performance boost observed with encoded input highlights that much of VLMs' struggle in visual spatial reasoning might stem from perceptual encoding and abstraction, rather than solely from the reasoning process itself.

#### 5.4. Guiding Future Research and Development

- **New Architectural Directions:** The findings strongly suggest that current VLM architectures are inadequate for complex 3D spatial deformation. This implicitly calls for research into novel designs, possibly incorporating physics-inspired attention mechanisms, symbolic reasoning tools, or external memory to better handle global consistency, long-sequence dependencies, and error correction.
- **Focus on Generalization:** The observation that 3D forward tasks remain limited even after SFT, along with the differential generalization from forward to inverse tasks, indicates the need for training methodologies that explicitly foster reusable "deformation operators" and robust generalization across varying task complexities and dimensions.
- **Rethinking Enhancement Strategies:** The minimal impact of traditional reasoning enhancement methods implies that more integrated, domain-specific approaches (e.g., neural-symbolic methods, embodied learning paradigms where models interact with and learn from physical environments) might be necessary for breakthroughs in spatial imagination.

In conclusion, this research not only provides a robust and innovative framework for evaluating a crucial yet overlooked aspect of VLM intelligence but also offers a sobering assessment of current models' limitations. By clearly delineating what VLMs *cannot* yet do in spatial deformation, the paper sets a critical research agenda for developing the next generation of more capable and truly spatially intelligent AI systems.
