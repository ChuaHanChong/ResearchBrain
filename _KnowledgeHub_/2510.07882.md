# Towards Proprioception-Aware Embodied Planning for Dual-Arm Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> The research introduces DualTHOR, a physically realistic simulation platform for dual-arm humanoid robots, and Proprio-MLLM, a multimodal large language model that incorporates proprioceptive information. Proprio-MLLM achieved an average 19.75% improvement in planning performance over baseline MLLMs for long-horizon dual-arm tasks within the new simulator.

## Problem

- A scarcity of high-level planning simulators specifically designed for dual-arm humanoid robots, leading to a lack of physically realistic training data for complex, long-horizon tasks.
- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit insufficient embodiment awareness, struggling with dual-arm selection logic, body state adjustments, and identifying physically plausible interaction points.
- Current MLLMs struggle with contingency planning and re-planning in uncertain real-world scenarios due to a lack of rich, contingency-aware training data.

## Method

- ==Developed DualTHOR==, a Unity-based simulation platform for dual-arm humanoid robots (Unitree H1, Agibot X1) featuring physically realistic bimanual manipulation, continuous states, and a stochastic contingency mechanism.
- Proposed Proprio-MLLM, an extension of Qwen 2.5-VL-7B-Ins, which integrates proprioceptive information through a novel motion-based position embedding (MPE) and a cross-spatial encoder (CSE).
- Created a robot motion-image-text alignment dataset by retargeting human motion data to robots and replaying these motions in DualTHOR, used for training Proprio-MLLM across motion, image, and text modalities.

## Results

- Proprio-MLLM achieved the highest overall success rates across all task categories and robot types in DualTHOR, showing an average **19.75%** improvement in planning performance compared to state-of-the-art MLLM baselines like DAG-Plan.
- The model demonstrated a reduction in navigation, body adjustment, and logical failure types due to more effective integration of image perception and proprioceptive states, leading to more coherent dual-arm plans.
- Proprio-MLLM, particularly when augmented with reflection prompts, exhibited improved re-planning abilities in tasks with stochastic outcomes (medium and hard difficulty), outperforming other methods in these scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly integrating proprioceptive information into MLLMs significantly improves their ability to reason about dual-arm selection logic and adapt body states for coherent, physically grounded plans.
> - High-fidelity simulation environments like DualTHOR, supporting continuous states and contingencies, are crucial for generating diverse, physically realistic data needed to train robust embodied AI for dual-arm humanoid robots.
> - Specific architectural components, such as motion-based position embedding and cross-spatial encoders, are vital for enhancing an MLLM's spatial reasoning and understanding of a robot's interaction range and body state.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work situates itself within the rapidly evolving field of embodied AI, particularly at the intersection of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) and robotics. Recent advancements have highlighted MLLMs as promising high-level planners for robots, enabling them to interpret and execute complex human instructions. A significant research direction involves integrating MLLMs with highly versatile dual-arm humanoid robots to tackle fine-grained, long-horizon, and human-like collaborative tasks.

However, the authors identify two fundamental bottlenecks that limit progress in this area. Firstly, there is a scarcity of high-level planning simulators specifically designed for dual-arm humanoid robots. Existing benchmarks for long-horizon planning are often restricted to wheeled robots or single-arm manipulators and frequently rely on idealized, instantaneous state transitions, which detaches training data from physical reality and limits real-world applicability. While some simulators support bimanual humanoid robots, they typically focus on low-level control, providing only fragmented data without coherent task sequences for complex activities. This creates a data gap for training and evaluating MLLMs for high-level, physically realistic humanoid interaction planning.

Secondly, current MLLMs exhibit insufficient embodiment awareness. They struggle to reason about dual-arm selection logic, body state adjustments (e.g., height changes), and the identification of physically plausible interaction points with objects. This lack of grounding in the robot's physical reality leads to suboptimal planning, frequent re-planning requirements, and reduced task success rates. The paper aims to address these identified limitations in both simulation platforms and MLLM capabilities to advance embodied intelligence for dual-arm humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to overcome the aforementioned challenges in developing MLLM-based high-level planners for dual-arm humanoid robots. These objectives are driven by two core motivations:

1. **Addressing the Simulation and Data Scarcity:** The first motivation stems from the absence of simulation platforms that systematically support task evaluation and data collection for complex, long-horizon tasks involving dual-arm humanoid robots. Current simulators either lack the appropriate robot morphology (e.g., single-arm or wheeled robots) or compromise on physical realism by using idealized state transitions. This leads to a dearth of high-quality, visually and physically realistic humanoid interaction planning data necessary for training and evaluating MLLMs. The objective is to create a novel simulation platform capable of generating such data, facilitating the development of MLLM planning policies that are more applicable to real-world scenarios and exhibit better sim-to-real transferability.

2. **Enhancing Embodiment Awareness in MLLMs:** The second motivation arises from the inherent limitations of existing MLLMs in understanding and reasoning about the physical embodiment of dual-arm humanoid robots. These models often fail to consider crucial factors such as arm selection logic, necessary body state adjustments (e.g., changing body height or position), and the feasible interaction range for objects based on joint configurations. This lack of "proprioception-awareness" results in plans that are physically implausible or inefficient, requiring frequent re-planning and hindering task success. The objective is to develop an MLLM that explicitly incorporates proprioceptive information and enhances spatial reasoning, enabling it to generate more coherent, physically grounded, and effective plans for dual-arm tasks.

In summary, the research aims to provide both an essential simulation platform (DualTHOR) and an effective model (Proprio-MLLM) to foster the advancement of embodied intelligence, specifically for dual-arm humanoid robots performing complex, long-horizon tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a two-pronged approach: the development of a novel simulation platform, DualTHOR, and a new MLLM, Proprio-MLLM, designed to enhance embodiment awareness.

#### 3.1. DualTHOR Simulation Platform

DualTHOR is introduced as a new dual-arm humanoid simulator built upon the Unity engine, extending the AI2-THOR series. It emphasizes physically realistic bimanual manipulation, continuous robot states, and environmental transitions, along with a contingency mechanism.

- **Physics Engine:** Built on Unity, DualTHOR supports physically realistic bimanual manipulation, parallel execution of coordinated arm actions, and smooth, continuous transitions via interpolation. It includes a multi-camera suite for comprehensive situational awareness.
- **Humanoid Robots:** The platform integrates two distinct humanoid robots: Unitree H1 (powerful, stable, dexterous hands) and Agibot X1 (dexterous, precise, parallel grippers). This diversity allows for research into different manipulation paradigms.
- **Task Categories:** Tasks are categorized based on the number of arms required:
 - **Dual-Arm Essential Tasks:** Physically impossible with a single arm (e.g., lifting heavy objects with two hands, holding an object while opening a container).
 - **Dual-Arm Optional Tasks:** Can be done with one arm but are enhanced by two (e.g., transporting two objects simultaneously).
 - **Single-Arm Tasks:** Basic interaction tasks adapted from AI2-THOR for foundational planning.
- **Low-Level Control:** A client-server model uses a Python API to send high-level commands to the Unity engine. For interaction actions, a modular Inverse Kinematics (IK) service (OmniManip) computes joint configurations. Trajectories are smoothed for realistic motion. Distinct IK models are tailored for each robot: Agibot X1 uses a decoupled approach for each arm, while Unitree H1 employs a whole-body coordination model that solves for both arms simultaneously, considering full posture information and maintaining balance.
- **Contingency Mechanism:** A stochastic mechanism simulates real-world uncertainty by mapping an action to a distribution of potential outcomes (e.g., picking up a cup has an 80% success rate, 10% chance of breaking, 10% chance of spilling contents). This mechanism is designed to necessitate error recovery and re-planning capabilities in MLLMs.

#### 3.2. Proprio-MLLM Model

Proprio-MLLM is developed by extending Qwen 2.5-VL-7B-Ins to incorporate proprioceptive information and enhance spatial reasoning for dual-arm planning.

- **Alignment Dataset Preparation:** To enable the MLLM to understand proprioceptive states, a robot motion-image-text dataset is built. This involves retargeting human motion data from HumanML3D (in SMPL format) to the H1 and X1 robots and replaying these motions in DualTHOR to obtain corresponding first-person visual observations. Joint positions represent robot motions, and a PHC-based filtering method is applied to obtain 11,000 high-quality trajectories.
- **Motion Alignment Training:** The training proceeds in three stages:
 1. **Motion Tokenizer:** A motion VQ-VAE model (encoder, decoder, codebook) is trained to discretize robot motion sequences into latent embeddings, aligning with the MLLM's token format. The VQ-VAE uses a reconstruction loss, a codebook loss, and a commitment loss, with EMA for codebook updates.
 2. **Alignment Training:** All modalities (motion, image, text) are combined into a unified token space using shared embedding layers and attention mechanisms. The MLLM is fine-tuned with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA). Initially, image and text weights are frozen to improve single-modality motion alignment, followed by joint training across all modalities.
 3. **Instruction Tuning:** A self-learning method, SELU, is used to collect dual-arm planning data, grounding the MLLM in specific tasks with multimodal understanding.
- **Motion-Based Position Embedding (MPE):** This component integrates embodiment information by replacing standard temporal, height, and width position encodings for visual tokens with robot-centric height and width. Spatial indices are encoded radially outward from the robot's centroid, enabling the model to distinguish token directions relative to the robot and improve long-horizon reasoning based on body state.
- **Cross-Spatial Encoder (CSE):** To address the challenge of estimating the robot's interactive range, a cross-spatial encoder is introduced. It retains the Qwen2.5-VL image encoder for 2D visual features and augments it with 3D point-map features predicted by CUT3R from a single RGB image. The 3D features are reshaped, aligned with 2D features, and fused via a lightweight MLP, providing enhanced awareness of the robot's interactive range for more accurate dual-arm interaction planning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on 359 tasks across 10 distinct room environments, involving 68 unique objects, with tasks categorized into Dual-Arm Essential, Dual-Arm Optional, and Single-Arm. Each task was evaluated on both X1 and H1 robots over 50 trials.

#### 4.1. Performance on Dual-Arm Tasks

- **Overall Performance:** Existing MLLMs, including proprietary (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro), open-source (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Ins, InternVL2.5-8B), and prompt-enhanced (LLM-Planner, RAP, DAG-Plan), demonstrated limited performance in long-horizon dual-arm planning tasks. Proprio-MLLM achieved the highest overall success rates across all task categories and robot types (Table II).
- **Improvement over Baselines:** Proprio-MLLM showed an average improvement of 19.75% in planning performance compared to DAG-Plan, which is a state-of-the-art method utilizing graph-based structures for dual-arm skills. The improvement was particularly notable in dual-arm essential tasks for the X1 robot, reaching up to 23.30%.
- **Task Type Discrepancy:** A substantial performance gap was observed between dual-arm and single-arm planning in current MLLMs, indicating the scarcity of dual-arm humanoid planning data in existing simulators. Proprio-MLLM's improvements were consistently observed across all tasks, with the largest gains in dual-arm scenarios.

#### 4.2. Impact of Proprioceptive Information

- **Failure Analysis:** An analysis of failed trajectories categorized failures into navigation failure (unable to reach target), body adjustment failure (mismatched height/posture), and logical failure (suboptimal arm selection). (Figure 6)
- **Reduction in Failures:** Both DAG-Plan and Proprio-MLLM showed reductions in failures compared to Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Ins, indicating that proprioceptive information improves arm selection logic. However, DAG-Plan's graph-based prompting did not fully exploit proprioception for body adjustment and navigation failures. Proprio-MLLM, through its motion-based position embedding and cross-spatial encoder, demonstrated more effective integration of image perception and proprioceptive states, leading to a greater reduction across all failure types and more coherent, adaptive dual-arm plans.

#### 4.3. Contingency Handling and Re-planning

- **Re-planning Ability:** DualTHOR's continuous interaction and contingency mechanism allowed for the evaluation of MLLMs' re-planning capabilities across tasks of varying difficulty (Easy: 100% action success, Medium: 50%, Hard: 20%). (Table III)
- **Proprio-MLLM Performance:** Proprio-MLLM leveraged proprioceptive information more effectively than DAG-Plan, achieving higher success rates, particularly in medium and hard difficulty tasks. The inclusion of a reflection prompt further substantially improved re-planning ability for both methods, indicating its importance for error recovery.
- **Limitations:** Despite the improvements, both methods exhibited limited performance on high-difficulty tasks, suggesting a scarcity of contingency-rich training data in current MLLMs. This observation underscores DualTHOR's role in providing a platform for developing robust planning datasets that include such failure modes.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Component Impact:** An ablation study investigated the contribution of the motion-based position embedding (MPE) and cross-spatial encoder (CSE) to Proprio-MLLM's performance (Table IV).
- **Proprioceptive Integration:** Removing both MPE and CSE (Proprio w/o ALL) resulted in a significant drop in success rate and an increase in all failure types compared to the full Proprio-MLLM, highlighting the overall benefit of proprioceptive information integration.
- **CSE's Role:** Removing the cross-spatial encoder (Proprio w/o CSE) primarily increased navigation failures, indicating its role in improving the robot's interaction range and spatial understanding.
- **MPE's Role:** Removing the motion-based position embedding (Proprio w/o MPE) led to increased body adjustment and logical failures, demonstrating its importance in accurately adjusting the robot's body state and minimizing arm selection errors. The combined effect of MPE and CSE enabled Proprio-MLLM to achieve proprioception-aware, long-horizon planning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of embodied AI for humanoid robots, particularly in the domain of dual-arm manipulation.

Firstly, the introduction of **DualTHOR** addresses a critical gap in simulation platforms. By offering a high-fidelity environment with dual-arm humanoid robots, continuous state transitions, and a stochastic contingency mechanism, DualTHOR provides an essential benchmark for both data collection and rigorous evaluation of MLLM planning capabilities. Its physically realistic interactions and diverse task categories are designed to foster the development of MLLM policies that are more robust and directly applicable to real-world humanoid robotic systems, improving sim-to-real transferability.

Secondly, the proposed **Proprio-MLLM** model contributes significantly to enhancing the embodiment awareness of MLLMs. By effectively incorporating proprioceptive information through motion-based position embedding and a cross-spatial encoder, the model demonstrates improved reasoning about dual-arm selection logic, body state adjustments, and feasible interaction ranges. The experimental results, showing an average improvement of 19.75% in planning performance over state-of-the-art baselines, suggest that explicitly grounding MLLMs in the robot's physical reality leads to more reliable logical and spatial reasoning. This capability is crucial for humanoid robots operating in complex, unstructured environments.

The findings regarding MLLMs' struggles with contingency planning also highlight the importance of DualTHOR's design. The platform enables the study of error recovery and re-planning, which are fundamental for safe and effective real-world robot deployment. The insights gained from Proprio-MLLM's ability to leverage reflection prompts for re-planning, while still acknowledging limitations in high-difficulty scenarios, can guide future research towards developing more resilient planning agents.

In sum, this work provides both foundational infrastructure and a methodological advancement. DualTHOR facilitates the generation of richer, more realistic datasets for dual-arm humanoid tasks, while Proprio-MLLM offers a blueprint for building MLLMs that are more deeply aware of their robotic embodiment. These contributions collectively advance the field towards developing more intelligent, adaptable, and robust dual-arm humanoid robots capable of executing complex, long-horizon tasks in dynamic environments.
