# Consistency Policy: Accelerated Visuomotor Policies via Consistency Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> Consistency Policy accelerates visuomotor control by distilling state-of-the-art diffusion models into faster student networks, achieving an order of magnitude faster inference speeds. This approach maintains competitive success rates across various simulation and real-world robot manipulation tasks, enabling deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art diffusion-based visuomotor policies (e.g., Diffusion Policy) suffer from slow inference speeds (often ~1 second per action) due to requiring many sequential denoising steps.
- This high inference latency and computational demand make these policies impractical for real-time control in dynamic robotic tasks or for deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms (e.g., mobile manipulators, quadrotors).
- Existing acceleration methods for diffusion models either degrade sample quality, increase memory requirements, or are not directly tailored for high-dimensional visuomotor control.

## Method

- A two-stage training process is employed: first, a multi-step EDM (Elucidating the ==Design Space== of Diffusion-Based Generative Models) diffusion model is trained as a teacher policy.
- Second, a faster student model (**Consistency Policy**) is distilled from the pre-trained teacher using a combined objective of Denoising Score Matching (DSM) loss and Consistency Trajectory Model (CTM) loss.
- The Consistency Policy enables single-step or few-step inference (e.g., 3-step chaining) by learning to perform large denoising steps along the Probability Flow ODE trajectory, directly predicting actions from noisy inputs.

## Results

- Consistency Policy achieves inference speeds of 1-3 Network Function Evaluations (NFE), compared to 9-27+ NFE for Diffusion Policy variants, translating to wall-clock times of 1ms (simulation) and 20-22ms (real-world on laptop GPU).
- It demonstrates an order-of-magnitude speedup (up to **9x**-**100x** faster than Diffusion Policy) while maintaining competitive success rates, often matching or surpassing diffusion baselines on tasks like Robomimic and real-world manipulation.
- The distilled policy exhibits robustness to the quality of the pre-trained teacher model, simplifying the practical application of the distillation process.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "CTM-local" consistency objective provides an effective balance between performance and training efficiency for visuomotor policy distillation.
> - Reducing the variance of the initial noise sample used for inference significantly improves the distilled policy's performance by keeping the trajectory within a more stable distribution.
> - Dropout within the Consistency Trajectory Model (CTM) objective is crucial for providing sufficient training signal and preventing the network from trivializing the consistency task.
