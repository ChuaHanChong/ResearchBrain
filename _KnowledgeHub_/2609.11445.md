# FARM: Reading Failure Signals from the Internal Predictive States of a Frozen Robotic World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> FARM tests whether a frozen robotic world model's internal predictive states already encode failure information. It trains only a 33,985-parameter attention-pooling readout over frozen VLA-JEPA predictor states, emitting step-wise failure scores aggregated causally into running trajectory risk via a running maximum. It reaches 85.68/88.59 pooled AUROC/AUPRC, beats 15 matched baselines on Seen tasks, and adds 0.2256 ms detector latency.

## Problem

- Human-in-the-loop monitors like HG-DAgger, ThriftyDAgger and Sirius need sustained operator supervision and timely takeover decisions, which does not scale across many parallel rollouts.
- Automatic monitors score uncertainty, novelty, OOD-ness or action consistency, but these proxies diverge from failure: confident consistent policies can stall, novel states need not fail.
- Prior world-model monitors train a monitoring-specific dynamics component or classifier, so results measure newly learned capacity rather than what the pretrained predictive state already encodes.

## Method

- ==Frozen WM-state extraction==: takes the 12th VLA-JEPA predictor block output before normalization and projection, strips action-conditioning tokens, keeping a 768x1024 state with gradients stopped.
- The readout applies a shared 32-dim token projection, LayerNorm, GELU, and content-dependent ==attention pooling== to one 32-dim failure vector, then a sigmoid head, totaling 33,985 parameters.
- ==Task-balanced BCE== with hierarchical task/trajectory/step sampling trains the readout; ==running-max aggregation== over observed scores yields causal trajectory risk, supporting fixed-readout zero-shot reuse and readout-only adaptation.

## Results

- Five-fold out-of-fold over seven source tasks gives **85.68**/**88.59** pooled AUROC/AUPRC, above a 7-statistic linear control at **77.19**/**78.81** and predictor-input extraction at **83.10**/**85.89**.
- On the 10-task benchmark FARM leads all four Seen metrics among 15 baselines, beating SAFE-MLP by **5.42**/**4.88** Macro points; STAC-Single wins strict-Unseen zero-shot.
- Real-robot readout-only adaptation improves every setting: **98.48**/**98.41** on PIPER X with the external policy, **75.78**/**74.31** on the 778-rollout Franka population, versus **55.89** zero-shot.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predictive world-model states trained only for prediction already organize execution outcomes linearly enough for a tiny probe, so monitoring can reuse rather than retrain backbones.
> - Transfer depends on source-readout training population, not representation alone: identical frozen features gave 41.35 versus 84.70 AUROC under two source lineages.
> - Broad task coverage matters more than detector capacity; source tasks scaling one to seven lifted Macro AUROC 62.55 to 85.43 with parameters unchanged.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a challenge in autonomous robotics: the need for reliable online failure monitoring to enable sustained robot deployment. While Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and generative visuomotor policies have expanded robot capabilities, they often lack an inherent mechanism to detect deviations, incorrect physical outcomes, or stalled progress during execution.

Current approaches to robot execution monitoring can be broadly categorized:

- **Human-in-the-loop learning:** Methods like HG-DAgger, ThriftyDAgger, EnsembleDAgger, and Sirius rely on human intervention or confidence gating to identify problematic states and collect corrective data. While effective for reliability and continual learning, they require constant human supervision and timely intervention decisions, which can be resource-intensive for large-scale or long-term operations.
- **Automatic monitors based on proxy signals:** Several systems derive alarms from signals such as uncertainty, novelty, out-of-distribution (OOD) behavior, action inconsistency, or explicit progress estimates. Examples include Multi-Modal Anomaly Detection, FAIL-Detect, FIPER, and Sentinel. The paper notes that these signals, while informative, do not always directly correlate with task failure; for instance, a policy might remain confident while failing to make progress, or a novel state might not indicate an error. SuccessVQA uses vision-language semantics for task completion.
- **Representation-based monitoring:** SAFE demonstrated that high-level VLA policy features could be used by lightweight supervised readouts to discern success or failure across tasks. This approach uses existing model representations rather than creating new ones.
- **Predictive world models for monitoring:** World models, which learn action-conditioned state evolution in compact latent spaces, have been employed for execution monitoring. However, this typically involves learning a monitoring-specific dynamics component or inferring risk from prediction error, uncertainty, or predicted future states (e.g., Model-Based Runtime Monitoring, Foundational World Models, Foresight, FoMo-FD, ContactGuard).

This paper distinguishes itself by focusing on a specific representation-reuse question: whether *unaltered internal predictive states* of a *frozen pretrained robotic world model* already contain directly decodable failure information. This contrasts with methods that train new dynamics models or adapt the world model's backbone for monitoring. The approach aligns with the concept of probing and frozen-feature transfer, where linear probes assess information accessibility in intermediate representations without updating the backbone, similar to how R3M and masked visual pretraining reuse large pretrained visual representations for downstream robotic tasks. By investigating the VLA-JEPA world model, which combines a V-JEPA2 world-state encoder with a temporally causal latent-action-conditioned predictor, the research explores whether a model primarily designed for prediction and control can also serve as a reusable, failure-monitoring interface without task-specific adaptation of its core predictive components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate whether the internal predictive states of a pre-trained and frozen robotic world model inherently contain sufficient information to directly decode task failures. The paper asks, "do the unaltered internal predictive states of a frozen pretrained robotic world model already contain failure-relevant information that a lightweight model can directly decode?"

This core question is motivated by several considerations:

- **Need for Automatic, Low-Overhead Monitoring:** As robot operating times and parallel rollouts increase, human-in-the-loop intervention becomes less scalable. An automatic monitoring system that can reliably signal execution issues is crucial for continuous data collection, online policy improvement, and long-term autonomous operation.
- **Limitations of Proxy Signals:** Existing automatic monitors often rely on proxy signals like uncertainty or novelty, which do not always directly correspond to actual task failure. A policy can be confident yet fail to make progress, or encounter novel states without failing. A more direct, semantic understanding of success or failure is desired.
- **Leveraging Existing Model Capabilities:** Robotic world models (such as VLA-JEPA) are designed to integrate observation history, action effects, task context, and state evolution for prediction, planning, and control. The motivation is to determine if this rich internal representation, built for fundamental robotic intelligence, implicitly encodes task failure signals, thereby avoiding the need to train entirely new, dedicated monitoring components or significantly modify the world model's backbone.
- **Enabling Representation Reuse:** If failure information is already present and decodable, a single, general-purpose world model could provide a reusable monitoring interface. This promotes efficiency by reducing computational overhead and the need for extensive failure-specific training data for separate monitoring modules. It aligns with the broader trend of reusing powerful pre-trained representations for various downstream tasks.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:

1. **Formulate Robot Failure Monitoring as a Frozen WM-State Readout Problem:** This involves defining a methodology where a lightweight, supervised readout module is trained exclusively on the internal predictive states of a fixed, pre-trained world model. This formulation aims to isolate the question of whether the failure information is *already accessible* within the world model's representation.
2. **Introduce FARM (Failure-Aware Readout from World Models):** Develop a specific lightweight readout architecture (33,985 parameters) that processes predictive tokens from the frozen world model to produce step-wise failure scores and causal trajectory risk. The design emphasizes minimal parameter count to ensure that observed performance gains are attributed to the underlying world model's representation rather than the readout's capacity to learn new dynamics.
3. **Extensively Evaluate FARM:** Conduct a multi-faceted evaluation across various settings:
 - **Simulation Benchmarks:** Compare FARM against numerous matched baselines on a 10-task simulation benchmark to assess its competitive performance in multi-task failure detection.
 - **Real-Robot Transfer and Adaptation:** Evaluate FARM's ability to transfer to new policies, different robot platforms, and varying visual domains in zero-shot settings, and assess the effectiveness of readout-only adaptation in addressing domain shifts.
 - **Causal Partial-History and Latency Analysis:** Analyze if failure discrimination emerges from partial causal histories and measure the computational overhead introduced by the detector, ensuring its suitability for online deployment.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Failure-Aware Readout from World Models (FARM), operates by training a lightweight readout module on the frozen internal predictive states of a pre-trained robotic world model, specifically VLA-JEPA. The core principle is to evaluate whether failure-relevant information is inherently present in these states without modifying the world model's backbone.

**Problem Formulation:**
The task is defined over a set of robot trajectories `D = {τ_i}` with task `k_i` and outcome `y_i ∈ {0, 1}` (1 for Failure, 0 for Success). At each replanning step `t`, FARM computes a world model (WM) state `H_WM_i,t` from available information `x_i,≤t` (observation history, task condition, action/state conditioning). A readout `G_θ` then processes `H_WM_i,t` to produce a step-wise failure score `s_i,t ∈`. Crucially, the world model's parameters `ϕ` are frozen (indicated by `sg[.]` for stop gradients), making `θ` the only optimizable parameter set for `G_θ`.

**Frozen WM-State Extraction:**
FARM utilizes a VLA-JEPA architecture for its world model, which consists of a V-JEPA2 encoder and a temporally causal VLA-JEPA predictor.
1. **V-JEPA2 Encoder:** Maps the observation history up to step `t` into world-state tokens.
2. **VLA-JEPA Predictor:** These world-state tokens, along with task-conditioned latent-action tokens, serve as input to the predictor.
3. **Extraction Point:** FARM extracts the output of the final (12th) predictor block *before* any normalization or output projection.
4. **Token Filtering:** Action-conditioning tokens are removed.
The resulting predictive state, `H_WM_i,t`, is a tensor of shape `R^(768x1024)`, where 768 is the world-state-token count and 1024 is the predictor's internal hidden width. Each row represents a world-state token after causal, action-conditioned predictive computation. This extraction process remains fixed across all evaluations.

**Lightweight Failure Readout (G_θ):**
To prevent a high-capacity detector from obscuring the representation-reuse hypothesis, FARM employs a small, 33,985-parameter readout with a 32-dimensional bottleneck.
1. **Token Projection:** Each world-state token `h_WM_j` (a row in `H_WM`) is projected into a lower-dimensional space using a shared weight matrix `W_p ∈ R^(32x1024)` and bias `b_p`. Layer Normalization (LN) and GELU activation are applied.
 `u_j = GELU(LN(W_p h_WM_j + b_p))`
2. **Attention Pooling:** A content-dependent attention mechanism pools these projected tokens. An attention vector `a ∈ R^32` is used to compute attention weights `α_j` for each token, which are then used to form a weighted sum `r`.
 `α_j = softmax_j(a^⊤ u_j)`, `r = Σ_j α_j u_j`
 The attention mechanism operates only within the current replanning step.
3. **Failure-Aware Representation:** The pooled representation `r` is then passed through a hidden layer (`W_f, b_f`) with GELU activation to produce a 32-dimensional failure-aware representation `z_F`.
 `z_F = GELU(W_f r + b_f)`
4. **Step-wise Failure Score:** A final output head (`w_o, b_o`) with a sigmoid activation function produces the step-wise failure score `s ∈`.
 `s = σ(w_o^⊤ z_F + b_o)`

**Supervised Readout Learning:**
Training is performed using trajectory-level outcomes, where every valid WM state `H_WM_i,t` from a trajectory `i` inherits the trajectory's outcome `y_i`.
1. **Loss Function:** A task-balanced binary cross-entropy (BCE) loss `L_FARM(θ)` is minimized.
 `L_FARM(θ) = E_{k,i,t}[ℓ_BCE(s_i,t, y_i)]`
2. **Hierarchical Sampling:** To ensure each task is weighted equally and prevent long rollouts from dominating, sampling is hierarchical: first a task `k`, then a trajectory `i` from `D_k`, and finally a replanning step `t` from `T_i`.
3. **Optimization:** Only the readout parameters `θ` are updated using AdamW optimizer.

**Causal Execution Monitoring:**
1. **Step-wise Score:** The readout produces an instantaneous step-wise score `s_i,t`.
2. **Causal Trajectory Risk:** This local signal is converted into a running trajectory risk `q_i,t` by taking the maximum score observed up to the current step `t`: `q_i,t = max_{τ≤t} s_i,τ`. This is implemented recursively as `q_i,t = max(q_i,t-1, s_i,t)`.
3. **Causality:** Both `H_WM_i,t` and `q_i,t` are designed to depend only on information available through the current step `t`, allowing for online, real-time risk assessment without future information.

**Fixed-Readout Reuse and Readout-Only Adaptation:**
The framework supports two deployment modes for target domains:
1. **Fixed-Readout Zero-Shot Monitoring:** The readout `G_θ` trained on source data (`θ*S`) is directly applied to target WM states without any target-specific training or model selection. This assesses transferability of the decoding rule.
2. **Readout-Only Adaptation:** If labeled target trajectories are available, `θ` is initialized from `θ*S` and fine-tuned on the target data using the same FARM objective. The world model backbone (`ϕ`) and WM-state interface remain frozen. This evaluates how effectively lightweight adaptation can address domain shifts.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Benchmark:** A 10-task simulation benchmark derived from LIBERO manipulation tasks, split into 7 source tasks (S1-S7) and 3 strict-Unseen tasks (U1-U3). Trajectory-grouped five-fold out-of-fold (OOF) evaluation is used for representation and causal analyses, while a fixed split (30 train/20 eval per source task) is used for detector comparison.
- **Metrics:** AUROC/AUPRC (0-100 scale), reported as Macro (averaged per-task) and Pooled (all trajectories combined).
- **Baselines:** A comprehensive set of 15 baselines, including supervised feature readouts (SAFE-MLP, SAFE-LSTM), feature-distance and OOD methods (Mahalanobis Distance, k-NN, PCA-KMeans, RND, LogpZO), and action uncertainty/consistency methods (Action Total/Translation/Rotation/Gripper Variance, Cluster Entropy, STAC).
- **Real-Robot Evaluation:** Conducted on four distinct robot platforms/execution policies: PIPER X (with `π*0.6` and VLA-JEPA policies), SO-101 (with Eval-RL policy), and Franka (with `π0`-FAST-DROID policy).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of FARM is structured around four primary questions, covering representation, detection capability, transferability, and online utility.

**A. Failure Information in Frozen Predictive States**
The initial analyses focused on whether the frozen World Model (WM) states inherently contain failure-relevant information.
- **Held-out Discrimination:** Five-fold out-of-fold (OOF) evaluation across seven source tasks showed that the frozen WM state supports consistent failure discrimination. FARM achieved a Pooled AUROC/AUPRC of 85.68/88.59 (Table IIa). This indicates that the failure information is accessible directly within the frozen WM state, as only the readout receives failure labels.
- **Representation Controls:** Compared to predefined low-order statistical controls (e.g., global mean, standard deviation, temporal delta) and other predictor extraction positions (e.g., predictor input, Block 6), the final WM-state readout demonstrated stronger aggregate discrimination (Table IIb). This suggests that the information goes beyond simple hidden-state statistics.
- **Training-Task Coverage:** Increasing the number of source training tasks from one to seven consistently improved both Macro and Pooled AUROC/AUPRC on the OOF evaluation (Figure 2). This indicates that broader source supervision enhances held-out multi-task detection without requiring a larger readout model.

**B. Competitive Multi-Task Failure Detection**
FARM's performance was compared against 15 baselines on a 10-task simulation benchmark.
- **Seen Tasks:** FARM exhibited the highest performance across all four Seen metrics (Macro and Pooled AUROC/AUPRC), surpassing the next best baseline (SAFE-MLP) by 5.42 and 4.88 Macro AUROC/AUPRC points, respectively (Table III). This was achieved using a lightweight readout over frozen WM states, without backbone adaptation.
- **Strict-Unseen Tasks:** In zero-shot evaluation on the three strict-Unseen tasks, STAC-Single, an action-consistency baseline, ranked first. However, FARM remained competitive, outperforming SAFE-MLP and SAFE-LSTM (Table III).
- **Readout-Only Adaptation:** Under matched Adapt-35 supervision (readout-only adaptation with 35 labeled target trajectories per task), FARM-Adapt achieved superior performance compared to SAFE-MLP/LSTM across all aggregate metrics. It also exceeded the zero-shot STAC-Single reference, indicating that the lightweight readout can effectively absorb residual task-semantic shifts with limited target supervision.

**C. Real-Robot Transfer Across Policies and Platforms**
The transferability of FARM was assessed across various real-robot platforms, execution policies, and visual domains.
- **Zero-Shot Transfer:** FARM-Expanded (trained on a larger source population) consistently out-performed FARM-Core (canonical source population) in zero-shot settings across all four real-robot scenarios (PIPER X with two policies, SO-101, and Franka) (Table IV). This highlights that the quality of the source-domain decoding rule, influenced by source data, affects transfer performance.
- **Readout-Only Adaptation:** FARM-Adapt, which initializes from FARM-Core and adapts only the 33,985-parameter readout on target data, consistently improved performance over its zero-shot initialization across all robot/policy settings (Table IV). For instance, on the Franka population, it achieved 75.78 AUROC and 74.31 AUPRC. This demonstrates that target supervision can effectively realign the monitoring interface without requiring modifications to the predictive backbone or WM-state extractor.
- **Qualitative Observations:** Representative real-robot rollouts for tasks like plug insertion, duck manipulation, and blue-cube grasping showed that the step-wise failure score `s_t` from FARM-Core remained low during normal execution and rose sharply as failures emerged (Figure 3).

**D. Causal Monitoring and Efficiency**
The study examined FARM's ability for causal monitoring and its computational efficiency.
- **Partial-History Discrimination:** Five-fold OOF predictions showed a steady improvement in discrimination as more causal history was observed. At 25% of the observed trajectory, Pooled AUROC/AUPRC were already 75.07/80.75, and at 75%, they reached 81.63/85.53 (Figure 5). This indicates that FARM can achieve a substantial portion of its full-history discrimination with incomplete causal information.
- **Qualitative Causal Traces:** Visualizations of instantaneous step-wise scores on simulated failure rollouts (e.g., wrong-object selection, prolonged stall, misplacement) indicated that elevated scores temporally aligned with observable abnormal execution states (Figure 4).
- **Efficiency:** The detector-side latency for FARM was measured to be 0.2256 ms (mean CUDA time) with a 0.2393 ms P99 on an RTX 5090, once the WM state is available. This translates to a high throughput of 4,432.8 steps/s, suggesting that FARM's overhead is minimal and suitable for real-time online monitoring.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic autonomy and AI by demonstrating a paradigm for execution monitoring that leverages the intrinsic predictive capabilities of existing, pre-trained world models.

The primary significance lies in **validating the hypothesis that failure information is already present and decodable within the internal predictive states of frozen robotic world models.** This challenges the conventional approach of training dedicated monitoring components or adapting the world model's backbone for failure detection. Instead, it proposes a more efficient representation-reuse strategy.

Key implications and potential impacts include:

- **Enhanced Robot Reliability and Autonomy:** By providing a direct, causal, and transferable signal for execution failure, FARM can significantly improve the reliability of robot deployments. This enables robots to operate more autonomously, identifying when to continue, intervene, recover, or reset without constant human oversight.
- **Computational Efficiency:** The low overhead of the lightweight readout (sub-millisecond latency) ensures that failure monitoring can be integrated into existing robot control loops without introducing significant computational burden, making it practical for real-time online deployment.
- **Broadened Utility of Pre-trained World Models:** The work highlights that large pre-trained robotic world models, initially developed for prediction and control, can serve as versatile feature extractors for diverse downstream tasks, including complex semantic tasks like failure detection. This encourages further research into reusing and probing the rich representations learned by foundational models in robotics.
- **Improved Transferability and Adaptability:** FARM demonstrates robust multi-task detection across various simulation scenarios. Crucially, its ability for zero-shot transfer and lightweight readout-only adaptation across different robot platforms, policies, and visual domains suggests a path towards more generalizable monitoring solutions. This reduces the need for extensive, failure-specific data collection for every new robotic task or platform.
- **Causal and Early Detection:** The causal nature of the monitoring system, with its ability to discriminate failures from partial causal histories, offers the potential for earlier detection of issues, which is critical for timely intervention and mitigation of negative outcomes.

While the research acknowledges that zero-shot transfer is not uniformly successful and that labeled outcomes are still required for readout training or adaptation, it lays a foundation for future work. This includes investigating the hierarchical distribution of failure information across predictor layers within world models and exploring how FARM scores can be directly integrated with proactive intervention or recovery policies. Ultimately, this work supports the strategic reuse of frozen robotic world-model states as a robust, efficient, and versatile source of features for execution monitoring.
