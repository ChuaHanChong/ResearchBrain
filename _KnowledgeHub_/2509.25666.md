# Nudging the Boundaries of LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> NuRL enables large language models (LLMs) to learn from previously "unsolvable" reasoning problems by adaptively injecting self-generated, abstract hints during reinforcement learning. This method improves average performance by up to +3.44% over GRPO and expands the model's fundamental reasoning capabilities, as evidenced by gains in pass@k on challenging tasks.

## Problem

- Existing online reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms fail to learn from problems where large language models (LLMs) consistently yield 0% correct answers, resulting in no reward signal and no policy updates.
- Current RL approaches primarily improve performance on easier, already solvable tasks (pass@1) but often do not expand the model's fundamental reasoning capacity or "upper limit" (pass@k) for harder problems.
- Valuable training signals from challenging, currently "unsolvable" examples are not utilized, limiting the full potential of RL for enhancing LLM reasoning.

## Method

- **NuRL** generates abstract, ==self-supervised hints== for each training sample by prompting the LLM with the gold answer and an initial Chain-of-Thought, followed by an abstraction step to create concise cues.
- Hints are adaptively injected into the online GRPO training process only when initial rollouts for a problem yield zero correct answers (a difficulty trigger), ensuring learning from previously intractable samples.
- A two-stage training strategy is employed: initial convergence with standard GRPO, followed by NuRL application with hints, and filtering of easy samples to maximize learning efficiency.

## Results

- NuRL consistently outperforms existing RL methods, yielding average performance improvements of +**1.62%** to +**1.75%** over GRPO across various LLM architectures and reasoning benchmarks, with up to +**3.44%** using external teacher-generated hints.
- The approach demonstrably expands the LLM's upper reasoning limit (pass@k) for challenging tasks, improving pass@k from **85.4%** to **94.0%** on Date Understanding and from **67.2%** to **69.7%** on GPQA with self-generated hints.
- NuRL significantly increases the fraction of solvable problems during training, showing an approximate **4%** rise in solvable problems for Qwen, thereby utilizing more potential learning signals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Abstract, high-level cues are more effective as hints for improving LLM reasoning and generalization, while explicit solutions or direct ground-truth answers can hinder performance.
> - Optimal hint application requires an adaptive, two-stage training strategy, introducing hints only for challenging, "unsolvable" problems after initial RL training has stabilized.
> - This "nudging" approach enables LLMs to genuinely expand their reasoning capabilities by making previously intractable problems learnable, pushing the model beyond merely sharpening existing skills.
