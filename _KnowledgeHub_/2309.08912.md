# Delving into Multimodal Prompting for Fine-grained Visual Classification

> [!summary] Summary
> MP-FGVC introduces a multimodal prompting and adaptation framework to effectively leverage pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP for close-set Fine-Grained Visual Classification. This approach achieved state-of-the-art Top-1 Accuracy across four datasets, demonstrating improvements up to 0.9% on Food101 while enhancing sensitivity to subcategory-specific visual and linguistic cues.

## Problem

- Fine-grained Visual Classification (FGVC) is challenging due to subtle inter-class differences and large intra-class variations.
- Existing visual-only methods struggle to capture minute discriminative features, while pre-trained vision-language models like CLIP lack fine-grained sensitivity for subcategory-level classification.
- Standard FGVC datasets typically provide only subcategory labels, hindering the direct application and full exploitation of rich textual knowledge from vision-language models.

## Method

- A Multimodal Prompting for Fine-grained Visual Classification (MP-FGVC) framework, built on pre-trained CLIP, employs a ==two-stage optimization strategy==.
- It introduces a ==Subcategory-specific Vision Prompt (SSVP)== to identify discriminative visual regions and a ==Discrepancy-aware Text Prompt (DATP)== with learnable tokens for fine-grained textual descriptions.
- A ==Vision-Language Fusion Module (VLFM)== uses cross-modal attention to align and integrate visual and textual embeddings, forming a more discriminative feature for classification.

## Results

- MP-FGVC achieved state-of-the-art Top-1 Accuracy on four fine-grained datasets (CUB-200-2011, Stanford Dogs, NABirds, Food101).
- The method demonstrated improvements of up to **0.9%** on Food101 and **0.4%** to **1.8%** over other ViT-based methods across datasets.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all proposed components, including SSVP, DATP, and VLFM, individually contribute to the overall performance gains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effectively adapting pre-trained vision-language models for fine-grained tasks requires explicitly engineering prompts to capture subcategory-specific visual and linguistic discrepancies.
> - A two-stage optimization strategy is crucial for successfully adapting large pre-trained models, allowing initial prompt refinement before full multimodal adaptation.
> - Learnable text tokens with supercategory labels can generate effective discrepancy-aware text descriptions, overcoming the lack of detailed annotations in FGVC datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper addresses the challenging task of Fine-Grained Visual Classification (FGVC), a critical area in computer vision that aims to distinguish between subtly different subcategories within a broader supercategory (e.g., different species of birds, different car models). Traditionally, FGVC methods have evolved through several stages.

Initially, **CNN-based approaches** dominated the field, broadly categorized into "feature-encoding methods" that learn comprehensive features and "part-localization methods" that identify discriminative local regions. While these methods achieved satisfactory performance, they suffered from drawbacks such as focusing on irrelevant image regions and a lack of robustness due to their reliance solely on visual concepts from a predefined closed set.

The advent of **Vision Transformers (ViTs)** marked a significant shift. ViTs, with their patch-by-patch processing and powerful self-attention mechanisms, demonstrated remarkable performance in general image classification and quickly found applications in FGVC. They offered advantages over CNNs in modeling long-range dependencies, but still primarily relied on visual representations and often struggled with the relatively limited data size characteristic of many FGVC datasets, leading to optimization instability.

More recently, the landscape has been revolutionized by **large-scale Vision-Language (VL) models** such as CLIP (Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training). These models learn powerful cross-modal representations by training on vast datasets of image-text pairs, enabling them to connect visual concepts with high-level language descriptions. This capability has opened new avenues for tasks like zero-shot classification and open-set recognition by leveraging text prompts.

However, the authors identify a critical gap: existing prompt strategies for VL models, while effective for high-level classification, are primarily designed to capture *supercategory-level semantics*. They are not sensitive enough to the *detailed subcategory-level discrepancies* that are fundamental to FGVC. Furthermore, the lack of specific, rich textual annotations in many existing FGVC datasets hinders the direct application and full exploitation of VL models in *close-set FGVC scenarios*, where the goal is to classify into a predefined set of subcategories. Previous works involving CLIP for fine-grained understanding often focused on open-set scenarios like image retrieval, rather than closed-set classification.

This paper positions itself directly within this critical gap. It aims to effectively adapt the pre-trained CLIP model to *close-set FGVC tasks* by enhancing its sensitivity to subcategory-specific discrepancies through novel multimodal prompting and adaptation schemes. By doing so, it pioneers the application of large-scale VL models to FGVC in a manner that addresses its unique challenges, thereby pushing the boundaries of what these powerful foundation models can achieve in fine-grained perception.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **fully exploit the capabilities of cross-modal description, specifically from pre-trained vision-language (VL) models like CLIP, to effectively tackle Fine-Grained Visual Classification (FGVC) tasks in close-set scenarios.** To achieve this, the authors propose a novel multimodal prompting solution named MP-FGVC.

The motivation behind this objective stems from several key observations and challenges:

1. **Inherent Difficulty of FGVC:** FGVC is intrinsically challenging due to the subtle inter-class discrepancies (e.g., distinguishing between very similar bird species) and large intra-class variations (e.g., different poses, lighting conditions, or occlusions of the same species). Existing visual-only methods (CNNs, ViTs) often struggle to reliably capture these minute yet crucial differences.
2. **Limitations of Uni-modal Visual Concepts:** Traditional deep learning approaches for FGVC primarily rely on uni-modal visual concepts. While advancements have been made, these methods tend to focus on generic features or small, sometimes irrelevant, regions, lacking the robustness and discriminability needed for fine-grained distinctions.
3. **Inspiration from Human Perception:** The human visual system intuitively uses prior linguistic knowledge (e.g., descriptive features) to adjust visual processing and facilitate fine-grained object recognition. This suggests that incorporating textual information could significantly enhance machine-based FGVC.
4. **Underutilized Potential of VL Models:** Pre-trained VL models like CLIP have demonstrated remarkable performance in connecting visual and linguistic representations across various high-level vision tasks. However, their applicability to FGVC, especially in close-set classification where precise subcategory distinctions are needed, remains uncertain and largely unaddressed. Existing prompt strategies are typically designed for broad category semantics, not fine-grained details.
5. **Lack of Specific Annotations in FGVC Datasets:** A practical barrier to adopting VL models in FGVC is the typical annotation format—FGVC datasets usually provide only subcategory labels or indices, lacking the rich, detailed textual descriptions of visual discrepancies that VL models could leverage.
6. **Need for Fine-grained Sensitivity:** The core motivation is to devise a mechanism that makes the powerful pre-trained CLIP model *sensitive* to these subtle subcategory-specific discrepancies, enabling it to go beyond broad categorization and perform accurate fine-grained classification.

By addressing these points, the authors aim to introduce a paradigm shift in FGVC by moving beyond purely visual methods and integrating the rich semantic understanding offered by cross-modal vision-language models, carefully adapted for the nuances of fine-grained tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed MP-FGVC (Multimodal Prompting for Fine-grained Visual Classification) solution is built upon the pre-trained Contrastive Language-Image Pre-training (CLIP) model and employs a two-stage optimization strategy. The framework is composed of two main schemes: a multimodal prompts scheme and a multimodal adaptation scheme.

#### 3.1. Multimodal Prompts Scheme

This scheme is designed to enhance the CLIP model's sensitivity to subcategory-specific discrepancies from both visual and linguistic perspectives.

- **Subcategory-specific Vision Prompt (SSVP):** This component addresses the limitation of generic ViT patch processing. Instead of treating all image patches equally, SSVP aims to identify and focus on discriminative regions crucial for distinguishing subcategories. It's a parameter-free operation applied to the penultimate layer of the ViT image encoder. The method aggregates attention scores from the multiple heads of the Multi-Head Self-Attention (MHSA) mechanism within the ViT. It then selects the top-k most salient patches/tokens—those with the highest aggregated attention values with respect to the class token—as input for the final transformer layer. This process encourages the image encoder to concentrate on subtle, discriminative visual features while discarding redundant information.

- **Discrepancy-aware Text Prompt (DATP):** Recognizing that existing text prompts are often too coarse for FGVC, DATP introduces a set of learnable text tokens. These tokens are designed to automatically generate appropriate text descriptions that highlight more detailed visual discrepancies among subcategories. The text prompt template is structured as `[X_1, X_2..., X_J, class++]`, where `X_j` are the learnable tokens, and `class++` represents the word embedding of the *supercategory label* (e.g., "bird," "dog," "food") rather than specific subcategory names. This strategy maintains semantic coherence across classes within a dataset while allowing the model to learn abstract, discrepancy-aware textual cues. This addresses the challenge of limited specific textual annotations in FGVC datasets.

#### 3.2. Multimodal Adaptation Scheme

This scheme ensures semantic coherence and facilitates collaborative reasoning between the vision and text prompts.

- **Vision-Language Fusion Module (VLFM):** VLFM is introduced to align the subcategory-specific visual embeddings (from SSVP) and the discrepancy-aware textual embeddings (from DATP) into a common semantic space. It employs a cross-modal attention mechanism. The visual embedding, derived from the ViT's class token, acts as the query, while the textual features from the learned text tokens serve as keys and values. This cross-modal attention transforms the subcategory-specific textual descriptions into subcategory-specific visual discrepancies. A "translation layer" (composed of two 1x1 convolution layers) further refines this fused embedding, aiming to recover missing discrepancies and form a more discriminative and generalized visual feature for final classification.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Optimization Strategy

To effectively leverage the pre-trained CLIP model and adapt it for FGVC tasks with limited specific annotations, a novel two-stage training strategy is proposed:

- **Stage 1: Prompt Refinement:** In this stage, the parameters of the CLIP text encoder are frozen. The image encoder (including SSVP) and the learnable tokens in DATP are optimized. The loss function combines a standard cross-entropy loss (`L_v`) for visual predictions (using the visual embeddings) and an image-to-text contrastive loss (`L_i2t`). `L_i2t` encourages the visual embeddings to align with the correct discrepancy-aware textual embeddings generated by DATP, thereby helping the learned tokens capture subcategory-specific descriptions. This stage focuses on making the visual and textual prompts sensitive to fine-grained differences.

- **Stage 2: Multimodal Adaptation:** After the prompts are refined, the parameters of both the image and text encoders, along with the optimized prompt components (SSVP and DATP), are frozen. Only the Vision-Language Fusion Module (VLFM) is optimized. The objective here is to align and fuse the already discrepancy-aware visual and textual embeddings into a unified feature space for final classification. A cross-entropy loss (`L_vlfm`) is used on the refined multimodal feature output by VLFM. This sequential approach prevents the randomly initialized text tokens from hindering the overall optimization in early stages and ensures that VLFM benefits from well-tuned prompts.

This meticulously designed methodology allows MP-FGVC to overcome the limitations of applying generic VL models to FGVC, by explicitly engineering mechanisms to pinpoint and leverage fine-grained visual and linguistic cues.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on four widely-used fine-grained datasets (CUB-200-2011, Stanford Dogs, NABirds, and Food101) consistently demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed MP-FGVC method.

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** MP-FGVC achieved the highest Top-1 Accuracy across all evaluated datasets, outperforming both traditional CNN-based methods and other state-of-the-art ViT-based approaches. Specifically, it showed improvements of 0.5% on CUB-200-2011, 0.3% on Stanford Dogs, 0.5% on NABirds, and a significant 0.9% on Food101 compared to the second-best methods. When compared against other ViT-based methods, MP-FGVC yielded even larger gains, ranging from 0.4% to 1.8% across datasets. This empirical evidence strongly validates the effectiveness of the proposed multimodal prompting solution.

- **Efficacy of Individual Components:** Ablation studies rigorously evaluated the contribution of each component within MP-FGVC:
 - The **Discrepancy-aware Text Prompt (DATP)** alone provided a noticeable boost (e.g., 0.5% on CUB, 0.4% on NABirds) over the baseline (pure ViT), confirming that learned discrepancy descriptions indeed complement image content.
 - The **Vision-Language Fusion Module (VLFM)** further enhanced performance when combined with DATP, indicating the value of aligning and fusing information across modalities.
 - The **Subcategory-specific Vision Prompt (SSVP)** consistently improved performance across all model variations, underscoring its effectiveness in guiding the image encoder to focus on discriminative visual regions. The full MP-FGVC, integrating SSVP, DATP, and VLFM, achieved the highest scores.

- **Comparison of Prompting Schemes:**
 - **Vision Prompt (SSVP):** SSVP proved superior to other token selection methods (like PSM and MHVM from existing FGVC solutions), highlighting its efficiency in identifying subcategory-specific discrepancies. Analysis of the hyperparameter *k* (number of selected visual tokens) revealed an optimal value of 14, beyond which performance degraded, suggesting that too many tokens might dilute the focus on truly discriminative regions.
 - **Text Prompt (DATP):** Experiments with various text prompt templates showed that using learnable text tokens with a *supercategory* label (`class++`) outperformed handcrafted prompts or those using specific subcategory names. This indicates the model's ability to learn generalized discrepancy descriptions. The optimal length *J* for learnable text tokens was found to be 16; exceeding this length could lead to the model prioritizing prompt optimization over fine-grained visual understanding.

- **Necessity of Two-Stage Training:** A direct comparison between the proposed two-stage training strategy and a single-stage approach unequivocally demonstrated the superiority of the two-stage method. One-stage training performed less effectively, primarily because randomly initialized text tokens in early stages provided inadequate descriptions, hindering overall model optimization. The two-stage approach successfully preserved the valuable pre-trained states of the CLIP encoders while adaptively learning fine-grained prompts and fusion mechanisms.

- **Visual Interpretability:** GradCAM visualizations provided qualitative evidence of MP-FGVC's effectiveness. Compared to the baseline model, MP-FGVC enabled the model to focus on more comprehensive and diagnostically relevant regions (e.g., specific parts of a bird like the head, back, abdomen, and wings, especially those with subtle color mutations), thereby confirming its ability to capture fine-grained distinctions crucial for classification.

In summary, the results robustly support the core hypothesis that effectively adapting pre-trained vision-language models through multimodal prompting and adaptation can significantly advance the state-of-the-art in close-set FGVC, moving beyond the limitations of uni-modal visual approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MP-FGVC framework represents a significant step forward in fine-grained visual classification, with several notable aspects of significance and potential impact:

- **Pioneering Adaptation of VL Models for Close-set FGVC:** The authors explicitly state this is the "first approach that efficiently adapts the image-text pre-trained CLIP model to FGVC tasks in close-set scenarios." This marks a crucial advancement, as previous applications of VL models in fine-grained contexts largely focused on open-set retrieval or broader conceptual understanding. MP-FGVC demonstrates how these powerful models can be precisely tuned for the highly discriminative demands of fixed-category classification.

- **Addressing the Core Challenges of FGVC:** By introducing subcategory-specific vision prompts (SSVP) and discrepancy-aware text prompts (DATP), the method directly tackles the twin challenges of FGVC: subtle inter-class discrepancies and large intra-class variations. It provides explicit mechanisms to highlight the most discriminative visual features and to generate linguistic cues that describe these fine-grained differences, leading to more robust and accurate classification.

- **Efficient Knowledge Transfer from Pre-trained Models:** MP-FGVC showcases an elegant and effective strategy for leveraging the vast knowledge embedded in large-scale pre-trained models like CLIP with minimal modifications. This approach reduces the need for extensive, task-specific dataset annotations, a common bottleneck in FGVC, by allowing the model to learn discrepancy descriptions itself. The two-stage training strategy is particularly impactful in demonstrating how to maintain the integrity of pre-trained encoders while adapting them to a new, challenging domain.

- **Advancing Cross-Modal Reasoning:** The Vision-Language Fusion Module (VLFM) underlines the power of cross-modal collaborative reasoning. By mutually aligning and fusing visual and textual prompts, MP-FGVC creates a richer, more discriminative semantic representation. This capability has broader implications for how artificial intelligence systems can integrate and interpret information from different modalities to achieve more nuanced understanding.

- **Practical Applications:** Improved FGVC accuracy has wide-ranging practical applications. In **biodiversity monitoring**, it can lead to more accurate automated species identification (e.g., bird species, dog breeds). In **manufacturing and quality control**, it can help distinguish minute defects in products. In **medical imaging**, it could aid in classifying subtle variations in disease manifestation. In **retail**, it can enhance product search and categorization. The versatility of FGVC makes advancements in this area valuable across many sectors.

- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The work explicitly aims to "serve as inspiration and facilitate further research on efficient adaptation methods of large-scale vision-language models for FGVC tasks." It lays a strong foundation for exploring other sophisticated prompting techniques, alternative fusion mechanisms, and adapting VL models to even more challenging fine-grained tasks or those with even scarcer annotated data. It also opens avenues for investigating how human-like top-down cognitive penetration, drawing on linguistic knowledge, can be further integrated into computer vision systems.

In conclusion, MP-FGVC not only pushes the state-of-the-art in FGVC but also significantly contributes to the broader field of multimodal AI by demonstrating a principled and effective way to harness the power of large vision-language models for highly specific and challenging discrimination tasks.
