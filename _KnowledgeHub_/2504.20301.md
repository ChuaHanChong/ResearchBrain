# Deformable Multibody Modeling for Model Predictive Control in Legged Locomotion with Embodied Compliance

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of California, Riverside developed a unified framework for modeling and controlling legged robots with both rigid and compliant spines, introducing the Centroidal Composite Predictive Deformed Inertia (CCPDI) tensor. This approach improved control stability and prediction accuracy for compliant-spine quadrupedal robots in simulations compared to rigid-body models.

## Problem

- Most successful legged robot designs rely on simplified models that treat the main body as a single rigid structure, limiting the benefits of incorporating flexibility.
- Traditional model-based control approaches struggle to accurately account for the complex dynamics introduced by spinal flexibility in robots.
- Model inaccuracies caused by ignoring compliance can lead to control instabilities and performance degradation in robots with deformable components.

## Method

- A novel unified framework models deformable multibody systems by approximating a deformable body as a collection of interconnected rigid sub-bodies.
- The Centroidal Composite Predictive Deformed Inertia (CCPDI) tensor is introduced, representing the inertia properties of a deformable multibody system accounting for current and predicted future deformations.
- The CCPDI tensor is integrated into a ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework, using discrete-time centroidal dynamics that incorporate the CCPDI as constraints.

## Results

- The CCPDI-enabled Model Predictive Control (MPC) successfully stabilized trot stepping gaits for compliant-spine quadrupedal robots in simulations, while standard MPC failed after approximately 9 seconds.
- The CCPDI-enabled approach provided significantly better prediction accuracy for the robot's inertial properties compared to the CCPDI-disabled method.
- The CCPDI-enabled MPC demonstrated improved ground reaction force distribution, contributing to better balance and stability during locomotion.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Approximating deformable bodies as interconnected rigid sub-bodies allows for capturing deformation through relative motion, enabling a systematic dynamic modeling approach.
> - The Centroidal Composite Predictive Deformed Inertia (CCPDI) tensor is crucial for effective control by representing inertia properties that account for both current and predicted future deformations.
> - Explicitly accounting for spinal compliance within a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework allows the controller to better anticipate and accommodate deformation effects, leading to improved stability and tracking.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Legged robots have made significant strides in recent years, demonstrating impressive capabilities in mobility and agility. However, most successful designs rely on simplified models that treat the robot's main body as a single rigid structure. This approach limits the potential benefits of incorporating flexibility and compliance into a robot's design, particularly in the spine.

The research by Keran Ye and Konstantinos Karydis from the University of California, Riverside addresses this limitation by developing a unified framework for modeling and controlling legged robots with both rigid and compliant spines. This work introduces a novel approach to accurately represent the dynamics of robots with flexible bodies, enabling more effective model-based control strategies.

### 2. Background and Motivation

Biological systems, particularly vertebrate animals, utilize spinal compliance to achieve efficient and agile locomotion. The combination of muscles (actuators) and ligaments/tendons (compliant elements) in the spine allows for energy storage and release during movement, enhancing both efficiency and maneuverability. Incorporating similar compliant elements into robotic spines could potentially yield comparable benefits.

However, controlling robots with compliant spines presents significant challenges. Traditional model-based control approaches, which rely on accurate predictions of the robot's dynamics, struggle to account for the complex behavior introduced by spinal flexibility. The resulting model inaccuracies can lead to control instabilities and performance degradation.

Key motivations for this research include:
- Addressing limitations of existing control methods that focus primarily on rigid-body models
- Leveraging the potential benefits of spinal compliance for enhanced robot performance
- Developing a general framework for describing deformable multibody systems
- Creating control strategies that explicitly account for embodied compliance

### 3. Deformable Multibody Modeling

The authors propose a novel approach to modeling deformable multibody systems. Instead of treating the entire robot as a single rigid body, they approximate a deformable body as a collection of interconnected rigid sub-bodies. This approximation allows them to capture the deformation through the relative motion between these sub-bodies.

Each sub-body in the system has its own local reference frame, center of mass, and moment of inertia. The kinematics of the entire system are described using a tree-like structure, where the motion of each body is related to its predecessor through a joint and the deformation of the connecting sub-body.

The mathematical formulation involves propagating both kinematics and dynamics through this tree structure. The position and orientation of each body are described relative to a global frame, and the forces and torques are propagated through the system according to the principles of rigid body dynamics.

### 4. Centroidal Composite Predictive Deformed Inertia

A key contribution of this work is the introduction of the Centroidal Composite Predictive Deformed Inertia (CCPDI) tensor. This novel concept represents the inertia properties of a deformable multibody system in a way that accounts for both current and predicted future deformations.

The CCPDI tensor is calculated recursively, starting from individual bodies and working up to the entire system. It captures the instantaneous centroidal properties as well as the predicted evolution of these properties due to the system's compliance. This predictive aspect is crucial for effective control, as it allows the controller to anticipate how the robot's dynamics will change as a result of its deformable components.

Mathematically, the CCPDI tensor incorporates both the rigid body inertia of each component and the additional terms that arise from their relative motion due to deformation. The tensor is defined in a way that preserves the fundamental properties of inertia tensors while accounting for the system's compliance.

The authors derive explicit formulas for calculating the CCPDI tensor based on the physical parameters of the system, such as the mass distribution, spring constants, and rest lengths of the compliant elements. These formulas provide a computationally efficient way to update the tensor during operation, making it suitable for real-time control applications.

### 5. Model Predictive Control Integration

The CCPDI tensor is integrated into a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework to achieve effective control of legged robots with compliant spines. MPC is a control strategy that predicts the future behavior of a system and optimizes control actions to achieve desired goals while respecting constraints.

In the proposed approach, the discrete-time centroidal dynamics of the robot, incorporating the CCPDI tensor, are used as constraints in the MPC optimization problem. This allows the controller to account for the effects of spinal compliance when planning the robot's motion and force distribution.

The MPC formulation includes:
- A cost function that penalizes deviations from desired states and control efforts
- Constraints on the robot's dynamics, incorporating the CCPDI tensor
- Constraints on physical limitations such as joint ranges and motor torques
- Constraints on contact forces to ensure stable interaction with the environment

By explicitly accounting for the robot's compliance in the MPC formulation, the controller can better anticipate and accommodate the effects of spinal deformation during locomotion. This leads to improved tracking performance and stability compared to controllers that treat the robot as a rigid body.

### 6. Simulation Results

The effectiveness of the proposed approach was verified through simulations using a digital twin of a physical quadruped robot. The robot was simulated with both rigid and compliant spines, and the performance of the CCPDI-enabled MPC was compared to standard MPC that treats the robot as a rigid body (CCPDI-disabled).

The simulation results demonstrate several key findings:

1. **Improved Stability**: The CCPDI-enabled MPC successfully stabilized trot stepping gaits on the quadrupedal robot with both rigid and compliant spines. In contrast, the CCPDI-disabled MPC failed to maintain stability for the compliant-spine robot after approximately 9 seconds of simulation.

### 7. Parametric Studies

The researchers conducted parametric studies to assess the robustness of their method to different compliance parameters. Specifically, they varied the spring constant and rest length of the compliant elements in the robot's spine and evaluated the controller's ability to maintain stable locomotion.

The parametric study results reveal important insights:

1. **Parameter Sensitivity**: The performance of the controller is sensitive to both the spring constant and rest length of the compliant elements. There exists a region of parameter values where stable locomotion is achievable, but outside this region, the controller fails to maintain stability.

2. **Robustness Envelope**: The CCPDI-enabled MPC demonstrates robustness within a suitable envelope of parameter values. This suggests that while the controller can accommodate variations in compliance parameters, there are limits to its adaptability.

3. **Spring Loading Effect**: The robot is more successful with more heavily spring-loaded systems (higher spring constants), indicating that while compliance is beneficial, excessive flexibility can make control more challenging.

These findings highlight the importance of properly designing the compliant elements in legged robots and tuning the control parameters accordingly.

### 8. Conclusion and Impact

This research makes significant contributions to the field of legged robotics by developing a comprehensive framework for modeling and controlling robots with compliant spines. The introduction of the Centroidal Composite Predictive Deformed Inertia tensor and its integration into Model Predictive Control represent important advances in addressing the challenges of controlling robots with embodied compliance.

The key advantages of the proposed approach include:

1. **Unified Framework**: The deformable multibody modeling provides a consistent framework for describing robots with both rigid and compliant components.

2. **Improved Performance**: The CCPDI-enabled MPC demonstrates superior stability and tracking performance compared to controllers that do not account for spinal compliance.

3. **Enhanced Prediction Accuracy**: By explicitly modeling the effects of deformation, the approach achieves better prediction of the robot's dynamics, leading to more effective control.

4. **Practical Applicability**: The method is computationally efficient and can be integrated into existing control frameworks, making it suitable for real-world implementation.

The impact of this work extends beyond the specific case of quadrupedal robots with compliant spines. The concepts and methodologies developed here could be applied to a wide range of robotic systems with flexible components, potentially enabling more agile, efficient, and robust robots for applications such as search and rescue, exploration, and inspection.

Future research directions identified by the authors include further integration of the CCPDI approach into overall control frameworks, verification on physical robot prototypes, exploration of the relationship between compliance parameters and control performance, and development of more advanced control strategies that leverage the predictive capabilities of the CCPDI tensor.
