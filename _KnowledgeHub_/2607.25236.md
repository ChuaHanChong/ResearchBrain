# VisualPatchWorld: Code World Models as Latent Structured Representations for Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> VisualPatchWorld (VPW) generates executable code world models from interaction data, improving planning performance by explicitly selecting the correct qualitative dynamics and optimizing for multi-step rollout error. This method achieved a mean planning success of 69.0% across diverse control tasks, surpassing prior programmatic baselines by 23.5 percentage points, and demonstrated near ground-truth physics engine performance, especially when augmented with hybrid scoring.

## Problem

- Neural latent world models are opaque, making learned dynamics difficult to inspect, understand, or repair.
- Hand-built physics engines, while transparent, are labor-intensive and challenging to scale for new or complex environments.
- Previous code-based world models often focus on single-step prediction accuracy, failing to capture the correct qualitative form of dynamics essential for effective long-horizon planning.

## Method

- **VPW** employs a ==four-stage pipeline== encompassing ==visual abstraction== to object-centric scene graphs, ==trajectory export==, ==two-level program induction==, and a ==planning protocol==.
- The core two-level program induction first actively probes the environment to select a qualitative ==dynamical sketch== and then identifies its parameters by minimizing ==multi-step rollout error== on offline data.
- Planning is conducted using ==receding-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC)== with the ==Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)==, evaluating both ==induced-only scoring== and a novel ==hybrid scoring== approach that selectively re-scores top candidates with a ground-truth physics engine.

## Results

- VPW achieved a mean planning success of **69.0%** across four control tasks, outperforming the strongest prior programmatic baseline (POMDP-Coder) by **23.5 percentage points** and PatchWorld by **26.0 points**.
- With induced-only scoring, VPW recovered a substantial portion of the MuJoCo physics engine's ceiling performance, reaching **96%** success in Two-room, **86%** in Cube, and **72%** in Reacher.
- Hybrid scoring significantly improved performance in contact-rich PushT, increasing success from **22%** (induced-only) to **88-96%**, effectively closing most of the gap to the MuJoCo ceiling while reducing physics queries by up to **70%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly selecting the correct qualitative dynamical form of an environment's physics (dynamical sketch) is more critical for effective long-horizon planning than simply synthesizing free-form code that fits local transitions.
> - Optimizing program parameters for multi-step rollout error directly enhances planning utility by penalizing models that drift over longer horizons, rather than just optimizing for single-step prediction accuracy.
> - A hybrid scoring mechanism, leveraging the induced code model for efficient candidate search and a high-fidelity physics engine for selective verification, effectively closes performance gaps in challenging contact-rich tasks while reducing overall computational cost.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The concept of a "world model" in artificial intelligence encompasses various approaches designed to capture how an environment evolves under an agent's actions. These models serve critical functions such as rendering observations, simulating future states, and informing planning. The existing landscape is primarily divided into two main categories:

1. **Neural Latent World Models:** These models, exemplified by methods like Dreamer and LeWM, learn environmental dynamics directly from interaction data and represent future evolution in continuous, high-dimensional vector spaces. They are data-driven and scalable, capable of effectively learning from vast amounts of experience. However, a significant limitation is their opacity; the learned dynamics are implicit within the neural network's weights, making them difficult for humans or algorithms to inspect, understand, or repair when predictions fail.

2. **Hand-built Physics Engines (Curated Simulators):** These models approximate the ideal world model by providing explicit representations of state and physical laws, allowing for inspectable and editable dynamics. They offer transparency and direct control over environmental parameters. The primary challenge with physics engines is their scalability; constructing detailed and accurate models for diverse and complex environments is often a labor-intensive, human-driven process.

A third, complementary line of work, which VisualPatchWorld (VPW) belongs to, focuses on **Executable Code World Models**. Methods such as PatchWorld, WorldCoder, and PoE-World aim to recover dynamics in the form of executable programs. These approaches move closer to the inspectability of physics engines while attempting to learn from data, offering a potential bridge between neural opacity and manual construction. However, previous code-based models often prioritize accurate next-step prediction, which does not always translate to effective long-horizon planning, particularly if the recovered program fails to capture the correct qualitative form of the dynamics.

VisualPatchWorld positions itself within this third category, specifically addressing the limitations of prior code-based models. It aims to develop an automatically constructed code world model that is both inspectable and effective for planning, by explicitly tackling the selection of the correct qualitative dynamical form and optimizing for multi-step prediction utility rather than just single-step accuracy. This work endeavors to bridge the gap between scalable but implicit neural models and explicit but hard-to-build physics engines, as illustrated in Figure 1 of the paper.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for VisualPatchWorld (VPW) stems from the recognized trade-offs between existing world model paradigms and the desire for models that combine the benefits of both. The authors identify three specific questions that drive their research:

1. **Automatic Recovery of Simulator-Style Dynamics from Interaction Trajectories:** Hand-built physics engines offer simulator-style dynamics (inspectable, editable, rollable forward). However, constructing them for new environments is challenging and costly. Neural networks can learn dynamics from data, but the resulting models are often opaque. The first objective is to develop a method that can automatically extract executable, simulator-like dynamics (i.e., code) directly from agent-environment interaction data, reducing the reliance on manual engineering. This addresses the scalability problem of hand-built simulators while retaining their desirable properties.

2. **Selection of the Correct Qualitative Form of Dynamics:** Current code-based world models often fit parameters for local transitions but may not capture the underlying qualitative physical laws (e.g., contact mechanics, kinematic chains). A program might parse and execute but represent the wrong fundamental type of interaction, leading to poor long-term predictions and ineffective planning. The second objective is to enable the learning process to select the appropriate qualitative form (or "sketch") of the dynamics, rather than merely fitting parameters to an ill-suited form. This ensures that the induced model's structure aligns with the true environmental physics.

3. **Utility for Planning and Comparison to Ground-Truth Physics Engines:** The ultimate utility of a world model lies in its ability to support effective action selection and planning over extended horizons. Previous code-based models, despite recovering executable programs, have not always demonstrated robust performance in goal-directed control. The third objective is to assess how well the recovered code models can rank candidate action sequences within a short-horizon planner and to quantify any remaining performance gaps compared to a ground-truth physics engine. This includes investigating whether specific planning strategies, such as hybrid scoring that selectively uses the physics engine, can mitigate these gaps, particularly in complex scenarios like contact-rich manipulation.

In summary, the core motivation is to create code-based world models that are automatically learned, transparent, editable, and sufficiently accurate in their dynamics representation to enable competitive goal-directed planning, thereby offering a practical alternative to both opaque neural models and labor-intensive hand-built simulators.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VisualPatchWorld (VPW) employs a four-stage pipeline to convert interaction trajectories into an executable code world model, as illustrated in Figure 2 of the paper.

#### 3.1. Stage 1: Visual Abstraction

The initial stage transforms raw pixel observations into structured, object-centric scene descriptions, termed "scene graphs." These graphs capture geometric and relational information crucial for dynamics, such as object poses (positions, orientations) and pairwise attributes (distances, near-contact flags, relative directions). The authors implement three interchangeable extractors, allowing for controlled evaluation of perception quality:
- **Oracle Path:** Reads simulator state directly, eliminating perception error to isolate the dynamics learning problem.
- **Tool Path:** Applies computer vision heuristics (e.g., color segmentation, PCA for orientation, affine calibration, temporal smoothing) to RGB frames. This is the default image-observation interface.
- **VLM Path:** Utilizes a vision-language model (Qwen/Qwen3.5-397B-A17B) over RGB frames to recover semantic information and qualitative relations. This path is primarily used as a semantic baseline due to its limitations in metric coordinate accuracy.

#### 3.2. Stage 2: Trajectory Export

This stage converts recorded interaction rollouts into symbolic transition records. Each record is a triple $(g_t, \tilde{a}_t, g_{t+\delta})$, comprising a scene description before an action, the discretized action, and the scene description after a frame stride $\delta$. A critical aspect is ensuring that action discretization aligns with the frame stride to prevent misaligned macro-actions from distorting one-step predictions and degrading multi-step rollouts. The output is a dataset of structured state-action traces used for program induction.

#### 3.3. Stage 3: Two-Level Program Induction

This is the core learning stage, which induces an executable Python transition program $f_\theta$ such that $g_{t+1} = f_\theta(g_t, a_t)$. The process is designed to separate the choice of qualitative dynamics from parameter estimation.

- **Level 1: Sketch Selection by Active Probing (Structure Discovery):** Instead of open-ended code generation, VPW first selects a qualitative "dynamical sketch" from a small, domain-specific hypothesis family (e.g., contact versus free motion, linear vs. nonlinear transport, grip-gated motion, joint-space kinematics). This selection is made through short, discriminating active probes: the method performs small experiments (black-box reset and step calls) in the environment and retains the sketch that best explains the observed outcomes. This pre-selection ensures the underlying program structure is qualitatively appropriate for the environment.

- **Level 2: Parameter Identification:** Once the sketch $h_\star$ is chosen, VPW instantiates it as a Python template with unknown constants (parameters). These parameters are then estimated by minimizing a multi-step rollout error (accumulated prediction error over the planner horizon) on offline state-action traces. This objective penalizes models that fit single steps well but drift over longer rollouts, directly optimizing for planner utility. Differentiable optimization with multiple restarts is used, with the best-fitting restart on held-out data selected. For contact-rich templates, collision-geometry features may be incorporated. The output is a complete, executable Python program. An optional acquisition loop can further refine parameters by gathering additional transitions.

#### 3.4. Stage 4: Planning Protocol

The final stage evaluates the utility of the induced program for goal-directed control using receding-horizon Model Predictive Control (MPC) optimized by the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM). At each replan step, the planner:
1. Obtains the current state from either the tool path (live images) or oracle simulator state.
2. Samples candidate action sequences.
3. Rolls them forward using a "scoring model" to evaluate goal distance.
4. Retains the best samples, updates the sampling distribution, and executes the first $r$ actions.
5. Re-observes the state and replans.

Three scoring settings are evaluated:
- **Induced Scoring:** The mined program $f_\theta$ evaluates all candidate action sequences.
- **Hybrid Scoring:** The induced program scores all $N$ candidates, but the ground-truth physics engine re-scores a top 30% shortlist. This aims to leverage the induced model for efficient search while using the high-fidelity engine for critical verification.
- **Simulator Scoring (Physics Ceiling):** The ground-truth physics engine evaluates all candidates. This sets the upper bound for planning performance.

The evaluation process systematically varies perception source (tool vs. oracle) and scoring model (induced, hybrid, sim) to attribute failures to perception, dynamics, or scoring. This entire methodology is applied across four LeWM control domains covering navigation, contact manipulation, and low-dimensional continuous control.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across four control domains: Two-room, Reacher, PushT, and Cube, representing navigation, continuous control, 2D contact pushing, and 3D manipulation, respectively.

1. **Superiority over Prior Code Baselines:**
 - VPW Oracle+Induced achieved a mean planning success of 69.0% across the four tasks, surpassing the strongest programmatic baseline (POMDP-Coder at 45.5%) by 23.5 percentage points, and PatchWorld (43.0%) by 26.0 points, under identical starts and planner settings (Table 1).
 - Significant gains were observed in domains where qualitative structure is critical:
 - **Reacher:** Success rose from 8% (PatchWorld) to 72% for VPW, attributed to VPW recovering joint-space dynamics and forward kinematics, unlike baselines that often treated the fingertip as a free Cartesian particle (Table 3, Figure 4).
 - **Cube:** VPW reached 86% success due to its grip-gated contact dynamics, exceeding WorldCoder (74%) and PoE-World (84%).
 - **PushT:** VPW improved from near-zero success for prior code methods to 22%, by recovering contact-driven PD control with a quasi-static block response, although this remained a challenging domain.
 - **Two-room:** Both VPW and strong baselines achieved near-ceiling performance, indicating simpler underlying dynamics were sufficiently captured by various methods.
 - This highlights that selecting the correct qualitative dynamical class (Level 1 of induction) is more critical for planning success than merely synthesizing free-form code that fits local transitions.

2. **Performance Relative to Ground-Truth Physics Engine (Induced-Only Scoring):**
 - When using only the induced VPW model for scoring (Oracle+Induced), it recovered a substantial portion of the MuJoCo physics engine's ceiling performance:
 - **Two-room:** 96% success, close to the 100% MuJoCo ceiling (ratio 0.96).
 - **Cube:** 86% success, close to the 94% MuJoCo ceiling (ratio 0.91).
 - **Reacher:** 72% success against a 100% MuJoCo ceiling (ratio 0.72). The paper notes that planner granularity (frame-level vs. macro-MPC) significantly affects Reacher's success, with frame-level CEM being more effective.
 - **PushT:** 22% success against a 96% MuJoCo ceiling (ratio 0.23), indicating a larger gap for contact-rich scenarios. This suggests that while VPW can recover contact-driven motion, small ranking errors during sparse, load-bearing contact events can still lead to plan failures when relying solely on the induced model.

3. **Impact of Hybrid Scoring for Contact-Rich Gaps:**
 - To address the remaining performance gap, particularly in contact-rich tasks, VPW explored "hybrid scoring." This approach uses the induced model as the primary search engine for all CEM candidates but re-scores a top 30% shortlist using the ground-truth physics engine.
 - Hybrid scoring significantly improved performance where induced-only scoring struggled:
 - **PushT:** Success rose dramatically from 22% (induced-only) to 88% (Tool+Hybrid) and 96% (Oracle+Hybrid), effectively closing most of the gap to the MuJoCo ceiling (96%).
 - **Two-room:** Hybrid scoring reached 100% success, matching MuJoCo.
 - For Cube and Reacher, where induced-only scoring was already high, hybrid scoring provided minor or no further gains.
 - This demonstrates that hybrid scoring offers a practical trade-off, achieving near ground-truth engine performance with a substantial reduction in physics queries (70% reduction in physics queries per CEM iteration for some environments, as shown in Table 4).

4. **Ablation of Structure Selection:**
 - An ablation study on PushT (Table 6) confirmed the importance of structure selection. While free-form PatchWorld achieved 0% planning success, and a linear template was misspecified, VPW's Level 1 (sketch selection) + Level 2 (parameter fitting) process achieved 22% planning success. This empirically supports the claim that choosing the correct qualitative dynamics is crucial, even before parameter fitting.

5. **Perception Analysis:**
 - The "tool path" for visual abstraction proved effective, recovering oracle geometry with sub-pixel to few-pixel error for PushT, Two-room, and Cube, enabling successful replanning (Table 8).
 - The VLM path, while recovering semantic structure, yielded uncalibrated metric coordinates (e.g., 236 px RMSE on PushT), making it unsuitable for direct metric planning.

In summary, VPW demonstrates that a two-level induction process focusing on qualitative dynamics selection and multi-step rollout fitting yields executable code world models that are significantly more effective for planning than prior code baselines. While induced models approach ground-truth performance on many tasks, a hybrid approach combining induced search with selective engine verification effectively closes remaining gaps, particularly in complex contact scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VisualPatchWorld (VPW) research offers several significant contributions and has potential impacts on the development of intelligent agents and robot control:

1. **Bridging the Gap between World Model Paradigms:** VPW successfully demonstrates a practical approach to building executable world models that combine the inspectability and editability of hand-built physics engines with the data-driven learning capabilities typically associated with neural networks. This addresses a critical trade-off in the field, offering a pathway to scalable, transparent, and interpretable dynamics models.

2. **Importance of Qualitative Dynamics Selection:** The work highlights the critical role of choosing the correct *qualitative form* of dynamics (dynamical sketch) before fitting parameters. By introducing a two-level induction process—first actively probing to select a sketch, then fitting its parameters for multi-step rollout prediction—VPW achieves substantial improvements over prior code-based methods that often default to open-ended code generation or purely local fitting. This suggests that incorporating structural prior knowledge, even in a data-driven learning context, is key to developing robust and generalizable world models for planning.

3. **Improved Planning Performance and Reliability:** VPW's induced code models achieve significantly higher planning success rates compared to existing programmatic baselines, especially in domains like arm reaching and object manipulation where the qualitative dynamics are complex. The ability to use these models within a receding-horizon MPC framework demonstrates their practical utility for goal-directed control. This makes code world models a more viable option for real-world applications where predictable and reliable planning is paramount.

4. **Hybrid Planning for Robustness in Challenging Scenarios:** The introduction of "hybrid scoring," where the induced model guides the search and the ground-truth engine selectively verifies promising plans, provides a robust solution for contact-rich tasks where induced-only scoring still struggles. This pragmatic division of labor allows agents to benefit from the efficiency of learned models while leveraging the accuracy of high-fidelity simulators when necessary, effectively closing performance gaps with minimal additional computational cost relative to full simulator-based planning.

5. **Foundation for Inspectable and Debuggable AI Systems:** Because VPW generates executable code, the learned dynamics are inspectable in source form. When a prediction fails, a human or automated system can examine and potentially repair the underlying transition law, rather than retraining an opaque neural embedding. This transparency is crucial for developing trustworthy and debuggable AI systems, particularly in safety-critical applications like robotics.

6. **Future Directions for End-to-End Visual Learning and Real-World Deployment:** The work lays a foundation for future research to push towards more end-to-end visual induction, where scene graphs are learned directly from pixels rather than relying on engineered perception modules. Expanding the library of dynamical sketches and testing these models in real-robot settings are natural next steps. The success of VPW indicates that code world models could play a significant role in enabling robots to learn, understand, and adapt to novel environments more effectively and safely.
