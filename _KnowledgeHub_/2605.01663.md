# Towards Efficient and Expressive Offline RL via Flow-Anchored Noise-conditioned Q-Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The University of Texas at Austin and independent researchers developed Flow-Anchored Noise-conditioned Q-Learning (FAN), an offline reinforcement learning algorithm that leverages expressive flow policies and distributional critics while significantly improving computational efficiency. This method achieved competitive or superior task performance across D4RL and OGBench benchmarks, exhibiting 5-14 times faster training runtime and competitive inference speed compared to previous distributional approaches.

## Problem

- Preventing value overestimation for out-of-distribution actions is a fundamental challenge in offline reinforcement learning.
- Existing expressive offline RL methods, such as iterative flow policies and multi-sample distributional critics, introduce significant computational overhead.
- Flow policies require multiple iterative sampling steps for action generation, and distributional critics often compute over numerous samples (e.g., quantiles) for value estimation.

## Method

- Introduced ==Flow-Anchored Noise-conditioned Q-Learning== (**FAN**), a behavior-regularized actor-critic algorithm designed for high performance and computational efficiency.
- Developed a ==noise-conditioned critic==, using a single Gaussian noise sample and a novel Tnπ operator, for efficient distributional value estimation.
- Proposed "==Flow Anchoring==" for efficient behavior regularization, eliminating the need for iterative ODE solutions during action sampling of the flow policy.

## Results

- FAN achieved performance within **95%** of the best methods in 7 out of 9 D4RL and OGBench task environments, outperforming many non-distributional and competing favorably with other distributional approaches.
- The algorithm demonstrated 5 to 14 times faster training runtime and competitive inference speed compared to prior distributional offline RL algorithms (CODAC, IQN, Value Flows).
- Ablation studies confirmed the independent benefits of both Flow Anchoring and the Tnπ operator, with optimal performance achieved using a single noise sample and an expectile coefficient of κ=0.9κ=0.9.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The proposed Tnπ operator for the noise-conditioned critic is a γ-contraction, ensuring convergence to a unique fixed point, and its essential supremum can be efficiently estimated using a single-sample upper expectile.
> - Flow Anchoring efficiently regularizes the policy's action distribution to the behavior flow, formally validated by minimizing an upper bound on the Wasserstein-2 distance without iterative action sampling.
> - Maximizing both the noise-conditioned Q-value and its upper expectile during policy training leads to superior average performance, indicating the benefit of capturing robust distributional information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) focuses on training policies from fixed, pre-collected datasets without further interaction with the environment. A central challenge in offline RL is preventing value overestimation for out-of-distribution (OOD) actions, as the lack of online feedback hinders error correction. To address this, current research trends leverage expressive models for both policy and value learning.

Two prominent directions for achieving expressivity include:
1. **Flow Policies and Diffusion Models:** These generative models, such as flow matching, are increasingly used for policy training. Unlike traditional Gaussian-based policies that are limited to unimodal distributions, flow policies can model complex and multimodal dataset behaviors. This allows for more expressive behavioral constraints, potentially enabling policies to surpass the performance of the offline dataset itself.
2. **Distributional Critics:** These methods aim to learn the entire distribution of future returns rather than just the expected return. Distributional critics can capture information like return uncertainty, which is not fully represented by expected values. This expressivity is often achieved by modeling multiple statistics of the return distribution, frequently through quantiles.

While these expressive approaches have demonstrated performance improvements in offline RL, they generally incur high computational costs. Flow policies typically require iterative sampling (multiple forward passes of the neural network) to generate a single action. Distributional critics often necessitate processing multiple samples or quantiles, leading to linear scaling of computational cost with the number of samples. This paper positions itself within this landscape by seeking to maintain the performance benefits of expressive flow policies and distributional critics while simultaneously addressing their inherent computational inefficiencies. It aims to develop an algorithm that is both high-performing and computationally efficient, challenging the notion that expressivity must come at a high computational expense.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an offline reinforcement learning algorithm that achieves state-of-the-art performance while significantly improving computational efficiency during both training and inference. The authors identify a trade-off between the expressivity of recent offline RL methods and their computational overhead.

The motivation for this work stems from the observed inefficiencies in current high-performing offline RL algorithms that utilize expressive models:
1. **High Computational Cost of Flow Policies:** Flow policies, which can model complex action distributions, typically rely on solving ordinary differential equations (ODEs) through iterative sampling. This process involves multiple forward passes of the policy network to produce a single action, substantially increasing computational demands during both training (for behavior regularization or action generation) and inference.
2. **High Computational Cost of Distributional Critics:** Distributional RL methods, which capture the full distribution of returns, often require computing over multiple samples (e.g., quantiles, expectiles) to estimate value distributions. This multi-sample processing scales linearly with the number of samples, leading to increased computational burden compared to non-distributional Q-learning that only estimates an expected value.

To address these specific inefficiencies while maintaining the performance benefits of expressive models, the authors pose the central research question: "How can we leverage flow policies and distributional critics to achieve state-of-the-art offline RL performance, while simultaneously improving computational efficiency?" This leads to two specific inquiries:
- Can behavior flow policies remain effective with only a single flow iteration?
- Can distributional critics be trained using a single sample?

The paper's proposed method, Flow-Anchored Noise-conditioned Q-Learning (FAN), is designed to directly answer these questions by introducing mechanisms that reduce computational complexity without compromising the expressive power derived from flow policies and distributional value functions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed algorithm, Flow-Anchored Noise-conditioned Q-Learning (FAN), is a behavior-regularized actor-critic method designed for efficiency and expressivity. It introduces two core components: a novel noise-conditioned distributional critic defined by the operator $T_{\pi}^{n}$, and a behavior regularization technique called Flow Anchoring.

#### 3.1. Value Networks and Noise-conditioned Critic

FAN employs two value function approximators: $Q_{\phi}(s, a, \epsilon)$ to model the noise-conditioned Q-value $Q_{\pi}(s, a, \epsilon)$, and $Z_{\psi}(s, a)$ to estimate the upper expectile of $Q_{\phi}(s, a, \epsilon)$.
- **The Operator $T_{\pi}^{n}$:** The core innovation for the distributional critic is the operator $T_{\pi}^{n}$, defined as:
 $T_{\pi}^{n} Q(s, a, \epsilon') \stackrel{d}{=} r + \gamma \text{ ess sup}_{\epsilon \sim N(0,I_d)} Q(s', \pi(s', \epsilon'), \epsilon)$.
 This operator aims to capture distributional return information efficiently. Instead of relying on multiple samples or quantiles, it uses a single Gaussian noise sample $\epsilon'$ to condition the critic and then employs the "essential supremum" (ess sup) of the next-state value distribution. The ess sup operation aggregates the entire next-step return distribution into a single deterministic scalar, thereby removing the critic-induced bootstrap noise and reducing target variance.
- **Noise-conditioned Critic Update (Equation 10):** The critic $Q_{\phi}$ is trained using a Temporal Difference (TD) learning objective:
 $L_Q(\phi) = E[(Q_{\phi}(s, a, \epsilon') - (r + \gamma q_{\pi_{\omega},v_{\theta}}^{\psi}(s', \epsilon')))^2]$.
 The target $q_{\pi_{\omega},v_{\theta}}^{\psi}(s', \epsilon')$ incorporates the upper expectile $Z_{\psi}$ and a critic behavior regularization term (explained below). The critical aspect is that this update only requires a *single* Gaussian noise sample $\epsilon'$ for the current state-action-noise triplet and for the next state, addressing the multi-sample inefficiency of prior distributional methods.
- **Upper Expectile Regression (Equation 11):** $Z_{\psi}(s, a)$ is trained to approximate the essential supremum, using the expectile loss with a high $\kappa$ value:
 $L_Z(\psi) = E[L_{\kappa}^2(Q_{\hat{\phi}}(s, a, \epsilon) - Z_{\psi}(s, a))]$.
 By setting $\kappa = 0.9$ (empirically determined) and using an expectile regression, $Z_{\psi}$ directly models the upper part of the return distribution, which converges to the essential supremum as $\kappa \to 1$. This avoids explicit Monte Carlo estimation of the essential supremum.
- **Value Maximization (Equation 12):** The one-step policy $\pi_{\omega}$ is trained to maximize the estimated future returns by minimizing:
 $L_P(\omega) = E[-Q_{\phi}(s, a_{\omega}, \epsilon') - Z_{\psi}(s, a_{\omega})]$.
 This objective guides the actor to seek actions that lead to high returns according to both the noise-conditioned critic $Q_{\phi}$ and its upper expectile $Z_{\psi}$.

#### 3.2. Policy Networks and Flow Anchoring

FAN uses two policy networks: $\pi_{\omega}(s, \epsilon)$ for the one-step policy and $v_{\theta}(s, t, a_t)$ for the behavior flow policy.
- **Behavior Flow Policy (Equation 13):** The behavior flow policy $v_{\theta}$ is trained to clone the dataset behavior using a Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) loss:
 $L_F(\theta) = E[\|v_{\theta}(s, t, a_t) - (a - \epsilon)\|_2^2]$.
 This network models the vector field that transforms a Gaussian distribution to the state-conditional action distribution of the offline dataset. Importantly, this training *does not* require iterative sampling for the behavior policy itself, only for modeling the velocity field.
- **Actor Flow Anchoring (Equation 14):** Instead of using iterative flow sampling to generate target actions for behavior regularization (as in prior flow-based methods), FAN proposes "Flow Anchoring." This technique directly regularizes the one-step policy $\pi_{\omega}$ towards the *velocity field* of the behavior flow policy $v_{\theta}$ using a single iteration:
 $L_B(\omega) = E[\|(\pi_{\omega}(s, \epsilon) - \epsilon) - v_{\theta}(s, t, a_{t,\omega})\|_2^2]$.
 Here, $a_{t,\omega} = (1-t)\epsilon + t\pi_{\omega}(s, \epsilon)$. This formulation avoids the computational bottleneck of solving ODEs or rejection sampling during policy regularization, significantly improving efficiency.
- **Critic Flow Anchoring (Equation 15):** The behavior regularization is also incorporated into the critic target, where a penalty term is subtracted from $Z_{\psi}$:
 $q_{\pi_{\omega},v_{\theta}}^{\psi}(s', \epsilon'):= Z_{\psi}(s', \pi_{\omega}(s', \epsilon')) - \alpha_2 E_{t \sim \text{Unif}}[\|(\pi_{\omega}(s', \epsilon') - \epsilon') - v_{\theta}(s', t, a'_{t,\omega})\|_2^2]$.
 This term, scaled by $\alpha_2$, penalizes actions from $\pi_{\omega}$ that deviate from the behavior flow's velocity field, thereby enforcing conservatism within the data distribution.

#### 3.3. Theoretical Guarantees

The paper provides theoretical foundations for FAN's components:
- **Convergence of $T_{\pi}^{n}$ (Theorem 4.1):** It is proven that $T_{\pi}^{n}$ is a $\gamma$-contraction in the supremum metric, guaranteeing convergence to a unique fixed point $Q_{\pi}$. This implies that the noise-conditioned critic learns a stable value function.
- **Upper Expectile and Essential Supremum (Theorem 4.2):** The authors demonstrate that the upper expectile $Z_{\kappa}$ (trained with $\kappa \approx 1$) converges to the essential supremum of the return distribution ($Q(s, a, \epsilon)$). This justifies using expectile regression to approximate the maximum possible distributional returns.
- **Validity of Flow Anchoring (Theorem 4.3):** Flow Anchoring is shown to be a valid behavior regularization technique. Minimizing $L_B(\omega)$ directly minimizes an upper bound on the Wasserstein-2 distance between the distributions induced by the training policy $\pi_{\omega}$ and the behavior flow policy $v_{\theta}$, provided $v_{\theta}$ satisfies Lipschitz continuity.

In summary, FAN integrates efficient noise-conditioned distributional critics with a novel, non-iterative flow-based behavior regularization, aiming for both high performance and computational efficiency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of FAN focuses on demonstrating its superior performance on offline RL benchmarks while concurrently achieving high computational efficiency.

#### 4.1. Offline RL Task Performance

FAN was evaluated on standard D4RL benchmarks (4 Antmaze and 12 Adroit tasks) and 29 OGBench tasks (25 state-based, 4 pixel-based). The performance was compared against non-distributional baselines (ReBRAC, IDQL, FQL) and distributional baselines (IQN, CODAC, Value Flows).
- **Overall Performance:** FAN achieved state-of-the-art performance (defined as being within 95% of the best final performance) in 7 out of 9 task environments, including D4RL Antmaze, and multiple OGBench categories (Antsoccer, Puzzle-3x3, Puzzle-4x4, Scene, Visual Locomotion, Visual Manipulation).
- **OGBench Tasks:** FAN consistently outperformed non-distributional approaches, particularly in tasks involving complex manipulation (e.g., OGBench puzzle and cube tasks). It also surpassed most distributional approaches on average. For instance, FAN achieved 100% success rate on Puzzle-3x3-play tasks, significantly higher than other methods.
- **D4RL Tasks:** While strong on Antmaze, FAN's average performance on Adroit tasks was competitive with other top-performing methods but not consistently superior across all D4RL environments, indicating task-specific strengths.

#### 4.2. Computational Efficiency

Computational efficiency was measured using Floating Point Operations (FLOPs) and wall-clock runtime for both a single training update and a single action-sampling call on an NVIDIA RTX 6000 GPU.
- **Training Runtime:** FAN demonstrated substantial efficiency gains during training, achieving approximately 5–14x faster runtime compared to prior distributional approaches (IQN, CODAC, Value Flows). These baselines incurred higher costs due to multiple samples for distributional value functions (IQN, CODAC) or intensive flow integration (Value Flows).
- **Inference Speed:** FAN exhibited the highest computational efficiency among all baseline methods (both non-distributional and distributional) in terms of both FLOPs and wall-clock runtime for action sampling. This indicates that its single-iteration Flow Anchoring and single-sample noise-conditioned critic significantly reduce the overhead typically associated with expressive models.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Flow Anchoring:** Comparing FAN (with Flow Anchoring), NBRAC (ReBRAC-style BC with noise critic), and NFQL (FQL-style BC with noise critic) showed that Flow Anchoring resulted in better or competitive performance across 4 out of 5 OGBench default tasks. This suggests Flow Anchoring is an effective and efficient behavior regularization strategy for the noise-conditioned critic.
- **Effect of $T_{\pi}^{n}$ Operator:** Comparing FAN (with $T_{\pi}^{n}$ and Flow Anchoring) with FAQL (standard non-distributional Bellman operator with Flow Anchoring) revealed that $T_{\pi}^{n}$ led to better or competitive performance on 4 out of 5 OGBench default tasks. This indicates the benefits of the noise-conditioned distributional critic.
- **Offline-to-Online Transfer:** When evaluated in an offline-to-online setting (initial offline training followed by online fine-tuning), FAN achieved state-of-the-art performance on 4 out of 5 OGBench default tasks, demonstrating strong adaptation capabilities when constraints are relaxed.
- **Sensitivity to $\kappa$:** Experimenting with $\kappa \in \{0.5, 0.7, 0.9, 0.99\}$ for expectile regression showed that $\kappa=0.9$ empirically yielded the best average performance. Values too close to 1 (e.g., 0.99) led to performance degradation.
- **Maximizing Both $Z_{\psi}$ and $Q_{\phi}$:** Training the policy by maximizing both the upper expectile $Z_{\psi}$ and the noise-conditioned critic $Q_{\phi}$ resulted in superior performance compared to maximizing either component individually, validating the design choice in the policy objective.
- **Multiple Noise Samples for $Q_{\phi}$ Training:** Increasing the number of noise samples used for training $Q_{\phi}$ did not yield significant performance improvements, while training runtime increased. This supports the decision to use a single noise sample for training $Q_{\phi}$, further contributing to efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper, Flow-Anchored Noise-conditioned Q-Learning (FAN), offers a significant contribution to the field of offline reinforcement learning by addressing the critical trade-off between algorithmic expressivity and computational efficiency.

### 6. Significance

- **Concurrent High Performance and Efficiency:** FAN successfully demonstrates that state-of-the-art performance in offline RL can be achieved without incurring the high computational costs typically associated with expressive models like flow policies and distributional critics. This challenges the prevailing paradigm that performance gains necessitate proportional increases in computational resources.
- **Novel Efficiency Mechanisms:** The introduction of "Flow Anchoring" and the "Noise-conditioned Critic" with its $T_{\pi}^{n}$ operator provides concrete, theoretically grounded methods for improving efficiency. Flow Anchoring streamlines behavior regularization by avoiding iterative flow sampling, while the $T_{\pi}^{n}$ operator and expectile regression enable distributional value learning with single-sample efficiency by focusing on the essential supremum.
- **Theoretical Grounding:** The theoretical analysis demonstrating the convergence of $T_{\pi}^{n}$ and the approximation of the essential supremum by upper expectiles provides strong justification for FAN's design choices, enhancing the reliability and understanding of the proposed methods.
- **Broad Applicability:** FAN's strong empirical performance across diverse D4RL and OGBench tasks (including complex manipulation, locomotion, and pixel-based environments) indicates its robustness and broad applicability in various offline RL scenarios. Its demonstrated capability in offline-to-online transfer further expands its potential utility.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Energy-Efficient AI:** By significantly reducing FLOPs and runtime for both training and inference (e.g., 5-14x faster training than prior distributional approaches), FAN contributes to the development of more energy-efficient AI. This is particularly relevant as the carbon footprint of AI models becomes a growing concern.
- **Deployment on Resource-Constrained Hardware:** The improved computational efficiency makes it more feasible to deploy capable policies on resource-constrained robotic hardware or edge devices where real-time inference speed is critical and computational budgets are limited. This could accelerate the adoption of RL in practical applications.
- **Advancing Offline RL Research:** FAN opens new avenues for research into efficient yet expressive model designs. The concepts of Flow Anchoring could be extended to online RL settings, and the $T_{\pi}^{n}$ operator could be further explored in model-based RL, risk-sensitive tasks, or goal-conditioned settings, potentially leading to more advanced and versatile RL agents.
- **Reduced Training Time and Cost:** Faster training times can lead to quicker iteration cycles for researchers and developers, reducing the overall cost of developing and fine-tuning RL agents.

The authors acknowledge that widespread deployment of autonomous agents carries inherent societal implications, such as safety and economic impacts. However, they emphasize that FAN's contribution is primarily algorithmic efficiency rather than enabling disruptive capabilities, thus not foreseeing negative consequences unique to this method beyond standard safety considerations inherent to reinforcement learning.
