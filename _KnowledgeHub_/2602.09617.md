# AnyTouch 2: General Optical Tactile Representation Learning For Dynamic Tactile Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> AnyTouch 2 presents a framework for general optical tactile representation learning and introduces ToucHD, a large-scale hierarchical dynamic tactile dataset, to enhance robots' understanding of fine-grained, action-specific, and force-aware dynamic interactions. The approach demonstrates superior performance across diverse dynamic tactile benchmarks and challenging real-world manipulation tasks, including delicate chip moving.

## Problem

- Real-world robotic manipulation requires sophisticated dynamic tactile perception, encompassing temporal feedback, subtle deformations, and underlying force dynamics.
- Existing tactile datasets primarily focus on static object properties or limited, shallow dynamics, lacking rich temporal variation and explicit physical grounding like force measurements.
- Current tactile representation models often struggle to capture the fine-grained deformations and intricate, force-aware dynamics unique to tactile interactions, and lack generalizability across diverse sensor types.

## Method

- A systematic 'Tactile Dynamic Pyramid' is proposed, categorizing tactile data and perception capabilities into a five-tier hierarchy based on increasing interaction complexity.
- ToucHD, a large-scale hierarchical dynamic tactile dataset, is introduced, comprising over 2.4 million contact samples across five optical tactile sensors, specifically enriching higher-tier dynamic interactions (atomic actions, real-world manipulation, touch-force pairs).
- AnyTouch 2, a general tactile representation learning framework, unifies object-level understanding with multi-level dynamic perception through pixel-level frame-difference reconstruction, semantic-level action matching, and physical-level force and delta-force prediction.

## Results

- AnyTouch 2 consistently outperformed prior static and dynamic tactile models on offline benchmarks, showing up to a 1.2x improvement in force prediction and significant gains in pose estimation and slip detection.
- The framework achieved superior performance across challenging real-world manipulation tasks, including a 15-20% higher success rate in delicate Tier 1 (Chip Moving) and Tier 2 (USB Insertion) tasks compared to baselines.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of frame-difference reconstruction, action matching, and force prediction modules, validating the design choices and the distinct value of each ToucHD dataset component.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The hierarchical Tactile Dynamic Pyramid provides a principled framework for organizing tactile data and guiding the development of increasingly complex dynamic perception capabilities.
> - Integrating pixel-level frame-difference reconstruction, semantic-level action matching, and physical-level force prediction within a unified model is crucial for comprehensive, multi-level dynamic tactile representation learning.
> - The ToucHD dataset, specifically designed with high-tier dynamic interactions, is essential for training models that achieve robust and physically-grounded dynamic tactile perception across various manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Tactile perception is a crucial component for intelligent robots to interact effectively with the physical world, especially in contact-rich manipulation tasks. It provides rich, localized contact information that complements visual and auditory cues, enabling robots to understand subtle surface deformations, material properties, and force dynamics. The rapid development of high-resolution optical tactile sensors has opened new possibilities for robots to acquire such detailed information.

However, the broader research landscape in tactile perception faces significant challenges that "AnyTouch 2" directly addresses. Historically, tactile datasets and models have been limited in their scope and complexity. Early datasets primarily focused on static, object-level attributes like material and hardness, often collected via simple "press-only" actions (e.g., Touch and Go, VisGel). While some later datasets introduced "random actions" like sliding or rotation, they still offered limited dynamic variation, primarily supporting shallow surface-level dynamics. These lower-tier datasets often lack the rich temporal dynamics and explicit physical grounding (like force measurements) necessary for complex, real-world manipulation tasks.

Furthermore, existing tactile representation learning models, frequently adapted from image-based self-supervised learning (e.g., MAE, VJEPA from vision communities) or multi-modal alignment frameworks (e.g., CLIP-style models), often struggle to capture the fine-grained deformations and intricate, force-aware dynamics unique to tactile interactions. While some efforts have explored dynamic tactile representation by leveraging video learning techniques or incorporating auxiliary force prediction tasks, a systematic framework to guide both data collection and model design for hierarchical dynamic perception capabilities has been missing. Sensor heterogeneity also remains a challenge, with models often struggling to generalize across diverse tactile sensor types.

"AnyTouch 2" positions itself as a significant advancement by introducing a principled, hierarchical framework—the "tactile dynamic pyramid"—that systematically categorizes and guides the development of dynamic tactile perception capabilities. It directly tackles the data scarcity in higher-tier dynamic interactions by releasing ToucHD, a large-scale, multi-modal dataset. Concurrently, it proposes a novel representation learning framework, AnyTouch 2, which unifies existing strengths (like multi-modal alignment and cross-sensor matching) with new modules tailored to capture fine-grained, action-specific, and force-aware dynamics. This work aims to bridge the gap between the rich data potential of optical tactile sensors and the current limitations in systematic data collection and comprehensive model understanding, thereby pushing the field towards more dexterous and physically-grounded robotic intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind "AnyTouch 2" stems from the understanding that real-world, contact-rich robotic manipulation demands sophisticated tactile perception capabilities. Robots need to continuously perceive temporal tactile feedback, capture subtle surface deformations, and accurately reason about object properties as well as underlying force dynamics to execute complex tasks. While modern optical tactile sensors are inherently capable of providing such rich information, the authors identify a critical gap: existing tactile datasets and models have largely overlooked these fine-grained temporal dynamics and their associated physical principles. Current resources predominantly focus on static, object-level attributes, lacking the hierarchical structure and dynamic richness required for advanced robotic skills.

To address this deficiency, the authors set forth several key objectives:

1. **Systematize Dynamic Tactile Perception:** A core objective is to establish a systematic paradigm for understanding and advancing dynamic tactile perception. This involves proposing a novel "tactile dynamic pyramid" that organizes tactile data and perception capabilities into a hierarchical structure, guiding both data collection and model design.
2. **Overcome Data Scarcity in Dynamic Interactions:** A crucial objective is to address the severe lack of large-scale tactile datasets rich in dynamic information, especially for higher-tier interaction complexities. This leads to the development and release of **ToucHD**, a large-scale hierarchical tactile dataset. ToucHD is designed to span tactile atomic actions, real-world manipulations, and touch-force paired data, thereby establishing a comprehensive ecosystem that explicitly supports hierarchical perception capabilities from a data perspective.
3. **Develop a General Tactile Representation Learning Framework:** The authors aim to create a general and unified tactile representation learning framework, **AnyTouch 2**, capable of processing data from diverse optical tactile sensors. This framework is intended to unify object-level understanding with progressively enhanced perception of fine-grained deformations, action-specific dynamics, and force-related physical properties.
4. **Model Multi-Level Dynamic Perception:** A specific objective for the AnyTouch 2 model is to inherently learn multi-level dynamic perception capabilities. This involves designing modules that capture pixel-level and action-specific deformations across frames, while explicitly modeling physical force dynamics, thus enabling a comprehensive understanding of dynamic interactions from a model perspective.

In essence, the motivation is to elevate robotic tactile intelligence beyond static property recognition to robust, dynamic, and physically-grounded interaction, thereby enabling robots to perform complex, dexterous manipulation tasks with human-like proficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of "AnyTouch 2" is structured around two synergistic components: a hierarchical data organization paradigm called the **Tactile Dynamic Pyramid** and a multi-level representation learning framework, **AnyTouch 2**, built upon a newly introduced dataset, **ToucHD**.

#### 3.1. Tactile Dynamic Pyramid

The authors first propose a systematic five-tier hierarchy for organizing tactile data and guiding perception capabilities (Fig. 1, Appendix A). This pyramid is based on increasing complexity of dynamic interactions, data rarity, and the difficulty of annotation:
- **Tier 5 (Basic Properties / Press Only):** Supports object-level attributes (e.g., material, hardness) with minimal temporal variation.
- **Tier 4 (Basic Dynamic Contacts / Random Action):** Introduces limited temporal changes from random sliding/rotation, for surface-related dynamics but lacking task relevance.
- **Tier 3 (Action-Specific Dynamics / Specific Action):** Captures structured dynamics associated with atomic interactions, facilitating action-level tactile understanding (e.g., controlled slides, rotations).
- **Tier 2 (Manipulation Dynamics / Manipulation Data):** Reflects task-driven, temporally evolving contact patterns crucial for learning real-world dexterous skills.
- **Tier 1 (Physical Dynamics / Force Data):** Explicitly grounds tactile dynamics in physical force principles, enabling reasoning about force-deformation relationships and supporting precise, force-sensitive manipulation.

Most existing datasets fall into Tiers 4 and 5, leaving significant gaps in higher-tier data.

#### 3.2. ToucHD: Tactile Hierarchical Dynamic Dataset

To address the data scarcity in the higher tiers, the authors introduce **ToucHD**, a large-scale hierarchical dynamic tactile dataset comprising 2,426,174 contact samples across five optical tactile sensors. ToucHD is specifically designed to enrich Tiers 1, 2, and 3:

- **Simulated Atomic Action Data (Sim) - Tier 3:**
 - **Content:** 1,118,896 multi-sensor contact frames from five optical tactile sensors (GelSight, DIGIT, GelSight Mini, GelSlim, DuraGel).
 - **Actions:** Performs eight atomic actions (sliding in four directions, rotating clockwise/counter-clockwise, pressing, leaving) on 1,043 objects from ObjectFolder and OmniObject3D.
 - **Method:** Uses an IMPM-based simulator for elastomer-object contact simulation and Blender-based rendering for sensor images, enabling diverse and precisely controlled dynamic interactions without manual annotation.
- **Real-World Manipulation Data (Mani) - Tier 2:**
 - **Content:** 584,842 contact frames from three optical tactile sensors (GelSight Mini, DIGIT, DM-Tac W).
 - **Tasks:** Collected from 46 carefully designed real-world manipulation tasks using a modified FastUMI robotic arm system with paired tactile sensors, capturing task-driven, temporally evolving contact patterns.
 - **Purpose:** Supports pre-training models to capture fine-grained dynamic tactile variations during real manipulation.
- **Touch-Force Paired Data (Force) - Tier 1:**
 - **Content:** 722,436 touch-force pairs using five optical tactile sensors (GelSight Mini, DIGIT, DuraGel, DM-Tac W, GelStereo BioTip).
 - **Method:** Sensors mounted on a fixed base, 71 distinct indenters sequentially attached to a robotic arm's end-effector, performing sliding motions in four directions while a wrist-mounted force sensor records 3D contact force sequences.
 - **Purpose:** Provides explicit supervision for models to perceive fine-grained contact forces (normal and shear) and serves as an evaluation benchmark for physical understanding.

#### 3.3. AnyTouch 2: General Tactile Representation Learning Framework

Building on ToucHD, AnyTouch 2 is proposed as a general tactile representation learning framework that unifies object-level understanding with multi-level dynamic perception, aligned with the hierarchical capabilities of the pyramid.

- **Pixel-Level Dynamic Details (Foundation):**
 - **Approach:** Utilizes a video masked autoencoder (MAE) to learn deformation patterns from sequences of tactile frames. To enhance focus on deformations, background frames are subtracted.
 - **Frame-Difference Reconstruction:** A unique addition where, besides reconstructing original masked frames (L_ori_rec), the model also reconstructs frame differences (D_n = T_n - T_1) (L_dif_rec). This explicitly strengthens sensitivity to subtle temporal variations. The total pixel-level loss is L_Pixel = L_ori_rec + L_dif_rec.

- **Semantic-Level Tactile Features:**
 - **Multi-Modal Alignment:** Aligns tactile features with paired visual and textual features (L_Align), following a CLIP-like paradigm, to embed tactile data into a shared semantic space covering object identity, material, and interaction descriptions.
 - **Cross-Sensor Matching:** Aligns tactile signals from different sensors contacting the same object, promoting sensor-invariant object-level feature learning (L_obj) using a binary cross-entropy loss for positive/negative pairs.
 - **Action Matching:** Introduced to capture the semantics of structured dynamic tactile interactions. The model learns to cluster representations of the same atomic action (from ToucHD (Sim) and (Force)) while separating different ones (L_act). This embeds semantic-level action information.
 - The total matching loss is L_Match = L_obj + L_act.

- **Physical-Level Dynamic Properties:**
 - **Force Prediction:** Directly predicts the 3D contact force (F) for each frame in a tactile video using ToucHD (Force) data, associating dynamic deformations with physical magnitudes.
 - **Delta-Force Prediction:** Predicts force increments (ΔF = F_n - F_{n-1}) to emphasize temporal variations of contact forces.
 - These are jointly trained with an L1 Loss (L_Force), grounding representations in underlying physics.

#### 3.4. Training Recipe

A curriculum task scheduling strategy is adopted to optimize these multi-level objectives, mitigating task interference. Pixel-level reconstruction starts first with the highest weight. Higher-level tasks (semantic and physical) are introduced later with gradually increasing weights, ensuring foundational low-level patterns are learned before complex capabilities.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of AnyTouch 2 spans both offline benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness as a general dynamic tactile perception model.

#### 4.1. Offline Benchmark Evaluation (Table 1)

- **Object-Level Understanding:** AnyTouch 2 achieves performance comparable to AnyTouch 1 on static object property benchmarks (TAG Material and Cloth Textile Classification), indicating it successfully preserves robust object-level semantic understanding despite its focus on dynamics.
- **Dynamic Physical Perception:** Crucially, AnyTouch 2 consistently and significantly outperforms all prior approaches (including static and other dynamic models like MAE (Sparsh) and VJEPA (Sparsh)) across all tasks requiring fine-grained dynamics and force-sensitive reasoning. This includes Force Prediction, Pose Estimation, Slip Detection (with an added delta-force prediction component), and the newly introduced ToucHD Bench (on unseen indenters).
- **Importance of Dynamic Models and ToucHD:** The results clearly show that models leveraging multiple consecutive frames (dynamic models) have a distinct advantage in dynamic benchmarks. Single-frame models often perform poorly, highlighting the indispensable role of temporal information. Furthermore, training MAE (Sparsh) with the additional ToucHD dataset (MAE (S)†) yields consistent improvements across all tasks, even without AnyTouch 2's specialized objectives, underscoring the unique value of ToucHD as a high-tier dynamic tactile dataset.

#### 4.2. Online Real-World Manipulation (Figures 3 & 4, Tables 7, 8, 10)

Four challenging real-world tasks were designed to span the tactile dynamic pyramid:
- **Tactile Grasping (Tier 5):** Differentiating objects by static attributes and maintaining stable grasp.
- **Whiteboard Wiping (Tier 4 & 3):** Perceiving action-specific tactile cues and adapting motion.
- **USB Insertion (Tier 2):** Processing continuous tactile deformations for precise alignment.
- **Chip Moving (Tier 1):** High-resolution, force-aware perception for delicate manipulation.

- **Superiority of Dynamic Perception:** Static, single-frame models perform significantly worse on higher-tier manipulation tasks, reaffirming the necessity of dynamic perception for contact-rich interactions.
- **Tiered Performance & ToucHD's Impact:** The performance of dynamic models varies according to the tier of their training data and objectives. Models trained only on lower-tier data (e.g., Tier 4) struggle with higher-tier tasks (Tier 1 & 2). Adding ToucHD data to MAE (S) (MAE (S)†) substantially improves performance across Tier 2 and lower tasks, but still struggles with precise force perception for Tier 1.
- **AnyTouch 2's Dominance:** AnyTouch 2, by integrating the ToucHD dataset with its multi-level dynamic enhanced modules, achieves the strongest Tier-1 dynamic perception capability. It consistently outperforms all baselines across all four real-world tasks, including the most delicate and challenging Tier 1 Chip Moving task. This confirms that ToucHD effectively supports higher-tier dynamic capabilities, and AnyTouch 2 successfully bridges all tiers of the tactile dynamic pyramid.
- **Sensor Insights:** The study notes that GelSight Mini (cleaner imaging) excels at fine details, while DIGIT (higher acquisition frequency) provides denser dynamic information, leading to superior performance on higher-tier manipulation tasks. AnyTouch 2 effectively integrates these diverse sensor strengths.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (Tables 2, 6, 9, 11, 12, 13)

Ablation studies confirm the contribution of each module and ToucHD subsets:
- **Frame-Difference Reconstruction:** Crucial for all dynamic perception tasks; its removal leads to significant performance drops.
- **Action Matching:** Important for slip detection, demonstrating its role in semantic action understanding.
- **Force Prediction (and Shear Force Labels):** Essential for accurate force prediction, particularly for tangential (shear) forces. Removing shear force labels significantly impacts prediction on X and Y axes.
- **Multi-Modal Alignment:** Shows a trade-off. It improves object-level static semantic features but can slightly degrade fine-grained dynamic perception due to coarse-grained text labels.
- **ToucHD Subsets:** Each subset (Sim, Mani, Force) uniquely contributes to specific dynamic tiers. Removing any subset, particularly the full ToucHD or the ToucHD (Force) component, leads to substantial performance degradation across relevant benchmarks.
- **Pyramid-Driven Strategy:** Training without the pyramid-guided data distribution or curriculum task scheduling results in inferior performance, validating the proposed paradigm.
- **Input Frame Count:** Using 4 input frames consistently outperforms 2 frames, highlighting the benefit of denser temporal tactile information.
- **Cross-Sensor Generalization:** AnyTouch 2 demonstrates stronger sensor-invariant capabilities compared to T3, attributing this to multi-sensor data integration, background removal, and explicit alignment modules. It also shows robustness to changes in gel pads.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "AnyTouch 2" paper makes several significant contributions that promise to advance the field of robotic tactile perception and embodied intelligence.

Firstly, the introduction of the **Tactile Dynamic Pyramid** provides a much-needed systematic paradigm for organizing and understanding the complexities of dynamic tactile perception. This hierarchical framework serves as a principled guide for future research, offering clear directions for both data collection and model design. By explicitly defining different tiers of dynamic capabilities, it allows researchers to pinpoint specific gaps and tailor solutions accordingly, moving beyond ad-hoc approaches.

Secondly, the creation and release of **ToucHD** is a monumental achievement. As a large-scale, hierarchical dynamic tactile dataset, it addresses the critical scarcity of high-tier dynamic data that existing resources have overlooked. By integrating simulated atomic actions, real-world manipulations, and extensive touch-force paired data across diverse sensors, ToucHD completes a comprehensive dynamic tactile data ecosystem. This dataset is poised to become a foundational resource for the community, enabling the training and evaluation of more sophisticated tactile models and accelerating research in dynamic tactile perception. Its explicit grounding in the tactile dynamic pyramid ensures that the data directly supports the development of increasingly complex perception capabilities.

Thirdly, the **AnyTouch 2** framework itself represents a substantial step forward in general tactile representation learning. By unifying sensor-invariant object-level understanding with multi-level dynamic perception, it offers a robust and comprehensive model capable of handling a broad spectrum of tactile challenges. The innovative integration of pixel-level frame-difference reconstruction, semantic-level action matching, and physical-level force and delta-force prediction, all guided by the tactile dynamic pyramid, empowers the model to capture fine-grained details, understand interaction semantics, and reason about underlying physics. This unification makes AnyTouch 2 a highly versatile and effective tool for various downstream tasks, from static object property recognition to dexterous, force-sensitive manipulation.

The potential impact of this work is profound for future robotics. By providing robots with enhanced, physically-grounded dynamic tactile perception capabilities, "AnyTouch 2" lays a solid foundation for:
- **More Dexterous Manipulation:** Robots will be able to perform highly precise and adaptive tasks, such as handling fragile objects (demonstrated by the Chip Moving task), inserting components with tight tolerances (USB Insertion), and adapting to unforeseen contact dynamics.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction:** A better understanding of touch dynamics will enable more intuitive and safer physical interactions between humans and robots.
- **Generalizable Robotic Skills:** Learning representations that are robust across different sensors, objects, and dynamic interaction patterns will significantly improve the generalization ability of robotic policies, reducing the need for extensive task-specific data collection.
- **Advancing Embodied AI:** This work contributes directly to the grand challenge of building truly intelligent embodied agents that can perceive and interact with the physical world in a sophisticated, human-like manner.

While the authors acknowledge limitations such as unexplored sensors within ToucHD, current constraints in force data collection, and underutilization of multi-sensor paired manipulation data, these limitations also highlight clear avenues for future research. Extending the framework to non-optical or array-based tactile sensors, developing more naturalistic force data collection methods, and further integrating cross-modal synergies will undoubtedly build upon this significant foundation. Ultimately, "AnyTouch 2" is a landmark contribution that pushes tactile intelligence into a new era of dynamic perception, fostering the development of more capable and intelligent robots.
