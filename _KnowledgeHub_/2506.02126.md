# Knowledge or Reasoning? A Close Look at How LLMs Think Across Domains

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Santa Cruz, Stanford University, and Tongji University developed a fine-grained evaluation framework to dissect Large Language Model reasoning into factual knowledge and logical inference components. Their analysis shows that while Supervised Fine-Tuning improves knowledge, Reinforcement Learning enhances reasoning by refining knowledge application across medical and mathematical domains.

## Problem

- Existing LLM evaluation methods largely focus on final-answer accuracy, obscuring the intricate interplay between factual knowledge and logical inference in their step-by-step reasoning processes.
- Earlier fine-grained reasoning metrics (e.g., ROSCOE) can be unreliable because LLMs often generate reasoning from internal knowledge, not just by rephrasing input, leading to potentially misleading similarity scores.
- A lack of transparent understanding of LLM 'thinking trajectories' makes it difficult to discern whether correct answers stem from robust factual recall, sound logical inference, or a fortuitous combination.

## Method

- Developed a novel, fine-grained ==three-stage evaluation framework== that decomposes LLM responses into explicit, successive logical steps.
- Introduced the =='Information Gain (InfoGain)' metric== to quantify reasoning efficiency by measuring how much each step reduces uncertainty towards the correct solution using step-wise perplexity.
- Established the =='Knowledge Index (KI)' metric== to evaluate the factual correctness of knowledge within each reasoning step by extracting knowledge points and judging their consistency against external ground truth databases.

## Results

- Trained Qwen-Base models consistently outperformed DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B in the medical domain, indicating that pre-training optimized for reasoning (like math/code) does not effectively transfer to knowledge-intensive domains.
- Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) increased average Knowledge Index (KI) by **6.2%** on medical tasks but resulted in a significant average **38.9%** reduction in Information Gain (reasoning efficiency) compared to untrained models.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) consistently enhanced Information Gain across both domains and boosted Knowledge Index (average **12.4 points** gain for Qwen-Base in medicine) primarily by guiding models to discard incorrect or irrelevant knowledge in reasoning paths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Knowledge and reasoning are distinct evaluation dimensions that should be measured independently, as models can have similar overall accuracy but differing strengths in factual correctness (Knowledge Index) and reasoning efficiency (Information Gain).
> - Domain demands vary, with medical tasks being more knowledge-intensive (Knowledge Index strongly correlates with accuracy), while mathematical tasks are more reasoning-intensive.
> - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) primarily boosts factual knowledge but can compromise reasoning clarity, whereas Reinforcement Learning (RL) enhances reasoning quality by guiding models to prune inaccurate or irrelevant knowledge.
