# Make-An-Agent: A Generalizable Policy Network Generator with Behavior-Prompted Diffusion

> [!summary] Summary
> Make-An-Agent generates deployable policy network parameters directly from a behavior trajectory via a conditional latent diffusion model, using a policy autoencoder and contrastive behavior embeddings, reaching 100% success on seen tasks and strong zero-shot transfer to unseen tasks and robots.

## Problem

- Hypernetwork-based weight generators mainly serve as meta-learning initializations needing further gradient-based fine-tuning per task, not direct deployable-policy synthesis.
- Prior multi-task and meta-RL/meta-IL methods require reward supervision or expert demonstrations and explicit adaptation steps to generalize across tasks.
- Existing policy-adaptation methods show limited robustness to environmental randomness and noisy trajectories when adapting from a single demonstration.

## Method

- ==Parameter autoencoder== compresses per-layer flattened policy network weights into fixed-dimension latent representations, with noise augmentation for robust reconstruction across domains.
- ==Contrastive behavior embedding== trains dual projections on trajectory segments and post-success states to encode mutual information between behavior and task success as a compact condition.
- **Conditional latent diffusion model** with 1D convolutional UNet backbone denoises latent parameter representations conditioned on frozen behavior embeddings, decoded into deployable policies without fine-tuning.

## Results

- On seen MetaWorld and Robosuite tasks, generated policies substantially outperform multi-task BC, CARE, hypernetwork meta-RL, and decision-transformer baselines under **5** random initializations.
- On **8** unseen tasks using only **50-100** trajectories without fine-tuning, the method retains superior success rates while baselines degrade sharply under environmental randomness.
- Qualified-policy rate (100% success) averages over **30%** on seen tasks and generated top-5 policies reach near **1.0** success on most unseen tasks with only **20** average trajectories needed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reframing policy learning as conditional generation in parameter space, rather than state-action prediction, can yield more diverse and robust policies than direct behavior cloning.
> - Behavior embeddings need only capture dynamics and goal information as generation conditions, not full state-action mappings, improving robustness to noisy demonstrations.
> - Compressing heterogeneous policy architectures into a shared fixed-size latent space enables cross-task and cross-embodiment generalization without per-task adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traditional approaches to policy learning, such as Reinforcement Learning (RL) and imitation learning, typically involve optimizing an agent's policy based on sampled trajectories or expert demonstrations. These methods often focus on modeling narrow behavior distributions, mapping states to actions to achieve specific tasks. This paper proposes a departure from this paradigm by investigating the inverse problem: generating optimal policy network parameters directly from suboptimal trajectories or desired behavior demonstrations.

The work builds upon the concept of hypernetworks, which train one neural network to generate weights for another. While previous hypernetwork applications primarily served as initialization networks for meta-learning, requiring downstream adaptation or fine-tuning, "Make-An-Agent" distinguishes itself by using behavior demonstrations as direct prompts to synthesize deployable policies without further gradient updates. This approach leverages the observation that behaviors across different tasks may share underlying skills or environmental information, which can be exploited in the parameter space to enhance cross-task generalizability.

Furthermore, the research draws inspiration from the advancements in diffusion models, which have demonstrated proficiency in generating high-dimensional outputs from conditional inputs, notably in text-to-image synthesis. By reframing policy parameter generation as a conditional denoising diffusion process, the authors aim to generate robust and efficient policies by progressively refining noise into structured policy parameters. This positions the work within the intersection of generative models, meta-learning, and robotics, offering an alternative to traditional policy adaptation or multi-task learning methods that typically operate on state-action distributions or require explicit reward signals for optimization. The methodology seeks to uncover implicit relationships between agent behaviors for specific tasks and the corresponding policy parameters, rather than modeling explicit behavior distributions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method capable of generating a control policy for an agent using minimal behavioral input, specifically a single demonstration of desired behaviors. This goal is articulated by drawing an analogy to the ease of generating an image from a textual description, implying a desire for a similarly intuitive and efficient policy generation process.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations and opportunities within current policy learning paradigms:

- **Paradigm Shift from Policy Training to Parameter Prediction:** Traditional policy learning focuses on training a policy to map states to actions based on collected data. The authors propose a shift to *reversely predicting* optimal policy network parameters using existing, potentially suboptimal, trajectories from offline data. This approach aims to circumvent the need for explicitly modeling complex behavior distributions, instead learning the underlying parameter distributions. This could reveal implicit relationships between agent behaviors and policy parameters.

- **Addressing the Challenge of High-Dimensional Output from Low-Dimensional Input:** Generating high-dimensional policy parameters from low-dimensional behavior demonstrations presents a technical challenge. The success of diffusion models in other domains (e.g., text-to-image synthesis) serves as a key inspiration, suggesting that a conditional denoising process could effectively transform noise into structured policy parameters. This is expected to yield policies that are not only high-performing but also robust and efficient.

- **Distinction from Prior Hypernetwork Approaches:** While hypernetworks have been used to generate neural network weights, their application typically involves providing an initialization for meta-learning, which then requires adaptation through gradient updates. The authors explicitly aim for an approach that generates optimal policies directly from agent behaviors as prompts, *without* the necessity of downstream policy fine-tuning or gradient-based adaptation. This is motivated by the hypothesis that agent behaviors across different tasks often share common underlying skills or environmental information, enabling the policy generator to exploit these correlations in the parameter space for enhanced cross-task generalizability.

- **Development of an End-to-End Behavior-to-Policy Generator:** The overarching objective is to create a complete system, "Make-An-Agent," that can directly translate observed behaviors into functional policy networks. This system comprises three key technical contributions:
 1. An autoencoder for compressing high-dimensional policy networks into compact latent representations that can be accurately reconstructed.
 2. A novel behavior embedding mechanism, utilizing contrastive learning, to capture mutual information between long-term trajectories and task success or future states.
 3. A conditional diffusion model, guided by these behavior embeddings, to synthesize latent parameter representations, which are then decoded into deployable policy networks.
 4. The construction of a specific dataset comprising policy network checkpoints and their corresponding deployed trajectories for training the proposed methodology.

In essence, the research seeks to establish a novel, efficient, and generalizable method for policy synthesis that bypasses explicit policy training or extensive adaptation, operating instead by directly generating policy parameters conditioned on observed behaviors.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Make-An-Agent" framework is designed to generate deployable policy networks conditioned on agent behavior demonstrations. The methodology integrates an autoencoder for parameter compression, a contrastive learning approach for behavior embedding, and a conditional diffusion model for parameter generation.

#### 3.1. Parameter Representation via Autoencoder

- **Problem:** Policy networks, typically MLPs, have high-dimensional parameter vectors when flattened, making direct generation computationally intensive and challenging for diverse policy network sizes.
- **Solution:** An autoencoder is employed to compress these high-dimensional policy parameters into a compact latent space. The policy network parameters, denoted as `x`, are unfolded layer by layer, `x = [x_0, x_1, ..., x_{m-1}]`.
- **Encoder (E):** Encodes each flattened layer `x_i` into a latent representation `z_i`, forming a combined latent representation `z = [z_0, z_1, ..., z_{m-1}]`.
- **Decoder (D):** Reconstructs the original parameters `x` from the latent representation `z`.
- **Robustness:** During training, random noise augmentation (`ξ_D`, `ξ_E`) is applied to both the input to the encoder and the output of the encoder within the latent space, to enhance the robustness of the reconstruction process. The objective function minimized is `L = MSE(x, D(E(x + ξ_D) + ξ_E))`.
- **Generalizability:** The autoencoder is trained once per domain and designed such that latent parameter representations have consistent dimensions across different domains, facilitating the generalizability of the policy generator.

#### 3.2. Behavior Embedding via Contrastive Learning

- **Goal:** To learn informative behavior embeddings that encapsulate crucial environmental dynamics and task goals, serving as effective conditional information for policy parameter generation, rather than modeling state-action distributions directly.
- **Principle:** The method aims to capture the mutual information between a preceding *n*-step trajectory segment and subsequent states indicative of success or future states. Specifically, `I = I(s_success; {s_i, a_i}_i=0^n)`.
- **Contrastive Method:** For a long trajectory `τ`, it is decoupled into an initial segment of `n` state-action pairs (`τ^n = (s_0, a_0, ..., s_n, a_n)`) and a subsequent segment of `m` states after the first success event at time `K` (`ˆτ = (s_K, ..., s_{K+m})`).
- **Embedding Functions:** Two projection functions, `ϕ_θ` and `ψ_θ`, are used to generate embeddings `h_i = ϕ_θ(τ^n_i)` and `v_i = ψ_θ(ˆτ_i)` respectively.
- **Objective:** A contrastive loss function is optimized to pull embeddings from corresponding trajectory parts closer while pushing apart non-corresponding pairs within a batch: `L(ϕ_θ, ψ_θ, W) = - (1/N) Σ log (h_i^T W v_i / Σ h_i^T W v_j)`, where `W` is a learnable metric.
- **Flexibility:** For tasks without explicit success signals (e.g., locomotion), trajectories are segmented, and the last `m` states of each segment are used as `ˆτ`, while the `0-n` state-action pairs serve as `τ^n`. Informative behavior embeddings from long trajectories are concatenated from these segments. The chosen behavior embedding for conditional input consists of `h` and `v` concatenated.

#### 3.3. Conditional Policy Generator via Latent Diffusion Model

- **Architecture:** A conditional diffusion model is trained to generate latent parameter representations `z`, conditioned on the learned behavior embeddings `τ_e`. The neural backbone `ϵ_θ(z_t, τ_e, t)` is implemented as a 1D convolutional U-Net.
- **Process:** The model iteratively denoises random Gaussian noise into structured latent parameter representations, guided by `τ_e`.
- **Training Objective:** The conditional latent diffusion model is optimized using the objective `L_LDM(θ) := E[||ϵ - ϵ_θ(z_t, τ_e, t)||²]`, where `ϵ` is Gaussian noise and `t` is a time step.
- **Deployment:** The generated latent representations are decoded by the pretrained autoencoder's decoder `D` into deployable policy networks.
- **Stability:** During the training of the diffusion model, both the parameter autoencoder and the behavior embedding layers are frozen to ensure stability and efficiency.

#### 3.4. Dataset Construction

A dataset is constructed comprising tens of thousands of policy network parameters and their corresponding trajectories obtained from deploying these policies. This dataset is generated from multiple Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) training runs across various tasks. This data is then used to train the autoencoder and behavior embedding models. Subsequently, the encoded parameter representations and derived behavior embeddings are utilized to train the conditional diffusion model for policy parameter generation. For each task, policy checkpoints are saved during SAC training once a success rate of 1 is reached, often requiring multiple training runs with different random seeds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates "Make-An-Agent" across simulated manipulation tasks (MetaWorld, Robosuite) and real-world quadrupedal locomotion, focusing on performance, generalizability, robustness, and diversity.

#### 4.1. Performance Analysis

- **Adaptability to Environmental Randomness on Seen Tasks (Figure 5):** "Make-An-Agent" demonstrated superior performance on tasks it had previously encountered during training compared to baselines (multi-task imitation learning, multi-task reinforcement learning, meta-RL with hypernetworks, meta-IL with transformers). The method, by generating diverse policy parameters, exhibited a strong capacity to adapt to environmental randomness during evaluation, even when provided with test trajectories from a single environmental initialization. In contrast, baselines, which rely on adapting existing policies using limited trajectories, showed more constrained adaptability. The qualification rate (100% success) for seen tasks was over 30% for 100 generated policies.

- **Generalizability to Unseen Tasks (Figure 6):** The algorithm exhibited strong generalization capabilities on unseen tasks. Without any fine-tuning, "Make-An-Agent" directly utilized test trajectories as input to generate policies that performed effectively in unseen scenarios. This capability is attributed to the model's ability to combine parameter representations related to shared features (e.g., arm dynamics, path to goals) across tasks. Baselines struggled to adapt effectively to unseen tasks, particularly under environmental randomness. Even on unseen tasks, the method could generate high-performing policies using an average of only 20 trajectories, with qualification rates ranging from 4% to 55% across different tasks.

- **Robustness to Noisy Trajectories (Figure 7):** When Gaussian noise was introduced to the actions in test trajectories used for policy generation or adaptation on unseen tasks, "Make-An-Agent" maintained its resilience. The performance of baseline methods, however, significantly degraded under noisy input. This robustness is posited to stem from the behavior embeddings capturing essential dynamic information for policy generation, rather than directly learning precise state-action relationships, making the model less sensitive to minor trajectory inaccuracies.

- **Trajectory Difference (Figure 9):** The trajectories produced by the policies generated by "Make-An-Agent" were observed to be significantly different from the input test trajectories used as conditions. This indicates that the diffusion generator synthesizes new and varied behaviors, rather than merely memorizing or replicating the input demonstrations. This highlights the generative capability of the proposed paradigm.

- **Parameter Distinction (Figure 11):** An analysis of cosine similarity between generated policy parameters and RL-trained or fine-tuned policies revealed that "Make-An-Agent" generated policies with significantly greater diversity on seen tasks compared to fine-tuned RL policies. On unseen tasks, the similarity between generated parameters and those learned by RL was minimal (mostly below 0.2), further underscoring the novelty and non-memorizing nature of the generated policy parameters.

- **Real-world Evaluation (Figure 10):** Policies generated by "Make-An-Agent" from simulation trajectories were successfully deployed on a Unitree Go2 quadruped robot. The synthesized policies demonstrated stable and effective responses in challenging real-world locomotion tasks, including high-speed turning, backward movement, and obstacle avoidance on uneven terrain. This indicates the practical applicability and robustness of the generated policies in physical environments.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Choice of Behavior Embeddings (Figure 8):** The concatenated `h` and `v` embeddings from the contrastive learning approach were found to be effective. While individual `h` or `v` embeddings also achieved comparable performance, highlighting the efficiency of the contrastive loss, they collectively outperformed a baseline that integrated a direct embedding layer into the diffusion model.

- **Choice of Trajectory Length (Figure 12a):** Trajectories shorter than 40 steps resulted in performance degradation, suggesting insufficient behavioral information. However, beyond 40 steps, increasing trajectory length had minimal additional impact, indicating a threshold beyond which the method is not sensitive to length.

- **Impact of Policy Network Size (Figure 12b):** A hidden size of 128 for the policy network was identified as a suitable choice. Smaller networks could hinder policy performance, while larger networks increased the complexity of parameter reconstruction by the autoencoder.

- **Impact of Parameter Number in Training (Figure 12c):** An insufficient number of policy checkpoints (e.g., <=1000 per task) in the training dataset led to a significant performance decline. Beyond 1000 parameters, no substantial improvement in performance was observed, suggesting a saturation point for training data quantity.

- **Impact of Latent Representation Size (Figure 12d):** Both excessively large and excessively small latent representations negatively affected the performance of the generative model. Larger representations could overcomplicate the generative process, while smaller ones might restrict the autoencoder's capacity to decode deployable policies, emphasizing the autoencoder's influence on overall effectiveness.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

"Make-An-Agent" presents a novel framework for policy learning that shifts the focus from directly training policies or adapting them via gradients to generating policy parameters from behavioral prompts using conditional diffusion models. This approach offers several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **New Paradigm in Policy Learning:** The research establishes a new paradigm for policy synthesis by leveraging generative models to create policy network parameters directly from observed behaviors. This moves away from explicitly modeling behavior distributions and instead explores the parameter space, potentially revealing underlying connections between behavior and network architectures.

- **Enhanced Generalizability and Versatility:** The method demonstrates strong generalizability, producing effective policies for unseen tasks and even across different robotic embodiments (e.g., Sawyer to Panda). This is achieved without fine-tuning, indicating a robust ability to generalize from limited, few-shot demonstrations. The versatility extends to generating policies for diverse objectives and behaviors within continuous control domains.

- **Increased Robustness:** "Make-An-Agent" exhibits resilience to environmental randomness and noisy input trajectories. This robustness is particularly valuable for real-world deployments where perfect demonstrations or consistent environments are rare, enhancing the practicality of the generated policies.

- **Efficiency in Policy Acquisition:** By generating deployable policies from suboptimal or sparse demonstration trajectories without requiring extensive downstream fine-tuning or explicit reward functions, the method can significantly improve the sample efficiency of policy acquisition. This reduces the computational cost and data requirements typically associated with traditional reinforcement learning or imitation learning.

- **Facilitating Real-world Robot Deployment:** The successful deployment of generated policies on real-world quadrupedal robots highlights the practical utility of the approach. This demonstrates that policies synthesized in simulation can effectively transfer to physical systems, addressing a critical challenge in robotics research.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work opens avenues for future exploration, including the generation of parameters for larger and more diverse policy networks, the development of more flexible parameter generation methods, and the application of this generative framework to other structures within policy learning, such as reward functions or environmental models.

Overall, "Make-An-Agent" contributes a conceptual and technical advance by demonstrating the feasibility and benefits of generating control policies via behavior-prompted diffusion, offering a pathway towards more adaptable, robust, and efficient robotic agents.
