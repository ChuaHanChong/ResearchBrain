# BundleSDF: Neural 6-DoF Tracking and 3D Reconstruction of Unknown Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> BundleSDF jointly tracks the 6-DoF pose of an unknown rigid object and reconstructs its textured 3D shape from a monocular RGBD video, given only a first-frame segmentation mask. A dynamic keyframe memory pool, online pose-graph optimization, and a concurrently learned Neural Object Field with hybrid SDF modeling suppress drift, running near real-time at 10Hz and outperforming prior methods on HO3D, YCBInEOAT, and BEHAVE.

## Problem

- Instance- and category-level 6-DoF pose methods require a textured CAD model or pre-captured posed reference views, so they break on novel out-of-distribution objects.
- Neural reconstruction methods assume known camera poses or ground-truth masks and static scenes, so a moving camera never observes occluded faces such as an object's underside.
- SLAM-style frame-to-model fusion accumulates irreversible pose error on untextured, specular, or occluded objects, and cannot re-register after long-term complete occlusion.

## Method

- Coarse pose initialization matches ==transformer==-based RGB features between consecutive video-segmented frames, then filters correspondences with depth via ==RANSAC== least-squares to seed an inter-frame SE(3) estimate.
- A compact keyframe memory pool feeds CUDA online ==pose-graph optimization== over K viewpoint-diverse frames, minimizing feature-correspondence, point-to-plane reprojective, and neural-SDF unary losses jointly.
- A per-video **Neural Object Field**, hash-encoded ==SDF== plus appearance network with hybrid SDF modeling of uncertain-free, empty, and near-surface space, trains in a parallel thread and rectifies memory-frame poses.

## Results

- On HO3D it reaches **96.52** ADD-S and **92.62** ADD AUC with **0.57 cm** chamfer distance, versus BundleTrack's **92.39/66.01** and **52.05 cm**.
- On YCBInEOAT it leads ADD-S at **93.77** and chamfer at **1.16 cm**; on the harder single-view BEHAVE it scores **83.63/67.52** versus BundleTrack's **59.06/45.03**.
- HO3D ablations drop ADD from **92.62** to **56.96** without memory, **76.69** without the Neural Object Field, **73.57** without hybrid SDF, and **59.99** without the compact pool.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Co-designing tracking with reconstruction pays off: a concurrently learned shape field supplies a frame-to-model term that corrects drift hand-crafted pose graphs cannot.
> - Treating segmentation as uncertain rather than fully trusted or ignored preserves contour evidence, making reconstruction robust to noisy masks and external occluders.
> - Keyframe viewpoint diversity beats keyframe count; ignoring in-plane rotation spreads a fixed memory budget over wider pose coverage at equal compute.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Accurate tracking and 3D reconstruction of objects is a fundamental problem in computer vision and robotics. While significant progress has been made in this area, most existing approaches require prior knowledge of the object, such as CAD models or reference views, which limits their applicability to novel objects encountered in real-world scenarios.

BundleSDF, developed by researchers at NVIDIA, addresses this limitation by proposing a method that can simultaneously track the 6-DoF pose and reconstruct the 3D shape of previously unknown objects from a monocular RGBD video stream. This capability is essential for various applications, including robotic manipulation, augmented reality, and learning from demonstration.

### 2. Background and Challenges

The task of simultaneously tracking an object's pose and reconstructing its shape presents several significant challenges:

1. **Unknown object geometry**: Without prior models, the system must infer the object's shape while also tracking it.
2. **Occlusions**: Objects may be partially occluded by other objects or hands during interaction.
3. **Texture-poor surfaces**: Many objects lack distinctive visual features, making tracking difficult.
4. **Specular reflections**: Shiny surfaces can cause erroneous depth readings and misleading visual cues.
5. **Fast motion**: Rapid object movement can cause motion blur and tracking failure.

Previous approaches typically address either pose tracking or 3D reconstruction separately. Methods like KinectFusion build 3D models but struggle with moving objects, while tracking methods often require prior knowledge of the object's shape. BundleSDF uniquely combines these tasks into a unified framework.

### 3. Method Overview

BundleSDF operates on an RGBD video stream and consists of four main components that work together to achieve robust tracking and high-quality reconstruction:

1. **Coarse Pose Initialization**: Segments the object and estimates its initial pose between consecutive frames.
2. **Memory Pool Management**: Maintains a compact set of keyframes that provide diverse viewpoints of the object.
3. **Online Pose Graph Optimization**: Refines the object's pose by leveraging multiple frames and the neural object field.
4. **Neural Object Field**: Learns a neural implicit signed distance field (SDF) representation of the object's geometry.

### 4. Coarse Pose Initialization

The first step in BundleSDF's pipeline is to obtain an initial estimate of the object's pose between consecutive frames. This process involves:

1. **Object Segmentation**: An object-agnostic video segmentation network isolates the target object in each frame.
2. **Feature Matching**: A transformer-based keypoint matching network identifies corresponding points between frames.
3. **RANSAC-based Pose Estimation**: Using depth information and feature correspondences, a robust pose estimator computes the initial rigid transformation.

This initial pose is represented as a 6-DoF transformation that maps points from one frame to the next. While this provides a reasonable starting point, it can accumulate errors over time, which is why the subsequent components are necessary.

### 5. Memory Pool Management

To prevent drift and maintain global consistency in tracking, BundleSDF maintains a "memory pool" of selected frames that provide comprehensive coverage of the object from different viewpoints. The memory pool serves two primary purposes:

1. Support pose graph optimization by providing constraints between temporally distant frames
2. Provide multi-view observations for learning the neural object field

The memory pool uses a strategic selection mechanism that:
- Adds new frames only when they provide sufficiently novel viewpoints
- Maintains a fixed maximum size by removing redundant frames when necessary
- Ensures diverse coverage of the object's surface

The authors note that efficient memory pool management is crucial for both tracking performance and computational efficiency.

```python
# Pseudocode for memory pool management
def update_memory_pool(current_frame, memory_pool, max_size, min_view_distance):
    # Compute view distance to existing frames
    distances = [compute_view_distance(current_frame, frame) for frame in memory_pool]

    if min(distances) > min_view_distance:
        # Add frame if it provides a novel viewpoint
        if len(memory_pool) >= max_size:
            # Remove most redundant frame if pool is full
            redundancy_scores = compute_redundancy_scores(memory_pool)
            most_redundant_idx = np.argmax(redundancy_scores)
            memory_pool.pop(most_redundant_idx)
        memory_pool.append(current_frame)

    return memory_pool
```

### 6. Online Pose Graph Optimization

The core of BundleSDF's tracking system is an online pose graph optimization that refines the object's pose by leveraging constraints from multiple frames. The pose graph consists of:

- **Nodes**: Individual frames with their estimated object poses
- **Edges**: Constraints between frames, including:
 - Pairwise feature correspondence constraints
 - Point-to-plane ICP constraints
 - Neural object field constraints

The optimization minimizes a combination of losses:

$$\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_c + \mathcal{L}_i + \mathcal{L}_p$$

Where:
- $\mathcal{L}_c$ is the feature correspondence loss
- $\mathcal{L}_i$ is the point-to-plane ICP loss
- $\mathcal{L}_p$ is the neural object field loss

The optimization is performed using an iterative Gauss-Newton algorithm with re-weighting to handle outliers. The neural object field, which is learned in parallel, provides an implicit geometric prior that helps constrain the pose, especially in challenging cases with limited visual or geometric cues.

### 7. Neural Object Field

A key innovation in BundleSDF is the integration of a neural signed distance field (SDF) representation that is learned online during tracking. The neural object field has several important characteristics:

1. **Implicit Geometry Representation**: The SDF encodes the object's shape implicitly as the distance to the nearest surface.
2. **Per-Video Learning**: The model is trained from scratch for each new video sequence.
3. **Hybrid SDF Approach**: The space is divided into three regions - uncertain free space, empty space, and near-surface space - to handle occlusions and segmentation errors.

The neural SDF is parameterized using multilayer perceptrons (MLPs) with a position encoding:

$$\Omega(x) = \text{MLP}_\theta(\gamma(x))$$

Where $\gamma(x)$ is a positional encoding function that maps 3D coordinates to a higher-dimensional space. The model is trained using loss functions that enforce the SDF properties in different regions of space:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{SDF}} = \lambda_s \mathcal{L}_{\text{surface}} + \lambda_f \mathcal{L}_{\text{free}} + \lambda_r \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}$$

The neural object field training runs in a separate thread parallel to the tracking pipeline, allowing it to continuously improve as more frames are processed.

### 8. Experimental Results

The authors evaluate BundleSDF on three challenging public datasets: HO3D, YCBInEOAT, and BEHAVE, which feature various objects undergoing manipulation, occlusions, and complex motions.

#### 8.1. Tracking Performance

BundleSDF significantly outperforms existing methods in terms of tracking accuracy:

- On HO3D, it achieves ~4° rotation error and ~1cm translation error, compared to 20-60° and 10-30cm for other methods
- On BEHAVE, it maintains accurate tracking even during human-object interactions
- On YCBInEOAT, it demonstrates robustness to robotic manipulation scenarios

The quantitative results show that BundleSDF achieves state-of-the-art performance on all datasets:

- Average Displacement Distance (ADD) metrics show 60-80% higher recall at 2cm threshold
- Rotation and translation errors are significantly lower across different object categories
- Performance is consistent across different object properties (textured/textureless, symmetric/asymmetric)

#### 8.2. Reconstruction Quality

Beyond tracking, BundleSDF also produces high-quality 3D reconstructions of the objects:

- Reconstructions capture detailed geometry even for challenging objects
- The neural SDF representation handles occlusions and incomplete observations effectively
- The learned model is suitable for visualization, simulation, and robotic planning

The authors conduct ablation studies to demonstrate the importance of each component:
- The memory pool significantly reduces drift over long sequences
- The neural object field provides critical constraints when visual features are limited
- The hybrid SDF approach successfully handles occlusions and segmentation errors

### 9. Applications and Impact

BundleSDF enables several important applications across different domains:

#### 9.1. Robotics

- **Manipulation**: Robots can track and model previously unknown objects for grasping and manipulation
- **Learning from Demonstration**: By tracking objects during human demonstrations, robots can learn manipulation skills
- **Navigation**: Mobile robots can track and avoid dynamic objects in their environment

#### 9.2. Augmented Reality

- **Object Interaction**: AR applications can integrate virtual content with real objects without pre-scanning
- **Dynamic Scene Understanding**: AR systems can maintain coherent tracking of moving objects
- **Content Creation**: Quickly capture 3D models of objects for use in AR/VR content

#### 9.3. Computer Vision

- **Dataset Creation**: Automatically generate annotated datasets for object pose estimation
- **Scene Understanding**: Better comprehend complex scenes with multiple dynamic objects
- **Human-Object Interaction Analysis**: Study how humans interact with objects by tracking both simultaneously

The near real-time performance (processing at ~10Hz) makes many of these applications practically feasible in real-world scenarios.

### 10. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its impressive performance, BundleSDF has several limitations that present opportunities for future research:

1. **Computational Requirements**: The neural SDF training requires a GPU for real-time performance
2. **Initialization**: The method assumes the object is visible in the first frame for initialization
3. **Multiple Objects**: Currently focused on tracking a single object rather than multiple objects
4. **Non-Rigid Objects**: The approach assumes rigid objects and cannot handle deformations
5. **Texture Dependence**: Initialization still relies on some texture for feature matching

Future directions identified by the authors include:
- Extending to multiple object tracking
- Incorporating non-rigid deformations
- Improving initialization for completely textureless objects
- Further optimizing computational efficiency for deployment on mobile devices

### 11. Conclusion

BundleSDF represents a significant advancement in the field of 6-DoF object tracking and 3D reconstruction. By jointly optimizing pose tracking and neural shape representation, it achieves robust performance on challenging scenarios involving unknown objects, occlusions, and complex interactions.

The method's ability to operate without prior object models opens up new possibilities for applications in robotics, augmented reality, and computer vision. Its near real-time performance and high accuracy make it suitable for practical deployment in various domains.

The key innovation of BundleSDF lies in its co-design of the tracking and reconstruction processes: the neural object field provides constraints for pose estimation, while accurate poses enable better reconstruction. This synergistic relationship results in performance that exceeds approaches that treat these problems separately.

As deep learning and neural representations continue to advance, approaches like BundleSDF that combine geometric reasoning with learned representations are likely to define the future of 3D vision systems.
