# Safe Exploration for Nonlinear Processes Using Online Gaussian Process Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ABB Corporate Research and KTH Royal Institute of Technology developed a data-driven control framework that guarantees stability and constraint satisfaction for nonlinear systems by augmenting a linear model with an online-learned Gaussian Process residual. The method demonstrated safe operation on benchmark systems, achieving up to a 97% reduction in prediction RMSE and enabling adaptive expansion of its certified safe operational set.

## Problem

- Integrating data-driven and learning-based control into safety-critical systems requires rigorous guarantees for stability and constraint satisfaction.
- Many existing safe learning methods rely on strong assumptions, such as complete knowledge of nonlinear dynamics or overly conservative uncertainty approximations, limiting real-world applicability.
- Prior guaranteed safe exploration frameworks were limited to systems with fully known dynamics, failing to address scenarios where only a stabilizable linear approximation is available.

## Method

- A nominal ==linear model== is combined with an ==online-learned Gaussian Process (GP)== to represent and quantify uncertainty in unknown nonlinear residual dynamics.
- A ==Lyapunov-based Probabilistic Control-Invariant Set (PCIS)== is constructed, integrating GP uncertainty confidence bounds to provide ==finite-sample probabilistic guarantees== for system stability and constraint satisfaction.
- An optimization-based controller synthesizes control inputs by solving a ==convex Quadratic Program (QP)== at each step, balancing exploration (information gain) with high-probability safety.

## Results

- On a 2D polynomial system, the framework maintained trajectories within safety bounds while reducing GP prediction RMSE by **97%** and expanding the certified safe set volume by approximately **27.7%** over iterations.
- For a three-tank water-level process, no state or input constraint violations were observed over a **2000-second** identification horizon, confirming the framework's ability to ensure safety in a realistic MIMO system.
- Sparse Gaussian Process models achieved **over 1000 times faster** inference compared to exact GPs, enabling real-time control with near-nominal accuracy and coverage for complex systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating GP uncertainty envelopes directly into a one-step Lyapunov decrease condition enables the formulation of a convex QP for safe, real-time control with probabilistic guarantees.
> - The certified safety set can adaptively expand as the GP model's uncertainty reduces over time from online data, allowing for progressively richer and less conservative exploration.
> - Sparse GP approximations can provide a favorable trade-off between computational efficiency and predictive accuracy, making the framework tractable for real-time control in more complex systems.
