# Safe Exploration for Nonlinear Processes Using Online Gaussian Process Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ABB Corporate Research and KTH Royal Institute of Technology developed a data-driven control framework that guarantees stability and constraint satisfaction for nonlinear systems by augmenting a linear model with an online-learned Gaussian Process residual. The method demonstrated safe operation on benchmark systems, achieving up to a 97% reduction in prediction RMSE and enabling adaptive expansion of its certified safe operational set.

## Problem

- Integrating data-driven and learning-based control into safety-critical systems requires rigorous guarantees for stability and constraint satisfaction.
- Many existing safe learning methods rely on strong assumptions, such as complete knowledge of nonlinear dynamics or overly conservative uncertainty approximations, limiting real-world applicability.
- Prior guaranteed safe exploration frameworks were limited to systems with fully known dynamics, failing to address scenarios where only a stabilizable linear approximation is available.

## Method

- A nominal ==linear model== is combined with an ==online-learned Gaussian Process (GP)== to represent and quantify uncertainty in unknown nonlinear residual dynamics.
- A ==Lyapunov-based Probabilistic Control-Invariant Set (PCIS)== is constructed, integrating GP uncertainty confidence bounds to provide ==finite-sample probabilistic guarantees== for system stability and constraint satisfaction.
- An optimization-based controller synthesizes control inputs by solving a ==convex Quadratic Program (QP)== at each step, balancing exploration (information gain) with high-probability safety.

## Results

- On a 2D polynomial system, the framework maintained trajectories within safety bounds while reducing GP prediction RMSE by **97%** and expanding the certified safe set volume by approximately **27.7%** over iterations.
- For a three-tank water-level process, no state or input constraint violations were observed over a **2000-second** identification horizon, confirming the framework's ability to ensure safety in a realistic MIMO system.
- Sparse Gaussian Process models achieved **over 1000 times faster** inference compared to exact GPs, enabling real-time control with near-nominal accuracy and coverage for complex systems.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating GP uncertainty envelopes directly into a one-step Lyapunov decrease condition enables the formulation of a convex QP for safe, real-time control with probabilistic guarantees.
> - The certified safety set can adaptively expand as the GP model's uncertainty reduces over time from online data, allowing for progressively richer and less conservative exploration.
> - Sparse GP approximations can provide a favorable trade-off between computational efficiency and predictive accuracy, making the framework tractable for real-time control in more complex systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of data-driven and learning-based control offers potential for enhancing the performance of complex dynamical systems characterized by incomplete or uncertain first-principles models. These approaches adapt to unknown dynamics and disturbances through collected operational data, aiming for improved accuracy and robustness. However, in safety-critical domains such as autonomous robotics, aerospace systems, and process industries, the integration of data-driven learning must ensure stability and constraint satisfaction. This necessitates the development of learning-based controllers that facilitate safe exploration, enabling online system behavior learning without violating safety or stability requirements.

Existing machine learning methodologies, including reinforcement learning and Bayesian optimization, often do not prioritize stringent safety constraints during initial exploration phases. This limitation has led to extensive research into safe learning for control, where machine learning models are incorporated into control-theoretic frameworks to enforce stability and constraint invariance. A common strategy involves utilizing prior system knowledge, such as approximate dynamics or stability certificates, to manage risk during learning. While these methods have demonstrated results, many rely on assumptions that may not hold in practical scenarios, such as complete knowledge of nonlinear dynamics or the use of conservative Lipschitz over-approximations for uncertainty. Practical systems often provide only a local linear approximation, requiring online learning of the true residual dynamics.

This work directly addresses a limitation identified in previous research, specifically, which demonstrated guaranteed safe exploration for nonlinear systems where dynamics were fully known. That framework synthesized exploratory inputs while maintaining the invariance of precomputed safety sets, thereby ensuring continuous constraint satisfaction. The present paper extends this concept to scenarios where only a stabilizable linear approximation of a nonlinear process is available. It introduces a safe data-driven control framework that guarantees stability and constraint satisfaction by augmenting a nominal linear model with an online-learned Gaussian Process (GP) residual.

Compared to other safe learning methods, the proposed framework balances tractability and conservatism. Unlike Safe Model Predictive Control (MPC) formulations, which can be conservative due to uncertainty propagation over extended horizons, this approach employs a one-step Lyapunov decrease combined with GP confidence bounds. This results in a convex Quadratic Program (QP) without the multi-step uncertainty accumulation typically associated with long-horizon predictions. In contrast to guaranteed safe exploration methods that assume known dynamics, this framework requires only a stabilizable linear model, with the nonlinear residual being learned online with finite-sample confidence. Furthermore, distinct from probabilistic invariance formulations, the proposed method provides a control Lyapunov function-based approach that integrates directly into a real-time controller, allowing for adaptive expansion of the safety set as GP uncertainty decreases.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and validate a safe data-driven control framework for nonlinear systems with partially known dynamics. This framework is designed to ensure stability and constraint satisfaction throughout the online learning process, addressing scenarios where only a stabilizable linear approximation of the system is initially available.

The specific key objectives include:
- **Nominal-Residual Decomposition**: To develop a framework that combines a known linear model with an online-learned Gaussian Process (GP) residual. This aims to enable safe learning of unknown nonlinearities without requiring complete prior knowledge of the full system dynamics.
- **Probabilistic Control-Invariant Set (PCIS) Construction**: To construct a Lyapunov-based PCIS that incorporates GP uncertainty. This construction is intended to provide finite-sample probabilistic guarantees for system stability and constraint satisfaction.
- **Optimization-Based Control**: To synthesize an optimization-based controller that solves a convex quadratic program (QP) at each control step. The purpose of this QP is to maximize information gain (promote exploration) while maintaining safety with high probability.
- **Adaptive Safety Set Expansion**: To allow the certified safety set to expand dynamically as the GP model uncertainty reduces over time due to online data collection, thereby enabling progressively richer and less conservative exploration.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges in data-driven control for safety-critical systems:
- **Bridging Performance and Safety**: While data-driven and learning-based control offer significant performance improvements for complex systems, their deployment in safety-critical applications necessitates rigorous guarantees of stability and constraint adherence. The core motivation is to design learning-based controllers that can learn system behavior online without compromising safety.
- **Limitations of Existing Methods**: Conventional machine learning techniques often neglect hard safety constraints during initial exploration, leading to potentially unsafe operations. Furthermore, many existing safe learning approaches rely on idealized assumptions, such as perfect knowledge of nonlinear dynamics or conservative over-approximations of uncertainty, which limit their applicability to real-world systems.
- **Extending Guaranteed Safe Exploration**: Prior work demonstrated guaranteed safe exploration for systems with fully known dynamics. However, in most practical scenarios, only a nominal linear model is available, and the true nonlinear residual dynamics must be learned online. This paper is motivated to address this gap by providing a framework that enables safe learning of these unknown nonlinearities with only a stabilizable linear approximation.
- **Balancing Tractability and Conservatism**: There is a need for control methodologies that are computationally tractable for real-time implementation while also providing strong safety guarantees without being overly conservative. The authors are motivated to develop an approach that achieves a favorable balance in these aspects by leveraging one-step Lyapunov conditions with GP confidence bounds within a convex optimization framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework addresses safe exploration for continuous-time control-affine nonlinear systems of the form `ẋ = Ax + Bu + g(x)`, where `(A, B)` is a known stabilizable linearization around an operating point, and `g(x)` represents unknown but smooth residual dynamics. The controller operates in discrete time using Zero-Order Hold (ZOH) with sampling period `∆t`. State and input constraints, `X` and `U` respectively, are considered hard limits for safety and actuation.

**Residual Model and Uncertainty Quantification**:
The unknown residual `g(x)` is modeled as a zero-mean Gaussian Process (GP) with a chosen kernel `k(·, ·)`. The GP is updated online with data `D_t = {(x_i, u_i, y_i)}`. The GP posterior provides a predictive mean `μ_t(x)` and standard deviation `σ_t(x)` for any query point `x`. To account for uncertainty, a high-probability confidence envelope is established: `Pr(||g(x) - μ_t(x)||_2 <= β_t σ_t(x)) >= 1 - δ`, where `δ ∈ (0, 1)` is a user-defined risk level, and `β_t` is a confidence parameter derived from GP-UCB schedules to bound the true function within a high-probability region. Empirical calibration `γ*` is optionally applied to `σ_t(x)` (i.e., `~σ_t(x) = √γ* σ_t(x)`) to align empirical coverage with the nominal level.

**Safety Notion (Probabilistic Invariance)**:
Safety is defined as the recursive feasibility of state and input constraints with high probability: `Pr(x_k ∈ X, u_k ∈ U, ∀k ≥ 0) ≥ 1 - δ`. This is certified by constructing a Probabilistic Control-Invariant Set (PCIS), denoted `S ⊆ X`. If the initial state `x₀` is within `S`, then subsequent states `x_k` are guaranteed to remain within `S` for all future times with probability `1 - δ`.

**Probabilistic Control-Invariant Set (PCIS) Construction**:
A Lyapunov function `V(x) = xᵀPx` is established for a stabilizing Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) feedback `u_lin(x) = -Kx` designed for the nominal linear system `(A, B)`. The GP-aware PCIS is defined as the set of states `x ∈ X` for which there exists an admissible control input `u ∈ U` such that the worst-case time derivative of the Lyapunov function satisfies:
`∇V(x)ᵀ(Ax + Bu + μ_t(x)) + β_t σ_t(x) ||∇V(x)||_2 + λV(x) <= 0`.
This condition ensures that the Lyapunov function decreases or stays constant even under the most adverse realization of the unknown residual `g(x)` within its high-probability confidence interval. As the GP model improves and `σ_t(x)` shrinks, the PCIS `S` is allowed to expand, reflecting reduced uncertainty and enabling a larger safe operational region.

**Safe Excitation Controller**:
At each discrete time step `k`, the controller computes the control input `u_k` by solving a convex Quadratic Program (QP):
`min_{u, s≥0} ||u - u_lin(x_k)||_2^2 + ρs`
`s.t. ∇V(x_k)ᵀ(A x_k + B u + μ_t(x_k)) + β_t σ_t(x_k) ||∇V(x_k)||_2 + λV(x_k) <= s`
`u_min <= u <= u_max`
The objective function `||u - u_lin(x_k)||_2^2` encourages the control input `u` to remain close to the nominal stabilizing control `u_lin(x_k)`. The term `ρs` penalizes the slack variable `s`, which allows for temporary, small violations of the Lyapunov decrease condition if strictly necessary, while striving for `s=0` to ensure strict safety. This QP formulation balances the objective of stable operation with the need for exploration (implicitly driven by `β_t σ_t(x)` term in the safety constraint, as areas of high uncertainty require more cautious control inputs unless exploration is explicitly allowed).

**Assumptions and Guarantee**:
The framework relies on several assumptions: (A1) existence of a nominal Control Lyapunov Function (CLF) for `(A, B)`; (A2) the true residual `g(x)` satisfies the GP-UCB envelope component-wise with a given probability; (A3) the local truncation error from continuous-to-discrete-time approximation is bounded; and (A4) if state estimation is used, the error `||x - ˆx||` is bounded. Under these assumptions, Theorem 4.1 formally guarantees probabilistic invariance: if the current state `x_k` is within the certified set `S_t` and the QP is feasible with `s=0`, then `x_{k+1}` remains in `S_t` with probability at least `1 - δ_t`, and all future states and inputs remain within their respective bounds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The framework was evaluated on two benchmarks: a 2D polynomial system and a three-tank water-level process, assessing GP model accuracy, PCIS evolution, performance against an unsafe baseline, and computational efficiency.

#### 4.1. Polynomial Benchmark (2D System)

This low-dimensional system, `ẋ₁ = x₂, ẋ₂ = -2x₂ + x₂² + u`, was used to examine kernel misspecification, uncertainty calibration, and PCIS conservatism.

- **GP Kernel Comparison**: When the true residual is quadratic, a polynomial kernel of degree 2 achieved the most accurate modeling (RMSE ≈ 0.0, R² = 1.0, 100% empirical coverage). RBF family kernels showed comparable mean accuracy (RMSE ≈ 0.227, R² ≈ 0.912) but suffered from severe under-coverage (∼34%) for nominal 95% confidence intervals. The Matérn-5/2 kernel yielded higher prediction error but better coverage (74.5%). This highlights the sensitivity of uncertainty quantification to kernel choice. Computational costs for different kernels were found to be marginally different.
- **Full GP vs. Sparse GP**: Sparse FITC–SGPR models achieved approximately 30–40 times faster training compared to the full GP. The configuration with 10 inducing points (M=10) offered the best trade-off between computational efficiency and predictive accuracy, though its performance remained below that of the full GP. Sparse GPs also exhibited empirical coverage below 95%, suggesting the need for variance calibration or an increased confidence parameter `β` for reliable safety certificates.
- **Unsafe vs. Safe Exploration**:
 - **Unsafe Baseline**: An LQR controller without the safety layer, though stabilizing, led to trajectories exiting the certified ellipsoid and violating state box constraints. This indicates that even with a nominal stable controller, unknown nonlinearities can lead to safety violations during exploration.
 - **GP-PCIS Safe Exploration**: The proposed GP-PCIS framework ensured that trajectories remained within the certified PCIS and the predefined state bounds throughout the experiment, without any recorded violations.
 - **Adaptive Safety Set Expansion**: Over 12 iterations, the certified safe set volume (`|S|`) expanded by approximately +27.7%. Simultaneously, the GP prediction accuracy significantly improved (RMSE decreased from 1.113 to 0.029, a 97% reduction; MAE from 0.678 to 0.017). The empirical coverage of 95% confidence intervals increased from 37% to 100%, and the mean predictive standard deviation (`¯σ`) dropped from 0.177 to 0.052 (a 70% reduction), narrowing the mean prediction-interval width (MPIW) from ∼0.694 to ∼0.204. The calibration error decreased from 0.58 to 0.05. The exploration ratio `ρ_k` peaked early (iteration 3, ≈0.32), indicating variance-driven exploration, then stabilized below 0.05, signaling a shift towards exploitation as uncertainty diminished. This demonstrates the framework's ability to safely expand the operational domain while improving model accuracy and uncertainty calibration.

#### 4.2. Three-Tank Water-Level Process (MIMO System)

This realistic, interconnected MIMO plant with water level constraints and valve saturations was used to stress-test certification tractability and real-time control capabilities.

- **GP Modeling**: Matérn kernels (ν ∈ {3/2, 5/2}) generally offered the best trade-off in terms of RMSE and empirical coverage (close to 95%) across the three residual channels. The RBF kernel, however, showed under-dispersion for Tank 2 (54.3% coverage), which is problematic for safety certificates relying on accurate uncertainty envelopes.
- **Full GP vs. Sparse GP**: The exact GP achieved the lowest aggregate RMSE (0.8971) and 100% coverage but incurred significant latency (66.5 seconds per query). Sparse FITC–SGPR with M ∈ {20, 30, 40} inducing points delivered near-nominal coverage (approximately 94.3–94.5%) and RMSE within 10% of the full GP, while providing over 1000 times faster inference (e.g., 0.026 seconds per query). Per-channel performance varied; for instance, SGPR with M ≥ 20 improved RMSE for Tank 1 compared to the full GP, but degraded performance for Tank 2 and Tank 3 in some aspects. This suggests that M ∈ represents a balanced operating point for online certification and control, and per-channel optimization or calibration might be beneficial.
- **Unsafe vs. Safe Exploration**:
 - **Unsafe Baseline**: Without the safety layer, water levels briefly violated the predefined [45%, 87%] band (e.g., H₃ dipped to 44.8%, below the 45% lower limit).
 - **GP-PCIS Safe Exploration**: Over five iterations of the GP-PCIS framework during an approximate 2000-second identification horizon, no state or input constraint violations were observed. The trajectories consistently remained within the [45%, 87%] level bands. Control performance improved over iterations, with steady plateaus matching target references and limited overshoot during transients. Valve inputs demonstrated adaptive behavior, with initial saturation to achieve targets, followed by relaxation as GP predictions improved. This confirmed the framework's ability to maintain safety while achieving system convergence and learning.

#### 4.3. Discussion on Limitations

The authors acknowledge several limitations: conservatism of the one-step worst-case PCIS certificate, cubic scaling of exact GP training with dataset size (necessitating sparse/localized GP updates for larger tasks), stepwise rather than cumulative risk guarantees, and critical dependence on properly calibrated predictive uncertainty. Mitigation strategies, such as conservative `β_t` scheduling, periodic empirical coverage checks, variance calibration, and tightening reachability bounds, are suggested.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a framework for safe exploration in nonlinear systems that represents a contribution to the field of data-driven control, particularly in safety-critical applications.

The primary significance lies in its ability to enable online learning of unknown nonlinear dynamics while providing probabilistic guarantees of stability and constraint satisfaction. Unlike previous approaches that often assumed complete knowledge of system dynamics or relied on highly conservative approximations, this framework specifically addresses the practical scenario where only a stabilizable linear approximation is available. By integrating Gaussian Process (GP) based uncertainty quantification with Lyapunov stability theory and convex optimization, the method offers a principled approach to managing the inherent trade-off between exploration for learning and the necessity of safety.

The potential impact of this research is multi-faceted:
- **Enabling Safe Deployment of Learning-Based Control**: The framework facilitates the deployment of data-driven and learning-based controllers in domains like autonomous robotics, aerospace, and industrial processes, where strict adherence to safety protocols is paramount. By guaranteeing high-probability stability and constraint satisfaction, it helps overcome a significant barrier to the adoption of advanced learning techniques in these sensitive environments.
- **Improved Operational Efficiency and Performance**: As demonstrated by the adaptive expansion of the probabilistic control-invariant set (PCIS) and the reduction in predictive uncertainty, the framework allows for increasingly efficient and less conservative exploration over time. This enables the system to learn and adapt to its true dynamics more effectively, potentially leading to improved operational performance and robustness.
- **Real-time Implementability**: The use of a convex quadratic program (QP) for control input computation, coupled with the demonstrated feasibility of real-time solve times (even with sparse GP approximations), indicates that the proposed approach is suitable for practical, online implementation. This aspect is crucial for industrial applications where computational latency is a critical factor.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work lays a foundation for future research directions aimed at addressing its current limitations, such as reducing conservatism through multi-step chance constraints, improving scalability for high-dimensional or long-horizon tasks via advanced GP techniques, and ultimately, validating the approach on hardware. Its integration with performance-oriented MPC is also identified as a natural next step, suggesting pathways towards more holistic control solutions.

In summary, this research advances the state of data-driven control by providing a unified and implementable framework that balances learning efficiency with rigorous safety guarantees for partially known nonlinear systems. It offers a practical methodology for overcoming critical challenges in the safe deployment of intelligent control systems.
