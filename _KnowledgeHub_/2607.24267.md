# FeelWorld: Visuo-Tactile World Model for Hierarchical Contact Prediction and Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> FeelWorld is a visuo-tactile world model that introduces hierarchical prediction of contact, 3D tactile latent, and slip states alongside visual states for robot manipulation. It employs a contact-gated asymmetric attention mechanism to fuse modalities, achieving up to 31.0% lower LPIPS in visual prediction and an 81.7% average success rate in zero-shot contact-rich planning tasks.

## Problem

- Existing action-conditioned world models for robot manipulation often prioritize visual fidelity but neglect underlying tactile states, leading to imagined futures that are visually plausible but physically inconsistent in contact-rich scenarios.
- Current visuo-tactile world models typically treat tactile signals as undifferentiated input, failing to distinguish hierarchical physical information (e.g., simple contact vs. slip), and naive integration can degrade overall prediction quality due to free-space tactile noise.
- Autoregressive world models are susceptible to compounding errors, where small prediction inaccuracies accumulate over time, leading to divergent and unrealistic long-horizon rollouts, hindering stable planning for complex tasks.

## Method

- The model employs ==hierarchical tactile state modeling== to explicitly predict contact state, detailed 3D tactile latent, and slip probability, providing structured physical information for robust dynamic learning.
- A ==contact-gated asymmetric attention mechanism== selectively fuses tactile information only when contact is predicted, suppressing irrelevant tactile interference during free-space motion and protecting visual prediction pathways.
- ==Rollout training with context noise injection== is utilized to combat compounding errors, improving the model's stability and accuracy for long-horizon predictions by making it resilient to internal prediction perturbations.
- A ==contact-aware Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) planner== leverages the predicted hierarchical tactile states to guide action planning, allowing for a transition from vision-guided approach to tactile-grounded interaction and penalizing unstable states like slip.

## Results

- FeelWorld achieved a 10-step LPIPS of **0.058**, a **31.0%** reduction compared to a visual-only V-JEPA 2 baseline (**0.084**), demonstrating superior visual prediction quality and temporal consistency, particularly during critical contact transitions.
- The model accurately predicted contact state with **98.6%** accuracy and **98.1%** F1 score, and slip state with **83.4%** F1, **81.7%** precision, and **85.2%** recall, reliably identifying critical but rare physical events.
- FeelWorld, with its contact-aware CEM planner, achieved an **81.7%** average success rate in zero-shot contact-rich manipulation tasks, outperforming visual-only (**49.2%**) and naive visuo-tactile (**57.5%**) baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling hierarchical tactile states (contact, 3D latent, slip) provides more meaningful physical supervision for latent dynamics, enhancing the physical consistency of imagined futures in manipulation tasks.
> - Selective fusion of visual and tactile information, achieved through a contact-gated asymmetric attention mechanism, effectively mitigates noise and improves prediction accuracy by integrating tactile data only when physically relevant.
> - Integrating predicted contact and slip states directly into the planning cost function enables robust robot planning for contact-rich tasks, allowing for stable interaction and avoiding physically unstable outcomes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "FeelWorld" addresses a limitation in existing action-conditioned world models for robot manipulation, particularly in contact-rich scenarios.

**Action-Conditioned World Models:** The field of robot learning has seen advancements in action-conditioned world models, notably the Dreamer family and more recent architectures like V-JEPA 2, Ctrl-World, UniSim, and RoboDreamer. These models enable robots to plan by simulating future outcomes based on learned latent dynamics, primarily focusing on visual fidelity and long-horizon imagination. While effective for predicting visual states, many of these models either neglect tactile information or integrate it without explicit physical structuring. This can lead to imagined futures that are visually plausible but physically inconsistent, especially in tasks requiring intricate physical interaction.

**Visuo-Tactile World Models and Representation Learning:** A growing body of research recognizes the complementarity of vision and touch for robust manipulation. Efforts such as VT-WM and ContactWorld have explored integrating tactile sensing into world models to improve physical consistency. Additionally, specialized vision-based tactile sensors like GelSight and DIGIT have emerged, along with methods for self-supervised tactile pretraining, visuo-tactile alignment, and unified multimodal representation learning. These developments provide the foundational encoding capabilities for incorporating tactile data effectively. Despite these advances, existing visuo-tactile world models often treat tactile signals as a monolithic input, without distinguishing different levels of physical information (e.g., simple contact vs. slip). Furthermore, the asymmetry between continuous visual information and sparse, contact-dependent tactile information poses challenges for effective fusion, as noise from free-space tactile signals can degrade overall prediction quality.

**Visuo-Tactile Manipulation Policies:** Concurrently, researchers have developed visuo-tactile manipulation policies that directly use multimodal observations or leverage predicted tactile futures for action generation. These policies aim to enable robots to perform tasks that require fine-grained contact reasoning, force control, or slip detection. However, many policy-oriented methods focus on direct mapping or use future predictions mainly for policy generation, rather than explicitly modeling the dynamics of contact states within the world model's predictive capabilities.

"FeelWorld" positions itself by addressing the gap between high-fidelity visual world models and the need for physically consistent, contact-rich interaction. It proposes a structured approach to tactile information within a world model, explicitly decomposing tactile states hierarchically and introducing a gated fusion mechanism that respects the asymmetric nature of vision and touch. This aims to provide a more principled integration of tactile sensing into world models, leading to more robust prediction and planning for complex manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind FeelWorld stems from the limitations of current robot manipulation systems in effectively reasoning about contact-rich physical interactions. While human planning involves imagining action outcomes that align with physical dynamics, existing visual world models frequently prioritize appearance, often neglecting the underlying tactile states crucial for stable contact. This discrepancy can result in imagined futures that appear visually coherent but are physically unrealistic.

The key objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

1. **Enhance Physical Consistency of Imagined Futures:** The central objective is to develop a world model that predicts not only future visual states but also accurately models the physical dynamics of contact. This is motivated by the observation that vision alone provides indirect evidence of contact physics, while tactile sensing offers direct measurements of force distribution, deformation, and slip, which are often occluded or produce subtle visual changes.
2. **Introduce Hierarchical Tactile State Modeling:** The authors observe that tactile interaction can be naturally decomposed into distinct, hierarchical states, each carrying specific physical meaning. The motivation is to move beyond treating tactile signals as undifferentiated input and instead explicitly model these states to provide structured supervision for latent dynamics.
 - **Contact State:** A binary state indicating initial contact. This is crucial for determining when tactile information becomes relevant.
 - **3D Tactile Latent:** This state captures detailed local surface deformation, pressure distribution, and shear direction, encoding the geometry of the immediate interaction.
 - **Slip State:** An inherently temporal state that assesses interaction stability, requiring observation over time. Predicting slip is critical for robust grasping and manipulation.
 The objective is to predict these states sequentially, with the contact state controlling fusion, the 3D tactile latent representing local interaction, and the slip state informing planning costs.
3. **Implement Selective Visuo-Tactile Fusion:** A significant motivation is to address the asymmetry between vision and touch. Tactile signals are physically meaningful only during contact, while in free space, they often consist of sensor noise. Naive integration of tactile signals throughout a trajectory can degrade visual prediction. The objective is to design a "contact-gated asymmetric attention mechanism" that suppresses irrelevant tactile interference during free-space motion and enables joint visuo-tactile prediction only when contact occurs, thereby protecting visual prediction pathways.
4. **Improve Robustness to Compounding Errors in Long-Horizon Rollouts:** Autoregressive world models are susceptible to compounding errors, where small prediction inaccuracies accumulate over time, leading to divergent and unrealistic long-horizon rollouts. The motivation is to improve the model's stability and accuracy in long-horizon predictions. The objective is to train the model with autoregressive rollouts and context noise injection, making it more resilient to internal prediction errors during inference.
5. **Enable Contact-Aware Planning for Contact-Rich Tasks:** The ultimate motivation is to leverage the improved world model for more effective robot planning. Traditional planning with world models often relies heavily on visual goals, which may not guarantee stable physical interaction. The objective is to develop a "contact-aware Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) planner" that uses the predicted hierarchical tactile states—specifically contact and slip—to guide planning, allowing for a transition from vision-guided approach to tactile-grounded interaction, and penalizing unstable states like slip.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FeelWorld learns an action-conditioned dynamics model that jointly predicts future visual latents and three hierarchical tactile states. The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

**A. Problem Formulation and Encoding:**
- **Inputs:** The model takes multi-view RGB images (I_t), 3D tactile point clouds (P_t), robot proprioception (q_t), and 7-dimensional robot actions (a_t).
- **Encoders:**
 - A frozen visual encoder (E_v) converts images (I_t) into a visual latent (o_t).
 - A frozen pretrained FG-CLTP encoder (E_τ) processes 3D tactile point clouds (P_t) into a compact d_τ-dimensional tactile latent (h_t). Importantly, LayerNormalization is omitted for h_t to preserve magnitude information related to contact intensity.
- **Latent Space:** Visual (o_t) and tactile (h_t) latents are projected into transformer tokens, z_o_t and z_τ_t, respectively, which are then fused into a visuo-tactile latent (z_t = [z_o_t; z_τ_t]).
- **Outputs:** The dynamics model (W_θ) predicts the next fused latent (ˆz_t+1). From this, a visual prediction head decodes the next visual latent (ˆo_t+1), and a tactile prediction head decodes the three hierarchical tactile states: contact (ˆc_t+1), 3D tactile latent (ˆh_t+1), and slip (ˆs_t+1).

**B. Hierarchical Tactile Modeling:**
This component structures tactile prediction into three levels with explicit supervision:
- **Shared Tactile Prediction:** A tactile prediction head (H_τ) decodes the 3D tactile latent (ˆh_t+1) from the predicted tactile component (ˆz_τ_t+1). This is directly supervised against the ground-truth tactile latent using an L1 loss: L_tis = ||ˆh_t+1 - sg(E_τ(P_t+1))||_1. Stop-gradient (sg) is used to prevent backpropagation through the frozen encoder.
- **Level 1: Contact State Prediction:** A two-layer MLP head (H_c) predicts the binary contact probability (ˆc_t+1 ∈) from ˆz_τ_t+1. It is supervised using Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss: L_contact = BCE(ˆc_t+1, c_t+1). The predicted contact probability also serves as the contact gate (g_t) for attention.
- **Level 2: Slip State Prediction:** A causal 1D convolutional head (H_s) with a kernel size of 3 predicts the slip probability (ˆs_t+1 ∈) from a temporal window of predicted tactile features (ˆz_τ_t-K+2:t+1). This explicitly models temporal changes. Slip labels are imbalanced, so Focal Loss is used: L_slip = -α_t * (1 - p_t)^γ * log p_t, applied only when ground-truth contact is present (c_t+1=1).

**C. Contact-Gated Visuo-Tactile Attention:**
This mechanism addresses the asymmetry of vision and touch by selectively fusing tactile information:
- **Asymmetric Attention Flow:**
 - Tactile tokens (z_τ_t) first attend to visual tokens (z_o_t) via cross-attention to ground local tactile observations in global scene context: ˆz_τ_t = z_τ_t + CrossAttn(z_τ_t, z_o_t, z_o_t).
 - Visual tokens (z_o_t) then conditionally attend to the refined tactile tokens (ˆz_τ_t). This cross-attention is gated by the binary contact gate (g_t): ˆz_o_t = z_o_t + g_t * CrossAttn(z_o_t, ˆz_τ_t, ˆz_τ_t). If g_t=0 (no contact), tactile modulation of visual tokens is suppressed.
- **Gate Control:** During initial training and teacher-forcing, g_t is the ground-truth contact label (c_t). During autoregressive rollout and inference, g_t is derived from the predicted contact probability (I[ˆc_t ≥ 0.5]).

**D. Rollout Training with Context Noise Injection:**
To combat compounding errors in autoregressive prediction:
- **Multi-step Autoregressive Rollouts:** The model is trained with multi-step rollouts (R steps) in addition to the single-step teacher-forcing objective. Starting from a random timestep, the model predicts forward using its own outputs as context.
- **Context Noise Injection:** Small Gaussian noise (ε_z ∼ N(0, σ^2I)) is added to the predicted visuo-tactile context (ˆz_t+r) before it is fed as input for the next step: ˜z_t+r = ˆz_t+r + ε_z. This encourages the model to be robust to input perturbations similar to those encountered during inference.
- **Joint Objective:** The total loss L_total combines the teacher-forcing loss (L_tf = L_tf_vis + λ_τ L_tis + λ_c L_contact + λ_s L_slip) and an averaged rollout loss (L_rollout), weighted by λ_roll.

**E. Contact-Aware CEM Planning:**
For action planning, FeelWorld integrates its predictive capabilities with a Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) planner:
- **CEM Process:** CEM iteratively refines a distribution over candidate action sequences by sampling, evaluating them through model rollouts, selecting elites, and updating the distribution.
- **Planning Cost Function:** The planning cost J(A) for an action sequence A evaluates the visual task progress and conditionally activates tactile interaction terms:
 J(A) = Σ_k=1^Hp [D_v(ˆo_t+k, o_goal) + g_k * (w_τ D_τ(ˆh_t+k, h_goal) + w_s D_slip(ˆs_t+k))]
 - D_v and D_τ are L1 distances in visual and tactile latent spaces, respectively.
 - D_slip(ˆs_t+k) directly penalizes predicted slip probability (ˆs_t+k).
 - The hard contact gate (g_k = I[ˆc_t+k ≥ 0.5]) activates tactile and slip costs only after predicted contact. This prevents optimizing for unreachable tactile goals during free-space approach.
- **Receding Horizon Control:** The robot executes the first two actions of the optimal sequence, observes the new state, and replans.

**F. Experimental Setup:**
- **Hardware:** Imeta-Y1 robot with three RGB cameras (left external for global context, wrist for local interaction) and DM tactile sensors for 3D tactile point clouds.
- **Tasks:** Chip grasping, fruit grasping, and USB insertion—all contact-rich.
- **Dataset:** 200 training and 40 test trajectories per task, recorded at 30 Hz and downsampled to 6 fps. Contact and slip labels derived from sensor API (slip is rare, 0.93% of data).
- **Implementation:** V-JEPA 2 visual backbone, pretrained FG-CLTP tactile encoder (frozen). Dynamics predictor is a spatio-temporal transformer. ViT-based decoders for fair visual comparison (used only for eval/viz). Training on 8 H100 GPUs.
- **Planning Parameters:** 9-frame context window (1.5s). 50-step control episodes. CEM: N=400 candidates, H_p=6 planning horizon, K=8 elites, I=8 iterations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on three contact-rich manipulation tasks (chip grasping, fruit grasping, and USB insertion) provide quantitative and qualitative evidence for FeelWorld's performance.

**A. Visual Prediction Evaluation**
FeelWorld demonstrated improvements in visual prediction quality and temporal consistency compared to visual-only baselines and ablations.
- **10-Step Rollout Quality (Table I):** FeelWorld achieved a 10-step LPIPS of 0.058, reducing it by 31.0% compared to the visual-only V-JEPA 2 baseline (0.084). It also showed improvements in PSNR (28.89 vs 24.37), SSIM (0.884 vs 0.819), FID (76.45 vs 106.7), and FVD (289.9 vs 473.5).
- **Contribution of Components:** Ablation studies confirmed the benefits of FeelWorld's design:
 - Naive visuo-tactile fusion (without hierarchical modeling or contact gate) improved LPIPS from 0.084 to 0.071, indicating that tactile observations provide complementary information.
 - Explicit hierarchical tactile modeling further reduced LPIPS to 0.067.
 - The contact-gated asymmetric attention mechanism provided the most significant improvement, reducing LPIPS to 0.058, suggesting it effectively mitigated noise and selectively integrated tactile data.
- **Prediction Around Contact Transitions (Table II):** FeelWorld exhibited more robust visual prediction during critical contact transitions. While V-JEPA 2's LPIPS increased by 42.7% (from 0.089 in free space to 0.127 at contact onset), FeelWorld's LPIPS increased only from 0.046 to 0.055, remaining 56.7% lower than V-JEPA 2 during these phases. FeelWorld also achieved a lower contact-onset error (0.44 frames) compared to a vision-only model (1.17 frames).
- **Long-Horizon Autoregressive Prediction (Fig. 5):** FeelWorld maintained superior performance over extended rollouts (up to 80 steps, or 13.3 seconds). Its LPIPS increased from 0.055 at step 1 to 0.062 at step 80, remaining 61% lower than the visual-only baseline. This indicates that contact-gated attention and joint visuo-tactile modeling contribute to stable and consistent long-horizon predictions by capturing fine-grained physical dynamics.

**B. Tactile State Prediction**
The model demonstrated high accuracy in predicting the hierarchical tactile states.
- **Contact State (Table III):** FeelWorld achieved 98.6% accuracy and 98.1% F1 score for contact prediction, with a low contact-onset error of 0.44 frames and an offset error of 0.69 frames.
- **Slip State (Table III):** Despite the rarity of slip events (0.93% of data), FeelWorld achieved 83.4% F1, 81.7% precision, and 85.2% recall for slip prediction, with 99.7% overall accuracy. This indicates reliable detection of critical but infrequent events.
- **Qualitative Visualization (Fig. 6):** Visualizations of a USB insertion task showed correct prediction of contact and slip states, with the model accurately identifying slip risk. The gated attention mechanism was observed to suppress visuo-tactile cross-attention during free-space motion and activate it during contact, selectively incorporating tactile information.

**C. Zero-Shot Planning Results**
FeelWorld, using its contact-aware CEM planner, significantly improved success rates in zero-shot contact-rich manipulation tasks.
- **Planning Success Rates (Table IV):**
 - FeelWorld (Contact-aware CEM) achieved an average success rate of 81.7% across the three tasks.
 - This compares to 49.2% for the Visual-only CEM (V-JEPA 2) and 57.5% for Naive Visuo-Tactile CEM.
 - Specific task performance: chip grasping (82.5%), fruit grasping (87.5%), and USB insertion (75.0%). Notably, USB insertion success rate improved from 37.5% (vision-only) to 75.0%.
- **Planning Objective Convergence (Fig. 7):** The contact-gated CEM effectively guided the planner. Visual-only planning could achieve visually plausible configurations but often failed to ensure stable contact. Naive visuo-tactile planning, by activating tactile objectives prematurely, interfered with free-space approach. The contact-gated approach maintained visual progress initially and then transitioned to joint visuo-tactile optimization upon predicted contact, leading to more successful task execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FeelWorld introduces a framework that addresses key challenges in integrating multimodal sensing for intelligent robotic manipulation, with several significant implications:

1. **Enhanced Physical Consistency in World Models:** By explicitly modeling and predicting hierarchical tactile states (contact, 3D tactile latent, and slip), FeelWorld moves beyond purely visual appearance dynamics. This allows world models to generate imagined futures that are not only visually plausible but also physically consistent, which is crucial for robots interacting with the physical world. This represents a step towards more physically grounded AI for robotics.

2. **Effective and Principled Multimodal Fusion:** The proposed contact-gated asymmetric attention mechanism offers a solution to the inherent asymmetry of visual and tactile signals. By suppressing irrelevant tactile noise during free-space motion and enabling visuo-tactile fusion only when contact is predicted, the model optimizes the utilization of each modality. This approach could be generalized to other multimodal systems where information relevance varies based on interaction context.

3. **Improved Performance in Contact-Rich Manipulation:** The significant improvements in zero-shot planning success rates, particularly in challenging tasks like USB insertion under occlusion or grasping fragile objects, demonstrate the practical value of FeelWorld. The ability to reason accurately about contact geometry, grasp stability, and incipient slip directly translates to more reliable and robust robotic task execution in real-world scenarios. This can accelerate the deployment of robots in domains requiring fine motor skills and sensitive interaction.

4. **Foundation for Future Robotic Learning:** FeelWorld provides a general framework for incorporating tactile sensing into world models, offering a blueprint for future research in multimodal robot learning. The explicit hierarchical structure of tactile states could inform the design of more interpretable and controllable robot policies. The robust long-horizon prediction capabilities could also serve as a basis for long-duration autonomy and complex sequential tasks.

5. **Addressing Computational Limitations:** While the current CEM-based planning is computationally intensive, the paper explicitly acknowledges this limitation and suggests integration with policy networks for high-frequency control and iterative refinement using real-world interaction data as future work. This indicates a pathway towards making the advanced predictive capabilities of FeelWorld practical for real-time applications.

In summary, FeelWorld contributes to the field by providing a structured and effective method for fusing visual and tactile information within an action-conditioned world model, leading to improved physical understanding, more accurate future state prediction, and enhanced planning capabilities for contact-rich robotic tasks.
