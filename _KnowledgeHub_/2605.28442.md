# Self-Supervised Online Robot-Agnostic Traversability Estimation for Open-World Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Freiburg developed COTRATE, a self-supervised framework for online robot traversability estimation that learns from proprioceptive data to create robot-agnostic terrain scores, enhancing navigation efficiency by up to 2.1 units of path effort and demonstrating cross-platform knowledge transfer.

## Problem

- Mobile robots struggle with online adaptation to continuously changing, novel terrains in unstructured, open-world environments.
- Existing self-supervised traversability methods often rely on robot-specific heuristics or handcrafted scores, limiting their generalizability and transferability across different platforms.
- Continual learning for visual traversability models typically incurs substantial memory and computational overhead, making it impractical for resource-constrained robotic platforms.

## Method

- **COTRATE** uses a compact Variational Auto-Encoder (==VAE==) with Vicreg and incremental losses to generate continuous, robot-agnostic traversability scores from multimodal proprioceptive and inertial sensor data.
- A visual traversability estimation model, leveraging a frozen DINOv3 backbone and a custom decoder, aligns monocular visual features with these dynamically learned traversability assessments using a novel multimodal alignment loss.
- An efficient continual learning strategy, employing diversity-aware selective feature replay with furthest-point sampling and Feature Cut-Mix, mitigates catastrophic forgetting using minimal memory and computational resources.

## Results

- COTRATE reduced navigation path effort by an average of 0.7 units for the Spot robot and approximately 2.1 units for the Husky robot, outperforming five self-supervised baselines and a zero-shot semantic segmentation baseline.
- The framework achieved superior 2.5D and 2D segmentation mIoU, surpassing baselines by at least **2.5** percentage points for Spot and **2.1** percentage points for Husky, accurately predicting energy-efficient terrain preferences.
- The learned traversability representation demonstrated cross-platform transferability, with zero-shot models (trained on one robot, deployed on another) sometimes outperforming platform-specific continually learned models in certain navigation environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Vicreg loss is critical for organizing the VAE's latent embedding space, leading to traversability scores that correlate robustly with energy consumption and enhance feature stability.
> - DINOv3-based visual features significantly improve ground segmentation and terrain mask generation compared to object-oriented or semantic segmentation distillation methods, crucial for accurate visual supervision.
> - Selective feature replay, combined with Feature Cut-Mix, effectively mitigates catastrophic forgetting during online adaptation, enabling long-term learning with substantially reduced memory and computational overhead by replaying compact features instead of raw images.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Mobile robot navigation in unstructured, open-world environments, such as off-road settings for agriculture or forestry, requires robust traversability estimation to ensure safe and energy-efficient motion planning. Traditional approaches often rely on static models trained offline, which are insufficient for environments where robots continuously encounter novel terrain types, varying surface conditions, and dynamic robot-terrain interactions. The ability to adapt online is a significant challenge in this field.

Self-supervised learning has emerged as a promising direction, leveraging proprioceptive and inertial sensor data that directly reflect robot-terrain interactions, providing physical traversal cost cues like motion irregularity, slip, vibration, and energy expenditure without manual annotation. These signals can supervise visual models to predict terrain cost before physical interaction. However, existing self-supervised traversability methods face several limitations. Some approaches derive traversability labels from handcrafted proprioceptive criteria (e.g., traction estimates, vibration intensity). While effective in specific scenarios, these criteria are frequently tied to particular robot morphologies, sensor configurations, or locomotion mechanisms, hindering robot-agnosticism and cross-platform knowledge transfer. Other methods learn terrain representations by clustering inertial, proprioceptive, or tactile sensor streams offline, often requiring pre-collected datasets and human ratings. This offline dependency prevents online adaptation to continuously evolving open-world environments.

Another critical challenge in this domain is continual learning for visual traversability models. As robots explore new terrain appearances, the visual model must incorporate new information while retaining previously acquired knowledge. A naive online update strategy typically leads to catastrophic forgetting. While various continual learning techniques exist, many incur substantial memory and computational costs, such as storing raw data for replay or training generative replay models. Such resource demands often preclude onboard deployment on mobile robotic platforms with limited computational budgets.

This research addresses these identified gaps by proposing a unified framework that aims to achieve online, self-supervised traversability estimation that is robot-agnostic, adaptable to novel terrains, and supports continual learning with minimal computational and memory overhead. It further investigates the transferability of learned traversability representations across different robot platforms, a capability often limited by platform-dependent scoring methods in prior work.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulties in deploying mobile robots autonomously in complex, dynamic, and previously unknown off-road environments. Reliable traversability estimation is crucial for such robots to plan safe and energy-efficient trajectories. However, several limitations in existing methods necessitate a new approach:

1. **Lack of Online Adaptation to Open-World Environments:** Robots operating in the open world frequently encounter novel terrains and conditions that cannot be fully characterized by models trained exclusively offline. There is a need for methods that can continuously learn and adapt from ongoing experiences.
2. **Limited Robot-Agnosticism and Transferability:** Many self-supervised traversability methods rely on handcrafted proprioceptive scores that are specific to a particular robot's morphology, sensor suite, or locomotion mechanism. This limits their generalizability across different robot platforms and impedes the transfer of learned experience, requiring re-calibration or re-training for each new robot.
3. **Inadequate Capture of Energy-Efficient Traversal:** While some methods ensure safe navigation by identifying non-traversable areas, they often do not explicitly prioritize energy-efficient paths, which is critical for extended autonomous operation in remote environments. Proprioceptive feedback, which can capture physical traversal costs like energy expenditure, offers a promising avenue for this.
4. **Computational and Memory Overhead in Continual Learning:** For a visual traversability model to continually adapt to new terrain appearances, it must integrate new supervision without forgetting previously acquired knowledge. Traditional continual learning strategies often demand significant memory (e.g., storing raw data) or computational resources (e.g., generative replay models), making them impractical for onboard deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of this work, as articulated by the authors, are:

- **Develop a novel robot-agnostic, online, self-supervised terrain assessment module:** This module is designed to generate continuous, robust traversability scores solely from multimodal proprioceptive and inertial sensor data, thereby removing the dependency on robot-specific heuristics or human labels.
- **Establish a visual traversability estimation framework:** This framework aims to align monocular visual features, derived from a pre-trained visual foundation model, with the dynamically learned multimodal traversability assessments. This is achieved through a novel alignment loss that correlates visual feature similarities with the continuous traversability scores.
- **Propose an efficient continual learning strategy:** The goal is to mitigate catastrophic forgetting during online adaptation of the visual model with minimal memory and computational overhead. This involves a diversity-aware feature selection strategy to maintain a compact yet representative replay memory.
- **Investigate cross-platform knowledge transfer:** The research aims to demonstrate that the learned traversability representation can be transferred effectively across different robotic platforms, even those with distinct locomotion kinematics, provided their terrain-dependent energy-efficiency ratings are compatible.
- **Conduct comprehensive evaluation and public release:** Evaluate the proposed framework extensively on a new, diverse outdoor dataset and validate its performance in navigation tasks across multiple environments and robot platforms. The dataset, code, and trained models are to be made publicly available to support further research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, COTRATE (Continual Online Transferable Robot-Agnostic Traversability Estimation), integrates several modules to achieve self-supervised, online, and robot-agnostic traversability estimation.

**A. Problem Definition and Data Handling**
COTRATE processes a continuous stream of sensor data, including RGB images, point clouds, and proprioceptive/inertial measurements. It learns normalized traversability scores (T_S ∈) periodically from these multimodal inputs. These scores are then projected onto image coordinates via odometry to generate supervision signals for a visual traversability model. Both the sensor-based score generation model and the visual model are updated online using a single training epoch per terrain encounter. For navigation, per-image traversability scores are back-projected onto 3D point clouds and aggregated into a 2.5D elevation map.

**B. Data Collection and Pre-Processing**
A multimodal off-road dataset was collected using two distinct robotic platforms: Boston Dynamics Spot (a quadruped) and Clearpath Husky (a wheeled robot). Data was gathered across 11 diverse terrain types over four months, encompassing varying lighting and weather conditions. The dataset includes approximately 50,000 images, alongside LiDAR, IMU, and robot-internal proprioceptive sensor data, all recorded in ROS bag format with synchronized timestamps. Odometry is computed using Fast-LIO2.

**C. Self-Supervised Traversability Score (T_S) Generation**
This module, operating on proprioceptive and inertial signals, is central to generating robot-agnostic traversability scores.
1. **Sensor Data Alignment:** Sensor readings (IMU, joint motors, odometry, velocity commands) are temporarily aligned and linearly interpolated. The synchronized data is then partitioned into fixed-size time windows (X_t ∈ R^(W×S), where W is window size, S is sensor dimensionality).
2. **Variational Auto-Encoder (VAE):** A compact VAE is incrementally trained on these time-series data. The encoder consists of two CNN layers with ReLU activation, adaptive average pooling, and two parallel linear layers to output mean (P_t ∈ R^(1×L)) and variance (σ_t) of the latent space. The decoder uses two CNN layers with ReLU to reconstruct the sensor data.
3. **Loss Functions for VAE:**
 - **Kullback-Leibler (L_KL) Loss:** Smoothes latent feature distributions.
 - **Reconstruction (L_REC) Loss:** An MSE-based loss on the decoder output and the last sensor reading of the input sequence, focusing on temporal dynamics and periodicity.
 - **Vicreg (L_VIC) Loss:** A non-contrastive representation learning method that minimizes the distance between correlated features (temporally adjacent samples) while ensuring sufficient variance among batch samples, using a hinge loss. This loss is hypothesized to organize the embedding space such that similarly "felt" terrains cluster together.
 - **Incremental (L_INC) Loss:** An MSE-based loss that ensures consistent features during online learning by replaying past sensor data (with a 20:80 old-to-new ratio). Given the small memory footprint of sensor data, full replay is feasible.
 - **Total Loss:** L = αL_KL + βL_REC + γL_VIC + (α+β+γ)/3 * L_INC.
4. **Score Computation:** After VAE training, a reference terrain 'A' (e.g., smooth asphalt) is defined from a brief period of robot navigation. Traversability scores (T_S,t) are computed as the normalized cosine similarity between a learned latent feature P_t and the mean reference feature P_A. This calibration produces scores hypothesized to correlate with energy consumption.

**D. Pixel-wise Visual Traversability (T_V) Generation**
This step converts the learned experience into visual supervision.
1. **Visual Footprints (T_F):** The self-supervised traversability scores (T_S) are projected onto image coordinates using odometry, robot center of mass, and camera calibration.
2. **Terrain Masks (T_M):** A frozen DINOv3 encoder (ViT-backbone) is used to extract visual features. These features are then grouped into fine-grained terrain segments by identifying regions with cosine similarity greater than a threshold (c=0.95). This method is stated to perform better for ground segmentation than object-oriented models like Fast-SAM or other semantic segmentation approaches like Stego.
3. **Final Traversability Mask (T_V):** The final pixel-wise visual traversability mask is computed by averaging the visual footprints (T_F) within each segment of the terrain masks (T_M).

**E. Visual Traversability Estimation Model**
1. **Architecture:** The visual network consists of a frozen ViT-backbone with DINOv3 pre-training (used for T_M generation) as the encoder. A custom decoder comprises two CNN layers with batch normalization and ReLU activations, outputting C-dimensional features (F ∈ R^(D×C)).
2. **Multimodal Alignment Loss (L_align):** This loss enforces that the cosine similarity between the current image's visual features (F_t) and the mean reference visual features (F_A) matches the learned pixel-wise traversability scores (T_V,t). The loss is an MSE between the predicted similarity (rescaled to) and the ground-truth T_V,t. This ensures visual features reflect relative terrain difficulty.
3. **Continual Terrain Learning:** To mitigate catastrophic forgetting, a selective feature replay strategy is introduced.
 - **Diversity-Aware Feature Selection:** Instead of replaying raw images, representative visual encoder features are stored in a replay buffer (B_R). Features are selected using furthest-point sampling (FPS) from a temporary buffer (B_T) of incoming visual terrain features to span the feature space effectively.
 - **Replay Loss (L_replay):** This MSE loss enforces consistent predictions from the decoder for the replayed features. It is applied periodically to retain knowledge of past terrains.
 - **Feature Cut-Mix (FCM):** To address sparsity in semi-supervised training, FCM replaces features in unannotated image regions with features from B_R, complementing L_replay.

**F. Traversability-Aware Navigation**
1. **3D Voxel Map Construction:** Time-synchronized LiDAR scans are projected onto the camera-based traversability predictions (T_V) to obtain point-wise traversability scores. These scores are then aggregated into a 3D voxel map and subsequently reduced to a 2.5D elevation map, incorporating ground plane estimation via RANSAC.
2. **Path Planning:** An A* search algorithm is used for path planning. The cost function for each edge (R_edge) integrates geometric distance (horizontal and elevation change) with the average traversability of adjacent cells (T_G), penalizing low-traversability regions. The heuristic uses Euclidean distance.
3. **Evaluation Metrics:** Navigation efficiency is assessed using "path effort" (U_effort), computed as the sum of terrain-specific average motor current (normalized) weighted by distance traveled, and "Effort Weighted Path Length" (EPL). Prediction quality is measured by 2.5D and 2D segmentation mIoU against manually annotated test images and grid maps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of COTRATE was conducted on a dataset of approximately 50,000 images across 11 outdoor terrains and two robotic platforms (Spot and Husky), including navigation experiments in three representative outdoor environments.

**A. Overall Performance and Navigation Efficiency:**
- COTRATE demonstrated superior performance compared to five self-supervised traversability baselines (WVN, I-MOST, Image replay, VAE-based replay) and LangSAM (a zero-shot semantic segmentation baseline) across all tested scenes and both robot platforms in terms of navigation effort and segmentation accuracy.
- For the Spot robot, COTRATE reduced the path effort by an average of 0.7 units compared to the best baseline. For the Husky robot, this reduction was approximately 2.1 units. These reductions in path effort and Effort Weighted Path Length (EPL) indicate that COTRATE effectively selects more energy-efficient routes.
- In terms of 2.5D and 2D segmentation mIoU, COTRATE surpassed baselines by at least 2.5 percentage points (pp) for Spot and 2.1 pp (2.5D mIoU) for Husky, demonstrating reliable prediction of energy-efficient terrain preferences.
- In certain instances (Husky E1, Spot E3), COTRATE even outperformed joint training models (trained on all data offline), which the authors attribute to the heterogeneous data distribution and reduced interference between very dissimilar terrains during online learning.

**B. Continual Learning Capabilities:**
- COTRATE and image-based feature replay approaches exhibited strong continual learning capabilities, maintaining consistent predictions over time. COTRATE achieved the best overall performance while incurring lower memory costs.
- Baselines such as I-MOST and VAE-based replay showed larger fluctuations in performance during continual learning, indicating less effective knowledge retention or greater susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting, particularly when new, visually similar terrains (e.g., different types of grass) were introduced.

**C. Cross-Platform Transferability:**
- The learned traversability representation showed evidence of cross-platform transferability. In some navigation environments (Husky E2, Spot E3), the zero-shot model (trained on one robot and deployed on another) even outperformed the platform-specific continually learned model. This suggests that COTRATE's traversability scores are well-calibrated and can effectively transfer across robotic embodiments with compatible terrain-dependent energy efficiency ratings.

**D. Ablation Studies:**
- **Effect of Loss Functions for Score Creation:** The Vicreg (L_VIC) loss was found to be critical, improving the Pearson correlation with energy efficiency by 0.46 on Spot and 0.57 on Husky, and boosting the stability of the feature space. The incremental learning loss (L_INC) also contributed to stability and reduced pairwise terrain overlap, particularly on Husky.
- **Effect of Self-Supervised Traversability Score and Visual Terrain Mask:** COTRATE's self-supervised scores consistently yielded the lowest-effort paths and higher IoU scores compared to handcrafted scores derived from vibration, traction, or raw motor torque. The handcrafted methods were found to be less reliable and poorly correlated with energy efficiency. The DINOv3-based terrain masking approach in COTRATE also significantly outperformed object-oriented (Fast-SAM) and segmentation distillation (Stego) methods, particularly in handling uneven terrain boundaries.
- **Ablation of Visual Model Components:** The proposed alignment loss (L_align) demonstrated better generalization and reduced path effort by 26% compared to a direct regression approach with a larger decoder. The selective feature replay strategy substantially improved mIoU metrics (24.5 pp for 2D segm., 27.1 pp for 2.5D segm.) by mitigating catastrophic forgetting. The feature cut-mix (FCM) strategy further enhanced navigation efficiency (0.04 EPL, 1.82 effort reduction) by stabilizing semi-supervised training.
- **Replay Buffer Settings:** Optimal performance was observed with a replay interval of 1 iteration, a buffer size of 200 features, and an update frequency of 100 iterations. Deviations from these optimal settings, such as longer replay intervals or inappropriate buffer sizes, led to decreased segmentation performance due to increased forgetting, insufficient diversity, or instability in feature selection.

**E. Qualitative Results:**
- Qualitative evaluations showed that COTRATE robustly differentiated similar terrains (e.g., asphalt vs. fine gravel) and produced smoother, more consistent traversability scores across terrain segments compared to baselines like LangSAM, which sometimes showed mixed areas at edges. COTRATE's path planning consistently favored energy-efficient routes, avoiding high-cost terrains like dense grass or challenging gravel, unlike some baselines that navigated longer distances on less efficient surfaces.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotic autonomy by addressing several long-standing challenges in traversability estimation for open-world environments.

**A. Enhanced Robot Autonomy in Unstructured Environments:**
COTRATE enables mobile robots to continuously learn and adapt their navigation behavior from unlabeled experiences in complex, dynamic, and unknown off-road settings. This capability is critical for applications in agriculture, forestry, search and rescue, disaster response, and planetary exploration, where robots must operate safely and efficiently without prior detailed maps or extensive human intervention.

**B. Online, Robot-Agnostic, and Self-Supervised Learning:**
The development of an online, self-supervised terrain assessment module that derives continuous traversability scores from multimodal proprioceptive and inertial data is a significant methodological advancement. By removing reliance on handcrafted, robot-specific heuristics or human labels, the framework promotes robot-agnosticism, making the learned models more generalizable across different robotic platforms and reducing the effort required for system deployment.

**C. Efficient Continual Learning:**
The proposed diversity-aware selective feature replay strategy offers an efficient solution to the problem of catastrophic forgetting in online learning scenarios. By operating on compact visual features rather than raw images and utilizing furthest-point sampling, it significantly reduces the memory and computational overhead typically associated with continual learning. This makes onboard deployment on resource-constrained robots more feasible, supporting long-term adaptation.

**D. Cross-Platform Knowledge Transfer:**
The demonstrated ability to transfer learned traversability representations across robots with different locomotion kinematics (e.g., quadrupedal Spot to wheeled Husky) when their energy-efficiency ratings are compatible has substantial practical implications. This capability can accelerate development and deployment by allowing knowledge gained from one platform to benefit others, fostering a more unified approach to terrain understanding.

**E. Foundation for Future Research:**
The comprehensive framework, encompassing robust score generation, visual-multimodal alignment, and efficient continual learning, provides a solid foundation for future research in several areas, including multi-robot learning, predictive traversability, and adaptive control strategies based on learned terrain costs.

**F. Community Contribution:**
The public release of the collected dataset, code, and trained models facilitates reproducibility, enables benchmarking of new methods, and fosters collaborative research within the robotics community.
