# MPViT: Multi-Path Vision Transformer for Dense Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from ETRI and KAIST developed MPViT, a Vision Transformer architecture that enhances multi-scale feature representation for dense prediction tasks through a novel multi-scale patch embedding and multi-path processing. It achieves state-of-the-art results across image classification, object detection, and segmentation benchmarks, often with fewer parameters and FLOPs than prior ViTs.

## Problem

- Dense computer vision tasks require effective multi-scale feature representation to accurately process objects of widely varying sizes.
- Existing Vision Transformers (ViTs) often use single-scale patches at each feature level, limiting multi-scale diversity, or incur high computational overhead for complex multi-scale interactions.
- Traditional ViTs may overlook fine-grained local relationships, an area where Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) historically excel due to their local connectivity inductive bias.

## Method

- Introduces a multi-stage architecture with a ==Multi-Scale Patch Embedding (MS-PatchEmbed)== module, which simultaneously tokenizes visual patches of different sizes at the same feature level using overlapping convolutional operations.
- Employs ==Multi-Path Transformer (MP-Transformer)== blocks featuring parallel independent Transformer encoders for each patch scale, combined with a ==Global-to-Local Feature Interaction (GLI)== module.
- Leverages efficient ==factorized self-attention== and a 'deeper and thinner' model scaling strategy to manage computational costs while maximizing performance across multiple processing paths.

## Results

- MPViT-Small achieves **83.0%** Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K with **22.8M** parameters and **4.7 GFLOPs**, surpassing several larger and more computationally intensive Vision Transformers.
- On COCO object detection with RetinaNet, MPViT-S obtains **47.6%** AP, outperforming Swin-T and Focal-T by **2.1-2.6%**, and on Mask R-CNN, MPViT-S reaches **43.9%** mask AP.
- For ADE20K semantic segmentation using UperNet, MPViT-S achieves **48.3%** mIoU, outperforming Swin-T, Focal-T, and XCiT-S12/16 by margins of **3.8%**, **2.5%**, and **2.4%** respectively.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Processing truly multi-scale patches concurrently within a Transformer block, rather than relying solely on hierarchical stages, leads to richer and more diverse feature representations for dense prediction.
> - A hybrid Global-to-Local Feature Interaction module that concatenates and learns interactions between convolutional local features and Transformer's global features effectively combines their respective strengths.
> - For multi-path Transformer architectures, a 'deeper and thinner' configuration (more layers, fewer channels per path) is more effective than wider, shallower designs for improving accuracy while managing computational resources.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of MPViT is situated at the intersection of two major paradigms in computer vision: Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs), particularly in the context of *dense prediction tasks* such as object detection, instance segmentation, and semantic segmentation.

Historically, CNNs have been the dominant architecture for these tasks, excelling due to their inherent inductive biases like local connectivity and translation invariance. Many successful CNN architectures (e.g., Inception, VoVNet, HRNet) achieve high performance on dense prediction by leveraging multi-scale feature representations—either through multi-grained convolutional kernels at the same feature level or by aggregating features across different resolutions. This ability to process information at various scales is crucial for detecting or classifying objects of widely varying sizes within an image.

More recently, Vision Transformers, inspired by their success in Natural Language Processing (NLP), have emerged as a powerful alternative, often surpassing CNNs in various vision tasks. Initial ViTs (e.g., the original ViT) typically treat images as sequences of fixed-size patches, leading to a single-stage, monolithic architecture. While effective for image classification, these early ViTs struggled with dense prediction tasks that require high-resolution feature maps and multi-scale understanding, primarily due to the quadratic computational complexity of self-attention with respect to sequence length.

The research landscape for ViTs in dense prediction has since evolved, with numerous "ViT-variants" attempting to address these challenges. Approaches include:
- **Reducing Quadratic Complexity:** Many works (e.g., Swin Transformer, Focal Transformer) constrain the attention range to local windows or use sparse global attention, or reduce sequence length through spatial reduction.
- **Multi-stage Hierarchies:** Like CNNs, several ViTs (e.g., Swin, PVT) adopt multi-stage, fine-to-coarse pyramid structures to generate multi-scale features, albeit often using single-scale patches at each stage.
- **Cross-layer Attention:** Models like CoaT introduce mechanisms like cross-layer attention to represent fine and coarse features simultaneously, but often incur heavy computational and memory overhead.
- **Single-stage Structures with Post-processing:** Some (e.g., XCiT) maintain a single-stage structure and add down/up-sampling layers *after* pre-training for multi-scale feature extraction, which may not be optimal for dense prediction from the outset.

MPViT differentiates itself by focusing on a more fundamental aspect of multi-scale representation *within a given feature level*, drawing direct inspiration from CNNs' multi-grained kernel concept. Unlike many existing ViTs that rely on a simple fine-to-coarse multi-stage structure with single-scale patches or computationally heavy cross-layer attention, MPViT proposes a novel way to embed and process *multiple scales of patches simultaneously* at each feature level through a multi-path structure. This approach aims to efficiently build richer, more diverse multi-scale feature representations directly into the Transformer backbone, thereby enhancing performance for dense prediction tasks without the heavy overhead of some prior methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind MPViT stems from the critical requirement of dense computer vision tasks for effective multi-scale feature representation. Objects and regions in images can vary dramatically in size, from small details to large foreground elements. To accurately detect, segment, or classify these entities, a model must be able to process and integrate information at different granularities.

The authors identify a gap in existing Vision Transformer (ViT) architectures regarding this multi-scale capability:
1. **Limited Multi-Scale Embedding:** Most ViTs, even those with multi-stage pyramid structures, typically use single-scale patches (tokens) at each feature level. This limits the diversity of features extracted at a given resolution.
2. **Computational Overhead:** While some advanced ViTs (like CoaT) attempt more sophisticated multi-scale interactions, they often introduce significant computational and memory costs, hindering their scalability and practical deployment.
3. **Ignoring Local Context:** Transformers, by design, excel at capturing global long-range dependencies but can sometimes overlook fine-grained local relationships and structural information, an area where CNNs traditionally thrive due to their local connectivity inductive bias.

Inspired by the success of CNN architectures such as Inception Network or VoVNet, which utilize multi-grained convolution kernels at the same feature level to generate diverse receptive fields, the authors propose to adapt this powerful concept to Vision Transformers.

Therefore, the **key objectives** of this work are:
- To design a Vision Transformer architecture that can effectively represent multi-scale features for dense prediction tasks.
- To achieve this by introducing a novel **multi-scale patch embedding** mechanism that tokenizes visual patches of different sizes simultaneously at the same feature level.
- To process these multi-scale tokens through a **multi-path structure** within Transformer encoders, allowing for parallel learning of fine and coarse features.
- To integrate the strengths of both CNNs (local connectivity) and Transformers (global context) through a **global-to-local feature interaction (GLI)** module.
- To demonstrate that the proposed Multi-Path Vision Transformer (MPViT) can serve as a versatile and efficient backbone, achieving superior performance across various vision tasks (image classification, object detection, instance segmentation, and semantic segmentation) compared to state-of-the-art ViTs, while maintaining competitive model size and computational complexity.

In essence, the motivation is to overcome the limitations of current ViTs in capturing diverse multi-scale information efficiently, thereby making Transformers more robust and versatile for complex dense prediction scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MPViT introduces a novel architecture built upon three core ideas: a multi-stage structure for hierarchical features, multi-scale patch embedding, and a multi-path Transformer block incorporating global-to-local feature interaction.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

MPViT adopts a **multi-stage architecture** with four hierarchical stages, similar to successful CNN and some ViT backbones (e.g., Swin Transformer), to generate feature maps of different scales.
- **Convolutional Stem Block:** An initial convolutional stem (inspired by LeViT) processes the input image. This stem, consisting of two 3x3 convolutional layers, helps in extracting robust low-level features and downsamples the image, providing a strong initial representation without losing salient information, a common advantage of convolutions over simple non-overlapping patch embeddings.
- **Transformer Encoders:** From stage 2 to stage 5, the architecture stacks proprietary Multi-Scale Patch Embedding (MS-PatchEmbed) and Multi-Path Transformer (MP-Transformer) blocks.
- **Factorized Self-Attention:** To manage the computational cost, especially with higher resolution features in a multi-stage setting, MPViT employs the efficient factorized self-attention mechanism from CoaT, which has linear complexity with respect to the number of tokens.
- **No [CLS] Token:** For simplicity and efficiency, the authors replace the traditional [CLS] token (used for classification in many ViTs) with Global Average Pooling (GAP) of the final feature map, as this has been shown not to negatively impact performance.

#### 3.2. Multi-Scale Patch Embedding (MS-PatchEmbed)

This module is crucial for generating multi-scale features at the *same feature level*. Instead of a single fixed-size patch, MS-PatchEmbed simultaneously tokenizes visual patches of different sizes.
- **Overlapping Convolutional Operations:** It uses parallel convolution operations with varying kernel sizes (e.g., 3x3, 5x5, 7x7) to extract features. The key is that by appropriately adjusting the stride and padding, these operations can yield output features that have the *same sequence length* (resolution) but represent different scales of visual information.
- **Efficient Implementation:** To reduce parameters and computational overhead, instead of truly parallel large kernels (5x5, 7x7), the authors implement the multi-scale embedding using *consecutive 3x3 depthwise separable convolutions in series*. For example, two consecutive 3x3 convolutions mimic a 5x5 receptive field, and three mimic a 7x7, but with fewer parameters. Each convolution is followed by Batch Normalization and a Hardswish activation function.
- **Independent Paths:** The distinct scale tokens generated by these parallel embedding layers are then fed independently into separate Transformer encoders, forming multiple processing paths.

#### 3.3. Multi-Path Transformer (MP-Transformer) Block

This block processes the multi-scale tokens generated by MS-PatchEmbed.
- **Parallel Transformer Encoders:** Each path (corresponding to a different patch scale) has its own independent Transformer encoder. These encoders perform global self-attention on their respective tokens.
- **Global-to-Local Feature Interaction (GLI):** This module is designed to combine the strengths of Transformers (global context) and CNNs (local connectivity).
 - **Local Feature Extraction:** A depthwise residual bottleneck block (1x1 conv, 3x3 depthwise conv, 1x1 conv with residual connection) is used to extract local features from the input feature map, leveraging CNN's inductive biases.
 - **Feature Aggregation:** The local feature is *concatenated* with the global features produced by each parallel Transformer encoder. This concatenation allows for a richer interaction than simple summation.
 - **Interaction Learning:** A 1x1 convolution then processes the concatenated features, learning to interact between the diverse local and global representations and adjusting the channel dimension for the next stage.

#### 3.4. Model Configuration and Scaling

- **Path Dimension:** The authors extensively explored the number of paths. While dual-path offers improvements, a **triple-path structure** (except for stage 2, which uses dual-path due to higher resolution) was found to be most effective for dense prediction tasks under resource constraints.
- **Efficiency Strategy:** To manage the computational burden of multiple paths, the strategy was to reduce the *channel dimension (C)* rather than the *number of layers (L)* for each path. Ablation studies confirm that a "deeper and thinner" structure (more layers, fewer channels) is more effective.
- **Model Variants:** MPViT is scaled from Tiny (5M parameters) to Base (73M parameters), maintaining a consistent design philosophy. All models use 8 Transformer heads, and MLP expansion ratios are tuned for different sizes.

In summary, MPViT's methodology fundamentally rethinks multi-scale feature extraction in ViTs by introducing parallel processing of different patch scales from the outset, combined with a hybrid local-global feature interaction, all within an efficient multi-stage architecture.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MPViT demonstrates consistent and significant performance improvements across a wide range of computer vision tasks, establishing its versatility and efficiency as a backbone network.

#### 4.1. ImageNet-1K Classification

- MPViT models consistently surpass state-of-the-art Vision Transformers (such as DeiT, XCiT, Swin, CvT, Focal, CoaT, CrossViT, PVT) when trained with standard 224x224 resolution and without distillation or larger input sizes.
- **Efficiency Highlight:** MPViT-Small (22.8M parameters, 4.7 GFLOPs) achieves 83.0% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming CoaT Small (22M, 12.6 GFLOPs) which has significantly higher FLOPs, as well as many larger models like PVT-L, DeiT-B/16, and XCiT-M24/16.
- **Scalability:** MPViT-Base (74.8M parameters, 16.4 GFLOPs) reaches 84.3% accuracy, outperforming larger competitors like Swin-Base (88M) and Focal-Base (89M).

#### 4.2. COCO Object Detection and Instance Segmentation

Evaluated with RetinaNet and Mask R-CNN, MPViT shows superior performance for dense prediction.
- **RetinaNet (Object Detection):** MPViT-S achieves 47.6% AP, significantly outperforming Swin-T and Focal-T (by 2.1-2.6%). Notably, MPViT-S (32M parameters in RetinaNet) even surpasses much larger models like Swin-S/B and Focal-S/B on RetinaNet AP, highlighting its effective multi-scale representation for scale-invariant tasks.
- **Mask R-CNN (Instance Segmentation):** MPViT-XS and MPViT-S significantly outperform single-path baselines like CoaT-Lite Mini and Small. MPViT-S (43M parameters in Mask R-CNN) achieves 43.9% mask AP, which is higher than larger models like XCiT-M24/8 or Focal-B, while requiring substantially fewer FLOPs.
- **Scalability for Dense Prediction:** Unlike some baselines (Swin-B and Focal-B showed performance drops compared to their 'S' variants), MPViT-B consistently improves over MPViT-S, demonstrating MPViT's robust scalability for dense prediction.
- **Deformable DETR:** MPViT-Small also showed improvements over CoaT-Lite Small and CoaT Small on Deformable DETR, with a notable gain in small object AP.

#### 4.3. ADE20K Semantic Segmentation

Using UperNet, MPViT again demonstrates strong pixel-wise dense prediction capabilities.
- MPViT-S achieves 48.3% mIoU, outperforming Swin-T, Focal-T, and XCiT-S12/16 by large margins (3.8%, 2.5%, and 2.4% respectively). It also surpasses several larger models like Swin-S/B and Focal-S.
- MPViT-B achieves 50.3% mIoU, surpassing the larger Focal-B. These results further validate the power of MPViT's diverse multi-scale feature representation for tasks requiring fine-grained understanding.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Path Dimension:** Experiments show that expanding the path dimension (e.g., to triple-path) significantly improves dense prediction performance. Crucially, when increasing paths, reducing the *channel dimension (C)* while maintaining or slightly increasing the *number of layers (L)* ("deeper and thinner") leads to better accuracy than reducing L while keeping C high. Triple-path proved to be the optimal choice under comparable parameter/FLOPs constraints.
- **Multi-Scale Embedding Implementation:** Using consecutive 3x3 depthwise separable convolutions in series (to mimic 5x5, 7x7 receptive fields) was found to be more effective and parameter-efficient than using separate parallel 3x3, 5x5, 7x7 convolutions. This suggests that the non-linearities introduced by stacked convolutions are beneficial.
- **Global-to-Local Feature Interaction (GLI):** Concatenating the convolutional local features with the Transformer's global features (followed by a 1x1 convolution) significantly outperformed simple summation. This indicates that allowing the model to learn complex interactions from preserved individual features is key to enriching representations.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Analysis

Visualization of attention maps from different paths (path-1 for small patches, path-3 for large patches) revealed that:
- **Path-1 (fine patches):** Concentrates on smaller objects or fine textures.
- **Path-3 (coarse patches):** Focuses on larger objects and their boundaries, suppressing smaller objects and background noise.
- **Complementary Action:** The paths act in a complementary manner, enabling MPViT to capture objects across a wide range of scales, which is highly beneficial for dense prediction.

In summary, the findings robustly demonstrate MPViT's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance across diverse vision tasks, often with greater efficiency, largely attributed to its innovative multi-scale patch embedding and multi-path processing, combined with effective global-to-local feature interaction.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MPViT architecture represents a significant step forward in the design of Vision Transformers, particularly for dense prediction tasks, by effectively addressing the challenge of multi-scale feature representation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

#### 5.1. A Versatile and Efficient Backbone for Dense Prediction

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** MPViT consistently achieves superior results on a wide array of dense prediction tasks, including object detection, instance segmentation, and semantic segmentation, as well as image classification. This versatility positions it as a highly competitive and general-purpose backbone for various computer vision applications.
- **Efficiency:** A major highlight is MPViT's ability to outperform larger and more computationally intensive state-of-the-art Transformers (e.g., Swin, Focal, XCiT) while using fewer parameters and FLOPs. This efficiency is critical for deploying models in real-world scenarios with limited computational resources, such as mobile or edge devices. Its memory-efficient design, partly due to the strategy of reducing channel dimension, further enhances its practical appeal.

#### 5.2. Novel Approach to Multi-Scale Feature Learning in ViTs

- **Bridging CNN and ViT Strengths:** MPViT successfully integrates the strengths of CNNs (multi-grained receptive fields, local connectivity) and Transformers (global context, long-range dependencies) in a novel and synergistic manner. The multi-scale patch embedding directly tackles the need for diverse scales at a given feature level, a concept historically well-understood in CNNs but less explored in early ViTs.
- **Distinct from Existing ViT-variants:** Unlike approaches that rely solely on simple multi-stage pyramids with single-scale patches or heavy cross-layer attention, MPViT introduces a unique parallel multi-path processing of truly multi-scale patches, followed by intelligent feature aggregation. This offers a new paradigm for designing efficient and powerful ViT backbones.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Feature Representation

- **Complementary Attention:** The qualitative analysis of attention maps clearly demonstrates that different paths within MPViT learn to focus on different scales—fine paths capture small objects and textures, while coarse paths capture large objects and their precise boundaries. The aggregation of these complementary features leads to a richer and more comprehensive understanding of the visual scene, directly benefiting tasks sensitive to object scale.
- **Strong Inductive Biases:** By incorporating a convolutional stem and a Global-to-Local Feature Interaction module, MPViT implicitly injects beneficial inductive biases (like local connectivity) that are native to CNNs, mitigating a common criticism of "pure" Transformers lacking such biases.

#### 5.4. Impact on Future Research

- **Inspiration for Backbone Design:** MPViT's success will likely inspire future research into hybrid architectures and more sophisticated multi-scale processing within Transformers. The idea of parallel multi-scale patch embedding and path-specific processing offers a fertile ground for further exploration.
- **Rethinking Efficiency:** The ablation studies provide valuable insights into efficient Transformer design, particularly the finding that "deeper and thinner" structures (more layers, fewer channels) are preferable and that concatenation for feature interaction is superior to summation. These principles can guide the development of future efficient models.

#### 5.5. Acknowledged Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge a current limitation: the inference latency of MPViT can be slower than some counterparts (e.g., Swin-T, XCiT-S12/16) due to suboptimal GPU utilization arising from the multi-path structure (similar to issues with grouped convolutions). This is a practical challenge for real-time applications.

Their proposed future work addresses this directly:
- **Efficient Implementation:** Integrating features from different scales into a single tensor for multi-head self-attention could improve parallelization and GPU utilization, thereby speeding up inference.
- **Compound Scaling Strategy:** Exploring compound scaling, which optimizes depth, width, and resolution, will be crucial for striking the best balance between accuracy and speed across different model capacities.

Despite the current inference speed challenge, MPViT's fundamental contributions to efficient and effective multi-scale feature representation in Vision Transformers make it a significant advancement with strong potential to influence the development of next-generation vision backbones for a wide range of computer vision applications.
