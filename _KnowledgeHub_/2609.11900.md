# MindTopo: Can Foundation Models Reason in Topological Space?

> [!summary] Summary
> MindTopo is a benchmark of topological intuition in foundation models, covering five cognitively grounded properties (continuity, separation, order, enclosure, knots) at two levels: visual-QA reasoning and closed-loop planning in interactive gyms. It contains 11,030 procedurally generated instances across 13 task types with controllable difficulty. Across 14 MLLMs the best model reaches 61.42% task-macro versus 97.87% human, and every model plans worse than it reasons.

## Problem

- Spatial benchmarks for foundation models overwhelmingly test metric, viewpoint-dependent relations such as distance, direction, size and 3D location, ignoring deformation-invariant topological relations that cognitive science places developmentally first.
- Prior topology-specific work isolates a single structure such as path connectivity or knots, leaving unclear whether models reason across a broader set of topological relations.
- Existing evaluations score static question answering only, so they cannot tell whether recognizing an invariant transfers to selecting actions that build or preserve it.

## Method

- A five-property taxonomy from Piaget plus formal topology (==continuity==, ==separation==, ==order==, ==enclosure==, ==knots==), each probed at two cognitive levels: reasoning visual QA and planning as a closed-loop agent policy in an interactive gym.
- A four-stage automated generation pipeline (scene generation, programmatic topological annotation, difficulty control, instance generation) with fixed seeds and per-task acceptance checks, yielding 8,030 reasoning questions and 3,000 planning episodes.
- Agent configurations augmenting the planner with image or video generation for imagined rollouts, audited by a strict ==video-level computer-vision protocol== of static and dynamic checks yielding a ==process-valid== metric.

## Results

- Best model **GPT-5.6-Sol** averages **61.42%** versus **97.87%** human, dropping from **66.83%** reasoning to **52.75%** planning; open-weight models collapse, scoring **0%** on Pipe and One Stroke.
- On Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct, SFT then GRPO lifts the nine-task average from **8.00%** to **31.44%**, but reasoning rises to **51.53%** while planning reaches only **6.33%** and Pipe stays **0%**.
- Imagined rollouts help locally, not globally: image interleaving averages **68.6%** on Sheep yet **0%** on One Stroke, and across 1,560 audited video rollouts no generator is process-valid.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Static recognition scores overstate competence: a model can name an invariant yet fail to act on it, so benchmarks should pair reasoning with closed-loop planning.
> - Endpoint success never certifies a world model; generated rollouts reach plausible final states while violating dynamics along the way, so evaluate every transition, not the terminus.
> - Planning improves when task state is compact and discrete, and post-training transfers unevenly across topological primitives, so strength on one primitive predicts little about others.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Spatial reasoning is a fundamental aspect of intelligence, crucial for interacting with the physical world. Existing benchmarks for evaluating foundation models often focus on metric properties such as distance, angle, size, shape, and 3D location, or on viewpoint-dependent relationships. While recent work has touched on aspects like path connectivity or knot reasoning, a comprehensive evaluation of a broader set of topological relations, especially in dynamic, interactive contexts, has been largely unaddressed.

Cognitive science, particularly the work of Jean Piaget, posits that topological relations (e.g., continuity, separation, order, enclosure, knots) are foundational to human spatial understanding, preceding Euclidean and projective relations in cognitive development. Studies in adult vision also suggest that the visual system extracts topological invariants before metric features. This cognitive perspective highlights a potential gap in the capabilities of current foundation models, as their evaluations often do not specifically target these cognitively foundational, deformation-invariant properties.

This work addresses this gap by introducing a benchmark designed to systematically evaluate foundation models' "topological intuition"—their ability to perceive, infer, and operate on topological relations. By focusing on properties invariant under continuous deformation, MINDTOPO distinguishes itself from most existing spatial reasoning benchmarks that primarily test metric or viewpoint-dependent aspects. It extends prior work on specific topological tasks by consolidating five core properties within a single, scalable suite and evaluating them across both static reasoning and interactive planning contexts. This positions MINDTOPO as a diagnostic tool for understanding a specific, yet critical, facet of spatial intelligence in advanced AI models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate the extent to which current foundation models can reason about topological structure and plan actions that transform it. This overarching goal is motivated by several detailed considerations:

1. **Cognitive Science Grounding**: The authors draw heavily from developmental psychology, particularly Piaget's theory of spatial cognition, which identifies topological relations as primary and foundational to spatial understanding. They note that the human visual system prioritizes the extraction of topological invariants. This perspective suggests that a complete evaluation of a model's spatial intelligence should include these foundational abilities, which are currently under-represented in existing benchmarks.
2. **Addressing Gaps in Existing Benchmarks**: Current spatial reasoning benchmarks predominantly focus on metric or viewpoint-dependent properties (e.g., distance, angle, size, shape, 3D location). While valuable, these do not assess the understanding of intrinsic spatial relationships that remain constant despite continuous deformation. The authors highlight a need for a benchmark that specifically isolates and evaluates these topological invariants.
3. **Evaluating Both Declarative and Procedural Topological Knowledge**: The research aims to understand if models can not only *identify* topological relations (reasoning) but also *apply* this understanding to guide actions in dynamic environments (planning). This distinction is crucial because recognizing an invariant does not necessarily imply the ability to manipulate it or predict how interventions alter it. For example, a model might identify a knotted rope but fail to plan how to untangle it.
4. **Systematic and Scalable Evaluation**: A key motivation is to provide a structured and scalable benchmark. By defining five topological properties (continuity, separation, order, enclosure, and knots) grounded in cognitive science and formal topology, and developing procedural generators for tasks, the authors aim to create a rich and controllable environment for evaluation. The procedural generation allows for controllable difficulty and the creation of numerous instances, facilitating extensive benchmarking and future research.
5. **Diagnostic Tool for Foundation Model Development**: By uncovering specific strengths and weaknesses in topological intuition, the benchmark aims to serve as a diagnostic tool. It seeks to identify "blind spots" in current models, guiding future development towards AI systems that possess a more comprehensive and human-like understanding of physical space.

In essence, the research is motivated by the hypothesis that topological reasoning is a distinct and fundamental layer of spatial intelligence that is inadequately assessed in current foundation model evaluations, and that a dedicated benchmark is necessary to probe this capability across both static and interactive contexts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce **MINDTOPO**, a benchmark designed to evaluate topological intuition in foundation models across five properties and two cognitive levels, using 13 procedurally generated task types.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Design: Topological Properties and Cognitive Levels

MINDTOPO organizes its tasks around five topological properties, informed by Piaget's classification and formal topology:
- **Continuity**: Whether a path or surface forms an unbroken whole (e.g., navigating a maze, connecting pipes).
- **Separation**: The ability to distinguish distinct elements or components (e.g., identifying subassemblies, partitioning regions with a line).
- **Order**: The sequential arrangement of elements along a path or boundary (e.g., reading bead sequences, tracking points during folding, block permutation).
- **Enclosure**: The inside/outside relation defined by a closed boundary (e.g., identifying trapped animals, counting holes, trapping a moving agent).
- **Knots**: Entanglement that persists under deformation (e.g., classifying knots, untangling ropes).

Each property is evaluated at two cognitive levels:
- **Reasoning**: Models are presented with one or more rendered scenes and asked to identify a topological relation or infer how a specified change affects it (visual QA).
- **Planning**: Models act as closed-loop agents in interactive environments, receiving observations and selecting actions to build, preserve, or alter topological structures over a sequence of steps.

#### 3.2. Task Types and Data Generation

MINDTOPO comprises 13 task types (8 reasoning, 5 planning), instantiated across the five properties:
- **Continuity**: `2D Maze` (R), `3D Maze` (R), `Pipe` (P)
- **Separation**: `Assembly` (R), `One Stroke` (P)
- **Order**: `Bead` (R), `Origami Point` (R), `Swap 2D Puzzle` (P)
- **Enclosure**: `Sheep` (R), `Hole` (R), `Chat Noir` (P)
- **Knots**: `Knots` (R), `Untangle` (P)

The entire dataset of 11,030 instances (8,030 reasoning questions, 3,000 planning episodes) is generated through a fully automated four-stage pipeline:
1. **Scene Generation**: Parametric scene states are sampled and rendered using simulators (e.g., Three.js, ProcTHOR) or rendering scripts.
2. **Topological Annotation**: Reference answers or success conditions are programmatically derived from the generated state and metadata (e.g., graph search, geometric membership tests). No human annotation is used for ground truth.
3. **Difficulty Control**: Parameters (e.g., grid size, wall count, crossing count) are varied to produce Easy, Medium, and Hard instances. Rejection sampling filters out trivially solvable or ambiguous scenes.
4. **Instance Generation**: Accepted states are converted into templated reasoning questions or planning episodes.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Protocol

- **Models**: 14 Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are benchmarked, including 5 proprietary (e.g., GPT-5.6-Sol, Gemini-3.1-Pro) and 9 open-weight models (e.g., Qwen3.5-397B-A17B, InternVL3.5-241B). Human performance and random chance baselines are also reported.
- **Decoding**: All models use deterministic decoding (temperature 0).
- **Reasoning Evaluation**: Tasks are scored by exact match for scalar/multiple-choice answers and by set equality for unordered lists.
- **Planning Evaluation**: Models predict action sequences. An episode is successful only if its execution in the environment reaches the task-defined terminal condition within a budget. Illegal actions consume a step but do not change the state.
- **Metrics**: Primary metric is task accuracy (reasoning) or episode success rate (planning). JSON parse rate and answer-format hit rate are also tracked.
- **Human Evaluation**: Five professional annotators evaluated 11,008 benchmark examples under the same instructions as models, achieving an inter-annotator agreement of 0.89 on a 200-example subset.

#### 3.4. Training Experiments

- **Model**: Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct.
- **Methods**: Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) using answer-only targets, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) reinforcement learning (RL), and SFT followed by GRPO.
- **Scope**: Evaluated on 5 reasoning tasks and 4 planning tasks. Leave-one-task-out RL policies are also trained to assess transfer.

#### 3.5. Error Analysis

- **Scope**: Gemini-3.1-Pro and InternVL3.5-241B.
- **Method**: 910 incorrect predictions are sampled and categorized into a seven-class taxonomy: Instruction Following, Task Understanding, Perception Grounding, Topological Invariance, Feature State Prediction, Dynamic, and Action Planning. Categories are assigned with fixed causal priority.

#### 3.6. Probing Experiment with Generative Models

- **Objective**: Investigate if explicit visual prediction (imagined future states) can help MLLM agents preserve topological state during planning.
- **Configuration**: GPT-5.6-Luna as the planner, augmented with image (GPT-Image-2) or video (Wan2.2-I2V-A14B, Seedance-2.0-Mini, LTX-2.3) generative models.
- **Evaluation**: Success rates for imagined rollouts and a computer-vision (CV) diagnostic audit of video rollouts to check for dynamics, invariance, and consistency violations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Benchmark Results on MLLMs

- **Overall Performance Gap**: The top-performing model, GPT-5.6-Sol, achieved a task-macro average accuracy of 61.42%, significantly below human performance (97.87%).
- **Reasoning vs. Planning Gap**: Every MLLM scored higher on reasoning tasks than on planning tasks. For GPT-5.6-Sol, reasoning accuracy was 66.83% but planning success was 52.75%. This gap was even more pronounced for open-weight models, which showed a "collapse" in planning performance (e.g., Qwen3.5-397B-A17B: 26.54% reasoning, 5.27% planning). This indicates that models can recognize topology statically but struggle to operate on it through action.
- **No Single Model Dominance**: Leadership across the five topological primitives split between GPT-5.6-Sol (Continuity, Separation, Order, Enclosure) and Gemini-3.1-Pro (Knots), suggesting diverse MLLM weaknesses.
- **Challenging Planning Environments**: `Pipe`, `One Stroke`, and `Untangle` remained highly challenging for all MLLMs, with most open-weight models scoring near 0% on `Pipe` and `One Stroke`. `Swap`, which targets discrete permutations, showed higher scores, indicating environment-specific differences in planning capabilities.

#### 4.2. Training Experiments on Qwen3-VL-2B-Instruct

- **Performance Improvement**: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), especially SFT followed by GRPO, substantially improved task-specific performance from a base average of 8.00% to 31.44%.
- **Persistent Reasoning-Planning Gap**: Despite training, the reasoning-planning gap remained. Average reasoning accuracy improved to 51.53%, but planning success only rose to 6.33%. `Pipe` remained at 0% under all training conditions. This suggests that training helps with task-specific visual patterns but does not resolve the challenge of executing long action sequences.
- **Selective Transfer**: Leave-one-task-out RL showed uneven transfer. Gains were substantial on some held-out tasks (e.g., Bead, Knots) but not on others (e.g., 2D Maze, Assembly, Sheep), indicating that training does not yet produce a general topological policy.

#### 4.3. Error Analysis

- **Shift in Failure Modes**: Reasoning errors were dominated by **Perception Grounding** failures (58.1%), followed by Instruction Following (17.0%). In contrast, planning errors concentrated in **Action Planning** (43.3%) and **Dynamic Violations** (24.5%), with Feature State Prediction contributing 16.0%. This indicates that static reasoning is primarily limited by extracting visual state, while interactive planning is limited by valid action sequences and state transitions.
- **Specific Error Signatures**: `2D Maze` and `3D Maze` showed a "connectivity-by-default bias." `Hole` tasks revealed consistent "hole undercounting" tied to perception grounding. `Knots` reasoning exposed explicit "knot-structure collapse" errors. Planning tasks like `Untangle` and `One Stroke` showed significant "instruction following," "dynamic violation," and "action planning" errors, highlighting deficiencies in understanding multi-step processes.

#### 4.4. Probing Experiment with Generative Models

- **Local Cues vs. Trajectory Invariants**: Generated observations (images/videos) could retain local task cues but did not reliably preserve valid state transitions or trajectory-wide invariants.
- **Endpoint Success vs. Process Validity**: While some video generative models (e.g., Wan2.2-I2V-A14B) could produce plausible endpoints that matched the task oracle (e.g., 54.29% for easy `Untangle`), comprehensive CV diagnostics revealed that virtually no generated rollout (across 1,560 audited videos) achieved "process-valid task success." This means that even if a video ended in a "solved" state, the intermediate transitions often violated environment dynamics, topological invariants, or cross-frame consistency (e.g., Wan2.2-I2V-A14B violated dynamics in 116 of 119 audited rollouts).
- **Implication**: Local visual plausibility and endpoint success in generated videos do not indicate an underlying topology-preserving world model. Evaluation must verify topological integrity throughout the entire process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces MINDTOPO, a systematically designed benchmark that addresses a significant gap in the evaluation of foundation models: their topological intuition. By grounding its five properties in cognitive science (Piaget's theory) and formal topology, and by evaluating both reasoning and planning capabilities, MINDTOPO provides a comprehensive framework for probing a foundational aspect of spatial intelligence.

The primary significance of this work lies in its revelation of a consistent "blind spot" in current MLLMs: while models demonstrate some ability to *recognize* topological relations in static scenes, they largely *fail* to operate on or maintain these relations across sequences of actions in dynamic environments. This disparity, the pronounced reasoning-planning gap, persists even after task-specific training, indicating a fundamental limitation in current model architectures or learning paradigms related to process-level topological understanding.

The findings from the error analysis further delineate the nature of these limitations, showing a shift from perception-grounding errors in reasoning tasks to action planning and dynamic violation errors in planning tasks. This suggests that different diagnostic targets and architectural improvements are needed for each cognitive level. The probing experiments with generative models underscore this by demonstrating that even advanced video generation capabilities do not reliably produce topology-preserving world models; plausible visual outputs can mask underlying violations of dynamics and invariants.

MINDTOPO has several potential impacts:
- **Guidance for Future AI Development**: It provides a clear diagnostic tool and a challenging set of tasks that can guide the development of future foundation models towards a more robust and human-like understanding of the physical world's qualitative structure.
- **Rethinking Embodied AI and World Models**: The benchmark highlights the need for AI agents and world models that can maintain topological invariants and adhere to dynamic constraints, rather than merely generating visually plausible but topologically inconsistent transitions.
- **Interdisciplinary Bridge**: By integrating insights from cognitive science and formal topology, the work fosters an interdisciplinary approach to AI evaluation, ensuring that models are assessed not just on engineering metrics but also on their alignment with foundational human cognitive abilities.
- **Foundation for Further Research**: The procedurally generated, scalable nature of MINDTOPO, with controllable difficulty, offers a rich environment for researchers to explore various aspects of topological reasoning, from novel model architectures to more effective training paradigms for embodied agents.

The authors acknowledge limitations, including the reliance on procedurally rendered scenes, the scope of video-policy evaluation, and the current coverage of topological concepts. These limitations also point to natural directions for future research, such as incorporating real-world visual variability and expanding the set of topological relations. Overall, MINDTOPO represents a significant contribution to the field of AI evaluation, providing a critical diagnostic for advancing foundation models towards more capable and intuitively intelligent systems.
