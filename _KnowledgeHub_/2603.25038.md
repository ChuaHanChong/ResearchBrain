# $π$, But Make It Fly: Physics-Guided Transfer of VLA Models to Aerial Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> AirVLA enables the transfer of manipulation-pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to dynamic aerial platforms through physics-aware guidance and synthetic data generation. The system achieved a 50% success rate for object placement and a 62.5% overall success in zero-shot compositional navigation-and-grasping tasks on an aerial manipulator.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models, designed for ground-based robots, struggle to transfer directly to underactuated, dynamic aerial manipulators.
- Aerial platforms introduce unique challenges like constant stabilization against gravity, significant ego-motion, and abrupt payload changes, which conflict with VLA models' quasi-static environment assumptions.
- Collecting diverse and high-quality real-world training data for complex aerial manipulation tasks is challenging and time-consuming.

## Method

- The **AirVLA** system adapts a pre-trained **pi0** VLA model to an aerial manipulator by incorporating ==real-time chunking== and a custom hardware setup.
- ==Physics-aware guidance== is introduced as an ==inference-time intervention==, modifying the VLA's action generation to account for aerial dynamics and payload changes.
- A ==Gaussian Splatting==-based data pipeline synthesizes diverse and physically plausible training trajectories to overcome real-world data scarcity.

## Results

- The system demonstrated a **50%** success rate for object placement in pick-and-place tasks when using payload-aware guidance, compared to **0%** for a naive fine-tuned pi0 baseline.
- It achieved an overall **62.5%** success rate for zero-shot compositional navigate-then-grasp tasks, indicating the policy's ability to chain distinct behaviors.
- Synthetic data augmentation, combined with Real-Time Chunking (RTC), increased gate traversal success in navigation tasks to **95%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models' visual and semantic representations exhibit robust transfer to aerial drone viewpoints, but their underlying control dynamics do not directly translate to stable aerial flight.
> - Inference-time interventions, such as Real-Time Chunking (RTC) and physics-aware guidance, are essential for stabilizing the execution of VLA policies on underactuated aerial platforms.
> - Generating synthetic data using Gaussian Splatting, particularly for dynamic and recovery behaviors, is an effective strategy to augment limited real-world demonstrations for robust training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models, such as RT-X, Octo, and π0, have demonstrated capabilities in cross-embodiment generalization for robotic manipulation. These models leverage large-scale multimodal pretraining across various robot morphologies, enabling them to learn representations that can transfer across manipulators with different kinematics, workspaces, and end-effector designs. However, prior demonstrations of VLA models predominantly involve robots operating from stable, fixed, or mobile bases in quasi-static environments. This research identifies a significant gap: the transferability of these models to aerial manipulators, which operate under fundamentally different dynamic constraints.

Aerial manipulation introduces challenges not present in fixed-base or ground-mobile manipulation. Quadrotors are underactuated, meaning thrust and attitude are tightly coupled, making them sensitive to small action errors. Onboard cameras experience considerable ego-motion, rapid viewpoint changes, and motion blur, which differ from typical tabletop scenarios in VLA datasets. Furthermore, contact with objects during manipulation introduces payload changes and aerodynamic disturbances, violating the quasi-static assumptions common in VLA training.

Existing related work has explored language-conditioned aerial systems or cross-embodiment transfer to drones, but none have systematically investigated the direct transfer of manipulation-pretrained VLA foundation models. For instance, UMI-on-Air trains embodiment-agnostic diffusion policies from scratch and uses inference-time guidance for aerial dynamics but does not utilize or evaluate VLA foundation models. SINGER and GRaD-Nav++ focus on language-conditioned drone navigation without physical manipulation. While datasets like Open X-Embodiment include diverse manipulators, they do not feature aerial platforms or underactuated flight dynamics.

This work directly addresses this unexplored transfer gap by systematically investigating whether manipulation-pretrained VLA foundation models can be fine-tuned for aerial manipulators, encompassing both manipulation and navigation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to investigate the transferability of pre-trained VLA foundation models, specifically π0, from fixed-base manipulators to highly dynamic, underactuated aerial platforms for tasks involving pick-and-place and navigation.

The motivation behind this work is multi-faceted:

- **Scientific Inquiry**: Aerial manipulation serves as an extreme test for the representations learned by VLA foundation models. If policies pretrained on fixed-base manipulators can partially transfer to flying robots through fine-tuning, it would suggest that these models capture manipulation primitives that are embodiment-agnostic, transcending specific physical constraints. This investigation aims to understand the representational generalization limits of existing VLA models.
- **Practical Relevance**: Enabling language-conditioned aerial manipulation would unlock new applications that are currently beyond reach. Examples include delivering medical supplies in inaccessible terrains, clearing debris in collapsed structures, or manipulating infrastructure at height. The adaptation of VLA policies to such platforms could allow non-expert operators to issue high-level natural language commands, with the policy handling low-level perception and control complexities.
- **Addressing the "Dynamics Gap"**: The primary challenge is the fundamental mismatch between the quasi-static dynamics assumed by most manipulation-pretrained VLAs and the highly dynamic, underactuated nature of flight. Aerial platforms must continuously stabilize against gravity while performing precise gripper motions, and grasping objects introduces abrupt changes in effective mass. The research aims to bridge this "dynamics gap" without requiring a complete retraining of the large foundation model.
- **Overcoming Data Scarcity**: Collecting extensive, diverse, and high-quality real-world demonstration data for aerial manipulation is challenging and time-consuming. A key motivation is to develop methods to efficiently generate training data, particularly for novel or recovery scenarios.
- **Evaluating Compositionality**: The study also aims to assess if a single, fine-tuned policy can reliably compose distinct behaviors, such as navigation and manipulation, into complex, multi-stage tasks specified by natural language.

In summary, the research seeks to determine the extent to which VLA models can adapt to the unique challenges of aerial robotics and to develop the necessary architectural and data-generation components to facilitate this transfer, thereby extending the utility of VLA foundation models to a new class of robotic systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AirVLA system is designed to enable the transfer of manipulation capabilities from foundation models (specifically π0) to an underactuated quadrotor. The approach integrates hardware, a modified policy architecture, physics-aware guidance, and a synthetic data generation pipeline.

**A. System Overview**
AirVLA receives multimodal inputs, including natural language commands, proprioceptive data, and multi-view camera observations. It outputs relative end-effector pose commands that are executed by a low-level flight controller. The core challenge addressed is the disparity between quasi-static tabletop manipulation and dynamic aerial manipulation, particularly the drone's need to stabilize against gravity while manipulating objects and compensating for payload changes.

**B. Hardware**
The system utilizes a ModalAI Starling 2 Max drone, powered by a VOXL 2 companion computer. A custom, lightweight, 3D-printed compliant Universal Manipulation Interface (UMI) gripper is attached to the drone's underside. The drone is equipped with one external camera and two onboard cameras (downward and forward-facing) that provide RGB images at 5 Hz, each at 256x256 resolution. The drone's control stack is ROS-based, treating the drone as a "flying end-effector," with the PX4 flight controller executing position setpoints.

**C. Observation and Action Spaces**
- **Observation Space**: Consists of RGB images from the three cameras, the drone's estimated pose (obtained from a motion capture system), and the gripper aperture.
- **Action Space**: Defined as a 4-degrees-of-freedom (DoF) relative end-effector pose (position and yaw) and gripper commands. Actions are represented as an action chunk A ∈ R^(H×D) (where H is the horizon and D is the dimension of the action space), generated at 10 Hz.

**D. Policy Architecture** The system builds upon π0, a VLA model that uses a flow-matching generative model to represent the conditional action distribution. Given an observation, π0 defines a velocity field to generate an action chunk. For real-time execution, Real-Time Chunking (RTC) is employed. RTC enables asynchronous inference by freezing a prefix of the next action chunk that will execute before inference completes, then inpainting the remaining suffix conditioned on the frozen prefix. This process uses a soft temporal mask to blend committed and newly generated actions, aiming to prevent discontinuities at chunk boundaries.

**E. Physics-Aware Guidance for Action Generation** To bridge the "dynamics gap" without retraining the π0 foundation model, an inference-time intervention is introduced: physics-aware guidance. This mechanism modifies the velocity field during the flow-matching sampling process by adding a gradient correction term derived from a loss function Φ(A; o) over the denoised action chunk. This biases the sampling process towards actions that are both probable under the base policy and minimize physical constraint violations.

1. **General Tracking-Error Guidance**: A tracking loss Φ_track(A; o) is defined to penalize deviations from a reference trajectory A_des(o). Temporal weights, informed by the RTC soft masking schedule, ensure strong enforcement of continuity with already committed actions while allowing flexibility for newly generated actions.
2. **Payload-Aware Vertical Guidance**: This is a specific instantiation of the tracking-error guidance focused on the altitude (z-component) of the action. The loss Φ_payload(A; o, A_t-1) biases the drone towards a slightly higher altitude (z_des = z_curr + Δz) when carrying a payload, compensating for sag. A payload confidence score α(o, A_t-1), derived from recent gripper command intent and measured gripper aperture, gates this vertical objective. When no payload is detected (α ≈ 0), the guidance vanishes; when a payload is detected (α ≈ 1), the sampler prefers altitude-biased chunks. This acts as a mode-dependent feedforward compensation injected into the generative policy's sampling dynamics.

**F. Gaussian Splat Data Pipeline**
To address data scarcity and efficiently generate diverse training trajectories, the research utilizes a "Flying in Gaussian Splats" approach.

1. **Scene Reconstruction**: Static environments are reconstructed as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) models using Nerfstudio from drone walk-throughs. These models render photorealistic images from arbitrary camera poses.
2. **Gripper Segmentation and Compositing**: To prevent observation bias from the persistently visible gripper in downward-facing camera views, the gripper is treated as a separate foreground layer. A gripper mask is computed using Segment Anything (SAM), and a library of gripper appearances is extracted. During synthesis, the clean scene is rendered from the 3DGS model, and a gripper patch consistent with the commanded aperture is composited onto the downward-view image.
3. **Drone Dynamics Model**: Drone motion is simulated using a semi-kinematic model comprising position, velocity, and orientation. Controls (normalized thrust and body angular velocity) are integrated using ACADOS, and images are rendered using the body-to-camera transform.
4. **Domain-Randomized Data Synthesis**: To generate varied and robust training data, especially for recovery behaviors, nominal trajectories are augmented with randomized rollouts. This involves perturbing initial states and randomizing intermediate waypoints, such as height offsets above goals, post-gate positions, and approaches near gate extremities. This procedure yields a large set of physically and geometrically plausible trajectories for both nominal executions and off-nominal approaches requiring recovery.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted 460 real-world experiments across various tasks to evaluate AirVLA, comparing it against baselines like naive π0, π0 +RTC, ACT, and Diffusion Policy.

**A. Single Task Performance (Table I)**
- **Inference-time Control is Critical for Pick-and-Place**: For the "Penguin Grasp" (pick-and-place) task, naive fine-tuning of π0 resulted in 0% success for placing the object, with failures attributed to missed grasps and post-contact disturbances. Integrating Real-Time Chunking (RTC) improved place success to 23.5%. The introduction of payload-aware guidance further increased place success to 50%, indicating its role in compensating for payload-induced altitude sag and other dynamic instabilities. Baseline methods ACT and Diffusion Policy showed very low success rates (0-10% for pick, 0% for place).
- **RTC Improves Navigation Performance**: For the "Gate Navigation" task, RTC significantly improved gate traversal success. In the non-synthetic data setting, RTC increased gate traversal success from 50% (naive π0) to 80%.
- **Synthetic Data Augmentation Benefits Navigation with RTC**: When synthetic data was used for training, RTC further boosted gate traversal success from 45% (naive π0) to 95%. This suggests that synthetic data, while valuable, is most effective when combined with real-time planning capabilities like RTC, which mitigates compounding errors during aggressive maneuvers. The policy sometimes reverted to "synthetic modes" for gate traversal, confirming the utility of the generated trajectories.

**B. Compositional Navigate-then-Grasp Success (Table II)**
- **Generalization to Compositional Tasks**: The system demonstrated an ability to generalize to novel, zero-shot compositional tasks. With synthetic data and payload-aware guidance, the policy achieved 85% gate traversal, 100% hover success (conditioned on gate traversal), 94.1% pick success (conditioned on hover), and an overall 62.5% place success (conditioned on pick). This indicates that the policy can effectively chain learned navigation and manipulation behaviors.
- **Importance of RTC and Synthetic Data for Compositionality**: The compositional task results reinforced the importance of closed-loop policy inference (RTC) and the necessity of Gaussian Splatting (GS) synthetic trajectories for reliable gate traversal.

**C. Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Robustness (Table III)**
- **Object Generalization**: The method exhibited some generalization capabilities for novel objects in pick-and-place tasks. For a "sandwich" object, it achieved 70% pick success and 57.1% place success. However, performance varied by object geometry; for a "bag of chips," pick success was only 10%, primarily due to the policy's inability to adapt grasp poses. Qualitatively, the policy often attempted to pick novel objects if they were within the trained workspace, suggesting a visuomotor prior. When distractors were present, the policy still prioritized grasping the trained "penguin" object.
- **Navigation Generalization**: Generalization to novel gate locations was limited. While the policy adapted to the "right" gate region with 40% traversal success, it failed completely in "front" and "left" regions, either bypassing the gate or colliding. This indicates a potential overfitting to the demonstration data in the small-data fine-tuning regime for navigation.

**D. Analysis Summary**
The results suggest a partial but significant transfer of manipulation skills from manipulation-pretrained VLAs to aerial platforms. Visual representations transfer effectively, but the specific control dynamics required for stable and precise flight necessitate domain-adapted components. Inference-time interventions like RTC and payload-aware guidance are crucial for stabilizing execution and compensating for underactuated dynamics. Synthetic data augmentation, particularly when combined with RTC, aids in expanding the policy's behavioral coverage for navigation tasks. While the policy shows promising zero-shot compositional capabilities, out-of-distribution robustness, especially for navigation and diverse object geometries, highlights the need for more extensive and varied training data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents AirVLA, representing the first systematic investigation into transferring manipulation-pretrained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) foundation models to aerial manipulation platforms. Its findings offer a nuanced understanding of cross-embodiment transfer, highlighting both the successes and the inherent challenges.

**A. Significance**

- **Demonstration of VLA Transfer to Aerial Platforms**: AirVLA provides the first demonstration that a pre-trained VLA foundation model, such as π0, can be fine-tuned to perform tasks on an underactuated aerial manipulator. This extends the scope of VLA models beyond stable ground-based robots.
- **Identification of Transfer Mechanisms and Gaps**: The work indicates that while visual and semantic representations from manipulation-pretrained VLAs transfer robustly to drone viewpoints, the intricate control dynamics specific to aerial platforms do not transfer directly. This "dynamics gap" necessitates specialized interventions.
- **Physics-Informed Guidance as an Intervention**: The introduction of a payload-aware guidance mechanism directly into the flow-matching sampling loop is a key methodological contribution. This approach allows the reconciliation of the generative diversity of a VLA policy with the strict physical constraints of flight dynamics, improving single-task placement success from 0% (naive baseline) to 50%. This demonstrates a method for injecting real-time physics-informed control into large generative models without costly retraining.
- **Data Augmentation via Gaussian Splatting**: The utilization of a Gaussian Splatting pipeline to synthesize diverse navigation training data is significant for overcoming data scarcity in aerial robotics. This method proves to be a key enabler, increasing navigation task success by 20% compared to fine-tuning on teleoperation data alone.
- **Compositional Task Capabilities**: The system's ability to execute long-horizon compositional tasks, combining navigation and grasping in a zero-shot manner with an overall 62% success rate, demonstrates potential for more complex, language-specified aerial robotic behaviors.

**B. Potential Impact**

- **Enabling New Aerial Robotic Applications**: By demonstrating the feasibility of language-conditioned aerial manipulation, AirVLA could pave the way for practical applications in difficult-to-access environments. These include autonomous delivery of goods, debris removal in disaster zones, or maintenance and inspection tasks on elevated infrastructure, where non-expert human operators can issue high-level commands.
- **Advancing Cross-Embodiment Learning**: The findings contribute to the broader field of robot learning by stress-testing the generalization capabilities of VLA foundation models across drastically different embodiments. Understanding where these models succeed and fail informs future research into more universally applicable robotic intelligence.
- **Informing VLA Model Design**: The necessity of inference-time physics-aware guidance highlights a direction for future VLA model development, where physical constraints might be more intrinsically integrated or learned in a more embodiment-agnostic manner.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity in Robotics**: The synthetic data generation pipeline, particularly for dynamic and recovery behaviors, offers a blueprint for creating robust datasets without extensive manual teleoperation, which is a common bottleneck in robot learning.

**C. Limitations and Future Work**

The research acknowledges several limitations. The current system relies on a motion capture system for drone localization, which simplifies the cross-embodiment transfer problem but restricts the operating domain. Future work aims to replace this dependency with onboard Visual Inertial Odometry (VIO) or Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) to enable operations in more diverse environments.

Furthermore, out-of-distribution evaluations revealed disparities in generalization, particularly for novel navigation scenarios and object geometries. The lower success rates in these settings suggest that current data diversity and volume (approximately 270 teleoperation demonstrations) are insufficient for robust generalization. Future work will focus on scaling demonstrations and investigating how data diversity and coverage impact OOD performance to improve adaptability to a wider range of objects and environments. This will be critical for achieving truly robust open-world aerial manipulation capabilities.
