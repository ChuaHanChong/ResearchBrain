# Meta-World: A Benchmark and Evaluation for Multi-Task and Meta Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Meta-World, an open-source benchmark featuring 50 diverse robotic manipulation tasks, provides a platform for evaluating multi-task and meta-reinforcement learning. Its evaluation shows current algorithms struggle to generalize effectively to new, distinct tasks, highlighting challenges in broad skill acquisition for robots.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art reinforcement learning (RL) methods are sample inefficient and lack broad generalization capabilities, typically requiring vast experience for single, narrowly defined skills.
- Existing multi-task RL benchmarks often feature tasks that are too disparate, leading to negative transfer rather than positive transfer.
- Prior meta-RL benchmarks predominantly use very narrow task distributions, such as parametric variations, which do not assess generalization to entirely new behaviors or qualitatively distinct tasks.

## Method

- Introduces **Meta-World**, an open-source simulated benchmark featuring 50 distinct robotic manipulation tasks implemented in the ==MuJoCo== physics engine with a shared Sawyer arm.
- The tasks exhibit both parametric (randomized object/goal positions) and non-parametric (qualitatively distinct behaviors) variations, ensuring diversity while maintaining shared underlying structure.
- Defines five ==evaluation protocols== (ML1, MT1, MT10, MT50, ML10, ML45) that progressively challenge algorithms to learn across or generalize to new, held-out tasks, benchmarking 7 state-of-the-art multi-task and meta-RL algorithms.

## Results

- All 50 individual Meta-World tasks are solvable by existing single-task reinforcement learning algorithms, with Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) solving all of them.
- Multi-task SAC achieved an average success rate of **68%** on 10 distinct tasks, but the performance of all multi-task algorithms dropped to 35-**38%** when scaled to the full suite of 50 distinct tasks.
- Meta-RL methods (MAML, RL^2, PEARL) showed low overall success rates (e.g., 30-**40%** for top methods) when evaluated on their ability to perform few-shot adaptation to entirely new, held-out test tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Meta-World addresses a critical benchmarking gap by providing a platform for evaluating meaningful generalization in multi-task and meta-reinforcement learning, extending beyond simple parametric task variations.
> - The evaluation reveals that current multi-task and meta-RL algorithms struggle to effectively scale to diverse task sets and generalize to entirely new, distinct tasks.
> - The benchmark identifies specific areas for future algorithmic research, particularly concerning robust optimization for broad task distributions and achieving stronger generalization capabilities.
