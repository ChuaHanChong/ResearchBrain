# Look, Focus, Act: Efficient and Robust Robot Learning via Human Gaze and Foveated Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from UC Davis and UC Berkeley developed GIAVA, a framework that integrates human gaze and foveated vision into robot learning, significantly boosting computational efficiency and robustness for manipulation tasks. Their approach, using foveated Vision Transformers, achieves a 7x speedup in training and enhanced performance in cluttered environments.

## Problem

- Most robot learning systems process entire camera images uniformly, leading to computational inefficiency and vulnerability to irrelevant visual information.
- Vision Transformers (ViTs), despite their power, incur high computational costs due to their quadratic scaling with the number of visual tokens.
- Robot vision lacks the active, attention-driven efficiency of human vision, which uses gaze and foveation to selectively process task-relevant information.

## Method

- ==Developed GIAVA==, a comprehensive framework extending the AV-ALOHA platform, which uses a VR headset to collect human gaze data synchronized with robot manipulation demonstrations.
- Introduced foveated tokenization for Vision Transformers, where high-resolution patches are concentrated around an estimated gaze point and sparser patches are used in the periphery.
- Implemented robot policies using Conditional Flow Matching, incorporating two methods for gaze estimation: a separate UNet-based model (Fov-UNet) and an end-to-end prediction within the main policy (Fov-Act).

## Results

- Foveated methods achieved an approximate **7x** speedup in training and **3x** in inference, along with a **5x** reduction in GPU memory consumption compared to uniform tokenization.
- Foveated policies consistently maintained or improved success rates in simulation tasks, notably achieving **92%** on ThreadNeedle and **92%** on PourTestTube with MAE pretraining, and demonstrated improved robustness in environments with distractors.
- The GIAVA platform, including its open-source dataset with synchronized human gaze and robot actions, establishes a new benchmark for research in active vision and human-inspired robot learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Biologically-inspired foveated vision provides a powerful inductive bias for robot learning, enabling more efficient and robust perception-control loops.
> - Foveated tokenization drastically reduces the number of visual tokens processed by ViTs (94% reduction), leading to substantial computational savings without compromising performance.
> - Actively focusing visual attention on task-relevant regions through gaze and foveation significantly improves robot policies' robustness to visual clutter and distractors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is strategically positioned at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields in robotics and artificial intelligence:

- **Imitation Learning (IL) for Dexterous Manipulation:** IL has become a dominant paradigm for teaching complex, dexterous robot behaviors, particularly for bimanual manipulation and humanoid control. Current IL systems often rely on passively processing uniform visual input. This paper challenges this norm by proposing a biologically inspired approach that could make IL more efficient and robust.

- **Human-Inspired Vision Systems:** Unlike typical robot vision, human vision is an active, dynamic process driven by gaze and foveation, which dramatically reduces the amount of visual data requiring high-resolution processing. There's growing interest in incorporating these biological principles into artificial systems, especially with the rise of deep learning and embodied AI. This work directly contributes to this trend by translating human foveated vision into a robot learning framework.

- **Active Vision (AV) and Viewpoint Control:** The paper extends the concept of active vision, where a robot dynamically controls its camera viewpoint to acquire more task-relevant information. Building on the AV-ALOHA platform, this work takes active vision a step further by integrating explicit gaze control, allowing the robot to not only move its "head" but also to focus its "eyes" on specific regions of interest, mimicking human head and eye movements.

- **Vision Transformers (ViTs) in Robotics:** ViTs have demonstrated impressive capabilities in visual representation learning and are increasingly adopted in robot learning. However, their computational cost, scaling quadratically with the number of visual tokens (O(N²)), remains a significant hurdle. This paper addresses this challenge by proposing a foveated tokenization scheme, which dramatically reduces the number of tokens processed by the ViT, thereby enhancing computational efficiency.

- **Gaze-Guided Robotics:** While still an emerging area, researchers have explored using human gaze to inform robot perception and control. Previous efforts include using manually annotated saliency maps, learning gaze behavior through reinforcement learning, or employing simpler gaze-based cropping strategies. This paper advances the state-of-the-art by implementing a more sophisticated, biologically-inspired foveated tokenization for ViTs, coupled with a more human-like hardware setup for data collection that captures synchronized gaze and manipulation actions. It bridges the gap between theoretical understanding of human vision and practical application in robot control.

- **Flow Matching for Policy Learning:** The paper utilizes conditional Flow Matching (CFM), a recent advancement in generative modeling, to learn robot policies. This choice reflects the authors' commitment to leveraging cutting-edge machine learning techniques for robust policy learning in complex robotic tasks.

In summary, this work is a crucial step towards developing more intelligent, efficient, and robust robot learning systems by integrating sophisticated biological insights into modern deep learning architectures and advanced robot platforms. It addresses critical limitations of current robot vision systems—their computational inefficiency and susceptibility to distractors—by drawing inspiration from the human visual system.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **enhance the efficiency and robustness of robot learning systems by incorporating human-like active gaze and foveated visual processing.** The authors are motivated by several key observations and hypotheses:

1. **Bridging the Gap Between Human and Robot Vision:** Human vision is an incredibly efficient and active process, utilizing gaze to direct attention and foveation to process task-relevant regions at high resolution while discarding unnecessary detail in the periphery. In stark contrast, most robot learning systems passively process entire raw camera images uniformly, leading to inefficiency and vulnerability to irrelevant visual information. The motivation is to imbue robots with a similar ability to selectively attend and process visual information.

2. **Leveraging Accessible Human Gaze Data:** The increasing prevalence of Virtual Reality (VR) headsets with built-in eye-tracking capabilities offers an unprecedented opportunity to collect human gaze data synchronously with robot manipulation demonstrations. This rich supervisory signal, indicating where a human demonstrator is focusing their attention, can provide valuable insights for training robot policies to emulate human visual efficiency.

3. **Optimizing Vision Transformers (ViTs) for Robotics:** ViTs are powerful vision models widely adopted in robot learning due to their strong representation learning capabilities. However, their computational cost, scaling quadratically with the number of visual tokens, makes them resource-intensive. The authors hypothesize that by conditioning ViT tokenization on gaze—concentrating high-resolution tokens around the fixation point and sparsifying them in the periphery—they can preserve or even improve performance while significantly reducing computational load.

4. **Improving Robustness to Visual Distractors:** Uniform visual processing can make robots susceptible to background clutter and distractors, as the system might equally process irrelevant visual information. By actively focusing on task-relevant areas through gaze and foveation, the robot policy is expected to become more robust to unseen and cluttered environments.

5. **Developing a Community Benchmark:** The limited integration of gaze and foveation into robot learning, despite its biological relevance, necessitates tools and resources for further exploration. The authors are motivated to open-source a comprehensive system and dataset to facilitate research and development in this promising area.

In essence, the research aims to move beyond simple "look and act" paradigms by enabling robots to "look, focus, and then act" in a computationally efficient and robust manner, drawing direct inspiration from the sophisticated mechanisms of human vision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose GIAVA (Gaze Integrated Active-Vision ALOHA), a comprehensive framework that integrates human gaze and foveated vision into robot learning.

#### 3.1. A. Data Collection with Eye Tracking

The foundation of GIAVA is an extended version of the AV-ALOHA platform.
- **Hardware Setup:** It utilizes two robotic manipulation arms (from the original ALOHA setup) and a third 7-DOF active vision (AV) arm equipped with a stereo camera.
- **Human-Robot Interface:** A Meta Quest Pro VR headset (replacing the Meta Quest 3) is used by a human operator. The headset’s pose controls the AV arm, while VR controllers operate the manipulation arms.
- **Eye Tracking Integration:** The Meta Quest Pro's built-in eye-tracking sensors allow simultaneous recording of the human operator’s gaze data. The stereo camera’s vertical field of view was upgraded from 52° to 105° to improve gaze tracking visibility.
- **Synchronization:** To counter potential latency between robot camera feeds and headset-recorded gaze, each image frame is tagged with a unique ID. Gaze data streamed from the headset includes this ID, ensuring proper synchronization. For missing gaze data, interpolation is used.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** The GIAVA simulation replica, along with open-source datasets containing synchronized human demonstrations and eye-tracking data, are released to support community research.

#### 3.2. B. Flow Matching Policy

- **Policy Formulation:** The policy, π(A|O), maps observations (O) to an action chunk (A) of length K=16.
- **Conditional Flow Matching (CFM):** CFM is employed to learn the policy. It models a time-dependent vector field v_θ(z_t, t, O) that transforms a simple prior distribution (e.g., N(0, I)) into the target conditional distribution of expert actions.
- **Optimization:** Model parameters θ are optimized by minimizing the mean squared error between the predicted velocity v_θ and the ground-truth velocity (A - z₀).
- **Inference:** During inference, a random noise vector z₀ is drawn, and the learned ordinary differential equation (ODE), dz_t/dt = v_θ(z_t, t, O), is numerically integrated using an 8-step Euler solver from t=0 to t=1 to generate the action chunk A.

#### 3.3. C. Policy Architecture

The core architecture consists of an observation encoder and an action decoder:
- **Observation Encoder:**
 - **Image Processing (O_img):** The left camera image from the stereo camera is processed by a Vision Transformer (ViT), producing a sequence of feature tokens.
 - **Q-Former:** A Q-Former module distills these extensive visual tokens into a compact set of 16 conditioning tokens (c_img) via cross-attention.
 - **Proprioception Processing (O_proprio):** The robot's proprioceptive state (joint angles) is encoded using a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) into conditioning tokens (c_proprio).
- **Action Decoder (Flow Transformer):**
 - A transformer network learns the velocity field v_θ.
 - Input: Action latent z_t, concatenated with c_proprio.
 - **Conditioning:** Time step t is embedded and injected via Adaptive Layer Norm (AdaLN) in each block. Each block also includes a cross-attention layer where the action-proprioception sequence attends to c_img, integrating visual information.
 - Output: Predicted velocity vector v_θ(z_t, t, O).

#### 3.4. D. Gaze Estimation

Since human gaze is unavailable at test time, the policy must learn to estimate it:
- **Two-Stage Method (Fov-UNet):**
 - A separate UNet with a ResNet18 backbone (pretrained on ImageNet) processes a downscaled camera image to predict a gaze heatmap.
 - Spatial softmax extracts the (x,y) gaze keypoint, supervised by ground-truth human gaze using MSE.
 - The gaze model is trained separately and frozen. The predicted gaze guides foveation and is appended to the robot's proprioceptive input.
- **End-to-End Method (Fov-Act):**
 - Gaze is treated as part of the robot's action space, predicting future gaze trajectories jointly with robot actions within the main flow matching policy.
 - An initial gaze point (center of the image) is used to start the sequence.
 - The predicted gaze is used for foveation in the next inference step and concatenated to proprioception.

#### 3.5. E. Foveated Tokenization

The core innovation for visual processing:
- **Biologically Inspired:** Mimics human foveation by placing small, densely packed patches (high resolution) at the estimated gaze point and larger, sparser patches (lower resolution) in concentric rings towards the periphery.
- **Gaze Alignment:** The input image is shifted so that the estimated gaze aligns with the center of the foveation pattern. Zero-padding handles image shifts beyond boundaries.
- **Patch Processing:** All patches are downscaled to match the size of the central patches, then fed into a standard ViT.
- **Efficiency:** A custom foveated pattern uses only 20 patches, significantly reducing the computational load compared to uniform tokenization (e.g., 324 patches for 'Fine').
- **ViT Pretraining:** To ensure a fair comparison and enable the use of pretrained weights, custom ViT-B models for Foveated, Fine, and Coarse tokenization patterns are pretrained from scratch using the Masked Autoencoder (MAE) objective on a subset of ImageNet-1K. This mitigates the issue of incompatible tokenization patterns with off-the-shelf pretrained ViTs.

#### 3.6. F. Experimental Setup and Evaluation

- **Tasks:** Six simulation tasks (from AV-ALOHA benchmark) and two real-robot tasks (Ball, Toothbrush).
- **Data:** 100 demonstrations with eye-tracking for each simulation task; 60-78 episodes for real-robot tasks.
- **Distractors:** Randomly placed small objects are introduced to evaluate robustness in cluttered environments.
- **Policies Evaluated:**
 - **Fine:** Uniform 18x18 patch grid (324 tokens).
 - **Coarse:** Uniform 4x5 patch grid (20 tokens).
 - **Fov-Act:** Foveated pattern with end-to-end gaze prediction.
 - **Fov-UNet:** Foveated pattern with two-stage (UNet) gaze prediction.
- **Training Settings:** Evaluated with randomly initialized ViT weights and MAE-pretrained ViT weights, both in "Standard" (no distractors) and "Distractors" settings.
- **Metrics:** Success rate, training/inference latency, and memory usage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate significant advantages of the gaze-integrated foveated vision approach, particularly in computational efficiency and robustness, while often maintaining or improving performance.

#### 4.1. A. Efficiency

- **Computational Overhead (Table I):**
 - **Training:** Foveated methods (Fov-Act, Fov-UNet) are approximately 7-8 times faster (108.2-123.8 ms/step) and consume about 5 times less GPU memory (3937-4041 MiB) compared to the Fine policy (833.2 ms/step, 20949 MiB). This substantial reduction is attributed to processing significantly fewer visual tokens.
 - **Inference:** While the multi-step flow matching inference loop lessens the relative difference, foveated methods still exhibit approximately 3 times lower latency (87.9-105.7 ms/chunk) compared to Fine (334.7 ms/chunk).
 - **Gaze Estimation Impact:** Fov-UNet, with its additional UNet model for gaze estimation, is slightly slower and more memory-intensive than Fov-Act, which predicts gaze end-to-end.
- **ViT Specific Efficiency (Table II):**
 - **Token Count:** The Foveated pattern uses only 20 tokens, a 94% reduction compared to the Fine pattern's 324 tokens. The Coarse pattern also uses 20 tokens.
 - **Latency and GFLOPs:** This reduction directly translates to a ~90% decrease in ViT inference latency and GFLOPs for Foveated and Coarse patterns compared to Fine. The Foveated pattern is marginally faster than the Coarse pattern, even with the same token count, due to smaller patch embedding computation.

#### 4.2. B. Success Rates: Simulation Tasks (Table III)

- **No Pretraining (Standard Setting):** Fov-UNet consistently performed well, matching or outperforming other methods in success rate. Notably, Fov-UNet achieved 100% on CubeTransfer and 84% on PourTestTube, compared to Fine's 72% and 34%, respectively. Fov-Act also surpassed Fine and Coarse on tasks like ThreadNeedle and PourTestTube, suggesting that foveation can aid fine-grained control.
- **No Pretraining (Distractors Setting):** The advantage of foveated tokenization became more pronounced with distractors. Foveated methods generally showed improved robustness. Interestingly, Fov-Act sometimes outperformed Fov-UNet in this setting (e.g., PourTestTube: 68% vs. 38%), implying that external distractors could sometimes mislead Fov-UNet's separately predicted gaze.
- **With MAE Pretraining (Standard Setting):** All methods saw performance improvements, indicating the value of pretraining. Fine achieved top performance on two tasks, while Fov-UNet continued to lead on three, particularly for high-precision tasks like ThreadNeedle (92% vs. Fine's 84%) and PourTestTube (92% vs. Fine's 68%).
- **With MAE Pretraining (Distractors Setting):** Foveated tokenization methods generally maintained better performance than uniform tokenization, further affirming their robustness to visual clutter. For example, Fov-UNet achieved 70% on ThreadNeedle and 64% on SlotInsertion, outperforming Fine (48% and 64%, respectively) or Coarse (40% and 54%, respectively).
- **Specific Task Limitations:** On HookPackage, Fov-Act underperformed all other methods. The authors attribute this to the robot needing to shift its gaze from a central package to a small peripheral hook, which is challenging for foveated vision with its downscaled peripheral regions, especially when gaze is predicted end-to-end.

#### 4.2.2. Real-Robot Tasks (Table IV)

- Differences between Fov-UNet and Fine were less significant than in simulation.
- **Standard Setting:** Fine slightly outperformed Fov-UNet on Toothbrush (24% vs. 18%), while Fov-UNet was comparable or slightly better on Ball (62% vs. 64%).
- **Distractors Setting:** Fov-UNet showed better robustness on the Ball task (56% vs. Fine's 48%), though both policies saw a performance drop compared to the standard setting.

Overall, the results highlight that foveation methods are not only significantly more computationally efficient but also surprisingly robust to distractors and can even improve performance on certain high-precision tasks. This suggests that focusing visual attention on select, task-relevant features is sufficient for effective robot control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant contribution to the field of robot learning, with profound potential impacts:

1. **Paradigm Shift Towards Biologically-Inspired Vision:** The work robustly demonstrates that biologically-inspired foveated vision, when integrated with modern Vision Transformers, is not merely a theoretical curiosity but a practical and highly effective approach for robot learning. It validates the hypothesis that mimicking human visual processing—specifically gaze-driven attention and foveation—can lead to superior robot capabilities.

2. **Unprecedented Efficiency Gains:** By drastically reducing the number of visual tokens processed (a 94% reduction) without compromising, and often enhancing, performance, the method achieves significant computational savings. This includes an approximately 7x speedup in training and a 3x speedup in inference. These efficiency gains are crucial for deploying advanced robot learning policies on resource-constrained hardware, enabling faster iteration in research, and making complex models more accessible. This addresses a major bottleneck in the widespread adoption of ViTs in robotics.

3. **Enhanced Robustness to Real-World Clutter:** The improved robustness of foveated policies to background distractors is a critical step towards deploying robots in complex, unstructured real-world environments. By focusing attention on task-relevant regions, the robot becomes less susceptible to irrelevant visual noise, leading to more reliable and adaptable behaviors.

4. **Novel Gaze Integration Framework:** The paper introduces and evaluates two distinct, well-conceived methods for integrating gaze estimation into robot policies (two-stage Fov-UNet and end-to-end Fov-Act). This exploration provides valuable insights into the trade-offs between modularity, efficiency, and robustness in gaze-guided learning, offering guidance for future architectural designs.

5. **Open-Source Contribution to the Research Community:** The release of the GIAVA platform (including the hardware setup, manipulation arms, active vision arm, and gaze synchronization pipeline), along with the first simulation benchmark and dataset for imitation learning with active vision and human gaze tracking, is a monumental contribution. It provides a robust and accessible foundation for other researchers to build upon, accelerating progress in this critical area.

6. **Paving the Way for Human-Level Dexterity:** The authors emphasize that developing "more human-like vision systems, capable of both searching and fixating on the most relevant information," is essential for advancing robot learning towards human-level dexterity. This work provides strong evidence that integrating gaze and foveation is a powerful inductive bias that can unlock new levels of performance and efficiency in perception-control loops.

**Future Work:** The authors outline clear priorities for future research, including systematically characterizing the types of manipulation tasks (e.g., high-precision, long-horizon) that benefit most from foveation, uncovering the underlying mechanisms of performance gains, and optimizing foveated policy design across a broader range of real and simulated tasks.

In conclusion, "Look, Focus, Act" is a landmark paper that not only presents an innovative and effective approach to robot vision but also underscores the enduring value of drawing inspiration from biological intelligence. Its contributions to efficiency, robustness, and the open-source community are poised to significantly influence the trajectory of robot learning and perception.
