# FLaRe: Achieving Masterful and Adaptive Robot Policies with Large-Scale Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> FLaRe is a framework that leverages large-scale reinforcement learning to fine-tune pre-trained robotic behavior cloning policies, enabling them to achieve adaptive and masterful performance. The framework improves success rates on mobile manipulation tasks by over 23% in simulation and 30% on real robots, demonstrating generalization to novel tasks and rapid adaptation to new robot embodiments using sparse rewards.

## Problem

- Large-scale behavior cloning (BC) policies in robotics, while robust, often struggle to generalize beyond observed demonstrations, leading to poor performance in unseen real-world scenarios and a lack of error recovery.
- Traditional reinforcement learning (RL) is highly sample-inefficient, typically requires complex, hand-crafted dense reward functions, and faces stability issues when attempting to fine-tune large pre-trained models.
- Existing methods for combining BC and RL have been limited by issues such as instability with high-capacity networks, computational cost of accessing full offline datasets, or destructive gradient updates during fine-tuning.

## Method

- Leverages a robust ==multi-task pre-trained transformer== model (**SPOC**) as a foundation, which provides strong visual features and behavioral priors for mobile manipulation tasks.
- Utilizes large-scale simulation environments (AI2THOR with Objaverse-Populated ProcTHOR) with extensive ==domain randomization== and a frozen foundational vision model (**DinoV2**) to facilitate sim-to-real transfer.
- Implements several key stabilization techniques for RL fine-tuning, including using **PPO** with a small learning rate, disabling the entropy bonus, and employing separate actor and critic networks to prevent training collapse.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **79.5%** in unseen simulated environments (+**23.6%** over prior state-of-the-art) and **80.7%** on real Stretch RE-1 robots (+**30.7%** over prior state-of-the-art) for mobile manipulation tasks using sparse rewards.
- Demonstrated state-of-the-art generalization on novel, out-of-distribution tasks requiring new reasoning capabilities (e.g., relative attribute comparison, room navigation) without additional hyperparameter tuning.
- Showcased efficient adaptation by successfully transferring policies to a new robot embodiment (LoCoBot) and by modifying behaviors like efficiency and collision avoidance with only 6 hours of fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale pre-trained behavior cloning policies can be effectively elevated to "masterful performance" by fine-tuning with reinforcement learning, aligning their actions with direct task completion rather than just imitation.
> - Stable fine-tuning of high-capacity pre-trained models with RL requires cautious algorithmic choices, including extremely low learning rates and a decoupled actor-critic architecture to preserve valuable pre-trained features.
> - Leveraging robust simulation and foundational vision models, complex robot behaviors can be learned efficiently from simple sparse rewards, significantly reducing the need for dense, hand-crafted reward functions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "foundation models" has recently revolutionized fields like natural language processing (e.g., GPT) and computer vision (e.g., SAM), demonstrating unprecedented capabilities across a wide range of tasks. Inspired by these successes, the robotics community has embarked on an ambitious journey to create similarly powerful "generalist robot policies" that can perform diverse tasks in complex, unstructured environments.

One dominant approach in this pursuit has been **large-scale multi-task Behavior Cloning (BC)**. Methods like RT-1, RT-2, RT-X, Octo, RoboCat, OpenVLA, and SPOC leverage vast datasets of human and simulated demonstrations to train high-capacity transformer models. These BC policies exhibit considerable promise, learning a broad spectrum of behaviors and developing robust representations. However, their fundamental limitation lies in their reliance on observed demonstrations. When deployed in the real world, these models often struggle with unseen states, fail to generalize effectively beyond expert trajectories, and lack the ability to recover gracefully from errors—a phenomenon known as "compounding errors." This leads to a performance plateau, where direct deployment of BC policies yields unsatisfactory results in novel or perturbed environments.

Conversely, **Reinforcement Learning (RL)** offers a powerful paradigm for direct performance optimization through trial-and-error. RL algorithms have achieved remarkable successes in various robotics domains, enabling agents to learn complex skills by maximizing a reward signal. The strength of RL lies in its ability to learn from interactions and implicitly generalize to novel situations by optimizing for task completion. However, traditional RL suffers from its own set of critical challenges: it is notoriously **sample-inefficient**, requiring extensive training time and massive amounts of data; it often demands **meticulously hand-crafted reward functions**, which is a significant burden for complex, long-horizon tasks and limits scalability to new tasks; and it struggles to "get off the ground" when the search space is vast, particularly in long-horizon tasks with large action spaces.

Prior work has explored combining BC and RL, often by using pre-trained BC policies to jump-start RL training. These approaches aim to leverage the prior knowledge from demonstrations to mitigate RL's sample inefficiency. However, most existing methods in this space have been limited to small-scale networks, single-task domains, or required access to the entire offline dataset during fine-tuning (which is computationally prohibitive for large-scale data and image observations). Furthermore, many of these approaches suffer from instability when attempting to fine-tune large-capacity pre-trained policies, where the abrupt shift from the BC objective to an RL objective can lead to destructive gradient updates, oscillations, or even training collapse.

FLaRe addresses these critical gaps. It stands at the intersection of large-scale behavior cloning and reinforcement learning, aiming to synthesize their strengths while overcoming their individual weaknesses. By proposing a robust and scalable RL fine-tuning framework for existing large-scale BC policies, FLaRe directly tackles the performance plateau of current foundation models in robotics. It seeks to unlock the full potential of these pre-trained models by aligning them with true task objectives through efficient and stable RL, thereby enabling generalization to novel tasks, environments, and even robot embodiments, with minimal human intervention. This work represents a significant step towards creating truly generalist, adaptive, and masterful robot policies for real-world deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind FLaRe stems from the observed limitations of current large-scale multi-task behavior cloning (BC) policies in robotics. While these "foundation models" have learned valuable features and behavioral priors from vast demonstration datasets, their direct deployment in the real world often leads to unsatisfactory performance. They struggle to generalize to unseen states, fail to recover from errors, and are fundamentally constrained by the states observed during their pre-training. The overarching objective of FLaRe is to **break through this performance plateau and elevate the capabilities of these models to new heights** by strategically integrating reinforcement learning (RL).

The key objectives and motivations can be summarized as follows:

- **Align Pre-trained Policies with Task Completion:** The core insight of FLaRe is to leverage RL to align the behaviors of robust pre-trained BC policies towards actual task completion objectives, rather than merely imitating demonstrated trajectories. This alignment is hypothesized to lead to "masterful performance" not only on tasks seen during BC training but also on entirely novel tasks and embodiments.

- **Enhance Generalization Beyond Pre-training Data:** A primary goal is to enable robot policies to generalize effectively to scenarios not covered by their initial pre-training data. This includes adapting to new tasks for which no expert demonstrations are available and operating in environments with unseen configurations or objects.

- **Improve Robustness and Error Recovery:** By allowing the robot to learn through trial-and-error, FLaRe aims to make policies more robust to unexpected situations and capable of recovering from errors, which is a major weakness of pure BC approaches.

- **Develop a Stable and Scalable RL Fine-tuning Framework:** Previous attempts at RL fine-tuning of BC policies have often faced stability issues, especially with high-capacity networks. A key objective for FLaRe is to introduce a set of algorithmic and architectural design choices that ensure stable, effective, and scalable RL fine-tuning, preventing destructive gradient updates and training collapse.

- **Reduce Human Effort through Sparse Rewards:** The researchers are motivated to enable learning with minimal human input, specifically by utilizing simple sparse rewards (binary success/failure signals) rather than requiring labor-intensive, hand-crafted dense reward functions. This is crucial for scalability to new and diverse tasks.

- **Facilitate Rapid Adaptation to New Embodiments and Behaviors:** To foster the development of flexible and versatile robotic systems, FLaRe aims to enable efficient adaptation of policies to different robot platforms (embodiments) and to new desired behaviors (e.g., efficiency, collision avoidance) with minimal fine-tuning time.

- **Demonstrate Real-World Applicability:** Ultimately, the work aims to bridge the sim-to-real gap, ensuring that policies fine-tuned extensively in simulation can directly transfer and achieve state-of-the-art performance on real robot hardware in real-world environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FLaRe proposes an effective, scalable, and robust solution for fine-tuning large-scale robot policies using Reinforcement Learning. The methodology is built upon three core design choices, integrating robust pre-trained representations, large-scale training, and specific gradient stabilization techniques.

#### 3.1. Fine-tuning from a Multi-task Robotics Model

The first critical design choice is to start the fine-tuning process from a **multi-task pre-trained large model**, specifically a foundational robotics model. In this work, FLaRe utilizes the **SPOC model**, a multi-task transformer model designed for mobile manipulation tasks. SPOC is pre-trained on extensive shortest path expert trajectories collected in procedurally generated Objaverse-Populated ProcTHOR houses. The rationale for this choice is threefold:
- **Robust Representations and Behavior Priors:** Foundation models, pre-trained on diverse tasks, inherently learn robust visual features and versatile behavioral priors that significantly benefit the subsequent RL fine-tuning process.
- **Highly Capable Network Architecture:** Large transformer models, which underpin these foundational robotics models, offer strong inductive biases that facilitate generalization, crucial for learning novel tasks.
- **Multi-task Reusability:** Leveraging a multi-task base model allows the same architecture to be fine-tuned for a multitude of different tasks, even those unseen during pre-training, enhancing flexibility.

The SPOC model architecture (a slightly modified version using a Llama 2 decoder block for efficiency) consists of:
- **Vision Transformer Model (DinoV2):** A frozen DINOv2 backbone extracts dense visual features from two ego-centric RGB images (navigation and manipulation cameras), ensuring sim-to-real transfer. Learnable camera-type embeddings differentiate the visual inputs.
- **Transformer State Encoder:** Summarizes observations (visual features and text instructions from a pre-trained T5 encoder) into a state feature vector, acting as a text-conditioned visual state representation.
- **Causal Transformer Decoder:** Performs explicit memory modeling over time, consuming state representations, combining them with temporal position encodings and action embeddings, to generate a belief vector for action prediction. The KV-cache technique is used here to reduce inference complexity from quadratic to linear, enabling affordable large-scale fine-tuning.

#### 3.2. Large-Scale Fine-tuning in Simulation

The second key aspect of FLaRe's methodology is to perform **large-scale fine-tuning through extensive use of simulation**. This is made possible by modern embodied AI platforms like AI2THOR, which provides diverse objects (800K+ annotated 3D objects from Objaverse) and scenes (150K procedurally generated ProcTHOR houses).
To mitigate the inherent **sim-to-real gap**, FLaRe employs two techniques:
- **Extensive Domain Randomization:** Applied to images, including color augmentation, random crops, and posterization, to create visual diversity and improve robustness to real-world variations.
- **Foundational Vision Model:** Using DinoV2 as a frozen visual feature extractor helps capture generalizable features that transfer well between simulation and the real world.

#### 3.3. Stabilize RL Fine-tuning

FLaRe introduces a set of simple yet critical algorithmic choices to ensure the stability and effectiveness of RL fine-tuning, especially when working with large pre-trained policies:
- **Using On-policy Algorithms (PPO):** FLaRe opts for PPO, a state-of-the-art on-policy policy gradient method, over potentially more sample-efficient but less stable off-policy methods. Given the extensive simulation capabilities, sample efficiency is less of a constraint than training stability.
- **Taking Small Update Steps (Small Learning Rate):** A crucial finding is that fine-tuning pre-trained policies requires significantly lower learning rates (an order of magnitude smaller) than training RL from scratch. FLaRe uses a consistent, small learning rate (2e-5) across all experiments to maintain stability.
- **Disabling Entropy Bonus:** The entropy bonus in the PPO objective, which promotes exploration by increasing action distribution entropy, is found to distort policy gradients and lead to "unlearning" of the pre-trained policy during early fine-tuning. FLaRe removes this term.
- **Disabling Feature Sharing (Separate Actor and Critic Networks):** Unlike standard practice in RL with high-dimensional observations, FLaRe initializes the policy (actor) and value (critic) networks as independent entities. Both use the pre-trained transformer policy's weights and architecture, but the critic's policy head is replaced with randomly initialized value layers. This prevents gradients from the critic loss from altering the pre-trained features of the actor, which are already robust and beneficial.

These design choices collectively enable FLaRe to learn efficiently from sparse rewards, overcoming the challenges associated with fine-tuning large-scale behavior cloning models with reinforcement learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FLaRe demonstrates significant advancements across several key aspects of robot learning, evaluated through comprehensive experiments in both simulation and real-world settings.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance on Seen Capabilities (Q1)

FLaRe was evaluated on the challenging CHORES-S benchmark, which includes long-horizon mobile manipulation tasks such as Object Navigation, Fetch, PickUp, and RoomVisit. These tasks involve diverse skills like navigation, object recognition, manipulation, and environment exploration.
- **Simulation Performance:** FLaRe achieved an average success rate of **79.5%** in unseen simulated environments, representing an absolute improvement of **+23.6%** over the best prior state-of-the-art (SoTA) methods. This includes outperforming baselines that used computationally expensive dense rewards (e.g., Poliformer with Dense Reward), despite FLaRe using only sparse rewards and significantly fewer training steps (15x reduction in training time for ObjectNav compared to Poliformer).
- **Real-World Performance:** On real robots (Stretch RE-1) in an unseen apartment layout, FLaRe achieved an impressive **80.7%** average success rate, outperforming the best prior work (SPOC) by a substantial **+30.7%**. For ObjectNav, FLaRe achieved 94.4% success, compared to SPOC's 50.0% and Poliformer's 83.3%.

#### 4.2. Generalization to Novel Capabilities (Q2)

FLaRe's ability to generalize beyond the pre-training data was tested on three novel navigation tasks requiring capabilities unseen by the base model: ObjNavRelAttr (relative attribute comparison, e.g., "find the largest apple"), RoomNav (navigate to room types, e.g., "go to the kitchen"), and ObjNavAfford (object affordance understanding, e.g., "find something I can sit on").
- FLaRe achieved state-of-the-art performance on all these out-of-distribution tasks (e.g., 71.0% SR on ObjNavRelAttr, 91.6% on RoomNav, 79.7% on ObjNavAfford), without any additional hyperparameter tuning. This demonstrates its capacity to acquire new reasoning skills and adapt to new task formulations with minimal engineering effort.

#### 4.3. Real-World Transfer (Q3)

The efficacy of FLaRe's sim-to-real transfer techniques (domain randomization, frozen DinoV2 features) was confirmed by its strong performance on a real Stretch RE-1 robot in an unseen apartment. Policies trained in simulation were directly deployed, achieving leading success rates on real-world navigation and mobile manipulation tasks, validating the practical applicability of the approach.

#### 4.4. Efficient Adaptation (Q4)

FLaRe showcased its adaptability in two critical scenarios:
- **New Embodiment (Cross-embodiment Transfer):** FLaRe successfully fine-tuned the Stretch-RE1-trained SPOC model to adapt to a LoCoBot (a robot with different action spaces and camera parameters). It achieved a 72.0% success rate on ObjectNav with the new embodiment, significantly outperforming zero-shot and sparse reward Poliformer baselines on LoCoBot, demonstrating efficient transfer of learned knowledge.
- **New Behavior (Cross-objective Adaptation):** FLaRe was used to shape robot behavior after initial training. By adding simple reward terms (e.g., step penalty for efficiency, collision penalty for safety), the policy rapidly adapted to new behaviors (e.g., reducing episode length or number of collisions) after just 6 hours of fine-tuning, with minimal impact on overall task success.

#### 4.5. Necessity of Stabilization Techniques (Q5)

A crucial ablation study demonstrated the indispensable role of FLaRe's stabilization techniques:
- Removing any single component—switching to an off-policy algorithm (SAC), using a larger learning rate, enabling shared actor-critic features, or re-introducing the entropy bonus—resulted in a rapid collapse of the success rate to 0% on the Fetch task. This unequivocally highlights the critical importance of each proposed technique for ensuring stable and effective RL fine-tuning of large-scale robotic policies.

In summary, FLaRe establishes a new benchmark for robot policy performance, showcasing superior generalization, robustness, and adaptability, all while leveraging sparse rewards and efficient training on both seen and novel capabilities, with validated real-world deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FLaRe represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and robotic learning, with profound implications for the development of generalist, adaptive, and highly capable robotic systems. Its significance and potential impact stem from its ability to effectively bridge the gap between powerful pre-trained behavioral priors and the dynamic requirements of real-world task completion.

**1. Breaking the Performance Plateau of Foundation Models in Robotics:** The most immediate impact of FLaRe is its demonstrated ability to overcome the inherent limitations of large-scale Behavior Cloning (BC) policies. By providing a stable and effective mechanism for Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning, FLaRe unlocks the full potential of these "foundation models," pushing their performance beyond mere imitation to "masterful" execution. This means robots can now not only perform a wide array of tasks but also adapt to unseen situations, recover from errors, and generalize in ways previously inaccessible to pure BC systems.

**2. Enabling Efficient and Scalable Learning with Sparse Rewards:** FLaRe's success in learning complex, long-horizon tasks using only sparse, binary reward signals is a critical breakthrough. This drastically reduces the human effort and expertise traditionally required for meticulous reward engineering in RL, making the deployment of robots to new tasks significantly more practical and scalable. The 15x reduction in training time compared to prior SoTA methods, despite using sparse rewards, underscores its efficiency and paves the way for faster iteration cycles in robot development.

**3. Advancing Generalization and Adaptability:** FLaRe fundamentally enhances the generalization capabilities of robot policies. Its ability to learn novel tasks that require entirely new reasoning skills (e.g., relative attribute comparisons, affordance understanding)—tasks never encountered by the base pre-trained model—is a major step towards truly intelligent and versatile robots. Furthermore, the demonstrated rapid adaptation to new robot embodiments (e.g., from Stretch RE-1 to LoCoBot) and new behavioral objectives (e.g., improved efficiency, collision avoidance) highlights a path towards highly flexible robotic platforms that can be quickly repurposed for diverse applications and environments.

**4. Robust Real-World Applicability:** The successful direct deployment of FLaRe policies on a real robot in an unseen apartment, achieving state-of-the-art performance, validates the effectiveness of its sim-to-real transfer strategies. This confirms that the advancements made in simulation are directly applicable to the physical world, bringing the vision of capable, general-purpose robots closer to reality.

**5. Towards Continual and On-the-Fly Adaptation:** The ease with which FLaRe can fine-tune for new tasks by simply specifying a success criterion and language instructions suggests a promising direction for continual learning in robotics. Robots could potentially learn new skills or adapt to new instructions "on-the-fly" with minimal human intervention, making them more autonomous and responsive to changing requirements.

**6. Methodological Contributions for Stable RL Fine-tuning:** Beyond the impressive empirical results, FLaRe's systematic identification and validation of critical stabilization techniques (small learning rate, on-policy algorithms, disabling entropy bonus, separate actor/critic networks) provide invaluable guidelines for future research in fine-tuning large pre-trained models with RL. These insights address a widespread challenge in the field and will likely serve as foundational principles for subsequent work.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** The paper candidly acknowledges FLaRe's main limitation: its reliance on robust simulation environments for fine-tuning. While advancements in simulation generation are promising, tasks involving complex physics (e.g., liquids, soft objects, deformable materials) remain challenging to simulate accurately. This points to future research directions that may require fine-tuning directly in the real world, potentially through more advanced sim-to-real techniques or real-world data collection strategies.

In conclusion, FLaRe marks a substantial stride towards developing highly generalizable robotic systems. By synergizing the strengths of large-scale behavior cloning with a carefully stabilized reinforcement learning fine-tuning process, it paves the way for robots that are not only proficient but also adaptive, capable of mastering complex tasks and behaviors across a diverse range of environments and embodiments.
