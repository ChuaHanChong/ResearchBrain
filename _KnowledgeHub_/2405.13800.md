# Dense Connector for MLLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> A Dense Connector module enhances Multimodal Large Language Models' visual perception by integrating multi-layer features from frozen visual encoders with minimal computational cost. This approach achieves state-of-the-art results across 19 image and video benchmarks, demonstrating improved efficiency and zero-shot video understanding capabilities.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) typically rely solely on the final high-level features from pre-trained visual encoders.
- This practice underutilizes the rich, multi-layered visual information present in intermediate layers of the visual encoder.
- Ineffective utilization of visual signals can limit an MLLM's comprehensive understanding and perception capabilities.

## Method

- Introduces the **Dense Connector (DC)**, a plug-and-play module that integrates ==multi-layer visual features== from a frozen visual encoder.
- The DC processes these multi-layer features and maps them into the Large Language Model's (LLM) text space via a learnable ==MLP projector==.
- Three instantiations are explored: **Sparse Token Integration (STI)**, **Sparse Channel Integration (SCI)**, and **Dense Channel Integration (DCI)**, with DCI proving most effective by comprehensively incorporating grouped multi-layer features.

## Results

- Dense Connector achieved state-of-the-art performance across **19** image and video benchmarks, including up to **81.2%** on MMBench and **59.2%** on MM-Vet.
- The Efficient Dense Connector, by downsampling visual tokens by **75%**, achieved a **3x** inference speedup while maintaining strong performance, occasionally outperforming baselines with fewer tokens.
- Performance consistently improved across a wide range of LLM sizes (**2.7B** to **70B**) and demonstrated compatibility with various MLLM architectures (LLaVA, Mini-Gemini, LLaVA-NeXT) and visual encoders.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Leveraging intermediate features from frozen visual encoders, which are already computed offline, provides a 'free lunch' to MLLMs by enhancing visual perception with minimal to no additional computational overhead.
> - Aggregating features across multiple layers (e.g., via Dense Channel Integration) results in more robust and informative visual representations compared to using only the final layer or sparsely selected layers.
> - Image-trained MLLMs can achieve strong zero-shot video understanding capabilities by effectively processing multiple frames with the Dense Connector, demonstrating the generalizability of enriched visual representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The work is situated within the rapidly evolving field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), which integrate Large Language Models (LLMs) with visual understanding capabilities. The prevailing architectural paradigm for MLLMs typically comprises three core components: a pre-trained visual encoder (often frozen), a pre-trained LLM, and a connector module that bridges these two modalities.

Current MLLM research has largely concentrated on enhancing the linguistic aspect of these models, through initiatives such as developing larger and higher-quality instruction datasets or incorporating larger-sized LLMs. In contrast, the paper identifies a relative lack of research directed towards optimizing the utilization of visual signals. Specifically, MLLMs commonly rely solely on the final high-level features extracted from a frozen visual encoder, assuming these features are sufficient. This approach, however, may not fully exploit the rich, multi-layered visual information inherently present within the pre-trained visual encoder.

This research addresses this gap by proposing a method to leverage multi-layer visual features from the frozen visual encoder. This approach is positioned as a "free lunch" because it utilizes pre-computed offline features, incurring minimal additional computational overhead. It complements existing techniques that aim to increase visual signals, such as using higher-resolution images or introducing additional visual encoders. By focusing on a more effective integration of visual features into the connector module, this work aims to improve the visual perception capabilities of MLLMs, drawing inspiration from classical computer vision models like DenseNet and Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN) that have historically benefited from multi-layer feature aggregation. The proposed Dense Connector is designed to be a generic and plug-and-play module, allowing seamless integration with diverse existing MLLM architectures.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the visual understanding capabilities of existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by more effectively utilizing the visual encoder. The authors identify a significant, yet often overlooked, aspect of current MLLM design: the common practice of feeding only the final, high-level features from a frozen visual encoder into the language model. This practice raises the question of whether the full potential of the pre-trained visual encoder is being leveraged.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:
- **Incomplete Visual Signal Utilization**: Different layers within a Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder specialize in extracting distinct visual information. Shallow layers typically capture fine-grained, local details, while deeper layers process more abstract and semantic representations. By discarding intermediate layer features, MLLMs may be losing valuable visual cues necessary for comprehensive understanding. Attention maps visualized across different ViT layers illustrate this, showing that various layers emphasize different regions of interest within an image.
- **Historical Precedent in Computer Vision**: Previous successful architectures in computer vision, such as DenseNet and Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN), have demonstrated the benefits of integrating multi-layer features to create more robust and informative visual representations. This historical success suggests a similar benefit could be realized in MLLMs.
- **Computational Efficiency ("Free Lunch")**: Given that the visual encoder is typically frozen in MLLMs to manage computational costs, the multi-layer features are already computed offline. Leveraging these readily available features for the connector incurs minimal to no additional computational overhead during MLLM inference, presenting an efficient way to enhance visual information without increasing training or inference expenses significantly.
- **Generality and Compatibility**: The proposed approach is designed to be highly generic and plug-and-play, allowing for seamless integration into various existing MLLM architectures without requiring substantial modifications or retraining of the core visual and language models.

Building on these motivations, the authors propose the Dense Connector, aiming to deliver a simple, effective, and easily deployable module that significantly enhances MLLMs' visual perception. A secondary objective is to develop an "Efficient Dense Connector" that can maintain comparable performance with a reduced number of visual tokens, thereby addressing the computational burden associated with high token counts and improving inference speed. Furthermore, the research aims to demonstrate the zero-shot video understanding capabilities of their image-trained model, showcasing the robustness of the learned visual representations when extended to dynamic temporal data.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed Dense Connector (DC) integrates into the standard MLLM architecture, which typically consists of a pre-trained visual encoder (e.g., CLIP's ViT), a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM), and a connector module. The DC is designed to be plug-and-play, compatible with various MLLM frameworks such as LLaVA, LLaVA-NeXT, and Mini-Gemini.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The visual encoder $Vis(\cdot)$ extracts visual features from an input image. These features, $V \in \mathbb{R}^{L \times N \times D_v}$, represent $L$ layers of visual information, where $N$ is the number of visual tokens and $D_v$ is the feature dimension. The Dense Connector $DC(\cdot)$ then processes these multi-layer features and maps them into the LLM's text space. The $DC(\cdot)$ comprises two main parts:
1. **Multi-layer Visual Feature Integration**: This component combines features from different layers of the visual encoder.
2. **Learnable MLP Projector**: An MLP, consisting of two linear layers with a GELU activation function, maps the integrated visual features from $D_v$ to the LLM's hidden dimension $D_t$. This results in visual embeddings $e_v \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D_t}$ that are then concatenated with text embeddings and fed into the LLM.

#### 3.2. Instantiations of Dense Connector

The paper explores three primary instantiations for integrating multi-layer visual features:

- **Sparse Token Integration (STI)**:
 - This method directly increases the number of visual tokens fed to the LLM.
 - It selects visual features from specific intermediate layers (e.g., 8th, 16th) and the final layer (e.g., 24th) of the ViT.
 - Features from intermediate layers are downsampled using average pooling with a stride $\alpha$ (e.g., $\alpha=8$) to reduce the number of tokens ($N' = N/\alpha$) and mitigate computational overhead.
 - All selected visual token sequences (downsampled intermediate layer tokens and full final layer tokens) are concatenated along the token dimension.
 - This concatenated sequence is then passed through a shared MLP projector.
 - The resulting visual embedding $e_v \in \mathbb{R}^{(N+(K-1)\times N') \times D_t}$ provides a richer visual input.

- **Sparse Channel Integration (SCI)**:
 - This method aims to avoid increasing the token count while leveraging multi-layer features.
 - It concatenates visual features from selected layers (e.g., 8th, 16th, and 24th layers) along the feature (channel) dimension.
 - The MLP projector then serves a dual function: fusing these concatenated multi-layer features and projecting them into the LLM's text space, implicitly handling dimensionality reduction.
 - The visual embedding $e_v \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times D_t}$ maintains the original number of tokens, minimizing LLM computational overhead.

- **Dense Channel Integration (DCI)**:
 - Building on SCI, DCI aims to incorporate features from *all* visual encoder layers more comprehensively.
 - It partitions the $L$ layers of visual features into $G$ groups, each containing $M=L/G$ adjacent layers (e.g., for a 24-layer ViT, 2 groups of 12 layers each).
 - Features within each group are averaged to produce $G$ fused group representations ($GV_g$).
 - These $G$ fused representations are then concatenated with the final layer's features along the channel dimension.
 - The final concatenated feature is passed through the MLP projector.
 - This approach seeks to densely connect a wider range of visual layers while managing redundancy and high dimensionality.

#### 3.3. Efficient Dense Connector for Visual Token Optimization

To mitigate the computational burden of a large number of visual tokens (hundreds to thousands), an Efficient Dense Connector is proposed. After the initial visual embedding $e_v$ is generated by any Dense Connector instantiation, a parameter-free 2D interpolation function is applied to downsample the visual tokens (e.g., by a factor of 4, reducing 576 tokens to 144). These fewer, discrete visual tokens $e'_v$ are then concatenated with text tokens for input to the LLM, aiming to achieve comparable performance with a notable increase in inference speed.

#### 3.4. Training-Free Extension from Image to Video Conversational Models

Following the FreeVA paradigm, the image-based models trained with Dense Connector can be extended to video understanding without specific video training. For a given video, $T$ frames are uniformly sampled. Each frame is individually processed by the visual encoder and the Dense Connector to obtain a visual embedding $e_v$. The resulting sequence of embeddings $\{e_{v_1}, \ldots, e_{v_T}\}$ is then fed into the LLM.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Visual Encoders**: CLIP-ViT-L-336px and SigLIP-ViT-SO.
- **LLMs**: Phi-2-2.7B, Vicuna-7B/13B, Hermes-2-Yi-34B, Llama3-8B/70B-Instruct.
- **Dense Connector Layer Selection**: For CLIP-ViT-L (24 layers), STI/SCI used 8th, 16th, and 24th layers. DCI used 2 groups (1-12, 13-24 layers). Similar groupings for SigLIP ViT-SO (27 layers).
- **Training Datasets**: LLaVA-1.5 (558K image captions, 665K conversations) and Mini-Gemini (1.2M image-text captions, 1.5M conversations).
- **Training Recipe**: Two stages:
 1. **Pre-training**: Visual encoder and LLM frozen; Dense Connector randomly initialized and trained for 1 epoch (global batch size 256, LR 1e-3).
 2. **Instruction Fine-tuning**: Visual encoder frozen; Dense Connector and LLM updated for 1 epoch (global batch size 128, LR 2e-5). LoRA fine-tuning applied for larger LLMs (rank 128, alpha 256).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks**: 11 image benchmarks (GQA, VQAv2, ScienceQA, TextVQA, POPE, MathVista, MMBench, MM-Vet, MMMU, LLaVA-Bench-In-the-Wild, MME) and 8 video benchmarks (MSVD-QA, ActivityNet-QA, MSRVTT-QA, and generative metrics CI, DO, CU, TU, CO from Video-ChatGPT benchmark).

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments and ablation studies demonstrate the efficacy and versatility of the Dense Connector across various settings.

#### 4.1. Ablation Study on Dense Connector Instantiations

(Table 1 and Appendix A.1)
- **Sparse Token Integration (STI)**: Integrating features from the 8th, 16th, and 24th layers yielded notable improvements (e.g., 2.9% on MMBench) over the baseline (using only the 24th layer). While more layers (8th, 16th, 20th, 24th) offered marginal gains, they increased token count and computational cost.
- **Sparse Channel Integration (SCI)**: This method demonstrated superior performance compared to STI with minimal additional computational cost, achieving a 1.7% improvement on GQA by integrating features from the 8th, 16th, and 24th layers. Expanding the layer range within SCI did not consistently lead to further gains.
- **Dense Channel Integration (DCI)**: DCI, particularly when dividing layers into two groups (1-12, 13-24 for CLIP-L), achieved the most substantial improvements: 2.7% on ScienceQA and 2.5% on MMBench compared to the baseline. This indicates that a comprehensive integration of grouped multi-layer features is effective.
- **Further Exploration**: Attempts to introduce additional learnable parameters (e.g., 1D convolution for STI, 2D convolution for SCI, linear layer for DCI) did not result in consistent performance improvements. This suggests that maintaining the simplicity of the connector, which aids convergence during training with limited data, is beneficial. DCI was selected as the default instantiation for optimal performance.

#### 4.2. Compatibility and Scalability

(Table 2)
- **Visual Encoder Impact**: Replacing CLIP-ViT-L with the more advanced SigLIP-ViT-SO (384px) further boosted performance when integrated with Dense Connector (e.g., 64.2 GQA, 70.5 ScienceQA for Vicuna-7B).
- **Training Dataset Scale**: Fine-tuning with a larger dataset (Mini-Gemini's 1.2M+1.5M data) significantly improved results across benchmarks. For instance, the Vicuna-13B model achieved 36.5% on MathVista and 77.1% on ScienceQA.
- **Comparison with Mini-Gemini (MGM)**: Using the same training data and LLM (Vicuna-7B), Dense Connector surpassed the dual-encoder Mini-Gemini model across most benchmarks (e.g., +2.4% on MMBench, +4.2% on MM-Vet, +1.7% on MathVista).
- **High-Resolution Integration**: When integrated into high-resolution architectures like Mini-Gemini (dual encoder) and LLaVA-NeXT (dynamic AnyRes), the Dense Connector consistently yielded performance gains (e.g., +1.1% MathVista, +2.2% MMBench, +3.8% MM-Vet for MGM).

#### 4.3. Efficient Dense Connector

(Table 3)
The Efficient Dense Connector, by downsampling visual tokens from 576 to 144 (a 75% reduction), achieved a 3x improvement in inference speed and reduced training time. Despite this reduction, it maintained strong performance, surpassing other efficient methods like TokenPacker on multiple benchmarks (e.g., +0.9% GQA, +1.5% VQAv2, +2.5% MMBench). Notably, it slightly outperformed the LLaVA-v1.5 baseline on VQAv2 (79.4% vs. 78.5%) with only 25% of the visual tokens.

#### 4.4. Main Results: Image Understanding

(Table 4 and Appendix A.3)
- **Scaling LLMs (2.7B to 70B)**: Dense Connector consistently improved performance across a wide range of LLM sizes.
 - **Lightweight Models**: With Phi-2-2.7B, Dense Connector surpassed TinyLlava by 1.7% on MM-Vet.
 - **Vicuna-13B**: Outperformed LLaVA-1.5 Vicuna-13B with substantial gains (e.g., +2.1% GQA, +3.7% MMBench, +5.5% MM-Vet). Its performance was comparable to Mini-Gemini (13B) despite using a smaller training dataset.
 - **Llama3-8B**: Achieved significant improvements over LLaVA-LLama3 (e.g., +5.5% on MMBench).
 - **Larger LLMs (34B, 70B)**: The 70B LoRA model attained high scores (82.4% on ScienceQA, 79.4% on MMBench).
 - **High-Resolution and Fully Fine-tuned LLM**: The 34B AnyRes model set new state-of-the-art results on MMBench (81.2%), MM-Vet (59.2%), and LLaVA-Bench-in-the-Wild (97.7%).
 - **Model Zoo**: A Llama3-70B LoRA model, fine-tuned with Mini-Gemini instruction data, achieved 97.8% on LLaVA-Bench-in-the-Wild, closely matching GPT-4V's reported performance.

#### 4.5. Main Results: Video Understanding

(Table 5)
Leveraging the training-free FreeVA paradigm, models trained solely on image-text datasets with Dense Connector demonstrated strong zero-shot video understanding capabilities.
- The 13B model surpassed the LLaVA-1.5 with FreeVA baseline on MSVD and ActivityNet.
- The 34B model achieved state-of-the-art performance among training-free video QA methods on MSVD (77.4% accuracy), MSR-VTT (62.1% accuracy), and ActivityNet (55.8% accuracy).

#### 4.6. Qualitative Results

(Fig. 3 and Appendix A.5)
Qualitative evaluations showcased the model's ability to handle diverse visual and linguistic tasks, including detailed reasoning, OCR, table reading, mathematical problem-solving from images, and video understanding and reasoning. However, a limitation noted was occasional misinterpretation, such as referring to video content as "the image."

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The introduction of the Dense Connector marks a significant advancement in the development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by addressing a previously underexplored area: the under-utilization of multi-layer visual features from frozen visual encoders.

The primary significance lies in its **efficiency and effectiveness**. By leveraging offline multi-layer visual features, the Dense Connector significantly enhances the visual perception capabilities of MLLMs with minimal to no additional computational overhead. This "free lunch" approach makes it a highly practical solution for improving existing models without demanding extensive retraining or increased inference costs.

Its **plug-and-play nature and versatility** are key attributes. The Dense Connector has been demonstrated to integrate seamlessly into diverse MLLM architectures (e.g., LLaVA, LLaVA-NeXT, Mini-Gemini) and to scale effectively across various visual encoders, image resolutions, training dataset sizes, and a broad range of LLM parameters (from 2.7B to 70B). This broad compatibility suggests its applicability as a foundational enhancement for future MLLM development.

The **empirical results underscore its impact**, achieving state-of-the-art performance across 19 image and video benchmarks. These improvements span detailed reasoning, OCR, complex question-answering, and even zero-shot video understanding, which is a testament to the enriched visual representations it provides. The **Efficient Dense Connector** further contributes by demonstrating that significant performance can be maintained or even surpassed with a substantially reduced number of visual tokens, leading to faster inference speeds and lower computational requirements, which are crucial for real-world deployment and resource-constrained environments.

The **remarkable zero-shot capabilities in video understanding**, achieved without specific video training, indicate the robustness and generalizability of the visual features learned through the Dense Connector. This suggests that better image feature utilization can effectively bridge the gap to dynamic visual tasks.

In conclusion, this work offers a simple yet powerful module that addresses a fundamental limitation in MLLMs. It is anticipated to serve as a valuable basic component, influencing future MLLM research and development by promoting a more comprehensive and efficient utilization of visual information.

**Limitations** identified in the paper include the observation that current Dense Connector instantiations do not introduce additional parameters, and attempts to incorporate more complex parameterized modules did not consistently yield superior results. This suggests an ongoing need for further research into more sophisticated yet efficient ways to connect visual and language models for enhanced modality alignment.
