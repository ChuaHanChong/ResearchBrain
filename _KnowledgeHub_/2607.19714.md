# Morphing MILR: Design and control of a cable-driven limbless robot with rolling joints for maneuvering in complex environments

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Georgia Institute of Technology developed Morphing MILR, a cable-driven limbless robot that actively reconfigures its body morphology and compliance. This platform integrates multiple locomotion modes like lateral undulation, sidewinding, and rolling, demonstrating robust movement in varied environments by leveraging passive compliance.

## Problem

- Prior mechanically intelligent limbless robots were limited by fixed hardware morphologies, often specialized for a single dominant bending plane or gait-specific configuration.
- Achieving versatile locomotion with diverse gaits often required different specialized robots or sacrificed the inherent robustness offered by passive compliance in contact-rich environments.
- Traditional high-fidelity sensing and complex control systems for limbless robots can face challenges with uncertainty when contact conditions change or environmental knowledge is incomplete.

## Method

- A modular, cable-driven limbless robot (**Morphing MILR**) was designed, featuring identical modules with both bending joints and rolling base joints for active body reorientation.
- The robot integrates ==bilateral cable actuation== for ==programmable compliance==, allowing for controlled slack in cables to enable adaptive terrain interaction and robust movement.
- An ==open-loop control strategy==, using predefined gait templates and compliance modulation, enables transitions between lateral undulation, sidewinding, rolling, and twisting gaits.

## Results

- Morphing MILR reliably reconfigured its rolling bases and generated distinct gaits (sidewinding, rolling, twisting) in obstacle-free environments, meeting commanded parameters within **5°**.
- The robot demonstrated repeatable in-lattice progression, preserving the benefits of mechanical intelligence and compliance for obstacle-aided undulation, with high compliance (**G=1**) yielding the most successful runs.
- In **13 out of 15** trials, the robot successfully transitioned between compliant lateral undulation in a lattice to rolling and then sidewinding in open terrain, maintaining continuous locomotion across varied environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Active morphological reconfigurability can be successfully integrated into mechanically intelligent, compliance-enabled designs without compromising the inherent robustness of the system.
> - Programmable compliance, especially during gait transitions in obstacle-rich environments, is crucial for accommodating contacts, reducing transmission loads, and preventing jamming.
> - Unifying diverse limbless locomotion modes within a single reconfigurable platform significantly enhances versatility and adaptability for continuous operation in heterogeneous environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Limbless robots, often inspired by biological snakes, are a field of active research due to their potential for navigating confined and complex environments. Their slender, hyper-redundant bodies offer access to spaces inaccessible to conventional wheeled or legged robots, making them suitable for applications such as search and rescue, environmental monitoring, and industrial inspection.

Within this landscape, two primary approaches have emerged for developing limbless locomotion:
1. **High-fidelity sensing and complex control:** Many platforms achieve advanced capabilities, including out-of-plane motions like sidewinding, lifting, climbing, and rolling, by employing sophisticated sensing, high-bandwidth feedback, and carefully tuned control algorithms. However, these systems can face challenges with uncertainty when contact conditions change or when environmental knowledge is incomplete.
2. **Mechanical intelligence through morphology and compliance:** An alternative strategy embeds "mechanical intelligence" into the robot's physical design, utilizing passive compliance and contact-mediated interactions to simplify control. This approach allows body-environment dynamics to contribute directly to effective locomotion, often reducing the need for explicit terrain knowledge or complex real-time computation. Previous work by the research group, for example, demonstrated robust obstacle-aided undulation using cable-driven bilateral actuation with programmable compliance (Wang et al., 2023) and sidewinding facilitated by anisotropic compliance (Kojouharov et al., 2024).

This current work, "Morphing MILR," extends the second paradigm. While previous mechanically intelligent limbless robots have shown robustness, they have generally been limited to fixed hardware morphologies, often specialized for a single dominant bending plane or gait-specific configuration. This limitation restricts the diversity of locomotion styles a single device can express. Morphing MILR directly addresses this limitation by introducing active reconfigurability to the mechanically intelligent, compliance-enabled design framework. It seeks to unify multiple limbless locomotion modes within a single platform, thereby enhancing versatility while aiming to preserve the robustness offered by passive compliance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a limbless robot platform that can achieve versatile locomotion by reconfiguring its body morphology and compliance, while simultaneously preserving the inherent robustness of compliance-mediated locomotion.

This objective is driven by several motivations:
- **Addressing the fixed morphology limitation:** Prior mechanically intelligent limbless robots, despite their robustness in specific locomotion modes or environments, are typically constrained by fixed hardware designs. Each morphology is often specialized for a single gait or a limited range of environments, necessitating different robots for different tasks or terrains. The research aims to overcome this "fixed configuration" challenge.
- **Enabling diverse locomotion modes within a single system:** A single limbless robot capable of executing multiple locomotion styles (e.g., lateral undulation, sidewinding, rolling, twisting) would significantly expand its utility in diverse, complex environments. The motivation is to integrate these capabilities into one unified platform.
- **Maintaining robustness in complex, contact-rich environments:** The advantage of mechanically intelligent designs is their ability to leverage body-terrain interactions and passive compliance for robust movement without extensive sensing or complex feedback. A key motivation is to introduce reconfigurability without sacrificing this robustness, particularly in confined and cluttered spaces where traditional sensing and control might fail.
- **Facilitating rapid and smooth gait transitions:** Real-world applications often require robots to adapt to changing terrain conditions dynamically. The ability to rapidly reconfigure body morphology and smoothly transition between locomotion modes is crucial for continuous operation in heterogeneous environments, rather than stopping, reconfiguring, and restarting.
- **Expanding applications in challenging domains:** By combining versatility and robustness, such a robot could find broader applications in areas like search and rescue (navigating debris fields), environmental monitoring (traversing varied natural landscapes), and industrial inspection (moving through complex machinery or pipelines), where environments are often complex, unknown, and contact-rich.

In summary, the motivation is to create a more adaptable and autonomous limbless robot that can leverage the benefits of mechanical intelligence across a wider range of scenarios by dynamically altering its physical form.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The researchers developed "Morphing MILR," a modular cable-driven limbless robot, designed to reconfigure its body morphology and compliance. The methodology involves a combination of novel mechanical design and an open-loop control strategy leveraging passive compliance.

**A. Robot Design Overview:**
Morphing MILR consists of six identical, repeated modules, with the capacity for expansion. Each module integrates two primary functionalities:
1. **Bending Joint with Bilateral Cable Actuation:** This mechanism, inspired by musculoskeletal actuation, allows each joint to bend.
2. **Rolling Base Joint:** This joint reorients the bending plane of the module.

**B. Rolling Base Design:**
The rolling base is responsible for reorienting body segments. Key features include:
- **High Torque Geared Transmission:** A compact servo motor (Robotis XC330) drives the rotating base through a worm, helical, and spur gear stages. This design provides a 1:13 gear reduction, determined to be sufficient for lifting adjacent modules and overcoming friction.
- **Reinforced Housing:** An integrated aluminum plate reinforces the housing to maintain consistent gear engagement under heavy loads, mitigating flexing of the 3D-printed parts.
- **Non-Backdrivable Worm Gear:** The worm gear within the transmission makes the system non-backdrivable, enabling the robot to hold a configured orientation without continuous power supply to the motor.
- **Slip Rings:** Embedded slip rings ensure continuous power and communication connectivity across the rolling joints, preventing internal wiring from twisting during continuous rotation.

**C. Bilateral Cable Actuation:**
Each bending joint utilizes a bilateral pulley-cable system:
- **Dual-Axis Servo Motor:** A Robotis 2XL430 servo motor drives opposing pulleys to control the length of high-strength, inextensible braided cables.
- **Joint Bending:** The cables are routed on a plane perpendicular to the joint axis. By differentially adjusting cable lengths, the joint angle can be set, allowing up to 70 degrees of bending before self-collision.
- **Programmable Compliance:** This bilateral actuation scheme allows for modulation of joint compliance. A generalized compliance variable (G) can be set, introducing controlled slack in the cables to enable adaptive terrain interaction. In this work, G=1 (fully compliant) was primarily used during obstacle interaction and gait changes, while G=0 (non-compliant) was used during obstacle-free locomotion.

**D. Electrical System:**
All actuators (cable motors and rolling base motors) are daisy-chained on a common power and communication bus (RS-485 serial). An external motor controller (Robotis U2D2) communicates with the actuator network. The system operates tethered to a power supply and an external computer.

**E. Robot Control:**
The control strategy is largely open-loop, relying on predefined gait templates and the robot's embedded mechanical intelligence.
- **Joint Control:** Cable lengths are precisely set based on robot geometry to achieve desired joint angles (Equation 1). The programmable compliance (Equation 2) is layered onto these commands, particularly during contact-rich scenarios.
- **Gait Implementation:**
 - **Lateral Undulation:** All rolling bases are aligned to a common plane (β_i = 0°). Motion is generated by a head-to-tail propagating "serpenoid" body wave (Equation 4).
 - **Sidewinding:** Rolling bases are set to an alternating orientation pattern (β_i ∈ {90°, 0°, -90°, 0°, 90°}). Orthogonal body waves are superposed with differing amplitudes (A_H < A_V) to produce characteristic sidewinding motion (Equation 6).
 - **Rolling:** Similar morphology to sidewinding (alternating β_i), but with equal amplitudes for both orthogonal body waves (Equation 7) and a spatial frequency below one, resulting in perpendicular motion with a lower profile.
 - **Twisting:** All bending joints are held straight (α_i = 0°). Motion is generated solely by the rolling base joints, actuated with constant, slightly offset angular velocities (Equation 9) to create a distributed twist along the body for in-place rotation.
- **Morphology Control for Gait Transition:** Transitions involve:
 1. Returning the robot to a "home" state (aligned joint planes, straight joints).
 2. Commanding the rolling bases to the new target configuration (e.g., from lateral undulation to sidewinding β_i pattern).
 3. Resuming undulation with the new gait.
 - **Compliance-mediated Transition:** During transitions, programmable compliance (high G value) is temporarily activated. This allows the robot to accommodate incidental contacts, reduce transmission loads, and prevent jamming, particularly in tight spaces, as the rolling bases reorient.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Morphing MILR focused on three main areas: reliable morphology change in obstacle-free environments, preservation of mechanical intelligence in organized obstacle fields, and the ability to transition between environments and gaits.

**A. Obstacle Free Testing:**
- **Reliable Morphology Change:** The robot was tested for its ability to repeatedly reorient its rolling bases to desired configurations for sidewinding, rolling, and twisting.
 - **Sidewinding (5 trials):** Achieved with A_V = 30°, A_H = 60°, spatial frequency 1.2, temporal frequency 4 Hz.
 - **Rolling (5 trials):** Achieved with A = 60° for both waves, spatial frequency 1.2, temporal frequency 4 Hz.
 - **Twisting (5 trials):** Demonstrated with individual module rotation speeds ranging from 5.6 to 22.5 °/s.
- **Performance Metrics:** Success was defined by wave amplitudes and rolling base angles being within ±5° of commanded values, and spatial/temporal frequencies matching instructed values without drift.
- **Outcome:** The robot passed all criteria checks across these trials, indicating reliable morphology reconfiguration and gait generation in an unconstrained environment. Generalized compliance (G=0) was not used in these tests.

**B. Lattice Testing:**
- **Purpose:** To evaluate if Morphing MILR maintains "mechanical intelligence" (robust progression without jamming) in organized obstacle fields, as seen in previous designs.
- **Methodology:** The robot navigated a lattice using compliant lateral undulation. Parameters included A = 60°, spatial frequency 1.1, temporal frequency 2 Hz. The generalized compliance variable (G) was varied over 20 trials.
- **Outcome:** Morphing MILR achieved repeatable in-lattice progression, consistent with prior findings that compliance improves obstacle-aided undulation. The majority of successful runs occurred with G=1 (high compliance). Lower G values frequently led to localized jamming, and higher G values sometimes led to a lack of forward progression. This confirmed that the platform successfully preserved the benefits of bilateral cable actuation's mechanical intelligence.

**C. Environment Transition Testing:**
- **Purpose:** To demonstrate the robot's ability to maintain locomotion while transitioning between obstacle-rich and obstacle-free terrains, a scenario where fixed-morphology mechanically intelligent systems previously faced limitations.
- **Methodology:** 15 trials were conducted. The robot started by traversing a lattice using compliant lateral undulation (A = 70°, ξ = 1.1, ω = 4 Hz, G = 1). Once a majority of the body exited the lattice, the robot transitioned to rolling (A = 60°, ξ = 0.8, ω = 4 Hz, G = 0) to fully disengage and change heading. Finally, it transitioned to sidewinding (A_V = 60°, A_H = 30°, ξ = 1.2, ω = 4 Hz, G = 0) to continue traversal in open terrain until the end of the testing zone.
- **Transition Strategy:** In clear environments, the robot returned to a straight, aligned "home" configuration before changing gait. In obstacle-rich regions, transitions were executed with high G (compliance) to prevent mechanical failures and motor overloads.
- **Outcome:** In 13 out of 15 trials, Morphing MILR successfully navigated the entire sequence, escaping the lattice and reaching the end of the platform. Failures were primarily attributed to the robot's tail jamming during environment transitions. These experiments demonstrated the platform's central contribution: active morphology reorientation without losing the benefits of compliance, enabling continuous operation in heterogeneous environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Morphing MILR represents a notable advancement in the field of limbless robotics, particularly within the paradigm of mechanical intelligence. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

- **Unification of Mechanically Intelligent Locomotion Modes:** The platform successfully unifies previously separate mechanically intelligent limbless robot morphologies into a single, reconfigurable architecture. This integration allows a single robot to perform diverse gaits such as lateral undulation, sidewinding, rolling, and twisting, moving beyond the limitations of fixed-configuration designs specialized for a single mode or environment.
- **Enhanced Versatility and Adaptability:** By enabling active remapping of bending planes and reorientation of body-wave directions in three dimensions, Morphing MILR significantly enhances the robot's versatility. This means the robot can adapt its locomotion style dynamically to different terrains and environmental conditions without needing to be manually reconfigured or requiring multiple specialized robots for various tasks.
- **Preservation of Robustness through Compliance:** A critical aspect of this work is demonstrating that the added complexity of reconfigurability does not compromise the robustness afforded by programmable passive compliance. The robot continues to exhibit reliable, contact-rich locomotion, even in cluttered environments, leveraging body-environment interactions and mitigating the need for high-bandwidth sensing and complex, real-time control.
- **Facilitation of Gait Transitions:** The ability to rapidly and smoothly transition between different locomotion modes, especially with compliance modulation to prevent jamming in tight spaces, is a crucial step towards truly autonomous operation in complex, unknown, and heterogeneous environments. This "on-the-fly" adaptation capacity addresses a long-standing challenge in robotic locomotion.
- **Foundation for Three-Dimensional Motion Patterns:** Beyond the demonstrated gaits, the modular design with rolling base joints provides a foundation for synthesizing novel three-dimensional motion patterns. Future work could exploit geometric descriptions of motion (e.g., curvature and torsion profiles) to map desired 3D paths into coordinated joint commands, opening up possibilities for more complex maneuvering.
- **Potential for Field-Relevant Applications:** The combination of versatility, robustness, and adaptability makes Morphing MILR suitable for challenging real-world applications. These include search and rescue operations in collapsed structures, environmental monitoring in varied natural landscapes, and industrial inspection in complex machinery, where the ability to navigate confined, cluttered, and changing environments is paramount.

In summary, this research advances limbless robotics by integrating active morphological reconfigurability with the established benefits of mechanical intelligence and programmable compliance. This approach offers a path toward more autonomous, adaptable, and robust limbless robots capable of generalized multi-terrain locomotion. Future work on continuous gait-transition controllers and quantitative performance comparisons will further refine and validate this versatile platform.
