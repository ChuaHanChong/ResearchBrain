# SpaceVista: All-Scale Visual Spatial Reasoning from mm to km

> [!summary] Summary
> SpaceVista introduces a comprehensive solution for advancing spatial reasoning capabilities in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) across physical scales ranging from millimeters to kilometers. This work provides a new dataset, SpaceVista-1M, and a model, SpaceVista-7B, demonstrating superior performance on the accompanying SpaceVista-Bench evaluation benchmark, outperforming existing proprietary and open-source models by over 3%.

## Problem

- Existing spatial reasoning research, particularly with MLLMs, is largely confined to constrained environments and limited physical scales, hindering generalization to diverse real-world applications.
- Current spatial reasoning datasets are costly, rely on laborious manual annotations, and lack diversity across broad scales and 'in-the-wild' scenes.
- MLLMs lack effective architectures and training paradigms to handle vast differences in object sizes, scene layouts, and semantic complexities across scales, leading to 'knowledge conflict' in reasoning.

## Method

- An automated, specialist-driven pipeline curates ==SpaceVista-1M==, a dataset of **1 million** spatial Question-Answer pairs from **38,000** video scenes, spanning five distinct scales and 19 task types.
- ==SpaceVista-7B==, an MLLM, integrates dense visual inputs (DINOv3), employs ==LoRA-like 'scale experts'== with a dynamic router to mitigate cross-scale knowledge conflicts, and utilizes a progressive reward system for Reinforcement Learning training.
- SpaceVista-Bench, a high-fidelity, hand-crafted evaluation benchmark, ensures accurate, physically grounded assessment across all scales using real-world measurements and human annotation.

## Results

- SpaceVista-7B achieves over **3%** higher overall performance on the new SpaceVista-Bench compared to state-of-the-art proprietary and open-source models, demonstrating robust all-scale reasoning capabilities.
- The model shows significantly higher scores in challenging scenarios like Tiny Tabletop, Tabletop, and Outdoor scenes within SpaceVista-Bench, delivering around **6%** improvement over other open-source models.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of scale experts, progressive rewards, and dense visual inputs in improving performance and mitigating cross-scale knowledge conflicts, with performance generally improving with more experts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating dense geometric features from self-supervised vision models (e.g., DINOv3) into MLLMs significantly enhances spatial perception beyond purely semantic understanding.
> - Scale-aware expert architectures, such as LoRA-like experts with a dynamic router, effectively mitigate 'knowledge conflicts' and enable MLLMs to specialize knowledge for reasoning across vastly different physical scales.
> - A progressive reward mechanism using semantic, scale, and answer anchors during reinforcement learning guides the model's Chain-of-Thought, promoting human-like, scale-centric spatial reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of spatial reasoning, particularly enhanced by Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), is an area of active research, aiming to enable intelligent systems to interpret and interact with environments across various scales. Current efforts in visual spatial reasoning have primarily focused on improving models and data. From a data perspective, previous works have utilized scanning-based or image-based datasets, often employing automated pipelines for acquiring information for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). Model advancements have involved leveraging latent features, incorporating geometric information, and refining training and thinking approaches, including multi-turn dialogues for self-correction.

However, existing methodologies and datasets for spatial reasoning primarily concentrate on indoor settings, specific objects, and constrained scales, as acknowledged by the authors. This limitation prevents a holistic understanding of environments ranging from microscopic objects (millimeters) to expansive landscapes (kilometers). Datasets often rely on indoor scanning video data and labor-intensive 3D annotations, which are not scalable for diverse, real-world, and wild scenes across a broad range of scales. Furthermore, current models tend to overfit to single scenes due to the absence of dedicated training frameworks for all-scale understanding.

This paper addresses these gaps by presenting a comprehensive solution designed to tackle the challenges of data curation, model development, and evaluation for all-scale scenarios. It positions itself as a pioneering attempt to broaden the scope of spatial intelligence, encompassing a six-order-of-magnitude range from millimeters to kilometers, thereby supporting diverse applications from advanced manufacturing and embodied intelligence to autonomous driving and remote sensing. The work aims to integrate structured spatial reasoning knowledge, scale-aware modeling, and progressive training to overcome the limitations of single-scale or indoor-focused approaches, offering a generalized framework for visual spatial reasoning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance all-scale visual spatial reasoning, enabling intelligent systems to comprehend and interact with environments across a wide spectrum of physical dimensions, from millimeters (mm) to kilometers (km). This objective is motivated by two key challenges prevalent in the existing research landscape:

1. **Heavy Reliance on Indoor 3D Scans and Labor-Intensive Annotations for Dataset Curation:** Current spatial reasoning datasets predominantly depend on indoor 3D scanning videos, which often feature relatively simple scenes and necessitate extensive manual 3D annotations. This approach is costly, time-consuming, and difficult to scale for large, diverse, and unconstrained real-world environments. The paper highlights that building large-scale, "wild" datasets that span a range from millimeters to kilometers is hindered by the prohibitive cost of annotation for complex scenes and the difficulty in obtaining precise evaluations aligned with the physical world. The motivation here is to overcome these limitations by developing a more efficient and scalable method for dataset creation.

2. **Absence of All-Scale Modeling, Leading to Overfitting to Single Scenes:** Existing spatial reasoning models are primarily optimized for specific settings, such as indoor environments. This specialization often results in models that struggle to generalize across different scales, leading to suboptimal performance or significant deviations when applied to diverse real-world scenes like tiny tabletops or expansive outdoor views. There is a recognized lack of dedicated training frameworks that can facilitate a holistic understanding across all scales. The motivation is to introduce a modeling approach that can effectively integrate and differentiate knowledge across varying scales, preventing knowledge conflicts and fostering robust generalization.

To address these motivations, the authors established several specific objectives:
- **Dataset Development:** To create a diverse, real-world, all-scale reasoning dataset, named **SpaceVista-1M**, comprising over 1 million question-answer (QA) pairs across 19 diverse tasks and 5 distinct spatial scales, using a task-specific, specialist-driven automated pipeline. This dataset is designed to support both Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with rationales and Reinforcement Learning (RL) with regression/multiple-choice answers.
- **Benchmark Creation:** To construct an accurate and reliable all-scale benchmark, named **SpaceVista-Bench**, through manual recording, retrieval of authoritative sources, and human annotation, to facilitate precise evaluation of all-scale spatial reasoning capabilities.
- **Model Innovation:** To introduce a novel spatial reasoning model, **SpaceVista-7B**, that accepts inputs beyond semantics (e.g., geometry-based and depth-based information), incorporates scale as an anchor for scale-aware experts, and employs a progressive training paradigm to manage potential cross-scale knowledge conflicts during fine-tuning.

In summary, the research is driven by the need for spatial intelligence that can operate robustly and accurately across the full spectrum of human-perceptible scales, moving beyond the constrained focus of much prior work in the field.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in this paper introduces a holistic solution for all-scale spatial reasoning, integrating a structured spatial reasoning knowledge system, scale-aware modeling, and a progressive training paradigm. This approach involves the creation of a new dataset and benchmark, and the development of a specialized model.

#### 3.1. Dataset and Benchmark Construction (SpaceVista-1M and SpaceVista-Bench)

The authors developed **SpaceVista-1M**, a dataset designed to address the limitations of existing single-scale datasets. The construction pipeline follows a multi-step procedure:

1. **Data Preparation:**
 - **Source Selection:** Videos from widely used datasets that provide 3D scene modeling and camera parameters were selected (e.g., uCO3D, WildRGB-D, SMOT, ScanNet, DL3DV). These sources primarily feature static scenes.
 - **Metric Depth and Normal Map Estimation:** Specialized metric depth models (Metric3Dv2, UniDepthV2) and video depth models (Video-Depth-Anything) were employed to obtain accurate metric depth maps and normal maps. Consistency across frames was ensured through an energy function minimization. Specific adjustments were made for extreme scales (drone-view and tiny objects).
 - **Semantic and Bounding Box Extraction:** Proprietary grounding specialists (DINO-X, Grounding DINO) were used to extract per-frame semantics and bounding boxes.
 - **Cross-Frame Object Consistency:** SAM2 was integrated with grounding experts to generate temporally consistent masks and unique object IDs across frames (Grounded-SAM2). This ensures both semantic and spatial consistency.

2. **Task Construction:**
 - **Canonical View Transformation:** Using known camera parameters, object positions and dimensions were obtained and transformed into a 3D Cartesian coordinate system centered at the camera's optical center.
 - **Task Definition:** 19 diverse tasks were designed, including general tasks (e.g., Position/Size Comparison, Object Counting, Distance Estimation) and scale-specific tasks (e.g., tabletop object manipulation, drone-view area estimation). Each task had a carefully designed computational workflow (e.g., for object counting: detect objects, propagate masks, track identities, filter scenes, derive counts).

3. **QA Construction:**
 - **Generation Strategies:** A hybrid approach combining GPT-based (for flexible questions like planning) and template-based (for fixed questions like counting/object size) methods was used to generate 1 million QA pairs. Over 3,000 templates were manually curated for diversity.
 - **RL Rewards:** Multiple-choice options were generated to serve as rewards for Reinforcement Learning (RL).

4. **CoT Annotation:**
 - **Rationale Generation:** Cognition-inspired few-shot prompting with advanced MLLMs (Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct) was used to generate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) rationales for step-by-step explanations.
 - **Quality Filtering:** A filtering policy was applied to remove low-quality or inconsistent rationale outputs, ensuring high-quality rationale for fundamental knowledge injection during SFT.

5. **Input Extension:**
 - The dataset supports multimodal inputs beyond plain visual video, including point, bounding box, and mask annotations, allowing for future interactive usage. Each input type is designed with its own template and CoT rationales.

6. **Quality Control & Evaluation (SpaceVista-Bench):**
 - **SpaceVista-1M (Training):** A portion of the training set was manually verified for quality control, achieving an average global accuracy of approximately 83% in terms of human preference.
 - **SpaceVista-Bench (Evaluation):** A highly accurate benchmark was created by manually measuring and recording real-world objects, retrieving information from authoritative sources (e.g., Wikipedia for landmarks), and employing human annotation for non-distance problems. This benchmark comprises about 1,600 QA pairs across approximately 300 unique video scenes, with a focus on strict physical world alignment.

#### 3.2. Model Development (SpaceVista-7B)

The **SpaceVista-7B** model is built upon the Qwen2.5-VL-7B architecture and designed to enhance spatial reasoning through specific architectural and training innovations:

1. **Beyond Semantics:**
 - **Self-Supervised Encoder Integration:** To overcome the semantic-only focus of typical MLLM tokenizers, dense features from self-supervised vision models (DINOv3) were incorporated. DINOv3's last layer produces patch-level dense features ($F_D$), which are mapped to the visual feature dimension ($d_V$) via an MLP.
 - **Feature Fusion:** The processed DINOv3 features ($F'_D$) are fused with the Qwen-2.5-VL visual encoder features ($F_V$) using multi-layer cross-attention: $F'_V = CA(F_V, F'_D, F'_D) + F_V$. This fusion aims to provide geometric and depth-based spatial understanding alongside semantic information.

2. **Scale Experts Design:**
 - **LoRA-like Module:** To mitigate "knowledge conflicts" arising from mixing cross-scale information, a LoRA-like module was introduced. This module adds *M* scale experts, each fine-tuning only a small percentage (0.5%) of overall parameters.
 - **Dynamic Scaling:** Each expert $i$ has a base weight $\alpha_i$ and is dynamically scaled by a learned factor $\lambda_i$, where $\lambda_i$ is determined by a scale router (an MLP and a softmax layer). This allows different layers of the foundation LLM to select and allocate appropriate experts, preserving knowledge independence between scales.

3. **Process Reward Design (for RL Training):**
 - **Human-Inspired Reasoning:** The training strategy is designed to mimic human spatial reasoning, which typically involves identifying task-specified semantics, perceiving global scale, and inferring answers from spatial relations.
 - **Anchor-Based Rewards:** Three anchor states were defined for RL: `<semantics>` (for referenced objects), `<scale>` (for overall scene scale), and `<answer>` (for correctness).
 - **Progressive Reward Formulation:** The updated correctness reward $\bar{R}_{answer}$ is formulated as a product of individual anchor rewards (R$_{answer}$, R$_{scale}$, R$_{semantic}$), where the order of application signifies importance (earlier rewards are stricter). R$_{scale}$ uses a logarithmic difference for scale estimation, and R$_{semantic}$ uses cosine similarity for semantic embeddings. Task-specific variations are incorporated (e.g., some tasks don't require semantic/scale rewards).

4. **RL Training Objective:**
 - The final policy $\pi_{\theta}$ is updated by maximizing a PPO-like objective function ($J(\theta)$), using groupwise normalized advantages ($A_i$) and KL divergence regularization.

5. **Training Strategy:**
 - **Cold-Start Phase:** Initial SFT training on SpaceVista-1M, optimizing input projection, feature-fusion modules, and scale experts.
 - **Specialization Phase:** Introduction of the scale router to train each scale-specific expert on appropriate inputs, encouraging specialization.
 - **RL Phase:** Application of RL training on the SFT model to obtain the final SpaceVista-7B reasoning model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across dataset creation, model performance, and ablation studies:

#### 4.1. Dataset and Benchmark Contributions

- **SpaceVista-1M:** The authors successfully curated SpaceVista-1M, a dataset comprising 1 million spatial question-answer (QA) pairs across 19 diverse tasks and 5 spatial scales (from mm to km), derived from over 38,000 video scenes. This dataset supports both Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with rationales and Reinforcement Learning (RL) with regression/multiple-choice answers, addressing the scarcity of all-scale spatial reasoning data.
- **SpaceVista-Bench:** A new, accurate video benchmark, SpaceVista-Bench, was meticulously hand-crafted. It consists of approximately 1,600 QA pairs across 300 unique video scenes, with ground truth established through real-world measurements, authoritative sources, and human annotations. This benchmark is specifically designed to evaluate all-scale reasoning capabilities with high precision.

#### 4.2. Overall Model Performance

- **Competitive Performance:** SpaceVista-7B (with RL) demonstrated competitive performance across five spatial reasoning benchmarks: MMSI-Bench, SPAR-Bench, VSI-Bench, STI-Bench, and SpaceVista-Bench.
- **Superiority on SpaceVista-Bench:** Notably, SpaceVista-7B achieved substantially superior performance on the novel all-scale SpaceVista-Bench, exceeding the average performance of both proprietary and open-source models by over 3%. This indicates its effectiveness in generalizing across diverse scales and scenarios, which is the core objective of the work.
- **Comparison on Subsets:** Analysis of SpaceVista-Bench subsets (Tiny, Tabletop, Indoor, Outdoor) revealed that small-scale scenarios particularly challenge commercial and general models. SpaceVista-7B achieved comparatively high comprehensive scores across all scenarios, showing a clear improvement of approximately 6% over other open-source models in all-scale spatial reasoning.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies on Model Components

- **Scale Experts:** Ablation studies confirmed that the introduction of scale experts yielded substantial performance gains, indicating their effectiveness in mitigating knowledge conflicts during cross-scale training. Experimentation with the number of experts (Table 4) suggested that 4 experts provided optimal performance, while excessive experts led to diminishing returns due to load imbalance on the router.
- **Progressive Reward Design:** The progressive reward mechanism, which integrates semantic, scale, and correctness anchors, achieved higher performance compared to unconstrained reasoning paths. This highlights the importance of guiding the model's thinking process with explicit anchors to ensure spatial compliance and human-like reasoning.
- **Modality Integration (Beyond Semantics):** Incorporating dense features from the DINOv3 self-supervised vision model yielded greater performance gains compared to VGGT. This was attributed to DINOv3's strong dense features, learned through self-supervised pretraining, which provide rich depth, normal, and pattern representations, complementing the semantic information from the base MLLM.

#### 4.4. Memorization vs. Reasoning Observation

- The research observed that models, including SpaceVista-7B, can exhibit a bias towards memorizing fixed sizes for common objects. While this reflects human reliance on reference objects, it can lead to inaccuracies in out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios with unusual object sizes (e.g., Guinness World Records data). SpaceVista-7B demonstrated better performance on OOD and varied-scale sets compared to general models, indicating some progress in reasoning beyond memorized priors, even if the challenge remains. The analysis distinguished between OOD categories with normal sizes and normal categories with OOD sizes, suggesting the former benefits from all-scale training while the latter remains challenging for both general and specialized models.

In summary, the main findings demonstrate the successful development of a novel all-scale spatial reasoning framework, comprising a comprehensive dataset, a robust benchmark, and a specialized model. The model's architectural and training innovations, particularly scale experts and progressive rewards, contributed to its enhanced performance and generalization across a wide range of spatial scales and scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of SpaceVista—comprising the SpaceVista-1M dataset, SpaceVista-Bench benchmark, and SpaceVista-7B model—represents a significant contribution to the field of visual spatial reasoning.

#### 5.1. Addressing Fundamental Gaps

This work directly addresses key limitations in current spatial reasoning research, primarily the heavy reliance on narrow-scope indoor datasets and the absence of models capable of holistic understanding across all physical scales. By creating the first open-source, all-scale spatial reasoning dataset and benchmark, it provides foundational resources that can accelerate research in this underdeveloped area.

#### 5.2. Advancing All-Scale Spatial Intelligence

The proposed holistic solution, integrating a structured knowledge system, scale-aware modeling, and progressive training, marks a notable advancement in equipping MLLMs with generalized spatial intelligence. The SpaceVista-7B model's ability to process inputs beyond mere semantics, coupled with its scale-expert architecture and human-inspired progressive reward system, demonstrates a robust approach to mitigating cross-scale knowledge conflicts and enhancing reasoning capabilities across diverse contexts.

#### 5.3. Catalyzing Broad Applications

The potential impact of all-scale spatial reasoning extends across a multitude of domains:
- **Micro-scale applications (μm to mm):** Precision manufacturing and medical surgery can benefit from enhanced perception and reasoning at fine granularities, enabling more accurate robotic manipulation, diagnostics, and intervention.
- **Human-scale applications (cm to m):** Embodied intelligence, robotics, and augmented reality systems can achieve a more nuanced understanding of immediate environments, leading to more intelligent and adaptive agents.
- **Macro-scale applications (10m to km):** Autonomous driving, drone-based sensing, remote sensing, and cartography can leverage improved large-scale spatial understanding for navigation, environmental monitoring, urban planning, and reconnaissance.

#### 5.4. Foundational for Future Systems

SpaceVista provides a critical foundational step for the development of next-generation intelligent systems. It empowers intelligent agents to perceive, interpret, and reason about complex, unconstrained environments in the wild, which is crucial for the advancement of industrial automation, embedded systems, and fully autonomous systems. The open-source nature of the dataset and model facilitates community engagement and iterative improvements, promoting further innovation in visual-spatial AI.

In conclusion, this research is significant not only for its technical contributions to dataset creation and model architecture but also for its potential to unlock a broader range of real-world applications by enabling AI systems to comprehend and interact with the spatial world across its full, multi-faceted scale.
