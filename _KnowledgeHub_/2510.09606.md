# SpaceVista: All-Scale Visual Spatial Reasoning from mm to km

> [!summary] Summary
> SpaceVista introduces a comprehensive solution for advancing spatial reasoning capabilities in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) across physical scales ranging from millimeters to kilometers. This work provides a new dataset, SpaceVista-1M, and a model, SpaceVista-7B, demonstrating superior performance on the accompanying SpaceVista-Bench evaluation benchmark, outperforming existing proprietary and open-source models by over 3%.

## Problem

- Existing spatial reasoning research, particularly with MLLMs, is largely confined to constrained environments and limited physical scales, hindering generalization to diverse real-world applications.
- Current spatial reasoning datasets are costly, rely on laborious manual annotations, and lack diversity across broad scales and 'in-the-wild' scenes.
- MLLMs lack effective architectures and training paradigms to handle vast differences in object sizes, scene layouts, and semantic complexities across scales, leading to 'knowledge conflict' in reasoning.

## Method

- An automated, specialist-driven pipeline curates ==SpaceVista-1M==, a dataset of **1 million** spatial Question-Answer pairs from **38,000** video scenes, spanning five distinct scales and 19 task types.
- ==SpaceVista-7B==, an MLLM, integrates dense visual inputs (DINOv3), employs ==LoRA-like 'scale experts'== with a dynamic router to mitigate cross-scale knowledge conflicts, and utilizes a progressive reward system for Reinforcement Learning training.
- SpaceVista-Bench, a high-fidelity, hand-crafted evaluation benchmark, ensures accurate, physically grounded assessment across all scales using real-world measurements and human annotation.

## Results

- SpaceVista-7B achieves over **3%** higher overall performance on the new SpaceVista-Bench compared to state-of-the-art proprietary and open-source models, demonstrating robust all-scale reasoning capabilities.
- The model shows significantly higher scores in challenging scenarios like Tiny Tabletop, Tabletop, and Outdoor scenes within SpaceVista-Bench, delivering around **6%** improvement over other open-source models.
- Ablation studies confirm the critical role of scale experts, progressive rewards, and dense visual inputs in improving performance and mitigating cross-scale knowledge conflicts, with performance generally improving with more experts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating dense geometric features from self-supervised vision models (e.g., DINOv3) into MLLMs significantly enhances spatial perception beyond purely semantic understanding.
> - Scale-aware expert architectures, such as LoRA-like experts with a dynamic router, effectively mitigate 'knowledge conflicts' and enable MLLMs to specialize knowledge for reasoning across vastly different physical scales.
> - A progressive reward mechanism using semantic, scale, and answer anchors during reinforcement learning guides the model's Chain-of-Thought, promoting human-like, scale-centric spatial reasoning.
