# KineSoft: Learning Proprioceptive Manipulation Policies with Soft Robot Hands

> [!summary] Summary
> KineSoft introduces a hierarchical framework enabling soft robot hands to learn dexterous manipulation skills from kinesthetic demonstrations, utilizing proprioceptive shape estimation. The system leverages the robot's compliance for intuitive teaching and achieves robust in-hand manipulation by tracking geometric shape deformations, showing improved success rates across various tasks compared to strain-based methods.

## Problem

- Dexterous in-hand manipulation for underactuated soft robots is challenging due to their high degrees of freedom and complex deformation.
- Traditional imitation learning frameworks struggle with soft robots because of difficulties in collecting demonstration data (teleoperation interfaces are inadequate), representing continuously deforming states, and transferring policies from simulation to real hardware.
- The 'demonstration-execution gap' means sensor signals during human kinesthetic teaching do not directly translate to autonomous tendon actuation, hindering robust skill transfer.

## Method

- The **KineSoft** framework employs ==kinesthetic teaching==, leveraging the soft robot's compliance for direct human guidance, simplifying demonstration data collection.
- A high-fidelity shape estimation model maps internal strain sensor readings to a precise, per-vertex mesh deformation state using a ==FoldingNet==-based architecture trained in simulation with sim-to-real domain alignment.
- A hierarchical control system combines a ==diffusion-based== imitation policy, which predicts desired surface deformations and end-effector motions, with a shape-conditioned low-level controller that tracks these geometric trajectories using real-time proprioceptive feedback.

## Results

- The shape estimation model achieved a mean error of **1.92 ± 0.39 mm**, representing a **41.3%** improvement over the best prior learning-based baseline.
- The KineSoft shape-conditioned controller demonstrated a **47%** improvement in tracking accuracy over a strain-tracking baseline, achieving an error of **3.29 ± 0.91 mm**.
- KineSoft exhibited superior performance across six in-hand manipulation tasks (e.g., **85%** for bottle unscrewing, **100%** for lid flicking), significantly outperforming a state-of-the-art strain-based policy baseline, particularly in tasks requiring precision and coordination.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The inherent compliance of soft robots can be leveraged as an advantage for intuitive kinesthetic teaching, rather than solely a control challenge.
> - Using a consistent, high-fidelity geometric shape representation (mesh deformation) for both demonstration and execution effectively bridges the 'demonstration-execution gap' in soft robot skill learning.
> - Accurate proprioceptive shape estimation from internal sensors is crucial for enabling feedback-driven control and dexterous manipulation in continuously deforming soft robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is actively exploring the potential of soft robots, particularly for tasks requiring safe and adaptable interaction with objects and environments. Underactuated soft robot hands inherently offer advantages such as material compliance, which enhances safety in human-robot interaction and allows for robust adaptation to varying object geometries. These characteristics make soft robots suitable for applications like assistive care, delicate object manipulation, fruit picking, and collaborative manufacturing.

Despite these benefits, imparting dexterous in-hand manipulation skills to soft robots remains a significant challenge. Traditional methods often rely on hand-crafted manipulation primitives, which require expert tuning and limit the robot's adaptability to novel situations.

Concurrently, imitation learning (IL) has emerged as a promising paradigm for acquiring complex manipulation skills, with diffusion-based policies demonstrating success in various applications, including rigid gripper manipulation and deformable object handling with simpler end-effectors. Unlike reinforcement learning, which depends on carefully designed reward functions and high-fidelity simulations that are particularly difficult to achieve for soft robots due to complex modeling of deformable materials and contact dynamics, imitation learning only requires demonstrations of successful task execution.

However, applying existing imitation learning frameworks to soft robots encounters several obstacles:
1. **Data Collection:** Methods designed for rigid, articulated robots are often unsuitable for underactuated soft robots, which possess a high number of degrees of freedom. Teleoperation interfaces for rigid anthropomorphic hands typically do not capture the unique capabilities and constraints of soft end-effectors, nor do soft robots always have a direct kinematic mapping to human hand joints.
2. **State Representation:** Standard robot state representations, such as rigid transformation poses, are inadequate for describing the continuously deforming structures of soft robots. While recent advancements have been made in learning expressive state representations for soft robots (e.g., using point clouds or meshes), these have not yet been effectively integrated into skill learning frameworks for dexterous in-hand manipulation.
3. **Simulation-to-Real Transfer:** The complex physics of deformable materials and contact dynamics in soft robots make accurate simulation challenging, hindering the transfer of policies trained in simulation to real-world hardware.

KineSoft addresses these specific gaps by proposing a framework that leverages the inherent compliance of soft robots as an advantage for kinesthetic teaching, thereby simplifying demonstration data collection. It further integrates novel and accurate proprioceptive shape estimation methods with learned imitation policies to enable dexterous in-hand manipulation, effectively bridging the absence of reliable proprioceptive feedback and practical demonstration frameworks for soft robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a framework that enables soft robot hands to learn and execute dexterous manipulation skills through kinesthetic demonstration, utilizing their internal proprioceptive sensing. This involves addressing several fundamental challenges associated with soft robot manipulation and imitation learning.

The central motivation for KineSoft stems from a key insight: the natural compliance of soft robots can be leveraged as an advantage for teaching rather than merely a control challenge. Unlike rigid robots, which often resist physical guidance due to strict kinematic constraints, soft hands can be easily manipulated by a human demonstrator into desired configurations. This allows for intuitive and direct collection of demonstrations by physically guiding the robot, without risking mechanical damage or creating kinematically infeasible states. This approach aims to overcome the difficulties in demonstration data collection that have historically limited the application of imitation learning to soft robots.

The specific problems and challenges that KineSoft aims to address are formally defined:
1. **State Estimation:** The true continuous deformation state of a soft robot hand is unobservable and high-dimensional. The first objective is to effectively estimate this complex deformation state (M) from indirect internal sensor measurements (S), such as strain sensor readings.
2. **Mapping from Sensor to State:** A reliable and accurate mapping must be learned from the raw internal sensor measurements (S) to the high-fidelity deformation state representation (M). This mapping is crucial for downstream control and policy learning.
3. **Policy Learning and Control:** The framework needs to learn a control policy (π) that can map the estimated deformation state (M) and other task-relevant observations (O, e.g., visual input) to control actions (U). This policy must effectively manage the continuous, theoretically infinite-dimensional nature of the deformation space and the inherent non-linearity or difficulty in simulating the actuation-deformation relationship in soft robots.

In summary, KineSoft seeks to develop a hierarchical framework that harnesses the unique properties of soft robots to facilitate the acquisition of complex manipulation skills. By embracing their compliance for kinesthetic teaching and addressing the challenges of state representation and control, the research aims to enable soft robots to perform dexterous tasks that benefit from their inherent safety and adaptability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

KineSoft is a hierarchical framework designed to enable dexterous manipulation skills in soft robot hands through kinesthetic teaching and autonomous execution. The methodology integrates five key components: a sensorized soft robot hand, a high-fidelity shape estimation model, a sim-to-real domain alignment procedure, a shape-conditioned low-level controller, and a diffusion-based imitation policy.

#### 3.1. MOE Soft Robot End-effector

The platform utilized is a three-finger variant of the Multifinger Omnidirectional End-effector (MOE) soft robot. Each modular finger unit is actuated by two servo motors that apply tension to four embedded tendons. To provide proprioceptive feedback, low-cost conductive elastic rubber sensors are integrated directly into the silicone elastomer body of each finger. Each finger contains four such sensors, positioned between the tendons, which measure deformation by changes in electrical resistance. A data acquisition (DAQ) circuit records these resistance readings at approximately 400 Hz. This design results in a fully deformable and sensorized finger body, with the combined 12 strain sensor readings forming the system's estimated internal state.

#### 3.2. Shape Estimation Model

This component maps the 12 strain sensor readings (R ∈ R^12) to per-vertex displacements (∆V) of the MOE fingers. The model learns a function `f` that predicts these displacements conditioned on both the sensor input and the initial, undeformed mesh geometry of the fingers. Specifically, `f(R, {V_j,0}^3_j=1) = {∆V_j}^3_j=1`, where `V_j,0` are the initial vertex positions of finger `j`, and `∆V_j` are the predicted displacements.
The architecture is based on FoldingNet. For each finger `j`, an encoder `h_enc` processes its four resistance values `R_j` into a latent code `z_j` (R^128). A decoder `h_dec` then predicts the displacement `∆v_j,i` for each vertex `v_i_j,0` by combining its initial position with `z_j`. This approach allows the model to capture local deformation patterns based on global strain input while preserving mesh topology. The model is trained using a mean squared error (MSE) loss on vertex displacements, `L = (1/(3N)) * Σ^3_j=1 Σ^N_i=1 ||∆v_j,i - ∆v^*_j,i||^2`, against a large dataset of simulated deformations and corresponding strain readings generated using SOFA (Simulation Open Framework Architecture), which models a tetrahedral finite-element mesh with randomized hyperelastic properties and tendon actuation.

#### 3.3. Sim-to-Real Domain Alignment

To facilitate the transfer of the simulation-trained shape estimation model to real hardware, a calibration procedure aligns physical sensor readings with simulated deformations. This involves determining per-sensor correction factors `κ_i` that compensate for domain mismatch, arising from fabrication imperfections or sensor placement.
The objective function `argmin_{κ_0,...,κ_{n-1}} Σ^{n-1}_{i=0} Σ^{T-1}_{t=0} (R_i,t/R_i,0 - κ_i * (L_S_i,t - L_S_i,0)/L_S_i,0 - 1)^2` is derived from a sensor model assuming incompressible and isotropic conductive rubber. Since the true simulated sensor lengths corresponding to real-world resistance readings are unknown, the alignment is achieved by minimizing the unsupervised Chamfer distance `L_UCD` between observed 3D points on the soft fingers (captured externally, e.g., via a camera) and the predicted surface points from the shape estimation model. This ensures that the internal deformation estimated from real resistance measurements is geometrically consistent with external observations.

#### 3.4. Shape-conditioned Controller

This low-level controller translates desired shape trajectories into tendon actuation commands. It uses real-time proprioceptive mesh state estimations of the MOE fingers (V_t) and compares them to desired target positions (V_D_t) generated by the imitation policy. For each finger, actuated by two antagonistic tendons, servo adjustments `δu_j,t` are computed by projecting the shape error `e_n_j,t = V_D_t,j - V_t,j` for each vertex `n` onto the two primary actuation directions `d_2j`, `d_2j+1`. The control law is `δu_j,t = k_p * Σ_n e_n_j,t · [d_2j, d_2j+1]^T`, where `k_p` is a scalar gain. The controller operates at 100 Hz, with actions clipped to prevent actuator overload, allowing for precise tracking of geometric shape trajectories.

#### 3.5. Imitation Policy

The imitation policy learns manipulation skills by combining the proprioceptive mesh state `V_t` (from the shape estimation model) with exteroceptive observations `P_t` (point clouds from a wrist-mounted RGB-D camera). The policy's state representation `s_t` integrates an MLP encoder `h_shape` for mesh vertices (with temporal correspondence), a DP3 encoder `h_pc` for the RGB-D point cloud, and the current end-effector pose `p_t`.
The policy is a diffusion-based model that predicts actions `a_t = {∆V_t, ∆p_t}`, which represent desired surface deformations and end-effector motion. It learns to denoise a vertex-level action trajectory via a reverse process, `a_t-1 = μ_θ(a_t, s_t, t) + σ_t * z`. This high-level policy, combined with the low-level shape-conditioned controller, enables robust, contact-rich manipulation by translating learned intent into precise shape tracking. Demonstrations for policy training are collected via kinesthetic teaching, where a human physically deforms the soft fingers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of KineSoft was evaluated through real-world experiments, focusing on the performance of its individual components and overall manipulation capabilities.

#### 4.1. Shape Estimation Fidelity

The shape estimation model's accuracy was quantified by comparing its predictions against ground-truth point clouds observed from the physical setup. The evaluation metric used was the unidirectional Chamfer distance.
- **KineSoft (model)** achieved a mean shape estimation error of **1.92 ± 0.39 mm**.
- This represented a **41.3% improvement** over the best prior learning-based baseline, DeepSoRo, which had an error of 3.27 ± 1.05 mm.
- It also surpassed analytical models like PneuFlex Sensor (3.70 ± 1.36 mm) and KineSoft's own ablations: a naive linear mapping (4.91 ± 2.85 mm) and an unconstrained fully connected model (4.36 ± 3.47 mm).
These results indicate that the FoldingNet-based architecture combined with simulated training data and domain alignment provides a high-fidelity and precise reconstruction of the soft finger's deformation state from internal strain sensor readings.

#### 4.2. Shape-conditioned Controller Performance

The ability of the shape-conditioned controller to track desired deformation trajectories was assessed against a strain-tracking baseline, which directly uses raw sensor readings for control, similar to prior soft robot manipulation methods. Reference trajectories were obtained through kinesthetic teaching.
- The **KineSoft shape-conditioned controller** achieved a tracking error of **3.29 ± 0.91 mm**.
- The **strain-tracking baseline** exhibited a significantly higher error of **6.20 ± 2.39 mm**.
This **47% improvement** by KineSoft demonstrates its capability to bridge the inherent "demonstration-execution gap." This gap arises because sensor signals during human kinesthetic deformation (demonstration) differ from those generated during autonomous tendon-actuated execution. By tracking the geometric shape itself rather than attempting to directly match raw sensor signals, the KineSoft controller facilitates more robust trajectory execution.

#### 4.3. In-hand Manipulation Task Performance

KineSoft's overall performance was evaluated on six diverse in-hand manipulation tasks, chosen to highlight soft robots' strengths in delicate and contact-rich interactions, as well as their challenges in precise multi-finger coordination. Each task involved 20 real-world trials using policies trained on kinesthetic demonstrations. The performance was compared against a "Strain Policy" baseline, which was a diffusion policy trained on the same demonstration data but using raw strain signals and visual input as states, representing a state-of-the-art soft in-hand manipulation method.

The success rates were as follows:
- **Bottle Unscrewing:** KineSoft achieved 17/20 (85%) success, while the Strain Policy achieved 0/20 (0%). This highlights KineSoft's capability for coordinated rotational and lifting motions.
- **Lid Flicking:** KineSoft reached 20/20 (100%) success, compared to the Strain Policy's 18/20 (90%). Both performed well, but KineSoft showed slightly higher reliability.
- **Berry Picking:** KineSoft succeeded in 16/20 (80%) trials, whereas the Strain Policy managed only 7/20 (35%). This task emphasizes precise, gentle grasping of fragile objects, where KineSoft demonstrated superior contact sensitivity.
- **Paper Grasping:** KineSoft achieved 19/20 (95%) success, significantly outperforming the Strain Policy's 13/20 (65%). This task involves initiating contact and lifting a flat, compliant object.
- **Fabric Grasping:** KineSoft had 13/20 (65%) success, while the Strain Policy showed limited success at 1/20 (5%). This task showcases adaptation to deformable objects and contact-rich manipulation.
- **Container Unlidding:** KineSoft succeeded in 14/20 (70%) trials, compared to the Strain Policy's 3/20 (15%). This task requires multi-finger coordination to grasp and open a snap-top lid.

Across all six tasks, KineSoft demonstrated superior performance, particularly in tasks requiring precise multi-finger coordination, contact-sensitive handling of delicate objects, and robust interaction with deformable materials. These results underscore the effectiveness of shape-based representations for robust control and highlight how embracing soft robot properties can lead to more capable manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

KineSoft represents a significant contribution to the field of soft robotics and robotic manipulation, particularly in addressing the challenges of dexterous skill acquisition for compliant systems. The core significance lies in its fundamental shift in perspective: viewing the inherent compliance of soft robots not merely as a control problem, but as an advantage for teaching and skill acquisition.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**

1. **Enabling Kinesthetic Teaching for Soft Robots:** KineSoft provides a practical framework for direct kinesthetic teaching of soft robot hands. This method allows human demonstrators to physically guide the soft robot into desired manipulation poses, overcoming the limitations of traditional teleoperation interfaces or complex programming required for rigid robots. This approach simplifies data collection for imitation learning, which has been a major bottleneck for soft robot skill development.
2. **Bridging the Demonstration-Execution Gap:** The framework effectively addresses the critical challenge of the "demonstration-execution gap" in soft robotics. By employing a hierarchical approach that relies on consistent geometric shape representations (predicted mesh deformations) rather than raw sensor signals or joint configurations, KineSoft ensures that manipulation strategies learned during human-guided deformation can be reliably reproduced through autonomous tendon actuation. This consistent representation is crucial for effective skill transfer.
3. **Advanced Proprioceptive Shape Estimation:** The development of a high-fidelity, mesh-based shape estimation model using internal strain sensors significantly improves the robot's self-awareness. This model accurately translates indirect sensor readings into precise geometric descriptions of the robot's deformation state, which is vital for feedback-driven control in soft robots where traditional kinematics are often inadequate.
4. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The proposed domain alignment procedure for strain sensors enables the robust transfer of simulation-trained shape estimation models to real hardware. This reduces the need for extensive real-world data collection for model training, accelerating the development and deployment cycle for new soft robot designs.
5. **Enhanced Dexterous Manipulation Capabilities:** By integrating precise shape estimation, a shape-conditioned controller, and a diffusion-based imitation policy, KineSoft enables soft robots to perform a range of dexterous in-hand manipulation tasks with high success rates. The experimental results, particularly the significant outperformance of the strain-matching baseline, demonstrate KineSoft's ability to leverage soft robots' strengths in contact-rich and delicate object interactions, such as berry picking and fabric grasping, while also handling tasks requiring coordinated force application like bottle unscrewing.
6. **Guidance for Future Imitation Learning Research:** The work highlights the importance of carefully considering the differences between demonstration and execution modes in imitation learning for continuously deformable systems. It advocates for the development of appropriate intermediate representations that can bridge this gap, offering a valuable lesson for future research in generalizable and robust robotic skill learning.

The potential impact of KineSoft is broad. By making dexterous manipulation more accessible for soft robots, it could accelerate their adoption in sensitive applications like assistive care for the elderly or individuals with disabilities, delicate industrial assembly tasks, and automated agricultural harvesting of fragile produce. Furthermore, the framework's open-source readiness, with its dataset and trained models deployable on platforms like MOE, fosters community engagement and further research in soft robot control and learning.
