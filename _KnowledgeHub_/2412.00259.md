# One-Shot Real-to-Sim via End-to-End Differentiable Simulation and Rendering

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Yale University developed a one-shot real-to-sim framework that identifies the geometry, appearance, and physical properties of novel rigid objects from a single robot interaction sequence, leveraging an end-to-end differentiable simulator and renderer. This approach demonstrated a 64% reduction in Chamfer distance and 64% reduction in positional error compared to baselines in predicting novel object trajectories in simulation, and successfully inferred occluded object geometry to be physically plausible in real-world scenarios.

## Problem

- Robots need comprehensive world models that can predict both realistic visual observations and physically consistent interactions for effective planning in novel environments.
- Current differentiable simulators typically require known object geometries and appearances, preventing the joint optimization of all object properties from observations.
- Learnable rendering models for geometry and appearance often lack the explicit surface definitions needed for rigid body physics and demand extensive data, making them unsuitable for sparse, online robot learning.

## Method

- A hybrid differentiable object representation combining ==Shape-as-Points (SaP)== for geometry and a 3D grid for appearance, which converts into an ==explicit mesh== for rigid body simulation.
- An ==end-to-end differentiable rigid body simulator== capable of propagating gradients through its physics engine to jointly optimize geometry, appearance, and physical parameters from robot interactions.
- A ==two-stage optimization== strategy that first refines geometry and appearance from a single observation (with a ==foundation model prior==) and then fine-tunes all parameters using a full robot trajectory.

## Results

- In simulation, achieved significantly lower prediction errors for novel trajectories compared to a baseline (e.g., **8.69 mm** unilateral Chamfer distance vs. **24.2 mm**, **15.5 mm** positional error vs. **42.8 mm**).
- Accurately identified physical parameters in simulation, with an average mass identification error of **0.0728 kg** and friction coefficient error of **0.106**.
- In real-world experiments, successfully adapted occluded object geometry to satisfy physics constraints, such as correcting an overly thin object to properly contact the terrain, and achieved an average mass identification error of **0.186 kg**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid object representation, bridging implicit fields for complex geometry/appearance and explicit meshes for physics, is crucial for end-to-end differentiable simulation.
> - Physics-constrained optimization, guided by sparse robot interaction data, effectively resolves ambiguities in occluded geometry and accurately identifies physical properties like mass and friction.
> - Leveraging a pre-trained 3D foundation model for an initial geometry guess significantly improves the robustness and efficiency of identifying complete and physically plausible object models.
