# Temperature as a Meta-Policy: Adaptive Temperature in LLM Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> The TAMPO framework introduces a learnable meta-policy for dynamically adapting the sampling temperature during large language model reinforcement learning. It achieves improved performance on reasoning tasks by efficiently balancing exploration and exploitation, without requiring additional rollouts.

## Problem

- Balancing exploration and exploitation is crucial in large language model (LLM) reinforcement learning, but the sampling temperature is typically treated as a static or heuristically tuned hyperparameter.
- Fixed temperature settings limit adaptive exploration and can lead to suboptimal policy learning or premature convergence.
- Direct optimization of sampling temperature is challenging due to the non-differentiable nature of LLM sampling and the high computational cost of generating additional rollouts for tuning.

## Method

- The Temperature Adaptive Meta Policy Optimization (**TAMPO**) framework formulates ==temperature== selection as a learnable meta-policy problem within a hierarchical two-loop optimization process.
- It jointly optimizes the core LLM policy (inner loop) and the temperature meta-policy (outer loop), which learns to select temperatures that yield high-advantage rollouts.
- TAMPO enables online temperature adaptation by reusing existing trajectories generated during the LLM policy update, thereby avoiding the computational expense of additional rollouts.

## Results

- TAMPO consistently improved performance, achieving an average of **1.9%** higher Pass@1 and **1.7%** higher Pass@8 accuracy on mathematical reasoning benchmarks compared to the best fixed-temperature baselines.
- The method incurred negligible additional computational overhead, with training times comparable to existing critic-free RL baselines, demonstrating its efficiency.
- Ablation studies confirmed that a moderate level of exploration in meta-policy temperature selection (nucleus sampling with p=**0.7**) and balanced smoothing of temperature advantages (EMA coefficient of **0.05**) were crucial for optimal performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Every generated trajectory carries implicit information about its 'preferred' temperature, which can be quantified via its likelihood under different candidate temperatures.
> - Scaling trajectory advantages by their temperature-specific likelihoods (Temperature-specific Advantage, TSA) provides an efficient and tractable signal for the meta-policy to reinforce effective temperatures.
> - The meta-policy can dynamically shift its temperature distribution, initially favoring higher exploration temperatures and gradually moving towards lower exploitation temperatures as training progresses.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of large language model (LLM) reinforcement learning (RL), specifically addressing the challenge of exploration-exploitation trade-offs during the post-training alignment phase.

Reinforcement learning has emerged as a significant paradigm for aligning LLMs with human preferences and specific task objectives. Traditional RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) often employs PPO-based methods, which typically involve a learned value network and can be computationally intensive. More recent advancements in LLM RL have introduced critic-free algorithms, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and REINFORCE++, which aim to improve scalability and stability by eliminating the need for value networks. These methods generate multiple rollouts at a given temperature to estimate advantages and policy gradients.

A fundamental challenge across all RL applications is balancing exploration (discovering new strategies) and exploitation (maximizing immediate rewards based on known strategies). In LLMs, the sampling temperature directly controls this balance: high temperatures encourage diverse but potentially noisy outputs, while low temperatures produce focused outputs but risk premature convergence to suboptimal solutions.

Current practices in LLM RL largely treat temperature as a static hyperparameter, either fixing it or manually tuning it through heuristic schedules. These approaches typically do not incorporate feedback from the ongoing learning process to adapt the temperature. While some methods like entropy regularization and nucleus sampling aim to manage exploration, and a few works explore adaptive inference-time temperature selection, the dynamic optimization of sampling temperature during RL training, guided by trajectory outcomes, has not been extensively addressed.

The paper differentiates temperature from other exploration-influencing hyperparameters like entropy regularization coefficients or KL penalties, arguing that temperature offers a more direct and transparent means of modulating the sampling distribution over text outputs.

In the broader RL landscape, meta-policies have been utilized in hierarchical RL to control low-level skills and in meta-gradient methods to optimize differentiable hyperparameters. However, applying meta-policies to non-differentiable parameters like LLM sampling temperature, particularly in a manner that reuses existing rollouts, represents a novel contribution. This work specifically aims to fill the gap where existing LLM RL approaches either fix or manually tune temperature, without a principled, feedback-driven mechanism for online adaptation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind this research is the observation that static or heuristically scheduled sampling temperatures limit the effectiveness of large language model (LLM) reinforcement learning (RL) by failing to adapt to the dynamic demands of training. The paper argues that temperature, which directly controls the exploration-exploitation trade-off in text generation, should be treated as a decision variable rather than a fixed hyperparameter.

The key objectives are detailed as follows:

1. **Formulate Temperature as a Learnable Meta-Policy:** The primary objective is to conceptualize and formalize temperature selection within LLM RL as a meta-policy optimization problem. This reframes the static choice of temperature into a dynamic, learning-driven process, aiming to enhance adaptive and reward-driven exploration. This contrasts with traditional methods that treat temperature as a fixed or manually tuned setting.

2. **Develop a Hierarchical Framework for Joint Optimization:** The authors aim to propose "Temperature Adaptive Meta Policy Optimization (TAMPO)," a novel hierarchical framework. This framework is designed to simultaneously optimize both the core LLM policy and the temperature meta-policy. The meta-policy's role is to learn and select temperatures that are most conducive to generating high-advantage rollouts, thereby directly aligning exploration strategies with policy improvement.

3. **Enable Online Adaptation Without Additional Rollouts:** A significant practical motivation is to achieve dynamic temperature adaptation efficiently. The goal is for TAMPO to learn and adapt temperature online by reusing trajectories generated during the inner-loop LLM policy optimization, thus avoiding the computational burden of generating extra rollouts solely for temperature tuning. This addresses the tractability challenge inherent in backpropagating gradients through non-differentiable sampling processes or exhaustively testing multiple temperatures.

4. **Demonstrate Effectiveness on Challenging Benchmarks:** The motivation extends to empirically validating TAMPO's performance. The objective is to show that this trajectory-guided adaptive temperature control significantly outperforms baselines that rely on fixed or heuristic temperature schedules, particularly on demanding tasks like mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need for more intelligent and adaptive exploration strategies in LLM RL to overcome the limitations imposed by static temperature settings, thereby making LLM policy learning more efficient and effective without increasing computational overhead.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Temperature Adaptive Meta Policy Optimization (TAMPO), is a meta-learning framework designed to jointly optimize an LLM policy and a meta-policy that controls the sampling temperature. It operates through a hierarchical two-loop process.

**Problem Formulation:**
The problem is formalized as a bilevel meta-optimization. The inner loop aims to optimize the LLM policy π_θ under temperatures T sampled from a meta-policy π_ϕ, maximizing the expected reward. The outer loop optimizes the meta-policy π_ϕ to maximize the overall performance of the LLM policy.

**Tractability Challenge and Key Observation:**
Direct gradient-based optimization of the temperature meta-policy is impractical because LLM rollout generation is often decoupled from policy optimization (using lower-precision models for efficiency), which prevents backpropagation through the sampling process. Furthermore, generating rollouts for every candidate temperature is computationally prohibitive.

The core insight is that every generated trajectory inherently carries information about its "preferred" temperature—the temperature under which it is most likely to be generated. This allows for a tractable, signal-based approach to adapt temperature without additional rollouts.

**Hierarchical Two-Loop Process:**

1. **Inner Loop: Critic-Free LLM Policy Optimization:**
 - The LLM policy π_θ,T is updated using a critic-free RL algorithm, such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which computes trajectory-level advantages.
 - For a given prompt, a group of G rollouts {τ_1..., τ_G} is sampled at a specific temperature T. This temperature T is chosen by the meta-policy.
 - Trajectory-level advantages A_i are computed by normalizing rewards r_i within the group: A_i = (r_i - mean({r_1..., r_G})) / std({r_1..., r_G}).
 - The LLM policy θ is then updated based on these advantages and a KL regularization term with respect to a reference policy.

2. **Outer Loop: Meta-Policy Update (Learning Temperature):**
 - The meta-policy π(T) maintains a probability distribution over a discrete set of candidate temperatures T = {T_1..., T_K}.
 - The update mechanism for the meta-policy leverages the inner-loop rollouts and their computed advantages. The intuition is to reinforce temperatures that produce high-advantage trajectories and suppress those that lead to low-advantage ones.

 - **Temperature-Dependent Trajectory Likelihood:**
 - For each sampled trajectory τ_i from the inner loop, its likelihood P_θ,T_k(τ_i) under each candidate virtual temperature T_k ∈ T is calculated. This is done by summing the log probabilities of individual tokens within the trajectory, where token probabilities are scaled by T_k.
 - The average log-likelihood ℓ_T_k(τ_i) = (1/|τ_i|) * log P_θ,T_k(τ_i) is used to account for trajectory length.
 - The paper proves that trajectory likelihood is unimodal with respect to temperature T (Appendix B), implying a unique likelihood-optimal temperature T* for each trajectory. Empirical analysis (Appendix C) further shows that positive-advantage trajectories tend to cluster around distinct T* values.

 - **Temperature-specific Advantage (TSA):**
 - The likelihoods ℓ_T_k(τ_i) are normalized across the K candidate temperatures using sparsemax, yielding normalized likelihoods ℓ̂_T_k(τ_i).
 - The original trajectory advantage A_i is then scaled by these normalized likelihoods to compute the temperature-specific advantage for each candidate temperature: A^(T_k)_i = ℓ̂_T_k(τ_i) * A_i.
 - This formulation means that positive-advantage trajectories contribute positively most strongly to their likelihood-optimal temperature, while negative-advantage trajectories penalize their likelihood-optimal temperature most.

 - **Meta-Policy Update Rule:**
 - For a batch of samples, the temperature-specific advantages A^(T_k)_B are aggregated for each candidate temperature T_k.
 - To stabilize updates, an Exponentially Weighted Moving Average (EMA) of these advantages is maintained: Ā^(T_k)_s = (1 − α) Ā^(T_k)_(s−1) + α A^(T_k)_B, where α is the decay coefficient.
 - Finally, the meta-policy distribution π_s(T_k) for the next step is computed by min-max normalization of the EMA-smoothed advantages Ā^(T_k)_s, ensuring a valid probability distribution where higher-advantage temperatures receive higher probability.

**Overall Algorithm Flow (Algorithm 1):**
1. Initialize EMA advantages and meta-policy (uniform distribution over temperatures).
2. For each training step:
 - **Sample Temperature:** Draw a temperature T_s from the current meta-policy π_s-1(T) (e.g., using nucleus sampling).
 - **Inner Loop (Policy Update):** Generate trajectories for a batch of prompts using the LLM policy at T_s. Compute rewards and GRPO advantages. Update the LLM policy parameters.
 - **Outer Loop (Meta-Policy Update):** For each candidate temperature T_k, calculate the temperature-specific advantages using the just-generated trajectories. Update the EMA of these advantages. Update the meta-policy distribution π_s(T_k) based on the smoothed advantages.

**Implementation Details:**
- A warmup phase (first 10% of training steps) uses a fixed sampling temperature (e.g., 1.0) for the LLM policy, while the meta-policy is updated.
- Candidate temperatures (T) are a discrete set (e.g., 0.6 to 1.5 with 0.1 intervals).
- The meta-policy's temperature sampling strategy can be nucleus sampling, allowing for a balance between exploiting the learned temperature distribution and exploring other temperatures.

This approach offers online, feedback-driven temperature adaptation without requiring additional rollouts, making it computationally efficient and compatible with existing critic-free LLM RL pipelines.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated TAMPO's performance against baselines using fixed or heuristic temperature schedules on mathematical reasoning and commonsense reasoning benchmarks.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Base Model:** DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (DS-Qwen-1.5B) for main experiments, and Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct for additional generalization tests.
- **Training Dataset:** Open-s1 math-reasoning dataset.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** Five mathematical reasoning benchmarks (AIME24, MATH-500, AMC23, Minerva, OlympiadBench) and one commonsense reasoning benchmark (ECQA).
- **Candidate Temperatures:** A discrete set from 0.6 to 1.5, with an interval of 0.1 (K=10).
- **Hyperparameters:** EMA coefficient α=0.05, nucleus sampling threshold p=0.7 for the meta-policy (default settings).
- **Warmup:** First 10% of training steps used a fixed sampling temperature of 1.0 for the LLM policy while the meta-policy was updated.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Pass@1 and Pass@8 accuracy.

**Main Results (Comparison with Baselines):**
- **Overall Performance (Table 1):** TAMPO demonstrated consistent improvements over all baselines on the five mathematical reasoning benchmarks.
 - On average, TAMPO achieved 1.9% higher Pass@1 accuracy and 1.7% higher Pass@8 accuracy compared to the best fixed-temperature GRPO baseline.
 - Across individual datasets, TAMPO consistently achieved either the best or second-best performance for both Pass@1 and Pass@8. For instance, on AIME24, TAMPO scored 44.5% (Pass@1) and 63.8% (Pass@8), outperforming all baselines. On MATH-500, it achieved 23.3% (Pass@1) and 40.0% (Pass@8), matching the best Pass@1 and surpassing all others for Pass@8.
- **Baselines:** Included GRPO with fixed sampling temperatures (0.9, 1.2, 1.5) and GRPO with a linearly increasing temperature schedule (0.9 to 1.5). TAMPO consistently surpassed these static or heuristically scheduled approaches.

**Complexity Analysis:**
- TAMPO introduced negligible additional computational overhead. The meta-policy model is lightweight, only maintaining a list of temperature advantages, and is discarded at inference.
- Crucially, TAMPO reuses rollouts generated by the inner-loop policy, eliminating the need for additional trajectory generations. This resulted in approximately the same training time as the GRPO baselines (e.g., 9 hours 54 minutes for 200 steps on an 8xV100 GPU machine).

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Influence of EMA Coefficient α (Table 2):** An α value of 0.05 was found to be optimal for TAMPO, yielding an average Pass@1 of 44.5%. Lower (0.01) or higher (0.10) values resulted in reduced performance, suggesting that a balanced smoothing of temperature-specific advantages is crucial.
- **Influence of Sampling Strategy on Meta-policy (Table 3):** Using nucleus sampling (top-p) with p=0.7 for selecting temperatures from the meta-policy achieved the best average Pass@1 (44.5%). Deviations (e.g., p=0.9, p=0.5, or greedy sampling with p=0) led to performance degradation, indicating that a moderate level of exploration in temperature selection is beneficial for the meta-policy itself.

**Analysis of Sampled Temperature (Figure 3):**
- After the initial warmup phase, the meta-policy initially favored higher temperatures (around 1.3), promoting greater exploration in LLM policy generation.
- As training progressed, the mean sampled temperature gradually decreased, indicating a shift towards exploitation.
- The observed large variance in sampled temperatures (due to nucleus sampling) highlights the meta-policy's continuous effort to balance exploitation of promising temperatures with exploration of the temperature space.

**More Experiment Results (Table 4):**
- TAMPO's effectiveness generalized beyond mathematical reasoning. When applied to the Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct model on the ECQA commonsense reasoning benchmark, TAMPO consistently improved Pass@1 and Pass@8 scores compared to both the base model (no RL) and a standard GRPO baseline. For example, Pass@1 improved from 75.07% (GRPO) to 76.12% (TAMPO).

These results collectively demonstrate that dynamically adapting temperature as a learnable meta-policy is an effective strategy for enhancing LLM reinforcement learning, yielding improved performance without significant computational overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research on Temperature Adaptive Meta Policy Optimization (TAMPO) presents several significant contributions and potential impacts:

1. **Principled Adaptive Exploration-Exploitation:** The work introduces a principled, feedback-driven mechanism for dynamically balancing exploration and exploitation in LLMs during RL training. By treating temperature as a learnable meta-policy, it moves beyond static or heuristic temperature schedules, allowing the model to adapt its generation strategy based on real-time trajectory advantages. This can lead to more efficient and effective policy learning.

2. **Performance Improvement:** TAMPO consistently demonstrates superior performance on various mathematical reasoning benchmarks and shows generalization to commonsense reasoning, outperforming baselines that use fixed or manually tuned temperatures. This indicates that adaptive temperature control is a valuable tool for improving the capabilities of LLMs, especially in tasks requiring complex reasoning.

3. **Computational Efficiency:** A key practical advantage is that TAMPO achieves its adaptive behavior without requiring additional rollouts. By reusing trajectories generated for the inner-loop LLM policy update, it incurs negligible additional computational cost compared to existing critic-free RL algorithms. This efficiency makes it highly scalable and practical for large-scale LLM training, where computational resources are a significant constraint.

4. **Automation and Reduced Manual Tuning:** TAMPO automates the critical hyperparameter tuning of sampling temperature, eliminating the need for manual sweeps or heuristic schedules. This reduces human effort and potential sub-optimality arising from fixed settings, making the LLM RL post-training process more streamlined and accessible.

5. **Novel Application of Meta-Policies in LLM RL:** This research is presented as the first to frame sampling temperature as a learnable meta-policy in the context of LLM reinforcement learning. It addresses the non-differentiable nature of sampling temperature by using trajectory likelihoods and advantages to update the meta-policy, providing a practical solution where direct gradient-based methods are not feasible. This opens avenues for exploring adaptive control of other non-differentiable hyperparameters in LLM training.

6. **Enhanced LLM Alignment:** By enabling more effective exploration and exploitation, TAMPO contributes to better alignment of LLMs with desired task-specific objectives and human preferences. This is crucial for developing LLMs that are not only powerful but also reliable and aligned with user intentions.

In summary, TAMPO represents an advancement in LLM reinforcement learning by offering an efficient, principled, and effective method for dynamically controlling the exploration-exploitation trade-off. Its ability to improve performance and automate a critical aspect of LLM training, without increasing computational burden, has significant implications for the development and deployment of more capable and adaptable language models.
