# GLAM-SLAM: Real-time Gaussian Large-scale Mapping via Flow Densification and Spatial Decomposition

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at HERON - Hellenic Robotics Center of Excellence and the National Technical University of Athens developed GLAM-SLAM, a real-time, decoupled monocular SLAM system that leverages 3D Gaussian Splatting with flow densification and spatial decomposition to create photorealistic, large-scale maps in outdoor environments. The system demonstrates improved photometric reconstruction quality, sustains real-time performance, and maintains low GPU memory consumption across long sequences where other methods fail.

## Problem

- Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) SLAM systems struggle with prohibitive GPU memory requirements and lack scalability for long-duration, large-scale outdoor environments.
- Sparse feature-based SLAM frontends, while robust for tracking, do not provide the dense initial point clouds necessary for stable 3DGS optimization.
- A single global 3DGS model struggles to capture the extensive environmental and illumination variability encountered in large outdoor scenes.

## Method

- Employs a ==decoupled architecture== with a CPU-based ==ORB-SLAM2== frontend for robust tracking and a GPU-based backend for Gaussian mapping, using an incremental ==Scaffold-GS== variant.
- Introduces a ==Flow-Guided Densification Module== that uses dense optical flow and epipolar filtering to generate geometrically consistent 3D anchors, overcoming sparse initialization limitations.
- Implements a ==Spatial Decomposition== strategy with localized MLPs, dynamically partitioning the scene into regions, each with independent MLPs to adapt to environmental variability and optimize memory.

## Results

- GLAM-SLAM consistently achieved superior photometric reconstruction quality (e.g., **11.6%** average PSNR, **9.2%** SSIM, **28.0%** LPIPS improvements over GigaSLAM pre-optimization on KITTI).
- It maintained real-time performance (**10-20 FPS**) and demonstrated the lowest peak GPU memory usage (average **11.6 GiB** on KITTI), successfully processing long outdoor sequences where other methods suffered from Out-of-Memory failures.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both flow densification (leading to **32%** richer Gaussian representation) and localized MLPs (increasing Gaussians by **30%**) significantly improved photometric metrics and contributed additively to overall performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling the robust sparse feature-based tracking frontend from the dense Gaussian mapping backend prevents mapping imperfections from degrading localization accuracy.
> - Generating dense 3D priors from optical flow between keyframes effectively provides the necessary geometric density for stable and faster 3DGS optimization even with a sparse SLAM frontend.
> - Spatially decomposing the scene and assigning localized MLPs enables the system to adaptively model diverse environmental and illumination conditions in large outdoor areas while maintaining memory efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) has progressed through various representations of the environment, each with distinct advantages and limitations. Early SLAM systems prioritized computational efficiency and robust ego-motion estimation, employing sparse, feature-based representations such as ORB-SLAM2. While effective for trajectory accuracy and long-term stability, these sparse maps often lack the density required for tasks involving dense geometric or semantic understanding.

This led to the development of semi-dense and dense mapping approaches, including point-cloud-based (e.g., LSD-SLAM) and volumetric TSDF representations (e.g., KinectFusion). These methods aimed to reconstruct environments as visually interpretable entities, enabling downstream robotic tasks like navigation. However, these dense systems are typically resource-intensive, requiring substantial memory and GPU computation, and are often vulnerable to dynamic environments and photometric inconsistencies.

A significant advancement in map representability came with Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), which allowed for photorealistic novel view synthesis using Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). NeRF-based SLAM systems (e.g., iMAP, NICE-SLAM) demonstrated fine-grained rendering and photorealistic scene reconstructions. Despite their capabilities, NeRFs are computationally expensive during rendering and lack practical editability, often requiring full model retraining for scene modifications.

More recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) emerged as an explicit radiance field representation, offering real-time rendering and faster training than NeRFs. 3DGS represents a scene using editable 3D Gaussians with spatial, color, and opacity information. This development has spurred various 3DGS SLAM systems, broadly categorized into coupled and decoupled approaches. Coupled systems (e.g., MonoGS, SplaTAM) perform tracking directly against the evolving Gaussian map, which can lead to degradation from map imperfections. Decoupled systems (e.g., PhotoSLAM, Splat-SLAM), in contrast, maintain an independent tracking module (often sparse feature-based, ICP-based, or TSDF-fusion-based) and treat the Gaussian map as a parallel reconstruction backend, thereby preserving tracking robustness.

Within this landscape, a significant challenge remains: effectively scaling 3DGS for photorealistic reconstruction in long-duration, large-scale outdoor sequences, such as driving scenarios. Existing 3DGS SLAM systems often face limitations related to short sequence applicability, non-real-time operation, or prohibitive GPU memory requirements. Specifically, sparse feature-based SLAM frontends, while robust for tracking, do not provide the dense initial point clouds typically required for stable 3DGS optimization. Furthermore, a single global MLP model struggles to capture the extensive environmental and illumination variability inherent in large outdoor environments, leading to reduced fidelity and potential catastrophic forgetting. Prior attempts to address long outdoor sequences, such as VGGT-Long and GigaSLAM, have presented limitations, including discrete representations that lack photorealism or heavy GPU reliance that restricts online operation.

This work, GLAM-SLAM, positions itself within the decoupled 3DGS SLAM paradigm. It specifically addresses the underexplored area of large-scale outdoor environments by tackling the challenges of sparse initialization for 3DGS and managing scene variability and memory footprint across long trajectories, aiming to deliver real-time, photorealistic mapping at scale.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a real-time, decoupled monocular SLAM system that leverages 3D Gaussian Splatting for photorealistic, large-scale mapping in challenging outdoor environments. The motivation stems from several limitations observed in the current state of 3DGS-based SLAM systems when applied to extensive, long-duration scenarios.

First, existing Gaussian Splatting SLAM systems often struggle with **scalability and memory efficiency** in long-horizon scenarios. Their GPU memory requirements can become prohibitive, restricting their applicability to short sequences or bounded environments. The authors aim to overcome this by introducing a scalable representation that maintains scene coherence without exhausting computational resources.

Second, there is a recognized **mismatch between sparse SLAM frontends and the initialization requirements of 3DGS**. While robust, feature-based SLAM systems like ORB-SLAM2 provide accurate pose estimation, they yield sparse 3D point clouds. 3DGS, however, typically benefits from dense initial point clouds for faster and more stable Gaussian optimization. The objective is to bridge this geometric density gap to ensure high-fidelity reconstruction even with a sparse tracking frontend.

Third, **environmental variability in large outdoor scenes** poses a challenge for traditional 3DGS models. Factors such as significant changes in illumination, diverse object scales, and varying surface properties across an expansive environment can degrade the representational fidelity of a single global model. The system aims to address this by adopting a mechanism that can adapt to and effectively model these localized variations.

Fourth, the system seeks to maintain **real-time performance** for both tracking and mapping operations. Many high-quality 3D reconstruction methods are either too computationally intensive for online use or rely on lengthy offline post-processing steps, which limits their utility in dynamic robotic applications. The motivation is to provide a system that can continuously build and update a photorealistic map in real-time.

Finally, the work is motivated by the desire to provide a **robust and reliable SLAM solution for unbounded outdoor scenes**. The decoupled architecture aims to preserve the accuracy and stability of tracking by separating it from the mapping process, preventing mapping imperfections from degrading localization, a common issue in tightly coupled systems. This modularity is intended to enhance the system's flexibility and long-term operational stability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

GLAM-SLAM employs a decoupled architecture consisting of a CPU-based frontend for tracking and a dedicated GPU-based backend for Gaussian mapping.

**A. System Architecture:**
The system is built upon a multi-threaded ORB-SLAM2 framework, which serves as the real-time frontend. This module performs robust camera tracking, local mapping, and loop closing. Concurrently, a Gaussian Mapping thread operates on a separate GPU, maintaining and expanding a voxelized Gaussian Model using an incremental variant of Scaffold-GS. The Gaussian optimization process runs independently, performing refinement, densification, and pruning to minimize a combined photometric and structural similarity loss. Loop closures detected by ORB-SLAM2 trigger non-rigid transformations of the Gaussian anchor grid to correct accumulated global drift.

**B. Structured Gaussian Scene Representation:**
The Gaussian mapping module adopts Scaffold-GS's sparse-based approach, representing the scene as an anchor grid to minimize memory usage and preserve geometric integrity. Each anchor, `a`, encodes local information, including a learnable 8-dimensional feature descriptor `f_a`, a scaling parameter `l_y`, and `k` trainable offset vectors. These offsets are used to generate a set of `k` associated Gaussians. All Gaussian attributes (position, covariance, opacity, and view-dependent color via spherical harmonics coefficients) are implicitly predicted by lightweight Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs). These MLPs are conditioned on the anchor's feature descriptor, the anchor-to-camera distance `δ_ac`, and the viewing-direction vector `d_ac`. The voxel size for anchor discretization is set to 0.001.

**C. Flow Densification Module:**
To address the density mismatch between sparse ORB-SLAM2 triangulations and the dense initialization requirements of 3DGS, a Flow-Guided Densification Module is introduced.
1. **Dense Flow Estimation:** The system employs LiteFlowNet3, an ultra-lightweight deep neural network, to establish dense optical flow correspondences `x ↔ x'` between consecutive keyframes. This operation is decoupled from the main tracking thread and is applied sparsely (every 7 keyframes) to prevent computational bottlenecks and redundant point generation.
2. **Correspondence Filtering:** To ensure geometric consistency, randomly sampled correspondence pairs are filtered using an epipolar constraint. The fundamental matrix `F` is computed from the known camera poses provided by ORB-SLAM2. Correspondences satisfying `x'T * F * x < τ` are retained as robust matches.
3. **Triangulation and Anchor Initialization:** The robust optical flow correspondences are triangulated using the known camera poses to yield supplementary 3D geometric anchors. These flow-densified points (P2) are then fused with the 3D points tracked by ORB-SLAM2 (P1) to create a denser initial point cloud, which is subsequently discretized onto a voxel grid to form the Gaussian anchors (V). This process provides a more uniform spatial coverage, improving 3DGS optimization in sparsely tracked regions.

**D. Localized MLP Initialization (Spatial Decomposition):**
To manage environmental and illumination variability in large-scale outdoor scenes, a spatial conditioning strategy is proposed.
1. **Scene Partitioning:** The mapped environment is dynamically partitioned into a set of `M` distinct regions, `R = {R_1, R_2, ..., R_M}`.
2. **Region-Specific MLPs:** Each region `R_m` is assigned an independent set of MLPs (`θ_m`). When Gaussians are spawned from an anchor `a`, their attributes are decoded exclusively by the MLP set assigned to the region `R_m` where the anchor is located.
3. **Dynamic Trigger:** A turn detection heuristic is used to trigger the introduction of new MLPs and define new regions. This allows the system to adapt to changes in viewpoint and environment without requiring a priori knowledge of the scene structure or number of regions.
4. **Optimization Strategy:** During Gaussian model training, samples are drawn from the most recent `k=25` keyframes with a high uniform probability (0.7) and from older keyframes with a lower probability (0.3). This sampling strategy aims to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and maintain overall map consistency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The GLAM-SLAM system was evaluated on challenging, long-sequence outdoor datasets, specifically KITTI Odometry, Oxford RobotCar, and M´alaga. The evaluation included comparisons against PhotoSLAM, GigaSLAM (both pre- and post-optimization), and MonoGS. Metrics included photometric reconstruction quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS), computational performance (FPS, peak GPU memory, number of primitives), and tracking accuracy (ATE RMSE).

**A. Photometric Evaluation:**
- **Overall Performance:** GLAM-SLAM consistently outperformed PhotoSLAM and GigaSLAM (pre-optimization) across all photometric metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) on all tested datasets.
 - On KITTI, GLAM-SLAM achieved average improvements of 11.6% in PSNR, 9.2% in SSIM, and 28.0% in LPIPS over GigaSLAM (pre-opt).
 - On Oxford RobotCar, improvements were 26.6% in PSNR, 15.7% in SSIM, and 35.3% in LPIPS.
 - On M´alaga, improvements were 15.7% in PSNR, 6.3% in SSIM, and 10.5% in LPIPS.
- **Scalability and Consistency:** Qualitative results (Fig. 4) demonstrate that GLAM-SLAM maintained consistent rendering quality throughout long sequences (e.g., KITTI Seq. 08, 4071 frames), while PhotoSLAM and GigaSLAM often terminated prematurely due to memory exhaustion on sequences exceeding 2000-3200 frames. GigaSLAM's post-optimization, while boosting scores, is an offline process and exhibited unpredictable degradation on some sequences.

**B. Computational Performance:**
- **Real-time Operation:** GLAM-SLAM consistently operated in real-time across all datasets (approximately 10 FPS on KITTI, 16 FPS on Oxford RobotCar, 20 FPS on M´alaga), similar to PhotoSLAM. In contrast, GigaSLAM (pre-opt) did not achieve real-time performance, operating at approximately 3 FPS on KITTI due to heavy neural networks and costly loop closures.
- **GPU Memory Usage:** GLAM-SLAM demonstrated the lowest peak GPU memory usage (average 11.6 GiB on KITTI, including a 3 GiB overhead for the optical flow model). This efficiency allowed it to process long sequences without interruption. Other methods encountered Out-of-Memory (OOM) failures on several long sequences, requiring truncation for evaluation. While GLAM-SLAM often used more Gaussian primitives than PhotoSLAM, its structured anchor representation resulted in lower overall GPU memory consumption.

**C. Trajectory Accuracy (ATE Evaluation):**
- **Tracking Robustness:** GLAM-SLAM achieved real-time tracking performance comparable to PhotoSLAM due to their shared ORB-SLAM-based frontend.
- **Scalability Limitations:** PhotoSLAM and GigaSLAM experienced severe scalability issues, leading to early termination on long sequences due to GPU memory exhaustion. MonoGS also frequently failed.
- **Trade-offs:** GigaSLAM, when successfully completing segments, sometimes exhibited lower average ATE (e.g., 3.4m vs. 10.8m on KITTI, excluding one catastrophic failure), but this was at the cost of non-real-time operation and higher memory demands. GLAM-SLAM, while having slightly higher ATE on some sequences compared to GigaSLAM's non-real-time mode, provided acceptable tracking performance and robustly scaled to unbounded, long-sequence outdoor trajectories without resource depletion, completing full sequences where others failed (e.g., KITTI Seqs. 00 and 05).

**D. Ablation Studies:**
- **Flow Densification:** Incorporating optical-flow-based correspondences led to substantial improvements in photometric reconstruction metrics across KITTI sequences, particularly in sparsely tracked regions. This resulted in a 32% richer representation of Gaussian primitives and improved quality with fewer raw Gaussian optimization iterations (10.6k vs. 11.7k baseline), indicating faster convergence due to better geometric priors.
- **Localized MLPs:** The introduction of localized MLPs in distinct regions yielded noticeable improvements in photometric metrics and increased Gaussian primitives by approximately 30%. Notably, this enhancement maintained the same VRAM footprint as the baseline. In several sequences, localized MLPs alone outperformed the optical flow module.
- **Combined Contributions:** The study confirmed that both flow densification and localized MLPs are complementary. Enabling both enhancements simultaneously led to the best overall performance, demonstrating additive gains.

**E. Real-world Evaluation (Parking Sequence):**
- On a custom real-world parking lot sequence captured by a ground vehicle, GLAM-SLAM again demonstrated significant photometric improvements over all other state-of-the-art methods.
- In this specific scenario, flow densification provided a clearer improvement over the baseline, while the spatial decomposition component yielded marginal gains due to relatively consistent illumination and appearance changes in the environment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

GLAM-SLAM represents a notable advancement in the field of real-time, photorealistic 3D mapping, particularly for large-scale outdoor environments. Its significance and potential impact stem from its ability to address several critical limitations that have hindered the practical deployment of Gaussian Splatting SLAM systems.

**Addressing Major Bottlenecks:** The paper successfully tackles the core challenges of prohibitive GPU memory requirements, lack of real-time performance in large scenes, and the difficulty of maintaining high reconstruction quality across extended outdoor trajectories. By providing a solution that simultaneously delivers real-time operation, scalable memory usage, and enhanced photorealistic quality, GLAM-SLAM advances the state-of-the-art for practical SLAM systems.

**Enhanced Reconstruction Quality at Scale:** The system demonstrates substantial improvements in photometric reconstruction quality (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) compared to existing methods, especially over long, complex outdoor sequences. This high-fidelity representation of the environment is crucial for applications that demand visual realism, surpassing the capabilities of traditional sparse or semi-dense mapping approaches.

**Robust Scalability:** GLAM-SLAM's design enables it to operate effectively in unbounded outdoor environments without encountering the memory exhaustion issues that limit other 3DGS SLAM systems to shorter sequences or constrained settings. This robust scalability is vital for applications requiring continuous mapping over extensive areas, such as urban driving or large-area surveillance.

**Real-time Performance for Online Deployment:** By achieving real-time performance for both tracking and mapping, GLAM-SLAM facilitates online deployment in autonomous systems. This capability differentiates it from methods that require lengthy offline post-processing, making it suitable for dynamic robotic tasks where immediate and continuous environmental understanding is necessary.

**Innovative Technical Contributions:**
- The **Flow Densification Module** is a significant contribution, effectively bridging the gap between sparse feature-based SLAM frontends and the dense initialization demands of 3DGS. This approach leverages optical flow and epipolar geometry to create a geometrically consistent and denser set of initial anchors, leading to improved convergence and fidelity in sparsely covered regions.
- The **Spatial Decomposition Strategy with Localized MLPs** offers an adaptive solution for modeling complex and variable outdoor scenes. By partitioning the environment and assigning region-specific MLPs, the system mitigates catastrophic forgetting and efficiently captures diverse illumination, object scales, and surface properties, which a single global model would struggle to represent accurately.

**Potential Impact on Robotic Applications:**
- **Autonomous Driving:** The ability to build and continuously update high-fidelity, photorealistic 3D maps in real-time makes GLAM-SLAM highly relevant for autonomous vehicles. Such detailed maps can enhance perception, localization, and planning capabilities, potentially contributing to safer and more efficient autonomous navigation.
- **Robotic Inspection and Exploration:** Robots could use GLAM-SLAM to create detailed visual records of large outdoor areas, facilitating inspection tasks (e.g., infrastructure monitoring) or aiding in exploration by providing rich contextual information.
- **Augmented and Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** The photorealistic and scalable mapping capabilities could support the creation of large-scale digital twins of real-world environments, enabling immersive AR/VR experiences that are grounded in accurate and visually rich representations.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** By open-sourcing its code, GLAM-SLAM provides a robust baseline for further research in scalable 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM. This could inspire advancements in dynamic scene modeling, semantic understanding, real-time editing of 3DGS maps, and the integration of diverse sensor modalities.

In summary, GLAM-SLAM makes a substantial contribution by providing a comprehensive solution to key challenges in real-time, large-scale, photorealistic 3D mapping. Its novel architectural design and technical innovations position it as a foundational work with broad implications for robotics, autonomous systems, and immersive technologies.
