# WorldCompass: Reinforcement Learning for Long-Horizon World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> WorldCompass is a reinforcement learning post-training framework that significantly improves interactive video-based world models' ability to accurately follow explicit actions and maintain visual quality over long sequences. It boosts composite action accuracy from approximately 20% to 55% and enhances overall visual fidelity across various generative tasks.

## Problem

- Existing video-based world models learn action-following implicitly from pixel-level supervision during pre-training, leading to difficulties in accurately executing complex or long-horizon explicit interaction signals.
- Current reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks for generative models are not directly applicable to autoregressive, interactive, and long-horizon video generation due to fundamental architectural differences.
- Models often struggle with maintaining long-term consistency, preventing drift, and accumulating errors when responding to complex action sequences, which limits their utility for embodied AI and interactive applications.

## Method

- Introduces **WorldCompass**, an RL post-training framework featuring a novel clip-level rollout strategy for efficient and fine-grained feedback in autoregressive video generation, mitigating sparse reward signals.
- Employs two complementary reward functions: an Interaction Following Score (IF Score) that measures action adherence using 3D foundation models, and a Visual Quality Score (VQ Score) based on human preference models (HPSv3) to ensure both accuracy and aesthetics.
- Utilizes an efficient RL optimization algorithm inspired by DiffusionNFT, with negative-aware fine-tuning and practical optimizations like timestep subsetting, Best-of-N sample selection, and progressive clip length training to enhance computational feasibility.

## Results

- Achieved substantial gains in interaction following accuracy for complex composite actions, improving from approximately **20%** to **55%** in base world models.
- Demonstrated consistent enhancements in HPSv3 visual quality scores and improved spatial geometric consistency across various scenarios and generation lengths (up to 381 frames).
- Showed strong generalizability across different base model variants (HunyuanVideo-1.5-8B and Wan2.2-5B) and video horizons, validating the robustness and broad applicability of the WorldCompass framework.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The proposed clip-level rollout strategy is crucial for providing dense, consistent feedback, leading to substantial improvements in interaction following for long-horizon video generation compared to sparse, sequence-level rollouts.
> - Combining complementary reward functions for interaction fidelity and visual quality is essential to prevent reward hacking and ensure balanced, stable improvements, as optimizing for one objective alone can degrade the other.
> - Sampling rollout data from different initial noises, as utilized by the DiffusionNFT-inspired approach, provides the necessary diversity for learning robust action control, outperforming methods that rely on SDE sampling from the same initial noise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of **world models**, **video generation**, and **reinforcement learning (RL) for large generative models**.

**World Models and Video Generation:** The paper directly addresses the rapidly evolving field of video-based world models. Recent advancements, exemplified by projects like Cosmos, HY-World, and the Genie series, have highlighted the transformative potential of these models in creating interactive, generative environments. These models are crucial for developing embodied AI agents that can learn and plan within virtual worlds, as well as for applications in generative media and content creation. The inherent need for interactive exploration in these models demands autoregressive and long-horizon generation capabilities, alongside precise adherence to diverse action conditions.

**Limitations of Existing World Models:** The authors identify a critical gap in the current landscape: most open-source video-based world models are primarily focused on the pre-training stage. These models typically learn to follow actions implicitly through pixel-level supervision from raw visual data. While effective to a degree, this implicit learning often falls short when confronted with complex action sequences, action switching, or the need for long-term consistency in interactive environments. The resulting models may struggle with accurately and consistently exploring a generative world based on explicit interaction signals.

**Reinforcement Learning for Generative Models:** The paper contextualizes its approach within the broader trend of employing reinforcement learning for post-training large generative models. This includes successful applications in Large Language Models (LLMs), such as DeepSeek-R1, which uses on-policy RL to guide autoregressive LLMs toward emergent capabilities. Inspired by this success, recent research has extended RL to diffusion models, with works like DiffusionDPO, Flow-GRPO, Dance-GRPO, and DiffusionNFT exploring alignment and fine-tuning using various RL algorithms.

**Bridging the Gap: RL for Interactive, Autoregressive Video World Models:** WorldCompass specifically aims to bridge a crucial gap identified by the authors: existing RL frameworks for diffusion models predominantly target a paradigm where the entire content sequence (e.g., an image or a short video clip) is generated in parallel within a single diffusion process. This approach is fundamentally incompatible with the unique requirements of video-based world models, which necessitate *sequential*, *autoregressive*, and often *very long-horizon* generation, all while responding to *interactive* action signals. The paper argues that this fundamental architectural difference prevents the direct application of existing RL pipelines. WorldCompass thus introduces a novel RL framework uniquely tailored to "steer" the exploration of these interactive, autoregressive world models, moving beyond implicit action learning towards direct and explicit teaching of interaction fidelity.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce **WorldCompass**, a novel Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training framework designed to enhance long-horizon, interactive video-based world models.

The key motivations driving this research stem from the limitations of current world models and the unique challenges posed by their interactive, autoregressive nature:

- **Addressing Implicit Action Learning:** Current video-based world models primarily rely on pixel supervision during pre-training, which leads to implicit learning of action-following behaviors. This approach often results in models that struggle to accurately and consistently execute explicit interaction signals, especially for complex or composite actions and over extended durations. The motivation is to move beyond this implicit learning by employing direct, explicit supervision via RL.
- **Enhancing Long-Horizon Interaction Accuracy and Consistency:** For world models to be truly useful for embodied AI or generative media, they must reliably respond to user or agent actions over long sequences of interactions. Existing models often suffer from error accumulation and drift, making long-horizon consistent exploration challenging. WorldCompass aims to directly improve this aspect by providing fine-grained feedback on interaction fidelity.
- **Improving Visual Quality in Interactive Contexts:** While interaction accuracy is paramount, maintaining high visual quality is equally important for a convincing and usable generative world. The motivation is to improve both aspects simultaneously, as often, models might optimize for one at the expense of the other.
- **Developing an RL Framework for Autoregressive Video Generation:** Existing RL techniques for generative models are not directly applicable to the distinct challenges of autoregressive, interactive, and long-horizon video generation. The authors are motivated to redesign the entire RL pipeline—including rollout strategies, reward functions, and optimization algorithms—to effectively "steer" the exploration of these specific types of world models. This involves tackling issues like sparse reward signals and computational inefficiency inherent in long-sequence generation.
- **Mitigating Reward Hacking and Instability:** RL training, especially for generative models, is prone to reward hacking where the model finds shortcuts to maximize rewards without achieving the desired behavior. The motivation is to design a robust reward system and optimization strategy that prevents such issues and ensures stable, meaningful improvements.
- **Boosting Computational Efficiency:** Training large generative models with RL is computationally intensive. A key motivation is to introduce efficiency optimizations that make the post-training process feasible for practical deployment and further research.

In essence, the authors aim to transform passively pre-trained world models into truly *interactive* and *controllable* generative environments by directly optimizing them with reinforcement learning, thereby unlocking their full potential for advanced AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

WorldCompass proposes a novel RL post-training framework specifically engineered for the unique characteristics of autoregressive, interactive, and long-horizon video-based world models. The methodology revolves around three core innovations: a specialized rollout strategy, complementary reward functions, and an efficient RL optimization algorithm, all carefully integrated into the world model's generation paradigm.

#### 3.1. Clip-level Rollout Strategy for Autoregressive Generation

Traditional RL for autoregressive models like LLMs often uses sequence-level rollouts, where an entire sequence is generated and then scored. The authors argue that this approach leads to sparse feedback signals for long-horizon video generation, making it difficult to pinpoint specific errors in individual clips. To address this, WorldCompass introduces a **clip-level rollout strategy**:
- **Shared Prefix Generation:** For a target clip index `n`, the model first autoregressively generates the preceding `n-1` video clips, establishing a shared historical context.
- **Candidate Rollouts:** Subsequently, `G` candidate rollouts are generated *only for the `n-th` target clip*, conditioned on the shared prefix, the current action `a_n`, and the world prompt `c`.
- **Fine-grained Evaluation:** Each of these `G` clips is then individually evaluated, providing a dense and fine-grained reward signal specific to that clip.
This strategy offers two key advantages:
 - **Rollout Efficiency:** It significantly reduces computational complexity from roughly `O(N * G)` (sequence-level) to `O(N + G)` (clip-level), as the `n-1` prefix clips are sampled only once and reused. This is crucial for long-duration videos.
 - **Consistent and Fine-grained Reward:** By generating candidates from identical historical observations, it ensures consistency and provides a more comparable reward signal, allowing for targeted optimization based on the quality of the current generation and mitigating exposure bias.

#### 3.2. Complementary Reward Functions for Interactive Generation

The effectiveness of RL hinges on reliable reward signals. For interactive video generation, WorldCompass designs two complementary reward functions to assess each sampled clip, targeting the two fundamental attributes of a good world model:
- **Interaction Following Score (IF Score):** This evaluates how accurately the generated clip adheres to the given action conditions (translation and rotation).
 - **Action Decomposition:** Action signals are decomposed into translation and rotation components, following established practices (e.g., Genie 3).
 - **Camera Trajectory Estimation:** Advanced 3D foundation models (e.g., Liu et al., 2025d; Lin et al., 2025) are employed to estimate the camera trajectory within the generated video clip.
 - **Accuracy Calculation:** The continuous trajectory is mapped to a predefined discrete action space. Rotation accuracy is determined by comparing estimated relative camera rotation against a fixed threshold (e.g., 1 degree). Translation accuracy is assessed using *multiple* predefined thresholds to account for varying scene scales, ensuring robustness.
 - **Final Score:** The rotation and translation accuracies are calculated independently and averaged to form the final IF score.
- **Visual Quality Score (VQ Score):** This assesses the aesthetic quality and text-visual alignment of the generated video.
 - **Reward Model:** HPSv3 (Ma et al., 2025), a human preference score model, is used for this purpose.
 - **Sampling:** Frames are sampled at 4-frame intervals from each clip, and their average HPSv3 score represents the clip's overall visual quality.
The authors highlight that these two reward functions act as **mutual regularizers**, preventing reward hacking (where optimizing one objective degrades the other) and leading to more robust and balanced training.

#### 3.3. Efficient RL Optimization

WorldCompass employs an efficient RL algorithm inspired by DiffusionNFT (Zheng et al., 2025b), tailored for video diffusion models, along with several practical optimizations:
- **Negative-aware Fine-tuning Strategy:** Unlike FlowGRPO which relies on SDE sampling from the same initial noise (leading to visual diversity but limited camera motion exploration), WorldCompass samples rollout data from *different initial noises*. The model is directly trained with a flow matching objective.
- **Advantage Calculation:** For `G` samples at clip `n`, normalized advantages `a^(i)_IF` and `a^(i)_VQ` are computed for each reward dimension by subtracting the mean and dividing by the standard deviation of scores across the rollout group.
- **Optimality Probability:** These normalized advantages are combined into an `optimality probability r^(i)` through a clipped linear combination, representing how "good" a sample is relative to others.
- **Optimization Loss:** The core optimization uses a modified flow matching loss (Equation 6), which implicitly creates positive and negative velocity predictions (`v^+_θ`, `v^-_θ`) for each sample. The loss function weights the squared difference between these implicit velocities and the target velocity (`v^(i)`) by `r^(i)` and `(1-r^(i))`, effectively pushing "good" samples towards `v^+_θ` and "bad" samples towards `v^-_θ`.
 - The old policy (`v_θ_old`) used for rollout sampling is EMA updated by the trained model, and a lower learning rate is used to prevent over-optimization, replacing the KL divergence loss used in original DiffusionNFT.

**Efficient Training Strategies:** To manage the computational demands of long-horizon video generation and large-scale RL, WorldCompass incorporates several practical optimizations:
- **Subsetting Timesteps (T):** Instead of processing all `T` diffusion sampling timesteps, a random subset of timesteps is selected for each training iteration, significantly reducing overhead.
- **Best-of-N Sample Selection (G):** Only a subset of rollout samples (specifically, the top 3 and bottom 3 in terms of reward) are selected for training. This focuses the model on the most informative cases.
- **Progressive Optimization for Clips Length (N):** The target clip index `n` (for rollout and optimization) incrementally cycles from 1 to `N` during training. This creates a curriculum learning effect, gradually increasing the task horizon and improving hardware utilization.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Base Models:** Post-training is applied to WorldPlay variants: HunyuanVideo-1.5-8B and Wan2.2-5B.
- **Actions:** Eight basic actions (move forward/backward/left/right, rotate up/down/left/right) are used, which can be combined into complex composite actions.
- **Generation Length:** Maximum 16 clips (approx. 256 frames), with evaluations at short (125f), medium (253f), and long (381f) horizons.
- **Training Data:** 4,000 diverse images/captions with randomly constructed complex action sequences. No manual annotations are required as supervision comes from rewards.
- **Hardware:** 3 days of training on 64 H20 GPUs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of WorldCompass on the state-of-the-art open-source world model, WorldPlay, demonstrates significant improvements in both interaction accuracy and visual fidelity across various challenging scenarios.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance Improvements

- **Interaction Following Accuracy:** WorldCompass yields substantial gains in action-following accuracy. For **complex composite actions**, the average accuracy improves dramatically from approximately **20% to 55%**. This represents a fundamental shift from the base model frequently failing to comprehend and execute actions, to successfully performing them. For **basic actions**, the method still provides a noticeable improvement of about **10%** (e.g., from 60% to 70%), primarily reflecting more rapid and precise responses during action switching.
- **Visual Quality Enhancement:** Beyond interaction fidelity, the RL training also enhances the overall **HPSv3 visual quality score** across all scenarios. This indicates that the complementary reward functions successfully guide the model towards generating not only accurate but also aesthetically pleasing video content.
- **Generalizability Across Models and Horizons:** The improvements are consistently observed across both base model variants (HunyuanVideo-1.5-8B and Wan2.2-5B) and across different video lengths (short, medium, and long-term generation, up to 381 frames). This demonstrates the high generalizability and robustness of the WorldCompass framework, strengthening the core capabilities of the world model regardless of its specific base architecture or the interaction horizon.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- Visual comparisons (Figures 3 and 4) corroborate the quantitative findings. Videos generated with WorldCompass post-training clearly show improved adherence to action sequences (e.g., correct forward-left movement followed by a right turn) and superior visual quality compared to the base model. The authors also observe an indirect benefit: the generated content exhibits **improved spatial geometric consistency**, which is a crucial aspect of believable world models and further implies the model's enhanced understanding of its environment.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies and Key Insights

The ablation studies provide crucial insights into the effectiveness of WorldCompass's core components:
- **Clip-level Rollout is Essential:** A comparison between clip-level and sample-level rollouts definitively shows that clip-level rollout is critical. Sample-level rollout leads to an overly sparse reward signal for long videos, failing to improve action following and yielding only minor visual quality gains. The fine-grained, consistent feedback of clip-level rollout is indispensable for targeted optimization of interaction capabilities.
- **Complementary Reward Functions Prevent Reward Hacking:** The study confirms that relying on a single reward function makes the model susceptible to reward hacking. Optimizing solely for Interaction Following (IF) improves accuracy but severely degrades visual quality, leading to instability or model collapse. Conversely, optimizing only for Visual Quality (VQ) results in aesthetically pleasing but static or motionless content. The synergistic use of both rewards acts as mutual constraints, ensuring balanced improvements and stable training.
- **DiffusionNFT Outperforms DanceGRPO for World Models:** Replacing the DiffusionNFT-inspired algorithm with DanceGRPO, an alternative RL algorithm for diffusion models, yields only marginal improvements in interaction-following. This is attributed to DanceGRPO's reliance on SDE sampling from the same initial noise, which diversifies visual scenes but limits the exploration of diverse camera trajectories—a critical requirement for enhancing action-following capabilities in world models. DiffusionNFT's strategy of sampling from different initial noises provides the necessary diversity for learning robust action control.
- **Efficiency Optimizations are Highly Effective:** The implemented strategies, including subsetting timesteps and Best-of-N sample selection, reduce training overhead by 50% without compromising performance. This demonstrates that these optimizations effectively preserve critical information while significantly enhancing computational efficiency, making large-scale RL post-training more feasible.

In summary, the main findings highlight that WorldCompass successfully addresses the long-standing challenge of enabling direct, accurate, and consistent interaction in autoregressive video-based world models through a cleverly designed RL framework and efficient training strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The WorldCompass framework represents a significant advancement in the field of generative AI, particularly for interactive and embodied artificial intelligence. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

- **Elevating World Model Capabilities:** WorldCompass fundamentally improves the core functionality of video-based world models by enabling them to accurately and consistently follow explicit interaction signals over long horizons. This moves world models beyond mere video generation to truly interactive, agent-ready environments. It addresses the crucial gap where existing models implicitly learn actions from pixel data, by introducing direct and explicit RL-based optimization for interactive control.
- **Advancing Embodied AI and Agent Learning:** More controllable and reliable world models are foundational for developing sophisticated embodied AI agents. Agents can now learn, plan, and execute complex behaviors in generative virtual environments with much greater fidelity. This could accelerate research in areas like robotic control, simulation-to-real transfer, and intelligent agent training within dynamic virtual worlds.
- **Enhancing Generative Media and Content Creation:** The improved interaction accuracy and visual quality directly benefit generative media applications. Creators can now "steer" their generated worlds with greater precision, leading to more tailored and compelling interactive narratives, game content, or virtual experiences. The framework makes the promise of explorable AI-generated worlds a tangible reality.
- **Pioneering RL for Autoregressive, Interactive Diffusion Models:** WorldCompass offers a tailored and effective RL framework for a unique and challenging class of generative models: autoregressive video diffusion models. This contribution extends the applicability of RL to a specific, critical domain where existing RL-for-diffusion techniques were insufficient. It provides a blueprint for future research in applying RL to complex, sequential generative tasks.
- **Practical Feasibility through Computational Efficiency:** The embedded efficiency optimizations (clip-level rollout, timestep subsampling, Best-of-N selection, progressive training) are not just minor tweaks; they are crucial enablers for making RL post-training of large video models computationally feasible at scale. This practical aspect is vital for industry adoption and for fostering further academic exploration in a resource-intensive field.
- **Opening New Research Avenues:** The identified limitation regarding visual quality drift and spatial memory retention in long-form generation, and the need for robust reward functions to address them, explicitly outlines promising directions for future research. WorldCompass not only solves current problems but also clearly delineates the next frontiers for improving long-horizon generative models.
- **Impact on Open-Source Ecosystem:** By demonstrating significant enhancements on an open-source world model like WorldPlay, WorldCompass contributes directly to the broader AI community. It makes advanced capabilities accessible, potentially inspiring more researchers and developers to build upon these foundations, fostering an accelerated pace of innovation in controllable generative AI.

In conclusion, WorldCompass is a pivotal contribution that enhances the controllability, fidelity, and practical utility of interactive world models, pushing the boundaries of what is achievable in generative AI for simulation, content creation, and embodied intelligence.
