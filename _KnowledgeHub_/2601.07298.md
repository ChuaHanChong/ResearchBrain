# Mimic Human Cognition, Master Multi-Image Reasoning: A Meta-Action Framework for Enhanced Visual Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from East China Normal University and ByteDance developed CINEMA, a framework that enhances multi-image reasoning in MLLMs by mimicking human cognitive steps through structured meta-actions and a two-stage reinforcement learning approach. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance on various multi-image and video benchmarks, surpassing models like GPT-4o, and demonstrates robust generalizability to single-image tasks.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) perform well on single-image tasks but struggle with complex multi-image reasoning, which requires integrating information and understanding relationships across multiple visual inputs.
- Current approaches often fail to effectively capture intricate semantic, spatial, or temporal dependencies between images, or synthesize distributed critical information.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) for reasoning tasks frequently suffers from policy entropy collapse, where models become overly deterministic and lose exploration capacity.

## Method

- Introduces **CINEMA**, a Cognition-Inspired ==Meta-Action== Framework, which defines five structured meta-actions (Global, Focus, Hint, Think, Answer) to guide MLLMs through human-like cognitive reasoning steps.
- Proposes Retrieval-Based Tree Sampling, a student-teacher inspired data generation strategy to create diverse and high-quality "meta-action trajectories" for training.
- Implements a two-stage reinforcement learning paradigm featuring a Diversity-Preserving Strategy (DPS) to mitigate policy entropy collapse, followed by an annealed exploitation phase.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on several multi-image and video benchmarks, including MUIR, MVMath, EMMA, VideoMME, and VideoMMMU.
- Outperformed leading closed-source models like GPT-4o on multi-image reasoning tasks (e.g., MUIR **71.6%** vs. **68.0%**, MVMath **36.9%** vs. **32.1%**).
- Demonstrated strong generalizability and competitive performance on single-image benchmarks, including setting state-of-the-art on MM-Math.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mimicking human cognitive processes through explicit meta-actions enables MLLMs to systematically address complex multi-image reasoning tasks.
> - Training with diverse, correct reasoning trajectories, generated via student-teacher and retrieval methods, significantly enhances learning and generalizability.
> - A two-stage reinforcement learning approach effectively balances exploration and exploitation, preventing policy entropy collapse and leading to more robust and varied solution strategies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated considerable proficiency in tasks involving single-image understanding and reasoning. Extensive research has focused on improving these capabilities, leading to advancements in areas such as general visual question answering and image captioning. However, a significant limitation of current MLLMs lies in their performance when confronted with multi-image reasoning scenarios. Real-world applications, including e-commerce product analysis, autonomous driving scene comprehension, and comprehensive video understanding, frequently necessitate the simultaneous processing and reasoning over multiple images.

The challenges in multi-image reasoning are primarily twofold: first, the need to identify and integrate complex inter-relationships (semantic, spatial, temporal) among images; and second, the ability to pinpoint critical information scattered across an image set while disregarding irrelevant details. Existing MLLM approaches for multi-image tasks, such as VISC, which employs a focus-centric visual chain, or MIA-DPO, which augments single-image datasets, tend to address specific aspects or rely on simpler forms of inter-image relationships. Specialized models for video understanding also exist, but a generalized framework for comprehensive multi-image reasoning remains an area of active development.

This paper positions its work within this landscape by proposing a framework inspired by human cognitive processes to bridge the gap between single-image and multi-image reasoning capabilities in MLLMs. It aims to develop a systematic approach that allows models to handle complex multi-image inputs more effectively, thereby enhancing their generalizability across diverse visual understanding tasks, including those traditionally addressed by specialized video models. The work also contributes to the field of reinforcement learning for MLLMs by introducing methods to address policy entropy collapse, a common challenge in training large models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to enhance the multi-image reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models, which currently exhibit degraded performance in scenarios involving multiple visual inputs. The authors aim to achieve this by developing a framework that explicitly models human-like cognitive processes.

The motivation for this work stems from several observations:
- **Limitations of existing MLLMs:** While MLLMs excel at single-image tasks, their performance significantly declines in multi-image contexts. This is attributed to the inherent complexities of multi-image reasoning, specifically the intricate inter-relationships among images (e.g., temporal sequences, spatial arrangements, semantic associations) and the challenge of identifying critical, yet scattered, information within a set of images.
- **Inspiration from human cognition:** Humans naturally employ a systematic and structured approach to complex visual reasoning tasks. Cognitive research indicates that humans effectively integrate global contextual understanding with focused analysis of local details and explicitly identify key information to improve performance. This suggests that artificial reasoning systems could benefit from a framework that mirrors these established cognitive principles.
- **Need for structured reasoning:** Current MLLMs often lack an explicit mechanism to guide their reasoning process in a sequential, human-like manner. The authors hypothesize that by decomposing complex tasks into discrete, structured meta-actions, models can better navigate the nuances of multi-image problems.
- **Addressing reinforcement learning challenges:** The application of reinforcement learning (RL) to MLLMs, while powerful, often suffers from issues like policy entropy collapse, where the model's exploratory capacity diminishes prematurely, leading to suboptimal or less diverse solutions. A secondary motivation is to develop RL strategies that maintain diversity and facilitate robust learning.

In summary, the key objectives are to:
1. Propose a cognition-inspired framework that structures multi-image reasoning into a sequence of human-like meta-actions.
2. Develop a robust data generation strategy that provides diverse and high-quality reasoning trajectories for model training.
3. Design a novel reinforcement learning approach that balances exploration and exploitation to prevent entropy collapse and improve generalization.
4. Demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of the proposed framework across various multi-image, multi-frame, and single-image visual understanding benchmarks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces a Cognition-Inspired Meta-Action Framework (CINEMA), which integrates a structured reasoning process, a novel data sampling strategy, and a two-stage reinforcement learning paradigm.

#### 3.1. Meta Action Definition

The CINEMA framework decomposes multi-image reasoning into five distinct meta-actions, designed to mimic human cognitive steps:
- **Global:** This action prompts the model to analyze the overall context, read the entire question, and identify relationships (temporal, spatial, semantic) among all input images. It establishes a holistic understanding of the problem.
- **Focus:** This action directs the model to concentrate on specific images or salient details within an image that are crucial for answering the question. It emphasizes local, in-depth analysis of relevant visual content.
- **Hint:** This action encourages the model to summarize key points, identify potential pitfalls, or process misleading information. It simulates the human tendency to articulate critical aspects and error-prone areas of a problem.
- **Think:** This action involves internal reasoning, where the model processes acquired information, leverages prior knowledge, and performs logical inference to formulate hypotheses or solutions.
- **Answer:** This final action is responsible for outputting the conclusive answer based on the insights generated by the preceding meta-actions, marking the end of a reasoning trajectory.

#### 3.2. Retrieval-Based Tree Sampling

To generate high-quality and diverse training data, the authors introduce a Retrieval-Based Tree Sampling strategy, analogous to a student-teacher learning paradigm. This process involves three steps:
1. **Initial Trajectory Generation:** A smaller "student model" is prompted to generate an initial reasoning trajectory for a given task using the defined meta-actions, irrespective of correctness.
2. **Teacher-Guided Trajectory Refinement:** A stronger "teacher model" (GPT-4o) then evaluates and refines the student's trajectory. It attempts to produce a correct solution while adhering to the original action sequence, ensuring accuracy.
3. **Retrieval-Based Diverse Sampling:** To enrich the dataset with diverse reasoning paths, the system retrieves an alternative correct trajectory from a database of meta-action trees. This retrieval prioritizes trajectories with low cosine similarity (calculated using BGE embeddings) to the teacher-refined trajectory, gradually searching for more similar ones until a distinct, correct path is found. This ensures each cold-start training instance has two unique, correct reasoning trajectories.

#### 3.3. Dataset Construction

A comprehensive training dataset is constructed, comprising 56,000 cold-start instances and 58,000 reinforcement learning (RL) instances. These instances span three categories of visual reasoning tasks: multi-image tasks (requiring at least two images), multi-frame tasks (sequential video or time-series data), and single-image tasks. Cold-start data includes problems where GPT-4o successfully provided correct answers, allowing for the generation of two distinct reasoning trajectories per problem. RL data consists of more challenging problems where GPT-4o initially failed to produce a correct answer, reserving them for reinforcement learning optimization.

#### 3.4. Balancing Exploration and Exploitation via Two-Stage Optimization

A two-stage reinforcement learning paradigm is implemented to address policy entropy collapse and effectively balance exploration and exploitation:

1. **Diversity-Preserving Strategy (DPS):** In the first stage, the model employs a DPS, built upon DAPO (Direct Advantage Policy Optimization), to maintain diversity in meta-action trajectories. The reward function (R) is a weighted combination of accuracy (R_acc) and format validity (R_format). A crucial element is a penalty term ($ \frac{N-1}{G-1} \cdot 0.1 $) applied when correct answers are generated by homogeneous meta-action patterns within a group of G rollouts, where N is the number of trajectories sharing identical patterns. This term discourages over-reliance on a single reasoning path, promoting broader exploration and a variety of solution strategies.
2. **Annealed Exploitation:** The second stage transitions to a more focused exploitation phase, using DAPO with an annealing schedule. This allows the model to consolidate the performance gains and learned diversity from the first stage, gradually shifting towards higher-accuracy, more deterministic behaviors while still benefiting from the maintained entropy. The details of DAPO, including its Clip-Higher mechanism, Dynamic Sampling, Overlong Punishment, and Token-level Loss, are elaborated in the supplementary material.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

The backbone model for CINEMA is Qwen2.5VL 7B. Cold-start training is conducted for two epochs with a learning rate of 1e-5. The RL procedure consists of 700 steps of DPS for entropy enhancement, followed by 300 steps of DAPO for annealed exploitation. Rollouts are generated with a batch size of 64, temperature of 1.0, and 8 rollouts per prompt. Reward mechanisms include math verification tools (math-verify, mathruler) for mathematical tasks and exact string matching for non-mathematical tasks, alongside format rewards for meta-action trajectory validity. During inference, decoding hyperparameters are set to temperature = 0.6, top-p = 0.7, and a maximum of 1024 tokens.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of CINEMA encompassed extensive experiments across multi-image, video, and single-image benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Results on Multi-Image Benchmarks

On multi-image benchmarks, CINEMA demonstrated notable performance improvements over the baseline Qwen2.5VL 7B.
- **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance:** The model achieved SOTA performance on several key benchmarks, including MUIR, MVMath, EMMA, VideoMME, and VideoMMMU.
- **Outperformance of Closed-Source Models:** CINEMA surpassed the performance of GPT-4o on both the MUIR and MVMath benchmarks, indicating its strong reasoning capabilities in multi-image contexts.
- **Significant Improvements over Baseline:** Across multi-image comprehensive benchmarks, CINEMA showed a 13.7% improvement on MUIR and 6.9% on MIRB compared to Qwen2.5VL. On multi-image reasoning benchmarks, it improved by 10.2% on MVMath and 8.9% on EMMA.
- **Superiority in Video Understanding:** The model notably outperformed specialized video reasoning models across all three video benchmarks (MVBench, VideoMME, VideoMMMU), suggesting its effectiveness in capturing both spatial and temporal dependencies in sequential visual data.

#### 4.2. Results on Single-Image Benchmarks

CINEMA's capabilities extended to single-image benchmarks, demonstrating its generalizability.
- **Competitive SOTA Performance:** The model achieved SOTA performance on MM-Math and competitive results on other mathematical benchmarks, often surpassing existing models specifically designed for single-image reasoning.
- **Improvements over Baseline:** CINEMA showed a 3% improvement on MMMU-Pro and 3.8% on M3COT over Qwen2.5VL 7B, even outperforming closed-source models like GPT-4V and GPT-4o on M3COT.

#### 4.3. Results on Pass@K Setting

Experiments using the Pass@K metric (where K represents the number of inference attempts, and success is counted if at least one attempt is correct) further validated the two-stage RL approach. The results showed that incorporating the two-stage RL consistently led to higher average accuracy across K=2, 4, 8, and 16 attempts compared to models without this training paradigm. This indicates that the method fosters more diverse sampling behavior and achieves a higher performance ceiling.

#### 4.4. Further Analysis (Ablation Studies and RQ Responses)

Detailed analyses were conducted to investigate specific aspects of the framework:

- **RQ1: Can diverse trajectories improve model performance?**
 - Comparison of training with two distinct trajectories (Ours), a single trajectory, conventional Chain-of-Thought (CoT), and direct prompting of MLLMs using meta-actions (without specific training).
 - Results showed that training with two trajectories yielded superior performance in both SFT and RL stages compared to single-trajectory training and conventional CoT.
 - Direct prompting demonstrated poor performance, highlighting the necessity of the constructed datasets for effective meta-action utilization.

- **RQ2: How does the model perform with different numbers of input images?**
 - Evaluations on MUIR (2-9 images) and MMIU (2-32 images) benchmarks indicated that CINEMA consistently outperformed the base model across varying numbers of input images, even with more than 17 images, demonstrating its robustness in handling multi-image inputs.

- **RQ3: How does the model perform across different tasks?**
 - On the MUIR benchmark (12 distinct tasks), CINEMA achieved improvements on almost all tasks. Notably, it showed significant improvements on tasks not included in its training set (e.g., Geographic, Cartoon, Visual Grounding), confirming the generalizability of the reasoning framework.

- **RQ4: Whether each meta-action plays a role in our framework?**
 - Ablation studies, where individual meta-actions (<global>, <focus>, <hint>, <think>) were removed from CINEMA, consistently resulted in performance degradation across MUIR, MIRB, and VideoMME benchmarks. This indicates that each meta-action contributes to the overall effectiveness of the framework.

- **RQ5: How does two-stage RL training influence entropy and training dynamics?**
 - Analysis of entropy and reward dynamics demonstrated that the Diversity-Preserving Strategy (DPS) in the first stage maintained moderate entropy levels, which then gradually decreased during the annealed exploitation stage.
 - Compared to a baseline without the two-stage RL, CINEMA preserved consistently higher entropy, effectively preventing entropy collapse and promoting sustained exploration. This, in turn, led to more diverse meta-action trajectories while matching the baseline in training accuracy. Visualizations confirmed richer and more varied meta-action trajectories with two-stage RL.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant contribution to the field of multimodal large language models by directly addressing their limitations in multi-image reasoning. The proposed Cognition-Inspired Meta-Action Framework (CINEMA) offers a structured and interpretable approach to complex visual understanding tasks, moving beyond mere pattern recognition to explicitly model human-like cognitive steps.

The key aspects of its significance and potential impact include:

- **Enhanced Multi-Image Reasoning:** CINEMA's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance on various multi-image and video benchmarks, and even surpass advanced closed-source models like GPT-4o in specific tasks, demonstrates a substantial leap in MLLMs' capacity to interpret and reason over complex visual narratives. This is crucial for real-world applications such as comprehensive video analytics, autonomous system decision-making, and intelligent e-commerce platforms.
- **Generalizability Across Task Types:** The framework's consistent performance across multi-image, multi-frame, and single-image tasks, including outperforming specialized video models and achieving strong results on tasks outside its direct training data, highlights its robustness and adaptability. This suggests a more unified approach to visual understanding, reducing the need for highly specialized models for different visual input formats.
- **Novel Data Generation and Training Strategies:** The Retrieval-Based Tree Sampling strategy provides an effective method for generating diverse, high-quality reasoning trajectories, which is essential for bootstrapping complex reasoning models. Furthermore, the two-stage reinforcement learning paradigm, incorporating Diversity-Preserving Strategy and annealed DAPO, offers a robust solution to the common problem of entropy collapse in RL, promoting sustained exploration and generalization.
- **Interpretability and Alignment with Human Cognition:** By explicitly decomposing reasoning into meta-actions (Global, Focus, Hint, Think, Answer), CINEMA moves towards more interpretable AI systems. This human-inspired design could foster greater trust and facilitate debugging or refinement of reasoning processes, as the model's "thought process" becomes more transparent.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays a foundation for future research in several directions, including further exploring the neural mechanisms underlying these meta-actions, integrating more complex human cognitive biases or strategies, and scaling the framework to even larger and more diverse datasets. It opens avenues for developing more robust, adaptable, and general-purpose multimodal AI.

Overall, CINEMA represents a step towards more robust and human-like multimodal reasoning systems, paving the way for advanced AI applications that can better understand and interact with the visually rich information in our world.
