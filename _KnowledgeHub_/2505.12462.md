# Model-Free Robust Average-Reward Reinforcement Learning with Sample Complexity Analysis

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Central Florida present a model-free Robust Halpern Iteration (RHI) algorithm for average-reward reinforcement learning in uncertain environments. This approach achieves finite-sample complexity guarantees for an ε-optimal robust policy, attaining bounds that are asymptotically comparable to model-based methods for various uncertainty sets, including KL and χ² divergences.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies often degrade when deployed in real-world environments due to discrepancies between training simulations and reality (the 'Sim-to-Real gap').
- Robust average-reward RL, critical for long-term, steady-state performance in continuous applications, presents greater analytical and algorithmic challenges than its discounted counterpart, with prior model-free methods lacking finite-sample guarantees.
- The robust average-reward Bellman equation involves double unknown variables (Q-value function and optimal average reward), a non-expansive operator, and non-linear 'worst-case expectation' terms that lead to biased estimations from samples.

## Method

- The Robust Halpern Iteration (RHI) algorithm is introduced, which operates in a quotient space to handle the dual unknown variables and uses Halpern iteration to ensure convergence for the non-expansive robust Bellman operator.
- A K-order multi-level Monte-Carlo (MLMC) estimator is developed to serve as a black-box sampling oracle, effectively reducing bias in estimating the non-linear robust Bellman operator by canceling out lower-order bias terms.
- A Parameter-Free RHI (PF-RHI) variant is proposed, utilizing a doubling trick for iteration horizons to eliminate the need for prior knowledge of intrinsic problem parameters like the robust optimal bias span (H).

## Results

- The RHI algorithm, combined with the K-order MLMC estimator, achieves an ε-optimal robust policy with a total sample complexity of Õ(SAH²ε^-(2+o(1))) for KL and χ² divergence uncertainty sets.
- For uncertainty sets defined by ℓp-norm or contamination models, RHI obtains a tighter sample complexity of Õ(SAH²ε⁻²), as simpler Monte-Carlo estimation suffices.
- The derived sample complexities asymptotically match state-of-the-art bounds from model-based robust average-reward RL methods, demonstrating the efficiency of the proposed model-free approach, and empirical validations confirm convergence to the optimal robust average reward.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Addressing the dual unknown variables (Q-value and average reward) in robust average-reward RL can be effectively managed by operating within a quotient space, abstracting away constant shifts.
> - Halpern iteration provides a robust and convergent mechanism for non-expansive operators, which are characteristic of robust Bellman updates, even when standard contraction-based analyses are not applicable.
> - Systematic bias reduction in sample-based estimation through K-order MLMC is crucial for achieving tight finite-sample complexity bounds in model-free robust average-reward settings, particularly for complex uncertainty sets like KL and χ² divergences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents often face performance degradation when deployed in real-world environments that differ from their training simulations, a phenomenon referred to as the "Sim-to-Real gap" or "model mismatch." Distributionally Robust Reinforcement Learning (RL) addresses this by considering an uncertainty set of plausible environmental dynamics and optimizing for worst-case performance within this set, aiming for policies with guaranteed resilience.

Within robust RL, two primary reward criteria exist: discounted-reward and average-reward. While discounted-reward RL is widely studied, it can lead to short-sighted policies due to its exponential weighting of immediate rewards. The average-reward criterion, conversely, focuses on long-term, steady-state performance, which is critical for continuous real-world applications such as queuing control or portfolio optimization.

Robust RL under the average-reward criterion presents greater analytical and algorithmic challenges compared to its discounted counterpart, including the non-contractive nature of its Bellman operator, high-dimensional solution spaces, and instability of standard iterative algorithms. Prior work in robust average-reward RL has primarily been model-based or offered only asymptotic convergence guarantees, leaving a gap in the understanding of finite-sample properties for data-driven, model-free approaches. Some methods attempt to reduce the average-reward problem to a discounted one, but these can be suboptimal or require prior knowledge of problem parameters. Other direct methods often assume strong structural properties like irreducibility to induce contraction.

This research aims to bridge this gap by developing a model-free algorithm for robust average-reward RL with finite-sample complexity guarantees. It focuses on addressing the specific challenges of the robust average-reward setting without relying on problematic reductions or strong structural assumptions beyond irreducibility. The work also distinguishes itself from prior MLMC-based robust RL studies by specifically addressing bias reduction to achieve tighter sample complexity, particularly in the average-reward context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are:

1. **Address the Sim-to-Real Gap and Model Mismatch**: Real-world deployment of RL policies is vulnerable to discrepancies between training and operational environments. Robust RL offers a principled way to mitigate this by designing policies that perform well even under adverse environmental variations within a defined uncertainty set.
2. **Focus on Long-Term Decision-Making with Average-Reward Criterion**: Many practical applications require policies optimized for sustained, steady-state performance rather than short-term gains. The average-reward criterion is suited for such scenarios, but robust average-reward RL is significantly more challenging and less studied than its discounted counterpart.
3. **Develop a Model-Free Algorithm**: Existing robust average-reward RL studies are predominantly model-based (planning-based) or provide only asymptotic convergence guarantees. There is a need for data-driven, model-free algorithms that can learn optimal robust policies without explicit knowledge of the environment's transition dynamics, which is crucial for practical deployment where models might be unavailable or complex.
4. **Derive Finite-Sample Complexity Guarantees**: A critical limitation of prior work is the lack of finite-sample analysis for model-free robust average-reward RL. This research aims to provide rigorous bounds on the number of samples required to achieve an ε-optimal robust policy, moving beyond asymptotic guarantees to offer a principled understanding of data efficiency.
5. **Overcome Algorithmic Challenges of Robust Average-Reward RL**: The robust Bellman equation for average rewards presents several difficulties:
 - **Double Unknown Variables**: The equation involves both the Q-value function and the optimal average reward, both of which are unknown.
 - **Non-Contractive Operator**: The robust Bellman operator is generally non-expansive, not contractive, making standard iterative methods unstable or slow to converge.
 - **Non-Linearity and Biased Estimation**: The robust Bellman operator is nonlinear, making direct estimation from samples biased (an "off-dynamic learning" problem), unlike the linear operators in non-robust settings.
6. **Improve Estimation Accuracy and Sample Efficiency**: Existing Monte-Carlo estimators for robust objectives can suffer from significant bias. The motivation includes designing a more accurate estimator that reduces bias to achieve tighter overall sample complexity, particularly for common uncertainty sets like KL and χ² divergences.
7. **Achieve Near-Optimal Sample Complexity**: The research seeks to develop an algorithm whose sample complexity asymptotically matches the best known theoretical bounds from model-based approaches, demonstrating the efficiency of the proposed model-free solution.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This paper introduces the Robust Halpern Iteration (RHI), a model-free algorithm designed to solve robust average-reward Markov Decision Processes (RAMDPs) with finite-sample guarantees.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The work considers Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) with a state space S and an action space A. Unlike standard MDPs, robust MDPs operate within a designated uncertainty set P of transition kernels, aiming to optimize performance for the worst-case model within this set. The paper focuses on (s,a)-rectangular uncertainty sets, where P is defined independently for each state-action pair, typically through a distribution divergence such as KL or χ² divergence with a given radius R around a nominal kernel P. The objective is to find a policy that maximizes the robust average reward, defined as the minimum average reward over all possible transition kernels in the uncertainty set. The problem is set in the generative model setting, where a learner has access to a simulator that can generate i.i.d. samples under any state-action pair according to the nominal kernel. The goal is to determine the sample complexity required to identify an ε-optimal robust policy. The analysis assumes an irreducible setting for the induced MDPs, a standard condition for the well-definedness and convergence of average-reward algorithms.

#### 3.2. Challenges in Robust Average-Reward RL

The authors identify three main challenges in solving the robust average-reward Bellman equation:
1. **Curse of Dual Variables**: The robust Bellman equation (Q = r - g + σ_P(Q_max)) involves two unknowns: the Q-value function (Q) and the optimal average reward (g*).
2. **Contraction-Free Analysis**: The robust Bellman operator (T_P) is generally non-expansive, not contractive, in the span semi-norm, which invalidates many standard convergence analyses for iterative methods.
3. **Non-Linearity and Off-Dynamic Learning**: The robust Bellman operator (T_P) includes a nonlinear "worst-case expectation" term (σ_s,a(Q_max)). Estimating this term accurately from samples generated under the *nominal* kernel introduces bias, a challenge known as off-dynamic learning, which is not present in non-robust or certain discounted robust settings.

#### 3.3. Robust Halpern Iteration (RHI)

To address these challenges, the paper proposes the Robust Halpern Iteration (RHI) algorithm, drawing inspiration from Halpern iteration in optimization.
- **Quotient Space**: To handle the dual variables (Q, g*), the algorithm operates in a quotient space. It aims to find a Q such that T_P(Q) - Q = ce for some constant c and an all-ones vector e. This is achieved by working with equivalence classes of Q-functions that differ only by a constant shift, effectively removing the dependence on the unknown g*.
- **Halpern Iteration for Non-Expansive Operators**: For the non-expansive operator T_P (in the quotient space), RHI updates Q_k+1 = (1 - β_k+1)Q_0 + β_k+1T_P(Q_k) with specific step sizes β_k = k/(k+2). This iteration scheme is known to converge for non-expansive operators to a fixed point.
- **Black-Box R-SAMPLE Oracle**: To deal with the non-linearity and sample-based estimation, RHI assumes access to a "black-box" sampling oracle called R-SAMPLE (Assumption 4.3). This oracle is designed to estimate the difference between two robust support functions, D_k(s,a) = F_s,a(h_k; ω_k,j) - F_s,a(h_k-1; ω_k,j), where h_k = Q_k,max. The oracle is assumed to be span-Lipschitz (error bounded by L_rs * sp(h - h')), provide unbiased increments for a "reference" robust Bellman operator T (which approximates the true T_P), have a uniform bias b_rs between T and T_P, and incur a fixed sample cost C_rs per call. RHI then uses these estimated differences to update T_k = T_k-1 + D_k.

#### 3.4. K-Order Multi-Level Monte-Carlo (MLMC) Estimator

To concretely design the black-box R-SAMPLE oracle, the authors propose a K-order multi-level Monte-Carlo (MLMC) estimator.
- **Moment Representation**: The robust support function σ_s,a(h) (or its dual objective) is typically a smooth functional of moments of h under the nominal kernel (e.g., E_p[e^(-h(S')/α)] for KL divergence or E_p[h-μ] - √σ Var_p(h-μ) for χ² divergence).
- **Bias Reduction**: Standard single-level Monte-Carlo (plug-in) estimators incur an O(1/N) bias. The K-order MLMC explicitly constructs a linear combination of estimators from different sample sizes (N_ℓ = 2^ℓ * N_0 for ℓ=0...,K). The weights for this linear combination are chosen to cancel out the first K terms of the bias expansion (O(1/N), O(1/N²)..., O(1/N^K)). This reduces the residual bias to O(f(K)/N_0^(K+1)), where f(K) grows factorially with K.
- **Comparison to Prior Methods**: This K-order approach is differentiated from previous MLMC methods (standard MLMC, truncated MLMC) used in robust RL, which typically only achieve an O(1/N) bias and lead to higher sample complexities.
- **Instantiation for Uncertainty Sets**:
 - **KL Divergence**: The KL support function involves a supremum over a dual parameter α. The method discretizes this α-range into a finite grid. For each α on the grid, the K-order MLMC is applied to estimate the dual objective g_s,a(α,h), where the moment function is e^(-h(S')/α). The maximum over the grid then forms the estimator. This introduces a small discretization bias in addition to the MLMC bias.
 - **χ² Divergence**: Similar to KL, the χ² support function has a dual representation involving a maximization over a parameter μ. The K-order MLMC is applied to estimate this dual objective.
 - **ℓp-norm and Contamination Models**: For these specific uncertainty sets, the robust Bellman operator has closed-form solutions that allow for simpler (often linear) estimations. The authors state that for these models, standard Monte-Carlo methods without the K-order MLMC bias reduction are sufficient to achieve the desired sample complexity, as the bias is not as problematic.

#### 3.5. Parameter-Free RHI (PF-RHI)

A parameter-free variant (PF-RHI) is proposed to make the algorithm fully implementable without requiring prior knowledge of the optimal robust bias span H. It uses a "doubling trick" where RHI is run for increasing horizons (n_i = 2^i) until the Bellman residual (sp(T_ni - Q_ni)) is sufficiently small, which serves as a stopping criterion. This allows PF-RHI to maintain the same sample complexity as RHI without needing to tune the iteration number based on H.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings and results regarding model-free robust average-reward reinforcement learning:

1. **Robust Halpern Iteration (RHI) Algorithm**: The paper introduces RHI, a novel model-free meta-algorithm for robust average-reward RL. It effectively addresses the challenges of double unknown variables (Q and g*), non-expansive operators, and the non-linearity of the robust Bellman operator by combining Halpern iteration with a quotient space formulation and a black-box sampling oracle.
2. **K-Order Multi-Level Monte-Carlo (MLMC) Estimator**: A K-order MLMC estimator is developed as a concrete design for the black-box R-SAMPLE oracle. This estimator significantly reduces bias compared to prior MLMC approaches by actively canceling out lower-order bias terms in the Taylor expansion of the robust support function, achieving a bias of Õ(f(K)/N₀^(K+1)).
3. **Finite-Sample Complexity for KL and χ² Divergence Sets**:
 - For uncertainty sets defined by KL and χ² divergences, the RHI algorithm, when combined with the K-order MLMC estimator, achieves an ε-optimal robust policy with a total sample complexity of **Õ(SAH²ε^-(2+o(1)))**.
 - Here, S is the number of states, A is the number of actions, H is the robust optimal bias span, and ε is the desired accuracy.
 - The term "o(1)" represents a sub-polynomial penalty that asymptotically vanishes as ε approaches zero (specifically, proportional to c/√log(1/ε)), indicating a near-optimal rate.
4. **Finite-Sample Complexity for ℓp-norm and Contamination Sets**:
 - For uncertainty sets defined by ℓp-norm or contamination models, where the robust support function has a more favorable structure (e.g., linear expectation terms), the RHI algorithm achieves an ε-optimal policy with a sample complexity of **Õ(SAH²ε⁻²)**.
 - This tighter bound is attributed to the fact that for these models, a simpler (linear) Monte-Carlo estimation without the need for K-order bias cancellation is sufficient, as the bias issues are less severe.
5. **Asymptotic Optimality**: The derived sample complexity of Õ(SAH²ε⁻²) and Õ(SAH²ε^-(2+o(1))) for different uncertainty sets asymptotically matches the state-of-the-art complexity bounds found in model-based robust average-reward RL methods. This suggests that the proposed model-free algorithm is as sample-efficient as its model-based counterparts in the asymptotic regime.
6. **Parameter-Free Implementation (PF-RHI)**: A variant of RHI, PF-RHI, is introduced, which does not require any prior knowledge of problem parameters, such as the optimal robust bias span H. It achieves the same sample complexity by employing a doubling trick for iteration horizons.
7. **Empirical Validation**: Numerical experiments on randomly generated Garnet problems for contamination, ℓ∞-norm, and ℓ2-norm models demonstrate that the RHI algorithm effectively converges to the optimal robust average reward, corroborating the theoretical findings. The K-order MLMC estimator is also numerically shown to reduce bias compared to standard MLMC.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of reinforcement learning by advancing the understanding and practical implementation of robust decision-making in long-term, uncertain environments.

1. **Bridging the Model-Free Gap in Robust Average-Reward RL**: The introduction of RHI as a model-free algorithm with finite-sample complexity guarantees for robust average-reward RL addresses a significant gap in the literature. Previous work was predominantly model-based or offered only asymptotic guarantees, limiting practical deployment. This work provides a concrete, data-driven framework.
2. **Enhanced Reliability and Generalizability**: By focusing on robust average-reward learning, the proposed policies are designed to maintain performance over extended periods, even when environment dynamics deviate from the training model. This directly addresses the critical need for reliable RL systems in real-world applications (e.g., robotics, finance, energy management) where model mismatch is prevalent.
3. **Near-Optimal Sample Efficiency**: The achieved sample complexity of Õ(SAH²ε⁻²) for certain uncertainty sets and Õ(SAH²ε^-(2+o(1))) for others is asymptotically competitive with the best-known model-based results. This demonstrates that principled model-free approaches can achieve high data efficiency, making robust average-reward RL more viable in data-limited scenarios.
4. **Novel Bias Reduction Technique**: The K-order multi-level Monte-Carlo estimator represents a significant technical contribution for estimating nonlinear robust Bellman operators. Its ability to systematically reduce bias through tailored weighting schemes provides a pathway for achieving tighter sample complexity bounds in robust learning tasks beyond this specific context.
5. **Practical Implementability**: The RHI algorithm is designed to be model-free, eliminating the need for explicit environment models. Furthermore, the development of a Parameter-Free RHI (PF-RHI) variant removes the requirement for prior knowledge of intrinsic MDP parameters (like the robust optimal span H), making the algorithm more universally applicable and easier to deploy in diverse and unknown problem settings.
6. **Foundation for Future Research**: The work lays a theoretical foundation for further advancements in robust average-reward RL. The identified limitations (e.g., reliance on a generative model, the o(1) gap for KL/χ² divergence) highlight specific avenues for future research, such as extending the framework to online (exploration-exploitation) or offline (dataset-driven) settings, and completely eliminating sub-polynomial penalties in estimation.

In summary, this paper provides a robust, sample-efficient, and implementable framework for long-term decision-making under uncertainty, offering a significant step towards deploying more reliable RL systems in complex real-world environments.
