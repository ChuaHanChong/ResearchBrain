# Normalizing Flow-Enhanced Message Passing for Multirobot Collaborative Localization

> [!summary] Summary
> A Normalizing Flow-enhanced Message Passing (MP-NF) algorithm unifies Gaussian Belief Propagation and Mean-Field approximation for robust, adaptive, and distributed multirobot collaborative localization. By leveraging Normalizing Flows within gradient estimators, it efficiently handles nonlinear measurements and uncertain noise, achieving superior accuracy and robustness in simulations and real-world autonomous surface vehicle experiments compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Traditional multirobot localization methods struggle to adapt to uncertain or time-varying noise statistics and non-Gaussian measurement models due to their reliance on predefined distribution updates.
- Existing message passing algorithms lack a principled mechanism to integrate closed-form updates for conjugate terms with gradient estimators for non-conjugate terms, especially for nonlinearities.
- Efficient and flexible Monte Carlo sampling for gradient estimation in complex, high-dimensional distributions is a challenge, particularly when dealing with robotic states defined on Lie groups rather than Euclidean spaces.

## Method

- A parametric ==Message Passing== framework unifies ==Gaussian Belief Propagation (BP)== for state dependencies and ==Mean-Field (MF) approximation== for noise statistics, optimizing natural parameters of exponential family distributions.
- ==Normalizing Flow (Real NVP)==-based gradient estimators are integrated and trained end-to-end, providing flexible and efficient sampling to evaluate expectations for non-conjugate, nonlinear measurement models.
- The **MP-NF** algorithm is extended to Lie group state spaces using a ==lift-solve-retract== scheme for accurate robotic pose estimation, fusing odometry (with adaptive noise), GNSS, and UWB ranging (modeled with robust ==Gaussian-Student's t Mixture== noise) in a distributed manner.

## Results

- MP-NF achieved the highest estimation accuracy (lowest **RMSE**) across Euclidean and Lie group simulations, outperforming centralized GTSAM and other MP variants, while demonstrating robustness to UWB ranging outliers.
- NF-enhanced methods (MP-NF, GBP-NF) converged to high accuracy within a small number of iterations (e.g., **5 iterations**), significantly faster than non-NF counterparts.
- Field experiments with Autonomous Surface Vehicles confirmed strong real-world performance, with NF parameters trained solely on simulation data generalizing well to physical systems and maintaining robustness against outliers and measurement loss.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unifying BP and MF approximation in a parametric message passing framework effectively combines the preservation of dependencies among robot states with adaptive inference of uncertain noise statistics.
> - Normalizing Flows can significantly improve the efficiency and accuracy of gradient estimators for nonlinear models in state estimation by learning expressive proposal distributions via end-to-end training.
> - The proposed framework effectively manages uncertain and time-varying noise, and outliers by jointly inferring state and noise parameters, leading to improved robustness over methods with fixed noise assumptions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Accurate, robust, and adaptive robot localization constitutes a foundational requirement for autonomous systems, supporting various downstream tasks such as motion planning and control. The development of multirobot systems has led to an increased interest in collaborative localization, which leverages both onboard and inter-robot relative measurements to enhance localization capabilities.

Backend state estimation for collaborative localization typically employs either optimization-based or probabilistic inference-based paradigms. Optimization-based methods include distributed pose graph optimization and factor graph optimization, which can accommodate various robot states and sensor biases. Probabilistic inference-based methods, such as Belief Propagation (BP) and Mean-Field Variational Inference (MFVI), offer distributed solutions, with MFVI manifesting as Variational Message Passing (VMP).

BP and MF approximation possess distinct characteristics. MFVI simplifies updates by assuming independence among latent variables, making it suitable for conjugate exponential family models, but potentially sacrificing accuracy. BP, particularly its Gaussian variant (GBP), often provides more accurate approximations by preserving dependencies, though it can become intractable for non-Gaussian models or complex factor graphs. Previous work has extended GBP to Lie groups and enhanced its robustness with mechanisms like dynamic covariance scaling (DCS), but these often exhibit limited adaptability to uncertain or time-varying noise statistics. Conversely, MF approximation's ability to jointly infer states and noise statistics has been explored in single-robot state estimation, incorporating models like inverse Gamma, inverse Wishart, and Gaussian-Student’s t mixture (GSTM) to improve adaptability and robustness. However, applying MFVI to collaborative localization often entails an independence assumption among robot states, which can degrade accuracy.

To harness the complementary strengths of BP and MF, unified message passing (MP) frameworks have been proposed, where factor nodes or variable nodes are partitioned into BP and MF parts. These frameworks aim to balance the accuracy of BP with the adaptability of MF. However, a limitation in many traditional MP algorithms is their optimization over a predefined set of candidate distributions, rather than direct optimization in the parameter space, which restricts their integration with modern gradient estimators.

Parametric inference methods, such as stochastic variational inference (VI) and natural gradient descent (NGD), address this by directly optimizing distribution parameters. NGD, for instance, has been extended to Kalman filters to handle nonlinear dynamics and measurement models through Monte Carlo gradient estimators. This capability, integrating closed-form updates for conjugate terms with gradient estimators for non-conjugate terms, has been largely absent in existing BP and unified MP algorithms, representing a gap in the literature.

Gradient estimators, which compute the gradient of an expectation with respect to distribution parameters, are central to parametric inference. While various types exist (score function, pathwise, measure-valued), they rely on Monte Carlo sampling. The advent of deep learning has introduced neural network-enhanced sampling, such as Normalizing Flows (NFs), which offer expressive and flexible density models for efficient sampling. The integration of NFs into gradient estimators for state estimation, especially within message passing frameworks, remains an area with limited exploration. This research aims to bridge these gaps by developing an NF-enhanced MP algorithm for multirobot collaborative localization.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an accurate, robust, and adaptive message passing (MP) algorithm for distributed multirobot collaborative localization. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations:

1. **Develop a Parametric MP Framework with Gradient Estimators**:
 - **Motivation**: Traditional message passing algorithms typically optimize over a predefined set of candidate distributions, making it challenging to explicitly handle non-conjugate terms arising from nonlinear measurement models. Existing BP algorithms, particularly in their unified forms with MF, lack principled mechanisms to integrate closed-form updates for conjugate terms with gradient estimators for non-conjugate ones.
 - **Objective**: To establish a parametric MP framework that unifies Gaussian Belief Propagation (GBP) and Mean-Field (MF) approximation. This framework aims to directly optimize the natural parameters of exponential family candidate distributions. This approach is intended to preserve dependencies among robot states (via GBP) while enabling the estimation of noise statistics for improved robustness and adaptability (via MF). A core component of this framework is the explicit treatment of non-conjugate terms from nonlinear models using gradient estimators. This parameterization also seeks compatibility with modern optimization and learning tools.

2. **Design Normalizing Flow (NF)-based Gradient Estimators with End-to-End Training**:
 - **Motivation**: Gradient estimators, crucial for handling nonlinearities in parametric inference, rely on Monte Carlo sampling. The efficiency and accuracy of these estimators are significantly influenced by the choice of proposal distribution. Traditional sampling methods may not be sufficiently flexible or efficient for complex, high-dimensional distributions found in robotic state estimation.
 - **Objective**: To develop a Normalizing Flow (NF)-based gradient estimator that enables flexible and learnable sampling. By integrating NFs into the MP algorithm, the aim is to leverage their ability to construct expressive and adaptable proposal distributions. Furthermore, the objective includes enabling end-to-end training, allowing NF parameters to be optimized based on the overall performance of the MP algorithm, thereby improving estimation accuracy and reducing the number of iterations required for convergence.

3. **Extend to Lie Group State Spaces for Real-World Applications**:
 - **Motivation**: Robotic states frequently involve rotations (attitudes) which are naturally defined on Lie groups rather than Euclidean spaces. Ignoring the manifold structure of these states can lead to suboptimal or inconsistent estimation.
 - **Objective**: To extend the proposed NF-enhanced MP algorithm to support states defined on Lie groups. This adaptation aims to enhance the algorithm's suitability for practical robotic applications where states involve rotations, such as the pose estimation of autonomous vehicles.

4. **Application to Multirobot Collaborative Localization**:
 - **Motivation**: Demonstrate the practical efficacy of the proposed framework in a relevant and challenging robotics task.
 - **Objective**: To apply the developed algorithm to a multirobot collaborative localization task. This involves fusing various sensor measurements, specifically odometry, Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS) data, and inter-robot Ultra-Wideband (UWB) ranging measurements, in a distributed manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed approach involves an NF-enhanced Message Passing (MP) algorithm for distributed multirobot collaborative localization.

**Overall Framework:**
The method begins by constructing a factor graph based on robot states, measurements (odometry, GNSS, UWB ranging), and assumed noise models. Variables in the factor graph are partitioned into a Belief Propagation (BP) part for robot states and a Mean-Field (MF) part for noise statistics. The algorithm iteratively updates messages and beliefs. A key innovation is the use of Normalizing Flows (NFs) within gradient estimators, which are trained end-to-end based on the MP algorithm's performance. The framework is extended to Lie group state spaces for robotic pose estimation.

**Parametric MP Framework:**
1. **Variable Partitioning**: Latent variables are divided into state variables (e.g., robot poses) assigned to the BP part ($x_i \in I_{BP}$) and noise parameters (e.g., noise covariances, mixing coefficients) assigned to the MF part ($\phi_i \in I_{MF}$).
2. **Bethe Free Energy Minimization**: The inference problem is formulated as minimizing the Bethe free energy, which approximates the true posterior. Factorization constraints are introduced for MF variables, while marginalization constraints maintain dependencies among BP variables.
3. **Exponential Family Beliefs**: Beliefs for $x_i$ and factors connecting BP variables ($b_a(x_a)$) are restricted to Gaussian distributions. Beliefs for $\phi_i$ are chosen from exponential family distributions that are conjugate to the noise models (e.g., Inverse Wishart for Gaussian covariance, Beta for Bernoulli mixing coefficient, Gamma for auxiliary variables in Student's t distribution).
4. **Parameter Space Optimization**: Instead of optimizing beliefs directly, the algorithm optimizes over the natural parameters of the exponential family distributions. This approach formulates the problem in the expectation parameter space, enabling a separation of conjugate and non-conjugate terms.
5. **Message Passing Equations**: Fixed-point iteration equations for natural parameters and messages are derived by taking gradients of a Lagrangian function, which incorporates the Bethe free energy and marginalization constraints. These equations define how messages are passed between variable nodes and factor nodes, and how beliefs are updated.

**Gradient Estimators:**
The core challenge in handling nonlinear measurement models is evaluating gradients of expectations. The paper proposes three types of gradient estimators for computing $(\nabla_{\mu_a} U_a)^{(l)}$ in the message passing updates:

1. **Linearization**: For nonlinear residual models $r_a(x_a)$, a first-order Taylor expansion is applied around the current mean estimate. This deterministic approach approximates the expected values by evaluating functions at the mean and using Jacobians.
2. **Sampling**: Monte Carlo integration is employed. The reparameterization trick is used to sample from a standard Gaussian and transform these samples, enabling the gradient to pass through the sampling process. A Gauss-Newton approximation is applied by neglecting second-order derivatives (Hessians) of the residual function.
3. **NF-based (Normalizing Flow)**: This method enhances sampling by using importance sampling with a Normalizing Flow (specifically Real NVP) as a learnable proposal distribution. The NF defines a bijective transformation from a simple base distribution (e.g., standard Gaussian) to a more expressive distribution. This allows for more efficient sampling in complex or highly nonlinear regions of the posterior. The NF parameters are part of the end-to-end training.

**Extension to Lie Groups:**
For robot states involving rotations (e.g., SO(3)), a "lift-solve-retract" scheme is adopted.
1. **Lift**: Initial estimates of states ($X_i$) are used as tangent points. Error states ($\delta x_i$) in the tangent Euclidean space are defined.
2. **Solve**: The MP algorithm (Algorithm 1) is executed on these Euclidean error states.
3. **Retract**: After inferring beliefs of error states, the estimates are retracted back to the Lie group manifold using appropriate exponential maps and right Jacobians to update the pose and covariance.

**Multirobot Collaborative Localization Application:**
1. **Measurement Models**:
 - **Odometry**: Relative motion between consecutive poses of a single robot, modeled with Gaussian noise. To enhance adaptability, the odometry noise covariance is treated as a latent variable with an Inverse Wishart prior, whose parameters are adaptively updated using a forgetting factor.
 - **GNSS**: Absolute position measurements, modeled with Gaussian noise.
 - **UWB Ranging**: Inter-robot distance measurements. Due to potential non-line-of-sight and multipath, the noise is modeled by a Gaussian-Student's t Mixture (GSTM) to achieve robustness to heavy-tailed outliers. This involves auxiliary latent variables (binary indicator $y$ for component selection, Gamma-distributed $\xi$ for Student's t).
2. **Factor Graph Structure**: The factor graph includes factors for odometry, GNSS, UWB, and prior states, as well as factors for the various noise parameters (Inverse Wishart prior for odometry covariance, Beta prior for GSTM mixing coefficient, Gamma prior for GSTM auxiliary variable, Bernoulli for GSTM indicator).
3. **Specific Gradient Computations**: Detailed expressions are provided for messages relating to noise parameters, such as those from the odometry factor to its covariance, or UWB factors to GSTM parameters (mixing coefficient, auxiliary variable, indicator). These involve expectations over relevant beliefs.
4. **Implementation Details**:
 - **Real NVP Features**: The Real NVP model's transformation is conditioned on a feature vector, including relative state estimates, measurements, and covariance features (logarithm of standard deviations and Fisher z-transform of correlation coefficients).
 - **Real NVP Architecture**: Consists of multiple affine coupling layers, with MLPs for scale and translation functions, activated by ReLU.
 - **End-to-End Training**: NF parameters are trained using truncated backpropagation through time, minimizing a loss function based on the squared error between estimated and ground-truth states. A monotonically increasing weight emphasizes later MP iterations.
 - **Distributed Computation**: States are allocated to their respective robots, and factors/noise parameters to the robots acquiring measurements, enabling local message and belief updates and decentralized message exchange.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of the proposed Normalizing Flow-enhanced Message Passing (MP-NF) algorithm was evaluated through extensive simulations in both Euclidean and Lie group spaces, and validated with field experiments using Autonomous Surface Vehicles (ASVs). Comparisons were made against various algorithms including GTSAM (centralized factor graph optimizer), MFVI, GBP-L (linearization), GBP-S (sampling), GBP-NF (NF-enhanced GBP), GBP-DCS (GBP with Dynamic Covariance Scaling), and MP-L/MP-S (linearization/sampling-based MP).

**Euclidean Space Simulation Verification:**
- **Accuracy and Robustness**: MP-NF achieved the highest estimation accuracy, with a lower Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) compared to all other methods, including the centralized GTSAM. It demonstrated robustness to UWB ranging outliers, unlike GBP-L and GBP-S, which showed marked increases in error during outlier events. GBP-NF also exhibited improved accuracy.
- **Adaptability**: Algorithms incorporating Mean-Field (MF) components (MP-L, MP-S, MP-NF) showed superior adaptability to uncertain or time-varying noise covariances, performing better than GBP-DCS, GBP-DCS-n, and GBP-NF, which lack direct noise parameter inference. MP-NF maintained the lowest errors across varying noise levels.
- **Efficiency**: NF-enhanced methods (GBP-NF, MP-NF) reached high accuracy within a small number of iterations (e.g., 5 iterations), outperforming non-NF methods that required significantly more iterations to converge to a higher error plateau. While having slightly higher per-iteration runtime than some counterparts, the faster convergence made them effective.

**Lie Group Simulation Verification:**
- **Translational Accuracy**: MP-NF demonstrated the highest translational accuracy (lowest RMSEt) among all algorithms, consistent with the Euclidean space findings. It effectively handled ranging outliers and uncertain noise statistics.
- **Rotational Accuracy**: Rotational errors (RMSEr) were generally comparable across most algorithms, with MP-NF exhibiting competitive performance. The maximum discrepancy in rotational errors was below 0.25 degrees.
- **Impact of Robot Number**: For MP-NF and GTSAM, estimation error decreased with an increasing number of robots, confirming the benefits of collaborative localization. In contrast, GBP-L's error increased with more robots, indicating its lack of robustness to outliers in relative measurements, which become more frequent with more interacting robots.
- **Generalization to New Models**: When tested with a different nonlinear UWB ranging model (logarithmic), MP-NF continued to show improved estimation accuracy and robustness, indicating the method's generalization capabilities beyond specific nonlinear functions.

**ASV Field Dataset Verification:**
- **Real-World Performance**: In field experiments with Autonomous Surface Vehicles, MP-NF consistently provided strong performance.
- **Generalization of Trained NFs**: NF parameters trained solely on simulation data (GBP-NF-SW, MP-NF-SW) performed comparably to those fine-tuned on experimental data (GBP-NF, MP-NF). This suggests the proposed methods have robust generalization capabilities, allowing for rapid deployment on physical systems even with simulation-only training.
- **Robustness to Outliers**: Across various datasets, algorithms without robust design (e.g., GBP-L) exhibited significantly larger estimation errors in the presence of UWB ranging outliers, highlighting the importance of robust inference. MP-NF maintained closer alignment with ground truth trajectories even during periods of pronounced ranging errors.
- **Efficiency in Practice**: MP-NF achieved high estimation accuracy within a few iterations, similar to simulation results.
- **Resilience to Measurement Loss**: MP-NF generally maintained higher estimation accuracy than GTSAM and GBP-L as UWB measurement loss rates increased. GBP-L's error sometimes decreased with increased loss, implying that unreliable measurements can degrade non-robust systems. This further demonstrated the generalization capability of MP-NF to varying factor graph structures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and implications:

1. **Advanced Parametric Inference Framework**: The proposed MP algorithm establishes a novel parametric framework that effectively unifies Gaussian Belief Propagation (GBP) and Mean-Field (MF) approximation. By directly optimizing over the natural parameters of exponential family distributions, it offers a principled way to integrate closed-form updates for conjugate terms with gradient estimators for non-conjugate ones. This approach is a methodological advancement over traditional MP algorithms that often rely on predefined distribution updates, offering increased flexibility and compatibility with modern optimization and machine learning tools.

2. **Normalizing Flow-Enhanced Gradient Estimation**: The introduction of Normalizing Flow (NF)-based gradient estimators is a notable innovation in state estimation. NFs provide expressive and flexible density models for Monte Carlo sampling, which is crucial for handling nonlinear measurement models. Coupled with end-to-end training, this allows the NF parameters to be optimized in a task-specific manner, learning intricate data mappings that are difficult to define analytically. This enhancement leads to improved sampling efficiency, higher estimation accuracy, and reduced algorithm iterations.

3. **Enhanced Robustness and Adaptability**: The unification of GBP and MF, where MF is specifically leveraged to infer uncertain noise statistics (e.g., in odometry and UWB ranging), significantly enhances the algorithm's robustness to outliers and adaptability to time-varying or unknown noise characteristics. This addresses a common limitation of many localization systems that assume fixed or perfectly known noise models.

4. **Practical Applicability to Robotic Systems**: The extension of the MP algorithm to Lie group state spaces makes it directly applicable to real-world robotic tasks involving rotations, such as the pose estimation of autonomous surface vehicles. The experimental validation on ASV field datasets, including scenarios with complex environments and sensor interference, demonstrates the method's practical utility and generalization capabilities, even when trained primarily on simulation data.

5. **Distributed and Decentralized Collaborative Localization**: The design supports distributed computation and decentralized fusion, where robots exchange messages without relying on a central node. This makes the approach scalable and resilient for multirobot systems, reducing communication bottlenecks and single points of failure, which is crucial for various applications like environmental monitoring, search and rescue, and logistics.

**Potential Impact**:
The development of this NF-enhanced MP algorithm has the potential to impact various fields requiring accurate and robust multirobot localization. It could lead to more reliable autonomous navigation for ground, aerial, and marine robot swarms, particularly in GPS-denied or challenging environments where sensor data might be noisy or unreliable. The framework's flexibility also suggests potential for adaptation to other complex inference problems in robotics and beyond, where nonlinearities and uncertain dynamics are prevalent. Future work, as suggested by the authors, could explore tighter sensor fusion architectures and broader generalization of NF-enhanced gradient estimators, further extending the impact of this foundational research.
