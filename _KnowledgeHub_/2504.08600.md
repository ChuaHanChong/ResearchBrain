# SQL-R1: Training Natural Language to SQL Reasoning Model By Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> IDEA Research developed SQL-R1, an NL2SQL reasoning model trained with reinforcement learning, achieving 88.7% execution accuracy on Spider-Test and 66.6% on BIRD-Dev using a 7B parameter model. This approach generates explicit reasoning processes and enhances complex query generation and interpretability compared to traditional supervised fine-tuning methods.

## Problem

- Existing Natural Language to SQL (NL2SQL) models, primarily based on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) of Large Language Models (LLMs), struggle with complex database queries involving multi-table joins, nested queries, and ambiguous semantics.
- SFT-based models often exhibit instability in domain adaptation, limited generalization to new environments, and a lack of interpretability in their reasoning logic.
- The scarcity of high-quality, diverse real-world NL2SQL datasets poses a challenge for training robust models, especially for complex scenarios.

## Method

- **SQL-R1** introduces a novel NL2SQL reasoning model trained using Reinforcement Learning (RL) with the ==Group Relative Policy Optimization== (GRPO) algorithm, designed to directly optimize for accurate SQL generation.
- A progressive, four-component reward function (format, execution, result, and length rewards) guides the RL training, providing specific feedback for synthesizing correct and structured SQL queries along with explicit reasoning.
- The training leverages a large-scale synthetic dataset, SynSQL-2.5M, with subsets used for initial Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and targeted RL on complex queries, addressing data scarcity.

## Results

- SQL-R1 (Qwen2.5-Coder-**7B**) achieved **88.7%** execution accuracy on Spider-Test and **66.6%** on BIRD-Dev, surpassing many open-source and some closed-source LLM-based NL2SQL solutions.
- The model demonstrated superior generalization and robustness on Spider-DK (**79.3%**), Spider-Syn (**78.5%**), and Spider-Realistic (**86.2%**) datasets compared to other NL2SQL solutions.
- Analysis on BIRD-Dev showed that increased model capacity (from **7B** to **14B** parameters) paired with RL led to a substantial 5.5 percentage point accuracy increase for challenging queries, confirming RL's effectiveness for complex reasoning tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A carefully designed, multi-faceted reward function is critical for effectively guiding RL training in NL2SQL, with execution and result rewards being most impactful for ensuring correctness and alignment with user intent.
> - While an SFT "cold start" can be beneficial, its necessity for RL-based NL2SQL models is contingent on the quantity and provenance of the synthetic training data, suggesting flexibility in initial model preparation.
> - Synthetic data engineering, particularly for generating complex NL-SQL pairs, proves highly effective in significantly boosting the reasoning and generalization capabilities of RL-trained NL2SQL models, even with relatively small, targeted datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Natural Language to SQL (NL2SQL), also known as Text2SQL, is dedicated to enabling more intuitive interactions with databases by automatically converting natural language queries into structured SQL statements. This capability is crucial for enhancing human-computer interaction in database applications and streamlining data science analysis tasks, democratizing access to data for non-technical users.

Recent advancements in NL2SQL have been significantly driven by the emergence of Large Language Models (LLMs). Many state-of-the-art NL2SQL models primarily leverage Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) to adapt open-source LLMs or utilize powerful, but often opaque, closed-source LLMs like GPT-4. These methods have seen success in optimizing various workflow components, including schema linking, content retrieval, and SQL correction.

However, the authors identify significant persisting challenges. A major hurdle lies in the reasoning performance of NL2SQL systems in complex scenarios, such as those involving multi-table joins, nested queries, and ambiguous semantics. SFT-based approaches, while effective for simpler cases, often struggle with adaptability and interpretability in new or domain-specific environments (e.g., finance, healthcare) because they rely heavily on the fixed structure of database schemas and the scale of training data. This dependency can lead to instability in domain adaptation and generalization. Furthermore, the lack of interpretability in the reasoning logic of these SFT-trained models limits their deployment in high-stakes fields where transparency and explainability are paramount.

SQL-R1 addresses these limitations by venturing into the application of Reinforcement Learning (RL) for training NL2SQL reasoning models. This approach aligns with a recent trend in LLM research where RL has demonstrated significant potential in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs across diverse tasks, including financial reasoning, search engines, and mathematical problem-solving. Unlike SFT, which learns from static input-output pairs, RL allows LLMs to dynamically adjust their decision-making strategies through continuous interaction with an environment, receiving direct feedback on the correctness and quality of their outputs. By introducing an RL-based framework, SQL-R1 aims to overcome the inflexibility and opacity of SFT models, fostering a more robust, adaptive, and interpretable NL2SQL system capable of handling intricate database queries. This work represents a significant step towards leveraging the interactive and exploratory nature of RL to imbue LLMs with superior reasoning abilities for database interactions, moving beyond mere pattern recognition to more generalized and interpretable problem-solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enhance the reasoning performance of Natural Language to SQL (NL2SQL) models, particularly in complex situations involving multi-table joins and nested queries**, which current Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) methodologies struggle with regarding adaptability and interpretability in new domains.

To achieve this overarching goal, the authors propose SQL-R1, a novel NL2SQL reasoning model trained by reinforcement learning (RL) algorithms. Their motivation stems from the observed limitations of existing SFT-based approaches, which often lead to models that lack stability in domain adaptation, generalization, and interpretability, especially in high-risk application areas like finance and healthcare.

The paper outlines three critical questions that motivate and guide their research:

- **Q1: Can a specific reinforcement learning algorithm be designed for the NL2SQL task to successfully train a NL2SQL reasoning model?**
 - **Motivation**: Traditional SFT indirectly optimizes reasoning by focusing on token generation. RL, in contrast, can directly optimize for accurate SQL generation by providing feedback based on query intent. The challenge lies in designing effective feedback mechanisms (reward functions) that guide the model to explore diverse and correct SQL generation strategies.

- **Q2: Is a specific form of "cold start" (initial SFT) necessary for an RL-based NL2SQL reasoning model?**
 - **Motivation**: Base LLMs might lack initial instruction-following and NL2SQL generation capabilities. An effective cold start could strengthen these abilities, enabling the model to generate higher-quality SQL queries during RL exploration. Determining the optimal form of this cold start is a critical design choice.

- **Q3: Can sustainable data engineering be deployed for training robust and efficient NL2SQL reasoning models?**
 - **Motivation**: RL training, like most LLM training, requires high-quality training data, yet real-world NL2SQL datasets are often limited. Leveraging existing data engineering technologies, particularly synthetic data generation, is crucial for supporting model training, improving robustness, and enhancing generalization capabilities.

By addressing these questions, the authors aim to develop an NL2SQL model that not only achieves competitive accuracy but also demonstrates improved reasoning, adaptability, and interpretability, thereby broadening the practical applicability of NL2SQL systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SQL-R1 introduces a novel approach to NL2SQL reasoning by integrating reinforcement learning with a focus on enhancing complex query generation and interpretability.

#### 3.1. Overview

The core of SQL-R1 lies in training NL2SQL models via RL algorithms, which involves two main training paradigms:
1. **Direct Reinforcement Training**: Applying RL directly to a base model.
2. **Reinforcement Training via Cold Start**: First, a base model undergoes Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) as a "cold start" to imbue it with initial reasoning and instruction-following capabilities, followed by RL.
The entire training process is supported by high-quality synthetic data due to the scarcity of real-world datasets.

#### 3.2. Data Preparation

The research heavily relies on synthetic data:
- **Source**: The **SynSQL-2.5M** dataset is the primary source, a million-scale synthetic NL2SQL dataset containing over 2.5 million diverse samples, each with a database, natural language question, SQL query, and a Chain-of-Thought (CoT) solution. It covers over 16,000 synthetic databases and a wide range of SQL complexities.
- **Preprocessing**:
 - **SFT Dataset (SynSQL-200K)**: A subset of 200,000 samples from SynSQL-2.5M, balanced across different difficulty levels (50,000 samples per level), is used for SFT. Each sample includes the NL, reasoning process (within `<think>...</think>` tags), and SQL (within `<answer>...</answer>` tags). All ground truth SQL queries yield non-null results.
 - **RL Dataset (SynSQL-Complex-5K)**: 5,000 complex NL-SQL pairs are randomly sampled from SynSQL-2.5M for RL training. This dataset focuses on challenging queries to address model deficiencies in complex SQL generation. Importantly, CoT data is *not* included in the RL dataset input, as RL aims to optimize direct answer accuracy.

#### 3.3. Training

##### 3.3.1. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

SFT is performed on **Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct** models to enhance instruction adherence and initial NL2SQL generation ability. Two strategies for SFT cold start are explored:
1. **Raw instructions**: Focusing solely on SQL generation, leveraging the OmniSQL-7B checkpoint as a reference.
2. **Full fine-tuning and reasoning generation instructions**: Promoting the development of compliant thought processes alongside final SQL answers.

##### 3.3.2. Reinforcement Training

- **Algorithm**: The **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** algorithm is employed. GRPO is chosen for its efficiency (no value model required), lower memory footprint, and clear definition of reward targets, making it suitable for optimizing the NL2SQL policy model.
- **Process**: For each natural language question and database schema, the policy model generates a group of *G* SQL candidates. These candidates are then evaluated using a composite reward function, and GRPO uses their relative performance within the group to guide policy updates.

##### 3.3.3. Reward Function Design

A progressive, four-component feedback mechanism is designed for NL2SQL tasks:
1. **Format Reward ($S_f$)**: Assigns +1 if the output follows the specified format (reasoning in `<think>`, SQL in `<answer>`, SQL within ````sql...````); -1 otherwise. This encourages standardized output.
2. **Execution Reward ($S_e$)**: Assigns +2 if the SQL candidate is syntactically correct and executable; -2 if unexecutable; 0 if the format is incorrect. This prevents generation of malformed SQL and includes a time limit to avoid overly complex, slow queries.
3. **Result Reward ($S_r$)**: Assigns +3 if the query result matches the ground truth (using Execution Accuracy, EX); -3 if incorrect; 0 if format is incorrect or unexecutable. This is the primary reward for aligning SQL with user intent.
4. **Length Reward ($S_l$)**: Incentivizes comprehensive but not overly verbose reasoning. It has two components: half based on the proportional relationship between the total answer length (think + answer) and maximum length, and the other half based on the ratio of SQL candidate length within `<answer>`. Penalties are applied for exceeding maximum length. This encourages detailed yet concise explanations.

#### 3.4. SQL Candidates Selection

During inference, the model generates multiple SQL candidates along with their thought processes. All candidates are executed, and the SQL with the highest score (based on self-consistency voting) is selected as the final answer. SQL-R1 also outputs the observable thinking and interpretation process, enhancing interpretability.

#### 3.5. Implementation Settings

Experiments are conducted on Qwen2.5-Coder series models. SFT uses a learning rate of 5e-5 and batch size 1. RL training uses a learning rate of 3e-7, rollout of actor model as 8, and max response length 2048. For inference, 8 SQL candidates are generated with a temperature of 0.8. Database schemas are formatted as `CREATE TABLE` statements with annotations, but representative values are omitted during RL training to enhance exploration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate SQL-R1's effectiveness, particularly in complex NL2SQL tasks, with significant contributions in leveraging reinforcement learning and synthetic data.

#### 4.1. Performance on Main Benchmarks

SQL-R1 was evaluated on the Spider and BIRD benchmarks using Execution Accuracy (EX).
- **Spider and BIRD Performance**:
 - With **Qwen2.5-Coder-3B**, SQL-R1 achieved 78.9% EX on Spider-Test and 54.6% on BIRD-Dev.
 - With **Qwen2.5-Coder-7B**, performance significantly improved to 88.7% on Spider-Test and 66.6% on BIRD-Dev, surpassing many open-source and even some closed-source (e.g., GPT-3.5-Turbo, some GPT-4 configurations) LLM-based solutions. This 7B model achieved competitive results with models like OmniSQL-7B (88.9% Spider-Test, 66.1% BIRD-Dev).
 - With **Qwen2.5-Coder-14B**, SQL-R1 achieved 88.1% on Spider-Test and 67.1% on BIRD-Dev, demonstrating consistent high performance across larger models.
- **Comparison with Reasoning-based NL2SQL**: SQL-R1 notably outperformed contemporaneous reasoning-based NL2SQL solutions like DeepRetrieval and Reasoning-SQL on Spider-Dev and BIRD-Dev datasets.
- **Generalization and Robustness**: On Spider-DK, Spider-Syn, and Spider-Realistic datasets, SQL-R1 (Qwen2.5-Coder-14B) achieved 79.3%, 78.5%, and 86.2% respectively, demonstrating superior generalization and robustness compared to other NL2SQL solutions.

#### 4.2. Complexity Analysis

An analysis on the BIRD-Dev dataset across different difficulty levels (Simple, Moderate, Challenging) revealed:
- SQL-R1 (Qwen2.5-Coder-7B) significantly outperformed baselines across all categories (72.1% Simple, 60.8% Moderate, 51.0% Challenging).
- Scaling from 7B to 14B parameters showed a substantial 5.5 percentage point increase in accuracy for **Challenging** queries (from 51.0% to 56.5%), while Simple and Moderate levels saw minimal changes. This indicates that increased model capacity, when paired with RL, primarily addresses the generalization challenges of complex queries involving multi-table joins and nested subqueries.

#### 4.3. Insights of Performance and Model Scale Trade-offs

The study showed that SQL-R1, particularly with the 7B model, can achieve accuracy levels that surpass those of larger-scale general LLMs and reasoning LLMs on the BIRD-Dev dataset. This highlights the cost-efficiency of the proposed model in optimizing NL2SQL performance without requiring excessively large models.

#### 4.4. Analysis of SFT Cold Start (Q2)

The experiments provided key insights into the role of SFT cold start:
- **Synthetic Data Quantity and Provenance**: The quantity and origin of synthetic data significantly influence SFT performance and the effectiveness of subsequent RL training. Models initiated with larger, diverse synthetic datasets (e.g., SynSQL-2.5M) performed better than those trained with smaller subsets (SynSQL-200K).
- **Necessity of SFT Cold Start**: SFT cold-start training is **not universally essential** for RL-based NL2SQL models. Its effectiveness is contingent upon the origin and volume of the training data. For instance, some models relying on synthetic data for SFT showed only marginal gains or no improvement on the BIRD-Dev dataset.
- **Conclusion for Q2**: These findings suggest that while a good cold start can be beneficial, its impact is highly dependent on data characteristics.

#### 4.5. Sustainable Data Engineering (Q3)

Experimental results corroborated the assertion that synthetic data engineering plays a pivotal role in augmenting the reasoning capabilities of NL2SQL models, especially when combined with RL.
- **Synergistic Effect**: SQL-R1, when trained on the SynSQL-Complex-5K (a relatively small, targeted dataset), surpassed the performance of both the 7B and 14B baseline models. This demonstrates the substantial potential of synthetic data engineering to enhance model capabilities.
- **Conclusion for Q3**: The research confirms the viability and importance of synthetic data engineering for training robust and efficient NL2SQL reasoning models.

#### 4.6. Ablation Study of Reward Components (Q1)

Removing any component from the progressive reward function adversely impacted reasoning performance, underscoring the critical importance of each reward type:
- **Execution Reward ($S_e$) and Result Reward ($S_r$)**: These were found to be most critical, providing essential feedback on syntactic correctness and query result accuracy.
- **Format Reward ($S_f$) and Length Reward ($S_l$)**: Also important for standardizing output and encouraging appropriate reasoning verbosity.
- **Conclusion for Q1**: The study confirms that a carefully designed, multi-faceted reward function is effective for optimizing NL2SQL reasoning using RL.

#### 4.7. Efficiency Analysis

SQL-R1 demonstrated favorable trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency. Using self-consistency with 8 candidates added only an average of 0.7 seconds per query compared to greedy search, while improving execution accuracy by 2.9%. It also showed superior execution accuracy, latency, and token usage efficiency compared to multi-component workflow solutions like CHESS.

#### 4.8. Ablation Study of SQL Candidate Rollouts

Generating 8 SQL candidates during inference with a temperature of 0.8 was found to strike the optimal balance between diversity and computational efficiency for the 3B and 7B models, maximizing accuracy without excessive overhead.

#### 4.9. Ablation Study of Database Value Retrieval for RL Training

The study found that incorporating database values during RL training did not statistically significantly improve overall performance and led to increased input sequence length, diminishing training efficiency. This supports the decision to omit representative value annotations during RL training, allowing the model to focus on schema linking and enhance exploratory capabilities.

#### 4.10. Case Study

Qualitative analysis of examples on the BIRD development dataset (Appendix A) illustrated that RL training enhanced the model's reasoning capabilities. The model exhibited a discernible top-down cognitive strategy in generating SQL queries for challenging samples, confirming that RL can improve reasoning ability in NL2SQL tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SQL-R1 makes several significant contributions to the field of Natural Language to SQL (NL2SQL), particularly by pioneering the application of reinforcement learning (RL) to address long-standing challenges in reasoning, generalization, and interpretability for complex database interactions.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Explicit NL2SQL Reasoning Model**: SQL-R1 is a novel NL2SQL reasoning model trained with RL, capable of achieving state-of-the-art performance on benchmark datasets like Spider-Test (88.6%) and BIRD (66.6% with Qwen2.5-Coder-7B), often surpassing models based on closed-source LLMs. Crucially, it outputs a detailed, explicit reasoning process, enhancing the transparency and interpretability of SQL generation. This interpretability is vital for high-risk domains such as finance and healthcare, where understanding *why* a particular SQL query was generated is as important as its correctness.
- **Innovative Training Strategy**: The research introduces and extensively explores a training strategy that integrates Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) for cold-start initialization with RL. It provides critical insights into the impact of cold-start training, synthetic data volume, and data provenance on model performance and robustness. The findings suggest that while SFT cold-start isn't universally essential, carefully engineered synthetic data, especially for complex queries, plays a pivotal role in boosting RL-trained model capabilities. This offers a blueprint for optimizing the training pipeline for future NL2SQL models.
- **Customized RL Framework**: The design of a specialized RL-based reward function (incorporating format, execution, result, and length rewards) tailored for NL2SQL tasks is a major innovation. This progressive feedback mechanism effectively guides the model to generate syntactically correct, semantically accurate, and appropriately structured SQL queries, directly optimizing the intended reasoning process. The ablation studies confirm the indispensable role of each reward component.
- **Improved Generalization and Domain Adaptability**: By leveraging RL, SQL-R1 moves beyond the limitations of SFT, which often struggles with new database schemas or unseen domains. The dynamic adjustment of decision-making strategies through environment interaction allows the model to explore and learn robust reasoning approaches, leading to better generalization capabilities and potentially reducing the costs associated with domain adaptation.
- **Efficiency and Scalability**: The study demonstrates that SQL-R1 can achieve superior performance even with moderately sized open-source LLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-Coder-7B), offering a cost-effective alternative to larger, closed-source models. The efficiency analysis also highlights a favorable trade-off between self-consistency (for accuracy) and latency.

**Potential Impact:**

The development of SQL-R1 has several far-reaching implications:

1. **Enhanced Database Accessibility**: It further democratizes access to databases by allowing users to interact with complex data through natural language, reducing the need for specialized SQL knowledge. This can empower a wider range of professionals, from business analysts to medical researchers, to extract insights from data more efficiently.
2. **Increased Trust in AI Systems**: The explicit reasoning traces provided by SQL-R1 foster greater trust in AI-driven database interfaces. Users can review the model's thought process, verify its logic, and better understand how a particular SQL query was derived, which is crucial in regulated or high-stakes environments.
3. **Advancement in LLM Reasoning**: This work contributes to the broader understanding of how reinforcement learning can be effectively applied to enhance the complex reasoning abilities of LLMs beyond general text generation, paving the way for more intelligent and autonomous AI agents in various domains.
4. **Foundation for Future Research**: SQL-R1 establishes a strong foundation for future research in several directions, including expanding support for diverse database dialects, integrating more advanced synthetic data generation techniques, and exploring methods for further improving model interpretability and multi-table join capabilities.

**Limitations:**

Despite its advancements, the study acknowledges current limitations:
- **Supported Database Dialect**: The current model primarily focuses on the SQLite dialect, while real-world applications require generalization across various database dialects (e.g., Snowflake, DuckDB).
- **LLM Coverage**: Due to rapid advancements, the evaluation is limited to Qwen2.5-Coder series models and cannot cover the entire spectrum of new LLMs (e.g., Llama4).

In conclusion, SQL-R1 represents a significant step towards creating more intelligent, robust, and transparent NL2SQL systems. By effectively harnessing reinforcement learning, the authors have demonstrated a powerful approach to bridging the gap between sophisticated reasoning capabilities and real-world applicability in database interactions.
