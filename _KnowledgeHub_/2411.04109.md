# Self-Consistency Preference Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Self-Consistency Preference Optimization (SCPO) is an iterative, unsupervised method that enhances large language models' performance on complex reasoning tasks by creating preference data from consistent model-generated answers. This approach achieves results comparable to supervised training and empowers smaller models, like Llama-3 8B, to surpass larger counterparts on challenging benchmarks such as ZebraLogic.

## Problem

- Large language model (LLM) training for complex reasoning tasks heavily relies on costly and time-consuming human annotation for ground truth and preference data.
- Existing self-training methods often struggle with reliably evaluating the correctness of complex reasoning outputs generated by LLMs themselves.
- External reward models can suffer from limitations when encountering out-of-distribution problems or requiring specialized training for specific reasoning tasks.

## Method

- ==SCPO== iteratively trains LLMs by generating diverse responses for problems and constructing preference pairs based on a =="vote function"== that measures the consistency of final answers across samples.
- It introduces a novel ==weighted loss function== that incorporates the "vote margin" between chosen (most consistent) and rejected (least consistent) responses, emphasizing more confident preference signals.
- The method includes a ==problem generation step==, where the model itself creates new reasoning problems to scale the training data, filtered for answerability by self-consistency.

## Results

- Unsupervised SCPO significantly outperforms other unsupervised baselines on GSM8K (**22.74%** greedy gain over seed) and MATH (**5.26%** gain), achieving performance nearly on par with gold-label supervised methods.
- A SCPO-trained Llama-3 8B model achieves higher accuracy on the complex ZebraLogic benchmark than Llama-3 Instruct 70B, Gemma-2 27B, and Claude-3 Haiku by up to **3.8%**.
- Semi-supervised SCPO, combining self-generated preferences with available human-labeled data, further boosts performance beyond purely supervised approaches on both GSM8K and MATH.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-consistency, traditionally an inference-time technique, serves as a robust and scalable unsupervised signal for correctness that effectively guides LLM training for reasoning tasks.
> - Iterative application of SCPO consistently improves the model's accuracy and internal consistency, indicating that models learn to produce more reliable single-sample outputs.
> - The instance-level weighting of the SCPO loss, based on the confidence derived from vote margins, is critical for effectively learning from self-generated preference data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses a critical challenge and a burgeoning trend in the development of large language models: **self-alignment and reducing reliance on human-annotated data, especially for complex reasoning tasks.**

The current landscape of LLM development heavily relies on fine-tuning models with human feedback, often through techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) or Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). While highly effective, human annotation is resource-intensive, costly, time-consuming, and hard to scale, particularly for tasks requiring domain expertise or multi-step reasoning (e.g., mathematics or logical puzzles). This bottleneck has spurred significant research into "self-training" or "self-alignment" methods, where models learn to improve themselves using their own generated data. Prior works in this area, such as "self-rewarding language models" (Yuan et al., 2024), aim to use the LLM itself to generate queries, responses, and even evaluate the quality of those responses.

However, a critical limitation highlighted by recent studies (e.g., Huang et al., 2024) is that LLMs often struggle to reliably evaluate the *correctness* of their own responses on complex problem-solving tasks. They might generate plausible-looking but incorrect rationales or answers, and then incorrectly judge them as correct. Similarly, external reward models (RMs), while helpful, can also suffer from limitations, especially when encountering out-of-distribution problems (Casper et al., 2023; Mahan et al., 2024). This makes it difficult to reliably create high-quality preference pairs (chosen vs. rejected responses) for training.

Into this landscape, the paper introduces Self-Consistency Preference Optimization (SCPO), which builds upon the well-established "self-consistency" concept (Wang et al., 2023). Self-consistency is an *inference-time* technique where an LLM generates multiple solutions to a problem, and the most frequently appearing final answer is chosen. This method leverages the observation that while individual mistakes might be random, consistent answers across multiple samples are more likely to be correct. SCPO ingeniously adapts this inference-time observation into a *training-time* mechanism.

By extending self-consistency to *train* models rather than just select answers at inference, SCPO offers a novel approach to generate high-quality, unsupervised preference data for complex reasoning tasks. It positions itself as a solution that bypasses the limitations of both human-in-the-loop annotation and unreliable LLM self-evaluation for tasks demanding objective correctness. This work thus represents a significant advancement in making self-alignment techniques more robust and broadly applicable to domains that have traditionally been challenging for unsupervised LLM improvement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an effective and scalable self-training method for large language models that significantly improves their performance on complex reasoning tasks *without requiring human-annotated gold labels*.

The primary motivations driving this objective are:

- **Addressing the Data Annotation Bottleneck:** Training LLMs on human-annotated data, especially for complex reasoning problems like math or logic puzzles, is extremely resource-intensive, costly, and time-consuming. Human annotators need to not only generate problems but also meticulously verify step-by-step solutions or final answers, which requires expertise. SCPO seeks to circumvent this by enabling models to learn from self-generated data.
- **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Self-Alignment Approaches:** Prior self-training methods often involve LLMs self-evaluating their own responses. However, as demonstrated by recent research (Huang et al., 2024), LLMs frequently struggle with accurately assessing the correctness of their own complex reasoning outputs. This makes the generated "feedback" noisy and unreliable for effective training. Similarly, relying on external reward models can be problematic, especially when those RMs are not specifically trained for the target task or perform poorly on out-of-distribution data.
- **Leveraging the Efficacy of Self-Consistency:** The paper is motivated by the proven effectiveness of self-consistency as an *inference-time* technique (Wang et al., 2023). The core intuition is that mistakes are often random, so if an answer appears multiple times when an LLM samples different reasoning paths, it is more likely to be correct. The authors aim to harness this signal, which has demonstrated improvements in correctness, to *train* models rather than just use it for selection at inference.
- **Improving Performance on Challenging Reasoning Tasks:** The ultimate goal is to enhance LLMs' ability to solve tasks like arithmetic word problems (GSM8K), high-school level mathematics (MATH), and complex logical puzzles (ZebraLogic), domains where robust reasoning is crucial and errors are unambiguous. SCPO aims to close the performance gap between unsupervised and supervised training methods on these tasks.
- **Reducing Inference-Time Overhead:** While self-consistency at inference improves performance, it does so by generating multiple samples, thus increasing computational cost. SCPO aims to distill this consistency into the model during training, so the improved performance can be realized with a single forward pass at inference time (though it can still be combined with inference-time self-consistency for further gains).

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Self-Consistency Preference Optimization (SCPO) is an iterative, unsupervised training method designed to improve LLMs' reasoning abilities by leveraging the consistency of model-generated answers. It operates through a multi-step pipeline as depicted in Figure 1 of the paper.

**1. Initialization:**
The process begins with a base language model (M0) and a small set of high-quality, *unlabeled* seed queries (problems). The model is iteratively updated across training iterations, yielding models M1, M2, and so forth. Crucially, SCPO does not require gold labels (correct answers) for responses in the training data; instead, it uses a "vote function" based on self-consistency to rank response quality.

**2. Generating New Problems:**
To scale the training data, SCPO employs few-shot prompting to instruct the current model (Mt) to generate additional reasoning problems. Unlike some prior methods, SCPO does not require the model to simultaneously generate correct answers for these new problems, which allows for greater diversity in problem generation. Generated problems are filtered: a query `x` is retained only if at least one of its sampled responses achieves a vote count above a specified threshold `τ`, ensuring a minimum level of consistency/answerability. These newly generated problems augment the seed queries for the next training iteration.

**3. Building Self-Consistency Preference Pairs:**
For each problem `x` in the augmented training data `Dt`, the current model `Mt` is used to generate `k` diverse responses (e.g., chain-of-thought rationales followed by a final answer) via temperature-based sampling.
- **Vote Function (V(·)):** A vote function `V(y)` is defined for each response `y`. It extracts the final answer from `y` and counts its relative frequency among all `k` sampled responses. This is the core "self-consistency" signal.
- **Preference Pair Creation:** Based on the vote function, preference pairs `(x, y+, y-)` are constructed:
 - `y+` (chosen/winning response) is the response with the *highest* vote count.
 - `y-` (rejected/losing response) is the response with the *lowest* vote count.
 - A preference pair is only created if `V(y+)` exceeds a threshold `τ`, ensuring that the chosen response is sufficiently consistent. Responses are clustered by final answer, and a random response from the highest/lowest vote cluster is chosen.

**4. SCPO Loss Function:**
SCPO trains the LLM using a modified objective function, `L_SCPO`, which integrates elements of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) and Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL). The key innovation here is an instance-level weighting `w(x)`:
`w(x) = (V(y+) - V(y-)) / k`
This weight `w(x)` reflects the "vote margin" between the chosen and rejected responses, indicating the model's confidence in the preference pair. Pairs with a larger vote margin (more confident preference) are weighted more heavily in the loss.
The `L_SCPO` is formulated as:
`L_SCPO(y+, y-|x) = -w(x) log σ[β log(Mθ(y+|x)/Mt(y+|x)) - β log(Mθ(y-|x)/Mt(y-|x))] - αw(x)/|y+| log Mθ(y+|x)`
where `Mθ` is the model being trained, `Mt` is the reference model (the model from the previous iteration), `σ` is the sigmoid function, and `α`, `β` are hyperparameters. This weighted loss encourages the model to increase the probability of consistent answers and decrease that of inconsistent ones, with stronger emphasis on more confidently consistent pairs, while also maintaining a general preference for high-likelihood correct answers (NLL term).

**5. Iterative Training:**
The process is iterative:
- `M0` (base LLM) generates the initial data `Dpairs0`.
- `M1` is trained using `L_SCPO` on `Dpairs0`.
- `M2` is then initialized with `M1`, generates `Dpairs1`, and is trained using `L_SCPO`.
The paper typically uses two iterations (M1, M2), finding diminishing returns beyond that.

**6. Semi-Supervised SCPO (Extension):**
SCPO can incorporate human-annotated gold labels when available. For queries with gold answers, preference pairs are created such that `y+` is a correct response and `y-` is an incorrect response (discarding problems where such pairs cannot be formed). For these gold-labeled instances, `w(x)` is set to 1, indicating maximum confidence. For unlabeled queries, the self-consistency mechanism is applied as usual. This hybrid approach allows SCPO to benefit from both human supervision and self-generated knowledge.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on Llama-3 8B models across various math and logical reasoning benchmarks demonstrate the strong efficacy of SCPO, both in unsupervised and semi-supervised settings.

- **Outperformance of Unsupervised Baselines:**
 - On **GSM8K** (grade school math), two iterations of unsupervised SCPO (SCPO
 Unsup.) improve the Llama-3 Base 8B model's zero-shot accuracy by 22.74% (greedy decoding) over the initial seed model. It significantly outperforms IRPO
 RM (an unsupervised baseline using an external reward model, ArmoRM-Llama3-8B) by 12.36% and LMSI (another self-consistency based unsupervised fine-tuning method) by 7.20% (greedy).
 - On **MATH** (challenging high-school math), SCPO
 Unsup. yields improvements of 5.26% over the seed model and 1.64% over IRPO
 RM, and 2.76% over LMSI.
 - The superiority over IRPO
 RM is particularly notable, given that ArmoRM was trained on in-domain data, suggesting SCPO's self-consistency signal is often more reliable than even a well-trained external RM, especially in out-of-distribution scenarios.

- **Iterative Improvements:**
 - The iterative nature of SCPO is beneficial. Two iterations (`M2`) consistently improve performance over one iteration (`M1`) for greedy decoding on both GSM8K (2.88% gain) and MATH (up to 2.36% gain), showing that models become more accurate and consistent over successive rounds of training.
 - However, 8-way self-consistency (SC) inference accuracy tends to saturate after the first iteration, sometimes even slightly decreasing. This suggests that the model is already becoming "more consistent" due to training, reducing the additional benefit of applying self-consistency at inference time.

- **Near-Parity with Supervised Gold-Label Training:**
 - A significant finding is that SCPO
 Unsup., without any access to gold labels, achieves accuracy comparable to `IRPO_Gold` (a strong supervised method using gold answers). The performance gap is less than 1% for greedy decoding and under 2% for 8-way SC accuracy on both GSM8K and MATH after two iterations. This highlights the effectiveness of self-consistency as a robust proxy for human-provided correctness signals. The paper attributes this to a high correlation (0.8 across datasets) between vote shares and accuracy.

- **Superiority of Semi-Supervised SCPO:**
 - When SCPO is combined with available gold labels (`SCPO_Semi-Sup.`), it further boosts performance. On GSM8K, `SCPO_Semi-Sup.` improves greedy accuracy by 2.35% and SC accuracy by 2.19% over `IRPO_Gold`. Similar trends are observed on MATH. This demonstrates that augmenting labeled data with model-generated, self-consistently aligned data provides additional performance gains, emphasizing the versatility of SCPO.

- **Success on Challenging Logical Reasoning (ZebraLogic):**
 - On ZebraLogic, a complex logical puzzle benchmark where only test puzzles are publicly available, unsupervised SCPO training of Llama-3 Instruct 8B leads to substantial improvements. `M2` SCPO
 Unsup. improves puzzle accuracy by 6.5% and cell accuracy by 8.5% over the base model.
 - Remarkably, the SCPO-trained Llama-3 8B outperforms significantly larger and more advanced LLMs, including Llama-3 Instruct 70B, Gemma-2 27B, and Claude-3 Haiku, by 0.9%, 1.8%, and 3.8% respectively in overall puzzle accuracy. This is a strong indicator of SCPO's ability to improve smaller models to compete with or surpass larger ones on challenging, out-of-distribution tasks.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Weighted Loss Importance:** Ablations confirm that the instance-level weighted `L_SCPO` loss (based on vote margin) consistently outperforms an unweighted version (`w(x)=1`). This highlights the importance of integrating model confidence (as reflected by vote margins) into the optimization process.
 - **Increased Model Consistency:** Analysis shows that SCPO training increases the majority vote share of consistent responses across iterations and tasks, demonstrating that the training objective effectively distills consistency into the model's single-sample distribution.
 - **Filtering Threshold Impact:** The choice of consistency threshold (`τ`) for filtering preference pairs is crucial, presenting a trade-off between data quantity and quality. The paper found `τ = 0.5k` to be optimal for MATH, balancing these factors.
 - **Self-Consistency vs. Reward Models:** A comparison revealed that self-consistency is better at distinguishing between correct and incorrect responses than ArmoRM, especially in out-of-distribution settings like ZebraLogic, explaining SCPO's superior performance over `IRPO_RM`.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Self-Consistency Preference Optimization (SCPO) represents a significant advancement in the field of large language model training and self-alignment, with several profound implications:

- **Scalable LLM Improvement without Human Labels:** The most direct impact of SCPO is its ability to significantly enhance LLM performance on complex reasoning tasks without the need for costly and labor-intensive human annotations. This addresses a major bottleneck in LLM development, paving the way for more scalable and efficient training paradigms, particularly for specialized domains where human expertise for labeling is scarce.

- **Robust Self-Alignment for Complex Reasoning:** Prior self-alignment methods often falter on tasks requiring objective correctness and multi-step reasoning due to LLMs' inability to reliably self-correct. SCPO offers a robust alternative by leveraging the inherent signal of self-consistency, which is shown to be a stronger indicator of correctness than direct LLM self-evaluation or even external reward models in certain contexts (especially out-of-distribution tasks like ZebraLogic). This makes self-alignment more viable for critical applications.

- **Empowering Smaller Models:** The striking achievement of a SCPO-trained Llama-3 8B model outperforming Llama-3 70B, Gemma-2 27B, and Claude-3 Haiku on ZebraLogic is highly impactful. It demonstrates that strategic, unsupervised fine-tuning can enable smaller models to achieve performance levels typically associated with much larger, more computationally expensive models. This has significant implications for democratizing access to powerful AI capabilities, reducing computational costs, and facilitating deployment on more resource-constrained platforms.

- **Bridging the Supervised-Unsupervised Gap:** SCPO's ability to achieve performance comparable to supervised training with gold labels (IRPO
 Gold) highlights its effectiveness as a proxy for human preferences concerning correctness. This finding suggests that for certain tasks, the "wisdom of the crowd" within an LLM's own sampled outputs can effectively mimic the signal provided by human ground truth.

- **Versatile Training Framework:** The semi-supervised variant of SCPO further extends its utility, allowing it to seamlessly integrate any available human-labeled data with self-generated, self-consistently aligned data. This flexibility ensures that SCPO can be applied effectively across a spectrum of data availability scenarios, from purely unsupervised to augmenting existing labeled datasets.

- **Novel Application of Self-Consistency:** By transforming self-consistency from a mere inference-time trick into a powerful training objective, SCPO opens new avenues for research into how internal model uncertainty and consistency can be harnessed to refine model behavior. The weighted loss function, which incorporates confidence derived from vote margins, further refines this approach.

In summary, SCPO pushes the boundaries of unsupervised LLM training, particularly for challenging reasoning tasks. Its potential to reduce annotation costs, enhance smaller models, and bridge the gap with supervised learning marks it as a highly significant contribution to the field, promising more efficient, scalable, and capable AI systems. Future work extending SCPO to tasks with less clearly defined "final answers" (e.g., summarization, open-ended generation) using concepts like "universal self-consistency" could further broaden its impact.
