# Deep RL Needs Deep Behavior Analysis: Exploring Implicit Planning by Model-Free Agents in Open-Ended Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces ForageWorld, a complex reinforcement learning environment, and employs neuroethology-inspired analysis to investigate model-free recurrent neural network agents. It demonstrates that these agents develop planning-like behaviors and encode allocentric spatial information within their recurrent states, allowing accurate decoding of past and future positions without explicit world models.

## Problem

- Current deep reinforcement learning (DRL) evaluation metrics offer limited insight into agent strategies, memory utilization, or planning capabilities, hindering diagnosis and verification of agent behavior.
- A lack of systematic application of sophisticated behavioral and neural analysis methods from neuroscience and ethology to DRL agents.
- Understanding whether model-free DRL agents can exhibit complex, planning-like behavior purely through emergent dynamics in open-ended environments.

## Method

- Developed "**ForageWorld**," a novel procedurally generated reinforcement learning environment featuring ecological pressures (sparse resources, predators, partial observability) and physiological states.
- Trained model-free DRL agents using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a GRU-based recurrent core, including variants with an auxiliary path integration objective and sparse neural networks.
- Applied a comprehensive "behavior-first" analysis framework combining ethological methods (e.g., behavioral segmentation, Generalized Linear Models for revisitation decisions) and neuroscientific techniques (e.g., recurrent state decoding, single neuron GLM analysis).

## Results

- DRL agents in ForageWorld developed structured foraging behaviors, transitioning from broad exploration to efficient, memory-guided revisitation based on a dynamic integration of multiple factors including resource availability, predator presence, and visit recency.
- Recurrent hidden states of model-free RNN agents consistently encoded allocentric spatial position, allowing significant decoding of past and future locations up to 100 timesteps from the current state.
- Pruned (**90%** sparse) networks showed improved allocentric position decoding accuracy, and an auxiliary position prediction objective enhanced both the richness of spatial representations and improved task-relevant behaviors like predator defense.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model-free recurrent neural network agents can exhibit complex, planning-like behaviors such as memory-guided revisitation and allocentric spatial encoding, challenging assumptions about the necessity of explicit world models.
> - Applying neuroethology-inspired analysis techniques to DRL agents provides rich, quantitative insights into their internal strategies, memory, and emergent planning abilities that are often missed by standard performance metrics.
> - Architectural choices like recurrence, network pruning for sparsity, and the inclusion of auxiliary objectives can significantly influence both DRL agent performance and the interpretability of their internal representations, suggesting pathways for more transparent AI design.
