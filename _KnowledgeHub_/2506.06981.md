# Deep RL Needs Deep Behavior Analysis: Exploring Implicit Planning by Model-Free Agents in Open-Ended Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces ForageWorld, a complex reinforcement learning environment, and employs neuroethology-inspired analysis to investigate model-free recurrent neural network agents. It demonstrates that these agents develop planning-like behaviors and encode allocentric spatial information within their recurrent states, allowing accurate decoding of past and future positions without explicit world models.

## Problem

- Current deep reinforcement learning (DRL) evaluation metrics offer limited insight into agent strategies, memory utilization, or planning capabilities, hindering diagnosis and verification of agent behavior.
- A lack of systematic application of sophisticated behavioral and neural analysis methods from neuroscience and ethology to DRL agents.
- Understanding whether model-free DRL agents can exhibit complex, planning-like behavior purely through emergent dynamics in open-ended environments.

## Method

- Developed "**ForageWorld**," a novel procedurally generated reinforcement learning environment featuring ecological pressures (sparse resources, predators, partial observability) and physiological states.
- Trained model-free DRL agents using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a GRU-based recurrent core, including variants with an auxiliary path integration objective and sparse neural networks.
- Applied a comprehensive "behavior-first" analysis framework combining ethological methods (e.g., behavioral segmentation, Generalized Linear Models for revisitation decisions) and neuroscientific techniques (e.g., recurrent state decoding, single neuron GLM analysis).

## Results

- DRL agents in ForageWorld developed structured foraging behaviors, transitioning from broad exploration to efficient, memory-guided revisitation based on a dynamic integration of multiple factors including resource availability, predator presence, and visit recency.
- Recurrent hidden states of model-free RNN agents consistently encoded allocentric spatial position, allowing significant decoding of past and future locations up to 100 timesteps from the current state.
- Pruned (**90%** sparse) networks showed improved allocentric position decoding accuracy, and an auxiliary position prediction objective enhanced both the richness of spatial representations and improved task-relevant behaviors like predator defense.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Model-free recurrent neural network agents can exhibit complex, planning-like behaviors such as memory-guided revisitation and allocentric spatial encoding, challenging assumptions about the necessity of explicit world models.
> - Applying neuroethology-inspired analysis techniques to DRL agents provides rich, quantitative insights into their internal strategies, memory, and emergent planning abilities that are often missed by standard performance metrics.
> - Architectural choices like recurrence, network pruning for sparsity, and the inclusion of auxiliary objectives can significantly influence both DRL agent performance and the interpretability of their internal representations, suggesting pathways for more transparent AI design.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a recognized gap in the field of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) regarding the comprehensive analysis of agent behavior and internal dynamics. While DRL has advanced significantly, especially in complex, open-ended tasks, standard evaluation metrics primarily focus on reward curves and aggregate performance. These metrics often provide limited insight into the specific strategies agents employ, their memory utilization, planning capabilities, or the underlying neural computations. This limitation hinders the diagnosis of algorithmic failures and the verification of expected behaviors in increasingly sophisticated and autonomous DRL agents.

The authors highlight that this challenge mirrors long-standing problems in neuroscience, where extensive methods for joint behavioral and neural analysis have been developed to understand biological intelligence. While machine learning has adopted neural representation analysis tools from neuroscience, a more systematic application of mature behavioral analysis methods from neuroscience, cognitive science, and ethology remains largely underexplored in DRL. The paper explicitly calls for more naturalistic benchmarks and analysis pipelines that study artificial agents with the same rigor applied to animal behavior and neural computation, thereby bridging the fields of neuroAI, interpretability, and broader RL applications.

Specifically, the work situates itself within the growing interest in open-ended RL tasks that demand rich exploration, subgoal discovery, and long-horizon reasoning. It contrasts with traditional bandit paradigms in foraging research, advocating for more ecologically grounded tasks that reflect real-world cognitive demands. By demonstrating that model-free recurrent neural network (RNN) agents can exhibit structured, planning-like behavior without explicit world models, the paper challenges common assumptions in both neuroscience and computer science regarding the necessity of explicit mechanisms for sophisticated cognition. This finding contributes to a body of work suggesting that emergent dynamics in RNNs can give rise to planning-like capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the understanding of deep reinforcement learning (DRL) agent behavior and internal neural dynamics, particularly in increasingly complex and open-ended environments. This objective is driven by several detailed motivations:

- **Addressing the Deficiencies of Standard DRL Analysis:** Current DRL evaluation predominantly relies on reward curves and overall performance metrics. While useful for high-level comparison, these metrics offer limited insight into the specific strategies, memory use, or planning mechanisms employed by agents to solve structured tasks. This lack of detailed behavioral analysis constitutes a bottleneck, restricting the ability to diagnose why agents succeed or fail and hindering the development of more robust algorithms.
- **Applying Neuroscientific and Ethological Tools to DRL:** Inspired by how biological intelligence is studied, the authors aim to adapt and apply sophisticated joint behavioral and neural analysis tools from neuroscience, cognitive science, and ethology to DRL agents. This "behavior-first" approach is motivated by the understanding that neural activity alone is often insufficient to explain computation; behavior provides crucial context for interpreting internal representations.
- **Developing a Naturalistic and Complex Environment:** A key motivation was to create a novel environment, "ForageWorld," designed to emulate critical aspects of real-world animal foraging. This environment introduces ecological pressures such as sparse and depleting resource patches, predator threats, partial observability, and spatially extended arenas. The intention is for this complexity to place significant demands on agent memory, spatial reasoning, and long-term planning, thereby providing a robust platform for revealing intricate behaviors and internal computations that might be overlooked in simpler benchmarks.
- **Investigating Implicit Planning in Model-Free Agents:** A central hypothesis driving the work is to explore whether model-free RNN-based DRL agents can exhibit structured, planning-like behavior purely through emergent dynamics, even in the absence of explicit memory modules or world models. This investigation challenges conventional assumptions in both AI and neuroscience, which often associate sophisticated planning with explicit world models or dedicated cognitive architectures.
- **Creating a Reusable Analysis Framework:** The researchers aim to distill the applied analysis tools into a general and reusable framework. This framework maps key behavioral and neural targets (e.g., goal inference, memory span, planning horizon, spatial structure) to specific diagnostic methods (e.g., GLMs, RNN state decoding, path analysis). The goal is to provide a systematic guide for future research in neuroAI, interpretability, and broader RL applications.
- **Bridging AI and Biological Intelligence:** The broader motivation is to foster closer collaboration between neuroscience, cognitive science, and AI. Understanding the detailed behavior and internal mechanisms of DRL agents is deemed essential not only for improving AI performance and safety alignment but also for gaining insights into the principles underlying biological intelligence. The authors argue that if neuroscience tools cannot explain DRL agents with full access, their utility for understanding the brain is limited, making DRL a valuable testbed for these tools.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a multi-faceted approach combining a novel, ecologically-inspired environment with advanced deep reinforcement learning architectures and neuroethology-inspired analysis techniques.

**Environment: ForageWorld**
The foundation of the study is "ForageWorld," a novel reinforcement learning task suite built upon the Craftax benchmark. This environment features:
- **Ecological Constraints:** Sparse, depleting food patches (cows that diffuse and are consumed), fixed unlimited water sources (lakes), and predators that intermittently appear and pursue agents.
- **Physiological States:** Agents manage hunger, thirst, fatigue (recovered through sleep, which immobilizes the agent), and health. Death occurs if health reaches zero.
- **Procedural Generation:** 96x96 grid arenas are procedurally generated with randomized layouts, ensuring novelty per episode and requiring generalization.
- **Partial Observability:** Agents receive a 9x11 grid-centered egocentric visual observation, an inventory vector, and internal states (health, satiation, fatigue).
- **Reward Function:** A survival-focused reward function (Equation 1) provides increasing positive reward for maintaining physiological resources (health, food, drink, energy) above half their maximum values, encouraging a buffer and preventing overconsumption.
- **Extensive Logging:** To facilitate analysis, neural states (full recurrent hidden state), actions, environment variables, and internal reward components are logged at each timestep.

**RL Architectures and Training**
- **Primary Architecture:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a GRU-based recurrent core (PPO-RNN). This architecture was chosen for its ability to handle partial observability through memory and its established performance in similar benchmarks. It utilizes a single-layer GRU with 512 hidden units, totaling approximately 6.5 million trainable parameters. Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE) was used for stability.
- **Auxiliary Objective:** For specific experiments, an auxiliary objective was added where a small fully connected head, sharing the RNN hidden state, predicted the agent's allocentric position (x, y) relative to the first-timestep origin. This "path integration" objective used an L2 regression loss (Equation 2) and was weighted during optimization (W_aux = 0.025).
- **Sparsity:** To approximate biological brain sparsity, magnitude-based pruning (via JaxPruner) was applied during training, targeting 90% sparsity across RNN core layers.
- **Ablation Studies:** Several variants were tested for comparison:
 - PPO agents without recurrence (feedforward network).
 - PPO agents without the auxiliary position prediction loss.
 - Agents with a limited, forward-facing field of view (FOV) instead of the default 9x11 rectangle.
 - Agents with the ability to damage predators removed (predator-blind).
- **Alternative RL Algorithm:** Parallelized Q-network (PQN) with an LSTM architecture was explored as an off-policy alternative.
- **Training Details:** Agents were trained for 3 billion timesteps (typically hundreds of thousands of episodes). PPO hyperparameters included a rollout horizon of 64, minibatch size of 8192, learning rate of 0.00025, and standard coefficients for policy, value, and entropy losses. Training was performed on a single GPU (NVIDIA A100 or H100) and typically converged within 24-48 hours.

**Analysis Framework and Methods**
The core of the methodology is a "behavior-first" analysis framework (Table 1), which maps specific analysis targets to diagnostic methods:
- **Behavioral Analysis:**
 - **Trajectory Visualization and Path Analysis:** Qualitative assessment of movement patterns (e.g., exploration loops, revisitation routes).
 - **Behavioral Segmentation:** Unsupervised clustering (using `bayesmove`) of locomotion features (turning angle, step size) to identify latent movement states, followed by conditional inference trees to link states to task variables.
 - **Generalized Linear Models (GLMs):** Used to assess the influence of historical task variables (e.g., reward rates, predator presence, spatial uncertainty, recency of visit) on patch revisitation decisions. VIF scores were used to check for multicollinearity.
 - **Performance Metrics over Training:** Tracking of policy entropy, spatial uncertainty, distance from origin, state occupancy entropy, angular orientation variance, predator exposure, tool-making rate, and satiation levels to characterize skill acquisition.
- **Neural Analysis:**
 - **Recurrent State Decoding:** Ridge regression decoders were trained to predict allocentric (relative to origin) and egocentric (relative to agent) spatial positions from the RNN's current hidden state ($h_t$). This was performed for various time horizons (past and future, $\Delta t$ steps) to assess memory span and planning horizon.
 - **Single Neuron GLM Analysis:** Generalized linear models (NeMoS) were fit for each neuron to predict its activity from coarse-grained positional information (14x14 bins of the 96x96 arena). This identified position-sensitive neurons and examined how their activity correlated with radial distance from the origin.
 - **Coefficient Structure and Modularity:** Analysis of regression weights from decoding models to understand how past and future spatial information is distributed across RNN units and whether modularity exists.
- **Generalization Assessment:** All behavioral analyses were conducted on held-out test arenas with frozen weights to ensure observed behaviors reflected generalization rather than ongoing learning. Decoders were trained on 75% of an episode and evaluated on the remaining 25% to prevent temporal leakage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research produced several findings related to the behavioral strategies, internal representations, and learning dynamics of DRL agents in ForageWorld:

- **Structured Exploration and Revisitation:**
 - DRL agents exhibited structured, phase-like foraging behaviors in novel environments, transitioning from broad exploration to efficient revisitation, analogous to animal foraging.
 - Early exploration trajectories qualitatively resembled known insect search patterns, characterized by outwardly spiraling, azimuthally rotating loops that gradually expanded.
 - Revisitation behaviors involved direct routes to previously discovered locations, demonstrating memory-guided foraging.
- **Multi-Objective Foraging and Strategic Patch Use:**
 - Model-free RNN agents developed memory-guided, multi-objective decision-making strategies for patch revisitation without explicit world models.
 - Generalized Linear Model (GLM) analysis revealed that agents integrated multiple factors when choosing patches to revisit. Significant predictors included a preference for patches with fewer prior eat actions, avoidance of predator encounters, preference for recently visited patches, those with more observed cows, longer dwell times, and higher prior position prediction error (uncertainty).
 - Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of recurrent memory, as agents without recurrence exhibited inefficient local loops and reduced survival.
- **Emergence of Behavioral Competencies Over Training:**
 - ForageWorld supported staged learning, with agents shifting from undirected exploration to structured, goal-aligned strategies over training.
 - Early training involved a "fishing" strategy near the origin, followed by an abrupt behavioral shift around 20,000 training iterations, leading to longer-distance travel, strategic food/water trade-offs, increased tool-making, and improved predator defense.
 - Agents with a front-facing field of view explored farther earlier in training and engaged in denser local search patterns.
 - Comparative analysis with Parallelized Q-Network (PQN)-LSTM agents showed comparable overall performance to PPO-GRU but revealed a noticeable behavioral difference: PQN-LSTM agents exhibited much lower rates of predator killing, suggesting a predator evasion strategy, while PPO-GRU converged to a predator fighting strategy.
- **Recurrent State Representations Support Memory and Planning:**
 - RNN hidden states encoded allocentric position (relative to the origin) over time, demonstrating an implicit capacity for long-horizon memory and planning.
 - Allocentric position could be decoded significantly above chance from the current RNN state for up to 50-100 timesteps into both the past and future. Decoding accuracy improved over the course of training.
 - Egocentric orientation (relative to the agent's body) could not be reliably decoded.
 - Single neuron GLM analysis identified approximately 100 out of 512 neurons as position-sensitive. The magnitude of neural activity response to position change increased with radial distance from the agent's origin, suggesting a distance accumulation circuit that may modulate origin-revisitation behavior.
- **Generalization and Modularity in Recurrent Dynamics:**
 - Pruned (90% sparse) networks exhibited moderately higher allocentric position decoding accuracy compared to fully connected networks, suggesting more efficient and potentially modular information encoding.
 - Removing the auxiliary position-prediction objective eliminated above-chance allocentric decoding, indicating its necessity for structured spatial representations and its contribution to task performance.
 - Past and future position encoding relied on overlapping but functionally modular subpopulations of RNN units, with clearer divergence at longer temporal horizons.
 - A single decoding model demonstrated generalization across multiple episodes and varied arena layouts, implying a stable, compass-like encoding of space by the agents.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several contributions across the fields of reinforcement learning, neuroscience, and cognitive science, with implications for the development and understanding of artificial intelligence.

- **Challenging Cognitive Assumptions:** A primary significance is the demonstration that model-free RNN agents can exhibit complex, planning-like behaviors such as revisitation, uncertainty-driven exploration, and allocentric spatial encoding, even without explicit world models or symbolic memory structures. This challenges a common view in neuroscience that sophisticated planning requires explicit map systems or mammalian-like brains, and it informs ongoing discussions in computer science about emergent planning in model-free architectures.
- **Advancing DRL Interpretability:** The study introduces and validates a comprehensive, neuroethology-inspired analysis framework for DRL agents. This framework moves beyond conventional reward-based metrics to provide detailed, quantitatively grounded insights into agent strategies, memory, and planning. By making these analysis tools available, the work offers a roadmap for researchers to uncover rich structure in DRL learning dynamics that would otherwise remain invisible.
- **Informing AI Design and Alignment:** The findings highlight that architectural choices—such as recurrence, pruning for sparsity, and the inclusion of auxiliary losses—can significantly influence both an agent's performance and the interpretability of its internal representations. For instance, pruning improved decoding accuracy without degrading performance, and the auxiliary position prediction objective enhanced both spatial encoding and predator-related behaviors. This suggests that certain design decisions can yield "win-win" scenarios, providing benefits for both task performance and the transparency of the agent's internal workings, which is critical for safe alignment and maximizing desirable behaviors in complex AI systems.
- **Bridging NeuroAI and Biological Research:** The research reinforces the value of NeuroAI by demonstrating that DRL agents can serve as tractable models for evaluating cognitive capacities, particularly for "small nervous systems" like insects that navigate effectively with limited neural resources. The observed similarities between DRL agent exploration patterns and real-world insect search behaviors support this. Furthermore, the ability to apply neuroscience tools to DRL agents with full behavioral and neural access offers a platform to validate these tools and generate hypotheses about biological intelligence, addressing practical limitations in naturalistic neuroscience studies.
- **Insights into Spatial Cognition:** The consistent spatial encoding, particularly the allocentric representation and its generalization across episodes, suggests that DRL agents develop a stable, compass-like understanding of space. This mirrors strategies used by biological systems to maintain orientation using global cues, providing a potential mechanism for flexible navigation in varied environments. The discovery of a "distance accumulation circuit" further adds to this understanding.
- **Tool Release and Reproducibility:** The open-source release of the ForageWorld environment, reproducible pipeline, and analysis codebase fosters future research. This provides a platform for investigating how learning history shapes internal representations and how staged training affects generalization and neural dynamics, paving the way for more interpretable RL evaluation.
- **Fostering Interdisciplinary Collaboration:** The authors emphasize the positive societal impact of fostering closer collaboration between neuroscientists, cognitive scientists, ethologists, and RL researchers. This interdisciplinary approach is presented as essential for advancing all fields through shared insights into the structure and function of learned behavior, ultimately contributing to more robust and understandable artificial intelligence.
