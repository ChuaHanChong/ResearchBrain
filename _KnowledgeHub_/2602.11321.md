# ExtremControl: Low-Latency Humanoid Teleoperation with Direct Extremity Control

> [!summary] Summary
> ExtremControl developed a low-latency humanoid teleoperation system that achieves end-to-end latencies of approximately 50-60 ms, a substantial reduction from the 200 ms typical in previous systems. It enables more responsive humanoid behaviors for dynamic tasks by utilizing direct extremity control with velocity feedforward and an efficient Cartesian-space mapping.

## Problem

- Existing humanoid teleoperation systems suffer from approximately 200 ms end-to-end latency, hindering the robot's ability to perform responsive, dynamic tasks.
- This latency is largely due to computational overhead from heavy pre-processed human-to-humanoid motion retargeting and the inherent tracking delays of position-only PD control.

## Method

- The **ExtremControl** framework uses direct SE(3) pose control for selected humanoid extremities (hands, feet, pelvis, torso) combined with an efficient Cartesian-space mapping for human-to-humanoid motion conversion.
- It integrates a velocity feedforward term into the low-level control, extending standard PD control to anticipate and react to target velocities, thereby reducing tracking delay.
- A three-stage learning strategy involving PPO for teacher policy training, DAgger for distillation, and PPO fine-tuning for the teleoperation policy is employed to achieve robust control.

## Results

- ExtremControl achieved end-to-end teleoperation latencies of 54 ± 4 ms with MoCap and 64 ± 8 ms with VR, representing a reduction of approximately 100 ms compared to the 170-230 ms typical in existing systems.
- The velocity feedforward mechanism effectively reduced low-level tracking delays, with empirical measurements closely matching theoretical predictions and showing optimal performance at η ∈ {**0.8**, **0.9**}.
- The significantly reduced latency allowed the Unitree G1 robot to successfully execute highly responsive loco-manipulation tasks such as dynamic balancing, juggling, and real-time object catching in both simulation and real-world environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Low-level tracking delay is significantly reduced by incorporating velocity feedforward, with a theoretical model predicting delay reduction and identifying an upper bound for the feedforward ratio (η) to prevent discrete-time overshoot.
> - An efficient Cartesian-space mapping for key extremities and global body links is substantially faster (0.29 ms) than traditional joint-space inverse kinematics retargeting (2.69-13.1 ms), critically lowering latency.
> - A three-stage policy learning process (RL teacher, DAgger student, RL fine-tuning) with a single-frame future observation horizon for the student policy optimizes for both robustness and responsiveness in teleoperation.
