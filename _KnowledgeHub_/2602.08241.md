# Do MLLMs Really See It: Reinforcing Visual Attention in Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Ou et al. present SAYO, a reinforcement learning framework designed to strengthen visual attention in multimodal large language models by directly rewarding precise region-level focus on high-entropy visual tokens. This approach leads to improved performance across diverse visual reasoning benchmarks, with SAYO-Qwen-8B achieving an average score of 64.03, and exhibits strong cross-domain generalization.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) frequently exhibit weak and unstable visual attention policies, especially in complex visual scenes.
- Early visual misalignment during MLLM inference propagates through Chain-of-Thought reasoning, leading to systematic and uncorrected errors.
- Existing MLLM training paradigms suffer from an "optimization deficiency," lacking effective credit assignment signals for learning robust visual attention behaviors.

## Method

- Introduces ==SAYO==, a reinforcement learning (RL) framework based on ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==, to explicitly train MLLMs for stable visual grounding.
- Develops an ==Entropy-Based Target Attention Reward== that selectively incentivizes MLLMs to allocate more attention to target visual regions for high-entropy (information-rich) tokens.
- Constructs specialized training data by aligning textual questions with precise bounding box coordinates, converted to visual token ranges, to emphasize visual attention focus.

## Results

- SAYO consistently outperforms its base models and other leading open-source MLLMs across diverse visual reasoning benchmarks, with SAYO-Qwen-8B achieving an average score of **64.03**.
- The method demonstrates strong cross-domain generalization, showing significant performance improvements on mathematical reasoning benchmarks like We-Math and MathVision, despite not being trained on mathematical datasets.
- Ablation studies confirm the effectiveness of the entropy-based attention reward, which yields substantially larger performance gains compared to conventional or uniformly applied rewards, leading to higher target attention scores and more efficient reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary bottleneck in MLLM performance for complex reasoning is often weak visual grounding and attention rather than insufficient reasoning capacity.
> - Explicitly rewarding precise, region-level visual attention, particularly for tokens indicating high uncertainty, is critical for learning stable visual focus.
> - The learned "Look-to-Verify" policy enables MLLMs to proactively consult visual evidence to resolve uncertainty, thus mitigating blind reasoning and hallucinations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, with models like GPT-4o, Gemini, Qwen-VL, and InternVL demonstrating remarkable capabilities in integrating visual and linguistic information for complex reasoning tasks. A key technique that has propelled these models forward is Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, which allows MLLMs to break down intricate problems into sequential, interpretable steps.

However, despite these successes, the paper identifies a fundamental limitation: MLLMs often exhibit *weak and unstable visual attention policies*, especially when dealing with visually complex scenes or requiring sustained visual grounding over long reasoning trajectories. Previous research has shown that MLLMs may struggle to precisely focus on relevant visual regions, leading to errors in interpretation and reasoning (Liu et al., 2025b; Yang et al., 2025a). Critically, early visual misalignment tends to propagate through the CoT process, resulting in systematic inference failures that are rarely corrected downstream (Tong et al., 2024; Verma et al., 2024).

Existing approaches to enhance visual reasoning largely fall into several categories, each with its own limitations:
- **Heuristic Visual Prompt Engineering**: Methods like programmatic region highlighting or prompt reflection (Yang et al., 2025b) attempt to guide visual perception indirectly through textual prompts. However, these do not explicitly teach the model *how* to learn robust visual attention behaviors during training.
- **External Visual Processing Tools**: Techniques such as Visual SketchPad (Hu et al., 2024), ReFocus (Fu et al., 2025), and ControlMLLM (Wu et al., 2025) use external tools to apply visual cues or modify images before input to the MLLM. The effectiveness of these methods, however, is contingent upon the model's inherent ability to comprehend and act upon these visual prompts, which many open-source MLLMs struggle with (BLINK, Fu et al., 2024). Furthermore, their procedural nature limits transferability to novel tasks.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Reasoning**: Some RL-based methods have been applied to MLLMs, often focusing on improving textual reasoning trajectories or providing overall "reflection" rewards (e.g., Reflection-V, Jian et al., 2025). Yet, the authors' analysis (Section 3, Figure 2) reveals that these existing RL techniques still result in consistently low target visual attention scores, indicating a failure to provide effective credit assignment signals for *precise visual focus*.

This paper uniquely positions itself by arguing that the core issue is an "optimization deficiency" – specifically, the inadequate credit assignment for visual attention during training. It addresses this gap by proposing a novel RL framework that directly integrates a *region-level visual attention-based reward*. This approach aims to explicitly align optimization signals with visually grounded reasoning steps, thereby teaching the MLLM to learn more reliable and stable visual attention behaviors from the ground up, rather than relying on external cues or indirect textual guidance. By tackling this fundamental problem, the work seeks to unlock the full reasoning potential of MLLMs, making them more robust and less prone to visual grounding errors.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from a critical observation: despite the remarkable advancements in MLLMs and the effectiveness of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning, these models consistently exhibit a "weak visual focus." This deficiency manifests as:
- **Early-stage visual misalignment**: MLLMs frequently attend to incorrect visual regions at the initial stages of inference.
- **Error propagation**: These initial misalignments are rarely corrected and instead propagate through subsequent reasoning steps, leading to systematic inference failures and incorrect final predictions.
- **Inadequate credit assignment**: The authors argue that this limitation is not due to a lack of representational capacity but rather an optimization deficiency in existing training paradigms, which fail to provide effective credit assignment signals for learning reliable visual attention behaviors during multimodal reasoning. Quantitative analysis, including the introduction of a "target attention ratio," confirms a strong positive correlation between accurate visual attention and overall task performance (Figure 2).

Given this motivation, the paper sets forth several key objectives:
1. **Optimization-centric analysis**: To provide a thorough analysis of visual attention in MLLMs, pinpointing visual attention misalignment as a credit assignment failure and demonstrating its detrimental effect on long CoT reasoning.
2. **Reinforcement learning framework**: To propose a novel reinforcement learning framework, SAYO, that introduces an "Entropy-Based Target Attention Reward." This reward mechanism is designed to explicitly enable stable and sustained visual grounding during multimodal reasoning.
3. **Generalizable visual reasoning model**: To train SAYO using this framework and demonstrate its consistent and significant performance improvements across diverse multimodal benchmarks, showcasing strong generalization in both reasoning and perception tasks.
4. **Learning a "Look-to-Verify" policy**: To incentivize the model to proactively focus on text-relevant visual regions and develop a domain-agnostic meta-skill of resolving uncertainty by actively consulting visual evidence, thus suppressing "blind" or hallucinatory reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, SAYO, addresses the problem of weak visual focus in MLLMs by introducing a reinforcement learning (RL) framework that explicitly rewards accurate region-level visual attention. The methodology involves two main stages: data construction and reward-based training.

#### 3.1. Construction of Data with Visual Focus

Existing MLLM training paradigms primarily focus on the accuracy and formatting of responses, often neglecting the dynamics of visual attention during reasoning. To rectify this, the authors constructed specialized training data designed to emphasize visual attention focus.
- **Data Source**: The process leverages visual reasoning datasets that contain detailed object annotation information, such as bounding box coordinates. Specifically, they used subsets of GQA (~16k samples) and ReFocus Data (~4k samples).
- **Toolchain for Alignment**: A precisely designed toolchain is employed to align textual questions with visual token information.
 - First, the final target object text is extracted from existing question-answer pairs.
 - This target text is then matched with corresponding image segmentation information to obtain the precise bounding box coordinates of the target object.
 - Finally, these bounding boxes are converted into corresponding visual token ranges, which represent the visual regions eligible for attention-based rewards. This ensures that the training data explicitly links specific textual elements to their relevant visual counterparts.

#### 3.2. Visual Attention Based Reward Training

The core of SAYO's training strategy lies in its novel reinforcement learning approach, specifically using the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, augmented with a new visual attention-based reward.

- **Base RL Algorithm**: Following previous studies, GRPO is utilized to enhance the perception, localization, and reasoning capabilities of visual models.
- **Entropy-Based Target Attention Reward ($r_v$)**:
 - **Motivation**: Building on observations that a small number of high-entropy tokens carry significant information and that attention to target visual regions correlates positively with accuracy, the authors designed a reward that selectively focuses on these critical tokens.
 - **Mechanism**: The reward is applied to generated tokens whose entropies rank in the *top 30%* among all generated tokens. These "high-entropy tokens" often represent points of high epistemic uncertainty, which the model might otherwise resolve by relying on linguistic priors (hallucination) rather than visual evidence.
 - **Computation**: For these selected high-entropy tokens, the average attention mass assigned to the target visual region ($a_q$) and to all visual tokens ($v_q$) in the final transformer layer (where multimodal fusion occurs) is calculated.
 - **Reward Function**: The visual attention-based reward is formulated as:
 $r_v = \tanh(\log(\frac{a_q + \epsilon}{v_q + \epsilon}))$
 where $\epsilon$ is a small constant for numerical stability. This reward ranges from (-1, 1) and incentivizes the model to allocate *relatively more* attention to the target region for these information-rich, uncertain tokens. This mechanism imposes a "visual verification constraint," forcing the policy to resolve uncertainty by consulting the visual evidence—a "Look-to-Verify" policy.
- **Overall Reward**: The total reward $r_o$ used in GRPO is a weighted sum of the novel visual attention-based reward ($r_v$) and a conventional format reward ($r_f$) (Shao et al., 2024), i.e., $r_o = r_v + r_f$. The format reward ensures adherence to desired output structures.
- **Implementation Details**:
 - **Base Models**: SAYO was trained using Qwen3-VL (4B and 8B parameters) and InternVL3.5-8B as base MLLMs.
 - **Training Environment**: The models were trained for 4 epochs on 6 NVIDIA H200 GPUs, leveraging the TRL (Transformer Reinforcement Learning) framework.
 - **Hyperparameters**: Details such as KL divergence coefficient (1e-3), learning rate (5e-6), and batch size (64 per device) were specified.

By integrating this entropy-selective, region-level visual attention reward into the RL training, SAYO aims to explicitly optimize the model's visual grounding capabilities, enabling it to learn stable and precise attention behaviors without requiring architectural changes or external visual prompts at inference time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of SAYO consistently demonstrates its effectiveness in reinforcing visual attention and significantly improving the performance of MLLMs across a diverse set of visual reasoning benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance on Benchmarks

As detailed in Table 1, SAYO achieves substantial performance gains across various visual reasoning benchmarks, including general visual reasoning (MMERealWorld, M3CoT, V*Bench, MMStar), mathematical reasoning (MathVision, We-Math), and structured image reasoning (ChartQA, AI2D, CharXiv).
- **Outperformance over Baselines**: SAYO consistently outperforms its base models (Qwen3-VL and InternVL3.5) and many other leading open-source MLLMs of comparable scale. For instance, SAYO-Qwen-8B achieves an average score of 64.03, surpassing its base Qwen3-VL-8B (59.64) and other reasoning-focused models like OpenVLThinker-7B (59.79) and Semantic-back-7B (61.29).
- **Competitive with Closed-Source Models**: Notably, SAYO demonstrates competitiveness with, and in some cases, even surpasses, larger closed-source models. For example, SAYO-Qwen outperforms Kimi-VL-16B and GPT-4o on the MMStar benchmark.
- **Broad Generalization**: Unlike some prior reasoning MLLMs that show specialized improvements in math but declines in general reasoning, SAYO exhibits significant improvements across a wide variety of reasoning tasks, indicating strong generalizability of the learned visual attention policy. The method's effectiveness is also demonstrated across different base model architectures (Qwen and InternVL) and scales.

#### 4.2. Cross-Domain Generalization to Mathematical Reasoning

A particularly noteworthy finding is the significant improvement of SAYO on mathematical reasoning benchmarks like We-Math and MathVision, despite the complete exclusion of mathematical datasets from its RL training phase. This "cross-domain generalization" is attributed to SAYO's ability to decouple visual parsing from logical reasoning. By learning a robust structure-aware attention policy on general visual reasoning datasets (GQA) and structured documents (ReFocus), SAYO ensures that the model correctly "sees" and grounds its attention in the visual primitives (e.g., specific lines in a geometric diagram or axes in a chart). This correction of the input signal eliminates visual misalignment errors that would otherwise propagate, allowing the pre-existing, strong latent mathematical reasoning capabilities of the base MLLM (derived from massive textual pre-training) to operate on valid visual premises, thereby unlocking performance gains without domain-specific training.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Ablation experiments provide strong evidence for the efficacy and optimal design of the proposed attention reward.
- **Effectiveness of Attention Reward (Table 2)**: Models trained with the visual attention-based reward (even "Attention-only reward") achieve substantial and consistent performance improvements across benchmarks. In contrast, models trained solely with conventional accuracy rewards show only marginal gains. This critically supports the hypothesis that visual perception and localization, rather than just reasoning capacity, are major bottlenecks in current MLLMs. Explicitly incentivizing correct visual focus unlocks the model's latent reasoning abilities.
- **Designing an Effective Attention Reward (Table 3)**: Selectively rewarding a subset of *high-entropy tokens* yields significantly larger performance gains than applying rewards uniformly across *all* generated tokens. This confirms the intuition that not all tokens contribute equally to visual grounding, and focusing the reward on critical decision points provides a clearer, less noisy learning signal, leading to more stable and faster convergence (Appendix E, Figure 9).
- **Entropy Selection Range (Table 6 in Appendix D)**: Further analysis shows an optimal range for entropy-based token selection (e.g., top 30%), where selecting too few or too many tokens can diminish training effectiveness.

#### 4.4. Results Analysis on Visual Attention Shifts

The paper's in-depth analysis confirms that SAYO's performance improvements directly stem from its enhanced visual attention capabilities.
- **Increased Target Attention Score**: SAYO consistently exhibits significantly higher visual attention weights towards target regions compared to baseline models, particularly during critical late stages of inference when generating answers (Figure 4, 6).
- **Focused High-Entropy Tokens**: SAYO maintains consistently high visual attention on most high-entropy tokens (which carry more information), demonstrating that it effectively resolves uncertainty by actively consulting relevant visual evidence. Baseline models, conversely, struggle to focus visually at these critical, information-rich points (Figure 4, 7).
- **Qualitative Evidence**: Case studies (Figure 5, F. Case Study) vividly illustrate SAYO's ability to correctly identify and maintain focus on task-relevant objects throughout the reasoning process. This sustained and accurate visual attention guides the model away from erroneous visual information, leading to correct conclusions, unlike the misdirected attention seen in baseline models.
- **Shorter Reasoning Lengths**: Due to improved visual attention, SAYO achieves higher reasoning performance with notably shorter reasoning lengths, indicating more efficient and direct inference (Appendix D, Figure 8).

In summary, the main findings robustly validate that SAYO's novel reinforcement learning framework effectively addresses the "weak visual focus" problem in MLLMs, leading to superior performance, better generalization, and more reliable visual reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Do MLLMs Really See It: Reinforcing Visual Attention in Multimodal LLMs" represents a significant step forward in the development of robust and reliable Multimodal Large Language Models. Its implications extend across various aspects of AI research and application.

#### 5.1. Addressing a Fundamental Bottleneck

The paper successfully identifies and addresses a critical, yet often overlooked, limitation in current MLLMs: the inadequate credit assignment for visual attention during training, leading to unstable and weak visual focus. By demonstrating that performance deficiencies stem less from limited reasoning capacity and more from insufficient visual perception and localization, the research reframes the problem and offers a direct, principled solution. This insight is crucial for unlocking the full potential of MLLMs that possess powerful abstract reasoning but fail due to poor visual grounding.

#### 5.2. Novelty and Technical Contribution

The introduction of **SAYO** and its core component, the **Entropy-Based Target Attention Reward**, marks a novel technical contribution. This RL-based framework explicitly incentivizes precise, region-level visual attention, especially for high-entropy (information-rich) tokens. This innovative reward mechanism is a more sophisticated form of supervision compared to previous methods that either relied on indirect textual prompts or general, rather than granular, visual feedback. The "Look-to-Verify" policy learned through this mechanism fundamentally changes how MLLMs process and verify visual information.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Performance and Generalizability

The consistent and significant performance improvements across a wide array of visual reasoning benchmarks (including general, mathematical, and structured image tasks) are a testament to SAYO's effectiveness. Outperforming state-of-the-art open-source models and even competing with larger closed-source models on specific tasks highlights the practical utility of this approach. Furthermore, the demonstrated cross-domain generalization, particularly in mathematical reasoning without domain-specific training, suggests that robust visual grounding is a transferable meta-skill that universally enhances an MLLM's existing reasoning capabilities.

#### 5.4. Towards More Trustworthy and Explainable AI

By enabling MLLMs to proactively and precisely focus on task-relevant visual regions, SAYO contributes to building more trustworthy and less hallucinatory AI systems. Models that "see" correctly are less likely to generate factually incorrect responses stemming from misinterpreting visual evidence. The ability to qualitatively analyze attention shifts (as shown in Figure 5) also offers a step towards more interpretable MLLMs, allowing researchers and users to better understand *why* a model arrived at a particular conclusion.

#### 5.5. Broader Impact and Future Directions

The implications of SAYO's advancements are far-reaching:
- **Real-world Applications**: Improved visual grounding is critical for MLLMs in domains requiring high-fidelity visual understanding, such as scientific discovery (analyzing charts, diagrams, microscopy images), medical imaging, autonomous systems, and content generation, where misinterpretations can have severe consequences.
- **Efficiency**: The finding that SAYO achieves higher accuracy with shorter reasoning lengths indicates a more efficient and direct reasoning process, which is beneficial for deployment in resource-constrained environments or for real-time applications.
- **Inspiration for Future Research**: This work opens new avenues for research into finer-grained control over multimodal attention, exploring different reward shaping strategies for complex visual-linguistic tasks, and further investigating the interplay between visual perception and various forms of logical or abstract reasoning. It also encourages a rethinking of MLLM training paradigms, shifting focus towards explicit optimization of visual grounding alongside textual generation capabilities.

In conclusion, "Do MLLMs Really See It" provides a compelling diagnosis of a core MLLM limitation and offers an elegant, effective solution. By reinforcing visual attention through intelligent reinforcement learning, SAYO not only pushes the boundaries of MLLM performance but also paves the way for more reliable, generalizable, and trustworthy multimodal AI systems.
