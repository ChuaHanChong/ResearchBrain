# DeepSearch: Overcome the Bottleneck of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards via Monte Carlo Tree Search

> [!summary] Summary
> DeepSearch introduces a framework integrating Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) into the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) training loop to enhance Large Language Model (LLM) reasoning. It achieves state-of-the-art performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating up to a 72x efficiency improvement in GPU hours compared to prior methods for similar performance gains.

## Problem

- Current Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for LLMs often reach training plateaus despite significant computational investment.
- Existing RLVR practices rely on sparse exploration patterns from direct rollouts, failing to systematically uncover diverse and effective reasoning paths.
- LLMs are expected to exhibit complex search behaviors during inference, yet they are not explicitly trained with such structured exploration mechanisms.

## Method

- DeepSearch embeds a modified ==Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS)== directly into the RLVR training loop, enabling systematic exploration of multi-step reasoning trajectories.
- It employs a ==hybrid MCTS selection strategy== with =='global frontier selection'== and an uncertainty bonus to efficiently identify and expand promising or uncertain nodes across the entire search tree.
- An ==adaptive training strategy== utilizes a replay buffer for caching verified solutions and progressively filters for 'hard' problems, focusing MCTS computation on the most challenging examples.

## Results

- DeepSearch-1.5B achieved a new state-of-the-art average accuracy of **62.95%** on a suite of mathematical reasoning benchmarks, surpassing all prior baselines for models of its size.
- The framework demonstrated remarkable computational efficiency, achieving its superior performance with only **330 GPU hours**, representing a **5.7x** reduction compared to extended training and a **72x** efficiency improvement over the full training of the previous SOTA model for equivalent gains.
- Ablation studies confirmed the positive contributions of key components, with global frontier selection and the strategy of learning from the most confident incorrect trajectories being particularly impactful for efficiency and performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematic training-time exploration, or scaling training 'breadth' through algorithmic innovation, is a more efficient path to improving LLM reasoning than simply scaling training 'depth' via extended optimization steps.
> - Integrating MCTS provides fine-grained credit assignment to intermediate reasoning steps within a trajectory, offering richer supervision than solely relying on outcome-based rewards.
> - Selecting the 'most confident incorrect trajectory' (lowest average entropy) for supervision proves highly effective for targeted learning, as it focuses on systematic, high-confidence mistakes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated progress in complex reasoning tasks, often facilitated by test-time computation scaling strategies such as tree search. However, these structured search mechanisms are typically applied solely during inference, rather than integrated into the training process. This separation has been identified as a limitation for the scalability of reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) in reasoning tasks.

Contemporary RLVR approaches for LLMs have encountered training plateaus, characterized by diminishing performance gains despite increased computational investment over thousands of optimization steps. This limitation is attributed to sparse exploration patterns inherent in current RLVR practices, which rely on limited rollouts. These limited rollouts may miss critical reasoning paths and restrict systematic coverage of the solution space. Prior work has primarily focused on scaling the number of training steps, an approach that the authors suggest may not be sufficient to fully leverage RLVR's potential.

DeepSearch addresses this by incorporating MCTS directly into RLVR training. This approach distinguishes itself from existing methods by shifting the focus from solely scaling training depth (number of steps) to scaling training breadth (systematic exploration). This integration aims to provide richer supervision by enabling models to learn from the systematic exploration process itself, rather than solely from outcome-based or direct rollout methods. The work connects to the fields of search-based reasoning in LLMs, advancements in RL from verifiable rewards, and the application of Monte Carlo Tree Search, particularly its integration with deep learning from systems such as AlphaGo.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for DeepSearch stems from the observed limitations in current RLVR methodologies for advanced reasoning in language models. Specifically, the authors identify two key issues:

- **Training Plateaus and Diminishing Returns:** Existing RLVR practices exhibit performance plateaus after extensive training, where further computational investment yields marginal performance improvements. This suggests a fundamental bottleneck that is not addressed by simply increasing training duration or data volume.
- **Sparse Exploration in Training:** Current RLVR models rely on direct rollouts, which often result in sparse exploration of the solution space. This means models may consistently miss critical reasoning paths during training, even if they are expected to exhibit sophisticated search behaviors during inference. The lack of systematic exploration during training limits the model's ability to learn from diverse solution trajectories and receive fine-grained feedback on intermediate reasoning steps.

To address these challenges, DeepSearch pursues the following key objectives:

1. **Expand Reasoning Coverage:** The framework aims to enable models to explore and learn from a wider range of reasoning paths than is possible with direct policy rollouts. This objective seeks to overcome the "blind sampling" nature of traditional rollouts, which can lead to quick convergence on high-probability but low-diversity reasoning regions.
2. **Provide Fine-Grained Credit Assignment:** DeepSearch is designed to offer more precise feedback by assigning credit to intermediate reasoning steps within a tree-structured backpropagation process. This contrasts with outcome-based rewards, which only provide a signal at the end of a reasoning chain and do not inform the model about the quality of individual steps.
3. **Maintain Computational Efficiency:** While integrating a computationally intensive search method like MCTS, a crucial objective is to achieve this without an excessive increase in computational cost. This involves developing strategies for intelligent node selection and efficient solution caching to optimize resource allocation during training.

The core insight driving DeepSearch is that shifting the focus to training-time exploration, by systematically expanding the reasoning frontier, can serve as a more effective driver for improved reasoning capabilities than merely scaling training depth.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DeepSearch integrates a modified Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) framework directly into the RLVR training loop. This framework involves three core components: Expansion, Heuristic Score Backup, and a Hybrid Selection Strategy, further supported by an Adaptive Training Strategy with a Replay Buffer and a Tree-GRPO Training Objective.

#### 3.1. Modified MCTS Framework

The MCTS process constructs a search tree for step-by-step solution exploration. The root node represents the problem (x), and child nodes correspond to intermediate reasoning steps (s) generated by the policy model (πθ). Each step si is assigned a q-value q(si). Paths from the root to a terminal node (s_end) form a trajectory (t), which can be correct, incorrect, or incomplete.

#### 3.2. Expansion with Entropy-Based Guidance

During expansion, the policy model generates *n* candidate next-step reasoning trails from the current observation. This process continues until terminal nodes are reached (either a final answer or maximum tree depth). A verification function V: S_end → {0, 1} evaluates the correctness of each terminal node. If no correct solutions are found among the newly generated terminal nodes, DeepSearch employs an entropy-based selection strategy. It identifies the *most confident wrong rollout* (s*neg) by selecting the terminal node with the lowest average entropy along its root-to-leaf trajectory. This strategy aims to prioritize incorrect reasoning sequences where the model exhibited high confidence, providing targeted supervision for systematic errors.

#### 3.3. Heuristic Score Backup

The framework backpropagates rewards along a selected trajectory (t*), which is either a correct solution trajectory or the most confident incorrect trajectory. Q-values for nodes along this trajectory are updated iteratively using a depth decay function (γ(i, l)), which assigns higher weights to nodes closer to the terminal node. Terminal nodes receive a reward of +1 for correct solutions and -1 for incorrect or incomplete solutions. DeepSearch utilizes a constrained update rule (Equation 5) to manage Q-values. This rule ensures that intermediate nodes previously associated with correct solutions maintain non-negative Q-values, preventing them from being penalized by subsequent incorrect rollouts. Conversely, nodes that have only been observed on incorrect trajectories can accumulate negative values. This asymmetric update aims to stabilize MCTS exploration by distinguishing potentially useful nodes from consistent dead ends.

#### 3.4. Hybrid Selection Strategy

DeepSearch employs a two-pronged selection strategy:

- **Local Selection for Sibling Comparison:** When expanding a chosen node, the standard UCT (Upper Confidence Bounds for Trees) algorithm is used to select among sibling candidate children, balancing local exploitation and exploration.
- **Global Frontier Selection for Next Expansion:** After the score backup phase, DeepSearch identifies the most promising node for the *next* expansion across the *entire search tree*, rather than relying on traditional root-to-leaf UCT traversals. It maintains a global set of frontier (leaf) nodes and calculates a "frontier priority score" for each. This score combines three components:
 - **Quality Potential:** `tanh(Q_parent(s))` to favor nodes whose parents demonstrated high value.
 - **Uncertainty Bonus:** `H(π_θ(s | o_s))` based on the policy's entropy, which can be configured to guide selection towards regions of high uncertainty or confidence.
 - **Depth Bonus:** `D(d(s))` (empirically, `sqrt(d(s)/d_T)` is most effective) to encourage deeper exploration.
 The node with the highest priority score is selected for the next expansion. This hybrid approach aims to improve computational efficiency by avoiding redundant traversals and enhance exploration coverage by preventing the algorithm from becoming trapped in locally optimal subtrees.

#### 3.5. Adaptive Training Strategy with Replay Buffer

To manage the computational cost of MCTS, DeepSearch incorporates an iterative filtering strategy and a replay buffer:

- **Iterative Training with Progressive Filtering:** The training process begins by using a base RL model to identify a "hard subset" of problems (D^(0)_hard) that the model struggles with (Pass1@K success rate below a threshold δ^(0)). In subsequent iterations, the updated policy is re-evaluated, and problems with insufficient success rates (below δ^(i), typically 25%) remain in the hard subset for further MCTS-based training. This progressively focuses computational resources on more challenging examples.
- **Replay Buffer with Cached Solutions:** A replay buffer (R) stores correct reasoning trajectories discovered in earlier training phases. When processing problems, DeepSearch first checks the buffer. If a correct solution (`t_cached`) exists, it is directly used and supplemented with additional direct rollouts from the current policy (`DirectRollouts(x, β)`). For problems without cached solutions, the full MCTS search (`MCTS_full(x)`) is applied. This strategy aims for computational efficiency by avoiding redundant MCTS computation for solved problems and helps prevent catastrophic forgetting. Incorrect samples containing garbled text or infinite repetitions are removed.

#### 3.6. Tree-GRPO Training Objective

The training objective, called Tree-GRPO, combines Q-value regularization with policy optimization. Intermediate node Q-values undergo soft clipping using a hyperbolic tangent function (`tanh(q/ϵ_q) * q_max`) to prevent value explosion while preserving gradients. The objective maximizes a PPO-like term, where the advantage function for a node `s_j` in trajectory `t_i` is computed as `ˆA_j,k = q(s_j) - μ_t`. Here, `μ_t` is the average reward of the terminal nodes across the entire tree, providing sequence-level normalization to help manage response length. The authors note that Tree-GRPO can revert to vanilla DAPO if `q(s_j)` is uniformly replaced by the terminal outcome reward `q(s_end)`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DeepSearch focused on its performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks and its computational efficiency.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Performance Evaluation

DeepSearch-1.5B was evaluated against state-of-the-art 1.5B reasoning models on six mathematical benchmarks: AIME 2024/2025, AMC2023, MATH500, Minerva, and Olympiad.
- **Accuracy:** DeepSearch-1.5B achieved an average accuracy of 62.95%, establishing a new state-of-the-art for 1.5B reasoning models. This represents an improvement over the previous best, Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B v2, which scored 61.70%.
- **Consistent Improvements:** DeepSearch-1.5B demonstrated consistent performance gains across all evaluated benchmarks. Notable improvements included AIME 2024 (53.65% vs. 51.77%) and AMC (90.39% vs. 88.83%).

#### 4.2. Training Efficiency Analysis

The study compared DeepSearch's computational costs against extended training approaches that scale solely by increasing the number of training steps.
- **Diminishing Returns of Extended Training:** Extended training approaches exhibited diminishing returns. For instance, an additional 325 steps achieved 61.78% accuracy with 326.4 GPU hours, while 1,875 steps yielded a marginal improvement to 62.02% accuracy, consuming 1,883.2 GPU hours. This suggested that scaling training depth alone may lead to diminishing performance gains.
- **DeepSearch Efficiency:** DeepSearch achieved a higher accuracy of 62.95% with only 50 additional training steps, utilizing 330 GPU hours. This outcome demonstrates that DeepSearch-1.5B achieved superior performance while using approximately 5.7 times fewer GPU hours than the most extensive extended training baseline (1,883.2 hours).
- **Efficiency Improvement:** Compared to the total training of Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B v2, DeepSearch-1.5B achieved a 72x efficiency improvement.
- **Convergence:** Training dynamics illustrated that DeepSearch exhibited more efficient learning and faster convergence properties than DAPO over comparable wall-clock training durations.

#### 4.3. Search Strategy Ablation

An ablation study was conducted to compare the global frontier selection strategy against vanilla UCT under various configurations.
- **Global vs. Local Selection:** The global frontier selection (with λ1 = 0.4) reduced the number of iterations by 10.4% and improved trajectory rewards compared to vanilla UCT, while maintaining similar search depth and entropy.
- **Depth Bonus Functions:**
 - A logarithmic depth bonus (`log(d(s) + 1)`) yielded minimal improvements.
 - A linear depth bonus (`d(s)`) showed the most aggressive efficiency gains (59% reduction in per-tree time) and deepest exploration, but at the cost of solution quality.
 - A square-root depth bonus (`sqrt(d(s)/d_T)`) provided a balance between computational efficiency, search quality, and stable performance, which was adopted as the default configuration.
- **Uncertainty Bonus:** Including an uncertainty weighting (λ2 = 0.4) increased exploration diversity but also introduced variability in computational cost.

#### 4.4. Algorithm Evolution and Component Contributions

A systematic ablation study demonstrated the incremental contributions of each DeepSearch component:
- The base Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B v2 achieved 61.70% average accuracy.
- Incremental additions of the constrained Q-value backup rule, fine-grained node-level advantages, mean-only advantage normalization, and the global frontier selection strategy each contributed to performance improvements.
- The global frontier selection provided the largest single improvement, culminating in the 62.95% average accuracy.
- An ablation on trajectory selection strategies demonstrated that prioritizing the *most confident incorrect trajectory* (lowest average entropy) when no correct solution is found achieved the best average performance (62.95%), outperforming random (62.09%) and least confident (61.90%) selections. This indicates that targeting high-confidence errors is beneficial for supervision.

#### 4.5. Adaptive Training Dynamics and Computational Profiling

- **Replay Buffer Efficacy:** Analysis of the adaptive training process (Appendix B.3) showed that the proportion of cached (solved) problems increased monotonically from 0% in Round 1 to 33.2% in Round 5. This indicates that the adaptive strategy effectively transfers problems to the replay buffer, allowing MCTS to focus computational effort on the remaining unsolved, challenging problems.
- **Computational Bottleneck:** Computational profiling (Appendix C) revealed that GPU-based policy inference dominated the per-tree runtime, accounting for over 99% of the computational cost. CPU-side operations were found to be negligible. This suggests that further speedups would primarily stem from optimizing model inference rather than modifying the MCTS logic itself.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DeepSearch addresses a bottleneck in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) by integrating tree-based search directly into the training process. This methodology shifts the focus from scaling training depth to scaling training breadth through systematic exploration.

The research establishes a new state-of-the-art for 1.5B reasoning models on mathematical benchmarks, achieving an average accuracy of 62.95%. A key aspect of this achievement is the demonstration of computational efficiency: DeepSearch attains these results using approximately 5.7 times fewer GPU hours than comparable extended training approaches and shows a 72-fold efficiency improvement over a baseline. This suggests that algorithmic innovation through strategic exploration can yield greater performance gains than prolonged, brute-force computational scaling.

The findings challenge the assumption that improvements in RLVR performance necessitate proportional increases in computational budget. Instead, they highlight the value of systematically exploring the solution space during training for extracting maximum value from each optimization step.

The implications of DeepSearch extend beyond mathematical reasoning. By bridging the gap between inference-time search capabilities and training-time learning, the framework proposes a new direction for scaling reasoning capabilities in LLMs. This approach emphasizes structuring the learning process to mirror the sophisticated reasoning patterns typically expected during inference. This work suggests a potential qualitative shift towards algorithmically driven optimization in LLM development.

Future work outlined includes extending DeepSearch to domains with distinct verification mechanisms (e.g., approximate verifiers for subjective tasks, human-in-the-loop validation) and exploring the learnability of MCTS components themselves, such as the frontier priority function and expansion width, which are currently fixed heuristics. Such advancements could further enhance the framework's adaptability and performance.
