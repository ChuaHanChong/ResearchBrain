# Rainbow Delay Compensation: A Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning Framework for Mitigating Delayed Observation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences and Tsinghua University developed the Rainbow Delay Compensation (RDC) framework, a multi-agent reinforcement learning approach designed to mitigate the impact of stochastic individual observation delays. The framework effectively reconstructs delay-free observations and enhances policy learning, achieving performance levels comparable to delay-free baselines across MPE and SMAC environments, even under complex unfixed delay conditions.

## Problem

- Multi-agent systems frequently experience stochastic individual observation delays, where different components of an agent's local observation can have varying, non-fixed delay characteristics.
- These delays exacerbate core MARL challenges, including non-stationarity, credit assignment, and the violation of the Markov assumption, significantly degrading system performance.
- Prior research primarily addressed single-agent systems or simplified fixed-delay scenarios in MARL, leaving a gap in handling complex stochastic individual delays.

## Method

- Formalized the problem as a Decentralized Stochastic Individual Delay Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (==DSID-POMDP==) to rigorously model multi-agent systems with individual and stochastic observation delays.
- Developed the **Rainbow Delay Compensation** (RDC) framework, which integrates a neural network-based delay compensator (in 'Flash' or 'Echo' modes), a delay-reconciled critic, curriculum learning for actors, and policy knowledge distillation.
- Integrated the RDC framework with established MARL algorithms like VDN and QMIX, and evaluated its performance on standard MPE and SMAC benchmarks using custom delay filters.

## Results

- RDC-enhanced models achieved performance levels comparable to, and occasionally slightly exceeded, delay-free Oracle baselines in MPE and SMAC tasks, demonstrating robust learning despite significant delays.
- Baseline MARL algorithms suffered near-zero win rates in complex SMAC tasks under delayed observations, while RDC-enhanced models maintained high performance.
- Knowledge distillation from a low-delay teacher significantly improved convergence speed and policy quality, with RDC-enhanced QMIX models completing training within **10.8** to **58.3** hours.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Observation compensation is critical for mitigating the detrimental effects of delays, with the autoregressive 'Echo' compensator showing better generalizability to varying and unknown delay patterns than the direct 'Flash' mode.
> - Combining observation compensation with curriculum learning, a delay-reconciled critic, and especially knowledge distillation, enables RDC to achieve near delay-free performance and significantly faster convergence.
> - The framework's effectiveness extends across different delay distributions (uniform, binomial, normal, Poisson) and out-of-distribution unfixed delays without requiring explicit prior knowledge of the delay characteristics.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) is a field that has seen broad application in areas such as multiplayer games, robot control, agent communication, and quantitative trading. However, beyond the inherent challenges of MARL (e.g., non-stationarity, partial observability, credit assignment, curse of dimensionality), the problem of observation delay has received less attention in the multi-agent context compared to single-agent systems.

Previous research into system delays primarily originated from control theory, where solutions often assumed fixed transition models. In reinforcement learning, the concept of augmented state spaces allowed model-based state estimation to address deterministic delays in single-agent systems. More recently, Delayed-Observation Markov Decision Processes (DOMDPs) have formalized stochastic delay modeling, but this work has largely focused on single-agent domains.

In the multi-agent setting, existing research has primarily involved simple extensions of single-agent delay theories, typically limiting solutions to fixed delay scenarios. Examples include Delay-Aware Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (DAMARL) for fixed observation delays, TimeNet for optimizing communication waiting times, and Delay-Adaptive Multi-Agent V-Learning (DAMAVL) for reward delays. While some approaches have explored predicting action effects through state prediction, a significant gap remains in addressing the fundamental challenge of stochastic partial observability in Multi-Agent Systems (MASs). This gap is particularly relevant given the inherent asynchrony and network-induced uncertainties common in real-world multi-agent applications.

This paper positions itself to address this identified gap by focusing on "stochastic individual delays," where different components of an agent's local observation may experience varying, non-fixed delay characteristics. This represents a more complex and realistic delay scenario than often considered in prior MARL literature, aiming to provide a comprehensive framework that goes beyond fixed or uniformly distributed delays across all observation components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the ubiquitous nature of observation delays in real-world multi-agent systems. These delays impede agents from basing decisions on the environment's true, instantaneous state. A significant aspect of this problem is that an individual agent's local observation typically consists of multiple components, originating from the agent itself, other agents, or dynamic environmental entities. These discrete observation components often exhibit varying and stochastic delay characteristics, which poses substantial challenges for MARL.

The authors identify several specific ways in which stochastic observation delays exacerbate existing MARL challenges:
- **Non-stationarity:** The evolving policies of other agents already make the learning environment non-stationary. Stochastic observation delays introduce further uncertainty, requiring agents to cope with both policy evolution and unpredictable observation timings.
- **Credit Assignment Problem:** Misaligned observations and rewards resulting from delays complicate the task of attributing actions to their subsequent outcomes, thereby hindering effective policy optimization.
- **Violation of Markov Assumption:** Unfixed delays mean agents receive outdated or inaccurate perceptions of the current state, which fundamentally violates the Markov assumption that a state contains all necessary information for future predictions. The paper notes that unfixed delays have a greater impact than fixed ones because agents cannot implicitly predict others' observations over time, rendering cognitive adaptation unreliable.

Given the identified limitations of prior work, which predominantly focused on single-agent systems or simplified fixed-delay scenarios in MARL, the authors articulate the following key objectives:
- **Formalize the Problem:** To provide a rigorous mathematical model for MASs with delayed observation, which they term the Decentralized Stochastic Individual Delay Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (DSID-POMDP). This aims to offer a universal framework for understanding and addressing the problem.
- **Develop a General Algorithmic Framework:** To propose a MARL training framework, Rainbow Delay Compensation (RDC), specifically designed to mitigate the impact of stochastic individual delays. The goal is for this framework to be adaptable, allowing practitioners to integrate suitable algorithms for various scenarios.
- **Design and Implement Compensation Mechanisms:** To introduce and implement novel compensator operation modes (Echo and Flash), leveraging neural network architectures like Transformer and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) to reconstruct delay-free observations.
- **Integrate with Existing MARL Algorithms:** To demonstrate the compatibility and effectiveness of the RDC framework by integrating it with established MARL algorithms such as Value Decomposition Networks (VDN) and QMIX.
- **Evaluate Performance and Generalizability:** To experimentally validate the proposed method on standard MARL benchmarks (MPE and SMAC), showing significant performance improvements under both fixed and unfixed delay conditions, with the aim of approaching delay-free performance levels and demonstrating generalizability.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers around the development of the Rainbow Delay Compensation (RDC) framework, built upon a novel formalization of multi-agent delayed observation.

**Formal Problem Definition:**
The authors first introduce the **Decentralized Stochastic Individual Delay Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (DSID-POMDP)**. This extends the standard Dec-POMDP by:
- Augmenting the set of entities `I_D` to include both agents and environmental entities.
- Extending the state space `X` to encompass the current delay-free state and `T` previous states (where `T` is the maximum possible delay).
- Defining `D_ij` as a user-defined probability distribution representing the delay values for agent `i` observing entity `j`. This allows for individual and stochastic delays for different observation components.
- Modifying the observation function `O_D` such that an agent's observation `z_ij` from entity `j` at time `t` is sampled from a historical state `s(-t)` based on the delay distribution `p(d_ij = t)`. This formulation assumes observations from different entities are mutually independent.

**RDC Training Framework Architecture:**
The RDC framework extends conventional MARL algorithms by integrating four key components:
1. **Delay Compensator Module:** This is the core component responsible for reconstructing delay-free observations.
 - **Input:** The compensator takes a sequence of historical observations, past actions, and delay values as input.
 - **Modes:**
 - **Flash:** This mode performs a direct, single-step compensation using all available historical information and outputs a reconstructed observation. It is designed for faster reconstruction and lower resource consumption, implicitly accounting for varying delays.
 - **Echo:** This is an autoregressive model that incrementally outputs next-step information based on known data at each compensation step. The T-th output represents the fully compensated observation. This mode is designed to adapt to variable delay values and unknown delay patterns more directly.
 - **Network Architectures:** Both GRU and Transformer networks are implemented to serve as compensators, leveraging their capabilities in processing sequential data. A dual-head residual compensator is designed to handle different data types (integer and float) within observations.
 - **Training:** Compensators are trained synchronously with the reinforcement learning process using a hybrid loss function that combines cross-entropy for discrete features and mean squared error for continuous features.
2. **Delay-Reconciled Critic:** Inspired by prior single-agent work, the RDC framework feeds the critic with delay-free global states during centralized training. This strategy aims to mitigate the adverse effects of delays on value estimation without impacting decentralized execution, as the critic is not used during inference.
3. **Curriculum Learning for Actors:** To address potential convergence issues arising from initial inaccuracies of the compensator, a curriculum learning strategy is applied to the actor. Initially, the actor is trained with ground-truth delay-free observations. Over time, the probability of using ground-truth observations is linearly annealed, gradually transitioning the actor to rely solely on the compensator's outputs. This is noted to be more critical for complex tasks.
4. **Policy Knowledge Distillation:** To further accelerate convergence and improve policy quality, knowledge distillation is incorporated. A "teacher" model is pre-trained under low-delay conditions. During student model training in higher delay environments, this teacher model (receiving compensated observations) guides the student actor and critic by influencing their hidden representations and output decisions. A combined loss function incorporates both the standard RL loss and a knowledge distillation loss (cross-entropy for actions, MSE for Q-values and critic hidden states), with an annealing strategy for the distillation weight. The teacher's compensator itself is not distilled.

**Baseline Algorithms and Environments:**
- The framework integrates with value-based MARL algorithms, specifically optimized versions of VDN (Value Decomposition Networks) and QMIX.
- Experiments are conducted on two widely used MARL benchmarks: Multi-Agent Particle Environment (MPE) (simple-tag, simple-spread, simple-reference scenarios) and StarCraft Multi-Agent Challenge (SMAC) (3s_vs_5z, 5m_vs_6m, 6h_vs_8z scenarios). SMAC tasks are considered more complex.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Delay Simulation:** Custom delay filters are developed for MPE and SMAC to simulate perception and communication delays, supporting fixed, partially fixed, and unfixed delay modes. Random delays are typically drawn from uniform distributions. Observation features are categorized (movement, enemy, ally, self), with only enemy and ally features typically delayed.
- **Evaluation:** Performance is measured by reward for MPE tasks and win rate (primary) and reward (secondary) for SMAC tasks.
- **Testing:** Models are evaluated under various fixed delay values, unfixed delay ranges (in-distribution and out-of-distribution), and different delay distributions (uniform, binomial, normal, Poisson) to assess generalizability.
- **Ablation Studies:** Comprehensive ablation studies are performed to quantify the contribution of each RDC component (delay-reconciled critic, history input, curriculum learning, knowledge distillation).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments provide a systematic evaluation of the RDC framework under various delay conditions and across different multi-agent environments.

- **Baseline Performance Degradation:** Without RDC enhancements, baseline MARL algorithms (FT-QMIX) experienced substantial performance degradation across all MPE and SMAC scenarios under delayed observation. In complex SMAC tasks like 5m_vs_6m and 6h_vs_8z, win rates approached zero, indicating a severe impact of delays on learning capabilities.
- **Limited Impact of Isolated Components:** Ablation studies demonstrated that individual components like curriculum learning (Base+C) or delay-reconciled training (Base+DR), when applied without a compensation mechanism, did not sufficiently counteract the detrimental effects of delayed observation, particularly in complex scenarios. This suggests a fundamental impact of delayed observations on both initial convergence and overall policy optimization.
- **Effectiveness of RDC-Enhanced Models:**
 - **Improved Convergence:** RDC-enhanced models, especially those incorporating knowledge distillation, exhibited significantly faster convergence during training.
 - **Near Delay-Free Performance:** In many scenarios, the RDC-enhanced models achieved performance levels comparable to, or marginally exceeding, the delay-free Oracle (a baseline trained without delays). This marginal improvement was attributed to additional training guidance provided by knowledge distillation. When the Oracle was given equivalent extended training, it surpassed RDC in zero-delay settings, affirming its role as an ideal upper bound.
 - **Robust Delay Adaptation:** RDC-enhanced models maintained superior performance across a range of fixed delay values. Furthermore, they demonstrated robust generalizability, exhibiting only marginal performance degradation in out-of-distribution unfixed delay tests and performing effectively across various delay distributions (uniform, binomial, normal, Poisson) without explicit prior knowledge of the distribution type.
- **Compensator Mode Characteristics:**
 - **Flash:** While offering faster inference times (e.g., 0.004s vs 0.02s for Echo in a TAG scenario), Flash showed more pronounced performance degradation with increasing delay magnitudes and poorer generalizability, especially under out-of-distribution delays. Its optimal performance required consistent input history lengths, suggesting a potential for overfitting to specific compensation patterns.
 - **Echo:** As an autoregressive model, Echo generally demonstrated better generalizability and robustness to varying and unknown delay patterns. Although its compensation errors could be higher than Flash in some scenarios (e.g., REFERENCE, 5m_vs_6m), its overall policy performance remained strong, partly due to the benefits of knowledge distillation.
- **Module Contributions:**
 - History observations (`H`) were found to significantly improve the performance of Flash+DR, indicating its need for richer input. For Echo, due to its autoregressive nature, the additional gains from historical data were smaller.
 - Knowledge distillation (`KD`) from a low-delay teacher model proved effective in guiding policy optimization, enabling superior performance even when compensator loss values did not show a proportional improvement. This suggests that effective policy guidance is crucial in addition to observation compensation.
- **Compatibility and Architecture:** The RDC framework was validated to be compatible with FT-VDN, demonstrating reasonable delay resistance even for algorithms without a dedicated critic network. Transformer-based compensators generally outperformed GRU-based variants across most tasks, except for the 5m_vs_6m SMAC scenario.
- **Computational Overhead:** The framework's approach of sharing a single neural network among agents (consistent with the CTDE paradigm) means that increasing the number of agents primarily increases observation dimensionality, which is considered a linear and acceptable scaling for many scenarios. Training times ranged from 10.8 to 58.3 hours for RDC-enhanced QMIX models on the tested environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable contribution to the field of multi-agent reinforcement learning, particularly concerning real-world applicability in dynamic and uncertain environments.

**Key Significance:**
- **Theoretical Advancement:** The formal definition of the Decentralized Stochastic Individual Delay Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (DSID-POMDP) provides a rigorous and universal mathematical model for analyzing and understanding multi-agent systems operating under complex, stochastic, and individualized observation delays. This contributes to the theoretical foundation of MARL.
- **Effective Algorithmic Solution:** The Rainbow Delay Compensation (RDC) framework offers a comprehensive and modular solution for mitigating the impact of delayed observations. By integrating a dedicated compensator, delay-reconciled critic, curriculum learning for actors, and policy knowledge distillation, the framework addresses the problem from multiple angles.
- **Demonstrated Real-World Applicability:** The robust performance of RDC-enhanced algorithms, achieving near delay-free performance levels under both fixed and unfixed delay conditions across diverse benchmarks (MPE and SMAC), indicates its practical utility in real-world scenarios where precise and timely state information is often unavailable.
- **Generalizability:** The framework's ability to generalize across different delay distributions and its compatibility with various baseline MARL algorithms and compensator architectures enhance its adaptability and potential for broad adoption.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a solid foundation for subsequent research. The authors explicitly identify future directions, including the design of more effective compensator architectures, advanced knowledge distillation techniques, and the development of theoretical frameworks with weaker assumptions (e.g., addressing scenarios where agents relay information).

**Potential Impact:**
The RDC framework has the potential to positively impact various domains where multi-agent systems operate with observation delays. This includes, but is not limited to, autonomous driving (e.g., cooperative adaptive cruise control), robot swarms, networked control systems, communication networks, and complex game AI. By enabling MARL agents to operate effectively despite significant and variable observation lags, the research can lead to more robust, efficient, and reliable multi-agent systems in practical applications.

The authors state that their work carries no negative societal implications, suggesting its application is primarily beneficial.
