# RoboTron-Mani: All-in-One Multimodal Large Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Meituan developed RoboTron-Mani, a multimodal large model that integrates 3D spatial perception and flexible outputs for robotic manipulation, along with RoboData, a unified and aligned dataset. The system achieves strong performance across diverse robotic manipulation tasks in both simulation and real-world environments, often matching or exceeding specialized expert models.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal models primarily focus on 2D image understanding, lacking robust 3D spatial perception capabilities essential for robots interacting in physical environments.
- The high cost and inefficiency of creating large-scale, consistent robotic datasets hinder the development of generalist policies, as existing public datasets often lack 3D information and have misaligned data spaces.
- Current approaches struggle to effectively integrate and leverage heterogeneous multi-platform, multi-robot datasets due to inconsistencies in world coordinate systems, action representations, and missing critical 3D data.

## Method

- Introduced **RoboTron-Mani**, a multimodal large model with a 3D Perception Adapter (UVFormer) for unified 3D view representation and occupancy supervision to enhance spatial understanding.
- Designed a Feature Fusion Decoder with a Modality-Isolation-Mask (MIM) and multimodal decoders, enabling flexible fusion of visual and textual inputs and generation of actions, images, and 3D occupancy maps.
- Curated **RoboData**, a comprehensive dataset by unifying nine public datasets, meticulously aligning 3D space, standardizing action representations, and imputing missing camera parameters and depth maps.

## Results

- RoboTron-Mani demonstrated competitive or superior success rates on various benchmarks, achieving 91.7% SR on LIBERO (vs. QueST 89.8%), 93.8% SR on CALVIN (vs. MDT 93.7%), and 60% SR on RT-1, comparable to the significantly larger RT-2-X model.
- The model outperformed the general model RoboFlamingo* by 19.6% SR on LIBERO and 14.8% SR on Meta-World across four simulation datasets, indicating enhanced generalization.
- Real-world experiments on a UR3 arm showed superior performance across varying task difficulties, achieving 82.5% SR for easy, 70.0% for medium, and 46.6% for hard tasks, outperforming existing baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating advanced 3D perception, specifically through a unified 3D view representation and occupancy supervision, significantly enhances a multimodal model's ability to perform robotic manipulation.
> - Meticulous alignment of heterogeneous robotic datasets, including 3D spatial coordinates and action representations, is crucial for successful joint training and achieving strong generalization across diverse tasks and platforms.
> - Employing multimodal outputs (actions, next-frame images, occupancy maps) with flexible supervision mechanisms (Modality-Isolation-Mask) improves overall action performance by providing richer contextual information during training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in artificial intelligence have been significantly driven by the development of large models, such as the GPT series, Llama, and Flamingo, and the availability of large-scale "internet-scale" datasets. These models have expanded capabilities in natural language processing and computer vision, prompting their integration into Embodied Artificial Intelligence (EAI) to enable more complex tasks in physical environments.

Within robotics, a shift is observed from single-task or single-dataset learning to transfer learning approaches. These methods leverage foundational models pre-trained on diverse data, subsequently fine-tuned on specific robotic datasets to generate control actions. Simultaneously, efforts in data collection have led to large multimodal datasets, such as Open X-Embodiment and RH20T, which combine vision-language-action pairs.

Despite these advancements, the direct application of multimodal models to EAI faces several challenges. First, current multimodal models are primarily designed for 2D image understanding and generation. Robots, however, operate and interact within a 3D physical space, necessitating enhanced 3D spatial perception capabilities for practical applicability. Second, the cost and efficiency of constructing large-scale robotic datasets remain substantial. For instance, collecting approximately 130,000 episodes for RT-1 required a significant duration of 17 months. This highlights the need for methods that can effectively integrate and leverage existing multi-platform, multi-robot datasets from various sources to mitigate collection costs and accelerate development. The present work positions itself within this context by proposing a multimodal model and a comprehensive dataset designed to address these limitations, particularly focusing on 3D spatial interaction and efficient data utilization for generalist robotic manipulation policies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to address two fundamental challenges in advancing robotic manipulation through large models: the limitations of current multimodal models in 3D spatial interaction and the high cost and inefficiency of creating large-scale robotic datasets.

**Detailed Objectives and Motivation:**

1. **Enhancing 3D Spatial Perception for Robotic Interaction:** Current multimodal models predominantly focus on 2D image understanding, which is insufficient for robots that need to interact physically with a 3D environment. Robots require robust perception of spatial geometry, depth, and object locations to execute manipulation tasks effectively. This work is motivated by the need to bridge this gap by integrating 3D perception capabilities into multimodal robotic models. Specifically, an objective is to enhance 3D perception through the incorporation of camera parameters and occupancy supervision, allowing the model to interpret and reason about the physical world more accurately.

2. **Improving Modality Fusion and Fine-grained Perception:** To achieve versatile robotic manipulation, models need to effectively fuse information from various modalities (e.g., visual, textual instructions) and process it with fine-grained detail. Leveraging existing large language models (LLMs) like OpenFlamingo, the research aims to design components that improve how different modalities interact and contribute to task execution. A specific objective is to introduce a Modality-Isolation-Mask (MIM) and multimodal decoder blocks to enhance modality fusion and fine-grained perception, enabling the model to flexibly process and generate multimodal outputs.

3. **Reducing Data Collection Costs and Improving Data Utilization:** The construction of large-scale robotic datasets is time-consuming and expensive. Existing efforts to amalgamate datasets, such as Open X-Embodiment, often lack critical 3D information (multi-view images, camera parameters, depth maps) and suffer from inconsistencies in data spaces, such as misaligned 6D robot poses across different platforms. This can hinder effective joint training and lead to suboptimal performance. The motivation is to create a comprehensive, unified dataset by integrating diverse existing publicly available datasets, meticulously addressing these data heterogeneity issues.

4. **Developing a Generalist Robotic Manipulation Policy:** The overarching goal is to develop a generalist policy that can perform a wide range of tasks across multiple robotic embodiments and environments, moving beyond models limited to specific data or task selections. This policy should not only demonstrate competitive performance compared to specialized expert models but also enable simultaneous evaluation across various tasks and datasets. This objective directly addresses the need for more versatile and broadly applicable robotic intelligence.

To achieve these objectives, the paper introduces **RoboTron-Mani**, a multimodal large model for robotic manipulation with integrated 3D perception and flexible multimodal output capabilities, and **RoboData**, a comprehensive dataset that unifies and aligns diverse existing robotic datasets to facilitate comprehensive learning and evaluation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves the development of a multimodal large model for robotic manipulation, RoboTron-Mani, and the curation of a comprehensive, aligned dataset, RoboData.

#### 3.1. RoboTron-Mani Architecture

RoboTron-Mani is structured upon principles of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), extending them to robotic manipulation tasks involving language instructions (T), historical image frames (I), and camera parameters (Cam). It is designed to output actions (O_A), images (O_I), and occupancy maps (O_o). The architecture comprises four key components:

1. **Vision Encoder:** This component extracts observational features (F_h,n_I) from input images across multiple time steps (H) and perspectives (N).
2. **3D Perception Adapter:** Utilizing UVFormer, a 3D environment perception model, this adapter takes the extracted image features (X_h), camera parameters (Cam_h), and learnable unified view queries (Q) as input. It generates a unified view representation (U_h_I) that contains information about the 3D grid within the robot's operational space, thereby enhancing spatial understanding. Occupancy supervision is integrated into this module.
3. **Feature Fusion Decoder:** Based on OpenFlamingo, this component serves as the core LLM. It integrates the unified visual representations (U_h_I) with a constructed text sequence (T'). This text sequence includes task instructions and specific modality read-out tokens (e.g., T_simg, T_gimg, T_occ, T_act) for various outputs. A Modality-Isolation-Mask (MIM) is incorporated within the self-attention layer of this decoder. The MIM regulates attention interactions among different modalities, allowing for flexible multimodal supervision during training and the omission of unnecessary modalities during inference. The decoder outputs multimodal features (F_h_simg, F_h_gimg, F_h_occ, F_h_act).
4. **Multimodal Decoders:** These modules process the multimodal output features from the Feature Fusion Decoder to generate the final task-specific outputs:
 - **Image Decoder:** Generates static images (O_h_simg) or wrist-view images (O_h_gimg) using attention decoder layers and patch assembly.
 - **Occupancy Decoder:** Reconstructs a 3D occupancy map (O_h_o) by processing features through reshaping, upsampling, and 3D convolutions. This can be derived either from the visual module (O_h_o_v) or the LLM (O_h_o_m).
 - **Action Decoder:** Outputs delta 6D poses (a_h_pose) and 1-DoF gripper actions (a_h_g) using a few Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) layers or Diffusion Transformer (DiT) blocks.

#### 3.2. Training Objective

The model is optimized using a comprehensive loss function (l) that combines losses from different modalities:
l = l_a + λ_image (l_simg + l_gimg) + λ_occ l_o.
- **Action Loss (l_a):** A combination of Mean Squared Error (MSE) for 6D pose prediction and Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) for gripper actions.
- **Image Loss (l_simg, l_gimg):** L2 loss to measure pixel-level differences between predicted and ground-truth next-frame images.
- **Occupancy Loss (l_o):** L2 loss for both spatial position and RGB color information of the 3D occupancy map.
The flexibility of this framework allows for the exclusion of image or occupancy losses if the corresponding modality data is unavailable.

#### 3.3. RoboData Dataset

To address data heterogeneity and enhance generalization, RoboData integrates nine publicly available datasets: CALVIN, Meta-World, LIBERO, Robomimic, RoboCasa, ManiSkill2, RoboCAS, RLBench, Colosseum, and RT-1. This curated dataset totals 70,000 episodes and 7 million samples, encompassing various tasks like placing, picking, turning, and stacking. Key aspects of RoboData's construction include:

1. **3D Space Alignment:** Datasets often use different world coordinate systems, workspaces, and action representations. RoboData unifies all data into a common coordinate system (X-axis right, Y-axis forward, Z-axis upward) and a standardized workspace range ([-0.5, -0.5, 0] to [0.5, 0.5, 1]). This involves applying specific rotation and translation transformations to align the original coordinate systems.
2. **Action Representation Alignment:** Different datasets employ varied methods for representing robot actions (e.g., Euler Angle Difference Method, Pose Composition Method). RoboData regenerates all action representations using the Composite Rotation Matrix Method (CRMM) to ensure consistency across the fused data.
3. **Missing Data Imputation:** Critical 3D information, such as camera intrinsics and extrinsics, and depth maps, are often missing in public datasets. The researchers address this by reconstructing original simulations and re-rendering them to obtain these parameters, driving robots with original data to ensure data completeness.

#### 3.4. Training and Evaluation Protocol

RoboTron-Mani, with 4 billion parameters (in bf16 precision) and a DiT action head, was trained for 10 epochs on 2.1 million samples. This training utilized 32 80G-A100 GPUs and took approximately 50 hours. Evaluation protocols adhere to the official configurations of each dataset, with task success rate (SR) being the primary metric. Real-world experiments were conducted on a Dalu mobile base with a UR3 robotic arm, equipped with a Robotiq gripper, an Intel D435 depth camera (wrist-mounted), and an Orbbec Gemini Pro depth camera (fixed). Hand-eye calibration and point cloud fusion were performed to facilitate occupancy-supervised training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding RoboTron-Mani's performance as a generalist policy, the contributions of its individual modules, and the impact of data alignment.

#### 4.1. Generalist Policy Performance

RoboTron-Mani was evaluated across multiple simulated and real-world datasets, demonstrating competitive performance relative to specialized expert models:
- **LIBERO:** Achieved a success rate (SR) of 91.7%, surpassing the 89.8% of QueST, a specialized model.
- **RoboCasa:** Achieved an SR of 47.4%.
- **CALVIN:** Achieved an SR of 93.8%, comparable to MDT (93.7%).
- **Meta-World:** Achieved an SR of 80.1%, comparable to PRISE (80.4%).
- **RT-1 Dataset:** Achieved an average task SR of 60%. This result is comparable to RT-2-X (55B), which achieved 60.7%, despite RT-2-X being a significantly larger model. The paper states RoboTron-Mani "significantly outperform[s] models with equivalent parameter sizes" in this context.

Across four simulation datasets (LIBERO, RoboCasa, CALVIN, Meta-World), RoboTron-Mani consistently outperformed the general model RoboFlamingo*, with improvements of 19.6% SR on LIBERO and 14.8% SR on Meta-World.

For real-world experiments conducted on a Dalu mobile base with a UR3 arm, RoboTron-Mani demonstrated superior performance across varying task difficulties:
- **Easy tasks:** 82.5% SR (compared to RoboUniView* at 75.0% and RoboFlamingo* at 35.0%).
- **Medium tasks:** 70.0% SR (compared to RoboUniView* at 36.6% and RoboFlamingo* at 6.6%).
- **Hard tasks:** 46.6% SR (compared to RoboUniView* at 23.3% and RoboFlamingo* at 6.6%).
These results indicate stronger generalization and 3D reasoning capabilities, particularly for more complex real-world scenarios.

A comparison with OpenVLA (LoRA) on the CALVIN dataset indicated that RoboTron-Mani achieved higher success rates for tasks 3-5 in a sequence and a higher average sequence length (2.15 vs. 1.86), suggesting an improved ability to capture longer dependencies.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study on RoboTron-Mani Modules

An ablation study on the CALVIN dataset examined the contribution of each RoboTron-Mani module:
- The baseline model achieved 81.0% SR for the first task and an average sequence length of 1.77.
- Introducing the Frame by Frame Action (FFA) module increased the first task SR to 85.0% and average length to 2.37.
- The addition of the Image module (for generating next-frame images) further enhanced overall success rates and average length to 3.13.
- The UVFormer module, which provides unified 3D view representation, led to a first task SR of 94.2% and average length of 2.88 (without Image module output), or 94.1% and 3.21 (with Image output).
- Incorporating occupancy (OCC) supervision further improved performance, resulting in a first task SR of 94.7% and average length of 3.31.
- Replacing the MLP action head with a DiT action head further increased the average sequence length on CALVIN from 3.31 to 3.51, and the first task SR to 96.9%.
These results indicate that while generated images and occupancy maps may not always exhibit ideal visual quality, their integration significantly contributes to enhancing action performance.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on RoboData Alignment

Experiments evaluated the impact of RoboData's alignment on model performance. Training RoboTron-Mani on non-aligned RoboData ("RoboTron-Mani-") resulted in lower success rates compared to training on aligned RoboData. For example, on LIBERO, the SR dropped from 90.7% to 64.2%, and on CALVIN, from 91.0% to 74.7%. The Meta-World dataset showed a relatively minimal impact of misalignment, attributed to its tasks being limited to 3-position changes without rotational variations, and its original spatial coordinate system being consistent with RoboData's aligned system. These results underscore that proper spatial alignment of diverse datasets is crucial for effective joint training and performance, especially for models like RoboTron-Mani that leverage 3D spatial input.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents RoboTron-Mani and RoboData as contributions toward advancing generalist robotic manipulation policies. The significance and potential impact of this work lie in several areas:

1. **Addressing 3D Interaction Limitations:** RoboTron-Mani integrates 3D perception capabilities, specifically through camera parameters and occupancy supervision via the UVFormer, into a multimodal large model. This addresses a critical limitation of existing MLLMs that primarily focus on 2D vision, enabling robots to interpret and interact with the physical 3D environment more effectively. This enhanced spatial understanding is fundamental for robust robotic manipulation in real-world scenarios.

2. **Enabling Generalist Robotic Policies:** The development of RoboTron-Mani, a generalist policy capable of simultaneously evaluating all tasks across multiple diverse datasets and embodiments, represents a step towards creating more versatile robots. The finding that RoboTron-Mani can achieve performance comparable to or surpassing specialized expert models across a range of benchmarks suggests a pathway to reduce the need for task-specific model development and fine-tuning.

3. **Standardizing Heterogeneous Robotic Data:** RoboData addresses the challenge of integrating disparate robotic datasets by unifying input and output spaces, including 3D space alignment and action representation alignment, and by imputing missing data such as camera parameters and depth maps. This meticulous data curation is critical for successful joint training across heterogeneous sources, as evidenced by the performance degradation when using non-aligned data. RoboData thus provides a standardized, comprehensive dataset that can facilitate the development and evaluation of more general-purpose embodied AI agents.

4. **Promoting Research in Foundational Robotic Models:** By providing both a novel model architecture and a unified dataset, this work aims to offer the research community a comprehensive and fair evaluation system. This standardized approach has the potential to inspire further exploration in foundational robotic model research, fostering the development of more general and higher-performing robotic learning systems. The flexibility offered by RoboTron-Mani's Modality-Isolation-Mask and multimodal outputs further allows for adaptable training and inference strategies, increasing the model's utility in diverse research contexts.

In summary, this research contributes to the field by demonstrating that integrating advanced 3D perception into multimodal large models, combined with a meticulously curated and aligned heterogeneous dataset, can lead to the creation of generalist robotic policies capable of robust performance across a variety of manipulation tasks in both simulated and real-world environments.
