# PRIOR: Perceptive Learning for Humanoid Locomotion with Reference Gait Priors

> [!summary] Summary
> PRIOR is a single-stage reinforcement learning framework that enables humanoid robots to achieve robust terrain traversal with natural gaits by integrating depth-based perception, parametric gait generation, and terrain-adaptive footstep rewards. The framework demonstrates a 100% traversal success rate across various complex terrains in simulation, while achieving a 3x training speedup and supporting 1024 parallel environments on a single GPU.

## Problem

- Developing humanoid locomotion controllers that can both robustly navigate varied terrain and produce natural, human-like gaits often requires complex, multi-stage reinforcement learning pipelines.
- Existing perception-driven locomotion methods typically lack natural gait synthesis due to intricate reward engineering or multi-stage training, which can compromise motion quality.
- Motion-prior-based approaches improve gait naturalness but frequently lack adaptability to unstructured terrains and suffer from instabilities like mode collapse when using adversarial objectives.
- The transition to new simulation platforms like Isaac Lab necessitates the development of efficient, natively built training frameworks.

## Method

- A single-stage, end-to-end reinforcement learning framework, named PRIOR, is developed using an asymmetric actor-critic architecture trained via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO).
- Integrates a GRU-based state estimator that fuses proprioceptive and egocentric depth image data for self-supervised terrain geometry inference.
- Utilizes a parametric gait generator to provide stable, velocity-adaptive reference joint trajectories derived from motion capture data, avoiding adversarial training.
- Incorporates terrain-adaptive footstep rewards and gait-aware tracking rewards to optimize foot placement and maintain natural motion patterns.
- Optimizes training infrastructure on Isaac Lab through heterogeneous observation buffer management and render-time pre-processing for improved efficiency and parallelism.

## Results

- The PRIOR framework achieved a 100% traversal success rate across diverse complex terrains, including Pyramid Stairs, Inverted Stairs, and Boxes, in simulation.
- Engineering optimizations led to a 3x training speedup over baseline Isaac Lab methods and enabled training with 1024 parallel environments on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU.
- Ablation studies showed the reference gait prior significantly improved motion quality, explicit terrain estimation was essential for understanding geometric structures, and temporal depth information was critical for anticipating terrain variations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A single-stage reinforcement learning pipeline can effectively unify robust terrain-adaptive locomotion and natural gait synthesis, eliminating the need for complex multi-stage training or adversarial objectives.
> - Egocentric depth images provide a reliable, drift-free signal for self-supervised terrain geometry inference, critical for perceptive locomotion without relying on external localization.
> - Parametric gait generation offers a stable and effective alternative to adversarial motion priors, providing consistent kinematic guidance through deterministic supervision for human-like gaits.
> - Optimized observation buffer management and render-time pre-processing are crucial for achieving high-throughput training on parallel simulation platforms like Isaac Lab, especially with high-dimensional visual inputs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The deployment of humanoid robots in environments designed for humans necessitates locomotion controllers capable of navigating varied terrain while maintaining natural, bipedal gaits. Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a primary method for developing such controllers. Recent advancements in RL-driven locomotion have demonstrated progress in two distinct areas: creating terrain-robust perceptive policies and synthesizing natural gaits through motion priors. However, achieving both capabilities concurrently typically introduces significant system complexity. This complexity often manifests as multi-stage training pipelines (e.g., teacher-student distillation), the use of adversarial objectives for style enforcement, or extensive calibration for sim-to-real transfer. These approaches can impede research reproducibility and increase the entry barrier for new studies in the field.

The paper identifies specific limitations in existing paradigms:

- **Perception-Driven Locomotion:** Early research focused on proprioception-based "blind walking," which offered some robustness but lacked proactive planning for complex, non-local terrain variations. More recent methods have adopted end-to-end learning, often using two-stage teacher-student frameworks or integrating temporal structures and attention mechanisms. While these have improved adaptability in complex environments, they frequently rely on intricate reward engineering or multi-stage training, which can compromise the naturalness of generated motions due to system complexity.
- **Motion-Prior-Based Locomotion:** This area primarily aims to enhance the human-likeness of robot movements. Approaches include explicit motion generation (imitation learning, latent variable models, diffusion models) and using reference motions as reward signals or discriminative criteria (e.g., adversarial imitation learning). While these methods significantly improve gait naturalness, they often struggle with adaptability and robustness in unstructured or complex terrains due to insufficient integration of environmental perception. Adversarial motion priors, in particular, are noted for potential training pathologies such as mode collapse, reward ambiguity, and hyperparameter sensitivity, especially when policies must deviate significantly from reference motions on challenging terrains.
- **Simulation Platforms:** The ongoing transition from Isaac Gym to Isaac Lab as a standard simulation platform highlights a need for new training pipelines developed natively on the updated stack.

This work, PRIOR, positions itself by aiming to bridge the gap between robust terrain traversal and natural gait synthesis within a single-stage RL framework. It seeks to achieve these objectives without resorting to adversarial training, distillation, or extensive calibration, thereby addressing the issues of complexity, reproducibility, and instability observed in prior research. It also specifically targets development on the Isaac Lab platform.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient and reproducible framework, named PRIOR, for perceptive humanoid locomotion. This framework aims to enable humanoid robots to traverse complex terrains with robust, human-like gaits using a simplified, single-stage reinforcement learning pipeline.

The motivation for PRIOR is derived from three key observations in the existing literature and technological landscape:

1. **Addressing System Complexity for Combined Capabilities:** Existing methods that attempt to achieve both terrain-robust locomotion (perceptive policies) and natural gait synthesis (motion priors) typically involve complex system designs, such as multi-stage training, adversarial objectives, or extensive real-world calibration. These complexities hinder research reproducibility and increase the barrier to entry for new investigations. The researchers were motivated to investigate if a simpler, single-stage RL pipeline, devoid of adversarial training or distillation, could achieve these dual capabilities simultaneously.
2. **Optimizing Terrain Perception:** Current LiDAR-based elevation mapping, while geometrically precise, is susceptible to odometric integration drift during prolonged locomotion. Egocentric depth images, by contrast, offer a self-contained terrain signal that is inherently free from such accumulated drift. This motivated the development of a depth-image-based perception system that could provide reliable terrain information without external localization.
3. **Enhancing Gait Guidance Stability:** Adversarial motion priors, a prevalent mechanism for imposing gait style, are known to suffer from training instabilities such as mode collapse, reward ambiguity, and hyperparameter sensitivity. These issues are exacerbated when policies must significantly deviate from reference motions on challenging terrains. The authors sought an alternative, more stable method for stylistic guidance, proposing that a parametric gait generator could offer comparable stylistic direction through deterministic supervision, thereby circumventing the instabilities associated with adversarial approaches.
4. **Leveraging New Simulation Environments:** The research community is in a transition phase from Isaac Gym to Isaac Lab as the standard simulation platform. This motivated the development of the entire PRIOR framework natively within Isaac Lab, including systematic optimizations to enhance training speed and efficiency on the new platform.

In summary, PRIOR's core objectives are to:
- Develop a single-stage RL framework for humanoid locomotion that unifies depth-based terrain perception, parametric gait generation, and terrain-adaptive footstep rewards.
- Eliminate the need for adversarial objectives, teacher-student distillation, or multi-stage training.
- Integrate a GRU-based state estimator for self-supervised terrain geometry inference from depth images.
- Utilize a parametric gait generator to supply stable, velocity-adaptive reference trajectories from motion capture data.
- Implement terrain-adaptive footstep rewards to guide foot placement.
- Conduct a systematic analysis of depth image resolution to optimize perceptual efficiency under real-time constraints.
- Achieve high traversal success rates across various complex terrains with natural gaits.
- Open-source the framework to promote reproducibility and serve as a baseline for future research on Isaac Lab.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PRIOR employs a single-stage, end-to-end reinforcement learning framework within the Isaac Lab environment, utilizing an asymmetric actor-critic architecture. The methodology integrates three primary components: a perceptive state estimator, a reference gait generator, and terrain-adaptive reward functions, complemented by high-throughput training infrastructure.

**A. Perception-Driven Locomotion Framework**
1. **Asymmetric Actor–Critic Architecture:** The framework uses an asymmetric actor-critic design, trained end-to-end via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). This allows the perception module and control policy to co-evolve, mitigating error amplification issues common in two-stage distillation approaches.
 - **Actor Network:** Inputs include a 45-dimensional current proprioceptive observation, a 163-dimensional state estimator output, and a 1-dimensional gait phase signal. The proprioceptive observation encompasses body angular velocity, gravity direction, velocity command, joint positions and velocities, and the previous action.
 - **Critic Network:** Receives privileged information from the simulation environment, specifically the noise-free base linear velocity, along with the proprioceptive observation, height map scan data, and reference gait phase.
 - **Reward and Action Space:** Reward functions are categorized into task tracking, stability, smoothness, and safety. Critical foot-related reward components (e.g., $r_{air}$ for gait rhythm, $r_{slide}$ for minimizing slip, $r_{swing}$ for leg lift height, $r_{stumble}$ for preventing trips, $r_{L/R\_edge}$ for safe foot placement) are designed to guide foot-terrain interaction. The actor outputs a 12-dimensional action vector for leg joints, which modulates a default standing joint configuration to produce target joint positions. These targets are then fed to a low-level PD controller to calculate joint torques.

2. **State Estimator:** This module fuses proprioceptive and depth visual perceptions.
 - **Input Modalities:** Proprioceptive observations are stacked over a horizon of H1=10. Temporally stacked egocentric depth images are used, with a horizon of H2=2 frames, each cropped to a 36x64 resolution.
 - **Feature Extraction:** An MLP encoder processes the proprioceptive history into a 128-dimensional feature. A CNN encoder processes the stacked depth images into a 128-dimensional depth feature.
 - **Fusion and Memory:** The encoded features are concatenated and fed into a single-layer Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) to generate a 163-dimensional memory representation ($e_t$). This output comprises a 3-dimensional base velocity estimate ($\hat{v}_t$), a 32-dimensional latent vector ($z_t$), and a 128-dimensional height map latent vector ($h_t$).
 - **Self-Supervised Learning:** The state estimator is trained using auxiliary objectives. The height map latent vector ($h_t$) is decoded by an MLP to reconstruct the estimated terrain state ($\hat{m}_t$). The full memory representation ($e_t$) is decoded by another MLP to predict the next-step proprioceptive state ($\hat{o}_{t+1}$). Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss is minimized between these estimates and their corresponding privileged ground truth values (linear velocity $v_t$, actual height map $m_t$, and actual next proprioceptive state $o_{t+1}$).

**B. Reference Gait Priors**
This component extracts motion priors to provide robust kinematic guidance without large-scale motion capture data or adversarial training.
1. **Motion Data Preprocessing:** High-precision human motion capture data ($D_{human}$) is collected and retargeted to the ZERITH Z1 robot ($D_{robot}$). This data includes joint angles, velocities, base linear/angular velocities, and foot contact information. A preprocessing pipeline extracts high-fidelity, periodic, and smooth gait segments by using foot contact information to define gait cycles ($T$). A 1D Gaussian filter is applied to joint sequences to reduce noise.
2. **Reference Gait Generation:** Given a commanded base velocity and gait phase, the system synthesizes a reference joint trajectory through a weighted interpolation framework.
 - For each velocity channel (e.g., $v_x$, $v_y$, $\omega$), an interpolation coefficient ($\alpha$) is determined based on the commanded velocity's magnitude relative to neighboring nominal velocities in the dataset.
 - The commanded gait period is linearly interpolated, and the gait phase is updated based on a normalized phase progression rule.
 - The final reference joint trajectory is synthesized by blending neighboring motion templates according to the calculated phase and interpolation factor. A standing threshold handles near-zero velocity commands, switching to a default standing pose. A velocity-dependent stance ratio is defined for contact supervision.
3. **Gait-aware Reward Design:** To maintain consistency with the reference gait, a set of exponential tracking reward terms ($r_i = \exp(-\lambda_i e_i)$) is introduced. These include rewards for pose consistency ($r_{pos}$), velocity matching ($r_{vel}$), dynamic motion trend tracking ($r_\Delta$), and stabilization of key support joints ($r_{ankle}$). These terms are combined into a weighted sum ($r_{gait}$).

**C. High-Throughput Training Infrastructure**
The framework incorporates systematic engineering optimizations on NVIDIA Isaac Sim and Isaac Lab to address computational overhead and VRAM bottlenecks from high-dimensional depth perception in massively parallel environments.
1. **Heterogeneous Observation Buffer Management:** VRAM is used only as a transient buffer for rendering outputs. Observation tensors are asynchronously transferred to host memory (RAM) for storage and indexing. This strategy significantly increases the maximum number of parallel environments (e.g., from 512 to 1024 on an RTX 4090 with 24GB VRAM).
2. **Render-time Pre-processing Optimization:** The system defines a core perceptual requirement: reliably distinguishing terrain features with a minimum height of 5 cm. Based on a geometric camera model, an effective vertical resolution of 36 pixels is determined to satisfy this requirement at typical measurement distances. Instead of rendering high-resolution frames, a low-resolution depth buffer (45x80) is rendered directly, from which a 36x64 region is center-cropped. Stochastic depth perturbations are applied for robustness. This optimization reduces perception overhead and GPU memory bandwidth, enabling higher frequency processing of visual observations.

**D. Training Details**
1. **Training Curriculum:** An adaptive terrain curriculum, based on the robot's traveled distance, is implemented to prevent early policy instability. Training is conducted simultaneously across four native Isaac Lab terrain generators: Pyramid Stairs, Inverted Stairs, Boxes, and Plane, with detailed parameter ranges and distribution weights.
2. **Domain Randomization:** To enhance robustness and sim-to-real transfer capabilities, domain randomization is applied to various physical dynamics (e.g., base payload, link mass, friction), initial conditions (e.g., base position/orientation, velocities), and visual inputs (e.g., depth image bias, noise, hole probability).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The PRIOR framework demonstrated robust performance in simulated environments, supported by systematic ablation studies and technical optimizations.

- **Overall Locomotion Performance:** The complete PRIOR framework achieved a 100% traversal success rate across various complex terrains, including Pyramid Stairs, Inverted Stairs, Boxes, and flat ground. The policy converged within approximately 12,000 training iterations. The full framework attained a mean curriculum level of 5.7533 and an average reward of 26.3462, which was noted to reflect both traversal capability and motion quality.

- **Computational Efficiency and Throughput:**
 - The systematic engineering optimizations on Isaac Lab resulted in a 3x training speedup compared to a vanilla Isaac Lab baseline.
 - The heterogeneous observation buffer management scheme significantly enhanced parallelism, enabling the training system to support 1024 parallel environments on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 (24GB) GPU.
 - The render-time pre-processing optimization, based on a depth resolution study, identified that a low vertical resolution of 36 pixels for depth images was sufficient to detect critical terrain features (minimum 5 cm height). This reduced perceptual overhead without compromising traversal performance.

- **Ablation Study Results:** The ablation studies systematically evaluated the contribution of each component to the overall performance, as summarized in Table V and Figure 5.
 - **Impact of Reference Gait Prior:** The variant "PRIOR w/o reference gait" achieved a slightly higher mean curriculum level (5.7735) but a lower average reward (23.7233) compared to the full PRIOR framework (mean level 5.7533, reward 26.3462). This indicates that while the policy could traverse terrains without the gait prior, the prior significantly improved motion quality and efficiency, leading to higher rewards. Policies without the gait prior were observed to exhibit unstable or high-frequency oscillatory motions, whereas the gait-constrained policy maintained smoother, more energy-efficient locomotion.
 - **Importance of Explicit Terrain Estimation ($\hat{m}_t$):** Removing explicit terrain estimation ("PRIOR w/o $\hat{m}_t$") led to a substantial reduction in average reward (13.1775) and a lower success rate on Inverted Stairs (0.7734). This demonstrates the necessity of explicit terrain representation and elevation map supervision for the policy's understanding of geometric structures.
 - **Role of Temporal Depth Information ($d_{H2,t}$):** The "PRIOR w/o $d_{H2,t}$" variant exhibited the lowest performance, with an average reward of 10.1463 and a significant drop in Inverted Stairs success rate (0.375). This highlights that temporal depth information is critical for anticipating terrain variations and planning stable footholds during dynamic locomotion.
 - **Effect of Proprioceptive History Length (H1):** Reducing the proprioceptive history length ("PRIOR with shorter H1") resulted in a lower average reward (19.3234). A longer history window (H1=10) was found to be beneficial for implicitly estimating physical properties like ground friction and center-of-mass deviation, thereby enhancing robustness.
 - **Contribution of Landing State Reward:** The absence of the designed landing state reward ("PRIOR w/o landing state reward") resulted in a decrease in mean reward (22.6262) and was qualitatively observed to lead to less stable foot placements (Figure 6). This confirms the effectiveness of these fine-grained rewards in optimizing behavior during the landing transient.

The comprehensive experiments demonstrated that each component within PRIOR contributed complementary and essential performance gains, with the full framework achieving a robust and efficient locomotion policy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PRIOR framework contributes to the field of humanoid locomotion by addressing several challenges simultaneously. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Reduced System Complexity:** PRIOR offers a unified, single-stage reinforcement learning framework that achieves robust terrain traversal with natural gaits. This eliminates the need for complex, multi-stage training pipelines, adversarial discriminators, or extensive real-world calibration, which are common in prior works. This simplification can lower the barrier to entry for researchers and potentially accelerate development in humanoid robotics.
- **Enhanced Reproducibility:** The commitment to open-sourcing the complete PRIOR framework, including its training pipeline, parametric gait generator, and evaluation benchmarks, provides a concrete and reproducible foundation. This promotes transparency and allows other researchers to build upon or compare their work against a well-defined baseline on Isaac Lab.
- **Efficient and Stable Perception:** The utilization of egocentric depth images, combined with self-supervised heightmap reconstruction, offers a drift-free and robust method for terrain perception. The systematic analysis of depth resolution demonstrates a method for optimizing perceptual overhead while maintaining performance, contributing to more efficient real-time robot operation.
- **Stable Gait Generation:** By employing a parametric gait generator that derives reference trajectories from motion capture data via deterministic supervision, PRIOR bypasses the training instabilities (e.g., mode collapse, reward ambiguity) often associated with adversarial motion priors. This approach contributes to more consistent and natural-looking gaits, crucial for deployment in human-centric environments.
- **High-Throughput Training Infrastructure:** The development of Isaac Lab-specific optimizations, such as heterogeneous observation buffer management and render-time preprocessing, significantly boosts training efficiency. These engineering contributions are valuable for leveraging the capabilities of advanced simulation platforms and scaling up RL research.
- **Comprehensive Humanoid Locomotion Baseline:** PRIOR establishes a robust and effective baseline for humanoid locomotion research on the Isaac Lab platform. Its proven ability to achieve 100% traversal success across diverse and challenging terrains, combined with natural gait generation, provides a benchmark for future advancements.
- **Foundation for Sim-to-Real Transfer:** While the current work acknowledges limitations in real-world deployment experiments, the robust simulation performance, coupled with comprehensive domain randomization, lays a foundation for future research in generalizable dynamics adaptation and seamless sim-to-real transfer for physical humanoid robots like the ZERITH Z1.

In conclusion, PRIOR presents a cohesive and effective solution for a key problem in humanoid robotics, contributing both novel architectural components and practical engineering optimizations that simplify the training process while achieving high-performance, natural locomotion.
