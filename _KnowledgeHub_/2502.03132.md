# SPARK: Safe Protective and Assistive Robot Kit

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University developed SPARK, a modular benchmark and toolbox for safe control of high-dimensional humanoid robots. It provides a framework for synthesizing, testing, and deploying safe controllers in simulation and on real hardware, demonstrating robust safety enforcement and real-time performance on a Unitree G1 humanoid.

## Problem

- Humanoid robots pose significant safety risks due to their physical complexity and potential for interaction with dynamic environments, requiring robust safety solutions.
- Existing safe control toolboxes and benchmarks often lack modularity for high-dimensional robots and do not explicitly prioritize or benchmark safety aspects for humanoids.
- Synthesizing and deploying safe controllers for diverse humanoid applications is difficult and inefficient for non-experts, as it often requires re-synthesis for each unique scenario and lacks standardized evaluation.

## Method

- **SPARK** introduces a ==modular framework== decomposing control systems into five core components (Configuration, Agent, Task, Policy, Safety modules) supporting both simulation and real-world deployment.
- It implements five ==energy-function-based safe control algorithms== (SSA, CBF, SSS, PFM, SMA) and provides a suite of configuration options for robot models, dynamics, task objectives, and constraints.
- The framework automates ==hierarchical computation of safety index derivatives== to efficiently support model-based safe control algorithms, enabling real-time operation for complex systems.

## Results

- SSS achieved the highest success rate for whole-body motion tasks, while SMA performed best for fixed-base tasks, with PFM consistently showing the lowest success rates across all benchmarks.
- Dynamic obstacles and increased obstacle density negatively impacted both arm tracking scores and environmental safety scores, demonstrating performance degradation with higher task complexity.
- SPARK achieved real-time operation on a laptop CPU, with control loop times around **0.011s to 0.015s** on the real Unitree G1 robot, demonstrating feasibility for real-time deployment in various scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimization-based safe control methods (SSA, CBF, SSS) generally offer a better balance between motion accuracy and environmental safety for humanoids compared to projection methods like PFM.
> - The framework effectively translates safe control performance from simulation to real-world humanoid robots, showing strong correlations in safety and goal tracking metrics.
> - Integrating real-time human perception (e.g., via Apple Vision Pro) enables humanoid robots to prioritize collision avoidance over task goals in dynamic human-robot interaction scenarios.
