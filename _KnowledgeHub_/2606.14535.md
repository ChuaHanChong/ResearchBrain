# Spatially Conditioned Diffusion Policy: Learning Precise and Robust Manipulation with a Single RGB Camera

> [!summary] Summary
> The Spatially Conditioned Diffusion Policy (SCDP) enables precise and robust robotic manipulation using only a single RGB camera by leveraging evolving end-effector trajectories as dynamic attention anchors. This approach achieves an 87.2% average success rate across 54 simulation tasks and a 73.8% average success rate in real-world scenarios, outperforming multi-camera and depth-based baselines in many challenging manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation via imitation learning often relies on multi-camera setups, including wrist-mounted cameras, for precise and contact-rich interactions.
- Achieving comparable performance with only a single global RGB camera is challenging due to the need to simultaneously capture fine-grained visual details and accurately identify task-relevant regions in complex scenes.
- Existing single-view visuomotor policies often infer visual relevance implicitly or rely on large, pre-trained models, which can be insufficient for fine-grained control and precise spatial attention.

## Method

- Spatially Conditioned Diffusion Policy (**SCDP**) introduces a novel ==spatial conditioning mechanism== within a diffusion-based visuomotor policy, operating solely with a single global RGB camera.
- It leverages intermediate end-effector trajectories as dynamic attention anchors, projecting them onto the image plane to explicitly query spatially selective, multi-scale visual features during the denoising process.
- The multi-scale image encoder and the spatial conditioning module extract both broad scene context and fine-grained local details around the predicted interaction points, which then conditions an action denoising network.

## Results

- SCDP achieved an **87.2%** average success rate across 54 diverse Meta-World and DexArt simulation tasks, outperforming Diffusion Policy (**54.9%**) and DP3 (**82.3%**) which used depth information.
- In real-world experiments, SCDP demonstrated an average success rate of **73.8%** across six tasks, showing robust performance in high-precision (e.g., USB insertion) and distractor-heavy scenarios with a single RGB camera.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of the multi-scale image encoder and dynamic reconstruction horizon, showing superior data efficiency and competitive performance compared to baselines using additional wrist cameras or depth sensors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evolving future end-effector trajectories, when dynamically projected onto the image plane, provide an effective and task-relevant sequence of attention anchors for visuomotor policies.
> - Explicitly sampling multi-scale visual features at these dynamic attention anchors allows the policy to simultaneously capture coarse scene context and fine local details, crucial for precise manipulation from a single-view.
> - Integrating this spatial conditioning into a conditional diffusion process enables robust action generation by focusing computational resources on predicted interaction regions, effectively reducing visual ambiguity and enhancing precision.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Recent advancements in robotic manipulation via imitation learning have largely adopted multi-camera setups, often incorporating wrist-mounted cameras. These configurations are considered a standard for achieving precise and contact-rich interactions, as wrist cameras offer close-range views that provide fine-grained local details and reduce visual ambiguity, allowing policies to focus on immediate task-relevant regions.

However, the field faces a challenge in achieving comparable performance using only a single global RGB camera. This single-view scenario demands that the policy simultaneously capture fine-grained visual cues necessary for precision tasks (e.g., USB insertion) and accurately identify task-relevant areas within complex, unstructured scenes (e.g., grasping an object amidst distractors). Existing visuomotor policy frameworks typically address visual input by compressing observations into global feature embeddings (e.g., Diffusion Policy, BC-Z) or by decomposing them into visual tokens (e.g., ACT, ALOHA Unleashed). These methods often infer visual relevance implicitly, rather than explicitly focusing on fine visual details or selectively attending to specific task-relevant regions.

This research aligns with ongoing efforts to develop efficient visual representations by emphasizing task-relevant areas. Prior works have explored various strategies:
- **Semantic Keypoints and Language Models:** SKIL utilizes segmentation masks to identify semantic keypoints, while OTTER employs Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to attend to language-relevant regions. While effective, these approaches often rely on large, pre-trained models, which can complicate the pipeline and potentially be suboptimal for the fine-grained control required in manipulation tasks.
- **3D Point-Cloud Representations:** DP3 and RISE incorporate 3D point-cloud data to provide richer geometric information in single-camera setups. However, the information from these representations is typically summarized into global feature embeddings, which may not explicitly highlight task-relevant visual details or focus on specific interaction regions.
- **Multi-Scale Visual Representations:** Inspired by architectures like Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN), other works have explored hierarchical representations across 2D and 3D domains to capture information at diverse granularities, leveraging lower-resolution features for semantic context and higher-resolution features for fine details.

The work presented in this paper, Spatially Conditioned Diffusion Policy (SCDP), distinguishes itself by proposing a novel spatial conditioning mechanism within a diffusion-based visuomotor policy. Instead of relying on multi-camera setups, complex pretrained models, or external perception modules, SCDP leverages intermediate end-effector trajectories as dynamic attention anchors. This allows the policy to explicitly extract spatially selective, multi-scale visual features from a single RGB image, thereby addressing the dual challenges of precise detail capture and robust task-relevant region identification in a unified framework.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable precise and robust robotic manipulation solely using a single RGB camera, eliminating the need for wrist-mounted cameras or other auxiliary visual inputs.

The motivation for this objective stems from the prevalent use of multi-camera setups in current visual imitation learning, particularly the reliance on wrist-mounted cameras. While wrist cameras provide critical close-up views for detailed observation and reduced visual ambiguity, their necessity adds complexity and cost to robotic systems. A single global-view camera offers a simpler, more scalable, and cost-effective alternative, but it introduces significant challenges for manipulation tasks that demand high precision and robustness to scene clutter.

Specifically, the authors identify two core challenges for single-camera manipulation:
1. **Capturing fine-grained interaction details:** Tasks like USB insertion require meticulous alignment and contact-rich interaction, necessitating the policy to perceive subtle visual cues from a distance.
2. **Identifying task-relevant regions:** In unstructured or cluttered environments, the policy must effectively filter out distractors and focus its attention on the specific object or area relevant to the task, such as grasping a particular cup handle amidst other items.

Existing visual imitation learning methods often fall short in explicitly addressing these challenges within a single-camera context. Policies that compress observations into global feature embeddings or token representations typically infer visual relevance implicitly, which can be insufficient for tasks requiring precise spatial attention or fine-grained visual discrimination.

The key insight driving SCDP is that the future end-effector trajectory, when projected onto the image plane, provides an inherently task-relevant sequence of attention anchors. This trajectory evolves dynamically during the action prediction process and directly indicates where the robot's end-effector is expected to interact with the environment. By leveraging these evolving intermediate action trajectories within the diffusion loop, the policy can dynamically:
- Directly query visual features at these anticipated interaction points, thereby focusing computation on task-relevant regions.
- Combine this spatially conditioned attention with multi-scale image encoding to simultaneously capture broad scene context and fine local details around the end-effector's path.

Therefore, the main contributions and objectives of the paper are:
- To propose Spatially Conditioned Diffusion Policy (SCDP) for precise and robust manipulation from a single global view, without wrist-mounted cameras.
- To introduce a novel scene conditioning mechanism that uses evolving action trajectories to sample point-wise visual features at task-relevant locations.
- To integrate multi-scale image encodings to capture both coarse scene context and fine-grained visual details essential for precise interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Spatially Conditioned Diffusion Policy (SCDP) framework is designed to operate with a single RGB camera by explicitly conditioning the action prediction process on task-relevant visual features. The architecture comprises three primary components: a multi-scale image encoder, a spatial conditioning module, and an action denoising network, all integrated within a conditional diffusion framework.

#### 3.1. Multi-Scale Image Encoder

This component is responsible for processing the raw RGB image input to extract a rich set of hierarchical visual features. The objective is to capture both broad scene context and fine-grained visual details essential for precise manipulation.
- **Backbone Architecture:** ResNet-18 is employed as the primary backbone.
- **Feature Extraction:** The encoder extracts feature activations from the last residual block of each stage of the ResNet-18, denoted as C_i for i ∈ {1..., 5}. These stages produce feature maps with effective strides of 4, 4, 8, 16, and 32 relative to the input resolution. This design naturally creates a hierarchy where earlier stages retain higher spatial resolution and finer details, while deeper stages capture more abstract semantic information.
- **Channel Alignment:** Independent 3x3 convolutions are applied to each feature map C_i. This step aligns their channel dimensions to a common depth 'd' and contributes to smoothing local features, which is beneficial for subsequent denoising operations.
- **Output:** The result is a set of multi-scale feature maps F = {F_1..., F_5}, where each F_i ∈ R^(d × H_i × W_i) represents the feature map corresponding to its ResNet stage.

#### 3.2. Spatial Conditioning Module

This is the central component of SCDP, implementing the core idea of using end-effector trajectories as dynamic attention anchors. The module operates in three steps during each denoising iteration of the diffusion process:
1. **Trajectory Reconstruction:**
 - **Action Parameterization:** Actions are parameterized as relative displacements from the current robot state.
 - **Reconstruction Horizon:** A reconstruction horizon 's' (where s ≤ h, the full prediction horizon) is defined. This determines how many future end-effector points are included in the trajectory estimate.
 - **Trajectory Calculation:** Given an intermediate action trajectory A_k = a_1:h at a specific denoising step 'k', the module reconstructs a sequence of 3D end-effector (EE) positions P_0:s = (P_0, P_1..., P_s). P_0 is the current EE position. Subsequent points P_i are derived cumulatively from the current position and the translational displacements (ΔP_i) extracted from the action sequence: P_i = P_(i-1) + ΔP_i.
 - **Dynamic Attention:** P_0 remains constant, fixed by the current robot state. However, the subsequent trajectory points P_1:s are iteratively updated throughout the diffusion denoising process. This dynamic update allows the model's spatial attention to continuously adapt to the evolving estimate of future robot motion.
2. **Trajectory Projection:**
 - **3D to 2D:** The reconstructed 3D end-effector trajectory points P_0:s, expressed in the robot base frame, are transformed into the camera coordinate frame using the camera's extrinsic matrix ([R|t]). These camera-centric 3D points are then projected onto the 2D image plane using the camera's intrinsic matrix K, yielding a sequence of 2D pixel coordinates (u_i, v_i).
 - **Attention Anchors:** These (u_i, v_i) pixel coordinates serve as "attention anchors" on the image plane, indicating the precise locations where the policy should extract visual features.
3. **Point-wise Feature Sampling:**
 - **Feature Extraction:** For each projected attention anchor (u_i, v_i) and for each multi-scale feature map F_n (from the encoder), a point-wise feature vector f_i,n ∈ R^d is sampled using bilinear interpolation. This ensures that features are extracted precisely at the estimated interaction points across different resolutions.
 - **Spatial Context Vector Aggregation:** The sampled feature vectors are then aggregated into a single "spatial context vector" F_bar. This aggregation first involves average pooling along the temporal axis (across all trajectory points 'i') for each feature map resolution 'n', yielding ¯f_n = (1/(s+1)) * Σ_(i=0 to s) f_i,n. Finally, these pooled multi-resolution features (¯f_1..., ¯f_5) are concatenated to form the compact spatial context vector F_bar ∈ R^(5d). This vector encapsulates multi-scale visual information localized around the end-effector's predicted trajectory.

#### 3.3. Diffusion Process (Action Denoising Network)

The policy is framed as a conditional diffusion process, building upon the Diffusion Policy (DP) framework.
- **Denoising Objective:** The action denoising network's primary role is to predict the noise ϵ that was added to a clean action trajectory A_0, given a noisy action trajectory A_k, the extracted spatial context, and the current diffusion step.
- **Conditioning:** The denoising network (ϵ_θ) is explicitly conditioned on:
 - The dynamic spatial context vector F_k (obtained from the spatial conditioning module at denoising step 'k').
 - The current robot state.
 - The current diffusion step 'k'.
 - This differs from standard DP, which typically conditions on a fixed global visual observation.
- **Training Loss:** The network is trained to minimize the L2 loss between the predicted noise ϵ_θ and the true noise ϵ: L = E_(k, A_0, ϵ) ||ϵ - ϵ_θ(A_k, F_k, k)||^2.
- **Network Backbone:** A conditional U-Net is used as the backbone for the action denoising network.
- **Conditioning Mechanism:** The spatial context vector F_k is integrated into the U-Net via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM), allowing it to modulate the activations of the denoising network.
- **Inference:** During execution, the DDIM scheduler is employed for efficient action generation, allowing high-quality trajectories to be reconstructed with a reduced number of denoising steps.

#### 3.4. Training Details

- **Benchmarks:** Meta-World (parallel-gripper tasks of varying difficulty) and DexArt (dexterous manipulation with articulated objects).
- **Demonstrations:** 20 scripted expert demonstrations for Meta-World tasks, and 100 RL-expert demonstrations for DexArt tasks.
- **Training Epochs:** 1,000 epochs for Meta-World; 3,000 epochs for DexArt and real-world experiments.
- **Hyperparameters:** Batch size 128, Adam optimizer, learning rate 1e-4 with a cosine scheduler, weight decay 1e-6.
- **Default Settings:** Reconstruction horizon 's' = 8, prediction horizon = 16, observation horizon = 2, action horizon = 8. Denoising steps are 100 during training, 10 for real-world inference, and 16 for simulation inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive evaluations in both simulation and real-world environments to demonstrate the capabilities of SCDP.

#### 4.1. General Performance across Diverse Manipulation Tasks (Table 1)

- SCDP demonstrated high overall performance across various manipulation tasks from the Meta-World and DexArt benchmarks using only 20 demonstrations and a single global camera.
- It achieved over 80% success rates in every Meta-World difficulty group (Easy, Medium, Hard, Very Hard).
- Notably, SCDP showed greater performance gains on more challenging tasks: on the "Hard" group, SCDP achieved 82.5% success (compared to 60.3% for DP3 and 16.7% for DP), and on the "Very Hard" group, it achieved 89.7% (compared to 82.7% for DP3 and 46.0% for DP).
- SCDP also attained the highest success rate on DexArt tasks (62.4%), which involve dexterous manipulation of articulated objects.
- The overall average success rate across all 54 tasks was 87.2%, outperforming DP3 (82.3%) and DP (54.9%).

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Spatial Conditioning (Table 2)

- Focusing on Meta-World "Hard" tasks, SCDP was compared against methods designed to extract task-relevant visual information (SKIL, OTTER) and a general visual conditioning baseline (DP).
- SCDP achieved an average success rate of 82.5% ± 11.5, significantly outperforming OTTER (47.2% ± 14.5), SKIL (27.5% ± 33.7), and DP (16.7% ± 7.0).
- This result suggests that SCDP's spatial conditioning mechanism provides effective and consistent visual representations for challenging single-view manipulation tasks.

#### 4.3. Single RGB View Competitiveness (Table 3)

- On Meta-World "Hard" tasks, SCDP (using a single RGB camera) achieved an average success rate of 82.5% ± 11.5.
- This performance was comparable to, and in terms of average success rate, higher than, DP equipped with an additional wrist-view camera (74.2% ± 29.4) and DP3 leveraging depth observations (60.3% ± 38.0). SCDP also exhibited the lowest standard deviation.
- These findings indicate that SCDP can achieve competitive performance with a significantly simpler visual input setup.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Meta-World "Hard" group)

- **Data Efficiency (Fig. 3):** SCDP demonstrated superior data efficiency, achieving higher success rates than DP and DP3 across varying numbers of demonstrations (10, 20, 30, 50). SCDP showed more consistent improvement and reached a higher performance ceiling as the number of demonstrations increased.
- **Effects of Multi-Scale Image Encoder (Fig. 4b):** Using all five feature map scales (F1–F5) yielded the best performance (82.5% success), an improvement of 13.9 percentage points over using only high-level semantic features (F5). This supports the utility of multi-scale information for precise action generation.
- **Effects of Reconstruction Horizon (Fig. 4a):** Performance consistently improved with a longer reconstruction horizon 's', increasing from 75.8% (s=0, static end-effector point) to 83.9% (s=12). The default 's=8' achieved near-optimal performance (82.5%). This confirms that incorporating future trajectory points enriches task-relevant context.
- **Choice of Visual Backbone (Fig. 4c):** ResNet-18 (82.5%) and ResNet-34 (85.0%) performed well. ViT-S/16 (31.7%) and CLIP-pretrained ResNet-50 (16.9%) performed significantly worse, likely due to ViT's weaker locality bias with limited data and CLIP's high-level semantic bias. Adding depth information to ResNet-18 (80.3%) did not improve performance over RGB-only ResNet-18, supporting the RGB-based design choice.

#### 4.5. Real-World Experiments (Table 4)

SCDP was evaluated on six real-world tasks, including high-precision, geometry-aware grasping, long-horizon, and distractor-robustness scenarios, using a single global-view camera.
- **High-Precision Manipulation:** For tasks like USB Insertion (Grasp: 85%, Insert: 40%, Push: 30%) and Battery Insertion (Grasp: 100%, Insert: 70%, Push: 55%), SCDP demonstrated the ability to perform precise, contact-rich interactions. This was attributed to leveraging reconstructed end-effector trajectories for attention and multi-scale visual features for fine-grained local details, allowing focus on anticipated interaction regions. Baselines largely failed the critical "Insert" and "Push" stages of these tasks.
- **Distractor Robustness:** In scenarios with unseen distractors or cluttered environments, SCDP showed robust performance. For Cup Handle Grasping, success rates were 95% (original) and 85% (distractor); for Cup Wall Grasping, 95% (original) and 80% (distractor). In the Push Cube task, SCDP maintained 75% success with distractors and 70% in cluttered scenes, while DP's performance dropped from 90% to 50% in both conditions. This robustness is attributed to SCDP's mechanism of focusing on predicted end-effector trajectory regions, reducing sensitivity to irrelevant visual clutter.
- **Long-Horizon Task:** SCDP achieved an average success rate of 70% on the Long-Horizon task in a cluttered setting, outperforming all baselines (DP 50.3%, DP3 35.3%, OTTER 31.8%, SKIL 43.2% across all tasks).
- **Overall Real-World Performance:** Across all real-world tasks and conditions, SCDP achieved an average success rate of 73.8%, demonstrating its effectiveness and robustness in practical settings.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research introduces the Spatially Conditioned Diffusion Policy (SCDP), demonstrating a method for achieving precise and robust robotic manipulation using only a single RGB camera. This work represents a notable contribution to visual imitation learning by showing that the reliance on multi-camera setups, particularly wrist-mounted cameras, may not be a prerequisite for high-performance manipulation.

The significance of these findings stems from several aspects:
- **Hardware Simplification and Cost Reduction:** By enabling effective manipulation with a single RGB camera, SCDP offers a path to simplifying robotic hardware configurations, reducing system complexity, and lowering deployment costs. This could broaden the accessibility of advanced robotic manipulation to a wider range of research labs and industrial applications.
- **Enhanced Practicality and Scalability:** Eliminating the need for multiple cameras and their associated calibration complexities can lead to more practical and scalable robot learning systems, especially in varied real-world environments.
- **Novel Spatial Conditioning Mechanism:** The core innovation of using intermediate action trajectories as dynamic spatial queries for multi-scale visual features provides an effective inductive bias. This mechanism allows the policy to explicitly focus on task-relevant regions while simultaneously capturing both broad scene context and fine-grained visual details, addressing a key challenge in single-view learning.
- **Improved Data Efficiency and Robustness:** The demonstrated superior data efficiency in simulation and robustness to visual distractors in real-world scenarios indicate that SCDP can learn more effectively from limited demonstrations and operate reliably in unstructured environments. This contributes to developing more adaptable and reliable robotic agents.
- **Advancement in Single-View Visuomotor Control:** The performance achieved by SCDP, which is comparable to or surpasses baselines using more complex visual inputs (e.g., wrist cameras or depth sensors), highlights the potential of single-camera learning as a viable and powerful alternative for various robot manipulation tasks.

Despite its demonstrated effectiveness, the authors acknowledge certain limitations:
- **Assumption of End-Effector Proximity:** SCDP's core assumption is that task-relevant visual information for manipulation lies near the end-effector trajectory. This may restrict its applicability to tasks involving tool-use or indirect interactions where critical contacts or points of interest occur away from the end-effector's immediate path.
- **Vulnerability to Occlusion:** In environments with heavy occlusion, the ability to extract reliable visual features from the attention region along the trajectory could be compromised, potentially limiting performance.

Future work could address these limitations by exploring extensions to the attention mechanism, allowing it to attend to regions beyond the direct end-effector trajectory, or by integrating complementary sensing modalities (e.g., force, tactile feedback) to provide additional robustness in occluded or complex interaction scenarios. Overall, SCDP presents a step towards more efficient and practical robotic manipulation systems.
