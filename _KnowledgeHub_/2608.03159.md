# Accelerating Human-Aware Robot Trajectory Generation via Diffusion and Consistency Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces an accelerated motion planning framework that generates human-aware, constraint-satisfying, and smooth robot trajectories. It leverages conditional diffusion models and consistency distillation to achieve a 98% reduction in inference time while a joint-weighted jerk regularization maintains trajectory smoothness.

## Problem

- Generating real-time, human-aware, and constraint-satisfying robot trajectories, especially for non-redundant manipulators, is computationally demanding.
- Conventional iterative diffusion models for trajectory generation suffer from long inference times, hindering real-time human-robot interaction (HRI).
- Ensuring intrinsic smoothness in generated robot trajectories without additional post-processing remains a challenge for many planning methods.

## Method

- A ==conditional diffusion model== learns distributions of human-aware, constraint-satisfying joint-space trajectories, conditioned on task details and obstacles.
- **Consistency distillation** is applied to the diffusion model to drastically reduce inference time by enabling few-step trajectory generation.
- A ==joint-weighted jerk regularization== term is integrated into the training loss to inherently improve trajectory smoothness and mitigate jerk increases during distillation.

## Results

- Consistency distillation reduced trajectory generation inference time by approximately **98%**, producing **150** candidate trajectories in under **100 ms** compared to **5.7 seconds** for the full diffusion model.
- The jerk regularization term decreased joint jerk by **36.89%** and end-effector jerk by **36.25%** in the full diffusion model, improving trajectory smoothness without affecting success rates.
- For the accelerated consistency model, jerk regularization significantly mitigated jerk increase, resulting in only a **9.6%** joint jerk increase and a **13.7%** end-effector jerk decrease relative to its teacher, contrasting with substantial increases in the unregularized consistency model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditional diffusion models can effectively learn to generate complex, human-aware, and constraint-satisfying trajectories for robot manipulators.
> - Consistency distillation enables a dramatic reduction in diffusion model inference time (approximately 98%), making it suitable for real-time human-robot interaction.
> - Integrating a joint-weighted jerk regularization term during training is essential to achieve intrinsically smooth trajectories and prevent jerk degradation when accelerating models via distillation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of robot manipulators into shared human workspaces necessitates advanced motion planning capabilities that prioritize safety, feasibility, and human acceptance. This work is situated within the field of human-robot interaction (HRI), specifically addressing the challenge of generating suitable robot trajectories in close proximity to humans.

Traditional motion planning approaches, such as graph-search, sampling-based (e.g., RRT, RRT*), and optimization-based methods, have been foundational in robot trajectory generation. While these methods can produce feasible paths, they often face limitations in computational efficiency, particularly in complex, high-dimensional environments or when a multitude of constraints (e.g., collision avoidance, self-collision avoidance, singularity avoidance) must be simultaneously considered. This becomes particularly challenging for manipulators with limited kinematic redundancy, where additional requirements cannot be easily handled as null-space secondary tasks. Sampling-based planners like RRT and RRT* can be computationally demanding for repeated trajectory generation, and optimization-based methods can struggle with the increasing complexity of non-linear constraints and real-time demands.

In recent years, diffusion models have emerged as a promising avenue in robot motion planning. They demonstrate an ability to learn complex trajectory distributions and generate diverse trajectory candidates. However, a notable limitation of conventional diffusion models is their iterative denoising process, which typically requires a large number of sampling steps, leading to extended inference times that can hinder their application in real-time HRI scenarios. Some prior work has explored using diffusion for learning trajectory priors and cost-guided posterior sampling or for hierarchical manipulation tasks. Efforts have also been made to reduce inference time, such as through imposing consistency constraints for one-step action generation.

This research contributes to this landscape by proposing a framework that leverages the generative capabilities of diffusion models for human-aware, constrained motion planning, while directly addressing the issue of high inference time through consistency distillation. Furthermore, it incorporates a mechanism to improve trajectory smoothness, a factor crucial for physical executability and human perception, which is often a post-processing step or a secondary consideration in many planning frameworks. By focusing on non-redundant manipulators and complex HRI constraints, this study aims to advance the real-time applicability of learning-based motion planning in safety-critical environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an accelerated motion planning framework for robot manipulators that can generate human-aware, constraint-satisfying, and smooth joint-space trajectories in real-time or near real-time for Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) environments.

The motivation behind this work stems from several challenges inherent in deploying robot manipulators in shared workspaces with humans:

- **Safety and Feasibility in HRI:** As robots increasingly operate in close proximity to humans, ensuring safety through collision avoidance (with humans and the environment) and self-collision avoidance becomes paramount. Concurrently, trajectories must be kinematically feasible and physically executable by the robot.
- **Challenges with Non-Redundant Manipulators:** For a non-redundant manipulator with a fully specified end-effector (EE) pose (e.g., a 6-DoF robot reaching a 6D pose), incorporating additional requirements like collision avoidance is difficult. Kinematic redundancy typically allows secondary tasks to be handled in the null-space; however, this option is constrained or unavailable for non-redundant systems. This necessitates a planning approach that can inherently generate trajectories satisfying multiple constraints simultaneously, even without null-space maneuverability.
- **Computational Limitations of Conventional Methods:** Traditional motion planning algorithms (graph-search, sampling-based, optimization-based) can become computationally intensive when tasked with solving complex problems involving numerous constraints and high-dimensional configuration spaces. This computational burden often renders them unsuitable for real-time HRI scenarios where quick and repeated trajectory generation is required.
- **Inference Time of Diffusion Models:** While diffusion models show promise in learning trajectory distributions and generating diverse candidates, their iterative denoising process typically requires many sampling steps, resulting in long inference times. This limitation impedes their practical application in time-critical HRI.
- **Trajectory Smoothness:** Generated robot trajectories need to be smooth to ensure physical executability, minimize actuator wear, reduce energy consumption, and be perceived as acceptable by human users. Jerk, representing the rate of change of acceleration, is a key metric for smoothness. Many planning methods, especially sampling-based ones, may produce inherently non-smooth paths that require additional post-processing for smoothing, further increasing computational overhead.

To address these motivations, the study sets forth the following key objectives:
1. To develop a constrained motion planning formulation capable of addressing secondary-task requirements (like collision avoidance) even under limited kinematic redundancy.
2. To establish a diffusion-based framework that learns to generate constraint-satisfying joint-space trajectories conditioned on initial state, goal pose, and obstacle information.
3. To significantly reduce the inference time of iterative diffusion sampling, making it suitable for faster applications, through consistency distillation.
4. To intrinsically improve the smoothness of generated trajectories by incorporating a joint-weighted jerk regularization term directly into the model's training objective.

By achieving these objectives, the research aims to provide a more efficient, safe, and user-friendly solution for robot motion planning in dynamic human-robot collaborative environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a multi-stage approach, combining data generation, a conditional diffusion model, constraint-guided sampling, consistency distillation for acceleration, and a novel jerk regularization technique for trajectory smoothness.

#### 3.1. Constrained Planning Formulation and Trajectory Dataset Generation

The problem is formulated as finding a joint-space trajectory for a 6-DoF manipulator, where the end-effector (EE) must reach a target near a human hand while avoiding collisions.
- **Robot Model:** A 6-DoF manipulator (e.g., UR5) is considered, where a fully specified EE pose offers limited kinematic redundancy.
- **Goal Configuration:** The desired EE goal pose (position $\mathbf{x}_g$ and orientation $\mathbf{R}_g$) is converted into joint-space goal candidates using numerical Inverse Kinematics (IK). A damped least-squares method is used, and the joint estimate is updated iteratively. Among collision- and self-collision-free IK candidates, the one maximizing manipulability, defined as $\sqrt{\text{det}(\mathbf{J}(\boldsymbol{\xi})\mathbf{J}(\boldsymbol{\xi})^\top)}$, is selected as the final goal configuration $\mathbf{q}_{T-1}$.
- **Trajectory Generation for Dataset:** Rapidly-exploring Random Tree (RRT) and RRT* algorithms are used to generate initial collision-free joint-space paths between the initial and selected goal joint configurations. These paths are then resampled into fixed-length trajectories with $T$ waypoints (where $\mathbf{q}_0$ is the initial state and $\mathbf{q}_{T-1}$ is the goal state). Each resampled trajectory is re-validated for joint limits, environment collisions (with human arm, torso, and other obstacles), and self-collisions.
- **Dataset Structure:** Each dataset sample comprises the initial joint configuration, the desired EE goal pose, an obstacle representation, and the corresponding fixed-length, constraint-satisfying joint-space trajectory generated by RRT/RRT*. A total of 51,000 such datasets were generated (2:8 RRT:RRT* ratio), split into training (42,000), validation (5,000), and testing (500) sets.

#### 3.2. Conditional Diffusion Model

A conditional diffusion model is trained to learn the distribution of feasible joint-space trajectories.
- **Conditioning Features:** The model is conditioned on:
 - Initial joint configuration.
 - Desired EE goal pose: Encoded as a 9-dimensional vector (3D position and first two column vectors of orientation matrix).
 - Obstacle representation: A set of capsule features, each defined by two endpoints and a radius ($\mathbf{p}_{i,1}, \mathbf{p}_{i,2}, \rho_i \in \mathbb{R}^7$). These are processed by a PointNet-style set encoder (shared two-layer MLP + symmetric max pooling) to yield a fixed-dimensional global obstacle feature.
- **Architecture:** The backbone is a temporal U-Net, which processes the noised joint trajectory through residual 1D convolutional blocks with downsampling and upsampling. The concatenated condition features are projected by a condition MLP and added to a sinusoidal diffusion timestep embedding, then injected into all residual temporal blocks.
- **Training:** The forward diffusion process corrupts clean trajectories by adding Gaussian noise over $K$ timesteps. The denoising network $\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_D^\theta$ is trained to predict this injected noise from the noised trajectory, diffusion timestep, and task condition. The training loss is the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the predicted noise and the actual injected noise.

#### 3.3. Constraint-Guided Sampling (for Diffusion Model Inference)

During inference, the trained diffusion model generates trajectory candidates via a reverse denoising process, starting from Gaussian noise.
- **Reverse Process:** At each step, the denoising network predicts the noise, and a mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta$ for the reverse transition is computed. The next trajectory state is sampled by adding Gaussian noise to this mean.
- **Guidance Mechanism:** To improve collision avoidance, the predicted mean $\boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta$ is corrected using the gradient of a differentiable constraint cost $C$ (covering environment and self-collision costs). The corrected mean $\tilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_\theta = \boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta - \eta_k \nabla_{\boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta} C(\boldsymbol{\mu}_\theta)$ guides the denoising process towards lower collision costs while staying close to the learned prior.

#### 3.4. Consistency Distillation for Fast Trajectory Generation

To reduce the high inference time of iterative diffusion sampling, consistency distillation is applied.
- **Consistency Model:** A consistency model, with the same temporal U-Net backbone, is trained to directly map noised trajectory states from different diffusion timesteps to a consistent clean trajectory estimate in a few steps.
- **Training Procedure:** An online student consistency model ($f_\theta$) and an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) target consistency model ($f_{\theta^-}$) are maintained. A pretrained diffusion model ($\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_D^\Phi$) serves as a teacher. The student is trained to ensure that its clean estimate from a highly corrupted noised trajectory matches the reference clean estimate produced by the EMA target network from an intermediate noised state derived using the teacher model. The consistency distillation loss ($L_{CD}$) minimizes the discrepancy between these estimates.
- **Few-Step Sampling (for Consistency Model Inference):** During inference, the consistency model performs few-step sampling over a selected sequence of diffusion timesteps. Starting from Gaussian noise, it predicts a clean trajectory estimate at each step, fixes the first waypoint to the initial configuration, applies constraint guidance, and then re-noises the estimate to the next timestep. This process significantly reduces the number of required steps.

#### 3.5. Joint-Weighted Jerk Regularization

To enhance trajectory smoothness, a jerk regularization term is incorporated into the training loss functions.
- **Jerk Calculation:** Discrete joint jerk at each waypoint is approximated using a third-order finite difference formula.
- **Weighting Factors:**
 - **Temporal Weight ($w_{time,t}$):** Assigns a larger penalty to waypoints closer to the goal configuration to improve smoothness near the goal.
 - **Manipulability-Based Weight ($w_{manip,t}$):** Penalizes joint variations more heavily in configurations with low manipulability, helping suppress undesired movements near singularities.
 - **Joint-Wise Weight ($\mathbf{W}_{joint,t}$):** Penalizes each joint based on its translational sensitivity at the EE, derived from the column norms of the Jacobian.
- **Loss Integration:** The weighted jerk loss term ($L_{jerk}$) is added to both the diffusion model's MSE loss ($L_{diffusion} = L_{MSE} + \lambda_{jerk}L_{jerk}$) and the consistency model's loss ($L_{consistency} = L_{CD} + \lambda_{jerk}L_{jerk}$), with $\lambda_{jerk}$ as a weighting coefficient.

#### 3.6. Simulation Setup

The methodology was validated using a 6-DoF UR5 robot model in a simulation environment featuring collision primitives for a human torso, upper arm, and forearm, along with other obstacles. Trajectories were deemed successful if the EE pose reached the goal within 5 cm position error and 10° orientation error. Performance metrics included inference time, success rate, manipulability, joint jerk, EE jerk, and EE path length.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents simulation-based validation results for both the diffusion model and the consistency model, with and without the proposed jerk regularization.

#### 4.1. Diffusion Inference Results (Table I)

- **Inference Time:** Both the diffusion model trained with jerk regularization and the one without it exhibited comparable average inference times of approximately 5.7 seconds for generating 150 trajectory candidates.
- **Success Rate:** The model with jerk regularization achieved a 99.60% success rate, while the model without regularization achieved 98.80%. Both models demonstrated a high capability in generating trajectories that satisfy the specified planning constraints.
- **Manipulability and EE Path Length:** No significant differences were observed in manipulability at the goal configuration (0.0931 vs. 0.0930) or end-effector path length (1.86 m vs. 1.87 m) between the two models.
- **Impact of Jerk Regularization:** The diffusion model trained with jerk regularization significantly reduced both joint jerk and EE jerk. Specifically, it achieved a 36.89% reduction in joint jerk (from $9.57 \times 10^2$ to $6.04 \times 10^2 \text{ rad}^2/\text{s}^6$) and a 36.25% reduction in EE jerk (from $3.20 \times 10^2$ to $2.04 \times 10^2 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}^6$). This indicates that jerk regularization effectively improved trajectory smoothness without compromising other key performance indicators like success rate or path efficiency.

#### 4.2. Consistency Inference Results (Table II)

- **Inference Time:** Consistency distillation dramatically reduced the average inference time. The consistency model with jerk regularization achieved 95.79 ms, and the one without achieved 93.59 ms. This represents approximately a 98% reduction in inference time compared to the full diffusion inference, allowing the generation of 150 trajectory candidates in less than 100 ms.
- **Success Rate:** Both consistency models maintained high success rates (98.00% with jerk regularization and 100.00% without).
- **Manipulability and EE Path Length:** Similar to the diffusion models, the consistency models showed comparable values for manipulability (0.0931 vs. 0.0939) and EE path length (1.87 m vs. 1.91 m).
- **Impact of Jerk Regularization in Consistency Models:** When comparing the consistency models to their respective teacher diffusion models:
 - Without jerk regularization, the consistency model showed a substantial increase in jerk compared to its teacher: joint jerk increased by 264%, and EE jerk increased by 219%.
 - In contrast, the consistency model with jerk regularization exhibited a more contained change relative to its teacher: joint jerk increased by 9.6%, and EE jerk decreased by 13.7%. This finding suggests that the jerk regularization penalty effectively mitigated the notable increase in jerk that would otherwise occur during the distillation process, preserving trajectory smoothness in the accelerated model.

In summary, the results demonstrate that the proposed framework successfully generates human-aware, constraint-satisfying trajectories. Consistency distillation effectively reduces inference time by 98%, making the approach significantly faster. The joint-weighted jerk regularization term proves effective in improving trajectory smoothness for both the full diffusion model and, critically, in preserving smoothness during the acceleration through consistency distillation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotics by addressing several critical challenges in human-robot interaction (HRI) and advanced motion planning.

**Enabling Faster and Safer HRI:** The most significant impact of this work is the substantial reduction in trajectory generation inference time. By leveraging consistency distillation, the framework can generate 150 trajectory candidates in under 100 milliseconds. This speed is crucial for real-time HRI applications, where robots need to react quickly and adaptively to human movements and changing environments while maintaining safety. The ability to rapidly propose multiple constraint-satisfying trajectories allows for more dynamic and responsive robot behavior, which is essential for collaborative tasks.

**Addressing Constraints for Non-Redundant Manipulators:** The proposed constrained motion planning formulation specifically addresses the difficulty of simultaneously handling multiple constraints (collision avoidance, self-collision avoidance, and goal reaching) for manipulators with limited kinematic redundancy. This is a common scenario in industrial and service robotics. The framework demonstrates that learning from diverse, pre-generated data can implicitly capture these complex constraint interactions, providing a viable solution where traditional null-space control might be insufficient.

**Improving Trajectory Quality through Inherent Smoothness:** The introduction of a joint-weighted jerk regularization term into the training process represents an advancement in generating physically executable and human-acceptable trajectories. By penalizing abrupt changes in joint acceleration, the method promotes smoother motions, which reduces wear and tear on robot actuators, minimizes energy consumption, and contributes to a perception of safety and predictability by human co-workers. Crucially, the regularization effectively mitigates the typical increase in jerk when distilling models for faster inference, ensuring that efficiency gains do not come at a significant cost to trajectory quality.

**Advancement in Learning-Based Motion Planning:** This study demonstrates a robust application of diffusion models for complex, high-dimensional motion planning problems. By combining the generative power of diffusion models with an effective acceleration technique like consistency distillation and a quality-enhancing regularization term, the research pushes the boundaries of what learning-based methods can achieve in real-world robotic systems.

**Foundations for Future HRI Systems:** While the current work is validated in simulation with static human configurations, it lays a strong foundation for future HRI systems involving dynamic human motion and online perception. The rapid trajectory generation capability is a prerequisite for such complex scenarios. The methodology’s ability to learn from diverse planning data (RRT/RRT*) also suggests potential for generalization to various environments and task requirements.

In summary, this research provides a promising step towards deploying more intelligent, safer, and efficient robots in shared human environments by offering a method for accelerated, human-aware, and smooth trajectory generation, directly addressing key limitations of existing planning and learning-based approaches.
