# MSJoE: Jointly Evolving MLLM and Sampler for Efficient Long-Form Video Understanding

> [!summary] Summary
> MSJoE, a framework developed by researchers from Renmin University, Tongji University, and Xiaomi Inc., introduces a joint evolution approach for Multimodal Large Language Models and a lightweight sampler to enhance long-form video understanding. The system achieves an average accuracy gain of 6.7 to 8.0 points on various video question-answering benchmarks by enabling the MLLM to generate visual queries for efficient key-frame selection and adapting its reasoning to sparse visual inputs.

## Problem

- Efficiently processing long-form videos with MLLMs is challenging due to the quadratic complexity of Transformer attention and the presence of redundant frames in uniform sampling.
- Existing key-frame sampling methods suffer from questions lacking explicit visual cues for retrieval and rely on brittle, non-generalizable heuristic algorithms for frame selection.
- Most trainable samplers are optimized independently, preventing crucial co-adaptation where the MLLM could learn to generate better queries or adapt its reasoning to sparse, selected frames.

## Method

- An MLLM-guided ==query generation== module, utilizing a sparse video preview, generates multiple visually descriptive queries to overcome the insufficiency of raw questions.
- A lightweight, trainable 1D U-Net acts as a key-frame sampler, intelligently transforming a CLIP-based query-frame similarity matrix into diverse and informative sampling probabilities.
- A novel joint reinforcement learning framework co-adapts both the MLLM and the sampler, enabling the MLLM to refine query generation and adapt its reasoning to the sparse frames provided by the sampler.

## Results

- MSJoE consistently outperformed its base MLLM (Qwen-2.5-VL-**7B**) by an average of +6.7 points (with 32 frames) to +8.0 points (with 64 frames) in accuracy across four long-form video QA benchmarks.
- The framework achieved an additional +1.1 points improvement on average compared to the strongest existing trainable sampling baseline, TSPO, with 64 frames.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of MLLM-guided query generation, the learned U-Net sampler, joint MLLM-sampler evolution, and particularly, the difficulty-aware reward for sampler pre-training, which prevented a 3.9 points accuracy drop.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - MLLM-guided generation of multiple, diverse visual queries significantly improves the specificity and effectiveness of key-frame retrieval compared to using raw, abstract questions.
> - A learned sampler, specifically a 1D U-Net, can effectively process query-frame similarity distributions to select a compact set of diverse and relevant key-frames, outperforming heuristic sampling methods.
> - Jointly optimizing both the MLLM and the sampler through reinforcement learning fosters crucial co-adaptation, where each component learns to better support the other's role in achieving accurate long-form video understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has witnessed rapid advancements, enabling impressive performance in various video understanding tasks, including captioning, reasoning, and question answering. However, a significant challenge remains: efficiently processing and understanding **long-form videos**. This paper directly addresses this fundamental bottleneck.

Existing approaches to long-form video understanding with MLLMs generally fall into two categories, each with inherent limitations that MSJoE aims to overcome:

- **Dense or Uniform Sampling Methods:** Models like LLaVA-Video, Qwen2.5-VL, and SlowFast-LLaVA often rely on uniformly sampling frames from a video. While simple, this approach suffers from two critical issues:
 1. **Computational Inefficiency:** The visual context length scales linearly with video duration, but the attention mechanism in Transformers (the backbone of MLLMs) grows quadratically with input length. This makes processing densely sampled long videos computationally prohibitive and memory-intensive.
 2. **Semantic Blindness:** Uniform sampling often includes redundant or uninformative frames while potentially missing critical, sparsely occurring events crucial for answering specific questions. LongVU highlighted this limitation.

- **Key-Frame Sampling Methods:** Recognizing the inefficiency of dense sampling, many works propose selecting a smaller, "key" subset of frames. These can be further divided:
 - **Training-Free Heuristic Methods:** Approaches like VaQuitA, AKS, QFrame, and BOLT leverage CLIP-based similarity to identify frames relevant to a given question. However, this raises two critical sub-challenges:
 - **Q1: Insufficiency of the Question:** The paper argues that a raw question often lacks explicit visual cues, making direct CLIP-based retrieval unreliable. Questions are often interrogative, specifying *what* information is needed rather than *how* it visually appears, leading to insufficient grounding for retrieval.
 - **Q2: Effective Sampling from Scores:** Even with similarity scores, a naive "top-k" strategy often selects redundant frames. Heuristic algorithms are then designed to transform these scores into diverse key-frame sampling weights, but these often require careful, task-specific tuning and lack generalization.
 - **Trainable Sampling Strategies:** More advanced methods like TSPO, K-frames, M-LLM Frame Selector, and ViaRL propose training a separate sampler network to predict key-frame importance. While these address Q2 by learning to sample, they introduce a third major challenge:
 - **Q3: Lack of Collaboration/Joint Evolution:** A critical limitation of most existing trainable samplers is that the MLLM remains frozen during sampler training or is optimized separately. This prevents true co-adaptation. The MLLM cannot learn to generate more effective queries to guide frame selection, nor can it adapt to reason over the sparse, potentially different keyframes provided by the sampler, as MLLMs are typically pre-trained on uniformly sampled videos. The sampler only receives sparse scalar rewards, lacking understanding of the MLLM's specific visual evidence needs.

MSJoE uniquely positions itself by addressing all three challenges (Q1, Q2, Q3) simultaneously. It moves beyond static or independently trained samplers by proposing a novel **joint evolution framework** where the MLLM actively guides query generation and both the MLLM and the sampler are optimized end-to-end through reinforcement learning. This integrated approach aims to bridge the gap between high-level language understanding and low-level visual perception/selection, fostering genuine collaboration between the components.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a highly efficient and accurate framework for understanding long-form videos using multimodal large language models. This is primarily motivated by the inherent limitations of current MLLMs when faced with extended visual sequences, leading to three specific challenges the authors aim to address:

1. **Overcoming the "Insufficiency" of the Question (Q1):** Traditional CLIP-based key-frame selection often relies on the raw question to determine frame relevance. However, questions in video QA are frequently abstract or interrogative, lacking the specific visual descriptive cues necessary for robust frame retrieval. For instance, a question like "Why did the character leave?" doesn't explicitly describe the visual events leading to the departure. The motivation here is to enable the MLLM itself to **reason out multiple, diverse, and visually grounded queries** that can better guide the search for relevant frames. This transforms the single, potentially vague, question into a set of explicit visual "search terms."

2. **Developing an Effective and Learnable Sampling Strategy (Q2):** Once query-frame similarity scores are obtained, simply picking the top-k frames often leads to redundancy or misses broader context. Heuristic sampling algorithms are brittle and don't generalize well across different video types or questions. The motivation is to replace these fixed heuristics with a **trainable, lightweight sampler** that can intelligently transform a query-frame similarity matrix into a compact, informative set of key-frame sampling weights, prioritizing diversity and relevance.

3. **Ensuring True Collaboration through Joint Evolution (Q3):** A critical weakness of prior trainable samplers is their decoupled optimization from the MLLM. A frozen MLLM cannot learn to generate better queries for the sampler, nor can it adapt its reasoning processes to the sparse, key-frame inputs the sampler provides. The core motivation for MSJoE is to establish **bidirectional co-adaptation** between the MLLM and the sampler. This means the MLLM learns to pose questions (queries) that facilitate effective frame selection, while simultaneously learning to reason more effectively from the sparse visual information provided by the sampler. This joint optimization is hypothesized to unlock higher performance by aligning the goals of visual perception and language understanding.

To support this ambitious joint optimization, a practical objective was also to **create a new, challenging long-video QA dataset.** Existing datasets lacked the necessary scale, duration, and multi-hop reasoning complexity required for effectively training and evaluating models like MSJoE, especially with reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MSJoE proposes a novel framework centered on the joint evolution of an MLLM and a lightweight key-frame sampler through reinforcement learning. The methodology can be broken down into its inference pipeline and training pipeline.

#### 3.1. Inference Pipeline

The inference process for MSJoE involves four sequential steps:

1. **MLLM-guided Query Generation:**
 - Initially, a sparse set of `N_init` low-resolution frames is uniformly sampled from the long video, providing a coarse preview.
 - The MLLM (with parameters `θ_MLLM`) takes the original question `q` and these preview frames `F_init` as input.
 - It then generates `N_q` descriptive queries ` {q_j} ` that describe specific visual patterns or events deemed helpful for answering `q`. This step addresses Q1 (Insufficiency) by decomposing the question into more visually actionable concepts.

2. **CLIP-based Similarity Computation:**
 - The entire video is densely sampled at 1 FPS to obtain `N_f` frames.
 - Each generated query `q_j` and each dense frame `f_i` are encoded using a frozen CLIP model (specifically, Clip-ViT-Large-Patch14).
 - Cosine similarity `sim(CLIP_text(q_j), CLIP_image(f_i))` is computed for all query-frame pairs, resulting in a similarity matrix `S ∈ R^(N_q × N_f)`.

3. **Learnable Key-Frame Sampler:**
 - A lightweight, trainable 1D U-Net (with parameters `ϕ`) acts as the sampler. It takes the similarity matrix `S` as input, treating the query axis as channels.
 - The U-Net processes `S` to produce per-frame sampling probabilities `p ∈ R^(N_f)`. This U-Net architecture is chosen for its dense-prediction and local-perception capabilities.
 - Finally, `K` informative key-frames are sampled (without replacement) according to the distribution `p`. These selected frames are then encoded at normal resolution. This step directly addresses Q2 (Sampling) by replacing heuristics with a learned model.

4. **Answer Generation:**
 - The MLLM processes the selected `K` high-resolution key-frames (`F_selected`) along with the original question `q`.
 - The MLLM generates the final answer `a`. Crucially, the sparse video preview used in query generation is *masked* during this step, ensuring a fair comparison to methods that don't use such a preview for final answer inference. The conversation history, including the generated queries, is maintained to provide context.

#### 3.2. Training Pipeline

MSJoE's training pipeline involves a two-stage process: sampler pre-training followed by joint reinforcement learning (RL).

1. **Sampler Pre-Training:**
 - A randomly initialized sampler can introduce instability during early joint RL. To mitigate this, the sampler is pre-trained on the `LongVideoQA-ALL` dataset.
 - The training objective for the sampler is based on the REINFORCE algorithm (Eq. 9), but with a novel **difficulty-aware reward** `A_sampler`.
 - This reward leverages an estimated pass rate `c` (MLLM's accuracy with uniform sampling) for each QA pair:
 - If `c=0` (extremely difficult question), `A_sampler = 10` for correct answers, `0` for incorrect, encouraging discovery of hard-to-find frames.
 - If `c ≠ 0`, `A_sampler = 1/c` for correct answers and `-1/(1-c)` for incorrect, encouraging the sampler to prioritize harder questions and penalizing failures on easier ones. This provides smoother, more informative gradients than a simple binary reward.

2. **Joint RL Training:**
 - After pre-training, both the MLLM and the sampler are jointly optimized end-to-end using reinforcement learning. This is the core mechanism for addressing Q3 (Collaboration).
 - **Reward Design:** The total reward `r` for a sample is composed of three parts:
 - `r_acc`: 0.8 for a correct answer, 0 otherwise (primary signal).
 - `r_format`: 0.1 for a well-formatted response.
 - `r_info`: 0.1 * `(max(s_ji) / min(s_ji) > τ_info)` - an informativeness reward that encourages the MLLM to generate queries producing peaked (focused) similarity distributions, discouraging overly generic queries.
 - **Optimization Algorithms:**
 - **MLLM:** Optimized using the Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm (Eq. 5-8). For each question, `G=8` trajectory samples (different query sets and frame selections) are generated. The MLLM updates its parameters `θ` to maximize an advantage function `A_i` calculated relative to the group's average reward, normalized by standard deviation. This encourages learning from outputs that perform better than average within a group.
 - **Sampler:** Optimized using the REINFORCE algorithm (Eq. 9), where the log probability of selected frames is weighted by `A_sampler` (which is `r_acc` during joint training).

This joint RL approach ensures that the MLLM learns to generate effective queries for the sampler, and simultaneously, the MLLM adapts to reason over the sparse, intelligently selected frames, fostering true co-adaptation.

#### 3.3. New Dataset Construction

To facilitate training and evaluation of long-form video understanding models, the authors collected a new dataset called **LongVideoQA**. The construction pipeline involved four steps:
1. **Dense Captioning:** 2.8k long videos were segmented into coherent scenes, and Gemini-2.5-Flash generated captions for each segment and an overall video summary.
2. **QA Generation:** Gemini-2.5-Pro generated multiple-choice questions and answers by combining segment captions and the overall video summary, targeting cross-event reasoning.
3. **QA Filtering:** A two-stage filtering process, using Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct with varying frame budgets (16 and 256 frames), removed "too easy" (answered >7/8 times with 16 frames) and "too hard/bad" (answered 0/4 times with 256 frames) QA pairs. This resulted in `LongVideoQA-ALL` (~9.4k QA pairs).
4. **Difficulty Labeling and Subset Selection:** Gemini-2.5-Pro ranked the difficulty of QA pairs within each video, creating `LongVideoQA-HARD` (one hardest question per video) for challenging evaluations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on four widely used long-form video understanding benchmarks (VideoMME, LongVideoBench, LVBench, and MLVU) highlight the superior performance and efficiency of MSJoE.

1. **Superior Performance over Baselines:**
 - **Significant Accuracy Gains:** MSJoE consistently outperformed its base MLLM (Qwen-2.5-VL-7B) across all benchmarks, achieving an average accuracy gain of **+6.7 points** with a 32-frame sampling budget and **+8.0 points** with a 64-frame budget. Even with only 16 frames (half the base budget), MSJoE still surpassed the base MLLM by +4 points.
 - **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) in Open-Source:** MSJoE achieved an additional **+1.1 points** improvement on average compared to the strongest existing trainable sampling baseline, TSPO (with 64 frames). It also significantly outperformed heuristic key-frame samplers like BOLT (0.3-3.0 points gain), demonstrating the effectiveness of learned sampling strategies.
 - **Efficiency:** Key-frame sampling methods, including MSJoE, consistently showed better accuracy than dense-frame models (e.g., LongVU, NVILA), supporting the claim that a small subset of frames is sufficient. MSJoE achieved equivalent or higher performance with substantially fewer frames, showcasing its efficiency.

2. **Ablation Study Insights (Answering Q1, Q2, Q3):** The ablation studies provided crucial evidence for the design choices:
 - **Q1 (Insufficiency):** Using the raw question as a query (setting vi) improved accuracy over uniform sampling but remained inferior to multi-query approaches. This confirmed that the question alone is insufficient, and MLLM-guided query generation is beneficial.
 - **Q2 (Sampling):** Naive top-k sampling from a multi-query similarity matrix (setting v) led to significant performance drops. A well-trained sampler (U-Net) was necessary to effectively transform the 2D similarity matrix into diverse and informative sampling weights (setting iv outperformed setting v).
 - **Q3 (Collaboration):** Comparisons between a trained MLLM (Ours, setting ii) and a frozen MLLM (setting iii, iv) clearly showed that **joint evolution is crucial**. A trained MLLM consistently generated better queries and understood sparse frames more effectively. This confirms that co-adaptation is essential for true collaboration.
 - **Sampler Pre-training:** Initializing with a pre-trained sampler significantly improved RL optimization stability and led to consistently better performance, validating its role.
 - **Reward Design:** The informativeness reward for MLLM query generation and, more notably, the difficulty-aware reward for sampler pre-training, proved effective. Removing the difficulty-aware reward caused a substantial 3.9 points average accuracy drop.
 - **Sparse Preview:** Providing even a few preview frames during query generation drastically improved accuracy compared to no visual context, demonstrating its necessity.

3. **Case Study Validation:** A qualitative example vividly illustrated MSJoE's advantages. For a question about dietary habit changes, uniform sampling missed relevant events, and top-k sampling focused redundantly on "eating," leading to incorrect answers. MSJoE, by generating diverse queries (e.g., "toothbrush," "doctor," "blood test") and using its U-Net sampler, identified a coherent narrative (child's tooth issues leading to a dentist visit), allowing the MLLM to correctly infer "tooth decay" as the motivation. This showcased how MSJoE selects temporally diverse and narratively rich key-frames, unlike other methods.

4. **Efficiency Analysis:** While MSJoE introduces additional steps (query reasoning, CLIP, U-Net), the time overhead for CLIP and U-Net was found to be negligible (<1% each). Although the total inference time increased by 30% compared to vanilla uniform sampling, the QA step itself was 10% faster due to prefix caching benefits from query generation. This demonstrates a strong balance between computational cost and accuracy gains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MSJoE framework represents a significant advancement in the field of multimodal large language models and long-form video understanding, with broad potential impact:

1. **Enabling Scalable Long-Form Video Understanding:** By efficiently selecting a compact set of informative key-frames and enabling MLLMs to actively guide this selection, MSJoE addresses a critical bottleneck. This makes it feasible for MLLMs to process and reason over videos that are minutes or even hours long, which was previously computationally intractable or severely degraded performance. This scalability is essential for real-world applications.

2. **Architectural Innovation through Joint Evolution:** The core contribution of MSJoE lies in its novel joint optimization framework. By training both the MLLM and the sampler together through reinforcement learning, it fosters a true co-adaptation. This paradigm shift, where MLLMs actively learn to guide their own visual perception system (the sampler) and adapt to its sparse outputs, is a significant step beyond existing decoupled or static sampling approaches. It demonstrates how "intelligence" can be instilled not just in understanding, but also in efficient information acquisition.

3. **Enhanced MLLM Reasoning Capabilities:** MSJoE empowers MLLMs to engage in more sophisticated reasoning. By generating diverse visual queries, the MLLM explicitly articulates its information needs, leading to more targeted and effective frame retrieval. This self-guided perception loop can lead to more robust and accurate answers, particularly for complex, multi-hop questions requiring reasoning across distributed events in a video.

4. **Valuable Data Resource for the Community:** The newly collected LongVideoQA dataset, especially the challenging `LongVideoQA-HARD` subset, fills a critical gap in the research landscape. It provides a much-needed benchmark for training and evaluating models specifically designed for long-duration videos and intricate reasoning tasks, benefiting future research in this area. The systematic approach to difficulty labeling is particularly useful for curriculum learning strategies.

5. **Generalizable Paradigm for Multimodal AI:** The principles demonstrated by MSJoE—MLLM-guided data selection and joint optimization of perception and reasoning components—are highly generalizable. This framework could inspire similar approaches for other long-form multimodal data (e.g., long audio, textual documents combined with relevant visual aids) or for tasks requiring fine-grained, intelligent data filtering. It offers a scalable direction for future multi-modality systems to handle ever-increasing data volumes effectively.

6. **Practical Applications:** The improved efficiency and accuracy of long-form video understanding have direct implications for various real-world applications. These include:
 - **Content Creation and Analysis:** Efficiently summarizing, indexing, or answering questions about long documentaries, educational videos, or surveillance footage.
 - **Personalized Media Consumption:** Intelligent agents that can quickly pinpoint relevant moments or answer user queries about lengthy video content.
 - **Autonomous Systems:** Enhanced contextual understanding from long visual streams for robots or autonomous vehicles.

In conclusion, MSJoE makes substantial contributions by overcoming key limitations in long-form video understanding. Its innovative joint evolution framework, coupled with its robust empirical validation and valuable dataset, sets a new standard for efficient and intelligent multimodal AI systems.
