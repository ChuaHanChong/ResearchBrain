# AnchorVLA4D: an Anchor-Based Spatial-Temporal Vision-Language-Action Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> AnchorVLA4D, developed by researchers including those from Peking University, enhances robotic manipulation by integrating an initial anchor frame and a lightweight spatial encoder into Vision-Language-Action models. This approach improves spatial awareness and temporal context, achieving a 13.6 percentage point increase in simulation success rates and an 80% success rate across real-world tasks with minimal latency overhead.

## Problem

- Conventional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often rely on single 2D image frames, lacking detailed spatial and geometric information, leading to inaccurate grasping and spatial disorientation.
- Standard VLAs operate without effective memory or temporal context, causing robots to 'forget' objects under occlusion and hindering recovery from repeated errors.
- Existing solutions for 3D perception and temporal understanding often incur significant computational overhead or require specialized, expensive sensors, limiting practical deployment.

## Method

- The AnchorVLA4D model introduces a single anchor frame (the first frame of an episode) alongside the current observation to preserve initial scene context and provide spatiotemporal cues.
- A lightweight spatial encoder (Any4D) is integrated to capture geometric relationships between the anchor and current frames, with its output concatenated into the VLM's hidden states.
- The system uses Qwen2.5VL 3B as the Vision-Language backbone and a 400M ScaleDP diffusion-based model as the action head, generating action chunks conditioned on combined visual-linguistic and spatial representations.

## Results

- AnchorVLA4D achieved an average success rate of 64.6% on the Simpler WidowX benchmark in simulation, a 13.6 percentage point improvement over the baseline VanillaVLA (51.0%).
- In real-world experiments on an xLerobot, the model demonstrated an 80% average success rate across three tasks, surpassing the 50% baseline of π0.5 after limited fine-tuning.
- Ablation studies showed that the anchor mechanism alone improved success rates by 9.4%, and the spatial encoder contributed an additional 4.2% improvement, with a minimal 16% increase in inference latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using the initial scene frame as an anchor effectively provides persistent memory and supports 3D understanding without the computational burden or noise of multiple historical frames.
> - A lightweight, pretrained spatial encoder can significantly enhance VLA performance by exposing geometric relationships, particularly when its parameters are frozen during fine-tuning.
> - The impact of proprioceptive states can vary between simulation and real-world environments; while beneficial in simulation, they can be detrimental in real-world tasks with limited data, potentially hindering visual grounding.
