# AnchorVLA4D: an Anchor-Based Spatial-Temporal Vision-Language-Action Model for Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> AnchorVLA4D, developed by researchers including those from Peking University, enhances robotic manipulation by integrating an initial anchor frame and a lightweight spatial encoder into Vision-Language-Action models. This approach improves spatial awareness and temporal context, achieving a 13.6 percentage point increase in simulation success rates and an 80% success rate across real-world tasks with minimal latency overhead.

## Problem

- Conventional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models often rely on single 2D image frames, lacking detailed spatial and geometric information, leading to inaccurate grasping and spatial disorientation.
- Standard VLAs operate without effective memory or temporal context, causing robots to 'forget' objects under occlusion and hindering recovery from repeated errors.
- Existing solutions for 3D perception and temporal understanding often incur significant computational overhead or require specialized, expensive sensors, limiting practical deployment.

## Method

- The AnchorVLA4D model introduces a single anchor frame (the first frame of an episode) alongside the current observation to preserve initial scene context and provide spatiotemporal cues.
- A lightweight spatial encoder (Any4D) is integrated to capture geometric relationships between the anchor and current frames, with its output concatenated into the VLM's hidden states.
- The system uses Qwen2.5VL 3B as the Vision-Language backbone and a 400M ScaleDP diffusion-based model as the action head, generating action chunks conditioned on combined visual-linguistic and spatial representations.

## Results

- AnchorVLA4D achieved an average success rate of 64.6% on the Simpler WidowX benchmark in simulation, a 13.6 percentage point improvement over the baseline VanillaVLA (51.0%).
- In real-world experiments on an xLerobot, the model demonstrated an 80% average success rate across three tasks, surpassing the 50% baseline of π0.5 after limited fine-tuning.
- Ablation studies showed that the anchor mechanism alone improved success rates by 9.4%, and the spatial encoder contributed an additional 4.2% improvement, with a minimal 16% increase in inference latency.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Using the initial scene frame as an anchor effectively provides persistent memory and supports 3D understanding without the computational burden or noise of multiple historical frames.
> - A lightweight, pretrained spatial encoder can significantly enhance VLA performance by exposing geometric relationships, particularly when its parameters are frozen during fine-tuning.
> - The impact of proprioceptive states can vary between simulation and real-world environments; while beneficial in simulation, they can be detrimental in real-world tasks with limited data, potentially hindering visual grounding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a developing paradigm in robot control, leveraging the extensive image-text understanding capabilities of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to generate robotic actions. These models typically condition action generation on a current visual frame and a natural language instruction, showing promise in generalizing across various manipulation tasks. Notable advancements in this field include models like OpenVLA, $\pi_0$, and CogAct, which have demonstrated generalization by scaling model capacity and improving action representations, exemplified by Octo, RDT-1B, and GR00T N1.

However, standard VLA models face limitations in spatial perception and temporal memory. Their reliance on single 2D image frames often results in a lack of detailed spatial information and an inability to retain past context. This can lead to issues such as forgetting objects under occlusion, spatial disorientation, and repeated errors without successful recovery.

To address limited spatial perception, prior research has incorporated explicit 3D spatial encoders, often utilizing additional sensing modalities such as point clouds (e.g., 3D-VLA, SpatialVLA, PointVLA), depth images (e.g., DepthVLA, QDepthVLA), or fused 2D images with 3D data (e.g., 3DS-VLA, GeoVLA). More recent methods have explored implicit 3D geometry injection (Evo-0), volumetric 3D inference (Avi), graph-based reasoning (GraphCoT-VLA), or 3D Gaussian splatting (RoboSplat). While these approaches enhance spatial understanding, many incur significant computational overhead or necessitate specialized sensors, thereby limiting their practical deployment.

Concurrently, efforts to enhance temporal understanding in VLAs have involved exploiting historical frames or memory mechanisms. Examples include MemoryVLA, which uses a memory bank, ST-VLA, which incorporates multiple past frames, and TraceVLA, which derives visual traces. Other strategies involve embodied chain-of-thought reasoning (ECoT), predicting future image frames (CoT-VLA), or using phase-aware input masking (Long-VLA). These temporal methods, however, often rely on extensive historical data, which can introduce substantial computational burdens.

This work, AnchorVLA4D, is positioned within this landscape as an approach that seeks to improve both spatial and temporal reasoning in VLA policies. It aims to overcome the identified limitations by introducing a single anchor frame (specifically, the first frame of an episode) and a lightweight spatial encoder. This design is intended to preserve initial scene context and expose geometric relationships between frames without requiring additional sensing modalities or imposing significant computational overhead from numerous historical frames, presenting a resource-efficient alternative to many existing methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to address two significant limitations observed in conventional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) systems for robotic manipulation: limited spatial perception and the absence of effective memory or temporal context throughout task execution.

**Limited Spatial Perception:**
Conventional VLA models typically condition actions on a single, current 2D image frame. The paper highlights that a 2D image inherently lacks detailed spatial and geometric information about the environment. This deficiency leads to several practical problems during manipulation:
- **Inaccurate Grasping:** The robot may fail to accurately grasp objects, repeatedly making the same spatial errors, which can lead to task failure. For instance, the paper provides an example where a robot repeatedly failed to grasp a carrot, consistently missing near the top, indicating spatial disorientation.
- **Spatial Disorientation:** Without sufficient spatial awareness, the robot can become disoriented within the workspace, making it difficult to execute precise movements.
While alternative modalities like depth images or point clouds offer richer 3D spatial information, they are often expensive to acquire, not universally accessible in everyday environments, and can restrict the generalizability of trained policies. The motivation was to improve spatial understanding without relying on these additional, costly sensing modalities.

**Absence of Memory / Limited Temporal Context:**
Standard VLAs largely operate by processing the current frame in isolation, leading to a lack of memory regarding past states or the initial scene configuration. This results in:
- **Forgetting Objects under Occlusion:** When an object becomes temporarily occluded by the robot's gripper or arm during manipulation, the model can "forget" the object's location or the task's target. The paper illustrates this with a "put carrot on plate" task, where the robot grasped the carrot but then remained confused because its arm blocked the view of the plate. This is particularly problematic with single-view observations.
- **Inability to Learn from Past Errors:** Without a memory of past actions or environmental states, the robot may not effectively recover from failures or improve its subsequent attempts, potentially entering failure loops where it repeatedly makes the same mistakes.
The motivation here was to imbue the VLA with a persistent representation of the scene context to mitigate forgetting and enable more effective recovery strategies, without the computational burden of processing extensive sequences of historical frames.

**Overall Objectives:**
To address these motivations, the key objectives were:
1. **Enhance Spatial Reasoning:** Develop a mechanism to improve the VLA's understanding of 3D geometric relationships between objects and the robot, using existing visual inputs efficiently.
2. **Improve Temporal Reasoning/Memory:** Provide the VLA with a persistent memory of the scene's initial state to overcome occlusion-induced forgetting and facilitate more robust task execution.
3. **Maintain Efficiency:** Achieve these enhancements with minimal additional computational overhead or the introduction of new, expensive sensing modalities.
4. **Demonstrate Efficacy:** Validate the proposed approach's effectiveness in both simulated benchmarks and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, showcasing improvements in success rates and robustness.
The paper proposes visual anchors (specifically, the first frame of an episode) and a lightweight spatial encoder as mechanisms to fulfill these objectives, aiming to provide a practical and efficient solution for robust robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The AnchorVLA4D model is designed to enhance spatial and temporal reasoning in robotic manipulation by augmenting traditional VLA inputs with an anchor frame and integrating a lightweight spatial encoder.

**A. Preliminaries**
The core task involves generating an action $a_i = [\Delta x_i, \Delta \theta_i, \text{Gripper}_i]$ (positional and rotational changes, gripper state) given a sequence of visual observations $I_0, \dots, I_i$, proprioceptive states $S_0, \dots, S_i$, and a textual instruction $T$. Unlike conventional VLA methods that primarily use only the current frame $I_i$, AnchorVLA4D uses an anchor image $I_{anchor}$ (specifically, the first frame $I_0$) alongside the current image $I_i$. The overall action generation process is formulated as:
$[a_i \dots a_{i+j}] = AH(VL(I_{anchor}, I_i, T), SE(I_{anchor}, I_i), S_i)$
where $VL$ is the Vision-Language processing module, $SE$ is the spatial encoder, and $AH$ is the action head.

**B. Model Architecture**
The model builds upon established VLA components:
- **Vision-Language Backbone:** Qwen2.5VL 3B is used as the foundational VLM, responsible for processing visual inputs and textual instructions.
- **Action Head:** A 400M ScaleDP diffusion-based model serves as the action head. It generates action chunks conditioned on the VLM's final hidden-layer representation and the robot's proprioceptive state.
- **Spatial Encoder:** Any4D, a unified feed-forward metric 4D reconstruction model, is employed as the spatial encoder. Its output is integrated into the hidden states of the VLM.

The research evaluates three main variants to demonstrate the efficacy of its components:
1. **VanillaVLA:** A baseline model that uses neither anchors nor a spatial encoder, adhering to conventional VLA design principles.
2. **AnchorVLA:** This variant extends VanillaVLA by incorporating the anchor mechanism.
3. **AnchorVLA4D:** The full proposed model, combining both the anchor mechanism and the spatial encoder.

**C. AnchorVLA4D Workflow**
The workflow integrates the anchor frame and spatial encoding as follows:
1. **Anchor Frame Utilization:** The first frame ($I_0$) of an episode is designated as the anchor. This anchor serves two primary purposes:
 - **Context Preservation:** It reminds the robot of the initial scene state, aiding in filtering out background noise and recalling task goals even if objects become occluded.
 - **3D Understanding Support:** When jointly processed with the current observation, it provides spatiotemporal cues that contribute to the model's understanding of the 3D physical environment and object trajectories.
2. **Input Processing:** The anchor frame ($I_{anchor}$) and the current frame ($I_i$) are fed into the vision-language backbone. The final hidden state from this backbone is extracted.
3. **Spatial Encoding:** Simultaneously, both $I_{anchor}$ and $I_i$ are processed by the spatial encoder (if enabled). The spatial encoder's output, which captures geometric relationships between the two frames, is then concatenated with the final hidden state from the VL backbone. This concatenation is identified as the most effective integration strategy in ablation studies.
4. **Action Generation:** The combined representation (from VL backbone and spatial encoder) is projected into a fixed-length conditioning vector. This vector then feeds into the diffusion-based action head. During training, the model optimizes by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) loss of predicted noise, and during inference, it performs a denoising process to reconstruct the appropriate action chunk. The model predicts a sequence of 5 actions, but only the first action is executed at test time to maintain responsiveness.

**D. Training Recipe**
- **Hardware:** Training is conducted on an Ascend 910B node, equipped with 8 NPUs, each possessing 64 GB of memory.
- **Learning Rates:** A learning rate of 2e-5 is used. It is kept constant during pretraining on the full BridgeV2 dataset and then follows a cosine decay schedule during finetuning on four task-specific subsets.
- **Regularization:** A weight decay of 1e-2 is applied.
- **Training Phases:**
 - **Pretraining:** Conducted for 30,000 steps with a global batch size of 512. All model parameters, including those of the image encoder, are unfrozen to allow adaptation to the robotic manipulation domain.
 - **Finetuning:** Performed for 15,000 steps with a global batch size of 256. During this phase, specific parameters are selectively frozen to focus learning on particular parts of the model relevant to the specific tasks.
- **Normalization:** Proprioceptive states and actions are normalized during training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates AnchorVLA4D in both simulation and real-world environments, presenting its performance relative to existing VLA methods and through detailed ablation studies.

**A. Simulation Experiments (Simpler WidowX Benchmark)**
The model was trained on BridgeV2 datasets and evaluated on the SimplerEnv benchmark across four categories: "Spoon on Towel," "Carrot on Plate," "Stack Cube," and "Eggplant in Basket."
- **Baseline Performance:** The VanillaVLA (without anchors or spatial encoder) achieved an average success rate (SR) of 51.0%. A finetuned VanillaVLA improved this to 61.5%.
- **AnchorVLA4D Performance:** AnchorVLA4D achieved an average success rate of 64.6%, demonstrating a 13.6 percentage point improvement over the baseline VanillaVLA.
- **Comparison to State-of-the-Art:** Despite being a considerably smaller model (4.4B parameters) and not having undergone large-scale pretraining on robotic data, AnchorVLA4D exhibited strong performance. It outperformed several established standard, spatial, and temporal VLA methods listed in the paper. For instance, it surpassed OpenVLA (4.2%), CogACT-Base (51.3%), $\pi_0$-Uniform (55.7%), SpatialVLA (42.7%), SoFAR (58.3%), FSD (40.6%), TraceVLA (27.7%), and ST-VLA (38.1%). It approached the performance of MemoryVLA (71.9%), which uses a larger (7.3B-parameter) backbone pretrained on extensive robot datasets.
- **Inference Latency:** Each inference in simulation required 0.215 seconds. The anchor and spatial encoder added a 16% latency increase relative to the original 0.185 seconds for VanillaVLA, resulting in an effective control frequency of 4.65 Hz.
- **Key Observations from Simulation:**
 - **Early Retrying:** The integration of an anchor mechanism prompted the model to initiate retry attempts earlier upon encountering a failure. In contrast, the vanilla version tended to follow a predetermined trajectory before retrying.
 - **Improved Spatial Awareness:** With anchor input, the model exhibited enhanced understanding of the environment and spatial relationships. After an initial failure, subsequent attempts became more accurate, suggesting improved spatial understanding.

**B. Real-World Experiments (xLerobot)**
AnchorVLA4D was evaluated on an xLerobot, a low-cost dual-arm mobile robot, after fine-tuning a BridgeV2-trained checkpoint with 30 episodes of collected real-world data per task. Three tasks were designed: "Lift Box with Two Arms" (bimanual), "Open Drawer" (hinge-joint, single-arm), and "Rotation" (pouring water from a cup).
- **Performance:** AnchorVLA4D achieved an average success rate of 80% across the three real-world tasks. This surpassed the 50% baseline success rate of $\pi_{0.5}$, which was used as a comparative baseline.
- **Generalization:** The policy, initially pretrained on single-arm tasks from BridgeV2, demonstrated generalization capabilities by successfully executing bimanual tasks after limited real-world fine-tuning (30 episodes, 7,500 steps).
- **Latency:** Real-world inference latency was approximately 300 ms, primarily due to higher image resolution (320x240x4 views) compared to simulation (224x224). This resulted in an effective control frequency of about 17 Hz when executing 5 actions per inference.

**C. Ablation Studies**
- **Performance Breakdown:**
 - **Anchor Contribution:** Adding the anchor mechanism to VanillaVLA (creating AnchorVLA) increased the overall simulation success rate by 9.4 percentage points (from 51.0% to 60.4%).
 - **Spatial Encoder Contribution:** Integrating the spatial encoder into AnchorVLA (creating AnchorVLA4D) further improved the success rate by 4.2 percentage points (from 60.4% to 64.6%).
 - **Spatial Encoder Training Strategy:** Freezing the spatial encoder's parameters during finetuning yielded the best performance (64.6%). Unfreezing all spatial encoder parameters resulted in a 6.2% drop in SR (to 59.4%), and removing the spatial encoder's output after training caused a 6.3% drop (to 58.3%). This suggests that a pretrained spatial encoder is beneficial even without explicit 3D supervision, but training it from scratch without such supervision is less effective.
- **Alternative Architectures and Design Choices:**
 - **Anchor Format:** Using the first frame ($I_0$) as the anchor (60.4% SR) proved more effective than using the previous 3 frames ($I_{i-3...i-1}$, 46.9% SR) or widely-spaced historical frames ($I_{i-60,i-40,i-20}$, 21.9% SR). This indicates that too many or too distant historical frames can introduce noise rather than useful context.
 - **Spatial Encoder Integration:** Concatenating the spatial encoder's output with the VL model's hidden states before the action expert yielded the highest SR (64.6%), outperforming cross-attention mechanisms either before the decoder (0% SR) or within the action head (46.9% SR).
- **Retry Statistics:** With anchors, the model exhibited fewer overall retries due to a higher success rate. However, in cases of failure, the number of retries was slightly higher, indicating an improved ability to attempt recovery.
- **Proprioceptive States:** Proprioceptive states were found to be beneficial in simulation (+10.4% SR), but detrimental in real-world environments (-10% SR for the "Open Drawer" task). This suggests that in the less diverse real-world data, proprioceptive states might encourage memorization of trajectories, thereby hindering visual grounding abilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AnchorVLA4D research contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by introducing an approach that enhances the spatial and temporal reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. This work addresses critical limitations of conventional VLAs in a resource-efficient manner, offering several areas of significance and potential impact.

Firstly, the primary significance lies in its **practical and efficient solution to VLA challenges**. By leveraging a single anchor frame (the initial observation) and a lightweight spatial encoder, AnchorVLA4D improves the model's ability to maintain scene context and understand geometric relationships. This advancement helps mitigate problems such as object forgetting during occlusion and spatial disorientation, which are common failure modes in current VLAs. Crucially, these improvements are achieved without requiring additional, expensive sensing modalities like depth cameras or point cloud sensors, and without incurring the heavy computational cost associated with processing numerous historical frames. The reported minimal increase in inference latency (8% for anchors, an additional 8% for the spatial encoder) underscores its efficiency for real-world deployment.

Secondly, the research demonstrates **enhanced robustness and generalization across diverse tasks and environments**. AnchorVLA4D achieved a 13.6% improvement on the Simpler WidowX benchmark in simulation and an 80% success rate on generalized real-world tasks using an xLerobot. The ability of a policy, primarily trained on single-arm simulation data, to successfully execute bimanual tasks with limited real-world fine-tuning highlights its strong generalization capabilities. This suggests that the approach could enable robots to perform a broader range of complex manipulation tasks in varied environments.

Thirdly, the **ablation studies provide valuable insights into VLA design principles**. The finding that using the initial frame as an anchor is more effective than incorporating multiple historical frames suggests that a concise, fixed temporal anchor can provide sufficient contextual memory without introducing excessive noise or computational burden. The observation that a pretrained spatial encoder can be leveraged for performance gains even without explicit 3D supervision, particularly when its parameters are frozen, indicates that existing vision models may already capture useful spatial priors that can be effectively integrated. Furthermore, the contrasting impact of proprioceptive states in simulation versus real-world settings (beneficial in simulation, detrimental in real-world for certain tasks) offers an important consideration for dataset diversity and model design, suggesting potential for visual grounding to be hindered by memorized trajectories in limited real-world data.

Finally, the **potential impact extends to broader robotic applications**. By offering a more robust, generalizable, and resource-efficient VLA system, AnchorVLA4D could accelerate the deployment of intelligent robots in household, industrial, and service scenarios where cost-effectiveness and adaptability are crucial. The research also encourages further exploration into how subtle visual signals and efficient temporal mechanisms can fundamentally strengthen VLA capabilities, paving the way for future advancements in spatio-temporal reasoning for robotic control. The identified limitation regarding the anchor becoming outdated in long-duration tasks also provides a clear direction for future work, such as incremental anchor updates, to further improve long-horizon task performance.
