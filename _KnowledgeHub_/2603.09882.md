# Emerging Extrinsic Dexterity in Cluttered Scenes via Dynamics-aware Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Dynamics-Aware Policy Learning (DAPL) is a two-stage framework that learns contact-induced object dynamics for non-prehensile manipulation in highly cluttered scenes. The method, evaluated on a new Clutter6D benchmark and real-world setups, achieved a 44.56% success rate in dense simulation environments, more than doubling prior methods, and demonstrated robust sim-to-real transfer with a 48% success rate on a real robot.

## Problem

- Traditional prehensile (grasping-based) manipulation struggles in cluttered environments where objects are tightly packed, occluded, or require complex reorientations.
- Existing non-prehensile methods often rely on hand-designed primitives or use learning-based approaches that are brittle with complex, coupled dynamics in dense clutter.
- Successful manipulation in cluttered scenes necessitates understanding how objects dynamically respond to contact, including sliding, toppling, and momentum transfer, which static geometric observations do not reliably capture.

## Method

- A two-stage **Dynamics-Aware Policy Learning (DAPL)** framework iteratively trains a physical world model and a reinforcement learning policy.
- The physical world model learns to predict ==contact-induced object-scene dynamics== using a patch-based Transformer that takes point clouds augmented with physical attributes (mass, velocity) and is trained with a ==variance-aware regularization loss==.
- The RL policy, conditioned on the learned dynamics representation, learns non-prehensile manipulation strategies in a simulator, employing a ==curriculum learning== approach that alternates between policy training and world model refinement.

## Results

- In simulation, DAPL achieved a **44.56%** success rate in dense clutter, outperforming the strongest baseline (CORN) by more than double (**22.22%**), while also reducing unintended disturbance to non-target objects.
- The framework demonstrated robust sim-to-real transfer, achieving a **48%** success rate in real-world cluttered scenes on a Franka robot, comparable to human teleoperation (**52%**), with higher efficiency (**42.6s** vs **55.9s** mean execution time).
- Qualitative analysis and controlled experiments revealed that DAPL learned adaptive behaviors, such as avoiding light objects or leveraging heavy ones as anchors, demonstrating purposeful contact strategies beyond simple pushing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly learning and integrating a dynamics-aware representation into reinforcement learning policies is crucial for enabling robust extrinsic dexterity in cluttered, multi-object environments.
> - Representing scene objects with physical attributes (mass, velocity) and predicting point-level future dynamics, rather than just geometric features or object-level poses, significantly enhances a policy's ability to reason about contact outcomes.
> - An iterative curriculum, where the policy generates diverse interaction data to refine the world model, and the refined model subsequently conditions policy learning, leads to more effective and sample-efficient training.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation in complex, cluttered environments presents significant challenges. Traditional prehensile (grasping-based) manipulation methods often prove insufficient when objects are tightly packed, occluded, or require specific reorientations that a gripper cannot achieve directly. This limitation has led to increased interest in **extrinsic dexterity**, which involves leveraging environmental contact through non-prehensile actions (e.g., pushing, sliding, toppling) to achieve manipulation goals.

Existing approaches to non-prehensile manipulation typically fall into a few categories:
1. **Model-based planning and hand-designed primitives:** These methods are often task-specific and require precise object pose estimation and extensive manual engineering, limiting their scalability and generalization.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods:** While offering better generalization, these methods have largely been confined to simplified contact scenarios and can struggle with the complex, coupled dynamics present in dense clutter.
3. **Geometry-centric representation learning:** Recent advancements like CORN and UniCORN attempt to learn representations based on object geometry. However, these methods have been shown to be brittle in highly cluttered scenes where object interactions involve complex physics beyond static shapes. They often lack explicit modeling of how objects respond dynamically upon contact (e.g., sliding, toppling, momentum transfer).

The current work addresses the identified gap by proposing a framework that explicitly models and learns contact-induced object dynamics. It positions itself as an advancement over prior work by providing policies with a dynamics-aware representation, enabling the selective exploitation or avoidance of contacts in cluttered, multi-object environments. This approach aims to facilitate the emergence of extrinsic dexterity in scenarios where geometric reasoning alone is insufficient, thereby expanding the applicability of robotic manipulation in real-world, unstructured settings.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust non-prehensile manipulation policy capable of achieving object rearrangement in highly cluttered scenes by effectively leveraging extrinsic dexterity. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations and aims:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Prehensile Manipulation:** In cluttered environments, objects are frequently tightly packed or occluded, making reliable grasping difficult or impossible. Even when grasping is feasible, collision-free movement can be highly constrained. The researchers are motivated to develop strategies that go beyond grasping to address these practical limitations.
2. **Addressing Deficiencies in Existing Non-Prehensile Methods:** Prior non-prehensile approaches either rely on specific designs that do not scale or simplify away the inherent complexity of contact-rich interactions. Model-based methods require extensive manual design, while current learning-based methods, including those leveraging geometry-centric representations (e.g., CORN, UniCORN), often prove brittle in dense clutter with multiple interacting objects. A core motivation is to move beyond these limitations.
3. **Modeling Complex Contact Dynamics:** The success of manipulation in cluttered scenes is not solely determined by object geometry but by how objects physically respond to contact—whether they slide, topple, or transfer momentum to neighboring objects. These complex, coupled interactions depend on underlying object dynamics that cannot be reliably inferred from static observations or sparse rewards. The work is motivated to explicitly model these dynamics.
4. **Enabling Selective Extrinsic Dexterity:** Achieving effective extrinsic dexterity requires a policy to selectively exploit beneficial contacts while avoiding disruptive ones. This means a robot needs to reason about when to bypass light objects, robustly traverse unavoidable obstacles, or actively leverage stable neighbors for complex actions like flipping objects. The research aims to enable such selective and intelligent contact strategies.
5. **Developing a Dynamics-Aware Policy Learning (DAPL) Framework:** The central motivation is to decouple dynamics representation learning from task-specific control. This involves equipping the policy with an explicit, learned representation that models contact-induced object motion. This learned representation would then condition reinforcement learning, allowing policies to reason about the consequences of contact without hand-crafted heuristics or complex reward engineering.
6. **Benchmarking and Real-World Applicability:** The researchers are motivated to create a comprehensive simulation environment (Clutter6D) for systematic training and evaluation of 6D object rearrangement in clutter. Furthermore, a key objective is to demonstrate robust sim-to-real transfer and practical applicability in real-world scenarios, including tasks like grocery retrieval, to validate the method's utility.

In summary, the key objective is to facilitate the emergence of robust and intelligent extrinsic dexterity in cluttered scenes by integrating explicit, learned dynamics representations into reinforcement learning, thereby addressing fundamental challenges in robotic manipulation that current methods do not adequately resolve.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Dynamics-Aware Policy Learning (DAPL), is a two-stage learning framework designed to enable non-prehensile manipulation in cluttered scenes through a learned representation of contact-induced dynamics.

**A. DAPL Framework Overview**

The framework iteratively trains two components:
1. **Physical World Model:** This model learns to predict contact-induced object-scene dynamics from robot interaction data.
2. **Reinforcement Learning (RL) Policy:** This policy operates in a physical simulator and is conditioned on the learned dynamics representation, allowing it to explore and learn effective manipulation strategies.

**B. Dynamics Representation Learning (Stage 1)**

The initial stage focuses on learning a physical world model that encodes a physics-informed representation of scene dynamics.

1. **Physical Scene Representation:**
 - At each timestep, the scene state is represented as a composite point cloud, including points from the target object, the surrounding scene (obstacles), and the robot end-effector.
 - The surrounding scene point cloud is spatially cropped around the target object to focus on relevant local geometry.
 - Crucially, each 3D point (p_i) is augmented with physical attributes: its mass (m_i) and instantaneous linear velocity (v_i), forming a 7-dimensional feature vector (x, y, z, m, v_x, v_y, v_z). Object mass is distributed across its sampled points.
 - Input to the world model is a fixed-size point cloud (1,280 points in total: 512 target, 512 environmental, 256 end-effector), with each point having these 7 features. All features are normalized.

2. **Architecture:**
 - The world model employs a patch-based Transformer encoder-decoder architecture.
 - **Tokenization:** The input point cloud is partitioned into 40 patches (16 target, 16 environment, 8 end-effector) using Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) and k-Nearest Neighbors (k-NN). Each patch is normalized and encoded into a fixed-dimensional token using a PointNet-style encoder. Sinusoidal Positional Embeddings are added based on patch center coordinates.
 - **Dynamics Encoder:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone (12 blocks, 8 heads, 128 hidden dimension) processes these patch tokens to capture multi-object coupling effects and global spatio-temporal features.
 - **Decoder:** The robot's action (end-effector flow) is projected into a latent token and fused with the patch tokens via a cross-attention Transformer layer. The action-conditioned tokens are then mapped back to point-wise resolution via an unpatchify scatter operation, where patch features are distributed to individual points, augmented with local positional embeddings. This setup predicts future point-wise dynamics.

3. **World Model Training Objective:**
 - The model predicts future per-point positions (p̂_t+1_i) and velocities (v̂_t+1_i).
 - The primary loss is a combination of L2 losses for position and velocity prediction (L_dyn).
 - To prevent trivial zero-velocity predictions in cluttered scenes where most points are static, a **variance-aware regularization loss (L_var)** is introduced. This term matches the standard deviations of predicted and ground-truth velocity fields, preserving the magnitude and spatial variability of motion in dynamic regions.
 - The total loss is L = L_dyn + αL_var, with λ_pos = 1.0, λ_vel = 1.0, and a high weight for variance regularization (λ_var = 100.0).

**C. Dexterous Policy Learning via RL (Stage 2)**

The learned dynamics representation conditions an Actor-Critic RL policy.

1. **Observation and Action Spaces:**
 - **Observation:** Consists of (i) dynamics-aware scene representation (latent embeddings from the pre-trained dynamics encoder), (ii) robot proprioceptive state (joint positions, velocities, end-effector poses), and (iii) task goal (relative pose between target and desired configuration).
 - **Action:** Continuous joint-space control commands, executed by an impedance controller.

2. **Architecture:**
 - The dynamics encoder processes scene point clouds into latent patch tokens.
 - An environment state (task goal, robot states, physics parameters, last action) is formed.
 - A cross-attention layer integrates the environment state (as query) with the latent patch tokens, creating a task-conditioned feature.
 - This feature is concatenated with the environment state and passed through a fusion MLP.
 - The resulting global representation feeds into Actor and Critic heads (two-layer MLPs).

3. **Reward Design:**
 - A sparse task success term is provided upon reaching the goal.
 - **Lightweight Shaping Terms:**
 - `r_contact`: Encourages interaction based on the minimum distance between the target object and the end-effector (1 - tanh(d_oe)).
 - `r_goal`: Measures distance between object and goal pose, activated only when the end-effector is near the object. Includes both coarse and fine-grained components.
 - **Clutter Disturbance Penalty:** The success reward is discounted by the displacement (Chamfer distance) of non-target objects, penalizing unintended scene disturbance.

**D. Curriculum Learning with Policy Interaction**

DAPL employs an iterative curriculum that alternates between policy learning and world model refinement.
1. **Initialization:** An RL policy is trained from scratch without a pre-trained dynamics representation.
2. **Iteration Cycle:**
 - The dynamics encoder is frozen, and the policy is trained until its performance (reward, success rate) plateaus (approx. 2x10^4 RL steps).
 - The trained policy is then used to collect interaction trajectories (approx. 60k steps), which include diverse contact dynamics.
 - This collected data is used to update and refine the world model for 500k training steps, improving its ability to capture contact-induced momentum transfer under realistic policy-induced distributions.
 - The refined dynamics encoder then conditions the RL policy in the next iteration, leading to improved exploration and stability.
3. This alternating procedure continues until both policy performance and the dynamics representation converge, facilitating the co-evolution of the world model and policy.

**E. Clutter6D: Object Rearrangement Environment**

To enable systematic training and evaluation, the researchers developed Clutter6D.
- **Simulator:** Built on IsaacLab with PhysX backend.
- **Object Assets:** 10K high-quality assets from Objaverse, processed for simulation (mesh simplification, convex decomposition), with auto-generated and human-verified physical (scale, mass) and semantic attributes.
- **Scene Generation:** Task scenarios are synthesized from object semantics as Task-oriented Scene Graphs (ToSG), enabling control over relational constraints and scene diversity. Objects are placed in topological order to ensure physical stability.
- **Difficulty Tracks:** Three levels based on clutter density: Sparse (4 objects), Moderate (8 objects), and Dense (12 objects).
 - Sparse: 1,024 training scenes, 128 evaluation scenes.
- **Metrics:** (i) Task success rate (target object within 0.05m and 0.1 rad of goal), (ii) Chamfer distance of non-target object offsets (measuring unintended disturbance). Episodes terminate on success, object drop, or after 300 steps.

**F. Sim-to-Real Deployment Details (from Appendix)**

- **Hardware:** Franka Research 3 robot, three Intel RealSense cameras for point cloud acquisition.
- **Perception Pipeline:** SAM2 for initial object segmentation, XMem for online tracking, FoundationPose for 6D pose estimation.
- **System Identification:** Object mass estimated using a vision-language model (GPT-5). Velocities obtained via temporal differentiation and EKF-based filtering.
- **Dynamics Distillation:** A teacher-student distillation framework is used to bridge the sim-to-real gap. The student policy is trained to recover the teacher’s latent dynamics from observations injected with Gaussian noise (μ=0, σ=0.05) on coordinates and velocities. This forces the policy to infer effective dynamics despite imperfect real-world perception and coarse physical attribute estimates.
- **Action Mapping:** Action magnitude curriculum (decaying scale for joint-space residuals) and Cartesian-space action clipping are implemented to ensure safe and compliant execution on the real robot, preventing high-force impacts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of DAPL was conducted extensively in both the Clutter6D simulation benchmark and real-world scenarios, demonstrating its capabilities in emergent extrinsic dexterity.

**A. Simulation Experiments**

1. **Performance Comparison Across Clutter Densities:**
 - DAPL consistently outperformed all baselines (Prehensile Manipulation, Human Teleoperation, and various Representation Learning+RL policies) across Sparse (4 objects), Moderate (8 objects), and Dense (12 objects) clutter settings (Table I).
 - The performance gap was most significant in dense environments. For instance, in the Dense setting, DAPL achieved a 44.56% success rate, effectively doubling the strongest baseline, CORN (22.22%).
 - DAPL also achieved a favorable balance between task success and minimizing disturbance to non-target objects (Mean Offset), with a lower Mean Offset (12.65 cm in Dense) compared to CORN (17.43 cm). Baselines with lower Mean Offset often reflected task failure rather than precise interaction.

2. **Training Efficiency and Convergence:**
 - DAPL demonstrated significantly higher sample efficiency, reaching approximately 70% success within the first few thousand training iterations (Fig. 4).
 - Geometry-based baselines (CORN, UniCORN) converged substantially slower, while other representations failed to learn meaningful policies, suggesting that DAPL's dynamics-aware representation provides a strong physical prior.

3. **Analysis of Dynamics Representation (Ablation Studies):**
 - Removing physical attributes (velocity and mass) from the input led to a substantial performance drop (Table II), affirming the importance of reasoning about motion potential.
 - Replacing point-level dense dynamics prediction with object-level 6-DoF pose prediction resulted in inferior performance, indicating that point-level granularity is crucial for capturing intricate contact physics.
 - Replacing the dynamics prediction objective with simple autoencoder reconstruction also degraded performance, highlighting that learning dynamics, not just static shape, is key.
 - Visualizations of world model predictions showed close agreement with ground-truth object motion (Fig. 6).

4. **Effectiveness of Curriculum Learning:**
 - The iterative curriculum (alternating policy learning and world model refinement) steadily improved policy performance. Starting from 61.3% success, it increased to 71.8% after three iterations (Fig. 7). This indicates that policy-collected data refines the world model, which in turn provides more accurate conditioning for subsequent policy learning.

5. **Behavior Analysis for Extrinsic Dexterity:**
 - Qualitative analysis of 50 simulated trajectories showed DAPL exhibited fewer unnecessary contacts and less disturbance (smaller large translations/rotations) to non-target objects compared to CORN (Table III).
 - DAPL demonstrated "Avoid," "Traverse," and "Leverage" behaviors more frequently (52%, 38%, 42% of trajectories, respectively), suggesting purposeful contact strategies rather than indiscriminate pushing.

6. **Sensitivity to Noisy Inputs:**
 - DAPL showed robustness to perturbations in object mass and velocity estimates, maintaining similar success rates over a broad noise range, although larger noise increased scene disturbance (Table IV).
 - However, goal-pose noise caused a sharper performance drop, indicating that accurate task specification is critical.

7. **Adaptive Behavior:**
 - Controlled experiments demonstrated adaptive interaction strategies. The policy autonomously adapted its trajectory based on objects' physical properties (e.g., using a heavy pie as an anchor vs. avoiding a lightweight pie), suggesting reasoning based on contact-induced dynamics rather than geometric heuristics (Fig. 8).

**B. Real-World Experiments**

1. **Setup:** The policy was deployed on a Franka Research 3 robot using Intel RealSense cameras, with object segmentation (SAM2, XMem) and pose estimation (FoundationPose). Object mass was coarsely estimated via GPT-5, and velocities were EKF-filtered. A teacher-student distillation framework with input noise aided sim-to-real transfer.
2. **Results:** Across 10 diverse real-world cluttered scenes, DAPL achieved an average success rate of 48%, which is comparable to human teleoperation (52%) (Table V).
3. **Efficiency:** DAPL demonstrated higher efficiency with a lower mean execution time (42.6s) compared to human teleoperation (55.9s).
4. **Robustness:** The performance indicates robust sim-to-real transfer, demonstrating that even with coarse estimates of physical attributes and noisy sensory data, the dynamics-aware policy can select appropriate interaction strategies.

**C. Applications**

- DAPL was integrated into a practical grocery retrieval task on a Galbot G1 humanoid robot.
- It served as a pre-grasping skill, leveraging extrinsic dexterity to slide and reorient target objects out of cluttered shelves into grasp-friendly configurations, enabling a downstream grasping module to succeed (Fig. 10).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks in cluttered and unstructured environments, by explicitly addressing the complex interplay of contact dynamics. The significance and potential impact of this work can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging the Gap in Extrinsic Dexterity:** The work successfully tackles the underexplored challenge of achieving extrinsic dexterity in cluttered multi-object scenes. By demonstrating the selective exploitation and avoidance of environmental contacts, it moves beyond the limitations of traditional prehensile manipulation and geometry-centric non-prehensile approaches that often fail in dense clutter.

2. **Introduction of Dynamics-Aware Policy Learning (DAPL):** The proposed DAPL framework is a key contribution. By decoupling dynamics representation learning from policy control and integrating a learned physical world model into reinforcement learning, it enables the emergence of sophisticated manipulation behaviors without relying on hand-crafted heuristics or complex reward engineering. This approach provides policies with a deeper understanding of interaction outcomes, which is crucial for robust performance in contact-rich scenarios.

3. **Development of Clutter6D Benchmark:** The introduction of Clutter6D is significant for the robotics research community. It provides a standardized, comprehensive, and challenging benchmark for 6-DoF object rearrangement in cluttered scenes with varying densities. This resource can facilitate future research and systematic evaluation of contact-rich manipulation policies.

4. **Demonstrated Performance and Robustness:**
 - **State-of-the-Art Simulation Performance:** DAPL significantly outperforms prior methods across all clutter densities in simulation, particularly in dense environments where other approaches exhibit sharp performance degradation. This suggests a more robust and generalizable solution for complex manipulation tasks.
 - **Effective Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The ability to deploy a policy trained entirely in simulation to diverse real-world cluttered scenes with comparable success rates to human teleoperation, and even improved execution times, highlights the practicality and robustness of the approach despite real-world sensing noise and imperfect physical parameter estimation.
 - **Adaptive Behavior:** The observed adaptive strategies, where the policy modifies its interactions based on the physical properties of objects (e.g., using heavier objects as anchors), demonstrate a sophisticated understanding of physics-based reasoning in manipulation.

5. **Potential for Real-World Applications:** The successful integration of DAPL into a grocery retrieval task on a humanoid robot illustrates its direct applicability. By serving as a pre-grasping skill to reorient objects into graspable configurations, it can significantly enhance the capabilities of robots in logistics, retail, household assistance, and other domains where cluttered environments are prevalent and direct grasping is often infeasible.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations, including the reliance on approximate physical attributes (mass, velocity) which can be noisy in real-world settings. The current approach also focuses on rigid tabletop objects and does not explicitly address articulated or deformable objects, nor long-horizon multi-stage tasks.

Future work aims to address these limitations by:
- Improving online dynamics estimation through richer sensing and system identification.
- Extending dynamics-aware representations to handle articulated and deformable objects.
- Integrating DAPL with higher-level planning for complex, long-horizon manipulation sequences.

Overall, this research offers a promising direction for advancing dexterous manipulation by equipping robots with a learned understanding of physical dynamics, moving beyond grasp-centric paradigms and enhancing their capabilities in challenging real-world environments.
