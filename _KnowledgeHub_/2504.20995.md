# TesserAct: Learning 4D Embodied World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> TesserAct introduces a 4D embodied world model that predicts sequences of RGB, depth, and normal maps, enabling robust reconstruction of dynamic 3D scenes over time. This approach allows robots to achieve higher success rates in various manipulation tasks by providing enhanced spatial and temporal understanding.

## Problem

- Existing 2D pixel-based world models lack explicit understanding of 3D geometry, limiting their use in robotic tasks requiring precise depth and pose.
- Directly generating or simulating full 4D world dynamics (3D + time) is computationally prohibitive.
- There is a scarcity of large-scale 4D datasets annotated with high-quality RGB, depth, and normal information for training.

## Method

- Curates a large-scale 4D embodied video dataset by annotating synthetic (RLBench) and real-world (RT1 Fractal, Bridge, Something Something V2) videos with RGB, depth, and normal maps.
- Develops a 4D generative model by fine-tuning a pre-trained video diffusion model (CogVideoX) to directly predict future sequences of RGB, depth, and normal maps.
- Introduces a 4D scene reconstruction algorithm that refines generated depth maps using normal integration and ensures temporal consistency via optical flow and custom loss functions.

## Results

- TesserAct generates 4D scenes with superior depth and normal accuracy and lower Chamfer Distance for reconstructed 4D point clouds compared to existing 2D video diffusion and 4D point cloud generation models.
- It achieves higher success rates across challenging robotic manipulation tasks in RLBench, outperforming 2D-based world models and behavior cloning agents.
- The model demonstrates efficient high-quality novel view synthesis, achieving better visual metrics (PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS) in minutes compared to hours for state-of-the-art 4D reconstruction methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generating sequences of RGB, depth, and normal maps (RGB-DN videos) serves as an efficient and information-rich intermediate representation for dynamic 4D scenes.
> - Fine-tuning large-scale 2D video diffusion models with specialized modules can effectively extend their capabilities to generate coherent 4D (dynamic 3D) representations.
> - Explicitly incorporating and optimizing for depth and normal information during generation and post-processing significantly improves the geometric accuracy and temporal consistency of reconstructed 4D scenes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of "world models" is a cornerstone of embodied artificial intelligence and model-based reinforcement learning. Traditional world models, especially those operating on visual data, have predominantly focused on **2D pixel-space representations**. While effective in simpler, pixel-based environments (like Atari games), these 2D models fundamentally struggle with the complexities of the **3D physical world**. They lack explicit understanding of depth, object pose, and spatial relationships, leading to limitations in tasks requiring precise manipulation, such as robotics. Issues like inconsistent object sizes or shapes across time steps in 2D video generation highlight this deficiency.

The broader research landscape is witnessing a significant shift towards **3D-aware and 4D (dynamic 3D) representations** to overcome these limitations. This includes advancements in:
- **3D Vision and Reconstruction:** Techniques like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian Splatting have revolutionized novel view synthesis and static 3D scene representation. However, applying them to dynamic, interacting scenes, especially those driven by agent actions, remains computationally intensive and challenging for real-time applications.
- **Embodied Foundation Models:** A burgeoning area aiming to build large, general-purpose models for agents that interact with environments. This includes Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that directly output actions or multimodal language models that reason about 3D inputs. However, many of these focus on policy learning rather than explicitly modeling the world's dynamics.
- **Video Generation Models:** Recent progress with large-scale diffusion models (e.g., OpenSora, CogVideoX) has enabled high-fidelity 2D video generation. The challenge lies in extending these to consistent 3D or 4D representations.

"TesserAct" directly addresses this gap by proposing a **4D embodied world model** that explicitly models the dynamic evolution of 3D scenes over time in response to an embodied agent's actions. Unlike prior 2D video-based world models, TesserAct generates not just RGB videos, but also **depth and normal maps (RGB-DN)**, providing crucial geometric information. This differentiates it from:
- **2D pixel-based world models:** TesserAct moves beyond pixel space to capture scene geometry.
- **Pure 3D goal state prediction:** Some works predict only the final 3D state; TesserAct models the full temporal dynamics.
- **Purely synthetic 3D world models:** TesserAct leverages real-world data and supports language conditioning and downstream robotic tasks, making it more practical for real-world applications.
- **Computational heavy 4D representations:** Instead of directly generating computationally expensive 3D volumes or dynamic NeRFs, TesserAct uses RGB-DN videos as a lightweight, yet information-rich, intermediate representation, leveraging the efficiency of existing video diffusion models.

By integrating robust 2D video generation capabilities with 3D scene understanding and reconstruction techniques, TesserAct positions itself as a significant step towards truly generalizable and physically grounded world models for embodied AI, particularly in robotics.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind TesserAct stem from the inherent limitations of current world models in addressing the three-dimensional nature of the physical world and the dynamic interactions of embodied agents within it.

**Motivation:**
1. **Limitations of 2D World Models:** Existing world models primarily operate in 2D pixel space. This leads to an "incomplete representation of spatial relationships," making them unsuitable for tasks requiring precise depth and pose information, such as robotic manipulation.
2. **Unrealistic Predictions:** 2D models can generate visually plausible but physically inconsistent results (e.g., varying object sizes/shapes across frames), which limits their utility for robust policy learning, data simulation, and long-horizon planning.
3. **Need for 3D/4D Information:** Robotic tasks fundamentally require understanding 6-DoF pose, depth, and the exact spatial arrangement of objects. A 4D model (3D space + time) can provide this essential information, enabling more realistic and effective interaction with the environment.
4. **Computational Efficiency:** Directly simulating or generating full 4D world dynamics is computationally prohibitive. There is a need for a lightweight yet expressive representation of the 4D world.
5. **Lack of 4D Datasets:** A significant hurdle for learning 4D world models is the scarcity of large-scale datasets with high-quality 4D annotations (RGB, depth, and normal information).

### 3. Key Objectives

1. **Learn a 4D Embodied World Model:** Develop a model, TesserAct, capable of simulating the dynamics of a 3D world, generating realistic 3D interactions (e.g., grasping, opening drawers) with detailed spatial and temporal consistency.
2. **Efficient 4D Representation:** Propose a "substantially more lightweight representation" of the 4D world by predicting sequences of RGB, depth, and normal maps (RGB-DN videos), which captures appearance, geometry, and surface details efficiently.
3. **Accurate 4D Scene Reconstruction:** Develop an efficient algorithm to convert the generated RGB-DN videos into high-quality 4D scenes that are coherent across both time and space, leveraging both depth and normal information.
4. **Data Curation for 4D Learning:** Collect and annotate a 4D embodied video dataset, combining synthetic data (with ground truth depth/normals) and real-world data (with estimated depth/normals) to address the data scarcity challenge.
5. **Enable Downstream Embodied Tasks:** Demonstrate that the learned 4D world model can significantly enhance performance in downstream embodied tasks, specifically robotic action planning, by providing superior geometric and temporal information.

In essence, TesserAct aims to bridge the gap between high-fidelity video generation and physically grounded robotic control by explicitly modeling the 3D geometry and its evolution over time, all while maintaining computational feasibility through an intelligent representation.

### 4. Methodology and Approach

TesserAct's methodology is multifaceted, encompassing data collection, model architecture design, 4D scene reconstruction, and integration with downstream robotic tasks.

#### 4.1. 4D Embodied Video Dataset Collection

Recognizing the lack of large-scale 4D datasets, the authors created one by annotating existing video datasets:
- **Synthetic Data:** 80,000 videos from **RLBench** (a robot learning benchmark) were used. Since RLBench provides metric depth, surface normals were estimated using `depth2normal` from DSINE. Scene randomization techniques (from Colosseum pipeline) were applied to enhance generalization.
- **Real-World Data:** 80,000 videos from **RT1 Fractal Data** and **Bridge** datasets, plus 100,000 videos from **Something Something V2** (human-object interaction), were used. For these, state-of-the-art estimators were employed:
 - **Depth Estimation:** RollingDepth (for affine-invariant depth).
 - **Normal Estimation:** Temporal-Consistent Marigold-LCM-normal.
This pipeline allowed for the generation of high-quality, temporally consistent RGB-DN annotations for both synthetic and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.2. Model Architecture and Training Strategy

The core of TesserAct is an RGB-DN prediction model, fine-tuned from **CogVideoX**, a large-scale video diffusion model. This leverages pre-trained knowledge to overcome the limited size of the custom 4D dataset.
- **Input Design:** The model uses a 3D Variational Autoencoder (VAE) (from CogVideoX, without fine-tuning) to encode RGB, depth, and normal videos into latent spaces. Separate "projectors" (neural networks) process these latent representations, along with the current image latent (x0), to extract embeddings.
- **Backbone:** The sum of these embeddings, conditioned on a text instruction (e.g., "[action instruction] + [robot arm name]") and denoising step, is fed into a **DiT (Diffusion Transformer)** backbone (from CogVideoX).
- **Output Design:**
 - RGB output: The original CogVideoX RGB output method is retained.
 - Depth and Normal output: Additional modules are introduced. A Conv3D layer encodes the concatenation of input latents and the predicted RGB denoised output. This is then combined with the DiT backbone's hidden states and passed through a separate output projector (`DNProj`) to obtain denoised depth and normal predictions.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained to minimize the L2 difference between the predicted noise and the actual noise added to the concatenated RGB, depth, and normal latents (standard diffusion loss).
- **Initialization:** The model is initialized with CogVideoX weights, and new modules (projectors, Conv3D, `DNProj`) are zero-initialized to preserve pre-trained knowledge.

#### 4.3. 4D Scene Reconstruction

After generating RGB-DN videos, TesserAct converts them into high-quality 4D scenes, addressing the challenge of relative depth maps and temporal inconsistencies.
- **Depth Optimization via Normal Integration:** For each frame, depth maps are refined using normal maps to ensure geometric consistency and smoothness. This spatial optimization utilizes a quadratic loss function (`Ls`) based on perspective camera model constraints and surface smoothness assumptions, as proposed by.
- **Temporal Consistency with Optical Flow:** To ensure coherence across frames, optical flow (`RAFT`) is computed between generated frames. This flow is used to distinguish between static (background) and dynamic regions of the scene.
- **Novel Loss Functions:**
 - **Consistency Loss (`Lc`):** Enforces that depth values in the current frame align with warped depth values from the previous frame, separately for dynamic and background regions. This leverages the idea that static backgrounds should remain consistent, and dynamic objects should move coherently.
 - **Regularization Loss (`Lr`):** Ensures that the optimized depth maps remain similar to the initially generated depth maps, preventing over-optimization.
- **Iterative Refinement:** The overall loss objective (combining `Ls`, `Lc`, `Lr`) is iteratively optimized, starting from the first frame, to refine depth maps and reconstruct temporally and spatially coherent 4D point clouds.

#### 4.4. Embodied Action Planning with 4D Scenes (Downstream Task)

The generated 4D point clouds are then utilized for robotic action planning.
- **Inverse Dynamics Model:** An inverse dynamics model (`ID`) is trained to predict the appropriate robot action (7-DoF) based on the current 4D scene, the predicted future 4D scene, and the instruction text.
- **Feature Extraction:** PointNet is used to encode the 4D point clouds (filtered to focus on table and objects) into 3D features.
- **Action Generation:** These 3D features are combined with instruction text embeddings and processed by a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to output the robot action.
- **Inference Strategy:** During inference, the model predicts future keyframes, and the inverse dynamics model is queried to generate actions based on the current state and predicted future state.

This comprehensive approach allows TesserAct to generate semantically rich, geometrically accurate, and temporally consistent 4D embodied world models that directly benefit practical robotic tasks.

### 5. Main Findings and Results

TesserAct's evaluation covers both the quality of 4D scene prediction and its utility in downstream embodied tasks.

#### 5.1. 4D Scene Prediction Quality

- **Superior 4D Scene Generation:** TesserAct significantly outperforms baseline video diffusion models (OpenSora, CogVideoX) and a 4D point cloud diffusion model (4D Point-E) in generating high-quality 4D scenes across both real-world (RT1 Fractal, Bridge) and synthetic (RLBench) datasets.
 - **Depth and Normal Accuracy:** TesserAct shows substantially better performance on depth metrics (AbsRel, δ1, δ2) and normal map metrics (Mean, Median, 11.25°) compared to video models with post-estimation. This validates the effectiveness of learning depth and normals directly within the generative model.
 - **Reconstructed Point Cloud Quality:** With improved depth and normal maps, the 4D point clouds reconstructed by TesserAct achieve the lowest Chamfer Distance (L1 Chamfer Distance), indicating higher accuracy in 3D geometry.
 - **Efficiency of Representation:** The paper notes that while 4D Point-E performs better than video diffusion models, direct point cloud generation is computationally expensive and limits the number of frames. TesserAct's RGB-DN video representation is more efficient, allowing for more precise capture of fine-grained details in dynamic scenes.
- **Efficient Novel View Synthesis:** TesserAct can also perform monocular video to 4D tasks and synthesize novel views. Compared to Shape of Motion, a state-of-the-art 4D reconstruction method, TesserAct synthesizes novel views with higher visual quality (better PSNR, SSIM, LPIPS, CLIP Score, CLIP Aesthetic) in significantly less time (minutes vs. hours), showcasing its efficiency and practical applicability.
- **Effectiveness of Consistency and Regularization Losses:** Ablation studies confirm the importance of the proposed loss terms (consistency loss `Lc` and regularization loss `Lr`):
 - `Lc` (consistency loss) improves the coherence of robot arm movements over time.
 - `Lr` (regularization loss) enhances the geometric accuracy of the reconstruction, leading to better aligned surfaces and shapes.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** The model demonstrates strong generalization to:
 - **Unseen scenes and novel objects:** Benefiting from the pre-trained knowledge of CogVideoX.
 - **Cross-embodiment scenarios:** E.g., using a robotic arm from the Bridge dataset in an RT-1 environment.

#### 5.2. Embodied Action Planning Performance

TesserAct significantly boosts the performance of downstream embodied action planning tasks in RLBench, outperforming strong baselines.
- **Superior Success Rates:** TesserAct achieves higher success rates across most of the 9 challenging RLBench tasks compared to:
 - **Image-BC:** A behavior cloning agent relying solely on image and instruction.
 - **UniPi\*:** A video-based world model (re-implemented with fine-tuned CogVideoX for fair comparison) that predicts future 2D videos for inverse dynamics.
- **Impact of 4D Information:** The improvement is attributed to the 4D point clouds' ability to "reveal the geometry of objects," providing superior spatial guidance for robotic planning. This is particularly evident in tasks requiring precise interaction, such as "close box," "open jar," "sweep to dustpan," and "water plants."
- **Limitations in Specific Tasks:** While generally superior, TesserAct's performance was not as good as the baseline in "open microwave" and "weighing off" tasks. The authors hypothesize this might be because 2D front images already provide sufficient information for these particular tasks, reducing the marginal benefit of 3D data.

In summary, TesserAct demonstrates that learning a 4D embodied world model by predicting RGB-DN videos is not only feasible but also leads to more accurate and coherent 4D scene reconstructions. Crucially, this rich 4D understanding directly translates into improved performance for real-world relevant robotic manipulation tasks, validating the practical utility of 3D-aware world models.

### 6. Significance and Potential Impact

TesserAct represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence and generative modeling, with substantial potential impact across several domains:

#### 6.1. Advancing Embodied AI and Robotics

- **Bridging the 2D-3D Gap:** The most direct impact is in overcoming the fundamental limitations of 2D pixel-based world models for physical interaction. By explicitly modeling 3D geometry and its dynamic evolution over time, TesserAct provides a more faithful representation of the physical world, which is crucial for robots.
- **Enhanced Policy Learning and Planning:** The high-fidelity 4D scene predictions offer richer state representations for reinforcement learning and planning algorithms. This allows for more robust and precise policy learning, enabling robots to perform complex manipulation tasks that require fine-grained spatial and temporal understanding. The improved success rates in RLBench tasks clearly demonstrate this.
- **Realistic Simulation and Data Generation:** Learned 4D world models can serve as powerful simulators for the physical world. This allows for generating diverse and realistic training data, which is especially valuable given the high cost and difficulty of real-world data collection in robotics. It opens possibilities for fully offline policy training and planning through imagined rollouts within the learned world.
- **Tool Use and Complex Interactions:** The ability to understand 3D geometry and its changes facilitates tool use and more complex interactions, as demonstrated in tasks like "sweep to dustpan" and "water plants."

#### 6.2. Novelty in Generative Modeling

- **Efficient 4D Representation:** TesserAct's innovation of using RGB-DN videos as a lightweight intermediate representation for 4D scenes is a key technical contribution. It leverages the success of 2D video diffusion models while incorporating critical 3D information, striking a balance between expressiveness and computational tractability.
- **Robust 4D Reconstruction:** The proposed algorithm for converting RGB-DN videos into coherent 4D scenes, especially with the novel consistency and regularization losses, provides a robust method for integrating spatial and temporal information. This makes the generated 4D scenes useful for precise geometric tasks.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** The model's ability to generalize to unseen scenes, novel objects, and cross-embodiment scenarios, largely due to fine-tuning a powerful pre-trained video diffusion model (CogVideoX), highlights a promising path for building general-purpose embodied world models.

#### 6.3. Future Directions and Limitations

The paper acknowledges a primary limitation: the RGB-DN representation captures only a single surface of the world. This means objects' hidden sides or complex internal structures are not fully modeled. The authors suggest an exciting future direction: generating multiple RGB-DN views, which could then be integrated to form a more complete and holistic 4D world model. This would bring the representation closer to more comprehensive 3D reconstruction techniques while still potentially maintaining better computational efficiency than direct volumetric generation.

In conclusion, TesserAct represents a pivotal step towards building intelligent embodied agents that possess a deep understanding of the dynamic physical world. By providing a practical and effective method for learning 4D embodied world models, it lays foundational groundwork for more capable, autonomous, and physically grounded robotic systems.
