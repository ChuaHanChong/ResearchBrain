# Imagine while Reasoning in Space: Multimodal Visualization-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> Multimodal Visualization-of-Thought (MVoT) enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to generate interleaved verbal and visual reasoning traces, enhancing their ability to perform complex dynamic spatial reasoning. This approach achieved superior accuracy and robustness on challenging grid-based tasks, demonstrating a more human-like cognitive process by integrating visual imagination into AI reasoning.

## Problem

- Traditional text-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) struggles with complex spatial reasoning tasks, as textual descriptions often fail to capture intricate visual patterns and dynamic changes.
- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) lack an inherent ability to "imagine" or reason visually within their internal thought process, often relying on external tools or simplistic textual proxies for visual states.
- Human cognition seamlessly integrates verbal and non-verbal (visual) thinking, a capability largely absent in most AI reasoning systems, leading to limitations in tasks requiring dynamic spatial understanding.

## Method

- MVoT formulates multimodal reasoning as an ==interleaved generation of verbal and visual thoughts==, recursively conditioned on previous states.
- It fine-tunes an autoregressive MLLM (Anole-7B based on Chameleon) on interleaved text and image token sequences using a next-token prediction objective.
- A novel ==token discrepancy loss (L_D)== is introduced during training to minimize the misalignment between the visual embedding space and the language model's token embedding space, ensuring high fidelity in generated image visualizations.

## Results

- MVoT consistently achieved over **90%** accuracy on MAZE and MINIBEHAVIOR, and **85.60%** on FROZENLAKE, outperforming Direct Prompting and GPT-4o with enhanced interpretability.
- MVoT demonstrated superior robustness and stability compared to text-only CoT, especially on challenging tasks like FROZENLAKE where CoT's performance deteriorated significantly due to issues with textual coordinate descriptions.
- The token discrepancy loss (L_D) was critical for MVoT's success, leading to **95%** correct and consecutive visualizations during recursive generation and enabling high-quality visual thoughts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly generating visual "thoughts" as part of the reasoning process significantly enhances interpretability and performance in complex spatial tasks for MLLMs.
> - The token discrepancy loss (L_D) is crucial for aligning visual and language model embedding spaces, leading to high-quality visual generations and improved reasoning fidelity.
> - MVoT's visual reasoning offers a robust alternative to textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT), particularly in dynamic environments where textual descriptions of spatial layouts are prone to error or ambiguity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the crucial intersection of advanced AI reasoning, multimodal learning, and the pursuit of more human-like cognitive abilities in AI systems. It directly addresses known limitations of current Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in complex spatial reasoning tasks, a domain where human cognition seamlessly integrates verbal and visual thinking.

**Evolution from Chain-of-Thought (CoT):** The paper builds upon the highly successful Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting paradigm, which revolutionized LLM reasoning by enabling models to articulate step-by-step reasoning traces. However, the authors correctly identify that traditional CoT, being purely text-based, struggles significantly with *spatial reasoning*. This limitation stems from the inherent difficulty of representing complex visual layouts and dynamic changes solely through textual descriptions. Previous attempts to extend CoT to multimodal models often involved two-stage processes (e.g., captioning images, generating scene graphs) or relied on external tools (ReAct-style pipelines). While these methods allow MLLMs to process multimodal input, the authors argue that they don't enable the *model itself* to genuinely "reason" in a multimodal space or to "imagine" visual changes.

**Addressing the Gap in Multimodal Reasoning:** Human cognition, as explained by dual-coding theory, processes information through both verbal and non-verbal (visual) channels. This paper directly takes inspiration from this, posing the key question: "can MLLMs imagine in visual modality while reasoning?" This moves beyond mere multimodal *understanding* or *generation* as separate tasks, towards multimodal *reasoning* where visual output becomes an *internal reasoning step*. This distinguishes MVoT from prior "Visualization-of-Thought" (VoT) work, which used simplified textual proxies for "mental images," by generating actual image visualizations.

**Leveraging Multimodal Generative Models:** The emergence of "multimodal-native" models like Chameleon, Gemini, and Sora, which are proficient in both interpreting and producing high-quality outputs across textual and visual domains, provides the technological foundation for MVoT. These models enable the proposed paradigm to transcend text-form thinking by integrating visual "thought" directly into the reasoning trace, moving towards a more holistic and integrated multimodal AI. This work positions itself as a significant step towards developing AI systems that can reason more effectively in environments requiring dynamic spatial understanding, akin to how humans use mental imagery.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **enable Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to engage in visual thinking by generating image visualizations as part of their reasoning traces**, thereby enhancing their performance and interpretability in complex spatial reasoning tasks.

The motivation for this objective stems from several critical observations and challenges:

- **Limitations of Verbal Chain-of-Thought (CoT) in Spatial Reasoning:** While CoT has been highly effective for various reasoning tasks (mathematics, logic), its performance deteriorates significantly when confronted with complex spatial reasoning problems. Textual descriptions often fail to adequately capture intricate visual patterns, spatial layouts, and dynamic changes in an environment.
- **Inspiration from Human Cognition:** Humans naturally think in both words and images. Dual-coding theory and working memory models explain this phenomenon, highlighting the crucial role of both verbal and non-verbal channels in reasoning, decision-making, and understanding the physical world. The authors aim to imbue MLLMs with a similar dual-processing capability.
- **Beyond External Tools and Textual Proxies:** Current multimodal reasoning pipelines often rely on external visual modules (e.g., captioning, object detection) or specialized tools. This dependency complicates adaptation to advanced tasks and limits the model's inherent ability to reason multimodally. Furthermore, previous attempts at "visualization-of-thought" used *textual proxies* for visual states, which are too simplistic and fail to capture true visual intricacies. The motivation is to enable *native visual thinking* within the MLLM itself.
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Reasoning Quality:** By generating explicit image visualizations of their reasoning traces, MVoT aims to provide more intuitive and direct illustrations of the model's thought process. This not only improves interpretability for users but also, crucially, enhances the quality and robustness of the reasoning itself, especially in scenarios where textual representations are ambiguous or insufficient.
- **Leveraging Emerging Multimodal Generative Capabilities:** Recent advancements in multimodal generation capabilities of foundation models (e.g., Chameleon, Gemini) present a unique opportunity. The motivation is to harness these capabilities not just for final output generation, but for *intermediate reasoning steps*, turning visual generation into a powerful "thought" process.

In essence, the paper seeks to bridge the gap between verbal reasoning and visual understanding in MLLMs, allowing them to "imagine" and track changes in dynamic environments, leading to more robust, accurate, and transparent spatial reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of Multimodal Visualization-of-Thought (MVoT) lies in enabling MLLMs to generate interleaved reasoning traces that combine both verbal (textual) and visual (image) thoughts.

#### 3.1. Formulation of MVoT

Given a multimodal input sequence, MVoT formulates the reasoning process as an interleaved generation of verbal thoughts (`z_i`) and corresponding visual thoughts (`v_i`). For a multi-hop spatial reasoning task, the model generates:
- A visual thought `v_i` conditioned on previous verbal and visual thoughts (`z_1...z_i`, `v_1...v_{i-1}`).
- A subsequent verbal thought `z_{i+1}` conditioned on the original input `x` and all previously generated verbal and visual thoughts (`z_1...z_i`, `v_1...v_i`).
This recursive, interleaved generation allows the model to dynamically update and "visualize" its understanding of the environment as it processes actions.

#### 3.2. Training with Autoregressive MLLMs

The authors implement MVoT by fine-tuning an established autoregressive MLLM, specifically **Anole-7B**, which is based on **Chameleon [Cha24]**. This architecture is well-suited for MVoT because it leverages a unified Transformer model capable of processing and generating both image and text tokens.
- **Tokenization:** The process involves two separate tokenizers: an image tokenizer (based on VQ-GAN like ERO21) that converts images into discrete image token sequences, and a text tokenizer that maps textual data into corresponding token sequences.
- **Multimodal Sequence Modeling:** These token sequences (interleaved text and image tokens) are concatenated and fed into a causal Transformer model. The model is fine-tuned using a next-token prediction objective, meaning it learns to predict the next token in the sequence, whether it's a text token or an image token, given the preceding context. The image and text tokenizers themselves are kept frozen during this fine-tuning.

#### 3.3. Token Discrepancy Loss (L_D)

A critical innovation introduced to ensure high-quality visualization is the **token discrepancy loss (L_D)**.
- **Problem:** While the language model unifies text and image tokens, the image tokenizer and visual embeddings are trained separately from the autoregressive decoder. This can lead to a "discrepancy" between the visual embedding space (where image tokens are defined) and the language model's token embedding space. This misalignment can degrade the quality and fidelity of generated images.
- **Solution:** L_D minimizes the discrepancy between the model's predicted probability distribution over image tokens and the ground-truth image tokens in the *visual embedding space*. It computes a similarity matrix (using Mean Squared Error) between the ground-truth visual embedding and all codebook entries. Then, L_D penalizes probabilities assigned to predicted tokens that are visually dissimilar to the ground truth. This loss helps bridge the gap and align the two embedding spaces, encouraging the model to generate visually coherent and faithful images.
- **Total Loss:** The overall loss function for training is a combination of the standard cross-entropy loss (L_C) for both text and image tokens and the token discrepancy loss (L_D) for image tokens: `L = L_C + L_D`.

#### 3.4. Spatial Reasoning Tasks and Data Collection

To validate MVoT, the authors select three dynamic spatial reasoning tasks within grid-based environments, offering varying levels of complexity:
- **MAZE:** Navigating abstract mazes to a final destination, focusing on path following and location prediction.
- **MINIBEHAVIOR (InstallingAPrinter):** An embodied AI task requiring object interaction (picking up, dropping, toggling) and understanding environmental changes, while maintaining symbolic representations.
- **FROZENLAKE:** Similar to maze navigation but with more complex visual patterns (e.g., holes, elf character) and fine-grained dynamic environments, demanding robust pattern recognition.
New datasets were collected for these tasks, structured as interleaved text-image pairs, to provide the necessary supervision for training MVoT.

The approach integrates visual reasoning as a native component of the MLLM's thought process, moving beyond external modules and relying on a specific loss function to ensure the quality of these generated visual thoughts.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of MVoT across the three dynamic spatial reasoning tasks (MAZE, MINIBEHAVIOR, FROZENLAKE) yielded several key findings:

#### 4.1. MVoT Outperforms Direct Prompting and GPT-4o with Enhanced Interpretability

- **Performance:** MVoT consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to "Direct Prompting" (where models output answers without intermediate reasoning) and GPT-4o (both zero-shot and CoT). Direct Prompting struggled with overfitting (around 70% accuracy), and GPT-4o performed even worse, highlighting the inherent difficulty of these spatial tasks for models primarily relying on text. MVoT achieved over 90% accuracy on MAZE and MINIBEHAVIOR, and significantly improved over Direct Prompting on FROZENLAKE (by 7%).
- **Interpretability:** A significant advantage of MVoT is its ability to provide explicit verbal *and visual* thoughts of intermediate reasoning states. This vastly enhances interpretability, offering a clearer and more intuitive understanding of the model's reasoning process compared to text-only approaches.

#### 4.2. MVoT Achieves Comparable or Better Performance than CoT with Enhanced Robustness

- **Performance Comparison:** While traditional CoT (especially when explicitly describing environment layouts in text) achieved very high accuracy (over 95%) on MAZE and MINIBEHAVIOR, it struggled severely on FROZENLAKE, performing worse than the Direct baseline. In contrast, MVoT maintained comparable performance on MAZE (92.95%) and MINIBEHAVIOR (95.14%) while demonstrating a substantial improvement on FROZENLAKE (85.60%), showcasing its better stability and robustness.
- **CoT Vulnerabilities and Limitations:**
 - **Sensitivity to Environment Complexity:** CoT's performance significantly deteriorated on FROZENLAKE, especially with larger grid sizes. Error analysis revealed that over 90% of CoT's mistakes on FROZENLAKE were due to inaccurate textual coordinate descriptions of environmental features (e.g., holes), a problem not observed in the simpler MAZE and MINIBEHAVIOR tasks.
 - **Reliance on Textual Environment Description:** CoT's strong performance on MAZE and MINIBEHAVIOR heavily relied on explicit textual descriptions (captioning) of the environment layout. When reasoning was performed using only agent coordinates *without* explicit environment descriptions, CoT consistently underperformed the Direct baseline. This highlights CoT's limited generalization in complex environments where such descriptions might be difficult to generate accurately.
- **MVoT Robustness and Interpretability Advantages:**
 - **Robustness to Environment Complexity:** MVoT maintained stable performance across varying grid sizes and increasing complexity (e.g., more "holes" in FROZENLAKE), consistently achieving over 83% accuracy on FROZENLAKE, unlike CoT's significant decline. This suggests MVoT's ability to ground reasoning visually makes it less susceptible to the fragility of textual descriptions.
 - **Better Interpretability:** MVoT's visual reasoning mitigates errors from inaccurate text-based captions and provides a more direct, intuitive way to track the reasoning process than aligning textual coordinates with images. This reinforces the need for true multimodal reasoning beyond just textual descriptions.

#### 4.3. Impact of Interleaved Multimodal Rationales and Token Discrepancy Loss

- **Interleaved Training:** The study found that even without generating visualizations during inference, training with interleaved visual cues (the "Interleaved" variant) improved reasoning performance on MAZE and MINIBEHAVIOR (over 10% improvement over Direct). This suggests that exposing the model to visual information during training helps it leverage visual cues for reasoning. However, "Interleaved" saw a performance drop on FROZENLAKE, possibly due to the task's visual complexity challenging this passive leveraging of cues.
- **Token Discrepancy Loss (L_D):** The L_D was crucial for visualization quality and overall task performance. MVoT models trained with L_D produced highly accurate visualizations with minimal "pattern redundancy" (unintended visual elements). They achieved 95% correct and consecutive visualizations during recursive generation. Without L_D, generation quality significantly degraded, with frequent redundant patterns and failures to capture state transitions accurately. This directly impacted task performance, highlighting L_D's role in aligning visual embeddings and improving visual generation fidelity.

#### 4.4. MVoT Complements CoT and Plug-in Potential

- **Complementary Strengths:** An analysis of combined predictions showed that MVoT and CoT's failures are often non-overlapping. Their "upper-bound" combined performance reached nearly 100% on MAZE and MINIBEHAVIOR, and 92% on FROZENLAKE. This indicates that MVoT offers an alternative reasoning strategy that complements textual CoT, suggesting the promise of hybrid reasoning approaches.
- **Plug-in for Proprietary Models:** MVoT can also serve as a plug-in. When visual thoughts generated by the fine-tuned MVoT model were provided to GPT-4o after its verbal thought, GPT-4o's performance improved by over 15% accuracy across all tasks.

In summary, MVoT demonstrates superior performance, robustness, and interpretability in complex spatial reasoning compared to text-only CoT, especially in challenging dynamic environments, largely due to its native visual thinking capabilities and the effectiveness of the token discrepancy loss.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Multimodal Visualization-of-Thought (MVoT) represents a significant advancement in the field of AI reasoning, particularly for multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Its significance and potential impact can be understood in several key areas:

#### 5.1. Pioneering Native Multimodal Reasoning

MVoT is presented as the first framework to naturally generate visual thought as an integral part of the reasoning process. This moves beyond merely processing multimodal inputs or generating multimodal outputs, towards a true *multimodal reasoning paradigm* where visual imagery contributes directly to the internal "thought" process of the AI. This is a crucial step towards developing AI systems that reason more akin to humans, leveraging both verbal and non-verbal cognition.

#### 5.2. Enhancing Spatial Reasoning Capabilities

The work directly addresses a known weakness of LLMs and MLLMs: complex spatial reasoning. By enabling visual imagination, MVoT allows models to dynamically track changes in environments, locate objects, and understand interactions, leading to superior performance and robustness in tasks like navigation, object manipulation, and environment simulation. This is particularly impactful for applications in robotics, autonomous systems, and interactive simulations, where understanding and predicting spatial dynamics are critical.

#### 5.3. Improving Interpretability and Trust in AI

Generating visual thoughts provides a more intuitive and direct window into the model's reasoning process. Unlike abstract textual coordinate descriptions, visual traces allow human users to easily follow and verify the AI's step-by-step "understanding" of the changing environment. This enhanced interpretability is vital for building trust in complex AI systems and for debugging their failures.

#### 5.4. Bridging Gaps in MLLM Architectures

The introduction of the `token discrepancy loss` is a notable technical contribution. It addresses a practical challenge in autoregressive MLLMs where independently trained text and image tokenizers can lead to misaligned embedding spaces. By bridging this gap, the loss significantly improves the quality and fidelity of generated image visualizations, which in turn contributes to better reasoning outcomes. This contribution has broader implications for the development and training of future multimodal generative models.

#### 5.5. Fostering Hybrid Reasoning Approaches

The finding that MVoT complements traditional Chain-of-Thought (CoT) by addressing different failure modes is highly significant. The near-perfect upper-bound performance when combining their predictions suggests that future advanced AI systems might leverage an ensemble of diverse reasoning strategies – some verbal, some visual – to achieve robust and comprehensive problem-solving capabilities. This opens avenues for designing hybrid AI architectures that capitalize on the strengths of multiple modalities and reasoning styles.

#### 5.6. Advancing World Models and Simulation

By enabling MLLMs to generate dynamic visual sequences as part of their reasoning, MVoT contributes to the development of more sophisticated "world models." These models can simulate environmental changes and predict future states more accurately, which is foundational for embodied AI, reinforcement learning, and content generation. The ability to "imagine" and visualize consequences of actions internally moves beyond simply observing, towards active simulation within the model's cognitive process.

**Potential Future Directions (as acknowledged by authors):**
The paper also highlights limitations that point to future research. Addressing the issue of generated visualizations reconstructing task-irrelevant details, perhaps through guidance techniques (similar to diffusion models), would further refine MVoT. Reducing the computational overhead of generating explicit visualizations through more compact image representations could also make the paradigm more practical for real-time applications.

In conclusion, MVoT marks a pivotal step towards more human-like, robust, and interpretable multimodal AI reasoning. By enabling MLLMs to "think in images," it unlocks new possibilities for complex tasks in dynamic and spatial environments, pushing the boundaries of what current AI systems can achieve.
