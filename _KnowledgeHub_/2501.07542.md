# Imagine while Reasoning in Space: Multimodal Visualization-of-Thought

> [!summary] Summary
> Multimodal Visualization-of-Thought (MVoT) enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to generate interleaved verbal and visual reasoning traces, enhancing their ability to perform complex dynamic spatial reasoning. This approach achieved superior accuracy and robustness on challenging grid-based tasks, demonstrating a more human-like cognitive process by integrating visual imagination into AI reasoning.

## Problem

- Traditional text-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) struggles with complex spatial reasoning tasks, as textual descriptions often fail to capture intricate visual patterns and dynamic changes.
- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) lack an inherent ability to "imagine" or reason visually within their internal thought process, often relying on external tools or simplistic textual proxies for visual states.
- Human cognition seamlessly integrates verbal and non-verbal (visual) thinking, a capability largely absent in most AI reasoning systems, leading to limitations in tasks requiring dynamic spatial understanding.

## Method

- MVoT formulates multimodal reasoning as an ==interleaved generation of verbal and visual thoughts==, recursively conditioned on previous states.
- It fine-tunes an autoregressive MLLM (Anole-7B based on Chameleon) on interleaved text and image token sequences using a next-token prediction objective.
- A novel ==token discrepancy loss (L_D)== is introduced during training to minimize the misalignment between the visual embedding space and the language model's token embedding space, ensuring high fidelity in generated image visualizations.

## Results

- MVoT consistently achieved over **90%** accuracy on MAZE and MINIBEHAVIOR, and **85.60%** on FROZENLAKE, outperforming Direct Prompting and GPT-4o with enhanced interpretability.
- MVoT demonstrated superior robustness and stability compared to text-only CoT, especially on challenging tasks like FROZENLAKE where CoT's performance deteriorated significantly due to issues with textual coordinate descriptions.
- The token discrepancy loss (L_D) was critical for MVoT's success, leading to **95%** correct and consecutive visualizations during recursive generation and enabling high-quality visual thoughts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly generating visual "thoughts" as part of the reasoning process significantly enhances interpretability and performance in complex spatial tasks for MLLMs.
> - The token discrepancy loss (L_D) is crucial for aligning visual and language model embedding spaces, leading to high-quality visual generations and improved reasoning fidelity.
> - MVoT's visual reasoning offers a robust alternative to textual Chain-of-Thought (CoT), particularly in dynamic environments where textual descriptions of spatial layouts are prone to error or ambiguity.
