# Inference Time Policy Optimization for Offline RL with Differentiable World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for offline reinforcement learning, termed MPCwDWM, dynamically refines a pre-trained policy during deployment by leveraging differentiable world models and imagined rollouts for inference-time policy optimization. This method achieved an average normalized score of 98.5 on D4RL Gym MuJoCo tasks, outperforming prior offline RL baselines, with a computationally efficient variant (FlowMPC) providing up to a 75x speedup.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) agents often suffer from performance degradation due to distribution shift, where the deployed policy's actions deviate from the training data, leading to unreliable value estimates.
- Standard offline RL methods deploy static policies that cannot adapt to the unique characteristics of specific states encountered during real-time deployment, limiting their ability to handle nuances or discrepancies.
- Existing approaches largely fail to leverage immediate inference-time state information for dynamic refinement of policy parameters during deployment.

## Method

- Introduces MPCwDWM, a framework that leverages a differentiable ==world model== for Inference Time Policy Optimization (ITPO) in offline **RL**.
- Offline, the method trains a differentiable diffusion sampler for state transitions, a reward model, and pre-trains a policy and terminal critic using a behavior-cloning regularized actor-critic (ReBRAC).
- At inference, the policy parameters are dynamically updated via gradient ascent on an MPC objective, calculated from imagined rollouts through the DWM, before action execution, with FlowMPC providing a computationally efficient variant.

## Results

- DiffMPC consistently outperformed strong offline RL baselines, achieving an average normalized score of **98.5** on D4RL Gym MuJoCo tasks and **85.07** on AntMaze, showing improvements over static policies.
- The method surpassed generative model-based approaches (e.g., Diffuser, **94.4** vs **77.5**) and gradient-based planning approaches optimizing action sequences (**99.66** vs **94.30** average normalized score).
- FlowMPC, a variant using a one-step MeanFlow model, recovered most performance gains while achieving an **8x** speedup in forward-pass latency and 15-**75x** total speedup compared to iterative diffusion samplers.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically adapting policy parameters at inference time, guided by differentiable world models, consistently enhances offline RL performance compared to static pre-trained policies.
> - Differentiable world models, including diffusion models, can be effectively leveraged to compute gradients for direct policy parameter optimization, rather than solely for action generation or sequence planning.
> - The computational overhead of iterative generative models can be addressed using one-step differentiable models (e.g., MeanFlow), maintaining performance benefits while drastically improving inference speed.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the field of Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL), a paradigm where agents learn optimal policies from a fixed dataset of pre-collected trajectories without any further interaction with the environment. This setting is particularly relevant in domains where online interaction is costly, risky, or impractical, such as healthcare, robotics, autonomous driving, and recommender systems.

Traditional offline RL methods typically train a policy once on the static dataset and then deploy it without further adaptation during inference. This approach faces core challenges related to distribution shift, where the learned policy might propose actions not well-represented in the training data, leading to unreliable value estimates and suboptimal performance. Existing solutions address this by either regularizing the policy to stay close to the data distribution (e.g., BRAC, IQL) or by employing conservative Q-learning (CQL) to penalize out-of-distribution actions. A separate line of research leverages generative models, such as Decision Transformers or diffusion-based policies, to model actions or trajectories, but these also generally produce a static policy or sample plans without continuous parameter adaptation.

Another relevant area is Model Predictive Control (MPC), which uses a model of the system dynamics to plan optimal action sequences over a finite horizon. While MPC often involves learned dynamics models, these are typically used in online settings with environment interaction (e.g., Dreamer, TD-MPC) or for gradient-free planning over action sequences, without modifying the underlying policy parameters at inference time.

This paper identifies a gap: most existing offline RL methods do not explicitly utilize inference-time information about the specific states encountered by the agent to adapt the policy parameters dynamically. Drawing inspiration from recent advancements in large language models that use inference-time computation for output refinement (e.g., self-consistency, search-based reasoning), this work proposes to integrate such adaptive mechanisms into offline RL. The key distinction is the optimization of *policy parameters* at inference time, rather than merely selecting actions or refining action sequences, through the use of a differentiable world model. This approach aims to address the limitations of static policies and the challenges of accurate Q-estimation in offline RL by allowing local adaptation based on immediate state context and imagined future consequences.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance of offline reinforcement learning agents by introducing an inference-time adaptation framework. This framework aims to refine a pre-trained policy dynamically during deployment by leveraging information about the specific states the agent encounters.

The motivation for this objective stems from several observations and challenges in current offline RL practices:
- **Static Policy Deployment:** Conventional offline RL algorithms produce a policy that is fixed after training on the offline dataset. This static policy cannot adapt to the unique characteristics or nuances of particular states encountered during deployment, potentially limiting its performance, especially when there are discrepancies between the training data distribution and the actual deployment environment.
- **Challenges in Q-value Estimation:** Accurate estimation of Q-values is inherently difficult in RL, as it involves predicting long-horizon returns. This challenge is compounded in offline RL by the problem of distribution shift, where the target policy's actions might deviate from the behavior policy that generated the data, leading to overestimation of Q-values for out-of-distribution actions. A static policy, reliant on these potentially inaccurate Q-estimates, may struggle.
- **Untapped Inference-Time Information:** The authors note that the specific state `s_t` an agent encounters at deployment represents new information not fully exploited by a pre-trained, fixed policy. If this information can be coupled with a model that predicts the consequences of actions from `s_t`, the agent could refine its decision-making in real time.
- **Inspiration from Large Language Models:** The success of inference-time computation in other AI domains, such as large language models employing self-consistency or search-based reasoning to improve outputs, suggests that similar benefits might be achievable in offline RL.
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC) Principles:** The local nature of one-step transition dynamics `P(s'|s,a)` and reward functions `r(s,a)` makes them suitable for learning from offline data. MPC offers a framework for utilizing such learned world models to inform decision-making. However, unlike traditional MPC which optimizes action sequences, the motivation here is to use the information from simulated rollouts to *update the policy parameters* continuously. This allows the initial policy to be refined based on immediate observations and predictions, aiming for better performance in the long run.

In summary, the core motivation is to overcome the limitations of static policies in offline RL by enabling on-the-fly policy parameter optimization at inference time, thereby making the agent more adaptive and potentially more robust to novel state encounters, through the use of a differentiable world model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Inference Time Policy Optimization (ITPO) with a Differentiable World Model (DWM), termed **MPCwDWM** (Model Predictive Control with Differentiable World Model), consists of an offline training stage and an inference-time adaptation stage.

#### 3.1. Offline Training Stage: World Model Components and Policy Pre-training

The offline dataset `D = {(s_t, a_t, r_t, s_{t+1})}` is used to train three main components:

1. **Differentiable Diffusion Sampler (`f_θ`):** This component learns the conditional state-transition dynamics `p_θ(s_{t+1} | s_t, a_t)`. It is implemented as a conditional diffusion model (DDPM).
 - **Forward Process:** A fixed Gaussian Markov chain progressively adds noise to `s_{t+1}` over `K` steps, creating `s^{(k)}_{t+1} = \sqrt{\bar{\alpha}_k} s_{t+1} + \sqrt{1 - \bar{\alpha}_k} \epsilon`.
 - **Reverse Process:** A neural network `$\hat{\epsilon}_θ$` is trained to predict the noise `$\epsilon$` given the noisy state `s^{(k)}_{t+1}` and the conditioning `c_t = (s_t, a_t)`. This reverse process, when unrolled from `s^{(K)}_{t+1} \sim N(0, I)` to `s^{(0)}_{t+1}`, serves as the differentiable sampler `f_θ(s_t, a_t, \epsilon_t)`. The differentiability with respect to `(s_t, a_t)` for a fixed `$\epsilon_t$` (collection of Gaussian samples) is crucial for backpropagation.
 - **Training Objective:** The model minimizes the standard conditional denoising loss `L_diff(θ)`, which is the mean squared error between the true noise and the predicted noise.

2. **Reward Model (`r_ξ`):** A parametric function `r_ξ: S × A → R` is trained to predict the immediate reward for a given state-action pair. It is fitted via supervised regression on the observed rewards in the offline dataset, minimizing `L_r(ξ):= E_{(s_t,a_t,r_t) \sim D} [r_ξ(s_t, a_t) - r_t]^2`. This model is also differentiable.

3. **Policy (`π_ψ`) and Terminal Critic (`Q_ϕ`):** A stochastic policy `π_ψ(a|s)` and an action-value function `Q_ϕ(s,a)` are pre-trained using a behavior-cloning (BC) regularized actor-critic algorithm, specifically ReBRAC.
 - **Critic Training:** `Q_ϕ` minimizes a one-step temporal-difference (TD) loss `L_Q(ϕ)` using a target network `$\bar{Q}_ϕ$` and actions sampled from the current `π_ψ`.
 - **Actor Training:** `π_ψ` maximizes `Q_ϕ` while being regularized to stay close to the dataset's behavior via a behavior-cloning term, minimizing `L_π(ψ):= E_{(s,a)\sim D, a_π \sim π_ψ(\cdot|s)} [-Q_ϕ(s, a_π) - λ \log π_ψ(a|s)]`.
 - The critic `Q_ϕ` serves as the terminal value function in the MPC objective.

#### 3.2. Inference-Time Adaptation Stage: MPCwDWM Algorithm

At each real environment state `s_t`, the MPCwDWM algorithm performs the following steps:

1. **Imagined Rollouts:** `M` independent imagined rollouts of horizon `H` are generated starting from `s_t`. For each rollout `m`:
 - Initialize `$\tilde{s}^{(m)}_0 = s_t$`.
 - For `j = 0, \dots, H-1`:
 - An action `$\tilde{a}^{(m)}_j = π_ψ(\tilde{s}^{(m)}_j)$` is sampled (or deterministically taken if `π_ψ` is deterministic).
 - The next state `$\tilde{s}^{(m)}_{j+1} = f_θ(\tilde{s}^{(m)}_j, \tilde{a}^{(m)}_j, \epsilon^{(m)}_{t+j})$` is simulated using the differentiable diffusion sampler `f_θ` and a unique noise sequence `$\epsilon^{(m)}_{t+j}$`.

2. **Objective Evaluation:** For each rollout, a finite-horizon return `L(ψ; \epsilon_{t:t+H-1}^{(m)})` is calculated:
 `$L = \sum_{j=0}^{H-1} \gamma^j r_ξ(\tilde{s}^{(m)}_j, \tilde{a}^{(m)}_j) + \gamma^H Q_ϕ(\tilde{s}^{(m)}_H, π_ψ(\tilde{s}^{(m)}_H))$`.
 The overall MPC objective `J_t(ψ)` is then the Monte Carlo average of these returns over `M` rollouts.

3. **Policy Optimization:** `E` steps of gradient ascent are performed on `J_t(ψ)` to update the policy parameters `ψ`. Crucially, `f_θ`, `r_ξ`, and `Q_ϕ` (which are also differentiable) are held fixed during this inner-loop optimization: `ψ ← ψ + η ∇_ψ J_t(ψ)`, where `η` is the step size. This backpropagation is end-to-end through the imagined rollouts, enabling direct policy parameter refinement.

4. **Action Execution:** After the `E` gradient steps, the updated policy `π_ψ` (with refined `ψ`) is used to select and execute the action `a_t = π_ψ(s_t)` in the real environment. The environment returns `(r_t, s_{t+1})`, and the process repeats for the next state `s_{t+1}`.

#### 3.3. FlowMPC: Computational Improvement

Recognizing the high computational cost of iterative diffusion sampling (each `f_θ` call requires `K` denoising steps), the authors propose **FlowMPC**.
- **One-Step MeanFlow Model:** The iterative diffusion sampler `f_θ` is replaced with a one-step MeanFlow model. This model maps `(s_t, a_t, \epsilon_t)` to `s_{t+1}` in a single forward pass, significantly reducing latency while retaining differentiability.
- **Policy-Tilted Training Objective:** To compensate for the potential loss of expressiveness from using a one-step model, the MeanFlow world model is trained under a policy-tilted objective. This objective upweights dataset transitions whose actions are likely under a strong pre-trained policy `π_{ψ_{pre}}`, effectively focusing the model's capacity on state-action regions that the deployed controller is likely to query.

This methodology combines model-based planning with policy-gradient-style optimization at inference time, distinguishing it from prior model-based or MPC-based RL techniques by adapting the policy parameters dynamically.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings across D4RL continuous-control benchmarks (MuJoCo locomotion and AntMaze tasks), demonstrating the effectiveness and characteristics of the proposed Inference Time Policy Optimization (ITPO) framework.

#### 4.1. Performance Against Offline RL Baselines (DiffMPC)

- **MuJoCo Locomotion:** DiffMPC (using a diffusion world model) consistently outperformed strong offline RL baselines, including TD3+BC, IQL, CQL, and ReBRAC. On the D4RL Gym MuJoCo tasks, DiffMPC achieved an average normalized score of 98.5, compared to 93.9 for the pre-trained ReBRAC policy. This indicates that inference-time adaptation of policy parameters provides measurable statistical benefits over static policies.
- **AntMaze Tasks:** Similar gains were observed on D4RL AntMaze tasks. DiffMPC attained an average score of 85.07, an improvement over ReBRAC's 77.62. The most significant gains were on challenging AntMaze variants like `large-play` (59 to 67) and `large-diverse` (51 to 66).

#### 4.2. Performance Against Generative Model Baselines

- DiffMPC was compared against generative model-based offline RL approaches such as Decision Transformer (DT), Trajectory Transformer (TT), MOPO, MOReL, MBOP, and Diffuser.
- On a subset of D4RL MuJoCo tasks used for direct comparison, DiffMPC achieved an average normalized score of 94.4, outperforming Diffuser, which scored 77.5. This suggests that explicit policy parameter optimization via differentiable world models can be more effective than purely generative sequence modeling or trajectory generation for action selection.

#### 4.3. Performance Against MPC-based Baselines

- **TD-MPC2:** While TD-MPC2 and its offline-adapted variant with behavior cloning (BC) were trained, they did not approach the performance of standard offline RL baselines, as TD-MPC2 is designed for online, sample-efficient regimes rather than purely offline settings. This comparison highlights the challenge of adapting online MPC methods directly to the offline setting.
- **Gradient-based Planning (Action Updates):** DiffMPC was compared to a variant that performs gradient-based planning by directly optimizing an open-loop action sequence (Grad-MPC), similar to V et al.. DiffMPC outperformed Grad-MPC on 14 of 15 datasets, improving the average normalized score from 94.30 to 99.66. This suggests that optimizing policy parameters `ψ` rather than ephemeral action sequences is more effective, leveraging the inductive bias of the pre-trained policy.
- **Diffusion-based MPC (D-MPC):** Compared to D-MPC (Zhou et al., 2024), which uses diffusion for action proposals and dynamics modeling for gradient-free random shooting, DiffMPC achieved an average normalized score of 86.87 versus D-MPC's 65.98. This indicates that backpropagating gradients through a differentiable world model to update policy parameters is more effective than selecting actions from diffusion-sampled open-loop sequences.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Planning Horizon (H):** DiffMPC was robust across H ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10, 20}. Moderate horizons (H=5 or H=10) generally performed well. Even short horizons (H=1, H=2) often captured significant gains, suggesting the immediate gradient signal is valuable. Longer horizons (H=20) sometimes showed higher variance or slight degradation due to compounding model errors.
- **Inner Gradient Steps (E):** The optimal number of inner gradient steps varied by environment. For Walker2d, performance generally improved with increasing E (up to E=5). However, for Hopper, increasing E led to performance degradation and increased variance, attributed to Hopper's sensitivity to policy perturbations and the amplification of model/value errors. E=1 was identified as a robust default.
- **Inner Step Size (η):** Similar to E, optimal η was environment-specific. Larger η improved Walker2d performance, while causing instability or degradation in Hopper and some HalfCheetah tasks. A moderate `η = 5 × 10^{-6}` provided a reasonable trade-off.
- **DDIM Sampling Steps (K):** K influences the fidelity of the diffusion sampler and computational cost. On some datasets, K had a small effect, with K=1 or K=2 being competitive. Other datasets benefited from larger K (e.g., `full-replay` datasets), suggesting that richer data can train more expressive models where higher sampling fidelity helps. However, larger K also increases latency linearly.
- **Number of Monte Carlo Particles (M):** DiffMPC was stable across M ∈ {512, 1024, 4096}, indicating that the gradient estimator is well-behaved and further variance reduction provides only marginal changes to policy performance.
- **Pre-trained Policy (SAC-RND):** When using SAC-RND as the pre-trained policy, DiffMPC still showed improvements, raising the average normalized score from 84.08 to 93.00, confirming the general applicability of the inference-time adaptation framework.

#### 4.5. Inference-Time Compute Cost and FlowMPC

- **DiffMPC Latency:** DiffMPC was significantly slower than a zero-shot policy (e.g., ~200 ms/step for H=5, K=10, compared to ~0.4 ms/step for zero-shot). Both H and K linearly contributed to this cost, with K being a primary bottleneck due to iterative denoising.
- **FlowMPC Performance:** FlowMPC, which replaces the diffusion sampler with a one-step MeanFlow model and uses a policy-tilted training objective, recovered much of the performance gains. It improved the average normalized score over the ReBRAC initialization from 94.93 to 97.87 on MuJoCo, remaining competitive with DiffMPC.
- **FlowMPC Latency:** FlowMPC drastically reduced computational cost. It achieved an approximate 8x speedup in forward-pass latency and a 15-75x speedup in total (forward+backward) time compared to a K=10 to K=50 diffusion sampler, addressing the key bottleneck of iterative sampling.

These results collectively demonstrate that inference-time policy optimization with differentiable world models can consistently improve performance in offline RL, and that computational costs can be significantly mitigated through efficient one-step generative models like MeanFlow.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a significant advancement in offline reinforcement learning by demonstrating the statistical benefits of inference-time policy optimization using differentiable world models. Its core contribution, **MPCwDWM**, establishes a novel paradigm where a pre-trained policy is dynamically refined during deployment based on the specific state encountered and imagined future trajectories.

The key significance of this work includes:
- **Enhanced Performance in Offline RL:** The consistent gains achieved by DiffMPC over strong offline RL baselines (TD3+BC, IQL, CQL, ReBRAC, and various generative/MPC methods) across diverse D4RL benchmarks indicate that dynamic policy adaptation at inference time can lead to more effective and robust policies than static, pre-trained ones.
- **Novel Application of Differentiable World Models:** The paper demonstrates a novel application of differentiable world models, particularly diffusion models, not just for generating imagined trajectories during training or sampling candidate actions, but for computing gradients to directly update policy parameters. This approach capitalizes on the rich, multi-modal dynamics captured by generative models.
- **Addressing Distribution Shift:** By allowing the policy to locally adapt to the current state and its predicted future, the method implicitly mitigates some aspects of distribution shift. The policy can locally optimize within relevant state-action regions, guided by the world model and a conservative critic, rather than relying solely on global approximations from potentially sparse offline data.
- **Computational Efficiency with FlowMPC:** The introduction of FlowMPC directly addresses the practical barrier of high inference-time computation associated with iterative diffusion sampling. By leveraging one-step MeanFlow models and policy-tilted training, the method recovers most of the performance benefits at a fraction of the computational cost, making real-world deployment more feasible. This highlights a crucial trade-off between model fidelity and computational overhead.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Improved Real-World Applications:** In domains like healthcare, robotics, autonomous driving, and recommender systems, where online exploration is constrained or costly, this framework could lead to more reliable and adaptive control policies derived purely from existing data. The ability to refine policies on the fly based on current observations could make these systems more responsive and robust to unforeseen circumstances or gradual environment changes.
- **New Research Avenues:** This work opens several promising directions for future research. The authors suggest exploring trajectory-level generative world models (generating multi-step futures in a single pass) to further amortize long-horizon costs. Extending the framework to generative policies (e.g., Flow Q-Learning) and investigating offline-to-online extensions with periodic world-model updates to maintain robustness under distribution shift are also mentioned.
- **Differentiable Planning and Control:** The success of end-to-end gradient computation through learned world models could inspire further integration of differentiable programming paradigms into model-based RL, leading to more sophisticated and efficient planning algorithms.

However, the authors also acknowledge broader impacts, noting that while the method can improve safety-critical systems, inference-time policy updates make the deployed policy state-dependent and potentially harder to audit than fixed policies, which might complicate verification. They advocate for careful domain-specific validation, uncertainty quantification, and human oversight before real-world deployment.
