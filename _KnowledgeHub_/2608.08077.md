# Explore, Map, Remember, Decide: Are Embodied VLMs Ready for Safety-Critical Scenarios?

> [!summary] Summary
> EMRD (Explore, Map, Remember, Decide) is an evaluation pipeline that drops vision-language models into a partially observable four-room 3D office under a simulated emergency, scoring exploration coverage, cognitive-map geometric fidelity, psychological memory laws, and focal-point spatial grounding. Across seven models, evacuation choices track pre-trained textual priors rather than explored evidence, and memory diverges sharply from human cognition.

## Problem

- Theory of Space and prior exploration benchmarks probe passive visual reasoning and map building, but never test whether spatial beliefs survive safety-critical, goal-driven decisions.
- Existing mapping metrics reward accuracy alone, so a model tracking one object scores perfect stability, and hallucinated or never-visited rooms go unpenalised.
- Known failures such as object hallucination, prior overriding, and long-horizon forgetting are measured in isolation, never against human cognitive laws under environmental hazards.

## Method

- **EMRD** formalises evacuation as a ==POMDP== in ==AI2-THOR==/==ProcTHOR== with discrete semantic actions and an explicit JSON cognitive map updated each step through a structured deduplication check.
- Explore and Map metrics: ==Absolute Environment Coverage== whose min operator blocks hallucination inflation, ==Temporal Efficiency==, plus ==Procrustes-aligned Positional Accuracy== and ==coverage-weighted Temporal Belief Stability==.
- Remember fits ==Ebbinghaus decay==, ==serial-position bins== and ==Miller's Law== forgetting curves; Decide adds ==Focal-Point Spatial Grounding== with interventions ablating room descriptors, names, and object inventory.

## Results

- Removing the prompt object inventory dropped Conference Room preference from **88.6%** to **27.5%** (chi-square **62.4**, p below **1e-13**, Cramer's V **0.63**); anonymised room labels still kept **68.4%**.
- Grounding is weak: global FPSG spans **0.096** to **0.338**; Pixtral-12B picked never-visited rooms in **47.9%** of episodes, Ovis2-34B **30.1%**, against **5.1%** for Gemini-3.1-Pro.
- Low light cut coverage up to **10** points (Gemini **59.4%** to **49.6%**) and collapsed belief stability, while texture randomisation barely mattered; GPT-5.4 and Pixtral gave negative Ebbinghaus R-squared.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High inter-agent consensus can mask zero spatial grounding; agreement across runs is not evidence a decision came from exploration rather than shared pre-training priors.
> - Model memory is architectural, not biological: no U-shaped serial position, and working-memory limits spread eightfold across models, so human-agent cognitive alignment cannot be assumed.
> - Robustness to surface appearance does not imply perceptual robustness; visibility loss degrades every stage, arguing for auxiliary sensing before deploying such agents in emergencies.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of embodied artificial intelligence (AI), specifically focusing on the spatial cognition capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Recent advancements in embodied AI have explored how VLMs understand and navigate physical environments, but understanding their robustness in safety-critical applications remains an area of active investigation.

The paper builds upon existing frameworks such as the Theory of Space (ToS), which evaluates a foundation model's ability to construct global spatial maps through curiosity-driven exploration and explicit externalization of internal spatial memory. While previous research has shown VLMs capable of strong passive visual reasoning, their performance often declines in active navigation tasks that require robust spatial knowledge. This study addresses several known limitations of VLMs, including object hallucination, reliance on pre-trained priors, susceptibility to visual perturbations, and challenges with long-horizon memory. By extending the ToS framework, this research specifically investigates VLM performance in goal-driven, safety-critical scenarios, aiming to assess whether their spatial understanding, memory processes, and decision-making align with human cognitive patterns and withstand environmental hazards.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to evaluate the spatial understanding and decision-making capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) in safety-critical scenarios, such as emergency building evacuation. The motivation stems from the increasing deployment of AI techniques in domains where reliability and alignment with human-like cognition are crucial for safe and effective operation. The authors highlight that while VLMs can generate coherent spatial narratives, their underlying mechanisms may not align with human cognitive processes or possess the necessary resilience to environmental stressors typical of emergencies.

The research addresses three specific questions:

- **RQ1 (Focal-Point Reasoning)**: This question investigates whether VLM decisions regarding a critical focal point (e.g., a safe gathering room during an evacuation) are primarily based on spatial evidence acquired through active exploration, or if they are unduly influenced by pre-trained semantic biases within the model. This probes the grounding of VLM decisions in physical reality versus abstract textual priors.

- **RQ2 (Vision)**: This objective assesses the robustness of VLM spatial memory and mapping capabilities when subjected to environmental hazards. Specifically, it examines whether conditions such as severe visibility reduction (simulating smoke or power failure) or alterations in texture and color significantly degrade the models' ability to perceive and represent their surroundings accurately. This evaluates the perceptual resilience of VLMs in adverse conditions.

- **RQ3 (Memory)**: This question explores whether VLM spatial memory processes during active exploration align with established human cognitive laws. The study examines aspects such as temporal decay (Ebbinghaus curve), primacy-recency effects (tendency to remember initial and final items), and working memory limits (Miller's law). Understanding these alignments or divergences is critical for predicting VLM behavior and ensuring predictable interactions in complex tasks.

To achieve these objectives, the authors introduce and utilize a goal-driven pipeline named "Explore, Map, Remember, Decide (EMRD)," designed to systematically evaluate VLM performance across these critical phases of spatial cognition.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a simulated 3D, partially observable office environment to model an emergency building evacuation scenario. Agents navigate this environment with discrete semantic actions and must continually update an explicit cognitive map to deduce an optimal safe gathering room (focal-point).

**Scenario and Agent Interaction:**
- The environment is a four-room office (Entrance, Office, Conference Room, Kitchen) generated using AI2-THOR (ProcTHOR).
- Agents receive first-person RGB images and basic topological feedback.
- They interact via a discrete semantic action space (Goto(·), Turn(·)) and a termination action Term(r), where 'r' is the chosen safe gathering room.
- The VLM policy π(·|h_t) maps visual histories to actions and updates a cognitive map (JSON object tracking object coordinates).
- Each episode is limited to 50 steps, enforcing time pressure.

**EMRD Pipeline and Metrics:**
The study extends the Theory of Space (ToS) framework with four metric categories corresponding to the EMRD phases:

1. **Explore: Exploration Competence**
 - **Absolute Environment Coverage (AEC)**: Measures how well the terminal cognitive map captures the environment by counting unique recorded object instances against ground-truth instances. It penalizes incomplete exploration and memory retention failures. The `min` operator prevents inflation from hallucinated objects.
 - **Temporal Efficiency (TE)**: Measures the number of discrete actions taken to complete an episode. Low TE with high AEC indicates superior spatial planning.

2. **Map: Spatial Fidelity (Modified ToS)**
 - **Positional Accuracy (PA)**: Quantifies the geometric fidelity of the terminal cognitive map. Predicted object coordinates are zero-shot text inferences. To handle arbitrary egocentric origins and partial mapping, the metric incorporates optimal rigid-body Procrustes alignment, computes the scale normalizer dynamically over the mapped sub-graph, and anchors the penalty denominator to the unperturbed baseline object count.
 - **Temporal Belief Stability (TBS)**: Tracks spatial drift of objects in the cognitive map across exploration steps relative to their initial perception. It weights spatial drift against real-time map coverage to prevent artificially high stability from sparse maps.

3. **Remember: Memory Persistence**
 - **Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve**: Models object retention decay after leaving the field of view. It estimates a Memory Strength parameter (EFC) and assesses goodness-of-fit (R²) against a non-parametric Kaplan-Meier survival curve. A high R² indicates human-like biological decay.
 - **Serial Position Effect (SPE)**: Examines if VLMs exhibit primacy (better recall of early items) and recency (better recall of late items) effects. Objects are binned into Early, Middle, and Late discovery orders, and mean Time-to-Forget (TTFb) is calculated. A U-shaped curve would suggest human-like memory prioritization.
 - **Miller’s Law (Working Memory Capacity)**: Measures the probability of a forgetting event as a function of prior cognitive load (number of mapped objects). It seeks to identify an inflexion point indicating the model’s effective working memory capacity, analogous to the human 7 ± 2 item limit. The study uses bins of width 5 for cognitive load.
 - **Coverage-Anchored Kaplan-Meier Survival Analysis**: (From Appendix) Anchors survival probability to the agent's initial discovery rate, penalizing models that fail to thoroughly explore.
 - **Coverage-Anchored Spatial Identity Resolution (SIR)**: (From Appendix) Quantifies the agent's ability to recognize previously visited locations and deduplicate objects upon room re-entry, anchored by global map coverage.

4. **Decide: Cognitive Decision-Making**
 - **Focal-Point Spatial Grounding (FPSG)**: Assesses whether the agent's chosen evacuation point is based on physical exploration or pre-trained textual biases. The global FPSG anchors the count of correctly retained objects in the focal room against the total objects observed in the episode. A complementary local FPSG (from Appendix) anchors against ground-truth objects within the focal room to avoid penalizing sparse rooms.

**Models Evaluated:**
Seven state-of-the-art VLMs were used: four open-weight models (Qwen3-VL-32B, Ovis2-34B, Pixtral-12B, InternVL3-14B) and three proprietary models (Gemini 3.1 Pro, Claude Opus 4.8, GPT-5.4). Each experiment consisted of 80 runs.

**Stress Tests and Controlled Interventions:**
- **Environmental Stress**:
 - *Low-Light and Visibility*: Lighting and maximum visibility distance reduced by nearly 50%.
 - *Procedural Texture and Colour*: Materials and colors of all objects and walls randomized.
- **Controlled Interventions on Qwen3-VL-32B**:
 - *Textual Priors Intervention*: Neutralized specific descriptive features (e.g., "open, unobstructed floor space") by appending identical descriptive suffixes to all room prompts and replaced room names with generic labels (Rooms A–D).
 - *Inventory-Free Intervention*: Ablated the initial object list from the system prompt.
 These interventions aimed to isolate whether focal-point selection was driven by physical evidence, textual descriptors, or pre-trained semantic associations. Statistical significance was assessed using Pearson χ² tests and two-sided Fisher’s exact tests.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study's findings indicate limitations in the spatial cognition of current VLM agents, particularly in safety-critical contexts.

**RQ1: Focal-Point Decisions Driven by Pre-trained Semantic Biases**
- **Inter-Agent Consensus and Divergence**: VLMs showed high consensus for specific gathering points regardless of their starting position. However, there was a divergence in *which* room was chosen: GPT-5.4 and Claude Opus 4.8 consistently selected the Entrance, while Gemini-3.1-Pro, Qwen3-VL-32B, and Ovis2-34B converged on the Conference Room. This divergence in identical environments suggests decisions stem from pre-trained priors rather than real-time spatial evidence.
- **Lack of Spatial Grounding**: Focal-Point Spatial Grounding (FPSG) analysis revealed that models often selected evacuation rooms they had not physically visited (e.g., Pixtral-12B at 47.9%). Even models with high consensus, like GPT-5.4, failed to visit their chosen room in a notable percentage of episodes (12.5%). This indicates that inter-agent consensus can mask a lack of spatial grounding.
- **Controlled Interventions**:
 - When environmental features were neutralized (e.g., by giving all rooms descriptions like "open, unobstructed floor space"), Qwen3-VL-32B's preference for the Conference Room remained high (52.5%–78.8%).
 - Replacing room names with neutral labels (Rooms A–D) still resulted in the physical Conference Room being selected in 68.4% of episodes. This suggests that focal-point decisions are influenced by the interaction between prompt inventory and pre-trained semantic associations, with visual exploration often serving for post-hoc justification.
 - Removing the object inventory from the system prompt significantly reduced the Conference Room preference (from 88.6% to 27.5%), highlighting the inventory as a major driver of bias. Without the inventory, a recency bias emerged, and visual features (e.g., large table, multiple doorways) influenced choices only under direct visual exposure.

**RQ2: Vulnerability to Illumination, Robustness to Texture/Color Tampering**
- **Impact of Low Light/Visibility**: Low light and reduced visibility consistently degraded performance across all EMRD phases. This included up to a 10-point reduction in Absolute Environment Coverage, degraded Temporal Efficiency, lower geometric Positional Accuracy, and a collapse in Temporal Belief Stability for all models.
- **Minimal Impact of Texture/Color Randomization**: In contrast, randomizing textures and colors had negligible effects on mapping capabilities across these metrics.
- **Safety Implications**: This asymmetry suggests that spatial mapping in VLMs is highly vulnerable to visibility degradation, mirroring human perceptual limitations, but robust to surface alterations. This has direct safety implications for real-world emergencies, potentially suggesting the need for auxiliary sensing in VLM deployments.

**RQ3: Non-Biological Memory Patterns**
- **Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve**: Only Gemini-3.1-Pro, Claude Opus 4.8, and Ovis2-34B exhibited exponential decay consistent with biological memory (R² ≥ 0.80). GPT-5.4 and Pixtral-12B showed negative R² values, indicating non-biological, abrupt memory loss mechanisms rather than gradual decay. Environmental stressors did not significantly alter memory decay rates for most models.
- **Serial Position Effect**: No model replicated the human U-shaped primacy/recency curve. Instead, models like Gemini-3.1-Pro and GPT-5.4 displayed primacy-dominated retention (Early > Middle > Late), while others showed flat profiles.
- **Miller's Law (Working Memory Capacity)**: VLMs demonstrated a wide architectural spread in working memory capacity, ranging from GPT-5.4 maintaining near-zero forgetting up to 40–50 objects to InternVL3-14B losing items at 5–10 objects. This contrasts with the human 7 ± 2 cognitive limit, indicating distinct underlying architectures and a lack of human-agent cognitive alignment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research holds significance by systematically exposing fundamental limitations in the spatial cognition of current Vision-Language Models (VLMs), particularly when considered for deployment in safety-critical environments. The study demonstrates that even when VLMs generate spatially coherent narratives, their underlying mechanisms may not align with human cognitive patterns or possess the necessary resilience to critical environmental stressors.

The development of the Explore, Map, Remember, Decide (EMRD) pipeline provides a structured, quantitative benchmark for evaluating these crucial capabilities. This framework can contribute to advancing the development of trustworthy embodied AI agents by offering a means to measure performance beyond superficial task success rates.

The findings have several potential impacts:
- **Risk Identification**: The research identifies specific vulnerabilities, such as the reliance on pre-trained semantic biases over physical evidence for decision-making, the degradation of geometric mapping under low visibility, and non-biological working memory patterns. These vulnerabilities highlight unpredictable risks of misalignment in real-world deployments.
- **Improved Model Development**: By pinpointing these gaps, the study informs future research and development efforts. It suggests the need for VLM architectures that explicitly decouple high-level reasoning from low-level control and enforce stricter physical spatial grounding to reduce over-reliance on semantic priors.
- **Application-Specific Design**: For safety-critical applications, the observed vulnerability to low visibility suggests the integration of VLMs with auxiliary sensing modalities (e.g., thermal imaging, lidar) to overcome perceptual limitations that mirror human weaknesses.
- **Human-AI Interaction**: The divergence of VLM memory from human cognitive laws implies that assumptions about human-agent cognitive alignment cannot be reliably made. This is crucial for designing intuitive and predictable human-in-the-loop systems, especially in emergencies where human operators need to anticipate agent behavior.

While acknowledging limitations such as testing in a single office environment, static hazards, one safety prompt, and a text-biased inventory, the study provides a robust foundation for future research. It encourages exploration into dynamic emergencies, multi-agent coordination, and human-in-the-loop scenarios to further ensure the trustworthiness of embodied AI.
