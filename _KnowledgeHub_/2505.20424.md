# Robot Operation of Home Appliances by Reading User Manuals

> [!summary] Summary
> ApBot, a robot system developed at the National University of Singapore, operates novel household appliances by interpreting user manuals and visual observations, demonstrating zero-shot generalization capabilities. The system significantly outperforms direct large vision-language model (LVLM) control policies in simulation, achieving higher task success rates and greater robustness across diverse appliance types.

## Problem

- Existing robotic methods for appliance operation often rely on handcrafted behaviors or explicit training, limiting their generalization to novel appliances.
- User manuals, while containing crucial operational knowledge, are unstructured documents, making it challenging for robots to extract actionable, visually grounded policies.
- Robots struggle with robust, long-horizon multi-step appliance operations due to potential perception inaccuracies, manipulation failures, and compounding errors from imperfect model interpretations.

## Method

- **ApBot** constructs a ==structured symbolic model== of an appliance (state space, action space, transition function) by prompting large vision-language models (LVLMs) with its user manual.
- Symbolic actions are visually grounded to physical control panel elements (buttons, dials) using a multi-modal detection pipeline combining ==SAM==, ==OWL-ViT2==, ==OCR==, and LVLM verification.
- A ==closed-loop execution== system with '==macro actions==' and real-time visual feedback allows for state estimation, model updates, and task re-planning to recover from errors and refine transition models.

## Results

- ApBot achieved significantly higher success rates, fewer execution steps, and better Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) compared to state-of-the-art LVLM control baselines on a simulated benchmark of **30 appliances** and **300 tasks**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the structured appliance model, code-based button policies, and closed-loop updates were crucial, with their removal leading to substantial performance degradation, particularly as appliance complexity increased.
- The system was successfully deployed on a Kinova Gen3 arm, operating **three** real-world appliances (blender, induction cooker, water dispenser) for previously unseen, long-horizon tasks, outperforming baseline methods for more complex real-world scenarios.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Structured internal representations, derived from unstructured text and grounded in the physical world, are critical for robust robot decision-making in complex, long-horizon tasks.
> - Combining LVLM reasoning with code-based policies for low-level action sequences enhances precision and correctness, especially for actions with large value ranges or complex transitions.
> - Closed-loop model calibration, enabled by visual feedback and state re-estimation, is essential for compensating for initial model inaccuracies or ambiguities in user manuals, significantly improving task robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research addresses the challenge of enabling assistive home robots to operate a diverse range of household appliances. This domain is situated at the intersection of robotics, natural language processing, computer vision, and artificial intelligence, particularly leveraging recent advancements in large vision-language models (LVLMs).

Previous work in robotic appliance operation has typically focused on three sub-categories: perception, low-level manipulation, and long-horizon plan generation. Perception-based efforts primarily involve button localization using visual features, RFID tags, or neural networks. Low-level manipulation research concentrates on physical interaction with various button types, often specializing robot fingertips or integrating additional sensory feedback. However, long-horizon appliance operation, particularly in a generalized manner for novel appliances, has remained relatively underexplored. Existing methods often rely on handcrafted behaviors, which limits their generalization capabilities to new appliances or tasks.

The emergence of foundation models, including large language models (LLMs) and LVLMs, has influenced robotic decision-making by offering pathways to generate structured representations (e.g., logic frameworks, code) or unstructured reasoning (e.g., textual reasoning trees, natural languages). Structured representations, while improving reasoning precision for constrained, long-horizon tasks, can sometimes compromise generality. Conversely, unstructured representations offer flexibility but may suffer from ambiguity and lack correctness guarantees. Techniques such as syntax validation, corrective feedback, and prompt optimization are being explored to combine the advantages of both approaches. Domain-specific languages (DSLs) have also been shown to enhance the reliability and robustness of LVLMs, a principle that the authors adopt.

The work also relates to Graphical User Interface (GUI) agents, which operate devices through software interfaces. While similar in goal, appliance operation deals with physical interfaces, adding challenges related to physical grounding and manipulation. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is another related field, where models are augmented with information retrieved from external sources. This paper builds on the concept of incorporating external knowledge by integrating information from user manuals into structurally grounded representations for robust plan generation in robotics.

This research distinguishes itself by focusing on zero-shot generalization to novel appliances. Unlike previous methods that require explicit training or handcrafted policies for each appliance, this work proposes a system that "reads" and interprets user manuals to derive operational policies, positioning it as a significant step towards more autonomous and adaptable assistive robots in domestic environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robot system, named ApBot, capable of operating novel household appliances by interpreting their user manuals. The overarching goal is to enable assistive home robots to perform general-purpose appliance operations autonomously, thereby dramatically extending their capabilities in daily environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical observations and challenges:

1. **Complexity of Appliance Operation:** Home appliances are fundamental tools in households, but their operation involves complex, high-level functionalities (e.g., cooking, cleaning, heating) that cannot be replicated through simple direct manipulation skills. Instead, they require understanding specific workflows, modes, and settings.
2. **Generalization to Novel Appliances:** Ideally, robots should be able to operate a wide variety of appliances, including those they have not encountered during training. Relying on pre-programmed behaviors or extensive training data for every new appliance is impractical and limits scalability. User manuals represent a ubiquitous source of knowledge for human users, and enabling robots to leverage this information is a key pathway to generalization.
3. **Unstructured Nature of User Manuals:** User manuals are typically unstructured documents containing a mix of text, symbols, and diagrams. Extracting relevant, actionable information from such documents presents a significant challenge for robotic systems. The information needed to define operational policies, such as specific actions, their effects, and conditional rules, must be inferred and formalized.
4. **Grounding Symbolic Policies to the Physical World:** Even if a robot can extract policies from a manual, these symbolic instructions (e.g., "press power button") must be grounded to actual physical elements on the appliance's control panel. This requires robust visual perception to identify, localize, and characterize interactive components like buttons, dials, and touchpads.
5. **Reliable Multi-Step Execution and Error Management:** Operating appliances often involves multi-step sequences. Compounding errors during execution, whether from perception inaccuracies, manipulation failures, or imperfect model interpretations, can lead to task failure. A robust system needs mechanisms to detect and recover from such errors, enabling reliable long-horizon operation.

To address these challenges, the research aims to answer the question: "How can we enable robots to generate visually grounded policies for novel appliance operation with user manuals?" This involves constructing a structured, symbolic model of an appliance from its manual, visually grounding the symbolic actions to control panel elements, and implementing a closed-loop execution system that updates the model based on visual feedback to enhance robustness.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ApBot system is designed as a generalizable framework for operating novel home appliances from natural language instructions, leveraging user manuals and visual observations in a zero-shot manner. The methodology is structured into four main components: symbolic model construction, action grounding, structured transition modeling with macro actions, and closed-loop task execution.

**Problem Formulation:**
The appliance is formally modeled as a state machine `M = <S, A, T, S_g>`. `S` represents the appliance's state, comprising variables (e.g., power on/off, time, temperature). `A` is the action space, categorized into `A_g` (actions that directly transition to a specific state, like "Menu") and `A_n` (actions that incrementally change a variable's value, like "+" or "-"). `T` is the deterministic transition model `s' = T(s, a)`. `S_g` is the set of goal states specified by a natural language instruction. The objective is to find a sequence of actions `a*` to reach a goal state in `S_g`. Since the true model `M` is inaccessible, ApBot constructs an approximated model `M` from the manual and visual observations.

**1. Construct Structured Appliance Model (Section 3.2):**
ApBot employs large vision-language model (LVLM) agents to construct the symbolic model `M = <S, A, T, S_g>`.
- **State Space (S), Action Space (A), and Transition Function (T) Generation:** LVLMs are prompted with the user manual, a predefined output format, and a list of valid options to sequentially generate `S`, `A`, and `T`. This process utilizes in-context learning by providing examples to improve robustness. Syntax checkers are applied to validate the output, with up to three regeneration attempts in case of errors.
- **Goal Extraction (S_g):** Given a natural language instruction, an LVLM agent infers the goal state by producing a partial assignment over the symbolic variables, fulfilling the task requirements.

**2. Action Grounding (Section 3.3):**
To make symbolic actions executable, they must be visually grounded to physical control panel elements (`ˆa = (a, b, σ)`), where `a` is the symbolic action, `b` is a bounding box, and `σ` is the manipulation primitive (press, hold, turn).
- **Control Element Detection:** Three models are run in parallel to generate candidate bounding boxes for control elements: Segment Anything Model (SAM) for general segmentation, OWL-ViT2 for detecting "button," "dial," and "switch," and an OCR model for text labels. The union of detections forms a candidate set. Overlapping boxes are filtered based on Intersection over Union (IoU) and confidence. Finally, an LVLM is used to verify if each remaining box contains a valid control panel element.
- **Actions Grounding:** LVLMs are queried to assign each symbolic action `a ∈ A` to a detected bounding box `b ∈ B`. If an action has multiple candidate boxes, heuristics are applied, prioritizing boxes with clear physical boundaries or icon-based labels. Actions without a corresponding visual grounding are removed. The manipulation type `σ` is then assigned based on the action description and visual region.

**3. Structured Transition Modeling with Macro Actions (Section 3.4):**
Recognizing the ambiguity in manuals and potential LVLM errors, ApBot models transitions using "macro actions" (`Φ`).
- **Definition:** A macro action `ϕ ∈ Φ` is a parameterized sequence of symbolic actions (`a_ϕ`) that represents a meaningful functionality (e.g., "PowerOn," "SetCookingMenu"). Each macro action includes variables it affects (`s_v`) and a macro transition (`Γ(s_v)`) describing these effects. This approach simplifies planning by reducing the reasoning horizon.
- **Modeling:** LVLMs extract the full list of macro actions, their associated variables, and target effects. The sequence of low-level actions `a_ϕ` for a macro action is computed based on the current state and target transition. For `A_n` actions (e.g., "+"), the number of repetitions is calculated. For `A_g` actions (e.g., "Menu"), descriptions are directly translated. This computation involves invoking code, providing correctness guarantees.

**4. Closed-loop Task Execution (Section 3.5):**
ApBot incorporates a closed-loop mechanism to ensure robustness and recover from model inaccuracies.
- **Automatic Execution from Macro Actions:** An LVLM generates a parameterized symbolic task policy `π = [ϕ_1, ..., ϕ_T]` based on the inferred goal `S_g` and applicable macro actions. Low-level primitive skills (e.g., button pressing, dial turning) then execute the actions within each macro action.
- **State Estimation and Model Updates:** After each macro action's execution, the system receives feedback (text in simulation, image in real-world). LVLMs infer the actual values of corresponding variables. If the observed state deviates from the expected state, ApBot initiates an exploration phase. For `A_n` actions, it repeatedly executes the action until a previously observed state recurs (assuming cyclical behavior), thereby mapping the full value range and step size. This observed trace is then used to update the transition model (`T`), regenerate the affected macro actions (`a_ϕ`), and re-plan the task.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of ApBot involved extensive experiments in both simulated and real-world environments, addressing questions regarding its performance compared to state-of-the-art LVLM agents, the contributions of its key components, and its effectiveness in real-world deployments.

**Comparison with State-of-the-Art LVLM Agents:**
ApBot demonstrated consistent and statistically significant improvements in task success rate compared to direct LVLM-based control policies. In a simulated benchmark of 30 interactive appliance instances across 6 categories (dehumidifier, bottle washer, rice cooker, microwave oven, bread maker, washing machine) and 300 goal-directed natural language instructions, ApBot significantly outperformed baselines that used LVLMs directly as control policies (e.g., "LLM as policy w/ image" and "LLM as policy w/ grounded actions").

- ApBot achieved higher success rates with fewer execution steps and a better Success weighted by Path Length (SPL).
- The system exhibited robustness against increasing appliance complexity (measured by the number of variables to adjust). While baseline methods showed significant performance degradation as complexity increased, ApBot maintained a relatively stable success rate.
- Statistical analysis using χ² tests indicated that the performance differences between ApBot and most baselines were significant, with the exception of "ApBot w/o close-loop update" and "LLM as policy w/ image," suggesting their comparable low performance.

**Main Contributors of ApBot (Ablation Studies):**
Ablation studies were conducted to identify the contributions of ApBot's core components:
- **Structured Appliance Models:** Removing the structured appliance model ("ApBot w/o model") resulted in significant performance degradation. This was primarily attributed to the LVLMs' inability to manage complex reasoning tasks involving numerous variables and constraints over long horizons, leading to skipped steps, premature termination, or hallucinated actions. This suggests the critical role of structured internal representations.
- **Code-based Button Policies:** The "ApBot w/o button policy" ablation, which relied on LVLMs for low-level action sequence generation instead of predefined transition functions, experienced a performance drop. This indicates the importance of invoking code to accurately compute required action sequences, especially for variables with large ranges, complex transitions, or semantically similar value options.
- **Closed-loop Updates:** The "ApBot w/o close-loop update" ablation showed a rapid and sharp performance decrease as appliance complexity increased, demonstrating its inability to recover from model errors. This result underscores the necessity of closed-loop model calibration, as current LVLMs often struggle with generating perfectly constrained structures in a single attempt.

**Benefit of Explicit Action Grounding:**
The research indicated that explicit visual action grounding contributed to an 18% average improvement in overall home appliance operation performance when comparing "LLM as policy w/ image" and "LLM as policy w/ grounded actions." This highlights the limitations of current LVLMs in open-vocabulary detection and fine-grained text recognition on appliance control panels.
- The proposed action grounding method statistically significantly outperformed Molmo, a state-of-the-art visual grounding method, across all appliance types (p < 0.001). This advantage was attributed to combining explicit text recognition, high-recall detection, and semantic understanding of graphical button icons.

**Real-World Deployment:**
ApBot was successfully deployed on a Kinova Gen3 arm to operate three real-world appliances: a blender, an induction cooker, and a water dispenser.
- The robot demonstrated its ability to execute previously unseen, long-horizon operation tasks by referencing user manuals and using visual observations.
- On simpler real-world tasks, ApBot achieved comparable performance to the "LLM as policy w/ image" baseline, while for more complex tasks it consistently outperformed it.

**Failure Analysis:**
Analysis of failed tasks revealed two primary causes:
- **Action grounding errors (16.7%):** Occurred when detectors misidentified soft-touch panels or icon-only buttons lacking clear physical boundaries.
- **Modeling errors (83.3%):** Primarily arose from ambiguities in goal state interpretation or persistent errors in the structured model even after syntax checking.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in this paper offers several significant contributions and has potential impacts across the fields of robotics and artificial intelligence.

**Significance:**
1. **Generalizable Zero-Shot Appliance Operation:** The primary significance lies in demonstrating a generalizable method, ApBot, that enables robots to operate novel household appliances in a zero-shot manner by leveraging user manuals and visual observations. This capability addresses a fundamental challenge in robotic autonomy, moving beyond specialized, pre-trained solutions for individual appliances.
2. **Importance of Structured Internal Representations:** The research empirically highlights the critical role of structured internal representations for robust robot decision-making, particularly in complex, long-horizon tasks. The consistent performance improvements of ApBot over direct LVLM control policies, combined with the degradation observed in ablation studies, underscore that a symbolic, structured model derived from unstructured text can enhance reasoning, control, and verifiability.
3. **Closed-Loop Model Calibration:** The implementation and demonstrated effectiveness of closed-loop model updates based on real-time visual feedback are significant. This mechanism allows the robot to detect and correct errors stemming from initial model inaccuracies or ambiguities in the user manual, contributing to overall system robustness and reliability in dynamic environments.
4. **Novel Evaluation Benchmark:** The creation of a comprehensive evaluation suite, including a simulated benchmark of diverse real-world appliances, corresponding manuals, open-ended task instructions, and symbolic simulators, provides a valuable resource for future research in home appliance operation. This benchmark facilitates systematic assessment of robot capabilities in this domain.
5. **Integration of Foundation Models and Classical AI:** ApBot exemplifies an effective integration of modern foundation models (LVLMs) for high-level reasoning (e.g., model construction, goal inference) with classical AI concepts (e.g., state machines, symbolic planning, closed-loop control). This hybrid approach capitalizes on the strengths of both paradigms.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Advancement of Assistive Robotics:** By enabling robots to operate common household appliances, this work directly contributes to expanding the capabilities of assistive robots in domestic environments. Such robots could perform a wider range of daily tasks, enhancing independence and quality of life for individuals needing assistance.
2. **Reduced Development Overhead:** The ability to derive operational policies directly from user manuals could significantly reduce the development time and effort required to deploy robots in new environments or with new appliances. Instead of extensive programming or data collection for each appliance, robots could adapt by "reading."
3. **Foundation for Embodied AI:** This research contributes to the broader goal of embodied AI by advancing robot interaction with complex physical interfaces. The challenges addressed, such as parsing unstructured information, visual grounding, and error recovery, are central to enabling intelligent agents to operate effectively in the physical world.
4. **Future Robotics Research Directions:** The identified limitations, such as handling touchscreens, incorporating subtle tactile feedback for diverse button types, improving action grounding for ambiguous visual elements, and executing complex manipulation skills (e.g., opening doors), suggest clear avenues for future research and development, potentially leading to more sophisticated and capable robotic systems.
