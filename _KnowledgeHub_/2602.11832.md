# JEPA-VLA: Video Predictive Embedding is Needed for VLA Models

> [!summary] Summary
> JEPA-VLA integrates video-based predictive embeddings, specifically V-JEPA 2, into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models to enhance their sample efficiency and generalization in robotic manipulation. This framework yields improved success rates by an average of +7.4% on LIBERO benchmarks and demonstrates superior robustness to environmental variations in real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models exhibit low sample efficiency, requiring millions of trajectories for training.
- These models show limited generalization capabilities, performing poorly on unseen tasks or under distribution shifts (e.g., changes in lighting or background).
- The limitations stem from inadequate visual representations, typically derived from static image-based self-supervised learning (DINOv2) or language-image contrastive learning (CLIP/SigLIP).

## Method

- Propose **JEPA-VLA**, a framework that integrates ==V-JEPA 2==, a video-based predictive embedding, into existing VLA models.
- ==V-JEPA 2== representations are leveraged for their ability to capture precise task-relevant object attributes and encode temporal dynamics as effective policy priors.
- Two fusion strategies are introduced: ==Early Fusion== for VLAs trained from scratch and ==Gated Fusion== (via ==cross-attention==) for extensively pretrained VLAs, allowing adaptive and stable integration.

## Results

- JEPA-VLA improved average success rates by **+7.4%** on LIBERO benchmarks and **+6.7%** on LIBERO-plus, demonstrating enhanced generalization across various visual perturbations.
- On real-world pick-and-place tasks, JEPA-VLA with one-fifth of the training data surpassed a baseline trained on full data, achieving **100%** success on layout and lighting variations with full data.
- V-JEPA 2 representations consistently outperformed DINOv2 and SigLIP in direct comparisons within VLAs (**+15.2%** on LIBERO-Long) and strong baselines like VC-1 on broader embodied AI benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Video-based predictive embeddings (V-JEPA 2) offer superior environment understanding by accurately capturing task-relevant states while being robust to task-irrelevant visual nuisances.
> - V-JEPA 2 effectively encodes 'policy priors' by learning temporal dynamics and anticipating future state changes associated with successful task execution, a capability largely absent in static image-based representations.
> - Integrating these dynamic, predictive visual features into VLAs through simple fusion mechanisms consistently boosts their performance, sample efficiency, and generalization across diverse manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, robotic manipulation, and self-supervised learning. Recent years have seen significant advancements in VLA models, which integrate visual perception, natural language understanding, and action generation to enable general-purpose agents to execute human instructions. These models often build upon large-scale pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) by adding action generation modules.

However, the paper identifies two critical limitations of current VLAs: low sample efficiency and limited generalization. Existing VLAs typically require millions of trajectories for training yet struggle to generalize to unseen tasks or distribution shifts. The authors argue that these limitations stem from deficiencies in the visual representations commonly employed by VLAs. These representations are usually derived from either image-based self-supervised learning (e.g., DINO, DINOv2) or language-image contrastive learning (e.g., CLIP, SigLIP). The paper posits that these static, image-centric pretraining paradigms fail to adequately capture two crucial forms of visual knowledge required for robust robotic control:
1. **Environment understanding:** The ability to precisely capture task-relevant object attributes while discarding task-irrelevant information.
2. **Policy priors:** Anticipatory knowledge of how the environment evolves under successful task execution, guiding action learning towards favorable future states.

The paper directly addresses this gap by advocating for **video-based predictive embeddings**, specifically V-JEPA 2 (Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture), as a superior alternative. V-JEPA 2, pretrained on internet-scale videos, learns to predict masked video patches in a latent space, thereby encouraging representations that focus on predictable, task-relevant factors and temporal dynamics. This approach aligns with the broader trend in AI research of leveraging large-scale, self-supervised pretraining, but specifically emphasizes the unique advantages of *video-based* pretraining for embodied AI tasks, where understanding dynamics and predicting future states are paramount.

By integrating V-JEPA 2 into existing VLA architectures, the work aims to enhance visual understanding and policy learning in a "plug-and-play" manner, offering a more general and efficient solution compared to complex, explicit perception modules or 3D modeling approaches explored in related work. This positions JEPA-VLA as a significant contribution towards building more robust, generalizable, and sample-efficient robotic agents by improving their fundamental visual comprehension.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the observed shortcomings of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models in robotic manipulation. Despite integrating powerful Vision-Language Models (VLMs), VLAs frequently suffer from:
- **Low sample efficiency:** Requiring vast amounts of demonstration data (millions of trajectories) to learn even a modest number of tasks.
- **Limited generalization:** Exhibiting substantial performance degradation when confronted with unseen tasks or distribution shifts (e.g., changes in lighting, background, or object layouts).

The authors meticulously motivate these issues by arguing that the underlying visual representations used in most VLAs are inadequate. Specifically, they highlight that visual features learned from static images, whether through self-supervised methods like DINO or contrastive methods like CLIP/SigLIP, lack the critical knowledge needed for robotic control:
1. **Insufficient environment understanding:** Image-based representations may be too general, failing to discern precisely task-relevant object states from task-irrelevant visual nuisances (e.g., lighting, background textures). Contrastive learning might focus only on text-referred entities, overlooking other crucial physical details.
2. **Weak policy priors:** Static image pretraining inherently fails to capture the temporal dynamics and evolution of environments under successful actions, which are vital for anticipating future states and guiding policy learning.

Given these motivations, the key objectives of the paper are:
- **To empirically demonstrate the deficiencies of static, image-based visual representations** (DINOv2, SigLIP) in providing robust environment understanding and policy priors for robotic tasks.
- **To show that video-based predictive embeddings, specifically V-JEPA 2, effectively overcome these limitations** by capturing crucial task-relevant information and encoding temporal regularities that serve as effective policy priors.
- **To propose JEPA-VLA, a simple and general framework, to integrate V-JEPA 2 representations into existing VLA models**, thereby improving their sample efficiency and generalization capabilities.
- **To validate the effectiveness of JEPA-VLA** across a diverse set of robotic manipulation benchmarks and real-world tasks, demonstrating consistent and substantial performance gains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research follows a two-pronged approach: first, an in-depth analytical comparison of visual representations, and second, the development and evaluation of the JEPA-VLA framework.

**Analytical Comparison of Visual Representations (Section 2):**
The authors hypothesize that V-JEPA 2, due to its video-based predictive objective, is better suited for robotics. To test this, they design probing experiments using the LIBERO-10 and LIBERO-plus benchmarks, comparing V-JEPA 2 against DINOv2 (image-based self-supervised) and SigLIP (language-image contrastive). The experiments assess:

1. **Environment Understanding:**
 - **Task-Relevant State Estimation:** A lightweight ViT head is trained to regress the states of the robot arm and task-relevant objects from frozen visual representations extracted from the two most recent frames. Mean Squared Error (MSE) is used as the metric. The goal is to see which representation more precisely encodes crucial operational information.
 - **Task-Irrelevant State Estimation:** Models are trained to regress task-irrelevant factors like lighting and background variations (from LIBERO-plus perturbations) from the first frame's visual representations. Higher error in this task indicates desirable insensitivity to nuisances.
2. **Policy Priors:**
 - **Future State Change Prediction:** A lightweight head is trained to predict the residual between the current task-relevant state and the state 10 steps later, again using frozen visual representations. This evaluates the representation's ability to encode transition-aware information that reflects successful action execution.

**JEPA-VLA Framework (Section 3):**
Building on the analytical findings, JEPA-VLA is proposed to integrate V-JEPA 2 representations into existing VLA models. The core idea is to condition the VLA's action model on these predictive visual features. Two fusion strategies are introduced based on the VLA's pretraining status:

1. **Early Fusion:** For VLAs trained from scratch or without extensive robot-specific pretraining, V-JEPA 2 representations are treated as additional input embeddings and concatenated with the VLA's original token sequence. A linear projection aligns dimensions. This simple, lightweight approach injects video-predictive knowledge directly.
2. **Gated Fusion:** For VLAs pretrained on large-scale robotic manipulation datasets (where direct concatenation could disrupt learned priors), a more adaptive approach is used. Inspired by Flamingo, V-JEPA 2 representations are integrated via gated cross-attention layers. The original VLA tokens serve as queries, and V-JEPA 2 representations serve as keys and values. This gating mechanism allows the VLA to selectively attend to predictive embeddings, preserving pretrained knowledge while adaptively leveraging complementary information. Gated cross-attention layers are sparsely inserted into the decoder stack, and their learning rate is set significantly lower than that of the VLA's original parameters to ensure stability.

**Experimental Evaluation (Section 4):**
JEPA-VLA is evaluated across a comprehensive suite of benchmarks:
- **LIBERO:** For lifelong learning and knowledge transfer (Spatial, Object, Goal, Long suites).
- **LIBERO-plus:** To test robustness under various controlled perturbations (camera, robot, language, light, background, noise, layout).
- **RoboTwin2.0:** For dual-arm manipulation, assessing robustness under extensive domain randomization.
- **Real-World Task:** A pick-and-place task on a single Piper robot arm, including limited-data settings and generalization tests under layout and lighting variations.
- **CortexBench:** A broader embodied AI benchmark, comparing V-JEPA 2 against other strong pretrained visual representations like VC-1 on MetaWorld and DMControl tasks.

Experiments are conducted with two base VLA architectures: a "Basic VLA" (using Chameleon as VLM backbone with a linear action head) and a "Mainstream VLA" (an OpenVLA-OFT configuration). This allows for evaluating JEPA-VLA's effectiveness in both simpler experimental settings and more complex, state-of-the-art VLA architectures.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents compelling evidence for the efficacy of V-JEPA 2 representations and the JEPA-VLA framework.

**Findings from Visual Representation Analysis (Section 2):**
- **Finding 1 (Task-Relevant State Estimation):** V-JEPA 2 consistently achieves a lower Mean Squared Error (MSE) when regressing current task-relevant states (robot arm and object positions) on LIBERO-10, outperforming DINOv2 and SigLIP. This indicates V-JEPA 2's superior ability to capture precise, task-focused environment information.
- **Finding 2 (Task-Irrelevant State Estimation):** V-JEPA 2 exhibits higher error in regressing task-irrelevant factors like lighting and background perturbations on LIBERO-plus. This demonstrates that V-JEPA 2 selectively encodes less information about nuisances, making its representations more robust and focused on manipulation-relevant dynamics.
- **Finding 3 (Policy Priors):** V-JEPA 2 achieves lower MSE when predicting future state changes (residuals over 10 steps) on LIBERO-10. This confirms that V-JEPA 2 effectively embeds "policy priors" by capturing transition-aware information and temporal regularities aligned with successful task execution, a capability largely absent in static image-based representations.

**Results from JEPA-VLA Evaluation (Section 4):**
- **Basic VLA Setting:**
 - **LIBERO:** JEPA-VLA improves the average success rate by +7.4% over the baseline, with notable gains of +11.0% in LIBERO-Spatial and +15.2% in LIBERO-Long tasks.
 - **LIBERO-plus:** JEPA-VLA achieves a +6.7% average increase in success rate, even outperforming the official WorldVLA model despite being trained with only one-tenth of the action-model data and no world-model training data, highlighting its generalization capabilities. Significant improvements are observed across various perturbation types, especially for lighting (+11.7%) and background (+13.8%).
 - **Real-World Task:** JEPA-VLA significantly outperforms the baseline. Crucially, even with only one-fifth of the training data, JEPA-VLA surpasses the baseline trained on full data. With full data, it achieves 80.0% average success rate, 100% on layout variations, and 100% on lighting variations, demonstrating robust generalization in real-world deployment under data scarcity and distribution shifts.

- **Mainstream VLA Setting (OpenVLA-OFT):**
 - **LIBERO:** JEPA-VLA improves over the OpenVLA-OFT baseline by +6.1% and even surpasses the officially reported OpenVLA-OFT results, confirming its effectiveness with state-of-the-art architectures.
 - **RoboTwin2.0:** JEPA-VLA yields substantial average gains of +18.7% in clean settings and +8.4% in domain-randomized settings for dual-arm manipulation tasks, further attesting to its robustness and generalization.

- **Comparative Evaluation of Visual Representations (Section 4.4):**
 - **VLAs:** When directly comparing visual representations within the basic VLA, V-JEPA 2 provides substantially larger performance gains (+15.2% on LIBERO-Long) compared to DINOv2 (marginal/negative gain) and SigLIP (modest gain of +7.8%).
 - **Broader Embodied AI (CortexBench):** V-JEPA 2 (VIT-L) consistently outperforms VC-1 (VIT-L), a high-performing pretrained visual representation, across most MetaWorld tasks (e.g., +8.0% in MetaWorld Assembly) and DMControl tasks (e.g., +98 average reward in Finger Spin), indicating superior transferability to a wider range of embodied decision-making scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The JEPA-VLA research makes a significant contribution to the fields of robotic manipulation and embodied AI, with several potential impacts:

1. **Validation of Predictive Visual Embeddings:** The paper provides strong empirical evidence that video-based predictive embeddings, exemplified by V-JEPA 2, are superior to static image-based representations for robotic control. It rigorously demonstrates that these embeddings offer more precise task-relevant environment understanding and effective policy priors, which are critical for robust manipulation. This fundamental insight challenges the current reliance on image-based pretraining for VLA models.

2. **Enhanced VLA Performance and Generalization:** JEPA-VLA consistently and substantially boosts the performance, sample efficiency, and generalization capabilities of VLA models across a diverse array of challenging simulation and real-world benchmarks. This includes improvements in sample-limited scenarios, robustness to visual perturbations, and transfer to new tasks and environments. Such advancements are crucial for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic real-world settings.

3. **Simplicity and Generality of Integration:** The proposed JEPA-VLA framework offers a simple, plug-and-play mechanism for integrating predictive embeddings into existing VLA architectures. This ease of integration, coupled with its demonstrated effectiveness across different VLA backbones (basic and mainstream), makes it highly accessible and adoptable for researchers and practitioners. It suggests that significant improvements can be achieved with minimal architectural changes, primarily by upgrading the underlying visual representation.

4. **Implications for Embodied AI Pretraining:** The work reinforces the growing understanding that pretraining paradigms tailored to the temporal and dynamic nature of embodied interaction, rather than static image analysis, are essential for advancing robotics. It highlights the value of self-supervised video learning (like JEPA) in developing models that can reason about physics, motion, and causality—capabilities inherently difficult to acquire from static data. This could spur further research into developing more advanced video-centric pretraining methods specifically for embodied agents.

5. **Path Towards More Capable Generalist Robots:** By addressing the limitations of sample efficiency and generalization, JEPA-VLA contributes to the development of more capable and adaptable generalist robots. Robots equipped with better visual understanding and anticipatory knowledge will require less human intervention and data, making them more practical and scalable for a wider range of tasks in diverse environments.

In conclusion, JEPA-VLA successfully identifies a critical bottleneck in current VLA models and proposes an elegant and effective solution by leveraging video-predictive embeddings. The findings underscore the importance of dynamic, predictive representations for robust robotic control and pave the way for future research into more sophisticated video-centric approaches for embodied intelligence.
