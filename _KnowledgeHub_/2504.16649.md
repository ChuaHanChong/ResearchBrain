# PP-Tac: Paper Picking Using Tactile Feedback in Dexterous Robotic Hands

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at BIGAI, ShanghaiTech University, and Beihang University developed PP-Tac, a system for picking up thin deformable objects like paper using a dexterous robotic hand equipped with novel vision-based tactile sensors. The system achieved an 87.5% success rate across various materials and terrains by employing a biomimetic strategy of inducing material buckling with tactile feedback.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of thin, deformable objects like paper and fabric is challenging due to the lack of salient visual features, unpredictable deformations, and visual occlusion during interaction.
- Traditional vision-only systems struggle with accurate perception of contact and state estimation for deformable materials, limiting their generalizability and robustness in real-world scenarios.
- Existing tactile sensors often face limitations in spatial resolution, ease of fabrication, calibration complexity, and data bandwidth, hindering their scalable integration into dexterous multi-fingered robotic hands.

## Method

- PP-Tac integrates a novel, easily fabricated, and calibrated monochrome vision-based tactile sensor (R-Tac) into the fingertips of a fully actuated Allegro dexterous robotic hand.
- A data-driven, diffusion-based control policy (PP-Tac policy) is developed to mimic human-inspired paper picking by executing multi-fingered sliding and pinching motions to induce material buckling and create graspable regions.
- Expert grasping motions are efficiently synthesized using trajectory optimization with rigid-body dynamics and then used to train the policy, which incorporates slip-based force control and robust domain randomization for sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The R-Tac sensor demonstrated high-resolution depth reconstruction with a mean absolute error of 0.35 mm and a median loss of 0.28 mm, with processing taking less than 10 ms.
- PP-Tac achieved an overall success rate of 87.5% in grasping various paper-like objects (paper, plastic bag, cloth, kraft paper bag) across diverse terrains (flat, sloped, uneven).
- Ablation studies confirmed that real-time tactile feedback and the proposed data disturbance (domain randomization) during training were critical, as removing them led to significant performance drops and failure on challenging objects/terrains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Inducing material buckling through multi-fingered inward sliding and pinching, inspired by human manipulation, is an effective strategy for grasping thin deformable objects from flat surfaces.
> - Vision-based tactile sensors, specifically a novel monochrome R-Tac design, provide crucial real-time contact force and slip information essential for robustly handling deformable objects and adapting to varied terrains.
> - Efficient sim-to-real transfer for complex manipulation policies can be achieved through trajectory optimization for expert data generation combined with extensive domain randomization, avoiding computationally intensive soft-body physics simulations.
