# PP-Tac: Paper Picking Using Tactile Feedback in Dexterous Robotic Hands

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at BIGAI, ShanghaiTech University, and Beihang University developed PP-Tac, a system for picking up thin deformable objects like paper using a dexterous robotic hand equipped with novel vision-based tactile sensors. The system achieved an 87.5% success rate across various materials and terrains by employing a biomimetic strategy of inducing material buckling with tactile feedback.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of thin, deformable objects like paper and fabric is challenging due to the lack of salient visual features, unpredictable deformations, and visual occlusion during interaction.
- Traditional vision-only systems struggle with accurate perception of contact and state estimation for deformable materials, limiting their generalizability and robustness in real-world scenarios.
- Existing tactile sensors often face limitations in spatial resolution, ease of fabrication, calibration complexity, and data bandwidth, hindering their scalable integration into dexterous multi-fingered robotic hands.

## Method

- PP-Tac integrates a novel, easily fabricated, and calibrated monochrome vision-based tactile sensor (R-Tac) into the fingertips of a fully actuated Allegro dexterous robotic hand.
- A data-driven, diffusion-based control policy (PP-Tac policy) is developed to mimic human-inspired paper picking by executing multi-fingered sliding and pinching motions to induce material buckling and create graspable regions.
- Expert grasping motions are efficiently synthesized using trajectory optimization with rigid-body dynamics and then used to train the policy, which incorporates slip-based force control and robust domain randomization for sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The R-Tac sensor demonstrated high-resolution depth reconstruction with a mean absolute error of 0.35 mm and a median loss of 0.28 mm, with processing taking less than 10 ms.
- PP-Tac achieved an overall success rate of 87.5% in grasping various paper-like objects (paper, plastic bag, cloth, kraft paper bag) across diverse terrains (flat, sloped, uneven).
- Ablation studies confirmed that real-time tactile feedback and the proposed data disturbance (domain randomization) during training were critical, as removing them led to significant performance drops and failure on challenging objects/terrains.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Inducing material buckling through multi-fingered inward sliding and pinching, inspired by human manipulation, is an effective strategy for grasping thin deformable objects from flat surfaces.
> - Vision-based tactile sensors, specifically a novel monochrome R-Tac design, provide crucial real-time contact force and slip information essential for robustly handling deformable objects and adapting to varied terrains.
> - Efficient sim-to-real transfer for complex manipulation policies can be achieved through trajectory optimization for expert data generation combined with extensive domain randomization, avoiding computationally intensive soft-body physics simulations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The manipulation of deformable objects remains one of the most significant and long-standing challenges in robotics, primarily due to the inherent complexities of perceiving and modeling their continuously changing shapes and unknown physical properties. Traditional vision-based systems, while powerful for rigid object manipulation, often falter when faced with thin, flat, and pliable materials like paper or fabric. These objects frequently lack salient features for stable grasp planning, suffer from visual occlusions during interaction, and undergo unpredictable deformations that undermine static environment models.

Within this challenging domain, **PP-Tac** positions itself at the intersection of dexterous manipulation and advanced tactile sensing. Prior work in deformable object manipulation (DOM) has explored visual perception for state estimation in tasks like rope handling or cloth folding. However, as the authors note, these vision-only approaches struggle with real-world variability in object appearance, unknown material properties, and dynamic occlusions, limiting their scalability and generalizability.

The emergence of **Vision-Based Tactile Sensors (VBTS)** has shown immense promise in overcoming these limitations by providing high-resolution contact feedback. VBTS have been successfully applied in object shape reconstruction, localization, and slip detection. Yet, existing implementations are often integrated into simple grippers, which lack the multi-fingered dexterity essential for intricate tasks like manipulating thin, deformable sheets. The paper explicitly states that "gripper-based approaches struggle with thin deformable objects, and lack sufficient adaptability for objects placed on non-flat surfaces," highlighting a critical gap that PP-Tac aims to fill.

Furthermore, while dexterous robotic hands are increasingly equipped with tactile sensors, many rely on capacitive, piezoresistive, or magnetic technologies that have limitations in spatial resolution and environmental robustness. Recent advancements in VBTS have produced curved elastomer surfaces, but their widespread deployment on multi-fingered hands is hampered by challenges in fabrication, complex calibration processes (especially for RGB-based sensors requiring extensive data and specialized test beds), and high bandwidth requirements for data transmission. PP-Tac directly addresses these practical barriers by introducing **R-Tac**, a novel monochrome VBTS specifically designed for ease of fabrication, simplified calibration, and efficient data handling, making it scalable for multi-fingered integration.

Finally, existing research utilizing VBTS-equipped dexterous hands has primarily focused on tasks such as grasping and in-hand manipulation of small, rigid objects like screws or for re-orienting objects. Crucially, the authors state, "To the best of our knowledge, existing research has not yet explored VBTS-equipped dexterous hands for manipulating thin, deformable objects such as paper sheets." This statement firmly establishes PP-Tac's novel contribution to the field: it represents the *first attempt* to tackle the challenging problem of picking up paper-like objects using a tactile-equipped dexterous hand, leveraging human-inspired multi-fingered actions and real-time tactile feedback to overcome long-standing limitations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enable robotic systems to robustly and effectively pick up thin, flat, and deformable objects, such as various types of paper, plastic bags, and fabric, which are commonly encountered in everyday household and industrial environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several critical challenges that current robotic systems face in manipulating such objects:

- **Perceptual Limitations:** Traditional vision systems, while highly effective for rigid objects, struggle significantly with deformable materials. They fail to accurately perceive contact information due to factors like visual occlusion during interaction, the lack of salient features on flat surfaces for robust state estimation, and the continuous, unpredictable deformation of objects, which drastically impairs the generalizability of vision-based methods.
- **Grasp Planning Challenges:** Thin, flat objects offer few discernible features or stable contact points, making it difficult to synthesize appropriate and stable grasp motions. This leads to high failure rates in manipulation tasks.
- **Dexterity and Adaptability Deficiencies:** While some gripper-based solutions exist for deformable objects, they often lack the multi-degree-of-freedom (DoF) dexterity and adaptive control necessary to handle diverse materials and varied supporting surfaces (e.g., uneven terrains). Humans, in contrast, effortlessly manipulate these objects by coordinating multi-fingered motions and employing real-time tactile feedback.
- **Sensor Practicality and Scalability:** Existing high-resolution tactile sensors, particularly vision-based ones, often present significant hurdles in terms of complex fabrication, cumbersome calibration procedures (especially for multi-fingered hands), and high data transmission requirements, limiting their widespread adoption and scalability in real robotic systems.

To address these motivations, the PP-Tac system sets out to achieve the following key objectives:

1. **Develop a Novel Tactile Sensor (R-Tac):** Design and fabricate a novel, round-shaped, high-resolution Vision-Based Tactile Sensor (R-Tac) that is easy to manufacture, calibrate (using a simplified monochrome sensing principle), and can be scalably deployed on multi-fingered robotic hands, while providing efficient data transmission for real-time operation.
2. **Integrate Tactile Sensing with Dexterous Manipulation:** Seamlessly integrate the R-Tac sensors into the fingertips of a fully actuated dexterous robotic hand (Allegro Hand) to provide omnidirectional, real-time contact feedback for precise force control and slip detection during manipulation.
3. **Develop a Biomimetic Grasp Motion Policy:** Create a data-driven, diffusion-based control policy (PP-Tac policy) that mimics human strategies for picking up flat objects, specifically leveraging sliding and pinching motions to induce buckling and create pinchable regions.
4. **Enable Robust Motion Generation:** Propose an efficient trajectory optimization framework to synthesize a large dataset of expert grasping motions, avoiding computationally expensive tactile or physical simulations, thereby enabling robust sim-to-real transfer.
5. **Achieve Generalization and Adaptability:** Train the PP-Tac policy using a diffusion model and critical domain randomization techniques, allowing it to generalize robustly across diverse materials (varying stiffness and texture) and challenging, unstructured terrain properties (flat, sloped, uneven surfaces).
6. **Demonstrate Real-World Performance:** Validate the entire PP-Tac system through comprehensive real-world experiments, demonstrating high success rates in grasping various paper-like objects under different conditions and showcasing the critical contributions of both the hardware and the learning-based control policy.

By pursuing these objectives, the authors aim to bridge the existing gaps in robotic manipulation of deformable objects, moving closer to robots capable of performing complex everyday tasks alongside humans.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PP-Tac system integrates novel hardware with a sophisticated learning-based control policy to achieve robust manipulation of thin, deformable objects.

#### 3.1. Hardware Design: The R-Tac Sensor and Robotic Hand System

The core hardware innovation is the **R-Tac (Round-shaped Tactile) sensor**, designed with five guiding principles: round shape for omnidirectional sensing, high resolution, ease of fabrication and low cost, efficient calibration, and efficient data transmission.

- **Contact and Illumination Module:** The sensor's sensitive surface is a hemispherical elastomer (PDMS and semitransparent silicone with an opaque protective layer) uniformly illuminated by a white LED ring. This monochrome sensing principle, where intensity changes indicate deformation, simplifies optical requirements and calibration compared to RGB-based sensors.
- **Camera Module:** A micro black-and-white CMOS camera (OV9281, 640x480, 120Hz) captures light intensity data. The monochrome output produces lightweight data, facilitating high-speed transmission.
- **Calibration:** A significant advantage of R-Tac is its simplified calibration. It computes 3D geometry from single-channel pixel intensity in two steps:
 1. **Extrinsic Parameters (A, b) and Reference Projection (D):** Achieved using an indentation-based setup with 3D-printed indicators and pins, allowing for precise mapping between known 3D points in the sensor frame and their 2D pixel coordinates. This avoids the need for complex CNC machines.
 2. **Depth Mapping Function (M):** Calibrated by pressing a ball of known size onto the sensor, relating grayscale intensity changes to deformation depth.
- **Contact Force Estimation & Slip Detection:**
 - **Force Estimation:** Contact force is implicitly estimated as proportional to the deformation depth, leveraging the elastomer's elasticity.
 - **Slip Detection:** A lightweight neural network (CNN and MLP) is trained to detect slip. It processes a temporal sequence of five grayscale tactile images (including a non-contact reference) to identify distinct "wrinkle" features that appear during slip events. The network outputs a slip probability, with a threshold of 0.75 empirically determined for optimal sensitivity and accuracy (86% accuracy).

The R-Tac sensors are custom-integrated into a **fully actuated dexterous robotic hand**, specifically an **Allegro Hand**. This hand features 16 controllable DoFs across its index, middle, ring fingers, and thumb, driven by Dynamixel motors. The entire hand assembly is mounted on a **Franka Research 3**, a 7-DoF robotic arm, allowing for coordinated arm-hand motions.

#### 3.2. Paper-Picking Problem Statement

The challenge is defined as grasping thin, deformable paper-like objects from various surfaces, including extremely flat scenarios where discernible edges are absent. The approach is **biomimetic**, inspired by human strategies. The key idea is to apply an inward force with multiple fingers to induce *buckling* of the material against the supporting surface. This dynamic deformation creates a "pinchable region" that can then be grasped. This process relies on ensuring that the static friction between the robotic fingers and the object exceeds both the object's critical buckling resistance and the dynamic friction between the object and the terrain. The methodology assumes terrain height variations of less than 3 cm.

#### 3.3. Policy Learning for Paper-Picking: The PP-Tac Policy

The PP-Tac policy is a vision-independent, tactile-based approach developed in two stages:

- **4.3.1. Grasp Motion Dataset Synthesis:**
 - **Motivation:** To avoid the high computational cost and real-to-sim transfer issues of soft-body simulations or complex teleoperation, the authors synthesize expert grasping motions using trajectory optimization with rigid-body dynamics.
 - **Process:**
 1. **Initial Contact:** The process begins by ensuring at least three fingertips establish contact with the object's surface (detailed in Appendix B). This involves sequential hand lowering and fixed-point/fixed-axis rotations around initial contact points until sufficient contacts are established.
 2. **Trajectory Generation:** Once contact is made, fingers gradually close to pinch. Individual finger trajectories are generated by taking (x,y) coordinates from pre-recorded teleoperated human grasping motions (capturing natural sliding-to-pinch actions) and projecting them onto randomized terrain profiles to obtain z-coordinates.
 3. **Force Modulation:** To account for varying material properties, sliding trajectories are adjusted based on target contact forces, which are implicitly controlled by the fingertip's *deformation depth* (d_tac). By controlling the distance between the finger joint and the terrain, different deformation levels are achieved.
 4. **Optimization Problem:** An optimization problem (Eq. 2-5) solves for the hand joint angles and arm poses for each frame of the desired trajectory. This minimizes the error between fingertip positions and targets, regularizes motion, and minimizes wrist movement. The SGD optimizer is used.
 - **Output:** A dataset of 500,000 grasp samples, each a sequence of 100 frames, is generated.

- **4.3.2. Diffusion Policy Training:**
 - **Framework:** A Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Model (DDPM) is employed to jointly control the hand and arm, enabling adaptation to varying terrain shapes and contact forces.
 - **State Representation:** The policy conditions on historical states (N_prefix steps) to predict future actions (N_pred steps). The state variables (D=152) comprehensively describe the robot's proprioception (joint positions/velocities, wrist pose/velocities) and tactile feedback (four fingertip deformation depths).
 - **Architecture & Training:** An encoder-only Transformer predicts the future state sequence (ˆx_pred_0). The loss function combines an MSE on the predicted state sequence with a consistency loss to enforce kinematic validity. The model is trained for 600,000 iterations.
 - **Inference:** For real-time performance, inference uses an accelerated denoising process (10 steps), achieving motion generation in 11ms on an RTX4090 GPU. The predicted joint angles control the hand, and wrist pose controls the arm.
 - **Slip-Based Force Control:** During grasping, if slip is detected by the R-Tac sensors, the desired deformation depth (d_tac) is incrementally increased, thereby increasing the contact force to mitigate slip and maximize material deformation.
 - **Domain Randomization (Sim-to-Real Transfer):** A crucial aspect for bridging the sim-to-real gap is the introduction of four types of disturbances to the historical states (x_prefix) during training. This includes adding Gaussian noise to joint angles, amplifying Gaussian noise to simulate uneven terrains, and randomly making frames static to simulate fingers getting stuck. This technique significantly enhances the policy's generalizability and robustness in the real world.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted extensively evaluate the PP-Tac system's performance, tactile sensor capabilities, and the effectiveness of its control policy.

#### 4.1. Depth Reconstruction of VBTS (R-Tac)

- **Qualitative Results:** The R-Tac sensor successfully reconstructs fine surface details from tactile images, as demonstrated by clear depth and normal maps for indenters with text ("RSS", "⋆", "2025"). This indicates the sensor's high resolution and fidelity in perceiving contact geometry.
- **Quantitative Results:** Through indentation tests with 3D-printed hemispherical indicators, the sensor achieved a mean absolute error (L1 error) of **0.35 mm** and a median loss of **0.28 mm** in depth reconstruction. Notably, 60% of reconstruction losses were below 0.3 mm.
- **Computational Speed:** The depth mapping process takes less than **10 ms**, ensuring real-time performance crucial for robotic applications.

#### 4.2. Evaluation of PP-Tac Policy on Materials and Terrains

The system was rigorously tested on four common deformable objects (paper, plastic bag, cloth, kraft paper bag) across four types of terrains (flat plane, 10-degree slope, flat plane with a 2 cm book, and randomly generated complex terrain), with 20 grasping attempts for each combination.

- **Overall Success Rate:** PP-Tac achieved an impressive overall success rate of **87.5%** in grasping these diverse paper-like objects on various surfaces.
- **Material Influence:**
 - **High Success:** Cloth and plastic bags (low stiffness) were generally easier to grasp due to their greater deformability, which allows them to buckle more readily.
 - **Lower Success:** Paper and kraft paper bags (stiffer) posed a greater challenge, exhibiting lower success rates because they resist buckling and forming a stable pinch region.
- **Terrain Influence:**
 - **Flat/Sloped Terrains:** High success rates were observed for plastic bags, cloth, and paper, as consistent frictional forces aid in grasp formation. However, stiffer kraft paper bags still presented difficulties due to a lack of initial buckling points.
 - **Uneven Terrain (Book):** Surprisingly high success rates were maintained across all objects when a book was placed underneath. This is attributed to the book's edge and the partial void space it created, which facilitated easier material buckling and separation from the surface.
 - **Highly Irregular Terrain:** Success rates dropped for all objects on highly irregular terrains. This is likely due to increased challenges for the force controllers, leading to a higher likelihood of finger slip.
- **Grasping Process:** Qualitative observations confirmed that the system successfully executed the intended strategy: initial fingertip contact, gradual finger closure inducing material buckling, creation of pinchable regions, and successful lifting.

#### 4.3. Comparison with Other System Configurations

PP-Tac was compared against three baselines to highlight its advantages:

- **Bi-finger Gripper (Teleoperation with Vision):** This baseline, mimicking vision-based methods, achieved some success with soft materials (cloth, plastic bags) due to human intervention and visual feedback. However, it **completely failed** on stiffer materials (paper, kraft paper), demonstrating the superiority of PP-Tac's multi-fingered dexterity and tactile sensing for challenging flat objects.
- **Open-loop Control (Without Tactile Feedback):** This baseline, which replayed pre-generated trajectories without real-time adaptation, showed significantly lower success rates. It was entirely **unfeasible** for uncertain terrains (book, complex terrain) where terrain shape is unknown. This underscores the critical role of real-time tactile feedback and adaptive control for robust performance in varied environments.
- **"Model-based Force Tracking":** While satisfactory on structured terrains, its effectiveness was limited on irregular or complex terrains. This emphasizes the need for the PP-Tac policy's enhanced adaptability in unstructured environments.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Influence of Material Stiffness (Table I):** Experiments with stacked paper layers (1, 3, 5, 7 layers) showed a clear correlation: increasing paper layers (and thus stiffness) led to a **drastic decrease in success rate** (from 90% for 1 layer to 5% for 7 layers) and a **significant increase in detected slip events**. This confirms that material stiffness is a major factor influencing grasp success and slip propensity.
- **Influence of Data Disturbance (Domain Randomization):** The "Non-disturbance" baseline, where domain randomization was omitted during policy training, showed a **notable performance drop** across all experiments, often resulting in complete failure for stiff objects like kraft paper bags. This emphatically validates the critical importance of the proposed data disturbance technique for enhancing generalization and robustness in real-world deployment, despite its drawback of increasing training time (requiring 400,000 additional iterations).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PP-Tac system represents a significant leap forward in robotic manipulation, particularly for challenging deformable objects. Its contributions and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Novelty and First-of-its-Kind Demonstration:** PP-Tac is, to the authors' knowledge, the first successful demonstration of picking up thin, deformable, paper-like objects using a dexterous robotic hand equipped with vision-based tactile sensors. This opens up a previously underexplored frontier in tactile-driven manipulation.
- **Accessible Hardware Innovation:** The introduction of R-Tac, a novel round-shaped VBTS, is a key enabler. Its design prioritizes ease of fabrication, simplified monochrome-based calibration, and efficient data transmission, making it significantly more practical and scalable for deployment on multi-fingered robot hands compared to existing complex VBTS. This addresses a major bottleneck in bringing advanced tactile sensing to real-world robotic applications.
- **Robust and Adaptive Control Policy:** The PP-Tac policy, leveraging a diffusion model trained on efficiently synthesized data and critical domain randomization, is a robust and adaptive control strategy. It effectively translates human-inspired grasping strategies (sliding and pinching to induce buckling) into robotic actions, demonstrating remarkable generalization across diverse materials (varying stiffness) and complex, unstructured terrains. This capability is paramount for real-world scenarios that often involve unknown and variable environments.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** The innovative data synthesis framework, combined with comprehensive domain randomization techniques, effectively bridges the simulation-to-real-world gap without relying on computationally expensive soft-body physics simulations. This approach offers a practical pathway for training complex manipulation policies for deformable objects.
- **Advancing Deformable Object Manipulation:** By successfully tackling the perception and planning challenges associated with thin, flat, and deformable objects, PP-Tac pushes the state-of-the-art in deformable object manipulation. It provides a robust solution where traditional vision-based or simple gripper-based methods fall short.
- **Practical Applications:** The high success rate (87.5%) on everyday objects like paper, plastic bags, and cloth, on various surfaces, demonstrates its immediate applicability. This technology could significantly enhance the capabilities of assistive robots in household environments (e.g., organizing documents, folding laundry, picking up dropped items), as well as in industrial and logistical settings (e.g., handling fabrics in manufacturing, packaging items in plastic bags).
- **Facilitating Future Research:** The authors' commitment to releasing both the hardware design and the code for the PP-Tac system is highly significant. This open-source contribution will empower the broader robotics community, accelerate further research, and foster the development of more sophisticated tactile-based dexterous manipulation systems.

While the system has limitations, such as the empirical tuning of initial contact force and potential improvements for extremely stiff materials, these are well-identified and provide clear directions for future research. Overall, PP-Tac represents a crucial step towards more dexterous, adaptable, and human-like robotic manipulation of everyday objects, bringing robots closer to becoming truly versatile companions and assistants.
