# HORIZON: Recoverability-Governed Curriculum for Physical-Domain Scaling

> [!summary] Summary
> The HORIZON framework introduces a recoverability-governed curriculum for robot learning, enabling policies to adapt to diverse physical conditions. This approach achieved robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on a Go2 robot under unseen morphology and payload perturbations, significantly outperforming traditional domain randomization methods.

## Problem

- Robot policies trained with online reinforcement learning struggle to generalize robustly across diverse physical conditions due to the limitations of traditional domain randomization.
- Excessively wide domain randomization ranges often lead to unrecoverable failures, generating poor on-policy training signals and potentially collapsing the learning process.
- Existing methods lack a principled way to expand physical domains incrementally while ensuring that new dynamics remain learnable and generate useful corrective data.

## Method

- **HORIZON** implements a recoverability-governed curriculum that adaptively expands physical domains by iteratively searching near the policy's learnable boundary.
- A two-part recoverability gate (locomotion feasibility and checkpoint-comparison) assesses candidate physical ranges, committing them only if rollouts are sufficiently recoverable and prior performance is maintained.
- The framework incorporates rollback and boundary refinement mechanisms, allowing the system to revert to a stable checkpoint or tighten search brackets when challenges are too difficult, preventing training collapse.

## Results

- HORIZON achieved **28.1%** OOD-all success, significantly outperforming 'Direct wide DR' (**10.3%**) and 'w/o rollback' baselines (**8.8%**) by maintaining higher recoverable rollout signals.
- The identified compact four-domain core curriculum reached **36.8%** OOD-all success, surpassing full-domain HORIZON (**28.1%**) and other methods like multi-expert distillation (**16.5%**), GRAM (**22.7%**), and ADR (**14.7%**) with **2.3x** lower training cost.
- The HORIZON-trained policy demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer on a real-world Go2 robot under unseen morphology (e.g., locked joints, stilts) and dynamic payload perturbations (3kg, 5kg), where baseline policies failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Recoverability is an operational requirement for effective on-policy physical-domain expansion, as hard-range exposure is beneficial only when rollouts remain recoverable enough to provide corrective data.
> - Physical-domain expansion is uneven and policy-dependent; some domains become bottlenecks but can be substantially expanded after intermediate recovery phases, while others show high robustness with moderate reachability.
> - A compact 'core-domain' composition (e.g., actuation, mass, disturbance, COM) focusing on critical domain interactions can achieve stronger generalization with less training cost than attempting full-domain coverage.
