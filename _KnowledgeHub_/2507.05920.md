# High-Resolution Visual Reasoning via Multi-Turn Grounding-Based Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> This paper introduces Multi-turn Grounding-based Policy Optimization (MGPO), a reinforcement learning framework that allows Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to perform high-resolution visual reasoning by iteratively focusing on key image regions. The approach enables LMMs to implicitly learn visual grounding capabilities from a simple binary reward, achieving state-of-the-art accuracy on challenging high-resolution benchmarks like V* Bench and MME-Realworld while surpassing large commercial models.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) struggle with high-resolution images due to the massive number of visual tokens generated, leading to computational inefficiency and a 'needle-in-a-haystack' problem.
- LMMs face maximum pixel constraints, often requiring aggressive resizing of high-resolution images, which results in the loss of critical fine-grained details necessary for many tasks.
- Integrating visual grounding into LMMs typically relies on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) which demands costly and scarce explicit bounding box annotations.

## Method

- The paper proposes ==Multi-turn Grounding-based Policy Optimization (MGPO)==, an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework that extends Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to a multi-turn conversational setting.
- MGPO enables ==iterative visual search== by having the LMM generate grounding coordinates in one turn, which are then used to crop a high-resolution sub-image from the original input for the subsequent turn.
- A simple ==binary reward function== (1 for correct final answer, 0 for incorrect) is used to optimize the LMM's policy across all intermediate grounding and reasoning steps, eliminating the need for explicit grounding annotations.

## Results

- Multi-turn Grounding-based Policy Optimization (MGPO) achieved **76.4%** accuracy on the out-of-distribution V* Bench, a **5.2%** absolute improvement over the GRPO baseline (**71.2%**), and **60.5%** accuracy on MME-Realworld, a **5.4%** gain over GRPO (**55.1%**).
- The MGPO post-trained model, based on a 7B Qwen2.5-VL, surpassed OpenAI’s o1 (**69.7%**) and GPT-4o (**73.9%**) models on the out-of-distribution V* Bench.
- During RL training, MGPO showed a steady increase in valid grounding coordinate generation, and **65.2%** of its generated grounding sub-images from the test set were sufficient to directly answer the question, demonstrating robust emergent grounding ability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) can develop robust visual grounding capabilities through reinforcement learning, leveraging only a binary reward signal from the final answer's correctness, without needing explicit grounding annotations.
> - Iteratively focusing on high-resolution sub-regions, mimicking human foveal vision, significantly improves an LMM's performance on fine-grained visual reasoning tasks.
> - A fixed multi-turn dialogue template can effectively guide an LMM to perform grounding actions in an RL setup, mitigating the 'cold start' problem without requiring extensive pre-training on grounding-annotated data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) have demonstrated capabilities in visual understanding by integrating powerful Large Language Models (LLMs) with vision components like Native Resolution Vision Transformers (NaViT). These architectures enable LMMs to process images at their original resolutions, theoretically capturing fine-grained details. However, this approach encounters practical limitations in real-world high-resolution scenarios. The number of visual tokens grows quadratically with image resolution, leading to a large proportion of task-irrelevant tokens and imposing context length constraints that necessitate image resizing, potentially blurring critical details. This presents a "needle-in-a-haystack" problem similar to that observed in LLMs for long contexts.

The human visual system offers a biological inspiration for addressing these challenges. Despite high theoretical resolution, humans achieve sharp vision primarily through a foveal area and employ task-driven visual search strategies to focus on important regions. Prior research in LMMs has largely focused on general visual understanding with common-resolution inputs. While some efforts address high-resolution image analysis, they often face high training costs and data scarcity, limiting generalization and performance.

Within multi-modal reasoning, approaches like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) have been used to decompose visual information or enhance reasoning through instruction tuning. More recently, reinforcement learning (RL) has been adopted to strengthen LMMs' reasoning abilities. However, many of these methods underemphasize direct reasoning over visual content, leading to weaknesses in perception-oriented tasks requiring accurate visual grounding. Closely related work, such as DeepEyes, has explored iterative grounding and cropping via RL to support reasoning, but typically relies on ground-truth bounding boxes for data filtering and intermediate grounding rewards.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing a reinforcement learning framework that enables LMMs to develop visual grounding capabilities for high-resolution images without requiring explicit grounding annotations. It aims to bridge the gap between LMMs' potential for high-resolution processing and their practical limitations by endowing them with a human-inspired visual search mechanism, using only the final answer's correctness as a reward signal.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework, Multi-turn Grounding-based Policy Optimization (MGPO), that enables Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) to iteratively focus on key visual regions within high-resolution images. This is achieved by automatically cropping sub-images based on model-predicted grounding coordinates within a multi-turn conversational structure.

The motivation for this work arises from several challenges inherent in current LMMs when processing high-resolution visual data:

1. **Inefficiency in High-Resolution Image Processing**: State-of-the-art LMMs, when processing high-resolution images, convert them into a large number of visual tokens. A significant portion of these tokens may be irrelevant to the downstream task, leading to computational inefficiency and a "needle-in-a-haystack" problem.
2. **Context Length Limitations**: LMMs have inherent context length limitations, which necessitate resizing high-resolution input images to comply with maximum pixel constraints. This resizing can result in a loss of fine-grained details, hindering the model's ability to capture critical visual information.
3. **Lack of Task-Driven Visual Search**: Unlike the human visual system, which employs foveal vision and task-driven search strategies to focus on relevant areas of a scene, LMMs typically process the entire image uniformly. This lack of a targeted visual search mechanism can limit their performance on tasks requiring fine-grained perception.
4. **Cost of Grounding Annotations**: Enabling LMMs to perform grounding-based visual reasoning usually requires accurate grounding annotations (e.g., bounding boxes). Acquiring such annotations for standard visual question answering (VQA) datasets is both scarce and costly, posing a significant barrier to the development of grounding-aware models through supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
5. **Cold Start Problem in RL**: The authors observed that LMMs struggle to autonomously trigger visual grounding during the reinforcement learning rollout process without explicit guidance, making stable optimization challenging.

To address these motivations, MGPO aims to:
- Demonstrate that robust grounding abilities can emerge within an RL framework, even when the training signal is limited to a binary reward derived solely from the correctness of the final answer, thereby obviating the need for expensive grounding annotations.
- Design a multi-turn conversational template and restrict policy loss computation to model outputs generated across multiple dialogue rounds to mitigate the cold start problem and promote stable optimization.
- Enable LMMs to overcome maximum pixel limitations by identifying relevant coordinates from a (potentially resized) input image and extracting clearer, higher-fidelity sub-images from the *original* high-resolution image for detailed analysis.
- Endow LMMs with a top-down, question-guided visual search mechanism, providing interpretable reasoning by explicitly indicating the attended image regions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MGPO is a reinforcement learning (RL) framework that builds upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm and incorporates a multi-turn grounding-based rollout strategy. The core idea is to enable LMMs to iteratively focus on specific image regions through an interleaved process of grounding, sub-image cropping, and reasoning.

**Core Formulation:**
The model operates over a sequence of *K* interactions, where at each turn *k*, it generates an output based on the complete history of visual and textual context, H^(k). If the model's output contains grounding coordinates, an image cropping function is triggered. This function extracts a relevant sub-image, X_i^(k+1), from the *current* visual input X_i^(k), which then becomes the new visual input for the subsequent turn. Both the new sub-image and the model's output are appended to the interaction history. This process continues until the model produces a final answer.

For each question, a group of *G* rollouts is sampled. A binary reward, r_g ∈ {0, 1}, is assigned based on the correctness of the final answer for each rollout. A group baseline, *b*, which is the average reward within the group, is used to reduce variance during optimization. The policy is optimized by maximizing an objective function derived from GRPO:
∇_θ J_MGPO(θ) = E_{{X_(j),g_a}}∼p_θ [(r_g - b) ⋅ Σ_{j=1}^{k_g} ∇_θ log p_θ (X_(j),g_a | H^(j))]
This formulation encourages the model to optimize all intermediate grounding and reasoning steps that contribute to the final correct answer, even without explicit intermediate rewards for grounding.

**Implementation Details:**

1. **Multi-turn Template without Cold Start:** To address the observation that LMMs struggle to autonomously generate grounding coordinates during initial rollouts, MGPO employs a fixed two-turn dialogue template:
 - **Turn 1:** The user prompt explicitly asks the model to output the coordinates of the key image area relevant to the question in JSON format.
 - **Turn 2:** The system then checks the validity of the predicted coordinates. If valid, a cropped sub-image is returned; otherwise, the original image is returned. The user prompt then asks the model to carefully analyze both the original and (sub-)image to answer the question, providing step-by-step reasoning and the final answer within `\boxed{}`.
 This template-based approach guides the model to perform grounding and reasoning in sequence, bypassing the need for an initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage with multi-turn grounding data. Policy loss is computed only on tokens generated across these conversational rounds to ensure stable optimization.

2. **Grounding Key Visual Areas:** Sub-images are extracted from the *original* high-resolution image, which is critical when the input image needs to be resized due to LMM pixel limitations. This ensures that the model can access higher-fidelity visual information even if the initial input image was downsampled.
 The bounding box coordinates predicted by the LMM (Qwen2.5-VL) are relative to the input image's resolution. These coordinates are normalized by the input image dimensions and then mapped back to the coordinate space of the original, high-resolution image before cropping:
 [`x̂₁`, `ŷ₁`, `x̂₂`, `ŷ₂`] = [`x₁`, `y₁`, `x₂`, `y₂`] / S_input ⋅ S_ori
 where S_input and S_ori represent the width and height of the input and original images, respectively.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-VL-7B (Bai et al., 2025), which integrates Qwen2.5 with a Native Resolution Vision Transformer (NaViT).
- **Training Datasets:** MME-Realworld (Zhang et al., 2024b) (21,690 training samples) for in-distribution learning, focusing on visual-question-short answering. No additional grounding annotations are used.
- **Evaluation Datasets:**
 - MME-Realworld (lite subset, 1,919 samples) for in-distribution (ID) evaluation.
 - V* Bench (Wu and Xie, 2024) (191 test samples) for challenging out-of-distribution (OOD) evaluation, focusing on detailed visual grounding on high-resolution images.
 - HR-Bench (Wang et al., 2025) (4K and 8K) and POPE (Li et al., 2023) are used for further generalization tests.
- **Metrics:** Accuracy for multiple-choice questions.
- **RL Baseline:** Naive GRPO, which uses a single-turn prompt to output both coordinates and the answer, assigned the same binary reward.
- **Computational Framework:** Verl (Sheng et al., 2024) for distributed training, vLLM (Kwon et al., 2023) for inference acceleration.
- **Optimization:** AdamW optimizer (Loshchilov and Hutter, 2017) with a fixed learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁶, global batch size of 512, mini-batch size of 128. PPO clip ratio set to 0.2.
- **Image Resolution Limit:** Maximum input image pixels limited to 1,003,520 (e.g., 1280 × 784, corresponding to 1280 visual tokens). Images exceeding this are resized for initial input, but sub-images are cropped from the original.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that MGPO effectively enhances LMMs' capabilities in high-resolution visual understanding and reasoning, particularly in visual grounding, without requiring explicit grounding annotations.

**Performance on Benchmarks:**
- **Overall Improvement:** MGPO significantly improved performance across benchmarks compared to Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and the GRPO baseline. It achieved a 5.2% absolute improvement over GRPO on the out-of-distribution (OOD) V* Bench and a 5.4% increase on the in-distribution (ID) MME-Realworld dataset.
- **GRPO vs. SFT:** The GRPO baseline did not show significant improvements over SFT in vision-centric tasks, suggesting that for high-resolution visual reasoning, the primary challenge is fine-grained detail perception rather than complex, lengthy reasoning chains. This finding contrasts with GRPO's efficacy in multi-modal mathematical tasks.
- **Competitiveness with OpenAI Models:** When trained with only 21,000 samples, the Qwen2.5-VL-7B model post-trained with MGPO surpassed OpenAI's o1 and GPT-4o models on the OOD V* Bench. This indicates MGPO's data efficiency and generalization capacity.
- **Training Trajectories:** While MGPO exhibited lower accuracy initially due to imperfect grounding formulation, its performance quickly surpassed GRPO as training progressed on the V* Bench, demonstrating its ability to handle complex high-resolution visual scenarios.

**Generalization and Robustness:**
- **Cross-Model Size and Benchmarks:** MGPO consistently outperformed the GRPO baseline on both Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B base models across five benchmarks, including HR-Bench (4K and 8K) for high-resolution understanding and POPE for object hallucination. Improvements on HR-Bench indicate effective exploitation of fine-grained details, while POPE results suggest that grounding in relevant visual evidence helps reduce object hallucination.
- **Comparison with DeepEyes:** Despite using weaker supervision (only final-answer accuracy reward, no grounding annotations or intermediate grounding rewards) and not training on V*-style data, MGPO remained competitive with DeepEyes on HR-Bench 4K/8K. DeepEyes incorporates V*-style data during training and uses explicit grounding rewards with bounding-box supervision. This supports the claim that strong grounding behavior can emerge from final-answer rewards alone.

**Emergent Grounding Ability:**
- **Valid Grounding Coordinates:** MGPO demonstrated a clear upward trajectory in the proportion of rollouts generating valid grounding coordinates throughout training, in contrast to GRPO, where the ratio remained low. This indicates that the model learns to localize correct image regions under MGPO.
- **Sufficiency of Sub-images:** A significantly higher proportion (65.2%) of sub-images generated by MGPO were sufficient for correctly answering questions on V* Bench, compared to 47.1% for the original Qwen2.5-VL and 50.2% for GRPO. This finding supports the conclusion that MGPO enables the model to autonomously identify and utilize highly informative regions without auxiliary grounding rewards.
- **Robustness to Imperfect Grounding:** The two-turn template provides a safety net by ensuring the model always has access to the original global image in the answering turn. This allows the model to fall back on global context if a cropped sub-image is uninformative or inaccurate, preventing critical dependence on perfect grounding at every step.
- **Impact of Input Image Resolution:** MGPO showed greater performance improvements on V* Bench when the maximum input pixel limit was lower. This highlights MGPO's ability to identify key regions and crop clearer sub-images from the original image, enabling more effective task completion under aggressive resizing constraints.

**Zero-Shot Generalization to More Interaction Turns:**
- Despite being trained with a fixed two-turn template, the MGPO-trained 7B model demonstrated zero-shot generalization to a 3-turn interaction at inference time. This extension yielded a further improvement on V* Bench (from 76.4% to 77.2%), suggesting that MGPO learns a transferable grounding-then-reasoning behavior rather than overfitting to the specific training template.

**Image Counting Tasks (Appendix C):**
- Experiments on image counting tasks using Pixmo-Points and Pixmo-Count datasets showed that incorporating an additional point reward (derived from grounding annotations) during GRPO post-training did not lead to significant performance improvements over using only the binary accuracy reward for the final count. This further supports the proposition that explicit grounding annotations are not strictly necessary for RL-based learning, as models can inherently learn to perform precise grounding to solve counting tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a novel approach to address critical challenges in high-resolution image processing by Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), with several significant implications:

1. **Enabling Human-like Visual Search in LMMs**: MGPO endows LMMs with a top-down, question-guided visual search mechanism, mimicking aspects of the human visual system. This capability allows LMMs to dynamically focus on key image regions, which is crucial for handling complex high-resolution real-world scenarios where fine-grained details are essential for accurate reasoning.

2. **Reducing Annotation Burden**: A central finding of this work is that robust visual grounding capabilities can emerge within a reinforcement learning framework using only a binary reward derived from the correctness of the final answer. This eliminates the need for expensive and labor-intensive grounding annotations, which are typically required for supervised fine-tuning approaches. This significantly broadens the applicability of grounding-based RL paradigms by making them accessible with standard visual question answering datasets.

3. **Overcoming Technical Limitations of LMMs**: By enabling models to predict grounding coordinates and crop sub-images from the original high-resolution input, MGPO effectively addresses the challenges of quadratically increasing visual tokens and maximum pixel constraints in LMMs. Even when an input image must be resized, the model can still access higher-fidelity information from the original image, preserving crucial details that would otherwise be lost.

4. **Improved Interpretability**: The multi-turn grounding process makes the LMM's reasoning more interpretable by explicitly indicating which image regions are being attended to at different stages of the inference process. This transparency can enhance user trust and facilitate debugging of model failures.

5. **Enhanced Performance and Generalization**: The experimental results demonstrate substantial performance gains on challenging benchmarks like V* Bench and MME-Realworld, even surpassing larger and more extensively trained commercial models like OpenAI's o1 and GPT-4o with a comparatively lightweight model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) and fewer training samples. This indicates MGPO's high data efficiency and strong generalization capacity.

6. **Advancing Reinforcement Learning for Multimodal AI**: This work provides insights into how LMMs can learn complex, multi-step visual reasoning behaviors through RL, even under weak supervision. The effectiveness of the multi-turn template in addressing the "cold start problem" for grounding in RL offers a practical strategy for future research in training agents to interact with multimodal environments.

The insights gained from MGPO contribute to the advancement of grounding-based visual reasoning and have the potential to lead to more generalizable and capable vision-language models, particularly for tasks requiring precise visual understanding in high-resolution contexts.
