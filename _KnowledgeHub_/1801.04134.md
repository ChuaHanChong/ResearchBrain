# Deep Episodic Memory: Encoding, Recalling, and Predicting Episodic Experiences for Robot Action Execution

> [!summary] Summary
> Deep Episodic Memory is an unsupervised composite convolutional-LSTM encoder-decoder that compresses a video of an action into a single 2000-dimensional latent vector, from which one decoder reconstructs the observed frames and a second predicts future frames. Cosine similarity over these latents retrieves conceptually similar past episodes, outperforming Fisher-vector and LSTM baselines and driving real humanoid manipulation.

## Problem

- Episodic memories in cognitive architectures store pre-defined symbolic representations tied to one problem domain, so knowledge transfer and generalization to unstructured real scenes stay very limited.
- Subsymbolic video encodings built from Fisher vectors of CNN percepts can match related segments but cannot reconstruct any perceptual content back from the stored representation.
- Composite LSTM video models operate on CNN features rather than raw pixels, and pixel-change prediction models forecast only short horizons, so neither recovers a stored episode.

## Method

- ==Composite encoder-decoder== with alternating convolutional-LSTM and strided convolution layers plus a fully connected LSTM top layer, whose concatenated hidden and cell states form the 2000-dimensional latent vector.
- Two mirrored transposed-convolution decoders share that single latent: a reconstruction decoder recovers the five input frames, a prediction decoder extrapolates the next five, forcing scene dynamics into the code.
- Training loss mixes pixel MSE with gradient-difference loss at eta 0.4 to suppress blur, with layer normalization, 10-20% dropout, and Gaussian noise of sigma 0.1 injected into the latent; retrieval uses cosine similarity.

## Results

- On ActivityNet first-match precision reaches **44.31%** (**45.55%** with 200 PCA components) versus **36.29%** for a ResNet-50 composite LSTM and **32.31%** for ResNet-50 Fisher vectors.
- On 20BN-something-something precision is **11.63%** (**11.81%** with PCA) and mAP **8.12%**, beating ResNet-50 LSTM at **10.08%** precision and SIFT Fisher vectors at **1.05%**.
- Cosine-similarity matrices show intra-class similarity well above inter-class across 93 and 174 classes; PSNR stays flat over reconstructed frames but decays over the five predicted frames.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adding a future-frame decoder on top of an autoencoder forces the bottleneck to encode dynamics, not just appearance, yielding latents whose geometry mirrors action semantics without labels.
> - Unsupervised retrieval of one similar episode plus an object tracker and movement primitives can trigger robot behavior that reinforcement learning would need thousands of trials to acquire.
> - Pixel-space reconstruction targets leak nuisance structure: matches become biased by background brightness, and prediction blur reflects the regression objective rather than weak representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated within the field of cognitive robotics, specifically addressing the development of artificial memory systems that enable robots to learn from and adapt to past experiences. The broader landscape encompasses both cognitive architectures and deep learning approaches for action understanding.

Traditionally, cognitive architectures like Soar have incorporated episodic memory to store and retrieve high-level symbolic representations of past experiences, supporting planning and inference. However, these approaches often rely on predefined, problem-specific representations, which can limit their generalization and transferability in complex, unstructured real-world scenarios. The challenge for such systems has been to account for nuances and fuzziness, which often requires interpolation between concepts, a capability that traditional declarative memory concepts struggle to provide. This work attempts to overcome these limitations by moving towards subsymbolic, data-driven representations.

Concurrently, the field of action understanding with deep neural networks has progressed significantly, often combining Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for spatial feature extraction with Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) or Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) networks for temporal information processing. Approaches include supervised learning for activity recognition, unsupervised video frame prediction, and unsupervised prediction of visual representations. Some models, such as those by Srivastava et al., utilize composite autoencoder-like architectures for unsupervised learning of video representations, combining sequence-to-sequence autoencoding with future frame prediction, but often process CNN features rather than raw video frames, thus not allowing for direct reconstruction of visual scenes. Other works focus on predicting pixel changes or single future frames, but do not necessarily create a reconstructable representation of an entire episode.

This paper distinguishes itself by proposing a unified deep neural network architecture that, in an unsupervised manner, generates subsymbolic latent representations directly from raw visual observations. This architecture not only encodes observed actions but also facilitates their comparison based on conceptual similarity, reconstructs the original visual episodes from these latent representations, and predicts future frames. This integrated approach extends beyond previous work by offering a comprehensive episodic-like memory system that can handle raw visual input and perform multiple memory functions within a single framework, aiming to provide a more flexible and generalizable mechanism for robotic cognitive abilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from observing human cognitive abilities, particularly the capacity to predict action consequences, recall relevant past experiences, and transfer knowledge to adapt to novel situations. The authors highlight the vital role of human episodic memory in introducing such capabilities. Translating these human cognitive functions into robotic systems, especially through a compact and generalizable mechanism for encoding, storing, and retrieving spatio-temporal patterns of visual observations, represents a core challenge in cognitive robotics.

The specific objectives and motivations of this research are:

- **Enabling Autonomous Robotic Learning and Adaptation:** Inspired by infants learning through observation, the goal is to enhance robots' cognitive abilities to autonomously infer probable behaviors and adapt them to current scenes. For instance, a robot observing a juice carton on a table should be able to determine the most suitable action (e.g., "pushing the juice" or "grasping the juice") and how to perform it based on past experiences.
- **Developing a Unified Deep Episodic Memory Framework:** The central objective is to introduce a novel deep neural network architecture that can encode, store, and recall past action experiences in an episodic memory-like manner. This framework is designed to integrate several key functionalities.
- **Encoding Visual Experiences into a Latent Space:** The network aims to transform observed action episodes into a lower-dimensional latent vector space. This representation is intended to allow robots to store visual experiences efficiently and compare them based on conceptual similarity.
- **Facilitating Reconstruction and Prediction from Latent Representations:** A key objective is to demonstrate that the same latent representation can be used to reconstruct the original action frames (reconstruction) and predict subsequent frames of an observed action (prediction). This implies that the latent space must capture both the static content and the dynamic evolution of an action.
- **Establishing a Mechanism for Action Matching and Retrieval:** Based on the conceptual similarity in the latent space, the research aims to introduce a mechanism that can retrieve the most similar episodes from memory given a query scene or action.
- **Evaluating Generalization in Real-World Robotic Scenarios:** The ultimate goal is to assess the practical utility and generalization capability of this episodic memory model in an unstructured real-world context, specifically for robot object manipulation using a humanoid robot.
- **Hypothesis Testing:** The authors hypothesize that the latent subsymbolic encodings generated by their network from raw visual observations are sufficiently rich and descriptive to allow for meaningful comparison with previously experienced episodes, thereby supporting tracing observations and adapting behaviors.

The work aims to address these objectives within a single, coherent framework, which, according to the authors, had not been comprehensively achieved previously for vision-based cognitive abilities encompassing action representation, storage, memorization, and prediction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research involves the design, training, and evaluation of a deep neural network architecture for unsupervised learning of episodic memory from visual data.

### 4. The Neural Network Model

The core of the methodology is a composite encoder-decoder network architecture, conceptually derived from, but adapted to use convolutional LSTM (ConvLSTM) cells for direct processing of raw video frames.

- **Architecture Overview:** The model consists of one encoder (E) and two independent decoders (D_r and D_p).
 - **Encoder (E):** Takes a sequence of `k` initial video frames (X_r = x_1..., x_k) as input and compresses them into a single, low-dimensional latent vector V.
 - **Reconstruction Decoder (D_r):** Receives the latent vector V and attempts to reconstruct the original input frame sequence X_r, producing Y_r = y_1..., y_k.
 - **Prediction Decoder (D_p):** Also receives the latent vector V and attempts to predict the future frames X_p = x_{k+1}..., x_n, producing Y_p = y_{k+1}..., y_n.
- **Latent Vector (V):** The latent vector V is generated by concatenating the hidden state (h_k) and cell state (c_k) of the fully connected LSTM (fc LSTM) layer, which is the top layer of the encoder after processing the entire input sequence X_r. V acts as the sole information bottleneck between the encoder and both decoders, forcing the encoder to compress comprehensive information about the observed action, including scene dynamics and trajectories. The dimension of V is set to 2000 in all experiments.
- **ConvLSTM Cells:** Both spatial and temporal information are captured using ConvLSTM cells, which allow for convolutional operations within the LSTM gates.
- **Layer Structure:**
 - The encoder network E comprises an alternating stack of ConvLSTM and normal convolutional layers. The convolutional layers use a stride of 2 to reduce spatial dimensions, while ConvLSTM layers maintain spatial size and pass information across time steps.
 - A fully connected layer followed by an fc LSTM layer is appended to the encoder stack.
 - Both decoders (D_r and D_p) have an inverted structure relative to the encoder, utilizing transposed convolution layers to gradually increase feature map spatial dimensions back to the original video frame resolution (128x128 pixels, 3 color channels).

### 5. Loss Function and Training

- **Combined Loss:** The network is trained using a linear combination of two loss functions:
 - **Mean Squared Error Loss (L_mse):** A pixel-wise reconstruction loss (‖y_i - x_i‖^2_2) that compares generated frames (y_i) to ground truth frames (x_i).
 - **Gradient Difference Loss (L_gd):** This loss compares horizontal and vertical image gradients, penalizing blurriness and promoting sharper edges in generated images.
 - The total loss is L = (1 - η) L_mse + η L_gd, with η set to 0.4. This weighting balances pixel-level accuracy with structural fidelity.
- **Training Procedure:**
 - Mini-batch gradient descent is employed with the ADAM optimizer.
 - An exponentially decaying learning rate schedule is used.
 - Layer normalization and dropout (10% to 20%) are applied after most network layers to aid training stability and prevent overfitting.
 - Gaussian noise (N(0, 0.1)) is added to the latent vector V during training to encourage the encoder to utilize the entire latent space and produce distinct representations.

### 6. Matching Visual Experiences in the Latent Space

- **Similarity Metric:** Cosine similarity is used to measure the conceptual similarity between latent vectors. For a given query representation V_q, its similarity is computed against all stored latent vectors V_i in the episodic memory.
- **Retrieval Mechanism:** The `n` memory instances with the highest cosine similarity to the query are retrieved.
- **PCA for Feature Emphasis:** To potentially refine the latent representations for matching, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is applied to the mean latent vectors of each class. This is intended to transform the latent space to emphasize features relevant for class separation while mitigating the influence of less important, uniformly distributed background features.

### 7. Experimental Setup and Datasets

- **Datasets:** The model was trained and evaluated on two large-scale video datasets:
 - **ActivityNet:** Comprises 10,024 training and 4,926 validation video snippets, organized into 203 activity classes focusing on higher-level concepts.
 - **20BN-something-something (20BN):** Contains 86,017 training and 11,522 validation videos, across 174 classes, emphasizing detailed physical properties and motions rather than object recognition or background.
- **Frame Sampling:** For training, 10 equally spaced frames were selected from each video. The first `k=5` frames were used as input for the encoder and reconstruction decoder, while the last `n-k=5` frames served as ground truth for the prediction decoder.

### 8. Robot Application

- **Humanoid Robot ARMAR-IIIa:** A pilot study was conducted using human demonstrations for the ARMAR-IIIa robot.
- **Custom Dataset:** 120 visual episodes of human subjects demonstrating 10 different manipulation actions were recorded. 100 episodes formed the robot's memory, and 20 served as queries.
- **Integration:** The trained 20BN model's encoder was used to generate latent representations for these custom robot demonstration videos. Matching was performed using cosine similarity on the first 50 principal components of these latent representations.
- **Action Execution:** The demonstration involved:
 1. ARMAR-IIIa observing a static scene (e.g., a sponge on a table).
 2. The static scene frames being fed into the matching mechanism to recall a similar past episode (e.g., "pushing a green cup").
 3. A real-time object detector tracking objects in the recalled episode.
 4. Extracting the pushing trajectory from the recalled episode.
 5. Learning the tracked motion using dynamic movement primitives.
 6. The robot executing the learned pushing motion on the perceived object (sponge).

### 9. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation yielded several findings across the different aspects of the proposed deep episodic memory model.

### 10. Conceptual Similarity and Proximity in the Latent Space

- **Intra-class vs. Inter-class Similarity:** Analysis of pairwise cosine similarities of latent vectors revealed that videos belonging to the same action class exhibit considerably higher intra-class similarity compared to inter-class similarity across different classes (Figure 3). This indicates that the unsupervised training process forces the encoder to map conceptually similar actions into proximate regions of the 2000-dimensional latent space for both ActivityNet and 20BN datasets.
- **Effect of PCA:** Applying Principal Component Analysis (PCA) on the mean latent vectors of each class further improved the distribution of latent vectors. This transformation pushed conceptually similar representations closer together while increasing the separation between different classes (Figure 3b, 3d), suggesting that PCA helps to filter out less relevant background features and emphasizes action-relevant characteristics.

### 11. Frame Reconstruction and Future Frame Prediction

- **Qualitative Assessment:** The network demonstrated the ability to reconstruct past video frames and predict future frames qualitatively, as illustrated by sample sequences from ActivityNet and 20BN (Figure 4).
- **Quantitative Assessment (PSNR):** Peak-Signal-to-Noise-Ratio (PSNR) was used to quantify the quality of reconstructed and predicted frames (Figure 5).
 - Reconstruction quality was consistently higher than prediction quality.
 - PSNR for reconstructed frames remained relatively constant.
 - PSNR for predicted future frames showed a decline over time. This decline was attributed primarily to an increase in blurriness, which is a known limitation of regression into pixel space, rather than a failure to capture object motions correctly. The model's performance surpassed a simple baseline of using the mean of input frames for prediction.

### 12. Matching and Retrieving Visual Episodes

- **Benchmark Performance:** The proposed method was quantitatively benchmarked against various state-of-the-art action descriptors, including Fisher vector encodings of CNN features (VGG-fc1, ResNet-50), SIFT, STIP features, and composite LSTM network encodings (Table I).
 - The proposed method achieved the highest precision for the first match and mean average precision (mAP) on both ActivityNet and 20BN datasets. It notably outperformed approaches using substantially deeper networks like ResNet-50.
 - The application of PCA (using 200 components) further improved matching precision.
 - ActivityNet generally yielded higher precision values, attributed to fewer categories and richer features among its categories compared to 20BN.
- **Qualitative Consistency:** Qualitative analysis indicated that the matching mechanism generally yielded conceptually consistent results. Even in cases of categorical mismatches, the retrieved episodes were often closely related in terms of action type (e.g., "pushing something off something" matched with "moving something away from something") (Figure 6a).
- **Limitations Observed:** A bias towards background color was noted, where videos with similar background luminance (dark or bright) tended to be matched.
- **Unique Feature:** Unlike the baseline methods, the proposed approach uniquely provides the ability to reconstruct the visual episode from its latent encoding.

### 13. Robot Manipulation Learned From Episodic Memory

- **Generalization to New Data:** The model demonstrated scalability and generalization by successfully matching human demonstration videos for the ARMAR-IIIa robot, despite being trained on a different, larger dataset (20BN).
- **Static Scene Querying:** A significant finding was the ability to recall relevant past action episodes even from static scene frames (without temporal cues). This enables the robot to infer potential actions in an observed scene.
- **Case-Based Reasoning for Action Execution:** In a pilot study (Figure 7), ARMAR-IIIa observed a sponge. The system matched this static scene to a stored episode of "pushing a green cup." By utilizing an auxiliary object detector to extract the pushing trajectory from the matched episode and learning it via dynamic movement primitives, the robot successfully executed a pushing action on the perceived sponge. This demonstrated the robot's ability to transfer relevant knowledge (e.g., motion profiles) from remembered experiences to novel objects in a scene.

### 14. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of cognitive robotics by proposing a novel deep neural network architecture that integrates multiple functionalities associated with episodic memory within a single, unsupervised framework.

### 15. Architectural and Functional Integration

The primary significance lies in the development of a coherent deep neural network that can encode, store, reconstruct, and predict visual action episodes from raw sensory input. Unlike prior work that often focused on subsets of these capabilities (e.g., unsupervised matching without reconstruction, or single-frame prediction), this model offers a comprehensive episodic memory system. This integration addresses limitations of earlier approaches that either relied on pre-defined symbolic representations, could not reconstruct visual information from latent features, or had restricted temporal prediction horizons.

### 16. Data-Driven Subsymbolic Representation

The unsupervised learning of subsymbolic representations directly from raw visual data bypasses the need for manually engineered features or predefined symbolic descriptions. This approach offers greater flexibility and generalizability compared to traditional symbolic episodic memory systems, which often struggle with complex, unstructured real-world data and nuanced concepts. The ability for the latent space to reflect conceptual similarity without explicit class labels demonstrates an emergent understanding of action dynamics.

### 17. Enhanced Robot Cognitive Abilities and Efficiency

The proposed episodic memory framework has the potential to significantly extend the cognitive abilities of robots.
- **Case-Based Reasoning:** It enables robots to perform case-based reasoning by retrieving conceptually similar past experiences to inform current decision-making and action planning.
- **Action Prediction and Adaptation:** The capacity to predict future frames and transfer motion profiles from recalled episodes allows robots to anticipate outcomes and adapt learned behaviors to novel objects or situations with minimal additional training.
- **Data Efficiency:** The demonstration that a robot can learn and execute complex manipulation tasks from a single memory instance, as opposed to thousands of trials required by reinforcement learning, highlights the potential for substantial data efficiency in robot learning.

### 18. Foundation for Future Robotic Autonomy

By enabling robots to autonomously trace observations, select relevant past experiences, reason about action consequences, and generate actions through knowledge transfer, this work lays a foundation for more autonomous and adaptable robotic systems. The ability to handle static scene queries further expands the range of scenarios in which such memory systems can be applied.

### 19. Addressing Limitations for Future Work

The identification of current limitations, such as the need for auxiliary object detectors for trajectory extraction in robot manipulation and the observed background bias in matching, also guides future research. Future work focusing on embedding action-relevant information (like trajectories) directly into the latent space and developing mechanisms to reduce sensitivity to background variations (e.g., using segmentation masks) could further enhance the model's robustness and utility in real-world applications.
