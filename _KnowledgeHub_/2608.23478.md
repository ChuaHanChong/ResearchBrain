# Act with Intent: Distilling Behavior Intent for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> INDI (Intention Distillation) supervises a VLA action decoder with behavior-level intent rather than actions alone. During training a frozen teacher VLM interprets a demonstrated segment from observation, instruction, coarse action summary and execution video; learnable intent queries recover that multimodal target at a mid decoder layer and organize action plus visual and textual grounding prediction. Teacher removed at deployment; GR00T-N1.7 rises 64.3% to 84.7% on SimplerEnv-Bridge.

## Problem

- Behavior cloning supervises which motor command was demonstrated but leaves implicit the local objective that behavior serves under the instruction, so purpose stays unlearned.
- Future-based supervision using generated frames, subgoal images, latent observations, trajectories or motion fields captures particular realizations of what may happen, not the shared semantic objective.
- Latent-plan methods learn reconstruction codes jointly with the policy, while intent work relies on trajectory abstractions, virtual targets or human gaze rather than interpreted executed behavior.

## Method

- ==Intent from executed behavior==: a frozen teacher VLM (Cosmos-Reason2-8B) reads observation, instruction, coarse action summary and video; its middle-layer hidden states form the intent target, with generated purpose statement and endpoint visual feature as groundings.
- ==Intent recovery==: eight learnable intent queries are cosine-aligned to the teacher target at 50% decoder depth, after which remaining ==flow-matching== layers jointly predict actions and self-conditioned visual and textual grounding rows.
- **INDI**'s ==intent-aware decoding==: asymmetric attention routes grounding and action rows through recovered intent, a ==Bernoulli(0.5) context gate== forces intent-mediated updates, and a batch-shifted ==mismatch hinge loss== (margin 0.05) enforces content dependence.

## Results

- SimplerEnv-Bridge: GR00T-N1.7 improves **64.3%** to **84.7%** (**+20.4 pp**), with EP-Basket jumping **36.0%** to **96.0%**; the same recipe lifts a flow-matching baseline from **52.3%** to **58.8%**.
- RoboCasa Kitchen with **100 demos** per task reaches **70.3%** versus **64.1%** baseline and **65.8%** future supervision, within **0.5 pp** of a **3,000-demo** checkpoint; real-world average rises **62.0%** to **68.7%**.
- Controls: intent only scores **76.0%** against **61.5%** baseline, **60.0%** groundings only and **57.0%** free latent; cross-objective intent swaps drop success from **84.5%** to **45.2%**, phase forcing to **7.3%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Supervising a behavior's semantic objective beats supervising its realization: intent alone exceeded future-state supervision by 8 pp under matched backbone, data and budget.
> - Semantic distillation can stay training-only; cached offline teacher targets add 0.04B parameters and roughly 5 ms per query, so richer supervision need not cost deployment compute.
> - Gains concentrate on long-horizon multi-stage tasks and shifted scenes; near-ceiling short tasks barely move, so expect limited value where execution is already reliable.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is experiencing rapid advancements, particularly with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and robot foundation models. These models leverage large-scale vision-language backbones, sophisticated action decoders, and extensive robot demonstration datasets to enable robots to translate multimodal contexts into actionable motor commands. This allows for the transfer of Internet-scale visual and linguistic knowledge to robot control tasks.

Current VLA models, however, primarily rely on imitation learning, specifically behavior cloning, for action decoder training. Behavior cloning supervises the direct reproduction of demonstrated motor commands given an observation and instruction. While effective in establishing a context-to-action mapping, this approach implicitly learns the *purpose* or *local objective* served by a particular behavior segment within the broader task instruction. The decoder learns *what* motor command to execute but not *why* that specific action sequence is performed.

A growing body of research aims to augment VLA policies with future-based supervision. These methods incorporate signals such as future scene states (e.g., generated frames, subgoal images, latent observations) or representations of forthcoming interactions (e.g., trajectories, motion fields, point tracks). While these signals provide information about future visual outcomes or motion dynamics, they often capture specific *realizations* of behavior rather than the underlying *semantic objective* that various realizations might serve. For instance, similar manipulation stages can contribute to different task objectives, and a single objective can be achieved through diverse physical executions. This distinction highlights a gap: existing methods often lack an explicit representation of the behavior-level intent, which would articulate the local objective that an action sequence aims to achieve under the task instruction.

This work positions itself to address this gap by proposing a method for explicitly modeling and distilling behavior-level intent into VLA action decoders. It differentiates itself from prior work by focusing on the semantic objective rather than just future states or motion patterns, aiming to provide the action decoder with a representation of *what the behavior is supposed to accomplish*. This approach seeks to enhance the coherence and temporal organization of generated behaviors, allowing the decoder to produce actions that are not only contextually appropriate but also semantically aligned with the task's local sub-goals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the capabilities of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by explicitly incorporating behavior-level intent into their action decoders. The motivation stems from a recognized limitation in current VLA training paradigms. While VLA models excel at mapping multimodal context to robot actions, their action decoders are predominantly trained via behavior cloning. This method supervises the reproduction of specific motor commands without making explicit the local objective or purpose that the demonstrated behavior serves under a given instruction. As a result, the action decoder learns a mapping from context to action, but the semantic goal of each action sequence remains implicit.

The authors identify several key limitations arising from this implicit intent:
1. **Lack of Semantic Understanding**: The decoder may effectively imitate actions but lacks an explicit representation of "what this action means" or "what is the intent?" This hinders the model's capacity to reason about the purpose of its actions.
2. **Ineffectiveness of Future-based Supervision for Intent**: While future-based supervision methods offer richer signals (e.g., future frames, trajectories, motion fields), they primarily capture particular *realizations* of what might happen or how motion might unfold. They do not directly supervise the *semantic objective* or *local goal* that these realizations serve. This means that a policy trained on future states might understand the visual outcome but not the underlying reason for achieving that outcome.
3. **Impact on Coherence and Generalization**: Without an explicit understanding of behavior-level intent, the action decoder might struggle to generate coherent, temporally extended behavior, especially in complex, multi-stage tasks. This can also limit generalization to new environments or variations where the *how* of execution might change but the *what* (the objective) remains constant.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this work are:
1. **Define and Operationalize Behavior-Level Intent**: To formally define intent as the local objective that the forthcoming behavior should achieve under the instruction and develop a mechanism to derive this intent from demonstrated behavior.
2. **Distill Intent into Action Decoders**: To design a framework, named Intention Distillation (INDI), that distills this behavior-level intent, derived from a teacher Vision-Language Model (VLM), into the action decoder of a student VLA model. This involves learning to recover a multimodal intent representation at an intermediate decoder layer.
3. **Enable Intent-Aware Action Decoding**: To integrate the recovered intent as a functional intermediate representation that organizes and guides subsequent action prediction, alongside representations of how the behavior unfolds and what it achieves (visual and textual groundings).
4. **Evaluate Performance and Generalization**: To empirically demonstrate that explicit behavior-level intent supervision improves policy performance and generalization across various simulation benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks, including scenarios with held-out objects and distractors.
5. **Analyze Intent Representation**: To conduct controlled studies and analyses to verify that the recovered latent representation genuinely functions as intent, capturing behavioral objective and execution progress, and causally influencing downstream predictions.

In essence, the research aims to shift VLA learning from merely reproducing motor commands to fostering a deeper understanding of the semantic objectives underlying robot actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology, Intention Distillation (INDI), is designed to instill behavior-level intent into pretrained VLA action decoders. It operates in a training-only phase using a frozen teacher VLM to derive intent targets, which are then learned by the student VLA decoder. At deployment, the teacher and target-generation modules are removed, and the student policy internally recovers and utilizes this intent.

The methodology can be broken down into three primary components:

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

A VLA policy, composed of a frozen vision-language module (F_vlm) and a trainable flow-matching action decoder, receives an RGB observation (o_t), a language instruction (ℓ), and a proprioceptive state (q_t). F_vlm produces context tokens (B_t). Standard behavior cloning models p_θ(A_t | B_t, q_t), where A_t is the action. INDI extends this to model (A_t, V_t, R_t) ∼ p_θ(A_t, V_t, R_t | B_t, q_t, I_t), where I_t is the recovered intent state, V_t is a visual-outcome, and R_t is a textual-purpose target.

#### 3.2. Intent Supervision from Executed Behavior

Since explicit intent labels are unavailable in robot demonstrations, a frozen teacher VLM is used to generate intent supervision.
- **Teacher VLM Input**: For an H-step demonstrated behavior segment (W_t), the teacher VLM processes a comprehensive input (E_t) comprising the current observation (o_t), the language instruction (ℓ), a coarse textual summary of the demonstrated actions (c_A(W_t)), and the corresponding execution video (vid_t:t+H).
- **Functional-Intent Prompt**: The teacher VLM receives a prompt designed to elicit a functional-purpose statement (S_t) describing the local objective of the behavior segment and how it advances the instruction.
- **Target Extraction**:
 - **Intent Target (I*t)**: This is extracted from the hidden states of a middle layer of the teacher VLM (e.g., layer 18 of 32) while it processes the multimodal input E_t. This representation is intended to capture the teacher's multimodal understanding of the executed behavior. It is formed by pooling K_I (e.g., 8) contiguous regions of the hidden states.
 - **Textual-Purpose Target (R*t)**: This is derived from the final-layer hidden states of the teacher's *generated functional-purpose statement* S_t. It provides a linguistic realization of the intent, pooling K_R (e.g., 8) contiguous regions of its tokens.
 - **Visual-Outcome Target (V*t)**: This target grounds the intent in the resulting scene change. It is obtained by encoding the observation at the *endpoint* of the behavior segment (o_t+H) using a separate frozen visual encoder (Φ_vis) and spatially pooling K_V (e.g., 16 per camera view) regional features.

These targets (I*t, R*t, V*t) are generated offline during training and are only used for supervision, never as inputs to the deployed policy.

#### 3.3. Intent-Aware Action Decoding

The core of INDI lies in how the student action decoder learns to recover and utilize the intent. The pretrained flow-matching action decoder is augmented with:
- **Intent Queries (Q_I)**: Learnable, clean queries that aim to recover the intent target.
- **Visual and Textual Grounding Rows (Z_V,λt, Z_R,λt)**: Self-conditioned inputs for predicting visual outcomes and textual purposes.
The decoder processes a sequence comprising proprioceptive state (Q_t), noised action (A_λt), grounding inputs, and intent queries.

- **Recovering Intent**: The intent queries are processed through the decoder layers. At an intermediate decoder layer (e.g., 50% depth), the contextualized intent-query states (H_Ltap_I,t) are extracted, projected into the teacher feature space (P_I), and supervised against the teacher's intent target (I*t) using a cosine distance loss (L_I). The decoder-space states (H_Ltap_I,t) then serve as the recovered intent for subsequent layers.
- **Action and Grounding Prediction**:
 - **Action Stream**: The action decoder continues to optimize its flow-matching objective (L_A) to predict the denoised action (ˆu_A) based on the VLA context, proprioceptive state, and the recovered intent.
 - **Grounding Streams**: The decoder also predicts visual (ˆV_t) and textual (ˆR_t) groundings. These are self-conditioned: a no-gradient pass predicts clean decoder-space grounding states, which are then re-noised and used as inputs for a gradient pass. The final grounding states (H_Lout_G,t) are aligned with their respective targets (V*t, R*t) using cosine distance losses (L_V, L_R).
- **Intent-Dependent Information Flow**: To ensure the recovered intent is a functional intermediate state, not just an auxiliary target:
 - **Asymmetric Attention**: Intent queries attend to the VLA context (B_t), proprioceptive state (Q_t), and themselves, but are isolated from noised action and grounding rows. Grounding rows attend to the recovered intent and each other. Action rows attend to both recovered intent and grounding rows.
 - **Context Dropout (Bottleneck Routing)**: A binary gate (α ∼ Bernoulli(0.5)) controls direct access of non-intent rows (action, grounding) to the VLA context (B_t). When α=0, these rows must obtain contextual information *through* the intent queries, forcing intent to serve as a bottleneck.
 - **Intent-Mismatch Training (L_mis)**: A loss encourages downstream predictions to be sensitive to the *content* of the recovered intent. It forces matched intent to yield lower downstream loss than mismatched (batch-shifted) intent, ensuring that the dependency is on specific intent content, not just the pathway's presence.
- **Training Objective and Deployment**: The total loss (L) combines the action, intent-alignment, grounding, and intent-mismatch losses. At deployment, the teacher and all target-generation modules are removed, α is set to 1, and the policy internally forms intent and grounding states alongside the action stream from standard VLA inputs.

This methodology aims to provide the action decoder with an explicit, semantic understanding of behavior objectives, leading to more robust and generalizable robot policies.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of INDI was conducted across three distinct environments: SimplerEnv-Bridge, RoboCasa Kitchen (both simulation benchmarks), and real-world tabletop manipulation tasks. The findings consistently indicate that behavior-level intent supervision improves policy performance and generalization.

#### 4.1. Results on Simulation Benchmarks

- **SimplerEnv-Bridge**:
 - INDI significantly improved GR00T-N1.7, increasing average success from 64.3% to **84.7%** (a +20.4 percentage point (pp) gain).
 - Improvements were observed across all four tasks, with the largest gain on EP-Basket (36.0% to 96.0%).
 - When compared to a future-supervision variant (68.0%), INDI demonstrated a further +16.7 pp improvement.
 - INDI also improved π_0.5, raising average success from 52.3% to **58.8%** (a +6.5 pp gain), with improvements on every task.
 - Among published results for this benchmark, INDI achieved the highest average success.

- **RoboCasa Kitchen**:
 - With GR00T-N1.7 (100 demonstrations per task), INDI improved average success from 64.1% to **70.3%** (a +6.2 pp gain).
 - Gains were consistent across all three task categories (pick-and-place, open-or-close, others).
 - The future-supervision variant achieved 65.8%, with INDI providing an additional +4.5 pp gain.
 - For π_0.5, average success increased from 34.9% to **41.4%** (a +6.5 pp gain), including a notable +14.0 pp gain on the "Others" category.
 - Notably, INDI with 100 demonstrations per task achieved an average success rate of 70.3%, which is within 0.5 pp of the reported GR00T-N1.7 checkpoint trained with 3,000 demonstrations per task.
 - In comparison to various reported prior methods on RoboCasa Kitchen, INDI achieved the highest average success rates.

#### 4.2. Results on Real-World Benchmarks

- **Overall Performance**: INDI improved average success from 71.0% to **76.0%** in "ID clean" conditions.
- **Generalization**: The gains persisted under out-of-distribution conditions:
 - Held-out objects: 62.0% to **68.0%**.
 - Distractors: 53.0% to **62.0%**.
 - Relative retention was comparable or higher for INDI, indicating sustained improvement as scene conditions deviated from training.
- **Task-Specific Gains**: Improvements were more pronounced in longer-horizon tasks. Cross-Bin Stacking and Drawer Storage saw average gains of 7.3 pp and 10.7 pp, respectively.
- **Stage-Level Analysis**:
 - **Cross-Bin Stacking**: INDI's improvement was concentrated at stage transitions, specifically when moving from the first subgoal (base cube centered) to retrieving and stacking the second cube.
 - **Drawer Storage**: INDI showed advantages from early grasping and transfer (50.0% to 70.0%), maintaining this lead through subsequent stages. This indicates a more reliable execution across the multi-stage sequence.

#### 4.3. Analysis and Controlled Studies

- **Source of Gain**:
 - Controlled ablations on SimplerEnv-Bridge showed that teacher-derived intent supervision was the primary driver of performance gains, yielding a 14.5 pp increase over the action-only baseline.
 - Visual and textual groundings provided complementary gains, further raising success.
 - "Groundings only" and "free latent" variants performed below the action-only baseline, while "future supervision" achieved 68.0%, showing INDI's distinct advantage.
- **Functional Role of Recovered Intent**:
 - **Necessity**: Zeroing the recovered intent states at the alignment layer reduced average success from 85.5% to 45.5%, demonstrating that the recovered state is actively used by downstream action decoding.
 - **Objective Structure**: Recovered intents separated by behavior objective across real demonstrations and simulation rollouts (92.0% task purity in raw space). A classifier trained on real demonstration intents could classify simulation rollout intents with 63.9% to 80.7% accuracy, suggesting cross-domain transfer of objective information.
 - **Progress Structure**: The representation captured shared execution progress, organizing segments from different tasks into early, middle, and late stages (77.8% three-way phase prediction accuracy).
 - **Intent vs. Skill**: Intent captured the objective beyond the specific action sequence. Recovered intent similarity was higher for the same objective with dissimilar action sequences than for different objectives with similar action sequences (gap of 0.167), distinguishing intent from a mere summary of motor commands.
- **Causal Effect on Execution (Interventions)**:
 - **Objective-Specific Interventions**: Replacing task-discriminative coordinates of intent with a different objective reduced success to 45.2%, compared to 84.5% for same-objective injections and 1.0% for Gaussian corruption. This indicated that the intent pathway carries meaningful objective content.
 - **Phase-Specific Interventions**: Forcing early-stage content reduced success to 7.3%, and late-stage content to 5.5%. Rollouts under stage-forced injection exhibited behavior consistent with the injected stage (e.g., perpetual approaching for early forcing, attempting placement with an empty gripper for late forcing), demonstrating causal influence.
- **Computational Cost**: INDI introduced a modest increase in parameters (1.3% for GR00T-N1.7, 0.64% for π_0.5) and inference time (10% for GR00T-N1.7, 21% for π_0.5). Teacher inference and target construction are offline.
- **Teacher Quality**: The choice of teacher VLM significantly impacted downstream performance. Teachers with higher "angular budget" and "within-episode share" in their intent targets (indicating non-degenerate, state-resolved variation) yielded better policy performance, suggesting that a useful teacher provides a rich, dynamic signal.

In summary, INDI consistently improved performance across various VLA backbones and tasks by providing explicit behavior-level intent supervision. The recovered intent state was demonstrated to be functionally utilized by the policy, encoding both objective and progress information that causally influenced execution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Intention Distillation (INDI) as a framework that introduces behavior-level intent supervision to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, marking a conceptual shift from merely imitating demonstrated actions to explicitly modeling the local objectives underlying those actions. The significance and potential impact of this work are multifaceted:

1. **Enhanced Policy Performance and Generalization**: The consistent performance gains observed across diverse simulation benchmarks (SimplerEnv-Bridge, RoboCasa Kitchen) and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate that explicit intent modeling directly improves robot policy effectiveness. The improvements under held-out objects and distractor conditions suggest enhanced out-of-distribution generalization, indicating that understanding *what* to achieve makes policies more robust to variations in *how* to achieve it or in the environment. This could lead to more reliable and adaptable robot systems in varied real-world scenarios.

2. **Richer Internal Representations**: INDI induces an internal representation within the action decoder that functions as a behavior-level intent. Analyses confirmed that this recovered latent state encodes both the behavior objective (e.g., differentiating between tasks like "spoon on towel" and "carrot on plate") and execution progress (e.g., distinguishing early, middle, and late stages of manipulation) in a disentangled manner. This provides a more semantically meaningful internal state compared to traditional action-only or future-state-only supervision.

3. **Improved Temporal Coherence of Behavior**: By making the policy aware of the local objective, INDI can lead to more organized and coherent long-horizon behaviors. The stage-level analysis in real-world tasks, showing gains during stage transitions (Cross-Bin Stacking) and sustained reliability across multi-stage sequences (Drawer Storage), supports this. This is crucial for complex tasks requiring sequential sub-goals and precise coordination.

4. **Causal Influence on Execution**: The controlled interventions, where modifying the objective- or phase-discriminative components of the recovered intent causally altered closed-loop robot behavior, highlight the functional importance of this representation. This demonstrates that INDI not only encourages the learning of intent but also ensures that this intent actively governs the policy's actions. This provides a basis for potentially steerable policies, where external signals could modulate intent to guide robot behavior.

5. **Efficiency in Data Utilization**: The achievement of 70.3% average success on RoboCasa Kitchen with only 100 demonstrations per task, nearly matching the GR00T-N1.7 baseline trained with 3,000 demonstrations, suggests that intent supervision can lead to more sample-efficient learning. By distilling higher-level semantic information, policies may require fewer raw demonstrations to learn complex skills.

6. **Guidance for Future VLA Development**: The work identifies behavior-level intent as a "missing supervision target," suggesting a valuable direction for future VLA research. It provides a blueprint for integrating cognitive understanding (intent) with physical action, moving beyond pure imitation. The findings regarding teacher VLM quality underscore the importance of physically-grounded knowledge in the teacher for effective intent distillation.

**Potential Impacts:**
- **More Intelligent and Intuitive Robots**: Robots equipped with an explicit understanding of intent could operate more intelligently, gracefully handling unforeseen circumstances by adjusting their execution while adhering to the intended objective.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction**: A robot that understands the *purpose* behind its actions could engage in more intuitive interactions with humans, for instance, by explaining its current objective or accepting high-level intent-based corrections.
- **Advanced Planning and Reasoning**: Intent could serve as a compact, abstract interface for higher-level planning systems, allowing robots to construct and execute more complex, multi-step plans.
- **Foundational for Embodied AI**: By integrating semantic understanding directly into the action generation process, INDI contributes to building more capable and robust embodied AI systems.

**Limitations and Future Work (as noted by the authors):**
- **Teacher VLM Dependence**: INDI's notion of intent is bounded by the teacher VLM's understanding. Future work could explore mechanisms for validating or revising intent against execution outcomes or acquiring intent through the policy’s own interaction.
- **Recovered vs. Chosen Intent**: The current policy recovers a deterministic intent. Future extensions could allow the policy to maintain a distribution over objectives, hold an intent across a rollout, or accept intent-based corrections from external sources (e.g., a human or a planner).
- **Single-Window, Successful Demonstrations**: Intent is derived from single segments of successful demonstrations. Future work could incorporate hierarchical intent and distillation from unsuccessful or corrected episodes, which often more explicitly convey objectives and failure modes.

Overall, INDI represents a significant step towards enabling robots to not just perform actions, but to understand and act with a semantic purpose, thereby improving their robustness, generality, and potential for complex, intelligent behavior.
