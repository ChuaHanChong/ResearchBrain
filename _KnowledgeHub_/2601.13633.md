# Scaling Test-time Inference for Visual Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NVIDIA, MIT, Oxford, and Tsinghua University developed EGM, a method that enables smaller Visual Language Models to achieve state-of-the-art visual grounding performance, including amodal grounding, by strategically scaling test-time computation. This approach allows 8B parameter models to outperform 235B parameter counterparts while being nearly six times faster, making high-performance VLMs more deployable.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art visual grounding VLMs are often too large and computationally demanding for practical deployment on edge devices or in latency-sensitive applications.
- Smaller VLMs struggle with visual grounding tasks involving complex linguistic prompts due to limitations in their language understanding and multi-modal reasoning capabilities.
- Current VLMs lack a robust ability to perform amodal grounding, which requires identifying objects' complete shapes, including occluded parts, based on natural language.

## Method

- The Efficient visual Grounding language Models (EGM) method scales ==test-time computation== by enabling small VLMs to generate ==multi-modal reasoning traces==, rather than increasing model size.
- A two-stage training paradigm is employed: ==Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)== leveraging proprietary VLM-generated reasoning traces, followed by ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)== specifically targeting challenging examples.
- A novel data curation pipeline generates high-quality reasoning traces for both vanilla and amodal grounding tasks, explicitly detailing how to locate and perceive occluded objects.

## Results

- EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B achieved **9**1.4%** IoU** on the RefCOCO benchmark, outperforming the much larger Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct (**9**0.5%** IoU**) while being **5.9 times faster**.
- The method consistently boosted vanilla visual grounding accuracy across various Qwen3-VL and InternVL models, showing improvements of up to **+6.0 percentage points** for smaller models.
- EGM demonstrated strong capabilities in the novel amodal grounding task, with EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B achieving **7**3.9%** amodal accuracy**, comparable to or surpassing larger baseline models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary performance gap between small and large VLMs in visual grounding stems from differences in language understanding and reasoning abilities, rather than inherent visual processing limitations.
> - Explicitly generating more reasoning tokens with a smaller model during inference is a more efficient strategy for achieving high-performance visual grounding compared to directly running a massive VLM.
> - Integrating targeted reasoning patterns through SFT and RL, supported by detailed generated data, can effectively teach VLMs to perform both standard and complex amodal grounding tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the rapidly evolving field of Visual Language Models (VLMs), specifically addressing challenges in visual grounding. VLMs, including both proprietary models like GPT, Gemini, and Claude, and open-source models such as QwenVL, InternVL, and LLaVA, have demonstrated capabilities across multiple modalities. A common architectural approach for many of these models involves a Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder that processes images, followed by an MLP projector that maps visual features to a Large Language Model (LLM).

Visual grounding is a fundamental VLM capability that involves precisely locating target objects within an image based on a natural language description. State-of-the-art visual grounding VLMs, such as InternVL-3.5 and Qwen3-VL, typically achieve high performance through large model sizes. This size, however, presents obstacles for deployment, particularly on edge systems with constrained computational resources, and results in slow inference speeds. Smaller VLM variants, while more deployable, generally exhibit inferior grounding performance.

Beyond standard visual grounding, this research introduces amodal grounding to VLMs. Amodal grounding requires models to predict not only the visible parts of an object but also its occluded or hidden portions, reflecting human perception of complete objects. While amodal completion has gained attention in computer vision, its application and performance within VLMs, even advanced proprietary ones, had not been extensively explored prior to this work.

The paper also leverages advancements in reasoning capabilities within LLMs. Recent research has shown that structured reasoning traces, often combined with reinforcement learning (RL) techniques like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), can significantly enhance an LLM's ability to solve complex problems like mathematical reasoning or code generation. This study applies a similar two-stage Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) and RL paradigm to imbue VLMs with multi-modal reasoning skills, extending the benefits of explicit reasoning to visual grounding tasks. By focusing on enhancing reasoning in smaller models, the authors aim to bridge the performance gap with larger models while improving efficiency, a critical area for practical VLM deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the efficiency and performance of visual grounding in Visual Language Models, particularly for smaller models, to make them more suitable for deployment in real-world applications such as autonomous driving and robotics.

The motivation for this work stems from several observed limitations in current state-of-the-art VLMs:

1. **Deployment Challenges of Large VLMs**: Current top-performing visual grounding VLMs are characterized by their substantial model sizes. While these large models achieve high accuracy, their computational demands render them difficult to deploy on resource-constrained edge systems (e.g., NVIDIA Jetson Thor, Spark) and lead to prolonged inference times. This constitutes a significant barrier to their practical application in scenarios requiring real-time processing and limited hardware.

2. **Performance Deficiencies of Small VLMs**: Smaller versions of VLMs, which are more amenable to edge deployment, generally exhibit inferior visual grounding performance compared to their larger counterparts. This performance gap often necessitates a trade-off between model size (and thus deployability) and accuracy.

3. **Root Cause Analysis of Performance Gap**: The authors conducted an investigation into why small VLMs underperform. By examining the scaling trends within model families like Qwen, they observed that the visual encoders across different-sized VLMs tend to be consistent, while the primary difference lies in the size of their constituent LLMs. This led to the hypothesis that the performance disparity in visual grounding primarily originates from differences in *text understanding capabilities* of the LLM component, rather than limitations in visual information processing by the ViT.

4. **Empirical Validation of "Complex Prompt" Failures**: To substantiate their hypothesis, the researchers visualized failure cases of a small VLM (InternVL-3-8B). They identified a recurring pattern, termed "COMPLEX-PROMPT" failures, where the model struggled with semantically intricate text queries, especially when multiple similar candidates were present in the image. Examples included prompts involving complex spatial relationships, ordinal numbers, or compositional reasoning. A quantitative analysis using proprietary VLMs (GPT-4, GPT-5, Gemini-3-Pro) confirmed that "COMPLEX-PROMPT" accounted for a substantial proportion (an average of 52.8%) of failure cases in small VLMs. Critically, these "COMPLEX-PROMPT" failures were observed to decrease as model size increased, further supporting the idea that enhanced language understanding could resolve them.

5. **Addressing the Gap with Extended Computation**: This insight motivated the central objective: to mitigate the performance gap by enhancing the text understanding abilities of small VLMs through extended test-time computation. Instead of relying on a single, direct prediction, the approach encourages the VLM to generate a detailed reasoning process before producing the final grounding results. This reasoning process is intended to provide the necessary multi-modal context and processing depth to resolve complex linguistic instructions, similar to how larger LLMs process complex text.

6. **Introducing Amodal Grounding**: To further validate the generality and robustness of their proposed method, the authors set an additional objective: to extend their approach to a novel and more challenging task, amodal grounding, within the VLM context. This task requires predicting both visible and occluded parts of objects, addressing a critical aspect of real-world perception not yet mastered by VLMs.

In summary, the key objective is to develop "Efficient visual Grounding language Models" (EGM) that equip small VLMs with sophisticated multi-modal reasoning, thereby enabling them to match or exceed the grounding performance of much larger models while significantly improving inference efficiency and deployment feasibility across various grounding tasks, including novel amodal scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of EGM involves equipping small VLMs with multi-modal reasoning capabilities through a two-stage training paradigm: Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT) followed by Reinforcement Learning (RL). This pipeline aims to teach the models to generate detailed reasoning processes before producing grounding results, thereby enhancing their understanding of complex prompts.

#### 3.1. SFT Data Curation

The SFT stage focuses on teaching the model to learn reasoning patterns for grounding tasks.

- **Vanilla Grounding Data Curation**:
 - Existing off-the-shelf vanilla grounding datasets, such as RefCOCO, containing images, textual queries, and ground-truth bounding boxes, are utilized.
 - For each sample, a proprietary VLM (Φ) is used to generate a detailed reasoning trace (z_i). This trace explains how to accurately locate the target object, particularly when distinguishing it from similar objects based on the text prompt.
 - The original sample (image, text query, GT bbox) is paired with its generated reasoning trace to create the SFT training dataset. An example includes reasoning about distinct features, like a "vase with handle" by focusing on the handle.
 - The dataset statistics for vanilla grounding indicate 575,208 samples across 24,407 images.

- **Amodal Grounding Data Curation**:
 - As no direct training data with both text prompts and amodal bounding boxes existed, a multi-step process was developed.
 - **Prompt Generation**: A proprietary VLM is used to generate detailed, RefCOCO-style text descriptions for objects in existing amodal segmentation datasets (e.g., COCO-Amodal, TAO-Amodal), which originally only provide short category names and amodal masks/bboxes.
 - **Prompt Verification**: A verification step is performed where the generated text prompt, the object's amodal bounding box, and the image are fed back to the proprietary VLM. The VLM verifies if the prompt uniquely and accurately specifies the target object. Approximately 50% of generated samples were filtered out during this stage to remove noise.
 - **Reasoning Generation**: Once reliable text prompts are obtained, the same proprietary VLM pipeline is used to generate reasoning processes. For amodal grounding, this reasoning is more elaborate, encompassing:
 1. Identification of object(s) causing occlusion.
 2. Description of the object's complete unoccluded shape.
 3. Instructions on direction(s) and magnitude (e.g., "significantly," "moderately") to extend visible parts to recover the complete object. An example involves reasoning for a "tiger behind a fallen log," detailing the log as the occluder and the need to extend downward for the hidden parts.
 - The curated amodal grounding dataset with reasoning consists of 23,698 samples across 15,798 images.

#### 3.2. RL Data Curation

The RL stage aims to refine the model's grounding capability, focusing on challenging yet learnable instances.

- **Vanilla Grounding RL Data Curation**:
 - A subset of the vanilla grounding training dataset is selected based on "learnability." Given a "learner" model (M_θ) and a "reference" model (M_ref, a strong, pre-trained VLM like InternVL-3.5-241B), learnability for each sample (d_i) is defined as l_i = S_ref,i - S_θ,i, where S indicates grounding success (IoU > 0.5).
 - A "learnable" sample set (D_learnable) is formed by instances where the learner model fails (S_θ,i = 0) but the reference model succeeds (S_ref,i = 1), meaning l_i > 0.
 - To prevent overfitting to hard samples, an equal-sized set of "easy" samples (D_easy), where the learner model succeeds, is uniformly sampled from the original dataset.
 - The final RL training dataset is the union: D = D_learnable ∪ D_easy.
- **Amodal Grounding RL Data Curation**:
 - Due to the comparatively limited size of the curated amodal dataset, the entire amodal grounding dataset (D_T) is used for RL training to maximize data utility.

#### 3.3. Training Pipeline and Reward

- **Training Pipeline**:
 - The base VLM first undergoes SFT using the curated reasoning data.
 - Subsequently, the model is trained with reinforcement learning using the VeRL framework, employing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a token-level mean loss.
 - KL divergence penalty and entropy regularization are incorporated into the GRPO objective to encourage exploration and prevent mode collapse during reasoning generation.
- **Reward Function**:
 - The reward (r_i) for each generated output is a weighted combination of the Intersection-over-Union (IoU) between the predicted bounding box and the ground truth, and the grounding success (S_θ,i, defined as 1 if IoU > 0.5, else 0). The weighting hyper-parameter α is set to 0.5.
 - r_i = α IoU_θ,i + (1 - α) S_θ,i.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Models**: The method is applied to existing open-source VLMs from the InternVL-3 family (1B, 2B, 8B) and Qwen3-VL-Thinking family (2B, 4B, 8B). EGM- prefix denotes models fine-tuned with this method.
- **SFT Configuration**: Learning rate of 1e-5, 1 epoch, batch size of 128, trained on 8 A100 GPUs.
- **RL Configuration**: Learning rate of 3e-6, 5 epochs, batch size of 256. Specific KL and entropy coefficients (β, γ) are used for QwenVL (β=0.005, γ=0.0) and InternVL (β=0.0, γ=0.01) series. InternVL-3.5-241B served as the reference model for RL data curation.

#### 3.5. Evaluation Benchmarks and Metrics

- **Vanilla Grounding**: Evaluated on the standard RefCOCO benchmarks (eight test splits). The metric is "accuracy," defined as the proportion of grounding successes (IoU > 0.5).
- **Amodal Grounding**: A new benchmark is created using the val and test splits of the COCO-Amodal dataset, paired with the generated text prompts. The same accuracy metric (IoU > 0.5) is used.
- **Efficiency**: Measured by the average inference latency (in milliseconds) per sample on a 10% subset of RefCOCO, reported as total GPU latency on L20 GPUs. Identical inference hyperparameters are used for all models.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the effectiveness and efficiency of the EGM approach across various model sizes, families, and tasks.

#### 4.1. Vanilla Grounding Performance

- **Consistent Performance Improvement**: EGM consistently improves the visual grounding accuracy of small VLMs. For instance, on the RefCOCO benchmark, EGM boosted Qwen3-VL-2B-Thinking by +6.0%, 4B-Thinking by +3.8%, and 8B-Thinking by +3.6% in average accuracy. Similarly, InternVL-3-1B saw a +5.2% improvement, 2B by +1.7%, and 8B by +1.1%.
- **Mitigation of Performance Gap**: EGM enables smaller models to achieve performance comparable to or surpassing much larger models. EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B achieved 91.4 IoU, exceeding Qwen3-VL-235B's 90.5 IoU. EGM-2B and EGM-4B models also outperformed the Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking baseline.
- **Role of Reasoning**: An ablation study (EGM w/o R) demonstrated that removing the reasoning process significantly reduced performance, confirming the necessity of explicit reasoning for these gains. For example, Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking with EGM without reasoning achieved 88.6% accuracy, while full EGM reached 91.4%.
- **Performance on Hard Samples**: A fine-grained analysis revealed that EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B resolved 49.8% of cases where the baseline model had an IoU below 0.5, converting them to successful groundings (IoU ≥ 0.5).

#### 4.2. Amodal Grounding Performance

- **Significant Gains in Amodal Grounding**: EGM consistently improved performance on the novel amodal grounding task. InternVL-3 models saw gains of +7.4% (1B), +6.4% (2B), and +11.5% (8B). Qwen3-VL models experienced improvements of +6.9% (2B), +2.3% (4B), and +2.5% (8B).
- **Real-world Application Potential**: Qualitative examples in autonomous driving ("ghost probe" scenario, occluded pedestrian/car) and robotics (occluded pen) demonstrated EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B's ability to reason about occlusion and accurately predict complete object shapes, outperforming baseline Qwen3-VL-8B and Qwen3-VL-235B models.

#### 4.3. Efficiency Comparison

- **Substantial Efficiency Improvement**: EGM models achieved significant efficiency gains while maintaining or improving accuracy. EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B, which achieved 91.4 IoU, operated 5.9 times faster than Qwen3-VL-235B (90.5 IoU), with respective latencies of 737ms vs. 4320ms.
- **Broad Efficiency Benefits**: EGM-Qwen3-VL-4B and 8B models both surpassed the 235B models in accuracy and were over 5 times faster. EGM-Qwen3-VL-2B also outperformed several larger models (e.g., Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking, 8B-Instruct) while exhibiting lower GPU latency.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Comparison

- Qualitative examples illustrated EGM's ability to correctly ground objects in scenarios with complex textual descriptions where baseline models failed. For instance, EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B correctly identified "girl third from right front row" or "chair next to striped chair" by generating reasoning steps involving counting or spatial relationship analysis. In amodal grounding, EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B accurately predicted the full shape of an object like "pink paper partially covered by a cellphone" by reasoning about the occluding object and necessary extensions. These detailed reasoning processes were more concise and effective than those from larger "Thinking" models, which often included redundant information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The EGM framework introduces a method for improving the efficiency and robustness of visual grounding in Visual Language Models, with several implications for research and application.

**Enhanced Efficiency and Deployment:** A primary significance of this work is its contribution to making advanced visual grounding capabilities more accessible and practical. By enabling smaller VLMs (e.g., 8B parameters) to achieve or surpass the performance of significantly larger models (e.g., 235B parameters) while offering substantially faster inference speeds (e.g., 5.9x faster), EGM addresses a critical bottleneck for VLM deployment. This efficiency allows for the integration of sophisticated visual grounding into resource-constrained edge systems prevalent in domains like autonomous driving, robotics, and mobile devices, where real-time processing and minimal latency are crucial.

**Improved Multi-modal Reasoning:** The research highlights and addresses a key limitation of smaller VLMs: their reduced ability to comprehend complex textual prompts in multi-modal contexts. By explicitly training models with detailed reasoning traces via SFT and then refining this capability with RL, EGM enhances the language understanding and compositional reasoning skills of VLMs. This structured approach to reasoning allows models to deconstruct intricate instructions, identify key distinguishing features, and resolve ambiguities that previously led to grounding failures.

**Advancement in Amodal Perception for VLMs:** The introduction and successful application of EGM to amodal grounding marks a notable advancement. Amodal grounding, the ability to predict both visible and occluded parts of objects, is a critical component for comprehensive scene understanding, particularly in dynamic environments. By demonstrating that VLMs can be equipped with this capability through tailored data curation and training, this work opens new avenues for research into more human-like perception in AI systems. The qualitative results in autonomous driving and robotics scenarios underscore the potential for EGM to enable more robust object awareness, for instance, by anticipating the full extent of partially visible objects.

**Generalizable Training Paradigm:** The two-stage SFT-RL training paradigm, coupled with strategic data curation, demonstrates a generalizable method for enhancing VLM capabilities. The consistent performance improvements observed across different model families (InternVL, Qwen3-VL) and various model sizes suggest that this approach can be broadly applied to imbue existing or future small VLMs with sophisticated reasoning skills for diverse visual-language tasks.

**Contribution to Benchmarking and Future Research:** By curating and evaluating a new amodal grounding benchmark for VLMs, this work also provides a valuable resource for the research community. This new benchmark can facilitate further development and comparison of methods aimed at improving amodal perception in visual-language understanding.

In conclusion, EGM's ability to achieve high-performance visual grounding with improved efficiency and enhanced reasoning, including novel amodal capabilities, has the potential to accelerate the practical adoption of VLMs in real-world applications requiring efficient and robust visual-language interaction.
