# Scaling Test-time Inference for Visual Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NVIDIA, MIT, Oxford, and Tsinghua University developed EGM, a method that enables smaller Visual Language Models to achieve state-of-the-art visual grounding performance, including amodal grounding, by strategically scaling test-time computation. This approach allows 8B parameter models to outperform 235B parameter counterparts while being nearly six times faster, making high-performance VLMs more deployable.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art visual grounding VLMs are often too large and computationally demanding for practical deployment on edge devices or in latency-sensitive applications.
- Smaller VLMs struggle with visual grounding tasks involving complex linguistic prompts due to limitations in their language understanding and multi-modal reasoning capabilities.
- Current VLMs lack a robust ability to perform amodal grounding, which requires identifying objects' complete shapes, including occluded parts, based on natural language.

## Method

- The Efficient visual Grounding language Models (EGM) method scales ==test-time computation== by enabling small VLMs to generate ==multi-modal reasoning traces==, rather than increasing model size.
- A two-stage training paradigm is employed: ==Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)== leveraging proprietary VLM-generated reasoning traces, followed by ==Reinforcement Learning (RL)== specifically targeting challenging examples.
- A novel data curation pipeline generates high-quality reasoning traces for both vanilla and amodal grounding tasks, explicitly detailing how to locate and perceive occluded objects.

## Results

- EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B achieved **9**1.4%** IoU** on the RefCOCO benchmark, outperforming the much larger Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Instruct (**9**0.5%** IoU**) while being **5.9 times faster**.
- The method consistently boosted vanilla visual grounding accuracy across various Qwen3-VL and InternVL models, showing improvements of up to **+6.0 percentage points** for smaller models.
- EGM demonstrated strong capabilities in the novel amodal grounding task, with EGM-Qwen3-VL-8B achieving **7**3.9%** amodal accuracy**, comparable to or surpassing larger baseline models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The primary performance gap between small and large VLMs in visual grounding stems from differences in language understanding and reasoning abilities, rather than inherent visual processing limitations.
> - Explicitly generating more reasoning tokens with a smaller model during inference is a more efficient strategy for achieving high-performance visual grounding compared to directly running a massive VLM.
> - Integrating targeted reasoning patterns through SFT and RL, supported by detailed generated data, can effectively teach VLMs to perform both standard and complex amodal grounding tasks.
