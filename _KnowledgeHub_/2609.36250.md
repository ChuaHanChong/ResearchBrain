# Action Chunking Proximal Policy Optimization with Feedback Correction

> [!summary] Summary
> ACPPO brings action chunking to PPO. A chunked actor predicts short action sequences, while the critic stays a standard state-value function and both the advantage and the importance ratio are computed per chunk, so no chunked Q-function is needed. ACPPO-Corr adds a regularized stepwise feedback corrector, trained jointly with the planner, that adjusts planned actions within each chunk. Across 25 IsaacGym and Bi-DexHands tasks it beats PPO by 30.4% in normalized IQM.

## Problem

- Most action-chunking RL methods learn chunked Q-functions over whole action sequences, which grow hard to fit, overestimate, and destabilize as action dimension and chunk length increase.
- Running predicted chunks open-loop removes feedback inside the chunk, so actions cannot adapt to contact changes, stochasticity, or perturbations in contact-rich dexterous manipulation.
- Existing residual or reactive fixes correct a frozen pretrained chunked policy and depend on demonstrations or offline pretraining, both costly to obtain for high-DoF online control.

## Method

- **ACPPO**: a ==chunked planner== outputs h-step action means at each chunk boundary, while the critic remains a state-only V(s) trained with the standard stepwise ==GAE== value loss.
- Chunked advantage and chunked ==importance ratio==: one h-step advantage at chunk start (an h-step head plus a GAE tail) and a clipped joint-chunk ratio over the valid chunk prefix.
- **ACPPO-Corr** corrector: a stepwise network adds a state-dependent correction and an exploration scale at every step, trained jointly with the planner using ==stop-gradient== decoupled updates, an L2 correction penalty, and a planner warm-up freeze.

## Results

- ACPPO-Corr improves final normalized IQM over PPO by **30.4%** across **25** tasks, while open-loop ACPPO and PPO-Repeat fall below PPO and QC-FQL and SAC trail further.
- Relative to PPO, ACPPO-Corr scores **1.33** IQM on frequency-sensitive tasks versus **0.61** for PPO-Repeat, and **1.24** on frequency-neutral tasks, landing in the top group on **9/11** and **10/10**.
- At h=16 ACPPO-Corr keeps a **1.21** score ratio to PPO versus **0.68** for ACPPO and **0.29** for QC-FQL. Moderate h=4 works best, and h=1 barely beats PPO.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal abstraction can live entirely in the actor and the advantage estimate, so a plain state-value critic suffices and avoids the scaling problems of chunked Q-functions.
> - Open-loop chunking hurts contact-rich control. Pairing low-frequency chunk planning with high-frequency closed-loop correction recovers reactivity and turns chunking into a net gain.
> - When a policy is split into a planner and a corrector, the corrector must be regularized, or it absorbs the action signal and the hierarchy collapses into a stepwise policy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

A dexterous robot hand turning a cube toward a target orientation faces a practical tension. A planned sequence can keep the hand moving coherently. Once the sequence is under way, though, a changing contact or slipping object may call for an adjustment before the next plan. Sanghyun Hahn and Jonghyun Choi keep the short action plan and add a learned correction, based on the hand's current state, at every step. In one illustrative simulated rollout, the corrected method reaches the target orientation, while the version without the corrector drops the cube.

Rollout snapshots of PPO, ACPPO, and ACPPO-Corr on AllegroHand. ACPPO-Corr reaches the target orientation fastest, ACPPO drops the cube, and PPO requires 128 additional environment interactions to reach the target.

This is one example from a single simulated task. It does not prove a general speedup. The paper is "Action Chunking Proximal Policy Optimization with Feedback Correction" (NeurIPS 2026; arXiv paper).

### 2. A plan that can still react

The idea is called action chunking. At a chunk boundary, the policy predicts several future actions rather than choosing only one. The authors identify two obstacles for action chunking in high-dimensional robotics. The first is learning a value function over whole action sequences. The second is losing feedback while a sequence runs. Their answer pairs a chunk planner with stepwise correction, in fully online, on-policy PPO.

In ACPPO, a planner proposes the chunk. The standard PPO state-value critic remains state-only, so it does not estimate a Q-value for the full action sequence. ACPPO-Corr adds a corrector. At every control step, the corrector observes the current state and adds a correction to that step's planned action. It also sets the stepwise exploration scale. The planner supplies temporal structure, and the corrector restores feedback within the chunk.

The two parts are trained jointly online. A penalty on the squared correction discourages the corrector from dominating the planned action. ACPPO-Corr combines several components, so its overall result alone does not identify which component caused the gain.

### 3. What the comparisons establish

The evaluation covers 25 simulated IsaacGym and Bi-DexHands tasks, with five random seeds per task. Each task is scored in one of two ways:

- Goal-reaching tasks use final success rate.
- Progress-based tasks use episodic return.

Each score is normalized using task-specific lower and upper bounds. The aggregate measure is IQM, the mean of the middle 50% of the resulting task-and-seed scores. On this measure, ACPPO-Corr has the strongest aggregate result among the evaluated methods, a 30.4% improvement over PPO in final normalized IQM.

A skeptic could ask whether the gain comes merely from running actions less frequently. Two controls address this. PPO-Repeat repeats actions to test reduced decision frequency. ACPPO removes the corrector. Both fall below PPO on the full benchmark, while ACPPO-Corr is above it. This weakens the "reduced frequency alone" explanation. It does not isolate the corrector as the only cause, because the methods differ in more than one factor.

Left: normalized IQM over training progress for PPO, PPO-Repeat, SAC, QC-FQL, ACPPO, and ACPPO-Corr. Shaded bands are pointwise 95% seed-bootstrap intervals with tasks fixed. ACPPO-Corr's curve finishes highest. Right: final relative IQM for frequency-sensitive and frequency-neutral task subsets, with 95% seed-bootstrap intervals.

A feedback-input ablation points the same way. Replacing the current state with the chunk-start state retains 84.0% of ACPPO-Corr performance when applied only at evaluation, and 88.1% when trained from scratch. This supports the value of within-chunk feedback in these tested conditions. It does not show that feedback is the sole cause.

### 4. Contribution and limits

The contribution is pairing chunk-level structure with stepwise feedback in an online PPO setting. In this setting, a short plan need not mean open-loop execution. The authors' takeaway is conditional: action chunking can work in online PPO when short chunk-level plans are paired with closed-loop correction and a regularizer that keeps the corrector from taking over.

The evidence has clear limits. It comes from state-based simulated continuous robotics, not a real-robot or vision/tactile demonstration. ACPPO-Corr does not win every task. It is also slower than PPO in training throughput in the reported implementation. The chunked importance ratio becomes less stable as chunks grow, and the authors report increased clipping at $h=8$ and say longer chunks may need additional stabilization.
