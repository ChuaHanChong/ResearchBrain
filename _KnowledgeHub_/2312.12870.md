# The Audio-Visual Conversational Graph: From an Egocentric-Exocentric Perspective

> [!summary] Summary
> AV-CONV predicts an Audio-Visual Conversational Graph from egocentric video: a dynamic directed graph whose edges mark who is speaking to and listening to whom, covering both the camera wearer and unseen exocentric pairs. Cropped head images, multi-channel audio, and positional masks are fused by self-attention across time, subjects, and modalities, reaching 86.15% egocentric and 81.04% exocentric average accuracy.

## Problem

- Prior egocentric social-interaction work models only behaviors involving the camera wearer, never inferring speaking and listening relations among the other partners in view.
- Exocentric F-formation and conversation-group methods use surveillance or overhead cameras and detect groups as wholes, without resolving directed individual-to-individual interaction.
- Existing benchmarks like Ego4D Talking-to-Me label only partner-to-wearer speech, provide no listening labels, and no partner-to-partner annotations, blocking full graph supervision.

## Method

- **Conversational Graph** formalism: bipartite ego subgraph plus non-bipartite exo subgraph, with four independent binary edge attributes (speaking-to and listening-to, both directions) per node pair.
- Location-aware inputs: ==ResNet-18== encoders over cropped head tubes and over multi-channel spectrograms concatenated with each person's binary head mask, fused channel-wise into per-subject audio-visual features.
- **Conversational Attention** stacks ==cross-time==, ==cross-subject==, and ==global-local cross-modality self-attention==; pairwise feature concatenation feeds eight classifiers trained jointly with cross-entropy loss.

## Results

- AV-CONV beats the SAAL baseline by **4.45%** average accuracy on egocentric edges and far exceeds the ASL+Layout heuristic, reaching **83.51%** overall versus **60.40%** for that baseline.
- Attention ablation: removing all three attention types (direct concatenation) costs **4.15%** ego and **4.61%** exo accuracy; cross-time attention alone gives the single largest gain.
- Modality ablation: audio plus mask beats head plus mask by **13.72%** ego and **8.55%** exo accuracy, with all three modalities best; the model costs **53M** parameters and **25.28 GFLOPs**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Coarse spatial priors matter more than appearance: binary head-position masks paired with multi-channel audio outperform head crops, since spatial layout carries most listening-direction evidence.
> - Jointly training closely related social sub-tasks in one graph-prediction framework beats specialized single-task models, even on the task those models were designed for.
> - Latent third-party relations are recoverable from a single first-person view, but concurrent nearby speakers remain a failure mode needing higher spatial-resolution audio representations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper significantly advances the fields of egocentric vision, multi-modal human interaction analysis, and social graph modeling, particularly within the context of conversational dynamics.

**Egocentric Vision:**
Egocentric vision, concerned with understanding the world from a first-person perspective (e.g., via a head-mounted camera), has gained substantial traction with the advent of large-scale datasets like Ego4D and EPIC-Kitchens. Prior work in this domain primarily focused on behaviors or actions directly involving the camera wearer (the "ego"). This includes tasks like social behavior classification, gaze estimation, active speaker localization (ASL), and selective auditory attention localization (SAAL). While valuable, these studies offer an incomplete picture of complex social interactions. This paper pushes beyond these limitations by introducing the novel concept of "Ego-Exocentric Conversational Graph Prediction," aiming to infer not only the camera wearer's interactions but also the interactions *among* other social partners (the "exocentric" perspective) present in the scene.

**Multi-modal Human Interaction Analysis:**
Human communication is inherently multi-modal, involving visual cues (facial expressions, gestures, head poses, eye gaze) and auditory cues (speech, tone, localization of sound). The paper builds upon existing audio-visual learning techniques in egocentric vision, which have been applied to action recognition and sound source localization. However, it applies a sophisticated multi-modal fusion strategy specifically to the nuanced problem of discerning conversational roles (speaking and listening) among multiple individuals. The proposed framework acknowledges the importance of combining visual appearance, directional sound, and spatial location for a holistic understanding of social dynamics.

**Social Graph Modeling:**
The concept of a "social graph" is well-established in computational social science and recommender systems, typically dealing with static relationships or digital interactions. This research extends social graph modeling to a dynamic, real-time, multi-modal context by constructing a "Conversational Graph" that continuously reflects speaking and listening behaviors. Traditional computer vision work on "F-formations" or crowd activity detection often focuses on identifying conversation *groups* as a whole, rather than the specific, directed conversational roles between individuals within a group. This paper tackles the more granular problem of understanding individual-level conversational relationships and their temporal evolution, which is a significant step towards more sophisticated social AI.

**Cognitive Science and Theory of Mind (ToM):**
A key motivation for the exocentric perspective stems from cognitive science, specifically the concept of Theory of Mind (ToM)—the human ability to understand the mental states (beliefs, intentions, desires) of others. By inferring exocentric interactions, the model attempts to emulate a rudimentary form of ToM, allowing an AI agent (or a user in an AR/VR environment) to "understand" what other people in a conversation are doing, even if they are not directly interacting with the camera wearer. This makes the work highly relevant for developing more socially intelligent AI systems.

In summary, this research addresses a critical gap in egocentric vision by extending its scope from ego-centric to ego-exocentric interaction analysis. It leverages advanced multi-modal learning and attention mechanisms to build a dynamic social graph of conversations, drawing inspiration from cognitive science. This positions the work as a foundational contribution for building more context-aware and socially intelligent AR/VR systems and embodied AI agents.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce and solve the novel **Ego-Exocentric Conversational Graph Prediction problem**. This problem entails inferring a complete, dynamic directed graph, termed the "Audio-Visual Conversational Graph," that describes the conversational behaviors—specifically, speaking and listening—for **all social partners** present in an egocentric video, including both the camera wearer (ego) and all other individuals (exocentric).

The key motivations driving this objective are:

1. **Bridging the Gap in Egocentric Vision:** While egocentric video research has flourished, most prior work primarily focuses on the camera wearer's actions and interactions. This "ego-centric" bias limits the understanding of broader social contexts. The authors identify this as a significant limitation, as real-world social interactions involve complex dynamics among multiple individuals, not just those directly engaging with the person wearing the camera.
2. **Emulating Human Social Intelligence (Theory of Mind):** Humans possess a remarkable ability to understand the beliefs, intentions, and focus of attention of others during social interactions, a concept known as Theory of Mind (ToM). This includes perceiving who is speaking to whom, who is listening, and who is observing. The paper is motivated by the desire to endow AI systems, particularly those operating in AR/VR environments, with a similar capacity to interpret these exocentric social cues, moving beyond mere self-referential understanding.
3. **Holistic Understanding of Multi-Participant Conversations:** Conversations are rarely isolated to two individuals. Multi-speaker and multi-conversation scenarios are common and highly complex. Existing methods often tackle individual sub-tasks, such as identifying the active speaker or the camera wearer's auditory attention target. The paper aims to unify these disparate tasks into a single framework that can simultaneously and jointly predict a dense set of conversational behaviors for all participants, thereby providing a more comprehensive and accurate understanding of the entire social dynamic.
4. **Leveraging Multi-modal Cues for Richer Context:** Conversational interactions are characterized by rich audio-visual signals. Visual cues include head pose, eye gaze, facial expressions, and body gestures, while audio cues provide information about speech content, speaker identity, and sound localization. The motivation is to effectively combine these diverse, subject-specific, and global audio-visual signals to accurately infer the subtle and overt indicators of speaking and listening behaviors, recognizing that each modality contributes uniquely to the overall understanding.
5. **Addressing Challenges for Future AR/VR Systems:** With the advancement of VR/AR technology, there's a growing need for intelligent systems that can understand and respond appropriately to complex human social environments. A system that can accurately map dynamic conversational graphs from a first-person perspective would be invaluable for developing more natural, context-aware, and socially intelligent AR/VR applications, as well as for embodied AI agents.

In essence, the paper aims to move egocentric vision from a "self-centered" view to a "socially aware" view, providing a richer, multi-faceted understanding of human interactions, which is crucial for the next generation of pervasive computing.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology introduces the **Audio-Visual Conversational Attention (AV-CONV) model**, a unified multi-modal framework designed to jointly predict speaking and listening behaviors for both the camera wearer and all other social partners from egocentric videos. The approach can be broken down into three main stages: audio-visual feature extraction, conversational attention modeling, and conversation edge attribute classification.

#### 3.1. Audio-Visual Feature Extraction

The model begins by extracting rich, localized features from both the visual and audio streams of the egocentric video.

- **Speaker Location:** The foundational step involves identifying and localizing each individual in the frame. This is achieved by leveraging head bounding box annotations to crop visible faces and binary masks that preserve the spatial positions of each social partner. This mask acts as a spatial anchor for individual-specific features.
- **Head Feature Extraction (Visual):** From the cropped head image tubes, a visual encoder (a ResNet-18 backbone pre-trained on ImageNet-1K) extracts high-level visual features. These features capture appearance information such as facial expressions, head orientation, and lip movements for each individual. The model assumes a fixed maximum number of individuals (N=4, based on dataset characteristics) and uses zero-paddings for non-visible subjects to maintain consistent tensor dimensions.
- **Audio Feature Extraction:** Multi-channel audio input, comprising channel correlation features and spectrograms, provides global sound information. To make this audio information subject-specific and location-aware, each person's binary mask is sequentially concatenated with the global audio input. This combined signal is then fed into an audio encoder (also a ResNet-18 backbone) to produce audio features, capturing vocal activity and its approximate source location relative to the camera wearer.
- **Audio-Visual Feature Fusion:** The extracted visual features (Z_v) and audio features (Z_a) for each subject are then concatenated along the channel dimension, forming a combined Audio-Visual feature (Z_av). This initial fusion creates a rich, subject-specific representation that incorporates both visual appearance and localized audio information.

#### 3.2. Conversational Attention (AV-CONV)

The core of the AV-CONV model is a self-attention mechanism, adapted from the Transformer architecture, designed to disentangle and integrate correlations across multiple dimensions critical for conversational analysis. This "Conversational Attention" module (T) comprises three distinct attention layers:

- **Cross-Time Attention (T_T):** Egocentric videos are dynamic, with rapid scene changes. This attention layer applies self-attention across the temporal dimension for each spatial patch. By aggregating information from current and neighboring frames, it enriches the temporal context, making it easier to detect fleeting conversational cues and voice activities, which are inherently temporal.
- **Cross-Subject Attention (T_N):** To understand interactions *among* individuals, it's crucial for their representations to influence each other. This layer applies self-attention across subjects at corresponding spatiotemporal locations. This mechanism allows the audio-visual features of different individuals to interact and be compared, enhancing the model's ability to distinguish and predict interpersonal dynamics. For instance, it can help differentiate who is looking or speaking to whom based on relative positions and cues.
- **Global-Local Attention (T_S):** This attention layer correlates the "global" audio-positional tokens (derived from the scene-wide audio and individual masks) with the "local" head appearance tokens. This ensures a comprehensive understanding by aligning individual visual details with the broader acoustic and spatial context of the scene, improving scene awareness and semantic consistency for each feature.

These attention layers are applied sequentially, with residual connections to aggregate outputs. The final output (Z_av_tns) is an augmented, context-rich Audio-Visual feature for each individual, incorporating temporal, inter-subject, and multi-modal correlations.

#### 3.3. Conversation Edge Attributes Classification

With the refined, individual-specific Audio-Visual features (Z_av), the model proceeds to classify the edge attributes of the conversational graph.

- **Egocentric Graph (G_Ego) Prediction:** For interactions involving the camera wearer (e.g., "camera wearer speaking to person i," "person i listening to camera wearer"), the individual-specific feature Z_av for the corresponding subject is directly fed into dedicated classifiers.
- **Exocentric Graph (G_Exo) Prediction:** For interactions between any two other social partners (e.g., "person i speaking to person j"), a pairwise feature fusion step is necessary. The Z_av features of the two subjects in a pair are concatenated to generate a "pairwise feature" (Z_pair). This Z_pair is then fed into its own set of classifiers.
- **Prediction Head:** A total of eight separate binary classifiers are used, one for each type of edge attribute (e.g., e_S_c→p_i, e_L_p_i→c, e_S_p_i→p_j, e_L_p_j→p_i). Each classifier consists of a pooling layer followed by a fully connected layer to output logits. All classifiers are trained jointly using cross-entropy loss, with an additional background category to handle cases where no interaction occurs.

The entire framework is implemented in PyTorch, leveraging pre-trained ResNet-18 backbones and trained for approximately 9 epochs using an Adam optimizer. The chosen dataset, the Egocentric Concurrent Conversations Dataset, is repurposed and re-annotated to provide the necessary ground-truth labels for the complex ego-exocentric speaking and listening tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed AV-CONV model in predicting both egocentric and exocentric conversational behaviors. The evaluation utilizes classification accuracy and mean Average Precision (mAP) for each of the eight binary edge attributes, as well as average performance for egocentric and exocentric tasks.

#### 4.1. Comparison to Prior Work

Since the Ego-Exocentric Conversational Graph Prediction problem is novel, the authors devised two baselines adapted from existing literature:
- **SAAL:** Originally for selective auditory attention localization of the camera wearer.
- **ASL + Layout:** A heuristic baseline combining 3D person layout estimation with active speaker localization.

**Key Findings:**
- **Superiority of AV-CONV:** AV-CONV consistently and significantly outperforms both SAAL and ASL+Layout across all sub-tasks and metrics (accuracy and mAP).
 - For egocentric-related tasks (Ego Avg Accuracy), AV-CONV achieves 86.15%, outperforming SAAL by approximately 4.45% and ASL+Layout by a large margin (over 25%).
 - For exocentric-related tasks (Exo Avg Accuracy), AV-CONV achieves 81.04%, significantly outperforming ASL+Layout (60.40%). SAAL could not predict exocentric interactions.
- **Joint Learning Benefits:** The fact that AV-CONV outperforms SAAL even on SAAL's original task (e_L_c→p_i, with 87.80% vs 86.48% accuracy) highlights the benefit of a unified framework that learns closely related sub-tasks jointly, enabling shared knowledge and richer context.
- **Challenging Nature of the Task:** The poor performance of the heuristic ASL+Layout baseline (e.g., 60.40% Exo Avg Accuracy, 41.40% Exo Avg mAP) underscores the complexity of inferring conversational graphs purely from egocentric videos, validating the need for a sophisticated, data-driven approach like AV-CONV.

#### 4.2. Ablation Study on Conversational Attention

This study explores the contribution of the different components within the Conversational Attention module (Cross-Time T, Cross-Subject N, Global-Local S).

**Key Findings (Accuracy, Table 2):**
- **"DIRECT CONCAT" is the weakest:** Without any attention mechanisms, simply concatenating features (DIRECT CONCAT) yields the worst performance, especially on exocentric speaking tasks (67.60% for e_S_p_i→p_j), emphasizing the critical role of attention for disentangling correlations.
- **Individual Attention Benefits:** Each attention type (T, N, S) individually improves performance over DIRECT CONCAT.
 - **Cross-Time (T)** shows the largest individual gain, suggesting that aggregating temporal information is paramount for robustly detecting speech activities and general conversational context.
- **Synergistic Effect of Combined Attention:** The full AV-CONV model, leveraging all three attention mechanisms (T, N, S), achieves the best overall performance (86.15% Ego Avg, 81.04% Exo Avg). This demonstrates a synergistic effect, where complementary insights from temporal, inter-subject, and global-local correlations contribute to a comprehensive understanding. The model significantly improves exocentric speaking tasks (e.g., 75.89% for e_S_p_i→p_j) compared to DIRECT CONCAT (67.60%).

#### 4.3. Ablation Study on Input Modality

This study assesses the impact of different input signals: head images (visual), multi-channel audio, and positional binary masks.

**Key Findings (Accuracy, Table 3):**
- **Audio is Crucial for Speaking:** Using "HEAD ONLY" or "MASK ONLY" leads to significant performance degradation on speaking-related tasks, highlighting the indispensable role of audio for detecting vocal activity. Conversely, "AUDIO ONLY" performs well on egocentric speaking (88.57% for e_S_c→p_i) but struggles with other tasks, indicating its lack of specific spatial or visual context.
- **Mask (Position) Matters for Listening:** "MASK ONLY" performs comparably to "HEAD ONLY" but surprisingly outperforms it on listening sub-tasks, suggesting that the abstract representation of people's relative spatial positions is highly informative for inferring listening behaviors, which often involve orientation towards a speaker.
- **Audio + Mask is Powerful:** Combining "AUDIO+MASK" (which provides global audio activity and an abstract visual prior of relative positions) yields strong results, notably outperforming "HEAD+MASK" by a large margin (e.g., 85.03% Ego Avg vs 71.31%). This emphasizes that multi-channel audio, when localized by masks, is highly effective, especially for speaking-related tasks.
- **Full Multi-Modality is Optimal:** The complete AV-CONV model, incorporating all three modalities (head images, audio, and positional masks), achieves the highest performance. This underscores the synergistic contribution of each modality: visual cues for appearance and fine-grained expressions, audio for vocal activity and directionality, and spatial masks for relative positioning.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Results

Visualizations of the predicted conversational graphs (Figure 5 and 7) illustrate the model's ability to accurately capture complex, dynamic interactions in multi-speaker, multi-conversation scenarios. Even with visually similar subjects and varying layouts, the model successfully infers who is speaking and listening. A typical failure case highlights a limitation in distinguishing speakers when they are physically close and speaking concurrently, suggesting areas for future improvement in spatial audio representation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a highly significant advancement in the understanding and modeling of human social interactions from a first-person perspective, with substantial potential impact across several domains.

**1. Pioneering the Ego-Exocentric Perspective:**
The most significant contribution is the introduction and successful tackling of the Ego-Exocentric Conversational Graph Prediction problem. By moving beyond solely camera-wearer-centric analysis, this work opens up new avenues for understanding the full complexity of social scenes. This shift is crucial for developing AI systems that can interpret a broader range of human behaviors, not just those directed at them. It’s a step towards achieving a more human-like "Theory of Mind" in artificial intelligence.

**2. Unified Multi-modal Framework for Social AI:**
The AV-CONV model provides a robust and unified framework for jointly predicting multiple, inter-related conversational behaviors. This holistic approach, fusing multi-channel audio, visual appearance, and spatial context through a sophisticated attention mechanism, sets a new standard for multi-modal social signal processing. Its ability to learn deep correlations across time, subjects, and modalities is key to its superior performance, especially in challenging multi-speaker, multi-conversation environments.

**3. Enhancing AR/VR and Embodied AI:**
The direct application of this research lies in advancing AR/VR technologies and embodied AI agents. Imagine an AR headset that can not only understand your interactions but also discern the conversational dynamics of everyone else in the room. This could enable:
- **Context-aware notifications:** Only alert you when a conversation directly involves you or if someone is attempting to get your attention.
- **Intelligent assistants:** Agents that can join or assist in conversations by understanding the current flow and participant roles.
- **Naturalistic human-robot interaction:** Robots that can seamlessly integrate into social groups by accurately interpreting conversational cues.
- **Enhanced social experiences:** More immersive and responsive virtual environments that mirror real-world social complexities.

**4. Advancing Cognitive Computing:**
By demonstrating an AI's capacity to infer exocentric social interactions, the work contributes to the broader field of cognitive computing. It provides a computational model for understanding complex human social cognition, particularly aspects related to attention, intention, and conversational turn-taking. This could lead to new insights into human social behavior itself.

**5. Foundational for Future Research:**
The paper lays a strong foundation for numerous future research directions:
- **Gaze Prediction and Complex Social Cues:** Extending the framework to include gaze tracking, body language analysis, and other non-verbal cues for an even richer conversational graph.
- **Dynamic Group Dynamics:** Analyzing more complex group behaviors like individuals entering or leaving conversations, forming sub-groups, or shifting attention in more fluid scenarios (e.g., free-standing conversations, walking scenarios).
- **Integration with Natural Language Processing (NLP):** Combining the conversational graph with linguistic content analysis to understand the semantic flow, topic changes, and deeper intentions within dialogues.
- **Scalability to Larger Groups:** Adapting the model to effectively handle very large social gatherings, which poses significant challenges for visual processing and inter-subject attention.
- **Real-world Deployment:** Addressing issues like robustness to varying lighting, occlusions, and diverse human appearances for practical applications.

In conclusion, this research is a significant leap forward in equipping AI with the ability to "see and hear" social interactions from a truly egocentric-exocentric perspective. It moves us closer to a future where AI systems can not only perceive but also intelligently interpret and participate in the nuanced tapestry of human social life, particularly within the immersive realities of AR/VR.
