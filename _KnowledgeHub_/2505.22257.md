# Revisiting Group Relative Policy Optimization: Insights into On-Policy and Off-Policy Training

> [!summary] Summary
> IBM Research and MIT-IBM Watson Lab researchers formalized an off-policy extension for Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), demonstrating it provides theoretical guarantees for policy improvement and achieves comparable or superior performance to on-policy GRPO on LLM alignment tasks while significantly reducing computational overhead through less frequent model updates.

## Problem

- Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and its variants like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) are inherently on-policy, requiring computationally expensive frequent model updates and sample generation for Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Training LLMs with RL, especially in distributed setups using frameworks like vLLM, incurs significant communication overhead due to continuous model updates required by on-policy methods.
- Some empirically observed benefits and heuristics in GRPO, such as performance gains from sample reuse and the utility of 'zero variance masking,' lacked formal theoretical grounding.

## Method

- Researchers formalized an ==off-policy extension of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== by introducing a distinct off-policy distribution for advantage function estimation and importance sampling.
- They derived a ==clipped surrogate objective== for off-policy GRPO from a policy improvement lower bound, analogous to PPO's clipped objective, to ensure stable optimization.
- The approach integrates a parameter to control the frequency of ==serving model updates to the vLLM server==, allowing for reduced communication costs by leveraging off-policy samples without sacrificing performance.

## Results

- Off-policy GRPO and on-policy GRPO with zero-variance masking stabilized training and improved Pass@1 on GSM8K to **50%**, compared to **45%** for the unstable on-policy baseline.
- On the Deepscaler math dataset, the proposed off-policy GRPO achieved comparable or slightly better performance (Aime24 up to **32%**, Math500 up to **87%**) while significantly reducing communication overhead by updating the serving model less frequently.
- The theoretical policy improvement guarantees and the justification for zero-variance masking were empirically validated, demonstrating a more stable and efficient training process for LLM alignment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The paper provides a rigorous theoretical lower bound for policy improvement in both on-policy and off-policy GRPO, explicitly showing how maximizing the GRPO advantage function increases expected reward.
> - Theoretical analysis justifies the 'zero variance masking' heuristic, demonstrating that samples with zero reward variance can destabilize the policy improvement lower bound, thus validating their exclusion from training.
> - The original iterative GRPO algorithm implicitly performs off-policy advantage estimation when samples are reused across multiple SGD iterations, which empirically improved performance and motivated the formal off-policy extension.
