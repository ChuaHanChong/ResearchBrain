# Revisiting Group Relative Policy Optimization: Insights into On-Policy and Off-Policy Training

> [!summary] Summary
> IBM Research and MIT-IBM Watson Lab researchers formalized an off-policy extension for Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), demonstrating it provides theoretical guarantees for policy improvement and achieves comparable or superior performance to on-policy GRPO on LLM alignment tasks while significantly reducing computational overhead through less frequent model updates.

## Problem

- Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) and its variants like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) are inherently on-policy, requiring computationally expensive frequent model updates and sample generation for Large Language Models (LLMs).
- Training LLMs with RL, especially in distributed setups using frameworks like vLLM, incurs significant communication overhead due to continuous model updates required by on-policy methods.
- Some empirically observed benefits and heuristics in GRPO, such as performance gains from sample reuse and the utility of 'zero variance masking,' lacked formal theoretical grounding.

## Method

- Researchers formalized an ==off-policy extension of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)== by introducing a distinct off-policy distribution for advantage function estimation and importance sampling.
- They derived a ==clipped surrogate objective== for off-policy GRPO from a policy improvement lower bound, analogous to PPO's clipped objective, to ensure stable optimization.
- The approach integrates a parameter to control the frequency of ==serving model updates to the vLLM server==, allowing for reduced communication costs by leveraging off-policy samples without sacrificing performance.

## Results

- Off-policy GRPO and on-policy GRPO with zero-variance masking stabilized training and improved Pass@1 on GSM8K to **50%**, compared to **45%** for the unstable on-policy baseline.
- On the Deepscaler math dataset, the proposed off-policy GRPO achieved comparable or slightly better performance (Aime24 up to **32%**, Math500 up to **87%**) while significantly reducing communication overhead by updating the serving model less frequently.
- The theoretical policy improvement guarantees and the justification for zero-variance masking were empirically validated, demonstrating a more stable and efficient training process for LLM alignment.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The paper provides a rigorous theoretical lower bound for policy improvement in both on-policy and off-policy GRPO, explicitly showing how maximizing the GRPO advantage function increases expected reward.
> - Theoretical analysis justifies the 'zero variance masking' heuristic, demonstrating that samples with zero reward variance can destabilize the policy improvement lower bound, thus validating their exclusion from training.
> - The original iterative GRPO algorithm implicitly performs off-policy advantage estimation when samples are reused across multiple SGD iterations, which empirically improved performance and motivated the formal off-policy extension.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of Reinforcement Learning (RL) and Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically within the domain of **LLM post-training and alignment**. It directly addresses the challenges of training LLMs to better align with human preferences or exhibit specific reasoning capabilities through methods like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLfVR).

**Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)** [Schulman et al., 2015, 2017] is a cornerstone algorithm in modern RL, widely recognized for its stability and empirical performance. However, PPO is inherently an **on-policy** algorithm, meaning it requires fresh samples from the *current* policy for each update, leading to inefficiencies in data collection and training, especially for complex models like LLMs where generating samples is computationally expensive.

**Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, introduced by Shao et al., emerged as a PPO variant tailored for LLM alignment. A key innovation of GRPO is its ability to **alleviate the need for a separate critic network** (which estimates value functions) by instead estimating the advantage function using a standardized reward computed from a "group" of Monte-Carlo samples. This simplifies the training pipeline and can be more efficient for LLMs when coupled with efficient inference frameworks like vLLM. GRPO's success in training models like Deepseek R1 has led to its widespread adoption in the open-source community.

The current paper builds upon GRPO by exploring its **off-policy extension**. The motivation for this comes from two main observations:
1. **Recent advancements in off-policy PPO:** A growing body of work (e.g., Generalized PPO [Queeney et al., 2021], Transductive off-Policy PPO [Gan et al., 2024], Off-Policy PPO [Meng et al., 2023]) has focused on adapting PPO to leverage **off-policy samples** (i.e., samples generated by older policies or from a replay buffer) to improve **sample efficiency, training stability, and memory usage**. This work seeks to bring similar benefits to GRPO.
2. **Implicit off-policy behavior in original GRPO:** A recent analysis [Mroueh, 2025] revealed that the iterative GRPO algorithm, as originally proposed, already exhibits an implicit off-policy estimation of the advantage function when samples are reused across multiple SGD iterations (`μ > 1`). This empirical observation suggested potential benefits of explicit off-policy formulation.

Therefore, this paper aims to formally adapt GRPO to an off-policy setting, provide theoretical guarantees for policy improvement, and demonstrate its practical benefits. It contributes to the broader landscape by:
- **Enhancing the efficiency of LLM alignment:** By enabling off-policy training for GRPO, it reduces the computational burden of frequent model updates, particularly crucial for large models requiring multi-GPU serving and tensor parallelism.
- **Providing theoretical grounding for empirical observations:** It rigorously analyzes the conditions under which GRPO (both on-policy and off-policy) guarantees reward improvement, shedding light on previously observed benefits like success rate amplification and validating heuristics like zero-variance masking (seen in DAPO [Yu et al., 2025]).
- **Bridging GRPO with the broader off-policy RL literature:** It establishes formal connections between off-policy GRPO and other off-policy PPO variants and actor-critic methods (e.g., ACER [Wang et al., 2016], P3O [Fakoor et al., 2020]), contributing to a more unified understanding of policy optimization algorithms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **revisit Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) in both on-policy and off-policy optimization regimes** to enhance its stability, sample efficiency, and reduce computational overhead in the context of Large Language Model (LLM) post-training.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Learning from Off-Policy PPO Advancements:** Recent developments in off-policy Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) have demonstrated significant improvements in training stability, sampling efficiency, and memory usage. The authors are motivated to apply these learnings to GRPO, an algorithm that has gained traction for LLM alignment due to its critic-free nature.
- **Addressing Implicit Off-Policy Behavior in GRPO:** A recent analysis of GRPO by Mroueh revealed that the original iterative GRPO algorithm implicitly performs off-policy advantage estimation when samples are reused across multiple policy updates. This empirical observation, noting improved performance in such scenarios, strongly suggests the benefit of a formalized off-policy GRPO.
- **Improving Computational Efficiency for Large Models:** In practical LLM training, especially with large models served via infrastructures like vLLM that may use tensor parallelism across multiple GPUs, frequent model updates (as typically required by on-policy methods) incur significant communication costs and latencies. By developing an effective off-policy GRPO, the authors aim to **reduce the communication burden and overhead** associated with serving the model for inference in each iteration, thereby enabling more efficient and scalable training.
- **Providing Theoretical Guarantees:** Beyond empirical improvements, the authors aim to provide a **rigorous theoretical framework** for both on-policy and off-policy GRPO. This includes:
 - Demonstrating that both on-policy and off-policy GRPO objectives lead to an improvement in the expected reward.
 - Stating conditions under which optimizing the off-policy advantage leads to policy improvement.
 - Deriving a clipped surrogate objective for off-policy GRPO, similar to those in off-policy PPO, to ensure stable optimization.
- **Validating Heuristics:** Specifically, the work aims to provide theoretical grounding for practices like "zero variance masking" (filtering out samples with fully correct or incorrect answers), which has been suggested in related work like DAPO [Yu et al., 2025].

In essence, the paper seeks to formally extend GRPO into the off-policy domain, provide theoretical backing for its effectiveness, and empirically demonstrate that this extension can achieve comparable or superior performance while offering significant practical advantages in terms of training efficiency and scalability for LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper combines a theoretical analysis of GRPO's policy improvement properties with practical algorithmic development and empirical validation.

#### 3.1. On-Policy GRPO Review and Motivation for Off-Policy Extension

The paper first reviews the standard on-policy GRPO objective from Shao et al.. GRPO is presented as a PPO variant that avoids training a separate critic network. Instead, it computes an "advantage" function by standardizing the reward `r(x,y)` using the empirical mean and standard deviation of rewards from a "group" of `G` Monte-Carlo samples, all drawn from the *current* policy `π_k`. The objective includes a KL-regularizer to keep the updated policy `π` close to a reference policy `π_ref`.

A key observation motivating the off-policy extension is that the original iterative GRPO in Shao et al. allows for multiple SGD iterations (`μ > 1`) using a fixed batch of samples from `π_k`. This inherently leads to an *off-policy* estimation of the advantage, as the policy `π` being optimized deviates from `π_k` over these `μ` iterations, while the advantage statistics remain fixed. This implicit off-policy behavior was empirically observed to improve performance [HuggingFace, 2025b] and analyzed in Mroueh as leading to "success rate amplification" for verifiable rewards.

#### 3.2. Formalizing Off-Policy GRPO

Building on these insights, the authors formally introduce off-policy GRPO. Instead of simply reusing fixed samples, they propose using a distinct *off-policy distribution* `α` (typically `π_{k-v}` for some `v > 0`) to estimate the advantage statistics and for importance sampling in the objective.
- **Definitions:**
 - Expected reward `J(π(·|x))`.
 - Mean (`μ_α,r(x)`) and standard deviation (`σ_α,r(x)`) of the reward under the off-policy `α`.
 - Off-policy GRPO advantage function `A_α(x,y) = (r(x,y) - μ_α,r(x)) / σ_α,r,ε(x)`.
 - Importance-sampled expected advantage `L_α(π(·|x)) = E_{y~α(·|x)} [ (π(y|x) / α(y|x)) * A_α(x,y) ]`.

#### 3.3. Policy Improvement Analysis

A core part of the methodology is providing theoretical guarantees for policy improvement. The authors aim to show that maximizing the expected advantage function `L_α(π)` indeed leads to an improvement in the true expected reward `J(π)`.
- **Theorem 1 (Policy Improvement Lower Bound in Off-Policy GRPO):** Assuming bounded rewards (`0 <= r <= 1`), the theorem establishes a lower bound on `J(π(·|x)) - J(π_k(·|x))` that involves `L_α(π(·|x))` and terms related to the total variation distance (`TV`) between `π`, `α`, and `π_k`.
- **Corollary 1 (On-Policy GRPO Lower Bound):** A specific case of Theorem 1 where `α = π_k`.
- **Interpretation:** The lower bound highlights that the improvement depends on a term `(1 - σ_α,r,ε(x)) / σ_α,r,ε(x)`. For verifiable (binary) rewards, this term can explode if the variance `σ_α,r(x)` approaches zero (i.e., when a policy consistently yields fully correct or incorrect answers for a given `x`). This theoretically grounds the heuristic of **"zero variance masking"** (as seen in DAPO [Yu et al., 2025]), which filters out such samples to maintain a well-behaved lower bound.

#### 3.4. Clipped Surrogate Objectives

To practically optimize the lower bound, the authors derive a **clipped surrogate objective** for off-policy GRPO, analogous to PPO's clipped objective.
- The lower bound motivates a constrained optimization problem: `max_π E_x[L_α(π(·|x))] subject to E_x[TV^2(π(·|x), α(·|x))] <= Δ^2`.
- Using Pinsker's inequality (`TV <= sqrt(1/2 KL)`), the TV constraint is relaxed to a KL divergence constraint.
- This constrained problem leads to the proposed clipped off-policy GRPO objective: `L_c_α(π(·|x)) = E_{y~α(·|x)} [ f_ε(π(y|x)/α(y|x), π_k(y|x)/α(y|x), A_α(x,y)) ]`. This formulation ensures the importance ratio `π/α` stays bounded.
- They show that setting `α = π_k` recovers the original on-policy GRPO clipped objective.
- The final objective includes a KL-regularizer: `E_x[L_c_α(π(·|x))] - βKL(π||π_ref)`.

#### 3.5. Algorithmic Implementation and Computational Considerations

The proposed method is integrated into **Algorithm 1 (Iterative GRPO)**, a modified version of Shao et al..
- **Parameters `i` and `v`:**
 - `i`: Number of SGD iterations per fixed batch of samples.
 - `v`: Frequency of updating the policy model served by the vLLM server for inference.
- **On-policy GRPO:** Corresponds to `(v=1, i=1)`, meaning the model is updated and served continuously, and each batch is used once.
- **Shao et al.'s Off-policy GRPO:** Corresponds to `(v=1, i>1)`, where the model is continuously served, but batches are reused `i` times.
- **Our Off-policy GRPO:** Corresponds to `(v>1, i=1)`, meaning the vLLM server model is updated less frequently (every `v` iterations), reducing communication overhead, while fresh samples are generated in each training iteration.
- **Computational Cost Reduction:** The primary practical benefit of `v>1` is to amortize the cost of updating the vLLM server model, which can be significant for large LLMs requiring tensor parallelism and inter-GPU/inter-node communication.

#### 3.6. Experimental Validation

The methodology includes empirical validation on two datasets:
- **GSM8K:** Ablation studies on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, evaluating Pass@1, comparing on-policy, on-policy with zero-variance masking, our off-policy, and Shao et al.'s off-policy variants.
- **Deepscaler Data:** Finetuning DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B on a larger dataset of math questions, evaluating Aime24 and Math500, comparing on-policy vs. our off-policy GRPO.

The experiments are conducted on an 8-GPU node, with one GPU dedicated to the vLLM server and the others for distributed training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several key theoretical insights and empirical results demonstrating the effectiveness and benefits of the proposed off-policy GRPO variant.

#### 4.1. Theoretical Findings

- **Policy Improvement Lower Bound:** The paper rigorously derives a lower bound on the policy improvement of the expected reward for both on-policy (Corollary 1) and off-policy (Theorem 1) GRPO. This theoretical foundation explicitly shows that maximizing the GRPO advantage function (or its clipped surrogate) leads to an increase in the true expected reward under certain conditions.
- **Justification for Zero Variance Masking:** The analysis of the lower bound reveals that a term `(1 - σ_α,r,ε(x)) / σ_α,r,ε(x)` can diverge when the reward variance `σ_α,r(x)` approaches zero (e.g., for prompts where the policy consistently gets all answers right or all wrong). This indicates that such "zero variance" samples can dominate and destabilize the lower bound. This theoretical insight provides a strong justification for **masking or filtering out these samples**, a heuristic previously suggested in works like DAPO [Yu et al., 2025] without explicit theoretical backing.
- **Clipped Surrogate Objective Derivation:** The paper demonstrates that the problem of maximizing the reward improvement can be formulated as a constrained optimization problem (constrained by KL-divergence or Total Variation distance to the off-policy `α`). This formulation naturally leads to a clipped surrogate objective for off-policy GRPO, similar to those used in off-policy PPO, ensuring stability during optimization. The original on-policy GRPO clipped objective is shown to be a special case.

#### 4.2. Empirical Results

The empirical validation was conducted on two datasets: GSM8K for ablation studies and Deepscaler data for finetuning a larger model.

**Ablation Studies on GSM8K (Qwen/Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct):**
- **On-Policy GRPO (v=1, i=1):** This standard configuration showed instability in training, converging to a maximum Pass@1 score of around 45% on the test set.
- **On-Policy GRPO with Zero Variance Masking (v=1, i=1):** By masking samples with zero reward variance, the training of on-policy GRPO was significantly **stabilized**, and the performance improved to a Pass@1 of **50%**. This directly confirms the theoretical prediction that filtering such samples is beneficial for stability and performance.
- **Our Off-Policy GRPO (v=10, i=1):** This novel configuration, where the model on the vLLM server is updated less frequently (every 10 iterations) while using fresh samples for each training batch, also **stabilized the training** and achieved an improved Pass@1 of **50%**, on par with the best on-policy variant. This demonstrates that reducing communication overhead does not sacrifice performance.
- **Shao et al.'s Off-Policy GRPO (v=1, i=10):** This variant (reusing fixed samples for 10 SGD iterations) showed slower convergence over a single epoch compared to the other methods.
- **General Observation:** Across all stable GRPO variants, resetting the reference policy (`π_ref`) to the latest optimized model at the end of each epoch consistently amplified the success rate, aligning with theoretical findings from Mroueh.

**Finetuning on Deepscaler Data (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B):**
- Comparing the standard on-policy GRPO (v=1, i=1) with their proposed off-policy GRPO (v=10, i=1), both methods successfully improved the base model's performance on mathematical reasoning tasks.
- **Aime24:** The base model's Aime24 score of 29% was improved to a maximum of **32%** by both on-policy and off-policy GRPO. The off-policy variant achieved a slightly higher maximum and median Aime24 (0.3250 max, 0.3047 median) compared to on-policy (0.3229 max, 0.3021 median).
- **Math500:** Similarly, the base model's Math500 score of 83% increased to **87%** with both GRPO variants. Again, the off-policy variant achieved a slightly higher maximum Math500 (0.872) compared to on-policy (0.870).
- These results demonstrate that the off-policy GRPO performs **on par with or slightly outperforms** its on-policy counterpart even on a larger model and dataset, validating the theoretical findings and the practical benefits of reduced communication.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes significant contributions to the field of Reinforcement Learning for Large Language Models, offering both theoretical advancements and practical implications.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Significance

- **First-Principles Derivation of GRPO:** The paper provides a fundamental derivation of GRPO's clipped objective from a lower bound on reward improvement. This offers a deeper theoretical understanding of why GRPO works and its relationship to policy gradient methods.
- **Rigorous Policy Improvement Guarantees:** By establishing clear lower bounds for policy improvement in both on-policy and off-policy GRPO, the authors provide a stronger theoretical foundation for the algorithm's effectiveness. This is crucial for building trust in and further developing GRPO and similar alignment algorithms.
- **Theoretical Justification for Zero Variance Masking:** The analysis explicitly shows how samples with zero reward variance can destabilize the policy improvement lower bound. This provides a formal reason for filtering or masking such samples, a heuristic previously adopted in practice (e.g., in DAPO), thereby validating and guiding future algorithmic design.
- **Bridging GRPO and Off-Policy RL Theory:** The work formally connects GRPO to the broader literature on off-policy PPO and generalized policy optimization frameworks. By adapting GRPO to the off-policy regime and deriving its clipped surrogate objective, the paper helps integrate GRPO into the established theoretical landscape of off-policy RL, fostering cross-pollination of ideas.

#### 5.2. Practical Significance and Potential Impact

- **Enhanced Stability of GRPO Training:** The empirical results consistently show that both zero-variance masking (for on-policy) and the proposed off-policy formulation (with `v > 1`) significantly stabilize GRPO training, leading to more reliable and robust optimization. This directly addresses a critical challenge in RL, where unstable training can hinder progress and adoption.
- **Reduced Communication Overhead and Improved Efficiency:** The most impactful practical contribution is the potential to drastically reduce the communication costs and latency associated with serving LLMs for inference during RL training. In large-scale setups, especially with models requiring tensor parallelism and multi-GPU/multi-node serving (e.g., via vLLM), frequent model updates (`v=1`) incur substantial overhead. By demonstrating that off-policy GRPO (with `v > 1`) can achieve comparable or superior performance while reducing these updates, the paper paves the way for **more computationally efficient and scalable RL training for LLMs**. This can lead to faster experimentation cycles, reduced energy consumption, and democratized access to advanced LLM alignment techniques.
- **Maintained or Improved Performance:** Crucially, the experiments show that the off-policy GRPO not only reduces communication burden but also maintains or even slightly improves performance compared to its on-policy counterpart on complex reasoning tasks (GSM8K, Deepscaler math problems). This "win-win" scenario of efficiency without performance degradation is highly desirable in practical applications.
- **Guidance for Practitioners:** The paper's insights, particularly on zero-variance masking and the benefits of controlling server update frequency (`v`), provide actionable guidance for practitioners implementing and tuning GRPO for LLM alignment.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge that the main limitation of their current work is the **constrained empirical validation**. Experiments were limited to smaller datasets and model architectures (up to 1.5B parameters) that could fit on a single compute node (8 H100 GPUs). This means the full benefits of reduced communication overhead, particularly those stemming from inter-GPU or inter-node communication in truly large-scale, multi-node distributed training environments with much larger models and context lengths, could not be fully demonstrated.

Despite this limitation, the theoretical backing and single-node empirical proof-of-concept suggest that the benefits will become even more pronounced at scale. Future work will naturally involve scaling up experiments to larger models and distributed environments to fully showcase the efficiency gains of their off-policy GRPO algorithm, solidifying its role in enabling stable and efficient reinforcement learning at the frontiers of LLM development.
