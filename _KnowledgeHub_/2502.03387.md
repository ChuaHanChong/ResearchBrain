# LIMO: Less is More for Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The LIMO (Less is More for Reasoning) research from Shanghai Jiao Tong University and SII-GAIR develops a supervised fine-tuning method for large language models, demonstrating that advanced mathematical reasoning can be achieved with a minimal dataset of 800 carefully curated examples. This approach yields an average performance of 78.1% across various benchmarks, outperforming models like OpenAI-o1-preview (61.1%) and QwQ-32B-Preview (66.9%) that were fine-tuned on significantly larger datasets.

## Problem

- The prevailing assumption that complex reasoning in large language models necessitates vast quantities of training data.
- High computational costs and resource demands associated with collecting and training on massive datasets.
- Limited generalization and potential memorization in models trained on large, uncurated reasoning datasets.

## Method

- A ==data-efficient supervised fine-tuning (SFT)== approach, named LIMO, which utilizes a minimal dataset.
- A highly curated dataset of **800** mathematical reasoning problems, constructed through ==multi-stage filtering== for question difficulty and detailed ==rule-based scoring== for reasoning chain quality.
- The training process enables the base model to integrate ==meta-reasoning tasks== into cohesive chains, leveraging its pre-trained knowledge.

## Results

- LIMO achieved an average performance of **78.1%** across all benchmarks, outperforming state-of-the-art LLMs like OpenAI-o1-preview (**61.1%**) and QwQ-32B-Preview (**66.9%**).
- The model exhibited strong out-of-distribution generalization, successfully tackling diverse mathematical and multilingual benchmarks, including novel Chinese-language exams.
- Ablation studies confirmed that high-quality reasoning chains and training on challenging problems significantly enhance performance, with diminishing returns observed for data beyond **800** samples.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex reasoning in LLMs can emerge from minimal, high-quality data if foundation models possess comprehensive pre-trained domain knowledge.
> - The quality of reasoning chains and the difficulty of training problems are critical factors in fostering robust and generalized reasoning abilities.
> - A rich pre-trained knowledge base in the foundation model is essential for efficient elicitation of reasoning capabilities through fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

"LIMO: Less is More for Reasoning" fundamentally challenges a pervasive assumption in the field of large language models (LLMs): that achieving complex reasoning capabilities, especially in domains like mathematics and programming, inherently demands vast quantities of training data. Historically, the pursuit of robust reasoning in LLMs has largely adhered to a data-intensive paradigm, involving fine-tuning on hundreds of thousands, if not millions, of examples. This approach, while achieving notable successes, has also been criticized for its immense computational cost and for potentially leading to memorization rather than true generalization, as observed in studies where models struggle with minor variations of learned problems.

This paper positions itself at the intersection of several critical trends in LLM research:
- **Data Efficiency and Quality over Quantity:** LIMO directly builds upon and extends the "less-is-more" philosophy pioneered by works like LIMA (Zhou et al., 2023), which demonstrated that carefully curated, high-quality data can be significantly more effective than massive, uncurated datasets for instruction following. LIMO argues this principle applies equally, if not more, to complex reasoning.
- **Leveraging Pre-trained Knowledge:** A key tenet of the LIMO hypothesis is the "knowledge foundation revolution." Modern foundation models, such as Llama 2/3 and Qwen, are pre-trained on trillions of tokens, including an unprecedented volume of mathematical, scientific, and coding content. This means LLMs already possess a rich latent knowledge base. The challenge shifts from *acquiring* this knowledge during fine-tuning to *eliciting* it effectively. LIMO demonstrates that minimal, high-quality examples can serve as potent "cognitive templates" to unlock this pre-existing knowledge.
- **Inference-time Computation Scaling:** The paper highlights the "inference-time computation scaling revolution," referring to advancements in techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, self-reflection, and tree-search methods. These techniques enable LLMs to perform extended, multi-step deliberation during inference, transforming the model's ability to apply its knowledge. LIMO posits that high-quality fine-tuning examples can teach models *how* to utilize this computational space effectively.
- **Generalization vs. Memorization:** By achieving strong out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization across diverse and multilingual benchmarks, LIMO directly counters the criticism that supervised fine-tuning (SFT) primarily leads to rote memorization. Its ability to solve unseen problems, even in different languages, suggests that it is learning underlying reasoning processes rather than merely recalling specific solutions.
- **Computational Sustainability:** The current trajectory of scaling LLMs and their training data leads to ever-increasing computational and energy demands. By demonstrating that comparable, or even superior, performance can be achieved with significantly less data, LIMO offers a path toward more computationally sustainable and accessible advanced AI research and deployment.

In summary, LIMO contributes to the broader dialogue by advocating for a paradigm shift from brute-force data scaling to intelligent, quality-driven data curation, especially when foundational models already possess extensive domain knowledge. It highlights the synergistic importance of rich pre-training, effective fine-tuning signals, and robust inference strategies for developing truly reasoning-capable LLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the LIMO research is to **challenge the prevailing assumption that complex reasoning in large language models necessitates massive training data.** The authors aim to empirically demonstrate that sophisticated mathematical reasoning can emerge with only a few carefully selected examples.

This overarching objective is supported by several more specific goals:

1. **To establish and validate the "Less-Is-More Reasoning Hypothesis" (LIMO Hypothesis):** This hypothesis posits that in foundation models where domain knowledge has been comprehensively encoded during pre-training, sophisticated reasoning can emerge through minimal but strategically designed demonstrations of cognitive processes. The authors aim to identify and demonstrate the two key factors dictating this emergence: (1) the completeness of the model’s pre-trained knowledge base and (2) the effectiveness of post-training examples as "cognitive templates."
2. **To develop a data-efficient approach for teaching complex reasoning:** Specifically, the goal is to show that simple supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on a highly curated, small dataset can lead to state-of-the-art reasoning performance.
3. **To achieve superior performance on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks:** The paper aims to demonstrate LIMO's effectiveness on both in-domain and out-of-distribution tasks, significantly outperforming existing fine-tuned models and competitive SOTA LLMs, particularly concerning data efficiency.
4. **To provide a comprehensive analysis of the factors contributing to data-efficient reasoning:** Through ablation studies, the authors intend to investigate the impact of reasoning chain quality, question difficulty, the quality of the LLM's pre-trained knowledge base, and model size on performance.
5. **To release models, code, and curated datasets:** This objective aims to support future research in data-efficient reasoning, promoting transparency and reproducibility within the scientific community.

The motivations behind these objectives stem from several critical observations and challenges in the current LLM landscape:

- **Computational Costs and Resource Intensiveness:** The prevailing data-intensive paradigm for teaching reasoning requires substantial computational resources for data collection, processing, and model training, making it expensive and environmentally impactful. The authors seek a more efficient alternative.
- **Underutilization of Pre-trained Knowledge:** With modern foundation models possessing vast amounts of pre-trained knowledge, the authors believe that current fine-tuning strategies might not be optimally *eliciting* this latent capability, instead focusing on *acquiring* new knowledge unnecessarily.
- **Questioning Generalization:** Concerns that large-scale SFT primarily leads to memorization rather than true generalization motivate the exploration of methods that foster robust out-of-distribution performance.
- **Extending the "Less-Is-More" Principle:** Drawing inspiration from LIMA's success in instruction following, the researchers are motivated to investigate if this principle can be successfully applied to the more computationally complex domain of reasoning.
- **Advancing the Understanding of LLM Cognition:** By isolating the impact of data quality and the role of pre-trained knowledge, the research aims to deepen our understanding of how LLMs learn to reason, moving beyond a purely empirical scaling approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LIMO's methodology is centered on validating the "Less-Is-More Reasoning Hypothesis" through meticulous data curation and efficient supervised fine-tuning, aiming to elicit sophisticated reasoning from pre-trained LLMs using minimal examples.

**Core Hypothesis:** The LIMO Hypothesis states that for foundation models with comprehensively encoded domain knowledge during pre-training, sophisticated reasoning emerges through minimal, precisely orchestrated demonstrations of cognitive processes. This relies on: (I) latent prerequisite knowledge in the model's parameters, and (II) high-quality reasoning chains that explicitly decompose complex problems.

**A. LIMO Dataset Construction Pipeline (800 Samples):**
The cornerstone of LIMO is its highly curated, compact dataset, designed to maximize reasoning elicitation. The process involves multi-stage filtering for both question and reasoning chain quality.

1. **Candidate Pool Generation:**
 - **Initial Collection:** Millions of mathematical problems were gathered from diverse, established datasets: NuminaMath-CoT, DeepScaleR, AIME historical exams (before 2024), MATH, and various Chinese elementary, middle, high school, and undergraduate-level exams.

2. **Question Selection (Multi-stage Filtering):**
 - **Coarse Difficulty Filtering:** An initial filter excluded trivial problems. A relatively weaker mathematical model, Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct, was used. Problems it solved correctly within four attempts were removed.
 - **Fine-Grained Difficulty Assessment:** The remaining problems were subjected to a more rigorous evaluation using a stronger reasoning model, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B. For each problem, 32 solution attempts were sampled, and the empirical success rate served as a difficulty indicator. Only problems successfully solved in 1–3 out of 32 attempts were retained, indicating high challenge level. This yielded an initial data pool of 2,125 problems, named "LIMO-Pool."
 - **Deduplication:** To ensure integrity and prevent data leakage, thorough deduplication against all evaluation benchmarks was performed using n-gram matching.

3. **Reasoning Chain Construction (Solution Quality Selection):**
 - **Multi-Model Solution Generation:** For the 2,125 problems in LIMO-Pool, multiple solutions were generated from three state-of-the-art reasoning models: DeepSeek R1, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-32B, and QwQ-32B, to obtain diverse reasoning approaches.
 - **Qualitative Analysis and Key Characteristics Identification:** All authors collaboratively analyzed these generated solutions to identify characteristics of high-quality reasoning chains:
 - **Elaborated Reasoning:** Comprehensive exploration of logical steps without premature conclusions.
 - **Self-Verification:** Regular validation of intermediate results and logical consistency.
 - **Exploratory Approach:** Consideration of multiple possibilities before reaching conclusions.
 - **Adaptive Granularity:** Appropriate detail level across simple and complex deductions.
 - **Rule-Based Scoring System:** To quantify these qualities, a rule-based scoring system was implemented:
 - **Elaborated Reasoning:** Measured by solution length (30% weight).
 - **Self-Verification:** Frequency of validation-related keywords (e.g., "check," "verify") (20% weight).
 - **Exploratory Approach:** Count of tentative expressions (e.g., "perhaps," "might") (25% weight).
 - **Adaptive Granularity:** Frequency of connective phrases (e.g., "therefore," "since") (25% weight). All keyword frequencies were normalized by text length.
 - **Final LIMO Dataset Selection:** From the 2,125 questions, the highest-scoring solution for each problem was selected. These question-solution pairs were then ranked by their quality scores, and the top 800 were extracted to form the final **LIMO Dataset**.

**B. Training Recipe:**
The training approach is based on simple supervised fine-tuning (SFT) to enable the base model to integrate meta-reasoning tasks into cohesive chains, leveraging its pre-trained knowledge and inference-time computational capacity.

- **Base Model:** Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct.
- **Fine-tuning Method:** Full-parameter fine-tuning.
- **Optimization:** DeepSpeed ZeRO-3 optimization and FlashAttention-2 for efficiency.
- **Hyperparameters:**
 - Learning Rate: 5.0e-6 with a cosine decay schedule (deliberately omitting warmup for rapid adaptation).
 - Epochs: 15.
 - Batch Size: 64.
 - Sequence Length Limit: 16,384 tokens (all SFT response sequences remained within this limit).

**C. Evaluation Framework:**
A comprehensive framework was established to assess mathematical reasoning across various dimensions, focusing on generalization.

- **In-domain Evaluation:**
 - American Invitational Mathematics Examination (AIME24).
 - MATH500 (Hendrycks et al., 2021).
 - American Mathematics Competitions (AMC23).

- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Evaluation:**
 - **Diverse Mathematical Competitions:** OlympiadBench (He et al., 2024).
 - **Novel Multilingual Benchmarks (Chinese-language problems to minimize data contamination and test cross-lingual reasoning):**
 - CHMath (2024 Chinese High School Mathematics League).
 - Gaokao (China’s 2024 College Entrance Exam).
 - Kaoyan (Chinese Graduate School Entrance Exams).
 - GradeSchool (new elementary mathematics benchmark).
 - **Multi-Disciplinary Benchmarks:**
 - Minerva (Lewkowycz et al., 2022) (undergraduate-level STEM).
 - GPQA (Rein et al., 2023).

- **Performance Metrics:**
 - **Pass@1 metric** in a zero-shot Chain-of-Thought (CoT) setting.
 - **Decoding Strategy:** Greedy decoding for larger benchmarks (single sample). For smaller benchmarks (<50 problems), 4 samples (temperature=0.6) were generated, and an unbiased pass@1 metric (Chen et al., 2021) was calculated.
 - **Answer Evaluation:** Rule-based for numerical answers; LLM-based for complex answer formats.
 - **Output Length:** All evaluations maintained a 32,768 token maximum output length.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that LIMO consistently achieves superior reasoning performance across diverse benchmarks, fundamentally validating the "Less-Is-More Reasoning Hypothesis."

**A. Overall Performance:**
LIMO, fine-tuned on just 800 curated samples, achieves an average performance of **78.1%** across all benchmarks. This substantially outperforms state-of-the-art LLMs like OpenAI-o1-preview (61.1%) and QwQ-32B-Preview (66.9%), as well as models fine-tuned on significantly larger datasets (OpenThoughts-114k at 58.3% and NuminaMath-100k at 32.3%). The NuminaMath-100k model even shows a significant degradation compared to the base Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct model (49.9%), highlighting the pitfalls of large, uncurated data.

**B. In-Domain Performance:**
- **AIME24:** LIMO achieved **63.3% accuracy**, significantly surpassing QwQ-32B-Preview (50.0%), OpenAI-o1-preview (44.6%), and a model fine-tuned on NuminaMath-100k (6.5%).
- **MATH500:** LIMO scored **95.6% accuracy**, outperforming QwQ-32B-Preview (89.8%) and OpenAI-o1-preview (85.5%).
- **AMC23:** LIMO reached **96.3% accuracy**, demonstrating a substantial lead over QwQ-32B-Preview (83.6%).

**C. Out-of-Domain Generalization:**
LIMO exhibits strong generalization capabilities across previously unseen scenarios, confirming that it learns transferable reasoning skills rather than just memorizing training examples.
- **OlympiadBench:** LIMO achieved **67.6% accuracy**, significantly outperforming QwQ-32B-Preview (58.5%).
- **Multilingual Benchmarks (Chinese):** LIMO showed impressive performance, with **84.2% on CHMath**, **91.1% on Gaokao**, **83.9% on Kaoyan**, and **76.2% on GradeSchool**, consistently outperforming other baselines, demonstrating cross-distribution and cross-lingual reasoning.
- **Multi-Disciplinary Benchmarks:** LIMO achieved **52.2% on Minerva** (outperforming OpenAI-o1-preview's 47.1%) and **70.7% on GPQA** (competitive with OpenAI-o1-preview's 73.3%).

**D. Analysis and Ablation Studies (Validation of LIMO Principles):**

- **RQ1: Impact of Reasoning Chain Quality:** Models trained on higher-quality reasoning chains (L5 scored by the rule-based system) consistently achieved better performance on AIME24 and MATH500 compared to models trained on lower-quality chains (L1). This confirms the critical role of well-structured "cognitive templates."
- **RQ2: Impact of Question Quality (Difficulty):** Fine-tuning on *more challenging* problems (Advanced-500, consisting of AIME-level problems) led to a **16% absolute improvement** on AIME2024 and maintained high performance on MATH500 (91.2%), despite no in-domain training data for MATH500. This suggests that training on difficult problems fosters robust, generalized reasoning abilities.
- **RQ3: LLM Backbone - Pre-trained Knowledge:** A comparative study between Qwen1.5-32B-Chat and Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct (LIMO's base model) revealed that Qwen2.5, with its improved pre-training data quality (especially in math/code), achieved dramatically higher reasoning performance (e.g., 63.3% vs. 9.2% on AIME2024) even with identical LIMO fine-tuning. This strongly supports the hypothesis that a rich pre-trained knowledge base is foundational for efficient reasoning elicitation.
- **RQ4: LLM Backbone - Model Size:** Performance generally scaled with model size (from 3B to 72B), particularly for challenging benchmarks like AIME24. However, diminishing returns were observed, with the performance difference between the 32B and 72B models being marginal on MATH500 and slightly less pronounced on AIME24, suggesting a potential saturation point for specific benchmarks.
- **RQ5: Sample Efficiency:** A dramatic performance increase was observed with just 400 curated samples (LIMO-400), significantly outperforming the base model. While performance continued to improve with larger datasets, diminishing returns were evident beyond 800 samples. This indicates that a small, highly-quality dataset can be extremely efficient, and raw data quantity rapidly loses its impact after a certain threshold.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The LIMO research holds significant implications for both the theoretical understanding and practical application of large language models.

**A. Paradigm Shift in LLM Reasoning Training:**
LIMO directly challenges the long-held belief that complex reasoning abilities in LLMs demand massive datasets. By empirically demonstrating that state-of-the-art performance can be achieved with only hundreds of carefully curated examples, the work advocates for a fundamental shift from "quantity-driven" to "quality-driven" data paradigms. This re-orients research efforts towards intelligent data curation and understanding the cognitive processes that foster true reasoning, rather than simply scaling up data and model size.

**B. Validation of the Less-Is-More Reasoning Hypothesis:**
The core contribution is the establishment and strong empirical validation of the LIMO Hypothesis. This theoretical framework highlights the critical interplay of two factors: the comprehensive domain knowledge latent within large foundation models from pre-training, and the effectiveness of minimal, strategically designed demonstrations acting as "cognitive templates" during fine-tuning. This offers a more nuanced understanding of how LLMs acquire and utilize complex cognitive abilities, moving beyond a purely black-box approach.

**C. Enhanced Data Efficiency and Computational Sustainability:**
By demonstrating performance surpassing models trained on 100 times more data, LIMO offers a pathway to significantly reduce the computational costs and environmental footprint associated with developing powerful reasoning LLMs. This data efficiency democratizes access to advanced LLM training, making it feasible for researchers and institutions with limited resources to develop highly capable, specialized models.

**D. Improved Generalization and Reduced Memorization:**
LIMO's strong out-of-distribution generalization across diverse mathematical and multilingual benchmarks suggests that its approach fosters genuine reasoning abilities rather than rote memorization. This is crucial for building robust and reliable AI systems that can adapt to novel situations and apply learned principles creatively. The inclusion of new, uncurated Chinese-language benchmarks further strengthens the claim of true generalization.

**E. Guidance for Future Research and Development:**
The detailed ablation studies provide invaluable insights for future research:
- **Data Curation as a Priority:** Emphasizes that investing in high-quality problem selection and meticulous reasoning chain construction is more impactful than simply collecting more data. This suggests future work in automated or semi-automated data curation and active learning strategies.
- **Importance of Pre-trained Knowledge:** The strong correlation between pre-training quality and fine-tuning efficacy underscores the ongoing importance of developing richly knowledgeable foundation models.
- **Synergy of Training and Inference:** Reinforces the idea that effective reasoning emerges from a synergy of pre-trained knowledge, fine-tuned "cognitive templates," and the intelligent utilization of inference-time computational resources (e.g., via CoT).

**F. Practical Impact:**
Practically, LIMO’s approach can enable the creation of highly capable, specialized reasoning models with substantially fewer resources and a shorter development cycle. This could accelerate the development of AI tools for education, scientific discovery, and complex problem-solving in various domains. The public release of LIMO models, code, and curated datasets will serve as a valuable resource, enabling other researchers to build upon this work and further explore the frontiers of data-efficient reasoning.

In conclusion, "LIMO: Less is More for Reasoning" represents a pivotal contribution to the field of LLMs, shifting the focus towards intelligent data strategies and deeper insights into the mechanisms of reasoning acquisition. It lays the groundwork for more efficient, generalizable, and sustainable AI development.
