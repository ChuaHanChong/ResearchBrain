# Task-Aware Identifiability: Observation Quotients, Statistical Geometry, and Representation Accessibility

> [!summary] Summary
> Task-Aware Identifiability (TAI) is a diagnostic framework formalizing when observations preserve the distinctions a task needs: an observation quotient with a refinement condition for structural identifiability, nuisance-efficient Fisher geometry for local resolvability, representation accessibility separating exact task sufficiency from decoder stability, and a four-term risk decomposition. Controlled synthetic, near-field sensing, and NYUv2 studies localize where task-relevant information is actually lost.

## Problem

- Learning formulations assume observations carry task-relevant information, so failures get blamed on model capacity or data rather than exact, structural collapse in the declared observation law.
- Classical identifiability and observability characterize parameter or state recovery, but are not organized around the equivalence a fixed channel induces relative to the specific distinctions a task requires.
- Representation evaluation conflates information preservation with geometric accessibility, lacking scale-controlled, decoder-conditioned diagnostics that separate encoder sufficiency from decoder stability and finite-sample channel loss.

## Method

- Observation quotient and task-observation compatibility theorem: structural identifiability holds iff the observation partition refines the task partition, plus a repetition-invariance result for conditionally iid channel repeats.
- Local statistical geometry: Fisher-Rao metric pulled back to regular quotient strata with kernel identity, and nuisance-efficient Fisher information via a Schur/Moore-Penrose nuisance-score projector.
- Representation accessibility and layered risk: law-valued encoder kernel versus deterministic quotient embedding, exact fixed-task sufficiency, scale-controlled decoder Lipschitz modulus, distortion bound, and telescoping structural/finite/representation/learning gaps.

## Results

- Bernoulli repetition study: observable-task Bayes risk falls 0.2 to 5.07e-8 by 64 repeats while the hidden task stays exactly 0.5; a task-relevant side channel zeroes the structural term.
- Nuisance alignment collapses efficient information 50 to 0.7812; efficient normalization gives 0.995-1.029 at 1024 samples versus raw-block 232.6. Near-field range CRLB standard deviation degrades 0.108 m to 27.6 m while angle holds near 0.083 degrees.
- NYUv2 frozen encoders reverse: DeiT beats MAE on semantics by +0.0434 mIoU but loses depth by -0.1877 AbsRel; interaction 0.2311 affine, 0.1597 under an MLP probe. Layered gaps decompose as 0.250/0.0167/0.0600/0.0081.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Repeating the same fixed sensing channel never recovers an exactly collapsed distinction; only changing the observation mechanism, not more data or capacity, refines the quotient.
> - Matched task-agnostic representation geometry does not imply matched task accessibility: encoder preferences reverse across tasks and their magnitude is modulated by the decoder family.
> - Fisher nullity is a first-order local diagnostic only; higher-order structure and global likelihood ambiguity, such as degraded correct-basin rates at weak information, escape it.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research contributes to the foundational understanding of intelligent systems by addressing a problem that precedes typical learning objectives: whether the inherent observation process preserves the distinctions necessary for a given task. It integrates and extends concepts from several established fields:

- **Statistical Identifiability and Observability:** Classical identifiability theory determines if model parameters or states can be uniquely recovered from data. This work refines this by distinguishing between *structural* non-identifiability (due to fundamental limitations of the observation mechanism) and *practical* non-identifiability (due to finite data or noise). The paper frames this around task compatibility with observation-induced equivalence relations.
- **Comparison of Statistical Experiments (Blackwell Ordering):** The framework builds on ideas of sufficient statistics and Blackwell comparison, which order statistical experiments based on their informativeness. It also connects to Information Bottleneck and sufficient-representation theories by formalizing task-relevant information retention, but separates exact fixed-task sufficiency from decoder conditioning.
- **Information Geometry:** The paper applies information geometry, which studies statistical manifolds and Fisher–Rao structure, to analyze local statistical strength. It utilizes the Fisher nullspace as a first-order probe of the observation quotient, distinguishing this law-level geometry from deterministic representation pullbacks.
- **Representation Learning:** This area focuses on learning useful data representations. The paper connects to concepts of state abstraction, identifiable representation learning, stable embedding theory, and task-relevant latent identification. It complements existing work on risk decomposition in representation evaluation by providing a diagnostic separation of observation availability, fixed-task sufficiency, scale-controlled decoder stability, and representation-channel risk.
- **Active Perception and Bayesian Experimental Design:** While the paper's diagnostics can motivate such formulations, it does not define an action-dependent sensing objective itself. It focuses on diagnosing a *given* observation mechanism rather than designing an *adaptive* one.

In essence, this work introduces a unified diagnostic framework that bridges these areas, aiming to determine where task-relevant information is lost—either at the observation stage, during representation, or in the learning process—rather than solely focusing on the learning algorithm's performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this work stems from a fundamental assumption often made in intelligent systems: that observations inherently contain all necessary information for a task. The authors argue that this assumption can fail structurally if the observation mechanism assigns identical observation laws to latent states that the downstream task must distinguish. This "knowability problem" precedes the challenges of learning, model capacity, or data sufficiency.

The key objectives of the paper are to develop a comprehensive diagnostic framework, termed "Task-Aware Identifiability" (TAI), that systematically addresses this problem. Specifically, the authors aim to:

1. **Formalize Structural Observation Quotient and Task Identifiability:** To develop a formal definition of observation-induced equivalence (the "observation quotient") and establish a compatibility condition, stating that structural identifiability holds if and only if the observation partition refines the task partition. This condition is posited to be invariant under conditionally i.i.d. repetitions of the same fixed observation channel.
2. **Characterize First-Order Local Identifiability via Fisher Geometry:** To use standard Fisher geometry to induce a Riemannian metric on regular quotient strata. The objective is to demonstrate that the nullspace of this metric locally characterizes directions that are insensitive to the observation mechanism, without conflating this local property with global observational equivalence.
3. **Define Task-Relative Representation Accessibility:** To distinguish between law-valued encoder experiments (which yield statistical distributions in representation space) and idealized deterministic quotient embeddings. The goal is to separate information preservation (via exact fixed-task sufficiency) from geometric accessibility (via a scale-controlled task-decoder Lipschitz modulus and local task pullback geometry).
4. **Propose a Layered Risk Decomposition and Controlled Diagnostics:** To decompose total task risk into distinct components: structural, finite-observation, representation-channel, and learning gaps. The objective is to provide a framework for localized diagnosis, enabling researchers to attribute performance limitations to specific stages of the information processing pipeline. This framework is validated through controlled synthetic studies, a physics-based model, and real-world data analysis.

Overall, the motivation is to equip researchers and practitioners with tools to diagnose *where* task-relevant information is lost, allowing for targeted interventions rather than assuming a single-point failure in the learning process.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology is structured into four main components, building from abstract structural properties to practical, layered risk analysis.

#### 3.1. Observation-Induced Structural Identifiability (Section 3)

The framework begins by defining an **observation mechanism** as a Markov kernel $O: U \times \mathcal{A}_Y \to$, where $U$ is the latent state space and $Y$ is the observation space.
- **Statistical Observation Equivalence ($\sim_O$)**: Two latent states $u_1, u_2$ are observationally equivalent if their conditional observation laws $O(u_1, \cdot)$ and $O(u_2, \cdot)$ are identical as probability measures.
- **Observation Quotient ($Q_O$) and Partition ($\mathcal{P}_O$)**: The observation quotient is the set of equivalence classes $U / \sim_O$. The canonical projection $\pi_O: U \to Q_O$ maps a state to its equivalence class.
- **Task Equivalence ($\sim_h$) and Partition ($\mathcal{P}_h$)**: For a task $h: U \to H$, two states $u_1, u_2$ are task-equivalent if $h(u_1) = h(u_2)$.
- **Task-Observation Compatibility**: The core condition for structural task identifiability is established: the observation partition $\mathcal{P}_O$ must refine the task partition $\mathcal{P}_h$ (i.e., $\mathcal{P}_O \succeq \mathcal{P}_h$). This implies that if two states are observationally equivalent, they must also be task-equivalent. If this condition fails, a structural ambiguity exists that no observation-only procedure can resolve.
- **Structural Ambiguity vs. Structural Risk**: The worst-case structural ambiguity is defined as the supremum of task differences over observationally equivalent states. Structural risk (for 0-1 loss) is the expected value of the optimal risk on the quotient space, where optimal decision rules are based only on the observation equivalence class.
- **Repetition Invariance**: It is formally proven that structural equivalence is invariant to conditionally i.i.d. repetitions of the same fixed observation channel. This means that if a distinction is structurally collapsed by one observation, it remains collapsed even with infinite repetitions.

#### 3.2. Local Statistical Geometry (Section 4)

This section introduces a local, differential perspective on identifiability, focusing on smooth parameter strata.
- **Statistical Model Image**: The observation mechanism maps the latent space $U$ to a family of probability distributions $\Theta_O = \{P_{Y|u} : u \in U\}$. The observation quotient $Q_O$ is in bijection with $\Theta_O$.
- **Fisher-Rao Geometry**: On regular, smooth strata where the Fisher-Rao metric is positive definite, this metric is pulled back to the regular observation quotient.
- **Metric Descent to Regular Quotient**: The paper proves that if the map from the regular observation quotient to the regular statistical image is a diffeomorphism, the pullback metric defines a Riemannian metric on the quotient. Crucially, the nullspace of the Fisher information matrix at a point $u$, $\text{ker}G(u)$, is shown to be equivalent to the nullspace of the differential of the observation map, $\text{ker }dO_u$. This identifies directions locally indistinguishable to first order, a local property distinct from global observational equivalence.
- **Target-Nuisance Decomposition**: For models with target variables $x$ and nuisance variables $\nu$, the concept of efficient Fisher information ($I_{eff}$) for $x$ is introduced. This is obtained by projecting out nuisance-score directions. Its kernel identifies directions in the target space that are locally indistinguishable when allowing for arbitrary changes in nuisance variables.

#### 3.3. Representation Accessibility (Section 5)

This part focuses on how information is preserved or lost when observations are transformed into representations.
- **Representation Channels**: An encoder kernel $K(dz|y)$ maps observations $y$ to representations $z$. This induces a law family $q \mapsto P_{Z|q}$ on the quotient. The "Automatic Quotient Descent" proposition states that any post-observation processing cannot separate states that were already observationally equivalent.
- **Fixed-Task Sufficiency**: For a deterministic representation $\psi: Q_O \to Z$, $\psi$ is considered sufficient for a task $\bar{h}: Q_O \to H$ if $\psi(q_1) = \psi(q_2) \implies \bar{h}(q_1) = \bar{h}(q_2)$. This allows for a well-defined task decoder $r_h: \psi(Q_O) \to H$.
- **Task-Decoder Geometric Accessibility**: The "task-decoder Lipschitz modulus" $L_{acc}$ is defined for the decoder $r_h$, measuring its stability. A finite $L_{acc}$ implies that small changes in representation correspond to small changes in task output. This modulus is metric- and scale-dependent. The "task-agnostic quotient distortion" $D_{geo}(\psi)$ is introduced as a bound on injectivity and stability. For smooth settings, local task-conditioned accessibility can be characterized by relating the pullback metrics $g_h = \bar{h}^*g_H$ and $g_\psi = \psi^*g_Z$.
- **Statistical and Information-Geometric Interface**: The Fisher monotonicity principle ($g_{FR,Z} \preceq g_Q$) is invoked, stating that the Fisher information of the representation cannot exceed that of the original observation, confirming the "garbling" effect of state-independent post-processing.

#### 3.4. Layered Task Risk Decomposition (Section 6)

The paper proposes a decomposition of the total task risk gap into four non-negative components, corresponding to distinct stages of information processing:
- **Information Levels**: The optimal risks are considered at four levels: $U$ (full latent state), $Q$ (observation-law equivalence class), $Y^{(m)}$ (finite observations), and $Z^{(m)}$ (representation output).
- **Monotonicity**: The optimal risks are monotonic: $R^*_U \leq R^*_Q \leq R^*_{Y^{(m)}} \leq R^*_{Z^{(m)}} \leq R(\hat{h})$, where $R(\hat{h})$ is the risk of a learned predictor.
- **Layered Gaps**:
 - $\epsilon_{struct} = R^*_Q - R^*_U$: The irreducible loss due to exact observational collapse.
 - $\epsilon_{finite}(m) = R^*_{Y^{(m)}} - R^*_Q$: Loss due to finite-sample statistical overlap.
 - $\epsilon_{rep} = R^*_{Z^{(m)}} - R^*_{Y^{(m)}}$: Loss due to the actual representation channel.
 - $\epsilon_{learn} = R(\hat{h}) - R^*_{Z^{(m)}}$: The gap between the learned predictor and the optimal rule from the representation.

#### 3.5. Experimental Evaluation (Section 7)

The methodology is validated through a series of controlled synthetic studies, a physics-based near-field sensing model, and an evaluation on real-world learned representations (NYUv2 dataset). These experiments are designed to isolate and demonstrate each layer of the framework.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluations systematically validate the different layers of the Task-Aware Identifiability (TAI) framework.

#### 4.1. Controlled Synthetic Validation

- **Structural Identifiability**: Experiments using Bernoulli latent variables (A, B) and an observation channel dependent only on A demonstrated that increasing repetitions ($m$) reduced finite statistical uncertainty for task $h_A$ (dependent on A) but left the risk for task $h_B$ (dependent on B) at a structural floor of 0.5. A changed observation mechanism (adding a B-informative side channel) *could* reduce this structural floor, illustrating that only channel modification, not mere repetition, resolves exact structural collisions.
- **Nuisance-Efficient Fisher Geometry**: A Gaussian observation model with target ($x_1, x_2$) and nuisance ($\nu$) variables was used. The study showed that as the target score aligned with the nuisance direction, the *raw* Fisher information for $x_1$ remained large, but the *nuisance-efficient* Fisher information for $x_1$ collapsed. This directly demonstrated that efficient Fisher information accurately predicts target estimation behavior, while raw information can be misleading, validating Fisher nullity as a local, first-order diagnostic. A singular case showed higher-order distinguishability even with zero first-order information.
- **Task-Relative Accessibility**: Using two representations ($\psi_A, \psi_B$) with identical task-agnostic geometric distortion properties but different scaling of components, and three tasks ($h_1=q_1, h_2=q_2, h_3=(q_1+q_2)/\sqrt{2}$), the study revealed task-specific preferences. One representation favored $h_1$, the other favored $h_2$, and they were equal for $h_3$. This demonstrates that matched task-agnostic geometry does not imply matched fixed-task accessibility, and representation quality is intrinsically task-relative. Further controls showed that task sufficiency does not require full quotient preservation and that ambient representative geometry is not intrinsic to the quotient.
- **Layered Risk Attribution**: A binary latent/channel setup was used to isolate the four risk gaps: $\epsilon_{struct}$, $\epsilon_{finite}$, $\epsilon_{rep}$, and $\epsilon_{learn}$. By varying specific parameters (e.g., prior for $\epsilon_{struct}$, number of repetitions for $\epsilon_{finite}$, bit-flip probability for $\epsilon_{rep}$, training data size for $\epsilon_{learn}$), the study confirmed that each gap could be independently manipulated and measured, validating the operational separability of the risk decomposition.

#### 4.2. Physics-Based Case Study: Near-Field Sensing

A synthetic forward model of a 32-element Uniform Linear Array (ULA) observing a source with unknown complex gain (nuisance) and known spherical-wave steering law was analyzed for range and angle estimation.
- The **Cramér–Rao Lower Bounds (CRLB)** showed sharply different local statistical strengths: range support degraded rapidly with distance (log-log slope of -4), consistent with theoretical predictions for this ULA configuration, while angular support remained approximately stable.
- Profile Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) performance aligned with CRLB in regular, well-resolved conditions (closer ranges). However, at farther ranges (e.g., $4r_R$), weak-information and global-estimation effects led to a significant drop in correct-basin rates and optimizer diagnostic flags, indicating that local Fisher geometry alone does not characterize global likelihood ambiguity. A plane-wave negative control confirmed the absence of range information in that specific model.

#### 4.3. Real-World Evaluation on NYUv2

Frozen learned representations from three different Vision Transformer (ViT-B/16) encoders (DeiT, DINO, MAE) were evaluated on NYUv2 for semantic segmentation and metric depth regression using 1x1 affine and MLP-128 decoder heads.
- **Task-specific encoder preferences** were observed: DeiT outperformed MAE for semantic segmentation, while MAE outperformed DeiT for depth regression. This demonstrated a clear cross-task interaction and reversal of preference among encoders.
- This **reversal was robust** across both affine and MLP-128 decoder families, maintaining its qualitative sign. However, the magnitude of the cross-task interaction was modulated by the choice of decoder family, indicating "decoder-regime-relative accessibility."
- Further feature perturbation and finite-data efficiency studies confirmed the observed task preferences and their robustness under varying conditions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Task-Aware Identifiability (TAI) framework presents a significant shift in diagnosing performance limitations in intelligent systems, moving beyond a sole focus on learning algorithms.

- **Identifiability Precedes Learning:** A primary implication is that the fundamental question of whether task-relevant information exists in observations must be addressed *before* designing complex learning architectures or optimization strategies. The framework highlights that additional data or model capacity cannot overcome structural limitations imposed by the observation mechanism itself.
- **Localized Failure Modes:** TAI provides a diagnostic toolkit to pinpoint *where* information is lost:
 - **Structural:** Information fundamentally absent at the observation level.
 - **Finite-Observation:** Uncertainty due to limited data samples.
 - **Representation-Channel:** Information degraded or lost during encoding into a representation.
 - **Learning:** Suboptimal performance due to the specific learning algorithm or its implementation.
 This decomposition enables targeted interventions, such as modifying the sensing mechanism for structural issues, collecting more data for finite-observation gaps, or refining the representation channel/decoder for the latter two.
- **Task-Relative Representation Evaluation:** The framework underscores that representation quality is inherently task-dependent. A representation effective for one task may be suboptimal for another, even if its task-agnostic geometric properties appear similar. This challenges the notion of a universal "good" representation and advocates for task-conditioned stability diagnostics.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The identified limitations point to clear future directions, including developing methods for learning quotient structure from data when the observation mechanism is unknown, extending to approximate identifiability, linking decoder stability to statistical and sample efficiency, and integrating the framework with active and adaptive sensing strategies.

In summary, TAI offers a principled, multi-layered approach to diagnosing the "knowability" of tasks from observations, providing a valuable framework for understanding the fundamental limits and potential bottlenecks in the design and performance of intelligent systems.
