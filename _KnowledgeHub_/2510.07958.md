# A$^2$Search: Ambiguity-Aware Question Answering with Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A2SEARCH introduces an annotation-free, end-to-end reinforcement learning framework that enables large language models to identify ambiguous questions and generate multiple valid answers. The approach achieves new state-of-the-art performance on multi-hop QA benchmarks, with A2SEARCH-7B yielding an average AnsF1@1 score of 48.4% and demonstrating superior inference efficiency with a single greedy rollout compared to larger baselines.

## Problem

- Most open-domain Question Answering (QA) benchmarks and training paradigms operate under a 'single-answer dogma,' failing to address questions that are inherently ambiguous and admit multiple valid responses.
- Existing methods for handling ambiguity in QA rely on resource-intensive manual annotation for alternative answers or merely detect ambiguity without generating comprehensive solutions.
- Current Reinforcement Learning (RL) pipelines for QA provide misleading training signals by implicitly penalizing equally valid alternative answers when only a single reference answer is provided.

## Method

- An automated pipeline is developed to generate ==ambiguity-aware== training data by sampling diverse trajectories from pre-trained LLMs, filtering unhelpful responses, verifying evidence, and grouping semantically equivalent answers.
- A reinforcement learning framework, utilizing Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), is employed to train models for multi-answer scenarios.
- A novel outcome-only reward mechanism, Answer-level F1 (AnsF1), is designed to balance precision and recall across multiple valid answers, encouraging comprehensive and accurate responses.

## Results

- A2SEARCH-**7B** achieved state-of-the-art performance on multi-hop QA datasets with an average AnsF1@1 score of **48.4%** (EM), surpassing larger baselines like ReSearch-**32B** (**46.2%**).
- The model demonstrates superior inference efficiency, achieving strong performance with only a single greedy decoding rollout (AnsF1@1), outperforming prior methods that require three or more sampled rollouts (AnsF1@3).
- A2SEARCH generalized effectively to the AmbigQA benchmark, outperforming specialized baselines trained on curated data, validating the robustness of its automated data pipeline and approach to ambiguity resolution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial portion of questions in existing QA benchmarks are inherently ambiguous (e.g., 27.6% in MuSiQue), indicating a fundamental mismatch between current QA paradigms and real-world information complexity.
> - Automated data generation for alternative answers is a scalable and effective alternative to manual annotation, overcoming previous bottlenecks in creating ambiguity-aware training datasets.
> - Training LLMs with explicit ambiguity awareness and a tailored multi-answer reward function leads to more robust, efficient, and human-aligned QA systems capable of navigating nuanced language.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Open-domain Question Answering (QA) has seen significant advancements, largely driven by the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL). Modern QA systems, particularly agentic models like Search-R1, ReSearch, and AFM, have demonstrated strong performance in multi-step reasoning and active tool usage (e.g., search engines). These models learn to interact with external tools to gather information and construct answers, moving beyond static knowledge bases.

However, the authors identify a critical oversight in the current QA landscape: the prevalent assumption of a single "gold" answer for each question. This assumption, embedded in most standard QA benchmarks (e.g., HotpotQA, MuSiQue, NQ), creates a mismatch with real-world scenarios and even inherent ambiguities within datasets. Many questions, especially complex multi-hop ones, naturally admit multiple valid answers, each supported by plausible reasoning paths and evidence. Current RL pipelines, by rewarding only the single reference answer and implicitly penalizing equally valid alternatives, provide misleading training signals and understate true model capabilities.

Existing attempts to address ambiguity have largely fallen into two categories:
- **Manual Annotation:** Benchmarks like AmbigQA and ASQA introduce human-annotated alternative answers. While valuable, this approach is resource-intensive and difficult to scale, especially for multi-hop questions or dynamically evolving knowledge.
- **Ambiguity Detection Only:** Some research focuses on merely detecting ambiguous questions without providing alternative answers, which doesn't fully resolve the problem for an end-to-end QA system.

A2SEARCH directly challenges this "single-answer dogma" by proposing an **annotation-free, end-to-end training framework** designed to *recognize and handle ambiguity* by generating *multiple valid answers*. This work positions itself as a crucial step towards building more robust, human-aligned, and reliable QA systems that can adapt to the inherent complexities of natural language and real-world information. By focusing on automated data generation and an ambiguity-aware reward mechanism, A2SEARCH aims to overcome the scalability limitations of manual annotation and provide a comprehensive solution for ambiguous QA.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the A2SEARCH research are multifaceted:

1. **Address the "Single-Answer Dogma" in QA:** The primary motivation is to overcome the fundamental limitation of existing open-domain QA benchmarks and training paradigms that assume a single correct answer. The authors empirically demonstrate that a significant portion of questions (e.g., 27.6% in MuSiQue, 19.3% in NQ training sets) are inherently ambiguous and admit multiple valid answers, rendering single-answer approaches incomplete and potentially flawed.

2. **Develop an Annotation-Free Approach to Ambiguity Handling:** Recognizing the high cost and scalability challenges of manual annotation for alternative answers, a key objective is to create a fully automated pipeline. This pipeline aims to detect ambiguous questions and automatically gather evidence-supported alternative answers from existing, single-answer datasets. This dramatically reduces the overhead associated with creating ambiguity-aware training data.

3. **Enable Models to Sense Ambiguity and Generate Multiple Valid Responses:** The research seeks to train LLMs not only to provide accurate answers but also to explicitly identify when a question is ambiguous and, in such cases, generate *all* plausible, evidence-backed answers. This moves beyond merely detecting ambiguity to actively resolving it by presenting a more complete picture to the user.

4. **Design a Reinforcement Learning Framework for Multi-Answer QA:** To effectively train models for ambiguity-aware QA, a new RL framework is required. This involves designing a reward mechanism that naturally accommodates multiple correct answers, balancing precision (not generating incorrect answers) and recall (covering all valid alternatives). The AnsF1 reward, tailored for this purpose, is central to achieving this objective.

5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance on Diverse QA Benchmarks:** Beyond merely handling ambiguity, a crucial objective is to demonstrate that an ambiguity-aware model can achieve competitive or superior performance across a wide array of existing QA benchmarks, including both multi-hop and general open-domain datasets, with greater efficiency (e.g., fewer rollouts).

In essence, A2SEARCH is motivated by the desire to build more intelligent and robust QA systems that better reflect human understanding and the inherent complexities of information, moving towards a paradigm where embracing ambiguity leads to more reliable and user-friendly AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

A2SEARCH introduces an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework built upon two main pillars: an automated pipeline for generating ambiguity-aware training data and an RL optimization strategy tailored for multi-answer scenarios.

#### 3.1. Automated Pipeline for Alternative Answer Generation

Instead of costly manual annotation, A2SEARCH constructs an automated pipeline to identify ambiguous questions and extract verifiable alternative answers from existing single-answer QA datasets. This process involves four key steps:

1. **Sampling:**
 - A collection of `N` pre-trained, search-capable Large Language Models (`S = {S_n}`) (e.g., ReSearch-7B/32B, Search-R1-7B/14B/32B) is used. These models are typically trained on single-answer QA datasets and can interact with an external search tool (e.g., Wikipedia via FAISS).
 - For each question `q`, each model `S_n` generates `M` trajectories (`τ = (a_1, o_1..., a_T)`), representing a sequence of reasoning steps, tool calls, and final answers. A total of `N × M` candidate answers are aggregated.
 - This step uses a sampling temperature (0.8) to encourage diversity in trajectories. The source questions are from training splits of MuSiQue, 2Wiki, and NQ, resulting in approximately 3.99 million trajectories for 49,938 questions.

2. **Filtering:**
 - This step prunes unhelpful trajectories based on three rules:
 - Remove trajectories whose answers are semantically equivalent to the reference answer (`ans*`), as judged by an LLM (Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct).
 - If *all* trajectories from a model for a given question fail to produce `ans*`, all its trajectories are dropped, indicating the model's inability to solve the question.
 - Deduplicate semantically identical candidate answers, keeping only one representative trajectory for each unique answer (after lower-casing, punctuation removal, and whitespace trimming).
 - This stage retains 208,829 trajectories (5.2% of original) spanning 33,997 questions (68.1% of source), with an average of 6.1 distinct trajectories per question.

3. **Verification:**
 - For the filtered trajectories, `K` LLM-based verifiers (e.g., Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet, OpenAI o3/o4-mini) assess whether each candidate answer (`ans`) is sufficiently supported by the evidence within its trajectory.
 - Verifiers output a binary judgment (`z_k ∈ {0, 1}`) for 'supported,' 'partially supported,' or 'not supported.' The 'partially supported' category acts as a safeguard.
 - Majority voting (`Verify(q, τ, ans)`) with a threshold `η` (set to 3 for 96% human agreement) is applied. Only trajectories deemed 'supported' are retained.
 - This step reduces the dataset to 19,529 trajectories (9.4% of the previous step's output).

4. **Grouping:**
 - A clustering procedure, performed by an LLM (Claude 3.7 Sonnet), merges semantically equivalent answers into groups. Each group is assigned a canonical form, with other answers serving as aliases.
 - This process identifies that approximately 28.6% of candidate answers are semantically grouped.
 - The final output is `A_alt`, a set of distinct, verifiable alternative answers for ambiguous questions. The resulting training dataset contains 49,938 questions, with around 19% having alternative answers (e.g., 27.6% in MuSiQue).

#### 3.2. Reinforcement Learning Framework

The generated ambiguity-aware dataset `A = {ans*, A_alt}` is then used to train the A2SEARCH model using reinforcement learning.

1. **Training Objective:**
 - The framework employs **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**, an RL algorithm that estimates a baseline directly from a group of sampled rollouts, simplifying training compared to PPO.
 - The objective function maximizes policy updates based on normalized advantage (`A_i = (r_i - mean(group_rewards)) / std(group_rewards)`), where `r_i` is the reward for the `i`-th rollout. The KL penalty term is discarded.

2. **Rollout with Search Tool:**
 - Rollout generation involves iterative interaction between the policy and a search tool. The model generates a trajectory (`τ`) of alternating actions (reasoning, tool-call, answer) and tool responses.
 - The policy `π_θ` samples actions based on the accumulated prompt (`s_t`). Training masks tokens in `tool-response` blocks as they are generated by the external tool, not the policy.

3. **Reward Design:**
 - An outcome-only reward mechanism is used, combining a **format check** with an **Answer-level F1 (AnsF1)** score.
 - `R(q, ans) = 0` if the output format is invalid (e.g., no tool calls, incorrect structure).
 - `R(q, ans) = 0.1` if the format is valid but no reference answers are matched.
 - `R(q, ans) = 1 - α(1 - AnsF1)` if the format is valid and at least one reference is matched. `α ∈` controls the precision-recall trade-off; it is set to 0.4.
 - **AnsF1** is calculated as `2 * Precision * Recall / (Precision + Recall)`, where `Precision = hits/preds` (number of matched references / total predicted answers) and `Recall = hits/refs` (number of matched references / total actual references). This metric naturally encourages covering all valid answers without over-generating.

This robust methodology ensures that A2SEARCH not only learns to answer questions correctly but also to navigate and articulate the inherent ambiguities, leading to a more comprehensive and human-like QA experience. The framework is instantiated with the Qwen2.5 model family (3B, 7B) for experiments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments comprehensively evaluate A2SEARCH on eight open-domain QA benchmarks, comprising four multi-hop datasets (MuSiQue, HotpotQA, 2Wiki, Bamboogle) and four general QA datasets (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, AmbigQA). Both Exact Match (EM) and LMJudge metrics are used for evaluation, with consistent conclusions.

Key findings include:

1. **State-of-the-Art Performance, Especially on Multi-Hop QA:**
 - A2SEARCH achieves new state-of-the-art performance across the benchmarks. On the four multi-hop datasets, **A2SEARCH-7B yields an average AnsF1@1 score of 48.4%** (EM), significantly outperforming much larger baselines, including **ReSearch-32B (46.2%)**. Even A2SEARCH-3B achieves competitive results (43.1% AnsF1@1).
 - This demonstrates that explicitly embracing ambiguity and training with appropriate reward signals can lead to substantial performance gains, even with smaller model sizes.

2. **Superior Efficiency with Single Rollout:**
 - A2SEARCH achieves its strong performance with only a **single greedy decoding rollout** (AnsF1@1), whereas prior methods typically require three or more sampled rollouts (AnsF1@3 or Recall@3) to reach comparable recall levels.
 - For instance, A2SEARCH-7B’s AnsF1@1 (48.4%) surpasses ReSearch-7B’s AnsF1@3 (42.1%) and often matches or exceeds 32B models' Recall@3. This highlights significant efficiency improvements in inference, as fewer generative steps are needed.
 - A2SEARCH-3B requires only 2.16 tool calls on average to achieve 44.7% Recall@1 (rptc = 0.21), comparable to Search-R1-32B's efficiency.

3. **Robust Ambiguity Resolution and Generalization:**
 - On **AmbigQA**, a human-annotated benchmark for ambiguous questions, A2SEARCH (trained without AmbigQA data) surpasses specialized baselines (AbgSearch) trained directly on AmbigQA's curated training set. This powerfully validates the robustness and transferability of the automated data generation pipeline and the ambiguity-aware RL approach.
 - Analysis shows that A2SEARCH-7B generates an average of 1.51 answers per question, while A2SEARCH-3B generates 1.23, indicating that the models learn to produce multiple answers when warranted by the inherent ambiguity of the question. MuSiQue, with a high inherent ambiguity ratio, sees the highest average answer count.

4. **Prevalence of Ambiguity in Benchmarks:**
 - The data generation pipeline's initial analysis revealed that a substantial portion of existing benchmark questions are ambiguous (e.g., 27.6% of MuSiQue's training examples, 19.3% in NQ). This empirical evidence underscores the practical importance of the problem A2SEARCH addresses.

5. **Stable Training Dynamics and Generalization to Base Models:**
 - The training process for A2SEARCH models (both 3B and 7B) exhibits stable and consistent improvements in AnsF1 and Recall without premature entropy collapse, indicating reliable learning dynamics.
 - The framework also successfully generalizes to Qwen2.5-Base models, achieving strong results, especially when augmented with an entropy regularization term to stabilize training.

In summary, A2SEARCH not only achieves new performance benchmarks but fundamentally shifts the paradigm of QA by proving the tangible benefits of explicitly acknowledging and handling question ambiguity, all while doing so in an automated and efficient manner.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The A2SEARCH research makes several significant contributions and has substantial potential impact on the field of Question Answering and Large Language Model development:

1. **Paradigm Shift in QA Evaluation and Training:** The most profound impact of A2SEARCH is its challenge to the long-standing "single-answer dogma" in QA. By demonstrating that ambiguity is pervasive in existing benchmarks and that models can be effectively trained to recognize and respond to it, A2SEARCH advocates for a fundamental shift in how QA systems are designed, trained, and evaluated. This could lead to a new generation of QA benchmarks and metrics that better reflect real-world complexities.

2. **More Reliable and Human-Aligned QA Systems:** By explicitly embracing ambiguity and providing multiple valid answers, A2SEARCH creates QA systems that are more robust, informative, and aligned with human expectations. Users often encounter questions with multiple plausible interpretations or answers, and a system that can articulate these nuances is inherently more trustworthy and helpful than one that arbitrarily commits to a single, potentially incomplete or misleading, response. This increases the reliability and perceived intelligence of AI agents.

3. **Scalable and Cost-Effective Ambiguity Handling:** The introduction of an entirely automated pipeline for identifying ambiguous questions and generating alternative answers (via trajectory sampling and evidence verification) is a major breakthrough. This eliminates the bottleneck of costly and time-consuming manual annotation, making ambiguity-aware QA scalable to large datasets and diverse domains. This is particularly impactful for multi-hop QA, where manual annotation is exceptionally complex.

4. **Advancement in Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** The carefully designed AnsF1 reward, which naturally accommodates multiple answers, is a novel contribution to RL-based training of LLMs. This reward mechanism provides precise and appropriate signals for models to balance precision and recall in multi-answer settings, opening new avenues for designing RL objectives for complex, nuanced language generation tasks. The use of GRPO further highlights effective RL strategies for LLMs.

5. **Improved Efficiency in Complex Reasoning:** A2SEARCH's ability to achieve state-of-the-art performance with a single greedy rollout, often outperforming larger models requiring multiple samples, signifies a crucial improvement in inference efficiency. This is vital for deploying LLM-based QA systems in real-world applications where latency and computational cost are significant concerns. The analysis of "recall per tool call" also points to more effective utilization of reasoning steps.

6. **Broader Applicability:** The principles and methodology introduced by A2SEARCH—automated ambiguity detection, multi-answer reward functions, and efficient RL training—are highly generalizable. They could be extended to other knowledge-intensive tasks, conversational AI, and even creative generation, where understanding and articulating multiple perspectives or interpretations is valuable.

In conclusion, A2SEARCH is a highly impactful work that moves beyond incremental improvements in QA performance. It addresses a foundational problem, offering a practical and scalable solution that can lead to more sophisticated, trustworthy, and human-centric AI systems capable of navigating the rich ambiguities of human language and knowledge.
