# OG-VLA: Orthographic Image Generation for 3D-Aware Vision-Language Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> OG-VLA presents a robot policy architecture that combines the generalization of Vision-Language Action (VLA) models with the 3D precision of keyframe-based policies by generating action annotations on canonical orthographic images. This approach achieves state-of-the-art generalization on simulation benchmarks and demonstrates data-efficient real-world adaptation with only a few demonstrations.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Action (VLA) models offer strong generalization to novel instructions and objects but lack the 3D spatial reasoning needed for precise robotic manipulation.
- 3D-aware keyframe-based policies provide precision and robustness to camera/object pose variations but often struggle to generalize to unseen objects or instructions.
- Existing state-of-the-art VLA models typically require massive datasets (hundreds of thousands to millions of demonstrations), making data collection costly and time-consuming.

## Method

- **OG-VLA** utilizes a multi-modal Vision and ==Language Model== (LLM with visual inputs) that processes natural language instructions and visual tokens from canonical orthographic views.
- The system constructs a 3D point cloud from multi-view RGBD observations, which is then rendered into fixed canonical orthographic projections to provide view-invariant 3D scene awareness.
- Instead of direct 3D pose prediction, the LLM generates 'image tokens' that are decoded by an image diffusion model to create annotated RGB images on canonical views, from which precise 6-DOF end-effector poses and gripper states are extracted.

## Results

- On the ARNOLD benchmark, OG-VLA achieved an average success rate of **37.7%** on the Novel Pose split (**10.8%** relative improvement over PerAct) and demonstrated significant gains (up to **53.8%** relative improvement) on Novel Object, Scene, and State generalization splits in simulation.
- OG-VLA dramatically outperformed other state-of-the-art VLAs (e.g., π0-FAST) on ARNOLD's "Pickup Object" task, reaching **90.0%** success on the Novel Object split compared to **50.0%** for baselines.
- The model adapted to four real-world manipulation tasks using only 3-5 demonstrations per task (22 total), showcasing strong generalization to novel object/scene variations (e.g., **100%** on novel pose, **80%** on novel object for "Pickup Object"), outperforming a VLA baseline that largely failed.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing actions as generated images on canonical 3D views allows an LLM to output precise spatial information, enhancing manipulation accuracy and robustness while preserving generalization.
> - Orthographic projections remove perspective distortion, making the system inherently invariant to input camera poses and facilitating consistent action prediction.
> - Combining pre-trained LLMs and vision foundation models with SE(3) data augmentation and a 3D-aware action representation enables strong generalization with significantly less training data than traditional large-scale VLAs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation is rapidly advancing, with a significant push towards developing policies that are both precise and generalizable. This paper directly addresses a critical dichotomy within this landscape: the trade-offs between **Vision-Language Action models (VLAs)** and **3D-aware keyframe-based policies**.

**Vision-Language Action (VLA) models** (e.g., PaLM-E, RT-2, OpenVLA) have revolutionized robot learning by leveraging vast pre-trained knowledge from large language models (LLMs) and vision foundation models. These models excel at understanding natural language instructions and generalizing to novel objects and scenarios unseen in training, often by mapping language and visual inputs to abstract action tokens or continuous control signals. However, they typically operate on single RGB camera views, making them sensitive to variations in camera and robot poses. This lack of explicit 3D spatial reasoning often hinders their ability to perform precise manipulation tasks, requiring massive datasets (e.g., 900k+ demonstrations for OpenVLA) to achieve robustness.

In contrast, **3D-aware keyframe-based policies** (e.g., PerAct, RVT, Act3D) are designed for precision and robustness to camera and object pose variations. They achieve this by building explicit 3D scene representations (e.g., voxel maps, canonical orthographic views, point clouds) and predicting 6-DOF (degrees of freedom) end-effector poses. These methods learn effectively from fewer demonstrations and achieve state-of-the-art performance on precise manipulation tasks within seen environments. Their primary limitation, however, is their tendency to overfit to training scenes and objects, struggling to generalize to novel instructions or unseen concepts, a strength where VLAs excel.

OG-VLA positions itself as a crucial bridge between these two paradigms. It seeks to **combine the generalization capabilities of VLAs with the 3D robustness and precision of keyframe-based policies**. This integration is a significant area of active research, as researchers strive to develop robotic systems that can both understand complex human instructions and execute them with high fidelity in dynamic, real-world environments. The paper contributes by demonstrating a concrete architecture and learning framework that achieves this synergy, particularly through its novel approach of using orthographic image generation for action prediction within a VLA framework. Furthermore, it addresses the data efficiency challenge, aiming to achieve VLA-like generalization with significantly less training data than typical large-scale VLA models.

The work also relates to **generative image and video models for robotics**, building on ideas where robots learn from generated visual annotations (e.g., Genima, RT-Trajectory). OG-VLA extends this by generating annotations on 3D canonical views, which simplifies decoding 3D poses and improves generalization through effective SE(3) data augmentation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of OG-VLA is to **develop a robust robot policy architecture that combines the strengths of Vision-Language Action (VLA) models with those of 3D-aware policies**. Specifically, the authors aim to address the following challenges and motivations:

- **Bridging the Generalization-Precision Gap:** VLAs offer excellent generalization to novel instructions, objects, and scenes but often lack the precision and 3D robustness required for complex manipulation. Conversely, 3D-aware policies are precise and robust in 3D but struggle to generalize beyond their training data. OG-VLA seeks to create a unified framework that enjoys both benefits.
- **Enhancing 3D Spatial Reasoning in VLAs:** Traditional VLAs often process single RGB inputs, leading to sensitivity to camera and robot pose variations and limited explicit 3D reasoning. OG-VLA aims to imbue VLAs with inherent 3D awareness by leveraging multi-view RGBD observations and a canonical 3D representation.
- **Improving Data Efficiency:** Many state-of-the-art VLAs require massive datasets (hundreds of thousands to millions of demonstrations). OG-VLA is motivated to achieve strong generalization with significantly less training data, making it more practical for real-world deployment where data collection is expensive.
- **Enabling Quasi-Static Manipulation:** The focus is on tasks decomposable into a sequence of end-effector keyframes (e.g., pick-and-place, opening doors), which are common in many industrial and household applications but still pose challenges for robust generalization.
- **Leveraging Foundation Models:** The work is motivated by the success of pre-trained large language models (LLMs) and vision foundation models, seeking to effectively adapt their generalization power to the specific demands of robotic manipulation.
- **Robustness to Real-World Variability:** The system aims to be robust to distractors, changing lighting conditions, and variations in object appearances and placements, as commonly encountered in real-world scenarios.

In essence, OG-VLA strives to create a robot policy that can understand "what to do" from language (VLA strength) and "how to do it" precisely in 3D space (3D-aware policy strength), all while being data-efficient and adaptable to novel situations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OG-VLA introduces a novel architecture and learning framework that integrates multi-modal inputs, 3D scene representation, and generative models to predict robust 6-DOF end-effector keyframes. The system comprises four main components, working in an end-to-end fashion:

**A. Multi-Modal Vision and Language Model Core:**
At its heart, OG-VLA uses a Large Language Model (LLM) (specifically, Vicuna-7b v1.5) that processes both text and visual inputs.
- **Input Tokens:** The LLM receives tokenized natural language instructions (from a text tokenizer) and visual tokens (CLS tokens from a visual encoder, mapped to LLM space via an MLP projection).
- **Output Tokens:** The LLM generates a sequence that includes special "image tokens" representing the next robot action, alongside optional text responses.

**B. 3D-Aware Reasoning with Orthogonal Orthographic Projections:**
To provide the LLM with 3D awareness and ensure view invariance, OG-VLA employs a sophisticated 3D scene processing pipeline:
- **Point Cloud Construction:** Multiple input RGBD observations (RGB image, depth image, camera pose, camera intrinsics) from diverse views are unprojected into a single, aggregated 3D point cloud in a canonical workspace.
- **Orthographic Rendering:** This point cloud is then rendered from a fixed set of canonical orthographic views (e.g., front, top, left, right directions). Orthographic projection is crucial as it removes perspective distortion, ensuring consistency and making the system invariant to input camera poses. These rendered images serve as the primary visual input to the VLM system.

**C. Image Token Decoding for Action Prediction:**
Instead of directly predicting precise 3D poses as text (which often lacks precision), OG-VLA decodes the LLM's "image tokens" into action annotations on the canonical orthographic images using an image diffusion model.
- **Visual Encoding:** Each canonical orthographic view is processed by a Visual Encoder (ImageBind) to obtain a CLS token and a sequence of image patch embeddings.
- **Image Generation:** An Image Diffusion Model (StableDiffusion 1.5) is conditioned by the LLM's output image tokens (projected to the visual embedding space) and the visual features (patch embeddings, CLS token) from the canonical views. The diffusion model generates an RGB image for each canonical view, with overlaid annotations encoding the gripper's position and orientation. A "faded reconstruction" mode for generation is favored, where actions are overlaid on a dimly visible scene reconstruction, reserving distinct color ranges for actions and easing decoding while still providing scene context.
- **3D Pose Extraction:**
 - **Position:** The 3D end-effector position is inferred by interpreting red Gaussian heatmaps across all canonical views. An optimization problem is solved to find the 3D point that best explains the predictions in all views.
 - **Rotation:** Euler rotations (roll, pitch, yaw) are encoded using different colored Gaussian distributions (yellow, blue, green) overlaid on specific canonical views (e.g., x-axis rotation on front view, y-axis on left/right, z-axis on top). The most likely pixel locations from these Gaussians, relative to the translation point, are used to compute rotation angles.
 - **Gripper State:** A binary colored hotspot in the top-left of the image indicates the gripper's open/close state.

**D. Training and Implementation Details:**
- **Initialization:** OG-VLA is initialized from X-VILA's pre-trained weights, leveraging its cross-modality alignment capabilities.
- **Fine-tuning:** The Visual Encoder (ImageBind) is frozen. The LLM, input/output projection networks, and Image Generator are fine-tuned end-to-end using DeepSpeed for efficiency.
- **Data Augmentation:** Each ground-truth keyframe in the training data is augmented with multiple SE(3) transformations (translation and rotation perturbations), significantly increasing the effective training data and enhancing robustness to pose variations.
- **Deployment:** The system predicts 6-DOF end-effector keyframes sequentially. A motion planner executes the predicted poses, and new observations are fed back into the system for the next keyframe.

This comprehensive approach allows OG-VLA to combine language understanding with precise 3D control, making it robust to various environmental changes and capable of generalizing to unseen task variations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OG-VLA demonstrates state-of-the-art performance and strong generalization capabilities across diverse robotic manipulation benchmarks in both simulation and real-world settings.

**A. Simulation Experiments:**

1. **ARNOLD Benchmark (Generalization to Unseen Objects and Environments):**
 - **Overall Performance:** OG-VLA significantly outperforms baselines like PerAct and 6D-CLIPort, achieving state-of-the-art results. At 100k iterations, it shows an average success rate of 37.7% on the Novel Pose split (seen objects/scenes), representing a 10.8% relative improvement over PerAct.
 - **Generalization Splits:** OG-VLA shows even more pronounced improvements on the generalization test splits:
 - **Novel Object:** 24.8% (vs. 16.7% for PerAct) at 100k iterations, a 48.5% relative improvement.
 - **Novel Scene:** 28.8% (vs. 21.0% for PerAct) at 100k iterations, a 37.1% relative improvement.
 - **Novel State:** 10.0% (vs. 6.5% for PerAct) at 100k iterations, a 53.8% relative improvement.
 - **Comparison with VLAs (π0-FAST, π0.5):** OG-VLA dramatically outperforms other state-of-the-art VLAs (e.g., π0-FAST, π0.5) on the "Pickup Object" task in ARNOLD. For instance, on the Novel Object split, OG-VLA achieves 90.0% success, compared to 50.0% for the best VLA baseline (π0-FAST Full). This strongly validates the benefit of injecting 3D awareness into VLAs while preserving their generalization.
 - **Latency:** While OG-VLA has higher per-step inference latency (4.5s) compared to continuous control VLAs (0.2-0.4s) due to its complex generative process, its keyframe-based nature means it requires far fewer inference steps per episode. Consequently, its overall episode time (10.2s) is significantly lower than VLA baselines (70.3s - 103.1s), making it more efficient for tasks where continuous high-frequency control is not strictly necessary.

2. **COLOSSEUM Benchmark (Robustness to Perturbations):**
 - OG-VLA achieves a task-averaged success rate of 10.5% on the challenging "all perturbation" test set, which simultaneously alters object/table/background appearance, lighting, camera pose, and adds distractors.
 - This represents a 45.8% relative improvement over the next best baseline (RVT at 7.2%). Although the absolute success rate remains low, the significant relative improvement highlights OG-VLA's enhanced robustness to diverse scene variations. The authors attribute the overall low absolute performance to the extreme difficulty of COLOSSEUM tasks, involving longer keyframe sequences leading to error accumulation.

**B. Real-World Experiments:**
- **Data Efficiency:** OG-VLA successfully adapts to four real-world manipulation tasks from as few as 3 to 5 demonstrations per task (22 demos total).
- **Generalization:** It exhibits strong generalization to novel object variations (e.g., unseen shapes/colors) and novel scene variations (e.g., distractors, lighting, background changes). For example, on "Pickup Object," it achieves 100% on novel pose, 80% on novel object, and 90% on novel scene.
- **Baseline Comparison:** The chosen VLA baseline (π0-FAST Full) failed on almost all real-world tasks, learning only to reach a block with 30% success. This stark contrast underscores OG-VLA's superior ability to learn from limited data and generalize in real-world settings, likely due to its 3D representation and SE(3) data augmentation.

**C. Design Choice Ablations:**
- **Action Prediction:** Direct text-based action prediction and MLP-decoded action tokens both failed to achieve effective policies under limited training data, highlighting the necessity of the image generation approach for precise manipulation.
- **Image Generation Modes:** Generating action annotations on a "faded reconstruction" of the scene performed best on generalization splits, offering a balance between scene context and clear action annotation.
- **Architecture Components:**
 - Tiling orthographic views (as opposed to batch processing them) unexpectedly decreased performance, suggesting the LLM effectively handles inter-view consistency.
 - Removing the LLM significantly reduced performance, underscoring its critical role in providing strong priors and conditioning consistent action generation across views.
 - Bypassing the instruction directly to the Image Generator also led to a performance drop, indicating that the LLM's image token outputs encode crucial task-relevant information that benefits action generation.

These findings collectively demonstrate that OG-VLA successfully integrates the generalization of VLAs with the precision and robustness of 3D-aware policies, offering a data-efficient and high-performing solution for robotic manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OG-VLA represents a significant step forward in the field of robot learning, particularly in bridging the long-standing gap between generalizable high-level reasoning and precise low-level control. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering a Hybrid VLA-3D-Aware Architecture:** The core contribution of OG-VLA is its novel framework that effectively combines the strengths of Vision-Language Action models (VLAs) with 3D-aware policies. This hybrid approach allows robots to interpret complex natural language instructions and generalize to unseen objects and environments, while simultaneously executing actions with the precision and robustness enabled by explicit 3D scene understanding. This fusion addresses a critical limitation of previous systems, which either excelled at generalization or precision but rarely both.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** By leveraging foundation models for language and vision, and integrating them with a 3D-aware representation through canonical orthographic views and image generation for action prediction, OG-VLA achieves state-of-the-art generalization on challenging benchmarks like ARNOLD and COLOSSEUM. Its ability to handle novel objects, scenes, and environmental perturbations (camera poses, lighting, distractors) is a major breakthrough, moving closer to robots that can operate reliably in diverse real-world settings.
- **Data Efficiency for Robot Learning:** Unlike many data-hungry VLAs that require millions of demonstrations, OG-VLA demonstrates strong performance and generalization with significantly less training data (e.g., only 3-5 demonstrations for real-world tasks). This data efficiency is crucial for practical robot deployment, reducing the prohibitive cost and time associated with large-scale data collection. This makes kinesthetic teaching and rapid adaptation to new domains much more feasible.
- **Practical Applicability:** The focus on quasi-static manipulation tasks (e.g., pick-and-place, opening/closing containers) covers a wide range of industrial and household applications. OG-VLA's robustness and generalization capabilities could accelerate the deployment of intelligent robots in areas like manufacturing, logistics, and assistive robotics, enabling them to handle unexpected variations in objects or environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** OG-VLA's architecture provides a robust foundation for future research directions. Its modularity allows for further improvements in each component (e.g., more advanced LLMs, better visual encoders, more efficient diffusion models). The identified limitations, such as handling long-horizon tasks, severe occlusions, and optimizing computational cost, present clear avenues for subsequent work, including exploring co-training techniques with larger datasets or developing hierarchical control structures.
- **New Paradigm for Action Representation:** Representing actions as generated images on canonical 3D views, rather than raw text tokens or continuous control vectors, offers a powerful and visually interpretable way for LLMs to output precise spatial information. This approach not only aids in precision but also facilitates reasoning about free space and allows for effective SE(3) data augmentation, which is difficult with purely image-based or text-based action representations.

**Potential Impact:**
The potential impact of OG-VLA is substantial. It pushes the boundaries of what's achievable in general-purpose robot manipulation, paving the way for:
- **More capable and adaptable robots:** Robots that can understand complex human commands and execute them reliably in dynamic, unstructured environments.
- **Reduced development costs:** Lower data requirements mean faster development cycles for new robotic applications.
- **Broader adoption of robotics:** By making robots more robust and easier to program, OG-VLA could facilitate their integration into various sectors where flexibility and adaptability are key.

While acknowledging limitations like computational cost, potential issues with severe occlusions, and the focus on keyframe-based policies, OG-VLA offers a compelling demonstration of how intelligent integration of cutting-edge AI models can lead to practical, generalizable, and robust robotic capabilities.
