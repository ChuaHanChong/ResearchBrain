# Unified Locomotion Transformer with Simultaneous Sim-to-Real Transfer for Quadrupeds

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from NVIDIA AI Technology Centre and Nanyang Technological University developed the Unified Locomotion Transformer (ULT), a framework that integrates knowledge transfer and policy optimization into a single network for quadrupedal robots. This approach enables zero-shot sim-to-real deployment on a Unitree A1 robot, demonstrating robust locomotion across various terrains while matching the performance of an "Oracle" policy with comparable data efficiency.

## Problem

- Traditional deep Reinforcement Learning (RL) teacher-student frameworks for quadruped locomotion involve complex, sequential training stages that are often data-inefficient.
- The sequential knowledge distillation process limits the student policy's robustness and generalization, especially when deploying data-hungry transformer-based controllers.
- Existing transformer-based locomotion controllers require extensive data generation and multi-stage supervised training, increasing computational cost and pipeline complexity for sim-to-real transfer.

## Method

- Introduced the **Unified Locomotion Transformer** (ULT) framework, which integrates policy optimization and knowledge transfer into a single transformer-based network.
- Developed an "Action Mixer" mechanism to dynamically combine teacher-guided and student-driven actions during training, facilitating simultaneous optimization of both policies.
- Incorporated privilege information as an additional modality in the transformer's input sequence, enabling continuous, indirect guidance during the unified training phase.

## Results

- The ULT framework achieved locomotion performance comparable to an "Oracle" policy, demonstrating high data efficiency in simultaneously optimizing teacher and student policies.
- It outperformed existing supervised and joint knowledge transfer baselines, showing enhanced robustness and generalization for both teacher and student policies across five terrain types.
- Successful zero-shot sim-to-real transfer was achieved on a Unitree A1 robot, enabling robust omnidirectional locomotion on diverse physical terrains without additional fine-tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A balanced exploration ratio (optimal at "alpha"=0.6) between teacher-guided and student-driven actions during unified training is essential for achieving high performance in both policies.
> - Integrating next state-action prediction and action imitation losses with the PPO objective is critical for the transformer to efficiently learn state transition dynamics and leverage privilege-guided actions.
> - Causal masking in the transformer architecture enables the student policy to operate solely on proprioceptive observations for zero-shot real-world deployment, despite benefiting from privilege information during training.
