# SplatCtrl: Perception-Action Coupling via Gaussian Scene Representations and Reactive Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> SplatCtrl unifies real-time 3D Gaussian Splatting reconstruction with reactive manipulator control. Voxel-based filtering plus dynamic Gaussian relocation keeps a fixed Gaussian budget updated from RGB-D streams; a Gaussian Process distance field turns isotropic Gaussians into a continuous, differentiable signed distance and collision-probability field feeding control barrier constraints in a QP-IK controller. It reaches 95.01% planning success versus 32.83% for plain 3D-GS and 95.8% on a real arm.

## Problem

- Point clouds are sparse and occlusion-prone, voxel grids trade resolution against compute, and neural radiance fields are too slow and static for changing robot workspaces.
- Robotics uses of Gaussian Splatting rely on offline fitting, dense multi-view input, or scene-specific optimization, so they cannot supply low-latency reactive motion under change.
- Splatting artifacts and free-space floaters create false collisions, and the min-over-Gaussians distance is discontinuous and non-differentiable, blocking gradient-based safe control.

## Method

- ==Isotropic 8-parameter Gaussians== fitted from RGB-D with color, depth and opacity-regularized losses, then pruned by opacity threshold and a GPU occupancy grid, with dead Gaussians relocated under a fixed count budget.
- A ==Gaussian Process distance field== extended to sphere radii yields a proxy ==SDF== with a log-shift reparameterization for numerical stability, variance-based collision probability, and a mass-lumped diagonal kernel approximation reducing cost to linear.
- Robot and human Gaussians are identified by axis-aligned bounding box plus 2-bit voxel entity encoding, and the SDF and its normalized gradient enter a ==QP-IK controller== (**SplatCtrl**) as external and self-collision ==barrier constraints==.

## Results

- On DTU MVS, PSNR of **23.33** versus **23.27** for 3D-GS while holding a fixed **10,000** Gaussian budget against the baseline's **50,000-100,000**, with fewer artifacts.
- Across **942** simulated planning trials, success averaged **95.01/92.32/96.07%** at 2/4/6 cameras versus **32.83/48.13/19.75%** for 3D-GS; reconstruction ran near **240 Hz** single-view, QP above **100 Hz**.
- Real Franka FR3 runs over **twelve** unseen scenes gave **95.8%** success for both QP-IK and BiRRT, QP-IK keeping larger obstacle clearance; the **8**-participant study beat safety-monitored stopping on time and separation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rendering fidelity is a poor proxy for control utility: near-identical PSNR hid a threefold planning success gap driven by free-space artifacts causing false collisions.
> - Casting a discrete min-distance field as a Gaussian Process gives differentiable gradients and calibrated collision probability, making implicit representations directly usable inside barrier-function controllers.
> - Fixed representation budgets with relocation beat unbounded densification for closed-loop use; more camera views can hurt when the fitting iteration budget stays constant.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulators have demonstrated capabilities in structured environments where tasks are repetitive and object locations are predefined. However, their deployment in unstructured, dynamic real-world settings remains challenging. Such environments are characterized by unpredictability, with objects and obstacles that can shift or emerge unexpectedly, necessitating real-time adaptation of robot motion based on sensory input.

A critical aspect for robotic manipulation in these environments is the ability to construct and maintain reliable representations of the surroundings. Traditional methods for scene representation, such as point clouds and voxel grids, face limitations. Point clouds are sparse and can suffer from occlusions and temporal inconsistency. Voxel grids offer structured occupancy reasoning but struggle with balancing resolution and computational efficiency. More recent approaches, like implicit neural representations such as Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs), provide high-fidelity scene representations but are computationally intensive and lack adaptability to dynamic scenes.

The emergence of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) has offered a more efficient explicit scene representation, primarily for high-quality rendering of static scenes. Recent efforts have explored extending 3D-GS to robotics for tasks like pose estimation, SLAM, and manipulation. However, these applications often rely on offline predictions, pre-fitted Gaussians, or expensive scene-specific optimization, which restricts their applicability in dynamic, unstructured settings that require low-latency, reactive robot motion. Existing methods in this area have also demonstrated issues such as inconsistent rendering and free-space artifacts, which can compromise the spatial accuracy essential for robotic manipulation.

The concept of Perception-Action Coupling, which involves continuous interaction between sensory input and motor output, is fundamental for robots to adapt their motion to environmental feedback. Conventional control methods, relying on prior knowledge of obstacles, are insufficient for dynamic scenarios. While some methods leverage motion planning networks trained on diverse datasets, achieving greater autonomy requires scene representations that capture both spatial and temporal dynamics, coupled with direct mappings to reactive motion without extensive offline training or datasets.

SplatCtrl aims to bridge this gap by extending 3D-GS to support real-time scene reconstruction from RGB-D streams in dynamic environments and by integrating this representation directly into a reactive control framework. The work seeks to provide a unified solution that addresses the limitations of existing scene representations regarding efficiency, adaptability, and direct utility for real-time collision-free robotic control in previously unseen and continuously changing environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop SplatCtrl, a unified framework that enables real-time scene reconstruction and reactive robot motion generation for collision-free robotic arm control in previously unseen and continuously changing environments. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and objectives:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Existing Scene Representations for Robotics**: Current scene representation methods, including classical approaches (point clouds, voxel grids) and modern implicit representations (NeRFs, 3D-GS), present trade-offs between fidelity, efficiency, and adaptability. While 3D-GS is efficient for static scene rendering, its direct application to dynamic robotic tasks is limited by its inability to handle continuous environmental changes, ensure physical consistency, provide real-time responsiveness, and integrate multi-modal data efficiently. The objective is to extend 3D-GS to overcome these limitations for robotic applications.

2. **Enabling Autonomous, Real-Time Scene Reconstruction from Sensor Feedback**: Robotic manipulators in unstructured settings require continuous, autonomous updates to their environmental understanding. The motivation is to develop a method that can efficiently generate and update scene reconstructions from live RGB-D sensor streams, adapting to dynamic changes in the environment without requiring offline training or prior knowledge of the scene. This involves developing strategies for dynamic management of the Gaussian elements.

3. **Achieving Tight Perception-Action Coupling for Reactive Control**: Conventional robotic control methods often rely on predefined maps or obstacle models, leading to difficulties in adapting to unexpected changes. More advanced methods may use trained motion planners, but these are often data-dependent. The motivation is to establish a principled integration between perception (the scene representation) and control, allowing the robot to react smoothly and reliably to real-time scene changes. This requires a scene representation that directly supports both motion planning and reactive control, providing stable and differentiable collision information.

4. **Developing Continuous Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) from Isotropic Gaussians**: Traditional SDFs are crucial for collision avoidance but can be challenging to derive from modern implicit or explicit scene representations while maintaining differentiability and numerical stability. The objective is to formulate a method to extract continuous SDFs from isotropic Gaussians. This approach aims to bridge the gap between classical distance fields and learning-based representations, ensuring computational efficiency, numerical robustness, and the provision of smooth gradient information for reactive control.

5. **Validation in Diverse and Dynamic Robotic Scenarios**: The framework aims to be robust and universally applicable to various unstructured settings. A key objective is to validate its effectiveness across different experimental setups: simulations, physical robot experiments, and especially in shared human-robot workspaces. This validation aims to demonstrate the system's capability to achieve full 6-DoF, collision-free robotic control while simultaneously reconstructing scenes in unknown and continuously evolving environments, including interactions with humans.

In essence, the motivation is to empower robotic manipulators with the ability to perceive and act in continuously changing, unknown environments with a high degree of autonomy, safety, and efficiency, by establishing a direct and robust link between real-time sensory perception and reactive motion control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SplatCtrl is presented as a unified framework designed for real-time scene reconstruction and reactive robot motion generation using RGB-D image streams. The approach focuses on isotropic 3D Gaussians for scene representation, adapting them for dynamic environments and integrating them into a control framework.

**A. 3D Gaussian Scene Representation**
The scene is represented by a set of isotropic 3D Gaussians, $G = \{G_i = (p_i, r_i, c_i, \alpha_i)\}_{i=1}^N$. Each Gaussian $G_i$ is defined by its 3D position $p_i$, RGB color $c_i$, radius $r_i$, and opacity $\alpha_i$. The covariance matrix $\Sigma_i$ is simplified to $r_i^2I$ (spherical symmetry) for computational efficiency. The framework utilizes differentiable rendering to compute pixel color $\hat{C}$ and depth $\hat{D}$ from any viewpoint, based on alpha-blending of Gaussians.

The scene is optimized using a loss function that combines color, depth, and regularization terms:
$L = \lambda_{color} L_{color}(\hat{C}, C_{gt}) + \lambda_{depth} L_{depth}(\hat{D}, D_{gt}) + \lambda_{reg} L_{reg}$,
where $L_1$ and $L_{D-SSIM}$ are used for color comparison, and $L_1$ for depth. The regularization term $L_{reg} = \Sigma_i |\alpha_i|$ encourages a smaller number of Gaussians.

**B. Gaussian Splatting for Robotics**
To enable continuous updates in dynamic environments, several extensions to standard 3D-GS are introduced:

- **Initialization and Radius Setting**: Gaussians are initialized from RGB-D point cloud data with an initial opacity of 0.5. The radius is set based on pixel size, clamped to $r = \min(D_{gt}/f, r_{max})$.
- **Dynamic Gaussian Management**:
 - **Adding Gaussians**: A mask $M_{add}$ is generated to identify regions where new Gaussians are needed, specifically where ground truth depth is closer than rendered depth and the depth error exceeds a threshold relative to the median depth error.
 - **Filtering and Removal**: To mitigate artifacts and false collision positives, Gaussians with opacity below a threshold ($\alpha_{min}=0.1$) are filtered out. A GPU-based occupancy grid with voxel sizes at least as large as the maximum Gaussian radius is used to remove Gaussians located in free ($<0.3$) or uncertain ($<0.7$) regions, and those outside the robot's reachable workspace.
 - **Relocation**: Gaussians are dynamically relocated based on the $M_{add}$ mask and RGB-D data to adapt to newly appearing objects.
 - **Gaussian Budget**: A maximum number of Gaussians is enforced. If below the limit, new and "dead" Gaussians (those removed by filtering) are repositioned. If the limit is reached, only dead Gaussians are replaced to maintain a fixed count.
- **Robot Segmentation**: To enable obstacle avoidance for the robot itself, Gaussians representing the robot are identified. Instead of complex learning methods, an occupancy grid and the robot’s axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) are used. Voxels intersecting the AABB identify relevant Gaussians through a fixed-radius search. A 2-bit encoding categorizes entities (e.g., objects, robot) within the voxel grid. A removal mask is applied to Gaussians previously classified as robot that have transitioned to "object" status to prevent "trails."

**C. SDF and Collision Probability**
The framework derives Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) and collision probabilities from the Gaussian representation.

- **SDF from Isotropic Gaussians**: Initially, SDF is defined as $SDF(x) = \min_i (\|x - p_i\|_2 - r_i)$. However, this can be discontinuous and non-differentiable.
- **Gaussian Process Distance Field (GPDF) Extension**: To address stability and differentiability, the Gaussian Process Distance Field (GPDF) is extended to incorporate Gaussian radii. The occupancy field $o(x)$ is modeled as a Gaussian process, $o(x) \sim GP(0, k(x, p))$, using a Matérn kernel. The inferred occupancy $\hat{o}(x)$ is computed, and the SDF $d_{SDF}(x)$ is defined as the inverse function of the kernel applied to $\hat{o}(x)$. The gradient of the SDF is derived, and then normalized to satisfy the Eikonal equation.
- **Numerical Stability**: The GPDF faces numerical instability for points far from the Gaussian centers due to the logarithmic inverse function. A reformulation using $d(x, p_1) - l \log(\dots)$ is introduced to mitigate this, where $d(x, p_1)$ is the minimum distance between query point $x$ and all Gaussian centers.
- **Collision Probability**: The variance of the Gaussian process, $var(o(x))$, provides a measure of collision probability $P(o(x) \geq 1)$. This probability is calculated using the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution, with a scaling factor applied to the variance to ensure it approaches zero further from the Gaussians.
- **Approximation for Efficiency**: To reduce the computational complexity of matrix inversion in GP, a mass-lumped matrix approximation is applied. The kernel matrix $K(P, P)$ is approximated by a diagonal matrix $D(P, P)$ composed of its row sums. This reduces complexity from $O(n^3)$ to $O(n^2)$ for matrix inversion. Further approximation of density via a uniform voxel grid (rasterizing Gaussian centers, kernel convolution, then interpolating densities) reduces complexity to linear in the number of points and voxels.

**D. Collision Avoidance with Reactive Control**
The derived SDF and its gradient are integrated into a Quadratic Programming Inverse Kinematics (QP-IK) controller, serving as a control barrier function for reactive collision avoidance.

- **QP-IK Formulation**: The controller minimizes a cost function comprising end-effector tracking error, slack penalties, and collision avoidance terms. It is subject to forward kinematics constraints, joint limits, joint velocity limits, and external and self-collision constraints.
- **Collision Constraints**: The collision constraints are formulated as $\nabla_q d(q) \dot{q} \geq -(d(q) - \epsilon) + \delta_{slack}$, where $d(q)$ is the SDF (external or self-collision distance) and $\nabla_q d(q)$ is its gradient in joint space. The QP solver operates at over 100 Hz, with state and Gaussian updates.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The SplatCtrl framework was evaluated across simulation, physical robot experiments, and a human-robot shared workspace study.

**A. Scene Reconstruction Quality**
- **Dataset**: DTU MVS dataset (synthetic RGB-D data from depth maps of).
- **Comparison**: Benchmarked against 3D-GS. Both methods were initialized with 10,000 Gaussians and used identical loss functions. 3D-GS dynamically increased its Gaussian count (50,000–100,000), while SplatCtrl maintained a fixed budget.
- **Metric**: Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR).
- **Results**: 3D-GS achieved a PSNR of 23.27, while SplatCtrl attained 23.33. This modest improvement in PSNR was attributed primarily to SplatCtrl's Gaussian relocation algorithm, which optimized Gaussian placement in critical regions, resulting in enhanced geometric accuracy and reduced artifacts.

**B. Simulation Robot Experiments**
- **Environments**: Two simulation environments (Cubby and Tabletop) across 942 trials, with previously unseen configurations.
- **Task**: Sampling-based motion planner (BiRRT) used the reconstructed scene to generate and execute collision-free trajectories from start to goal.
- **Comparison**: SplatCtrl's scene representation was benchmarked against 3D-GS. GPDF with 2 cm voxels was used for collision checking.
- **Ablation Study**: The number of RGB-D cameras (2–6) for reconstruction was varied.
- **Metrics**: Success rate (robot reaching target within 1° rotation and 3 cm translation without collisions).
- **Results**: SplatCtrl consistently outperformed the 3D-GS baseline.
 - **Average Success Rates (942 trials)**: 3D-GS w/ MP: 32.83% (2 cameras), 48.13% (4 cameras), 19.75% (6 cameras). SplatCtrl w/ MP: 95.01% (2 cameras), 92.32% (4 cameras), 96.07% (6 cameras).
 - The baseline frequently generated artifacts leading to false collisions, especially at robot's start and end-effector positions.
 - While increasing camera viewpoints could improve 3D-GS performance up to a point, it also increased computational demands, potentially introducing more artifacts with a fixed iteration budget.
 - SplatCtrl demonstrated greater robustness and efficiency, producing more stable scene representations and consistent performance, achieving approximately 240 Hz for single-view iterations with its limited Gaussian budget.

**C. Real Robot Experiments**
- **Platform**: Franka Emika Panda FR3 robotic arm.
- **Environments**: Twelve previously unseen real-world environments with diverse obstacles (geometry, spatial configuration, color, distribution).
- **Task**: Transporting a blue cube into a bowl while avoiding collisions.
- **Motion Generation Pipelines**: Evaluated with two distinct pipelines:
 1. Proposed reactive QP-IK controller.
 2. Deliberative, sampling-based BiRRT planner.
- **Trials**: Each pipeline tested twice per environment (24 trials per method).
- **Scene Reconstruction Settings**: Maximum 5,000 Gaussians, GPDF interpolation length 2 cm.
- **Metrics**: Joint-space trajectory length, end-effector error and trajectory length, minimum distance to obstacles, success rate.
- **Results**:
 - Both pipelines demonstrated high reliability with SplatCtrl.
 - BiRRT generally produced shorter joint-space trajectories in cluttered scenarios due to direct sampling.
 - QP-IK consistently minimized end-effector error and produced shorter end-effector trajectories.
 - QP-IK maintained larger minimum distances from obstacles (Figure 6c), resulting in safer motions, with one exception.
 - Both methods largely adhered to the 2 cm safety threshold. BiRRT occasionally violated it due to interpolation between sampled joint positions.
 - **Success Rate**: Each method failed once out of 24 trials, yielding a 95.8% success rate overall.

**D. Study: Human-Robot Shared Workspace**
- **Scenario**: Robot transports a yellow cube while a human operator de-stacks and reassembles cubes within the same workspace. Dynamic setting with continuous human pose changes.
- **Setup**: SplatCtrl framework with scene reconstruction and reactive motion generation. An additional Intel L515 camera (Camera 4) and human segmentation were integrated using voxel-grid intersection.
- **Baseline**: Industry-standard Safety Monitored Stopping (SMS) method (ISO 10218-1/2), where the robot halts when a human enters the workspace.
- **Participants**: Eight healthy participants without prior robotic arm experience. Four trials per participant (randomized conditions for human task proximity).
- **Metrics**: Average distance maintained from humans, task completion time.
- **Results**:
 - SplatCtrl safely avoided obstacles and adapted its motion to maintain separation from the human in all trials.
 - SplatCtrl consistently maintained a greater average distance from the human compared to the SMS baseline (Figure 8), demonstrating more proactive avoidance.
 - The SMS approach often led to the robot halting and remaining idle, reducing separation during these pauses.
 - SplatCtrl enabled faster task completion by avoiding unnecessary idle time while maintaining safety. These findings support its applicability in dynamic human-robot interaction scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SplatCtrl represents an advancement in integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting (3D-GS) beyond its conventional applications in computer graphics and visualization, specifically adapting it for real-time robotic control.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Unified Perception-Action Framework**: This work introduces a unified framework that tightly couples real-time scene reconstruction with reactive robot motion generation. This coupling is achieved without reliance on extensive datasets, offline training, or predefined maps, which distinguishes it from many data-driven or static-map-based approaches. This autonomy and adaptability are critical for deploying robots in previously unknown and continuously evolving environments.
2. **Efficient and Dynamic Scene Representation**: SplatCtrl extends 3D-GS with a hybrid voxel-based filtering and dynamic Gaussian relocation strategy. This mechanism enables efficient and continuous updating of scene reconstructions from live RGB-D streams, effectively addressing the challenge of applying 3D-GS to dynamic environments where traditional methods struggle with computational demands or adaptability. The ability to manage a fixed Gaussian budget while maintaining high performance is a significant step towards practical deployment.
3. **Principled SDF and Collision Probability from Gaussians**: The method for deriving continuous Signed Distance Functions (SDFs) from isotropic Gaussians, enhanced by a Gaussian Process Distance Field (GPDF) extension, provides stable and differentiable collision probability estimates. This bridges the gap between discrete distance fields and modern implicit representations, offering robust, continuous, and computationally efficient distance metrics. These metrics are crucial for generating smooth gradient information for reactive robotic control.
4. **Real-time Reactive Control in Unstructured Environments**: By incorporating the continuous distance metrics into control barrier functions within a Quadratic Programming Inverse Kinematics (QP-IK) controller, the framework enables smooth, reliable, and collision-free robot motion generation in real time. This capability is demonstrated across diverse and previously unseen environments, including those with dynamic obstacles and human presence.
5. **First-of-its-Kind System**: The paper reports SplatCtrl as the first real-time system capable of achieving full 6-DoF, collision-free robotic control while simultaneously reconstructing scenes from RGB-D streams in previously unknown and continuously evolving environments.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy**: By providing robots with the ability to perceive and react to dynamic changes in their surroundings in real-time, SplatCtrl has the potential to significantly enhance the autonomy of robotic manipulators in various applications, including logistics, service robotics, and manufacturing.
- **Improved Human-Robot Collaboration**: The validation in shared human-robot workspaces, demonstrating safer and more efficient interactions compared to traditional safety protocols, suggests a strong potential for fostering more seamless and productive collaborative robotics. This could lead to wider adoption of robots in environments shared with humans, such as factories and homes.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The framework provides a compact, expressive, and incrementally updatable representation that can serve as a foundation for further research in areas such as object-centric manipulation, long-term environmental understanding, and adaptive control strategies that leverage richer geometric and uncertainty information from Gaussian distributions.
- **Reduced Development Overhead**: The framework's independence from large datasets and offline training could reduce the development time and cost associated with deploying robots in new or changing environments.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors):**

1. **Scalability**: The current method's scalability to very large-scale environments and a broader range of manipulation tasks requires further investigation.
2. **Object-Level Collision Avoidance**: Explicit object-level collision avoidance for grasped items was not incorporated, limiting fine-grained and context-aware interactions. Future work will focus on an object-centric formulation.
3. **Hyperparameter Tuning and Approximations**: The approach requires hyperparameter tuning, and approximating Gaussians as spheres overlooks their full probabilistic characteristics. Future work aims to compute distances directly between Gaussian distributions to preserve richer geometric and uncertainty information.
4. **Reliance on Voxel Grids**: The current method relies on GPU-based occupancy grids for filtering. Future work intends to incorporate image-level spatial filtering to reduce this dependency.
5. **Larger-Scale Human-Robot Study**: The pilot user study on human-robot shared workspaces will be further evaluated through a larger-scale study.

Despite these limitations, SplatCtrl's demonstration of a robust, adaptive, and real-time perception-action coupling framework offers a promising direction for robotic control in dynamic and unstructured settings.
