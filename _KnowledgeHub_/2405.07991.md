# SPIN: Simultaneous Perception, Interaction and Navigation

> [!summary] Summary
> The SPIN framework enables mobile manipulators to reactively navigate and interact in complex, dynamic environments by integrating perception, interaction, and navigation into a single end-to-end policy. It employs an active visual system and a two-phase training strategy to achieve higher success rates in real-world scenarios compared to classical methods, demonstrating emergent behaviors like dynamic obstacle avoidance and whole-body coordination.

## Problem

- Current mobile manipulation approaches struggle with performing diverse, long-horizon tasks in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments.
- Classical planning methods require precise a priori environment knowledge and computational tractability, failing to adapt to dynamic changes or uncertainties.
- Modular learning solutions for mobile manipulation often lead to compounding errors, decision-making delays, and uncoordinated whole-body actions, with fixed camera views limiting perception.

## Method

![[2405.07991-method.png]]
*The training pipeline transitions from a privileged, scandot-based teacher to a vision-based student policy.*

- Develops **SPIN**, an ==end-to-end reactive framework== that integrates perception, interaction, and navigation through a single policy.
- Employs a ==two-phase training strategy==: an initial ==reinforcement learning== phase using ==scandots== for efficient low-level control learning, followed by ==supervised distillation== into a depth-image-based policy.
- Incorporates an ==active visual system==, enabling the robot to dynamically control its ==camera gaze== to acquire relevant information for navigation and interaction.

## Results

- SPIN demonstrated higher success rates in real-world deployments (**0.8** in static, **0.6** in dynamic environments) compared to a classical planning baseline (**0.6** and **0.0-0.2** respectively).
- The framework produced emergent behaviors such as dynamic obstacle avoidance, adaptive rerouting, and whole-body coordination (e.g., lowering the arm for clearance) in unseen real-world conditions.
- Simulations confirmed the necessity of active vision, with SPIN achieving **90-96%** success in hard navigation tasks where fixed-camera baselines fell below **26%** success.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The tight coupling between perception and motion is essential for effective mobile manipulation, allowing the robot to actively "move to see and see to move."
> - Active vision with an actuated camera significantly outperforms fixed-camera systems, demonstrating its necessity for effective navigation in cluttered environments.
> - A two-phase training strategy, starting with reinforcement learning on simplified "scandot" representations and then distilling to depth images, provides a computationally tractable path for end-to-end visuomotor policy learning.
