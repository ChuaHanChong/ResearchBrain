# EgoAERO: Learning Dexterous Manipulation from a Single Egocentric Video without Object Assets

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoAERO introduces a framework that enables learning dexterous manipulation policies from a single egocentric RGB-D human demonstration, eliminating the need for object assets. It reconstructs contact-consistent hand-object trajectories from raw video and achieves a 49.5% success rate in simulation, demonstrating comparable performance to CAD-based methods.

## Problem

- Traditional methods for collecting human demonstration data for dexterous robot manipulation require specialized hardware or intrusive setups.
- Existing large-scale egocentric datasets lack crucial 3D object information (6-DoF pose, geometry, contact state) necessary for robot learning.
- Many hand-object datasets rely on pre-scanned CAD models or multi-view capture systems, limiting scalability and applicability to novel objects.

## Method

- **EgoAERO** integrates MLLM-guided semantic preprocessing, robust ==asset-free object tracking== under occlusion, neural object field reconstruction, and ego-motion compensation to generate contact-consistent 6-DoF hand-object trajectories.
- A two-stage residual policy learning approach transfers reconstructed human trajectories to robot policies, first tracking hand motion, then learning residual corrections based on object state and contact.
- An online quality assessment mechanism evaluates the physical usability of egocentric demonstrations, facilitating the creation of the large-scale, asset-free EgoDex-R dataset.

## Results

- EgoAERO achieved a **49.5%** success rate in simulation with object rotation error of **9.7**° and translation error of **0.82** cm, outperforming ablations that lacked object reconstruction or adaptive contact optimization.
- Its asset-free reconstruction produced trajectories with comparable or slightly superior performance (**44.7%** success rate, **10.9**° E_r, **0.68** cm E_t) to CAD-based annotations on the HOI4D dataset.
- The framework successfully transferred reconstructed human hand-object trajectories to a physical Unitree G1 robot with an Inspire Hand, demonstrating real-world dexterous manipulation capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Asset-free reconstruction of hand-object trajectories from egocentric RGB-D video is achievable by combining robust tracking, neural object fields, and ego-motion compensation.
> - Adaptive contact optimization is crucial for refining reconstructed hand-object interactions, ensuring physical plausibility and significantly improving manipulation performance.
> - A two-stage residual policy learning paradigm effectively bridges the gap from human demonstrations to robot control, enabling stable hand tracking and object-contact adherence.
