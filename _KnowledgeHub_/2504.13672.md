# Magnecko: Design and Control of a Quadrupedal Magnetic Climbing Robot

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ETH Zürich developed Magnecko, a quadrupedal magnetic climbing robot with an insect-inspired design and custom actuators. It demonstrates stable locomotion on diverse ferrous surfaces, including ground, vertical walls, and ceilings, reaching speeds of up to 0.15 m/s and carrying payloads up to 7.5 kg. A model predictive control framework enabled speed improvements of 2 to 4.5 times over a baseline controller and facilitated transitions across 90° concave corners.

## Problem

- Industrial inspection and maintenance tasks on complex structures are often hazardous, labor-intensive, and challenging for human workers.
- Existing wheeled climbing robots are limited to smooth, unobstructed surfaces, while many legged climbing robots lack the dexterity for complex three-dimensional environments, such as navigating concave corners.
- Developing robotic platforms with robust, energy-efficient adhesion and comprehensive motion control for varying orientations (ground, walls, ceilings) remains a challenge.

## Method

- Designed **Magnecko**, a quadrupedal robot featuring an insect-inspired leg configuration with three active degrees of freedom per leg, chosen for its large range of motion and high manipulability for climbing.
- Integrated custom, torque-controllable actuators with high power density and electro-permanent magnets (EPMs) in each foot, providing strong, energy-efficient adhesion and maintaining attachment during power loss.
- Developed a hierarchical control framework incorporating a kinematic-aware ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) for whole-body motion optimization on various orientations and a specialized controller for seamless 90° concave corner transitions.

## Results

- Magnecko achieved stable crawling speeds of up to **0.15** m/s on the ground, **0.058** m/s on horizontal overhangs, and **0.033** m/s on vertical walls, with the MPC improving speeds by a factor of 2 to **4.5** compared to a baseline IK controller.
- The robot successfully performed transitions over 90° concave corners (e.g., ground-to-wall, wall-to-ceiling) in approximately 18 seconds per transition, demonstrating its enhanced maneuverability.
- It demonstrated a payload capacity of up to **7.5** kg (**65%** of its body weight) on all tested surfaces, with the custom actuators and EPMs operating within safe margins, showcasing its potential for practical applications.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The insect-inspired leg design significantly enhances manipulability and range of motion, proving effective for complex climbing maneuvers like 90° concave corner transitions, which are difficult for many other designs.
> - Integrating system kinematics directly into the MPC formulation enables more comprehensive whole-body motion optimization, leading to substantial improvements in locomotion speed and stability across different orientations.
> - Custom actuators operating well within their torque and speed limits, combined with robust EPMs, enable Magnecko to carry significant payloads (up to 65% of its body weight) while maintaining stable and controlled locomotion.
