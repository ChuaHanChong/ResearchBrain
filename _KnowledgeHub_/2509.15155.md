# Self-Improving Embodied Foundation Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Google DeepMind developed a two-stage post-training framework that integrates online reinforcement learning with embodied foundation models, utilizing the model's self-predicted "steps-to-go" as a data-driven reward signal. This approach allows robots to autonomously refine existing skills and acquire novel behaviors, demonstrating substantial performance gains and superior sample efficiency across various robotic tasks.

## Problem

- Embodied Foundation Models (EFMs) relying solely on behavioral cloning are limited to imitating demonstrated behaviors and struggle with acquiring novel skills or improving beyond initial data.
- The 'reward engineering problem' poses a significant challenge for applying reinforcement learning (RL) to real-world robotics, requiring complex and often task-specific reward functions.
- Current EFM training paradigms lack the crucial RL-tuning stage, a method that has proven highly effective in boosting performance for large language models (LLMs).

## Method

- A two-stage post-training framework combines initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with an auxiliary objective of predicting =='steps-to-go'== to a given goal.
- An ==online reinforcement learning stage== is introduced where the EFM autonomously improves using a ==self-predicted, data-driven reward function== derived from its 'steps-to-go' predictions.
- The system uses a robust ==success detector== based on thresholding the predicted 'steps-to-go', eliminating the need for manual reward engineering or external success labels.

## Results

- Self-improvement consistently boosted policy success rates; for example, from approximately **62-63%** to **87-88%** on real-world LanguageTable tasks.
- The method demonstrated superior sample efficiency, with policies trained on **10%** of imitation data plus **1%** additional robot interaction time outperforming behavioral cloning policies trained on **80%** of the imitation dataset.
- Multimodal foundation model pretraining was essential for effective and sample-efficient self-improvement, enabling the acquisition of novel skills such as effectively manipulating a banana (BananaTable task) with success rates increasing from ~**63%** to ~**85%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large foundation models can leverage their learned representations to self-generate dense and well-shaped reward signals and success detectors, addressing a major hurdle in robot reinforcement learning.
> - Combining web-scale foundation model pretraining with a minimal amount of online self-improvement data dramatically enhances sample efficiency, outperforming models trained with significantly more imitation data alone.
> - The framework enables embodied agents to learn genuinely novel behaviors and generalize effectively to tasks not present in their initial training datasets, pushing beyond semantic understanding to behavioral adaptation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the crucial intersection of embodied AI, large-scale foundation models, and reinforcement learning, aiming to bridge a significant gap in current robotic learning paradigms.

**Evolution of Embodied Foundation Models (EFMs):** Recent advancements have seen foundation models, initially designed for natural language processing or multimodal understanding, adapted to robotics. These "Embodied Foundation Models" (EFMs) have demonstrated impressive generalization and robustness, primarily through behavioral cloning (BC) (e.g., RT-2, PaLM-E, Octo). In this approach, pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) are fine-tuned to predict low-level robot actions based on visual observations and language instructions, essentially imitating human demonstrations. While effective, this supervised learning paradigm is limited by the quality and diversity of the demonstration data and typically struggles with acquiring novel skills or improving beyond the observed behaviors.

**Inspiration from Large Language Models (LLMs):** The paper draws a direct parallel with the training paradigm of Large Language Models (LLMs). LLMs undergo three main stages:
1. **Pretraining:** On vast amounts of web-scale text (and often images for VLMs).
2. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** On a smaller, high-quality dataset of instructions and responses to align with specific tasks.
3. **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Often Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), where the model learns to generate preferred outputs by optimizing a reward signal derived from human preferences. This RL stage has been shown to rapidly and substantially improve downstream task performance beyond SFT alone.

**The Research Gap:** The authors identify that while EFMs have embraced pretraining and SFT (behavioral cloning), the third, crucial RL-tuning stage has largely been absent in robotics due to significant algorithmic and engineering challenges. Foremost among these is the "reward engineering problem" – the difficulty of designing effective, measurable, and scalable reward functions for complex real-world robot tasks. Prior work in robotics RL either relies on hand-engineered rewards (which are brittle and not generalizable), sparse binary success signals (which hinder learning), or computationally expensive human feedback.

**Distinction from Related Work:**
- **Existing EFMs:** This work pushes beyond the current state-of-the-art EFMs (like RT-2, Octo) by introducing an RL stage, rather than solely relying on behavioral cloning.
- **Reward Engineering Alternatives:** While other methods exist to learn rewards (e.g., Code-As-Rewards using LLMs to write reward code, learning latent representations for distance-based rewards, or simple hindsight relabeling), this work proposes a "reward-engineering-free" approach. It uniquely leverages the *foundation model itself* to predict "steps-to-go," which implicitly defines a dense, well-shaped reward function and a robust success detector. This direct integration with the foundation model's learned representations, without requiring external ground-truth labels or bespoke instrumentation, is a key differentiator.
- **Steps-to-Go Prediction in RL:** While the concept of learning "steps-to-go" or goal-conditioned value functions has been explored in prior RL literature, this paper's novelty lies in integrating it directly into a two-stage post-training framework for *web-scale pretrained embodied foundation models*, demonstrating its efficacy for online self-improvement and novel skill acquisition in real-world robotics.

In essence, this research aims to bring the proven success of LLM-style RL fine-tuning to embodied agents, unlocking more capable, autonomously improving, and generalist robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind this research stems from the observed success of reinforcement learning in rapidly improving the performance of large language models (LLMs) after their initial supervised fine-tuning stage. The authors contend that despite the widespread adoption of web-scale pretraining and behavioral cloning for Embodied Foundation Models (EFMs) in robotics, the critical RL post-training stage remains largely unexplored due to significant challenges.

The key objectives of this work are to:

1. **Develop a Two-Stage Post-Training Framework:** Design a practical, general-purpose framework for EFMs that extends beyond supervised learning, incorporating an online reinforcement learning stage inspired by LLM fine-tuning.
2. **Overcome Reward Engineering Challenges:** Address the notorious difficulty of designing and instrumenting reward functions in real-world robotics by enabling the EFM to "self-improve" using data-driven, self-predicted rewards and success detection, thus minimizing human supervision and task-specific engineering.
3. **Demonstrate Sample-Efficient Policy Improvement:** Investigate whether the proposed two-stage approach (SFT + Self-Improvement) is significantly more sample-efficient than merely scaling up imitation data collection for supervised learning alone.
4. **Validate Robustness for Real-World Robotics:** Confirm that the Self-Improvement procedure is reliable and reproducible enough to be effectively deployed and utilized on physical robot hardware.
5. **Quantify the Role of Foundation Model Pretraining:** Ablate the contribution of web-scale, multimodal pretraining in enabling the effectiveness and sample-efficiency of the Self-Improvement process.
6. **Unlock Novel Skill Acquisition and Generalization:** Determine if the combination of pretrained foundation models and online Self-Improvement can enable robots to autonomously acquire novel skills and generalize beyond the specific behaviors observed in their initial imitation learning datasets, moving from semantic to behavioral generalization.

In essence, the authors seek to transform EFMs from mere imitators into truly self-improving agents capable of acquiring new, out-of-distribution skills with minimal human intervention, thereby accelerating the path towards generalist robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is a novel two-stage post-training approach for Embodied Foundation Models (EFMs), directly inspired by the successful fine-tuning paradigm of Large Language Models (LLMs). The chosen EFM architecture is based on RT-2, which leverages the PaLI-3B vision-language model, converting continuous robot actions into discrete tokens that the model predicts.

**Stage 1: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**

In the initial stage, a pretrained multimodal foundation model (PaLI-3B) is fine-tuned on an imitation learning dataset, denoted as `D`. This dataset consists of tuples `(o_t, a_t, g_t')`, where `o_t` is the observation at timestep `t`, `a_t` is the action, and `g_t'` is a future goal or outcome within the same trajectory. The SFT stage uses two primary objectives:

1. **Behavioral Cloning (L_BC):** This standard supervised learning objective maximizes the log-likelihood of dataset actions `a_t`, conditioned on the observation `o_t` and the goal `g_t'`. This teaches the EFM to imitate the demonstrated behaviors.
2. **Steps-to-Go Prediction (L_steps-to-go):** This crucial auxiliary objective trains the EFM to predict the number of timesteps remaining (`t' - t`) until the intended goal `g_t'` is accomplished, given the current observation `o_t` and goal `g_t'`. This objective is key to enabling the second stage.

The fine-tuning is performed on the entire PaLI model, using the AdamW optimizer. For LanguageTable experiments, an additional auxiliary objective of predicting the episode instruction from the first and last frames was included.

**Stage 2: Self-Improvement (Online Reinforcement Learning)**

This stage leverages the EFM's learned "steps-to-go" prediction from Stage 1 to enable online reinforcement learning, allowing the robot to autonomously practice and improve on downstream tasks. A key innovation here is the *data-driven reward function* and *success detector*, eliminating the need for manual reward engineering.

1. **Data-Driven Reward Function:**
 - The model's expected steps-to-go prediction `d(o, g)` is defined as the expected value of "steps-to-go" to accomplish goal `g` given observation `o`.
 - The reward function for online RL is then defined as the *difference* in expected steps-to-go between consecutive observations: `r(o_t, a_t, o_t+1, g) := d(o_t, g) - d(o_t+1, g)`.
 - Intuitively, this reward function quantifies how much closer the robot gets to the goal after taking an action. It implicitly acts as a *shaped reward function*, providing higher rewards in states where the original dataset policy (approximated by `d(o, g)`) is proficient. This also helps in reducing variance for the REINFORCE estimator.
2. **Robust Success Detector:**
 - Instead of relying on a separate binary classification head, success is detected by thresholding the predicted steps-to-go: `success(o, g) := 1[d(o, g) <= s]`, where `s` is a small number of timesteps. This leverages the robustness of the foundation model's predictions.
3. **Online RL with REINFORCE:**
 - A frozen Stage 1 checkpoint is used for computing rewards and success detection to ensure stability, while the Stage 2 policy is initialized from another (potentially different) Stage 1 checkpoint.
 - The robot, using its current policy, collects trajectories by sampling an instruction and executing actions. Episodes terminate upon predicted success, reaching maximum length, or human intervention.
 - Monte Carlo returns `R_t` are computed for each timestep using the self-predicted rewards.
 - Policy updates are performed using the REINFORCE loss: `−c · R_t · log p_EFA_action(a_t|o_t, g)`.
 - The authors chose on-policy REINFORCE without data reuse for better training stability, avoiding challenges of off-policy learning and bootstrapping.
 - The process is iterative: collect data, update policy, clear buffer, repeat. A small positive constant `c` is used in the REINFORCE loss for stable training.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Foundation Model:** PaLI 3 billion parameter vision-language model (PaLI-3B). Inputs are images + text (instruction, auxiliary info, prediction type); outputs are tokenized actions or steps-to-go.
- **Robot Embodiments:**
 - LanguageTable (simulated and real-world): 2D action space, simple manipulation tasks (e.g., "move blue cube to green star").
 - Aloha (simulated): Bimanual 14-DoF platform, complex insertion task.
 - BananaTable (real-world): Novel task on LanguageTable hardware with a banana, requiring behavioral generalization.
- **Ablation Studies:** Comparisons were made with models initialized from scratch and "Uni-PaLI" (unimodally pretrained vision and language models without joint multimodal fine-tuning) to assess the impact of foundation model pretraining.

The overall methodology emphasizes leveraging the strong generalization capabilities of large foundation models, coupled with a self-supervised RL mechanism, to enable efficient and autonomous skill acquisition in robotics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence for the efficacy and transformative potential of the proposed two-stage post-training approach, addressing all stated objectives:

1. **Significant Policy Performance Improvement (Q1):**
 - Across all tested domains (LanguageTable simulated, LanguageTable real-world, Aloha simulated), Self-Improvement consistently and significantly boosted policy success rates beyond policies trained with supervised behavioral cloning (BC) alone.
 - **Example (LanguageTable Real-world):** Self-Improvement increased policy success rates from approximately 62-63% to 87-88%.

2. **Superior Sample-Efficiency (Q2):**
 - The combination of SFT and Self-Improvement proved far more sample-efficient than simply scaling up imitation data for supervised learning.
 - **Example (LanguageTable Simulated):** Policies trained with 10% of the imitation dataset plus a mere 1% additional robot interaction time (for Self-Improvement) *outperformed* BC policies trained on 20% and even 80% of the full imitation dataset. This highlights an incredible return on investment for online interaction.
 - **Example (Aloha Simulated):** 5K imitation episodes + 2.5K Self-Improvement episodes outperformed 10K imitation episodes (RT-2 baseline) and rivaled 15K imitation episodes.

3. **Robustness and Reproducibility for Real-World Robotics (Q3):**
 - The Self-Improvement procedure was successfully deployed and demonstrated robust performance on real-world LanguageTable robots. A single human operator could monitor a fleet of robots, with automated reward labeling and success detection, minimizing human effort.
 - The results were consistent across multiple random seeds and experimental setups, indicating the reliability of the approach.

4. **Critical Role of Foundation Model Pretraining (Q4):**
 - Ablation studies comparing PaLI with "Scratch" (randomly initialized) and "Uni-PaLI" (unimodally pretrained components) models showed that multimodal foundation model pretraining is essential.
 - PaLI-derived reward models consistently led to better and significantly more sample-efficient Self-Improvement, especially in low-data regimes. Scratch models showed high variance and poor performance, while Uni-PaLI models performed worse than PaLI. This underscores that the rich, generalized knowledge embedded in pretrained foundation models is a key enabler for effective self-improvement.

5. **Unlocking Novel Skill Acquisition and Generalization (Q5):**
 - **Domain Transfer (Real2Sim LanguageTable):** Self-Improvement enabled effective transfer. A policy trained on 80% real-world data, then self-improved in simulation, saw performance jump from ~22% to ~59% with only 3% additional simulated episodes. This performance was equivalent to BC policies trained on 80% of the *target domain's* imitation dataset. Pretraining was again crucial here, with Uni-PaLI reward models leading to slower improvement.
 - **Behavioral Generalization (BananaTable):** Crucially, the approach allowed policies to acquire *novel skills* not present in the initial imitation dataset. Starting from a LanguageTable policy (no bananas seen), Self-Improvement on the "BananaTable" task (pushing a banana) improved success from ~63% to ~85%. The policy learned effective strategies for manipulating the banana (e.g., pushing from tips/middle to prevent rotation), demonstrating true behavioral generalization beyond semantic understanding.

In summary, the research demonstrates that by integrating a data-driven online reinforcement learning stage, pretrained embodied foundation models can achieve significantly higher success rates, with remarkable sample efficiency, and can even learn new, complex behaviors that were entirely absent from their initial training data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a substantial step forward in the field of robotics and embodied AI, holding significant implications for the development of generalist robots:

1. **Democratization of Robot Reinforcement Learning:** By developing a "reward-engineering-free" framework, this work significantly lowers the barrier to entry for applying reinforcement learning to complex real-world robot tasks. Manual reward design is notoriously difficult, time-consuming, and often task-specific. The ability for robots to autonomously derive meaningful reward signals from their own learned representations makes RL more scalable and applicable to a wider range of open-ended problems.
2. **Autonomous Skill Acquisition:** The most profound impact is the demonstration of autonomous skill acquisition and behavioral generalization. Current EFMs excel at semantic generalization (e.g., executing known skills in novel contexts) but often struggle with truly learning *new ways to act*. This work shows that by combining web-scale pretraining with online self-improvement, robots can acquire entirely novel motor skills that go beyond their initial imitation data, moving closer to the vision of truly adaptive and versatile robots.
3. **Enhanced Sample Efficiency in Robotics:** The findings reveal that intelligently combining offline supervised learning with online reinforcement learning is dramatically more sample-efficient than simply collecting more imitation data. This is crucial for robotics, where real-world data collection is expensive, time-consuming, and often dangerous. Reducing the need for massive human-teleoperated datasets will accelerate research and deployment.
4. **Scalability of Robot Fleets:** The minimal human supervision required for the Self-Improvement stage (one operator for multiple robots) is a critical enabler for scaling robotic deployments. Instead of a one-to-one human-to-robot ratio for data collection, robots can autonomously practice and refine skills, allowing human operators to focus on higher-level oversight or intervention when necessary.
5. **Bridging the Gap between LLM and EFM Development:** This work successfully transfers a key paradigm from the highly successful LLM training pipeline (SFT + RL-tuning) to embodied agents. This cross-pollination of ideas highlights a promising avenue for future research, suggesting that many other advancements in language models might be adapted to enhance robot capabilities.
6. **Robust Success Detection:** The novel method of deriving a robust success detector from steps-to-go prediction offers a more generalizable and less brittle alternative to traditional binary classification methods, especially in real-world scenarios without ground-truth success labels.

In conclusion, "SELF-IMPROVING EMBODIED FOUNDATION MODELS" presents a compelling vision for the future of robotics, where robots can autonomously learn, adapt, and acquire new skills with minimal human intervention. This work lays crucial groundwork for developing generalist robots capable of operating effectively and robustly in diverse, unstructured real-world environments.
