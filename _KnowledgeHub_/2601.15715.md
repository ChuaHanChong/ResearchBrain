# RebuttalAgent: Strategic Persuasion in Academic Rebuttal via Theory of Mind

> [!summary] Summary
> RebuttalAgent, developed by researchers at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, integrates Theory of Mind (ToM) into Large Language Models (LLMs) to generate strategic and persuasive academic rebuttals. The framework achieved an average score of 9.42 on an automated evaluation and 9.57 in human evaluations, demonstrating a 7.36% improvement in persuasiveness over GPT-4.1.

## Problem

- Academic rebuttal is a complex, strategic game of incomplete information, requiring authors to persuade reviewers without full knowledge of their biases or knowledge bases.
- Existing LLM approaches for rebuttal often imitate surface-level linguistic patterns, lacking strategic depth and the essential perspective-taking (Theory of Mind) needed for effective persuasion.
- Successful rebuttals demand sophisticated strategic analysis, including when to concede, defend, or reframe arguments, which current AI models struggle to achieve.

## Method

- A novel ToM-Strategy-Response (TSR) framework was developed to systematically model reviewer mental states, formulate tailored persuasion strategies, and generate evidence-based responses.
- RebuttalBench, a large-scale synthetic dataset containing complete ToM-Strategy-Response chains, was constructed, and **RebuttalAgent** was trained using a two-stage process combining supervised fine-tuning with reinforcement learning via a self-reward mechanism.
- Rebuttal-RM, a specialized automated evaluator, was created and trained on a multi-source dataset to achieve high scoring consistency with human preferences, surpassing general-purpose LLM judges.

## Results

- RebuttalAgent achieved the highest overall average score of 9.42 on automated evaluation (R2-test) and 9.57 in human evaluations, significantly outperforming all baselines including GPT-4.1.
- The model demonstrated particularly strong gains in persuasiveness, showing a **7.36%** improvement over the GPT-4.1 baseline in human evaluations and up to **34.6%** over its Qwen3-**8B** base model in automated evaluation.
- Rebuttal-RM, the specialized evaluator, showed superior alignment with human judgments, achieving an average consistency score of **0.812** and surpassing proprietary models like GPT-4.1 by **9.0%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly grounding academic rebuttal in Theory of Mind (ToM) principles transforms the task for LLMs from linguistic imitation into strategic reasoning and perspective-taking.
> - A structured, multi-stage reasoning process (TSR framework) enables LLMs to systematically analyze reviewer profiles, plan specific strategies, and generate context-aware, evidence-based responses.
> - Combining supervised fine-tuning with a novel self-reward mechanism during reinforcement learning allows for scalable self-improvement in strategic persuasion without relying on external, expert-annotated reward models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of artificial intelligence (AI), particularly Large Language Models (LLMs), into various stages of the scientific research workflow has been a notable development. LLMs have been applied to auxiliary tasks such as literature summarization and data visualization, and increasingly to core tasks including hypothesis formulation, experimental design, and even autonomous paper authorship. This widespread adoption positions LLMs as significant collaborators in the research process.

However, a specific and complex phase of the research workflow, academic rebuttal, has remained comparatively underexplored by current AI approaches. The paper identifies academic rebuttal as a dynamic game of incomplete information, characterized by strategic communication under conditions of severe information asymmetry. Authors, in this context, must persuade reviewers without complete knowledge of their individual biases, knowledge bases, or the potential cascading effects of their responses.

Existing LLM-based approaches for rebuttal, primarily relying on supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on review datasets, are described as limited by their tendency to imitate surface-level linguistic patterns. These models may generate polite responses but often lack strategic depth and the essential element of perspective-taking required for effective persuasion. This limitation stems from their inability to perform the strategic, Theory of Mind (ToM) reasoning that is central to successful academic debate. ToM, a concept from cognitive science, involves modeling the internal states (beliefs, intentions, perspectives) of others to predict and understand their actions.

This research addresses this gap by introducing Theory of Mind into the academic rebuttal process for LLMs. It extends the concept of Machine Theory of Mind (MToM) to this specific domain, aiming to enable AI systems to infer and model reviewers' mental states more effectively. The work positions itself as a development beyond mere linguistic imitation, seeking to transform rebuttal into a strategic reasoning task. It draws upon existing work in MToM, multi-agent debate (where LLMs interact to enhance reasoning and fact-checking), and the broader application of LLMs in academic peer review (e.g., generating reviews, enhancing quality analysis), but specifically focuses on the strategic generation of author responses during the rebuttal phase.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the recognized complexity and strategic nature of academic rebuttal, which current AI models struggle to address effectively. The authors posit that rebuttal is not merely a technical exchange but a dynamic game involving persuasion under significant information asymmetry. Authors lack full insight into reviewers' internal states, such as their specific knowledge, predispositions, or the downstream effects of responses. Traditional LLM approaches, often based on supervised fine-tuning, tend to generate responses that are linguistically acceptable but often lack strategic depth, failing to genuinely engage with the reviewer's underlying concerns or leverage opportunities for persuasion. This limitation arises from the absence of perspective-taking, a capacity referred to as Theory of Mind (ToM). A successful rebuttal requires sophisticated strategic analysis, including decisions on when to concede, defend, offer new evidence, or reframe arguments, all of which depend on understanding the reviewer's perspective.

Given this motivation, the research outlines several key objectives:

1. **Introduce Theory of Mind into Academic Rebuttal:** The overarching objective is to develop the first framework that explicitly grounds academic rebuttal in ToM principles, thereby moving beyond superficial linguistic imitation to facilitate strategic reasoning.
2. **Develop the ToM-Strategy-Response (TSR) Framework:** To operationalize ToM, the paper aims to introduce a novel three-stage generation framework (TSR) that systematically models reviewer mental states, formulates tailored persuasion strategies, and generates evidence-based responses.
3. **Construct a Large-Scale Training Dataset (RebuttalBench):** A critical objective is to create a high-quality, large-scale synthetic dataset (RebuttalBench) containing complete ToM-Strategy-Response chains. This dataset is designed to equip the agent with the necessary complex reasoning capabilities.
4. **Implement a Two-Stage Agent Training Process:** The research aims to train the RebuttalAgent using a two-stage process: an initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT) phase to establish foundational ToM-based analysis and strategic planning, followed by a reinforcement learning (RL) phase that leverages a novel self-reward mechanism for scalable self-improvement.
5. **Develop a Specialized Automated Evaluator (Rebuttal-RM):** For reliable and efficient assessment of generated rebuttals, an objective is to create Rebuttal-RM, a specialized reward model trained on a large, multi-source dataset to achieve high scoring consistency with human preferences, surpassing existing general-purpose LLM judges.
6. **Demonstrate Performance Superiority:** A final objective is to demonstrate, through extensive automated and human evaluations, that RebuttalAgent significantly outperforms existing base models and advanced proprietary models in generating persuasive and strategically aligned academic rebuttals.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RebuttalAgent framework is designed to address academic rebuttal as a strategic communication task, grounded in Theory of Mind (ToM). The methodology consists of a multi-stage process encompassing task formulation, data preparation, a ToM-Strategy-Response (TSR) generation framework, agent training, and a specialized evaluation model.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

The task is defined as generating a convincing, context-aware, and evidence-grounded response ($r_{target}$) to a specific target comment ($c_{target}$). Inputs include the full manuscript ($M$), the relevant review ($R_i$), and the target comment.

#### 3.2. Data Preparation

To process raw reviews and efficiently retrieve relevant information, a two-step pipeline is employed:
- **Comment Extraction:** An LLM-as-Extractor is used with a specific prompt to segment raw reviews into discrete, actionable critical statements. This process achieved 98% accuracy in manual verification.
- **Context Retrieval:** To avoid overwhelming the model with the entire manuscript, a three-stage retrieval module identifies and extracts the top-k most relevant text chunks (typically paragraphs) from the manuscript for each target comment. This involves segmenting the manuscript, encoding chunks and the target comment using a pre-trained embedding model, and computing cosine similarity.

#### 3.3. ToM-Strategy-Response (TSR) Framework

The core of RebuttalAgent is the TSR framework, which explicitly implements ToM through a multi-stage reasoning and generation process:
- **Hierarchical Reviewer Profile Modeling (T-stage - Theory of Mind):** This stage involves a two-level analysis to infer the reviewer's perspective.
 - **Macro-level:** An LLM infers the overall intent, attitude, and core concerns of the reviewer across four dimensions: Overall Stance (e.g., Accept, Reject), Overall Attitude (e.g., Constructive, Skeptical), Dominant Concern (e.g., Methodology, Experiments), and Reviewer Expertise (e.g., Domain Expert, Generalist). This informs the global strategy and tone.
 - **Micro-level:** For each specific target comment, an LLM classifies its primary concern across four dimensions: Significance, Methodology, Experimental Rigor, and Presentation. This fine-grained profile guides tactical responses.
- **ToM-Driven Strategy Generation (S-stage - Strategy):** This intermediate step translates the diagnostic reviewer profile into an actionable plan. Conditioned on the complete reviewer profile and the target comment, an LLM synthesizes these inputs to output a concise, high-level strategy as a numbered list of actions. This explicit decomposition encourages the LLM to strategize before generating the response text.
- **Evidence-Based Strategic Response Generation (R-stage - Response):** The final response ($r_{target}$) is generated through a guided synthesis process. This process is conditioned on both strategic inputs (the ToM-based reviewer profile and the tailored rebuttal strategy) and contextual inputs (retrieved relevant manuscript chunks and, during data synthesis, the original author response as a reference). The model integrates these components to produce a strategically aligned, factually grounded, and coherent rebuttal.

#### 3.4. Agent Training for Strategic Persuasion

The RebuttalAgent undergoes a two-stage training process:
- **RebuttalBench Dataset Construction:**
 - **Data Source:** Based on the Re$^2$-rebuttal dataset, which contains papers, reviews, and author responses.
 - **Processing:** GPT-4.1 parses reviews into over 200K comment-response pairs. Each review and comment is then annotated with hierarchical profiles (macro- and micro-level). Comments requiring new experiments are excluded to prevent hallucination of data. A diverse subset of 70K comments is curated.
 - **Data Synthesis:** For each selected comment and its authentic response, the TSR framework generates the corresponding reviewer analysis, rebuttal strategy, and a new synthetic response. Multiple powerful teacher models (e.g., GPT-4.1, Claude 3.5) are used to mitigate bias and enrich stylistic variety. The generated analysis, strategy, and response are concatenated into a structured target sequence for holistic learning.
- **Instruction Tuning with ToM-Driven Reasoning (SFT):** A Qwen3-8B model is supervised fine-tuned on RebuttalBench to learn the structured reasoning process of the TSR framework and develop core rebuttal competencies.
- **Reinforcement Learning with Self-Reward:**
 - **Self-Reward Mechanism:** To optimize for strategic superiority and persuasiveness without an external reward model, a self-reward mechanism is introduced. The SFT-tuned model ($G_{SFT}$) evaluates its own generated outputs along four dimensions: Format Adherence ($R_{format}$), Reasoning Quality ($R_{think}$), Response Quality ($R_{resp}$), and Response Diversity ($R_{div}$). The $R_{div}$ component specifically discourages generic or templated responses by rewarding semantic distinctness from negative samples, acting as an anti-reward hacking mechanism.
 - **Optimization Algorithm:** The defined rewards are used to optimize the policy with the Group Reward Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm.

#### 3.5. Rebuttal-RM as Judge

For automated evaluation, a specialized scoring model, Rebuttal-RM, is developed.
- **Training Data Construction:** A dataset of over 102K instances is constructed from three sources: original author responses (manually scored), GPT-4.1-refined responses, and diverse model-generated replies (scores and explanations generated by Gemini 2.5 Pro).
- **Rebuttal-RM Training:** Qwen3-8B is fine-tuned on this dataset to output multi-dimensional scores (Attitude, Clarity, Persuasiveness, Constructiveness) and an explanation, with the goal of aligning with human preferences.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate RebuttalAgent's performance and the effectiveness of its components yielded several findings:

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Rebuttal-RM

Rebuttal-RM, the specialized evaluator, was benchmarked against human ratings using six statistical metrics (Mean Absolute Error, Pearson, Spearman, Kendall, Coarse-grained Accuracy, Fine-grained Accuracy).
- **Alignment with Human Judgments:** Rebuttal-RM demonstrated superior alignment with human judgments compared to all baseline models, achieving the highest average consistency score of 0.812. This performance surpassed powerful proprietary models such as GPT-4.1 (by 9.0%) and DeepSeek-r1 (by 15.2%), indicating its reliability for automated evaluation.

#### 4.2. Benchmarking RebuttalAgent (Automated Evaluation)

RebuttalAgent was compared against a range of foundation models (o3, GPT-4.1, DeepSeek-R1, Deepseek-V3, Gemini-2.5, GLM-4-9B, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Qwen3-8B) and agent-based methods (Self-Refined, Strategy-Prompt, RebuttalFT). Evaluation was conducted on an in-domain test set (R2-test) and an out-of-domain test set (Rebuttal-test), using a holistic quality score (0-10) and a breakdown into four dimensions: Clarity (C), Persuasiveness (P), Constructiveness (Co), and Attitude (A).
- **Overall Performance:** RebuttalAgent achieved the highest overall average score of 9.42 on the R2-test, significantly outperforming all baselines. It excelled across key rebuttal dimensions, particularly in Clarity (9.43) and Persuasiveness (9.20).
- **Improvement over Base Model:** Compared to its Qwen3-8B base model, RebuttalAgent showed an average performance improvement of 18.3%, with the most substantial gains observed in Persuasiveness and Constructiveness (up to 34.6%).
- **Generalization:** Performance on the out-of-domain Rebuttal-test also demonstrated strong generalization capabilities, indicating the framework's robustness beyond its training distribution.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

An ablation study was conducted to assess the contribution of RebuttalAgent's various design components.
- **Component Necessity:** Removing key components such as ToM, Strategy, or Thinking (referring to the reasoning steps in the TSR framework), or omitting core training stages like SFT and RL, resulted in significant performance degradation. This indicates the necessity of each component to the model's overall efficacy.
- **Reward Signal Impact:** Among the self-reward components, the reward signal for final response quality ($R_{resp}$) was identified as the most impactful. The Response Diversity reward ($R_{div}$) was crucial in preventing the model from converging to templated, repetitive outputs.
- **Backbone Agnosticism:** The study showed that applying the TSR framework and self-reward mechanism to other backbone models, such as Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-4B, also yielded significant performance gains (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B improved from 7.44 to 9.20), suggesting the approach is model-agnostic.

#### 4.4. Effectiveness of ToM-Driven Reasoning

To isolate the impact of ToM analysis and strategy generation, these components (produced by RebuttalAgent) were provided as contextual inputs to external base models (Qwen3-8B and Llama3.1-8B).
- **Consistent Gains:** Both external models showed consistent performance gains across all data categories when augmented with the ToM analysis and Strategy. Qwen3-8B, for instance, achieved a 21.0% gain in Presentation when guided by the full ToM and Strategy. This confirmed the robustness and transferability of the ToM-driven reasoning approach.
- **Integrated Architecture Advantage:** Despite these gains, the fully integrated RebuttalAgent maintained a performance advantage, suggesting that optimal efficacy is achieved when ToM-driven reasoning is inherent to the model's architecture and training.

#### 4.5. Human Evaluation

A human evaluation, considered the gold standard, was conducted on 100 randomly sampled comments. Three annotators with AI/ML research and reviewing experience blindly evaluated responses on a 10-point scale across Attitude, Clarity, Persuasiveness, and Constructiveness, achieving a strong inter-annotator agreement (Cohen's κ = 0.79).
- **Superiority of RebuttalAgent:** RebuttalAgent achieved the highest average score of 9.57 in human evaluations, surpassing all strong baselines, including o3 and GPT-4.1. It consistently outperformed other models across all four evaluation dimensions.
- **Persuasiveness Gain:** The largest relative gain for RebuttalAgent was observed in Persuasiveness, achieving a score of 9.34, representing a 7.36% improvement over the GPT-4.1 baseline. This indicates RebuttalAgent's effectiveness in generating convincing and balanced rebuttals.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of RebuttalAgent represents a development in the application of AI to complex academic communication tasks, specifically academic rebuttal. By integrating Theory of Mind (ToM) into its framework, this work moves beyond previous LLM approaches that primarily replicated surface-level linguistic patterns, instead enabling strategic reasoning and perspective-taking.

#### 5.1. Significance of the Work

- **Paradigm Shift in Rebuttal Generation:** RebuttalAgent is presented as the first framework to explicitly ground academic rebuttal in ToM, transforming it from a task of linguistic imitation to one of strategic reasoning. This allows the model to infer reviewer intent and underlying concerns, leading to more targeted and persuasive responses.
- **Structured Reasoning through TSR Framework:** The novel ToM-Strategy-Response (TSR) framework provides a structured approach to a complex task, decomposing rebuttal into coherent stages of reviewer analysis, strategic planning, and evidence-based response generation. This explicit reasoning chain contributes to the clarity and effectiveness of the generated rebuttals.
- **Creation of Specialized Resources:** The development of RebuttalBench, a large-scale dataset of ToM-strategy-response chains, and Rebuttal-RM, a specialized evaluator highly aligned with human preferences, provides valuable resources for future research and development in this domain. Rebuttal-RM's performance surpassing general-purpose models like GPT-4.1 highlights the benefit of domain-specific evaluation.
- **Robust Training Methodology:** The two-stage training process, combining supervised fine-tuning with reinforcement learning using a self-reward mechanism, demonstrates an effective strategy for instilling complex reasoning capabilities and enabling scalable self-improvement in LLMs without relying on external, expert-annotated reward models during RL.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Enhancing Academic Dialogue and Fairness:** RebuttalAgent has the potential to facilitate more clear, constructive, and persuasive academic dialogue. By helping authors craft strategically aligned and evidence-based rebuttals, it could improve the quality of peer review interactions and potentially contribute to fairer outcomes for papers.
- **Support for Researchers:** The tool is intended to serve as a valuable reference and guidance resource, particularly for new scholars navigating the complex rebuttal stage. It can offer strategic suggestions and practical tips, thereby reducing the burden on authors and allowing them to focus on the core scientific content.
- **Improved Human-AI Collaboration:** RebuttalAgent exemplifies effective human-AI collaboration in scientific research. By serving as an AI assistant, it can augment human capabilities in communication, allowing researchers to refine their arguments and address critiques with greater precision.
- **Generalizability of Methodology:** The demonstrated effectiveness of the TSR framework and self-reward mechanism across different backbone models (e.g., Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-4B) suggests that this approach is model-agnostic and could be generalized to other complex communication or reasoning tasks beyond academic rebuttal.
- **Ethical Considerations and Mitigations:** The authors acknowledge ethical considerations, such as the potential for AI models to learn and reinforce biases or fabricate evidence. They address this by explicitly designing the training process to exclude comments requiring new, unprovided experiments, thereby focusing the agent on linguistic persuasion and strategic argumentation rather than data fabrication. The disclaimer that generated content is for reference and inspiration, not replacement of critical analysis, underscores responsible use.

In summary, RebuttalAgent contributes to the field by enhancing the strategic capabilities of LLMs in a critical academic process, with implications for improving scholarly communication and supporting researchers.
