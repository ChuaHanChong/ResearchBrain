# DexDeform: Dexterous Deformable Object Manipulation with Human Demonstrations and Differentiable Physics

> [!summary] Summary
> DexDeform is a framework that learns dexterous manipulation of deformable objects using multi-fingered robot hands by combining skill abstraction from human demonstrations with differentiable physics for trajectory refinement. The approach achieves a normalized improvement in Earth Mover's Distance of up to 0.972 across various complex manipulation tasks, outperforming existing reinforcement learning and trajectory optimization methods.

## Problem

- Robotic manipulation of deformable objects is challenging due to their effectively infinite degrees of freedom and complex, unpredictable contact dynamics.
- Reinforcement learning (RL) methods struggle with the immense sample complexity and high-dimensional state/action spaces involved in deformable object manipulation.
- Differentiable physics-based trajectory optimization methods are prone to local optima, particularly with multi-fingered contact interactions, requiring good initial conditions.

## Method

- A low-cost teleoperation system collects a small set of human demonstrations, retargeted to a multi-fingered robot hand in a differentiable physics simulator (PlasticineLab).
- An implicit scene representation and a hierarchical skill model are learned from these demonstrations, enabling high-level planning in a compact latent skill space.
- Differentiable physics refines trajectories for novel, augmented goal shapes, and successful refined trajectories are iteratively added back to the dataset, creating a bootstrapping loop for improved generalization.

## Results

- DexDeform consistently outperformed baselines, achieving normalized Earth Mover's Distance improvements ranging from 0.842 to 0.972 across six complex dexterous deformable manipulation tasks.
- The full DexDeform method, including differentiable physics-guided exploration, performed better than a 'Skill-Only' baseline (without physics refinement), underscoring the importance of refinement for fine-grained control and generalization.
- The learned skill embeddings captured semantic categories of manipulation, and the framework demonstrated robustness to partial point cloud observations, indicating potential for real-world application.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intelligent initializations derived from human-demonstrated skill abstractions significantly mitigate the local optima problem in differentiable physics-based trajectory optimization for deformable objects.
> - Learning a latent skill space from demonstrations allows for efficient long-horizon planning by abstracting away low-level finger movements and compositionally understanding deformation.
> - A bootstrapping loop that uses differentiable physics to explore novel goals and refine trajectories enables the system to generalize beyond initial demonstrations and continuously improve data efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements, particularly in dexterous manipulation of *rigid* objects using multi-fingered hands, often powered by reinforcement learning (RL). However, a vast array of real-world tasks, from cooking to healthcare, involves interacting with *deformable* objects (e.g., dough, cloth, organs). Manipulating deformable objects presents a substantially harder challenge due to their infinite degrees of freedom (DoFs), complex and often unpredictable contact dynamics, and the high-dimensional state spaces required to describe their configurations.

**Existing Gaps and Challenges:**
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** While successful for rigid objects, RL approaches struggle with deformable objects due to immense sample complexity and the difficulty of exploring high-dimensional state and action spaces effectively.
- **Differentiable Physics and Trajectory Optimization:** Recent advancements in differentiable physics simulators have enabled gradient-based trajectory optimization for deformable object manipulation. These methods leverage first-order gradients to efficiently find control sequences. However, they are highly susceptible to local optima, especially when complex contact interactions (like those with multi-fingered hands) create an "explosion of contact modes," making initialization critical and often leading to sub-optimal solutions.
- **Learning from Demonstrations:** Human demonstrations are a natural way to teach complex manipulation skills. However, collecting high-quality, diverse demonstrations for deformable tasks is challenging, and generalizing these demonstrations to novel object shapes or manipulation goals is difficult for standard imitation learning techniques, especially without explicit models of deformation.

**DexDeform's Contribution to the Landscape:**
DexDeform strategically positions itself at the intersection of these challenges by proposing a novel framework that synergistically combines the strengths of human demonstrations, skill abstraction, and differentiable physics.
1. **Bridging Human Intuition and Machine Precision:** It acknowledges the value of human intuition in handling deformable objects by collecting teleoperated demonstrations. These demonstrations provide crucial initial guidance, circumventing the arduous exploration phase of RL and providing good initial conditions for optimization.
2. **Addressing Local Optima:** By abstracting skills from human demonstrations, DexDeform provides a principled way to initialize trajectories for differentiable physics optimization. This "warm start" helps the gradient-based optimizer escape poor local optima often encountered when starting from random or naive initializations.
3. **Enhancing Generalization and Exploration:** The framework doesn't just imitate; it actively *explores* and *generalizes*. It uses shape augmentation to generate novel goals and then leverages the efficiency of differentiable physics to refine the skill-model-planned trajectories to achieve these new goals. These refined trajectories are then added back to the dataset, iteratively improving the skill model's generalization capabilities—a form of self-supervised data augmentation.
4. **Novelty Claim:** The paper makes a significant claim of being "the first investigation on the learning-based dexterous manipulation of deformable objects." While differentiable physics for deformable objects and dexterous manipulation for rigid objects exist, combining *both* with a *learning-based* approach using *multi-fingered hands* is indeed a novel and critical step forward, pushing the boundaries of what autonomous robots can achieve.

This work marks a crucial step toward creating robots that can dexterously interact with the complex, deformable world around them, moving beyond the limitations of rigid-body assumptions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **learn dexterous manipulation of deformable objects using multi-fingered hands**. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations and aims to address specific technical challenges inherent in this domain:

**Motivation:**
1. **Ubiquity of Deformable Objects:** A significant portion of human dexterous manipulation involves deformable objects (e.g., making bread, stuffing dumplings, using sponges). Equipping robots with similar capabilities is essential for applications in domestic robotics, food preparation, healthcare, and manufacturing.
2. **Versatility of Multi-Fingered Hands:** Multi-fingered hands offer unparalleled versatility. They can act as tools (e.g., fingers together like a spatula), perform stable pinches for fine manipulation, or execute complex in-hand reconfigurations. Learning to leverage this versatility for deformable objects is crucial for human-like dexterity.
3. **Human-Centric Data Collection:** Humans intuitively interact with deformable objects using their hands. This makes teleoperation a natural choice for collecting demonstrations, potentially facilitating large-scale data collection using vision-based techniques from existing human videos.

**Key Challenges and Objectives Addressed by DexDeform:**
1. **High Dimensionality and Sample Complexity:**
 - **Challenge:** Deformable objects have effectively infinite degrees of freedom, leading to extremely high-dimensional state spaces. Reinforcement learning (RL) approaches suffer from prohibitive sample complexity in such scenarios.
 - **Objective:** To develop a framework that can learn complex deformable manipulation policies with high data efficiency, avoiding the need for vast amounts of trial-and-error.
2. **Local Optima in Differentiable Physics:**
 - **Challenge:** Trajectory optimization methods relying on differentiable physics are powerful but prone to getting stuck in local optima, especially with the complex, non-smooth contact mechanics involved in multi-fingered manipulation of soft bodies.
 - **Objective:** To overcome the sensitivity of gradient-based optimizers to initial conditions by providing intelligent, human-inspired initializations.
3. **Generalization to Novel Deformable Shapes:**
 - **Challenge:** Policies learned from a limited set of human demonstrations often struggle to generalize to novel target shapes or object configurations not explicitly seen during training.
 - **Objective:** To enable the learned manipulation skills to generalize effectively to a wide range of novel goals and deformable object states, going beyond simple imitation.

To achieve these objectives, DexDeform proposes a principled framework that abstracts manipulation skills from human demonstrations and subsequently refines these learned skills using differentiable physics. This hybrid approach seeks to leverage the best of both worlds: the high-level understanding and "common sense" from human data, and the precise, fine-grained control and exploration capabilities offered by differentiable simulators.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexDeform is a principled, iterative framework designed to learn dexterous manipulation of deformable objects. It combines human demonstrations, skill abstraction, and differentiable physics in a bootstrapping loop. The core methodology consists of three main components:

1. **Collection of Human Manipulation Demonstrations:**
 - **System:** A low-cost ($100) and simple teleoperation system is built using a Leap Motion Controller. This optical hand tracking device captures human finger positions.
 - **Retargeting:** An inverse kinematics (IK) model for the simulated Shadow Dexterous Hand (which has 28 DoFs) retargets the human finger positions to robot joint positions. A position-based PD controller then controls the simulated hand.
 - **Data Size:** A relatively small initial dataset is collected: 10 demonstrations per task variant across six challenging tasks (Folding, Wrap, Flip, Bun, Rope, Dumpling), totaling approximately 60,000 environment steps (about 2 hours of human interaction). This highlights the data efficiency goal of the method.
 - **Simulator:** All experiments are conducted within PlasticineLab, a differentiable physics simulator based on the MLS-MPM (Material Point Method) algorithm, which allows for simulating elastoplastic objects like dough and clay.

2. **Learning Abstractions of Dexterous Skills:**
 - **Goal:** To learn high-level "skills" that abstract away the fine-grained finger movements, allowing for more efficient planning in a lower-dimensional space.
 - **Implicit Scene Representation (ConvONet + VAE):**
 - **ConvONet:** A Convolutional Occupancy Network (ConvONet) is used to create a continuous implicit representation of the scene from unordered point cloud observations (3D position and color). Its encoder maps point clouds to 2D feature maps, which are translationally equivariant.
 - **VAE for Latent Scene Encoding:** A Variational Autoencoder (VAE) then encodes these 2D feature maps into a compact latent vector `s_t`, representing the scene at time `t`. A decoder reconstructs the scene representation from `s_t`. This latent space is critical for dynamics modeling and planning.
 - **Skill Model Architecture:** Inspired by hierarchical imitation learning, the skill model comprises three components:
 - **Skill Encoder (`f`):** Maps a sequence of K-step observation-action pairs (`s_t, a_t..., s_{t+K}, a_{t+K}`) into a latent *skill embedding* `z_t`. This `z_t` encapsulates the high-level intention or effect of the K-step action sequence.
 - **Skill Dynamics Predictor (`T`):** Predicts the future latent scene encoding `s_{t+K}` that results from applying skill `z_t` to the current scene `s_t`. (`s_{t+K} = T(z_t, s_t)`). This allows for planning in imagination.
 - **Skill Action Decoder (`π`):** Decodes the low-level actions `a_t` for each step within the K-step skill from the skill embedding `z_t` and current scene `s_t`. (`a_t = π(a_t | z_t, s_t)`).
 - **Long-Horizon Planning:** Given a target deformable shape (`g`), the system plans a sequence of skill embeddings (`z_1, z_K..., z_H`) by iteratively applying the skill dynamics predictor `T`. The objective is to minimize an occupancy loss (`L_occ`) between the predicted final scene (`s_{H+K}`) and the target goal `g`. This optimization is performed in the latent skill space, making it more efficient than optimizing raw actions.

3. **Differentiable Physics Guided Exploration:**
 - **Challenge of Novel Goals:** How to sample new deformable shapes and generalize beyond initial demonstrations?
 - **Shape Augmentation:** Simple geometric transformations (translation on xz-plane, rotation around y-axis) are applied to existing demonstration shapes to generate new, *novel target goals*. This systematically explores the space of deformable shapes.
 - **Differentiable-Physics Based Trajectory Refinement:**
 - **Initialization:** Trajectories planned by the skill model (using the skill dynamics and decoder) are used as *initializations* for a gradient-based optimizer. This is the crucial step that leverages human-like skill abstractions to provide good starting points for the physics engine.
 - **Refinement:** The differentiable physics simulator (PlasticineLab) then refines these trajectories using first-order gradients to achieve the augmented goals. This fine-tunes the actions for precise deformable manipulation.
 - **Bootstrapping:** Successful refined trajectories are added to the dataset as new demonstrations. This expanded dataset is then used to fine-tune the skill model in subsequent iterations, creating a continuous learning loop where the skill model learns from its own successful "explorations."

In summary, DexDeform iteratively learns from human demonstrations, abstracts these into a skill model for efficient latent space planning, and then uses differentiable physics to explore novel goals and refine trajectories, effectively bootstrapping its own learning process and overcoming the limitations of individual approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on a suite of six challenging dexterous deformable object manipulation tasks (Folding, Wrap, Flip, Bun, Rope, Dumpling) demonstrate the significant effectiveness of DexDeform. The evaluation metric used is the normalized improvement in Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) between the current and goal shapes, indicating how well the policy reduces the shape difference.

**Quantitative Performance (Q1: Comparison with Baselines):**
DexDeform consistently and significantly outperforms all tested baselines across all six tasks.
- **DexDeform Scores:** Achieves high normalized improvement scores, ranging from 0.842 to 0.972, often nearing a perfect match (score of 1).
- **Baselines' Struggles:**
 - **Trajectory Optimization (TrajOpt):** Performed poorly (scores near 0.0-0.1), primarily failing to create meaningful shapes. This confirms its sensitivity to local optima in complex contact scenarios.
 - **Model-free Reinforcement Learning (PPO & DAPG):** Showed moderate performance (scores up to ~0.5-0.6), but struggled with sample complexity and the high-dimensional state space of deformable objects. They could achieve general shape matching but lacked fine-grained dexterity.
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC):** Performed better than RL and TrajOpt (scores up to ~0.6-0.7) but exhibited limited generalization. For instance, it might fold dough in the wrong direction or fail to match specific details, indicating its inability to compositionally reason about deformation.

**Qualitative Results:**
Visualizations (Figure 3) clearly show DexDeform achieving highly accurate and dexterous manipulations, closely matching the target goal shapes, even for complex tasks like "Flip" (in-hand manipulation) or "Rope" (dual-hand fine-grained deformation). Baselines, in contrast, produced less accurate or completely failed deformations.

**Ablation Analysis (Q2: Differentiable Physics Guided Exploration):**
- **Skill-Only vs. DexDeform:** A baseline "Skill-Only" (DexDeform without differentiable physics for exploration) performed worse than the full DexDeform (scores around 0.7-0.9 vs. 0.8-0.97 for DexDeform). This highlights that while the skill model from demonstrations provides a good foundation, the differentiable physics guided exploration is critical for refining trajectories, achieving fine-grained control, and generalizing to novel goals unseen in the initial demonstrations.

**Ablation Analysis (Q3: Benefits of Skill Model):**
- **NN-TrajOpt vs. DexDeform:** The "NN-TrajOpt" baseline replaced the skill model with a heuristic of finding the nearest neighbor demonstration based on EMD and then refining it with differentiable physics. Qualitatively, NN-TrajOpt struggled significantly (Figure 4). This confirms that raw EMD is not a robust metric for identifying suitable policies for soft bodies with large topological variations. DexDeform's skill embeddings, by contrast, offer a more compositional and effective representation of the deformation process, allowing for finding appropriate policies even for novel goals.

**Skill Model Visualization (Q4 & Q5: Consistency and Latent Space):**
- **Consistency of Skill Dynamics:** Visualizations show a high level of consistency between the predicted future latent states from the skill dynamics model and the ground truth states resulting from the actions decoded by the skill decoder (Figure 7). This validates that the learned skill dynamics accurately predict the outcomes of abstracted actions.
- **Latent Space Structure:** t-SNE visualization of skill embeddings for the "Folding" task (Figure 8) reveals distinct clusters that correlate with different folding directions (left, right, front, back, center). This indicates that the latent skill space successfully captures and organizes meaningful semantic categories of manipulation skills.

**Additional Findings (Appendices):**
- **Partial Point Cloud Input:** DexDeform's performance with partial point cloud observations (from four RGBD cameras) showed only a slight drop compared to perfect point cloud input, suggesting robustness to realistic sensing limitations.
- **Baselines with Latent Embeddings:** Providing the same latent scene embeddings to baseline methods (PPO, DAPG, BC) did *not* significantly improve their performance, reinforcing that the architectural design of DexDeform (skill abstraction, planning, and differentiable physics refinement) is the primary driver of its success, rather than merely a superior scene representation.

In conclusion, DexDeform represents a robust and highly effective solution for dexterous deformable object manipulation, significantly outperforming conventional approaches by intelligently combining skill learning from human demonstrations with the power of differentiable physics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

DexDeform makes several significant contributions that could have a profound impact on the field of robotics and beyond:

1. **Pioneering a New Frontier:** By claiming the "first investigation on the learning-based dexterous manipulation of deformable objects," this work opens up a critical and challenging research area. It demonstrates that learning-based methods can indeed tackle the complexities of multi-fingered interaction with highly non-linear, infinitely deformable materials, a domain previously limited to simpler grippers or highly constrained scenarios.

2. **Enabling Complex, Human-like Dexterity:** The successful completion of tasks like "Flip" (in-hand manipulation) and intricate dual-hand tasks (Bun, Rope, Dumpling) showcases a level of dexterity and control over deformable objects that was previously unattainable for autonomous learning systems. This brings robots closer to performing tasks requiring human-like finesse and adaptability, such as food preparation, delicate assembly, or medical procedures.

3. **Powerful Hybrid Learning Framework:** DexDeform presents a highly effective paradigm that synergizes the strengths of different AI methodologies. It leverages:
 - **Human Demonstrations:** For high-level behavioral guidance and intelligent initialization, addressing the exploration problem.
 - **Skill Abstraction:** For decomposing complex, long-horizon tasks into manageable sub-skills, enabling efficient planning in a low-dimensional latent space.
 - **Differentiable Physics:** For fine-grained trajectory refinement, overcoming local optima issues, and providing an efficient mechanism for exploring and achieving novel deformable states.
 This hybrid approach effectively tackles the "chicken-and-egg" problem of exploration and exploitation in high-dimensional, complex environments.

4. **Data Efficiency and Bootstrapping:** The ability to learn complex skills from a *small* initial set of human demonstrations (only 10 per task variant) and then iteratively expand the dataset through self-generated, refined trajectories is a major breakthrough. This addresses a critical bottleneck in robotics, where collecting large, diverse datasets for manipulation tasks is often time-consuming and expensive. The bootstrapping mechanism allows the system to continuously improve and generalize.

5. **Platform for Future Research:** The development of a platform integrating low-cost teleoperation with a differentiable soft-body simulator (PlasticineLab) is itself a valuable contribution. This platform provides researchers with the tools to further explore and accelerate progress in dexterous deformable manipulation, enabling easier data collection and experimentation.

**Potential Impact and Future Directions:**
- **Real-World Applications:** The advancements in dexterous deformable manipulation directly impact industries such as food processing (e.g., automated baking, dumpling making), textile manufacturing (e.g., fabric handling, garment assembly), medical robotics (e.g., manipulating organs, suturing), and domestic robotics (e.g., tidying, cooking).
- **Generalization to Real-World Sensors:** The demonstrated robustness to partial point cloud observations is promising for real-world deployment. Future work on integrating real-world observations (e.g., from RGB-D cameras) and directly transferring policies to physical robots will be crucial for practical impact.
- **Scaling Up Learning:** The paper highlights leveraging "in-the-wild" human videos (e.g., cooking demonstrations on YouTube) as a future direction. Combining DexDeform's framework with large-scale unstructured video data could unlock even broader and more versatile manipulation capabilities.
- **Improved Simulation:** Further speeding up soft-body simulations could enable larger-scale reinforcement learning within the framework, potentially enhancing robustness and generalization.
- **Advanced Skill Composition:** Exploring more sophisticated temporal skill priors and compositional skill learning could allow for even more flexible and adaptable manipulation policies for highly diverse and novel tasks.

In essence, DexDeform pushes the boundaries of robotic dexterity, demonstrating a powerful and practical framework for learning how to interact with the deformable world. Its hybrid approach, combining human intuition with physics-based optimization, sets a strong foundation for future advancements in intelligent manipulation.
