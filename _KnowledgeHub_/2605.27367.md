# SpatialBench: Is Your Spatial Foundation Model an All-Round Player?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and Nanyang Technological University introduced SpatialBench, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating spatial foundation models across diverse scenarios and input densities. The benchmark, which includes 19 datasets and 31 models, found that existing models exhibit generalization limitations, particularly in embodied and egocentric views, and demonstrated how the new DA-Next model, trained on the DA-Next-5M dataset, improves depth and pose estimation in these domains by up to 59% (AbsRel) and 5.5% (AUC@30) respectively.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for spatial foundation models cover limited paradigms and lack standardization, hindering comprehensive cross-model and cross-condition comparisons.
- Current evaluation protocols rarely assess model performance and resource consumption effectively across varying input sequence densities (e.g., sparse images vs. dense video streams).
- Test domains typically used for evaluation (indoor/object-centric) do not adequately represent the diverse environments and viewpoints found in real-world applications like robotics and egocentric perception.

## Method

- **SpatialBench** was developed with a deterministic multi-density evaluation protocol, defining frame indices across four input density regimes (SINGLE, SPARSE, MEDIUM, DENSE) for consistent assessment.
- The benchmark integrates 19 publicly available, real-world, and synthetic datasets, encompassing 546 scenes with diverse environments, dynamics, and viewpoint types (Normal, Egocentric, Wrist-view).
- Unified adapters for 31 state-of-the-art models across six paradigms (optimization-based, end-to-end feed-forward, streaming, chunk-based, SLAM-based, ==test-time== training) were provided for comparison on five task suites.
- A new large-scale dataset, DA-Next-5M (5.5M frames, 22K scenes), focusing on embodied and egocentric views, was introduced, alongside DA-Next, a baseline model specifically designed to address this domain gap.

## Results

- DA-Next, trained on DA-Next-5M, achieved substantial performance gains in embodied domains, showing improvements of **47%** and **59%** in depth AbsRel and **3.1%** and **5.5%** in camera pose AUC@30 on sparse and medium inputs compared to DA3-Giant.
- Test-time training (TTT) methods primarily improve performance on dense, long sequences by enhancing trajectory consistency but can lead to regressions on short, sparse, or discontinuous sequences.
- The relationship between input density and reconstruction quality is not monotonically increasing; while sparse to medium density typically improves results, further increasing to dense inputs does not consistently yield additional gains and can sometimes degrade performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current spatial foundation models are not truly 'all-round players,' demonstrating limitations in generalizing across diverse domains and input densities.
> - Full-context attention models maximize accuracy on high-memory GPUs, while bounded-memory (streaming/chunk-wise) models enable long-sequence scalability at the expense of some pairwise geometric accuracy.
> - Data quality and domain alignment are more decisive than sheer data volume for effective training, with egocentric and wrist-view domains identified as dominant out-of-distribution failure modes.
