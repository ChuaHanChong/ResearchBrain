# SpatialBench: Is Your Spatial Foundation Model an All-Round Player?

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and Nanyang Technological University introduced SpatialBench, a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating spatial foundation models across diverse scenarios and input densities. The benchmark, which includes 19 datasets and 31 models, found that existing models exhibit generalization limitations, particularly in embodied and egocentric views, and demonstrated how the new DA-Next model, trained on the DA-Next-5M dataset, improves depth and pose estimation in these domains by up to 59% (AbsRel) and 5.5% (AUC@30) respectively.

## Problem

- Existing benchmarks for spatial foundation models cover limited paradigms and lack standardization, hindering comprehensive cross-model and cross-condition comparisons.
- Current evaluation protocols rarely assess model performance and resource consumption effectively across varying input sequence densities (e.g., sparse images vs. dense video streams).
- Test domains typically used for evaluation (indoor/object-centric) do not adequately represent the diverse environments and viewpoints found in real-world applications like robotics and egocentric perception.

## Method

- **SpatialBench** was developed with a deterministic multi-density evaluation protocol, defining frame indices across four input density regimes (SINGLE, SPARSE, MEDIUM, DENSE) for consistent assessment.
- The benchmark integrates 19 publicly available, real-world, and synthetic datasets, encompassing 546 scenes with diverse environments, dynamics, and viewpoint types (Normal, Egocentric, Wrist-view).
- Unified adapters for 31 state-of-the-art models across six paradigms (optimization-based, end-to-end feed-forward, streaming, chunk-based, SLAM-based, ==test-time== training) were provided for comparison on five task suites.
- A new large-scale dataset, DA-Next-5M (5.5M frames, 22K scenes), focusing on embodied and egocentric views, was introduced, alongside DA-Next, a baseline model specifically designed to address this domain gap.

## Results

- DA-Next, trained on DA-Next-5M, achieved substantial performance gains in embodied domains, showing improvements of **47%** and **59%** in depth AbsRel and **3.1%** and **5.5%** in camera pose AUC@30 on sparse and medium inputs compared to DA3-Giant.
- Test-time training (TTT) methods primarily improve performance on dense, long sequences by enhancing trajectory consistency but can lead to regressions on short, sparse, or discontinuous sequences.
- The relationship between input density and reconstruction quality is not monotonically increasing; while sparse to medium density typically improves results, further increasing to dense inputs does not consistently yield additional gains and can sometimes degrade performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current spatial foundation models are not truly 'all-round players,' demonstrating limitations in generalizing across diverse domains and input densities.
> - Full-context attention models maximize accuracy on high-memory GPUs, while bounded-memory (streaming/chunk-wise) models enable long-sequence scalability at the expense of some pairwise geometric accuracy.
> - Data quality and domain alignment are more decisive than sheer data volume for effective training, with egocentric and wrist-view domains identified as dominant out-of-distribution failure modes.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Spatial foundation models are central to various applications, including robotics, augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), autonomous driving, and embodied artificial intelligence (AI). These models function as general-purpose visual geometry backbones, capable of reconstructing accurate 3D structures from images or videos. Their widespread adoption underscores their perceived ability to generalize across diverse conditions.

However, the authors identify several limitations in the existing evaluation landscape that hinder a holistic assessment of these models' true generalization capabilities:
1. **Narrow Paradigm Coverage:** Current benchmarks typically evaluate only a limited number of model paradigms (e.g., feed-forward, optimization-based, streaming, SLAM-based, chunk-based, test-time training), often under separate protocols, making cross-paradigm comparisons difficult.
2. **Lack of Standardization:** Even when models are evaluated on the same datasets, variations in scene splits, subsets, frame indices, temporal windows, and input densities lead to ambiguous comparisons.
3. **Limited Assessment of Density Scaling:** Existing protocols rarely explore how model performance and resource consumption change with varying input sequence densities (e.g., from sparse images to dense video streams), which is critical for real-world deployment.
4. **Restricted Test Domains:** Standard indoor or object-centric reconstruction datasets do not adequately represent the diverse environments and viewpoints encountered in real-world applications such as robotics, autonomous driving, egocentric perception, and wrist-mounted manipulation.

SpatialBench positions itself as a response to these gaps, aiming to provide a comprehensive and standardized evaluation framework that can rigorously assess the robustness and generalization of spatial foundation models across a wider spectrum of conditions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the overarching question: **Are spatial foundation models truly all-round players capable of generalizing robustly across diverse downstream tasks, arbitrary viewpoints, shifting scene domains, varying input densities, and specific hardware constraints?**

The motivation for this detailed inquiry stems from the critical role these models are expected to play in demanding real-world applications. The authors argue that merely demonstrating impressive performance on standard datasets is insufficient. A truly robust model must maintain reliability when faced with:
- **Unpredictable Scene Domain Shifts:** Real-world scenarios involve a wide array of environments, objects, and lighting conditions that may differ significantly from training data.
- **Variable Input Densities:** Models need to perform consistently whether processing sparse, unordered image sets or dense, long video streams. This highlights the need to understand how memory constraints and accumulated drift affect performance across different densities.
- **Hardware Memory Constraints:** Practical deployments often operate under strict GPU memory limitations, which can impact the feasibility of certain models, particularly those requiring large computational graphs or context.
- **Diverse Viewpoints:** Applications such as embodied AI and robotics require models to handle views from egocentric cameras, wrist-mounted manipulators, or dynamic platforms, which present unique geometric and visual challenges.

To achieve this, the researchers set out to design a benchmark that is:
- **Cross-paradigm:** Capable of evaluating models from different architectural and operational categories under a unified protocol.
- **Deterministic and Reproducible:** Employing standardized sampling strategies to ensure that evaluations are consistent and comparable across different runs and research groups.
- **Density-aware:** Systematically probing model performance across various input densities to understand scaling behavior.
- **Domain-diverse:** Incorporating a broad range of real-world and synthetic datasets covering different environments, dynamics, and viewpoint types.

Beyond merely evaluating existing models, a secondary objective of this work is to address identified data gaps. Specifically, the paper introduces a new large-scale dataset, DA-Next-5M, and a corresponding baseline model, DA-Next, to push the boundaries of spatial representation learning in underexplored embodied and egocentric domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of SpatialBench is based on three core design principles:

**1. Deterministic Multi-Density Evaluation Protocol:**
To systematically assess model robustness, SpatialBench employs a deterministic sampling strategy to define frame indices across four distinct input density regimes for each scene:
- **SINGLE:** Evaluates monocular depth priors using a single, consistent frame index.
- **SPARSE:** Probes wide-baseline reconstruction from a few unordered views, selected to maximize voxel coverage within a fixed frame budget using a greedy set-cover approach.
- **MEDIUM:** Reflects inputs with moderate overlap, typical of Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and SLAM systems, using a length-adaptive frame budget with a set-cover formulation that favors view overlap.
- **DENSE:** Targets long-horizon, online estimation, requiring models to process nearly every frame of a trajectory up to a maximum frame budget (500 frames) to preserve temporal continuity while ensuring feasibility.

**2. Broad Domain Coverage Across 19 Datasets:**
SpatialBench integrates 19 publicly available, real-world, and synthetic datasets, totaling 546 scenes. These datasets cover a comprehensive range of conditions tagged along axes such as:
- **Environment:** Indoor, Outdoor
- **Dynamics:** Static, Dynamic
- **Viewpoint:** Normal, Egocentric, Wrist-view
- **Data Type:** Real, Synthetic
Examples include 7-Scenes, ADT, DROID, DTU, KITTI-Odometry, NRGBD, ScanNet++, Tanks & Temples, TUM-Dynamic, Waymo, Xperience, RLBench, RoboLab, RoboTwin, and Virtual KITTI. A unified data ingestion pipeline normalizes these heterogeneous datasets into a common representation (RGB, metric depth, camera poses, intrinsics) and applies a five-stage post-processing pipeline for depth maps to ensure quality (including range clipping, flying point removal, bilateral filtering, small isolated region removal, and sky masking for outdoor scenes).

**3. Comprehensive and Cross-Paradigm Model Comparison:**
SpatialBench provides unified adapters for 31 state-of-the-art models and 41 variants across six primary reconstruction paradigms:
- **Optimization-based methods:** E.g., DUSt3R, MASt3R.
- **End-to-end feed-forward methods:** E.g., VGGT, DA3, Pi3, MapAnything, OmniVGGT, AMB3R, WorldMirror.
- **Online/streaming methods:** E.g., Spann3R, CUT3R, MonST3R, Point3R, Stream3R, StreamVGGT, PAGE4D, InfiniteVGGT, WinT3R, LongStream, LingBot-Map.
- **Chunk-based methods:** E.g., VGGT-Long, Pi-Long, DA3-Streaming.
- **SLAM-based methods:** E.g., MASt3R-SLAM, VGGT-SLAM.
- **Test-time training (TTT) methods:** E.g., TTT3R, Scal3R, LoGeR.

The models are evaluated on five task suites:
- **Camera Pose Estimation:** Using Relative Rotation Accuracy (RAcc), Relative Translation Accuracy (TAcc), and Area Under the Curve (AUC).
- **Camera Trajectory Estimation:** For continuous sequences, using Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE), Relative Translation Error (RPE_t_), and Relative Rotation Error (RPE_r_) after Sim(3) alignment.
- **Depth Estimation:** Using AbsRel, SqRel, RMSE, LogRMSE, and threshold inlier ratios (𝛿_𝜏_).
- **Dense-View Reconstruction:** Evaluated using Precision, Recall, F-score, and Overall Chamfer distance.
- **Prior-Enhanced Prediction:** Assesses models capable of incorporating auxiliary priors (e.g., ground-truth depth or camera poses).

**Addressing the Embodied Domain Gap: DA-Next-5M and DA-Next**
To address a significant data gap in embodied and egocentric domains identified by the benchmark, the authors introduce:
- **DA-Next-5M Dataset:** A new large-scale 3D dataset comprising 5.5 million high-quality frames from 22,000 scenes, primarily collected from egocentric and robot wrist-view perspectives. This dataset includes data from Aria Digital Twin (ADT), Colosseum, HOI4D, RLBench, Robolab, RoboTwin, and Xperience.
- **DA-Next Model:** An extension of the DA3 architecture capable of end-to-end absolute scale prediction. It incorporates additional scale tokens and optionally leverages ground-truth camera pose information as auxiliary input. The model uses a transformer encoder with alternating frame-wise and global self-attention, and dual-DPT heads for depth/ray predictions, along with an MLP for scale regression. Training employs a multi-task loss (depth, depth gradients, ray maps, points, and scale) with confidence-weighted ℓ1 terms, coordinate canonicalization, and scale normalization. A stochastic pose-conditioning scheme is used during training, where ground-truth camera information is injected with a 20% probability. DA-Next is trained on DA-Next-5M combined with 11 other general 3D datasets.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluation conducted using SpatialBench yielded several insights:

- **Current Models are Not All-Round Players:** The benchmark's comprehensive assessment indicates that existing spatial foundation models exhibit limitations in generalizing across diverse domains and input densities.
- **Full-Context Attention Maximizes Accuracy on High-Memory GPUs:** Models employing full-context attention (e.g., DA3-Giant, Pi3) consistently demonstrate superior accuracy in depth and pose estimation under a fixed input budget when sufficient GPU memory is available. This suggests that global context is beneficial for geometric reasoning and scene consistency.
- **Bounded-Memory Models Enable Long-Sequence Scalability:** Streaming, chunk-wise, and test-time training (TTT) methods, by restricting active context, exhibit flatter GPU memory consumption curves and can process sequences longer than what full-context models can handle on limited hardware. This comes at the expense of some geometric estimation accuracy for pairwise predictions but yields improved trajectory-level consistency (lower ATE) over long horizons. These two model families occupy complementary operational niches based on accuracy versus scalability requirements.
- **Data Quality Outweighs Data Volume:** While there is a positive correlation between the number of training datasets and overall performance, the quality of training data is a more decisive factor. Methods that utilize carefully curated pseudo-ground-truth supervision (e.g., DA3) can achieve higher composite scores than those relying on larger but noisier training mixtures.
- **Egocentric-View and Wrist-View are Dominant Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Failure Modes:** The most significant generalization gap identified by SpatialBench is in embodied-view domains. Model performance decreases sharply on egocentric and, particularly, wrist-view sequences. This limitation is attributed to the underrepresentation of real robot wrist-view and sparse egocentric data in current pre-training mixtures, indicating a field-level challenge that cannot be resolved solely by scaling existing training data.
- **DA-Next Addresses the Embodied Domain Gap:** The proposed DA-Next model, trained on the DA-Next-5M dataset which specifically includes egocentric and wrist-view data, demonstrates substantial performance gains over DA3-Giant. For instance, it achieved improvements of 47% and 59% in depth AbsRel and 3.1% and 5.5% in camera pose AUC@30 on sparse and medium inputs, respectively. This outcome supports the strategy that targeted in-domain data curation effectively mitigates OOD generalization issues.
- **Test-Time Training (TTT) is Task-Dependent:** TTT methods show performance gains primarily on dense, long sequences, where they improve both pairwise camera pose accuracy and global trajectory consistency. However, these benefits are less consistent or even lead to performance regressions on short, sparse, or discontinuous sequences. This suggests that TTT functions as a mechanism for length generalization rather than a universal performance booster.
- **Impact of Ground-Truth (GT) Priors:** Injecting GT depth priors consistently leads to near-perfect depth estimation accuracy across various models. In contrast, injecting GT camera pose priors yields inconsistent gains, with some models partially overriding the provided poses under challenging conditions, indicating varied adherence to geometric constraints.
- **Optimal Input Density:** The relationship between input density and reconstruction quality is not monotonically increasing. While increasing frames from sparse to medium generally improves results, further increasing to dense inputs does not consistently provide additional gains and can sometimes degrade performance or lead to out-of-memory errors in full-context models due to redundant data and increased memory pressure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SpatialBench establishes a robust and standardized framework for evaluating spatial foundation models, addressing critical limitations in existing benchmarks. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Reproducible and Fair Evaluation:** By providing a deterministic multi-density evaluation protocol and unified adapters for a diverse range of models, SpatialBench enables fair and reproducible comparisons across different paradigms and input conditions. This reduces ambiguity in model assessment and fosters more objective research.
- **Identification of Critical Gaps:** The comprehensive analysis reveals specific weaknesses of current models, particularly concerning domain generalization (especially in embodied and egocentric views) and robustness to varying input densities. This diagnosis is crucial for guiding future research efforts toward developing more general-purpose spatial intelligence.
- **Guidance for Model Design and Data Curation:** The findings offer actionable insights for researchers and developers. The trade-offs between full-context and bounded-memory models inform architectural choices based on application requirements (accuracy vs. scalability). The emphasis on data quality and domain alignment over sheer volume for effective training provides a strategic direction for dataset creation and model pre-training.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** The introduction of DA-Next-5M and DA-Next directly addresses a significant data scarcity in embodied and egocentric domains. This contribution provides valuable resources and a strong baseline for developing spatial foundation models that are more capable of operating in human-centric and robotic manipulation environments.
- **Informing Real-World Deployment:** By simulating diverse real-world conditions (varied domains, densities, and hardware constraints), SpatialBench helps practitioners select and fine-tune spatial foundation models better suited for specific downstream applications in robotics, AR/VR, and autonomous driving, thus improving the reliability and performance of these technologies.
- **Promotion of Holistic Model Development:** The benchmark encourages a shift from specialized model development to creating "all-round players" that can adapt across tasks and environments. This fosters research into models that are inherently more robust and broadly applicable, accelerating the progress of general 3D perception.

Overall, SpatialBench serves as a foundational tool that not only evaluates the current state of spatial foundation models but also actively shapes the direction of future research in 3D vision and embodied AI.
