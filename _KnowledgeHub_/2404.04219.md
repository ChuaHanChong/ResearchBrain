# Continual Policy Distillation of Reinforcement Learning-based Controllers for Soft Robotic In-Hand Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from the University of Pisa and Sant’Anna School of Advanced Studies introduces a Continual Policy Distillation framework that enables a single soft robotic controller to learn dexterous in-hand manipulation of various objects sequentially. The framework effectively consolidates knowledge from multiple object-specific expert policies, mitigating catastrophic forgetting and achieving high manipulation performance on diverse objects, with replay-based strategies like Reward Prioritized Experience Replay showing performance comparable to training on all data simultaneously.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning (RL) controllers for dexterous manipulation are often object-specific, limiting their generalizability and adaptability to diverse objects without extensive retraining.
- Controlling soft robots for precise in-hand manipulation is complex due to their high degrees of freedom, deformability, and the challenge of maintaining stable contact and preventing slippage.
- Robotic systems face computational and memory constraints when continuously learning new tasks, making traditional batch learning impractical and leading to catastrophic forgetting of previously acquired skills.

## Method

- A Continual Policy Distillation (CPD) framework is proposed, leveraging offline demonstrations from object-specific expert RL policies.
- Knowledge from multiple expert policies is consolidated into a single student policy via supervised learning using Policy Distillation, primarily with KL Divergence loss.
- Catastrophic forgetting is mitigated through exemplar-based rehearsal strategies (e.g., ReplayBR, ReplayEX, ReplayRP, ReplayRPR) that store and replay a limited subset of past expert demonstrations.

## Results

- Object-specific expert controllers were successfully trained, with an average rotation of 242 degrees for the cube and 126 degrees for the teddy bear, showcasing diverse manipulation gaits.
- KL Divergence loss achieved the highest performance in offline policy distillation (average 142 degrees rotation), outperforming MSE (140 degrees) and NLL (105 degrees).
- Continual Policy Distillation with Reward Prioritized Experience Replay (ReplayRP) achieved performance comparable to cumulative training (e.g., 174 degrees vs. 170 degrees with 10^3 buffer size), demonstrating effective mitigation of catastrophic forgetting compared to naive incremental learning (average performance dropped to 21 degrees).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - KL Divergence proved superior among tested loss functions for policy distillation, effectively capturing the expert's policy distribution.
> - Exemplar-based rehearsal strategies, particularly Reward Prioritized Experience Replay (ReplayRP) and Ex-Model Experience Replay (ReplayEX), effectively mitigate catastrophic forgetting in continual learning for robotic control.
> - The effectiveness of continual learning strategies is significantly influenced by the size of the replay buffer, with larger buffers generally yielding better knowledge retention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper sits at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields within robotics and artificial intelligence: dexterous manipulation, soft robotics, reinforcement learning (RL), policy distillation (PD), and continual learning (CL).

**Dexterous Manipulation** is a long-standing grand challenge in robotics, aiming to emulate the human ability to grasp, rotate, and translate objects within the hand. Traditional approaches often rely on complex control strategies or precise models, which are difficult to achieve for highly deformable or irregular objects.

**Soft Robotics** is an emerging paradigm that offers inherent compliance, adaptability, and safety, making soft robots well-suited for interaction with unpredictable environments and delicate objects. However, their continuum nature and infinite degrees of freedom pose significant challenges for precise control and modeling compared to rigid robots. This paper specifically addresses the control development for soft robotic hands, which is inherently more intricate due to their compliant behavior and the need for refined finger coordination.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL)** has emerged as a powerful tool for learning complex control policies for robots, including dexterous manipulation. RL agents can learn directly from interaction with the environment, often bypassing the need for explicit models. However, a significant limitation of standard RL is its tendency to produce object-specific or task-specific policies. Training a new policy for every new object or scenario is computationally expensive, time-consuming, and resource-intensive, severely limiting real-world applicability. This work acknowledges this limitation, stating that RL typically results in "specific object controllers without retaining the training environment or data."

**Policy Distillation (PD)** is a technique originating from knowledge distillation, where knowledge is transferred from a larger, more complex "teacher" model to a smaller, simpler "student" model. In RL, PD involves training a student policy to mimic the actions and, more importantly, the action distribution of an expert (teacher) RL policy. This can enable model compression, transfer learning, or, as in this paper, the aggregation of knowledge from multiple experts into a single, more versatile student policy.

**Continual Learning (CL)**, also known as lifelong learning, addresses the problem of learning from a continuous stream of data or tasks without forgetting previously acquired knowledge – a phenomenon known as "catastrophic forgetting." For robots, CL is crucial because they operate in dynamic environments where new tasks, objects, or environmental conditions constantly arise, and it's impractical to retrain the entire system from scratch or store all past data. Real-world robotic applications often face constraints on memory, computational resources, and sequential access to training data.

This research synergizes these concepts by proposing a **Continual Policy Distillation (CPD) framework**. It addresses the challenge of creating a *single, versatile controller* for *general-purpose in-hand manipulation* using *soft robots*. It tackles the problem of disparate, object-specific RL policies by using PD to consolidate knowledge from multiple "expert" policies into a continually evolving "student" policy. By integrating exemplar-based rehearsal methods from CL, the framework aims to mitigate catastrophic forgetting and enable the student policy to generalize across various objects it has sequentially "seen" the experts manipulate. This positions the work as a significant step towards more autonomous, adaptive, and practical soft robotic systems capable of lifelong learning in diverse real-world scenarios, where traditional batch learning is insufficient and data privacy is increasingly important.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a **versatile controller for soft robotic in-hand manipulation** that can effectively rotate various objects differing in shape and size within a four-fingered soft gripper.

The motivation for this objective stems from several critical challenges in the current state of robotic manipulation:

- **Limited Generalizability of RL-based Controllers:** While Reinforcement Learning has shown prowess in training controllers for dexterous manipulation, these policies are often highly specialized for specific objects or tasks. This object-specificity limits their adaptability and generalizability to novel objects or slight variations, making them impractical for real-world applications that demand versatility.
- **Complexity of Soft Robot Control:** Soft robotic hands, despite their advantages in compliance and adaptability, introduce significant control complexity due to their high degrees of freedom, continuous action and observation spaces, and inherent deformability. Developing precise control policies that also ensure stable contact and prevent slippage is a demanding task.
- **Computational and Memory Constraints in Sequential Learning:** Real-world robotic systems often encounter new learning scenarios sequentially, making it difficult to apply traditional batch learning paradigms that require access to all data simultaneously. Training multiple object-specific controllers for soft robots is computationally intensive and memory-demanding.
- **Catastrophic Forgetting in Continual Learning:** When a learning agent is trained sequentially on new tasks, it tends to forget previously acquired knowledge, a phenomenon known as catastrophic forgetting. Overcoming this is essential for robots that must continuously learn and improve over time without losing prior capabilities.
- **Data Privacy Concerns:** Traditional replay-based approaches in CL often store raw or processed data, which could raise data privacy concerns. The authors aim for a method that does not require storing the raw training data of the expert policies.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes the Continual Policy Distillation (CPD) framework. The core idea is to leverage Policy Distillation to transfer knowledge from multiple, pre-trained (object-specific) expert RL policies to a single, continually evolving student policy network. By integrating exemplar-based rehearsal methods, the student policy can retain knowledge from past expert demonstrations while learning from new ones, thereby building a consolidated, versatile controller that can handle a range of objects without forgetting how to manipulate previously encountered ones.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed Continual Policy Distillation (CPD) framework integrates Reinforcement Learning (RL), Policy Distillation (PD), and Continual Learning (CL) strategies. The methodology unfolds in a pipeline consisting of Policy Learning (for experts), Offline Experts’ Demonstrations and Policy Distillation, and Knowledge Integration (Continual Policy Distillation).

#### 3.1. In-Hand Manipulation Task & Simulation Environment

The in-hand manipulation task involves rotating objects within a four-fingered soft gripper. The simulation is conducted using **SoMo**, an open-source framework for simulating soft/rigid hybrid systems, integrated with **SoMoGym** for custom RL training environments, all built upon **PyBullet**. The soft gripper features two independently controlled degrees of freedom (DoFs) per finger (flexion/extension, adduction/abduction) linked by a rigid palm. A variety of non-deformable objects with diverse shapes (cube, rectangle, bunny, cross, teddy bear) are used to evaluate the controller’s versatility.

#### 3.2. Learning Control Policy via Reinforcement Learning (for Experts)

- **Algorithm:** The Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm from **Stable-Baselines3** is used to train object-specific "expert" control policies.
- **Reward Function:** The reward function is designed to promote counter-clockwise rotations around the z-axis (orthogonal to the palm) while maintaining a secure grip. It penalizes changes in object position along the x and y axes.
 - $R_t = 1000(\phi_t - \phi_{t-1}) - |\psi_t - \psi_{t-1}| - |\theta_t - \theta_{t-1}| - |x_t - x_{t-1}| - |y_t - y_{t-1}|$
 - Where $\phi, \psi, \theta$ are rotation angles around z, y, x axes respectively, and $x, y, z$ are positional coordinates.
- **Training and Expert Selection:** Each expert policy is trained for 4 million steps across five concurrent environment instances. The best performing PPO agent (based on evaluation episodic reward) is selected as an expert. Additional criteria for expert selection include stable training history, demonstration of a complex manipulation gait (e.g., repeated grasping/re-grasping), and a unique gait for each object. This ensures the experts are well-trained and provide diverse manipulation strategies.

#### 3.3. Offline Experts’ Demonstrations and Policy Distillation

- **Demonstration Generation:** Instead of directly accessing the expert policy’s training data or environment, the CPD framework uses the *expert policies themselves* to generate state-action pair demonstrations ($D_{\pi^*_i}$). For each expert, $10^3$ episodes of demonstrations are sampled. This addresses data privacy concerns by avoiding the storage of raw training data.
- **Policy Distillation (PD):** The goal is to merge knowledge from multiple experts into a single distilled (student) agent. This is achieved through supervised learning, where the student policy learns to map expert observations to expert actions.
- **Loss Functions for PD:** Three loss functions are evaluated for matching the expert's action distributions:
 - **Mean Squared Error (MSE):** $\ell_{mse}(\pi, s^*, a^*) = ||(\pi(s^*) - a^*)||_2^2$
 - **Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL):** $\ell_{nll}(\pi, s^*, a^*) = - \ln \pi(a^*|s^*)$
 - **Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence:** $\ell_{kl}(\pi, \pi^*) = \log \frac{\sigma^*}{\sigma} + \frac{\sigma^2 + (\mu - \mu^*)^2}{2\sigma^{*2}} - \frac{1}{2}$
 - For KL, expert actions $a^*_j$ are treated as the mean of a deterministic distribution with a fixed small standard deviation ($\sigma^* = 1e^{-6}$).

#### 3.4. Knowledge Integration: Continual Policy Distillation (CPD)

- **Assumptions for CL:** The process assumes object-specific environments are only for evaluation, experts are only for demonstration generation, demonstrations are sequential, and the memory buffer size for demonstrations is limited.
- **CL Framework:** The Avalanche framework is used to facilitate the CL implementation. The scenario is defined as **domain-incremental learning**, where experiences share the same action space but have different data distributions (e.g., different objects). Object IDs are embedded in the observation space to distinguish tasks, though sensory-based recognition is highlighted as a future improvement.
- **Student Policy Architecture:** A "blank" student policy with the same architecture as the expert PPO agent is initialized. It is then trained via supervised learning (policy distillation) using the expert demonstrations.
- **Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting:** Exemplar-based rehearsal methods are employed. These methods store a subset of previously seen expert demonstrations (exemplars) in a limited memory buffer ($B_{exp}$) and replay them during training with new data. This helps retain performance on past tasks while learning new ones.
- **Replay Strategies:** Four specific replay strategies for managing the limited-size buffer are compared:
 - **Experience Balanced Replay (ReplayBR):** Ensures a balanced selection of samples across all encountered experiences, with each experience contributing equally to the buffer size ($M_i = M/N$).
 - **Ex-Model Experience Replay (ReplayEX):** Randomly subsamples demonstrations from the current buffer and the new experience to fill the buffer.
 - **Reward Prioritized Experience Replay (ReplayRP):** A greedy strategy that prioritizes storing exemplars from episodes with the highest episodic rewards.
 - **Reward Weighted Reservoir Sampling Experience Replay (ReplayRPR):** Enhances ReplayRP by introducing randomness into the sampling process, aiming for greater diversity while still favoring higher-rewarding examples.
- **Baselines:**
 - **Naive Task Incremental Learning:** A lower bound, where the model is fine-tuned sequentially on new tasks without any explicit forgetting mitigation strategy.
 - **Joint Training (Cumulative Training):** An upper bound, where the model is trained offline with simultaneous access to *all* expert demonstrations accumulated over time.

Experiments systematically vary the replay buffer size (M = $10^3$, $10^2$, $10^1$ episodes) to evaluate the robustness of the CL strategies under memory constraints. Performance is measured by the average task score (rotation in degrees) across all tasks encountered so far.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results provide a comprehensive evaluation of the proposed CPD framework, highlighting the effectiveness of different components and strategies.

#### 4.1. Expert Control Policy Learning

- **Successful Expert Training:** PPO successfully trained object-specific expert controllers for in-hand manipulation of various objects (cube, rectangle, bunny, cross, teddy bear). Learning curves (Fig. 4 for cube) showed stable training and consistent performance across different random seeds.
- **Varying Task Complexity:** Different objects posed varying levels of complexity, resulting in diverse task scores (Fig. 5). Regular shapes like the cube achieved higher average rotation (242 degrees) compared to irregular shapes like the teddy bear (126 degrees), highlighting the inherent challenges of manipulating complex geometries.
- **Unique Manipulation Gaits:** Analysis of expert policy rollouts (Fig. 6) revealed distinct manipulation strategies tailored to each object's geometry. For instance, rectangular objects involved maximal finger extension for smooth passage, while cross-shaped objects used multi-step pushes and re-grasps. Irregular objects like the bunny and teddy bear required more complex, less stable interactions. These unique gaits confirmed the suitability of the experts for knowledge distillation and observing forgetting.

#### 4.2. Continual Policy Distillation

- **Policy Distillation Loss Function:** When evaluating offline Policy Distillation with Joint Training (Table I), the **KL Divergence (KL)** loss function demonstrated the highest performance (average 142 degrees across all objects), slightly outperforming MSE (140 degrees) and significantly outperforming Negative Log-Likelihood (NLL) (105 degrees). This indicates KL's effectiveness in capturing the expert's policy distribution, including its exploration and exploitation strategies. Consequently, KL was chosen for subsequent CPD experiments.
- **Performance Baselines (Table II):**
 - **Naive Incremental Learning (Lower Bound):** This baseline exhibited significant **catastrophic forgetting**. As the agent learned new tasks sequentially, its performance on previously learned tasks drastically decreased. For example, after learning the "Teddy" task, the average performance across all tasks dropped to 21 degrees from 242 (for the "Cube" task alone), illustrating the severe knowledge loss.
 - **Cumulative Training (Upper Bound):** This baseline, which trains on all accumulated data from past and current experiences, represents the ideal performance without memory constraints. It maintained high performance across all tasks as new experiences were added, demonstrating the potential for versatile control when all data is available.

- **Continual Learning Strategies (Table III):** The performance of replay-based CL strategies was evaluated across different replay buffer sizes (M = $10^3$, $10^2$, $10^1$).
 - **General Improvement over Naive:** All replay-based CL strategies (ReplayBR, ReplayRP, ReplayRPR, ReplayEX) significantly mitigated catastrophic forgetting compared to Naive Incremental Learning, achieving much higher average scores across tasks.
 - **Impact of Buffer Size:** As expected, the performance of all replay strategies deteriorated when the replay buffer size decreased from $10^3$ to $10^1$. This highlights the critical role of sufficient memory for effective knowledge retention.
 - **ReplayRP Performance:** The **Reward Prioritized Experience Replay (ReplayRP)** consistently showed strong performance, often exhibiting results comparable to the upper-bound Cumulative Training, especially with larger buffer sizes (e.g., 174 avg for M=$10^3$ vs. 170 for Cumulative). This is attributed to its greedy strategy of prioritizing high-rewarding exemplars.
 - **ReplayEX Performance:** **Ex-Model Experience Replay (ReplayEX)** demonstrated favorable performance with relatively larger buffer sizes (M=$10^3$, $168$ avg; M=$10^2$, $156$ avg). Its ability to incorporate a diverse set of current and past data proved effective, but its performance dropped sharply with a very small buffer (M=$10^1$, $81$ avg), indicating its reliance on data diversity.
 - **ReplayRPR Performance:** **Reward Weighted Reservoir Sampling Experience Replay (ReplayRPR)** achieved its best performance when the buffer size was M=$10^2$ (170 avg), sometimes outperforming ReplayRP at this specific size. This suggests that the added randomness for diversity, even with reward prioritization, can be beneficial at certain memory capacities.
 - **ReplayBR Performance:** **Experience Balanced Replay (ReplayBR)** showed comparable performance when a sufficiently large buffer was provided (M=$10^3$, $162$ avg; M=$10^2$, $161$ avg), performing reasonably well by balancing samples across experiences.

In summary, the results demonstrate that Continual Policy Distillation with appropriate replay strategies effectively consolidates knowledge from multiple expert policies, creating a versatile soft robotic controller while successfully mitigating catastrophic forgetting. ReplayRP and ReplayEX (with sufficient memory) emerged as particularly effective strategies, closely approaching the performance of training on all data simultaneously.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Continual Policy Distillation (CPD) framework proposed in this paper offers several significant contributions and holds substantial potential impact for the field of robotics, particularly in soft manipulation and AI.

- **Advancement in Soft Robotic Manipulation:** The work directly addresses a critical challenge in soft robotics: developing robust and versatile control strategies for dexterous manipulation. By demonstrating a controller capable of rotating diverse objects using a multi-fingered soft gripper, the research pushes the boundaries of what compliant robots can achieve, moving beyond simple grasping to complex in-hand reorientation. This is crucial for deploying soft robots in real-world applications where object characteristics are varied and often unpredictable.

- **Mitigation of Catastrophic Forgetting in Robotics:** The successful application of continual learning techniques, specifically exemplar-based rehearsal methods, to this complex robotic control problem is a major achievement. Catastrophic forgetting is a fundamental hurdle for any lifelong learning agent, and its effective mitigation in a continuous action/observation space setting for soft robots significantly contributes to the feasibility of truly adaptive and continuously learning robotic systems. The comparison with the "Naive Incremental Learning" baseline vividly illustrates the importance and success of the chosen CL strategies.

- **Versatile and Adaptive Controllers:** The core impact is the creation of a single, versatile controller that can handle multiple distinct objects, rather than requiring individual, object-specific policies. This reduces the burden of training and deployment, making robotic systems more practical and autonomous. The ability to integrate knowledge from different expert behaviors into a unified policy enhances the adaptability of the robot to new tasks and environments.

- **Efficiency in Learning:** The CPD approach offers significant advantages in terms of time efficiency compared to traditional online Reinforcement Learning (RL) that would train a single agent from scratch for each object, or even for multiple objects simultaneously. By learning from "offline" expert demonstrations, the training time is substantially reduced. This is a crucial practical benefit for real-world robot deployment where extensive online interaction for training is often prohibitive due to time, safety, and resource constraints.

- **Enhanced Data Privacy and Resource Management:** By relying on generated expert demonstrations rather than the raw data used to train the experts, the framework inherently offers a degree of data privacy. Furthermore, the use of a limited-size memory buffer for exemplars optimizes memory efficiency, which is vital for resource-constrained robotic platforms. This demonstrates a pathway towards learning without needing to store or re-access vast amounts of potentially sensitive raw interaction data.

- **Guidance for Future Research:** The paper provides valuable insights into the crucial choices for continual policy distillation, such as the selection of appropriate loss functions (KL divergence proved superior) and replay strategies. It highlights the trade-offs between memory buffer size and strategy effectiveness, offering practical guidelines for future implementations. The discussion also points to exciting future directions, including the development of generative models for demonstrations (to eliminate reliance on simulated environments for demonstration generation) and the integration of sensory-based object recognition (to move beyond explicit object IDs, enabling true zero-shot learning for novel objects).

Overall, this research represents a significant step towards developing intelligent, adaptive, and scalable soft robotic systems that can continually acquire and retain knowledge, enabling them to perform a wide range of dexterous manipulation tasks effectively and efficiently in diverse, dynamic, and unconstrained environments.
