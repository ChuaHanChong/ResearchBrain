# VGR: Visual Grounded Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> VGR introduces a framework that enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to perform self-driven, selective visual analysis and integrate fine-grained visual details directly into their reasoning processes. This approach improves performance on perception-heavy tasks, such as achieving +4.5 on MMStar and +9.0 on ChartQA, while utilizing 70% fewer image tokens compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from an inherent 'language bias,' often relying predominantly on reasoning within the pure language space.
- Current multimodal reasoning approaches struggle with 'perception-heavy' tasks due to limited fine-grained visual understanding and an inability to comprehensively utilize intricate image details.
- Direct application of chain-of-thought (CoT) paradigms to MLLMs often leads to performance degradation in perception-heavy tasks due to the accumulation of this language bias.

## Method

- Introduces ==VGR==, a framework featuring a =='self-driven selective visual replay'== mechanism, enabling MLLMs to autonomously generate signals for specific bounding boxes and retrieve corresponding fine-grained visual features.
- Integrates these replayed visual features directly into the Large Language Model's input sequence, augmenting its reasoning with precise visual clues at relevant steps.
- Develops ==VGR-SFT==, a large-scale, multi-stage supervised fine-tuning dataset specifically designed to train MLLMs for reasoning with mixed vision grounding and language deduction, using a cold-start, reject sampling, and annotation model pipeline.

## Results

- VGR-7B consistently outperforms a wide range of existing MLLMs on multimodal benchmarks, achieving gains such as **+4.5** on MMStar (**41.7**) and **+9.0** on ChartQA (**67.7**) compared to the LLaVA-NeXT-7B baseline.
- The framework achieves these performance improvements with high computational efficiency, utilizing only **30%** of the image token count (**864 tokens**) compared to the LLaVA-NeXT baseline (**2880 tokens**).
- Ablation studies confirm that both explicit visual grounding cues, structured reasoning processes, and an auxiliary detection loss for bounding box prediction are critical for effective multimodal understanding and accurate spatial localization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly grounding reasoning steps in selected visual regions effectively mitigates language bias in MLLMs, leading to improved performance on perception-heavy tasks.
> - A self-driven selective visual replay mechanism, allowing the model to autonomously choose when and which fine-grained visual details to integrate, enhances perception while significantly improving computational efficiency.
> - The VGR-SFT data curation pipeline, involving cold-start generation, multi-step rejection sampling, and an annotation model, is crucial for creating high-quality, visually grounded reasoning data that enables autonomous region attention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work is situated within the field of multimodal chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning, specifically addressing limitations observed in existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs). Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated advanced reasoning capabilities in domains such as mathematical deduction and scientific analysis. Recent efforts have extended these capabilities to vision-language tasks by distilling knowledge from LLMs into multimodal architectures.

However, a consistent challenge in multimodal reasoning has been "language bias," where models tend to over-rely on linguistic priors, leading to performance degradation in tasks that require detailed visual perception. Previous studies indicate that CoT prompting, while effective in text-only contexts, can sometimes hinder performance in perception-heavy tasks due to this bias. Current approaches for reasoning MLLMs largely focus on solving math and science problems with image inputs, often involving generating detailed captions for images, using reinforcement learning (RL) for open-ended visual grounding, or employing supervised CoT training for high-resolution grounding (e.g., simulating human-like zooming or interactive reasoning). These methods have shown gains on specific benchmarks but do not universally resolve the language bias issue.

VGR introduces an alternative approach that complements these existing methods. It aims to integrate targeted visual analysis directly into the reasoning process, moving beyond reasoning solely in the linguistic space. The paper argues that effective multimodal reasoning, similar to human cognition, should involve recalling and mentally simulating visual content on demand, rather than being confined to sequential linguistic deduction or rigid multi-turn interactions. This positions VGR as an attempt to enhance the granularity and accuracy of visual-linguistic reasoning by enabling MLLMs to actively select and utilize fine-grained visual information during their thought processes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work arises from identified limitations in current multimodal chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning approaches. Existing MLLMs predominantly perform reasoning within a purely linguistic space, which leads to several issues:

- **Language Bias**: Over-reliance on linguistic priors can cause systematic performance drops in tasks that heavily depend on visual perception. The model may prioritize textual information or pre-learned linguistic patterns over actual visual evidence.
- **Limited Scope for Complex Visual Reasoning**: Current methods are largely confined to domains like math or science, where problems can often be reduced to symbolic manipulation or rule-based deduction, rather than demanding a comprehensive understanding of intricate image details across diverse scenarios.
- **Inadequate Handling of Fine-Grained Visual Information**: The inability to dynamically access and utilize specific visual details limits the models' capacity to address complex queries requiring precise visual grounding. Models may struggle with small objects, subtle visual cues, or dense information within high-resolution images.

To address these limitations, the paper introduces VGR with the following key objectives:

- **Enable Visual Grounded Reasoning**: The central objective is to develop a novel reasoning MLLM that can "replay the visual memory during thinking just like humans." This means the model should not only process an image once but actively revisit and extract relevant visual information as reasoning unfolds.
- **Integrate Targeted Visual Analysis**: VGR aims to move beyond reasoning solely in the linguistic space. It seeks to empower the model to first "think" about the question, then dynamically detect and focus on relevant regions within the image that are critical for problem-solving.
- **Dynamic Visual Memory Replay**: A specific goal is to design a mechanism that allows the model to selectively retrieve and integrate visual memory from identified critical areas into its reasoning process on demand. This enhances multimodal comprehension by providing contextually relevant visual clues at precise moments.
- **Improve Efficiency and Resolution Handling**: The design intends to maintain fine-grained visual details from high-resolution inputs while simultaneously improving computational efficiency. This involves strategies for processing and pooling visual tokens effectively.
- **Curate a Specialized Dataset**: To train the model in this novel reasoning paradigm, a large-scale supervised fine-tuning (SFT) dataset, VGR-SFT, is needed. This dataset must contain reasoning data that blends vision grounding with language deduction, teaching the model to actively select grounding areas.
- **Enhance Performance on Perception-Heavy Benchmarks**: The ultimate goal is to demonstrate superior performance on multimodal benchmarks that specifically require comprehensive understanding of image details, thereby validating the effectiveness of visual grounded reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VGR introduces a novel framework that enables an MLLM to dynamically attend to visual content during its reasoning process. The core components of the methodology include a dynamic visual memory replay module, a specialized training objective, and a three-stage data curation pipeline.

**VGR Framework Overview:**
Unlike conventional MLLMs that process an image and then primarily reason in a linguistic space, VGR first initiates a thought process based on the question. During this thinking phase, it is designed to detect relevant regions within the input image that may be crucial for solving the problem. Subsequently, visual memory corresponding to these critical areas is extracted and integrated to assist the ongoing reasoning. This approach aims to mirror human cognitive processes where individuals recall and mentally simulate visual information while reasoning.

**Dynamic Visual Memory Replay Module:**
This module is responsible for retrieving and replaying image tokens generated by the vision encoder and adapter. It leverages a modified version of LLaVA's AnyRes approach for high-resolution image encoding.
1. **Image Encoding**: The input image is resized to dimensions divisible by a patch size (p=336). It is then partitioned into non-overlapping `p x p` patches. Each patch is processed by a vision encoder and adapter to yield token embeddings in the language space.
2. **Visual Memory Construction**: The feature representations of these patches are concatenated to form a unified image feature, `S`, which serves as the visual memory.
3. **Expand-then-Compress Strategy**: To preserve high-resolution details while maintaining efficiency, VGR scales up the maximum crop count of LLaVA’s AnyRes from 4 to 16 patches. A 2D pooling strategy is applied to compress these features: 2x2 pooling for the snapshot (base image) and 4x4 pooling for high-resolution AnyRes token compression. This design reduces token usage significantly (e.g., 144 tokens for snapshot, up to 720 for high-resolution crops, compared to LLaVA-NeXT's 2880 tokens) while expanding supported resolutions.
4. **Replay Control Signal**: The MLLM is trained to generate a predefined special signal, `sot>[x1, y1, x2, y2]<eot>`, during reasoning when it requires visual grounding. `[x1, y1]` and `[x2, y2]` denote the top-left and bottom-right corners of the region of interest.
5. **Feature Retrieval**: Upon detecting the `<eot>` signal, the model parses the coordinates. If valid, the corresponding feature patch is extracted from the visual memory `S`. This extracted feature map is then down-sampled using 2x2 pooling and flattened into a 1D token sequence, which is immediately appended to the LLM's input sequence following the control signal.

**Training Supervision:**
The model is trained using supervised fine-tuning.
1. **Standard SFT**: The retrieved image tokens, along with the replay signal and text tokens, are included in the training sequence. Cross-entropy loss is applied to the signal and text tokens, while all image tokens (from original input and replay regions) are excluded from loss computation.
2. **Auxiliary Detection Loss**: To enhance the model's ability to accurately select and localize regions, an auxiliary detection loss (`L_det`) is introduced. This loss operates as a regression task for bounding box coordinates, combining `ℓ1` loss and Generalized IoU (GIoU) loss (`L_det = ℓ1 + βℓGIoU`). A small Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) detection head maps the hidden states of the `<eot>` token to a 4-dimensional bounding box.

**Visual Reasoning Data Curation (VGR-SFT):**
A large-scale SFT dataset is curated using a three-stage pipeline to teach VGR the "grounding-then-answering" capability.
1. **Cold-Start Data Annotation**: An initial dataset is generated using a powerful 72B MLLM (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B). This model is prompted to generate both a reasoning chain and an answer, explicitly localizing key image regions relevant to the answer using a JSON format (bounding boxes and semantic labels). These regions are designated as replayed areas.
2. **Reject Sampling**: This stage refines the cold-start data through a pipeline with three verification steps:
 - **Format Verification**: Ensures answers are extractable and bounding boxes/labels are in valid JSON format.
 - **Correctness Verification**: Assesses answer accuracy. ANLS (Average Normalized Levenshtein Similarity) is used for closed-ended tasks, while an MLLM semantically aligns reasoning chains for open-ended tasks. Incorrect answers are discarded, and inaccurate ones are rewritten.
 - **Visual Grounding Verification**: An MLLM checks alignment between cropped visual content and annotated labels. Bounding box areas are intentionally expanded to encourage contextual information retention.
3. **Data Scaling with Annotation Model**:
 - Due to the high rejection rate and slow generation speed of the cold-start model, a smaller 14B MLLM (e.g., InternVL3) is trained as an annotation model using the refined cold-start data and additional reasoning data from sources like Open-R1 (a distilled dataset for conventional reasoning).
 - This annotation model significantly improves the pass rate (from 14% to 40%) and annotation speed (3.2x), enabling cost-effective data scaling.
 - Finally, the accepted annotated data undergoes a refinement process where an MLLM revises reasoning chains to enhance robustness, conciseness, and alignment with a predefined template, forming the VGR-SFT dataset (158.1k entries curated from various VQA, OCR, and ScienceQA datasets).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of VGR was evaluated through extensive experiments, primarily building upon the LLaVA-NeXT-7B baseline.

**Experiment Settings:**
The models were trained with a CLIP-ViT-L/14@336 visual encoder and Vicuna-v1.5 (7B and 13B versions) as the base LLM. Training involved two stages: pre-training on LLaVA-558K and supervised fine-tuning on a combination of LLaVA-NeXT-770K and the newly constructed VGR-SFT 158K data. Learning rates were set at 1e-5 for pre-training and 2e-5 for fine-tuning, with the ViT learning rate at 1/10th of the base rate. All datasets were derived from original LLaVA-NeXT SFT data to ensure fair comparison.

**Comparison with Existing Methods:**
VGR consistently demonstrated superior performance across multiple multimodal benchmarks, particularly those requiring fine-grained comprehension of high-resolution images.
- Compared to the LLaVA-NeXT-7B baseline, VGR-7B (using 864 visual tokens) achieved improvements of +4.1 on MMStar, +7.1 on AI2D, and +12.9 on ChartQA.
- Notably, VGR achieved these gains while utilizing approximately 0.3x the number of visual tokens (864 vs. 2880) compared to the original LLaVA-NeXT, indicating substantial efficiency improvements. This performance gap widened further with increased image token counts.
- VGR also outperformed other strong MLLMs such as Qwen-VL-Chat, Visual CoT, and DeepSeek-VL-7B on various benchmarks (MMStar, ChartQA, DocVQA, TextVQA, InfoQA, AI2D, RWQA, POPE).
- An additional comparison (Appendix A.2) showed VGR to be significantly faster than ZoomEye, a recursive tree-based search method, with average inference times of 7.5s (V* Bench) vs. 48.5s (ZoomEye) and 9.2s (HR-Bench 8K) vs. 55.5s (ZoomEye).

**Ablation Studies:**
1. **Generalizability with Different Backbones**: Experiments replacing visual encoders (SigLIP, InternViT) and base LLMs (Qwen2.5) confirmed VGR's consistent performance improvements. It specifically enhanced performance on high-resolution benchmarks like V\* Bench and HR-Bench 8K, which demand visual grounding capabilities.
2. **Data Formulations**: Removing either the visual memory component ("w/o Memory") or the reasoning process ("w/o Reasoning") led to performance degradation, indicating that both visual grounding and linguistic reasoning components contribute complementarily to VGR's effectiveness.
3. **Public CoT Data**: VGR's explicit guidance using regions of interest outperformed vanilla CoT datasets like LLaVA-CoT and MMPR. Direct application of these complex reasoning datasets sometimes yielded results inferior to the baseline, possibly due to accumulated language bias, reinforcing the benefit of integrating visual features of regions of interest.
4. **Auxiliary Detection Loss**: The auxiliary detection loss, designed for accurate bounding box regression, improved overall performance (e.g., +1.9 on MMStar), demonstrating its role in precise spatial localization.
5. **Dynamic Visual Memory Replay**: Excluding the dynamic visual memory replay (i.e., only outputting regions without incorporating corresponding image features) resulted in limited performance gains, highlighting the critical role of integrating image features from boundary regions into the LLM's input sequence.
6. **Reasoning Data Type**: Using refined, "rewritten" (shorter, condensed) reasoning data in SFT led to better performance than raw, longer annotated data. The rewritten data was less confusing and enabled the model to develop stronger grounded reasoning abilities.
7. **Replayed Strategies**: Optimal pooling configurations involved 2x2 pooling for base images and replayed visual memory, and 4x4 pooling for local images. This strategy balanced the retention of critical details with redundancy reduction. The use of visual memory for feature retrieval achieved comparable performance to re-inputting cropped images into the ViT but with reduced training and inference costs.
8. **Test-Time Replay Token Scaling**: Increasing the number of cropped images during testing, while keeping the pooling strategy consistent, further improved performance, particularly for OCR-related tasks.
9. **Inaccurate Bounding Box Replay (Appendix B.2)**: Experiments with inaccurate bounding boxes (random visual features with correct text tokens, or both random) showed significant performance degradation, underscoring the necessity of accurate replay predictions.
10. **Relationship Between Replay and Grounding (Appendix B.3)**: VGR training also led to consistent improvements on Referring Expression Comprehension (REC) tasks, indicating an enhancement in grounding performance.
11. **VGR on Other Backbones (Appendix B.4)**: Even a VGR-style post-training stage applied to InternVL3-8B (without the full multi-stage pipeline) yielded improvements on key benchmarks (DocVQA, ChartQA, TextVQA), suggesting that the proposed strategy is transferable across different architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents VGR, a framework designed to enhance multimodal comprehension in MLLMs by integrating dynamic visual grounding into the reasoning process. The work makes several contributions:

1. **Novel Reasoning Framework**: VGR introduces a new paradigm for MLLMs to perform visual reasoning. It enables models to dynamically and selectively attend to visual content during inference, which can lead to enhanced reasoning accuracy, particularly for tasks demanding fine-grained visual details within high-resolution inputs. This approach, mirroring human cognitive processes of recalling visual memory, offers a more robust method for integrating visual information.
2. **Specialized Data Curation**: The paper details the creation of VGR-SFT, a large-scale reasoning dataset that explicitly incorporates visual cues and replay signals. This dataset is designed to train MLLMs to actively identify and utilize relevant visual regions, moving beyond text-only chains-of-thought or rigid interaction protocols. The three-stage pipeline (cold-start, reject sampling, data scaling) provides a structured method for building high-quality, task-specific multimodal reasoning data.
3. **Improved Efficiency and Performance**: VGR demonstrates that focusing computational resources on pertinent regions, rather than processing the entire image uniformly, can lead to both superior performance and increased efficiency. The model achieves competitive results using significantly fewer visual tokens (e.g., 0.3x compared to baseline LLaVA-NeXT), suggesting a more economical use of computational resources without sacrificing detail understanding. This establishes a new direction for enhancing computational efficiency in MLLMs.

The potential impact of VGR extends to various applications requiring deep visual-linguistic understanding, such as complex document analysis, chart interpretation, and nuanced visual question answering. By mitigating language bias and enabling MLLMs to actively engage with visual information, VGR could lead to more reliable, interpretable, and robust AI systems in multimodal domains.

**Limitations and Future Directions**:
The paper acknowledges certain limitations and outlines avenues for future research. Currently, VGR is implemented on the LLaVA architecture. Exploring its integration with stronger visual encoders and more advanced LLMs could further enhance performance. Additionally, incorporating reinforcement learning (RL) into the training process is suggested as a method to achieve a more generalized and diverse reasoning capability, potentially allowing for more flexible and less constrained visual grounding strategies.
