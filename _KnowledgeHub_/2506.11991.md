# VGR: Visual Grounded Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> VGR introduces a framework that enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to perform self-driven, selective visual analysis and integrate fine-grained visual details directly into their reasoning processes. This approach improves performance on perception-heavy tasks, such as achieving +4.5 on MMStar and +9.0 on ChartQA, while utilizing 70% fewer image tokens compared to baselines.

## Problem

- Existing Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) suffer from an inherent 'language bias,' often relying predominantly on reasoning within the pure language space.
- Current multimodal reasoning approaches struggle with 'perception-heavy' tasks due to limited fine-grained visual understanding and an inability to comprehensively utilize intricate image details.
- Direct application of chain-of-thought (CoT) paradigms to MLLMs often leads to performance degradation in perception-heavy tasks due to the accumulation of this language bias.

## Method

- Introduces ==VGR==, a framework featuring a =='self-driven selective visual replay'== mechanism, enabling MLLMs to autonomously generate signals for specific bounding boxes and retrieve corresponding fine-grained visual features.
- Integrates these replayed visual features directly into the Large Language Model's input sequence, augmenting its reasoning with precise visual clues at relevant steps.
- Develops ==VGR-SFT==, a large-scale, multi-stage supervised fine-tuning dataset specifically designed to train MLLMs for reasoning with mixed vision grounding and language deduction, using a cold-start, reject sampling, and annotation model pipeline.

## Results

- VGR-7B consistently outperforms a wide range of existing MLLMs on multimodal benchmarks, achieving gains such as **+4.5** on MMStar (**41.7**) and **+9.0** on ChartQA (**67.7**) compared to the LLaVA-NeXT-7B baseline.
- The framework achieves these performance improvements with high computational efficiency, utilizing only **30%** of the image token count (**864 tokens**) compared to the LLaVA-NeXT baseline (**2880 tokens**).
- Ablation studies confirm that both explicit visual grounding cues, structured reasoning processes, and an auxiliary detection loss for bounding box prediction are critical for effective multimodal understanding and accurate spatial localization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly grounding reasoning steps in selected visual regions effectively mitigates language bias in MLLMs, leading to improved performance on perception-heavy tasks.
> - A self-driven selective visual replay mechanism, allowing the model to autonomously choose when and which fine-grained visual details to integrate, enhances perception while significantly improving computational efficiency.
> - The VGR-SFT data curation pipeline, involving cold-start generation, multi-step rejection sampling, and an annotation model, is crucial for creating high-quality, visually grounded reasoning data that enables autonomous region attention.
