# CAMP: Cooperative Arm-Hand Motion Planning in Constrained Spaces

> [!summary] Summary
> CAMP is a cooperative arm-hand motion planner for constrained spaces. It lifts multiple low-dimensional arm-space guides into full 22-DoF trajectories via layered bidirectional hand-RRT with bounded local arm relaxation, encodes candidates as endpoint-preserving via-point movement primitives, and refines them by parallel coarse-to-fine joint optimization. Across six constrained simulation tasks it reaches 84.2-98.5% planning success, beating sampling, optimization, and hybrid baselines at competitive planning time.

## Problem

- Decomposing into arm planning then hand motion shrinks the search space but misses solutions needing coordinated adaptation, since a fixed hand shape restricts the collision-free arm set.
- Planning directly in the joint 22-DoF space preserves coupling, but sampling-based planners degrade in the enlarged nonconvex space and trajectory optimizers depend heavily on initialization.
- Multilevel lifting methods assume a simplified-robot path admits a collision-free lift; hand-designed local patterns fail when the guide route itself is infeasible for the full system.

## Method

- ==Feasible hand fiber== formulation: project the collision-free joint space onto arm space, defining per-arm-configuration sets of collision-free hand configurations that make the coupling geometric and guide joint adaptation.
- **Arm-relaxed layered Hand-RRT** initialization: ==RRT-Connect== supplies diverse arm guides discretized into layers; bidirectional hand search connects layers, and local arm-hand replanning perturbs arm joints within a bound when interpolated midpoints collide.
- Endpoint-preserving ==VMP== encoding plus coarse-to-fine joint optimization: Gaussian-basis weights with a vanishing envelope compress trajectories; a coarse stage anchors arm weights to favor hand corrections, a fine stage prioritizes quality.

## Results

- Over 10 batches of 100 trials per task, CAMP leads every planner: **92.5%** versus **71.5%** for A*+CHOMP on Narrow Passage, **84.2%** versus **18.8%** on Cabinet Cylinder Pregrasp.
- Decoupled baselines collapse where hand shape must change: Cabinet Cylinder Pregrasp yields **1%** arm-then-hand and **6%** preshape-then-arm-then-hand against CAMP's **84%**, and CAMP also cuts planning time versus CHOMP variants.
- Ablations on Ball-in-Box Pregrasp: **92%** success full method versus **74%** fixed-arm, **79%** waypoint-wise (4,356 variables, 84.97 s), **81%** fine-only; real-robot trials reach **80-90%** across three tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fixing one subsystem's configuration shrinks the other's feasible set; when subsystems share collision constraints, treat feasibility as configuration-dependent fibers, not a product space.
> - Compact smooth-basis trajectory parameterization can beat waypoint-wise optimization on both success and speed, since fewer coupled variables suppress high-frequency local changes that stall constraint satisfaction.
> - Staging optimization matters: restraining the global guide first while correcting the cheaper subsystem, then relaxing for quality, outperforms letting all variables move at once.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Dexterous manipulation in tight spaces can require changing hand shape while the arm is moving. An arm may have a collision-free route through a tight space, yet the hand may be unable to change shape continuously along that fixed route. Planning arm and hand separately can rule out feasible coordinated motions. Searching over every arm and finger joint at once makes global exploration difficult. The authors call this the “Arm–Hand Planning Dilemma.” Their planner, CAMP, lets arm pose and finger shape adjust together.

Paper Fig. 1: the authors’ schematic of cooperative arm–hand planning in a constrained opening. In this example, decoupled planning leads to a thumb collision, while CAMP coordinates the arm and hand to avoid it.

### 2. A moving target for the hand

The key conceptual move is to treat the collision-free hand shapes as a set that changes with the arm’s position, rather than as a fixed choice made once before the motion. For one arm configuration, this set, which the authors call a *feasible hand fiber*, contains the hand configurations that avoid environmental and self-collision. As the arm moves, that set can change shape and connectivity. A fixed hand shape can therefore restrict which arm configurations are available. A fixed arm route can leave no continuous collision-free hand transition between the prescribed endpoints.

CAMP uses arm paths as broad guides and searches for hand motions along them. It can locally adjust the arm when a hand transition is blocked. It then smooths and refines both motions together while preserving the start and goal. A layered, bidirectional hand search samples collision-free hand configurations along each guide and tries to connect adjacent layers. If an interpolated midpoint is in collision, local arm–hand replanning adjusts the hand and bounded arm offsets in a small window. It then reconnects the local segment to the original path.

The candidates are then encoded as VMPs, which are smooth basis-function representations of a trajectory. A coarse stage prioritizes feasibility and favors hand-dominant adjustments. CAMP then refines the top three candidates in a fine stage that gives more weight to trajectory quality and allows more arm adjustment. Both arm and hand remain adjustable in both stages.

Paper Fig. 3: arm-guided layered hand search, local arm relaxation at a blocked transition, VMP encoding, and coarse-to-fine optimization of selected candidates.

### 3. Tested against a guided alternative

Success here means the percentage of trials yielding a complete 22-joint trajectory that reaches the prescribed goal, satisfies task constraints, and passes exact MuJoCo collision checks. It does not mean successful grasping or object manipulation. Across six constrained simulation tasks, CAMP’s task-specific success rates range from 84.2% to 98.5%. On each task, it has the highest reported success rate of the compared planners.

The relevant skeptical baseline is A*+CHOMP. It uses A* wrist waypoints with interpolated hand joints, then optimizes the full trajectory. Results average ten batches of 100 trials. On Ball-in-Box Pregrasp, CAMP reaches 92.00% success (±2.09), versus 50.40% (±0.50) for A*+CHOMP. This compares complete planners and does not isolate which CAMP component caused the difference.

The Ball-in-Box ablation uses one 100-trial setting per configuration. A fixed-arm variant reaches 74% success, while full CAMP reaches 92%. Replacing VMP with waypoint-wise optimization changes success from 92% to 79%. It also changes mean planning time from 32.45 to 84.97 seconds. Under this setup, the paper counts 4,356 waypoint-wise variables versus 500 VMP weights. This evidence is consistent with local arm relaxation helping initialization and VMP reducing the optimization dimension. Planning time is solver wall-clock time, with setup and several validation-related costs excluded.

### 4. Plan the changing hand

CAMP’s central idea is to let arm and hand adapt to each other as the collision-free options change along the path. Physical trials show feasibility on one UR7e–LinkerHand system, but not full task completion. The pregrasp trials do not require grasping or lifting, and the button task does not actuate a button. The evaluation uses static environments with known obstacle geometry. Real execution remains sensitive to localization, calibration, and robot errors.
