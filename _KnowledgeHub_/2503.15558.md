# Cosmos-Reason1: From Physical Common Sense To Embodied Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA's Cosmos-Reason1 project introduces multimodal large language models (Cosmos-Reason1-7B and -56B) designed to reason about physical common sense and embodied interactions. These models achieve substantial improvements, including an average accuracy of 81.5% on intuitive physics benchmarks for the 7B variant after reinforcement learning, by employing novel ontologies, specialized datasets, and a two-stage training approach.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision Language Models (VLMs) often lack grounding in the physical world, struggling with real-world physics, dynamics, and intuitive common sense.
- Training embodied AI systems purely in real-world environments is expensive, time-consuming, and carries safety risks.
- There was a lack of standardized ontologies and comprehensive benchmarks to define and measure physical common sense and embodied reasoning in AI.

## Method

- Developed **Cosmos-Reason1**, a family of multimodal LLMs (7B and 56B parameters) with a hybrid Mamba-MLP-Transformer architecture for the 56B variant, capable of processing video input.
- Introduced two new ontologies to define physical common sense and embodied reasoning, and curated specialized large-scale datasets (~4M annotations) for supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning covering common sense, embodied reasoning, and intuitive physics.
- Employed a two-stage training approach: initial supervised fine-tuning followed by reinforcement learning with rule-based, verifiable rewards using a novel asynchronous training framework.

## Results

- Cosmos-Reason1-56B achieved an average accuracy of **60.2%** on physical common sense and **63.7%** on embodied reasoning, outperforming its backbone and several commercial models.
- The 7B variant demonstrated substantial gains in intuitive physics, reaching **74.5%** average accuracy after supervised fine-tuning (**+32.4%** over its backbone) and further improving to **81.5%** after reinforcement learning.
- Reinforcement learning post-training consistently boosted performance across benchmarks, also leading to qualitative improvements in ambiguity handling and more concise physical reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multimodal models, including state-of-the-art VLMs, often exhibit limitations in fundamental intuitive physics reasoning, even when performing well on other benchmarks.
> - A two-stage training methodology combining supervised fine-tuning with reinforcement learning (RL) on specialized data effectively enhances complex physical common sense and embodied reasoning.
> - Rule-based, verifiable rewards in the RL stage are essential for post-training Physical AI models, leading to more robust decision-making and improved handling of ambiguous physical scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has witnessed remarkable progress, particularly with the advent of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision Language Models (VLMs). These models, trained on vast amounts of internet data, have demonstrated impressive capabilities in abstract reasoning tasks like coding, mathematics, and general knowledge. However, a significant limitation of these models lies in their ability to **ground their knowledge in the physical world**. They often struggle to connect abstract textual knowledge with real-world interactions, physics, and dynamics. This gap is precisely where Cosmos-Reason1 aims to make its primary contribution.

The broader research landscape for Physical AI systems is characterized by a need for agents (e.g., robots, autonomous vehicles) that can perceive, understand, and interact effectively with complex, dynamic physical environments. Historically, approaches to integrating large pre-trained models into embodied agents often treated LLMs or VLMs as static, high-level planners, extracting action plans without deep physical grounding. While this showed promise in generalizing across tasks, these models frequently lacked the nuanced physical common sense needed for robust real-world interaction, leading to suboptimal or unsafe behaviors.

More recent advancements have focused on end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which fine-tune pre-trained VLMs directly on robotics data to generate embodied actions. This has improved generalization and robustness. Furthermore, the concept of "embodied chain-of-thought" (CoT) has emerged, enabling robotic agents to reason through sequential decisions before execution, thereby improving interpretability and adaptability. This work builds on the success of CoT from abstract reasoning domains (e.g., math, coding, where LLMs benefit greatly from CoT prompting and reinforcement learning) and extends it to the physical domain.

Cosmos-Reason1 distinguishes itself by explicitly addressing two crucial, yet often undertrained, capabilities for Physical AI:
1. **Physical Common Sense Reasoning:** This involves a general, embodiment-agnostic understanding of the physical world, its laws, and plausible interactions. While infants acquire this intuitively, AI systems often lack it, hindering their ability to learn efficiently and avoid critical mistakes in uncertain scenarios.
2. **Embodied Reasoning:** This capability focuses on enabling AI systems to perceive, reason about, and plan future interactions with the physical environment, grounded in action. Unlike abstract reasoning, it must process complex, often incomplete sensory inputs, predict action effects, respect physical constraints (e.g., inertia, friction), and ideally learn from continuous interaction.

By proposing new ontologies to define these capabilities and building specialized datasets for training, Cosmos-Reason1 directly tackles the problem of physical grounding. It leverages recent successes in LLM post-training, particularly reinforcement learning (RL) techniques (inspired by models like OpenAI o1 and DeepSeek-R1 which excel in abstract reasoning), and adapts them for the unique challenges of physical world interaction. This marks a significant effort to bridge the cognitive abilities of large models with the pragmatic demands of embodied intelligence, moving beyond purely symbolic manipulation towards intelligent, physically-grounded decision-making. The paper also highlights that while VLMs perform well on standard benchmarks, they often struggle with fundamental intuitive physics, underscoring the need for specialized training like Cosmos-Reason1.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the Cosmos-Reason1 research is to develop **Physical AI systems capable of perceiving, understanding, and performing complex actions in the physical world by leveraging long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning processes.** This overarching goal is broken down into several specific objectives and is driven by critical motivations.

#### 2.1. Key Objectives

1. **Define Core Capabilities for Physical AI Reasoning:** The paper aims to establish a clear framework for what constitutes effective Physical AI reasoning. This involves defining "physical common sense" (general understanding of the environment) and "embodied reasoning" (action-grounded interaction and planning).
2. **Propose Ontologies:** To formalize the defined capabilities and enable measurable progress, the authors propose two structured ontologies:
 - A **hierarchical ontology for physical common sense**, categorizing knowledge into Space, Time, and Fundamental Physics (with 16 fine-grained subcategories).
 - A **two-dimensional ontology for embodied reasoning**, encompassing four key reasoning capabilities across five types of embodied agents (e.g., humans, robot arms, autonomous vehicles).
3. **Develop Multimodal Large Language Models (LLMs):** The core technical objective is to build sophisticated multimodal LLMs, specifically Cosmos-Reason1-7B and Cosmos-Reason1-56B, that can take video input, understand it, and reason about it to generate natural language responses, including explanatory insights and embodied decisions.
4. **Curate Specialized Datasets:** A major objective is to overcome the scarcity of suitable training data by curating high-quality datasets for Physical AI. This includes datasets for both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL), tailored to the defined ontologies and reasoning capabilities (e.g., common sense, embodied reasoning, intuitive physics).
5. **Innovate Reinforcement Learning for Physical AI:** The paper seeks to design and implement rule-based, verifiable rewards for training Physical AI reasoning models with RL, addressing the challenge of defining objective rewards in physical interaction scenarios where free-form responses are common. They also aim to develop a robust and efficient RL training framework.
6. **Build Comprehensive Benchmarks:** To rigorously evaluate the developed models and foster future research, a key objective is to create comprehensive benchmarks for physical common sense and embodied reasoning, aligned with their proposed ontologies.

#### 2.2. Motivation

The motivation behind Cosmos-Reason1 stems from fundamental limitations of current state-of-the-art AI systems:
- **Lack of Physical Grounding in LLMs:** While LLMs excel at symbolic and abstract reasoning, they often fail to connect their vast textual knowledge to the concrete, dynamic laws of the physical world. This makes them unsuitable for direct deployment in embodied systems that require robust interaction.
- **High Cost and Risk of Real-World Training:** Training AI systems purely in real-world environments is incredibly expensive, time-consuming, and can pose significant safety risks to both the AI system and its surroundings. Equipping AI with physical common sense allows for more efficient learning and error avoidance in uncertain scenarios.
- **Need for Intelligent Embodied Agents:** For robots and autonomous systems to truly operate intelligently and adaptively, they must move beyond rote task execution to genuinely understand their environment, predict consequences of actions, and make intelligent, physically-aware decisions. This requires capabilities beyond just perceiving or acting, extending to deeper reasoning about physical interactions.
- **Absence of Standardized Frameworks and Benchmarks:** Before this work, there was a relative lack of standardized ontologies to define and measure physical common sense and embodied reasoning specifically for AI, making it difficult to systematically track progress in this domain.

In essence, Cosmos-Reason1 is motivated by the ambition to make AI truly "physically intelligent," capable of navigating and manipulating the real world with human-like intuition and reasoning, thereby unlocking new possibilities for embodied AI applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Cosmos-Reason1 project employs a multi-faceted methodology, integrating advanced multimodal model architectures, a two-stage training pipeline (SFT followed by RL), sophisticated data curation strategies, and dedicated benchmarks for evaluation.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

Cosmos-Reason1 is a family of multimodal large language models, consisting of Cosmos-Reason1-7B and Cosmos-Reason1-56B.
- **Decoder-Only Multimodal LLM:** The architecture follows a common decoder-only design, similar to LLaVA. This involves a **vision encoder** (InternViT-300M-V2.5 for 56B, ViT-676M for 7B) to process input video (or images), followed by a **projector** (a two-layer MLP) to align visual tokens with the LLM's text token embedding space. Video inputs are processed by uniformly sampling frames (up to 32 frames at 2 fps), resizing them, and feeding them to the vision encoder.
- **LLM Backbones:**
 - **Cosmos-Reason1-7B:** Uses Qwen2.5-VL as its pre-trained model and backbone, which is a dense Transformer architecture.
 - **Cosmos-Reason1-56B:** Leverages Nemotron-H as its LLM backbone. Critically, this is a **hybrid Mamba-MLP-Transformer architecture**. This hybrid design combines the linear-time sequence modeling efficiency of Mamba (for long sequences) with a small portion of Transformer layers to capture finer details, addressing the quadratic complexity of standard Transformers.

#### 3.2. Training Stages

The models are trained in two sequential stages:

#### 3.3. Physical AI Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)

This stage specializes the pre-trained multimodal models on domain-specific data to enhance their vision-language capabilities for Physical AI.
- **Data Curation:** Approximately 4 million annotations of video and text pairs are curated. This includes:
 - **Physical Common Sense SFT:** Based on a hierarchical ontology (Space, Time, Fundamental Physics). Data involves VQA (free-form and multiple-choice) generated through a five-stage pipeline: human-in-the-loop video curation, detailed captioning (human/VLM), LLM-prompted QA pair construction (distinguishing "understanding" vs. "reasoning" questions), reasoning trace extraction (using DeepSeek-R1 with captions as context), and final cleaning/rewriting.
 - **Embodied Reasoning SFT:** Based on a two-dimensional ontology (capabilities x agents). Focuses on "task-completion verification," "action affordance," and "next plausible action prediction." Data is curated from public (BridgeData V2, RoboVQA, AgiBot, HoloAssist) and proprietary (Autonomous Vehicles) datasets. The pipeline involves extracting short-horizon segments, annotating state-action context (VLM), curating reasoning QA pairs, extracting reasoning traces (DeepSeek-R1), and cleaning.
 - **Intuitive Physics SFT:** Aims to develop fundamental reasoning in intuitive physics (e.g., spatial continuity, arrow-of-time, object permanence). These tasks are designed to be self-supervised. Data for spatial puzzles (shuffled video patches), arrow-of-time (forward/reversed videos), and object permanence (simulation-synthesized clips with vanishing objects) are generated. Reasoning traces are extracted using VLMs or an intermediate Cosmos-Reason1-7B.

#### 3.4. Physical AI Reinforcement Learning (RL)

Following SFT, models are further post-trained using RL to enhance their physical common sense and embodied reasoning abilities.
- **Algorithm:** GRPO (Generalized Policy Optimization) is adopted for its simplicity and efficiency, as it avoids training a separate critic model. Advantage functions are computed by normalizing rewards within cohorts of generated responses.
- **Training Framework:** A novel, fully asynchronous, and robust RL training framework is introduced. It employs a heterogeneous deployment strategy for policy training and actor rollout, enabling asynchronous parallelism and significantly improved efficiency (~160% gain). It also features fault tolerance and dynamic scaling.
- **Rewards:** Rule-based and verifiable rewards are crucial for RL. For Physical AI, responses are converted into Multiple Choice Questions (MCQs) for easier verification.
 - **Accuracy Reward:** Binary reward if the model's answer (within `<answer>` tags) matches the ground truth MCQ choice.
 - **Format Reward:** Encourages the model to output thinking processes within `<think>` tags and answers within `<answer>` tags using regular expression matching.
 - The RL dataset draws samples from all SFT sources, focusing on MCQs, particularly intuitive physics data (spatial puzzles, AoT, object permanence) which are naturally in binary/MCQ format.

#### 3.5. Evaluation

New comprehensive benchmarks are built according to the defined ontologies to evaluate model performance:
- **Physical Common Sense Benchmark:** 604 questions from 426 videos (binary/MCQ) covering Space, Time, and Fundamental Physics.
- **Embodied Reasoning Benchmark:** 610 questions from 600 videos (MCQ) across various embodiments (humans, robot arms, humanoid robots, autonomous vehicles), assessing task-completion verification, action affordance, and next plausible action prediction. Includes datasets like RoboVQA, RoboFail (a harder split), BridgeData V2, AgiBot, HoloAssist, and AV data.
- **Intuitive Physics Benchmark:** 100 videos/questions each for Arrow-of-Time, Spatial Puzzle, and Object Permanence tasks, specifically designed to test fundamental physics reasoning.
Models are evaluated by answer accuracy, often using 5 inferences with different random seeds for Cosmos-Reason1, and zero-shot chain-of-thought prompting for comparison models like GPT-4o, Gemini 2.0 Flash, OpenAI o1, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and Nemotron-H-56B.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on Cosmos-Reason1 demonstrate significant improvements in physical world understanding and reasoning capabilities compared to existing state-of-the-art models and their own backbone architectures.

#### 4.1. Physical AI Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Results

- **Physical Common Sense:** Both Cosmos-Reason1-7B and -56B show marked improvements. Cosmos-Reason1-56B achieved an average accuracy of **60.2%**, slightly outperforming OpenAI o1 (59.9%) and significantly surpassing its backbone Nemotron-H-56B (58.2%) and other commercial models like GPT-4o (55.6%) and Gemini 2.0 Flash (50.2%). The 7B variant also showed a notable **+6.9%** improvement over its Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone. This indicates the effectiveness of the curated common sense dataset.
- **Embodied Reasoning:** Cosmos-Reason1 models demonstrated substantially stronger performance on the embodied reasoning benchmark. Cosmos-Reason1-7B achieved **61.8%** average accuracy, an **+11.0%** improvement over its Qwen2.5-VL-7B backbone (50.8%). Similarly, Cosmos-Reason1-56B reached **63.7%**, a **+10.2%** gain over Nemotron-H-56B (53.5%). These results highlight the efficacy of Physical AI SFT in boosting models' embodied reasoning.
- **Intuitive Physics:** This benchmark proved particularly challenging for existing models, with many performing near or below chance level on tasks like Arrow of Time and Object Permanence. GPT-4o and OpenAI o1 fared better on spatial puzzles, suggesting some proficiency in spatial relationships. However, Cosmos-Reason1-7B, after targeted SFT, showed dramatic improvements, achieving an average of **74.5%**, an impressive **+32.4%** increase over its backbone (42.1%). This indicates a fundamental leap in intuitive physics reasoning.

#### 4.2. Physical AI Reinforcement Learning (RL) Results

RL post-training further enhanced the models' Physical AI reasoning abilities.

- **Physical Common Sense and Embodied Reasoning:** RL training led to overall performance boosts. Cosmos-Reason1-7B, for instance, saw its average accuracy across these benchmarks rise from 60.7% (after SFT) to **65.7%** (after RL), a **+5.0%** gain. Improvements were observed across most sub-benchmarks, with the notable exception of RoboFail, where performance remained challenging. This consistent difficulty on RoboFail is attributed to its design, which requires highly observant perception, comprehensive temporal context processing, and reasoning about complex physical constraints, suggesting insufficient representative training data for these extreme scenarios.
- **Intuitive Physics:** RL proved particularly effective for intuitive physics. Cosmos-Reason1-7B's performance increased from 74.5% (SFT) to **81.5%** (RL), a **+7.0%** boost. Significant gains were observed in spatial puzzle and object permanence tasks, though Arrow of Time reasoning remained a challenge for further improvement.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Observations from RL

- **Ambiguity Handling:** RL enabled the model to more carefully evaluate ambiguous questions and, in some cases, reject all provided options if none were physically plausible, demonstrating a more robust and conservative decision-making approach (e.g., in the AV benchmark example, Figure 9).
- **Improved Temporal and Spatial Reasoning:** The RL-trained models showed enhanced ability to identify "anti-physics" motions (e.g., powder defying gravity in Arrow of Time tasks, Figure 10) and reason about spatial relationships without conflating them with temporal order (Figure 11).
- **Concise Object Permanence Reasoning:** RL led to more direct and concise reasoning for object permanence tasks, with the model accurately inferring disappearances not attributable to camera movement (Figure 12), overcoming the verbose and sometimes confused reasoning observed before RL.

Overall, the results conclusively demonstrate that the two-stage training approach, combined with carefully curated data and verifiable rewards, significantly enhances a multimodal LLM's capacity for physical common sense and embodied reasoning, pushing the boundaries of Physical AI capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Cosmos-Reason1 project by NVIDIA holds substantial significance and promises far-reaching impacts across the field of Artificial Intelligence, particularly in the realm of embodied agents and real-world applications.

#### 5.1. Advancing Physical AI Capabilities

The most direct impact is the significant leap in enabling AI systems to understand and reason about the physical world. By explicitly defining and training for "physical common sense" and "embodied reasoning," Cosmos-Reason1 directly addresses the critical limitation of current LLMs and VLMs – their lack of grounding in real-world physics and interactions. This work contributes directly to building more intelligent, robust, and safer Physical AI systems, such as robots, autonomous vehicles, and industrial automation.

#### 5.2. Establishing a Foundational Framework and Benchmarks

The introduction of detailed and hierarchical ontologies for physical common sense and embodied reasoning is a crucial contribution. These ontologies provide a much-needed standardized framework for researchers to define, categorize, and measure progress in Physical AI. Coupled with the development of comprehensive new benchmarks, this work offers a clear path for systematic evaluation and comparison of future models, accelerating research and development in this domain. These benchmarks can become a de facto standard for assessing physical intelligence.

#### 5.3. Novel and Scalable Data Curation Strategies

The meticulous curation of a large, diverse dataset (~4M annotations) for both SFT and RL, especially the innovative methods for generating "intuitive physics" data (spatial puzzles, arrow-of-time, object permanence) in a self-supervised and scalable manner, is highly impactful. This addresses a major bottleneck in Physical AI research – the scarcity of high-quality, physically-grounded reasoning data. The detailed pipelines shared in the paper provide a valuable blueprint for other researchers to create similar domain-specific datasets.

#### 5.4. Effective Training Methodology for Complex Reasoning

The demonstration of a two-stage training approach (SFT followed by RL) using rule-based, verifiable rewards and a robust, asynchronous RL framework is a testament to effective model optimization. This methodology, adapted from successes in abstract reasoning domains, shows how to systematically instill complex physical reasoning abilities into large multimodal models. The qualitative results, where RL helps models make more grounded decisions and reject ambiguous options, highlights the power of this fine-tuning.

#### 5.5. Unveiling and Addressing Model Limitations

The finding that even powerful state-of-the-art VLMs struggle with fundamental intuitive physics (e.g., arrow-of-time, object permanence) despite their performance on other benchmarks is a significant insight. This highlights that existing evaluations often fail to truly probe models' understanding of the physical world. Cosmos-Reason1 not only reveals these limitations but also provides a concrete method for overcoming them through targeted SFT and RL, pushing the frontier of what multimodal models can achieve.

#### 5.6. Fostering Open Science and Collaboration

NVIDIA's commitment to open-sourcing the code and pre-trained models under the NVIDIA Open Model License is a commendable step. This open approach will undoubtedly democratize access to advanced Physical AI research, enable broader community engagement, facilitate replication and extension of the work, and accelerate the overall progress of building highly capable Physical AI systems.

**In summary**, Cosmos-Reason1 represents a pivotal step towards developing truly intelligent embodied AI. By enabling multimodal LLMs to effectively reason about physical common sense and embodied interactions, this work paves the way for a new generation of robots, autonomous vehicles, and other physical AI applications that can operate more safely, efficiently, and adaptively in the complex and uncertain real world. It lays foundational groundwork that will likely influence future research in AI for physical interaction and understanding.
