# Temporal Logic-Based Multi-Vehicle Backdoor Attacks against Offline RL Agents in End-to-end Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel backdoor attack targets end-to-end autonomous driving systems by exploiting coordinated trajectories of multiple surrounding vehicles as triggers, moving beyond pixel-level manipulations. Researchers from Purdue University utilized temporal logic to automate the generation of these sophisticated multi-vehicle triggers and incorporated a negative training strategy to achieve precise and stealthy activation of unsafe target actions in poisoned offline reinforcement learning agents.

## Problem

- Existing backdoor attacks in autonomous driving primarily rely on pixel-level visual triggers, which face practical challenges in real-world deployment due to sensitivity to environmental factors and physical alteration requirements.
- Manually specifying and coordinating complex multi-vehicle spatiotemporal trajectories for attack triggers is impractical and time-consuming.
- Basic data poisoning methods for backdoor attacks often lead to false positives, activating the target action in non-triggering but similar scenarios, reducing stealthiness.

## Method

- Developed a framework to automatically generate realistic multi-vehicle ==trigger trajectories== using rule-based behavior models and temporal logic (TL) specifications to define and refine vehicle coordination.
- Integrated a negative training strategy during data poisoning, incorporating "patch trajectories" (similar non-triggering scenarios) to enhance attack precision and stealthiness by preventing false positives.
- Targeted end-to-end autonomous driving systems trained with offline reinforcement learning by injecting generated trigger trajectories with manipulated rewards and target actions into the training dataset.

## Results

- The attack successfully induced dangerous target actions in poisoned agents, leading to significantly lower cumulative rewards (from >200 to <60), high average displacement error (from <1.0 to >50), and near **100%** mission violation rate across various offline RL algorithms.
- Multi-vehicle trigger patterns (e.g., sync-bypass, overtake, brake-overtake) were deployed, with the `sync-bypass` pattern demonstrating superior attack performance and all designed patterns occurring at very low frequencies in real-world data (e.g., **0.130%** for `sync-bypass`), validating their stealthy design.
- Existing defense mechanisms, including trajectory smoothing and Differentially Private Stochastic Gradient Descent (DP-SGD), proved largely ineffective against this type of trajectory-based backdoor attack.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal logic provides an effective formal method for automatically designing and evaluating complex, coordinated multi-vehicle behaviors, overcoming the limitations of manual trigger specification.
> - A negative training strategy is essential for increasing the precision and stealth of backdoor attacks, ensuring the target behavior is activated only under exact trigger conditions.
> - Multi-vehicle trajectory-based triggers offer a more practical and adaptable alternative to pixel-level triggers for backdoor attacks in real-world autonomous driving scenarios, as they exploit contextual awareness.
