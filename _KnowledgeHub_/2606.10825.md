# MODIP: Efficient Model-Based Optimization for Diffusion Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> MODIP (Model-Based Optimization for Diffusion Policies) is a framework that fine-tunes Diffusion Policies (DPs) by leveraging model-based planning and distilling improved trajectories back into the DP. This approach enhances DPs beyond imitation learning while addressing computational inefficiencies in online settings, achieving competitive or superior performance across D4RL MuJoCo, Kitchen, and RoboMimic tasks, and speeding up inference by 2.90x and training by 1.6x on HalfCheetah.

## Problem

- Diffusion Policies (DPs) excel in imitation learning but are limited to the performance of demonstration datasets and struggle to discover improved behaviors in online settings.
- Direct reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning of DPs is computationally intensive and prone to instability due to their multi-step denoising action generation process.
- Integrating DPs into model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) and model predictive control (MPC) frameworks incurs significant computational overheads, especially with policy-dependent Q-value estimates during planning and critic learning.

## Method

- **MODIP** employs ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) as an indirect policy improvement operator, generating higher-quality trajectories that are subsequently distilled into the Diffusion Policy (DP) via behavioral cloning.
- The framework integrates DPs as expressive action sequence priors within a sampling-based MPC planner, which guides action proposals in a learned latent space.
- It uses a terminal state-value function V(s) instead of a policy-dependent Q(s, pi(s)) for planning and decouples critic learning from the DP using policy-independent TD targets to enhance computational efficiency.

## Results

- MODIP achieved competitive or superior performance across D4RL MuJoCo, Kitchen, and RoboMimic tasks, outperforming TD-MPC2 on MuJoCo and obtaining the highest success rates on D4RL Kitchen "Complete" and "Partial" tasks.
- The use of a terminal state-value function V(z) instead of a policy-dependent Q(z, pi(z)) improved inference speed by approximately **2.90x** (e.g., from 85.75s to 29.54s per 10^3 steps on HalfCheetah) while also enhancing performance.
- Policy-independent critic learning reduced total training time by approximately **1.6x** (e.g., from 40 hours to 25 hours on HalfCheetah), demonstrating substantial computational efficiency gains without performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Indirect policy improvement through MPC-to-policy distillation and behavioral cloning offers a stable and effective mechanism for enhancing Diffusion Policies beyond their initial imitation learning capabilities.
> - Replacing policy-dependent Q-value estimates with a terminal state-value function V(s) in MPC significantly boosts planning efficiency and improves final task performance.
> - Decoupling critic learning from the Diffusion Policy, by forming TD targets using state-value estimates, dramatically reduces overall training time by avoiding expensive multi-step DP queries.
