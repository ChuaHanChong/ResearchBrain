# MODIP: Efficient Model-Based Optimization for Diffusion Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> MODIP (Model-Based Optimization for Diffusion Policies) is a framework that fine-tunes Diffusion Policies (DPs) by leveraging model-based planning and distilling improved trajectories back into the DP. This approach enhances DPs beyond imitation learning while addressing computational inefficiencies in online settings, achieving competitive or superior performance across D4RL MuJoCo, Kitchen, and RoboMimic tasks, and speeding up inference by 2.90x and training by 1.6x on HalfCheetah.

## Problem

- Diffusion Policies (DPs) excel in imitation learning but are limited to the performance of demonstration datasets and struggle to discover improved behaviors in online settings.
- Direct reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning of DPs is computationally intensive and prone to instability due to their multi-step denoising action generation process.
- Integrating DPs into model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) and model predictive control (MPC) frameworks incurs significant computational overheads, especially with policy-dependent Q-value estimates during planning and critic learning.

## Method

- **MODIP** employs ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) as an indirect policy improvement operator, generating higher-quality trajectories that are subsequently distilled into the Diffusion Policy (DP) via behavioral cloning.
- The framework integrates DPs as expressive action sequence priors within a sampling-based MPC planner, which guides action proposals in a learned latent space.
- It uses a terminal state-value function V(s) instead of a policy-dependent Q(s, pi(s)) for planning and decouples critic learning from the DP using policy-independent TD targets to enhance computational efficiency.

## Results

- MODIP achieved competitive or superior performance across D4RL MuJoCo, Kitchen, and RoboMimic tasks, outperforming TD-MPC2 on MuJoCo and obtaining the highest success rates on D4RL Kitchen "Complete" and "Partial" tasks.
- The use of a terminal state-value function V(z) instead of a policy-dependent Q(z, pi(z)) improved inference speed by approximately **2.90x** (e.g., from 85.75s to 29.54s per 10^3 steps on HalfCheetah) while also enhancing performance.
- Policy-independent critic learning reduced total training time by approximately **1.6x** (e.g., from 40 hours to 25 hours on HalfCheetah), demonstrating substantial computational efficiency gains without performance degradation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Indirect policy improvement through MPC-to-policy distillation and behavioral cloning offers a stable and effective mechanism for enhancing Diffusion Policies beyond their initial imitation learning capabilities.
> - Replacing policy-dependent Q-value estimates with a terminal state-value function V(s) in MPC significantly boosts planning efficiency and improves final task performance.
> - Decoupling critic learning from the Diffusion Policy, by forming TD targets using state-value estimates, dramatically reduces overall training time by avoiding expensive multi-step DP queries.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research operates at the intersection of several subfields within machine learning and robotics, primarily focusing on policy learning for continuous control tasks. It addresses challenges associated with integrating expressive policy representations, specifically diffusion policies (DPs), into reinforcement learning (RL) frameworks.

Traditional policy representations, such as deterministic or Gaussian Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) policies, often model unimodal action distributions. While effective in many tasks, this can be a limitation when demonstrations exhibit multiple valid behaviors, a common occurrence in robotic manipulation. Diffusion policies have emerged as an alternative, offering the ability to model complex and multimodal action distributions through an iterative denoising process. They have demonstrated effectiveness in imitation learning (IL), where they learn directly from demonstration datasets.

However, the transition from imitation learning to reinforcement learning for DPs presents challenges. While IL allows DPs to replicate observed behaviors, it does not inherently facilitate improvement beyond the provided demonstrations. Direct RL fine-tuning of DPs is complicated by their multi-step denoising action generation process, which can lead to instability and increased computational cost during policy optimization. Existing methods for RL fine-tuning of DPs, such as Diffusion-QL, EDP, DPPO, and DSRL, attempt to integrate RL objectives directly into the diffusion framework or adapt DPs through latent space RL.

Concurrently, model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) and model predictive control (MPC) aim to improve sample efficiency by learning a predictive model of the environment and using it for planning. Latent model-based planners, exemplified by TD-MPC and TD-MPC2, perform trajectory optimization in a learned latent space and utilize value functions to estimate long-horizon returns. Hybrid planners combine model-based trajectory optimization with prior policies to guide action sampling, biasing planning towards relevant action space regions. A common component in such planners is the terminal value estimate, often a policy-dependent Q-function, which can become computationally expensive when the policy is a multi-step generative model like a DP.

Furthermore, the work relates to offline-to-online RL, which leverages pre-existing datasets for initialization before online fine-tuning. This setting often involves considerations regarding the coupling between critic learning and the current policy. Some methods, like IQL, employ policy-independent critic learning, while others, such as Cal-QL, use policy-dependent targets. For DPs, policy-independent critic updates offer computational advantages due to the expense of querying the policy.

MODIP aims to bridge these areas by proposing an indirect policy improvement mechanism for DPs, leveraging model-based planning rather than direct RL optimization. It seeks to integrate DPs as expressive priors within an efficient MPC framework and distill the resulting improved trajectories back into the DP, while also addressing the computational inefficiencies associated with DPs in planning and critic learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop an efficient framework, MODIP (Model-Based Optimization for Diffusion Policies), for the offline-to-online fine-tuning of Diffusion Policies (DPs). This framework aims to improve DPs beyond their initial imitation learning performance by integrating them with model-based planning, while circumventing the computational and stability challenges associated with direct reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning of DPs.

The motivation for this approach stems from several observations:
- **Expressiveness of Diffusion Policies:** DPs are recognized for their ability to model complex and multimodal action distributions, which is beneficial for robot learning tasks that may have multiple valid solutions or require diverse behaviors. This expressiveness is particularly valuable when learning from multimodal demonstration datasets.
- **Limitations of Imitation Learning (IL):** While DPs excel in IL, learning solely from demonstrations inherently limits their performance to the behaviors present in the dataset. They cannot autonomously discover improved strategies or adapt to novel situations without further optimization.
- **Challenges of Direct RL Fine-Tuning for DPs:** Directly applying RL algorithms to fine-tune DPs is computationally intensive and can be unstable. The multi-step denoising process required to generate actions from a DP translates to increased inference time for policy queries. When these queries are embedded within an RL optimization loop (e.g., for policy gradients or Q-value estimation), the overall training time increases substantially, and the complexity can introduce optimization instabilities.
- **Potential of Model Predictive Control (MPC):** MPC, particularly when guided by a learned world model, offers a mechanism for generating high-quality action sequences through planning. Combining this with a policy that provides strong initial proposals can lead to robust control.
- **Efficiency Considerations in Hybrid Planning:** Standard hybrid MPC planners often use policy-dependent terminal Q-value estimates. For DPs, computing such estimates would require executing the multi-step denoising process at each terminal state for numerous candidate trajectories during planning, leading to significant computational overhead. Similarly, policy-dependent temporal difference (TD) targets in critic learning require repeated DP queries, increasing training time.

To address these motivations, MODIP sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- **Develop an Indirect Policy Improvement Mechanism:** Instead of directly applying RL to the DP, MODIP seeks to use Model Predictive Control (MPC) and behavioral cloning (BC) as an indirect policy improvement operator. This involves leveraging a world model to guide policy adaptation and then distilling the MPC-generated trajectories back into the DP using a stable supervised learning objective.
- **Integrate DPs as Expressive Priors in MPC:** The framework aims to utilize the DP as an expressive prior for action sequence proposals within a sampling-based trajectory optimizer (e.g., MPPI). This guides the planner toward relevant and potentially multimodal action regions, which can enhance planning efficiency.
- **Enhance Computational Efficiency of Planning:** To mitigate the inference cost associated with DPs, MODIP aims to replace policy-dependent terminal Q-value estimates with a terminal state-value function V(s). This would allow for a single forward pass estimation of long-horizon returns at terminal states, reducing planning-time inference.
- **Improve Computational Efficiency of Critic Learning:** The framework seeks to decouple critic learning from the current policy by forming Temporal Difference (TD) targets using state-value estimates rather than actions sampled from the DP. This design choice is intended to avoid repeated, expensive DP queries during critic updates, thereby reducing overall training time.

By achieving these objectives, MODIP aims to provide a competitive and computationally efficient method for fine-tuning DPs that benefits from their expressive power while leveraging the stability and data efficiency of model-based planning and supervised learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MODIP is a framework for offline-to-online fine-tuning of Diffusion Policies (DPs) that employs Model Predictive Control (MPC) as an indirect policy improvement operator. The method avoids direct reinforcement learning (RL) optimization of the DP, instead leveraging a world model to generate improved trajectories which are then distilled into the DP through behavioral cloning (BC).

The MODIP framework consists of five learned components:
1. **Encoder ($h_\eta$):** Maps observations to latent states ($z_t = h_\eta(s_t)$).
2. **Latent Dynamics Model ($f_\theta$):** Predicts future latent states given current latent states and actions.
3. **Reward Model ($\hat{R}_\xi$):** Scores short-horizon imagined rollouts.
4. **Terminal State-Value Function ($V_\psi$):** Estimates long-horizon returns beyond the MPC horizon.
5. **Diffusion Policy ($\pi_\phi$):** Serves as an expressive action sequence prior for the planner.

The training process for MODIP proceeds in two stages:
1. **Offline Pretraining:** The DP and all MPC components (encoder, latent dynamics, reward model, terminal value function) are initially trained on an offline dataset.
2. **Online Fine-tuning:** Actions are selected by a DP-guided MPC planner, and the resulting trajectories are stored in an online replay buffer. Updates are performed on minibatches that combine both offline and online data. A time-varying mixing ratio $\beta(t)$ is employed, which gradually increases the fraction of online samples over a warm-up duration, thereby shifting training from offline demonstrations to planner-generated data.

Three specific design choices contribute to the efficiency of the MODIP loop for DPs:
- **MPC-to-policy Distillation:** The DP is improved by training it via BC on trajectories generated by a stronger MPC planner. This maintains the simplicity and stability of a supervised denoising objective (Equation 4). When a pretrained policy is available, its actions are used to regularize policy learning (Equation 5), stabilizing the transition from offline imitation to planner-guided improvement.
- **Inference-Efficient Hybrid Planning with Terminal State Value:** The DP-guided MPC planner optimizes action sequences in the learned latent space (Equation 6). A key aspect is the use of a terminal state-value function $V_\psi(z)$ instead of the common policy-dependent $Q(z, \pi(z))$ estimate. This allows the planner to score the final latent state with a single forward pass, avoiding multiple expensive multi-step denoising processes required for DP queries at each terminal state of candidate trajectories, thus reducing inference cost during planning. The planner samples candidate action sequences from a hybrid proposal, combining high-likelihood sequences generated by the DP with additional samples from a Gaussian distribution for exploration. These sequences are rolled out in the latent model and scored using predicted rewards from the reward model and the terminal state-value.
- **Policy-Independent Critic Learning:** To reduce training time, the critic learning mechanism is decoupled from the current DP. Temporal Difference (TD) targets are formed using target state-value estimates from slowly updated Exponential Moving Average (EMA) networks, rather than actions sampled from the DP (Equation 9). This eliminates the need for repeated, expensive DP denoising iterations during critic updates. The action-value function $Q_\omega$ is trained by regression (Equation 10). Additionally, two value heads $V_{\psi_i}$ and two advantage heads $A_{\zeta_i}$ are trained using a value-advantage decomposition objective (Equation 11 in Appendix B) to recover policy-independent state-value estimates, with the value heads providing the terminal value for the planner.

The overall model components are trained with a joint objective (Equation 3), which combines:
- **Consistency Loss ($L_{cons}$):** Trains the latent dynamics model to predict future latent states by matching targets encoded by an EMA target encoder (Equation 7). This loss is critical for ensuring the model remains accurate over the planning horizon.
- **Reward Loss ($L_{rew}$):** Trains the reward model to predict immediate task rewards. For dense-reward tasks, it is a Mean Squared Error (MSE) regression. For sparse-reward tasks, it is a Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss on a success classifier (Equation 8).
- **Value Loss ($L_Q + L_{V,A}$):** Combines the action-value function regression loss and the value-advantage objective for the state-value and advantage heads.

The implementation details specify a DDPM denoising process for the DP, MLP-based architectures for the encoder, dynamics model, reward model, and critic networks, and the use of EMA target networks for stability. Hyper-parameters for these components, including learning rates, discount factors, and various coefficients, are detailed in Appendix D.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated MODIP against a suite of baselines across D4RL MuJoCo locomotion, D4RL Kitchen manipulation, and RoboMimic state-based manipulation tasks. The evaluation focused on whether MODIP could improve DPs beyond BC, its competitiveness against direct RL fine-tuning of DPs and model-based baselines, and the impact of its design choices on performance and efficiency.

#### 4.1. Main Results

- **Overall Performance:** MODIP achieved competitive or superior performance across all three benchmark families.
 - **D4RL MuJoCo (dense-reward locomotion):** MODIP consistently outperformed TD-MPC2 across HalfCheetah, Walker2d, and Hopper, and was competitive with PA-RL, a method that also focuses on reactive control. Notably, MODIP and PA-RL exhibited stronger performance on HalfCheetah compared to other DP-specific baselines (DQL, DSRL, DPPO), suggesting a benefit from frequent feedback and action correction in this task.
 - **D4RL Kitchen (long-horizon manipulation):** MODIP achieved the highest success rates on both "Complete" and "Partial" tasks. This performance indicates that DP-guided MPC can generate robust trajectories, and that distilling these trajectories effectively improves the DP for tasks requiring long-horizon coordination and sparse reward discovery.
 - **RoboMimic (state-based manipulation):** MODIP was competitive with the strongest DP baselines. It achieved near-saturated performance on Lift, strong performance on Can, and improved over DPPO and PA-RL on Square, although DSRL obtained the highest score on Square.

These results suggest that MODIP is effective in scenarios where model-based refinement and expressive DP priors are complementary.

#### 4.2. Analysis and Ablations

A series of ablation studies investigated the contribution of key MODIP components:

- **MPC-to-policy Distillation and Hybrid Planning:** A comparison against a "Policy-only" variant (executing the DP without MPC refinement) demonstrated that the full MODIP planner substantially improved performance on HalfCheetah and both Kitchen tasks. This indicates that model-based refinement is beneficial when the policy alone is insufficient, and that the online trajectories generated by the stronger planner effectively fine-tune the DP.
- **Choice of Terminal Value for Inference Efficiency:**
 - **Performance:** Replacing MODIP's terminal state-value $V(z)$ with a policy-dependent $Q(z, \pi(z))$ resulted in reduced performance across all evaluated tasks (e.g., HalfCheetah performance dropped from 13775 to 7113). This suggests $V(z)$ provides a more effective value signal for DP-guided planning.
 - **Inference Time:** Using $V(z)$ reduced inference time from 85.75 seconds to 29.54 seconds per 10^3 environment steps on HalfCheetah, representing approximately a 2.90x speedup. This confirms that the state-value formulation improves both planning efficiency and final performance.
- **Policy-Independent Critic Learning for Training Time:**
 - **Training Time:** The policy-independent TD targets, which avoid querying the DP during critic updates, reduced total training time on HalfCheetah from approximately 40 hours to 25 hours, a 1.6x speedup. This highlights the computational benefits of decoupling critic learning from the DP.
- **Offline Initialization:** Removing offline pretraining for the latent dynamics, reward model, and critic components resulted in lower performance on MuJoCo and Kitchen Complete tasks, particularly on Hopper. This indicates that a well-initialized planner from offline data is beneficial for robust online data collection and subsequent policy distillation.
- **Policy Prior Parameterization:** Replacing the DP prior with a Gaussian MLP policy within the MPC framework (labeled "MLP policy") led to reduced performance across all tasks, with significant drops observed on Hopper and Kitchen tasks. This finding supports the hypothesis that the expressiveness of the DP prior, capable of capturing richer action sequence distributions, contributes to the overall effectiveness of MODIP.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of MODIP presents a framework for the fine-tuning of Diffusion Policies (DPs) that addresses several challenges in contemporary robot learning. Its significance lies in demonstrating an effective and efficient approach to improving DPs beyond their initial imitation learning capabilities, without resorting to direct and potentially unstable reinforcement learning (RL) optimization.

The primary contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Robust DP Improvement Beyond Imitation Learning:** MODIP provides a method for DPs to improve upon offline demonstration data, which is a fundamental limitation of pure behavioral cloning. By leveraging model-based planning to generate high-quality, improved trajectories, and then distilling these back into the DP, the approach allows DPs to learn superior behaviors. This could lead to more capable and adaptable robotic agents trained initially from diverse, potentially suboptimal, datasets.
- **Addressing Computational Inefficiencies of DPs in RL:** The paper identifies and mitigates key computational bottlenecks associated with DPs in an RL context. The use of a terminal state-value function ($V(s)$) instead of a policy-dependent Q-function significantly reduces inference time during MPC planning, making the planner more reactive and efficient. Furthermore, policy-independent critic learning substantially decreases overall training time by avoiding expensive multi-step DP queries during critic updates. These efficiency gains are crucial for scaling DP-based methods to more complex tasks and larger models.
- **Synergy of Expressive Priors and Model-Based Planning:** MODIP demonstrates that combining the multimodal action distribution modeling capabilities of DPs with the predictive power of a learned world model through hybrid MPC yields strong performance. The DP acts as an expressive prior, guiding the planner towards relevant and potentially diverse action sequences, while the model-based refinement allows for robust trajectory optimization. This hybrid approach appears to be particularly beneficial in tasks requiring long-horizon reasoning and frequent action correction.
- **Competitive Performance Across Diverse Tasks:** The experimental results show that MODIP achieves competitive or superior performance compared to existing DP fine-tuning methods and strong model-based baselines across a range of continuous control and robotic manipulation tasks (D4RL MuJoCo, Kitchen, and RoboMimic). This broad applicability suggests the framework's robustness and generalizability.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a robust baseline and methodology for integrating DPs into model-based frameworks. The identified limitation regarding the use of real-valued vectors as state information, rather than visual observations, suggests a clear avenue for future work. Extending MODIP to handle pixel-based inputs would be a logical next step, potentially unlocking the full potential of DPs in visuomotor control tasks where their expressive power is particularly valuable for handling perception uncertainties and complex visual states.

In summary, MODIP offers an efficient and effective paradigm for harnessing the expressiveness of Diffusion Policies within a model-based optimization framework, addressing both performance and computational challenges, and thereby contributing to the advancement of policy learning in robotics.
