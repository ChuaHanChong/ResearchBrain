# HECTOR: Human-centric Hierarchical Coordination and Supervision of Robotic Fleets under Continual Temporal Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers developed HECTOR, a human-centric hierarchical framework enabling online coordination and supervision of large-scale heterogeneous robotic fleets performing continual, uncertain temporal tasks. The system reduced average mission response time to 34.6s and maintained planning times under 2.0s for fleets up to 170 robots, demonstrating efficient human-in-the-loop adaptability.

## Problem

- Human operators face significant challenges in coordinating and supervising large, heterogeneous robotic fleets for complex, dynamic missions in uncertain environments.
- Existing multi-robot task allocation (MRTA) and temporal logic planning methods struggle with scalability, dynamic task changes, and real-time environmental uncertainties, often relying on static assumptions.
- Prior approaches often lack robust mechanisms for online human interaction and real-time adaptation, leading to computational intractability or unstable plans for large fleets.

## Method

- A human-centric hierarchical framework named **HECTOR** is proposed, structured into three layers: human-fleet interaction, task assignment/team formation, and local team coordination.
- Implements a ==bidirectional, multimodal online interaction protocol== with a GUI, enabling operators to supervise and intervene at various granularities with lean communication.
- Utilizes a ==receding-horizon, automaton-guided search tree== for simultaneous task decomposition and team assignment, followed by a ==Mixed-Integer Linear Program (MILP)== for capacity-based robot instantiation within teams.

## Results

- HECTOR successfully managed **32** tasks and **482** subtasks over **12** replanning events in **180.4s**, with average replanning times of **0.36s** and a maximum of **0.87s** in human-in-the-loop simulations.
- The framework achieved an average mission response time of **34.6s** and a **100%** success rate, outperforming **eight** baseline methods in response time and planning efficiency metrics.
- Planning times remained below **2.0s** even when scaled to **170** robots, **100** missions, and **1509** subtasks, demonstrating strong scalability and robustness to robot failures and communication delays.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hierarchical decomposition of planning, from fleet-level assignment to local team coordination, combined with a receding-horizon strategy, enables scalable management of large robotic fleets.
> - Seamless, bidirectional human-fleet interaction with a multimodal interface is critical for effective human oversight and dynamic mission adaptation in complex, evolving environments.
> - Automaton-guided search, in conjunction with MILP for robot instantiation, allows for efficient and optimal task decomposition and team formation under temporal logic constraints.
> - Task-specific local coordination strategies, such as distributed dynamic coalition formation, are vital for efficient execution and real-time adaptation to diverse and uncertain subtasks.
