# GeoGS-SLAM: Geometry-Only Gaussian Splatting for Dense Monocular SLAM

> [!summary] Summary
> A new dense monocular SLAM system, GeoGS-SLAM, leverages a geometry-only Gaussian Splatting (GeoGS) representation to enhance reconstruction accuracy and efficiency by reducing primitive parameters over 80%. This approach yields improved geometric fidelity (2.09 cm accuracy on Replica) and robust camera tracking (0.28 cm ATE on Replica), along with a coherent map update strategy for global corrections.

## Problem

- Existing 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) SLAM methods couple appearance and geometry, leading to inefficient optimization and geometrically imprecise "floating artifacts" that are preserved due to photometric error reduction.
- The large number of appearance parameters in 3DGS hinders geometry convergence and increases the optimization burden, particularly under the limited computational budgets of online dense mapping.
- Current 3DGS-based SLAM systems often suffer from "map tearing" and inconsistencies when applying global pose corrections (e.g., loop closure), as per-viewpoint updates lack spatial coherence.

## Method

- Introduces **GeoGS** primitives, reducing per-primitive parameters by over 80% by removing appearance modeling to focus solely on geometry, simplifying the optimization problem.
- Employs a comprehensive ==geometry-oriented loss function==, combining single-view geometric supervision (normal consistency, depth smoothness, distortion) and multi-view photometric consistency.
- Implements a ==coherent map update strategy== that applies a unified global ==Sim(3) transformation== to all affected Gaussians after global pose corrections, maintaining structural consistency and preventing map tearing.

## Results

- Demonstrated improved geometric reconstruction quality, achieving **2.09 cm** accuracy on Replica and a mean Chamfer Distance of **8.67 cm** on ScanNet++, outperforming several existing NeRF and 3DGS-based methods.
- Achieved highly competitive camera tracking accuracy, recording the lowest average Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) of **0.28 cm** on Replica and **7.00 cm** on ScanNet datasets.
- The GeoGS representation required significantly fewer Gaussians (**23k** vs. **161k** for 2DGS) and reduced model storage (**1.2 MB** vs. **37.5 MB** for 2DGS) for comparable or better geometric quality under time-constrained optimization on the DTU dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling appearance from 3D Gaussian Splatting primitives drastically reduces parameter count (over 80%) and accelerates geometric convergence, proving effective for SLAM tasks where geometry is primary.
> - High-fidelity geometric reconstruction can be achieved using a combination of geometry-oriented loss functions and multi-view photometric consistency, even without explicit appearance modeling.
> - A local-plane driven initialization strategy and a coherent global map update mechanism are crucial for improving optimization efficiency and preserving structural integrity during long-term SLAM operation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Dense visual Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) is a foundational challenge in robotics, focused on concurrently estimating camera poses and reconstructing a comprehensive 3D map of an environment. Traditionally, dense SLAM systems have employed various representations, including depth maps (DTAM), Truncated Signed Distance Fields (TSDF) (KinectFusion, BundleFusion), surfel-based maps (ElasticFusion), or semi-dense approaches (LSD-SLAM). More recently, deep learning-based methods have emerged, utilizing convolutional neural networks for depth prediction (CNN-SLAM, CodeSLAM) or robust dense visual SLAM (DROID-SLAM).

The advent of Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) introduced a new paradigm, enabling photorealistic scene rendering within SLAM systems (iMAP, NICE-SLAM, HI-SLAM). While these methods achieve high rendering quality, they often entail computationally intensive neural optimization and rely on substantial memory for scene representation, limiting their efficiency and scalability for real-time dense SLAM.

The introduction of 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) marked a significant development, offering an explicit, differentiable, and efficiently optimizable scene representation for novel view synthesis (NVS). Following its success in NVS, 3DGS has gained attention for 3D reconstruction and is increasingly being integrated into dense visual SLAM systems (GS-SLAM, SplaTAM, MonoGS, Splat-SLAM, HI-SLAM2).

Existing 3DGS-based reconstruction and SLAM methods typically couple appearance and geometry modeling within their Gaussian primitives. These methods often introduce explicit geometric regularizations or adapt Gaussian parameterizations to improve geometry. However, this coupling can lead to geometrically imprecise "floating artifacts" that are preserved because they reduce photometric error, and the extensive parameters required for view-dependent appearance can hinder geometry optimization, especially under the constrained budgets of online mapping. A further challenge for 3DGS-based SLAM systems is the efficient and spatially coherent updating of the Gaussian map after global pose corrections, such as loop closure or bundle adjustment. Previous approaches, which propagate per-viewpoint corrections to associated Gaussians, can introduce inconsistencies and "tearing" artifacts in the reconstructed map.

GeoGS-SLAM positions itself within this landscape by addressing the limitations of appearance-geometry coupling and map update mechanisms in 3DGS-based SLAM. It proposes a geometry-only representation to prioritize accurate geometric reconstruction and introduces a coherent map update strategy to maintain structural consistency during global corrections, thereby aiming to enhance the efficiency and quality of dense monocular SLAM.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The core motivation behind this work stems from an observation regarding the priorities of robotic tasks that utilize SLAM output. Downstream applications in robotics, such as autonomous navigation and obstacle avoidance, primarily depend on accurate spatial geometry rather than photorealistic rendering of novel views. Existing 3DGS frameworks, which model both appearance and geometry, allocate a significant portion of their primitive parameters to appearance (e.g., Spherical Harmonics coefficients). This design choice leads to several challenges in the context of dense SLAM:
1. **Inefficient Geometry Optimization:** The coupled appearance and geometry parameters result in a larger number of parameters per Gaussian primitive. This increases the optimization burden, making geometry convergence slower, particularly under the limited computational budget available for online dense mapping.
2. **Compromised Geometric Accuracy:** The photometric objective, aiming for photorealistic rendering, can sometimes tolerate or even preserve geometrically imprecise "floating artifacts" if they contribute to reducing overall photometric error. This compromises the fidelity of the reconstructed geometry.
3. **Sensitivity to Illumination Variations:** Appearance modeling inherently ties the representation to specific lighting conditions, which can reduce robustness in environments with changing illumination.
4. **Map Tearing from Global Corrections:** When accumulated pose drift is corrected by mechanisms like loop closure or global Bundle Adjustment (BA), the updated camera trajectory becomes inconsistent with the existing Gaussian map. Existing methods that propagate per-viewpoint pose corrections to individual Gaussians often neglect spatial coherence, causing neighboring Gaussians on the same surface to receive conflicting transformations, leading to "tearing" and other structural artifacts in the map.

Motivated by these issues, the key objectives of this research are:
1. **Develop a Geometry-Only Gaussian Splatting (GeoGS) representation:** The primary objective is to investigate the feasibility of 3DGS-based reconstruction without modeling scene appearance. GeoGS aims to directly represent scene geometry by retaining only spatial parameters (position, rotation, scale, opacity), significantly reducing the number of parameters per primitive. This is hypothesized to enable faster geometric convergence and improve robustness to illumination changes.
2. **Construct a Dense Monocular SLAM System (GeoGS-SLAM) based on GeoGS:** Integrate the GeoGS representation into a complete SLAM pipeline, leveraging its geometric efficiency for real-time performance.
3. **Design an effective training framework for GeoGS optimization:** Since conventional color rendering supervision is unavailable without appearance parameters, the objective is to formulate a new set of geometry-oriented loss functions. This framework incorporates single-view and multi-view geometric and photometric supervision.
4. **Accelerate geometric convergence with a specialized initialization strategy:** Introduce a local-plane driven initialization method for Gaussian primitives that aligns them with local structural information (e.g., planar surfaces) to improve optimization efficiency under online constraints.
5. **Propose a coherent map update strategy for global corrections:** Address the map tearing problem by developing a mechanism for loop closure or global BA that transforms the Gaussian map globally and consistently, rather than through inconsistent per-viewpoint corrections. This strategy aims to preserve local structural coherence while aligning the map with corrected pose estimates.

### 3. Methodology and approach

GeoGS-SLAM is a dense monocular SLAM system that leverages a novel geometry-only Gaussian Splatting (GeoGS) representation for efficient and accurate 3D scene reconstruction. The system comprises a frontend for pose and depth estimation, a Gaussian backend for map optimization, and a global pose correction module with a coherent map update strategy.

**A. GeoGS Primitive**
The core of the methodology is GeoGS, a specialized Gaussian primitive. Unlike standard 3DGS primitives that use Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients for appearance, GeoGS primitives retain only geometry-related parameters: position (μ ∈ R³), rotation (q ∈ R⁴), scale (s ∈ R²), and opacity (α ∈ R). By eliminating appearance parameters, GeoGS reduces the number of per-primitive parameters by over 80%. This design aims to simplify the optimization problem, require fewer Gaussians to represent geometry, and enhance robustness to illumination variations.

**B. Depth and Normal Rendering**
Given a set of GeoGS primitives and camera poses, depth and normal maps are rendered. For each Gaussian, its tangent directions (tx, ty) and normal (n) are derived from its rotation matrix. The Gaussian is treated as a 2D disk projected onto the image plane. Ray-splat intersections are calculated to determine the depth (zi) and normal (ni) for each intersecting Gaussian along a ray. The final rendered depth `D(u)` and normal `N(u)` at a pixel `u` are computed as a weighted average of the depths and normals of individual Gaussians, using alpha blending weights `ωi(u)`.

**C. Loss Function**
Without color rendering supervision, GeoGS employs a geometry-oriented loss function combining single-view and multi-view objectives:
- **Single-view losses:**
 - **Normal consistency (L_nc):** Encourages Gaussian normals to align with surface normals derived from the rendered depth map, weighted by image gradients to preserve edges.
 - **Depth smoothness (L_ds):** Promotes smooth surfaces while preserving sharp boundaries by penalizing depth gradients in areas with low image gradients and vice versa.
 - **Distortion loss (L_dist):** Minimizes depth discrepancies among overlapping Gaussians along the same ray to prevent floating artifacts.
 - **Depth and Normal L1 losses (L_d, L_n):** Directly supervises rendered depth and normal maps against dense depth and monocular normal estimates provided by the SLAM frontend.
- **Multi-view losses (L_mv):** Utilizes photometric consistency between neighboring RGB images. The rendered depth map of a reference view (`D_r`) is used to back-project pixels into 3D and reproject them onto a target view. A validation mask (`M`) filters out occluded or out-of-bounds projections. L1, SSIM (Structural Similarity), and NCC (Normalized Cross-Correlation) losses are then applied between the reference image and the reprojected image (`I_r→t`) over valid correspondences, providing cross-view photometric supervision for geometry.

**D. Local-Plane Driven Initialization**
To accelerate geometric convergence, GeoGS-SLAM incorporates a local-plane driven initialization strategy for new Gaussian primitives. For each candidate point (sampled from the current frame's depth map), a local neighborhood is constructed from nearby sampled points and existing Gaussian centers. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is performed on this neighborhood to estimate the local surface normal (eigenvector corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue, `v0`) and planarity (`P`). The Gaussian's rotation is initialized by aligning its normal with `v0`. Its scale is adjusted based on planarity: in regions of low planarity, the scale is reduced to limit spatial support where geometry is uncertain.

**E. GeoGS-SLAM System Architecture**
- **Frontend:** Built upon DROID-SLAM, it handles keyframe selection, camera tracking, and dense depth estimation. It also extracts monocular depth and normal priors using a pretrained network (Omnidata). Local Bundle Adjustment (BA) is performed with a JDSA module to refine poses and depths.
- **Gaussian Backend:**
 1. **Initialization:** New GeoGS primitives are initialized from the frontend's dense depth estimates using the local-plane driven strategy.
 2. **Geometry Optimization:** GeoGS primitives are optimized within a sliding window of recent and historical keyframes using the geometry-oriented loss function `L_geo`.
 3. **Pose-Only Correction:** After geometry optimization, camera poses within the active frame graph are refined while keeping Gaussians fixed, using rendered depth, normals, and multi-view reprojection as supervision.
- **Global Pose Correction:**
 1. **Loop Closure:** Detected in parallel via optical-flow distance, orientation, and temporal separation. A Sim(3)-based pose-graph bundle adjustment (PGBA) corrects accumulated pose and scale drift.
 2. **Global Bundle Adjustment (BA):** A final dense BA is performed on a global factor graph constructed from all keyframes after the online stage concludes, further refining the trajectory.
- **Coherent Map Update:** This strategy addresses map inconsistencies after global pose correction. Instead of propagating per-frame pose corrections to individual Gaussians, it first calculates the target positions (`μ'j`) of all revisited Gaussians based on their associated keyframe's corrected pose. Then, a single global Sim(3) transformation (`sg, Rg, tg`) is estimated by fitting it to the original and target positions of these revisited Gaussians. This unified transformation is then applied to update the position, rotation, and scale of all affected Gaussians, ensuring local structural coherence and preventing map tearing.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments conducted on synthetic and real-world datasets evaluate GeoGS-SLAM's performance in terms of geometry reconstruction quality, camera tracking accuracy, and the effectiveness of its core components.

**A. Geometry Reconstruction Performance**
- **Replica Dataset (Synthetic Indoor):** GeoGS-SLAM exhibits improved reconstruction quality compared to DROID-SLAM, NeRF-based methods (NICER-SLAM, GO-SLAM, HI-SLAM), and GS-based methods (Splat-SLAM, HI-SLAM2) in online mode. It achieved an average accuracy of 2.09 cm, completeness of 3.95 cm, and a completeness ratio of 83.67%, outperforming the baselines across these metrics (Table I). Qualitative comparisons show cleaner scene geometry and fewer artifacts (Fig. 7).
- **ScanNet++ Dataset (Real-world Indoor):** GeoGS-SLAM demonstrated superior performance in geometric reconstruction, achieving a mean Chamfer Distance (CD) of 8.67 cm, which is an improvement over HI-SLAM2 (12.43 cm) (Table II). Qualitative results further support the reconstruction of cleaner surfaces (Fig. 8, Fig. 9).
- **Robustness to Illumination:** Qualitative evaluations illustrate that GeoGS-SLAM maintains consistent scene geometry despite significant appearance variations caused by different lighting conditions (Fig. 10), attributing this robustness to its geometry-only focus.

**B. Tracking Performance**
- **ScanNet++, ScanNet, and Replica Datasets:** GeoGS-SLAM consistently achieved competitive or superior camera tracking accuracy (measured by Absolute Trajectory Error, ATE).
 - On ScanNet++, it achieved an ATE of 4.41 cm, outperforming HI-SLAM2 (4.81 cm) (Table II).
 - On ScanNet, GeoGS-SLAM recorded the lowest average ATE of 7.00 cm among all compared methods (Table III).
 - On Replica, it also achieved the lowest average ATE of 0.28 cm (Table V). These results indicate accurate camera pose estimation across various environments.

**C. GeoGS Reconstruction Evaluation (DTU Dataset)**
To validate the GeoGS representation itself, an evaluation was performed on 15 DTU sequences under a time-constrained optimization budget of 2 minutes:
- **Chamfer Distance:** GeoGS significantly outperformed 2DGS, PGSR, and QGS, achieving a mean CD of 0.62 cm compared to 1.38 cm, 2.07 cm, and 1.73 cm, respectively (Table IV). This demonstrates the effectiveness of GeoGS for high-quality geometry reconstruction under limited time.
- **Geometric Convergence:** GeoGS exhibited faster geometric convergence, with qualitative results showing cleaner and more complete surfaces rapidly (Fig. 11, Fig. 13). Quantitative analysis of convergence curves confirmed a steeper decrease in Chamfer Distance during early optimization (Fig. 12).
- **Model Efficiency:** GeoGS required substantially fewer Gaussian primitives (23k vs. 161k for 2DGS, 231k for PGSR, 200k for QGS) and significantly reduced model storage (1.2 MB vs. 37.5 MB for 2DGS, 54.6 MB for PGSR, 8.1 MB for QGS) to represent the scene geometry (Table VI).

**D. Ablation Studies**
- **Local-plane Driven Initialization:** Removing this strategy resulted in a degradation of geometric reconstruction quality (e.g., average accuracy of 2.77 cm vs. 2.09 cm with initialization) (Table VII). The initialization facilitated faster convergence to more accurate surfaces and fewer artifacts (Fig. 14, Fig. 15).
- **Coherent Map Update:** The proposed coherent update strategy improved reconstruction metrics (e.g., average accuracy of 2.09 cm vs. 2.16 cm for per-frame propagation) (Table VIII). Qualitatively, it effectively alleviated local structural tearing and reduced artifacts compared to per-frame propagation after global BA (Fig. 16).
- **Loss-term Contribution:** An ablation study on the DTU Scan24 sequence showed that removing any individual loss term (L_mv, L_nc, L_ds) led to performance degradation. L_mv (multi-view loss) had the most significant impact, indicating its critical role in maintaining multi-view geometric consistency (Table X).
- **Pose-step Learning Rate:** The system demonstrated stable tracking accuracy across a range of `pose_lr` values (Table IX).

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The work presented in GeoGS-SLAM offers several advancements and holds potential for significant impact within the fields of robotics, computer vision, and augmented reality.

**A. Technical Contributions and Efficiency:**
The primary technical contribution is the introduction of Geometry-only Gaussian Splatting (GeoGS). By systematically removing appearance-related parameters from 3D Gaussian primitives, the method demonstrates that high-fidelity geometric reconstruction, critical for SLAM, can be achieved without the computational overhead of photorealistic rendering. This design choice leads to:
- **Parameter Efficiency:** An over 80% reduction in per-primitive parameters directly translates to a more compact representation and reduced memory footprint.
- **Faster Geometric Convergence:** The simplified optimization problem, with fewer parameters to manage, allows GeoGS to converge to accurate geometry significantly faster than appearance-coupled 3DGS variants, which is highly beneficial for online, real-time SLAM systems with limited per-keyframe update budgets.
- **Model Compactness:** GeoGS requires fewer Gaussian primitives to represent scene geometry and has a substantially smaller total model storage, making it suitable for deployment on resource-constrained platforms.

**B. Robustness and Geometric Quality:**
- **Illumination Invariance:** By focusing solely on geometry, GeoGS inherently reduces sensitivity to illumination changes. This improves the robustness of SLAM systems in environments with varying lighting conditions, a common challenge for appearance-based methods.
- **Enhanced Geometric Accuracy:** The dedicated geometry-focused loss functions and the local-plane driven initialization strategy promote the reconstruction of cleaner, more consistent surfaces with fewer floating or tearing artifacts. This higher geometric fidelity is directly relevant to real-world robotic applications.

**C. Improved Map Consistency for Long-Term Operation:**
The proposed coherent map update strategy addresses a critical limitation in existing 3DGS-based SLAM systems regarding map corrections after loop closure or global Bundle Adjustment. By estimating and applying a unified Sim(3) transformation to revisited Gaussians, the method ensures that the dense map remains structurally consistent with the globally corrected poses. This prevents local tearing and preserves the overall geometric integrity of the map, which is crucial for long-term SLAM operation in large environments.

**D. Potential Impact:**
- **Robotics:** The enhanced efficiency, robustness, and geometric accuracy of GeoGS-SLAM have direct implications for robotic applications. Accurate and consistent dense maps are vital for navigation, path planning, obstacle avoidance, manipulation, and interaction with the environment. Robots equipped with GeoGS-SLAM could operate more reliably and effectively in complex, dynamic, and unconstrained settings.
- **Augmented Reality (AR) and Virtual Reality (VR):** While GeoGS prioritizes geometry over photorealistic rendering, accurate 3D geometry is fundamental for AR/VR applications that require precise environmental understanding for content placement, occlusion handling, and spatial interaction. The efficiency of GeoGS could enable more robust and responsive AR/VR experiences, especially on mobile devices.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work opens avenues for further research into specialized scene representations for SLAM. It challenges the assumption that NVS-optimized representations are always ideal for SLAM, suggesting that task-specific representations can yield significant performance benefits. Future work might explore hybrid approaches that incorporate minimal appearance modeling where necessary, or further refine geometry-only representations for specific geometric tasks (e.g., plane detection, object recognition from geometry).

In conclusion, GeoGS-SLAM advances the state-of-the-art in dense monocular SLAM by introducing an efficient, robust, and geometrically accurate 3DGS-based framework. Its focus on geometry, combined with intelligent initialization and map update strategies, addresses key challenges in the field, paving the way for more capable and reliable autonomous systems.
