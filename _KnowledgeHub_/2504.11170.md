# A real-time anomaly detection method for robots based on a flexible and sparse latent space

> [!summary] Summary
> An unsupervised deep learning model, Sparse MAF-AAE, has been developed for real-time multivariate time-series anomaly detection in robots, effectively addressing challenges with limited, noisy sensor data. It achieved an AUROC of 89.23% (±7.45) on torque signals for pick-and-place operations and demonstrated sub-millisecond inference speeds, making it suitable for resource-constrained robotic systems.

## Problem

- Existing deep learning anomaly detection models (e.g., VAEs, NFs, Transformers) face limitations in robotics due to their computational cost, high data requirements, and issues with accurately modeling complex, noisy sensor data from resource-constrained embedded systems.
- Variational AutoEncoders often struggle with rigid Gaussian assumptions and inefficient latent space construction when input features are limited, while Normalizing Flows can misassign high likelihoods to out-of-distribution anomalous data.
- Transformer-based models, despite their effectiveness, suffer from high computational complexity (O(L^2 N)) and memory demands, making them impractical for real-time deployment on commercial robot platforms with limited processing power.

## Method

- The proposed **Sparse MAF-AAE** model integrates ==Masked Autoregressive Flow== (MAF) layers for flexible latent space modeling, ==Adversarial AutoEncoders== (AAE) for robust distribution matching, and principles of ==sparse autoencoders== for efficient feature learning.
- It employs an ==LSTM encoder== to capture temporal dependencies, transforming a base Gaussian latent space into a more complex, expressive representation via MAF layers, and a decoder for accurate data reconstruction.
- ==Sparsity constraints==, implemented through ==L1 regularization== on the encoder, promote efficient feature selection and logical compression, enabling the model to perform robustly even with a limited number of input sensor signals.

## Results

- Sparse MAF-AAE achieved an AUROC of **89.23%** (±7.45) at 100 Hz using only torque signals, outperforming MVT-Flow (**84.27%** ± 11.55) by **4.96%** and LSTM-VAE (**73.66%** ± 12.05) by **15.57%** for overall anomaly detection.
- The model demonstrated real-time inference capabilities, completing signal reconstruction and anomaly scoring within **0.86 ms** on 50 Hz data, making it suitable for deployment on resource-constrained embedded robot systems.
- It showed superior performance in detecting subtle anomalies, such as collisions with lightweight objects (e.g., **87.37%** AUROC at 100 Hz, **19.67%** higher than MVT-Flow for these categories), and maintained robust performance across various training data sizes.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The combination of Masked Autoregressive Flow within an Adversarial AutoEncoder framework allows for learning a flexible latent space that accurately models normal robot behavior, avoiding restrictive distribution assumptions and improving anomaly discrimination.
> - Sparsity constraints, applied to the encoder, are more effective than simple latent space compression for processing limited sensor features, enabling the model to logically compress data and preserve crucial information.
> - A lightweight model architecture, coupled with efficient data processing, achieves sub-millisecond inference speeds, crucial for real-time anomaly detection on embedded robot systems and enhancing operational safety.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The increasing integration of robots into diverse and unstructured environments, beyond traditional factory settings, necessitates advanced real-time anomaly detection capabilities. This is particularly crucial for ensuring safety and efficiency in human-robot shared spaces. Anomaly detection in robotics traditionally employs model-based, knowledge-based, or data-driven approaches. Model-based methods rely on analytical equations and are sensitive to configuration and model accuracy, while knowledge-based systems depend on expert-defined rules and struggle with unmodeled anomalies. The trend has shifted towards data-driven, particularly machine learning-based methods, due to their ability to handle complex data patterns, scalability, and adaptability, especially for detecting unknown faults without explicit system models.

Within data-driven approaches, deep learning models for time-series anomaly detection, often utilizing unsupervised learning due to the rarity and labeling challenges of anomalous data, have gained prominence. Existing deep learning models face specific challenges in robotic applications. Variational AutoEncoders (VAEs), like those in LSTM-VAE, often struggle to build efficient latent spaces with small input features and are limited by assumptions of Gaussian distributions for posterior and prior, which may not accurately represent noisy robot data. Normalizing Flow (NF)-based models, such as MVT-Flow, can mimic complex data distributions but may misassign high likelihoods to out-of-distribution (anomalous) data, reducing detection accuracy. Transformer-based models, exemplified by Anomaly Transformer, capture global time-series features effectively but suffer from high computational cost and memory requirements (O(L^2 N) complexity), making them unsuitable for real-time applications on resource-constrained embedded robot systems and demanding extensive training datasets which are difficult to acquire in robotics.

This work addresses these limitations by proposing a novel unsupervised learning framework designed for real-time operation with limited, noisy sensor signals. It aims to overcome the inflexibility of latent space assumptions in VAEs, the out-of-distribution sensitivity of normalizing flows, and the computational burden of transformer models, specifically tailoring a solution for the constraints of commercial robotic platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust, real-time multivariate time-series anomaly detection method for robots, specifically addressing the challenges posed by limited training data, noisy signal features, and computational constraints inherent in robotic operations. The authors identify several motivations for this objective:

1. **Ensuring Robustness with Limited Training Data and Noisy Signals:** Deep learning models typically require large datasets for effective training. However, in robotics, collecting extensive, diverse datasets, especially for rare anomaly events, is challenging and time-consuming. Robotic sensor data, such as torque signals, can also be inherently noisy. Current models may struggle to learn effective representations under these conditions.
2. **Achieving Real-time Inference Capabilities:** For practical applicability and safety in dynamic environments, anomaly detection must occur in real-time. This implies that the model must complete inference (signal reconstruction and anomaly scoring) within milliseconds, before the next data window is formed from streaming sensor signals. This requirement is particularly stringent for low-cost commercial robots with limited computational power and communication speeds on embedded boards.
3. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Deep Learning Models:**
 - **Variational Autoencoders (VAEs):** VAEs, often used in approaches like LSTM-VAE, typically assume Gaussian distributions for their posterior and prior. This assumption can be restrictive, failing to capture the intricate, non-Gaussian nature of real-world robot sensor data, leading to suboptimal anomaly detection rates, especially with rare or poorly represented data. They also struggle with building efficient latent spaces when input features are limited.
 - **Normalizing Flows (NFs):** While NFs, exemplified by MVT-Flow, can accurately model complex data distributions, they exhibit inductive biases that can cause them to assign high likelihoods to out-of-distribution (anomalous) data. This makes it difficult to distinguish true anomalies, reducing robustness.
 - **Transformer-based Models:** Models like Anomaly Transformer offer strong performance in capturing temporal and global features but demand significant computational resources (O(L^2 N) complexity) and large datasets. These requirements make them impractical for real-time deployment on embedded robot systems with limited computational power and memory.
4. **Effective Feature Preservation with Limited Sensor Signals:** Robots often rely on a limited number of proprioceptive sensors (e.g., joint angles, currents, torques). Traditional autoencoders, which compress input to a smaller latent space, might discard crucial information when the initial feature space is already small. A method is needed that can logically compress data and preserve essential characteristics even with sparse input signals.

To address these motivations, the authors propose an integrated framework combining Masked Autoregressive Flow (MAF) for flexible latent space modeling, Adversarial AutoEncoders (AAE) for robust distribution matching, and Sparse autoencoder principles for efficient feature learning with limited data, all while maintaining a lightweight architecture for real-time inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Sparse Masked Autoregressive Flow-based Adversarial AutoEncoder (Sparse MAF-AAE), is an unsupervised learning model designed for real-time multivariate time-series anomaly detection in robotic tasks. It consists of an offline training stage and a real-time detection stage.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

During **offline training**, the model is exclusively trained on normal operational data. Input time-series data from robot sensors (e.g., torque) are preprocessed into fixed-size sliding windows $W_{x[t:t+T_W]}$ (length $T_W$, stride $T_S$). The model learns to accurately reconstruct these normal windows. Upon completion of training, statistics (mean $\mu_{normal}$ and standard deviation $\sigma_{normal}$) of reconstruction errors from normal data are calculated.

In the **real-time detection** stage, new incoming data windows are fed to the trained model. The model reconstructs the data and calculates the $L_1$ norm of the reconstruction error. This error is then converted into an anomaly score $AS(w)$ using the pre-calculated normal statistics. If $AS(w)$ exceeds a predefined threshold $\theta$, the current robot operation is classified as anomalous. Retraining is suggested when platform or environmental adjustments occur, which is simplified by the unsupervised nature of the model, only requiring new normal operational data.

#### 3.2. Sparse MAF-AAE Architecture

Sparse MAF-AAE is built upon the Adversarial AutoEncoder (AAE) framework, integrating Masked Autoregressive Flow (MAF) layers and principles of Sparse autoencoders. The model acts as a generator (G) within the AAE framework, alongside a discriminator (D).

- **Flexible Latent Space with MAF-enhanced AAE:**
 - **Encoder:** For multivariate time-series input $W_{x[t:t+T_W]}$, an LSTM network is used to capture temporal dependencies and create a hidden space. From this hidden space, linear layers derive the mean vector $\mu$ and standard deviation vector $\sigma$, forming a base Gaussian distribution $q_0(z_0)$. A base latent space $z_0$ is sampled using the reparameterization trick ($z_0 = \mu + \sigma \odot \epsilon$, where $\epsilon \sim N(0, I)$).
 - **Masked Autoregressive Flow (MAF) Layers:** Unlike VAEs that assume Gaussian posteriors, Sparse MAF-AAE transforms $z_0$ into a more complex final latent space $z_K$ using a series of invertible mappings from MAF. MAF leverages autoregressive properties by stacking Masked Autoencoder for Distribution Estimation (MADE) networks to compute mean ($\mu_i$) and log standard deviation ($\alpha_i$) of each conditional variable, ensuring that each component $x_i$ only depends on $x_{1:i-1}$. This allows for learning flexible posterior $q_K(z_K)$ and prior $p(z_{prior})$ distributions that better capture intricate data structures.
 - **Sparsity Constraints:** To address the challenge of limited sensor signals in robotics, the model implements principles from Sparse autoencoders. The hidden space and latent space are designed to be larger than the input feature space (doubled in experiments). $L_1$ regularization is applied to the encoder's loss function. This constraint promotes sparse activation of hidden units, meaning only a few important cells are activated to construct the latent representation, thereby enabling logical compression and effective feature preservation even with a small number of input features.
 - **Decoder:** The decoder maps the final latent space $z_K$ back to a hidden space and then reconstructs the input data $W'_{x[t:t+T_W]}$ through a series of linear layers. For normal inputs, reconstruction is accurate; for anomalous inputs, it produces significant deviations.

- **Adversarial Training with Discriminator:**
 - **Discriminator (D):** A simple multi-layer perceptron (MLP) acts as the discriminator. It is trained to distinguish between samples drawn from the model's learned posterior distribution $q_K(z_K)$ (generated by the encoder) and samples drawn from the prior distribution $p(z_{prior})$.
 - **Prior Distribution:** The prior distribution $p(z_{prior})$ is dynamically defined as the aggregated posterior distribution $q_K(z)$ from previous learning epochs or batches, conceptually similar to updating priors in Bayesian inference. This allows the prior to adapt to the data's characteristics.

#### 3.3. Offline Training Procedure

Training involves iteratively updating the parameters of the generator (G, which includes the encoder, MAF layers, and decoder) and the discriminator (D).

- **Generator Loss ($L_G$):** $L_G = L_{MSE} + L_{L_1} + L_{BCE}$
 - $L_{MSE}$: Mean Squared Error between the original input window and its reconstruction, promoting accurate data reconstruction.
 - $L_{L_1}$: $L_1$ regularization applied to the encoder's parameters ($w_{encoder}$), enforcing sparsity and feature selection. The hyperparameter $\lambda$ controls its influence.
 - $L_{BCE}$: Binary Cross-Entropy loss calculated from the discriminator's output for $q_K(z_K)$ with a target label of 1 (real). This term, scaled by hyperparameter $\beta$, encourages the generator to produce latent codes that are indistinguishable from the prior. $\beta$ is gradually increased during training (KL annealing) to balance reconstruction accuracy and distribution matching. The MAF layer is implicitly trained through its contribution to the posterior distribution that is minimized by $L_{MSE}$ and $L_{BCE}$.
- **Discriminator Loss ($L_D$):** $L_D = \frac{1}{2} [BCE(D(z_{prior}), 1) + BCE(D(z_K), 0)]$
 - This BCE loss trains the discriminator to correctly classify samples from the true prior $p(z_{prior})$ as 'real' (label 1) and samples from the generator's posterior $q_K(z_K)$ as 'fake' (label 0). Critically, $z_K$ is detached from the computation graph during $L_D$ minimization to prevent inadvertent parameter updates to the generator.

#### 3.4. Real-time Detection Procedure

1. **Normalization Statistics:** After offline training, the $L_1$ norms of reconstruction errors for a set of normal data are computed, and their mean ($\mu_{normal}$) and standard deviation ($\sigma_{normal}$) are stored.
2. **Anomaly Scoring:** For a new incoming data window $w$:
 - The model reconstructs $w$ to $w'$.
 - The $L_1$ norm of the reconstruction error, $L_1(w, w') = ||w - w'||_1$, is calculated.
 - An anomaly score $AS(w)$ is computed: $AS(w) = (L_1(w) - \mu_{normal}) / \sigma_{normal}$.
3. **Anomaly Classification:** If $AS(w)$ exceeds a user-defined threshold $\theta$, the data window $w$ is classified as anomalous.

The use of unsupervised learning facilitates straightforward retraining by simply providing new normal data, making the model adaptable for changing operational conditions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated Sparse MAF-AAE against Anomaly Transformer, LSTM-VAE, and MVT-Flow using a modified voraus-AD dataset for pick-and-place operations, focusing on torque signals across various sampling frequencies and training data sizes.

#### 4.1. Overall Anomaly Detection Performance

Sparse MAF-AAE consistently demonstrated superior anomaly detection performance across all tested sampling frequencies (100 Hz, 50 Hz, 25 Hz, 10 Hz) when using only torque signals.
- At 100 Hz, Sparse MAF-AAE achieved an AUROC of 89.23% (±7.45), outperforming MVT-Flow (84.27% ± 11.55) by 4.96% and LSTM-VAE (73.66% ± 12.05) by 15.57%.
- At 50 Hz, it achieved 87.14% (±8.69), surpassing MVT-Flow (77.39% ± 14.54) by 9.75%.
- The model also exhibited the smallest standard deviation across different anomaly types compared to LSTM-VAE and MVT-Flow, indicating consistent and robust performance.

#### 4.2. Performance per Anomaly Type

Sparse MAF-AAE outperformed other models in 7 out of 12 anomaly categories at 100 Hz and 9 out of 12 at 50 Hz.
- **Collision Detection:** The model showed a notable advantage in detecting collisions with lightweight objects (foam, cables, cardboard). For these categories, Sparse MAF-AAE achieved 87.37% (±4.20) at 100 Hz, which is 19.67% higher than MVT-Flow (67.70% ± 1.50). At 50 Hz, it achieved 80.50% (±9.71), 13.37% higher than MVT-Flow (59.31% ± 8.02). This indicates the model's effectiveness in discerning subtle but critical errors that manifest as minor torque fluctuations.
- **Miscommutation:** Sparse MAF-AAE did not perform as well in detecting miscommutation errors (75.87% at 100 Hz, 74.86% at 50 Hz). The authors suggest this is likely because miscommutation primarily affects current signals rather than torque values, which were the sole input feature in this specific experiment. This implies that the model's anomaly detection is relevant to the input signals provided.
- **Anomaly Transformer:** Anomaly Transformer generally yielded the lowest performance, attributed to its high data requirements, which are challenging to meet in robotics where data collection is limited.

#### 4.3. Real-time Inference Efficiency

- Sparse MAF-AAE demonstrated real-time inference capabilities, completing signal reconstruction and anomaly scoring within 0.86 milliseconds on the 50 Hz dataset. This performance is comparable to LSTM-VAE (0.57 ms) and significantly faster than MVT-Flow (1.15 ms) and Anomaly Transformer (2.29 ms), which did not meet the 1 ms threshold. This computational efficiency makes the model suitable for deployment on embedded robot systems.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Evaluation

- Visualizations confirmed that Sparse MAF-AAE accurately reconstructed normal torque signals while producing clear, distinguishable deviations for anomalous inputs. These deviations were observed even for subtle anomalies like lightweight collisions affecting only a subset of sensors (e.g., torque sensors 1 and 6).
- The log-scale anomaly score distribution for Sparse MAF-AAE showed less overlap between normal and anomalous data distributions compared to LSTM-VAE, indicating better discriminative power.

#### 4.5. Impact of Training Data Size

- Sparse MAF-AAE maintained strong performance across various training data sizes (12.5% to 100% of the dataset), demonstrating its robustness in scenarios with limited available normal data. Its performance also showed potential for improvement with larger datasets.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

- **Effects of Components:** Both the sparsity constraints and MAF layers significantly contributed to the model's performance. Removing MAF layers or replacing sparsity constraints with physical compression (smaller latent space) led to a decrease in AUROC. This confirms that mathematically constrained sparsity is more effective than simple compression for limited features, and flexible latent space transformation improves anomaly discrimination.
- **Signal Selection:** Experiments comparing encoder values, current values, and torque values as input features revealed that torque signals yielded the highest overall AUROC (89.23% for Sparse MAF-AAE with 100 Hz data), indicating their effectiveness in representing various anomalies. Current signals performed best for miscommutation errors (98.65% for Sparse MAF-AAE), which aligns with the physical nature of this anomaly. This supports the experimental design of using torque signals as a primary evaluation feature.
- **Utilizing All Machine Signals:** When tested with all 130 machine signals from the dataset (as in the original voraus-AD paper), MVT-Flow achieved an AUROC of 91.81%, validating the experimental setup against published results. Sparse MAF-AAE's performance declined to 77.25% with all signals, suggesting that its architecture is optimized for scenarios with a limited number of relevant features. However, achieving comparable AUROC with significantly fewer signals (12 torque signals vs. 130 all signals) highlights its practical advantage for resource-constrained applications.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Sparse MAF-AAE presents several significant contributions and potential impacts in robotics and related fields:

1. **Enhanced Real-time Anomaly Detection for Commercial Robots:** The model's sub-millisecond inference time, coupled with high detection accuracy, makes it particularly suitable for real-time anomaly detection in commercial robots. This capability is crucial for ensuring immediate response to malfunctions, thereby preventing accidents, minimizing downtime, and enhancing the overall reliability and safety of robotic systems operating in dynamic or human-shared environments.

2. **Robustness with Limited Resources:** By effectively utilizing sparsity constraints and a flexible latent space, Sparse MAF-AAE demonstrates strong performance even with a limited number of sensor signals and restricted training data. This is a critical advantage for low-cost commercial robots and embedded systems, which often have constraints on computational power, memory, and the number/type of sensors. The ability to perform well under such conditions broadens the applicability of advanced anomaly detection to a wider range of robotic platforms.

3. **Improved Anomaly Discrimination:** The integration of Masked Autoregressive Flow (MAF) within an Adversarial AutoEncoder (AAE) framework allows the model to learn more flexible and accurate representations of data distributions. This approach mitigates the common issue in other flow-based models of misassigning high likelihoods to out-of-distribution (anomalous) data, leading to more robust and reliable anomaly discrimination, especially for subtle anomalies like lightweight collisions.

4. **Practicality and Scalability:** The unsupervised learning paradigm of Sparse MAF-AAE simplifies the retraining process. When robot platforms or operating environments change, users only need to provide new normal operational data, making the model highly adaptable and scalable for long-term deployment. This reduces the significant effort and resources typically associated with labeling anomalous data for supervised learning approaches.

5. **Broader Industrial Applications:** Beyond robotics, the principles and capabilities of Sparse MAF-AAE—such as real-time performance, robustness with limited data/features, and efficient anomaly detection—have potential applications in other industrial sectors where operational safety, reliability, and productivity are paramount. Examples include manufacturing systems, predictive maintenance for industrial machinery, and monitoring of complex engineering systems where rapid detection of deviations is essential.

In summary, Sparse MAF-AAE addresses critical gaps in existing deep learning anomaly detection methods for robotics, offering a practical, high-performing solution that aligns with the specific constraints and requirements of real-world robotic deployments. Its advancements contribute to safer, more efficient, and more reliable robotic automation.
