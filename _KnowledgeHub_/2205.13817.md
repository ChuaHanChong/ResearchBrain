# Iso-Dream: Isolating and Leveraging Noncontrollable Visual Dynamics in World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Iso-Dream is a model-based RL framework that splits a Dreamer-style world model into three branches, action-conditioned, action-free, and static, using an inverse-dynamics objective to isolate controllable from noncontrollable visual dynamics. An attention mechanism fuses the current controllable state with rolled-out future noncontrollable states, letting the agent act on anticipated external motion. It beats DreamerV2 across DMC-with-video-background, CARLA driving, and action-conditioned video prediction.

## Problem

- Existing world models entangle all visual change into one latent stream, so action-independent motion like moving vehicles or video backgrounds corrupts learned dynamics.
- Prior disentanglement work in video prediction separates content from motion or long-term from short-term states, never by controllability, so it does not aid downstream decision-making.
- Dreamer-style agents condition policies only on the current latent state, so they cannot anticipate external forces that matter for long-horizon tasks such as collision avoidance.

## Method

- A three-branch world model: an ==action-conditioned RSSM== branch for controllable states, an ==action-free== branch for noncontrollable states, and a static branch for time-invariant background, composed via learned masks.
- An **Inverse Cell**, a two-layer MLP regressing the action from consecutive controllable posteriors, adds an ==inverse-dynamics loss== that pushes action-driven change into the action-conditioned branch only.
- ==Future state attention== rolls the action-free branch ahead by a window and softmax-attends the current controllable state over those imagined noncontrollable states, feeding the actor-critic in imagination and at deployment.

## Results

- On DMC with video_easy backgrounds it scores **911** on Walker Walk, **659** on Cheetah Run, **800** on Finger Spin, and **746** on Hopper Stand, beating DreamerV2 at **655**/**475**/**755**/**260**.
- Video prediction reaches **19.51** PSNR / **0.768** SSIM on BAIR and **21.71** / **0.769** on RoboNet, improving PSNR over SVG by **7.7%** and **9.3%** and topping PhyDNet on both metrics.
- CARLA ablations show drops when removing the Inverse Cell or the rolling-out of noncontrollable states, and roughly **15%** degradation without the separate time-invariant branch.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Controllability, not appearance or timescale, is a useful axis for disentangling latent dynamics, because it directly determines which states a policy can act upon.
> - Inverse dynamics is a cheap supervisory signal for forcing an action-conditioned pathway to absorb only action-driven variation, preventing one branch from swallowing all information.
> - Predicting external, uncontrollable motion ahead of the agent's own rollout yields forward-looking policies, but costs training time and requires per-environment structural choices.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper is situated within the field of model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL), specifically focusing on vision-based interactive systems and the development of "world models." World models are differentiable simulators of an environment learned from observation and control signals, enabling agents to predict the consequences of actions and plan behaviors in a latent space. This approach has gained attention in robot visual control and forms the foundation for recent MBRL advancements such as PlaNet and Dreamer/DreamerV2.

A significant challenge in learning effective world models in complex visual scenes is the presence of high-dimensional, non-stationary observations often driven by multiple sources of physical dynamics. Existing methods have explored action-conditioned video prediction, dynamics uncertainty modeling, object-centric scene decomposition, and space-time disentanglement. Previous disentanglement efforts have typically separated visual dynamics into content and motion vectors or long-term and short-term states.

Iso-Dream differentiates itself by focusing on disentanglement based on *controllability*. It addresses the common scenario in real-world applications, such as autonomous driving, where observed spatiotemporal dynamics can be divided into a controllable part (responding to the agent's actions) and a noncontrollable part (independent of the agent's actions, such as the movement of other vehicles or environmental changes). By explicitly isolating these dynamics, Iso-Dream aims to improve upon the Dream-to-Control framework, offering a more robust and effective approach to MBRL compared to methods that do not explicitly distinguish between controllable and noncontrollable elements, such as DreamerV2 or InfoPower. The paper builds upon the recurrent state-space model (RSSM) architecture used in PlaNet and Dreamer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel reinforcement learning approach, named Iso-Dream, that enhances the effectiveness of world models in vision-based interactive systems by addressing the challenge posed by noncontrollable visual dynamics. The core motivation stems from the observation that in practical environments, such as autonomous driving, agents encounter dynamics that are independent of their actions. Failing to explicitly account for these noncontrollable elements can hinder the learning of effective world models and limit the performance of reinforcement learning agents.

The specific objectives and motivations are detailed as follows:

- **Decoupling Controllable and Noncontrollable Dynamics:** A central objective is to develop a world model that can explicitly isolate and learn distinct representations for controllable dynamics (those directly influenced by the agent's actions) and noncontrollable dynamics (those external to the agent's control). The motivation here is that a mixed representation of these dynamics can confuse the world model, making it difficult to predict the consequences of the agent's own actions accurately.
- **Improving World Model Representations:** The paper aims to introduce a new form of world model representation that achieves this decoupling. By separating latent states into action-conditioned and action-free branches, the goal is to allow each branch to model different sources of visual dynamics independently. This modularity is hypothesized to lead to more robust and semantically meaningful representations.
- **Enhancing Disentanglement via Inverse Dynamics:** To further refine the isolation of controllable states, an objective is to optimize the action-conditioned branch with an inverse dynamics task. By requiring the model to infer the actions that caused observed state transitions, the controllable branch is encouraged to specifically encode action-responsive information, thereby promoting a cleaner disentanglement from noncontrollable elements.
- **Leveraging Noncontrollable Dynamics for Long-Horizon Decision-Making:** A key objective is to enable the agent to utilize the predicted future states of noncontrollable dynamics to inform its current decision-making. The motivation is that for complex, long-horizon tasks (e.g., autonomous driving), anticipating the movement of other agents or environmental changes can significantly improve planning and risk avoidance. This goes beyond simply filtering out noise and actively incorporates external dynamics into the policy.
- **Developing a Novel Actor-Critic Algorithm:** To achieve the objective of leveraging future noncontrollable dynamics, the paper aims to propose a new actor-critic algorithm that operates on decoupled latent imaginations. Specifically, the algorithm should integrate predicted future noncontrollable states with the current controllable state using mechanisms like attention, enabling more "forward-looking" decisions.
- **Improving Generalization to Non-Stationary Environments:** By isolating controllable dynamics, the research aims to improve the agent's generalization capabilities in environments with time-varying backgrounds or other non-stationary noises, where the noncontrollable components can act as distractions.
- **Demonstrating Effectiveness Across Diverse Domains:** The research seeks to validate Iso-Dream's effectiveness in a range of environments, including modified DeepMind Control Suite tasks with noisy backgrounds, the CARLA autonomous driving simulator with other vehicles as noncontrollable elements, and real-world robot datasets for video prediction. This broad evaluation aims to confirm the approach's robustness and applicability.

In essence, the motivation is to move beyond simply learning to predict future observations to learning *what causes* those observations, disentangling the agent's influence from external factors, and then using this deeper understanding to make more intelligent, anticipatory decisions in complex, interactive environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Iso-Dream is an MBRL framework that extends the Dream-to-Control (Dreamer) paradigm by explicitly decoupling controllable and noncontrollable visual dynamics. Its methodology can be broken down into basic assumptions, representation learning, behavior learning, and policy deployment.

#### 3.1. Basic Assumptions

The underlying dynamics `u_1:T` observed as `o_1:T` are assumed to be decomposable into controllable latent states `s_1:T` and noncontrollable latent states `z_1:T`.
- Controllable states evolve dependently on actions: `s_t+1 ~ p(s_t+1 | s_t, a_t)`.
- Noncontrollable states evolve independently of actions: `z_t+1 ~ p(z_t+1 | z_t)`.
The framework provides an option to include or exclude noncontrollable states `z` during behavior learning, depending on whether they are considered task-relevant noise or important future predictors.

#### 3.2. Representation Learning of Controllable and Noncontrollable Dynamics

The core of Iso-Dream's representation learning is a novel three-branch world model architecture, designed for explicit disentanglement:

- **Action-Conditioned Branch:** This branch models `p(~s_t | s_<t, a_<t)` using a Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM) structure, specifically a GRU (`GRU_s`) to maintain historical information for controllable dynamics. It predicts a prior representation `~s_t`.
- **Action-Free Branch:** This branch models `p(~z_t | z_<t)` with a similar RSSM structure, employing a separate GRU (`GRU_z`) for noncontrollable dynamics. It predicts a prior representation `~z_t`.
- **Static Branch:** This branch is designed to extract time-invariant information, such as the background, from an initial sequence of K frames `o_1:K`. This component is reconstructed separately and merged later.

**State Inference and Posterior Representations:** From the current observation `o_t`, a shared encoder `Enc_θ` is used, followed by branch-specific encoders (`Enc_φ1` for `s_t`, `Enc_φ2` for `z_t`) to derive posterior representations `s_t ~ q(s_t | h_t, o_t)` and `z_t ~ q(z_t | h'_t, o_t)`.

**Inverse Dynamics for Disentanglement:** To enhance the disentanglement of controllable dynamics, an "Inverse Cell" (a 2-layer MLP) is introduced. This cell is trained to infer the action `~a_t-1` that caused the transition between `s_t-1` and `s_t`: `~a_t-1 = MLP(s_t-1, s_t)`. By regressing the true action `a_t-1`, the action-conditioned branch is encouraged to specifically capture action-dependent features.

**Image Reconstruction:** The reconstruction of the observation `o_t` is performed by combining outputs from all three branches:
`^o_t = M_s_t ^o_s_t + M_z_t ^o_z_t + (1 - M_s_t - M_z_t) ^o_b`
where `^o_s_t` (controllable visual component) is generated from `~s_t` with mask `M_s_t`, `^o_z_t` (noncontrollable visual component) is generated from `z_t` with mask `M_z_t`, and `^o_b` (time-invariant background) is generated by the static branch. This masked reconstruction helps in explicit visual disentanglement.

**Reward Modeling:** Reward models can be learned either as `p(r_t | s_t)` (if `z_t` is treated as noise) or `p(r_t | s_t, z_t)` (if `z_t` is relevant for decision-making), depending on the task's prior knowledge.

**World Model Loss Function:** The world model is optimized using a comprehensive loss function (Eq. 6), which includes:
- Image log loss for `p(o_t | h_t, s_t, h'_t, z_t)`.
- Reward log loss for `p(r_t | h_t, s_t, h'_t, z_t)`.
- Discount log loss for `p(γ_t | h_t, s_t, h'_t, z_t)`.
- Action loss `L2(a_t, ~a_t)` from the Inverse Cell.
- KL divergence terms for `s_t` and `z_t` to match posteriors with priors.

#### 3.3. Behavior Learning in Decoupled Imaginations

Iso-Dream proposes an improved actor-critic learning algorithm that leverages the decoupled world model:

- **Future State Attention Mechanism:** During imagination, the policy network does not simply rely on `~s_t`. Instead, it first uses the action-free branch to roll out `τ` steps of future noncontrollable states `~z_t:t+τ`. This sequence is then combined with the current controllable state `~s_t` using a self-attention mechanism to create a "visionary" representation `e_t`: `e_t = softmax(~s_t ~z^T_t:t+τ) ~z_t:t+τ + ~s_t`. This allows the agent to explicitly consider future external dynamics.
- **Actor-Critic Update:** The action model `π(a_t | e_t)` and value model `v_ξ(e_t)` are then updated based on this `e_t`. The action model is trained to maximize the λ-return, and the value model to regress it.

#### 3.4. Policy Deployment by Rolling-out Noncontrollable Dynamics

During actual environment interaction, if noncontrollable dynamics are relevant to the task, the agent first calculates the posterior `s_t` and `z_t`. It then uses the action-free branch to predict `τ-1` future noncontrollable states `~z_t+1:t+τ` from `z_t`. These predicted states, along with `s_t` and `z_t`, are integrated via the future state attention network to form `e_t`, from which the action `a_t` is drawn. If noncontrollable dynamics are irrelevant (e.g., background noise), only `s_t` is used for policy generation.

The overall training procedure (Algorithm 1) alternates between representation learning (updating the world model), behavior learning (updating the policy and value models on imagined trajectories), and environment interaction (collecting new data for the replay buffer).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Iso-Dream was evaluated across various benchmarks, encompassing visual control tasks and action-conditioned video prediction.

#### 4.1. DeepMind Control Suite (DMC) with Noisy Backgrounds

- **Setup:** Iso-Dream was tested on modified DMC environments (`video_easy` background) where natural video backgrounds introduced noncontrollable visual dynamics. For these tasks, the action-free branch was trained solely with reconstruction loss and not used in imagination or policy deployment, treating noncontrollable elements as noise.
- **Quantitative Results:** Iso-Dream consistently outperformed DreamerV2 and other baselines (SVEA, CURL, DBC) across all four tested tasks: Walker Walk, Cheetah Run, Finger Spin, and Hopper Stand. For instance, in Walker Walk, Iso-Dream achieved 911 ± 50, compared to DreamerV2's 655 ± 47. This indicates its ability to learn task-relevant visual representations and mitigate interference from complex background noise.
- **Qualitative Results:** Video prediction visualizations showed Iso-Dream's capacity for long-term sequence prediction and successful disentanglement. The action-conditioned branch accurately isolated the controllable agent, while the action-free branch captured noncontrollable elements like sea waves in the background, as evidenced by corresponding masks and reconstructed visual components.

#### 4.2. CARLA Autonomous Driving Environment

- **Setup:** Iso-Dream was applied to a highway driving task in CARLA, where other vehicles represent noncontrollable but relevant dynamics. The reward function incentivized highway progression and penalized collisions and excessive steering. Here, the rolling-out strategy for noncontrollable states and the attention mechanism were utilized.
- **Quantitative Results:** Iso-Dream demonstrated significant performance advantages over baselines, substantially outperforming DreamerV2 in episode return.
 - **Ablation Studies:**
 - Removing the "Inverse Cell" (which optimizes inverse dynamics) led to a performance drop, highlighting its role in isolating controllable and noncontrollable components.
 - Removing the rolling-out strategy for noncontrollable states (i.e., not using the attention mechanism to preview future `z` states) also reduced performance, confirming the benefit of forward-looking decision-making based on noncontrollable dynamics.
 - The absence of a separate static branch for time-invariant information resulted in approximately a 15% performance degradation, indicating the importance of modeling static components.
- **Qualitative Results:** Reconstruction predictions in CARLA showed that "action-free masks" identified other vehicles as hot spots, while "action-cond masks" retained attention values in corresponding areas. This visual disentanglement suggests the agent could effectively preview future states of other vehicles, aiding in collision avoidance.

#### 4.3. BAIR & RoboNet for Action-Conditioned Video Prediction

- **Setup:** Iso-Dream's world model was tested for video prediction on the BAIR robot pushing and RoboNet datasets. To increase complexity, bouncing balls (as predictable, noncontrollable dynamics) were added to raw observations. The model predicted 28 frames (BAIR) or 18 frames (RoboNet) from 2 input frames.
- **Quantitative Results:** Iso-Dream achieved competitive performance, showing higher PSNR and SSIM scores compared to existing video prediction models like SVG, SA-ConvLSTM, and PhyDNet. For example, on BAIR, Iso-Dream improved SVG's PSNR by 7.7% and PhyDNet's PSNR by 3.1%. This indicated a stronger ability for disentanglement learning and long-term prediction.
 - **Robustness to Noise:** When trained on BAIR with bouncing balls and tested on the standard BAIR dataset (without balls), Iso-Dream was the only approach that showed an improvement, suggesting enhanced robustness and generalization from noisy data.
 - **Ablation Studies (Video Prediction):** Removing the action-free branch or the Inverse Cell led to performance decreases, further validating their contributions to disentanglement and predictive learning.
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations of predicted frames demonstrated that Iso-Dream's world model was more accurate for long-term predictions. The action-free branch successfully learned the dynamics of the bouncing balls, while the action-conditioned branch learned the dynamics related to the robot arm's actions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in Iso-Dream addresses a fundamental challenge in model-based reinforcement learning, namely the presence of noncontrollable visual dynamics in complex real-world environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced World Model Robustness:** By explicitly disentangling controllable and noncontrollable dynamics, Iso-Dream offers a more robust and semantically organized world model. This approach can lead to models that are less susceptible to noise and irrelevant changes in the environment, which is crucial for deploying AI agents in dynamic and unpredictable settings.
- **Improved Long-Horizon Decision-Making:** The novel integration of predicted future noncontrollable states into the policy learning process enables agents to make more forward-looking decisions. This capability is particularly impactful for tasks requiring anticipation and planning over extended time horizons, such as autonomous driving (anticipating other vehicles' movements) and complex robotic manipulation.
- **Greater Sample Efficiency:** While acknowledging increased computational time per episode, the paper indicates that Iso-Dream exhibits greater sample efficiency compared to existing MBRL methods. This is a significant advantage in real-world applications where data collection can be costly or time-consuming.
- **Stronger Generalization:** The ability to isolate controllable dynamics helps agents generalize better to non-stationary environments, such as those with dynamic backgrounds. This modular representation can lead to agents that perform reliably despite variations in task-irrelevant visual information.
- **Advancement in Disentangled Representation Learning:** Iso-Dream introduces a novel approach to disentangling visual dynamics based on controllability, distinct from prior work focusing on content/motion or short/long-term states. This contribution extends the field of representation learning by providing a task-relevant disentanglement criterion.
- **Applicability to Real-World Scenarios:** The strong performance demonstrated in benchmarks like CARLA (autonomous driving) and real-world robot datasets (BAIR, RoboNet) suggests a high potential for Iso-Dream to be applied in practical, safety-critical domains where understanding and reacting to external dynamics is paramount.

The identified limitations, such as computational efficiency and the need for environment-specific architectural customization, represent avenues for future research. However, the conceptual framework and empirical evidence presented indicate that Iso-Dream offers a valuable advancement towards building more intelligent, adaptive, and predictive AI agents capable of operating effectively in complex and dynamic visual environments.
