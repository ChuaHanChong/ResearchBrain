# CIRCLE: Capture In Rich Contextual Environments

> [!summary] Summary
> CIRCLE captures full-body human motion in VR: actors move in an empty mocap studio while perceiving cluttered virtual apartments, giving occlusion-free motion paired with exact scene geometry and egocentric RGB-D. The 10-hour, 5-subject, 9-scene reaching dataset trains a Transformer refinement model that turns a naive interpolated trajectory into scene-aware motion, reaching 79.2% success with near-ground-truth collision depth.

## Problem

- Optical mocap records kinematics only; capturing motion inside a contextual scene demands costly physical set construction and props, limiting scene diversity and scale.
- Physical clutter placed in the capture volume occludes markers, degrading motion quality, so prior scene-conditioned datasets used only a few sparse objects.
- IMU-based and camera-reconstructed scene datasets suffer drift and noisy geometry, so no dataset pairs clean full-body motion with exact 3D scene state.

## Method

- Acquisition system couples a ==Vicon== 12-camera 120 FPS rig with a WebXR AI Habitat app on Quest 2; Mephisto logs sequences, live skeleton streams back for visual feedback.
- Post-processing aligns headset and mocap by maximizing velocity-profile convolution, fits ==SMPL-X== via ==MoSh++==, then renders synthetic egocentric RGB-D and auto-flags marker swaps and joint jumps.
- Motion generation initializes a constant pose linearly interpolated to the goal, encodes scene by ==Basis Point Set== or pretrained ==PointNet++== features, and refines with a four-block self-attention ==Transformer==.

## Results

- On the random split, PointNet++ encoding reaches **79.2%** success versus **74.6%** without scene features and **12.1%** for the GOAL autoregressive baseline, whose goal distance is **44.5 cm**.
- Scene conditioning cuts cumulative collision depth from **15.87 cm** to **12.09 cm** (BPS), essentially matching the **11.02 cm** ground-truth level, despite no collision term in the loss.
- On held-out tasks, BPS reaches **76.1%** success versus **64.9%** no-scene and **7.04 cm** collision depth, below the **8.17 cm** ground truth; foot sliding worsens to **20.7%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Swapping the physical set for a virtual one inverts the mocap tradeoff: scene complexity becomes free, occlusion vanishes, and scene state is exact simulator output.
> - Scene observations alone reduce interpenetration even without collision supervision, showing perceptual conditioning can substitute for explicit geometric loss terms.
> - Baseline metrics can deceive: a near-static autoregressive prediction scores excellent foot-sliding while failing the task, so motion metrics need joint interpretation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the fields of 3D human motion synthesis, embodied artificial intelligence (AI), computer animation, robotics, and augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR). The overarching goal in these domains often involves simulating or generating realistic human activities that occur within complex, ecological environments.

Existing research has focused on generating 3D human motion and behaviors using machine learning models. The efficacy of these models largely depends on the availability of high-quality, voluminous, and diverse human motion data. Traditional motion capture (mocap) techniques, primarily optics-based systems, have been instrumental in recording human kinematics. However, these systems primarily capture human movement itself, often neglecting the intricate details and constraints of the surrounding environment. Capturing humans interacting within a contextual scene typically necessitates the physical construction of elaborate sets and specific props within a capture studio. This requirement introduces significant costs, logistical challenges, and limitations, making it difficult to holistically capture realistic human activities in diverse and complex real-world settings. A notable drawback of physical setups is the potential for occlusions, where objects or the environment obstruct the motion capture cameras, leading to degraded data quality.

Previous human motion datasets, such as the CMU Motion Capture Database and AMASS, provide extensive collections of human motion but generally do not incorporate detailed 3D environmental context that influences movement. While some efforts, like those utilizing the GPA and SAMP datasets, have introduced scene-conditioned human motion by incorporating a limited number of physical objects into the mocap area, they still involve physical construction. Other datasets, such as BEHAVE, GIMO, and EgoBody, integrate multi-view RGBD videos, 3D pose, and contact information, or IMU-based and egocentric image capture with 3D scene reconstructions. However, these often face challenges like IMU drift or inherent noise in LiDAR and camera-based 3D reconstructions compared to virtual ground truth.

In the domain of 3D human motion synthesis, significant progress has been made using various machine learning architectures, including recurrent neural networks (RNNs), conditional variational autoencoders (cVAEs), Transformers, and diffusion models. Constrained pose generation, particularly for hand-object manipulation, has also been explored. Efforts have been made in full-body scene-aware motion synthesis, addressing global trajectory and local pose prediction, as seen in works like COUCH and GOAL. However, a common limitation across many of these efforts is the reliance on either simplified environmental contexts or data acquired through methods that do not provide rich, ground-truth 3D scene information synchronized with human motion in diverse, cluttered settings. The present work aims to address these limitations by introducing a novel data acquisition system and a corresponding dataset that captures human motion within highly contextual virtual environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the collection of high-quality, high-volume, and diverse 3D human motion data within rich, contextual, and ecological environments. This objective is motivated by the recognized need for such datasets to advance machine learning models in applications like Embodied AI, Computer Animation, Robotics, and AR/VR, where realistic human-environment interactions are crucial.

The specific motivations and objectives include:

- **Addressing limitations of traditional motion capture:** Conventional optics-based motion capture systems are not well-suited for simultaneously capturing intricate human movements and complex environmental scenes. The requirement for physical construction of production sets and props in a capture studio is costly, time-consuming, and prone to issues such as occlusion, which can compromise data quality. The research aims to overcome these limitations by proposing an alternative acquisition paradigm.
- **Facilitating contextual data collection:** The goal is to develop a system where actors can perceive and operate within a highly contextual virtual world while their motion is captured in an empty real-world space. This approach is intended to enable rapid collection of human motion in diverse and complex virtual scenes without the need for physical scene construction or concern for occlusion.
- **Creating a novel contextual motion dataset:** A key objective is to generate a new dataset, named CIRCLE, that provides extensive hours of full-body human motion in various realistic indoor scenes. This dataset is designed to be paired with ego-centric environmental information, such as RGBD videos, offering a comprehensive view of the actor's perceived surroundings. The motivation for this is to provide a critical resource for training generative models that require context-aware human motion.
- **Enabling scene-conditioned motion generation:** The research aims to demonstrate the utility of the collected CIRCLE dataset by training a data-driven model capable of generating human motion conditioned on scene information. This model is expected to learn how to produce movements that are appropriate and functional within complex 3D scenes, specifically focusing on non-trivial reaching tasks. The motivation here is to move beyond mere kinematic reproduction to understanding the "why" behind movements in specific contexts.
- **Ensuring data quality, volume, and diversity:** The system design is driven by principles to maximize the efficiency of data collection to achieve high volume, maintain quality by using an untethered VR experience with visual feedback, and support diversity across subjects, scenes, and tasks.

In summary, the core motivation is to bridge the gap between high-quality human motion capture and rich environmental context, which is a roadblock to developing advanced generative human motion models capable of simulating realistic and intelligent human-like behavior in complex scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on a novel motion acquisition system designed to capture human motion within virtual environments, followed by the creation of a dataset (CIRCLE) and the development of a scene-conditioned motion generation model.

#### 3.1. Motion Acquisition System

The system integrates real-world motion capture with a virtual reality (VR) simulation. It consists of two main components:
- **Motion Capture System:** A Vicon optical motion capture system with 12 cameras operating at 120 FPS is used to record the actor's movements in an empty physical space. Vicon Shogun software controls the cameras and processing.
- **Virtual Reality App:** A Meta Quest 2 headset runs a WebXR application built on top of the AI Habitat simulator. AI Habitat provides access to diverse, interaction-ready virtual environments and assets. The VR app allows the actor to perceive and interact with the virtual scene.

**System Integration and Feedback:**
- A local webserver connects Vicon Shogun and the VR app, enabling the app to control mocap recording start/end times.
- Vicon's DataStream SDK streams the reconstructed skeleton to the VR app with low latency, allowing the actor to see their virtual body and receive visual feedback (e.g., collisions), promoting natural performance.

**Design Goals:**
- **High Volume:** The VR app is optimized for performance to minimize motion sickness and streamline data collection, maximizing efficiency during active VR time.
- **High Quality:** The system uses optics-based mocap for detailed kinematic data. The immersive VR environment with real-time skeletal feedback aims to maintain natural human behavior.
- **High Diversity:** Tools are developed for planning and preparing diverse motion sequences across various subjects, scenes, and tasks, leveraging Habitat's wide range of virtual worlds.

#### 3.2. Data Collection Process

The collection process is divided into three phases:

- **Preparation:**
 - Manual annotation: Users define "start" and "goal" regions within Blender for each virtual scene.
 - Task generation: Pairs of points are sampled from these regions to create specific "sequence specifications" (tasks). Unreachable goal positions are rejected.
 - Sequence sorting: Generated sequences are sorted to minimize scene resetting and asset import overhead during collection.
- **Collection:**
 - Calibration: Daily calibration aligns the VR headset's frame of reference with the Vicon skeleton using an alignment heuristic involving the head bone's forward vector and the midpoint of the eyes.
 - Recording: The user performs reaching tasks in VR. Both "reaching clips" (target-oriented movements) and "transition clips" (movements between tasks) are recorded and labeled.
 - Data logging: Collected data (headset trajectories, simulation state) are logged to a Mephisto server.
- **Post-processing:**
 - Mocap data processing: Captured clips are processed using Shogun Post and exported to BVH and C3D formats.
 - Offline synchronization: Headset and mocap data are synchronized by maximizing the convolution between their head bone/headset velocity profiles to account for latency. Sequences are then trimmed and interpolated.
 - Human mesh fitting: MoSh++ is applied to C3D files to obtain SMPL-X body model parameters for each frame.
 - Synthetic sensor information: Ego-centric RGB-D videos are extracted from Habitat and rendered with Blender using SMPL-X meshes.
 - Quality assurance: Automated tools check for task completion (wrist within 1 cm of goal), marker swaps, and large jumps in joint values (linear acceleration, angular velocity) to identify and discard problematic sequences.

#### 3.3. CIRCLE Dataset

The CIRCLE dataset was generated using this system.
- **Content:** It contains 10 hours (over 7000 sequences) of right and left hand reaching motion.
- **Subjects & Scenes:** Data from 5 subjects across 9 distinct indoor household scenes (e.g., bathroom, bedroom, kitchen).
- **Diversity:** The scenes and tasks involve a range of motions, including reaching high places, bending, crawling, crouching, kneeling, and lying down.
- **Physics Simulation:** Physics simulation was disabled during collection, meaning environments are static.
- **Output Data per Sequence:** SMPL-X parameters, synchronized VR headset trajectory, ego-centric RGB-D video (Habitat and Blender), and task-specific data (initial/goal conditions, scene information).

#### 3.4. Motion Generation Model

The research proposes a Transformer-based model to generate scene-aware human poses.
- **Goal:** Generate a sequence of human poses (root translation, global joint positions, local joint rotations based on SMPL-X) given a start pose, a target wrist joint position, and a 3D scene point cloud.
- **Approach:** A motion refinement model that takes a coarse initial motion and scene features to output a refined, scene-aware sequence.
- **Motion Initialization:** A naive initial motion sequence is created by translating a constant base pose such that its wrist reaches the target, and then linearly interpolating the root translation between the start pose and this goal pose.
- **Scene Encoding:** Two methods are explored to represent the scene information at each time step:
 - **Basis Point Set (BPS):** Calculates the Euclidean distance between downsampled human mesh vertices and their nearest neighbors in the scene point cloud (human-scene interaction features). It also calculates distances between points sampled from a cylinder around the human and their nearest scene points (geometry features). These are concatenated and processed by an MLP.
 - **PointNet++:** A pre-trained PointNet++ model is used for semantic segmentation on the scene point cloud. Features for human mesh vertices (interaction) and nearest points of a cylindrical volume around the human (geometry) are extracted using inverse distance weighted interpolation, then averaged and concatenated.
- **Model Architecture:** A Transformer-based architecture with four self-attention blocks is used to process the concatenated initialized poses and scene features.
- **Training Loss:** The training loss consists of L1 terms for root translation, global joint positions, local joint rotation, and joint positions computed via forward kinematics. Scene information is not directly used for supervision in the loss function to enable a comparative evaluation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation assesses the generated motions using standard metrics, comparing the proposed model against two baselines across two dataset splits.

#### 4.1. Evaluation Setup

- **Data Downsampling:** All sequences were downsampled to 20 FPS, with a maximum length of 240 frames.
- **Training:** Models were trained for 1800 epochs using AdamW optimizer.
- **Dataset Splits:**
 - **Random:** 2565 training sequences, 453 testing sequences, randomly split.
 - **Task:** Specific start/goal region pairs (tasks) were held out for testing, resulting in 2578 training sequences and 440 testing sequences (109 training tasks, 19 testing tasks).
- **Baselines:**
 - **GOAL:** An MLP architecture from previous work (GOAL), modified to condition on the environment's BPS representation, autoregressively predicts the next pose. Ground truth goal pose is provided to strengthen this baseline.
 - **NO-SCENE:** The authors' proposed Transformer architecture, but without the scene encoding inputs (F0...FT). This baseline serves to isolate the impact of scene conditioning.

#### 4.2. Evaluation Metrics

- **Task Completion:** Success rate determined by whether the generated right-hand wrist position is within 10 cm of the ground truth goal position at the last frame.
- **Collision Avoidance:** Sum of interpenetration depths between the human mesh and the scene, filtering out collisions with depth less than 2 cm (to account for MoSh fitting inaccuracies).
- **Foot Sliding:** Percentage of frames where the velocity of the lowest vertex exceeds 1 cm per frame.
- **Similarity to Dataset:** Mean Vertex Position Error (MVPE), Mean Joint Position Error (MJPE), Mean Root Translation Error, and Mean Joint Orientation Error (Frobenius norm of rotation matrix).

#### 4.3. Results

The results are presented for both the random and task-based splits:

**Random Split (Table 2):**
- **Task Completion:**
 - The proposed models (Ours BPS: 74.12%, Ours PN2: 79.20%) achieved significantly higher success rates compared to GOAL (12.14%).
 - Our models also showed a moderate improvement over the NO-SCENE baseline (74.56%).
 - Distance to goal for our models was around 8 cm, comparable to NO-SCENE, while GOAL had a much higher error (44.48 cm).
- **Collision Avoidance:**
 - Our models (Ours BPS: 12.09 cm, Ours PN2: 11.63 cm) exhibited lower cumulative collision depths compared to NO-SCENE (15.87 cm) and GOAL (20.89 cm). This suggests that scene conditioning contributes to collision avoidance even without explicit collision supervision in the loss function.
- **Foot Sliding:**
 - GOAL demonstrated the lowest foot sliding (12.78%), but this was attributed to its tendency to produce nearly static poses, as indicated by visual inspection.
 - Our models (Ours BPS: 17.76%, Ours PN2: 18.83%) and NO-SCENE (18.89%) had higher foot sliding, potentially due to greater overall motion adaptation to scene constraints.
- **Similarity to Dataset:**
 - Our models (e.g., MVPE BPS: 10.45 cm, PN2: 11.09 cm) performed similarly to NO-SCENE (MVPE: 11.36 cm).
 - GOAL demonstrated substantially higher errors across all similarity metrics, indicating a lower quality of generated motion compared to ground truth.

**Task Split (Unseen Tasks) (Table 3):**
- **Generalization:** Overall performance degraded for all models on this split, as expected, but the relative performance trends remained consistent.
- **Task Completion:** Our models (Ours BPS: 76.08%, Ours PN2: 70.84%) maintained a significant advantage in task completion over GOAL (8.43%).
- **Collision Avoidance:** The BPS-conditioned model (7.04 cm) continued to outperform NO-SCENE (10.18 cm) in reducing collision depth, while the PN2 model (9.22 cm) also performed well.
- **Other Metrics:** Foot sliding remained higher for our models compared to GOAL (due to GOAL's static poses), and similarity metrics were comparable to NO-SCENE and superior to GOAL.

**Qualitative Observations (Figures 7 and 8):**
- Visualizations confirmed that GOAL often produced minimal character movement, failing to achieve the objective, despite low foot sliding.
- The inclusion of scene information in our model visibly reduced collisions compared to the NO-SCENE baseline.

The main findings indicate that the proposed system effectively captures contextual motion data, leading to the creation of the CIRCLE dataset. Models trained on CIRCLE, particularly with scene encoding, demonstrate improved performance in task completion and collision avoidance compared to baselines, suggesting the value of rich contextual information for generating scene-aware human motion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "CIRCLE: Capture In Rich Contextual Environments" carries significance in several areas of research and application.

**Addressing a Data Gap:** The primary significance lies in introducing a novel method for acquiring contextual motion data and the subsequent creation of CIRCLE, a new dataset. The lack of high-quality, high-volume, and diverse 3D human motion datasets that explicitly incorporate rich environmental context has been identified as a critical impediment to developing advanced generative human motion models. This research directly addresses this gap by providing both a system and a dataset that integrate detailed human motion with precise 3D scene information.

**Advancements in Motion Capture Technology:** The proposed motion acquisition system represents a methodological innovation. By capturing human motion in a physical mocap studio while the actor interacts with a highly contextual virtual environment, the system circumvents several limitations of traditional optical motion capture. These include the high cost and logistical complexity of physically staging intricate scenes, the practical difficulties of placing specific props, and the problem of occlusions in cluttered physical environments. The virtual setup offers advantages in terms of cost-effectiveness, ready availability of virtual world state, and inherently occlusion-free data capture in the real space.

**Fueling Generative AI for Human Motion:** The CIRCLE dataset, with its 10 hours of full-body reaching motion across diverse indoor scenes, paired with synchronized ego-centric environmental information (RGBD videos, SMPL-X parameters, VR headset trajectories), provides a valuable resource for training machine learning models. The demonstration of a Transformer-based model successfully generating scene-aware human motion for reaching tasks highlights the dataset's utility. This can lead to more robust and realistic generative models capable of producing human movements that are not only kinematically plausible but also contextually appropriate and functional within complex environments.

**Broad Impact on Embodied AI and Related Fields:** The ability to synthesize 3D human motion that reflects nuanced interactions with complex environments has potential to impact several fields:
- **Embodied AI:** Training intelligent agents that can navigate and interact with virtual worlds in a human-like manner. The dataset can inform models to understand the "why" behind human movements in context, crucial for developing agents that perform realistic tasks.
- **Computer Animation:** Generating more natural and believable character behaviors, particularly in cluttered or constrained virtual settings, reducing the need for manual animation.
- **Robotics:** Developing robots capable of safer and more efficient human-robot collaboration, particularly in shared or cluttered workspaces, by better understanding human motion and intent in context.
- **AR/VR:** Creating more immersive and interactive experiences where virtual characters or agents exhibit highly realistic and context-sensitive behaviors.

**Foundation for Future Research:** The work establishes a foundation for continued research. The authors identify future directions such as further improving motion generation quality, comparing human motion in virtual versus real environments, exploring generalization with even larger datasets, and expanding the system to capture interactive contextual tasks involving both human and object trajectories. This last point is particularly significant for advancing embodied AI by enabling the study of human-object interaction in everyday life.

In conclusion, this research provides a practical solution to a longstanding problem in contextual motion data acquisition, offers a valuable new dataset, and demonstrates its immediate application in training scene-aware motion generation models, thereby contributing to the advancement of multiple domains that rely on realistic human-environment interaction.
