# SKILL0: In-Context Agentic Reinforcement Learning for Skill Internalization

> [!summary] Summary
> The SKILL0 framework introduces In-Context Reinforcement Learning (ICRL) to enable Large Language Model (LLM) agents to internalize skills into their parameters, thereby achieving autonomous behavior without external skill descriptions at inference time. This approach yielded superior performance, such as an 87.9% success rate on ALFWorld (+9.7% over AgentOCR), while substantially reducing context token costs by over 5 times compared to skill-augmented methods.

## Problem

- Existing inference-time skill augmentation methods suffer from retrieval noise, where irrelevant or misleading guidance can corrupt the agent's contextual understanding.
- Injected skill content creates high token overhead in the context window, leading to increased computational costs and limiting scalability in multi-turn interactions.
- Current LLM agents merely follow explicit skill descriptions, remaining perpetually dependent on external guidance rather than truly internalizing knowledge into their model parameters.

## Method

- **SKILL0** employs an ==In-Context Reinforcement Learning== (ICRL) framework where skills are provided as in-context guidance during training, but are entirely removed during inference to promote internalization.
- An ==Adaptive Curriculum Learning== mechanism progressively reduces the active skill budget and dynamically selects beneficial skills based on a ==helpfulness metric==, compelling agents to internalize knowledge over time.
- The system utilizes ==visual context modeling== to map textual interaction history and skills into compact RGB images, significantly reducing token overhead, alongside a composite reward incentivizing both task success and context compression.

## Results

- **SKILL0** achieved superior performance, with a 3B model reaching an **87.9%** success rate on ALFWorld (+**9.7%** over AgentOCR) and **40.8%** on Search-QA (+**6.6%**), demonstrating its ability to internalize skills effectively.
- The framework significantly reduced context token costs during inference, consuming over **5x** fewer tokens per step compared to text-based or skill-augmented methods like SkillRL.
- Validation accuracy tracked during training provided empirical evidence of skill internalization, as models evaluated without skill prompts eventually surpassed skill-augmented variants by the end of optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Skill internalization is a viable and advantageous alternative to inference-time skill augmentation, allowing LLM agents to transition from context-dependent execution to truly autonomous, memory-based behavior.
> - A dynamically decaying skill budget, combined with filtering and ranking skills by their 'helpfulness,' is crucial for successful internalization, preventing agents from over-relying on external prompts.
> - Providing skills as transient scaffolding during training, and then gradually withdrawing them, effectively guides the agent to consolidate knowledge within its parameters, leading to superior skill-free inference performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in complex real-world tasks such as code generation, GUI automation, gameplay, and embodied control. A common approach to augmenting these LLMs for specialized tasks involves structured agent skills. These skills are typically organized into libraries and retrieved at inference time, then injected into the LLM's context as natural language guidance. This paradigm, referred to as "inference-time skill augmentation," has become a prevalent mechanism for extending agent capabilities.

Despite its effectiveness, this established approach has inherent limitations. Current methods face challenges with "retrieval noise," where irrelevant or misleading information can corrupt the agent's context. The injection of skill content also contributes to substantial "token overhead," which can escalate in multi-turn interactions and limit scalability. Fundamentally, these methods mean the LLM is merely executing skills provided in its prompt rather than acquiring or internalizing the knowledge. The competence resides in the external context rather than within the model's parameters.

This observation highlights a gap in the existing research: while significant effort has been directed towards skill extraction, organization, and retrieval, the question of whether skills can be internalized into the model's parameters has remained largely unexplored. Analogous to human skill acquisition, where explicit instruction eventually gives way to autonomous, memory-based execution, there is a recognized need for LLM agents to transition from context-dependent execution to intrinsic competence. Reinforcement Learning (RL) presents a potential avenue for this transition, as it can drive agents to consolidate effective strategies. However, a naive application of RL is insufficient; without initial skill context, complex multi-step behaviors are difficult to learn, and with persistent full skill context, the model remains perpetually dependent on external knowledge. The research aims to address this gap by developing a training paradigm that systematically transfers competence from external context to internal model parameters.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable the internalization of agent skills directly into the parameters of Large Language Models, thereby achieving zero-shot autonomous behavior that does not require runtime skill retrieval or augmentation. This objective is encapsulated by the motto "Skills at training, zero at inference."

The motivation for this objective stems from several limitations identified in the prevailing "inference-time skill augmentation" paradigm:

- **Retrieval Noise:** When relevant skills are retrieved from a skill bank and injected into the model's context, there is a risk of retrieving irrelevant or misleading information. This "retrieval noise" can corrupt the agent's context, leading to suboptimal decision-making or errors.
- **Token Overhead:** Injected skill content adds to the total token count within the LLM's context window. This "token overhead" accumulates over multi-turn interactions, significantly increasing computational costs and potentially limiting the model's ability to process longer, more complex sequences. Scaling such systems becomes challenging due to the growing context window demands.
- **Lack of True Skill Acquisition:** A core limitation is that models operating under inference-time skill augmentation are merely following explicit instructions. The knowledge and competence reside in the external skill descriptions provided in the prompt, not within the model's internal parameters. The model does not genuinely "learn" or "acquire" the skill; it only executes it when prompted. This prevents the development of truly autonomous and self-sufficient agents.

Drawing an analogy to human learning, the authors highlight that skill acquisition progresses from an explicit instruction phase to an internalized phase where behavior is executed autonomously from memory. Current LLM agent paradigms, by continuously relying on external skill descriptions, remain anchored in the "explicit instruction" stage. The research is motivated by the desire to move LLM agents beyond this stage, enabling them to consolidate effective strategies as intrinsic policy rather than external knowledge.

While reinforcement learning (RL) offers a natural pathway for skill consolidation, a direct application presents its own challenges:
- Without any skill context, an agent might lack the structured guidance necessary to learn complex, multi-step behaviors from scratch.
- Conversely, if full skill context is provided throughout RL training, the model may remain dependent on external knowledge and fail to internalize the skills.

Therefore, a specific training regime is required that strategically begins with skill guidance and progressively withdraws it, facilitating the systematic transfer of competence from the external context to the internal parameters of the LLM. SKILL0 aims to provide this framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors introduce SKILL0, an In-Context Reinforcement Learning (ICRL) framework designed to internalize agent skills. This framework combines structured skill guidance during training with a dynamic curriculum that progressively removes this guidance, ultimately enabling zero-shot autonomous behavior at inference time.

The methodology can be broken down into three main components:

#### 3.1. Agent Loop

The agent automation is formulated as a sequential decision-making problem. Given a task instruction `I`, the agent generates a sequence of actions `{a_1..., a_T}`. At each step `t`, the environment `E` provides a textual observation `o_t`, and the agent samples an action `a_t` from its policy `π_θ(a_t | I, h_t)`, where `h_t` is the interaction history.

- **Skill Management:** SKILL0 utilizes a hierarchical `SkillBank`, which contains both general skills (universal strategic principles) and task-specific skills (specialized knowledge for particular task categories). Skills are organized into Markdown files based on task and skill categories (e.g., `skills/search/entity_attribute_lookup.md`). During training, instead of retrieving individual skills via semantic similarity, a subset `S` of `m` skill *files* is selected based on an "on-policy helpfulness" criterion, which evaluates the learning utility of each skill file `S_k` for the current policy.

- **Context Rendering:** To address token cost challenges, a context rendering mechanism is employed. This mechanism maps the textual interaction context (history `h_t` and selected skills `S`) into a compact RGB image. A vision encoder `Enc` compresses this rendered image into visual representations `V_t ∈ R^d`. This `V_t` serves as the compressed visual context embedding for the policy, aiming to reduce token overhead while retaining structural information. A notable feature is that the policy itself `π_θ` is trained to self-generate the compression ratio `c_t ∈ (0, 1]` alongside the task action `a_t` at each step.

#### 3.2. In-Context Reinforcement Learning (ICRL)

SKILL0's ICRL component combines the benefits of skill prompting (sample efficiency, inductive bias) with RL's exploration capabilities. The progressive internalization of skills into the model's parameters eliminates the need for explicit skill retrieval during inference.

- **Composite Reward:** To encourage both efficient context compression and skill internalization, a composite reward `˜r_t` is introduced. It combines a binary task success indicator `I_succ(τ)` (reward `ln(c_t)` if successful, `0` otherwise) with a compression efficiency term `ln(c_t)`. The logarithmic form of `ln(c_t)` reflects diminishing returns for higher compression. The composite reward is defined as `˜r_t = r_t + λ ⋅ r_comp_t`, where `r_t` evaluates task correctness with skill enhancements and `λ ≥ 0` controls the trade-off.

- **Training Objective:** For each query `q`, the policy `π_θ_old` samples `G` trajectories. The training objective `L_SKILL0(θ)` is a PPO-like formulation, involving an advantage `A_i` (computed by normalizing total rewards `˜r(τ_i)` within the sampled group) and an importance sampling ratio `r_i,t(θ)`. A KL-divergence regularization term `β ⋅ D_KL[π_θ || π_ref]` is also included to prevent the policy from drifting too far from a reference model `π_ref`.

#### 3.3. Adaptive Curriculum Learning

The core mechanism for skill internalization is an adaptive curriculum that gradually anneals the agent's reliance on external skills, ensuring a smooth transition to a skill-free inference state.

- **Linear Decay of Skill Budget:** The curriculum operates across `N_S` progressive stages, with the maximum allowed number of active skill files `M^(s)` linearly decaying towards zero. This linear decay limits the step-wise reduction of available skills, constraining the change in the active skill set `S^(s)` and the rendered visual context `V^(s)_t`. This design aims to keep the policy's distribution shift `π_θ(a_t, c_t | I, V^(s)_t)` smooth and stable.

- **Relevance-Driven Skill Grouping (Offline Phase):** Prior to training, the validation dataset is partitioned into `N` sub-tasks `{T_k}`. Each `T_k` groups validation instances whose skill requirements align with a specific skill file `S_k`. This offline grouping provides a dedicated sub-task for evaluating the utility of each `S_k`.

- **Helpfulness-Driven Dynamic Curriculum (Online Phase):** During training, at regular intervals (every `d` steps), the curriculum performs the following:
 1. **Helpfulness Evaluation:** For each active skill file `S_k`, a helpfulness metric `∆_k` is quantified. This is calculated as the difference in accuracy (`Acc`) of the current policy `π_θ` on its associated validation sub-task `T_k`, evaluated under two conditions: with `S_k` provided (`Acc_w/skill_k`) and without `S_k` (`Acc_w/o skill_k`).
 2. **Filter & Rank:** Only skill files `S_k` for which `∆_k > 0` (i.e., those from which the policy still benefits) are retained in the active skill pool. These retained skills are then sorted in descending order based on their `∆_k` scores.
 3. **Select:** From the filtered and ranked pool, the top `m` skill files (where `m ≤ M^(s)`) are selected to form the active skill subset `S` for the current training stage. If `M^(s)` reaches zero, `S` becomes empty.

This dynamic process progressively prunes less helpful skills, compelling the policy to internalize knowledge into its parameters, moving towards a fully self-reliant state where no skill context is provided at inference.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using SKILL0 demonstrated its performance across ALFWorld, Search-QA, and WebShop benchmarks. The key findings are summarized below:

#### 4.1. Method Performance

- **Superiority over Baselines:** SKILL0 consistently achieved substantial improvements over various RL-based baselines. For the 3B Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct model, SKILL0 achieved an average success rate of 87.9% on ALFWorld, 40.8% on Search-QA, and a score of 78.6% with 66.4% accuracy on WebShop. This represents an improvement over AgentOCR by +9.7% for ALFWorld, +6.6% for Search-QA, and +10.1% (accuracy) for WebShop.
- **Performance with 7B Models:** Similar trends were observed with 7B models, where SKILL0 achieved 89.8% on ALFWorld, 44.4% on Search-QA, and 85.1% score with 74.2% accuracy on WebShop, surpassing other RL methods like EvolveR, AgentOCR, and GRPO. Notably, on WebShop, SKILL0 (7B) improved over AgentOCR by +6.5 in score and +14.9 in accuracy, indicating robust internalization of complex behaviors.
- **Competitive with Skill-Augmented Methods:** SKILL0, despite operating without external skill prompting during inference, achieved performance competitive with or superior to skill-augmented methods such as SkillRL.
- **Outperformance of Memory-Augmented and Closed-Source Models:** On ALFWorld, SKILL0 (89.8%) largely outperformed memory-augmented learning methods like ExpeL (46.3%), Mem0 (54.7%), and MemRL (21.4%), as well as closed-source models such as GPT-4o (48.0%) and Gemini-2.5-Pro (60.3%).
- **Strong Generalization on Search-QA:** For Search-QA, SKILL0 (44.4%) surpassed retrieval-augmented and search-based methods like Search-R1 (38.5%) and ZeroSearch (39.1%). It showed particularly strong performance on out-of-domain multi-hop datasets, such as Bamboogle (66.9%), indicating robust generalization.

#### 4.2. Token Efficiency

- **Significantly Reduced Token Cost:** A key finding is SKILL0's ability to achieve high performance with a substantially lower average context token cost per step due to visual context modeling and skill internalization. For 3B models, the cost was 0.38k tokens per step on ALFWorld, 0.18k on Search-QA, and 0.49k on WebShop.
- **Comparison to Baselines:** This represents a significant reduction compared to text-based or skill-augmented methods. For instance, SkillRL incurred 2.21k and 0.87k tokens per step on ALFWorld and Search-QA, respectively, which is more than 5 times higher than SKILL0. On WebShop, skill-augmented Few-Shot inference required 0.78k–0.91k tokens, while skill-free SKILL0 achieved superior accuracy at only 0.46k–0.49k tokens, demonstrating a nearly 2x reduction in context cost for over 3x the accuracy.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics

- **Reward Curves:** SKILL0 consistently maintained higher reward curves throughout RL optimization compared to the AgentOCR baseline for both 3B and 7B model backbones.
- **Validation Accuracy Trends:** When validated with skill augmentation, the model showed faster early-stage performance improvement. However, when validated *without* skill prompts, the initial performance was lower but gradually caught up towards the end of optimization, indicating a clear trend of skill internalization. SKILL0 consistently outperformed AgentOCR and text-based RL baselines (GRPO, SkillRL) in skill-free inference settings, demonstrating higher performance ceilings and steady improvement throughout optimization.
- **Helpfulness Metric (∆_k):** The helpfulness of skills (`∆_k`) exhibited a consistent "rise-then-fall" pattern across all sub-tasks. It was initially low, increased as the policy learned to leverage skill prompts, and then converged back towards zero in later stages as the dynamic curriculum compelled the policy to internalize the knowledge. This trajectory supports the concept of skills serving as effective, yet transient, scaffolding during policy optimization.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Skill Budget M:** Experiments confirmed the effectiveness of the dynamic linear decay of the skill budget (e.g., `` for ALFWorld). This approach led to superior skill internalization and even a positive transfer (`+1.6%`) when skill prompts were removed at inference. In contrast, static full budgets (``) or constantly low budgets (``) either led to performance collapse or limited early exploration, respectively. The curriculum strategy outperformed a "Fixed Full" setting in skill-free inference, supporting that it mitigates the training-inference gap and over-reliance on external skills.
- **Dynamic Curriculum Strategy (Filter & Rank & Select):** The three-step helpfulness-driven strategy was validated as essential. It achieved the highest performance (87.9% w/o S) and was the only setting to show positive transfer (+1.6%) when skills were removed. Removing the filtering step ("w/o Filter") led to performance degradation due to context noise, while random skill selection ("w/o Rank") caused a severe performance collapse, underscoring the importance of retaining only demonstrably helpful skills.
- **Validation Interval d:** An interval `d=10` was selected as an optimal trade-off, balancing high task performance with computational efficiency, providing marginal gains over a smaller interval (`d=5`) with significantly less overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SKILL0 represents a methodological advancement in the development of LLM agents, shifting the paradigm from inference-time skill augmentation to skill internalization. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Foundation for Autonomous LLM Agents:** By demonstrating that complex agent skills can be internalized into model parameters, SKILL0 establishes a principled alternative to relying on external knowledge at inference time. This moves LLM agents closer to truly autonomous behavior, where competence is intrinsic to the model rather than being dependent on dynamically retrieved information. This concept of "self-sufficient intelligence" has implications for agent robustness and independence.
- **Enhanced Efficiency and Scalability:** The framework significantly reduces the "token overhead" associated with skill augmentation. By moving to a skill-free inference mode and utilizing visual context compression, SKILL0 achieves substantial reductions in average context token cost per step (e.g., >5x reduction compared to some baselines). This efficiency is critical for scaling LLM agents to more complex, long-horizon tasks and for reducing the computational resources and costs associated with their deployment.
- **Improved Agent Performance:** Beyond efficiency, the research demonstrates that skill internalization can lead to superior task performance compared to both zero-shot baselines and, in many cases, even skill-augmented methods. This suggests that internally acquired knowledge can be more effectively utilized by the agent than externally provided guidance, potentially leading to more robust and generalized capabilities.
- **Adaptive Learning and Generalization:** The Dynamic Curriculum mechanism, which adaptively withdraws skills as the policy learns to internalize them, contributes to a more stable and effective training process. The ability of SKILL0 to achieve strong performance on out-of-domain tasks, particularly in Search-QA, suggests improved generalization capabilities derived from internalized reasoning and tool-use behaviors.
- **Addressing Fundamental Limitations:** The work directly addresses the fundamental limitations of retrieval noise, token overhead, and the lack of true knowledge acquisition inherent in previous skill augmentation approaches. By internalizing skills, the reliance on potentially noisy retrieval systems is eliminated, context windows are freed up, and the LLM itself becomes more capable.
- **Future Research Directions:** SKILL0 opens avenues for further research into curriculum learning for LLMs, optimal strategies for skill representation, and the mechanisms by which LLMs consolidate and leverage diverse forms of knowledge. It may also inspire work on how agents can learn and evolve their own skill sets over time without human intervention.

**Limitations:** The paper acknowledges that SKILL0's performance relies on the quality of the initial `SkillBank`. Furthermore, the offline relevance-driven skill grouping mechanism requires re-partitioning when the framework is applied to entirely new task domains, which could entail manual effort or new automated grouping methods. These aspects suggest areas for future development to enhance the framework's adaptability.
