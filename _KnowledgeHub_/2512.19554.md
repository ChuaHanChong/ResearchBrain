# CARE What Fails: Contrastive Anchored-REflection for Verifiable Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed CARE (Contrastive Anchored REflection), a post-training framework that enhances large multimodal models' verifiable reasoning by transforming informative errors into constructive learning signals. The system improved macro-averaged accuracy by 4.62 points over GRPO on six benchmarks, demonstrating increased training stability and efficient use of failure data.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for MLLMs often underutilize failed rollouts, leading to stalled gradients or inefficient credit assignment.
- Limited rollout budgets in RLVR exacerbate issues like high gradient variance and training instability, potentially reinforcing spurious reasoning paths.
- Current MLLMs struggle with complex visual-grounded compositional reasoning tasks, indicating that outcome-only supervision is insufficient for long-term reasoning processes.

## Method

- **CARE** integrates an ==Anchored-Contrastive== Objective to extract stable learning signals by contrasting the best rollout (anchor) with semantically proximate hard negatives, preventing vanishing updates in all-negative batches.
- It employs Reflection-Guided Resampling (RGR), a training-only self-repair mechanism that actively converts near-miss errors into successful examples by inserting repair cues and re-scoring.
- A Token-Weighted Policy Objective focuses learning on answer tokens and selectively on positive rationale tokens, combined with negative-penalty scaling and an "all-negative rescue" for stability.

## Results

- CARE improved macro-averaged accuracy across six benchmarks by 4.62 points (**50.57%** vs. **45.95%**) compared to the GRPO baseline using a Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** backbone.
- The Anchored-Contrastive objective accounted for the majority of performance gains (**84.4%** on Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**), with Reflection-Guided Resampling providing consistent additional boosts.
- Ablation studies confirmed that components like negative-penalty scaling, all-negative rescue, and cosine-similarity-based negative selection significantly enhanced training stability, learning speed, and convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly leveraging informative failures, especially "close-but-wrong" negatives, provides more constructive learning signals than solely focusing on successful rollouts.
> - Within-group normalization and negative-penalty scaling are crucial for stabilizing gradients and preventing over-penalization in RLVR, particularly with small rollout budgets.
> - A structured, one-shot self-reflection mechanism during training can proactively convert near-misses into positive learning experiences without requiring reflection at inference time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Large Language Models (LLMs) has advanced text understanding, generation, and reasoning capabilities. This progress has extended to Large Multimodal Models (MLLMs), which integrate vision and language. Early MLLMs, such as GPT-4V and LLaVA, have demonstrated capabilities in tasks like captioning and conversational visual-question answering (VQA). However, challenges persist in more complex visual-grounded compositional reasoning tasks found in domains such as mathematics, science, and engineering. Benchmarks like MathVista, MathVerse, MATH-Vision, and MMMU continue to identify performance gaps in these areas, indicating that outcome-only supervision provides insufficient guidance for long-term reasoning processes.

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a method to address these limitations. RLVR fine-tunes models using programmatic verifiers, such as answer checkers, to provide deterministic and scalable rewards, which has been applied successfully in fields like mathematics and coding. Group-based RLVR approaches, like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), enhance the practicality of RLVR by comparing multiple rollouts for each query. Despite these advances, existing RLVR methods face issues, particularly when rollout budgets are limited. These issues include high gradient variance, training instability, and inaccurate credit assignment, which can inadvertently reinforce spurious reasoning chains even when a correct answer is produced. This work positions itself within the RLVR landscape by proposing a failure-centric approach designed to mitigate these identified challenges and enhance the reliability and efficiency of multimodal reasoning in MLLMs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from observed inefficiencies in existing Group-relative Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) frameworks. The authors note that current RLVR methods often underutilize valuable information contained in rollouts that result in failure. Specifically, two main issues are highlighted:
1. **Stalled Gradients and Zero-Signal Batches**: When all sampled rollouts for a given query are incorrect, the learning gradient can become very small or effectively zero, leading to stalled training.
2. **Inefficient Credit Assignment**: Even when one rollout is correct, the learning update frequently overlooks why other rollouts were "close-but-wrong." This can lead to misattribution of credit, potentially reinforcing irrelevant or spurious reasoning steps. The problem is exacerbated under small rollout budgets, contributing to high gradient variance and training instability.

To address these challenges, the main objective of this work is to introduce CARE (Contrastive Anchored REflection), a post-training framework explicitly designed to transform informative errors into constructive learning signals for multimodal reasoning. The framework aims to improve the accuracy and training smoothness of MLLMs while increasing the proportion of learning signal derived from failures.

The key objectives are achieved through two complementary components:

- **Anchored-Contrastive Objective**: To establish a stable and informative learning signal, this objective aims to:
 - Form a compact subgroup around the highest-performing (anchor) rollout and a selection of semantically proximate hard negatives.
 - Perform within-subgroup z-score normalization of rewards, coupled with negative-only scaling, to create a stable, scale-aware contrast.
 - Incorporate an "all-negative rescue" mechanism to prevent vanishing updates when all rollouts in a batch are incorrect. This objective is intended to teach the policy to distinguish correct solutions from plausible failures and assign credit in a manner that accounts for common failure modes.
- **Reflection-Guided Resampling (RGR)**: To actively convert near-miss errors into usable positives, this component aims to:
 - Implement a one-shot, structured self-repair mechanism during training.
 - Rewrite a representative failing rollout by inserting a brief repair cue.
 - Re-score the repaired rollout with the same verifier, allowing near-misses to become successful examples without requiring reflection at test time. This component is designed to proactively leverage mistakes for improvement rather than passively ignoring them.

Through these objectives, CARE seeks to establish a more stable and effective training pathway for robust multimodal reasoners.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CARE (Contrastive Anchored REflection) is a verifier-driven post-training framework that integrates an anchored-contrastive objective with a training-only reflection step. The framework fine-tunes a policy `πθ` that generates rationales and answers for multimodal inputs `x = ⟨I, q⟩` (images and text query). A programmatic verifier `V` provides binary accuracy (`acc`) and format compliance (`fmt`) signals, combined into a reward `r`. For each prompt, `G` rollouts `Y = {y1..., yG}` are sampled.

#### 3.1. Anchored-Contrastive Subgroup

This core objective aims to extract a robust learning signal by contrasting the best rollout with closely related failures.
- **Anchor Selection**: If at least one rollout is verifier-correct (`P ≠ ∅`), the anchor `y+` is chosen as the successful rollout with the *shortest rationale* (`<think>` span), with ties broken by the shortest answer (`<answer>` span). This prioritizes concise and correct reasoning. If no rollouts are correct, anchoring is bypassed, and the "all-negative rescue" mechanism is triggered.
- **Hard Negative Selection**: The framework identifies hard negatives (`N`) from the pool of failed rollouts (`F`). These negatives are selected to be "close in rationale but wrong in outcome."
 - Rationale embeddings are formed by mean-pooling the final-layer hidden states over the `<think>` span and `ℓ2`-normalizing.
 - The cosine distance `d_cos` between each failed rollout's embedding and the anchor's embedding (`h+`) is calculated.
 - Failures are ranked by ascending `d_cos`, and the top `K'` (min of target `K` and available failures) are selected. To reduce redundancy, a preselection of `M > K'` nearest failures is made, followed by a farthest-first pass within this subset.
 - The subgroup `S` is then formed by the anchor and the `K'` selected hard negatives: `S = {y+} ∪ {y−1..., y−K'}`.
- **Group-Normalized Advantages**:
 - **Within-Group Normalization**: Mean reward `μS` and standard deviation `σS` are computed for the subgroup `S`. Raw sequence-level advantages `A_raw[y]` are calculated as `(r[y] - μS) / σS` for `y ∈ S`, and `0` otherwise.
 - **Negative-Penalty Scaling**: To prevent over-sharpening, negative advantages are down-weighted. For negatives `y−j ∈ S`, `A[y−j] ← -s ⋅ A_raw[y−j]`, where `s ∈ (0, 1]` is a scaling factor (default `s=0.5`). The anchor's advantage `A[y+]` remains `A_raw[y+]`.
 - **Update-Size Equalization**: If the actual number of negatives `K'` is less than the target `K`, advantages are rescaled by `√K/K'` to maintain a consistent update magnitude across groups.

#### 3.2. Token-Weighted Policy Objective

The policy update uses a PPO-style clipped surrogate objective.
- **Region Weights**: Tokens within the `<answer>` span receive a weight of `1`. Tokens within the `<think>` span receive a weight `γ+` (default `γ+=0.005`) if the sample is positive and `0` if negative.
- **Per-Token Advantages**: These are derived from the sequence-level advantages `A[yi]` and the region weights.
- **Loss Function**: The clipped surrogate loss `L_PG` is combined with a KL regularization term `β KL(πθ || πref)` against a fixed reference policy.

#### 3.3. Reflection-Guided Resampling (RGR)

RGR is a training-time-only augmentation that activates when a group contains at least one successful rollout.
- **Procedure**: One positive `y+` and one hard negative `y−` are selected. A concise repair cue (e.g., "Your previous reasoning was incorrect. Identify the failing operation, correct it...") is inserted into the rationale of `y−`, and a new rollout `y_rgr` is sampled.
- **Outcome Handling**:
 - If `y_rgr` is verifier-correct, it replaces the original failed `y−` in the subgroup `S`.
 - If `y_rgr` still fails, it remains a negative in `S` but receives a *reduced negative-penalty scaling* `s_refl` (default `s_refl = s/2`), mitigating over-penalization. RGR is not used in all-negative groups.

#### 3.4. All-Negative Rescue with Partial Balance

When all rollouts in a group yield a reward close to zero, this mechanism prevents gradients from stalling.
- **Procedure**: A pseudo-anchor `t` is chosen as the failure with the highest `log π_old(y|x)`. `K'` representative negatives `N` are selected. Pseudo-rewards are assigned: `r'[t] = δ` and `r'[j] = -δ/K'` for `j ∈ N` (default `δ=0.1`). Advantages are then computed based on these pseudo-rewards, ensuring a non-zero, well-conditioned update without modifying the true reward signal.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Training Data**: A mixture of ChartQA, Geometry3K, and ViRL39K, totaling approximately 49.3K unique multimodal prompts.
- **Training Process**: Models undergo a cold-start Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stage for format adherence and basic visual-math, followed by RL post-training with CARE.
- **Evaluation**: Performance is assessed on six verifiable visual-reasoning benchmarks: MathVista, MathVerse, MATH-Vision, MMMU_val, MMMU–Pro_standard, and MMMU–Pro_vision. All evaluations use a single decode per example with identical hyperparameters and exact-match scoring.
- **Models and Baselines**: Qwen2.5-VL-3B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct serve as backbones. Baselines (GRPO, DAPO, GSPO) are re-trained on the same data with identical rollout budgets (G=8) and optimization budgets for fair comparison.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates that CARE consistently improves accuracy and training stability in verifiable multimodal reasoning tasks, effectively utilizing failure signals for learning.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Quantitative Gains

- **Qwen2.5-VL-7B Backbone**: CARE improves the macro-averaged accuracy over six benchmarks by **4.62 points** compared to GRPO (50.57% vs. 45.95%). It also surpasses DAPO by 2.27 points and GSPO by 0.70 points on the same macro-average.
- **Qwen3-VL-8B Backbone**: The Qwen3-VL-8B + CARE model achieves 82.1% accuracy on MathVista_mini, positioning it competitively with or slightly above strong baselines like MiMo-VL-SFT (81.8%) and Qwen3-VL-8B-Thinking (81.4%). On MMMU-Pro_standard, it reaches 46.7%, outperforming MiMo-VL-7B (46.2%). For MMMU-Pro_vision, it records 41.7%, exceeding MiMo-VL-7B (40.3%) and MiMo-VL-SFT (39.4%).
- **Backbone Scaling**: The observed performance improvements from CARE increase with the size of the underlying language model. For the Qwen3-VL-8B backbone, CARE yields gains of +4.9 points on MathVista_mini, +7.6 on MathVerse_mini, +7.8 on MATH-Vision_full, and +1.4 on MMMU_val, in addition to strong results on MMMU-Pro splits, compared to the Instruct baseline.

#### 4.2. Mechanistic Signature

Empirical analysis of the raw advantages `A_raw[y+]` and `-A_raw[y−]` with respect to `√K'` and `1/√K'` respectively, aligns with the theoretical predictions derived from z-scoring a two-level mixture. The study shows clear linear trends for bucket means, indicating that the within-group normalization mechanism functions as hypothesized.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

Detailed ablations confirm the contribution of each CARE component:
- **Contribution of Components**: The Anchored-Contrastive objective is identified as the primary driver of gains, accounting for 84.4% of the total improvement on Qwen2.5-VL-7B and 62.3% on Qwen2.5-VL-3B. Reflection-Guided Resampling (RGR) provides a consistent, albeit smaller, additional boost (15.6% and 37.7%, respectively).
- **Training Stability**: Training curves indicate that CARE variants achieve higher rewards faster than GRPO, with fewer and shallower dips, suggesting improved stability due to less volatile negative-side updates and the all-negative rescue mechanism preventing stalls.
- **Reflection-Guided Resampling (RGR)**: The inclusion of a repair cue in RGR is critical. RGR with a cue achieves a 76.6% success rate on triggered groups, significantly higher than cue-free resampling (19.3%/12.8%), leading to improved macro-average accuracy.
- **Negative Selection**: Using cosine similarity to select "near-miss" negatives (`COSINE-TOPK'`) results in faster learning and higher convergence (50.57%) compared to random negative selection (43.06%). The study also shows that nearest negatives are more effective than mixed or farthest ones for contrastive learning.
- **Negative-Penalty Scaling**: Decreasing the negative scaling factor `s` (e.g., to 0.5 or 0.3) leads to a reduction in the branch-clip rate on negative-answer tokens and a decrease in the variance ratio of negative to positive advantages. This supports the hypothesis that scaling stabilizes learning by mitigating negative-side over-updates.
- **All-Negative Rescue**: Activating the rescue mechanism prevents stalls in all-negative batches, shifting the accuracy gain (`∆Acc@B`) upward and increasing the per-step update magnitude (`∆KL`) from near-zero to consistent values, thus promoting continuous progress.
- **Region-Weighted Objective**: The region-weighted objective with `γ+=0.005` demonstrates superior sample efficiency, requiring fewer decoded tokens to reach target accuracies and achieving a higher late-stage plateau compared to uniform, answer-only, or unmasked region-weighted objectives. Masking negative rationale tokens prevents noise injection and enhances learning.
- **Anchor Selection**: The "shortest-think" anchoring rule leads to more compact rationales, significantly reducing the average `<think>` token length compared to a random anchor, while maintaining comparable task accuracy.
- **Subgroup Size `K` and Equalization**: The `√K/K'` equalization factor accelerates accuracy growth, stabilizes KL divergence, and reduces seed-to-seed variance, particularly benefiting configurations with `K=4`.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of large multimodal models and reinforcement learning by introducing a post-training framework that explicitly leverages failure data for improved learning. The significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Learning from Failures**: CARE establishes a methodical approach to transform errors and near-misses into constructive learning signals. This is a departure from conventional RLVR strategies that often prioritize successful rollouts or treat failures uniformly. By extracting richer information from incorrect attempts, the framework can guide the policy more effectively toward correct reasoning.
- **Improved Multimodal Reasoning Capabilities**: The consistent gains in accuracy on diverse and challenging visual-reasoning benchmarks (MathVista, MMMU-Pro) indicate that CARE contributes to the development of more robust and reliable MLLMs. These improvements are particularly relevant for complex, visually-grounded tasks in scientific, mathematical, and engineering domains where compositional reasoning is critical.
- **Increased Training Stability and Efficiency**: The components of CARE, including the anchored-contrastive objective, negative-penalty scaling, all-negative rescue, and region-weighted objective, address key issues of gradient variance and training instability in RLVR. This leads to smoother, more predictable, and potentially more sample-efficient training processes, which is valuable given the computational cost of training large models.
- **Refined Credit Assignment**: By forming a compact subgroup around the best rollout and hard negatives, CARE allows for more precise credit assignment. The mechanism helps the model differentiate between truly correct reasoning paths and plausible but ultimately flawed ones, thereby reducing the reinforcement of spurious reasoning.
- **Reproducible and Verifiable Results**: The authors provide a reproducible setting and conduct extensive comparisons against established RLVR baselines. This rigorous evaluation methodology enhances the credibility and practical applicability of the proposed framework.
- **Guidance for Future MLLM Development**: The findings offer insights into effective strategies for post-training MLLMs, particularly for tasks requiring objective verification. The identified advantages of failure-centric learning and structured self-repair mechanisms can inform the design of future RL-based optimization techniques for multimodal intelligence.
- **Acknowledged Limitations and Future Directions**: The paper transparently discusses its limitations, such as the reliance on binary correctness signals and potential trade-offs between conciseness and exhaustive reasoning. It also outlines clear future research directions, including step-level verification, multi-anchor ensembles, and adaptive scaling, providing a roadmap for further advancements in this area.
