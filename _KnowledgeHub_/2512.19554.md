# CARE What Fails: Contrastive Anchored-REflection for Verifiable Multimodal Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers developed CARE (Contrastive Anchored REflection), a post-training framework that enhances large multimodal models' verifiable reasoning by transforming informative errors into constructive learning signals. The system improved macro-averaged accuracy by 4.62 points over GRPO on six benchmarks, demonstrating increased training stability and efficient use of failure data.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods for MLLMs often underutilize failed rollouts, leading to stalled gradients or inefficient credit assignment.
- Limited rollout budgets in RLVR exacerbate issues like high gradient variance and training instability, potentially reinforcing spurious reasoning paths.
- Current MLLMs struggle with complex visual-grounded compositional reasoning tasks, indicating that outcome-only supervision is insufficient for long-term reasoning processes.

## Method

- **CARE** integrates an ==Anchored-Contrastive== Objective to extract stable learning signals by contrasting the best rollout (anchor) with semantically proximate hard negatives, preventing vanishing updates in all-negative batches.
- It employs Reflection-Guided Resampling (RGR), a training-only self-repair mechanism that actively converts near-miss errors into successful examples by inserting repair cues and re-scoring.
- A Token-Weighted Policy Objective focuses learning on answer tokens and selectively on positive rationale tokens, combined with negative-penalty scaling and an "all-negative rescue" for stability.

## Results

- CARE improved macro-averaged accuracy across six benchmarks by 4.62 points (**50.57%** vs. **45.95%**) compared to the GRPO baseline using a Qwen2.5-VL-**7B** backbone.
- The Anchored-Contrastive objective accounted for the majority of performance gains (**84.4%** on Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**), with Reflection-Guided Resampling providing consistent additional boosts.
- Ablation studies confirmed that components like negative-penalty scaling, all-negative rescue, and cosine-similarity-based negative selection significantly enhanced training stability, learning speed, and convergence.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly leveraging informative failures, especially "close-but-wrong" negatives, provides more constructive learning signals than solely focusing on successful rollouts.
> - Within-group normalization and negative-penalty scaling are crucial for stabilizing gradients and preventing over-penalization in RLVR, particularly with small rollout budgets.
> - A structured, one-shot self-reflection mechanism during training can proactively convert near-misses into positive learning experiences without requiring reflection at inference time.
