# MM-Zero: Self-Evolving Multi-Model Vision Language Models From Zero Data

> [!summary] Summary
> MM-Zero develops a reinforcement learning framework that enables Vision Language Models to self-evolve their reasoning capabilities without any external training data. Utilizing a tri-role architecture, the system programmatically generates its own visual content and progressively improves multimodal reasoning. It demonstrated consistent performance gains across diverse VLM benchmarks, with the Solver achieving up to a 5.1% average accuracy increase.

## Problem

- VLM training traditionally relies on vast, costly, and labor-intensive human-curated multimodal datasets and annotations.
- Previous VLM self-evolution frameworks for reasoning still depended on pre-existing static image datasets, creating a data acquisition bottleneck.
- Extending self-evolution to VLMs requires a scalable mechanism to generate or provide relevant visual inputs within the learning loop.

## Method

- Introduces **MM-Zero**, an RL-based framework with a ==tri-role architecture==: Proposer (generates abstract concepts), Coder (synthesizes visuals from concepts), and Solver (performs multimodal reasoning).
- Employs ==Group Relative Policy Optimization== (**GRPO**) in a closed, iterative training loop, where each role is sequentially optimized while others are frozen.
- Utilizes ==Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards== (**RLVR**) and a ==multi-stage reward formulation== for the Proposer to incentivize valid, diverse, and appropriately challenging task generation.

## Results

- The Solver consistently improved performance on diverse VLM benchmarks; for example, the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model's average accuracy increased from **50.7%** to **54.1%** over three iterations, and the MiMo-VL-7B-SFT model saw a **5.1%** gain.
- The Coder's ability to generate compilable code and render solvable images improved throughout training, indicating enhanced visual synthesis capabilities.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of balancing solvability and difficulty rewards and incorporating content diversity, which were critical for robust, non-exploitative, and generalizable self-evolution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision Language Models can autonomously evolve their reasoning skills from zero external data by generating their own visual content and learning through multi-agent interaction.
> - A tri-role (Proposer-Coder-Solver) architecture effectively allows abstract language, executable code, and visual reasoning to mutually reinforce each other's development.
> - Carefully designed and balanced reward mechanisms, including solvability caps and diversity bonuses, are crucial to prevent reward hacking and ensure generalizable skill acquisition during self-evolution.
