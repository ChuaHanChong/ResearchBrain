# MM-Zero: Self-Evolving Multi-Model Vision Language Models From Zero Data

> [!summary] Summary
> MM-Zero develops a reinforcement learning framework that enables Vision Language Models to self-evolve their reasoning capabilities without any external training data. Utilizing a tri-role architecture, the system programmatically generates its own visual content and progressively improves multimodal reasoning. It demonstrated consistent performance gains across diverse VLM benchmarks, with the Solver achieving up to a 5.1% average accuracy increase.

## Problem

- VLM training traditionally relies on vast, costly, and labor-intensive human-curated multimodal datasets and annotations.
- Previous VLM self-evolution frameworks for reasoning still depended on pre-existing static image datasets, creating a data acquisition bottleneck.
- Extending self-evolution to VLMs requires a scalable mechanism to generate or provide relevant visual inputs within the learning loop.

## Method

- Introduces **MM-Zero**, an RL-based framework with a ==tri-role architecture==: Proposer (generates abstract concepts), Coder (synthesizes visuals from concepts), and Solver (performs multimodal reasoning).
- Employs ==Group Relative Policy Optimization== (**GRPO**) in a closed, iterative training loop, where each role is sequentially optimized while others are frozen.
- Utilizes ==Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards== (**RLVR**) and a ==multi-stage reward formulation== for the Proposer to incentivize valid, diverse, and appropriately challenging task generation.

## Results

- The Solver consistently improved performance on diverse VLM benchmarks; for example, the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model's average accuracy increased from **50.7%** to **54.1%** over three iterations, and the MiMo-VL-7B-SFT model saw a **5.1%** gain.
- The Coder's ability to generate compilable code and render solvable images improved throughout training, indicating enhanced visual synthesis capabilities.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of balancing solvability and difficulty rewards and incorporating content diversity, which were critical for robust, non-exploitative, and generalizable self-evolution.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Vision Language Models can autonomously evolve their reasoning skills from zero external data by generating their own visual content and learning through multi-agent interaction.
> - A tri-role (Proposer-Coder-Solver) architecture effectively allows abstract language, executable code, and visual reasoning to mutually reinforce each other's development.
> - Carefully designed and balanced reward mechanisms, including solvability caps and diversity bonuses, are crucial to prevent reward hacking and ensure generalizable skill acquisition during self-evolution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of self-evolving mechanisms represents a significant area of investigation for advancing foundational models, including Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision Language Models (VLMs). This paradigm aims for models to learn and improve by autonomously generating, refining, and learning from their own experiences, thereby reducing reliance on static, human-curated supervision.

Recent advancements in LLMs have demonstrated that these models can achieve self-evolution from minimal or zero human-annotated data. Approaches such as self-play, synthetic data generation, or code execution feedback have enabled LLM agents to autonomously improve their reasoning capabilities.

However, extending this self-evolution paradigm to Vision Language Models introduces additional complexities. VLMs require visual inputs, necessitating a mechanism to generate or provide images alongside textual queries and answers within the self-evolution loop. Prior VLM self-evolution frameworks, such as those adapting LLM proposer-solver pipelines (e.g., VisPlay, Evolmm, V-Zero), have addressed the challenge of reducing human-annotated labels for reasoning tasks. Nevertheless, these approaches have remained constrained by their reliance on pre-existing, collected static image datasets. This dependency introduces a bottleneck, as the VLM's evolution becomes bounded by the distribution, quality, and diversity of the fixed image corpus, and the process of sourcing, filtering, and curating such image data can be time-consuming and costly.

MM-Zero addresses this specific limitation by proposing a framework that enables zero-data self-evolution for VLM reasoning. Unlike prior methods that are conditioned on existing images, MM-Zero allows the model to generate its own visual content programmatically. This approach aims to unlock a more scalable and unconstrained path for VLM self-improvement, moving beyond the limitations imposed by static image datasets. Furthermore, MM-Zero extends the traditional dual-role (proposer-solver) setup common in self-evolving systems to a multi-role (tri-role) interaction, which is a novel architectural consideration for multimodal self-evolution.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce a reinforcement learning (RL)-based framework, MM-Zero, that enables Vision Language Models (VLMs) to self-evolve their reasoning capabilities from a state of *zero external data*. This means the framework aims to improve VLM performance without relying on any pre-existing human-annotated tasks, labels, or even static image datasets.

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several limitations observed in the current landscape of VLM development and self-evolution:

1. **Cost and Labor of Human-Curated Data**: Traditional VLM training paradigms typically depend on vast volumes of expert-curated tasks and corresponding labels. This process is inherently costly, labor-intensive, and presents a fundamental bottleneck due to the finite availability of high-quality human annotations. The development of self-evolving systems aims to mitigate this dependency.
2. **Constraints of Static Image Datasets in VLM Self-Evolution**: While recent VLM self-evolution approaches have succeeded in generating textual questions and answers, they generally remain tethered to existing collections of static images. This dependency merely shifts the data acquisition bottleneck rather than eliminating it. The model's capacity for evolution becomes limited by the scope, quality, and diversity of the pre-collected image data, hindering its ability to explore novel or more complex visual scenarios.
3. **Scalability of Synthetic Visual Data Generation**: The authors motivate the programmatic generation of visual content as a fundamentally more scalable alternative to static datasets. Programmatically rendered scenes can simulate a virtually unlimited array of complex and diverse scenarios. This principle is well-established in fields like robotics, where simulated environments are crucial for training systems to handle rare or dangerous events that are impractical to collect in real-world data.
4. **Advancing Multi-Model Interaction**: The research is motivated by the hypothesis that expanding beyond the conventional dual-role (Proposer and Solver) setup could unlock more sophisticated self-evolution dynamics for multimodal tasks. By introducing a dedicated component for visual content generation, the framework aims to create a closed-loop system where abstract language, executable code, and visual reasoning can mutually reinforce each other's development.

In essence, MM-Zero seeks to establish a completely autonomous VLM training pipeline that can progressively generate its own diverse and challenging multimodal tasks, thereby extending the frontier of self-improvement for foundational models to the visual domain without external data dependencies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MM-Zero is presented as a reinforcement learning (RL)-based framework designed to achieve zero-data self-evolution for Vision Language Model (VLM) reasoning. It moves beyond conventional dual-role setups by introducing a tri-role interaction system. All three roles are initialized from the same base VLM model and are trained iteratively using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), leveraging carefully designed reward mechanisms.

**Tri-Role Architecture:**

1. **Proposer (π_P) - Abstract Conception**: This agent is responsible for generating abstract visual concepts. It formulates high-level textual descriptions of visual scenes (captions), along with a corresponding "easy question" and its verifiable answer (used for rendering quality verification), and a "hard question" that requires complex reasoning over the visual content. The Proposer learns to generate increasingly rich captions and challenging questions.
2. **Coder (π_D) - Visual Synthesis**: The Coder translates the Proposer's textual descriptions into executable code (e.g., Python, SVG) to render precise visual images. Its objective is to produce code that accurately represents the Proposer's intent and results in a valid, viewable image.
3. **Solver (π_S) - Multimodal Reasoning**: This agent engages with the synthesized visual content (images rendered by the Coder) and the questions formulated by the Proposer to perform multimodal reasoning tasks. It attempts to answer both the easy and hard questions.

**Training Paradigm - Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) and GRPO:**

- **RLVR**: The framework operates under the RLVR paradigm, where the correctness of model outputs can be programmatically verified. This is particularly effective for reasoning-intensive tasks where objective evaluation is possible. Rewards are assigned based on verification outcomes.
- **GRPO**: Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) is employed for policy updates. GRPO normalizes rewards within a group of responses generated from the same prompt, yielding response-level advantages. The policy is then updated by maximizing a clipped surrogate objective, regularized by a KL divergence term to manage policy drift. This eliminates the need for a separate learned value function.

**Closed Training Loop and Sequential Optimization:**

The three roles operate in a closed, iterative training loop, with each model optimized sequentially via GRPO. During one role's training phase, the other two roles are frozen.

1. **Proposer Training**: The Proposer generates quadruples (caption, easy question, easy answer, hard question). These proposals are then processed by the Coder to render images and subsequently by the Solver to generate answers. The Solver's performance and image quality metrics contribute to the reward signal for the Proposer.
2. **Coder Training**: The Coder receives captions and questions from the current Proposer checkpoint. It generates SVG code, which is then rendered into images. The Solver evaluates these rendered images by answering the easy questions, providing reward signals back to the Coder.
3. **Solver Training**: The Solver utilizes the successfully rendered images and their associated questions (both easy and hard) generated by the Proposer and Coder. For the hard questions, where no gold answer exists, Test-Time Reinforcement Learning (TTRL) via majority voting is used to generate pseudo-labels for training.

**Training Data Filtering:**

Stage-specific filters are applied to ensure training quality and appropriate difficulty:
- **Coder**: Only examples where the rendering success rate (across 4 rollouts) falls between 0.25 and 0.75 are retained, excluding trivially simple or excessively difficult captions.
- **Solver**: Only examples where easy-question accuracy exceeds 0.5 and hard-question accuracy falls between 0.27 and 0.75 are kept, ensuring the Solver is trained on challenging yet solvable tasks.

**Reward Formulations:**

- **Proposer Reward**: A multi-stage, hierarchical mechanism. Components include an execution indicator (1_exec) for successful rendering, a solvability score (R_solv) for easy questions (capped at 0.5 to prevent "reward hacking"), a difficulty score (R_diff) for hard questions (peaking at 0.5 based on Solver uncertainty, following the Goldilocks principle), an easy-hard penalty (r_eh), a content-type diversity penalty (r_ct), and a caption/question diversity bonus (r_div). This comprehensive reward incentivizes generating valid, diverse, and appropriately challenging tasks.
- **Coder Reward**: A weighted sum of execution status (R_render), solvability (R_solv), difficulty (R_diff), and penalties for syntax errors or rendering issues (λ_err). This encourages the Coder to produce executable code that faithfully represents the Proposer's intent and results in solvable images.
- **Solver Reward**: For hard questions without ground truth, TTRL is used. The Solver generates K independent reasoning paths, identifies a "silver answer" via majority vote, and is rewarded based on accuracy against this silver answer (R_acc) and adherence to a Chain-of-Thought output format (R_fmt).

**Base Models and Evaluation:**

The framework was applied to Qwen3-VL-Instruct (4B and 8B parameters) and Mimo-VL-7B-Instruct. Evaluation of the Solver's performance was conducted using LLM-as-a-judge (Qwen-2.5-14B-Instruct) across a range of benchmarks covering general visual reasoning (MMMU, ChartQA, MM-Vet), mathematical visual reasoning (MathVerse, MathVision, MathVista, VisNumBench), and hallucination detection (HallusionBench, MMSI).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using MM-Zero demonstrated its capacity to improve VLM reasoning performance across various multimodal benchmarks without relying on any external training data.

**Solver Performance Improvements:**
- The Solver consistently improved its performance on diverse VLM benchmarks across training iterations compared to the base models.
- For the Qwen3-VL-4B-Instruct model, the average accuracy increased from 50.2% (base model) to 53.4% after three iterations.
- For the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model, the average accuracy improved from 50.7% (base model) to 54.1% after three iterations.
- The MiMo-VL-7B-SFT model also showed gains, increasing its average score from 50.9% (base model) to 56.0% after three iterations.
- These gains were observed across general visual understanding, visual mathematical reasoning, and hallucination detection benchmarks. The most significant improvements were noted in visual mathematical reasoning tasks, which typically demand more intensive and multi-step reasoning.

**Generalizability and Scaling Behavior:**
- MM-Zero exhibited generalizability, showing consistent improvements across different base VLM models (Qwen3-VL and MiMo-VL) and model sizes (4B, 7B, 8B).
- The Qwen3-VL-4B model showed the smallest percentage gain (3%). The authors attribute this to its comparatively weaker base capabilities, as evidenced by a lower image rendering success rate (approximately 40%) compared to the 7B and 8B models (around 70%). This suggests that stronger base capabilities can lead to more effective self-evolution by providing more valid training samples.

**Coder Evolution:**
- Beyond Solver performance, the Coder's ability to generate compilable code and render images also improved throughout training.
- The render success rate of the Coder steadily increased, indicating enhanced code generation capabilities.
- The solvability of the rendered images also increased, meaning the synthesized visuals became more faithful to the original captions and contained sufficient information for the Solver to answer easy questions correctly.

**Continued Learning Potential:**
- Further training of the Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct model beyond the initial three iterations (up to five iterations) indicated that solver performance had not saturated. The average accuracy continued to improve, reaching 54.5% at Iteration 5 (compared to 54.1% at Iteration 3). This suggests the framework supports ongoing self-improvement.

**Qualitative Observations of Proposer and Coder Evolution:**
- **Base Model**: Initially, figures generated from Proposer outputs suffered from cluttered layouts and overlapping elements, leading to low solvability.
- **Iteration 1**: Visual organization improved, and questions began to demand greater reasoning, although some answers were still easily extractable (e.g., embedded annotations), a pattern penalized by the difficulty score.
- **Iteration 2**: Visuals became cleaner, and hard questions required multi-step reasoning rather than simple value extraction.
- **Iteration 3**: Layouts were more polished, and questions demanded genuine compositional reasoning, such as computing derived quantities from extracted values and annotations.

**Ablation Study Results:**
- **Removing Solvability and Difficulty Balance**: Ablating the cap on the solvability reward (min(R_solv, 0.5)) resulted in slower improvement (2.3% gain for Qwen3-VL-8B vs. 3.9% for full MM-Zero). The model disproportionately optimized for solvability, leading to "reward hacking" where answers were directly embedded as text in rendered images, neglecting the generation of truly difficult questions.
- **Removing Content Diversity**: Without the content diversity reward, the Solver's accuracy initially improved but then steadily declined in subsequent iterations (from 51.7% at Iter 1 to 49.4% at Iter 3 for Qwen3-VL-8B). This indicated that the model overfit to a narrow subset of easy-to-render visual types (e.g., histograms), leading to degraded performance on diverse evaluation benchmarks. These ablation studies underscore the importance of these specific reward components for balanced and generalizable self-evolution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MM-Zero framework represents a notable advancement in the field of Vision Language Models (VLMs) and self-evolving AI systems, with several key points of significance and potential impacts:

1. **Pioneering Zero-Data Self-Evolution for VLMs**: MM-Zero is presented as the first reinforcement learning framework to achieve self-evolution for VLM reasoning *without any external training data*. This addresses a fundamental limitation of previous VLM self-evolution approaches that, while reducing annotation needs, still relied on pre-existing static image datasets. This breakthrough suggests a pathway to developing VLMs independently of human-curated multimodal datasets.

2. **Enhanced Scalability and Reduced Resource Dependency**: By enabling programmatic generation of visual content, MM-Zero offers a highly scalable solution for VLM training. It eliminates the costly and labor-intensive processes of human data annotation and the continuous sourcing and curation of diverse image datasets. This could significantly lower the barriers to entry for developing advanced VLMs, making their improvement more accessible and efficient.

3. **Introduction of a Multi-Role Self-Evolving Paradigm**: The framework's tri-role architecture (Proposer-Coder-Solver) extends beyond the traditional dual-role setups. This demonstrates the effectiveness of more complex agent interactions in self-evolving systems, where abstract language, executable code, and visual reasoning capabilities are mutually reinforced. This multi-model approach could serve as a blueprint for designing more sophisticated and robust self-improving AI systems in other complex domains.

4. **Emergence of Reasoning Capabilities**: The findings indicate that visual reasoning capabilities can emerge and progressively improve purely from self-play and internal feedback mechanisms within the MM-Zero framework. The qualitative analysis shows the system evolves from generating simple, cluttered visuals to producing complex scenes with challenging, multi-step reasoning questions. This highlights the potential for autonomous discovery and skill acquisition in AI models.

5. **Robustness and Generalizability**: The demonstrated improvements across multiple base models and diverse VLM benchmarks (including general visual understanding, mathematical reasoning, and hallucination detection) suggest the framework's robustness and generalizability. Its effectiveness in improving performance on tasks requiring intensive reasoning, such as visual math, is particularly significant.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Democratization of VLM Development**: By reducing the reliance on massive, proprietary datasets, MM-Zero could democratize the development and refinement of VLMs, making advanced models more accessible to researchers and organizations with limited data resources.
- **Accelerated Research and Development**: The ability of VLMs to self-evolve from zero data could significantly accelerate research into multimodal AI, allowing for faster iteration and exploration of new capabilities.
- **Foundational for General AI**: The concept of self-evolving from zero external data, particularly through multi-agent interaction and verifiable rewards, contributes to the broader goal of developing more generally intelligent and autonomous AI systems capable of continuous learning and adaptation.

The authors also outline future directions, including extending the framework to support more diverse tool usage beyond code generation (e.g., 3D renderings), scaling to larger base models to fully understand the self-evolution behavior, and exploring additional agent roles. These future works suggest MM-Zero is not only a significant contribution in itself but also a foundational step towards understanding and building self-improving general artificial intelligence.
