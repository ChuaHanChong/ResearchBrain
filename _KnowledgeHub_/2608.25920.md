# Repair or Resample? Rethinking Failure Debugging in LLM Multi-Agent Systems

> [!summary] Summary
> SymTrace is a record-and-replay framework for LLM multi-agent systems: it logs model and tool boundary interactions into an event-dependency graph, then reconstructs the exact recorded execution prefix before a chosen node and resumes live with an intervention applied. Paired with the 536-trajectory SymFail dataset, a symptom-guided single-node intervention repairs 20.15% of failures versus 6.90% for unguided full rerun.

## Problem

- Existing repair methods rerun whole trajectories and resample upstream decisions, so terminal success cannot be attributed to repair rather than lucky model resampling.
- Debugging benchmarks and taxonomies yield traces used only post-hoc; task-level verdicts and automatic labels miss the trace-localized behavior that actually needs intervention.
- Repeated stochastic reruns of one task produce different failure types or no symptom at all, making failure identity unstable and reproduction unreliable.

## Method

- Snapshot Mode: framework hooks log request-result pairs at model and tool boundaries into ordered records, an event-dependency graph, observed order, and a restartable replay bundle.
- Replay Mode: fail-closed selective replay injects recorded results, verifying event position, canonicalized request content and node content hashes, then Live Resume appends repair guidance and returns execution to live endpoints.
- SymFail plus Suspicious-Node Intervention: 536 human-annotated failures under four symptom categories; deterministic rules and a semantic judge score each node, suspend execution, and inject category-conditioned repair.

## Results

- Replay raises per-execution failure reproduction from 67.97% to 80.78% and three-execution consistency from 41.42% to 52.43%, at 100% prefix-hash exactness; gains reach 25.69 points when nine or more nodes are reused.
- Task-level baselines are weak: unguided rerun 6.90% pass@3, Self-Reflection 4.29%, Critic-Agent 3.73%; rerunning 54 originally successful executions regressed 39 of them at least once.
- Suspicious-Node Intervention repairs 20.15% with one intervention, versus Random-Node 3.73% and Last-Node 1.31%, and wins on all three frameworks after Holm correction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Evaluate repair against a fixed execution history; without controlling the prefix, reported gains conflate genuine correction with the luck of resampling.
> - Localized symptom evidence beats sampling budget: one targeted intervention outperformed three full regenerations, so precise execution control matters more than retry count.
> - Retrying is not free, since re-execution destabilizes previously successful runs, trading repaired failures for newly introduced ones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Model (LLM)-based multi-agent systems (MASs) are increasingly applied to complex, long-horizon tasks, such as web interaction and software development. These systems coordinate specialized agents through mechanisms like message passing, shared context, and structured workflows to distribute planning, information gathering, tool use, execution, and verification. As MASs are deployed in more complex and critical settings, their reliability has emerged as a significant bottleneck.

Existing research has focused on improving MAS reliability through several avenues. Benchmarking and diagnostic studies aim to collect execution trajectories, develop failure taxonomies, and localize responsible agents or steps within an execution. Concurrently, repair methods typically involve rerunning executions, employing reflection, utilizing critic feedback, or providing trajectory-level guidance to generate alternative executions in response to identified failures.

However, the current research landscape faces two primary limitations that this work addresses. First, existing repair methods, often relying on complete reruns, resample upstream model decisions. This makes it challenging to attribute terminal success specifically to the applied repair rather than to mere stochastic variation in LLM behavior. The paper poses a fundamental question: do these methods causally repair MAS failures, or do they achieve stochastic recovery by leveraging the inherent randomness of LLM sampling? Second, traditional task-level failure verdicts or automatically assigned categories may not precisely identify the specific, trace-localized behaviors that necessitate intervention. This lack of fine-grained localization can hinder effective and targeted repair.

This paper positions itself as a response to these limitations by introducing a controlled evaluation framework (SymTrace) and a human-annotated dataset of failure trajectories (SymFail). By enabling verifiable prefix reconstruction and targeted intervention, the work aims to move beyond stochastic rerunning to facilitate a more causal understanding and systematic repair of MAS failures. It seeks to provide a foundation for distinguishing between true repair and mere lucky resampling, thus advancing the robustness and deployability of multi-agent systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the increasing application of LLM-based multi-agent systems (MASs) to long-horizon complex tasks and the corresponding emergence of reliability as a core bottleneck for their real-world deployment. The authors identify a fundamental question underpinning current MAS debugging and repair practices: whether these methods causally address MAS failures or merely achieve stochastic recovery by exploiting the inherent randomness in LLM sampling. The inability to definitively answer this question hinders the development of systematically effective repair strategies.

To address this critical gap, the research establishes several key objectives:

1. **Develop a controlled evaluation framework (SymTrace):** A primary objective was to design and implement a logging system that can reliably reproduce MAS failures. SymTrace aims to capture the entire MAS execution trajectory, including model and tool boundary interactions, and establish intervention anchors. This framework's core capability is to reconstruct the execution precisely up to a specified anchor point using recorded logs and then regenerate only the downstream trajectory, ensuring that any subsequent changes can be attributed directly to an applied intervention rather than upstream stochastic variations.

2. **Construct a comprehensive dataset of MAS failures (SymFail):** The researchers aimed to create a robust dataset of MAS failures that includes detailed human annotations. SymFail comprises 536 human-annotated failure trajectories. These annotations include graph-linked locations of failures, their categorized symptoms (derived from a multi-agent system failure taxonomy), and supporting trace evidence. This dataset serves as a foundational resource for a rigorous study of MAS debugging and repair.

3. **Conduct a large-scale empirical study on existing MAS debugging methods:** A significant objective was to evaluate the reliability of current MAS failure reproduction and repair methods. This involved a large-scale empirical study across three mainstream MAS frameworks (AG2, CrewAI, Magentic-One). The study sought to quantify how reliably existing unguided rerun methods reproduce and repair recorded failures, thereby assessing their effectiveness beyond stochastic outcomes.

4. **Explore and validate a symptom-driven intervention method:** Building upon the insights gained from the empirical study, the researchers aimed to develop and evaluate a novel, symptom-driven intervention method. This method localizes replayable anchors based on observed failure symptoms and applies evidence-conditioned repair, hypothesizing that such targeted interventions would yield higher repair rates than unguided approaches.

5. **Provide actionable insights and resources for future research:** Ultimately, the study intended to offer practical guidance for the MAS debugging and repair research community. By releasing SymTrace, SymFail, and the experimental results, the authors aim to support reproducibility and foster future advancements towards the robust deployment of multi-agent systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a two-pronged approach centered around the SymTrace framework and the SymFail dataset, followed by an empirical study using these resources to evaluate and develop MAS repair methods.

#### 3.1. SymTrace Framework Design

SymTrace is presented as a replay-oriented logging system with two main modes: Snapshot Mode and Replay Mode.

- **Snapshot Mode:** This mode transforms a native MAS execution into a structured, replayable trajectory.
 - **Boundary Logging:** During native MAS execution, framework-specific hooks intercept LLM request-response pairs and tool call-observation pairs. SymTrace records the realized request, result, and event position without altering the MAS's internal logic. These are stored as ordered boundary records (R).
 - **Trace Construction:** The recorded boundary records are then organized into an event-dependency graph (G) and an observed event order (O). The graph captures data or control dependencies, while the observed order preserves the specific linear sequence of events.
 - **Replay Bundle:** A replay bundle (S) is created, combining the recorded values and execution structure with the task (T), initial state (s0), and runtime configuration (c) required to restart the native MAS. This bundle is the output of Snapshot Mode.

- **Replay Mode:** This mode takes a replay bundle (S) and an intervention target as input to reconstruct a prefix of the execution and apply an intervention.
 - **Result Injection:** The native MAS is restarted using T, s0, and c. When the MAS makes a model or tool call *before* the designated intervention target, Result Injection retrieves the corresponding historical record from R and injects its result, rather than invoking the live endpoint.
 - **Boundary Matching:** Each intercepted call is validated against the expected record using its event position and canonicalized request content. A mismatch terminates the replay, ensuring strict adherence to the recorded history. Upon successful match, the recorded result is returned, and content hashes validate the reused prefix nodes.
 - **Live Resume:** Once the verified prefix reaches the designated intervention target, SymTrace stops injecting historical results. An intervention (e.g., a modified prompt) is applied, and model and tool interactions are restored to their live endpoints. The MAS then continues live execution from this intervention point, generating a new downstream trajectory.
 - **Replay Scope:** This defines the guarantee of replay. The portion of the trajectory *before* the target is strictly reconstructed from recorded results, preserving the represented logical history. The portion *from* the intervention onward is live and may diverge. This mechanism allows changes to be attributed to the intervention, assuming deterministic native transitions under recorded values and reset/isolated external states.

#### 3.2. SymFail Dataset Construction

SymFail is a human-annotated dataset of MAS failure trajectories.
- **Candidate Selection:** The dataset was built from 200 tasks (167 from WebArena-Verified Hard and 33 from AssistantBench), selected for their multi-step web tasks and externally verifiable outcomes.
- **Trajectory Collection:** Each of the 200 tasks was executed once with three representative MAS frameworks: AG2, CrewAI, and Magentic-One, yielding 600 initial executions. Of these, 536 evaluator-confirmed failures with complete trace and graph artifacts were retained for annotation.
- **Failure Annotation:** Four annotators with software engineering backgrounds were involved. They were calibrated using reference examples and practice trajectories. Three annotators independently assigned all applicable failure categories (C1-C4), selected a primary category, and identified the earliest trace-supported actionable node with evidence. A fourth annotator reviewed all inputs to produce the final, adjudicated annotation. The categories (Table 1 in the paper) were derived from a Multi-Agent System Failure Taxonomy:
 - C1: Task-Constraint Violation
 - C2: Repeated or Stalled Progress
 - C3: Unresolved Runtime Condition
 - C4: Plan–Action–Outcome Inconsistency
- **Annotation Reliability:** Fleiss’ κ for primary category was 0.62 and for failure-node type was 0.81, indicating substantial agreement. Exact node agreement was 73.88% among all three annotators.

#### 3.3. Empirical Study Setup

- **Study Scope:** The study focused on the 536 SymFail source failures.
- **Baselines:** Three task-level debugging and repair methods were compared:
 - **Unguided Full Rerun:** Restarts the complete MAS execution from the original task input without using information from the failed execution.
 - **Self-Reflection:** Provides the MAS with its failed final output and asks it to identify mistakes before retrying the task.
 - **Critic-Agent:** Employs a separate critic agent to review the failed output and provide corrective guidance for a new complete execution.
- **Proposed Method:** **Suspicious-Node Intervention:** This node-level repair method uses SymTrace. It identifies a trace node as a candidate intervention anchor if its local behavior exhibits observable failure symptoms (C1-C4) connected to the unsuccessful outcome. When a suspicious node is detected, SymTrace reconstructs the prefix, applies a repair instruction conditioned on the judge's C1-C4 classification, and resumes live execution.
- **Node-Level Controls:** Two additional node-level methods were used:
 - **Random-Node Intervention:** Receives a single selective-replay opportunity at a randomly chosen node.
 - **Last-Node Intervention:** Receives a single selective-replay opportunity at the final node.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Failure Reproduction (rep_k):** Proportion of execution subsets where all *k* executions reproduce the source failure (evaluated for *k*=1 and *k*=3).
 - **Repair Success (pass@k):** Proportion of source failures receiving at least one evaluator-accepted repair within *k* attempts (pass@3 for task-level, pass@1 for node-level).
- **Model and Efficiency:** All experiments used `deepseek-v4-flash` at temperature 0.00. API call counts and repairs per 1,000 calls were reported as secondary efficiency measures.
- **Statistical Analysis:** 95% Wilson confidence intervals, 10,000 case-level bootstrap resamples for paired rate differences, two-sided exact McNemar tests, and Holm correction for family-wise error rate control were employed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study systematically investigated failure reproduction and repair across different MAS architectures and debugging approaches.

#### 4.1. RQ1: How Reliably Can MAS Failures Be Reproduced?

- **SymTrace's Advantage:** SymTrace Replay demonstrated a consistent advantage over Unguided Full Rerun in reproducing failures across all three MASs (AG2, CrewAI, Magentic-One). The per-execution failure reproduction rate (rep_1) for SymTrace was 80.78%, compared to 67.97% for Unguided Full Rerun. For consistent reproduction across three executions (rep_3), SymTrace achieved 52.43% versus 41.42%.
- **Prefix Exactness:** SymTrace achieved 100% prefix exactness, meaning all reused prefix nodes passed content-hash validation. This confirmed that SymTrace precisely reconstructed the recorded execution history before the intervention point.
- **Impact of Prefix Length:** The reproduction advantage of SymTrace increased with the length of the reused prefix. For cases reusing 2-3 nodes, the rep_1 gain was 9.80 percentage points. This increased to 17.76 points for 4-8 reused nodes and 25.69 points for at least nine nodes.
- **Factors for Remaining Gap:** The gap between exact prefix reconstruction and end-to-end reproduction was attributed to short replay prefixes, post-target execution variation (where live execution might diverge), and evaluation uncertainty from the LLM-as-a-judge pipeline.
- **Finding 1:** SymTrace significantly improves failure reproduction by exactly reconstructing the execution before the failure target. This advantage is particularly pronounced for longer-horizon tasks where more preceding steps can be reused, making it suitable for realistic MAS debugging.

#### 4.2. RQ2: Can Task-Level Re-execution Reliably Repair Failed MAS Executions?

- **Limited Repair Rates:** Task-level repair methods exhibited low success rates. Unguided Full Rerun achieved the highest overall pass@3 rate (6.90%) among the task-level baselines, outperforming Self-Reflection (4.29%) and Critic-Agent (3.73%). This pattern held across all individual MAS architectures.
- **Lack of Consistent Advantage for Feedback Methods:** Neither Self-Reflection nor Critic-Agent provided a consistent repair advantage over unguided task restart. The category breakdown (C1-C4) also showed no evidence that feedback-based methods systematically addressed particular failure types more effectively. This suggested that their additional feedback did not reliably target the source failure mechanism.
- **Outcome Instability:** Complete re-execution demonstrated significant outcome instability. In a separate analysis of 54 initially successful executions, 85 out of 162 subsequent attempts resulted in failures, and 39 of the 54 source cases regressed to a failure at least once. This indicated that success in a rerun did not necessarily imply a repair of the original failure but could result from stochastic variation.
- **Case Analysis:** A specific CrewAI case highlighted that an evaluator-accepted answer could be produced without resolving the underlying source failure mechanism. The unguided rerun returned a correct answer based on general patterns, while reflection/critic methods acknowledged the lack of required evidence, illustrating that observed repair does not guarantee actual problem resolution.
- **Finding 2:** Task-level methods repair primarily through stochastic regeneration rather than targeted, failure-specific correction. Re-execution can lead to both successful outcomes and destabilization of previously successful ones. Feedback-based variants, under equal attempt budgets, offered no discernible advantage, indicating that terminal success alone is insufficient evidence that the source failure was identified and repaired.

#### 4.3. RQ3: Are Node-Level Symptom Signals Actionable for Targeted Repair?

- **Superior Repair Effectiveness:** Suspicious-Node Intervention achieved the highest overall repair rate, repairing 20.15% of failed cases with a single intervention (pass@1). This represented a 191.89% improvement over the strongest task-level baseline (Unguided Full Rerun, pass@3: 6.90%).
- **Outperformance of Node-Level Controls:** The proposed method substantially outperformed other node-level controls. Random-Node Intervention (pass@1: 3.73%) and Last-Node Intervention (pass@1: 1.31%) both achieved significantly lower repair rates, despite receiving the same single selective-replay opportunity. This indicated that selective replay alone was not sufficient; symptom-based target selection and repair guidance were critical.
- **Cross-MAS Consistency:** The superior performance of Suspicious-Node Intervention was consistent across all three MAS architectures (AG2, CrewAI, Magentic-One), where it performed best on each system. This consistency was notable, as task-level baselines had three attempts to benefit from stochastic resampling, while the proposed method used only one symptom-guided intervention. Statistical tests (McNemar with Holm correction) confirmed its significant advantage over all baselines.
- **API-Call Efficiency:** Suspicious-Node Intervention demonstrated greater repair efficiency, yielding both the highest number of accepted repairs per 1,000 API calls and the lowest number of API calls per repair across MASs.
- **Finding 3:** Repair effectiveness is more dependent on precise execution control and localized symptom-based intervention than on repeated full-trajectory regeneration. Localized symptoms provide actionable repair guidance, whereas full-trajectory resampling often obscures whether the original failure mechanism was truly corrected.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This study presents a significant contribution to the understanding and improvement of reliability in LLM-based multi-agent systems, with several potential impacts:

- **Shift in Debugging Paradigm:** The work fundamentally re-evaluates MAS failure debugging, shifting the focus from stochastic rerunning to controlled, targeted intervention. By distinguishing true causal repair from mere stochastic recovery, it provides a more rigorous framework for evaluating debugging strategies.
- **Enabling Causal Attribution:** SymTrace, as a controlled evaluation framework, provides a crucial tool for researchers and developers to rigorously test MAS repair methods. Its ability to reconstruct execution prefixes and apply interventions without upstream resampling allows for causal attribution of repair efforts, which was previously a significant challenge. This can accelerate the development of more effective and predictable MAS repair techniques.
- **Valuable Research Resource:** The SymFail dataset, with its 536 human-annotated failure trajectories, offers a rich and structured resource for the MAS research community. It provides detailed, graph-linked failure locations, categories, and trace evidence, serving as a robust foundation for future studies in MAS debugging, diagnosis, and repair.
- **Improved MAS Reliability and Efficiency:** The empirical findings demonstrate that symptom-driven, node-level interventions can substantially improve repair rates compared to existing task-level methods, even with a reduced budget of attempts. This suggests a pathway towards more robust and efficient MAS deployment in complex, real-world applications by enabling more precise and economical debugging.
- **Actionable Insights for Future Research:** The study highlights the limitations of current debugging practices that rely on full-trajectory resampling and emphasizes the value of precise execution control and localized evidence. This provides clear directions for future research to focus on developing more sophisticated failure detection, localization, and intervention mechanisms that leverage execution history and symptom-based signals.
- **Facilitating Reproducibility and Open Science:** The commitment to releasing SymTrace, SymFail, and the experimental results promotes transparency, reproducibility, and collaborative advancement in the field of MAS debugging and repair, lowering the barrier for other researchers to build upon this work.
- **Enhanced Safety and Trust:** By enabling a more systematic and verifiable approach to repairing MAS failures, this research contributes to increasing the overall reliability and safety of these systems, fostering greater trust in their application across various domains.
