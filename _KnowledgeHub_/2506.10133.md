# Statistical Guarantees for Offline Domain Randomization

> [!summary] Summary
> This research provides statistical guarantees for Offline Domain Randomization (ODR), demonstrating its theoretical reliability in learning real-world system dynamics from offline data by establishing weak and strong consistency for ODR estimators.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning policies trained in simulation often struggle with deployment in real-world environments due to the 'sim-to-real gap', caused by discrepancies in dynamics.
- Traditional Domain Randomization (DR) lacks theoretical guarantees regarding the quality of its randomized distributions, often relying on inefficient, hand-tuned parameter ranges.
- Offline Domain Randomization (ODR), while empirically successful in bridging the sim-to-real gap, lacked a rigorous theoretical foundation, specifically concerning the convergence of its learned parameter distributions to true real-world dynamics.

## Method

- The authors formalize **Offline Domain Randomization** (ODR) as a ==Maximum Likelihood== Estimation (MLE) problem, aiming to learn a distribution over simulator parameters that best explains an offline dataset.
- A set of statistical assumptions, including simulator regularity, parameter-space compactness, mixture positivity, and identifiability, are introduced to establish a framework for theoretical analysis.
- Classical statistical theory is then applied to these assumptions to prove the consistency of the ODR estimator, demonstrating its ability to recover true dynamics.

## Results

- The ODR estimator is proven to achieve both weak and strong consistency, converging in probability and almost surely, respectively, to the true underlying dynamics parameter (represented as a degenerate distribution).
- The research introduces a concept of 'α-informativeness,' demonstrating that the learned randomization distribution concentrates its probability mass around the true real-world parameters.
- Key theoretical assumptions are shown to be relaxable to more practical conditions like stationary and ergodic data, broadening ODR's real-world applicability, and the framework extends to scenarios of partial data coverage where the estimator converges to an 'identified set' of parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Offline Domain Randomization, when formulated as a Maximum Likelihood Estimation problem, can be theoretically proven to consistently identify the true real-world dynamics from offline data.
> - The level of statistical guarantee (weak vs. strong consistency) depends on specific theoretical assumptions, particularly uniform Lipschitz continuity, which ensures stronger almost sure convergence.
> - Several strict theoretical assumptions, such as i.i.d. data and uniform mixture positivity, can be relaxed to more practical conditions (e.g., ergodic data, logarithmic tail conditions) without compromising consistency.
