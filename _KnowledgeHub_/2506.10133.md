# Statistical Guarantees for Offline Domain Randomization

> [!summary] Summary
> This research provides statistical guarantees for Offline Domain Randomization (ODR), demonstrating its theoretical reliability in learning real-world system dynamics from offline data by establishing weak and strong consistency for ODR estimators.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning policies trained in simulation often struggle with deployment in real-world environments due to the 'sim-to-real gap', caused by discrepancies in dynamics.
- Traditional Domain Randomization (DR) lacks theoretical guarantees regarding the quality of its randomized distributions, often relying on inefficient, hand-tuned parameter ranges.
- Offline Domain Randomization (ODR), while empirically successful in bridging the sim-to-real gap, lacked a rigorous theoretical foundation, specifically concerning the convergence of its learned parameter distributions to true real-world dynamics.

## Method

- The authors formalize **Offline Domain Randomization** (ODR) as a ==Maximum Likelihood== Estimation (MLE) problem, aiming to learn a distribution over simulator parameters that best explains an offline dataset.
- A set of statistical assumptions, including simulator regularity, parameter-space compactness, mixture positivity, and identifiability, are introduced to establish a framework for theoretical analysis.
- Classical statistical theory is then applied to these assumptions to prove the consistency of the ODR estimator, demonstrating its ability to recover true dynamics.

## Results

- The ODR estimator is proven to achieve both weak and strong consistency, converging in probability and almost surely, respectively, to the true underlying dynamics parameter (represented as a degenerate distribution).
- The research introduces a concept of 'α-informativeness,' demonstrating that the learned randomization distribution concentrates its probability mass around the true real-world parameters.
- Key theoretical assumptions are shown to be relaxable to more practical conditions like stationary and ergodic data, broadening ODR's real-world applicability, and the framework extends to scenarios of partial data coverage where the estimator converges to an 'identified set' of parameters.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Offline Domain Randomization, when formulated as a Maximum Likelihood Estimation problem, can be theoretically proven to consistently identify the true real-world dynamics from offline data.
> - The level of statistical guarantee (weak vs. strong consistency) depends on specific theoretical assumptions, particularly uniform Lipschitz continuity, which ensures stronger almost sure convergence.
> - Several strict theoretical assumptions, such as i.i.d. data and uniform mixture positivity, can be relaxed to more practical conditions (e.g., ergodic data, logarithmic tail conditions) without compromising consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has demonstrated capabilities across various domains, including games and robotics. However, a significant challenge in real-world deployment is the high cost and safety concerns associated with data collection. Sim-to-real transfer aims to mitigate this by training policies in simulation and deploying them in physical environments. This approach is hindered by the "sim-to-real gap," which refers to discrepancies between simulated and real-world dynamics, leading to performance degradation upon transfer.

Domain Randomization (DR) is a prominent technique for bridging the sim-to-real gap. It involves training an RL policy across a family of simulators, each instance produced by sampling physical parameters (e.g., masses, friction) from a predefined distribution. This exposure to varied dynamics aims to make the policy robust to real-world uncertainties, enabling "zero-shot" transfer without further real-world data. Traditional Uniform Domain Randomization (UDR) often relies on hand-tuned, broad uniform ranges for parameters, which can be inefficient and lack theoretical guarantees on the resulting policy's performance or the quality of the randomization distribution. Existing theoretical analyses for UDR indicate that performance bounds can scale unfavorably with the number of candidate simulators, partly due to the uniform sampling approach.

Offline Domain Randomization (ODR) is an evolution of DR that leverages pre-existing, static "offline" datasets collected from the real system. Instead of relying solely on expert-tuned uniform distributions, ODR fits a sampling distribution over simulator parameters to this offline data, aiming to concentrate on plausible dynamics. Empirical studies, such as DROID (Tsai et al., 2021) and DROPO (Tiboni et al., 2023), have reported performance improvements with ODR variants over UDR in zero-shot transfer scenarios. These algorithms learn parameter distributions that explain the observed real-world data.

Despite its empirical success, ODR has largely lacked a formal theoretical foundation. Key questions regarding ODR's mechanisms remained unanswered: (i) whether the fitted parameter distribution converges to the true underlying real-world dynamics as the offline dataset grows, and (ii) how effectively it reduces the sim-to-real gap compared to UDR. This research addresses these theoretical gaps by providing statistical guarantees for ODR, thereby establishing a principled framework for its application.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to establish a rigorous theoretical foundation for Offline Domain Randomization (ODR), addressing the current lack of statistical guarantees despite its empirical success in sim-to-real transfer. Specifically, the authors aim to answer two fundamental questions:
1. **Convergence of Fitted Distribution:** Does the parameter distribution learned by ODR converge to the true real-world dynamics as the volume of offline data increases? This objective seeks to provide confidence in the data-driven approach of ODR, ensuring that the learning process reliably identifies the underlying real-world characteristics.
2. **Impact on Sim-to-Real Gap:** While not directly quantifying the reduction in sim-to-real gap compared to UDR, the convergence analysis aims to justify ODR's potential for robust transfer by demonstrating that it focuses on accurate dynamics. A foundational understanding of the fitted distribution's quality is a prerequisite for understanding its downstream impact on policy performance.

To achieve these objectives, the authors cast ODR as a maximum-likelihood estimation (MLE) problem over a parametric family of simulators. This formulation allows for the application of established statistical theory to derive convergence properties. The specific contributions outlining how these objectives are met are:

- **Proving Weak Consistency:** To establish a basic level of reliability for ODR, the authors aim to demonstrate that, under certain mild regularity, positivity, and identifiability assumptions, the ODR estimator converges in probability to the true dynamics as the offline dataset grows. This means that as more data becomes available, the probability of the estimated distribution deviating significantly from the true distribution approaches zero.
- **Proving Strong Consistency:** To provide a more robust guarantee, the research seeks to show that by introducing an additional uniform Lipschitz continuity assumption on the likelihood function, the convergence can be upgraded to strong consistency. This implies that the ODR estimator converges almost surely to the true dynamics, meaning that with probability one, the estimated distribution will eventually match the true dynamics exactly.
- **Analyzing Practicality of Assumptions:** A critical objective is to examine the practical implications of the theoretical assumptions required for consistency. This involves discussing when these assumptions hold in real-world scenarios and outlining relaxations or diagnostics that broaden ODR's applicability. This addresses concerns about the theoretical framework's relevance to practical robotics and control settings.

In essence, the motivation is to move ODR from an empirically driven heuristic to a principled method with statistically verifiable properties, thereby clarifying when and how offline data can be soundly leveraged for domain randomization in downstream offline RL tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This research formulates Offline Domain Randomization (ODR) within a statistical framework, specifically as a Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE) problem. The core methodological steps are as follows:

#### 3.1. Problem Setup and ODR Formulation

- **Episodic MDPs:** The work considers episodic Reinforcement Learning settings defined by Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) M = (S, A, P_M, R, H, s_1). The state space (S), action space (A), reward function (R), episode horizon (H), and initial state (s_1) are shared. The key variable is the transition probability matrix P_M.
- **Simulator Class:** A parametric simulator class U = {M_ξ : ξ ∈ Ξ ⊂ R^d} is defined. Each M_ξ represents an MDP whose dynamics P_M are parameterized by a vector ξ, representing physical factors (e.g., masses, friction coefficients). The true real-world environment M* is assumed to be an instance M_ξ* ∈ U.
- **Offline Data:** ODR utilizes a static dataset D = {(s_i, a_i, s'_i)}^(N)_{i=1} comprising i.i.d. transitions collected from the real system M* under an unknown behavior policy.
- **ODR Objective:** The goal is to estimate a distribution p*(ξ) over the parameter space Ξ that best explains this offline data. The authors propose to model p_ϕ(ξ) as a Gaussian distribution N(μ, Σ) (where ϕ = (μ, Σ) are the parameters of the distribution), and learn ϕ by maximizing the log-likelihood of the offline data:

 L_N(ϕ) = (1/N) Σ^(N)_{i=1} log [ E_{ξ~p_ϕ(ξ)} [P_ξ(s'_i | s_i, a_i)] ]

 This objective seeks the parameters ϕ that maximize the probability of observing the transitions in the dataset, averaged over the uncertainty in ξ described by p_ϕ(ξ). The expectation E_{ξ~p_ϕ(ξ)} [P_ξ(s' | s, a)] defines a "mixture kernel" q_ϕ(s' | s, a), which is the effective transition probability under the randomization distribution p_ϕ(ξ).

#### 3.2. Theoretical Framework and Assumptions

The core of the methodology involves proving consistency results for the ODR estimator by relying on classical statistical theory for Maximum Likelihood Estimators, conditioned on a set of assumptions.

- **Simulator Regularity (Assumption 1):** Assumes that the transition probability density p_ξ(s' | s, a) is uniformly bounded and continuous with respect to ξ. This ensures well-behaved likelihood functions.
- **Parameter-Space Compactness (Assumption 2):** Assumes the parameter space Φ for ϕ (which includes μ and Σ) is compact. This is a common practical assumption for optimization and a theoretical requirement for uniform convergence results.
- **Mixture Positivity (Assumption 3):** Requires that the induced mixture kernel q_ϕ(s' | s, a) is uniformly bounded away from zero for all transitions in the dataset and all ϕ ∈ Φ. This prevents the log-likelihood from becoming undefined (negative infinity).
- **Identifiability (Assumption 4):** States that the only mixture distribution that perfectly reproduces the true transition kernel p_ξ*(· | s, a) for the observed state-action pairs is the degenerate distribution concentrated at the true parameter ξ* (i.e., ϕ = (ξ*, 0)). This ensures a unique maximizer for the population log-likelihood.

#### 3.3. Consistency Proofs

- **Weak Consistency:** The proof for weak consistency relies on showing that the population log-likelihood L(ϕ) (the expected value of L_N(ϕ) under the true data distribution) has a unique maximizer ϕ* (Lemma 1). It then uses a Uniform Law of Large Numbers (ULLN) to establish that the empirical log-likelihood L_N(ϕ) converges uniformly in probability to the population log-likelihood L(ϕ) (Lemma 2). Finally, a uniform separation property (Lemma 3) ensures that points outside an ϵ-neighborhood of ϕ* have significantly lower log-likelihood. Combining these, it's shown that the empirical maximizer b_ϕ_N converges in probability to ϕ*.
- **Strong Consistency:** To achieve almost sure convergence, an additional assumption is introduced:
 - **Uniform Lipschitz Continuity (Assumption 5):** Requires that the single-step log-likelihood a(x, ϕ) = log q_ϕ(s' | s, a) is uniformly Lipschitz continuous with respect to ϕ. This strong regularity condition enables the application of a Uniform Strong Law of Large Numbers (Lemma 4), which states that L_N(ϕ) converges uniformly almost surely to L(ϕ). This stronger convergence then directly implies almost sure convergence of b_ϕ_N to ϕ*.

#### 3.4. Assumption Analysis and Relaxations

The methodology includes a critical examination of the proposed assumptions:
- **I.I.D. Data:** The i.i.d. assumption for the offline dataset is discussed as stronger than necessary, with strict stationarity and ergodicity being sufficient for the ULLN.
- **Mixture Positivity:** A relaxation is provided, replacing the uniform lower bound with a logarithmic tail condition on the probability of q_ϕ(x) being small.
- **Uniform Lipschitz Continuity:** A more interpretable sufficient condition is provided, requiring the transition kernel p_ξ(s' | s, a) to be twice continuously differentiable and its gradients (first and second order with respect to ξ) to be bounded.
- **Identifiability:** The case of partial coverage (where Assumption 4 might not hold) is discussed, leading to a modified consistency result where the estimator converges to an "identified set" of parameters that are observationally indistinguishable given the available data. Upper hemicontinuity of this identified set is also shown.

This comprehensive statistical approach allows the authors to provide theoretical guarantees for ODR, detailing the conditions under which it can reliably learn the underlying system dynamics.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several key theoretical results concerning the statistical properties of Offline Domain Randomization (ODR), particularly its ability to recover the true dynamics parameters from offline data.

#### 4.1. Weak Consistency of the ODR Estimator

- **Uniqueness of Population Maximizer:** The paper first establishes (Lemma 1) that, under Assumptions 1 (Simulator Regularity), 3 (Mixture Positivity), and 4 (Identifiability), the population log-likelihood function `L(ϕ)` has a unique maximizer. This maximizer is `ϕ* = (ξ*, 0)`, meaning the true dynamics parameter `ξ*` with zero variance (a degenerate distribution). This result is derived by showing that `L(ϕ)` is maximized when the mixture kernel `q_ϕ(.|s,a)` perfectly matches the true kernel `p_ξ*(.|s,a)`, which occurs uniquely at `ϕ*` due to identifiability.
- **Uniform Convergence of Empirical Log-likelihood:** A crucial intermediate result (Lemma 2) demonstrates that the empirical log-likelihood `L_N(ϕ)` converges uniformly in probability to the population log-likelihood `L(ϕ)` over the compact parameter space `Φ`. This uniform convergence is a necessary condition for establishing consistency of the maximizers.
- **Weak Consistency Theorem (Theorem 1):** Under Assumptions 1 (Simulator Regularity), 2 (Parameter-Space Compactness), 3 (Mixture Positivity), and 4 (Identifiability), any measurable maximizer `b_ϕ_N` of the empirical log-likelihood `L_N(ϕ)` is weakly consistent. This means `b_ϕ_N` converges in probability to `ϕ*` as the size of the offline dataset `N` approaches infinity (`b_ϕ_N -> P ϕ*`). This implies that with a sufficiently large dataset, the ODR estimator will produce a distribution arbitrarily close to the true, degenerate distribution `(ξ*, 0)`.

#### 4.2. Strong Consistency Under Uniform Lipschitz Conditions

- **Uniform Strong Law (Lemma 4):** By adding an extra assumption, 5 (Uniform Lipschitz Continuity) on the log-likelihood function `a(x, ϕ)`, the research proves a Uniform Strong Law of Large Numbers. This stronger result states that the empirical log-likelihood `L_N(ϕ)` converges uniformly almost surely to the population log-likelihood `L(ϕ)`.
- **Strong Consistency Theorem (Theorem 2):** Under Assumptions 1 through 5, any measurable maximizer `b_ϕ_N` of `L_N(ϕ)` is strongly consistent, i.e., `b_ϕ_N` converges almost surely to `ϕ*` (`b_ϕ_N -> a.s. ϕ*`). This provides a stronger guarantee: with probability one, as `N` grows, the estimated distribution will eventually match the true simulator parameters exactly and remain in its vicinity.

#### 4.3. A Notion of α-Informativeness

- **Concentration of Learned Distribution (Lemma 5):** A direct consequence of strong consistency for the Gaussian ODR procedure is that if `(μ_N, Σ_N)` converges almost surely to `(ξ*, 0)`, then the Gaussian distribution `P_{b_ϕ_N}` fitted by ODR eventually assigns more than half of its probability mass to any fixed ϵ-ball around `ξ*`. This indicates that the learned randomization distribution concentrates around the real-world parameters.
- **α, ϵ-Informativeness (Definition 1):** Based on this observation, the paper introduces a general, model-agnostic definition of "informativeness" for an ODR algorithm. An algorithm is `α, ϵ-informative` if, for sufficiently large `N`, the fitted distribution `P_{b_ϕ_N}` assigns at least `α` probability mass to an `ϵ-ball` around `ξ*`. The Gaussian ODR procedure from the paper is shown to be `α-informative` for every `α < 1`.

#### 4.4. Practicality, Violations, and Relaxations of Assumptions

The paper critically examines the practical implications of its assumptions and provides relaxations to broaden ODR's applicability:
- **I.I.D. Assumption:** The strict i.i.d. requirement for the offline dataset can be relaxed to strict stationarity and ergodicity, which is often met when data is collected by a fixed behavior policy.
- **Mixture Positivity (Assumption 3):** This strong assumption, which requires the density to be uniformly bounded away from zero, can be relaxed to a logarithmic tail condition (Lemma 6). This condition, `P(inf_ϕ q_ϕ(X) ≤ ϵ) ≤ 1/(log(1/ϵ))^2` for sufficiently small `ϵ`, is strictly weaker and allows for common light-tailed families (e.g., Gaussian-like).
- **Uniform Lipschitz Continuity (Assumption 5):** A more interpretable sufficient condition is provided (Lemma 7). This assumption holds if the function `ξ -> p_ξ(s' | s, a)` is twice continuously differentiable with bounded first and second gradients (with respect to `ξ`). This condition is argued to be practical for simulators governed by smooth physics.
- **Identifiability (Assumption 4):** In cases of partial coverage where the dataset may not be sufficiently informative to uniquely identify `ξ*`, the estimator converges to an "identified set" `Q*μ` (Lemma 8). This set contains all parameter distributions that are observationally indistinguishable on the state-action pairs covered by the data (Theorem 3). This set `Q*μ` is shown to be non-empty and compact, and its correspondence to the data distribution `μ` is upper hemicontinuous (Lemma 9), implying stability against small perturbations in data coverage. The paper also discusses the implications for misspecified models where the true dynamics may not be within the simulator class.

These findings collectively place ODR on a principled statistical footing, demonstrating its theoretical reliability under specified conditions and offering insights into its behavior under more general practical scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a rigorous theoretical foundation for Offline Domain Randomization (ODR), transforming it from an empirically successful heuristic into a statistically principled method for sim-to-real transfer. The significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Principled Foundation for ODR:** By casting ODR as a maximum-likelihood estimation problem and proving weak and strong consistency, the paper provides formal guarantees that the ODR estimator converges to the true underlying system dynamics as the amount of offline data increases. This is a fundamental result that was previously lacking, offering statistical assurance to a technique widely used in practice.
- **Clarification of ODR's Mechanism:** The consistency proofs clarify *when* offline data can soundly guide the choice of a randomization distribution. This theoretical understanding helps practitioners interpret ODR's output and trust its ability to characterize the real world, rather than relying solely on black-box optimization.
- **Guidance for Algorithm Design:** The identified assumptions for consistency, particularly the Lipschitz continuity requirement and the identifiability condition, provide valuable insights for future ODR algorithm design. Developers can prioritize building parametric simulator families that satisfy these conditions or design data collection strategies that enhance identifiability.
- **Robustness and Reliability:** Strong consistency, achieved under the uniform Lipschitz continuity assumption, provides a particularly robust guarantee: with probability one, the estimated distribution will eventually match the true dynamics. This strengthens the confidence in ODR's long-term behavior with ample data. The concept of α-informativeness further quantifies this by showing the learned distribution concentrates near the true real world.
- **Practical Applicability and Relaxations:** The detailed analysis of assumptions and their relaxations is crucial for practical impact. Demonstrating that weak consistency holds under less stringent conditions (e.g., stationary and ergodic data instead of i.i.d., or a logarithmic tail condition instead of strict mixture positivity) significantly broadens ODR's applicability to real-world datasets that may not perfectly meet ideal statistical assumptions. The interpretable sufficient condition for Lipschitz continuity also makes the theory more accessible to engineers working with physical simulators.
- **Addressing Misspecification and Partial Coverage:** The extension of the consistency theory to cases with partial coverage or potential misspecification (where the true dynamics might not be perfectly within the simulator class) is valuable. Convergence to an "identified set" rather than a single point acknowledges the inherent limitations of sparse data and provides a more realistic understanding of what can be learned.
- **Enabling Safer and More Data-Efficient Sim-to-Real Pipelines:** By providing a reliable method to learn real-world dynamics from offline data, ODR reduces the need for extensive, costly, and potentially unsafe online interaction with physical systems. This can accelerate the development and deployment of RL agents in high-stakes domains like robotics and autonomous vehicles. The theoretical validation supports increased confidence in policies trained via ODR for zero-shot transfer.

In conclusion, this research significantly advances the theoretical understanding of Offline Domain Randomization, providing statistical guarantees that were previously absent. This work moves ODR closer to being a fully validated, principled tool, fostering its responsible and effective application in sim-to-real transfer to create more robust and adaptable intelligent systems.
