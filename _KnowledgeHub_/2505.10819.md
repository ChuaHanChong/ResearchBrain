# PoE-World: Compositional World Modeling with Products of Programmatic Experts

> [!summary] Summary
> PoE-World represents a world model as an exponentially weighted product of many small LLM-synthesized Python programs, each encoding one causal law, with weights fit by maximum likelihood and low-weight experts pruned. From under one minute of demonstration it learns 4000+ line models of Pong and Montezuma's Revenge. A hierarchical planner using these models outperforms PPO and WorldCoder in the low-data regime and generalizes to unseen level variants.

## Problem

- Deep-learning world models such as Dreamer and Diamond need huge amounts of data and update poorly from sparse observations, still imagining agents walking through walls after about 100 hours of training.
- Programmatic world models such as WorldCoder synthesize one monolithic Python program through combinatorial search, so they stay confined to text and gridworld domains and cannot scale to complex environments.
- A single monolithic program cannot handle stochasticity or partial observability, and it offers no fine-grained way to downweight buggy parts, so the model hallucinates dynamics such as flying or nonexistent bullets.

## Method

- ==Product of programmatic experts==: hundreds of small deterministic Python programs, each read as a peaked distribution over object attributes, are multiplied with ==scalar exponent weights== and normalized per feature under a factored, ==object-centric state==.
- Learning loop: an LLM synthesizes experts from batches of transitions, ==L-BFGS== fits expert weights by ==maximum likelihood==, experts below a weight threshold are pruned, and the loop repeats as new observations arrive.
- Learned ==hard constraints== rule out physically impossible scenes, and a ==hierarchical task-and-motion-style planner== searches an abstract ==object-contact graph==, removes false edges when plans fail, and can also pretrain PPO inside the model.

## Results

- With under **1000** demonstration frames and at most **3k** training steps, PoE-World plus Planner scores **-12.33** on Pong and **-13.67** on Pong-Alt, versus **-17.00** and **-19.00** for WorldCoder plus Planner. It is the only method with positive reward on Montezuma's Revenge (**100** on both base and Alt).
- PPO needs over **a million** environment steps to beat PoE-World and never scores on Montezuma's Revenge even at **20M** steps. PPO pretrained in the learned model beats random after **200k** steps, versus **1M** for vanilla PPO.
- Next-observation accuracy on Montezuma's Revenge training data is **0.75** versus WorldCoder's **0.36**. Removing hard constraints cuts key-collection planning successes from **5/9** to **2/9** on MR and from **4/9** to **2/9** on MR-Alt, with prediction accuracy unchanged.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing world-model learning into many small independent experts makes program synthesis tractable and compositional; the factoring, not the use of symbols alone, is what allows scaling and extrapolation to new object arrangements.
> - Soft probabilistic aggregation with learned weights tolerates imperfect LLM-written code: buggy or irrelevant experts are downweighted or pruned instead of corrupting a single monolithic program.
> - Hard constraints improve planning usability without improving next-frame accuracy, so one-step prediction metrics can miss what long-horizon planning needs. Synthesized models are also useful as simulators to warm-start RL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

PoE-World positions itself at the intersection of several critical areas in artificial intelligence: world modeling, reinforcement learning (RL), program synthesis, and cognitive science-inspired AI. Its contribution directly addresses key limitations of existing approaches to building intelligent agents that can adapt to complex, dynamic environments.

Traditionally, world models in RL have been dominated by deep neural network architectures such as recurrent neural networks, transformers, and diffusion models. While these "neural network world models" like Dreamer are highly flexible and capable of modeling complex dynamics, they are notoriously data-hungry, requiring vast amounts of training data (often hundreds or thousands of hours of gameplay) and exhibiting poor sample efficiency. Furthermore, they often struggle with generalization and can fail in counterintuitive ways, such as imagining a player walking through walls, even after extensive training.

An alternative approach, which has gained traction recently, involves learning "symbolic world models" represented as source code through program synthesis, often leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs). These "programmatic world models" offer significant advantages in data efficiency and systematic extrapolation, as programs naturally encode abstract, compositional knowledge. However, previous programmatic approaches, such as WorldCoder, have been limited in their scalability. They typically attempt to synthesize a single, monolithic program to describe the entire world's dynamics, leading to a discrete combinatorial search problem that becomes intractable for complex, non-gridworld domains beyond simple text-based or grid-world games.

PoE-World seeks to bridge this gap by combining the strengths of programmatic world models (data efficiency, strong generalization) with a novel modular architecture inspired by cognitive science. The paper draws direct inspiration from the "community of interacting experts" view of the mind, proposing that a world model can be more effectively learned and represented as an aggregate of many small, context-specific expert programs. This modularity is a critical departure from monolithic program synthesis, allowing the system to scale to more complex environments like Atari games, which were previously out of reach for symbolic methods.

Furthermore, the work contributes to the neuro-symbolic AI paradigm by using LLMs for code generation to create symbolic representations. It also integrates hierarchical planning principles, reminiscent of Task and Motion Planning (TAMP), to enable long-horizon decision-making in complex environments with sparse rewards, like Montezuma's Revenge. By demonstrating the learning of the "first symbolic world model for environments of this complexity," PoE-World pushes the boundaries of what data-efficient, generalizable AI agents can achieve, moving towards more human-like learning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of the PoE-World research is to develop intelligent AI agents capable of understanding and adapting to the dynamics of complex environments. To achieve this, the authors aim to create a world model representation that is efficiently learnable, robust enough to handle stochasticity and partial observability, and fundamentally supports effective planning and decision-making.

The motivation for this specific approach stems from the inherent limitations observed in existing world modeling paradigms:

1. **Data Inefficiency of Deep Learning World Models:** Traditional deep learning-based world models, while powerful, demand "vast amounts of training data" compared to humans. For instance, models like Dreamer require extensive experience, making them impractical for scenarios where data is scarce or costly to obtain. Their learning process is not "flexible" enough to update knowledge quickly from sparse observations, and they often struggle with systematic generalization, sometimes exhibiting "failure modes such as imagining that the player can walk through a wall or teleport".

2. **Scalability Challenges of Prior Programmatic World Models:** Recent advancements in program synthesis, particularly those utilizing LLMs, offer an alternate, data-efficient approach by representing world models as source code (e.g., WorldCoder). These models inherently provide stronger generalization. However, their application has been "limited to natural language and grid-world domains." The core issue is that they attempt a "discrete combinatorial search to find a single large program describing everything about how the world works," which becomes computationally intractable and unscalable for the intricate dynamics found in complex, non-gridworld environments like Atari games.

To overcome these challenges, the authors propose a new computational account of learning world models. Their key objective is to introduce a "novel program synthesis method for effectively modeling complex, non-gridworld domains" that leverages the strengths of LLM-based code generation while addressing the scalability bottleneck. The method focuses on learning "context-specific expert programs, which are refined through practice and reused compositionally to support flexible, goal-directed behavior."

In essence, PoE-World aims to provide a solution that is:
- **Data-efficient:** Learns complex world models from "just a few observations" or "brief demonstration."
- **Scalable:** Decomposes the problem into learning hundreds of small programs, overcoming the combinatorial search problem of monolithic programs.
- **Modular and Compositional:** Enables robust generalization to novel situations by recombining learned knowledge.
- **Probabilistic:** Handles stochasticity in environment dynamics.
- **Supports Planning:** Facilitates efficient model-based planning and decision-making.

The ultimate goal is to enable an intelligent agent, given limited demonstrations of a new environment, to "quickly assemble a working world model that compositionally generalizes to new situations."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The PoE-World methodology introduces a novel approach to world modeling that centers on a "Product of programmatic Experts" (PoE) representation and an iterative learning algorithm leveraging Large Language Models (LLMs) for program synthesis.

#### 3.1. World Model Representation: Product of Programmatic Experts (PoE-World)

The core innovation is representing the world model as an exponentially-weighted product of many small, context-specific Python programs, termed "programmatic experts." Mathematically, the probability of the next observation `o_t+1` given the history `o_1:t` and actions `a_1:t` is proportional to the product of individual expert probabilities `p_expert_i` raised to their respective learned weights `θ_i`:
`p_θ (o_t+1 | o_1:t, a_1:t) ∝ Π_i p_expert_i (o_t+1 | o_1:t, a_1:t)^θ_i`
Each `p_expert_i` acts as an independent expert, expressing opinions on specific aspects of the world (e.g., "if player touches skull, player dies"). This modularity addresses the scalability issues of monolithic program representations.

Key elements of this representation include:
- **Object-centric State:** The model assumes symbolic observations derived from an object detector (specifically, OCAtari for Atari games), where the world state is a list of objects, each with attributes like bounding boxes and velocities. This abstracts away from raw pixel data.
- **Factored State Representation:** For tractability, each feature (e.g., `player.x_velocity`) of the next observation `o_t+1` is assumed to be conditionally independent. This allows for separate normalization constants for each feature, simplifying probability calculations.
- **Full-History Formulation:** The model conditions on the full observation and action history `o_1:t, a_1:t` rather than a compressed latent state (as in POMDPs). This choice preserves modularity, as learning new latent variables would necessitate global updates to all experts.
- **Hard Constraints:** To mitigate the "fuzzy" nature of a purely probabilistic model and enforce "physically impossible" scenes, PoE-World learns a collection of Boolean hard constraints `{c_j : O → {0,1}}`. These constraints are applied as a conjunction (or disjunction in practice for complex game physics) to sharpen the model's predictions.

#### 3.2. World Model Learning: Program Synthesis and Weight Optimization

Learning in PoE-World is an iterative, online process that refines the world model as the agent interacts with the environment:
1. **Demonstration Input:** The process begins with a brief demonstration trajectory (e.g., <1 minute of gameplay) that illustrates core causal mechanics, even if it doesn't achieve game success.
2. **Program Synthesis (Step 1):** Large Language Models (LLMs), specifically `gpt-4o-2024-08-06`, are used to synthesize programmatic experts. A small batch of transitions (e.g., `o_t:t+H+1, a_t:t+H`) is converted into natural language descriptions and fed into the LLM.
 - The LLM is prompted in a two-stage process: first, to generate natural language "causal explanations" for observed object changes (e.g., "The player objects that touch an ladder object set their y-axis velocity to -4").
 - Second, these explanations are translated into simple, deterministic Python functions.
 - To enforce modularity, each synthesized expert focuses on changes to a single attribute of a specific object type.
 - Special helper classes (`Obj`, `ObjList`, `RandomValues`, `SeqValues`) are used, with their docstrings provided to the LLM. `RandomValues` ensures that attribute values set by the LLM are interpreted as distributions, allowing for noise and non-zero probabilities over alternatives.
 - Multiple LLM-based "synthesis modules" are employed, each specializing in different aspects of dynamics (e.g., `ActionSynthesizer` for action-object interactions).
 - Hard constraints are synthesized similarly using LLMs.
3. **Weight Optimization (Step 2):** Once experts are synthesized, their scalar weights `θ_i` are optimized using maximum likelihood estimation (MLE) over the observed trajectories. The L-BFGS optimizer with L1 regularization is employed, favoring sparser models.
4. **Pruning (Step 3):** Experts whose weights fall below a threshold (`δ = 0.01`) are removed, refining the model by discarding irrelevant or low-confidence rules. Hard constraints that contradict observations or explain less than 1% of observations are also pruned.
5. **Iterative Refinement (Step 4):** This entire loop (synthesis, optimization, pruning) is repeated as the agent gathers more interaction data, allowing for continuous model "debugging" and improvement.

#### 3.3. World Model Usage: RL in Simulation and Planning

PoE-World's learned world models serve two primary purposes in decision-making:
- **RL Pre-training in Simulation:** The world model acts as a high-fidelity simulator for reinforcement learning algorithms (e.g., PPO). Policies can be learned rapidly in this simulated environment, leading to "warm-started" and more sample-efficient policy learning when transferred back to the real environment.
- **Hierarchical Lookahead Planning (PoE-World + Planner):** For long-horizon, complex tasks (e.g., finding a key in Montezuma's Revenge), a hierarchical planner is implemented, inspired by Task and Motion Planning (TAMP).
 - **Abstract Graph Construction:** The planner first builds an abstract graph where nodes represent high-level "abstract states" (defined by object contact) and edges signify traversability between them. This is achieved by running a low-level motion planner in the *simulated* world model for all pairs of nodes.
 - **High-level Planning:** A breadth-first search (BFS) is used on this abstract graph to find a sequence of subgoals leading to the overall objective.
 - **Low-level Execution and Online Replanning:** A low-level planning agent then attempts to execute each subgoal using a variant of Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) or a greedy search (with a Manhattan distance heuristic) in the world model. This low-level planner incorporates "sticky actions" (repeated action chunks) to improve efficiency. The agent performs online replanning if the current plan becomes invalid in the real environment, and "false" edges are removed from the abstract graph, triggering further world model updates.

This comprehensive methodology allows PoE-World to learn and leverage complex, modular, and interpretable world models for effective and data-efficient agent behavior in challenging environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of PoE-World + Planner focused on its performance in two representative Atari games, Pong and Montezuma's Revenge (MR), along with "Alt" versions (Pong-Alt and MR-Alt) designed to test compositional generalization. The "Alt" versions involve recombining and rearranging objects from the base games without new demonstrations. The key metrics included agent scores, next observation prediction accuracy, and next observation's object attributes prediction accuracy.

#### 4.1. Agent Performance and Generalization

- **Superior Low-Data Performance:** PoE-World + Planner consistently outperformed all baselines—PPO (model-free RL), LLM as Agent (ReAct), and WorldCoder + Planner (symbolic, monolithic)—in the low-data regime across all environments. For Pong and MR, only a brief demonstration (less than 1 minute, often not even successful gameplay) was provided to initialize PoE-World and WorldCoder.
- **Montezuma's Revenge Breakthrough:** PoE-World + Planner was the *only* method capable of achieving a positive score (by collecting the key) in Montezuma's Revenge, both in its base and Alt versions. This is a significant achievement, as Montezuma's Revenge is widely considered a "grand challenge" in RL due to its sparse rewards and complex, long-horizon requirements.
- **Data Efficiency vs. Deep RL:** While PPO eventually surpassed PoE-World on Pong after millions of training steps, PoE-World achieved strong performance with dramatically less training data. For example, PPO required over a million steps to outperform PoE-World, and even with 20 million steps, PPO never scored positively on Montezuma's Revenge, highlighting PoE-World's superior sample efficiency for complex tasks.
- **Compositional Generalization:** The strong performance of PoE-World + Planner on Pong-Alt and Montezuma's Revenge-Alt, without additional demonstrations for these modified environments, directly validates its ability for "compositional extrapolation" and zero-shot generalization to novel game levels and variations.

#### 4.2. World Model Quality

- **Prediction Accuracy:** PoE-World consistently achieved higher next observation and next observation's object attributes prediction accuracies compared to WorldCoder, both on training data and on 1000 random test frames. This indicates a more faithful and robust internal model of the environment dynamics.
- **Role of Hard Constraints:** The inclusion of learned "hard constraints" proved crucial for Montezuma's Revenge. Removing these constraints significantly degraded the agent's planning success (e.g., 2/9 successes without constraints vs. 5/9 with constraints on MR). Interestingly, the raw "next observation prediction accuracy" did not significantly change, suggesting that constraints primarily serve as "damage control," refining predictions enough to make them usable for long-horizon planning rather than fixing fundamental predictive errors.
- **Qualitative Model Superiority:** Qualitatively, PoE-World produced significantly more elaborate world models (4000+ lines of code for Montezuma's Revenge) compared to WorldCoder (<100 lines). PoE-World's models accurately captured complex causal laws, such as player movement constraints, while WorldCoder's models often failed to represent basic mechanics and exhibited "hallucinations" (e.g., non-existent bullet firing). The probabilistic aggregation and pruning mechanism in PoE-World allowed it to create more precise models by down-weighting or removing buggy experts.

#### 4.3. Pre-training Deep RL

- **Accelerated Policy Learning:** The study demonstrated that pre-training a PPO policy within PoE-World's simulated environment significantly accelerated policy learning. The fine-tuned PPO achieved much higher scores earlier in training (e.g., outperforming vanilla PPO by 200k steps) on Pong, effectively providing a "warm-start" for deep RL training. While both converged asymptotically, the pre-training offered substantial initial advantages.

Overall, the findings strongly support PoE-World's effectiveness in learning complex, compositional, and data-efficient world models from limited demonstrations, leading to state-of-the-art performance for symbolic methods in challenging Atari environments and demonstrating superior generalization compared to both deep learning and prior symbolic baselines.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PoE-World represents a significant advancement in the field of artificial intelligence, particularly in the domain of model-based reinforcement learning and neuro-symbolic AI. Its primary significance lies in demonstrating a viable path for learning complex, compositional world models from sparse data, a long-standing challenge for intelligent agents.

### 6. Key Significance

- **Scalability of Symbolic World Models:** PoE-World successfully addresses the fundamental scalability issue that plagued previous programmatic world models. By decomposing a complex world model into a "product of programmatic experts," it enables the synthesis of highly intricate models (e.g., 4000+ lines of code for Montezuma's Revenge) that were previously intractable for monolithic symbolic approaches. This proves that symbolic methods can, indeed, scale to complex, non-gridworld environments like Atari.
- **Data Efficiency and Generalization:** The work showcases unparalleled data efficiency for learning world models in complex environments, requiring only brief demonstrations to achieve strong performance and generalize compositionally to novel, unseen variations of the games. This is a critical step towards human-like learning, which also benefits from rapid learning from few examples and robust generalization.
- **Effective Integration of LLMs:** PoE-World effectively leverages the power of Large Language Models for program synthesis, demonstrating their utility not just for generating natural language but for structuring executable code that captures causal dynamics. The iterative learning and refinement process with weight optimization and pruning ensures the generated programs are relevant and accurate.
- **Cognitive Alignment:** The modular "community of interacting experts" architecture aligns with theories in cognitive science and philosophy regarding the organization of the mind. This alignment suggests that PoE-World's approach could contribute to developing AI systems that learn and reason in ways more analogous to human cognition, fostering explainability and robustness.
- **Practical Utility for Agents:** The learned world models are immediately usable and highly effective for two crucial purposes: direct planning for decision-making (especially with the hierarchical planner for long-horizon tasks) and as high-fidelity simulators for pre-training deep RL policies. This offers a powerful way to "warm-start" RL agents and dramatically reduce their sample complexity in real environments.

### 7. Potential Impact

- **Advancing Model-Based RL:** By providing data-efficient and generalizable world models, PoE-World could unlock new possibilities for model-based RL in domains where data collection is expensive or dangerous (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving, complex simulations).
- **Neuro-Symbolic AI Research:** This work strengthens the argument for neuro-symbolic approaches, demonstrating how the strengths of neural models (LLMs for generation) can be combined with the advantages of symbolic representations (modularity, interpretability, generalization). It could inspire further research into hybrid architectures that bridge the gap between connectionist and symbolic AI.
- **Cognitive Modeling:** The computational model offers a concrete framework for testing hypotheses about how humans might learn compositional world knowledge and plan hierarchically, potentially impacting cognitive psychology and neuroscience research.
- **Interpretable AI:** Programmatic world models, by their very nature, are more interpretable than opaque neural networks. This inherent interpretability could facilitate trust and debugging in AI systems, allowing developers to understand *why* an agent made a particular prediction or decision.

### 8. Limitations Acknowledged

The authors are transparent about several limitations:
- **Symbolic Input Requirement:** PoE-World does not learn directly from raw pixels; it assumes symbolic object-centric observations from an object detector (like OCAtari). Learning visual perception from pixels remains a future challenge.
- **Focus on World Modeling:** The paper primarily focuses on faithfully learning a world model from demonstrated trajectories and does not directly address efficient exploration, decision-making, or reward function learning as standalone problems within the full RL pipeline.
- **Hierarchical Planner Efficiency:** While effective, the hierarchical planner's efficiency and performance could still be improved, as acknowledged by the authors.

### 9. Future Directions

The limitations naturally point to exciting avenues for future research:
- **Integrating Perception:** Learning to extract symbolic representations directly from raw pixel inputs would make the system fully end-to-end.
- **Enhanced Exploration:** The interpretable, program-structured world model could be leveraged with ideas from "software testing and program analysis" to develop novel, more efficient model-based exploration strategies.
- **Synergy with High-Level Models:** Combining PoE-World's low-level object motion models with higher-level abstract world models (e.g., for task planning) could yield more comprehensive and powerful agents.

In conclusion, PoE-World offers a compelling solution to a critical problem in AI, demonstrating that a modular, cognitive science-inspired approach to programmatic world modeling can achieve unprecedented scalability and data efficiency in complex environments, paving the way for more robust and human-like AI systems.
