# Robustness without Wrinkles: Parallel Simulation and Robust MPC for Certified Deformable Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The CORD-SLS framework enables real-time, robust, and certifiably safe manipulation of high-dimensional deformable objects like ropes and cloth under uncertainty. It integrates a GPU-parallel differentiable simulator with contact smoothing, a robust output-feedback Model Predictive Control (MPC) algorithm based on System Level Synthesis (SLS), and uncertainty calibration using Conformal Prediction, demonstrating up to 700 times faster simulation and 100% safety rates in hardware experiments.

## Problem

- Manipulating deformable objects presents challenges due to their high-dimensional, nonlinear dynamics and the absence of compact state representations, making planning and control complex.
- Existing deformable object control methods often lack formal robustness or safety guarantees, especially in contact-rich scenarios and under uncertainties from perception or dynamics.
- Current differentiable simulators for deformable objects struggle with discontinuous gradients arising from contact dynamics, which hinders efficient gradient-based planning and optimization.

## Method

- A GPU-parallel differentiable simulator for ropes and cloth was developed, featuring contact smoothing to provide informative, non-vanishing gradients through contact interactions.
- A robust output-feedback ==Model Predictive Control== (**MPC**) algorithm, based on System Level Synthesis (SLS), was implemented with GPU-parallel acceleration to synthesize controllers that account for process and observation disturbances.
- Conformal Prediction (CP) was employed to calibrate data-driven uncertainty bounds for both dynamics and perception models, providing high-probability safety guarantees for the true system state.

## Results

- The GPU-parallel differentiable simulator achieved up to 700 times faster forward simulation and 2000 times faster dynamics Jacobian computation compared to prior differentiable simulators.
- CORD-SLS demonstrated **100%** safety rates and low goal errors across various contact-rich rope and cloth manipulation tasks, with MPC updates for 300 DoF cloth systems executing in under one second (e.g., **0.758** s/step for folding).
- Hardware validation confirmed successful execution of obstacle avoidance for ropes and cloth folding tasks, maintaining safety despite real-world sensor noise and model discrepancies, using a CP-calibrated perception error bound of **0.03** m.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating a highly efficient, differentiable simulator with contact smoothing effectively resolves gradient discontinuity issues, enabling robust gradient-based planning for contact-rich deformable manipulation.
> - GPU-parallel System Level Synthesis (SLS) can be extended to output-feedback control for high-dimensional deformable systems by leveraging associative operators for efficient observer and controller recursions.
> - Calibrating uncertainty bounds with Conformal Prediction allows for the derivation of explicit reachable tubes and probabilistic safety guarantees, bridging the gap between model predictions and real-world uncertainties.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Manipulation of deformable objects, such as ropes and cloth, presents significant challenges in robotics. These objects often possess high-dimensional, nonlinear dynamics and lack compact state representations, making control and planning complex. Applications demanding such manipulation, including home robotics, logistics, and surgery, necessitate reliable and safe operation, especially in contact-rich scenarios and under uncertainty.

Prior approaches to deformable object manipulation have several limitations. Classical methods, while providing tractability through geometric abstractions, often yield open-loop plans that offer limited robustness to real-world variations. Learned policies, which operate in visual or latent spaces, require extensive training data and typically lack formal robustness guarantees, crucial for safety-critical tasks. Differentiable simulators have emerged as a promising tool for gradient-based planning due to their efficiency. However, existing differentiable simulators often suffer from discontinuous gradients, particularly those arising from contact dynamics, which can impede efficient contact discovery and optimization processes.

In the realm of deformable object simulation, methods like Position-Based Dynamics (PBD) and its extensions offer speed and stability but may sacrifice physical fidelity, especially regarding stiffness and contact. More physically grounded models, such as Cosserat rod and discrete elastic rod formulations, capture complex deformations but their differentiable counterparts can be less suited for contact-rich control due to the need for explicit contact-mode discovery. Differentiable cloth simulators also face challenges with inconsistent gradients stemming from the nonconvexity of contact resolution.

Safety and robustness in deformable object manipulation are areas that have received less attention. While some methods attempt nominal safety or use barrier functions, they often rely on approximate models without accounting for model error, provide only local corrections, or do not scale to high-dimensional or contact-rich scenarios with perception uncertainty. Reachability analysis has been applied to deformable linear objects (DLOs) but has not broadly addressed contact-rich planning or cloth manipulation under perception error. Furthermore, existing safe output-feedback control methods tend to struggle with the high-dimensional state spaces characteristic of deformable objects.

This work, CORD-SLS, aims to bridge these gaps by integrating a GPU-parallel differentiable simulator with contact smoothing, a robust output-feedback model predictive control (MPC) algorithm based on System Level Synthesis (SLS), and data-driven uncertainty calibration. It specifically addresses the need for real-time, robust, and safe control of deformable objects in high-dimensional and contact-rich environments, a domain where current methods often fall short in combining efficiency, physical fidelity, and formal guarantees.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are to develop a real-time control method for safe deformable object manipulation, particularly for ropes and cloth, while addressing significant challenges posed by their complex dynamics, contact interactions, and inherent uncertainties. The core motivations driving this work include:

1. **Addressing the Difficulty of Deformable Object Control:** Deformable objects present challenges due to their high-dimensional, nonlinear dynamics and the absence of compact state representations. Effective manipulation requires navigating these complexities, including deciding where and when to make contact and satisfying various constraints (e.g., obstacle avoidance, goal reaching).

2. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Simulators and Planners:**
 - **Lack of Scalable and Robust Planning:** Traditional planning methods often produce open-loop plans with limited robustness. Learning-based approaches, while flexible, typically lack formal robustness assurances and demand extensive, slow training.
 - **Inefficient Contact Handling in Differentiable Simulators:** Differentiable simulators offer efficient gradient-based planning, but discontinuous gradients arising from common contact formulations hinder effective contact discovery and can lead to planning stagnation. A key motivation is to provide planning-friendly gradients for contact-rich scenarios.
 - **Need for Real-time Performance:** For practical robotic applications, manipulation policies must operate in real-time. This necessitates highly efficient simulation and control algorithms capable of handling high-dimensional systems.

3. **Ensuring Robustness and Safety under Uncertainty:** Real-world environments are characterized by uncertainties in both dynamics (e.g., model inaccuracies) and perception (e.g., sensor noise, estimation errors). Current methods often provide insufficient robustness assurances or struggle to scale safe control approaches to high-dimensional deformable systems. The motivation is to develop a method that explicitly accounts for these uncertainties and provides high-probability safety guarantees.

To achieve these aims, the paper outlines two specific problems:

- **Problem 1: Differentiable contact-smoothed deformable simulator.** The objective is to develop a simulator for deformable linear objects and cloth that is differentiable, GPU-parallel, and provides informative gradients for planning and policy learning, particularly through contact.
- **Problem 2: Reachability-informed MPC for robust deformable manipulation.** The objective is to optimize a nominal trajectory and a causal output-feedback controller. This controller must stabilize the true deformable dynamics (which are uncertain), satisfy state and control constraints, and ensure closed-loop safety against bounded disturbances in both the process and observation models, leveraging the differentiable simulator.

Overall, the motivation centers on closing the gap between scalable planning, accurate deformable contact dynamics, and robust, certifiably safe control for real-world deformable object manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CORD-SLS framework integrates four main components: a GPU-parallel differentiable simulator with contact smoothing, a GPU-parallel output-feedback robust Model Predictive Control (MPC) algorithm based on System Level Synthesis (SLS), an acceleration strategy for model-based Reinforcement Learning (RL) using analytical policy gradients, and a method for calibrating uncertainty bounds using Conformal Prediction (CP).

#### 3.1. Differentiable Deformable Object Simulator with Contact Smoothing

The simulator models deformable objects as nodal systems with `n_v` nodes and `n_u` actuated degrees of freedom, described by a configuration vector `q`. It operates under a quasi-static assumption and utilizes an implicit time-stepping scheme for numerical stability, which evaluates elastic and constraint forces at the next time step. The system dynamics are defined by a balance of inertial, elastic, external, and constraint forces (Equation 3).

- **Constraint Handling:** Holonomic constraints (e.g., gripper attachments) are enforced via a complementarity condition (Equation 4). Contact constraints, which include non-contact, stiction, and sliding, adopt Anitescu’s convex relaxation (Equation 5) to encode the friction cone and its dual.
- **Simulation Solver:** A two-stage procedure, implemented in JAX for GPU acceleration, solves the dynamics. The first stage computes an intermediate configuration under holonomic constraints using Newton's method (Equation 6). The second stage solves an optimization problem to resolve contact interactions (Equation 7), whose KKT conditions recover the contact constraints and linearized dynamics.
- **Differentiating Through Simulation:** Implicit differentiation is employed to obtain gradients through the solver without unrolling iterative steps. This involves computing partial derivatives of the implicit functions defining the intermediate and next configurations (Equations 8 and 9). A custom backward pass leverages the matrix factorizations from the forward pass, ensuring efficient gradient computation (Equation 10).
- **Contact Smoothing:** To mitigate vanishing gradients in contact-rich scenarios, surrogate gradients are introduced. The holonomic constraint stiffness `D_i` is smoothed using an exponential function, and the contact complementarity condition is relaxed by introducing a smoothing parameter `κ`. The smoothed dynamics are used exclusively for gradient computation during planning, while the nonsmooth dynamics are used for physical forward rollouts to maintain fidelity.

#### 3.2. Simulator-Informed GPU-Parallel Output Feedback SLS

This component aims to synthesize robust output-feedback control policies for deformable objects, approximately solving an idealized optimization problem (Equation 11) to ensure closed-loop satisfaction of state and control constraints under process and observation disturbances.

- **SLS Formulation:** The problem is formulated as an SLS synthesis problem (Equation 12), which optimizes a nominal state and control trajectory `(z, v)` and closed-loop response operators `Φ` and `τ`. The approach linearizes the nonlinear dynamics and observation models around the nominal trajectory. Output-feedback reachable tubes, which characterize the propagation of process and observation disturbances, are computed (Equations 12d, 12e) to tighten constraints and ensure the closed-loop reachable set remains within the safe set.
- **GPU-Parallel Implementation:** The method extends GPU-SLS to the output-feedback setting by building on previous work. It decomposes the optimization into nominal trajectory generation, state-feedback controller optimization, and output-feedback controller optimization. The crucial aspect for GPU acceleration is expressing the observer recursions (Kalman recursions, Equation 13) and N independent forward propagations (Equation 14) as associative operators. This allows for GPU-parallel prefix scans, enabling the solution of observer-dependent closed-loop response in logarithmic time, similar to GPU-SLS.

#### 3.3. Accelerating Model-Based RL via Analytical Policy Gradients (APG)

The differentiable simulator facilitates model-based reinforcement learning (RL). It supports two primary mechanisms:
1. **Expert Trajectory Generation:** The simulator can generate expert trajectories, which are particularly beneficial for guiding policy learning in contact-rich deformable manipulation tasks with sparse rewards and intermittent contact.
2. **Analytical Policy Gradients:** By integrating the contact-smoothed simulator as a differentiable layer, analytical policy gradients can be computed (Equation 10). This guides neural control policies towards discovering and maintaining contact, thereby reducing reliance on stochastic exploration and improving the stability and sample efficiency of policy learning.

#### 3.4. Calibrated Reachability Guarantees via Conformal Prediction (CP)

The SLS formulation requires accurate uncertainty sets for the dynamics and observation models. In real-world hardware, these disturbance bounds are often unknown.

- **Uncertainty Calibration:** Conformal Prediction (CP) is used to calibrate an aggregate one-step prediction error in the controller's coordinate space. This is done by measuring the discrepancy between model-predicted observations and actual perception outputs from a held-out calibration dataset.
- **Perceived-State Surrogate Model:** The perception system is assumed to estimate the full rope state from RGB-D images, treating `y_k+1 = q_k + e_k` as a noisy full-state estimate `q_bar_k`. SLS is instantiated on a perceived-state surrogate model, where the process noise `w_k` is scaled by a CP-calibrated radius `r_CP` (e.g., `q_bar_k+1 = f(q_bar_k, u_k) + r_CP * I * w_k`).
- **True-State Containment:** A theorem (Theorem 1) is provided, demonstrating that if the perceived state and control trajectories remain within their CP-calibrated tubes with high probability, then the true state and control trajectories are contained within inflated tubes (obtained by adding the perception error bound `ρB_nq` to the perceived-state tubes) with the same high probability. This allows for tightening perceived-state safety constraints to ensure true-state safety.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates CORD-SLS and its components through simulations and hardware experiments, comparing against baselines like MPPI and model-free RL methods (PPO).

**Simulator Benchmark Comparison (Appendix F):**
- The developed GPU-parallel differentiable simulator achieved competitive accuracy compared to physically more complex models (e.g., DEFORM) while demonstrating significantly higher computational efficiency.
- It accelerated forward simulation by up to 700 times and dynamics Jacobian computation by up to 2000 times compared to prior differentiable simulators (DEFORM, DaXBench). This efficiency is crucial for real-time control.
- While DEFORM showed slightly lower L2 prediction error due to modeling additional physical degrees of freedom (e.g., twisting), both simulators benefited from a learned residual model. The computational speed of CORD-SLS allows for rapid replanning to compensate for modeling inaccuracies.

**Planning Benchmark on Deformable Manipulation Tasks (Table 1, Figure 2):**
The framework was evaluated on four contact-rich deformable manipulation tasks:
- **Lift Rope:** CORD-SLS maintained a 100% safety rate and low goal error (0.0548 m) with an average execution time of 0.050 seconds per step. MPPI was slower and less efficient. The APG variant of CORD-SLS (leveraging analytical policy gradients) achieved comparable speed and the lowest goal error (0.0573 m) while maintaining 100% safety, outperforming PPO, which required expert demonstrations for successful task completion.
- **Drag Rope (with obstacle avoidance):** CORD-SLS achieved a 99.9% safety rate and low goal error (0.0360 m) at 0.070 seconds per step, effectively navigating around an obstacle due to explicit disturbance accounting and reachable tube computation. MPPI exhibited a very low safety rate (1.0%), often failing to avoid the obstacle. Both PPO and APG, while achieving low goal errors, showed constraint violations due to their lack of explicit disturbance handling during execution. APG demonstrated faster convergence to a lower cost than PPO.
- **Cloth Folding and Flattening:** For these high-dimensional tasks (300 DoF), CORD-SLS managed MPC updates in under one second (0.758 s/step for folding, 0.711 s/step for flattening), achieving 100% safety and low goal errors. MPPI was significantly slower with higher goal errors and lower safety rates. Model-free RL methods were deemed impractical for these high-dimensional systems due to prohibitive sample intensity.

**Output Feedback (Figure 3, Appendix E):**
- Simulated experiments demonstrated the robust navigation of a rope over an obstacle under both measurement and dynamical noise.
- The generated robust tubes, accounting for both types of uncertainty, successfully contained the actual rollouts of the system under the robust perception-based controller, illustrating the efficacy of the output-feedback SLS method.

**Hardware Validation (Figure 1):**
- CORD-SLS was validated on real hardware using two KUKA LBR iiwa manipulators and an NVIDIA RTX4070 Ti GPU for the solver.
- A perception pipeline combining SAM3 for segmentation and TAPNext++ for keypoint tracking (with periodic re-grounding) was used to estimate rope and cloth states from RGB-D images.
- Conformal prediction was employed to calibrate a perception-error bound (0.03 m radius covering 87% of residuals) on hardware, which was then used in the robust MPC formulation.
- **Obstacle Rope Manipulation:** The manipulators successfully made contact with the rope and navigated it over an obstacle. Robust tubes were visualized, confirming safety despite real-world uncertainties.
- **Cloth Folding:** The manipulators successfully completed a cloth folding task, demonstrating the scalability and real-world applicability of the simulator and solver to high-dimensional deformable objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

CORD-SLS represents an advancement in the field of deformable object manipulation by integrating several critical components to address long-standing challenges. The significance and potential impact of this work stem from its ability to provide real-time, robust, and certifiably safe control for complex, contact-rich deformable objects.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**

1. **Real-time Deformable Manipulation for High-Dimensional Systems:** The development of a GPU-parallel differentiable simulator with highly optimized forward and Jacobian computation (up to 700x and 2000x faster than baselines, respectively) enables millisecond-speed planning. This efficiency, combined with the GPU-parallel output-feedback SLS solver, makes real-time control of high-dimensional deformable objects (e.g., 300 DoF cloth systems) a practical reality, moving beyond the capabilities of many prior methods.
2. **Enhanced Robustness and Safety Guarantees:** By integrating robust output-feedback Model Predictive Control (MPC) via System Level Synthesis (SLS) and calibrating uncertainty bounds using Conformal Prediction (CP), CORD-SLS offers probabilistic safety guarantees against both model and perception uncertainties. The generation of explicit reachable tubes provides a mechanism to ensure constraint satisfaction and safe operation, a critical feature for deploying robots in safety-critical applications.
3. **Improved Contact Handling and Gradient-based Optimization:** The contact smoothing technique implemented in the differentiable simulator provides informative, non-vanishing gradients through contact transitions. This innovation addresses a common limitation in differentiable physics, enabling gradient-based optimizers to effectively discover and navigate contact-rich manipulation tasks without requiring task-specific skills or extensive manual tuning.
4. **Accelerated Model-Based Reinforcement Learning:** The differentiable simulator's ability to provide analytical policy gradients significantly enhances the efficiency and stability of model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL). This allows for more sample-efficient training of neural manipulation policies, reducing the prohibitive data requirements often associated with learning-based approaches for complex physical interactions.
5. **Demonstrated Hardware Efficacy:** The validation of CORD-SLS on real robotic hardware for diverse rope and cloth manipulation tasks (obstacle avoidance, folding) underscores its practical applicability. The robust performance in the presence of real-world sensor noise and model discrepancies indicates its readiness for deployment in complex environments.

**Potential Impact and Future Directions:**

- **Broadening Robotic Capabilities:** The framework has the potential to expand the capabilities of robots in areas such as home assistance (e.g., laundry, organizing), logistics (e.g., packing irregularly shaped items), and surgery (e.g., manipulating tissue or sutures), where deformable object handling is essential.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work lays the groundwork for several future research directions, including the integration of more sophisticated perception pipelines to handle occlusions and complex topologies, the development of low-rank SLS response operators to manage memory usage for even higher-dimensional systems or longer planning horizons, and further enhancing recursive feasibility guarantees in robust MPC.
- **Advancing Differentiable Physics:** The proposed contact smoothing and efficient differentiation techniques contribute to the ongoing advancement of differentiable physics engines, making them more robust and practical for control and learning applications.

In conclusion, CORD-SLS addresses fundamental challenges in deformable object manipulation by providing an integrated solution that is fast, robust, and certifiably safe, thereby significantly advancing the state of the art and paving the way for more capable and reliable autonomous systems.
