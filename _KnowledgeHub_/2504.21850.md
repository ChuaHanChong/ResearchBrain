# COMPACT: COMPositional Atomic-to-Complex Visual Capability Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> COMPACT introduces a method for generating compositionally complex visual instruction tuning data, enabling MLLMs to achieve 100.2% relative performance compared to a full LLaVA-665K baseline while using only 10% of its data. This approach significantly improves performance on complex benchmarks like MM-Vet (+8.6%) and MMStar (+2.9%) by increasing the information density per training sample.

## Problem

- Existing Visual Instruction Tuning (VIT) datasets often contain simplistic questions that under-utilize the rich visual information in images, leading to data inefficiency.
- The prevailing "quantity over quality" paradigm in MLLM training prioritizes dataset scale without explicitly designing questions for compositional reasoning.
- MLLMs frequently struggle with complex visual reasoning tasks requiring the combination of multiple fundamental visual capabilities due to inadequate learning during training.

## Method

- COMPACT develops a ==four-step data recipe== to generate high-quality, compositionally complex visual instruction tuning data by synthesizing questions that integrate multiple "atomic visual capabilities."
- It establishes a ==taxonomy of 10 atomic visual capabilities== and introduces a =="k-value"== to formally quantify the compositional complexity of questions.
- The method employs Gemini-2.0-Flash with strict prompt engineering and multi-stage quality verification to ensure the generated question-answer pairs naturally integrate the sampled capabilities and maintain high data quality.

## Results

- COMPACT achieves an average relative performance of **100.2%** compared to the full LLaVA-665K baseline, utilizing only **10%** (65K samples) of its data, significantly outperforming other data reduction methods.
- The approach demonstrates substantial performance gains on complex benchmarks, including **+8.6%** on MM-Vet, **+2.9%** on MMStar, and **+13.8%** on InfoVQA compared to models trained on the full LLaVA-665K dataset.
- The generated compositional data exhibits a higher mean k-value of **2.89** (compared to **1.95** in LLaVA-665K) and is more token-efficient, using **46.88%** fewer total tokens per entry.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Scaling data *complexity* through compositional tuning is more data-efficient and effective for MLLM training than merely scaling data *volume*.
> - Explicitly training models on tasks requiring the integration of multiple atomic visual capabilities leads to superior performance on complex, multi-modal reasoning benchmarks.
> - A small, carefully selected subset (approximately 5%) of conventional VIT data is sufficient for MLLMs to acquire general instruction-following and response formatting, separate from core visual reasoning capabilities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) has been characterized by a continuous scaling of Visual Instruction Tuning (VIT) datasets. Datasets such as Cambrian-10M and Eagle 2 represent substantial increases in scale compared to earlier datasets like LLaVA-665K. This trend has generally supported a "quantity over quality" paradigm in multimodal training, where larger datasets are assumed to lead to improved performance across various vision-language tasks. Consequently, many research efforts have focused on simply expanding dataset size.

However, a parallel line of research has begun to question the informativeness of individual training samples, exploring whether smaller, more carefully curated datasets could achieve similar or superior performance. This includes methods for dataset selection, which aim to identify informative subsets of existing large datasets.

The present work positions itself within this evolving landscape by addressing the overlooked aspect of sample complexity and informativeness in VIT. It argues that while visual reasoning often relies on the combination of multiple fundamental visual capabilities (e.g., object recognition, spatial relationships, color attribution), existing VIT datasets frequently contain simplistic questions that under-utilize the rich visual information available in images. These datasets often focus on individual capabilities or limited combinations, neglecting the interplay between these skills required for complex reasoning. This research departs from the sole emphasis on data quantity or simple data reduction by proposing a method to systematically scale the *complexity* and thus the *informativeness* of individual training samples. It aims to develop a data curation method that enhances data efficiency not by reducing data indiscriminately or merely scaling its volume, but by enriching the informational content of each training example.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a more effective and data-efficient method for curating Visual Instruction Tuning (VIT) datasets for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by focusing on the complexity and informativeness of individual training samples.

The motivation stems from several observations within the current state of MLLM training:
- **Blind Scaling vs. Informative Samples:** Despite the rapid growth in scale of VIT datasets, the informativeness of individual training samples has often been overlooked. The prevailing "quantity over quality" axiom has led to an emphasis on ever-larger datasets, but the authors question whether this approach is the most efficient or effective.
- **Compositional Nature of Visual Reasoning:** Visual reasoning tasks frequently require the integration of multiple fundamental visual capabilities. For instance, answering a question like "What color is the object on the left side of the car?" necessitates object recognition, spatial relationship understanding, and color attribution.
- **Simplistic Nature of Existing VIT Datasets:** Analysis of current VIT datasets reveals a prevalence of simplistic questions that often focus on a single or a limited combination of visual capabilities (e.g., "What color is the car?"). Such questions are posited to under-exploit the rich visual information present in images. This can lead to models that struggle with compositional tasks when components or their combined patterns are not adequately learned during training.
- **Inefficiency of Current Data Curation:** Traditional upscaling approaches in VIT are data- and compute-heavy. While data selection methods have shown promise in reproducing performance with smaller subsets, they often treat complexity as a byproduct of scale rather than a controllable metric.
- **Need for a Quantifiable Complexity Metric:** The concept of compositional complexity in vision-language tasks has been loosely defined. A quantifiable measure is needed to systematically assess and control the complexity of training samples.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of the research are:
1. **Demonstrate the impact of sample complexity:** To show that increasing the complexity of training samples enables more effective utilization of the information content within a given dataset, thereby addressing limitations in complexity-agnostic scaling.
2. **Define and analyze a complexity measure:** To introduce the "k-value" as a quantifiable metric for the complexity of a vision-language task, representing the number of atomic capabilities required to answer a question. Furthermore, to analyze the optimal distribution of this complexity in training data for maximal efficiency and performance.
3. **Propose a scalable and efficient data recipe:** To introduce COMPACT (COMPositional Atomic-to-complex Visual Compositional Tuning) as a method for generating synthetic, rich, and informative text questions for images. This recipe aims to significantly reduce the number of training examples required for effective VIT while maintaining or improving performance on complex multimodal benchmarks.

An exploratory experiment, where the k-value was increased in a small subset of VIT data, provided empirical evidence that training on higher k-value datasets leads to improved downstream performance. This result served as a direct motivation for developing the COMPACT framework.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this work, termed COMPACT (COMPositional Atomic-to-complex Visual Compositional Tuning), focuses on constructing a training dataset by scaling the complexity of individual samples. This involves defining foundational visual capabilities and systematically combining them to generate complex, informative training examples.

#### 3.1. Atomic Visual Capabilities and Complexity Metric

The approach begins by identifying a set of fundamental visual skills, termed *atomic capabilities*, which serve as building blocks for complex tasks. These capabilities are chosen based on two criteria:
- **Vision-centric:** They primarily rely on visual information from the image, excluding non-perceptual skills like cultural knowledge or advanced mathematics.
- **Relevance to existing VIT data:** They align with capabilities implicitly or explicitly found in current VIT datasets for fair comparison.

This process yields a taxonomy of 10 atomic capabilities, categorized into:
- **Attribution:** Color, Shape
- **Recognition:** Object Recognition, Action Recognition, Text Recognition, Counting, Spatial Recognition
- **Relation:** Spatial Relationship, Object Interaction, Scene Understanding

The complexity of a given task (or question) is quantified by its *k-value*, defined as the number of distinct atomic capabilities required to answer it. This metric allows for systematic control and analysis of sample complexity.

#### 3.2. COMPACT Data Recipe: A Four-Step Generation Pipeline

COMPACT employs a four-step pipeline to generate multi-capability questions, denoted as $D_{comp}$:

- **Step 1: Capability Sampling:**
 - Random images are selected from the LLaVA-665K dataset.
 - For each image, a target number of capabilities, $k_{gen} \in \{1, 2, 3\}$, is uniformly sampled.
 - A subset of $k_{gen}$ atomic capabilities is randomly selected from the predefined pool of 10.
 - To ensure broad coverage, the sampling process prioritizes capabilities not yet selected for that image and drops duplicate combinations for the same image. $k_{gen}$ functions as a lower bound for the actual k-value of the generated conversation, as some capabilities might be implicitly required or correlated.

- **Step 2: Conversation Generation:**
 - Using Gemini-2.0-Flash as a vision-language model (VLM) generator, a conversational question-answer pair is created for each sampled capability combination.
 - A carefully designed prompt enforces several constraints:
 - Questions must necessitate visual information from the image.
 - Answers must be concise (single word or short phrase).
 - Questions must naturally integrate all specified capabilities without simple conjunctions.
 - Questions must refer only to objects and features present in the image.
 - The generator also provides a confidence score (0-100) for the quality of the generated conversation.

- **Step 3: Quality Verification:**
 - A multi-stage filtering process is applied to ensure data quality and diversity:
 - Questions with uninformative answers (e.g., "unknown," "not visible") or confidence scores below 70% are discarded.
 - Questions sharing more than 60% of words with previously accepted questions for the same image are filtered to avoid near-duplicates.
 - Capability verification is performed, where Gemini-2.0-Flash analyzes if the generated question genuinely requires all $k_{gen}$ specified capabilities.
 - Steps 2 and 3 iterate (up to 10 attempts) until 2-3 high-quality conversations per $k_{gen}$ are collected for each image.

- **Step 4: Dataset Assembly:**
 - The synthetically generated compositional tuning data ($D_{comp}$) is combined with a small, random 5% subset of the original LLaVA-665K VIT dataset.
 - This mixture serves a dual purpose: $D_{comp}$ trains advanced visual reasoning skills through complex questions, while the VIT subset helps the model acquire instruction-following capabilities and adapt to diverse response formats (e.g., multiple-choice, open-ended) required by benchmarks.
 - The size of $D_{comp}$ is determined by the minimum required to match full LLaVA-665K performance, preserving original image content for fair comparison.

#### 3.3. Model, Baselines, and Evaluation

- **Model:** The LLaVA-v1.5-7B-LoRA model (pre-visual-instruction-tuning checkpoint) is used for training. Models are trained for one epoch using official LLaVA-v1.5 LoRA fine-tuning settings.
- **Baselines:** Performance is compared against:
 - **LLaVA-665K:** The full 665K-sample LLaVA-665K dataset.
 - **Random:** A 65K-sample random subset of LLaVA-665K (matching COMPACT's total data volume).
 - **Data Reduction Methods:** 65K-sample subsets curated by EL2N, Perplexity, SemDeDup, D2-Pruning, Self-Sup, Self-Filter, and ICONS.
- **Benchmarks:** Models are evaluated on eight diverse multimodal benchmarks assessing complex visual capabilities: MM-Vet, MME, LLaVA-in-the-Wild, SeedBench2Plus, MMStar, CV-Bench, TextVQA, and InfoVQA. These benchmarks cover perception, cognition, spatial reasoning, OCR, and various question formats.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the effectiveness, efficiency, and characteristics of the COMPACT data recipe:

- **Overall Performance and Data Efficiency (Table 2):**
 - COMPACT, utilizing a total of 65K training samples (32K compositional tuning data + 33K from a 5% LLaVA-665K subset), achieves an average relative performance of 100.2% compared to training on the full 665K LLaVA-665K dataset.
 - This performance exceeds the full LLaVA-665K baseline (100.0%) and significantly outperforms other data reduction methods and a random subset of equivalent size (e.g., 95.4% for the Random baseline, 97.5% for ICONS).
 - Specifically, COMPACT demonstrates notable gains on complex benchmarks such as MM-Vet (+8.6% over LLaVA-665K), MMStar (+2.9%), InfoVQA (+13.8%), and SeedBench2Plus (+3.4%). It maintains competitive performance on TextVQA and LLaVA-in-the-Wild.

- **Data Scaling and Efficiency of Complex Samples (Figure 3):**
 - The performance of COMPACT consistently improves as the amount of compositional tuning data scales from 2K to 32K samples, even when the 5% VIT subset is fixed.
 - Models trained on smaller amounts of compositional tuning data (e.g., 2K-8K samples) often match or exceed the performance of random baselines trained on significantly larger VIT datasets. For instance, a COMPACT model with 2K compositional data outperforms a random baseline trained on 32K VIT data on MM-Vet.
 - This indicates that COMPACT leverages training data more efficiently, with higher-k samples encouraging models to extract more visual information from images.

- **Complexity Distribution Analysis:**
 - **LLaVA-665K:** An analysis of LLaVA-665K shows a mean k-value of approximately 1.95, with the mode at k=2. Around 77% of questions require two or fewer atomic visual capabilities, suggesting an under-exploitation of visual information. A small fraction (0.06%) exhibits very high complexity, while 1.1% require zero visual capabilities.
 - **COMPACT:** The generated compositional tuning data in COMPACT exhibits a higher mean k-value of 2.89 and a mode of k=3. The representation of each capability is also more balanced compared to LLaVA-665K (Appendix Fig. 6). The researchers note that $k_{gen}$ serves as a practical lower bound for the final k-value due to implicit object recognition and correlations among spatial capabilities during generation.

- **Effect of Complexity on Performance (Table 3):**
 - To isolate the impact of complexity, a version of COMPACT (COMPACT$_{\text{llava}}$) was generated with a capability and k-value distribution matching LLaVA-665K, using 16K compositional tuning data.
 - COMPACT$_{\text{llava}}$ achieved 97.6% relative performance, while the original COMPACT with 16K compositional data reached 98.8%. This suggests that at least half of COMPACT's performance gain (1.3% out of 2.6% improvement over the random baseline) can be attributed to the higher complexity inherent in its compositional tuning data.

- **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Impact of k-value Range (Figure 4):** Training with a wider range of $k_{gen}$ values (e.g., $k_{gen} = \{1, 2, 3\}$) yields higher average performance than training with only higher $k_{gen}$ (e.g., $k_{gen} = 3$). This indicates that while complex samples are information-dense, a combination of simple and complex samples is necessary for optimal learning and disentanglement of atomic capabilities.
 - **Impact of Instruction Tuning Data Ratio (Figure 5):** A small amount (1% to 5%) of the LLaVA-665K VIT subset significantly improves the model's instruction-following capabilities and overall performance across diverse question types. Performance gains diminish beyond 5%, suggesting that instruction-following is largely orthogonal to visual reasoning and can be acquired with minimal instruction tuning data.
 - **Token-level Analysis (Appendix Table 9):** COMPACT-generated data is token-efficient, achieving a 46.88% reduction in total tokens per entry compared to an equivalent-sized LLaVA-665K subset. This translates to faster training and lower storage requirements.

- **Failure Mode Analysis (Appendix A.6):**
 - Approximately 21% of generated questions were rejected during quality verification, primarily due to high word overlap (40%), capability mismatch (37.5%), uninformative responses (12.5%), and low confidence scores (10%). Capability mismatch became more prevalent with higher k-values.
 - Remaining failure patterns include overly complex k=3 questions, VLM misidentification errors, ambiguous spatial reasoning errors, and attribute questions for occluded objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented by COMPACT introduces a novel perspective on data curation for Visual Instruction Tuning (VIT), shifting the focus from mere data quantity to the inherent complexity and informativeness of individual training samples. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Challenging the "Quantity Over Quality" Paradigm:** COMPACT empirically demonstrates that by systematically increasing the complexity of training samples, MLLMs can achieve performance on par with or even exceed models trained on datasets ten times larger. This finding challenges the prevailing belief that simply scaling data volume is the primary driver of performance, suggesting that smart data curation is equally, if not more, impactful for certain capabilities.

- **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** The ability to match full-scale VIT performance with less than 10% of the original data budget represents a substantial improvement in data efficiency. This has significant practical implications for reducing computational costs, training time, and storage requirements associated with developing and fine-tuning MLLMs. It makes advanced MLLM training more accessible, particularly for researchers and institutions with limited resources.

- **Improved Compositional Reasoning:** By explicitly focusing on combining atomic visual capabilities, COMPACT trains models to better integrate multiple visual skills. This leads to improved performance on complex, multi-capability benchmarks like MM-Vet and MMStar, which are designed to test integrated reasoning rather than isolated skills. This suggests that compositional tuning can directly foster more robust and versatile visual reasoning abilities in MLLMs.

- **Scalable Synthetic Data Generation:** The proposed four-step recipe for generating compositional tuning data provides a systematic and scalable approach to creating high-quality, complex synthetic data. This is crucial given the challenges and costs associated with manually annotating vast amounts of diverse and complex multimodal data. Leveraging advanced VLMs like Gemini for data generation offers a pathway to continuously enrich training datasets with targeted complexities.

- **Guidance for Future Data Curation:** The introduction of the k-value as a quantifiable metric for task complexity offers a new tool for analyzing and designing VIT datasets. The ablation studies provide insights into the optimal distribution of complexity, suggesting that a balanced range of simple to complex samples is most beneficial for learning. This can guide future efforts in crafting more effective and purpose-built training data.

- **Addressing Limitations and Future Directions:** While impactful, the research acknowledges limitations, such as reliance on closed-source models for data generation and a primary focus on vision-centric capabilities. These limitations also highlight promising avenues for future work, including exploring the interplay of image and text complexity, optimizing capability groupings for different visual content, and investigating the integration with other learning algorithms like reinforcement learning to further enhance multimodal models.

In conclusion, COMPACT offers a scalable and efficient recipe for generating high-quality synthetic data that can significantly improve MLLM capabilities on vision-language tasks, particularly those requiring complex compositional reasoning, while dramatically reducing the necessary data volume.
