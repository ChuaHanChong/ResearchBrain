# Diversity-Enhanced Reasoning for Subjective Questions

> [!summary] Summary
> The Hong Kong University of Science and Technology and Johns Hopkins University developed MultiRole-R1, a two-stage reinforcement learning framework that enhances large reasoning models' ability to handle subjective questions by integrating perspective and token-level diversity during training. MultiRole-R1 achieved an average accuracy gain of 10.6% and an 18.3% diversity gain across tasks, demonstrating generalizability to out-of-domain subjective and objective reasoning problems while reducing response lengths.

## Problem

- Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) optimized with RLVR often suffer from reduced generation diversity, which is critical for subjective questions that inherently have multiple valid answers.
- Existing diversity-enhanced training methods are primarily designed for objective reasoning with a single correct answer, making them unsuitable for the inherent multiplicity of subjective tasks.
- Current approaches for subjective reasoning, such as multi-agent debate or prompting, operate at inference time and lack training paradigms to intrinsically instill diverse, role-dependent reasoning.

## Method

- **MultiRole-R1** is a two-stage diversity-enhanced reinforcement learning framework that integrates both perspective and token-level diversity into LRM training.
- Stage 1 focuses on Multi-Role Reasoning Path Synthesis and Finetuning, where the LRM generates and refines context-relevant roles with conflicting viewpoints, which are then combined and used for supervised finetuning.
- Stage 2 employs Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) with a hybrid reward function that combines verifiable accuracy and a composite token-level diversity metric to further enhance the diversity of generated reasoning chains.

## Results

- MultiRole-R1 achieved an average accuracy gain of **10.6%** and an **18.3%** diversity gain across tasks compared to baselines.
- The framework demonstrated robust generalizability, showing a **14.1%** accuracy gain on in-domain subjective tasks and a **7.64%** improvement on out-of-domain tasks, including a **5.78%** gain on advanced mathematics reasoning (AIME 2024).
- The unsupervised self-consistency filtering method for synthesizing training data performed comparably to supervised ground-truth filtering, offering a practical way to generate diverse data without extensive human annotation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Perspective diversity, enhanced through multi-role supervised finetuning, contributed significantly to accuracy gains for subjective reasoning (7.5% of total 10.6% gain).
> - On-policy RL (GRPO) is more effective for diversity enhancement in subjective tasks than off-policy methods like DPO, as it better handles the multiple equally valid ground truths inherent in subjective questions.
> - Diversity shows a stronger positive correlation with accuracy (average r=0.736) than reasoning length (average r=0.554), challenging the notion that longer reasoning chains always imply better LRM performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) and large reasoning models (LRMs) has seen substantial advancements, particularly with the development of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) capabilities and reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). These techniques have significantly improved performance on objective reasoning tasks such as mathematical problem-solving and code generation. Models like DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI o1-style models exemplify these achievements.

However, a recognized limitation of RLVR is its tendency to degrade generation diversity. This characteristic, while manageable in objective tasks where a single correct answer is sought, presents a significant challenge for subjective reasoning. Subjective questions inherently possess multiple valid answers that often depend on different role perspectives or stakeholder viewpoints. Current diversity-enhanced training approaches, primarily developed for objective reasoning, typically rely on a single ground truth for optimization. This design trains models to converge on a single "correct" answer, making them unsuitable for generating the varied outcomes required by subjective questions.

Existing research addressing subjective reasoning primarily falls into categories such as multi-agent debate (where different agents simulate different perspectives) and prompting-based methods (which guide models through explicit instructions). While these methods explore multiple viewpoints, they often lack specific training paradigms designed to fundamentally enhance a model's inherent ability to generate diverse, role-dependent reasoning chains. Consequently, there has been limited attention to diversity-enhanced *training methods* tailored for subjective tasks.

This research by Wang et al. addresses this gap by proposing a novel training framework that specifically targets diversity enhancement for subjective reasoning. It aims to overcome the diversity degradation issue of RLVR in the context of subjective tasks, where multiplicity of valid perspectives is a core requirement, rather than a deviation from a single correct answer. By introducing and optimizing both perspective diversity and token-level diversity within the training process, the work seeks to move beyond prompting or debate-based approaches to instill a more intrinsic capability for diverse reasoning in LRMs, particularly for questions lacking a universal answer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) on subjective reasoning tasks by addressing the diversity degradation associated with reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR).

The motivation stems from several observations:
- **Limitations of Current LRMs in Subjective Reasoning:** While LRMs excel at objective tasks due to their Chain-of-Thought (CoT) capabilities and RLVR optimization, this optimization method is known to reduce generation diversity. Subjective questions, by definition, lack definitive right or wrong answers and require responses that can vary significantly based on different roles or stakeholder perspectives. Current LRMs, trained with RLVR, are ill-equipped to handle this inherent requirement for diversity.
- **Gap in Diversity-Enhanced Training:** Existing solutions for diversity-enhanced RL training have predominantly focused on objective reasoning. These methods rely on a single ground truth during optimization, thereby training models to find *one* correct answer. This design fundamentally hinders the generation of reasoning that leads to multiple valid outcomes, which is crucial for subjective questions.
- **Ineffectiveness of Existing Subjective Reasoning Approaches:** Current research on subjective reasoning, such as multi-agent debate and prompting-based methods, primarily operates at the inference stage or relies on external scaffolding. These approaches do not integrate diversity directly into the model's training paradigm, limiting their ability to fundamentally alter how LRMs generate varied reasoning.
- **Importance of Perspective and Token-level Diversity:** The authors hypothesize that improving subjective reasoning necessitates incorporating two specific levels of diversity:
 - **Semantic-level diversity (perspective diversity):** This involves training the model to consider and integrate multiple relevant real-world stakeholder perspectives. The motivation here is that roles provide "coherent scaffolding" that ensures diverse outputs are semantically relevant and anchored to real-world viewpoints, rather than simply being random variations.
 - **Token-level diversity:** This aims to broaden the search space of the reasoning chains themselves, enabling the model to explore a wider range of linguistic expressions and thought processes.

Therefore, the key motivation is to develop a training framework that can equip LRMs with the intrinsic ability to generate diverse, context-dependent reasoning paths for subjective questions, thereby overcoming the limitations of RLVR's diversity degradation and the shortcomings of existing inference-only subjective reasoning methods. The work aims to demonstrate that directly enhancing diversity during training, particularly by incorporating role perspectives, leads to improved accuracy and generalizability for subjective tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, MultiRole-R1, is a two-stage diversity-enhanced reinforcement learning (RL) training paradigm designed to improve subjective reasoning in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). It integrates both perspective diversity and token-level diversity.

**Stage 1: Multi-Role Reasoning Paths Synthesis & Finetuning (Enhancing Perspective Diversity)**
This stage focuses on enabling the model to incorporate and reason from multiple relevant stakeholder perspectives.
1. **Multi-Role Exploration and Sampling:**
 - The process begins by identifying 'n' context-relevant roles (e.g., domain experts, stakeholders, personas) for a given subjective question. These roles are generated by the LRM itself using few-shot prompting, specifically encouraging the generation of roles with conflicting viewpoints to maximize exploration of diverse opinions.
 - A selection probability `P(R_i|Q)` for each role `R_i` is computed. This probability prioritizes roles that are relevant to the question while also being contrastive to other existing opinions. The formulation `softmax(E[M(R_i|Q)] + αE_R_i[1 − sim(R_i, R_j)])` encourages selecting roles that offer distinct perspectives.
2. **Self-Consistency Filtering:**
 - For each selected role `R_i`, `k` reasoning paths (`T_R_i`) are sampled from the LRM's decoder with a temperature `τ = 1`.
 - To ensure coherence, a self-consistency filtering step is applied. This involves majority voting among the `k` sampled paths for a given role, keeping only the most consistent answer (`T_R_i`). This decouples role-specific reasoning, allowing conflicting viewpoints to be generated independently while maintaining internal consistency for each role.
3. **Reasoning Structure Generation:**
 - The `m` filtered role perspectives (`T_R_1..., T_R_m`) are then combined into a single, multi-role reasoning chain. To mitigate position bias, random combinations of role orderings (`Π`) are generated. The training data `D_train` is constructed by concatenating these ordered role reasoning paths, forming a longer Chain-of-Thought (CoT) that integrates multiple viewpoints.
 - Two merging strategies are employed based on the task type:
 - **Divergent Merging:** For tasks where different roles are expected to provide different valid answers, the final prediction is derived through a weighted aggregation of the various viewpoints.
 - **Convergent Merging:** For tasks where roles should ideally converge on a consistent answer, a consensus is reached through majority voting within the reasoning sequence.
4. **Multi-Role Supervised Finetuning (SFT):**
 - The synthesized multi-role reasoning paths are used to finetune the base LRM. Data quality control includes rule-based and automatic filtering: removing the top and bottom 10th percentiles of responses by length (to mitigate verbosity bias and reasoning shortcuts) and discarding instances with formatting errors. A final training set of 2,700 entries was produced. The efficacy of this unsupervised self-consistency filtering is compared against a supervised ground-truth filtering approach.

**Pilot Analysis:**
A pilot analysis was conducted using DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B on subjective questions from GlobalOpinion QA and BBQ. This analysis aimed to determine optimal parameters for multi-role reasoning:
- **Reasoning Length:** It was found that "longer reasoning chains" (referred to as "more think" in the paper, achieved by suppressing the end-of-thinking token and appending a continuation string like "wait, I need to think from {role}'s perspective") generally outperformed shorter or regular reasoning lengths, with gains peaking around three "Wait" tokens.
- **Number of Roles:** The number of distinct opinions increased with more roles, with `n=3` roles identified as a turning point where additional roles provided diminishing salient information gain. Consequently, three roles were incorporated in path generation.

**Stage 2: Diversity Enhanced Reinforcement Learning (GRPO) (Enhancing Token-level Diversity)**
This stage builds upon the SFT model to further enhance the diversity of the generated reasoning chains at the token level.
1. **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** MultiRole-R1 adopts GRPO, an on-policy RL algorithm, which optimizes policy by comparing the rewards of a group of candidate outputs for each prompt.
2. **Reward Shaping:** The total reward `R` is a combination of two components: `R = δR_acc + (1 − δ)R_div`.
 - **Multirole-aware verifiable reward (R_acc):** Provided by a verifiable reward model that assesses the correctness of role-based answers. This reward adapts based on whether the ground-truth varies by roles (divergent or convergent).
 - **Diversity reward (R_div):** This is computed from the input text using a composite metric that combines eight existing token-level diversity signals (lexical, token entropy, sentence length, sentence pattern, adjacent sentence, Yule’s K, distinct N-gram, and function word diversity). It acts as a shaping signal to guide exploration.
 - The reward shaping paradigm ensures that the auxiliary `R_div` guides learning without altering the optimal policy, while also preventing learning stagnation in GRPO when all verifiable rewards within a group are uniform (which would result in zero advantage and halt learning without the diversity term).

**Models and Datasets:**
- **Base Models:** DeepSeek-R1 series (R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, R1-Distill-Llama-8B, R1-Distill-Qwen-14B) and Qwen3-8B.
- **Training Datasets (In-domain subjective):** BBQ, GlobalOpinionQA (GLOQA), and ETHICS.
- **Evaluation Datasets (In-domain and Out-of-domain):** BBQ, GLOQA, ETHICS (in-domain subjective); CALI (OOD subjective); CSQA, GSM8K, AIME 2024 (OOD objective). GLOQA and CALI have role-dependent ground truths.

**Metrics:**
- **Accuracy:** Pass@1 accuracy.
 - **Divergent Merging:** For role-dependent ground truths (e.g., CALI, GLOQA), individual role answers are compared to their respective ground truths, and the average correctness is computed.
 - **Convergent Merging:** For role-invariant ground truths (e.g., BBQ, ETHICS, CSQA, GSM8K, AIME 2024), a consensus answer is derived from aggregated role answers via majority voting and then compared to the single ground truth.
- **Diversity:** A composite metric `D_final = Σ ω_i D_i` combining lexical, token entropy, sentence length, sentence pattern, adjacent sentence, Yule’s K, distinct N-gram, and function word diversity. Equal weights (`ω_i`) were adopted for simplicity and interpretability, with human alignment showing high correlation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the efficacy and characteristics of the MultiRole-R1 framework:

- **Overall Effectiveness and Generalizability:** MultiRole-R1 demonstrated an average accuracy gain of 10.6% and an 18.3% diversity gain across tasks compared to baselines. When trained solely on subjective questions, the framework achieved a 14.1% accuracy gain on in-domain (ID) subjective tasks and a 7.64% improvement on out-of-domain (OOD) tasks, which included both subjective and objective questions. Notably, it showed a 5.78% accuracy gain on the challenging OOD advanced mathematics reasoning dataset, AIME 2024. These results suggest that the diversity training is transferable and generalizable to unseen tasks and domains.

- **Suitability of On-Policy RL for Diversity:** On-policy reinforcement learning, specifically GRPO, was found to be more effective for diversity enhancement than off-policy RL algorithms like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). GRPO led to responses that were 19.73% more accurate and notably more diverse than DPO (+2.44% diversity). This is attributed to a fundamental mismatch where DPO's positive-negative pair format struggles to model the multiple equally valid ground truths inherent in subjective questions, unlike GRPO's group-based comparison which benefits from intra-group reward variance.

- **Drivers of Performance Gain:** An ablation analysis indicated that perspective diversity, primarily enhanced during the Supervised Finetuning (SFT) stage, contributed 7.5% to the total 10.6% average accuracy gain. Token-level diversity, introduced through GRPO with diversity reward shaping, accounted for an additional 3.1%. This indicates the critical role of incorporating varied perspectives for subjective reasoning.

- **Diversity vs. Reasoning Length:** Contrary to some recent findings suggesting that longer reasoning chains lead to higher performance, MultiRole-R1 achieved accuracy gains while simultaneously reducing average response lengths. Average response lengths were 1572.9 words for SFT, 849.5 for SFT+GRPO, and 657.8 for MultiRole-R1. This suggests that performance gains are not due to verbosity but rather to enhanced diversity. A per-task correlation analysis revealed a strong positive correlation between diversity and accuracy (average r = 0.736), which was markedly higher than the correlation between reasoning length and accuracy (average r = 0.554).

- **Impact of Perspective Diversity:** The model generated 968 distinct roles during the training data synthesis, encompassing a broad range of categories such as moral philosophies, nationalities, identity groups, and specific individuals. For multiple-choice questions, MultiRole-R1 consistently yielded the highest number of distinct opinions in the model outputs (e.g., GLOQA: 2.07 distinct opinions vs. 1 for base model), indicating a genuine expansion of perspectives rather than minor semantic variations.

- **Efficacy of Unsupervised Filtering:** The unsupervised self-consistency filtering method, used for synthesizing SFT data, performed comparably to, and in some cases outperformed, supervised ground-truth filtering. This finding suggests that sufficient and viable training data can be generated without relying heavily on pre-existing ground truth role examples, which might otherwise limit role diversity.

- **Diversity Reward Shaping Effectiveness:** The diversity reward shaping component in GRPO demonstrated its intended effect. Training with a diversity reward led to a steady increase in pass@k accuracy (a proxy for reasoning path diversity) on the GLOQA dataset, indicating that the approach successfully expanded the solution search space. In contrast, training with only a verifiable reward resulted in a decreasing pass@k, pointing to convergence towards homogeneous reasoning paths.

- **Diversity Metric Alignment:** The composite diversity metric used in this study, employing equal weights for its eight sub-components, showed high alignment with human ratings (correlation coefficients ranging from 0.88 to 0.95 across different models and tasks). This supports the reasonableness of the chosen metric and weighting strategy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces MultiRole-R1, a diversity-enhanced training framework, which represents a notable contribution to the field of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) and their application to subjective reasoning tasks. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Diversity-Enhanced Training for Subjective Reasoning:** MultiRole-R1 is presented as the first framework to specifically introduce and optimize for diversity in the training of LRMs for subjective reasoning tasks. This addresses a critical gap in the existing literature, which has largely focused on objective reasoning or employed inference-time methods for subjectivity. By integrating perspective and token-level diversity directly into the training pipeline, the work provides a foundational approach for developing LRMs that are inherently capable of generating nuanced, multi-faceted responses to complex subjective questions.

- **Enhanced LRM Capabilities for Real-World Subjectivity:** The framework significantly improves LRMs' ability to handle subjective questions, which are prevalent in real-world applications (e.g., ethical dilemmas, cultural understanding, opinion-based queries). By training models to consider diverse, real-world stakeholder perspectives, MultiRole-R1 enables LRMs to produce more comprehensive, contextually appropriate, and acceptable answers, moving beyond single-truth limitations.

- **Robust Generalizability:** The demonstrated performance gains across both in-domain subjective tasks and a variety of out-of-domain tasks, including challenging objective mathematical reasoning (AIME 2024), highlight the robustness and broad applicability of the diversity-enhanced training paradigm. This suggests that cultivating a rich diversity in reasoning for subjective tasks can yield beneficial inductive biases that transfer to and improve performance in other reasoning domains, even those considered purely objective.

- **Re-evaluation of Reasoning Metrics:** The finding that diversity is a more consistent indicator of accuracy than reasoning length challenges the prevailing notion that "thinking longer" necessarily leads to better LRM performance. This could encourage a shift in how reasoning capabilities are evaluated and optimized, emphasizing the quality and breadth of thought processes over sheer verbosity. Furthermore, the framework demonstrates that reasoning efficiency can be improved while enhancing accuracy, contrasting with some previous findings.

- **Practical Framework for Data Synthesis:** The unsupervised multi-role reasoning path synthesis pipeline offers a practical method for generating high-quality, diverse training data without extensive human annotation or reliance on limited ground truth roles. This accessibility could democratize the development of more sophisticated subjective reasoning models.

- **Advancements in Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** The successful application of diversity reward shaping within GRPO provides insights into stable and effective methods for integrating diversity objectives into RL training, particularly when standard reward functions might lead to policy degradation or stagnation. This contributes to the broader understanding of how to mitigate issues like "diversity collapse" in RL-optimized generative models.

In summary, MultiRole-R1 offers a significant advancement in empowering LRMs to navigate the complexities of subjective reasoning. By demonstrating the critical role of diversified training, it sets a new direction for developing more versatile, robust, and human-aligned AI systems capable of engaging with the ambiguity and multiplicity of human thought and opinion.
