# OpenResearcher: A Fully Open Pipeline for Long-Horizon Deep Research Trajectory Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenResearcher introduces a fully open pipeline for synthesizing long-horizon deep research trajectories, addressing limitations of proprietary web search APIs. It enables the creation of large-scale training data at zero search cost, resulting in a 30B parameter model that achieves 54.8% accuracy on BrowseComp-Plus, outperforming proprietary baselines like GPT-4.1 and Claude-4-Opus.

## Problem

- The scarcity of high-quality, long-horizon reasoning trajectories hinders the training of deep research agents capable of complex, multi-step tasks.
- Current data synthesis methods rely on expensive, unstable, and unreproducible proprietary live web search APIs, limiting scalability and controlled analysis.
- Existing datasets typically provide short-horizon tasks, which are insufficient for modeling the sustained exploration and information reconciliation required for deep research.

## Method

- Developed OpenResearcher, an open pipeline that constructs an offline search engine from gold documents and 15 million distractors, enabling cost-free and reproducible trajectory generation.
- Employed GPT-OSS-120B as a ==teacher model==, interacting with the offline environment using explicit browser primitives (`search`, `open`, `find`) to synthesize over 97,000 multi-turn research trajectories.
- Fine-tuned an open-weight ==student model== (NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B) using ==rejection sampling== of correct trajectories to learn deep research behaviors and tool use.

## Results

- The OPENRESEARCHER-**30B**-A**3B** model achieved **54.8%** accuracy on the BrowseComp-Plus benchmark, a +34.0 percentage point improvement over its base model, surpassing proprietary models like GPT-4.1 (**36.4%**) and Claude-4-Opus (**36.8%**).
- The offline retriever enabled 5.76 million search requests at zero cost, saving an estimated 5,760to5,760 to 5,760to28,800 compared to using online APIs, while offering deterministic behavior.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of one-time online bootstrapping for corpus coverage and the substantial performance benefits derived from explicit browser tools compared to a `search-only` agent.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling corpus construction from trajectory generation and operating in an offline environment enables scalable, cost-effective, and reproducible synthesis of deep research data.
> - Modeling browsing explicitly with minimal primitives (`search`, `open`, `find`) is crucial for teaching nuanced information-seeking behaviors and significantly improves agent performance.
> - Training on both successful and unsuccessful trajectories provides valuable structural and tool-use supervision, indicating that filtering only for correct final answers may not be strictly necessary for effective learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the broader context of developing advanced reasoning capabilities in large language models (LLMs), particularly for complex, long-horizon tasks. Since the introduction of models like DeepSeek-R1, there has been increasing interest in collecting extensive reasoning trajectories from large reasoning models (LRMs) to post-train smaller models through supervised fine-tuning (SFT). Efforts such as OpenThoughts, OpenMathReasoning, and OpenCodeReasoning exemplify this trend.

A key frontier in LLM capabilities involves "deep research agents," which are systems designed for iterative search, aggregation of evidence, and multi-step reasoning. These agents differ from those tackling short-horizon tasks (e.g., multi-hop QA requiring 2–5 rounds of retrieval) by necessitating sustained exploration across numerous tool calls, reconciliation of diverse information sources, and the capacity to determine when sufficient evidence has been gathered for a conclusive answer. The primary challenge in training these agents lies in the scarcity of high-quality, long-horizon trajectories that accurately reflect realistic web browsing and research behaviors.

Existing methods for generating such trajectories often rely on proprietary live web search APIs (e.g., Google Search), as seen in systems like WebExplorer and MiroThinker. This reliance introduces several limitations:
1. **Cost:** Large-scale data synthesis becomes expensive, as every search, including those on failed paths, incurs API costs.
2. **Instability and Reproducibility:** The dynamic nature of the live web makes data pipelines difficult to reproduce consistently over time.
3. **Limited Controlled Analysis:** The constantly changing environment makes it challenging to conduct controlled analyses of internal search events, such as precisely tracking when relevant evidence is retrieved or missed.

OpenResearcher aims to address these identified limitations by offering a reproducible, cost-effective, and analytically amenable pipeline for synthesizing the necessary long-horizon deep research trajectories. It seeks to provide an open-source solution that rivals the performance of proprietary systems in this domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation of this research is to overcome the limitations of current deep research trajectory synthesis methods, particularly the high cost, lack of reproducibility, and difficulty in controlled analysis associated with reliance on live web APIs. The authors explicitly pose the central question: "How can we synthesize high-quality, long-horizon deep research trajectories in a scalable, low-cost, reproducible, and analytically useful manner?"

This question drives the following key objectives:

1. **Scalable and Cost-Effective Trajectory Synthesis:** To develop a pipeline that significantly reduces the financial burden of generating large volumes of deep research trajectories. This is motivated by the prohibitive costs of proprietary web search APIs, where even unsuccessful search attempts incur charges.
2. **Reproducibility of Data Generation:** To ensure that the trajectory synthesis process is consistent and can be replicated across different instances and over time. The instability of the live web is a significant hindrance to reproducibility, making consistent training data generation challenging.
3. **Facilitation of Controlled Analysis:** To create an environment where the internal workings of deep research agents can be meticulously studied and understood. Unlike live-web settings where search events are influenced by an ever-changing environment, an offline setup allows for precise tracking of evidence surfacing, opening, and missing.
4. **Generation of Long-Horizon Trajectories:** To produce trajectories that accurately reflect the complexity and multi-step nature of deep research, often involving dozens or even hundreds of tool calls. Existing datasets are typically short-horizon, which is insufficient for training models capable of extended exploration and complex reasoning.
5. **Development of an Explicit Browsing Model:** To move beyond simple document retrieval and model the nuanced human behaviors involved in web research, such as identifying candidate sources, inspecting full document content, and locating specific passages. This aims to enable the teacher model to learn not just *what* to retrieve, but *how* to interact with information sources.
6. **Creation of an Open-Source Ecosystem:** To release the entire pipeline, including synthesized trajectories, model checkpoints, and the offline search environment, to foster community research and development in search supervision, tool design, and failure analysis for deep research agents.

By addressing these objectives, the research aims to democratize access to high-quality training data for deep research agents, enabling the development of open-weight models that can compete with or surpass proprietary systems.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The OPENRESEARCHER pipeline is designed around two core principles: decoupling corpus construction from trajectory generation, and explicitly modeling browsing behavior with minimal primitives in an offline environment. The methodology proceeds in three main stages:

#### 3.1. QA Question Collection

- **Source Selection:** The authors selected questions from MiroVerse-v0.1, a dataset specifically designed to require long-horizon, multi-hop reasoning over heterogeneous evidence, deemed more suitable than standard benchmarks (e.g., 2WikiMultiHopQA, NQ) which often have short retrieval paths and clear evidence.
- **Sampling and Post-processing:** A random sample of approximately 6,000 question-answer pairs (10% of MiroVerse-v0.1) was extracted. These were then post-processed to normalize answers into concise, verifiable forms suitable for evaluation and corpus bootstrapping.
- **Trajectory Regeneration:** Despite MiroVerse providing partial trajectories, they were not used directly due to issues such as evidence not fully supporting answers, short search traces, and inconsistent tool-use patterns. Instead, all trajectories were regenerated from scratch.

#### 3.2. Offline Search Engine Construction

This stage ensures that the relevant evidence is retrievable in the offline environment, minimizing ambiguity during trajectory synthesis.
- **Gold Document Retrieval via Online Bootstrapping:**
 - **Purpose:** To guarantee corpus coverage of answer-supporting documents. This is a one-time online step.
 - **Process:** For each of the 6,000 QA pairs, search queries were constructed by concatenating the question and its reference answer to enhance recall. Web content was then retrieved using the Serper API.
 - **Output:** 10,000 "gold documents" (containing sufficient evidence for the ground-truth answer) were extracted after cleaning and deduplication.
- **Offline Corpus Construction:**
 - To simulate realistic web complexity and scale, the 10,000 gold documents were merged with 15 million documents (approximately 10 trillion tokens) sourced from FineWeb. FineWeb documents serve as distractors, while gold documents provide the necessary evidence.
- **Corpus Indexing:**
 - For efficient retrieval, all documents in the combined corpus were embedded using Qwen3-Embedding-8B (with last-token pooling and an empty passage prefix).
 - These embeddings were then indexed using FAISS, enabling the offline search engine to return ranked documents in response to natural language queries, mimicking a live web search API.

#### 3.3. Trajectory Synthesis

- **Explicit Browser Primitives:** To enable long-horizon deep research, the authors model browsing explicitly using three minimal operations, implemented as tools:
 - `Search`: Returns the top-K results (title, URL, snippet) for a given query, facilitating broad information retrieval.
 - `Open`: Fetches the full content of a document from a URL, mimicking a user clicking a link.
 - `Find`: Locates exact string matches within the currently opened document, crucial for named-entity lookup and factual verification.
- **Teacher Model and Interaction:** GPT-OSS-120B was employed as the teacher model. It interacts with the offline search engine using the three browser tools, instructed to reason step-by-step before each tool call and terminate only upon confidence in a final answer. The teacher model does not have access to the reference answer during generation.
- **Trajectory Generation Procedure:** For each QA pair, 16 trajectories were generated with different random seeds, allowing for diverse reasoning paths. Each trajectory had a maximum context length of 128K tokens and an interaction budget of up to 150 turns (retrieving top-10 documents per step). A temperature of 1.0 and top-p of 0.95 were used to promote exploratory reasoning.
- **Filtering:** Lightweight filtering was applied to remove trajectories exceeding context length, containing malformed tool calls, or failing to reach a conclusive answer within the budget. This process yielded over 97,000 trajectories, including a substantial number with 100+ tool calls.

#### 3.4. Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Evaluation

- **Student Model Training:** A base model, NVIDIA-Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B-Base-BF16, was fine-tuned using supervised learning. Training data was curated by applying rejection sampling, retaining only trajectories that led to correct final answers (approximately 55,000 trajectories). Megatron-LM was used for distributed training on 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs for 8 hours, with sequences pre-packed to a 256K token context length.
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:**
 - **Closed-web:** BrowseComp-Plus (using the officially released corpus and a Qwen3-Embedding-8B FAISS index for offline search).
 - **Open-web:** BrowseComp, GAIA, and xbench-DeepSearch (all relying on the Serper API for online search).
- **Metrics and Judging:** Exact-match accuracy against reference answers was used. GPT-4.1 served as an LLM-based judge to determine the correctness of final answers.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings concerning the performance of OPENRESEARCHER, the characteristics of the synthesized trajectories, cost efficiency, and insights from ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Performance on Deep Research Benchmarks

- **BrowseComp-Plus (Closed-Web):** OPENRESEARCHER-30B-A3B achieved 54.8% accuracy, representing a +34.0 percentage point improvement over the base Nemotron-3-Nano-30B-A3B model (20.8%). This performance surpassed strong proprietary baselines such as GPT-4.1 (36.4%), Claude-4-Opus (36.8%), and DeepSeek-R1 (16.4%). These results indicate the efficacy of SFT on the synthesized long-horizon trajectories.
- **Open-Web Deep Research Benchmarks:** On live-web benchmarks, OPENRESEARCHER-30B-A3B demonstrated competitive performance: 26.3% on BrowseComp, 64.1% on GAIA, and 65.0% on xbench-DeepSearch. It notably outperformed existing open-source deep research systems (e.g., ASearcher-QwQ-32B at 5.2%/52.8%/42.0% and WebDancer-QwQ-32B at 3.8%/51.5%/39.0%), despite being trained solely on offline synthesized data. This suggests generalization capabilities to dynamic, real-world search environments.

#### 4.2. Analysis of Synthesized Trajectories

- **Overall Statistics:** Over 97,000 trajectories were synthesized. Successful trajectories averaged 38.4 tool calls, while failed trajectories averaged 71.7, suggesting that failures stemmed more from inefficient search strategies than insufficient exploration.
- **Tool Usage Distribution:** Failed trajectories exhibited a higher number of `search` operations (48.7 vs. 22.1 for successful ones), indicating a tendency to repeatedly reformulate queries without converging. `Open` calls were also moderately higher in failed trajectories (19.6 vs. 13.4), while `find` usage was similar.
- **Reasoning Horizons:** Successful trajectories were concentrated in the 10-40 tool-call range, though a substantial portion extended beyond 100 tool calls, up to the maximum horizon of 185, exposing downstream models to diverse reasoning complexities.
- **Pass@k Analysis:** Pass@1 was 0.567, increasing to 0.792 at Pass@16, indicating solution diversity and that many questions are solvable along multiple reasoning paths. The per-question solve-rate distribution was bimodal, with many questions being either very hard or very robustly solvable.

#### 4.3. Cost Efficiency

- The offline retriever incurred $0 cost for 5.76 million search requests, in contrast to estimated costs of $5,760 to $28,800 for using online APIs like Serper or SerpAPI. This highlights significant cost savings. Additionally, the offline approach provided advantages such as no rate limits, deterministic behavior, and independence from proprietary infrastructure.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies and Discussion

- **RQ1: Correctness as a Filtering Signal:** Training the student model on only correct, only incorrect, or all synthesized trajectories resulted in comparable downstream BrowseComp-Plus accuracies (54.81%, 55.06%, and 54.46% respectively). This suggests that even failed trajectories provide valuable structural and tool-use supervision.
- **RQ2: Corpus Coverage through Bootstrapping:** One-time online bootstrapping of gold documents proved critical. Removing gold documents from the corpus drastically reduced the gold-document hit rate (from 29.54% to 1.73%), trajectory accuracy (56.86% to 43.81%), and downstream BrowseComp-Plus accuracy (54.81% to 6.35%). This demonstrates its essential role in effective offline synthesis.
- **RQ3: Turn Budget:** Both accuracy and gold hit rate increased with higher turn budgets on BrowseComp-Plus, showing benefits of long-horizon exploration. However, improvements plateaued beyond approximately 100 turns, indicating diminishing returns for excessively long horizons.
- **RQ4: Explicit Browser Tools:** The use of explicit browser tools (search, open, find) significantly impacted performance. A `search-only` agent performed poorly (43.86% accuracy). Adding `open` led to a substantial improvement (56.39%), demonstrating the necessity of document access. Further adding `find` boosted accuracy to 62.17%, improved gold-document hit rates, and reduced tool calls and average token usage, affirming the value of in-page evidence localization.
- **RQ5: Retrieval Success vs. Final Answer Accuracy:** Merely surfacing a gold document in search results (`search-hit`) was a weaker predictor of correctness (P(correct | search-hit) = 61.84%) than explicitly opening it (`open-hit`, P(correct | open-hit) = 86.72%). While almost all correct trajectories involved gold-evidence exposure (P(search-hit | correct) = 99.38%, P(open-hit | correct) = 95.01%), retrieval alone did not guarantee a correct answer. This differentiates retrieval failure from reasoning failure over retrieved evidence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The OPENRESEARCHER project contributes to the field of large language models and autonomous agents in several ways, with potential impacts across research and development:

1. **Enhanced Reproducibility and Cost-Effectiveness:** By shifting the computationally intensive search-and-browse loop from live web APIs to a controllable, offline environment, OPENRESEARCHER significantly reduces the cost and improves the reproducibility of large-scale deep research trajectory synthesis. This mitigates reliance on proprietary infrastructure and variable online content, making the process more accessible and consistent for researchers.
2. **Advancement in Deep Research Agent Training:** The synthesized long-horizon trajectories, which include complex multi-turn interactions and a substantial tail exceeding 100 tool calls, prove effective for post-training open-weight deep research agents. The demonstrated performance gains, particularly on BrowseComp-Plus and competitive results on open-web benchmarks, suggest that high-quality offline data can yield models capable of challenging real-world tasks.
3. **Introduction of an Explicit Browsing Abstraction:** The proposed minimal browser abstraction, comprising `search`, `open`, and `find` operations, provides a structured and intuitive interface for modeling realistic information-seeking behaviors. This allows the teacher model to learn more nuanced interaction patterns beyond simple document retrieval, supporting multi-scale knowledge discovery from broad search results to specific in-page evidence.
4. **Empirical Insights for Pipeline Design:** The systematic analyses conducted through ablation studies offer practical insights into key design choices for deep research pipelines. These include findings on the utility of training with both correct and incorrect trajectories, the necessity of one-time online bootstrapping for corpus coverage, the diminishing returns of excessively long turn budgets, the critical role of explicit browser tools, and the distinction between evidence retrieval success and final answer accuracy. Such insights can guide future research in data construction, tool engineering, and failure analysis.
5. **Community Empowerment and Open Science:** The commitment to releasing the pipeline, synthesized trajectories, model checkpoints, and the offline search environment as open-source assets is a significant contribution. This aims to democratize access to advanced deep research agent development, fostering systematic study of search supervision and accelerating community-wide progress in the domain.
