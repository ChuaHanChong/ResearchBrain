# MMaDA: Multimodal Large Diffusion Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> MMaDA introduces a unified diffusion architecture that simultaneously handles textual reasoning, multimodal understanding, and text-to-image generation through a modality-agnostic design. It leverages novel post-training methods, including Mixed Long Chain-of-Thought fine-tuning and a Unified Reinforcement Learning algorithm (UniGRPO), to achieve competitive performance across these diverse tasks, notably improving reasoning and factual consistency.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal large language models (MLLMs) often rely on modality-specific components or hybrid autoregressive/diffusion architectures, leading to architectural complexity and inconsistencies.
- There is a critical gap in exploring effective post-training methodologies for non-autoregressive, unified multimodal diffusion models, particularly concerning complex reasoning capabilities.
- Many current unified models primarily focus on generic understanding and generation, often lacking explicit mechanisms for knowledge-intensive reasoning and maintaining factual consistency.

## Method

- **MMaDA** developed a unified ==diffusion architecture== that employs a ==shared probabilistic formulation== and ==modality-agnostic tokenization== (using ==LLaDA== for text, ==MAGVIT-v2== for images) for both text and visual data.
- Introduced ==Mixed Long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) fine-tuning==, curating a high-quality dataset of unified CoT trajectories to enhance textual and multimodal reasoning abilities.
- Pioneered ==Unified Reinforcement Learning (UniGRPO)==, a ==policy-gradient-based RL algorithm== specifically adapted for ==diffusion foundation models== to improve performance on knowledge-intensive tasks and complex generation scenarios.

## Results

- **MMaDA-8B** achieved comparable or superior performance against dedicated understanding-only models (e.g., LLaVA-v1.5) and consistently outperformed other unified models across various multimodal understanding benchmarks.
- Attained the highest CLIP Score and ImageReward among both generation-only models (e.g., SDXL) and other unified models for text-to-image generation, demonstrating superior compositionality and world knowledge-aware generation.
- Showcased strong textual reasoning, outperforming the diffusion-based LLaDA-8B on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH) and achieving performance comparable to strong autoregressive baselines (e.g., Qwen2-7B, LLaMA3-8B) on general language tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A truly unified diffusion architecture can effectively serve as a general-purpose foundation model for multimodal intelligence, challenging the prevailing reliance on autoregressive or hybrid approaches.
> - Sophisticated post-training strategies, such as Cold Start Long-CoT fine-tuning and a diffusion-adapted RL algorithm (UniGRPO), are critical for boosting reasoning, factual consistency, and overall performance in non-autoregressive multimodal models.
> - Diffusion models' inherent parallelism and masked token prediction objective provide significant efficiency advantages and architectural flexibility, naturally supporting tasks like inpainting and extrapolation across modalities.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen a rapid evolution from large language models (LLMs) to increasingly sophisticated multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs). Initial successes in natural language processing (NLP) with models like ChatGPT and DeepSeek-R1 paved the way for integrating other modalities, notably vision, to create models capable of both understanding and generating diverse data types.

The current research landscape for MLLMs can be broadly categorized:
- **Early Multimodal Approaches:** These typically combined separate language models and diffusion models, handling discrete (text) and continuous (images) modalities distinctly. Examples often involved using an LLM to generate text prompts for a diffusion model to create images.
- **Autoregressive (AR) Models:** A significant line of work has simplified architectures by training a single transformer with a next-token prediction objective. These models aim for a unified framework where all modalities are tokenized into a shared discrete space and generated sequentially (e.g., Emu3, Janus, Chameleon). While powerful for text, they can sometimes struggle with the high-fidelity and parallel generation capabilities often associated with visual tasks.
- **Hybrid AR + Diffusion Models:** Some approaches combine modality-specific training objectives within a shared architecture. For instance, models like Show-o and Transfusion leverage autoregressive modeling for text and diffusion modeling for vision, attempting to harness the strengths of both paradigms. However, these often maintain separate generation mechanisms for different modalities.

**MMaDA distinguishes itself by addressing a critical gap:** while prior work has extensively focused on architectural design and pre-training strategies for unified multimodal foundation models, there has been a "critical gap in the exploration of post-training methodologies, particularly in non-autoregressive settings." Many existing unified models also primarily focus on generic understanding and generation, often lacking explicit mechanisms for complex reasoning and factual consistency.

MMaDA tackles this by introducing a **unified diffusion architecture** that applies a *shared probabilistic formulation* and a *modality-agnostic design* for both text and vision, moving beyond hybrid or AR-only approaches. Crucially, it pioneers **novel post-training strategies** specifically designed for this unified diffusion framework: a **Mixed Long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) fine-tuning** to enhance reasoning and a **Unified Reinforcement Learning (UniGRPO)** algorithm to align generation and reasoning with diversified rewards. By doing so, MMaDA aims to bridge the gap between pre-training and effective post-training in a truly unified, diffusion-centric multimodal model, challenging the dominance of autoregressive models in this space.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the MMaDA project is to introduce a novel class of multimodal diffusion foundation models capable of achieving superior and balanced performance across diverse domains, specifically textual reasoning, multimodal understanding, and text-to-image generation.

The core motivations driving this research are:
- **Unified and Modality-Agnostic Design:** Existing multimodal models often rely on modality-specific components or hybrid architectures (e.g., AR for text, diffusion for images), leading to architectural complexity and potential inconsistencies. MMaDA aims to simplify this by adopting a *single unified diffusion architecture* with a shared probabilistic formulation and modality-agnostic design, eliminating the need for separate components and ensuring seamless integration.
- **Addressing the Post-Training Gap in Diffusion Models:** While diffusion models have shown immense promise in generative tasks, their application in unified multimodal *reasoning* and the development of effective post-training strategies for *non-autoregressive* settings remain underexplored. MMaDA is motivated to systematically investigate and fill this gap, offering methodologies that can enhance reasoning and generation capabilities within a diffusion framework.
- **Enhancing Reasoning and Factual Consistency:** Traditional autoregressive MLLMs often struggle with complex, knowledge-intensive reasoning tasks and maintaining factual consistency in generation. The authors propose that structured reasoning processes, particularly through Chain-of-Thought (CoT), combined with reinforcement learning, can significantly improve these aspects. The goal is to integrate these mechanisms effectively into a unified diffusion model.
- **Leveraging Diffusion's Strengths for Generalist Models:** Diffusion models inherently offer advantages such as global context modeling and parallel generation. The research is motivated to harness these strengths to create a general-purpose multimodal foundation model that not only generates high-quality content but also excels at complex understanding and reasoning tasks, potentially outperforming specialized or hybrid models.
- **Developing a Holistic Framework:** MMaDA seeks to provide a comprehensive framework that integrates architectural innovation with advanced training paradigms, offering a blueprint for future research in unified multimodal diffusion models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MMaDA's methodology is characterized by three key innovations applied across its training pipeline: a unified diffusion architecture, a mixed long chain-of-thought (CoT) fine-tuning strategy, and a novel unified reinforcement learning algorithm called UniGRPO.

#### 3.1. Pre-training with Unified Diffusion Architecture and Objective

- **Data Tokenization:** To create a truly unified framework, MMaDA employs a consistent discrete tokenization strategy for both text and visual data. Text is tokenized using the LLaDA tokenizer, while images are converted into sequences of discrete semantic tokens using a pre-trained image quantizer based on MAGVIT-v2 (from Show-o). This transforms a 512x512 image into 1024 discrete tokens.
- **Unified Probabilistic Formulation:** MMaDA frames its core task as a mask token prediction problem for both image and text tokens. The model is trained with a single, unified cross-entropy loss function ($L_{unify}$) that is computed only on the masked tokens. This objective aligns the noise corruption and semantic recovery processes across modalities, fostering effective cross-modal interactions during pre-training. The forward diffusion process progressively corrupts data by masking tokens, and the model learns to predict the original masked tokens from their noised versions.

#### 3.2. Post-Training with Mixed Long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Fine-tuning

This stage addresses the model's ability to reason and aligns reasoning processes across modalities.
- **Cold Start Long-CoT Data Curation:** A compact, high-quality dataset of long CoT trajectories is curated for textual reasoning, multimodal reasoning, and text-to-image generation. This data adheres to a *unified CoT format*: `|<special_token>| <reasoning_process> |<special_token>| <result>`. This format is task-agnostic, bridging modality-specific outputs and facilitating knowledge transfer.
- **Diversity, Complexity, and Accuracy:** Open-source LLMs/VLMs are used to generate diverse reasoning trajectories, which are then filtered by state-of-the-art verifiers to ensure quality, accuracy, and depth. The focus is on reasoning-intensive tasks (e.g., mathematical problem-solving) and world-knowledge-aware image generation.
- **Mixed Long-CoT Fine-tuning:** The pre-trained MMaDA model is jointly optimized across heterogeneous tasks. The original prompt is preserved, while tokens in the result are independently masked. The concatenated input (prompt and masked result) is fed into the mask predictor to reconstruct the masked regions, thereby learning to recover masked tokens while aligning with the prompt and reasoning logic.

#### 3.3. Post-Training with Unified Reinforcement Learning (UniGRPO)

To further enhance performance on knowledge-intensive tasks and complex scenarios, MMaDA introduces UniGRPO, a policy-gradient-based RL algorithm specifically for diffusion foundation models.
- **Challenges of Adapting AR GRPO to Diffusion:** The authors identify three critical challenges: local masking dependency (log-likelihoods valid only in masked regions), mask ratio sensitivity (diffusion dynamics depend on masking patterns), and non-autoregressive sequence-level likelihoods. Existing solutions (e.g., LLaDA's Monte Carlo sampling, d1's fixed mask ratio) are computationally expensive or suboptimal.
- **Unified Formulation for Diffusion GRPO:** UniGRPO addresses these challenges with:
 1. **Structured Noising Strategy:** For each response, a masking ratio $p_i \in$ is uniformly sampled, and a perturbed version $~o_{i,p}$ is constructed. The random seed for $p_i$ varies across gradient steps, exposing the model to various stages of denoising and leveraging multi-step generative power. Questions remain unmasked.
 2. **Efficient Log-Likelihood Approximation:** The expected per-token log-likelihood is approximated by averaging over masked tokens.
 3. **Policy Gradient Objective:** The UniGRPO objective integrates clipped surrogate rewards (similar to PPO) with a KL regularization term between the current and reference policies, maximizing rewards while preventing drastic policy shifts.
- **Diversified Reward Modeling:** UniGRPO utilizes a unified rule-based reward system tailored for different tasks:
 - **Textual Reasoning:** Correctness reward (2.0) and format reward (0.5).
 - **Multimodal Reasoning:** Correctness, format, and a scaled CLIP reward (0.1 * CLIP score) for text-image alignment.
 - **Text-to-Image Generation:** Scaled CLIP reward and ImageReward (for human preference alignment), both scaled by 0.1.

#### 3.4. Flexible Sampling Strategies at Inference Time

- **Semi-Autoregressive Sampling for Text:** Adopts LLaDA's approach, partitioning the output sequence into blocks and generating tokens from left to right. Within each block, tokens with the lowest confidence are iteratively unmasked, following a linear masking schedule. This yields more detailed descriptions than fixed-length generation.
- **Parallel Non-Autoregressive Sampling for Image:** Uses a low-confidence remasking strategy with a cosine noise schedule (consistent with MAGVIT-v2), treating the entire output sequence as a single generation block. Classifier-free guidance is applied.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

MMaDA-8B demonstrates strong generalization capabilities and achieves state-of-the-art or highly competitive performance across a diverse range of multimodal tasks.

- **Multimodal Understanding (Table 2):**
 - MMaDA-8B achieves comparable or superior results against dedicated understanding-only models (e.g., LLaVA-v1.5, InstructBLIP, Qwen-VL-Chat) across benchmarks like POPE, MME, Flickr30k, VQAv2, GQA, MMMU, and MMB.
 - It consistently outperforms other unified understanding & generation models (e.g., SEED-X, DreamLLM, Show-o, Gemini-Nano-1) on several key metrics, showcasing the effectiveness of its post-training stages.
 - This marks a significant achievement as the first demonstration of a diffusion-based MLLM exhibiting such strong understanding capabilities, highlighting the potential of unified diffusion architectures.

- **Text-to-Image Generation (Table 3):**
 - MMaDA-8B achieves the highest CLIP Score and ImageReward among both generation-only models (e.g., SDXL, DALL-E 2) and other unified models (e.g., Janus, Show-o), directly attributable to the UniGRPO training stage which explicitly aligns rewards with these metrics.
 - It demonstrates superior compositionality and object counting on GenEval.
 - Notably, MMaDA significantly outperforms prior approaches on the WISE Cultural benchmark (designed for world knowledge-aware generation), a direct benefit of its joint training on text-based reasoning, a feature often absent in existing unified models.

- **Textual Reasoning (Table 4):**
 - Despite being trained on limited task-specific tokens and solely open-source text data, MMaDA achieves comparable performance to strong autoregressive baselines like Qwen2-7B and LLaMA3-8B on general language tasks (MMLU, ARC-C).
 - It consistently outperforms LLaDA-8B, a diffusion-based LLM, on mathematical reasoning benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH).
 - MMaDA is one of the pioneering unified diffusion models to jointly train for text generation, multimodal reasoning, and image generation.

- **Ablation Study (Table 5):**
 - The study clearly demonstrates the incremental value of MMaDA's post-training stages. The base model after pre-training (Stage 1) lags behind baselines.
 - Adding Mixed Long-CoT fine-tuning (Stage 2) substantially boosts reasoning capabilities in mathematical and geometric domains (e.g., GSM8K from 17.4 to 65.2, MATH from 4.2 to 26.5).
 - The final UniGRPO stage (Stage 3) further improves performance across all tasks, bringing MMaDA to state-of-the-art levels in reasoning and generation metrics (e.g., CLIP Score from 29.4 to 32.5, ImageReward from 0.84 to 1.15).

- **Design Choices of UniGRPO (Figures 3 & 4):**
 - **General Masking Strategy:** UniGRPO's approach (unmasked questions, iteratively varied partial masking of answers) consistently achieves higher and more stable reward values during training compared to d1's strategy (random question masking, fully masked answers). This validates that learning from multi-step denoising better leverages diffusion models' characteristics.
 - **Uniformly Random Masking:** The proposed strategy of sampling a starting timestep and then uniformly generating remaining denoising timesteps across the full diffusion process leads to improved stability and faster convergence during RL training compared to fully random timestep selection.

- **Synergy Across Tasks (Figure 5 & 6):**
 - The joint training process exhibits clear synergy, with all key performance metrics for text generation, multimodal understanding, and image generation showing consistent improvements across training stages.
 - Qualitative examples demonstrate that improved textual reasoning leads to more coherent and logically structured textual outputs, more precise visual understanding descriptions, and more accurate, detailed, and contextually aligned generated images.

- **Sampling Efficiency (Table 6):**
 - Diffusion models' parallel token generation offers a significant efficiency advantage over AR models. MMaDA can achieve strong image generation performance with as few as 15 or 50 denoising steps (out of 1024), and coherent text/multimodal outputs with 256 or 512 steps.

- **Task Extension (Figure 7):**
 - MMaDA inherently supports inpainting and extrapolation across modalities (text, multimodal QA, image) without additional fine-tuning, a natural capability derived from its masked token prediction objective. This demonstrates the architectural flexibility and generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The MMaDA project represents a significant step forward in the development of general-purpose multimodal foundation models, offering several key contributions and potential impacts:

- **Pioneering Unified Diffusion for Multimodality:** MMaDA is the first to systematically explore and demonstrate the viability of a truly unified diffusion architecture for simultaneously handling textual reasoning, multimodal understanding, and text-to-image generation. This challenges the prevailing reliance on autoregressive or hybrid models in multimodal AI. It suggests that diffusion models, traditionally strong in generative tasks, can be adapted to be equally powerful in complex understanding and reasoning.
- **Highlighting the Importance of Post-Training:** By introducing Mixed Long-CoT fine-tuning and UniGRPO, MMaDA underscores the critical role of sophisticated post-training methodologies, especially for non-autoregressive diffusion models. These strategies enable cold-start training for RL, align reasoning processes across modalities, and enhance factual consistency and generation quality. This could inspire new research directions in post-training for generalist models.
- **Enhanced Reasoning and Factual Consistency:** The integration of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and reinforcement learning tailored for diffusion models significantly improves the model's ability to perform complex, knowledge-intensive reasoning and produce factually consistent generations across diverse tasks. This is crucial for developing more reliable and trustworthy AI systems.
- **Efficiency and Flexibility:** The inherent parallelism of diffusion models, demonstrated by MMaDA's ability to achieve strong performance with fewer denoising steps, promises greater computational efficiency compared to sequential autoregressive generation. Furthermore, the natural support for inpainting and extrapolation across modalities, without explicit fine-tuning, showcases a high degree of architectural flexibility.
- **Open-Source Contribution:** By open-sourcing its code and trained models, MMaDA significantly contributes to the research community, providing a robust framework and valuable baselines for future work in diffusion-based multimodal AI. This will accelerate innovation and allow other researchers to build upon their foundations.
- **Future Direction for Multimodal Intelligence:** MMaDA posits diffusion models as a potential "next-generation foundation paradigm for multimodal intelligence." Its success, even with a relatively compact 8B parameter model, suggests that scaling up this approach could yield even more powerful and versatile generalist AI models.

In conclusion, MMaDA offers a compelling alternative to current autoregressive-centric approaches, demonstrating that a unified diffusion architecture, when coupled with innovative post-training strategies, can achieve superior and balanced performance across the challenging landscape of multimodal AI. This work has the potential to reshape how we design and train general-purpose multimodal models, pushing the boundaries of what diffusion-based systems can achieve.
