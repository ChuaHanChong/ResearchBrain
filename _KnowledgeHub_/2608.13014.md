# EgoPHI: Estimating Contact and Force from Egocentric Vision

> [!summary] Summary
> EgoPHI estimates dense per-vertex contact maps and 3D force distributions on both hands and manipulated articulated objects from one egocentric RGB image plus object geometry. It fuses ViT visual features with graph-based intra-mesh attention and inter-mesh cross-attention, iteratively refines object pose, and trains on physics-simulated force labels, cutting hand-force MAE from 6.62 N to 4.03 N.

## Problem

- Vision-based interaction understanding has stopped at contact localization, yet contact alone cannot capture the physical dynamics that the underlying forces ultimately govern.
- Prior force estimators predict pressure only in image space or on flat planar surfaces, leaving non-planar rigid and articulated objects entirely unaddressed.
- Dense per-vertex force ground truth is impractical to annotate at scale, so no training data exists for supervising 3D force on meshes.

## Method

- A ==SOFA physics simulation== pipeline augments existing hand-object datasets with ==penalty-based virtual-spring forces== (f = -k d n, k = 10), masked by valid contact for dense supervision.
- ==Graph-Based Interaction Blocks== combine ==intra-mesh graph attention== over mesh connectivity with ==inter-mesh cross-attention== among left hand, right hand, and object, plus feed-forward refinement.
- An ==Iterative Refinement Module== composes incremental ==6D rotation and translation== updates over three iterations; a second block stack predicts contact probability, force magnitude, and direction separately.

## Results

- On ARCTIC, hand-force MAE drops to **4.03 N** versus the contact baseline's **6.62 N**, and EgoPHI uniquely reports object force at **4.42 N** MAE.
- Removing the Iterative Refinement Module collapses 2D contact IoU from **0.157** to **0.021** and volumetric IoU from **15.4** to **1.6**; refinement cuts mean per-vertex error from **64** to **19 cm**.
- Real instrumented cube and cylinder give object-force MAE of **0.48 N** and **1.35 N**; simulated labels match optical measurements with Spearman correlations **0.760** and **0.604**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Anchoring predictions to 3D mesh geometry beats image-space appearance cues like skin blanching, which collapse under the heavy occlusion typical of egocentric views.
> - Physics simulation can substitute for unobtainable force annotations; simulated supervision transferred to unseen datasets and real instrumented objects with no tactile sensing at test time.
> - Caution: geometry-anchored contact labels degrade when inference-time estimated hand meshes diverge from ground-truth geometry, trading force regression accuracy against contact accuracy.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Understanding hand-object interaction is a fundamental aspect of modeling human engagement with the physical environment. Recent advancements in computer vision have facilitated the detection of contact, ranging from whole-body human-scene contact to fine-grained hand-object contact. Several methods have emerged to predict contact points and regions from visual input, supported by an increasing number of datasets and benchmarks.

However, contact localization alone does not fully capture the physical dynamics of interaction, which are fundamentally governed by forces. Inferring these forces from egocentric RGB images presents substantial challenges. These include pervasive occlusions, weak visual cues that offer limited evidence of interaction intensity, and insufficient depth information from monocular images to accurately disambiguate 3D force directions. Consequently, prior research in vision-based force estimation has largely been restricted to 2D image-space predictions or interactions with flat surfaces.

EgoPHI addresses this limitation by developing a method that estimates 3D force distributions and dense contact maps on both hands and manipulated objects from a single egocentric monocular RGB image. This work extends the capabilities of vision-based force estimation beyond prior constraints of 2D or planar interactions, moving towards a more comprehensive, physically grounded understanding of hand-object manipulation in three dimensions. It builds upon existing work in 3D hand and object pose estimation, as well as graph-based reasoning for human-object interaction, to integrate visual and geometric cues for a more complete physical inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance the understanding of hand-object interaction from egocentric vision by moving beyond mere contact localization to encompass the estimation of underlying physical forces. The authors aim to enable physically grounded interaction reasoning, which is crucial for applications such as inferring human manipulation strategies in robotics or learning stable grasping from observation.

The key motivations and specific objectives driving this work are:

- **Bridging the Gap from Contact to Force:** The paper identifies that while contact detection has seen significant progress, it provides only partial information about physical interaction. Force estimation offers a more complete description of how humans engage with objects, including intent, stability, and control. The goal is to develop a model that can jointly estimate dense contact maps and 3D force distributions.
- **Overcoming Challenges of 3D Force Estimation from Egocentric Vision:** The unique difficulties of egocentric vision—such as frequent self-occlusion of the hands and objects, the subtlety of visual cues related to force intensity (e.g., skin blanching), and the inherent ambiguity of 3D geometry from a monocular view—motivate the development of robust 3D reasoning techniques. The objective is to extend force estimation beyond the limitations of image-space or planar surfaces to complex 3D hand and articulated object meshes.
- **Addressing the Scarcity of Ground-Truth Force Data:** A significant impediment to training vision models for force estimation is the lack of scalable ground-truth force annotations in existing hand-object interaction datasets. A core objective is to introduce a physics-based simulation pipeline to augment existing datasets with dense, per-vertex force supervision, thereby generating the necessary training data.
- **Developing a Generalizable Model:** The authors aim for a model that not only performs effectively on in-distribution data but also generalizes to unseen objects and datasets (out-of-distribution scenarios). This requires robust learning of underlying physical principles rather than rote memorization of visual patterns.
- **Validating Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To ensure the practical utility of the proposed vision-based force estimation, a critical objective is to evaluate its ability to transfer from simulated training data to real-world interactions. This necessitates the construction of a novel physical dataset featuring instrumented objects capable of capturing dense spatial contact and force distributions during diverse human interactions.

In summary, the research is motivated by the need for a deeper, physically informed understanding of human-object interaction from egocentric perspectives, driven by the challenges of data availability and the complexities of 3D inference from limited visual input.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

EgoPHI is a vision model designed to jointly estimate per-vertex contact points and 3D interaction forces on left hand, right hand, and object meshes. It takes a single egocentric RGB image and the object geometry as input. The methodology involves a three-stage pipeline, augmented by a physics-based simulation approach for generating supervision data and a novel real-world dataset for evaluation.

**A. Input and Output:**
- **Input:** An egocentric RGB image (I), 3D coordinates of the left and right hand meshes (P_L, P_R), and the 3D coordinates of the object template mesh (O_template).
- **Output:** Per-vertex contact probabilities (C_L, C_R, C_O) and 3D interaction force vectors (F_L, F_R, F_O) for each mesh.

**B. Physics-Based Simulation for Contact and Force Supervision:**
To address the lack of ground-truth force data, EgoPHI employs a physics-based simulation pipeline.
- **Dataset Augmentation:** The SOFA simulator is used to augment existing hand-object datasets (specifically ARCTIC) with dense, per-vertex contact and force distributions.
- **Force Calculation:** For each frame, given the ground-truth hand and object meshes, a penalty-based formulation models interactions as soft virtual springs. When vertices enter a predefined contact proximity zone, a restoring force `f_i = -k * d_i * n_i` is generated, where `k` is the stiffness constant (set to 10), `d_i` is the collision displacement, and `n_i` is the surface normal.
- **Contact Masking:** Raw simulated forces are filtered by a binary contact mask (M) to retain only physically valid contact regions, yielding `˜F = M ⊙ F`. This process provides dense, physically consistent 3D force vectors and contact regions as supervision.
- **Data Preprocessing:** Meshes are normalized. Force vectors are decomposed into normalized magnitude and unit direction to improve numerical stability and training efficiency.

**C. EgoPHI Model Architecture (Three Stages):**

1. **Visual and Geometric Feature Extraction:**
 - **Visual Backbone:** A Vision Transformer (ViT) backbone extracts 2D patch-level features from the input RGB image, generating a 2D feature map.
 - **Feature Projection and Fusion:** Each 3D mesh vertex is projected onto the 2D image plane using camera intrinsics. Visual features are sampled from the 2D feature map at these projected coordinates via bilinear interpolation.
 - **Hand Feature Initialization:** Sampled visual features are concatenated with the linearly embedded 3D camera-space coordinates of hand vertices and passed through a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) to form initial hand vertex embeddings.
 - **Object Feature Initialization (Pose Stage):** For the object, features are dynamically computed within an iterative pose refinement loop. These include RoI-aligned visual features, local geometry from the template mesh, and relative geometries to the left and right hand centers.

2. **Iterative Pose Refinement (IRM):**
 - This module refines the object's 3D pose (rotation R and translation t) in the camera frame.
 - **Graph-Based Interaction Blocks:** A stack of these blocks performs core 3D reasoning. Each block consists of:
 - **Intra-mesh Graph Attention Networks (GAT):** Capture geometric relationships within each mesh (hands and object) using mesh connectivity.
 - **Inter-mesh Cross-Attention:** Model relationships between the vertex features of different meshes (e.g., hand attends to object), capturing interaction cues.
 - **Feed-forward Network (FFN):** Refines vertex features.
 - **Refinement Loop:** The process involves an initialization stage (identity rotation, zero translation) followed by `N` (3 in this work) refinement iterations. In each iteration, object vertex features are updated based on the current pose estimate, passed through Interaction Blocks with fixed hand features, globally pooled, and then decoded to predict an incremental rotation (`∆R_i`) and translation (`∆t_i`). The object's pose is updated by composing these increments.

3. **Force Estimation:**
 - This stage is conditioned on the final refined object pose.
 - **Feature Preparation:** The final hand features from the last pose refinement iteration are reused. Object features are recomputed by transforming object vertices into camera space using the refined pose and fusing visual features (from the full backbone map) with the final 3D coordinates.
 - **Interaction Blocks (Force-Specific):** These updated hand and object features are processed by a second, separate stack of Interaction Blocks specialized for contact and force prediction.
 - **Prediction Heads:** Output heads for each hand and the object predict per-vertex contact probabilities and force vectors (magnitude and direction).
 - **Consistency Enforcement:** Predicted forces are multiplied by predicted contact probabilities to ensure physical consistency and suppress non-contact forces.

**D. Loss Functions:**
EgoPHI is trained with a multi-objective loss function:
- **Contact Loss (`L_contact`):** Weighted binary cross-entropy (BCE), vertex-level class-balanced (VCB) loss, dataset-level contact regularization, and a smoothness loss.
- **Force Magnitude Loss (`L_force-mag`):** Contact-weighted L2 loss and a regularization term for mean magnitudes.
- **Force Direction Loss (`L_force-vec`):** Normalized L2 loss, weighted by contact probabilities.
- **Pose Estimation Loss (`L_pose`):** Vertex reconstruction loss (`L_verts`), geodesic rotation loss (`L_rot`), and translation loss (`L_trans`).
- **Total Loss (`L_total`):** A weighted sum of all these components.

**E. Real-World Evaluation Dataset:**
- **Instrumented Objects:** Two objects (cube and cylinder) fabricated from 8mm acrylic, instrumented with LED strips and opaque tape. They leverage Frustrated Total Internal Reflection (FTIR) to allow a fisheye camera mounted below to capture light intensity, which correlates with touch force magnitude. ArUco markers track object poses.
- **Data Collection:** Eight participants interacted with these objects in various predefined touch and grasp types, recording synchronized egocentric RGB video and FTIR-based contact/force data.
- **Force Calibration:** A calibration procedure using a scale mapped captured light intensities to physical force (Newtons) to establish ground truth for real-world object contact and force.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

EgoPHI was evaluated on in-distribution (ARCTIC) and out-of-distribution (H2O) datasets, as well as a custom real-world dataset. Performance was measured using precision, recall, F1 score, and IoU for contact, and Masked Mean Absolute Error (MAE), Masked Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE), and volumetric IoU (vIoU) for force, computed within ground-truth contact regions.

**A. Performance on Simulated Datasets (ARCTIC & H2O):**

- **Comparison to 2D Image-Based Force Estimation (PressureVision):** EgoPHI consistently outperformed PressureVision in 2D contact and force metrics across both ARCTIC and H2O. For example, on ARCTIC, EgoPHI's 2D IoU was 0.157 compared to PressureVision's 0.068, and vIoU was 15.4 compared to 6.6. This suggests that EgoPHI's 3D geometric reasoning, which anchors force predictions to mesh topology, contributes to higher localization accuracy, especially under occlusion.
- **Comparison to 3D Mesh-Based Contact Estimation (HACO):**
 - **ARCTIC (in-distribution):** EgoPHI surpassed HACO in hand force estimation (e.g., MAE of 4.03N vs. 6.62N, RMSE of 5.06N vs. 7.29N, vIoU of 2.2 vs. 1.0). For hand contact, EgoPHI achieved higher precision (0.136 vs. 0.120) with comparable F1 (0.190 vs. 0.196) and IoU (0.112 vs. 0.113), while HACO had higher recall (0.886 vs. 0.500). Significantly, EgoPHI provided the first estimates for object contact and force, a capability not supported by HACO (object force MAE 4.42N, RMSE 5.28N, vIoU 2.0; object contact F1 0.060, IoU 0.032).
 - **H2O (out-of-distribution):** In this challenging setting, EgoPHI remained the only method to produce object contact and force estimates. For hand forces, EgoPHI achieved lower MAE (5.16N vs. 6.37N) and RMSE (6.62N vs. 7.13N) with comparable vIoU (0.6 vs. 0.7) compared to HACO, despite being trained only on ARCTIC. HACO, trained on a broader range of datasets including H2O, maintained higher hand contact recall (0.831 vs. 0.350).

**B. Ablation Study of Iterative Refinement Module (IRM):**
Removing the Iterative Refinement Module (EgoPHI w/o IRM) consistently led to degraded performance in both contact and force estimation.
- **2D Image-Level:** On ARCTIC, IoU increased from 0.021 (w/o IRM) to 0.157 (full EgoPHI), and volumetric IoU from 1.6 to 15.4. Similar improvements were observed on H2O.
- **Mesh-Level:** The full model with IRM demonstrated better hand and object contact and more accurate object force estimation, with IoU and vIoU improving up to 3x. The only minor exception was slightly worse H2O hand force performance (MAE 5.16N vs. 4.65N) for the full model.
- **Pose Refinement:** The IRM significantly improved 3D geometric understanding, reducing Mean Per-Vertex Error (MPV) by over 70% on ARCTIC (64cm to 19cm) and over 79% on H2O (48cm to 10cm). This indicates that accurate object pose is critical for effective interaction reasoning.

**C. Error-Isolation Analysis with Ground-Truth Poses:**
When EgoPHI was provided with ground-truth hand and object poses, bypassing the pose refinement, misalignment errors were significantly reduced. This suggests that a substantial portion of the observed misalignment in the full system originates from the difficulty of egocentric object pose estimation under occlusion, rather than from inaccuracies in the simulated force labels.

**D. Hand Pose Domain Gap:**
Fine-tuning EgoPHI with HAMER-estimated hand poses (instead of ground-truth poses used in training) improved hand force prediction (MAE 4.03N to 3.32N) but degraded hand contact prediction (F1 0.190 to 0.128) and slightly worsened object force estimation. This trade-off is attributed to a mismatch between the ground-truth geometry-anchored SOFA supervision and the noisier HAMER-predicted vertex positions at inference.

**E. Evaluation on Real-World Instrumented Objects:**
- EgoPHI successfully recovered real-world object contact and force magnitudes despite being trained exclusively on simulated data.
- Force errors on the real-world cylinder (MAE 1.35N, RMSE 3.24N) and cube (MAE 0.48N, RMSE 1.54N) were lower than on the ARCTIC dataset, indicating accurate capture of force magnitudes on these objects.
- Contact prediction, although challenging due to sparse real contact regions, showed EgoPHI successfully recovering main contact areas (e.g., cylinder object contact F1 0.121, IoU 0.067; cube object contact F1 0.151, IoU 0.085).
- These results demonstrate the sim-to-real transfer capability of EgoPHI for 3D contact and force distributions on non-planar real-world objects.

**F. Validation of Simulated Force Supervision:**
Comparison of SOFA-generated forces with real-world FTIR measurements revealed consistency. For the cylinder, SOFA's mean force was 1.02N (FTIR 1.21N) with a per-frame MAE of 0.36N and Spearman correlation of 0.760. For the cube, SOFA's mean force was 1.61N (FTIR 1.67N) with a per-frame MAE of 0.25N and Spearman correlation of 0.604. These metrics confirm the reliability of the SOFA simulation as a source of supervision for rigid objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

EgoPHI represents an advancement in egocentric perception, transitioning the focus from merely recognizing contact to estimating the underlying physical interaction. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Pioneering 3D Force Estimation:** EgoPHI is the first vision-based method to estimate dense 3D force distributions and contact regions on both hands and articulated objects from a single egocentric image. This moves beyond the limitations of prior work confined to 2D image space or planar surfaces, offering a more comprehensive understanding of physical interaction in real-world scenarios.
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** The introduction of a physics-based simulation pipeline for generating per-vertex contact and force supervision is a significant contribution. This method effectively transforms existing hand-object datasets into valuable training resources, overcoming a major bottleneck in the development of force-aware vision models.
- **Validation of Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The creation and utilization of a novel instrumented real-world dataset for evaluating contact and force provide empirical evidence for EgoPHI's ability to generalize from simulated training data to actual physical interactions. This demonstrates the practical viability of vision-only force estimation without requiring tactile sensors at inference time.
- **Enhanced Understanding of Human-Object Interaction:** By providing a "force-aware layer" to visual hand-object understanding, EgoPHI offers deeper insights into how objects are manipulated. Force distributions convey critical cues about grasping stability, control, and user intent, which are not accessible through contact information alone.
- **Broadening Applications:** The capabilities introduced by EgoPHI have potential implications across various domains:
 - **Robotics:** Robots could infer human manipulation strategies and learn more robust and stable grasping or object handling techniques by observing human demonstrations with force awareness.
 - **Augmented Reality/Virtual Reality:** Enhanced understanding of physical interaction could lead to more realistic and responsive virtual environments where virtual objects react physically to user input.
 - **Human-Computer Interaction:** Development of more intuitive interfaces that respond to the force and pressure of human interaction.
 - **Embodied Scene Understanding:** Contributing to a more complete computational model of how humans physically engage with their surrounding environment.

While some limitations remain, such as the current lack of temporal reasoning, dependence on object mesh templates, uniform stiffness assumption, and focus on normal forces, EgoPHI establishes a foundation for inferring physically grounded hand-object interaction dynamics from vision alone. Its robust performance on unseen objects and real-world scenarios indicates a promising direction for future research in egocentric physical perception.
