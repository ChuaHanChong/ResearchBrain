# End-to-End Driving with Online Trajectory Evaluation via BEV World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> WoTE is an end-to-end autonomous driving framework that predicts multiple candidate trajectories, then uses a lightweight BEV-space world model to imagine each trajectory's future BEV states, scoring them with a reward model trained via off-the-shelf traffic simulators. It achieves state-of-the-art results on NAVSIM and closed-loop CARLA benchmarks with real-time latency.

## Problem

- Prior end-to-end driving methods evaluate trajectories only from the current state, ignoring future consequences a trajectory would actually lead to.
- Image-space world models for driving use multi-step diffusion denoising, making them too slow for real-time onboard trajectory evaluation.
- Supervising imagined future states is hard since real driving logs contain only one ground-truth future, not one per candidate trajectory.

## Method

- ==BEV World Model==: a ==transformer-encoder== that recurrently predicts future BEV feature states and action tokens from state-action pairs in compact BEV space, avoiding costly image-space denoising.
- ==Reward Model==: aggregates current plus multi-step future BEV states per trajectory via convolutions and pooling to predict imitation and simulation-based rewards for selection.
- ==BEV Space Supervision==: decodes predicted BEV states into semantic maps supervised by off-the-shelf BEV traffic simulators (nuPlan), providing dense multi-future targets and rule-based reward labels.

## Results

- Achieves PDMS of **88.3** on NAVSIM test set, beating Hydra-MDP's **86.5** and TransFuser's **84.0** despite using far fewer trajectory candidates (**256** vs **8192**).
- On closed-loop Bench2Drive/CARLA, improves Driving Score to **61.71** and success rate to **31.36**, versus TCP baseline's **59.90** and **30.00**.
- Ablations show future-state prediction adds **2.4** PDMS over evaluation without imagined futures, and total inference latency stays at **18.7ms** for **256** trajectories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting future states in a compact latent space, rather than raw pixels, can make world-model-based evaluation fast enough for real-time closed-loop control.
> - Off-the-shelf simulators can supply dense multi-future supervision when real logs only provide a single observed outcome per scenario.
> - Imitation and rule-based simulation rewards capture complementary failure modes, so combining both types outperforms using either alone.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research directly addresses critical challenges and advancements within the rapidly evolving field of autonomous driving, specifically within the "end-to-end" paradigm.

Historically, autonomous driving systems have been modular, breaking down the task into distinct stages: perception, prediction, planning, and control. While robust, this approach can suffer from error propagation between modules and difficulties in joint optimization. End-to-end driving, in contrast, aims to learn a direct mapping from sensor inputs to control commands or trajectories using a single, differentiable network. This approach has shown remarkable progress, offering potential benefits in efficiency and seamless integration.

However, a key limitation of many existing end-to-end methods is their primary focus on generating a single, high-quality trajectory. Recent research has recognized the multi-modal nature of driving scenarios, where multiple plausible future trajectories often exist for the ego vehicle and other agents. This shift necessitates an effective mechanism to *evaluate* these multiple trajectories to ensure safety and reliability.

Traditional trajectory evaluation methods are often rule-based, relying on explicit perception outputs like bounding boxes and maps. These are susceptible to perception errors and are not easily integrated into an end-to-end differentiable framework. More recent end-to-end approaches that incorporate evaluation typically assess trajectories based solely on the current state, lacking foresight into future environmental dynamics caused by a chosen trajectory. The authors highlight that, similar to human drivers, an effective autonomous system must anticipate future outcomes before committing to a decision.

This is where the concept of a "world model" becomes relevant. World models, originating from reinforcement learning, are learned representations of environmental dynamics that enable prediction of future states based on current observations and actions. They have proven highly effective in model-based reinforcement learning by allowing agents to "imagine" consequences. Integrating world models into autonomous driving promises to provide the crucial foresight needed for robust trajectory evaluation.

However, directly applying world models to driving introduces its own set of challenges:
1. **Representation Efficiency:** Many existing driving world models predict future raw images using diffusion models, which are computationally intensive and too slow for real-time autonomous driving.
2. **Supervision Scarcity:** Real-world datasets typically provide only one ground-truth future, making it difficult to supervise a world model that needs to imagine *multiple* possible future states corresponding to different trajectory candidates.

This paper positions itself as a solution to these challenges. It proposes leveraging the **Bird's Eye View (BEV) space** as an efficient and compact representation for future state prediction, making the world model latency-efficient. Furthermore, it addresses the supervision problem by utilizing **off-the-shelf BEV-space traffic simulators** (like nuPlan), which can generate diverse future BEV semantic maps and corresponding rule-based rewards for multiple hypothetical trajectories. By doing so, the work pushes the boundaries of end-to-end driving by integrating model-based foresight into the decision-making process, making it more robust and intelligent.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to enhance the safety and reliability of end-to-end autonomous driving systems by integrating a sophisticated online trajectory evaluation mechanism. This objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing the Gap in End-to-End Trajectory Evaluation:** While end-to-end autonomous driving has made significant strides in directly mapping sensor inputs to vehicle actions, most approaches primarily focus on predicting a single "best" trajectory or a set of candidate trajectories without a robust, online method for *evaluating* their potential future outcomes. The authors argue that merely predicting trajectories is insufficient; effectively evaluating them is crucial for ensuring safety, especially in complex, multi-modal driving scenarios where multiple valid actions might exist.

2. **The Need for Future Outcome Anticipation:** The paper emphasizes that intelligent decision-making, both for human drivers and autonomous agents, inherently involves anticipating the future consequences of chosen actions. Current end-to-end evaluation methods that rely solely on the current environmental state are inherently limited. To make truly informed decisions, an autonomous system must be able to "imagine" and assess the future states of the environment that would result from executing a given trajectory candidate.

3. **Leveraging the Power of World Models from Reinforcement Learning:** Inspired by the success of model-based reinforcement learning, where world models effectively capture environmental dynamics and predict future states, the authors are motivated to adapt this powerful concept to end-to-end autonomous driving. They recognize that world models can provide the essential foresight needed for advanced trajectory evaluation.

4. **Overcoming Practical Limitations of Existing Driving World Models:** The direct application of world models to autonomous driving is hindered by two significant practical issues:
 - **Computational Inefficiency:** Many existing world models for driving operate in high-dimensional image space, employing computationally expensive techniques like diffusion models to predict future images. Such methods are too slow for the real-time constraints of autonomous driving. A more efficient representation for future scenario prediction is needed.
 - **Lack of Multi-Future Supervision:** Real-world driving datasets offer only one observed future trajectory and corresponding environmental state. World models, however, ideally need to be trained to predict *multiple* plausible futures, each corresponding to a different potential action or trajectory. This "multi-future supervision" is scarce in conventional datasets.

To address these motivations and overcome the identified challenges, the paper proposes WoTE, an end-to-end driving framework that leverages a **BEV (Bird's Eye View) World Model**. This specific design choice aims to provide a **latency-efficient** mechanism for predicting future states and to enable **seamless supervision** using readily available BEV-space traffic simulators, thereby making online trajectory evaluation practical and effective.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, WoTE (End-to-End Driving with Online Trajectory Evaluation via BEV World Model), is designed to predict and evaluate trajectories in a fully differentiable, end-to-end manner. It comprises four key components: a Trajectory Predictor, a BEV World Model, a Reward Model, and a robust BEV Space Supervision strategy.

### 4. Trajectory Predictor

The Trajectory Predictor is responsible for generating multiple potential trajectory candidates for the ego vehicle.
- **Multi-modal BEV Encoder:** This module takes raw sensor inputs, specifically multi-view RGB images and LiDAR point clouds. It processes these diverse modalities using a BEV encoder (e.g., a ResNet34 backbone, aligned with TransFuser architectures) to produce a unified Bird's Eye View (BEV) feature map, denoted as $B_t$. This BEV feature map acts as the current state representation.
- **Trajectory Anchors:** To provide a starting point for diverse trajectory generation, a fixed set of $N$ trajectory anchors are pre-computed using K-Means clustering on expert trajectories from the training dataset. These anchors represent common driving patterns.
- **Trajectory Refinement:** Given the current BEV state $B_t$ and the trajectory anchors, a refinement module (consisting of an MLP-based trajectory encoder, cross-attention, and an MLP head) predicts offsets to modify these anchors. This process yields $N$ refined trajectory candidates, $\hat{\tau}$, each corresponding to a potentially viable path for the ego vehicle.

### 5. BEV World Model

The core innovation of WoTE is its BEV World Model, designed to predict future BEV states corresponding to each refined trajectory. This model operates in two steps:
- **Input Construction:** For each of the $N$ refined trajectories $\hat{\tau}_i$, the trajectory encoder (shared with the refinement module) converts it into an action embedding $a^i_t$. This action embedding is paired with the current BEV state $B_t$ to form $N$ state-action pairs, $(B_t, a^i_t)$.
- **Recurrent Future Prediction:** The world model, implemented as a Transformer Encoder, takes these state-action pairs as input. Unlike image-level world models, it operates on the compact BEV feature space. For each pair, the current BEV state $B_t$ is flattened and concatenated with its corresponding action embedding $a^i_t$. The Transformer Encoder then recurrently predicts the future BEV state $B^i_{t+k}$ and a future action embedding $a^i_{t+k}$ for $K$ steps into the future. This recurrent prediction for all $N$ trajectories happens in parallel, ensuring efficiency. The smaller dimensions of BEV features ($h \times w$) ensure that this recurrent prediction incurs minimal computational overhead, making it suitable for real-time applications.

### 6. Reward Model

The Reward Model evaluates the quality of each refined trajectory based on its anticipated future states.
- **Reward Types:** The framework utilizes a combination of two types of rewards:
 - **Imitation Reward ($r_{im}$):** Quantifies how well a predicted trajectory aligns with expert driving behavior.
 - **Simulation Rewards ($r_{sim}$):** A set of five metrics commonly used in autonomous driving benchmarks: No Collisions (NC), Drivable Area Compliance (DAC), Time-to-Collision (TTC), Comfort (Comf), and Ego Progress (EP). These are designed to evaluate safety, adherence to rules, and efficiency.
 - **Final Reward ($r_{final}$):** A weighted logarithmic combination of these individual rewards, reflecting overall trajectory quality.
- **Reward Prediction:** Crucially, the Reward Model predicts a reward for each trajectory $\hat{\tau}_i$ by considering *both* the current BEV state and the *entire sequence of predicted future BEV states* ($B^i_{all}$) and future action embeddings ($a^i_{all}$) from the BEV World Model. This deep, temporal context allows for a more informed evaluation. The model uses 2D convolutions on the concatenated BEV states, followed by global average pooling, and then an MLP head to output the reward.
- **Trajectory Selection:** The trajectory with the highest predicted final reward is selected as the optimal action for the ego vehicle.

### 7. BEV Space Supervision

To train the BEV World Model and Reward Model, WoTE employs a novel supervision strategy that leverages BEV-space traffic simulators to overcome the limitations of real-world datasets.
- **Simulator Usage:** The nuPlan simulator is chosen for its realism and ability to generate diverse scenarios in BEV space.
- **Supervision of BEV States:** To provide explicit supervision for the predicted future BEV states, the framework decodes a semantic BEV map from each predicted BEV state. This semantic map represents the road, objects, and agents. The decoded map is then supervised using Focal Loss against a ground-truth simulated BEV semantic map, $B^*_{t+k}$, obtained from the simulator for each trajectory candidate.
- **Supervision of Simulation Rewards:** For each candidate trajectory, the simulator's rule-based evaluator computes target simulation rewards, $r^*_{sim}$. The Reward Model's predicted simulation rewards are then supervised using Binary Cross Entropy (BCE) loss.
- **Supervision of Imitation Reward:** The target imitation reward, $r^*_{im}$, is derived from the L2 distance between trajectory anchors and the expert trajectory. Cross-Entropy loss is used for supervision.
- **Supervision of Trajectory:** For the Trajectory Predictor, a "winner-take-all" strategy is typically used: only the refined trajectory closest to the expert trajectory is supervised using L1 loss. However, for certain benchmarks like Bench2Drive, supervising all trajectories might yield better results.
- **Overall Training Loss:** The total loss for training is a sum of these individual loss components: $L_{total} = L_{BEV} + L_{sim\_reward} + L_{im\_reward} + L_{traj}$.

This comprehensive methodology allows WoTE to effectively integrate future anticipation into end-to-end driving, benefiting from efficient BEV representations and scalable simulator-based supervision.

### 8. Main Findings and Results

The experimental validation of WoTE demonstrates its effectiveness and robustness across multiple challenging benchmarks for autonomous driving.

### 9. State-of-the-Art Performance

- **NAVSIM Benchmark:** WoTE achieved a Predictive Driver Model Score (PDMS) of 88.3 on the NAVSIM test set, setting a new state-of-the-art. This significantly outperforms previous model-free approaches like Hydra-MDP (86.5 PDMS) and other strong baselines (e.g., DRAMA at 85.5 PDMS), highlighting the superiority of model-based trajectory evaluation. The high scores across individual metrics (NC, DAC, EP, TTC, Comf) indicate comprehensive improvement in safety, compliance, and efficiency.
- **Bench2Drive (Closed-Loop CARLA):** In closed-loop simulations on the CARLA-based Bench2Drive benchmark, WoTE improved the Driving Score by 1.81 points compared to the baseline TCP, achieving 61.71. It also demonstrated a higher success rate (31.36%), further validating its ability to perform robustly in realistic, safety-critical scenarios.

### 10. Importance of Trajectory Evaluation and Future State Prediction

Ablation studies rigorously confirmed the critical role of the proposed components:
- **Trajectory Evaluation:** Simply adding the trajectory evaluation module (without future states) to a baseline (TransFuser) improved PDMS from 81.0 to 83.2, demonstrating the value of evaluating multiple candidates.
- **Future States:** Integrating the BEV World Model to predict future states for trajectory evaluation further boosted PDMS significantly from 83.2 to 85.6. This is a crucial finding, underscoring that anticipating future environmental dynamics is essential for robust decision-making.

### 11. Complementary Nature of Reward Types

The study showed that both imitation rewards and simulation rewards are vital and complementary:
- Using only imitation rewards yielded a PDMS of 83.5, performing better in "No Collision" (NC) and "Time-to-Collision" (TTC).
- Using only simulation rewards resulted in a PDMS of 83.6, excelling in "Drivable Area Compliance" (DAC) and "Ego Progress" (EP).
- Combining both reward types achieved the highest PDMS of 85.6, leveraging their respective strengths for a more balanced and effective evaluation.

### 12. Impact of Recurrent Future State Prediction

The recurrent nature of the BEV World Model for future prediction was shown to be beneficial. Predicting future states at finer time steps (e.g., 0s → 2s → 4s) provided richer temporal information, leading to an improved PDMS of 85.6 compared to coarser prediction (0s → 4s, which yielded 84.0 PDMS).

### 13. Influence of Number of Trajectories

The number of candidate trajectories evaluated impacted performance. Increasing the number from 64 to 128 led to a noticeable improvement in PDMS (from 84.5 to 85.4). Further increasing to 256 trajectories resulted in a marginal gain (85.6), suggesting diminishing returns beyond a certain point. The authors chose 256 as the default for optimal balance.

### 14. Real-time Efficiency

Despite the added complexity of a world model, WoTE demonstrated excellent real-time performance. The total latency on an NVIDIA L20 GPU was measured at only 18.7 ms (for 256 trajectories), well within the typical requirements for autonomous driving systems (e.g., 100ms for 10Hz operation). This efficiency is largely attributed to operating in the compact BEV space and parallel processing of trajectories.

### 15. Generalization Ability

A significant finding was the strong generalization capability of the trajectory evaluation module. When trained with 256 trajectory anchors but tested with unseen, more diverse 1024 anchors, the model still showed a 1.3 PDMS increase. Furthermore, using dynamically refined trajectories (which continuously change based on sensor inputs) as input to the world model further improved performance. This indicates that the BEV World Model is robust and can effectively handle novel or varying trajectory inputs, which is crucial for real-world adaptability.

### 16. Visual Analysis

Visualizations provided qualitative evidence of WoTE's efficacy. They showed that the framework effectively filters out low-quality or unsafe trajectories, preventing collisions and off-road excursions. It also successfully preserves diverse, multi-modal trajectories that comply with traffic rules and are safe, while suppressing unreasonable ones by assigning them significantly lower rewards.

In summary, the results convincingly demonstrate that integrating a BEV world model for online trajectory evaluation significantly enhances end-to-end autonomous driving performance, improves safety, and maintains real-time efficiency.

### 17. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, "End-to-End Driving with Online Trajectory Evaluation via BEV World Model," carries significant implications for the future development and deployment of autonomous driving technology.

### 18. Establishing Trajectory Evaluation as a Core End-to-End Component

The work strongly highlights and validates the importance of *online trajectory evaluation* within end-to-end autonomous driving frameworks. By moving beyond mere trajectory prediction to an informed selection process based on anticipated future states, this research advocates for a more intelligent and proactive decision-making paradigm. This could solidify trajectory evaluation as an indispensable module, potentially driving future architectural designs in end-to-end systems.

### 19. Enhancing Safety and Reliability in Autonomous Driving

The most direct and impactful contribution is the improvement in safety and reliability. By enabling the autonomous vehicle to "imagine" the consequences of its actions and evaluate multiple candidate trajectories based on these anticipated futures, WoTE can proactively avoid hazardous situations (e.g., collisions, off-road driving). This foresight is crucial for operating in complex and dynamic real-world environments, directly addressing a primary concern in the public acceptance and regulatory approval of autonomous vehicles.

### 20. Practicality for Real-Time Deployment through BEV World Models

A key hurdle for advanced world models in autonomous driving has been computational cost, especially with image-level predictions. The adoption of a latency-efficient BEV world model is highly significant. By demonstrating real-time performance (18.7ms latency) while providing predictive power, WoTE offers a practical pathway for integrating complex world models into actual self-driving systems. This paves the way for more sophisticated, model-based planning to become standard in real-time applications.

### 21. Effective Leveraging of Simulators for Data Scarcity

The paper presents an ingenious solution to the perennial problem of data scarcity for supervising diverse future outcomes. By using off-the-shelf BEV-space traffic simulators to generate dense ground truth for future BEV states and rule-based rewards, the authors effectively circumvent the limitations of single-ground-truth real-world data. This approach opens new avenues for training complex models that require multi-modal and multi-future supervision, potentially reducing reliance on costly and difficult-to-collect real-world data for certain aspects of training.

### 22. A Strong Baseline for Future Research

WoTE's state-of-the-art performance on respected benchmarks like NAVSIM and Bench2Drive positions it as a robust baseline. This will likely inspire further research into refining BEV world models, exploring alternative evaluation metrics, developing more sophisticated reward functions, and investigating novel ways to integrate human-like foresight into autonomous systems. Researchers may explore multi-agent world models, uncertainty-aware prediction, or adaptive planning strategies building upon this foundation.

### 23. Bridging the Gap Towards Human-like Driving Intelligence

By allowing the vehicle to anticipate and evaluate future scenarios, WoTE takes a step closer to mimicking human-like cognitive processes in driving. Human drivers constantly assess risks and opportunities based on predicted outcomes. Integrating this predictive capability into end-to-end systems moves autonomous driving beyond reactive control towards proactive, intelligent decision-making, which is essential for handling unforeseen events and complex interactions.

In conclusion, WoTE represents a significant advancement in end-to-end autonomous driving. By successfully integrating an efficient BEV world model for online trajectory evaluation, it addresses crucial challenges related to safety, reliability, and real-time performance, while also providing a strong foundation for future innovations in this critical field.
