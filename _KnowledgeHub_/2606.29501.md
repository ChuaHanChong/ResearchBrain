# Learning Transferable Dynamics Priors from Action to World Modeling

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Fudan University and McGill University developed A2World, a multi-view diffusion world model pretrained on over 2.1 million robot manipulation trajectories, to learn transferable dynamics priors from action-to-video. This framework, adaptable as both a long-horizon simulator (A2World-sim) and an instruction-conditioned policy (A2World-policy), enables high-fidelity simulation for policy evaluation with a Spearman "ρ" of 0.916 and achieves an 98.6% success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming baselines in complex real-robot tasks.

## Problem

- Existing robot learning models often lack large-scale pretraining on diverse, action-grounded robot data, limiting transferability.
- Current large-scale efforts are often specialized for single downstream objectives, hindering flexible reuse across different robot learning pipelines.
- A need for a unified pretraining method that captures reusable action-to-dynamics priors for both world model-based simulation and policy learning.

## Method

- Introduced **A2World**, a ==multi-view action-conditioned diffusion world model== pretrained on over 2.1 million diverse robot manipulation trajectories with real action annotations.
- Developed **A2World-sim**, an adaptation of A2World into a ==history-aware autoregressive simulator== for long-horizon rollouts using ==pose-guided history sampling==.
- Created **A2World-policy**, an adaptation of A2World into an instruction-conditioned ==vision-action joint prediction model== (MoE-like shared attention) for robot control.

## Results

- A2World-sim demonstrated superior rollout quality, improving appearance and action-faithfulness metrics, and showed strong agreement (Spearman ρ = **0.916**, R² = **0.930**) with real-world policy success rates for evaluation.
- A2World-policy achieved a **98.6%** success rate on the LIBERO benchmark and consistently outperformed baselines on a custom suite of 5 challenging real-robot manipulation tasks.
- Action-to-video pretraining (A-pre) delivered a transferable prior with comparable OOD policy evaluation performance (**88.5%**) to policy-targeted pretraining (**88.6%**), while also serving as a simulator initialization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action-conditioned world modeling provides a scalable and effective strategy for acquiring transferable dynamics priors in robot learning.
> - Pretraining on diverse, action-grounded robot data yields a robust "dual-use" prior, beneficial for both simulator-centric and policy-centric applications.
> - The fidelity of the learned world model (A2World-sim) as a simulator can significantly reduce dependence on real-robot interactions for policy evaluation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot learning is increasingly leveraging video generative models, with existing efforts largely evolving along two principal directions. One direction involves adapting these models into vision-language-action (VLA) policies, with more recent advancements focusing on joint video-action prediction. The other direction centers on developing action-conditioned world models, utilized for purposes such as data augmentation, policy evaluation, and reinforcement learning-based policy post-training.

Despite progress in these areas, a gap persists in effectively leveraging robot-data pretraining to learn transferable dynamics priors. Many current approaches either fine-tune from generic video-generation checkpoints, lacking large-scale pretraining on action-grounded robot data, or pretrain on single datasets, which restricts the diversity of embodiments, camera setups, and motion patterns that the models can learn. Furthermore, existing large-scale efforts are frequently optimized for a singular downstream objective, rather than being explicitly designed for flexible reuse across both simulator-centric and policy-centric pipelines. This context indicates a need for a unified robot-data pretraining paradigm that can capture reusable action-to-dynamics priors and consistently benefit both world model-based simulation and downstream policy learning. This paper addresses this requirement by proposing and implementing such a paradigm.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to investigate action-conditioned world modeling as a scalable methodology for acquiring transferable dynamics priors in robot learning. The core motivation stems from the intuition that actions provide a natural causal supervision signal within robot manipulation contexts. While specific objects, scenes, and viewpoints can vary substantially across different datasets, the fundamental rules governing interactions—such as contacts, grasps, pushes, and releases—are determined by how actions induce state changes. Therefore, pretraining a model to predict the visual evolution of a scene conditioned on specific actions compels the model to encode controllable, action-grounded dynamics, rather than merely performing appearance-level video prediction. This process aims to yield reusable interaction priors.

More specifically, the paper outlines several key objectives:
- **Introduce Action-to-Video World Model Pretraining:** To establish a robot-data-driven approach for learning transferable dynamics priors, moving beyond generic video generation checkpoints or single-dataset pretraining.
- **Instantiate a Multi-View Interactive Diffusion World Model:** To concretely implement this pretraining paradigm through a model named A2World, which is pretrained on a large volume of diverse robot manipulation data featuring real action annotations, thereby avoiding reliance on auxiliary latent-action models for pseudo labels.
- **Demonstrate Transferability for Simulator-Centric Learning:** To adapt the pretrained A2World into a history-aware autoregressive simulator, A2World-sim. This variant is intended to enable long-horizon action-conditioned rollouts, support simulator-based robot policy evaluation, and facilitate scalable "what-if" analysis by reducing reliance on real-robot interactions.
- **Demonstrate Transferability for Policy-Centric Learning:** To adapt the same pretrained A2World weights into a video-action joint prediction model, A2World-policy. This model is conceptualized as a MoE-like instruction-conditioned robot policy, designed to predict actions while reusing the pretrained visual dynamics prior through shared attention mechanisms.
- **Validate Superiority of Pretraining:** To empirically demonstrate that action-to-video pretraining yields more robust and transferable dynamics priors compared to text-conditioned video pretraining or other robot pretraining baselines, resulting in consistent improvements in downstream tasks and policy performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology presented in the paper centers on the development and adaptation of a base action-conditioned world model, A2World, and its specialized variants, A2World-sim and A2World-policy.

#### 3.1. A2World: The Base Robot World Model

A2World is designed as an action-conditioned generative world model that predicts future observations ($o_{t+1:t+k}$) given an initial conditioning frame ($o_t$) and a chunk of future actions ($a_{t+1:t+k}$).
- **Action Conditioning:** Action chunks are encoded using a Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) into an embedding. This action embedding is integrated into the diffusion timestep embedding, which is then consumed by adaptive layer normalization within each DiT block to produce dynamic modulation parameters.
- **Architecture:** The model operates within a continuous latent space generated by a WAN2.1 tokenizer. It employs a standard latent video diffusion pipeline, injecting timestep conditions into DiT blocks. The training utilizes the EDM denoising score matching objective to reconstruct clean latent sequences from corrupted versions.
- **Multi-view Generation:** A2World is designed to jointly generate multi-view videos, packing multiple camera views into the temporal dimension for processing. Learnable view embeddings are introduced and concatenated to the latent input tokens to provide explicit camera identity. Cross-view attention modules are incorporated into each DiT block, allowing tokens from one view to attend to tokens from other views, thereby encouraging spatiotemporal consistency across views while retaining view-specific details.
- **Pretraining:** A2World is pretrained on a curated, large-scale dataset of robot manipulation trajectories (over 2.1 million trajectories from 20+ embodiments). Actions are unified into a shared dual-arm format (with zero-padding for single-arm robots). To handle diverse camera setups across datasets, a dataset-consistent batching strategy is employed. The base DiT backbone is initialized from a Cosmos-Predict2-2B-Video2World checkpoint.

#### 3.2. A2World-sim: Adapting A2World into a Long-Horizon Simulator

A2World-sim adapts the base A2World into a history-aware autoregressive world model for long-horizon simulation, predicting future observations conditioned on past observations ($H_{t-1}$) in addition to the current frame and future actions.
- **Pose-Guided History Sampling:** To manage the history budget, a pose-guided sampling algorithm is introduced. It computes a weighted arc-length from relative actions and uniformly samples frames along this arc-length. This method aims to preserve key states and motion-informative frames, such as turning points, from the history sequence. For dual-arm setups, the algorithm considers concatenated dual-arm absolute poses.
- **History Injection:** The sampled history latent sequence is tokenized into "history tokens." These tokens are then injected into the DiT block in two ways: (1) they replace the generic cross-attention condition (set to zero during pretraining) and are attended by target video latent tokens; (2) they are projected into key and value memories and concatenated with the current latent self-attention, providing a global history memory.
- **Autoregressive Generation:** A2World-sim performs chunk-wise autoregressive generation. Predicted frames are appended to a history buffer and reused as conditions for subsequent rollout steps, effectively converting the short-horizon predictor into a long-horizon action-conditioned simulator. A "Self-forcing" training strategy is employed, where the model is periodically conditioned on its own generated frames, helping it learn to recover from rollout errors and improve long-horizon stability.

#### 3.3. A2World-policy: Adapting A2World into a Robot Policy

A2World-policy adapts the pretrained A2World into a MoE-like vision-action joint prediction model. It jointly models future observations and actions, conditioned on a language instruction ($l$) and an initial frame ($o_t$).
- **Instruction Conditioning:** Language instructions are encoded using a pretrained T5 text encoder, and the resulting token embeddings are used as the cross-attention context in each DiT block.
- **Joint Diffusion Formulation:** Gaussian perturbations are applied independently to video latents ($z_v$) and action latents ($z_a$). Modality-specific noise levels are derived by scaling a shared base noise level, promoting video-action alignment.
- **MoE-like Video-Action Blocks:** The model employs a block design where a single self-attention module is shared across video and action modalities. However, each modality retains its own Adaptive Layer Normalization (AdaLN) and MLP branches. Action tokens attend to both video and action tokens via the shared self-attention.
- **Training Objective and Guided Inference:** A weighted joint denoising objective is used to predict clean video and action latents. During inference, modality-wise classifier-free guidance allows for separate control over video fidelity and action accuracy, using distinct scaling factors.
- **Initialization:** A2World-policy is initialized from the pretrained A2World. Cross-attention modules are initialized from a Cosmos-Predict2-2B checkpoint (as A2World pretraining set cross-attention to zero), and action-specific modules are initialized by copying corresponding video-branch parameters for stable joint fine-tuning.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setups

- **World Model Training:** A2World pretraining involved 64 H200 GPUs, a batch size of 12 per GPU, and gradient accumulation for 2 epochs. Fine-tuning A2World-sim used 8 H200 GPUs.
- **A2World-policy Training:** Initialization from pretrained A2World. Fine-tuning used 32 H200 GPUs, a global batch size of 256, and 20k steps for LIBERO and real-robot tasks.
- **Custom Real-Robot Dataset:** A dual-arm Flexiv robot platform was used to collect data via VR teleoperation for 5 tasks involving contact-rich, articulated, and deformable objects.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yielded several findings regarding the capabilities and transferability of the proposed models.

#### 4.1. Base World Model Capability Demonstration (A2World)

- A2World demonstrated strong action-conditioned multi-view rollouts. On the DROID dataset, it could simulate grasping different objects and reproduce failed grasps from the same initial observation, indicating modeling of action-to-visual dynamics beyond just successful outcomes.
- Precise full-DoF control was demonstrated on RoboCoin, with consistent predictions across heterogeneous views, suggesting strong counterfactual controllability even for scripted controls not present in pretraining data.
- A2World also showed robust generalization in out-of-distribution (OOD) rollouts on RoboMind and VIOLA, maintaining coherence in scenes and camera setups unseen during pretraining.

#### 4.2. Fine-tuned World Model Evaluation (A2World-sim)

- **Rollout Quality:** A2World-sim consistently achieved the best rollout quality on both LIBERO and custom real-robot datasets compared to strong baselines (Cosmos-Predict2, Ctrl-World, Prophet, and a text-conditioned A2World variant). Improvements were observed in appearance metrics (PSNR, SSIM, tSSIM) and action-faithfulness metrics (EPE, cos), suggesting more accurate dynamics modeling under action-conditioned rollouts. Qualitatively, A2World-sim exhibited greater long-horizon stability and visual quality, completing tasks where baselines drifted or collapsed (Fig. 7). It also demonstrated strong video prediction performance on RoboNet against autoregressive baselines.
- **OOD Simulator Evaluation:** When fine-tuned on LIBERO and evaluated on LIBERO-Plus Spatial (with distribution shifts), A2World-sim improved action-faithfulness metrics over DreamDojo. Qualitatively, A2World-sim better preserved novel scene appearances and interaction dynamics compared to DreamDojo, which tended to drift towards training-domain appearances.
- **World Model as Real-World Simulator:** A2World-sim demonstrated strong agreement with real-world policy success rates on custom robot tasks (Spearman ρ = 0.916, Pearson r = 0.965, R² = 0.930, N = 8). This indicated its fidelity as a simulator for policy evaluation, enabling reliable assessment without extensive real-robot interaction.

#### 4.3. Vision-Action Joint Prediction Evaluation (A2World-policy)

- **Evaluation on LIBERO:** A2World-policy achieved an overall success rate of 98.6% on the LIBERO benchmark, outperforming existing baselines.
- **OOD Policy Evaluation:** On LIBERO-Plus Spatial, action-to-video A2World pretraining (A-pre) achieved an 88.5% average success rate, which surpassed direct initialization from Cosmos-Predict2 (C-init, 80.2%) and text-conditioned A2World pretraining (T-pre, 85.8%). Its performance was comparable to policy-targeted pretraining (P-pre, 88.6%), indicating that action-to-video pretraining effectively captures the necessary dynamics prior for policy transfer, while also serving as a simulator initialization.
- **Evaluation on Real-Robot:** A2World-policy consistently outperformed recent baselines (π0.5, LingBot-VA) on a custom real-robot suite of 5 tasks involving diverse and contact-rich manipulation. Significant gains were observed on challenging long-horizon tasks where baselines often failed or stalled early, demonstrating improved robustness to error accumulation and better handling of precise state transitions.

#### 4.4. Ablations and Discussions

- **Ablation on History Sampling:** Pose-guided history sampling for A2World-sim consistently yielded better rollout quality than a standard sliding-window memory strategy or no history injection. This suggests that selecting motion-informative historical frames is beneficial for stable and history-faithful generation.
- **Pretraining Variants:** Comparisons of policy variants after LIBERO fine-tuning showed that policy-targeted pretraining offered the best in-domain average (98.8%), but action-video A2World pretraining was very close (98.6%). This finding supports the hypothesis that the action-conditioned objective of A2World provides a less ambiguous and stronger action-to-dynamics prior, which transfers effectively to policy learning. The dual-use nature of A-pre, serving as both a policy initialization and a simulator, was highlighted.
- **Coupling between Video Modeling and Action Learning:** A consistent positive correlation was observed between video prediction consistency and action generation quality during A2World-policy training. This indicates that shared representations learned for forecasting and control mutually reinforce each other, leading to improvements in both video and action performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robot learning by establishing action-conditioned world modeling as a scalable and effective strategy for learning transferable dynamics priors. The introduction of A2World, a multi-view diffusion world model pretrained on diverse real-robot data, demonstrates a unified pretraining paradigm that is beneficial for both simulator-centric and policy-centric robot learning.

The ability of A2World-sim to function as a faithful real-world simulator for long-horizon rollouts and policy evaluation could significantly reduce the reliance on costly and time-consuming real-robot interactions during development and assessment phases. This represents a tangible impact on the efficiency and scalability of robot learning pipelines. Furthermore, the effectiveness of A2World-policy in instruction-conditioned control, demonstrating strong performance across diverse tasks and generalization to out-of-distribution settings, suggests a path toward more generalist robot policies.

The finding that action-to-video pretraining yields a strong transferable prior, comparable to policy-targeted pretraining while also enabling simulator capabilities, signifies a dual-use prior that offers both efficiency and versatility. This approach could accelerate the development of robust robot policies and world models by providing foundational models that capture general interaction dynamics, fostering advancements in areas such as contact-rich manipulation and long-horizon tasks. The work addresses a previously identified gap in the field by providing a unified pretraining strategy, which has the potential to become a standard for future robot learning research and applications.
