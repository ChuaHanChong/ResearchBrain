# Dynamic Resolution Routing for Efficient Egocentric Grounding

> [!summary] Summary
> SmartRes presents an efficient framework for egocentric visual grounding in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) by dynamically routing high-resolution processing to object-centric regions in the pixel space. This approach reduces visual token processing costs by up to 64% in FLOPs and 54% in latency, while retaining over 86% of full-resolution grounding performance, particularly benefiting small object localization.

## Problem

- High computational cost for MLLMs processing the high-resolution inputs necessary for fine-grained perception in egocentric scenes.
- Egocentric visual grounding requires fine-grained details for localizing small, often partially occluded objects, but high-resolution inputs create a significant computational bottleneck for MLLMs.
- Existing latent-space token reduction methods often rely on unreliable heuristics in cluttered egocentric scenes, leading to the erroneous discarding of critical object-centric information and degrading performance.

## Method

- **SmartRes** implements ==dynamic resolution routing== in the pixel space, proactively allocating high-resolution processing only to task-relevant image regions.
- It processes dual views: a low-resolution image for global context and routing decisions, and a high-resolution image for selectively encoding fine details in identified regions.
- A learnable ==MLP-based routing head==, supervised with a ==margin-based hinge regularizer==, identifies object-centric patches, ensuring robust selection despite foreground-background imbalance.

## Results

- SmartRes-Lite achieves **86.4%** of full-resolution performance with an average of **33%** visual tokens, reducing FLOPs by **64%** and latency by **54%** compared to full-resolution baselines.
- SmartRes-Pro retains **89.9%** of full-resolution performance using **55%** of the token budget, demonstrating superior accuracy among evaluated efficient methods.
- The framework significantly preserves small-object grounding accuracy, retaining up to **89.5%** of P@0.5 for small objects on EgoIntention, outperforming uniform down-scaling and token pruning methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proactive pixel-space dynamic resolution routing offers a superior balance of efficiency and performance compared to post-hoc latent-space token pruning for high-resolution egocentric grounding.
> - The introduction of a margin-based hinge regularizer is crucial for training a robust router that can reliably identify sparse foreground objects amidst severe foreground-background imbalance in egocentric data.
> - Assembling visual tokens while preserving the canonical raster-scan order allows for selective high-resolution detailing without requiring significant architectural changes to existing MLLM backbones.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual grounding, the task of localizing objects in an image based on natural language queries, is a fundamental capability in computer vision and natural language processing. Recent advancements in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have significantly improved performance on general visual grounding benchmarks, achieving high accuracy. However, their application to egocentric (first-person) visual grounding presents distinct challenges.

Egocentric scenes are characterized by the presence of small, often partially occluded target objects, rapid viewpoint changes, and cluttered interaction contexts. These characteristics necessitate high-resolution input images to preserve the fine-grained visual details required for accurate perception and localization. Consequently, the native resolutions of egocentric datasets are frequently higher and more diverse than those typically used in general visual grounding.

The use of high-resolution inputs with MLLMs leads to a substantial increase in computational cost, particularly for visual token processing. This constitutes a major bottleneck for inference speed and limits deployment on edge devices. Prior research has attempted to mitigate this cost through visual token reduction strategies, which include pruning or merging tokens in the latent space of the model. These methods often rely on heuristics such as decoder attention, feature similarity, or activation statistics. However, the authors observe that such heuristics can be unreliable in egocentric scenarios, leading to the erroneous discarding of object-centric spatial evidence while retaining irrelevant background tokens. This degradation in crucial fine-grained details undermines grounding performance, especially for small objects.

This work positions itself as an effort to address the efficiency problem in high-resolution egocentric visual grounding by shifting the optimization strategy from post-hoc latent-space token reduction to a proactive pixel-space resolution allocation. It contrasts with existing token reduction methods that operate in the latent space and also distinguishes itself from other selective high-resolution processing methods (e.g., crop-based, region-selective sampling, tool-augmented approaches) by learning task-supervised resolution routing at the patch level without external agents or multi-stage pipelines. By proposing a dynamic resolution routing framework, the paper aims to enable efficient MLLM inference for egocentric grounding while preserving the fine-grained perception necessary for this domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an efficient framework for egocentric visual grounding that can handle high-resolution inputs without incurring prohibitive computational costs, thereby enabling the deployment of MLLMs in resource-constrained environments.

The motivation for this work stems from several key observations and challenges:

1. **High-Resolution Requirement in Egocentric Grounding**: Egocentric scenarios often involve small target objects, which necessitate high-resolution visual inputs to preserve the fine-grained details critical for accurate localization. Traditional MLLMs, while effective on general benchmarks, experience limited accuracy in egocentric settings when forced to process lower resolution inputs.
2. **Computational Bottleneck of High-Resolution Inputs**: Scaling MLLMs to process the high and diverse native resolutions prevalent in egocentric data results in an excessive number of visual tokens. For instance, a 3780 × 1920 frame can generate approximately 9.3k visual tokens, leading to visual encoding costs dominating the total end-to-end inference budget (up to 66.5%). This computational burden creates a significant bottleneck for real-time inference and deployment on edge devices.
3. **Limitations of Existing Token Reduction Strategies**: Current efficient methods for MLLMs typically operate by pruning or merging tokens in the latent space. These methods rely on heuristics (e.g., attention scores, feature similarity, activation statistics) which, in egocentric scenes, are identified as unreliable. Such heuristics can be misled by noisy context, high-activation artifacts, or salient but irrelevant regions (like hands), causing relevant object-centric evidence to be discarded while less critical background tokens are retained. This results in significant degradation of grounding performance, particularly under moderate token retention ratios.
4. **Need for Proactive Pixel-Space Optimization**: To overcome the unreliability of latent-space heuristics, the authors are motivated to shift the efficiency optimization to the pixel space. The goal is to proactively allocate computational resources (i.e., high-resolution processing) only to the object-centric regions where fine-grained details are essential, rather than attempting to prune tokens after they have already been processed at a potentially high cost.
5. **Robust Routing under Foreground-Background Imbalance**: Egocentric data inherently exhibits severe foreground-background imbalance, where target objects occupy a small fraction of the image area. This imbalance poses a challenge for training a router that can reliably identify foreground regions. The motivation here is to introduce a regularization mechanism that specifically addresses this imbalance, ensuring robust object-centric region selection even when foreground evidence is sparse.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to establish a more effective and efficient approach for egocentric visual grounding, offering a better trade-off between perception quality and inference cost.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The SmartRes framework is designed to address the computational cost of high-resolution egocentric grounding by implementing dynamic resolution routing in the pixel space. This approach replaces post-hoc latent-space token pruning with proactive patch-level resolution allocation.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: MLLMs and Compute Bottleneck

The paper leverages standard MLLM architecture, consisting of a vision encoder, a modality bridge, and a language model (LM) decoder. An input image is converted into visual features by the vision encoder, which are then aligned with text embeddings via a linear projection. The concatenated visual and textual tokens are processed by the LM. In egocentric grounding, the process involves querying an MLLM with an image and text instruction to output a bounding box. The authors confirm that for high-resolution egocentric images, vision encoding and LLM prefilling constitute the dominant computational costs, significantly outweighing autoregressive decoding.

#### 3.2. SmartRes Architecture

SmartRes operates by processing two aligned views of the input image: a low-resolution (LR) view for global context and routing, and a high-resolution (HR) view for selective encoding of fine details.

- **Image Preparation**: The original image `I` is resized isotropically to create `I_LR` and `I_HR` based on target token ratios `r_LR` and `r_HR`. Resized dimensions are snapped to multiples of the effective visual-token stride (`F = P * M`, where `P` is patch size and `M` is spatial merge size) to ensure alignment with the vision encoder's processing. This defines aligned LR and HR routing grids and establishes a deterministic HR-to-LR token correspondence. `I_LR` is passed through the vision encoder to obtain LR features `F`.

- **Learnable Patch-Level Resolution Routing**: A lightweight MLP-based routing head takes the LR features `F` as input and predicts a routing saliency map `S`. This saliency map, which assigns a score to each LR location, is then binarized into a routing mask `M` using a threshold `τ` and the Straight-Through Estimator (STE). A value of `M_i = 1` for a location `i` signifies that the corresponding LR evidence is marked for high-resolution processing, activating its HR patch group. Otherwise, the compact LR feature is retained. This process converts dense HR encoding into sparse, learnable patch activation.

- **Order-Preserving Visual Token Assembly**: Based on the routing mask `M`, selected patches from `I_HR` are processed through the vision encoder to obtain high-resolution visual features (`V_HR`). The unselected LR locations yield low-resolution visual features (`V_LR`). These `V_HR` and `V_LR` tokens are then assembled into a single visual sequence, `V_assembled`. A critical aspect of this assembly is that it preserves the canonical LR raster-scan order: unselected LR locations remain as compact tokens, while selected LR locations are expanded *in place* into their corresponding contiguous HR token groups. `V_assembled` is zero-padded for divisibility by the spatial merge unit and passed through a projection layer to yield the final visual token sequence (`V_final`) for the LM.

#### 3.3. Routing Optimization with Margin Regularization

The routing strategy is trained end-to-end, optimizing both the primary grounding task and an object-centric routing supervision.

- **BCE Loss for Routing**: The predicted saliency scores `S` are supervised using a standard Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss (`L_route`). This involves rasterizing the ground-truth bounding box (`B_hat`) onto the LR patch grid to obtain token-wise labels (`M_hat`), encouraging high saliency for tokens overlapping the target box and suppressing background tokens.

- **Margin Hinge Regularization**: To counter the severe foreground-background imbalance common in egocentric data, where background tokens vastly outnumber foreground tokens, a margin-based hinge regularizer (`L_hinge`) is introduced. This regularizer operates on the routing logits `z` by computing class-wise logit means (`μ_P` for foreground, `μ_N` for background). The `L_hinge` objective encourages a predefined margin `m` between `μ_P` and `μ_N`, thereby enforcing separation between foreground and background logit distributions. This mechanism aims to improve foreground recall and robustness in sparse object scenarios.

- **Overall Optimization Objective**: The total training objective (`L_total`) combines the standard next-token cross-entropy loss for grounding (`L_grounding`) with the routing supervision terms: `L_total = L_grounding + λ_route * L_route + λ_hinge * L_hinge`, where `λ_route` and `λ_hinge` are weighting hyperparameters.

The implementation utilizes Qwen2.5-VL as the base MLLM, fine-tuned with LoRA. Experiments are conducted on Ego4D and EgoIntention datasets, with additional evaluation on standard REC benchmarks. Performance is assessed using Precision at IoU (P@0.3, P@0.5), mean IoU (mIoU), and scale-specific P@0.5. Efficiency is measured by LLM prefilling FLOPs and GPU latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance and efficiency of SmartRes in egocentric grounding and generalization to standard REC benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Egocentric Grounding Performance and Efficiency

Evaluations on Ego4D and EgoIntention datasets demonstrate that SmartRes establishes a competitive accuracy-efficiency trade-off.

- **SmartRes-Lite**: This configuration retains 86.4% of the full-resolution model's performance while using an average of 33% of visual tokens. This surpasses a comparable uniform down-scaling baseline by a 34.1% margin in performance retention, improving overall P@0.5 from 29.23 to 51.11. SmartRes-Lite also reduces FLOPs by 64% and latency by 54% compared to the full-resolution Vanilla model. It operates 1.66 times faster than the Dyn-LLaVA method (at 50% tokens) while achieving 5.0% higher performance retention.
- **SmartRes-Pro**: This performance-oriented variant achieves 89.9% of full-resolution performance with an average 55% token budget, exhibiting the highest grounding accuracy among the evaluated efficient methods.
- **Comparison to Baselines**: Token-reduction methods (ToMe, VisionZip, FastV, Dyn-LLaVA) show notable performance degradation under compression. For instance, at 70% tokens, ToMe and VisionZip retain only 42.5% and 54.5% of performance, respectively, while FastV and Dyn-LLaVA achieve 83.3% and 85.1% but with higher FLOPs and latency compared to SmartRes. This supports the observation that latent-space pruning can discard critical fine-grained spatial evidence.

#### 4.2. Evaluation on Small Objects

Analysis of performance by object scale (small, medium, large) reveals that resolution loss disproportionately affects small objects.

- Uniform down-scaling to 50% token budget leads to a 10.7% reduction in P@0.5 for small objects (P_s) on Ego4D and 7.8% on EgoIntention, whereas the drops for large objects (P_l) are 6.3% and 5.8%, respectively. Token pruning methods further exacerbate this imbalance.
- SmartRes-Pro, by contrast, effectively preserves small-object performance. At a 55% token ratio, it retains 88.9% of full-resolution P_s on Ego4D and 89.5% on EgoIntention, while maintaining competitive performance for medium and large objects. This indicates the effectiveness of routing high-resolution computation to object-centric regions for mitigating resolution loss in egocentric scenes with small targets.

#### 4.3. Generalization to Standard REC Benchmarks

SmartRes also demonstrates generalization capabilities on standard referring expression comprehension (REC) benchmarks (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg).

- SmartRes-Pro, utilizing 42% of tokens on average, achieves 70.88 Overall P@0.5, corresponding to an 80.5% performance retention averaged across eight evaluation splits.
- It outperforms uniform down-scaling at 50% tokens and VScan by 6.10 and 2.59 Overall P@0.5, respectively.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis

Qualitative visualizations illustrate that SmartRes selectively allocates high-resolution computation to object-centric regions, complemented by a low-resolution context. This approach is shown to preserve fine-grained details necessary for localizing small objects like "detergent" or "spoon," which alternative methods like FastV might miss due to discarding critical visual evidence. The routing mask provided by SmartRes also enhances interpretability by highlighting object-aware regions.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Component Analysis**: Random HR patch selection improves overall P@0.5 by 4.68% compared to uniform down-sampling, demonstrating the benefit of local high-resolution details. The learned router further improves overall P@0.5 to 45.35%.
- **Margin Hinge Regularizer (`L_hinge`)**: Adding `L_hinge` significantly enhances performance, raising overall P@0.5 to 49.39%. It boosts foreground recall (FG-Recall) from 37.0% to 54.0% and foreground precision (FG-Precision) from 24.7% to 31.1%, indicating effective mitigation of foreground-background imbalance.
- **Attn2HR Baseline**: A fixed attention-guided selection baseline (Attn2HR) improves over uniform down-sampling and random routing but remains substantially inferior to the learned router, suggesting that fixed attention heuristics are insufficient for optimal patch selection.
- **Router Hyperparameters**: Deeper features (e.g., layer 30) for routing improve routing quality and performance. The routing threshold `τ` allows for a trade-off between performance and efficiency, where a smaller `τ` activates more regions for potentially higher accuracy at increased computational cost, and vice versa.
- **Imbalance-Aware Routing Losses**: Empirical comparison shows that the margin hinge regularizer yields superior routing quality and grounding performance (Overall P@0.5, FG-Recall, FG-Precision) compared to other imbalance-aware objectives such as Dice, Focal, and Tversky losses, under comparable token budgets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a notable challenge in the application of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to egocentric visual grounding: the high computational cost associated with processing the high-resolution inputs required for perceiving fine-grained details, particularly for small objects. The proposed SmartRes framework offers a method for efficient egocentric grounding through dynamic resolution routing in the pixel space.

The significance of this work lies in several areas:

- **Computational Efficiency**: By optimizing efficiency in the pixel space via dynamic resolution routing, SmartRes reduces the visual token processing cost at its source. This directly translates to substantial reductions in FLOPs (up to 64%) and inference latency (up to 54%), making MLLM-based egocentric grounding more feasible for real-world applications.
- **Performance Preservation**: Unlike conventional latent-space token reduction methods that can degrade fine-grained perception, SmartRes maintains high grounding accuracy. It achieves this by selectively activating high-resolution patches only in object-centric regions, thereby preserving critical details while discarding redundant information. The reported performance retention (86.4% to 89.9% of full-resolution performance) demonstrates its effectiveness.
- **Robustness to Small Objects**: Egocentric scenarios often feature small objects, which are highly sensitive to resolution loss. SmartRes's ability to selectively allocate high-resolution processing to relevant regions helps mitigate this issue, leading to better preservation of small-object grounding performance compared to uniform down-sampling or token pruning methods.
- **Generalizability**: The framework demonstrates generalization beyond egocentric benchmarks to standard referring expression comprehension datasets, indicating its potential applicability across broader visual grounding tasks where resolution optimization is beneficial.
- **Mitigation of Imbalance**: The introduction of a margin-regularized routing objective effectively addresses the severe foreground-background imbalance in egocentric data, enhancing the robustness of object-centric region selection. This methodological contribution could be applicable to other tasks with similar data characteristics.
- **Practical Deployment**: The improved efficiency and sustained accuracy make SmartRes a practical solution for deploying advanced visual grounding models on resource-constrained edge devices, such as those found in robotics, augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR) systems, and assistive technologies.

Future research directions identified by the authors include extending SmartRes to egocentric videos, which would require developing motion-aware patch selection, temporal token reuse, and streaming memory management to ensure temporal consistency in routing decisions. Further evaluation in closed-loop embodied settings is also suggested, to assess the impact of efficient high-resolution routing on downstream task success, grasp accuracy, manipulation robustness, and response latency, thus connecting efficient visual token allocation with practical deployment outcomes.
