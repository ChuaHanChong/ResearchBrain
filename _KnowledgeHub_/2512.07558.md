# ReLaX: Reasoning with Latent Exploration for Large Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Hong Kong Polytechnic University and Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Laboratory introduce ReLaX, a framework that counters "entropy collapse" in Large Reasoning Models by promoting heterogeneity in their latent dynamics, rather than just token-level entropy. By employing Dynamic Spectral Dispersion based on Koopman operator theory, ReLaX improved average multimodal reasoning accuracy by up to 8.3 percentage points and consistently outperformed token-level exploration methods.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) often leads to 'entropy collapse,' where policies become over-deterministic, limiting exploration and resulting in suboptimal performance.
- Existing token-level exploration methods are limited as they address superficial symptoms, struggle to maintain high token entropy, and conflict with RL's natural tendency towards low-entropy policies.
- For Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs), token-level feedback can be misaligned with underlying cross-modal computations, reducing its effectiveness in reflecting deeper processing.

## Method

- **ReLaX**, a framework that integrates a novel metric for ==latent dynamics== into the policy optimization process of RLVR, intervenes at the level of internal computational flexibility rather than token-level stochasticity.
- Utilizes Koopman operator theory and Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) to linearize the complex, nonlinear latent dynamics of LRMs, making them tractable for analysis.
- Introduces Dynamic Spectral Dispersion (DSD), a metric quantifying the heterogeneity of latent dynamics, which is then integrated into the GRPO objective with advantage shaping and adaptive KL regularization to guide exploration.

## Results

- ReLaX-VL-**3B** and ReLaX-VL-**7B** achieved absolute improvements of 8.3 and 5.3 percentage points, respectively, in average mean@1 accuracy across 7 multimodal reasoning benchmarks, setting a new performance level for **7B**-scale models.
- ReLaX consistently outperformed existing token-level exploration methods across both multimodal and text-only mathematical reasoning tasks, demonstrating better generalization across model families and more robust, semantically grounded reasoning.
- Ablation studies confirmed that DSD-based regularization, advantage shaping, and adaptive KL regularization are crucial for maintaining diverse latent dynamics, preventing entropy collapse, and ensuring stable, high-reward policy optimization.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Entropy collapse in LRMs is a symptom of rigid internal computations within the latent space, which lose flexibility under RLVR, suggesting a deeper problem than token-level over-determinism.
> - Koopman operator theory provides a robust framework for linearizing and analyzing the high-dimensional, nonlinear latent dynamics of LRMs, allowing for a principled quantification of their exploration capacity.
> - Guiding exploration by promoting heterogeneity in latent dynamics (quantified by DSD) offers a more effective and stable approach to the exploration-exploitation tradeoff than interventions in the discrete token space.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of scalable and verifiable reasoning capabilities is considered a foundational aspect for advancing large foundation models towards artificial general intelligence. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has emerged as an approach for enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Multimodal LLMs (MLLMs) on complex tasks. However, a known challenge in RL is the tendency for policies to become over-deterministic and reduce entropy, a phenomenon referred to as "entropy collapse." This issue can lead to premature policy convergence and suboptimal performance due to insufficient exploration of the solution space. The problem is exacerbated in RLVR by sparse rewards. Empirical observations have indicated an exponential relationship between policy entropy and reward.

Existing research efforts predominantly focus on addressing entropy collapse by promoting token-level diversity. These approaches include reshaping rewards, redesigning policy objectives with entropy-based regularization, and heuristically anchoring salient tokens to increase local stochasticity. Despite some progress, these token-level interventions face inherent limitations: maintaining high token-level entropy conflicts with the natural tendency of RL towards low-entropy policies. Furthermore, for MLLMs, there is often a misalignment between internal cross-modal computations and unimodal, text-centric outputs, which can limit the effectiveness of token-level feedback in reflecting underlying multimodal processing.

This paper proposes that entropy collapse is a symptom of a more fundamental issue: under RLVR, the internal computations governing token generation become rigid, losing flexibility. These computations are instantiated through the high-dimensional latent dynamics of hidden states, which are posited to embed richer and more stable inductive biases than those observable in the discrete token space. The work aims to address this by moving beyond token-level interventions and instead focusing on enabling tractable analysis and intervention in the continuous latent dynamics of LRMs. It introduces a framework to regulate exploration and exploitation by explicitly considering these latent dynamics, thereby fitting into the landscape as an alternative and deeper approach to a recognized limitation in RLVR.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed phenomenon of "entropy collapse" in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) applied to Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). While RLVR has demonstrated utility in enhancing reasoning capabilities, it frequently leads to policies that are overly deterministic. This over-determinism limits the model's ability to explore diverse solution paths, resulting in premature policy convergence and ultimately suboptimal performance. The existing paradigm of promoting exploration largely focuses on manipulating token-level entropy, such as adjusting reward functions, applying entropy regularization to policy objectives, or selectively encouraging stochasticity in specific tokens.

The authors argue that this token-level focus addresses merely the superficial symptoms of a deeper problem. Their core argument is that under RLVR, the internal computational processes that drive token generation progressively lose flexibility, settling into rigid and undiversified patterns. These internal computations are represented by the high-dimensional latent dynamics of the model's hidden states. The latent space is considered a richer and more stable environment for encoding inductive biases compared to the discrete and sensitive token space. Therefore, the researchers are motivated to intervene at this more fundamental, latent level.

To achieve this, the work identifies a critical need to make the continuous latent dynamics of LRMs analytically tractable. Modern Koopman operator theory is leveraged as a framework capable of linearizing complex, nonlinear dynamical systems within an infinite-dimensional space of observables. This theoretical foundation enables the development of a quantifiable measure for the flexibility of these internal computations.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this research are:
1. **Develop a Metric for Latent Dynamics Heterogeneity:** To propose a novel metric, Dynamic Spectral Dispersion (DSD), that quantifies the heterogeneity or flexibility of an LRM's latent dynamics. This metric is intended to serve as a direct indicator of the policy's exploration capacity by examining internal computational processes rather than surface-level token statistics.
2. **Propose a Latent Dynamics-Aware Framework:** To introduce Reasoning with Latent eXploration (ReLaX), a framework that integrates this DSD metric explicitly into the policy optimization process of RLVR. The goal is for ReLaX to regulate the exploration–exploitation tradeoff by counteracting the rigidification of internal computations.
3. **Demonstrate Performance and Generalizability:** To conduct extensive experiments across a range of multimodal and text-only reasoning benchmarks. The objective is to demonstrate that ReLaX consistently improves reasoning capabilities, outperforms existing token-level exploration methods, and generalizes across different model families and tasks. This aims to establish that guiding exploration within the latent space offers a more principled and effective approach than token-space interventions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ReLaX framework aims to mitigate entropy collapse in RLVR by promoting heterogeneity in the latent dynamics of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). This is achieved through three main methodological components: leveraging Koopman operator theory for linear representation of latent dynamics, introducing Dynamic Spectral Dispersion (DSD) to quantify latent exploration, and integrating DSD-based regularization into the GRPO objective.

#### 3.1. Leveraging Koopman Operator Theory and Dynamic Mode Decomposition

The paper posits that the explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT) in LRMs is decoded from latent hidden representations that evolve as a stochastic nonlinear dynamical system. To make these complex, high-dimensional latent dynamics tractable, the authors utilize Koopman operator theory. The Koopman operator linearizes nonlinear dynamics by representing their evolution in an infinite-dimensional space of "observables" (functions of the state). Dynamic Mode Decomposition (DMD) is employed as a data-driven, finite-dimensional approximation technique to estimate the Koopman operator (K) from observed state trajectories. Specifically, for a sequence of observables $V = \{g(x_0)..., g(x_{t-1})\}$ and their successors $V^+ = \{g(x_1)..., g(x_t)\}$, K is estimated via a least-squares problem: $K = V^+V^\dagger$. The approach incorporates ResDMD to filter out spurious eigenvalues that can arise from discretization when applying DMD to complex systems, aiming for a more accurate spectral characterization.

#### 3.2. Dynamic Spectral Dispersion (DSD)

Building on the Koopman operator, the paper introduces Dynamic Spectral Dispersion (DSD) as a metric to quantify the heterogeneity of an LRM's latent dynamics, which serves as an indicator of policy exploration. The spectral decomposition of the Koopman operator reveals fundamental dynamical modes (e.g., growth, decay, oscillation). A concentrated spectrum implies degenerate or repetitive behavior, whereas a dispersed spectrum signifies a richer and more expressive dynamical system, indicating computational flexibility.
Formally, DSD for a sequence of hidden states $x$ and its approximated Koopman operator $K$ is defined as the variance of the magnitudes of the Koopman eigenvalues: $DSD(x) = Var(|\Lambda|)$, where $K\Phi = \Phi\Lambda$. A higher DSD score implies richer intrinsic dynamical spectra, suggesting the model avoids rigid computational patterns and can leverage stochastic perturbations for diverse latent trajectories, thereby supporting sustained exploration.

#### 3.3. Koopman Dictionary Learning

A critical challenge in applying the Koopman operator is selecting an appropriate function space (Koopman dictionary, $g$) where the latent dynamics can be faithfully linearized. To address the high dimensionality and nonlinearity of LRMs, the authors adopt ResKoopNet, an extension of ResDMD that learns a neural Koopman dictionary. The Koopman observables $g(x)$ are parameterized by a single linear layer $W$ followed by a sigmoid activation: $g(x) = \sigma(Wx)$, where $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times m}$ and $m$ is the dimensionality of the approximated Koopman operator.
The dictionary $W$ is optimized by minimizing the spectral residual of the Koopman operator (Eq. 9) using latent trajectories collected from the last hidden layer of the initial policy. This optimization is performed once at the beginning of training (specifically, during the first step of sampling), and then $W$ is frozen. This ensures a consistent function space for analyzing policy latent dynamics throughout the subsequent policy optimization.

#### 3.4. ReLaX Framework with DSD-based Regularization

DSD is integrated into the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) objective to guide policy optimization towards an effective exploration–exploitation tradeoff.
- **Sequence-Level Regularization ($L_{xp}$):** For a group of responses with corresponding hidden states $\{x_i\}$, a regularization term is defined: $L_{xp} = \log \left( \frac{1}{R} \sum_{i=1}^R \exp(-DSD(x_i)) \right)$. The log-mean-exp computation is used to smooth DSD numerically and enhance gradient stability, encouraging higher DSD scores.
- **Advantage Shaping:** To ensure exploration occurs in meaningful subspaces, DSD scores are weighted by the positive part of the advantage values: $\tilde{L}_{xp} = \log \left( \frac{1}{R} \sum_{i=1}^R \exp(- \text{clip}(\hat{A}_i, 0) \cdot DSD(x_i)) \right)$. This mechanism encourages flexibility only along trajectories that yield positive rewards, preventing uninformative exploration.
- **Adaptive KL Regularization:** To prevent excessive dynamic divergence within positive trajectories, an adaptive KL regularization is applied. The KL divergence $D_{KL}(\pi_\theta(o_i) || \pi_{ref}(o_i))$ is penalized only for a subset of trajectories $I = \{ i | DSD(x_i) > \xi \}$, where $\xi$ is a DSD threshold. This softly constrains policy updates for trajectories with overly dispersed dynamics while allowing others to explore freely.
- **Overall Objective:** The final objective of ReLaX combines the GRPO objective with the advantage-shaped DSD regularization and the adaptive KL regularization: $J(\theta) = J_{GRPO}(\theta) + \alpha \tilde{L}_{xp} + \beta \sum_{i \in I} D_{KL}(\pi_\theta(o_i) || \pi_{ref}(o_i))$, where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are coefficients controlling the strength of the respective regularizations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate ReLaX encompassed a range of multimodal and text-only reasoning benchmarks, using various LRM scales (3B and 7B).

#### 4.1. Multimodal Reasoning Performance

- **Base Models:** Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct models at 3B and 7B scales were used.
- **Performance Gains:** ReLaX-VL-3B achieved an absolute improvement of 8.3 percentage points, and ReLaX-VL-7B achieved 5.3 percentage points, in average mean@1 accuracy across 7 multimodal benchmarks compared to their respective base models.
- **State-of-the-Art:** ReLaX-VL-7B attained an average score of 53.2, which establishes a new performance level among existing 7B-scale multimodal reasoning models, surpassing the previous best, VL-Rethinker-7B (52.5). ReLaX-VL-3B also demonstrated competitiveness, outperforming several previous 7B-level models like R1-VL (40.9) and OpenVLThinker (45.3).
- **Training Dynamics:** Analysis of training dynamics showed that under vanilla GRPO, both policy entropy and DSD rapidly declined, indicating a collapse into rigid computational patterns. In contrast, ReLaX maintained substantially more diverse latent dynamics and stabilized entropy at a higher level, leading to sustained policy improvement and relative performance gains of 10% on both 3B and 7B models. This suggests ReLaX facilitates a more effective exploration-exploitation tradeoff in MLLMs.

#### 4.2. Text-only Mathematical Reasoning Performance

- **Base Models:** Qwen2.5-3B-Base, Qwen2.5-7B-Base, and Qwen2.5-7B-Math were used.
- **Performance Gains:** ReLaX demonstrated substantial improvements over all three base models and their vanilla GRPO counterparts across 6 mathematical reasoning benchmarks (MATH500, Minerva, AMC 2022 & 2023, AIME 2024 & 2025).
- **Comparison to Baselines:** ReLaX outperformed existing GRPO variants designed for token-level exploration. For instance, it surpassed FR3E by 4.3 points on Qwen2.5-7B-Base and 6.3 points on Qwen2.5-7B-Math.
- **Generalization:** Supplementary experiments indicated that ReLaX generalized across model families, showing consistent improvements on Llama3.2-3B-Instruct and Qwen3-4B models compared to GRPO and other token-level methods like HICRA.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Hyperparameter Sensitivity (DSD regularization coefficient α):** The study showed that an optimal coefficient (0.1) led to the highest policy reward, correlating with increased policy entropy and mitigation of entropy collapse. Very low (0) or excessively high (1.0) coefficients either failed to prevent collapse or harmed performance.
- **Adaptive KL Regularization and Advantage Shaping:** Both components were found to be crucial for stable policy optimization. Removing adaptive KL regularization resulted in degraded performance due to uniform penalties. Removing advantage shaping led to a severe performance drop and training collapse, as exploration became indiscriminate, extending to trajectories with negative rewards.

#### 4.4. Comparative Analysis with Token-Level Methods

- **Multimodal Generalization:** ReLaX consistently outperformed token-level methods like entropy regularization and KL-Cov on VLMs. ReLaX showed notably larger gains on visually grounded tasks (e.g., +7.7 on EMMA-Physics) compared to mathematics-dominant benchmarks where visual grounding is less critical. This indicates that encouraging flexible latent dynamics is more effective for MLLMs, especially when cross-modal computation is significant.
- **Qualitative Results:**
 - **Text-only Reasoning (AMC23):** While both ReLaX and a token-level method (R1-zero-Div) could arrive at the correct answer, ReLaX performed self-verification using relevant mathematical principles (e.g., Law of Cosines). In contrast, R1-zero-Div attempted verification by generating Python code without an execution environment, suggesting a form of hallucination resulting from coarse-grained entropy encouragement.
 - **Multimodal Reasoning (DynaMath):** ReLaX demonstrated consistent visual understanding and correct reasoning across variants of a problem with modified visual numerical values. KL-Cov, however, failed on several variants due to misinterpretation of visual cues or incorrect reasoning, indicating degraded visual comprehension and reasoning capability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of ReLaX signifies a shift in addressing the exploration-exploitation tradeoff in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). By focusing on the intrinsic latent dynamics rather than solely the discrete token space, this work proposes a more fundamental approach to enhancing reasoning capabilities.

**Novelty in Exploration:** The core significance lies in identifying and quantifying computational rigidity within LRMs' internal processing as a root cause of entropy collapse. The Dynamic Spectral Dispersion (DSD) metric, derived from Koopman operator theory, provides a novel and principled way to measure the heterogeneity of latent dynamics, directly reflecting exploration capacity. This moves beyond traditional token-level entropy measures, offering a deeper diagnostic and intervention point.

**Improved Performance and Generalizability:** The consistent and substantial performance gains demonstrated across diverse benchmarks—including complex multimodal and text-only mathematical reasoning tasks—and various LRM families (e.g., Qwen2.5, Llama3.2, Qwen3) highlight the practical effectiveness of ReLaX. Outperforming existing token-level methods suggests that latent space interventions are more effective, particularly for MLLMs where cross-modal computations are critical. The qualitative results further underscore this, illustrating that ReLaX promotes more robust and semantically grounded reasoning, in contrast to superficial or "hallucinated" diversity seen with some token-level approaches.

**Theoretical Advancement and Future Directions:** This research contributes to a deeper understanding of how LRMs reason and how their internal states evolve during learning. By leveraging advanced dynamical systems theory (Koopman operators), it opens up a new avenue for analyzing and controlling complex behaviors in large neural networks. The finding that steering exploration via latent dynamics offers a more principled solution to the exploration-exploitation tradeoff positions the latent space as a promising frontier for further advancements in LRM capabilities. Future work could build upon this foundation to explore other aspects of latent dynamics, such as stability, predictability, or task-specific modes, potentially leading to more sophisticated control mechanisms. Further optimization of the computational efficiency of DSD calculation could also broaden the applicability of this methodology.
