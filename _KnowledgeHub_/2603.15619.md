# Mixture-of-Depths Attention

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Huazhong University of Science & Technology and ByteDance Seed developed Mixture-of-Depths Attention (MoDA), an architectural component that allows deep layers in large language models to dynamically retrieve information from all preceding layers. MoDA consistently improved perplexity and downstream task performance by 1.17-2.11 percentage points across model scales from 700M to 1.5B parameters, while achieving near FlashAttention-2 efficiency with a hardware-aware implementation.

## Problem

- Information dilution and signal degradation occur in deep Transformer models, causing valuable features from shallower layers to be lost or attenuated in deeper layers.
- Standard residual connections compress the entire depth history into a single hidden state, which does not fully resolve information dilution.
- Existing solutions like dense cross-layer connections are computationally prohibitive for large language models due to significant parameter and FLOPs overhead.

## Method

- Introduces **MoDA** (Mixture-of-Depths Attention), a ==unified attention mechanism== where each head attends to both standard sequence KV pairs and ==depth KV pairs== from all preceding layers at the same token position.
- Utilizes a single ==softmax normalization== across all attention scores (sequence and depth) to enable data-dependent, adaptive retrieval of historical depth information.
- Developed a ==hardware-aware CUDA kernel== with ==Triton==, featuring ==chunk-aware depth KV layout== and ==group-aware indexing==, to ensure efficient memory access and computation for large-scale training.

## Results

- MoDA consistently reduced language modeling perplexity across diverse validation domains, with average PPL decreasing from **15.61 to 15.46** for 700M models and from **13.67 to 13.47** for 1.5B models.
- It improved average downstream task performance by **1.76%** for 700M models and **2.11%** for 1.5B models compared to existing baselines.
- The hardware-optimized MoDA kernel achieved **97.3%** of FlashAttention-2's efficiency at a sequence length of 64K, demonstrating an approximate **1**458x** speedup** over naive PyTorch and only **2.73%** relative extra time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Enabling deep layers to explicitly attend to historical depth information through a unified attention mechanism effectively mitigates information dilution, leading to performance gains.
> - Generating depth KV pairs from the FFN layers' inputs offers a favorable balance between improved accuracy and computational efficiency.
> - A highly optimized hardware-aware implementation, including chunk-aware and group-aware KV layouts with fused online softmax, is essential for practical deployment and achieving near optimal GPU utilization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large language models (LLMs) has been characterized by scaling across several dimensions: context length, training data volume, model width, and model depth. While advances have been observed across these fronts, the paper highlights that increasing model depth, despite its potential for richer hierarchical computation, often presents challenges. Deep Transformers can suffer from optimization difficulties and "information dilution," where salient features generated in shallower layers become progressively degraded or difficult to retrieve in deeper layers due to repeated residual updates. Standard residual connections, while improving optimization stability, compress the entire depth history into a single hidden state, thus failing to fully resolve information dilution.

Previous attempts to address this issue include dense cross-layer connections (DenseNet-style), which preserve more extensive layer-wise history. However, these methods typically incur substantial parameter and computational costs, limiting their practical application in very large LLMs. The success of attention mechanisms in sequence modeling, which dynamically mix information, suggests that a similar data-dependent, adaptive approach could be beneficial for aggregating information along the depth dimension. This work positions itself within this context by proposing a novel architectural component that aims to enable efficient and effective depth scaling in LLMs, balancing expressivity, efficiency, and hardware friendliness, without incurring the prohibitive costs of dense connections.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the problem of information dilution and signal degradation that occurs as large language models increase in depth. The authors observe that while depth scaling is a key driver for LLM performance, valuable features from early layers are often lost or attenuated through successive residual connections, making them less accessible to deeper layers. This hinders the model's ability to fully leverage its increased depth.

The core motivations for introducing Mixture-of-Depths Attention (MoDA) are:
- **Mitigate Information Dilution:** To provide a mechanism that allows deeper layers to directly access and utilize informative features that originate in shallower layers, thus preventing their dilution or loss.
- **Improve Depth Scaling Efficiency:** To develop a method that facilitates effective scaling of model depth without incurring the significant computational or parameter overhead associated with existing approaches like dense cross-layer connections. The goal is to achieve this with minimal additional computational cost and memory footprint.
- **Enhance Representational Capacity:** By enabling adaptive retrieval of historical depth information, MoDA aims to enrich the information available to each layer, potentially leading to more robust and powerful representations within the model.
- **Develop a Hardware-Efficient Implementation:** To ensure that the proposed architectural change is practical for large-scale LLM training, a critical objective was to design a hardware-aware algorithm that resolves common efficiency bottlenecks, such as non-contiguous memory access patterns, inherent in novel attention mechanisms. This includes achieving performance comparable to highly optimized kernels like FlashAttention-2.
- **Demonstrate Empirical Effectiveness:** To empirically validate that MoDA consistently outperforms strong baseline models across various evaluation metrics, including perplexity on diverse validation benchmarks and performance on a range of downstream tasks, under different model scales and architectural configurations.

In essence, the research aims to establish MoDA as a practical and effective primitive for designing deeper, more capable, and computationally efficient Transformer-based LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The paper introduces Mixture-of-Depths Attention (MoDA) by conceptualizing Transformer block stacking through a "read, operate, write" framework along the depth stream, comparing it with existing mechanisms.

**Conceptual Framework for Depth Stream Mechanisms:**
- **Depth Residual:** This is the standard Transformer approach, where a layer reads the previous layer's output (identity), operates on it (attention or FFN), and writes back by adding its output to the input (residual connection). This compresses historical information into a fixed-size tensor, leading to information dilution.
- **Depth Dense:** Inspired by DenseNet, this approach reads a set of all preceding representations, linearly projects them, operates, and writes back by concatenating the current layer's output with the historical set. This preserves information but incurs a high computational cost, scaling quadratically with depth and width (O(T L² D²)).
- **Depth Attention:** An intermediate formulation proposed by the authors. It reads historical depth information using an attention mechanism, allowing data-dependent retrieval. Specifically, a query from the current layer attends to key-value (KV) pairs from preceding layers at the same token position. The layer then writes by concatenating its own KV projections to the depth stream. This reduces computational cost to O(T L² D) compared to Depth Dense.
- **Mixture-of-Depths Attention (MoDA):** This is the core contribution. MoDA integrates depth attention with standard causal sequence attention into a single unified attention mechanism.
 - **Unified Attention:** Each attention head in a layer jointly attends to:
 - **Sequence KV pairs:** From the current layer's input tokens (standard self-attention).
 - **Depth KV pairs:** From *all* preceding layers at the *same token position*.
 - **Single Softmax Normalization:** All attention scores (from both sequence and depth KVs) are jointly normalized by a single softmax function, creating a unified representation space.
 - **KV Projection Strategy:** For the "write" step, the current layer's KV pair is appended to the depth stream. The paper explores variants:
 - Reusing preceding attention layers' sequence KV as depth KV (minimal overhead).
 - Projecting the FFN's input to generate additional depth KV pairs ("Extra FFN KV Proj.").
 - Creating individual depth KV projections specifically for attention layers ("Extra Attn KV Proj.").
 - **Complexity Analysis (Table 1):** MoDA achieves favorable complexity, with parameters scaling as O(L D²/G) (where G is GQA group size), decoding cache as O(L D/G), and FLOPs as O(L² D) for decoding and O(T L² D) for prefilling. This makes it more parameter-efficient than Depth Attention (O(LD²)) by reusing query projections from sequence attention.

**Hardware-aware Implementation (Algorithm 1):**
To ensure MoDA is practical for large-scale training, the authors developed a highly optimized CUDA kernel using Triton, addressing GPU memory access and computation efficiency.
- **Memory Hierarchy Optimization:** Recognizes that GPUs are optimized for contiguous memory access and large-scale parallel computation.
- **Flash-Compatible Depth KV Layout:** Initially, the depth KV cache is flattened along a T × L axis, allowing contiguous block reads for depth lookup. However, this is inefficient as only a block-diagonal region (utilization 1/T) is relevant.
- **Chunk-aware Depth KV Layout:** Queries are processed in chunks (length C). Depth KV is reorganized by chunk, reducing the effective depth span scanned by each query chunk from T × L to C × L. This improves depth utilization to 1/C.
- **Group-aware Depth KV Calculation:** Leverages Grouped Query Attention (GQA) where `G` adjacent query rows share the same base-time index. This means a query chunk of length C only needs to access (C/G) × L depth-KV blocks, increasing effective depth utilization to G/C. This also ensures index consistency between sequence causality (floor(i_q/G) >= i_k) and depth matching (floor(i_q/G) == floor(j_d/L)).
- **Fused Computation with Online Softmax:** Both sequence and depth attention scores are accumulated into shared on-chip online-softmax states (running maximum logit, normalizer, and unnormalized output accumulator) without intermediate materialization to High Bandwidth Memory (HBM). This minimizes HBM traffic and improves compute utilization.

This comprehensive methodology aims to demonstrate MoDA's theoretical soundness and practical viability through both architectural design and efficient implementation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding MoDA's performance, efficiency, and architectural characteristics.

#### 4.1. Performance of MoDA Variants and Scaling

- **Effectiveness of Depth KV:** Integrating "Depth KV" (reusing preceding attention layers' sequence KV) into the attention mechanism, even with a negligible 0.12% increase in FLOPs, led to notable improvements. On 700M models, it improved training perplexity (PPL) by 0.41, C4 validation PPL by 0.11, and average downstream task performance by 1.17 percentage points compared to a vanilla attention baseline.
- **Importance of FFN Layers' Depth KV:** Adding "Extra FFN KV Projection," which involves generating depth KV pairs from the FFN layers' inputs, further improved performance. This variant (termed "Ours" and corresponding to row 4 in Table 3) provided the most favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off, showing improvements of 0.18 train PPL, 0.27 C4 validation PPL, and 0.77 downstream average over the "Depth KV only" variant. It also outperformed a more-parameter baseline (OLMo2 with two additional layers) with similar parameter and FLOP counts, demonstrating that FFN-side depth information contributes meaningfully.
- **Saturation of Extra Attention KV Projection:** Introducing additional, specific depth KV projections for attention layers ("Extra Attn KV Projection") yielded only marginal further gains (0.07 train PPL, 0.04 C4 validation PPL, 0.10 downstream average) while introducing a non-trivial increase in parameters and FLOPs, suggesting saturation.
- **Consistent Gains Across Model Scales:** MoDA demonstrated stable performance improvements when scaled from 700M to 1.5B parameters. On downstream benchmarks, average performance increased by 1.76% for 700M models and 2.11% for 1.5B models compared to the OLMo2 baseline. These gains were observed across diverse tasks, including commonsense (HellaSwag, WinoGrande, COPA), reasoning (OpenBookQA, ARC-Challenge, SciQ), and broad-knowledge (BoolQ, MMLU).
- **Broad Perplexity Reductions:** Validation perplexity across ten diverse domains showed consistent improvements, with average PPL decreasing from 15.61 to 15.46 at 700M, and from 13.67 to 13.47 at 1.5B. All ten domains showed reduced perplexity.

#### 4.2. Analysis of MoDA with Layer Number and Normalization

- **Effectiveness Across Depth Budgets:** MoDA remained effective in both deeper (48-layer) and shallower (24-layer) model configurations. Introducing "Depth KV" consistently reduced validation loss across these different depths.
- **Interaction with Normalization:** In deeper models (48 layers), the post-normalization configuration (post-norm) benefited more significantly from the introduction of "Depth KV" compared to the pre-normalization (pre-norm) setting, indicating a stronger optimization impact in post-norm deeper stacks. "Extra FFN KV Projection" provided additional gains in both pre-norm and post-norm settings.

#### 4.3. Attention Visualization

- Heatmap visualizations demonstrated that a substantial and persistent portion of attention mass was allocated to the "Depth KV" block, particularly in middle and later layers. This indicates that the model actively retrieves information from preceding layers.
- The visualization suggested that MoDA alters the typical "attention sink" behavior often observed in Transformers, distributing probability mass more broadly across potentially informative sequence and depth locations rather than collapsing it onto a few fixed positions.

#### 4.4. Hardware Efficiency

- **Incremental Optimization:** A detailed ablation study on the hardware-aware implementation quantified the impact of each optimization:
 - Flash-compatible depth-KV layout provided a 162.5× speedup over a naive PyTorch implementation.
 - Chunk-aware depth-KV layout further reduced runtime by 52.0%.
 - Group-aware indexing yielded an additional 4.31× speedup.
 - Cumulatively, these optimizations achieved an approximate 1458× end-to-end speedup compared to the naive baseline.
- **Near FlashAttention-2 Efficiency:** The hardware-efficient MoDA kernel achieved 97.3% of FlashAttention-2's efficiency at a sequence length of 64K (under specific configurations), with only 2.73% relative extra time. The extra time percentage decreased as sequence length increased and GQA group size increased, and it increased with model depth, consistent with predictable scaling behavior.

These results collectively indicate that MoDA is an effective and efficient architectural enhancement for deep LLMs, capable of improving performance while maintaining high hardware utilization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Mixture-of-Depths Attention (MoDA) represents a significant development in the architectural design of large language models, particularly concerning their ability to scale effectively with increasing depth.

### 6. Significance

- **Effective Mitigation of Information Dilution:** MoDA directly addresses the long-standing challenge of information dilution in deep Transformer networks. By enabling layers to dynamically and adaptively retrieve relevant information from all preceding layers via an attention mechanism, it ensures that valuable features from shallower layers remain accessible, preventing their degradation or loss.
- **Hardware-Efficient Depth Scaling:** The work demonstrates that this architectural enhancement can be implemented with high hardware efficiency. The proposed hardware-aware kernel, utilizing chunk-aware layout, group-aware indexing, and fused online-softmax states, achieves near FlashAttention-2 efficiency, making MoDA practical for the training of very large models with long context lengths. This low computational overhead (a negligible 3.7% FLOPs for observed performance gains) is crucial for large-scale industrial deployment.
- **Stable and Consistent Performance Gains:** MoDA consistently improves language modeling perplexity and downstream task performance across various model sizes (700M to 1.5B parameters) and diverse benchmarks. This stability across scales and tasks suggests a robust underlying mechanism that is not limited to specific model configurations or data distributions.
- **New Design Primitive for Transformers:** MoDA offers a novel architectural primitive for depth scaling that balances expressivity (data-dependent retrieval) with efficiency, avoiding the prohibitive costs of dense connections. This provides a new avenue for designing more capable and optimized LLMs.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enabling Deeper and More Powerful LLMs:** MoDA could facilitate the development of even deeper Transformer architectures that are less prone to performance plateaus or degradation associated with increased layer count. This could lead to LLMs with enhanced reasoning capabilities, richer hierarchical understanding, and improved performance across a wider array of complex tasks.
- **Broader Application Beyond Language:** The architectural principle of principled depth-aware information aggregation, where attention is extended from sequence to depth, is architecture-agnostic. The authors suggest its potential applicability to other domains where Transformers are increasingly used, such as multimodal intelligence, visual understanding, and world models. This could generalize the benefits of MoDA to a broader range of AI applications.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper opens several avenues for future research, including:
 - **Advanced CUDA Engineering:** Further optimization of the MoDA kernel for industrial-scale training, involving improved memory scheduling, deeper computation pipelining, and tighter overlap with distributed communication.
 - **Memory Bottleneck Mitigation:** Investigating strategies like bounded depth-KV slot caching to address the memory overhead of storing all historical depth-KV states in extremely deep networks. This would involve dynamic selection policies (e.g., utility-based or sliding-window) for depth-KV entries.
 - **Understanding Attention Redistribution:** Further investigation into how MoDA alters attention patterns, particularly regarding the observed reduction in "attention sink" behavior, could yield new insights into Transformer mechanisms.

Overall, MoDA represents a practical and effective step toward improving the depth-scaling properties of large language models, offering a foundation for future architectural innovations and broader applications in artificial intelligence.
