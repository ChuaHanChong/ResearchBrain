# MotionVLA: Injecting Geometric Motion into Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> MotionVLA is a motion-history interface for VLA policies that converts a past-only video window into compact, time-continuous trajectory-field tokens, retrieved by current visual tokens via cross-attention and recoupled under trajectory-grounded supervision. It improves long-horizon manipulation success and produces smoother, more direct executions than discrete 4D-frame injection baselines.

## Problem

- Discrete 4D injection samples finite historical frames and lifts each independently via RGB-D back-projection, causing the same physical point to drift across frames.
- Sparse or non-uniform keyframe/memory sampling fragments temporal continuity, making historical evidence irregular and hard for the policy to exploit.
- More spatiotemporal evidence is assumed to help VLA policies, but injected history is not verified to be motion-consistent before conditioning action generation.

## Method

- **Past-only motion history** built via a frozen ==trajectory extractor== (TraceAnything) that converts a strided head-camera window into compact final-layer decoder tokens as a queryable ==key-value memory==.
- **Decouple-then-Recouple fusion**: current visual tokens query the motion history via ==multi-head cross-attention== to retrieve task-relevant motion-conditioned tokens aligned to the present observation.
- **Trajectory-grounded supervision** adds an auxiliary history-action reconstruction loss on retrieved tokens plus a ==token-mixing MLP== recoupling motion tokens with multi-view visual tokens for the ==flow-matching== action expert.

## Results

- On RoboTwin2.0, MotionVLA reaches **53%** average success versus **41%** for the pi-0 baseline, with the long-horizon Touch task improving from **19%** to **41%**.
- On LIBERO, MotionVLA achieves **95.4%** average success and **91.2%** on LIBERO-LONG, a **6.0-point** gain over pi-0, and lower path efficiency (**1.05/1.10** vs **1.23/1.19**) than baseline on GOAL/LONG.
- On real-world Agilex Piper tasks, average success rises from **28.7%** to **41.9%** and average completion steps drop from **338** to **282**; component ablation shows naive history alone drops success to **13%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - More spatiotemporal context is not inherently better; motion-consistency of injected evidence matters more than context quantity for control stability.
> - Path efficiency, not just terminal success rate, exposes whether a policy is producing motion-consistent versus detour-prone executions.
> - Query-conditioned retrieval plus auxiliary grounding supervision is needed; naive concatenation of history or 4D tokens can actively harm performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language-action (VLA) models represent a significant development in robot control, enabling language-conditioned manipulation by integrating visual observations, proprioception, and natural language instructions. These models frequently build upon pre-trained vision-language models (VLMs) and incorporate an action head for continuous control. A common characteristic of recent VLA approaches, particularly for long-horizon manipulation tasks, is the increasing reliance on various forms of temporal or spatial evidence, such as historical observations, depth maps, 3D geometry, or more comprehensive 4D spatiotemporal features. This trend aims to address ambiguities that arise when purely reactive policies, which primarily consider the current observation, encounter visually similar states that require different actions based on past interactions or ongoing subgoals.

However, the authors identify a limitation within this prevalent strategy. They posit that simply injecting more spatiotemporal evidence does not guarantee improved performance. Instead, if the injected evidence lacks motion consistency, it can introduce issues such as geometric drift, fragmented temporal cues, and contribute to unstable action generation. Existing methods often construct this 4D evidence by sampling a finite set of historical frames, independently lifting each into geometry-aware tokens, and then sparsifying this history. This frame-wise approach, while yielding a 4D representation, may not maintain physical coherence across time. Independent lifting can lead to inconsistencies where the perceived 3D location of a physical point drifts across frames, and sparse sampling can fragment temporal information, making it difficult for the policy to effectively utilize motion cues. These issues can manifest as inefficient end-effector paths, jittery corrections, or unstable optimization during policy execution.

This work positions itself to address this specific challenge by proposing that effective VLA memory should prioritize representing the *motion* that connects observations, rather than merely accumulating more individual observations. By focusing on motion-consistent evidence, MotionVLA aims to provide a more stable and usable historical context for robot control, thereby enhancing long-horizon manipulation capabilities and leading to more direct and smoother robot executions compared to methods that rely on fragmented or geometrically inconsistent historical information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenges encountered by robot policies in long-horizon manipulation tasks, particularly the issue of visual aliasing. In multi-stage tasks, an identical current visual observation might correspond to different control states depending on the robot's recent movements or the stage of the task. For instance, the robot might need to continue a subgoal, terminate it, or transition to a new one, all while observing a similar scene. Relying solely on the current visual frame makes reactive policies susceptible to fragility and potential state chaos, where the robot struggles to interpret its state and make consistent decisions. This underscores the necessity for memory that provides context beyond the immediate observation.

Current VLA models have increasingly incorporated additional temporal or spatial information (e.g., history, depth, 4D features) to mitigate such ambiguities. However, the authors argue that the mere quantity of spatiotemporal evidence is not the sole determinant of policy performance. A critical assumption often implicit in these approaches is that the injected evidence is inherently coherent and useful for control. Through their analysis, the authors observe that this assumption does not consistently hold. Specifically, VLA variants employing 4D injection methods can still exhibit less direct end-effector paths and substantial detours compared to expert demonstrations or baseline policies. This suggests a fundamental bottleneck: the quality and consistency of the injected historical evidence.

The core objective of this work is to address this bottleneck by developing a novel motion-history interface that provides *motion-consistent* evidence to VLA models. The central question guiding this research is whether a VLA should remember past frames individually or rather remember the underlying motion that connects these frames. To this end, the specific objectives are:

1. To design and implement a compact, time-continuous motion-history interface, named MotionVLA, that converts a short window of past observations into trajectory-field tokens, representing recent observations as physically coherent motion evidence.
2. To integrate this motion history into the VLA stream using a "Decouple-then-Recouple" design. This involves current visual tokens querying the motion history to retrieve task-relevant motion information, which is then recoupled into the VLA stream.
3. To ensure that the retrieved motion tokens are effectively grounded in control-relevant dynamics through trajectory-grounded supervision during training.
4. To demonstrate that this motion-consistent approach leads to improved long-horizon manipulation success and produces smoother, more direct robot executions in both simulated and real-world environments, in contrast to methods that rely on fragmented or geometrically inconsistent historical data.

By achieving these objectives, MotionVLA aims to provide a more stable and interpretable form of memory for VLA policies, thereby enhancing their robustness and efficiency in complex manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MotionVLA introduces a motion-history interface designed to provide compact, time-continuous trajectory-field tokens to a VLA policy, contrasting with methods that treat historical frames as a sparse collection of independently processed observations. The methodology is built upon a standard VLA policy backbone, specifically `π0`, and augments it with a structured approach for integrating motion history.

**Preliminaries:**
The base VLA policy (`π0`) operates at discrete timesteps, receiving multi-view RGB observations, proprioception, and natural language instructions. It predicts an H-step continuous action chunk. Actions are modeled using a flow-based action expert parameterized by a conditional velocity field, which is integrated to generate actions. For summarizing short-horizon dynamics, MotionVLA leverages a frozen, pre-trained trajectory extractor, TraceAnything. Given a sequence of head-camera frames, this extractor represents each pixel by a continuous-time 3D trajectory function and provides compact spatiotemporal tokens (denoted as Z) from its final decoder layer. These tokens serve as the foundation for the motion history.

**Building a Past-only Motion History:**
At each timestep `t`, MotionVLA constructs a queryable motion history `M_kv` from past head-camera observations. Critically, this history explicitly excludes the current frame `I_head_t`, ensuring that the memory is strictly past-only. The history is formed from a selected window `W_t` of `T_hist` past frames, sampled with a temporal stride `Δ`. This window effectively spans `T_hist * Δ` raw camera frames. To handle initial timesteps where insufficient past frames are available, the window is "left-padded" by repeating the earliest available frame. During training, `W_t` is indexed from the episode's recorded frames, while during inference, it is maintained via a per-episode FIFO buffer, ensuring consistent operation.

The selected head-camera sequence `W_t` is fed into the frozen trajectory extractor, which outputs the compact spatiotemporal tokens `Z_t`. These tokens are then projected to the VLA's hidden embedding size using a learned weight matrix `W_z` and augmented with positional encodings to form `M_kv`. This token-level interface maintains cross-frame motion evidence while managing computational complexity and sensitivity to noise that might arise from dense trajectory-field outputs. `M_kv` functions purely as a key-value memory for subsequent retrieval.

**Decouple-then-Recouple 4D Fusion:**
The integration of this motion history into the VLA stream follows a three-step "Decouple-then-Recouple" strategy:

1. **Decouple: Query-conditioned Retrieval**: The current head-camera visual tokens (`V_head`), produced by the VLA's visual encoder, are utilized as queries in a Multi-Head Attention (MHA) mechanism. This MHA retrieves motion-conditioned evidence (`M`) from the previously constructed past-only motion history `M_kv`. This design ensures that historical motion evidence is selectively aligned and retrieved based on the current visual context, rather than being passively concatenated or uniformly integrated. The output `M` has the same token length as the current head-view queries.

2. **Trajectory-grounded Motion Tokens**: To prevent the retrieved motion-conditioned tokens `M` from merely acting as static appearance summaries, an auxiliary loss, `L_traj`, is incorporated during training. The overall objective becomes `L = L_action + α L_traj`. `L_action` is the standard flow-matching behavior cloning loss from `π0`. For `L_traj`, a lightweight prediction head `g_ϕ` is attached to `M` to predict an action sequence `a_hist` that corresponds to the history window `W_t`. This auxiliary supervision encourages `M` to encode control-relevant dynamics and temporal progress, grounding the tokens in meaningful robot actions rather than static features. The auxiliary head is detached during inference.

3. **Recouple: Lightweight Fusion**: The motion-conditioned tokens `M` are then fused with the VLA's current multi-view visual tokens (`V_mv`). This is achieved by concatenating `M` and `V_mv` and feeding them through a token-mixing MLP to obtain fused tokens `F`. These `F` tokens are subsequently fed into the VLA backbone along with text and robot-state tokens. This allows the flow-based action expert to condition its action predictions on both current perception and the retrieved, motion-consistent historical evidence through shared self-attention mechanisms.

**Training Strategy:**
The training process adopts a generic-to-specific two-stage approach. Stage I involves training on a heterogeneous manipulation dataset (100 hours of data) to align the motion-history interface with the VLA embedding space, utilizing both the `L_action` and `L_traj` losses. Stage II then adapts the policy to specific target tasks, with the auxiliary trajectory head detached, optimizing solely for `L_action`.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MotionVLA was structured around three key questions: its impact on long-horizon manipulation success, its ability to produce motion-consistent executions, and the specific contributions of its design components.

**Simulation Benchmarks:**
Experiments were conducted on RoboTwin2.0 and LIBERO benchmarks.
- **RoboTwin2.0**: MotionVLA achieved an average success rate of 53%, representing a 12 percentage point improvement over the `π0` baseline (41%). This improvement was particularly evident on long-horizon tasks, such as "blocks touching rgb," where success increased from 19% to 41%. The full MotionVLA model (with Recouple) generally outperformed a variant where recoupling was omitted.
- **LIBERO**: MotionVLA obtained an average success rate of 95.4%, which was the highest among the compared methods. It also achieved the best score on the LIBERO-LONG suite (91.2%), demonstrating a 6.0 percentage point improvement over `π0` for long-horizon tasks. These results indicate that the structured injection of motion history contributed to robust performance across diverse manipulation challenges.

**Real-world Validation:**
Preliminary real-robot rollouts were performed on an Agilex Piper setup across three temporally demanding tasks (Pick&Place, Ranking, Touching).
- MotionVLA improved the average success rate from 28.7% for `π0` to 41.9%.
- It also reduced the average completion steps from 338 to 282, suggesting more efficient executions. The improvements were observed across different task complexities, including the temporally structured Ranking and Touching tasks.

**Motion Consistency and Path Efficiency:**
Beyond terminal success, the study evaluated motion consistency using path efficiency (PE), defined as the ratio of the end-effector travel length of the policy to that of an expert demonstration. A PE of 1 indicates an expert-like path.
- On LIBERO-GOAL and LIBERO-LONG, MotionVLA achieved PE values of 1.05 and 1.10, respectively. These values were closer to the expert path length compared to `π0` (1.23 for GOAL, 1.19 for LONG) and 4D-VLA (1.36 for GOAL, 1.32 for LONG).
- A variant, `4D-VLA*` (using pseudo-RGBD), showed notably higher PE values (1.88 for GOAL, 2.06 for LONG), suggesting that noisy or geometrically inconsistent per-frame information can indeed lead to substantial detours and less efficient paths.

**Component Evidence and Ablation Studies:**
A series of ablation studies investigated the contribution of MotionVLA's individual components.
- **Naive History/4D Injection**: Simply adding visual history tokens or unaligned 4D tokens without the proposed architecture often resulted in a reduction of success rates compared to the `π0` baseline (e.g., average 13-16% for naive injections vs. 37% for `π0` on two RoboTwin2.0 tasks). This highlights that merely providing more context is insufficient and can be detrimental without proper structuring.
- **Stage-I Alignment**: The initial alignment stage significantly improved performance (average success increased to 40%).
- **Auxiliary Trajectory Grounding (`L_traj`)**: Adding the auxiliary trajectory reconstruction objective further boosted success rates (average to 50%), confirming its role in grounding motion-conditioned tokens in control-relevant dynamics.
- **Recoupling (Projector)**: The final recoupling step (projector) provided an additional performance gain (average to 54%), demonstrating the importance of the integrated fusion mechanism. This pattern supports the proposed "decouple-then-recouple" interface.
- **Training Efficiency**: MotionVLA exhibited faster convergence during downstream adaptation, suggesting that motion-history tokens provide a beneficial optimization prior.
- **History Window and Stride**: An ablation on the "blocks touching rgb" task showed that a history window of `T_hist = 5` past frames with a stride of `Δ = 2` (covering 10 raw frames) provided the optimal balance of success rate (41%) and inference speed. Longer or denser histories did not consistently improve performance and could increase computation or introduce irrelevant early-stage motion.
- **Robustness to History Corruption**: Controlled experiments revealed that MotionVLA degrades progressively under increasing levels of corruption applied *only* to the history frames fed to the trajectory extractor. Even under severe corruption, MotionVLA maintained performance above the clean `π0` baseline, indicating bounded robustness to failures in the motion-history branch.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces MotionVLA, a novel approach that redefines the integration of historical information into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation. Its significance lies in shifting the focus from simply providing more spatiotemporal context to ensuring the *motion consistency* of the historical evidence.

The primary contribution is the development of a time-continuous motion-history interface that leverages compact trajectory-field tokens. This interface effectively transforms fragmented, frame-based historical observations into a physically coherent representation of motion. The proposed "Decouple-then-Recouple" design, coupled with trajectory-grounded supervision, is crucial for aligning task-relevant motion evidence with current perception and ensuring that the retrieved information is directly usable for stable control.

The empirical results demonstrate that MotionVLA significantly advances long-horizon manipulation capabilities. It achieves superior success rates across challenging simulation benchmarks like RoboTwin2.0 and LIBERO, particularly for complex, multi-stage tasks. Beyond mere task completion, a critical finding is MotionVLA's ability to produce smoother and more direct robot executions, as evidenced by its improved path efficiency compared to both the baseline and other 4D-injection methods. This suggests that resolving spatiotemporal inconsistencies in historical data is as important as, if not more important than, simply augmenting the policy with additional contextual frames or raw geometric information. The preliminary real-world validation further supports the practical applicability of this interface, indicating its potential to transfer beyond simulation.

The conceptual impact of MotionVLA lies in its argument that effective VLA memory should represent the *dynamics* connecting observations rather than just storing discrete observations. This perspective can guide future research in designing memory architectures for robot learning, moving towards representations that intrinsically encode physical processes and temporal continuity.

Potential impact includes:
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy**: By enabling more stable and efficient long-horizon manipulation, MotionVLA could contribute to the development of more capable and reliable autonomous robotic systems in complex real-world environments.
- **Improved Safety and Efficiency**: Smoother and more direct trajectories reduce unnecessary movements, potentially leading to faster task completion, lower energy consumption, and reduced wear and tear on robotic hardware. The reduced jitter and detours also contribute to safer interactions with objects and environments.
- **Inspiration for Memory Design**: The "decouple-then-recouple" and trajectory-grounded supervision mechanisms offer a blueprint for future memory modules in VLA models, emphasizing selective retrieval and dynamically relevant conditioning.
- **Bridging Perception and Control**: By explicitly modeling motion, MotionVLA strengthens the link between visual perception of dynamics and actionable control commands, which is fundamental for advanced robot behaviors.

While the research notes limitations such as the reliance on a frozen trajectory extractor and preliminary real-world validation, it provides a substantive advancement in understanding and addressing the crucial role of motion-consistent memory in VLA models. Future work on more robust trajectory extraction and broader real-world deployment could further expand its impact.
