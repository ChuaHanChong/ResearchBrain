# RLinf: Flexible and Efficient Large-scale Reinforcement Learning via Macro-to-Micro Flow Transformation

> [!summary] Summary
> RLinf, developed by researchers from Tsinghua University, Infinigence AI, and other institutions, presents a system for large-scale reinforcement learning using a Macro-to-Micro Flow Transformation (M2Flow) paradigm. It decouples logical workflow programming from physical execution to dynamically optimize resource utilization and execution modes, achieving substantial speedups in complex RL tasks involving LLMs and embodied agents.

## Problem

- Modern RL training systems struggle with the inherent heterogeneity and dynamicity of workflows involving diverse components like LLM generation, inference, and training.
- Existing fixed execution modes (collocated or disaggregated) lead to low hardware utilization due to 'long-tail problems' and resource imbalances.
- Current systems lack flexibility, requiring extensive manual tuning or significant code changes to adapt to varying workloads, increasing programming complexity and hindering optimal training speed.

## Method

- **RLinf** introduces Macro-to-Micro Flow Transformation (M2Flow), which separates high-level logical workflow programming from fine-grained physical execution planning.
- It employs a 'Worker' abstraction with adaptive communication, elastic pipelining for spatial scheduling, and automatic context switching for temporal scheduling.
- A profiling-guided scheduler automatically determines optimal execution plans by evaluating temporal, spatial, and hybrid scheduling policies based on estimated execution time and memory usage.

## Results

- RLinf achieved **1.10x** to **1.58x** speedup for Reasoning RL (Qwen2.5-GRPO) and **38.7%** to **69.6%** speedup for PPO compared to veRL, a state-of-the-art baseline.
- For Embodied RL, RLinf-Hybrid showed 52.2–**69.1%** higher throughput on ManiSkill, and RLinf-Temporal was **37.8%** to **143.4%** faster than SimpleVLA-RL on LIBERO tasks.
- The profiling-guided scheduler accurately estimates performance (errors below **5%**) and efficiently finds optimal plans (within 7x10^-4 to 5.98 seconds), demonstrating its effectiveness in adapting to diverse workloads.
- Models trained with RLinf demonstrated improved performance, including up to 20 points higher on math reasoning benchmarks for Qwen2.5 models and increased success rates for OpenVLA on embodied tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decoupling logical workflow from physical execution provides a flexible and efficient way to orchestrate diverse and dynamic RL components.
> - Dynamically adapting between temporal, spatial, and hybrid execution modes is crucial for maximizing hardware utilization and training throughput across heterogeneous RL workloads.
> - An efficient, profiling-guided search policy can effectively navigate complex scheduling spaces to identify optimal resource allocation and execution strategies without manual intervention.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current landscape of Artificial Intelligence is heavily influenced by the rapid advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs). However, the paper posits that merely scaling LLMs further is yielding diminishing returns. Instead, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a critical paradigm to push intelligence beyond pretraining, enabling capabilities such as aligning LLMs with human preferences (RLHF), improving reasoning, and fostering autonomous interaction in complex environments (embodied intelligence). This shift is so significant that OpenAI and others predict RL workloads will soon consume more computational resources than LLM pretraining itself.

Existing RL training systems, however, struggle to cope with the "inherent heterogeneity and dynamicity" of modern RL workflows. These workflows combine diverse components like LLM generation, inference, training, reward models, critic models, agent tooling, and embodied environment simulators, each with distinct workload characteristics, memory requirements, and parallelization strategies. Traditional systems typically adopt either a "collocated" execution mode (components sequentially share accelerators) or a "disaggregated pipelining" mode (components run concurrently on separate accelerators). Neither of these extremes is universally optimal. Collocated execution suffers from the "long-tail problem" (e.g., varying response lengths in LLM generation leading to idle accelerators), while disaggregated pipelining can introduce memory and computation imbalances. The challenge lies in supporting flexible, hybrid scheduling without requiring extensive manual tuning or significant changes to the programmed workflow.

RLinf positions itself as a critical advancement in this context by addressing the fundamental inflexibility of current RL systems. It aims to bridge the gap between intuitive, high-level logical workflow programming and highly efficient, fine-grained physical execution. By doing so, RLinf seeks to unlock the full potential of large-scale RL for LLM-based agents, embodied AI, and general intelligence, providing the necessary system infrastructure to handle the growing complexity and computational demands of these frontier AI applications. It contrasts with existing RL frameworks (e.g., DeepSpeed Chat, veRL, NeMo-Aligner, OpenRLHF, AReal) by offering a more flexible component-to-device placement and dynamic execution strategy, moving beyond fixed task-colocated or task-separated designs.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind RLinf stems from the observation that while Reinforcement Learning is becoming increasingly vital for advancing LLMs towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), current RL training systems are inefficient. The paper identifies several key challenges:

- **Heterogeneity and Dynamicity of RL Workflows:** Modern RL for LLMs involves a complex interplay of diverse components (LLM generation, inference, training, reward models, simulators), each with unique resource demands (GPU memory, CPU cores, accelerator types) and parallelization strategies. This heterogeneity makes it difficult for a single, rigid execution mode to be efficient.
- **Low Hardware Utilization:** The dynamic nature of RL tasks, particularly in the rollout phase (e.g., varying response lengths in LLM generation, different step counts in embodied tasks), leads to the "long-tail problem." This results in significant GPU idle time as faster tasks wait for slower ones, wasting computational resources.
- **Suboptimal Training Speed:** The inefficiencies arising from heterogeneity, dynamicity, and poor hardware utilization translate directly into slower end-to-end training times, hindering rapid experimentation and development of large-scale RL models.
- **Lack of System Flexibility:** Existing systems are largely confined to simplistic "collocated" or "disaggregated pipelining" modes. They struggle to support hybrid scheduling strategies that combine the benefits of both, which is often necessary for optimal performance across diverse RL workloads. Furthermore, achieving such flexibility typically requires significant manual tuning or changes to the underlying program structure, increasing programming complexity.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of RLinf are:

1. **Maximize System Flexibility:** To enable diverse execution modes, including temporal multiplexing, spatial pipelining, and hybrid scheduling, to dynamically adapt to varying workload characteristics and hardware configurations.
2. **Enhance Training Efficiency:** To achieve higher hardware utilization and faster end-to-end training throughput for large-scale RL workloads, particularly those involving LLMs and embodied agents.
3. **Improve Programmability:** To allow developers to program complex RL workflows intuitively at a high-level (macro logical flow) without needing to manage the intricate details of low-level execution planning and resource orchestration (micro execution flow). This reduces programming complexity and facilitates rapid iteration.

In essence, RLinf aims to be a high-performance RL training system that overcomes the limitations of existing approaches by prioritizing flexibility, thereby enabling efficient and scalable development of next-generation AI agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RLinf addresses the challenges of efficient and flexible RL training through a novel design paradigm called **Macro-to-Micro Flow Transformation (M2Flow)**. M2Flow fundamentally decouples the high-level, logical programming of RL workflows from their fine-grained physical execution planning. This decoupling allows developers to define intuitive workflows while the system automatically transforms them into optimized execution plans across spatial and temporal dimensions.

The M2Flow paradigm is realized through a cohesive set of mechanisms:

- **Worker Abstraction and Adaptive Communication:**
 - **Worker Abstraction:** Each RL component (e.g., simulator, LLM generation, actor training) is encapsulated as a `Worker`. This abstraction allows for flexible placement of components across available accelerators. Workers are equipped with `onload` and `offload` functions to manage device resources, enabling temporal multiplexing.
 - **Adaptive Communication:** A custom communication layer ensures flexible and efficient data exchange between any two workers, irrespective of their placement (same device, different devices on the same node, or across nodes). It features:
 - **Transparent Connection Lifecycle Management:** Automates connection establishment and teardown, adapting to dynamic worker placement.
 - **Placement-Aware Primitives:** Automatically selects the most efficient backend (NCCL for GPU-GPU, `cudaIPC` for intra-GPU, Gloo for CPU) based on data and worker placement.
 - **Dynamic Data Handling:** Supports arbitrary Python objects as payloads, extracting data buffers for direct, zero-serialization/deserialization communication.
 - **Load-Balancing Data Channel:** A high-level FIFO queue that decouples producer-consumer control and data flows, enabling flexible pipelining. It can offload GPU data to CPU and supports custom load-balancing policies for multiple consumers.

- **Elastic Pipelining and Automatic Context Switching:** These mechanisms enable the dynamic transformation of the logical workflow into spatial and temporal execution plans.
 - **Elastic Pipelining (Spatial Scheduling):** Leverages the SPMD (Single Program, Multiple Data) nature of most RL workers, allowing them to process data at varying batch sizes. The Execution Flow Manager dynamically adjusts data granularity (chunking input data) to allow downstream workers to start earlier with smaller batches or later with larger batches, achieving flexible pipeline granularity tuning.
 - **Automatic Context Switching (Temporal Scheduling):** Enables workers that cannot co-reside on the same devices (due to memory constraints) to share them sequentially. This is managed via a distributed `device_lock` implemented using the data channel. A worker acquires the lock, `onload`s its resources, performs its task, and then `offload`s resources before releasing the lock, freeing up devices for subsequent workers. The lock mechanism uses data dependency information to prioritize access and avoid deadlocks.

- **Profiling-Guided Scheduling Policy:** To navigate the large scheduling space offered by M2Flow, RLinf employs a two-stage approach:
 - **Profiler:** Measures and estimates the execution time and memory usage of each component under different data parallel sizes. It uses polynomial extrapolation to predict performance for unprofiled scales and captures the overall RL workflow as a graph by tracing data flow.
 - **Scheduler (Algorithm 1):** Utilizes the profiling data to search for the optimal execution plan. It recursively partitions the workflow graph into subgraphs, evaluating the time cost of both temporal (shared devices, sequential execution) and spatial (disjoint devices, pipelined execution) scheduling policies. For temporal scheduling, the cost is the sum of execution times (plus offload/reload overhead). For spatial scheduling, it models pipelining time as `T_critical + (M/m - 1) * T_bottleneck`, where `T_critical` is warm-up/cool-down, `T_bottleneck` is the slowest stage, `M` is total batch size, and `m` is data processing granularity. Cycles in the workflow graph are collapsed into single nodes to manage search complexity.

- **Implementation Details:** RLinf is implemented in Python, leveraging Ray for cluster management, remote worker launch, and function dispatch. Unlike Ray's rigid resource allocation, RLinf implements its own flexible device allocation, allowing workers to be assigned to any device across the cluster. It also supports abstraction of non-accelerator hardware like robot arms. Robust performance profiling tools are built-in to gather data for the scheduler and aid developers in bottleneck identification.

This comprehensive methodology allows RLinf to dynamically adapt execution strategies, from purely temporal to purely spatial or hybrid modes, without requiring developers to rewrite their logical workflows, thus delivering both high flexibility, efficiency, and programmability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RLinf across various large-scale RL workloads consistently demonstrates its superior performance and flexibility compared to state-of-the-art systems.

#### 4.1. End-to-End Experiments (Reasoning RL and Embodied RL)

The experiments were conducted on a 32-node cluster with 8 NVIDIA H100-80GB GPUs per node, showcasing the system's capability on large-scale distributed setups.

- **Reasoning RL Training (Qwen2.5 & Qwen3-MoE models):**
 - **Qwen2.5 with GRPO:** RLinf-Temporal consistently outperformed veRL (a state-of-the-art system) by **1.10x to 1.58x speedup** across 1.5B, 7B, and 32B models on 64 to 256 GPUs. The gains are attributed to better GPU memory management (larger KV-cache for rollouts) and reduced synchronization overhead. veRL showed poor scaling due to inference becoming a bottleneck and inefficient rollout. RLinf-Spatial underperformed here due to long sequence lengths and smaller batch sizes, making pipelining less effective.
 - **Qwen2.5 with PPO:** RLinf-Spatial demonstrated significant advantages, outperforming veRL by **38.7% to 69.6%** and RLinf-Temporal by **18.6% to 44.8%** across 1.5B, 7B, and 14B models on 16 to 256 GPUs. This highlights that for PPO, where rollout, inference, and training can overlap effectively, spatial pipelining is more beneficial. RLinf-Temporal still exceeded veRL due to better handling of "Others" overhead (context switching, parameter resharding).
 - **Qwen3-30B-A3B (MoE) with GRPO:** RLinf was compared against Slime (optimized for MoE). Slime (spatial mode without pipelining) was the slowest. RLinf-Spatial (with 1:1 rollout-to-training GPU ratio) was **7.2% to 31.2% faster than Slime-Colocate** (temporal mode) on 32 and 64 GPUs. This was due to spatial mode allowing larger parallelism for rollout by avoiding memory contention with training, and effective rollout-training overlap. On 128 GPUs, RLinf-Temporal surpassed Slime-Colocate by 3.7% due to its decoupled design, while RLinf-Spatial underperformed, indicating the complexity of optimal scheduling based on workload characteristics and scale.

- **Embodied RL Training (OpenVLA & OpenVLA-OFT models):**
 - **ManiSkill Environment:** On the "PutCarrotOnPlateInScene-v2" task, RLinf-Hybrid achieved **52.2–69.1% higher throughput than RLinf-Temporal and 60.7–87.2% higher than RLinf-Spatial** on 8, 16, and 32 GPUs. This indicates that for environments where simulators require substantial GPU memory and benefit from parallel execution, a hybrid approach (temporal sharing of GPUs between rollout and training, but with rollout potentially using dedicated GPUs) is most effective.
 - **LIBERO Environment:** RLinf-Temporal was **37.8% to 143.4% faster than SimpleVLA-RL** (built on veRL) on 8, 16, and 32 GPUs. This improvement stemmed from eliminating redundant environment initialization and more efficient action/log probability computation. Unlike ManiSkill, RLinf-Hybrid performed worse here, suggesting that for CPU-intensive environments like LIBERO, allocating environments to a subset of GPUs can limit CPU utilization, making a purely temporal approach more effective.

#### 4.2. Effectiveness of Search Policy

- **Estimation Accuracy:** The profiling-guided scheduling policy demonstrated high accuracy. For Qwen2.5-GRPO settings, temporal mode achieved estimation errors below 2%, while spatial mode showed slightly higher errors (<5%), primarily due to response-length variability causing pipeline imbalance. Crucially, these errors were small enough to ensure the scheduler consistently identified the most efficient execution mode.
- **Search Speed:** For graphs with up to ten nodes (typical for RL workflows) and GPU counts ranging from 8 to 1024, the dynamic-programming-based search algorithm completed within 7 x 10^-4 to 5.98 seconds. This demonstrates the efficiency of the search policy, despite the exponential growth with GPU count.

#### 4.3. Model Performance

Beyond speed, RLinf ensures the quality of trained models:

- **Reasoning RL:** Qwen2.5 models fine-tuned with RLinf using GRPO achieved the best average performance on math reasoning benchmarks (AIME 24, AIME 25, GPQA-diamond) compared to other open-source models, improving by up to 20 points over base models.
- **Embodied RL:** OpenVLA models trained with RLinf for ManiSkill tasks achieved higher success rates (81.93%) compared to the RL4VLA baseline (79.15%). For LIBERO, RLinf significantly improved the success rate of the OpenVLA-OFT model from 34.33% (one-trajectory fine-tuned) to 97.83% across various task categories.

In summary, RLinf consistently delivers significant speedups (1.07x-2.43x) over baselines in both reasoning and embodied RL tasks, demonstrating its ability to adapt to diverse workloads. Its profiling-guided scheduler efficiently identifies optimal execution modes, and the models trained with RLinf achieve state-of-the-art or improved benchmark scores, validating the effectiveness of the system for real-world AI development.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RLinf presents a significant advancement in the field of large-scale Reinforcement Learning by addressing the critical challenges of inefficiency, inflexibility, and complexity in modern RL training workflows. Its contributions have several far-reaching implications:

- **Paradigm Shift in RL System Design:** The introduction of the Macro-to-Micro Flow Transformation (M2Flow) paradigm is a novel conceptual leap. By decoupling logical workflow programming from physical execution planning, RLinf offers an unprecedented level of flexibility. This approach allows developers to maintain intuitive, high-level code while the system intelligently optimizes low-level resource utilization, eliminating the need for tedious manual tuning or structural code changes when adapting to different hardware or workload characteristics. This could become a standard design pattern for future AI systems.
- **Enabling Next-Generation AI:** As RL is poised to become the dominant computational workload for advancing LLMs, agentic intelligence, and embodied AI, RLinf provides the essential system infrastructure to handle their inherent heterogeneity and dynamicity. Its ability to accelerate training throughput by 1.07x to 2.43x directly translates into faster iteration cycles for AI researchers and practitioners, accelerating the development of more capable and intelligent agents. This is particularly crucial for complex domains like embodied robotics and multi-modal reasoning.
- **Optimal Resource Utilization:** The adaptive communication, elastic pipelining, context switching, and profiling-guided scheduling mechanisms work in concert to maximize hardware utilization, especially for GPUs. By dynamically selecting between temporal, spatial, or hybrid execution modes, RLinf ensures that expensive computational resources are not left idle due to long-tail effects or component imbalances. This is economically beneficial and environmentally responsible given the immense energy consumption of large-scale AI training.
- **Enhanced Programmability and Accessibility:** By abstracting away the complexities of distributed execution and resource orchestration, RLinf makes large-scale RL more accessible. Developers can focus on the core RL logic and algorithms rather than wrestling with system-level optimizations, reducing the barrier to entry for researchers and enabling a wider range of innovations. The open-sourcing of RLinf further amplifies this impact, providing a robust, high-performance platform for the community.
- **Blueprint for Future AI Runtimes:** The paper boldly suggests that M2Flow is a "blueprint for future AI runtimes." This implies a broader vision where similar decoupling and dynamic orchestration principles could be applied to a wider array of heterogeneous AI components—inference, simulation, reasoning, data processing—within a unified execution framework. This vision positions RLinf as an early step towards a more intelligent "operating system for AI workloads," capable of managing the diverse and dynamic demands of increasingly complex AI applications.

In conclusion, RLinf addresses a critical bottleneck in the evolution of AI. By providing a flexible, efficient, and programmable system for large-scale reinforcement learning, it not only accelerates current research but also lays foundational groundwork for how future AI systems might be designed and operated, pushing the boundaries of artificial general intelligence.
