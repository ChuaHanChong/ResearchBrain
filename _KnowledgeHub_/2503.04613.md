# Whole-Body Model-Predictive Control of Legged Robots with MuJoCo

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from MIT, Google DeepMind, and Carnegie Mellon University developed a real-time whole-body Model-Predictive Control (MPC) framework for quadrupedal and humanoid robots, utilizing an iterative Linear-Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) with MuJoCo for dynamics and finite-difference derivatives. This framework enabled dynamic locomotion, including open-loop unstable tasks like bipedal walking, while incorporating whole-body dynamics and collision detection on physical hardware.

## Problem

- Model-based control research frequently relies on custom, difficult-to-reproduce implementations of robot models and optimization solvers, hindering broader adoption.
- Existing open-source whole-body MPC tools often struggle to simultaneously integrate whole-body dynamics and collision detection in real-time or require highly specialized configurations.
- Achieving agile, human-level locomotion in high-dimensional legged robots presents challenges due to complex contact interactions and demanding online computation for MPC.

## Method

- Employed the iterative ==Linear-Quadratic Regulator== (iLQR) algorithm, a standard gradient-based method, for nonlinear trajectory optimization.
- Leveraged the widely adopted MuJoCo physics engine for robot dynamics modeling and efficient finite-difference methods for approximating all necessary derivatives.
- Implemented an asynchronous control loop with iLQR policy updates at approximately 50 Hz and a Time-Varying LQR (TV-LQR) feedback policy at approximately 300 Hz for real-time stabilization on hardware.

## Results

- Successfully deployed real-time whole-body MPC for dynamic quadrupedal locomotion on Unitree Go1/Go2 robots and bipedal locomotion for a full-sized Unitree H1 humanoid robot.
- Demonstrated control of open-loop unstable tasks, including a quadruped robot walking on its hind legs and transitioning into a handstand pose.
- The TV-LQR feedback policy improved humanoid trotting performance by **30.1%** over open-loop control, and a custom GUI facilitated real-time parameter tuning and interactive observation of hardware behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A straightforward approach combining iLQR, MuJoCo dynamics, and finite-difference derivatives is capable of real-time whole-body MPC for complex legged robot tasks on physical hardware.
> - Adjusting MuJoCo's `impratio` contact parameter is crucial for preventing nonphysical foot slipping and achieving stable, physically plausible real-world robot behaviors.
> - Applying Time-Varying LQR feedback gains to the nominal control sequence significantly improves task performance and stability, preventing policy failures in challenging, unstable locomotion scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of legged robotics aims to develop robots capable of exhibiting human- or animal-level agility, a persistent challenge due to their high dimensionality and the complexity of managing contact interactions. Over the past decade, advancements in both model-based control and reinforcement learning (RL) have significantly expanded the capabilities of legged robots in varied environments. Concurrently, robotics simulation environments have progressed in terms of physical accuracy, differentiability, and parallelization performance. These simulation enhancements, coupled with tools from the broader machine learning community like PyTorch and JAX, have driven the accelerated progress of sim-to-real RL, making it a standard approach for problems such as humanoid whole-body control.

In contrast, model-based control research has frequently relied on novel, custom implementations of robot models and optimization solvers. This trend is partly due to the demanding online computation requirements of the Model-Predictive Control (MPC) paradigm. These custom approaches can make research difficult to reproduce and contribute to slower community adoption.

Existing open-source efforts in model-based control, such as MuJoCo MPC, Pinocchio, OCS2, Crocoddyle, Aligator, and Drake, offer various functionalities. For example, Drake provides advanced modeling and simulation for contact dynamics but is generally too slow for real-time control. Pinocchio, OCS2, Crocoddyle, and Aligator offer efficient dynamics computations and trajectory optimization but often require custom robot models and analytical derivatives for discontinuous contact, posing a steep learning curve. Many of these tools also lack integrated whole-body collision detection or the flexibility to handle contact modes without explicit pre-specification, or do not offer real-time interactive user interfaces for hardware deployment. Previous works on MuJoCo MPC have demonstrated sampling-based MPC for stable locomotion tasks but struggled with open-loop unstable dynamics.

This paper addresses a gap in the existing tooling by demonstrating a real-time, whole-body MPC approach for legged robots that leverages a widely adopted, off-the-shelf physics engine, MuJoCo. It aims to provide an accessible, open-source baseline that incorporates whole-body dynamics and collision detection using a standard gradient-based algorithm (iLQR) and finite-difference derivatives, thereby simplifying the development and deployment of advanced legged robot controllers.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to demonstrate the practical effectiveness of a relatively straightforward approach to whole-body Model-Predictive Control (MPC) for both quadrupedal and humanoid robots in real-world scenarios. This approach specifically employs the iterative Linear-Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) algorithm, utilizing MuJoCo for dynamics simulation and finite-difference methods for derivative approximation.

The motivation behind this work stems from several challenges and opportunities within the robotics community:

- **Bridging the Reproducibility Gap:** Model-based control research often involves custom implementations of robot models and optimization solvers. These bespoke solutions can be complex, difficult to reproduce, and slow to be adopted by the broader community. The authors aim to reduce this gap by providing an open-sourced baseline MPC algorithm that is built upon a widely recognized and user-friendly open-source robotics simulator, MuJoCo.
- **Demonstrating the Efficacy of Standard Tools:** The paper seeks to show that a standard gradient-based MPC algorithm, specifically iLQR, when integrated with an efficient physics engine like MuJoCo, is capable of solving challenging real-world tasks. These tasks include dynamic quadruped locomotion, a quadruped walking on two legs, and full-sized humanoid bipedal locomotion, all in real time. This challenges the notion that highly specialized or custom solvers are always necessary for real-time whole-body control.
- **Leveraging Whole-Body Dynamics and Collision Detection:** Previous open-source whole-body MPC algorithms have faced difficulties in simultaneously incorporating whole-body dynamics and collision detection in real-time. By leveraging MuJoCo's efficient C implementation for its backend forward model and derivative computations, this research aims to integrate both aspects into the real-time MPC framework.
- **Lowering the Barrier to Entry:** A significant motivation is to make advanced model-based control more accessible to researchers. By using a popular, off-the-shelf simulator and open-sourcing the implementation, the authors intend to lower the barrier to entry for model-based control research on legged robot hardware, potentially accelerating research momentum in this domain.
- **Facilitating Interactive Development and Tuning:** The development of robust robot behaviors often requires extensive parameter tuning. The creation of an interactive Graphical User Interface (GUI) system is motivated by the need to enable users to modify key MPC parameters and observe corresponding real-world robot behaviors in real-time, thereby making task-specific parameter tuning more intuitive and efficient.

In summary, the core objective is to validate a simple, reproducible, and effective whole-body MPC approach for complex legged robot tasks, utilizing existing, accessible tools to foster broader community engagement and accelerate research.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology centers on deploying a whole-body Model-Predictive Control (MPC) system using the iterative Linear-Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) algorithm, with robot dynamics modeled in the MuJoCo physics engine.

#### 3.1. Core Algorithm: Iterative Linear-Quadratic Regulator (iLQR)

The iLQR algorithm is used for nonlinear trajectory optimization, a variant of Differential Dynamic Programming (DDP). It aims to minimize a sum of running and terminal costs over a control sequence, subject to discrete-time nonlinear dynamics. In each iteration, the problem is approximated locally with quadratic cost and linear constraints around the current solution. The iLQR algorithm, often a Gauss-Newton approximation of DDP, is chosen for its computational efficiency, ability to warm-start, and suitability for unstable systems due to its single-shooting formulation that maintains dynamically feasible state trajectories. It generates not only a nominal control sequence but also a time-varying linear feedback policy.

#### 3.2. MuJoCo Physics Engine and Derivative Computation

- **Soft Contact Model:** MuJoCo's soft contact model is employed, which provides a convex approximation of non-convex contact and friction. This formulation offers speed, efficiency, and guaranteed solutions, despite potentially physically unrealistic interpenetration. Crucially, the soft contact model theoretically offers smooth derivatives through contact.
- **Derivative Approximation:** Since analytical derivatives for MuJoCo's contact model are not explicitly provided, finite-difference approximation is used. The forward difference method, requiring one additional simulation evaluation per dimension, approximates dynamics Jacobians ($\partial f / \partial x, \partial f / \partial u$) and residual Jacobians ($\partial r / \partial x, \partial r / \partial u$). Derivatives of norm functions within the cost are computed analytically. Cost Hessians are approximated using a Gauss-Newton approach. The MuJoCo finite difference utilities function is leveraged for efficient computation of all necessary Jacobians in a single call.

#### 3.3. Cost Function Formulation

The cost function is defined as a sum of weighted residual terms. These residuals encapsulate desired robot behaviors (e.g., maintaining an upright torso, tracking a target position, enforcing gait patterns, balancing, penalizing control effort, maintaining posture, controlling heading, and rotational dynamics). While cost categories are designed offline, their relative weights and specific goal parameters can be interactively adjusted by the user through a GUI.

#### 3.4. Real-time Implementation and Control Loop

The system operates with an asynchronous control loop:
- **iLQR Policy Update:** The iLQR algorithm performs one update per iteration, without waiting for full convergence, and warm-starts using the latest state estimation. This update occurs at approximately 50 Hz.
- **Time-Varying LQR (TV-LQR) Feedback:** The iLQR solution provides a nominal control trajectory and a time-varying linear feedback policy. This TV-LQR policy is used to stabilize the robot between iLQR solves, updating at a higher frequency of approximately 300 Hz. This policy's output is then sent to a joint-level PD controller on the robot.
- **State and Action Representation:** Robot state includes floating base position, attitude quaternion, joint angles, and corresponding velocities. Control inputs to the iLQR are joint angle references, which are then tracked by the robot’s low-level joint-space PD controllers. This enables direct whole-body control.
- **State Estimation:** Robot state is estimated by fusing data from an Optitrack Motion Capture (MoCap) system (100 Hz for position and attitude) and onboard joint encoders (500 Hz for position and velocity). Floating base linear and angular velocities are derived by applying a low-pass filter to finite-difference calculations of position and attitude measurements.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details and Considerations

- **Contact Modeling Adjustment:** To mitigate nonphysical foot slipping and jerky control trajectories caused by MuJoCo's default contact solver settings, the `impratio` parameter was increased from 1 to 100. This modification enhances the solver's ability to enforce friction constraints at the cost of increased computation time per iLQR iteration (e.g., from ~10 ms to ~20 ms on a 12th-gen Intel i7 CPU).
- **Dynamics Derivative Approximation Heuristic:** An optional heuristic, `skip_deriv`, allows skipping dynamics derivative evaluations at some knot points along the planning horizon and interpolating from nearby evaluations. This can reduce computation time, especially for more articulated systems.
- **Interactive GUI:** A custom GUI allows real-time interaction with the robot. Users can dynamically change target positions (represented by a green sphere), update planner parameters (e.g., residual weights, iLQR hyperparameters), and visualize both simulated and real-world robot behaviors simultaneously.

#### 3.6. Hardware Platforms

Experiments were conducted on Unitree Go1 and Go2 quadruped robots (12 DoF) and a Unitree H1 full-sized humanoid robot (10 DoF relevant for locomotion, with upper body DoFs disabled for humanoid tasks due to unreliable encoders). Motion capture provided external localization for robust state estimation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates the effectiveness of a model-predictive control approach utilizing iLQR with MuJoCo dynamics and finite-difference derivatives on real-world legged robots.

#### 4.1. Real-World Effectiveness of a Simple Approach

The combination of iLQR with MuJoCo's physics engine and finite-difference approximated derivatives was observed to be capable for whole-body MPC of quadruped and humanoid robots. This approach achieved real-time control while reasoning about whole-body dynamics and collision detection.

#### 4.2. Real-time Performance and Sim-to-Real Transfer

The system achieved real-time MPC, with iLQR policies updated at approximately 50 Hz and the Time-Varying LQR (TV-LQR) feedback policy stabilizing the robots at approximately 300 Hz. The policies demonstrated generalization to real-world hardware with minimal sim-to-real considerations. Notably, the moderate ground penetration inherent in MuJoCo's soft contact model did not significantly impede sim-to-real transfer.

#### 4.3. Impact of Contact Modeling Parameters

Adjusting MuJoCo's `impratio` parameter from its default value of 1 to 100 was crucial for preventing nonphysical foot slipping and reducing jerky control trajectories observed in simulated quadruped standing tasks. This adjustment, while increasing the computational time for a single iLQR iteration (e.g., from ~10 ms to ~20 ms for a quadruped), was found to be acceptable for real-world deployment, enabling more stable and executable control.

#### 4.4. Role of Time-Varying Linear Feedback

Comparison on an H1 humanoid robot trotting task indicated that applying the TV-LQR feedback gains to the nominal control sequence improved task performance by approximately 30.1% over an 8-second window compared to executing the nominal open-loop control alone. This feedback policy contributed to stabilizing the robot and preventing policy failures in challenging scenarios.

#### 4.5. Quadruped Locomotion

The system successfully enabled basic quadrupedal locomotion on Unitree Go1 and Go2 robots. The robots were able to follow a reference gait and track interactively defined target locations through the GUI. The planner model retained whole-body collision geometry, though during typical locomotion, only the four spherical foot contact points were predominantly active.

#### 4.6. Bipedal Locomotion for Quadruped Robots

The iLQR policies demonstrated capability in open-loop unstable tasks, specifically a quadruped robot walking on its hind legs and transitioning into a handstand pose from an initial quadruped configuration (as depicted in Figure 1). This contrasts with previous sampling-based MPC methods that were reported to struggle with such unstable dynamics.

#### 4.7. Humanoid Locomotion

The iLQR policy was deployed on a Unitree H1 full-sized humanoid robot to track a periodic trotting gait and follow target positions (Figure 6). For this more complex system, certain simplifications were made: collision checking was disabled for the main robot body (retaining only foot contact points), and degrees of freedom (DoFs) in the upper body were disabled due to reliance on unreliable relative encoders. These adjustments facilitated real-time control, although the authors noted they might not be strictly necessary for task success.

#### 4.8. Interactive GUI for Real-World Control

The developed GUI system enabled users to interactively modify key MPC parameters, such as target height, goal positions, and residual weights, in real-time. This functionality facilitated rapid parameter tuning and observation of robot behaviors on hardware alongside a simulated twin.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions and has the potential for considerable impact within the robotics community:

#### 5.1. Lowered Barrier to Entry for Model-Based Control

By demonstrating the effectiveness of a "simple yet surprisingly capable" approach using the widely adopted MuJoCo physics engine and open-sourcing the code, this work significantly lowers the barrier to entry for model-based control research on legged robot hardware. It provides an accessible baseline that researchers can leverage without needing to develop custom robot models or optimization solvers from scratch. This can democratize access to advanced control techniques.

#### 5.2. Validation of Standard Tools for Complex Tasks

The paper challenges the prevailing trend in model-based control research of relying on custom, often complex, implementations. It demonstrates that a standard gradient-based algorithm like iLQR, combined with an off-the-shelf simulator and finite-difference derivatives, can effectively solve complex, real-time, whole-body control problems for both quadrupedal and humanoid robots, including inherently unstable tasks like bipedal locomotion. This validation may encourage a shift towards leveraging existing, well-maintained tooling.

#### 5.3. Enabling Real-time Whole-Body Dynamics and Collision Detection

The successful integration of whole-body dynamics and collision detection into a real-time MPC framework using MuJoCo's efficient implementation is a notable achievement. This capability, previously difficult for open-source MPC algorithms, allows for more realistic and robust planning, particularly in contact-rich environments.

#### 5.4. Enhanced Research Agility through Interactive Tools

The development of an open-source, interactive GUI system for real-time control of legged robots on hardware is a practical contribution. This GUI facilitates rapid prototyping, parameter tuning, and iterative refinement of robot behaviors, accelerating the development cycle for researchers.

#### 5.5. Bridging the Gap between Theory and Practice

The work helps bridge the gap between theoretical advancements in model-based control and their practical, reproducible deployment on physical hardware. By offering a concrete, validated, and open-source implementation, it facilitates the transfer of knowledge and techniques, potentially fostering more collaborative and reproducible research in legged robotics.

#### 5.6. Identification of Future Research Directions

The paper also constructively highlights current limitations and outlines clear directions for future work. These include developing more robust state estimation techniques that do not rely on external motion capture, improving system identification methods to reduce sim-to-real gaps, exploring MPC algorithms better suited for contact mode exploration and parallel computation, and extending the framework to multiple-shooting and collocation methods for enhanced numerical stability and convergence over longer horizons. This provides a roadmap for subsequent advancements in the field.
