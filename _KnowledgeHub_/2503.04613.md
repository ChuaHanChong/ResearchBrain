# Whole-Body Model-Predictive Control of Legged Robots with MuJoCo

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from MIT, Google DeepMind, and Carnegie Mellon University developed a real-time whole-body Model-Predictive Control (MPC) framework for quadrupedal and humanoid robots, utilizing an iterative Linear-Quadratic Regulator (iLQR) with MuJoCo for dynamics and finite-difference derivatives. This framework enabled dynamic locomotion, including open-loop unstable tasks like bipedal walking, while incorporating whole-body dynamics and collision detection on physical hardware.

## Problem

- Model-based control research frequently relies on custom, difficult-to-reproduce implementations of robot models and optimization solvers, hindering broader adoption.
- Existing open-source whole-body MPC tools often struggle to simultaneously integrate whole-body dynamics and collision detection in real-time or require highly specialized configurations.
- Achieving agile, human-level locomotion in high-dimensional legged robots presents challenges due to complex contact interactions and demanding online computation for MPC.

## Method

- Employed the iterative ==Linear-Quadratic Regulator== (iLQR) algorithm, a standard gradient-based method, for nonlinear trajectory optimization.
- Leveraged the widely adopted MuJoCo physics engine for robot dynamics modeling and efficient finite-difference methods for approximating all necessary derivatives.
- Implemented an asynchronous control loop with iLQR policy updates at approximately 50 Hz and a Time-Varying LQR (TV-LQR) feedback policy at approximately 300 Hz for real-time stabilization on hardware.

## Results

- Successfully deployed real-time whole-body MPC for dynamic quadrupedal locomotion on Unitree Go1/Go2 robots and bipedal locomotion for a full-sized Unitree H1 humanoid robot.
- Demonstrated control of open-loop unstable tasks, including a quadruped robot walking on its hind legs and transitioning into a handstand pose.
- The TV-LQR feedback policy improved humanoid trotting performance by **30.1%** over open-loop control, and a custom GUI facilitated real-time parameter tuning and interactive observation of hardware behavior.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A straightforward approach combining iLQR, MuJoCo dynamics, and finite-difference derivatives is capable of real-time whole-body MPC for complex legged robot tasks on physical hardware.
> - Adjusting MuJoCo's `impratio` contact parameter is crucial for preventing nonphysical foot slipping and achieving stable, physically plausible real-world robot behaviors.
> - Applying Time-Varying LQR feedback gains to the nominal control sequence significantly improves task performance and stability, preventing policy failures in challenging, unstable locomotion scenarios.
