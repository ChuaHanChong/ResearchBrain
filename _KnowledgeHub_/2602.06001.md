# Visuo-Tactile World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Washington and FAIR, Meta, developed Visuo-Tactile World Models (VT-WMs) that integrate vision and touch to enhance robot manipulation by better understanding contact physics. This approach improves object permanence and causal compliance during imagined scenarios, resulting in up to 35% higher real-robot planning success rates on contact-rich tasks compared to vision-only models.

## Problem

- Vision-only world models (V-WMs) frequently fail in contact-rich robot manipulation tasks due to visual occlusion and the inherent ambiguity of inferring contact forces.
- These V-WMs often hallucinate object disappearances, teleportation, or unphysical movements, hindering robust robot planning and interaction.
- A lack of direct, local contact information limits V-WMs' ability to ground their predictions in accurate physical interactions.

## Method

- A multi-task **Visuo-Tactile World Model (VT-WM)** was developed, combining pre-trained ==vision (Cosmos tokenizer)== and ==tactile (Sparsh-X model for Digit 360 sensors)== encoders.
- A ==12-layer Transformer-based autoregressive predictor== fuses these multimodal latent states with robot actions to predict future visuo-tactile states.
- The VT-WM was trained on a diverse teleoperated dataset of 124 demonstrations across eight contact-rich tasks, utilizing a combined ==teacher forcing and short-horizon autoregressive sampling== objective.

## Results

- VT-WM achieved an average reduction of approximately **33%** in normalized Fréchet distance for objects in motion and **29%** for static objects during autoregressive rollouts, demonstrating improved imagination quality.
- In zero-shot planning experiments on a real robot, VT-WM demonstrated superior performance, achieving up to **35%** higher success rates in contact-rich tasks like "reach & push" and "wipe cloth" compared to V-WMs.
- The model exhibited strong downstream versatility, achieving a **77%** success rate on a novel manipulation task after fine-tuning with only **20** demonstrations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Integrating tactile sensing into world models is crucial for overcoming visual ambiguities, significantly enhancing object permanence under occlusion and ensuring causal compliance in imagined robot interactions.
> - Tactile feedback provides the necessary local contact information to ground the world model's imagination in physical reality, leading to more accurate and plausible predictions of robot-object interactions.
> - A multi-task visuo-tactile world model can generalize learned contact dynamics, enabling robust zero-shot planning for a variety of novel contact-rich manipulation tasks with improved data efficiency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: **robot learning, world models, multimodal perception, and tactile sensing**.

**World Models (WMs)** have emerged as a leading paradigm in machine learning, endowing robots with the ability to build an internal representation of the physical world and plan actions through "imagination." Recent advancements in this area have primarily focused on **vision-only world models (V-WMs)**, which leverage advancements in computer vision and video generation to predict future states based on visual input and robot actions. Leading examples include general-purpose action-conditioned video-generation models and latent-state world models that learn dynamics in a compressed visual space. These models have shown promise in tasks involving spatial reasoning and understanding robot kinematics in free space.

However, a significant limitation of V-WMs, which this paper explicitly addresses, is their **fragility in contact-rich manipulation tasks**. Visual occlusion (e.g., a hand grasping an object) and the inherent ambiguity of inferring contact forces solely from exocentric camera views lead to common failure modes such as objects disappearing, teleporting, or moving in ways that violate basic physics. This problem is particularly acute in real-world robotics, where robust contact interaction is crucial for tasks like grasping, pushing, and stacking.

Concurrently, **multimodal perception** is gaining traction, recognizing that real-world intelligence often requires integrating information from various sensory modalities. While vision provides global context, it lacks the direct, local information critical for physical interaction. **Tactile sensing**, particularly **vision-based tactile sensors** like GelSight, Digit, and Digit 360, has seen considerable development. These sensors provide rich, high-resolution information about contact forces, slip, and material properties, which are difficult to infer from vision alone. The emergence of **foundational models for tactile sensing** (e.g., Sparsh, Sparsh-X) mirrors the success of general-purpose visual encoders (e.g., CLIP, DINO, Cosmos), providing robust, low-dimensional representations of tactile data.

This paper positions itself as the **first multi-task visuo-tactile world model (VT-WM)**, aiming to bridge the gap between advanced vision-only world models and the critical need for direct contact perception in robotics. By integrating these modalities, the authors aim to overcome the inherent limitations of vision-only approaches, making world models more robust and physically grounded for real-world manipulation. The ambition is to create a *general-purpose* multimodal world model, distinguishing it from prior works that investigated tactile dynamics models in a more task-specific manner or with limited integration with vision. This work represents a significant step towards developing truly intelligent robotic systems that can robustly interact with their physical environment by understanding the physics of contact.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a multi-task Visuo-Tactile World Model (VT-WM) that effectively integrates vision and touch to improve a robot's understanding of contact physics and enable more reliable planning for contact-rich manipulation tasks.**

This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Overcoming Limitations of Vision-Only World Models:** Existing vision-only world models, despite their advancements in spatial reasoning and robot kinematics, frequently fail in contact-rich scenarios. They suffer from "hallucinations" such as objects disappearing under occlusion (e.g., when a robot hand grasps something), teleporting without physical cause, or violating basic laws of motion due to ambiguous visual contact states. The authors aim to directly address these common failure modes.
2. **Grounding Imagination in Contact Physics:** Robot manipulation heavily relies on precise physical interaction. Vision provides global context but lacks the direct, local signals about contact forces, slip, and subtle object deformations. The motivation is to use tactile feedback to "ground" the robot's imagination in the fundamental physics of contact, leading to more accurate and physically plausible predictions of object-robot interactions.
3. **Enhancing Object Permanence and Causal Compliance:** A key goal is to enable world models to maintain a consistent representation of objects even when they are occluded (object permanence) and to ensure that predicted changes in object state are physically plausible consequences of robot actions (causal compliance). These are crucial for intelligent manipulation.
4. **Improving Zero-Shot Planning Reliability:** The ultimate motivation is to translate improved imagination quality into superior real-world robot performance. By better understanding contact in imagination, the VT-WM should generate more effective plans that transfer robustly to a physical robot for complex, multi-step contact-rich tasks without requiring task-specific fine-tuning.
5. **Achieving Downstream Versatility and Data Efficiency:** The authors are motivated to demonstrate that a multi-task VT-WM, once trained on a diverse set of tasks, can quickly adapt to novel manipulation tasks with limited new data. This versatility is vital for practical robot deployment, reducing the need for extensive data collection for every new task.

In essence, the paper seeks to demonstrate that by leveraging tactile sensing alongside vision, robots can gain a more profound and physically accurate understanding of their environment, leading to significantly enhanced capabilities in dexterous and contact-intensive manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the methodology is the design, training, and evaluation of the Visuo-Tactile World Model (VT-WM).

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

The VT-WM architecture comprises three main components: a vision encoder, a tactile encoder, and an autoregressive predictor (Figure 3).
- **Vision Encoder:** Utilizes the **Cosmos tokenizer** (Agarwal et al., 2025), a pre-trained general-purpose visual encoder, to extract latent states (s_k) from exocentric video frames. This provides global context about the robot and its environment.
- **Tactile Encoder:** Employs the **Sparsh-X model** (Higuera et al., 2025) for Digit 360 tactile sensors to compress high-frequency contact feedback into compact latent states (t_k). This emphasizes salient physical interactions and local contact information.
- **Autoregressive Predictor:** This is a 12-layer Transformer-based forward dynamics model that takes fused vision and tactile representations, augmented with sinusoidal positional embeddings, along with control actions (a_k) to predict the next-step latent states (s_k+1, t_k+1). The predictor processes multimodal tokens through two types of attention mechanisms:
 - **Spatio-Temporal Self-Attention:** Factorized attention that operates in two stages: spatial attention for interactions within a timestep and temporal attention for evolution across past timesteps. This efficiently captures local dynamics and global context.
 - **Action Conditioning via Cross-Attention:** After each self-attention block, vision-touch tokens cross-attend to action tokens, integrating robot control inputs into the predictions.
 - **Outputs:** Modality-specific output heads project the refined representations back to their original dimensions, yielding predictions for s_k+1 and t_k+1.

#### 3.2. Training Visuo-Tactile World Model

- **Training Data:** A multi-task visuo-tactile dataset was collected using teleoperation on a Franka Panda arm with an Allegro Hand, equipped with Digit 360 sensors on each fingertip (Appendix B). The dataset includes 124 demonstrations (112k datapoints) across eight diverse contact-rich tasks (e.g., pick and place, push, wipe, stack cubes), with both successful and failure sequences. Data streams (proprioception, exocentric video, tactile video) were synchronized and downsampled to 6 FPS.
- **Input Streams:**
 - Vision: 1.5-second videoclip (9 frames at 6 fps, 320 × 192 resolution).
 - Tactile: Two frames per Digit 360 sensor (four sensors total) covering the most recent 0.16 seconds, reflecting its higher frequency and local nature.
 - Actions: Changes in proprioceptive state (translation, quaternion rotation) and a binary hand state, chunked from 30Hz into groups of 5.
- **Training Objective:** A combined objective balancing teacher forcing with autoregressive sampling:
 - **Teacher Forcing Loss:** L1 distance between predicted and ground-truth next-step latents, providing dense supervision and stability.
 - **Sampling Loss:** L1 distance between predicted and ground-truth latents after autoregressively sampling future states for a short horizon (H=3-5 steps, without gradients). This improves long-horizon coherence.
- **Parameters:** The model has 173M parameters, with 96M trained. The Cosmos tokenizer was kept frozen, while the Sparsh-X encoder was fine-tuned to account for sensor-specific variations. Optimized using AdamW with a learning rate scheduler.

#### 3.3. Planning in Imagination

The action-conditioned nature of the VT-WM enables its use as a simulator for planning.
- **Algorithm:** The **Cross-Entropy Method (CEM)** (Rubinstein, 1997) is employed for goal-conditioned energy minimization.
- **Process:** At each step, CEM samples a population of action sequences over a fixed horizon. The VT-WM autoregressively generates future visuo-tactile latents for each sequence. A cost function, typically an L2 distance between the final predicted *visual* latent (s_k+H) and the latent of a goal image (s_goal), scores each trajectory. CEM then refines the action distribution based on top-performing sequences.
- **Role of Tactile:** Critically, the *planning objective remains purely vision-based* (goal image). The tactile modality's role is indirect: it enhances the physical fidelity of the learned world model during training and disambiguates initial contact states during rollouts, leading to more accurate imagination and thus better plan generation. Plans are executed on the real robot in an open-loop manner.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence for the advantages of the Visuo-Tactile World Model (VT-WM) over vision-only counterparts (V-WMs) across imagination quality, zero-shot planning, and downstream versatility.

#### 4.1. Contact Perception (Imagination Quality)

Evaluated via autoregressive rollouts conditioned on real robot actions, comparing V-WM and VT-WM against ground-truth videos.
- **Object Permanence:** VT-WM significantly improved the ability to maintain consistent object representations, even under heavy occlusion.
 - **Quantitative:** VT-WM achieved an **average reduction of ≈33%** in normalized Fréchet distance for objects in motion compared to V-WM (Figure 4). Statistically significant gains were observed in tasks like "place fruits," "push fruits," and "cube stacking." This indicates more physically coherent rollouts where objects do not disappear or teleport.
 - **Qualitative:** In the cube-stacking task (Figure 1), VT-WM consistently represented the cube in-hand during transport and placement, reappearing correctly upon release, unlike V-WM which often showed the cube disappearing.
- **Causal Compliance:** VT-WM demonstrated better adherence to physical laws, preventing hallucinated motion of passive objects.
 - **Quantitative:** VT-WM showed an **average reduction of ≈29%** in normalized Fréchet distance for static objects (Figure 6). Statistically significant improvements were seen in "place fruits," "push fruits," and "wipe with cloth."
 - **Qualitative:** When the robot hand hovered above a cloth without contact (Figure 5), VT-WM correctly predicted the cloth remaining stationary. In contrast, V-WM often hallucinated deformations and displacements of the cloth, violating Newton's first law of motion.
- **Action Controllability:** Qualitatively, VT-WM generated coherent rollouts aligned with commanded Cartesian movements and hand open/close actions, demonstrating that it internalizes robot kinematics (Appendix C, Figure 9). Predicted tactile states also captured plausible contact events congruent with visual predictions (Figures 10, 11, 12).

#### 4.2. Zero-Shot Planning Transfer to Real-World

The study assessed whether improved imagination quality translates to better planning performance on a real robot using CEM.
- **Superior Planning in Contact-Rich Tasks:** VT-WM consistently achieved equal or superior success rates compared to V-WM across all evaluated tasks (Figure 7, left).
 - On simple kinematic tasks like "reach button," both models achieved 100% success.
 - For contact-rich tasks, VT-WM showed substantial gains: **10% higher success on "push fruits," 35% on "reach & push," 31% on "wipe cloth," and 11% on "stack cubes."**
- **Resolution of Visual Aliasing:** Qualitative analysis of planning (Appendix D) revealed that VT-WM resolved cases of visual aliasing. For example, in "reach & push," V-WM often planned to hover slightly above the object, leading to failed pushes. VT-WM, using tactile feedback, explicitly planned for contact, resulting in successful manipulation (Figure 15). Similarly, for "wipe cloth," VT-WM maintained consistent contact in imagination, leading to effective wiping in reality, unlike V-WM which often failed to establish contact (Figure 16). In "stack cubes," VT-WM maintained object permanence in-hand, preventing failures seen in V-WM during placement (Figure 17).

#### 4.3. Downstream Versatility

VT-WM's ability to adapt to a novel task with limited data was tested.
- **Rapid Adaptation to Novel Tasks:** When fine-tuned on only 20 demonstrations for a new task ("place plate in dish rack"), VT-WM achieved a **77% success rate** in zero-shot transfer to the real robot (Figure 7, right).
- **Data Efficiency:** This high success rate with limited new data highlights the model's ability to leverage its pre-existing knowledge of contact dynamics learned from prior tasks, demonstrating significant data efficiency for novel, contact-rich manipulation tasks. Failure modes were minor precision errors rather than a complete misunderstanding of the task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research on Visuo-Tactile World Models (VT-WM) represents a significant advancement in the field of robot learning and manipulation, with profound implications for the development of more intelligent and capable robotic systems.

**1. Foundation for Robust Robotic Manipulation:** The most significant contribution is the demonstration that integrating tactile sensing is **crucial for grounding world models in the physics of contact**. This moves beyond the limitations of vision-only approaches, which are inherently fragile in real-world, contact-rich tasks. By addressing issues like object permanence under occlusion and adherence to physical laws, VT-WM provides a more robust foundation for robots to understand and interact with their environment.

**2. Enhanced Real-World Robot Performance:** The dramatic improvements in zero-shot planning success rates on real robots (up to 35% in complex tasks) are a strong testament to the practical utility of VT-WM. This means robots can execute tasks requiring precise hand-object interactions more reliably, reducing errors and increasing autonomy in unstructured environments. This is particularly impactful for applications such as assembly, logistics, domestic robotics, and delicate handling where contact is inevitable.

**3. General-Purpose Multimodal World Models:** The development of a *multi-task* VT-WM is a key step towards general-purpose robot intelligence. Instead of task-specific models, VT-WM learns a broad understanding of visuo-tactile dynamics that can be applied across a variety of manipulation scenarios. This generalizability is a long-standing goal in AI and robotics.

**4. Data Efficiency for Novel Tasks:** The demonstrated downstream versatility and rapid adaptation to a novel task with limited demonstrations is highly impactful. It suggests that robots trained with VT-WMs can learn new skills much faster, significantly reducing the laborious and costly process of data collection and model training for every new manipulation task. This accelerates the deployment of robotic solutions in diverse settings.

**5. Advancing Multimodal AI:** This work reinforces the importance of multimodal perception in AI, highlighting how complementary sensory information can overcome the inherent ambiguities of single-modality systems. It provides a blueprint for integrating vision and touch effectively within a predictive learning framework.

**6. Future Research Directions:** The paper also opens several promising avenues for future research.
 - **Diverse Tactile Modalities:** While focusing on vision-based tactile sensors, the framework's applicability to other tactile modalities (e.g., force/torque, electronic skin) can be explored.
 - **Generalization to Novel Objects/Scenes:** Further research is needed to evaluate VT-WM's generalization capabilities to entirely new objects, materials, and complex scenes beyond the training distribution.
 - **Computational Efficiency:** Planning with world models via CEM remains computationally expensive, suggesting a need for more efficient planning algorithms or closed-loop control policies that leverage the learned world model.
 - **Long-Horizon Planning:** While improvements were shown, scaling to very long-horizon, sequential manipulation tasks remains a challenge.

In conclusion, "Visuo-Tactile World Models" makes a compelling case for the necessity of touch in robust robot manipulation. By enabling robots to imagine and plan with a grounded understanding of physical contact, this research brings us closer to a future where intelligent machines can confidently and dexterously interact with our complex physical world.
