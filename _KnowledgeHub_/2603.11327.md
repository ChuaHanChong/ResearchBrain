# Meta-Reinforcement Learning with Self-Reflection for Agentic Search

> [!summary] Summary
> MR-Search introduces an in-context meta-reinforcement learning framework that enables LLM-based search agents to improve exploration capabilities during test-time through explicit self-reflection. This approach achieved average relative performance gains of up to 19.3% over sparse-reward baselines on diverse QA benchmarks by providing internal process rewards and consolidating knowledge across search episodes.

## Problem

- Current RL-based agentic search methods rely on sparse rewards, typically provided only at the end of an interaction, making credit assignment for intermediate steps difficult.
- Sparse rewards lead to inefficient exploration, susceptibility to local optima, and suboptimal search dynamics in multi-turn interactions with tools.
- Existing solutions like process reward models or LLM judges require costly external annotations, lack reusability, and can introduce issues like reward hacking or bias.

## Method

- ==MR-Search== formulates agentic search as an iterative, self-reflection driven process within an ==in-context meta-reinforcement learning== framework.
- A 'meta-episode' consists of a sequence of 'inner episodes', where each subsequent inner episode is conditioned on accumulated context from previous episodes and explicit self-reflections.
- A critic-free, multi-turn RL algorithm optimizes the LLM policy using ==grouped relative advantages== at the turn level, providing dense, unbiased feedback without requiring auxiliary value models.

## Results

- MR-Search consistently outperformed sparse outcome-reward baselines, achieving average relative improvements of **9.2%** for Qwen2.5-7B-Base and **19.3%** for Qwen2.5-3B-Base models across eight benchmarks.
- The proposed multi-turn RL algorithm with grouped relative advantages outperformed standard PPO and MT-GRPO when applied with episode turn-level rewards.
- The framework demonstrated effective test-time scaling, with performance significantly increasing when extrapolated to more reflection turns, suggesting enhanced ability to iteratively refine search across multiple turns.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLMs' inherent self-reflection capabilities can be formalized as 'free' internal process rewards within a meta-RL framework, providing denser and more informative learning signals.
> - Conditioning subsequent search attempts on past episodes and explicit reflections transforms search from disconnected efforts into a cohesive, incrementally improving process.
> - Utilizing a discounted cumulative advantage from turn-level relative rewards is crucial for effective long-horizon credit assignment in multi-turn agentic search tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research paper addresses challenges in developing autonomous and multi-step decision-making agents using large language models (LLMs), particularly in the context of agentic search tasks such as deep research and information seeking. These tasks involve LLMs interacting dynamically with search tools over multiple turns.

The broader landscape has seen significant progress in LLM reasoning capabilities. Reinforcement Learning (RL), often employing the ReAct paradigm, has emerged as a framework for training search agents. However, existing RL-based methods primarily rely on sparse rewards provided only at the end of each trajectory, typically focusing on the correctness of the final answer. This sparse reward structure presents several challenges:
- **Ambiguous Credit Assignment:** It is difficult to attribute success or failure to specific intermediate steps.
- **Inefficient Exploration:** Agents may struggle to explore complex processes effectively in early stages.
- **Susceptibility to Local Optima:** The agent might converge on suboptimal search strategies.
- **Amplification of Errors:** In multi-turn tool interactions, small errors can propagate and obscure credit assignment.

To mitigate the sparse reward problem, some prior works have explored using process reward models or LLM judges. However, these alternatives introduce their own limitations:
- **Dependency on External Annotations:** Process reward models often require costly human annotations or external evaluators, which are difficult to reuse across different tasks.
- **Reward Hacking and Bias:** Model-based rewards can lead to agents exploiting flaws in the reward model rather than truly improving task performance.
- **Computational Overhead:** Incorporating additional models for reward generation adds to the computational cost of RL training.

This work introduces MR-Search, a meta-RL approach designed to address these limitations. It positions itself within the field of in-context meta-reinforcement learning, where agents learn to adapt their strategy by conditioning on past experiences. Unlike traditional meta-RL applications in robotics or games, MR-Search specifically targets open-domain agentic search tasks with tool interactions and aims to improve without continuous environmental reward feedback during inference. It also integrates with the growing body of research on LLMs with self-reflection capabilities, moving beyond simple prompting strategies to a fine-tuned, meta-learned reflection process. The work further relates to research on scaling test-time computation for LLMs, specifically within the sequential refinement paradigm, by proposing a training framework that enables more effective in-context exploration across multiple attempts.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop an effective meta-RL approach that enables search agents to improve their in-context exploration during test time, thereby addressing the limitations of sparse outcome rewards in agentic search.

The key motivations driving this work are detailed as follows:

1. **Addressing Sparse Rewards and Credit Assignment:** Existing RL methods for agentic search typically receive only a final outcome reward, making it challenging to assign credit to intermediate actions or thoughts. This sparsity hinders the learning of complex, multi-step search processes and leads to inefficient exploration. MR-Search aims to provide a denser, more informative learning signal without relying on external, costly, or potentially biased process reward models.

2. **Leveraging LLM Exploration Capabilities:** The authors observe that LLM agent limitations often stem from insufficient exploration within a single trajectory rather than a lack of reasoning capacity. Traditional RL methods treat episodes independently, failing to consolidate knowledge across multiple search attempts for a given query. MR-Search is motivated by the idea that by treating a series of search attempts as a meta-episode, the agent can learn to conduct more structured and informed exploration.

3. **Harnessing LLM Self-Reflection:** LLMs have demonstrated capabilities in self-reflection and self-correction, allowing them to refine their outputs iteratively based on intrinsic feedback. The research is motivated by the potential to formalize and optimize this inherent capability within an RL framework. By generating explicit self-reflections after each episode and incorporating them as context for subsequent attempts, the agent can progressively inform its search process, transforming disconnected attempts into a more coherent and adaptive strategy. This also aligns with the in-context learning paradigm of LLMs.

4. **Enabling In-Context Meta-Learning for Agentic Search:** The paper aims to formalize and advocate for in-context meta-reinforcement learning as a practical and scalable approach for agentic search where ground-truth rewards are often absent during inference. This involves training a policy that learns to adapt its search strategy across episodes, essentially learning "how to learn" an effective search strategy through self-reflection. This meta-learning approach is intended to promote better generalization and adaptation to varied and complex search tasks.

5. **Developing Fine-Grained, Critic-Free Policy Optimization:** To support the multi-turn reflective setting, the research is motivated by the need for an efficient and unbiased credit assignment mechanism. Instead of relying on auxiliary value models (critics), which can add complexity and computational overhead, the authors aim to introduce a multi-turn RL algorithm that estimates dense relative advantages at the turn level. This is designed to enable fine-grained credit assignment on each episode while maintaining the benefits of policy optimization without a separate critic.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

MR-Search formulates agentic search as an iterative, self-reflection-driven process within a meta-Reinforcement Learning (meta-RL) framework. This approach contrasts with traditional RL agents that treat each search episode as an independent event.

#### 3.1. MR-Search Framework Design

1. **Episode Definition:** In MR-Search, each complete interaction trajectory, culminating in a final answer, is considered an "episode."
2. **Meta-Episode Structure:** A sequence of N such episodes forms a "meta-episode." The core idea is that the policy conditions on past episodes and adapts its search strategy across these episodes.
3. **Self-Reflection Mechanism:** After each episode, the search agent performs an explicit self-reflection step. This involves applying a specific reflection prompt (detailed in Appendix A.1.3 of the paper) that instructs the model to refine its answer by conditioning on the context of all previous episodes. This process allows for cross-episode knowledge consolidation and progressive improvement.
4. **Contextual Learning:** Each subsequent episode ($a_n$) is generated by the policy conditioned on the accumulated context of all preceding episodes ($a_0, \ldots, a_{n-1}$). This accumulated context is continuously expanded by incorporating the interaction trajectory and the explicit self-reflection from the most recent episode. The context length can grow linearly with the number of reflection steps. The paper discusses context management protocols, such as retaining only the immediately preceding episode or summarizing prior episodes, to mitigate scalability issues, though the default is to use all prior context.
5. **Balancing Exploration and Exploitation:** The agent learns an end-to-end search strategy by updating its policy based on the final task performance across the meta-episode. This design aims to enable a dynamic balance between exploring new information and exploiting accumulated knowledge.

#### 3.2. Policy Optimization with Multi-Turn Advantages

To optimize the policy in this multi-turn, reflective setting, MR-Search employs a multi-turn RL algorithm that estimates turn-level reward signals without requiring a separate value function (critic).

1. **Meta-Level Objective:** The overall objective is to maximize the expected discounted cumulative reward of the meta-episode: $J_{meta}(\pi_{\theta}) = E_{y \sim \pi_{\theta}} \left[ \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} \gamma^n f_{verifier}(o_n, o^*) \right]$, where $o_n$ is the answer from episode $a_n$, $o^*$ is the ground-truth answer, $f_{verifier}$ is a reward function (e.g., exact match), and $\gamma$ is the discount factor (set to 1 by default).

2. **Grouped Relative Advantages (RLOO):** To address sparse rewards and provide fine-grained credit assignment, the method samples a group of G meta-episodes for each question. For each episode $n$ in a meta-episode $i$, a relative reward $\tilde{r}_{i,n}$ is computed using the Leave-One-Out (RLOO) estimation:
 $\tilde{r}_{i,n} = r(s_{i,n}, a_{i,n}) - \frac{1}{G-1} \sum_{j \neq i} r(s_{j,n}, a_{j,n})$
 This $\tilde{r}_{i,n}$ represents the reward of episode $i$ relative to the average reward of other episodes at the same turn within the group. This approach provides an unbiased estimation of the turn-level advantage.

3. **Discounted Cumulative Advantage:** To account for long-horizon dependencies, these turn-level relative rewards are aggregated into a discounted cumulative advantage $A_{i,n}$ by summing future relative rewards:
 $A_{i,n} = \sum_{n'=n}^N \gamma^{n'-n} \tilde{r}_{i,n'}$
 This ensures that rewards from later episodes can influence the credit assignment for earlier actions.

4. **Policy Update:** The policy is optimized using a clipped surrogate off-policy objective, similar to Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). The advantage signal $A_{i,n}$ is broadcast to all tokens generated within that step, enabling token-level credit assignment. Tool output tokens are masked from the loss calculation.

#### 3.3. Discussion and Extensions

- **Exploration & Exploitation Strategy:** An optional mechanism allows for designated "exploration episodes" where rewards are masked (set to zero) during backpropagation. These episodes contribute to the context for adaptation but do not directly drive gradients, encouraging the policy to prioritize long-term contextual learning.
- **Step-Level Meta-RL:** The framework can be extended to finer granularities, treating each tool-interaction step as a "micro-episode" to obtain denser, step-level rewards and promote informative intermediate reasoning.
- **Context Management:** Strategies for managing increasing context length, such as retaining only the immediately preceding episode, are explored to ensure scalability.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Datasets:** Evaluated on both general (NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA) and multi-hop (HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Musique, Bamboogle, ASearcher) question answering benchmarks. Training utilized a merged NQ and HotpotQA dataset.
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-3B-Base and Qwen2.5-7B-Base.
- **Tools:** 2018 Wikipedia dump as the knowledge source, E5 as the retriever (fixed to 3 retrieved documents).
- **Evaluation Metric:** Exact Match (EM) score, normalized for both predicted and ground-truth answers. A single trajectory per question is sampled, and the EM for the last valid prediction is reported.
- **Baselines:** Compared against direct inference, Search-o1, ReSearch, Search-R1 (outcome reward RL), PPRM, and StepResearch (process reward RL using external models).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluations demonstrate the performance of MR-Search across various question answering benchmarks.

1. **Overall Performance Superiority:** MR-Search consistently achieved higher accuracy compared to all baseline methods across both single-hop and multi-hop QA benchmarks.
 - For the Qwen2.5-7B-Base model, MR-Search showed an average relative improvement of 9.2% over Search-R1, a strong baseline relying on outcome rewards.
 - For the smaller Qwen2.5-3B-Base model, MR-Search yielded an even larger average relative improvement of 19.3% over Search-R1. This suggests that the method is particularly effective for smaller models where sparse outcome rewards are more problematic.
 - MR-Search also outperformed methods that utilize external process reward models, such as PPRM and StepResearch, indicating the effectiveness of its internal, free process reward mechanism.

2. **Effectiveness on Complex Tasks:** On the ASearcher dataset, which demands longer-horizon and multi-turn search interactions, MR-Search demonstrated substantial advantages over Search-R1, achieving relative improvements of 10.2% in EM and 9.5% in F1 scores. This highlights its capability in handling more intricate agentic search scenarios.

3. **Ablation Study Insights:**
 - **Optimization Algorithm:** The proposed multi-turn RL algorithm, which estimates grouped relative advantages at the turn level, consistently outperformed PPO and MT-GRPO when applied within the Meta-RL framework with episode turn-level rewards. This supports the efficacy of the designed optimization strategy for dense feedback.
 - **Discount Factor:** Setting the discount factor $\gamma = 0$ (removing future credit assignment) resulted in a substantial degradation of performance. This suggests that incorporating long-horizon dependencies via a non-zero discount factor is important for effectively propagating rewards and learning from sequential reflections.

4. **Training Dynamics:**
 - MR-Search exhibited stable convergence during training and consistently achieved higher training rewards compared to Search-R1. This indicates that the iterative reflection and process rewards contribute to more effective and stable learning.
 - MR-Search demonstrated a higher frequency of search engine calls, suggesting its ability to dynamically adjust its search behavior based on task complexity.

5. **Test-Time Scaling:**
 - When the number of reflection turns was extrapolated beyond the training setting (3 turns), MR-Search showed significant performance gains, exhibiting a steep improvement curve. In contrast, Search-R1 with the same reflection mechanism yielded only marginal gains. This implies that MR-Search meta-learns how to effectively leverage multi-turn reflection for iterative refinement and extrapolation.

6. **Extensions' Performance:**
 - Variants of MR-Search, including encouraging explicit exploration (by masking rewards for initial episodes), applying meta-RL at the step level (treating each tool interaction as a micro-episode), and using a short context (keeping only the immediately preceding episode), all outperformed the Search-R1 baseline.
 - Encouraging exploration was particularly beneficial for the more complex ASearcher dataset, indicating its utility for tasks requiring extensive tool interaction.

7. **Qualitative Case Studies:**
 - Case studies illustrated MR-Search's ability to correct initial incorrect answers by performing additional searches and self-reflection, leading to accurate final answers through iterative revision.
 - The model also demonstrated the ability to preserve an already accurate answer, avoiding unnecessary revisions and indicating selective refinement based on retrieved information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of MR-Search carries several implications for the advancement of agentic search and reinforcement learning for language models.

1. **Addressing the Sparse Reward Problem:** MR-Search offers a method to mitigate the pervasive sparse reward problem in agentic search for LLMs without recourse to external, costly, or potentially biased process reward models. By internally generating "free" process rewards through self-reflection and turn-level advantage estimation, it provides a scalable and self-contained solution for denser credit assignment.

2. **Novel Meta-RL Framework for LLMs:** The work formalizes in-context meta-reinforcement learning as a practical paradigm for agentic search. It demonstrates that training policies to condition on cross-episode experiences and explicitly reflect on past attempts can lead to more structured and effective exploration, which is a departure from independent episode-based RL.

3. **Enhanced Exploration and Adaptability:** The framework transforms disconnected search attempts into a progressively informed search process. This capability allows LLM agents to learn to explore more effectively at test time, adapting their strategies based on accumulated in-context knowledge. This can lead to more robust performance in dynamic and open-ended information-seeking environments.

4. **Improved Generalization and Robustness:** The empirical results show consistent performance improvements across a diverse set of QA benchmarks and model sizes (3B and 7B parameters). This suggests that MR-Search contributes to better generalization capabilities and robustness of LLM agents, particularly on complex multi-hop reasoning tasks where traditional RL struggles.

5. **Efficient Policy Optimization:** The introduction of a multi-turn RL algorithm that uses grouped relative advantages (RLOO) at the turn level provides an unbiased and fine-grained credit assignment mechanism without requiring a separate value function or critic. This can reduce computational complexity during training and avoid potential issues associated with value function estimation.

6. **Potential for Broader Agentic AI:** The principles demonstrated by MR-Search—leveraging self-reflection for cross-episode learning and fine-grained credit assignment—could extend beyond search tasks. They may be applicable to other forms of complex, multi-step agentic behaviors where LLMs interact with environments and tools. This could influence the development of more autonomous and adaptive LLM agents across various domains.

7. **Future Research Directions:** The work opens avenues for future research, including scaling MR-Search to long-form generation benchmarks, integrating with heterogeneous tool environments (beyond fixed Wikipedia search), and further investigating the scaling properties of meta-RL with frontier base models.
