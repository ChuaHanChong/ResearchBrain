# Self-CriTeach: LLM Self-Teaching and Self-Critiquing for Improving Robotic Planning via Automated Domain Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The Self-CriTeach framework enables a Large Language Model (LLM) to self-teach and self-critique its robotic planning capabilities by autonomously generating and utilizing symbolic planning domains. This approach provides scalable training data and structured feedback, leading to a 20% overall planning success rate improvement and enhanced robustness to imperfect perception in robotic tasks.

## Problem

- LLM-based robotic planning often struggles with imperfect logical states or perceptual noise due to externalizing planning logic, making solutions brittle.
- Generating high-quality, large-scale Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning traces for robotic tasks is a costly and labor-intensive manual process.
- Applying reinforcement learning (RL) to improve LLM planning is difficult in robotics due to long-horizon tasks, sparse rewards, and the complexity of manual reward engineering.

## Method

- An LLM autonomously generates, refines, and validates PDDL (Planning Domain Definition Language) planning domains through an iterative feedback-driven repair process.
- Symbolic plans derived from these self-generated domains are automatically transformed into context-rich natural language Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories for supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
- The self-generated PDDL domains are reused to define dense, structured reward and constraint cost functions for ==reinforcement learning== (RL) via Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO).

## Results

- The SCT-4B model achieved a **20%** absolute gain in overall planning success rate compared to its Qwen3-4B base model and consistently outperformed larger state-of-the-art LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o, Mistral-24B).
- Demonstrated stronger generalization to unseen tasks, achieving a **0.45** success rate on the "Prepare Experiment" task compared to Qwen3-4B's **0.24** and GPT-4o's **0.10**.
- Showed improved resistance to incomplete or noisy logical states in real-robot experiments, allowing successful planning with partial symbolic observations where PDDL solvers were brittle.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - LLM self-generated symbolic planning domains can serve as a unified, scalable source for both reasoning supervision (for SFT) and structured reward/constraint feedback (for RL).
> - Transforming symbolic plans into natural language CoT is crucial for the LLM to internalize generalized planning logic and avoid mere memorization of solutions.
> - Reinforcement learning with domain-derived dense rewards and explicit constraint costs effectively complements SFT, significantly reducing state-transition violations and reinforcing robust planning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) into robotic task planning has shown potential, primarily due to their reasoning capabilities and capacity for cross-task generalization. Early approaches treated LLMs as direct planners, but these often encountered challenges such as stochastic outputs, error accumulation over long-horizon tasks, and difficulties in maintaining global consistency. Subsequent research sought to mitigate these issues by combining LLMs with symbolic search-based algorithms, which improved planning robustness for complex tasks. More recently, LLMs have demonstrated the ability to automatically infer planning domains, often expressed in formal languages like PDDL (Planning Domain Definition Language), from various inputs such as natural language descriptions or demonstrations.

Despite these advancements, existing LLM-based planning systems face several limitations. A common approach treats inferred planning domains primarily as utilities for external symbolic planners, rather than as internalizable sources of reasoning supervision or structured reward signals for learning. This externalization can lead to brittleness when LLMs encounter imperfect logical states or perceptual noise, as the core planning structure remains largely outside the learned model.

Simultaneously, the broader field of reasoning-oriented LLMs has advanced significantly through the "supervised fine-tuning (SFT) + reinforcement learning (RL)" paradigm, often relying on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) supervision. However, applying this paradigm to robotics presents unique challenges:
1. **Expensive CoT Supervision:** Generating high-quality, large-scale CoT reasoning traces for robotic tasks typically requires manual curation, which is costly and labor-intensive.
2. **Reward Engineering in RL:** Robotic tasks often involve long-horizon, combinatorial decision-making, leading to sparse rewards. This makes reward engineering for RL-based improvements difficult and less scalable.

This research addresses these gaps by proposing that LLM self-written planning domains can serve a dual role, acting as both a scalable source of reasoning supervision and structured reward functions. By internalizing symbolic planning structures, the authors aim to develop learning-based planners that are more resilient to imperfect or noisy logical state representations. The work builds upon prior findings that symbolic planning can generate scalable robot task plans and that transformations between symbolic and language representations are effective. It positions itself as a framework that leverages the structured nature of self-generated symbolic domains to unify data generation for SFT and feedback for RL, thereby enhancing the LLM's intrinsic planning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the planning capabilities of a base Large Language Model (LLM) for robotic tasks, making it more robust, generalizable, and efficient, particularly in environments with imperfect logical states or perception noise. This overarching goal is motivated by several critical limitations in current LLM-based robotic planning approaches:

1. **Brittleness to Imperfect Logical States:** Existing methods often treat LLM-generated planning domains as external tools for symbolic search. This means the underlying LLM does not deeply internalize the planning logic, rendering it susceptible to errors when faced with imperfect logical state representations or perceptual inaccuracies. The motivation is to enable the LLM to learn and internalize this symbolic planning structure, thereby increasing its resilience.

2. **Cost of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Supervision:** The development of reasoning-oriented LLMs has heavily relied on CoT supervision, which involves providing models with step-by-step reasoning traces. For complex robotic tasks, manually collecting large-scale, high-quality CoT data is expensive, time-consuming, and often impractical. The researchers are motivated to find an automated, scalable method for generating such supervision.

3. **Challenges in Reinforcement Learning (RL) Reward Engineering:** Enhancing LLM performance through RL post-training requires effective reward signals. However, robotic tasks typically feature long-horizon decision-making and sparse rewards, making the manual engineering of structured and scalable reward functions difficult. This limitation hinders the effective application of RL to improve robotic planning LLMs.

To address these motivations, the **key objectives** of the Self-CriTeach framework are:

- **To reinterpret LLM self-generated planning domains:** Rather than using these domains solely as external planning tools, the objective is to leverage them as reusable knowledge sources that provide both training data and structured feedback.
- **To enable large-scale, automated generation of robotic planning data:** Specifically, the framework aims to use self-written PDDL domains to automatically generate executable task plans and transform these into context-rich Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories. This serves as scalable, human-annotation-free supervision for supervised fine-tuning (SFT).
- **To provide structured, dense reward functions for reinforcement learning:** The objective is to reuse the same self-generated PDDL domains as reward signals for reinforcement learning, eliminating the need for manual reward engineering and offering fine-grained feedback for self-critiquing.
- **To yield a planning-enhanced LLM with specific performance improvements:** The ultimate goal is to produce an LLM that demonstrates:
 - Higher planning success rates.
 - Stronger cross-task generalization capabilities.
 - Reduced inference token costs (improved efficiency).
 - Increased resistance to imperfect logical states and perception noise in real-world scenarios.

By pursuing these objectives, the research aims to establish a unified and automated training pipeline that significantly advances the capability and applicability of LLMs in robotic task planning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Self-CriTeach framework proposes a unified training pipeline that allows an LLM to self-teach and self-critique using autonomously generated symbolic planning domains. The methodology consists of four main stages: automatic planning domain generation, symbolic-CoT transformation, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL).

#### 3.1. Automatic Planning Domain Generation

The first stage focuses on creating robotic planning domains (in PDDL format) that capture task logic and physical constraints. This process involves:
1. **Initial Domain Skeleton Construction:** A base LLM ($M_0$) infers predicates (object properties/relations) and action schemas (preconditions, effects) from unstructured inputs such as natural language descriptions or robot task demonstrations. This leverages existing domain induction frameworks (e.g., Huang et al., 2025b). The result is a preliminary PDDL domain skeleton ($D_0 = \langle \hat{P}, \hat{A} \rangle$).
2. **Feedback-Driven Planning Domain Repair:** To refine $D_0$, a closed-loop correction procedure is implemented. The generated domain is validated by attempting to solve sampled planning problems using a symbolic planner (Fast-Forward). If planning failures occur, error traces (e.g., undefined predicates, unreachable goals) are reformulated into structured diagnostic feedback. This feedback is then provided to $M_0$ via tailored prompts, guiding it to perform targeted logical repairs. This iterative process continues until a consistent and executable domain ($D'$) is generated.
3. **Domain Redundancy Pruning:** To ensure the generated domain is compact and efficient, a symbolic hill-climbing algorithm is applied. This algorithm prunes redundant predicates and actions while preserving the solvability of planning problems, resulting in a minimal, executable planning domain ($D^{\star}$).
4. **Automatic Problem-Plan Pair Generation:** Once $D^{\star}$ is validated and optimized, a large library of problem-plan pairs ($ \langle Q, \tau \rangle $) is generated. This involves sampling initial and goal states consistent with $D^{\star}$ and solving them using the symbolic planner. Both optimal and suboptimal solutions are included to provide diverse training data.

#### 3.2. Symbolic-CoT Transformation for Training

This stage converts the symbolic plans generated from $D^{\star}$ into natural language Chain-of-Thought (CoT) representations. This transformation is considered crucial because directly training LLMs on raw symbolic structures can lead to memorization rather than generalized reasoning. The process involves prompting the base model ($M_0$) with a designed template that elicits decision reasoning:
- **Plan Explanation:** The model explains why each action is selected, justifying its contribution to goal progression and exposing intermediate decision structures.
- **State Transition Checking:** The model verifies that action preconditions are met and that effects are correctly applied, ensuring global plan consistency through step-wise validity checks.
- **Alternative Exploration:** The model enumerates other applicable actions at each state and discusses why they were not prioritized, demonstrating a broader understanding of the planning space.
- **Failure Backtracking:** The model explains how to reason over infeasible planning branches by tracing constraint violations back to earlier decisions.

This structured elicitation converts symbolic plans ($ \tau $) and intermediate states ($X_t$) into detailed natural-language CoT trajectories ($CoT_{\tau}$), explicitly aligning symbolic planning semantics with LLM reasoning. Allowing the planning domain to generate diverse valid solutions for the same problem further augments the dataset with varied planning strategies.

#### 3.3. Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)

The generated problem-plan-CoT tuples ($ \zeta = \langle Q, \tau, CoT_{\tau} \rangle $) form the training corpus for SFT. The base LLM ($M_0$) is fine-tuned to become $M_{SFT}$.
- **Training Objective:** The model is trained to generate both the action sequence ($ \tau $) and the explanatory CoT trajectory ($CoT_{\tau}$) conditioned on the input planning problem ($Q$). This is achieved using a standard autoregressive language modeling loss function.
- **Purpose:** This process compels the model to produce accurate plans while simultaneously generating coherent reasoning chains that mimic the learned symbolic planning behavior, thereby internalizing the planning dynamics. Dynamic paraphrasing of problem settings during data generation is employed to prevent overfitting to specific prompting styles.

#### 3.4. Reinforcement Learning (RL)

The SFT-trained model ($M_{SFT}$) is further optimized through RL to become $M_{SCT}$, leveraging the self-generated planning domain ($D^{\star}$) as a structured reward signal. This addresses the challenge of sparse rewards in robotics.
- **Algorithm:** Constrained Policy Optimization (CPO) is primarily adopted, which formulates policy updates as a constrained optimization problem.
- **Reward Function:** A dense, step-level reward function $R(X_t, a_t)$ is defined based on goal predicate satisfaction at state $X_t$, normalized by the total number of goal predicates.
- **Constraint Cost Function:** A constraint cost $C(X_t, a_t)$ penalizes actions that violate domain preconditions or produce inconsistent states, explicitly enforcing symbolic legality. This provides fine-grained feedback on failures beyond binary success/failure.
- **Purpose:** RL refines the model's planning by reinforcing constraint satisfaction and reducing state-transition violations, complementing the structural bias learned during SFT.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

The methodology is evaluated using a base model (Qwen3-4B-Instruct) across various planning tasks, including established benchmarks like Blocksworld variants (Classic, Hard, Align) and novel, unseen tasks (Prepare Experiment, Reorganize Room, Machine Parts Assembly). Evaluation metrics include planning success rate and a progress score (quantifying similarity to the goal state after the first invalid action). Ablation studies are conducted to isolate the contributions of SFT, RL, and symbolic-CoT transformation. Real-robot experiments are performed to assess robustness to imperfect logical states.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Self-CriTeach (SCT) framework demonstrates consistent improvements in LLM-based robotic planning, as evidenced by a series of experiments and ablation studies.

**RQ1: Effect of Self-CriTeach**
The Self-CriTeach framework significantly enhances the planning capabilities of a base LLM. The SCT-4B model (built on Qwen3-4B) exhibited substantial improvements over its base model. Specifically, SCT-4B achieved a 20% absolute gain in overall planning success rate compared to Qwen3-4B. On the more challenging "Blocks World Hard" benchmark, SCT-4B showed a 21% improvement, indicating enhanced ability in long-horizon reasoning. It also demonstrated consistent gains in progress score across both seen and unseen tasks, suggesting increased robustness beyond the training distribution.

**RQ2: Comparison to Other Models and Approaches**
SCT-4B consistently outperformed other state-of-the-art LLMs of comparable scale, including larger variants like Qwen3-8B, Mistral-24B, Gemma3-12B, and even the closed-source GPT-4o, across all evaluation tasks. This performance advantage was particularly noticeable on long-horizon tasks and unseen task distributions. When compared against alternative reasoning-enhancement techniques using the same Qwen3-4B backbone (e.g., knowledge distillation (30B-Distill, Self-Distill), Majority Voting, Prompt-CoT), SCT-4B also showed superior performance. This suggests that the gains from Self-CriTeach stem from a fundamental strengthening of the LLM's planning capability, rather than mere task-specific memorization or superficial prompting strategies. While some baselines achieved moderate progress scores, their lower success rates indicated difficulties in maintaining global plan consistency, a challenge that SCT-4B addressed more effectively. The study also noted that more recent LLM models generally exhibited stronger baseline planning capabilities.

**RQ3: Generalization**
SCT-4B exhibited stronger generalization to unseen tasks compared to all baselines. By internalizing symbolic representations and learned planning behaviors, the model demonstrated transferable planning capabilities. This enabled it to scale robustly across diverse novel scenarios involving new objects, environments, and goals not explicitly seen during training. For instance, on the unseen "Prepare Experiment" task, SCT-4B achieved a 0.45 success rate, significantly higher than the base Qwen3-4B (0.24) and GPT-4o (0.10).

**RQ4: Planning Efficiency**
SCT-4B achieved superior planning performance while also maintaining higher efficiency, characterized by a lower average token cost per plan. This efficiency is attributed to the symbolic-CoT transformation, which provides concise supervision by focusing on reasoning steps that directly contribute to planning. The method effectively eliminated extraneous reasoning steps found in general CoT prompting. SCT-4B even outperformed distillation from a much larger model (30B-Distill) in terms of efficiency, indicating that symbolic-CoT provides a higher-quality training signal than simply imitating larger models' reasoning.

**RQ5: Roles of Components (Ablation Study)**
The ablation studies clarified the contributions of the individual components within Self-CriTeach:
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT):** Training with self-generated symbolic-CoT data (SCTSFT-4B) alone yielded substantial gains in progress score across both seen and unseen tasks. This indicated that symbolic-CoT supervision provides a strong inductive bias, guiding the model toward structured planning behaviors.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL):** Combining SFT with RL using CPO (SCTCPO-4B) further improved both progress score and success rate. The greater improvement in progress score suggested a substantial reduction in state-transition violations, demonstrating that CPO effectively complements SFT by reinforcing constraint satisfaction and step-wise feasibility. CPO consistently outperformed DPO in this context, likely due to its explicit constraint enforcement aligning well with symbolic planning's feasibility checks.
- **Symbolic-CoT Transformation:** Directly training the base model on raw symbolic traces without CoT transformation (SCTSymbol-4B) showed unstable generalization despite some improvements on seen tasks. This indicated a tendency towards solution memorization rather than true internalization of planning structure, underscoring the critical role of symbolic-CoT transformation for achieving transferable and generalizable planning.

**Real Robot Experiment**
In real-robot experiments using a UR5e manipulator, SCT-4B demonstrated improved resistance to incomplete or noisy logical states, a common issue for PDDL-based planners under imperfect perception. When perception was provided by a VLM (Qwen3-VL-4B) or even a rule-based classifier (both prone to noise), the PDDL solver was brittle. In contrast, SCT-4B successfully reasoned and planned with partial symbolic observations, showcasing its superior deployability and compatibility with practical perception pipelines in real-world robotic systems.

**Backbone Ablation**
Applying the Self-CriTeach pipeline to Llama-3.1-8B, a different LLM architecture, also yielded substantial improvements in planning success rate and progress score. This indicates that the benefits of Self-CriTeach are not limited to the Qwen family of models but generalize across different LLM backbones, enhancing models even if they start with weaker symbolic-planning capabilities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Self-CriTeach framework presents a notable advancement in the field of LLM-based robotic planning, addressing several critical limitations of prior approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Automated and Scalable Data Generation:** Self-CriTeach provides an automated mechanism for generating large-scale, high-quality training data for robotic planning. By leveraging LLM-generated PDDL domains, the framework eliminates the need for expensive, manual annotation of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) trajectories, which has been a significant bottleneck for applying SFT+RL paradigms in robotics. This scalability opens avenues for training more robust and capable robotic LLMs without human intervention for data curation.

2. **Internalization of Symbolic Planning Logic:** A key contribution is enabling LLMs to internalize symbolic planning structures rather than treating external planners as black boxes. Through symbolic-to-CoT transformation and subsequent fine-tuning, the LLM learns to reason about planning dynamics, preconditions, effects, and goal progression. This internalization enhances the LLM's intrinsic planning capabilities, moving beyond mere sequence prediction or reliance on external tools.

3. **Structured and Dense Reward Signals for RL:** The framework reuses self-generated PDDL domains as structured, dense reward functions for reinforcement learning. This innovative approach addresses the challenge of sparse rewards common in long-horizon robotic tasks, providing fine-grained feedback that guides the LLM to reinforce constraint satisfaction and correct planning failures more effectively. This reduces the burden of manual reward engineering and makes RL a more practical tool for robotic planning.

4. **Enhanced Robustness to Imperfect Perception:** The real-robot experiments demonstrate that LLMs trained with Self-CriTeach exhibit improved resistance to imperfect logical states resulting from noisy or incomplete perception. This is a critical capability for deploying robotic systems in real-world, unstructured environments where perfect state estimation is rarely achievable. The ability to plan effectively despite perceptual ambiguities enhances the practical applicability of LLM-based robot control.

5. **Improved Generalization and Efficiency:** The resulting planning-enhanced LLMs show stronger cross-task generalization, allowing them to adapt to unseen tasks, objects, and environments more effectively. Furthermore, the symbolic-CoT transformation contributes to reduced inference token costs, making the planning process more efficient without sacrificing performance. This combination of generalization and efficiency is valuable for diverse and dynamic robotic applications.

6. **Broader Applicability to LLMs:** The backbone ablation studies indicate that the Self-CriTeach methodology is not confined to a specific LLM architecture but can generalize to different models (e.g., Llama-3.1), strengthening their symbolic planning capabilities. This suggests a generalizable paradigm for improving reasoning in a wider range of language models.

**Potential Impact:** The Self-CriTeach framework could significantly impact the development of more autonomous and intelligent robotic systems. By enabling robots to reason, plan, and self-correct with greater sophistication and robustness in complex, real-world scenarios, it moves towards more flexible and deployable robot agents. This could accelerate progress in various domains, including domestic assistance, industrial automation, logistics, and scientific discovery, where robots need to perform long-horizon, multi-step tasks with varying levels of information certainty.

**Limitations and Future Work:** The paper acknowledges limitations, including the framework's reliance on the base LLM's initial capability to induce coherent PDDL domains and the assumption that target tasks can be accurately modeled in symbolic space. Future work aims to explore domain generation methods with lower base model requirements, improved symbolic abstraction for perceptually complex tasks, and more adaptive skill discovery with online learning during real-robot execution. These future directions indicate pathways for further enhancing the framework's robustness and applicability.
