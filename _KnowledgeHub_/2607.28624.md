# PhiZero: A World Model Built Around Physical Language

> [!summary] Summary
> PHIZERO introduces a physical world model centered on a 'physical language' that represents state-transition patterns, explicitly separating the inference of world dynamics from pixel-level synthesis. This approach enables the generation of videos with improved physical coherence, scoring 41.2 on Physics-IQ Verified, and demonstrates strong performance in physical understanding tasks and zero-shot transfer across embodiments.

## Problem

- Existing video world models often predict future frames directly in pixel space, leading to implicit physical dynamics and frequent physically inconsistent outcomes.
- Natural language, while powerful for human reasoning, is often too coarse to faithfully represent the intricate and fine-grained state transitions of the physical world.
- A need exists for AI systems to learn explicit, granular representations of physical-world transitions to enable robust reasoning beyond mere visual fidelity.

## Method

- **PhiZero** introduces a ==physical language== as a compact, discrete, and self-supervised representation for state-transition patterns in the physical world.
- The model employs a ==reason-then-render== paradigm, consisting of a **Physical Language Tokenizer** (to encode video transitions) and a **Physical Language Reasoner** (to predict future physical language sequences).
- It leverages a pretrained video diffusion model as a decoder for high-fidelity visual rendering, allowing the physical language bottleneck to focus on encoding state changes rather than redundant appearance information.

## Results

- PhiZero achieved an IQ-Score of **41.2** on the Physics-IQ Verified benchmark, outperforming existing models like Sora 2 (**26.5**) and Cosmos3-Super (**39.5**), demonstrating superior physical outcome fidelity.
- The model scored highest in Physics Score (**3.01**) on PhyGround and attained the best Physics Adherence (**4.88**) and Total scores (**8.19**) on WorldModelBench, indicating strong adherence to physical laws and general world modeling capabilities.
- The Physical Language Tokenizer achieved competitive reconstruction quality (PSNR **28.9**, SSIM **0.903**, LPIPS **0.087**) using only **256** discrete tokens, showcasing compact and informative encoding of state transitions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly separating physical dynamics inference from pixel-level rendering through a 'reason-then-render' paradigm significantly improves physical consistency in generated videos.
> - A compact, discrete physical language can effectively disentangle state transitions from visual appearance, enabling versatile transferability of motion patterns across diverse visual contexts and embodiments.
> - Initializing the Reasoner with a pretrained Vision-Language Model and specialized training on motion-rich, physically informative data is crucial for accurate and plausible physical-language prediction.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this preprint contributes to the field of artificial intelligence, specifically within the domain of generative models for video and physical world modeling. Existing research in video world models often focuses on directly predicting future video frames in pixel space. While these models have achieved visual fidelity, a limitation is that the underlying physical dynamics of the world remain implicit within high-dimensional visual representations. This can lead to generated outcomes that lack physical consistency.

The paper draws inspiration from human cognition, where observed visual patterns are abstracted into conceptual knowledge, often organized through language, to enable explicit reasoning about world evolution. In the realm of AI, language models have demonstrated significant success in digital domains, but the complexity and fine-grained nature of physical state transitions pose a challenge for natural language representations.

PHIZERO addresses this gap by proposing a "physical language" as a compact, discrete, and self-supervised representation for state-transition patterns in the physical world. This approach distinguishes itself from conventional video world models that predict pixels directly. It also differs from other lines of research such as latent-action world models, which typically infer compact transition variables for control within specific domains, and video tokenizers, which primarily aim for video compression and reconstruction. While these related fields contribute to understanding dynamics or efficient representation, PHIZERO integrates a learned "physical language" specifically for explicit reasoning about generalized physical world evolution, aiming for both physically realistic generation and understanding across diverse, open-domain scenarios. The "reason-then-render" paradigm introduced by PHIZERO aims to separate the inference of world dynamics from the synthesis of pixel-level visual details, a departure from many end-to-end pixel-space prediction methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce PHIZERO, a physical world model designed around a novel concept referred to as "physical language." This physical language is defined as a compact, discrete representation that captures state-transition patterns in the world.

The motivation for developing PHIZERO stems from several identified limitations and inspirations:

1. **Implicit Dynamics in Existing World Models:** Current video world models primarily aim for visual fidelity by directly predicting future videos in pixel space. This approach often leaves the fundamental physical dynamics governing world evolution implicit within complex, high-dimensional visual predictors. Consequently, these models frequently produce physically inconsistent or implausible outcomes, even if visually appealing. The authors highlight that for AI systems to truly support "Physical AI," their objective must extend beyond mere visual fidelity to actively learning and representing how the physical world evolves.

2. **Inspiration from Human Intelligence:** Humans do not simply memorize visual outcomes. Instead, they abstract generalizable patterns and knowledge from visual experiences, which they then organize and express using natural language. This symbolic space allows for explicit reasoning about world dynamics. The success of large language models in digital contexts further demonstrates the scalability and utility of language as a substrate for learning and reasoning.

3. **Limitations of Natural Language for Physical Reasoning:** While natural language is powerful for human reasoning, the authors observe that it can be too coarse to faithfully represent the intricate and fine-grained state transitions observed in the physical world. This limitation suggests a need for a more specialized, granular representation specifically tailored for physical dynamics.

4. **Need for Explicit Reasoning:** To overcome the problem of implicit dynamics, the authors pose the question: "Can we move beyond natural language and learn a finer-grained representation of physical-world transitions that enables explicit reasoning?" This question underpins the core motivation: to create a representation that allows AI systems to explicitly reason about physical world evolution, rather than merely predicting visual sequences.

By addressing these motivations, PHIZERO aims to establish a "reason-then-render" paradigm. In this paradigm, the model first infers the future world evolution as a sequence in the discrete physical language space and subsequently renders these inferred transitions into video. This separation is intended to make world evolution an explicit target for reasoning, thereby improving physical coherence and consistency in generated content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PHIZERO is structured around a "reason-then-render" paradigm, comprising two main components: a Physical Language Tokenizer and a Physical Language Reasoner. The overall problem of future prediction is factorized into physical-language reasoning and future-video rendering: $p(V, z | I_0, c) = p(z | I_0, c) * p(V | I_0, z)$, where $V$ is the future video, $I_0$ is the first frame, $c$ is the textual action intent, and $z$ is the discrete physical language.

#### 3.1. Physical Language Tokenizer

The Physical Language Tokenizer is designed to compress video state transitions into a compact, discrete physical-language sequence.

- **Encoder with Transition-level Q-Former:**
 - A spatiotemporal encoder first processes an input video $V$ to obtain latent features $x$.
 - Instead of a global representation, the model explicitly focuses on transitions between *adjacent* latent states ($x_i, x_{i+1}$).
 - A shared Q-Former (adapted from Li et al., 2023) extracts transition features $q_i$ for each temporal interval by jointly attending to the adjacent states. This provides a local temporal inductive bias, simplifying the complexity of each compressed transition while preserving temporal order.
 - The concatenated transition features $q$ are then discretized using Finite Scalar Quantization (FSQ) (Mentzer et al., 2024), resulting in the discrete physical-language sequence $z$. FSQ avoids learned codebooks by using a Cartesian product of scalar quantization levels.
 - Finally, $z$ is projected to a hidden dimension to form the physical-language context $P_c$.

- **Diffusion-prior Decoder:**
 - A pretrained video diffusion model (Wan et al., 2025) serves as the decoder. Its generative prior is leveraged to recover fine-grained visual details, allowing the compact physical-language bottleneck to focus on encoding state transitions rather than redundant appearance information.
 - The decoder's text condition is replaced with the physical-language context $P_c$.
 - The first frame $I_0$ is provided as a clean visual condition, anchoring static scene appearance and further encouraging the discrete bottleneck to encode only future state changes.
 - The decoder reconstructs the target video using a standard flow-matching objective.

- **Pure-noise Warm-up:** To prevent the pretrained diffusion decoder from initially bypassing the new physical-language context, a warm-up stage is introduced where all future-frame latents are initialized from pure noise. This forces the decoder to rely on $P_c$ and $I_0$ for reconstruction, providing stronger supervision for learning informative physical-language representations.

- **Data Curation Pipeline (Tokenizer):** A hierarchical data curation pipeline is used.
 - **Pretraining:** Starts with a 50K-hour pool of in-the-wild real-world videos. After deduplication and filtering for technical defects, resolution, duration, and shot transitions, approximately 10K hours of unlabeled videos are used for tokenizer pretraining.
 - **SFT (Supervised Fine-Tuning):** A stricter second-pass filtering is applied to the real-world corpus (aesthetic quality, motion magnitude, VLM-assessed state-transition observability). This is combined with approximately 1K hours of filtered simulated videos (rendering quality, duration, object motion). This yields approximately 5M four-second video clips for tokenizer SFT.

- **Curriculum Training Recipe:** A two-stage training scheme with a joint temporal and spatial curriculum is employed.
 - **Pretraining:** Videos are processed at 256x448 resolution, progressively increasing clip duration from 1s to 4s. This allows learning local state changes before longer-range evolution.
 - **SFT:** Reconstruction resolution is increased to 512x896 on the 5M-clip corpus. In a final refinement stage, the tokenizer is frozen, and only the diffusion decoder is refined to improve reconstruction quality. Entropy regularization and REPA loss are applied during this stage.

#### 3.2. Physical Language Reasoner

The Physical Language Reasoner predicts the physical-language sequence from the current visual state ($I_0$) and a textual action intent ($c$).

- **Initialization with Pretrained VLM:** The reasoner is initialized from a pretrained Vision-Language Model (VLM) (e.g., Qwen3-VL-4B). This leverages the VLM's existing visual-semantic knowledge and commonsense world priors.
- **Vocabulary Extension:** The VLM's vocabulary is extended with distinct atomic symbols for each FSQ index.
- **Autoregressive Prediction:** Given $I_0$ and $c$, the reasoner autoregressively predicts a length-N physical-language sequence $z$ over the FSQ-induced vocabulary. The conditional distribution is factorized in temporal order.
- **Optimization:** Supervised offline by physical-language sequences $z$ encoded from training videos by the frozen Tokenizer. Optimized with an autoregressive cross-entropy objective under teacher forcing.

- **Data Curation Pipeline (Reasoner):** Also two stages.
 - **Continued Pretraining:** Reuses the 5M curated real-world and simulation clips from Tokenizer SFT. VLM-generated captions (summarizing high-level initiating actions, not fine-grained details) and the first frame are inputs; the frozen Tokenizer provides target physical-language sequences.
 - **SFT:** A VLM-based rich-motion filter and a physical filter are applied to create a corpus of approximately 1M motion-rich and physically informative clips, along with curated simulator-generated samples.

- **Two-stage Training Recipe:**
 - **Stage 1 (Continued Pretraining):** Adapts the VLM to autoregressive physical-language prediction, establishing correspondence among textual intent, visual state, and state transition.
 - **Stage 2 (SFT):** Specializes the model on motion-rich and physically informative clips to improve the physical plausibility and precision of inferred state transitions.

#### 3.3. Implementation Details

- The spatiotemporal encoder uses the Wan2.2 VAE encoder architecture, and the diffusion decoder is initialized from Wan2.2-5B. LoRA is used for fine-tuning the diffusion decoder.
- FSQ uses scalar quantization levels (8, 5, 5, 5, 5, 5), yielding a 25K vocabulary. A 33-frame video is encoded into a 256-length physical-language sequence using 32 learnable queries in the Q-Former.
- The Reasoner is initialized from Qwen3-VL-4B and its vocabulary extended.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PHIZERO was evaluated across a range of video generation and understanding benchmarks, and its components were subject to ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Video Generation Results

PHIZERO demonstrated strong performance on three video-generation benchmarks designed to assess physical fidelity and world-modeling capabilities:

- **Physics-IQ Verified (Rädsch et al., 2026):** Measures physical outcome fidelity by comparing generated videos with real reference videos using rule-based metrics (S-IoU, ST-IoU, WS-IoU, IQ-Score). PHIZERO achieved the best IQ-Score (41.2), outperforming models like Sora 2 (26.5), Grok-Video (34.8), and Cosmos3-Super (39.5).
- **PhyGround (Lin et al., 2026):** Evaluates adherence to physical laws using physics-specialized VLM judges. PHIZERO scored highest in Physics Score (3.01) and Overall score (2.97), surpassing models such as Veo3.1 (2.93) and Wan2.2-14B (2.95).
- **WorldModelBench (Li et al., 2026a):** Assesses general world-modeling capability across diverse domains based on physics adherence and overall video quality. PHIZERO attained the best Physics Adherence (4.88) and Total scores (8.19), exceeding Luma (7.72) and Runway (8.08).

These results indicate that PHIZERO generates state transitions that closely align with real-world physical outcomes across varied phenomena.

#### 4.2. Video Understanding Results

PHIZERO also exhibited competitive performance on three video-understanding benchmarks, evaluated through a pairwise likelihood comparison protocol:

- **IntPhys2 (Bordes et al., 2025):** Evaluates intuitive physics understanding by distinguishing physically possible from impossible videos. PHIZERO achieved an Overall accuracy of 56.34%, competitive with Gemini-2.5 Flash (55.63) and V-JEPA (53.75).
- **LikePhys (Yuan et al., 2025):** Assesses physical-plausibility discrimination in simulated scenes. PHIZERO recorded the lowest average Plausibility Preference Error (41.7), indicating superior discrimination of physical inconsistencies compared to models like Hunyuan-Video (43.6) and Wan2.1-14B (43.8).
- **YoCausal (Xie et al., 2026):** Examines temporal and causal understanding in real-world videos. PHIZERO showed the highest Causality Cognition Index (6.20) and the lowest Aggregate Rank (2.0), suggesting strong causal understanding.

These findings suggest that PHIZERO can identify physical inconsistencies and causal violations effectively.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative comparisons with the Wan2.2-5B baseline on Physics-IQ Verified cases (e.g., tennis ball striking a duck, ball falling into kinetic sand, blocks in a chain reaction, rotating objects with shadows) illustrated that PHIZERO generates coherent downstream physical effects such as collision-induced displacement, gravity-driven deformation, chain reactions, and dynamically changing shadows, which the baseline often failed to capture.

#### 4.4. Physical Language Tokenizer Reconstruction Performance

The Physical Language Tokenizer was evaluated for its reconstruction quality using 256 discrete physical-language symbols. It achieved a PSNR of 28.9, SSIM of 0.903, and LPIPS of 0.087, which was the best reconstruction quality among highly compressed tokenizers, significantly outperforming others while using only 256 tokens, compared to a baseline VAE needing 44,800 continuous tokens. This indicates compact and informative encoding of state transitions.

#### 4.5. Ablation Studies

- **Physical Language Tokenizer Ablations:**
 - Replacing the pretrained diffusion decoder with a deterministic decoder led to the largest performance drop, confirming the importance of the diffusion prior for fine-grained appearance recovery.
 - Removing the transition-level Q-Former (i.e., using a global Q-Former) also degraded reconstruction quality, validating the local temporal inductive bias.
 - Disabling the pure-noise warm-up consistently reduced performance, showing its role in encouraging reliance on physical language.
- **Physical Language Reasoner Ablations:**
 - Prompt enhancement improved the Wan2.2-5B baseline but remained significantly below PHIZERO, suggesting natural language alone is insufficient.
 - Removing simulation data decreased performance, supporting its role in predicting physically plausible transitions.
 - Replacing the two-stage training with joint training degraded performance, indicating the benefit of dedicated SFT on motion-rich, physically informative videos.

#### 4.6. Analysis of Physical Language

- **Transferability:** By encoding a source video's state transition into physical language and then decoding it with an edited first frame (e.g., changing object appearance), PHIZERO demonstrated that physical language disentangles state transitions from visual appearance. The transferred videos preserved motion patterns (e.g., pouring, spreading, liquid flow) across significant appearance and background changes.
- **Semantic Structure:** UMAP projections of physical-language representations for autonomous driving (nuScenes) and robotic manipulation (AGI-Bot RealRobot) showed clear kinematic organization. Stationary driving clips formed a distinct cluster, and steering patterns arranged along a continuous manifold. Robotic gripper actions formed compact, separated clusters. This suggests that physical language captures meaningful state-transition semantics and organizes videos by how the world changes.

#### 4.7. Broader Applications

PHIZERO's capabilities extend to several broader applications:

- **Physically Realistic Video World Model:** Generates physically coherent future evolution for diverse real-world dynamics, including natural phenomena (ocean waves), liquid pouring, object impacts with splashes, and explosions.
- **Controllable and Interactive World Model:** Separates reasoning from pixel synthesis, allowing fine-grained control over state evolution. Demonstrated on autonomous driving (following trajectory variations) and robotic manipulation (precise gripper movements). Supports interactive rollouts through sequential natural language controls and sliding-window autoregressive generation.
- **Zero-Shot Cross-embodiment and Sim-to-real Transfer:** Physical language's appearance-disentangled nature enables transfer without target-specific training. Examples include transferring human full-body and hand motions to humanoid and dexterous robots, and simulating interactions from LIBERO into realistic visual domains.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The PHIZERO research introduces a distinct approach to physical world modeling, centered on the concept of a "physical language." This contribution holds several points of significance and potential impact:

1. **Novel Representation and Paradigm:** The introduction of "physical language" as a compact, discrete, and self-supervised representation of state-transition patterns is a novel contribution. By learning this language from unlabeled, in-the-wild videos, the work moves beyond direct pixel-space prediction towards a "reason-then-render" paradigm. This paradigm explicitly separates the inference of world dynamics from pixel-level synthesis, offering a different architectural approach to achieving physical consistency.

2. **Enhanced Physical Coherence:** The experimental results demonstrate that PHIZERO generates videos with notably improved physical coherence and consistency compared to existing state-of-the-art models. Its superior performance on benchmarks assessing physical outcome fidelity, physical-law adherence, and general world modeling suggests a step forward in creating AI systems that genuinely understand and replicate physical interactions.

3. **Improved Physical Understanding:** Beyond generation, PHIZERO also exhibits competitive capabilities in physical understanding tasks, such as distinguishing physically plausible events, recognizing intuitive physics, and discerning causal relationships in real-world videos. This indicates that the learned physical language is not merely for synthesis but also encodes meaningful knowledge about world dynamics.

4. **Versatile and Transferable Interface:** The analysis of physical language reveals its ability to disentangle state transitions from visual appearance. This disentanglement provides a general-purpose interface for representing, controlling, and transferring state transitions. Its demonstrated transferability across visual appearances, embodiments (human to robot), and domains (sim-to-real) is a significant capability.

5. **Broad Applications for Physical AI:** The framework's ability to support physically realistic video generation, fine-grained action-conditioned simulation, interactive world modeling, and zero-shot motion transfer has wide implications for "Physical AI." This could include advancements in robotics (e.g., demonstration retargeting, cross-morphology transfer), autonomous systems, and content generation requiring high physical fidelity. The potential to alleviate the scarcity of real-robot interaction data by transferring patterns from abundant human videos is particularly noteworthy.

6. **Scalability and Future Development:** While the current implementation operates at a certain scale, the data-driven and self-supervised nature of learning physical language from diverse unlabeled videos suggests potential for scalable development. Future work could explore grounding physical language in more interpretable symbolic physical laws, extending capabilities to visually ambiguous interactions, and further scaling the models and training data to enhance performance and coverage of the physical world. This research lays a foundation for more controllable, transferable, and physically intelligent AI systems.
