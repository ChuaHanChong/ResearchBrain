# PaliGemma: A versatile 3B VLM for transfer

> [!summary] Summary
> PaliGemma is presented as a sub-3 billion parameter Vision-Language Model (VLM) designed for versatile transfer learning. It achieves strong performance across nearly 40 diverse vision-language tasks, frequently matching or exceeding larger predecessors, and is released as an open-source base model to democratize VLM research.

## Problem

- The increasing computational costs and resource demands of large Vision-Language Models (VLMs) limit their accessibility for wider research and development.
- There is a need for highly versatile base VLMs that can be efficiently adapted and fine-tuned for a broad range of downstream tasks.
- The VLM research community benefits from open-source foundational models to foster innovation and specialized application development.

## Method

- **PaliGemma** combines a pre-trained ==400M SigLIP vision encoder== with a ==2B Gemma language model==, connected by a simple ==linear projection layer==.
- It employs a ==multi-stage training paradigm==: initial multimodal pretraining where both the vision encoder and language model are unfrozen, followed by resolution-increase training stages.
- The model utilizes a ==prefix-LM masking strategy==, allowing full attention on image and prefix tokens while applying ==autoregressive masking== and loss only on the generated suffix.

## Results

- PaliGemma, a sub-3B parameter VLM, achieves strong performance across nearly 40 diverse vision-language tasks, often matching or surpassing much larger models (e.g., PaLI-X, PaLM-E, PaLI-3).
- The model demonstrates robust transferability, with low standard deviation in fine-tuning results across runs and high data efficiency, enabling substantial performance gains with surprisingly few transfer examples.
- It performs effectively on both standard VLM benchmarks and specialized domains like remote sensing VQA, TallyVQA, video tasks, and referring expression segmentation, showcasing its broad applicability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Longer multimodal pretraining (Stage 1) is crucial for building a versatile base VLM with strong transfer capabilities.
> - Not freezing the image encoder or language model during multimodal pretraining improves spatial understanding and overall performance, contrary to common practice.
> - A simple linear projection layer is sufficient to effectively connect the vision and language components, performing as well as or better than more complex MLPs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

PaliGemma positions itself within the rapidly evolving field of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which aim to enable AI systems to understand and generate content across both visual and textual modalities. The paper explicitly traces the evolution of VLMs:

- **First Generation (e.g., CLIP, ALIGN):** These models scaled up classification pretraining by leveraging web-scale image-text pairs, effectively replacing fixed classification categories with flexible caption embeddings. This "opened up the vocabulary" for tasks like classification and retrieval, moving beyond pre-defined classes.
- **Second Generation (e.g., T5-like, Flamingo, BLIP-2, PaLI):** This era saw the unification of tasks like captioning and visual question-answering through generative encoder-decoder architectures. Models became more versatile in generating text outputs based on visual inputs and textual prompts.
- **Recent Works (e.g., Gemini, GPT-4, LLaVA):** The most recent trend involves an additional "instruction tuning" step. This fine-tuning process adapts raw base models to be more "user-friendly" and follow diverse instructions, leading to conversational AI capabilities.

PaliGemma is presented as a direct continuation and evolution of the **PaLI (Pathways Language-Image) lineage** of models developed by Google. This series has progressively pushed the boundaries of VLM performance while also emphasizing computational efficiency:

- **PaLI:** Initial scaling of VLM performance up to 17B parameters.
- **PaLI-X & PaLM-E:** Further scaling to much larger models (e.g., PaLM-E with a 540B PaLM LM), demonstrating increased performance but also observing saturation on standard tasks.
- **PaLI-3:** A pivotal development showing that with improved pretraining (using SigLIP) and careful data curation, a significantly smaller model (5B VLM, comprising 2B vision + 3B language) could match or surpass the performance of 10x to 100x larger predecessors like PaLI-X and PaLM-E across many benchmarks. This established a critical precedent for efficiency.

PaliGemma directly builds on the PaLI-3 philosophy. It aims to achieve comparable performance to these larger models with an even smaller footprint: a sub-3B VLM, specifically combining the 400M SigLIP vision encoder and the 2B Gemma language model. This focus on efficiency and "small-is-still-SOTA" is a key contribution in a landscape often dominated by ever-larger models.

Crucially, the paper highlights PaliGemma's nature as an **"open base VLM without instruction tuning."** This distinguishes it from many "user-friendly" instruction-tuned models like LLaVA or Mini-Gemini. By providing a versatile base model, Google DeepMind aims to equip the broader research community with a powerful foundation that can be specifically fine-tuned for a wide array of downstream applications, encouraging further innovation and specialized development. This open-source release, consistent with the Gemma philosophy, promotes transparency and reproducibility in VLM research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations behind the development of PaliGemma are multifaceted:

1. **Provide a Versatile Base VLM:** The primary goal is to create a broadly knowledgeable and effective base VLM that excels at *transfer learning*. This means the model is designed to be easily adaptable and fine-tunable for a wide variety of downstream tasks, rather than being optimized solely for zero-shot performance out-of-the-box (though it demonstrates strong zero-shot capabilities in some cases).
2. **Achieve Strong Performance with Efficiency:** A key motivation is to demonstrate that state-of-the-art results on a diverse set of open-world vision-language tasks can be achieved with a relatively *small* model. At sub-3 billion parameters (400M vision encoder + 2B language model), PaliGemma continues the efficiency trend set by PaLI-3, aiming to match or surpass models that are orders of magnitude larger. This addresses the increasing computational costs and resource demands associated with large AI models, making VLM capabilities more accessible.
3. **Broad Task Coverage:** The authors aim for PaliGemma to perform well on a comprehensive range of tasks. This includes standard VLM benchmarks like COCO captions, VQAv2, and InfographicVQA, but critically extends to more specialized domains such as remote-sensing VQA, TallyVQA (counting), video captioning/QA, and referring expression segmentation. This broad evaluation demonstrates the model's generalist capabilities and adaptability.
4. **Open-Source Contribution:** As an "open model," PaliGemma is motivated by a desire to contribute to the open-source AI ecosystem. By releasing the base model, the authors hope to facilitate further research in areas like instruction tuning, developing specialized VLM applications, and promoting a clearer distinction between foundational base models and their instruction-tuned derivatives in academic discourse.
5. **Leverage Best-in-Class Components:** The choice of SigLIP-So400m (for vision) and Gemma-2B (for language) is motivated by their established state-of-the-art performance within their respective unimodal domains and their "shape-optimized" efficiency, providing a strong foundation for multimodal integration.

In essence, PaliGemma seeks to be a highly performant, computationally efficient, and openly accessible VLM that serves as a robust foundation for transfer learning across an extensive range of visual and language understanding tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PaliGemma's methodology is characterized by its modular architecture, a multi-stage training paradigm adapted from previous PaLI models, and a strong emphasis on empirical validation through extensive ablations.

#### 3.1. Architecture

PaliGemma employs a "vision encoder-decoder language model" architecture, despite its underlying Gemma component being decoder-only. The system processes visual and textual inputs to autoregressively generate text outputs.
- **Image Encoder:** A publicly available **SigLIP (ViT-So400m)** checkpoint is used. This model was contrastively pretrained at a large scale using a sigmoid loss, known for its state-of-the-art performance relative to its small size. It converts an image into a sequence of $N_{img}$ tokens.
- **Language Model:** The publicly available **Gemma-2B v1.0** pretrained checkpoint is utilized. This decoder-only LLM offers a balance between performance and size. It processes text inputs tokenized by Gemma's SentencePiece tokenizer.
- **Linear Projection Layer:** A simple linear layer connects the SigLIP output tokens to Gemma-2B's embedding space, allowing their concatenation. Ablations confirmed that more complex MLPs did not offer a significant advantage, favoring simplicity.
- **Input Sequence & Attention:** The input sequence fed to the Gemma decoder is structured as `[image tokens..., BOS, prefix tokens..., SEP, suffix tokens..., EOS, PAD...]`. Images are resized to fixed square sizes (224, 448, or 896 pixels), resulting in a fixed number of image tokens. A key architectural choice is the **prefix-LM masking**: full (unmasked, bidirectional) attention is allowed on the image and prefix tokens, enabling image tokens to "look ahead" at the task query. The suffix (the generated output) is covered by an autoregressive mask. The next-token-prediction loss is applied *only* on the suffix.

#### 3.2. Training Stages

The training of PaliGemma follows a multi-stage procedure, a hallmark of the PaLI series, with specific modifications:

- **Stage 0: Unimodal Pretraining:** This stage leverages existing, independently pretrained components. The authors do not perform custom unimodal pretraining but rely on the strong foundational capabilities of the publicly available SigLIP and Gemma checkpoints. This significantly reduces the training burden and benefits from well-established unimodal training recipes.
- **Stage 1: Multimodal Pretraining:**
 - The combined SigLIP and Gemma models are trained on a broad mixture of large-scale vision-language tasks.
 - **Crucial Departure from Common Practice:** Unlike many VLMs that freeze the image encoder, PaliGemma's image encoder (SigLIP) is *not* frozen during this stage. This is motivated by recent findings that multimodal tuning can enhance the image encoder's spatial and relational understanding. To prevent degradation from initial misalignment, a slow linear warm-up is applied to the image encoder's learning rate.
 - The goal is to imbue the model with a broad range of "skills" for transferability, rather than out-of-the-box user-friendliness.
 - Pretraining tasks include diverse `caption {lang}`, `ocr`, `answer en {question}` (VQA), `question {lang} {English answer}` (VQG), `detect {thing}`, `segment {thing}`, and `caption <bbox>` (grounded captioning). Notably, no instruction-following data generated by larger commercial VLMs (like GPT-4) is used in pretraining.
 - Training involves 1 billion examples at 224px resolution and a text sequence length of 128 tokens.
 - A critical step is the detection and removal of near-duplicates of images from evaluation transfer tasks in the pretraining datasets, ensuring a fair assessment of transfer capabilities.
- **Stage 2: Resolution Increase:**
 - The model from Stage 1 is further pretrained at higher resolutions (448px, then 896px). This short continuation stage (50M and 10M examples, respectively) extends the model's ability to parse higher-resolution images, crucial for tasks involving small objects or fine text.
 - The task mixture remains the same as Stage 1, but tasks benefiting from high resolution (e.g., OCR, detection, segmentation) are significantly up-weighted. Text sequence length is also increased to 512 tokens.
- **Stage 3: Transfer:**
 - The pre-trained base models (available at 224px, 448px, and 896px) are fine-tuned on individual academic benchmarks.
 - A simple, unified transfer recipe is used, with a few tunable hyperparameters (e.g., epochs, learning rate, label smoothing, dropout). All model parameters are fine-tuned.
 - The effectiveness for multitasking is also demonstrated by fine-tuning on a mixed subset of tasks simultaneously.

#### 3.3. Training Details

The model was trained using Google's `big_vision` codebase on Cloud TPUv5e, leveraging JAX and GSPMD for efficient distributed training (fully-sharded data-parallel, FSDP). Training maintains parameters in float32 for stability but supports bfloat16 for inference. Image preprocessing details are randomized to prevent model brittleness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PaliGemma's evaluation on almost 40 diverse tasks, none of which were part of its pretraining data, yielded several key findings:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance in a Small Package:** PaliGemma (sub-3B parameters) achieves strong performance across a wide array of VLM benchmarks, often matching or surpassing the results of much larger models (e.g., PaLI-X, PaLM-E, PaLI-3) on comparable tasks. This is a central validation of the paper's efficiency thesis.
- **Versatility Across Domains:** The model demonstrates robust performance on standard VLM tasks (e.g., COCOcap, VQAv2, GQA) and also extends effectively to specialized domains like Remote-Sensing VQA, TallyVQA (counting), various video tasks (ActivityNet, MSRVTT, MSVD), and referring expression segmentation (RefCOCO family).
- **Impact of Multimodal Pretraining Duration:** Ablations show that longer multimodal pretraining (1 billion examples in Stage 1) is significantly beneficial. Skipping Stage 1 entirely or using much shorter durations severely impacts downstream performance, emphasizing the importance of extensive general multimodal pretraining.
- **Optimal Masking and Learning Objective:** The prefix-LM strategy (full attention on image and prefix, autoregressive on suffix) with loss applied *only* on the suffix tokens proved to be the most effective for pretraining. Using task prefixes during pretraining also improves predictability and performance on specific tasks.
- **New Token Initialization:** For newly introduced tokens (e.g., location, segmentation), a standard small Gaussian noise initialization (𝜎=0.02) performs better for downstream transfer tasks, despite initially lower perplexity from matching average embeddings.
- **The Advantage of Unfrozen Components:** Contrary to common practice, not freezing the image encoder or the language model during multimodal pretraining (Stage 1) is found to be advantageous, particularly improving spatial understanding and overall performance. Leveraging strong unimodal pretraining (Stage 0) is, however, crucial.
- **Simple Connector Suffices:** A simple linear projection layer to connect the vision encoder to the language model performs as well as, or even slightly better than, more complex MLP connectors, promoting architectural simplicity.
- **Value of a Dedicated Image Encoder:** While a "Fuyu-style" architecture (passing raw image patches directly to the LLM without a dedicated image encoder) is feasible, it is significantly less sample-efficient and yields lower performance at the explored training scales, underscoring the benefits of leveraging a pre-trained vision encoder like SigLIP.
- **Resolution and Checkpoint Strategy:**
 - Increasing image resolution (from 224px to 448px/896px) significantly boosts performance on tasks requiring fine-grained visual details (e.g., OCR-VQA, DocVQA, segmentation).
 - This performance gain is attributed roughly equally to increased information content in the image and the increased model capacity provided by longer sequence lengths at higher resolutions.
 - The research justifies providing **separate resolution-specific checkpoints** (224px, 448px, 896px), as models explicitly trained at higher resolutions (via Stage 2) significantly outperform models transferred to higher resolutions without dedicated pretraining or basic "windowing" approaches.
 - Simple "resize to square" image preprocessing, when combined with appropriate regularization (label smoothing, dropout), works as well as more complex aspect-ratio-preserving augmentations for segmentation tasks.
- **Robust Transferability:**
 - **High Repeatability:** Transfer fine-tuning results show very low standard deviation across multiple runs, indicating high reliability.
 - **Hyperparameter Robustness:** A single, simple set of recommended hyperparameters can achieve near-optimal performance for most tasks, reducing the burden of extensive hyperparameter search for practical applications.
 - **Data Efficiency in Transfer:** Many tasks can achieve substantial performance gains with surprisingly few transfer examples (e.g., within 10% of full-data performance with 4096 examples, and good prototyping scores with just 64 examples).
- **Noteworthy Tidbits:** The paper also introduced `CountBenchQA` as a more robust counting benchmark, highlighted issues in existing `WidgetCaps` evaluations by other works, and demonstrated that image annotations (e.g., drawing a bounding box) are as effective as tokenized coordinates in prompts for grounding tasks. PaliGemma also showed strong zero-shot generalization to 3D renders from Objaverse and achieved state-of-the-art paired accuracy on the MMVP benchmark (47.3% vs. GPT-4V at 38.7%).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PaliGemma makes several significant contributions and has the potential for considerable impact on the VLM research and application landscape:

1. **Democratization and Accessibility of VLMs:** By releasing an open-source VLM that achieves state-of-the-art performance at a compact size (sub-3B parameters), PaliGemma substantially lowers the barrier to entry for VLM research and development. Researchers and developers with limited computational resources can now leverage a powerful foundation model, fostering broader participation and innovation in the field. This is a direct counter-narrative to the trend of increasingly large, proprietary models.
2. **Efficiency and Resource Optimization:** PaliGemma demonstrates conclusively that achieving top-tier VLM performance does not strictly require models with hundreds of billions of parameters. Its success with a 3B parameter count encourages a focus on more efficient architectures, refined pretraining strategies, and effective transfer learning. This shift towards efficiency is crucial for sustainable AI development, reducing energy consumption and hardware requirements.
3. **Robust Base Model for Transfer Learning:** The paper establishes PaliGemma as a highly versatile "base model" specifically designed for transfer. This paradigm, where a generalist model acquires a broad set of skills during pretraining and is then fine-tuned for specific tasks, is highly effective. It provides a strong starting point for numerous specialized applications without the need for extensive training from scratch, accelerating deployment and customization.
4. **Guidance for Future VLM Design:** The extensive ablation studies presented in the paper offer invaluable insights into what truly matters in VLM training. Findings regarding the benefit of unfrozen vision encoders, the efficacy of simple linear connectors, the importance of long multimodal pretraining, and the nuances of handling image resolution provide empirical guidance for future VLM architecture design and training methodologies. This contributes to the scientific understanding of multimodal learning.
5. **Refinement of Evaluation Practices:** The introduction of CountBenchQA and the identification of issues in other benchmarks (like WidgetCaps) highlight the paper's commitment to rigorous and reliable evaluation. This helps to mature the VLM evaluation landscape, ensuring more accurate comparisons and progress.
6. **Broad Applicability and Real-World Impact:** PaliGemma's demonstrated versatility across a wide range of tasks—from general image captioning and Q&A to specialized tasks like remote sensing, document analysis, and referring expression segmentation—suggests immense practical utility. It could serve as a backbone for applications in diverse fields, including visual search engines, assistive technologies for visually impaired individuals, content moderation, scientific discovery, and automated data analysis for various industries.

In conclusion, PaliGemma represents a significant step forward in the development of accessible, efficient, and highly capable Vision-Language Models. Its open release, coupled with detailed insights into its design and performance, positions it as a foundational contribution that will undoubtedly stimulate and accelerate future research and application development in multimodal AI.
