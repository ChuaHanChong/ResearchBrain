# ERGeoBench:A Comprehensive Benchmark for Embodied Reasoning and Geo-localization in Multimodal Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> ERGeoBench introduces a comprehensive benchmark for evaluating multimodal large language models (MLLMs) in embodied geo-localization, enabling active environmental exploration via camera controls. The framework diagnoses MLLM capabilities across foundational perception, spatial awareness, and commonsense reasoning, finding that top proprietary models can leverage active exploration to improve localization while all MLLMs struggle with fine-grained metric precision.

## Problem

- Worldwide image geo-localization has traditionally been treated as a static recognition problem, limiting models to passive observation without interaction or evidence refinement.
- Existing geo-localization benchmarks do not reflect human-like active observation, sequential evidence integration, and spatial reasoning for ambiguity resolution.
- There is a recognized lack of fine-grained evaluation to diagnose MLLMs' underlying capabilities in foundational perception, spatial awareness, and commonsense reasoning for embodied geo-localization.

## Method

- Developed **ERGeoBench**, a diagnostic benchmark that integrates real-world panoramas into an embodied simulation, allowing MLLM agents to actively acquire views through controllable yaw, pitch, and zoom actions.
- Implemented three visual information conditions: Single-view (static baseline), Panorama-view (passive omniscient view), and Embodied-view (active exploration with kinematic constraints).
- Introduced the Geo-localization Score (GLS) as a unified metric, combining semantic alignment, metric precision across distance thresholds, and log-normalized error penalization, alongside a capability-oriented assessment across four dimensions.

## Results

- Proprietary MLLMs consistently achieved Geo-localization Scores (GLS) above **60.0**, outperforming open-source models and showing a performance gap in knowledge-intensive global geo-localization.
- Top models like Gemini-3-Flash showed an increase in Acc.@1km in the Embodied setting compared to Panorama, demonstrating that sequential evidence accumulation can resolve street-level ambiguity.
- While proprietary models achieved average foundational perception scores above **70.0**, their performance for spatial awareness tasks dropped significantly (e.g., GPT-4o from **82.78%** to **43.86%**), with precise geometric inference remaining a major challenge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intelligent viewpoint selection in the embodied setting allows top-performing MLLMs to effectively gather salient visual cues, approaching or even exceeding performance achieved with an omniscient panorama view.
> - Current MLLMs demonstrate robust foundational visual perception but show a significant performance drop when transitioning to spatial awareness tasks, particularly in maintaining a consistent 3D mental map across viewpoint changes.
> - MLLMs are capable of high-level semantic geo-inference (e.g., country-level accuracy) but struggle with fine-grained metric localization, exhibiting heavy-tailed geospatial prediction errors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper addresses a specific gap within the broader fields of visual geo-localization and multimodal large language models (MLLMs). Worldwide image geo-localization, which involves inferring the geographic location of an image captured anywhere globally, has traditionally been approached as a static recognition problem. Methods in this area typically rely on retrieving matches from geo-tagged databases or classifying images into predefined regions. While MLLMs have recently advanced image understanding and cross-modal reasoning, leading to improved geo-localization capabilities, most existing approaches still operate under a passive observation paradigm. This means geographic attributes are inferred from a single image or a pre-captured panorama without allowing for interaction or dynamic evidence refinement.

This static approach contrasts with human localization behavior, where individuals actively observe their surroundings, select informative viewpoints, integrate sequential evidence, and reason about spatial layouts and commonsense context to resolve ambiguity. Prior geo-localization benchmarks, such as IMAGEO-Bench, GAEA-Bench, GRE Suite, and GLOBE, primarily focus on passive, single-view inference, evaluating MLLMs' ability to extract cues from fixed inputs.

ERGeoBench positions itself as a development that introduces an embodied and interactive dimension to geo-localization evaluation. It moves beyond static passive observation by enabling MLLMs to act as embodied agents capable of actively exploring an environment through controllable camera actions (yaw, pitch, zoom). This shift allows for the evaluation of capabilities related to active evidence acquisition, cross-view memory, and hypothesis refinement, which were underexplored in previous benchmarks. The paper thus contributes to the evolving research landscape by providing a framework for diagnosing and advancing MLLMs towards more human-like, location-aware embodied intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to evaluate whether Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) can perform global geo-localization through embodied and interactive visual reasoning, thereby better reflecting human localization behavior. This overarching goal is driven by several motivations:

First, existing geo-localization methods largely treat the task as a static recognition problem, relying on fixed visual inputs such as a single image or a pre-captured panoramic observation. This paradigm limits models to inferring geographic attributes from available cues without mechanisms for interaction or evidence refinement. The authors argue that such a static approach struggles with ambiguity in visually complex scenes.

Second, human localization in the absence of external positioning systems is an inherently sequential process. Humans actively observe their surroundings, choose informative viewpoints, integrate evidence over time, and reason about spatial layouts and commonsense context. Actions like turning, zooming, and revisiting landmarks are employed to reduce uncertainty. This active, information-gathering behavior is consistent with cognitive science theories of active vision and spatial memory. The research aims to bridge the discrepancy between the static nature of current MLLM geo-localization and this dynamic human approach.

Third, there is a recognized lack of fine-grained evaluation for embodied geo-localization within MLLMs. The authors identify a need for a benchmark that not only provides realistic environments but also enables diagnostic assessment beyond coarse accuracy metrics. This includes understanding model capabilities in foundational perception, spatial awareness, commonsense reasoning, and geo-localization reasoning, and how these capabilities integrate to support accurate localization.

To address these motivations, the paper outlines several key objectives:

- **Introduce ERGeoBench:** Develop a diagnostic benchmark that integrates real-world panoramas into an embodied simulation, allowing agents to actively acquire views through yaw, pitch, and zoom actions. This establishes geo-localization as a progressive reasoning process.
- **Establish Progressive Task Settings:** Evaluate models under three progressively rich perception and action settings: single-view (static baseline), panorama-view (passive omniscient view), and embodied-view (active exploration).
- **Implement Capability-Oriented Evaluation:** Provide a fine-grained evaluation across four dimensions: foundational perception, spatial awareness, commonsense reasoning, and geo-localization reasoning. This design supports a deeper diagnosis of model strengths and weaknesses.
- **Diagnose MLLM Performance:** Conduct large-scale evaluations of proprietary and open-source MLLMs to understand their current capabilities and limitations in embodied geo-localization, particularly concerning metric localization, spatial consistency across views, and the integration of different reasoning types.
- **Provide a Unified Framework:** Offer a standardized platform for future research to advance human-like embodied geo-localization.

The ultimate motivation is to inspire and guide the development of MLLMs towards creating more intelligent, location-aware embodied agents that can interact with and understand the physical world in a manner analogous to humans.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The ERGeoBench framework is designed to evaluate MLLMs as embodied agents in vision-driven geo-localization tasks. The methodology encompasses problem formulation, visual information conditions, evaluation metrics, dataset construction, and capability-oriented assessment.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem of Embodied Reasoning Geo-localization is formulated as a sequential decision-making process. Given a dataset of scenarios, each represented by a high-resolution 360-degree equirectangular panorama ($P_i$), and a natural language query ($Q_i$), the MLLM agent must generate a response ($\hat{a}_i$) matching the ground-truth answer ($A_i$). This generation is based on an observation sequence ($\Omega_i$) that is actively acquired.

The agent interacts with the environment through a controllable action space defined by yaw ($\psi$), pitch ($\phi$), and zoom ($\zeta$), forming a viewpoint $v = (\psi, \phi, \zeta)$. At each step $t$, the visual observation ($I_{v_t}$) is rendered via perspective projection from the panorama, with the field-of-view (FOV) inversely determined by the zoom level. The agent operates sequentially, deciding whether to output a final geo-localization answer or select a new viewpoint to gather additional evidence, based on the interaction history ($H_t$) and the query.

#### 3.2. Visual Information Conditions

ERGeoBench implements three distinct visual information conditions to assess varying levels of agency:

- **Single-view:** This serves as a static baseline, restricting observations to a fixed 90-degree FOV street-level perspective (Pitch ≈ 0°). The agent cannot alter its view.
- **Panorama-view:** This condition provides the full equirectangular projection of the scene, representing a passive information upper bound with maximal visual evidence immediately available. The agent still cannot actively change its viewpoint beyond processing the full panorama.
- **Embodied-view:** This is the core interactive setting. Agents can actively explore the panorama using a continuous action space for yaw, pitch, and zoom. Kinematic constraints are applied (e.g., yaw changes ≥ 45°, pitch bounded to [-60°, 60°], zoom levels 1x to 5x), forcing agents to make deliberate choices in trading off broad context for fine details. This setting measures active evidence selection, cross-view memory, and action-conditioned hypothesis revision under partial observability.

#### 3.3. Evaluation Metrics

A hybrid evaluation strategy is employed, combining semantic classification accuracy and metric localization precision. The **Geo-localization Score (GLS)**, ranging from 0 to 100, is introduced as a unified ranking index. It is the arithmetic mean of three components:

- **Semantic Alignment Score (S_sem):** Evaluates correct prediction of hierarchical geographic labels (street, city, country). It is the average accuracy across these three levels.
- **Metric Precision Score (S_met):** Assesses physical proximity using the Haversine formula to calculate geodesic distance between ground-truth and predicted GPS coordinates. It is the average "hit rate" across five distance thresholds: 1km, 25km, 200km, 750km, and 2500km.
- **Error Penalization Score (S_err):** Measures the magnitude of deviations using the Median Distance Error (MdDE) for robustness against outliers. This unbounded error is log-normalized to a scale, penalizing short-range deviations more strictly.

#### 3.4. Data Collection Pipeline

The dataset construction involves three stages:

- **Global Sampling & Filtering:** Candidate locations are sourced from the top 1,000 populated cities globally, excluding restricted Street View areas. A 5km radius around city centers is prioritized, and OpenStreetMap (OSM) data is used. A weighted scoring mechanism prioritizes roads closer to city centers and major arterial roads, ensuring diversity and information density.
- **High-Fidelity Projection:** A precise mapping model translates spherical coordinates to perspective FOV images. Standard views are rendered with a fixed focal length to simulate human visual observation and minimize distortion.
- **Dataset Size:** The benchmark comprises 2,207 globally distributed street-view panoramas.

#### 3.5. Capability-Oriented Evaluation

ERGeoBench assesses models across four hierarchical capabilities:

- **Geo-localization Reasoning (GR):** The integrative capability requiring synthesis of perception, spatial awareness, and commonsense to infer geographic attributes and GPS coordinates.
- **Foundational Perception (FP):** Evaluates fine-grained visual recognition across a 5-Slot Framework: Architecture, Signage, Vegetation, Terrain, and Infrastructure. Tasks involve Multiple Choice and True/False questions tied to specific FOVs. Quality control includes Target View Declaration, independent VLM checking, and manual verification.
- **Spatial Awareness (SA):** Assesses egocentric geometric reasoning through 7 sub-capabilities: Yaw Estimation, 2D Positioning, Depth Estimation, Distance, Left/Right Relative, Front/Back Relative, and Cross-view Reasoning. The Cross-view task specifically tests the ability to maintain a consistent 3D mental map across viewpoint changes.
- **Common Sense Reasoning (CS):** Focuses on Functional Affordance Alignment, where models identify Points of Interest (POIs) that satisfy abstract user needs (e.g., "I am thirsty" → Convenience Store). Tasks vary in complexity from Single-View to Dual-View and Cross-View accessibility comparisons, simulating pedestrian decisions based on visual evidence.

#### 3.6. Experimental Setups

Nine MLLMs (five proprietary: GPT-4o, Gemini-2.0-Flash, Gemini-2.5-Pro, Gemini-3-Flash, Qwen-VL-Max; and four open-source: Qwen2.5-VL-7B, Qwen3-VL-8B, InternVL2.5-8B, InternVL3-8B) were benchmarked. All models used a temperature of 0.1, a maximum generation length of 4,096 tokens, and a unified structured prompt with a JSON schema for deterministic and complete responses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of MLLMs on ERGeoBench yielded several findings regarding their capabilities in embodied geo-localization.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance and Model Comparison

A performance gap was observed between proprietary and open-source MLLMs. Proprietary models, particularly from the Gemini series and GPT-4o, consistently achieved Geo-localization Scores (GLS) above 60.0, indicating a higher overall capability. Open-source models generally exhibited lower GLS scores. This suggests that global geo-localization is a knowledge-intensive task benefiting from extensive pretraining, robust visual perception, and strong instruction-following abilities. Gemini-2.5-Pro demonstrated the highest overall GLS across the three settings, while Gemini-3-Flash excelled in several fine-grained distance-threshold metrics.

#### 4.2. Impact of Active Exploration

Comparing performance across the three task settings—Single-view, Panorama-view, and Embodied-view—revealed the efficacy of active exploration. The Panorama-view setting, providing an omniscient field-of-view, consistently resulted in higher performance than the restricted Single-view baseline, affirming its role as an information upper bound. Notably, top-performing models, such as Gemini-3-Flash, achieved GLS scores in the Embodied setting that approached or exceeded their Panorama performance. This indicates that intelligent viewpoint selection can enable agents to effectively gather salient visual cues, compensating for initially restricted observations. The improvement was particularly evident at fine-grained localization thresholds, with strong proprietary models showing an increase in Acc.@1km in the Embodied setting compared to Panorama, suggesting that sequential evidence accumulation can resolve street-level ambiguity. Conversely, weaker models often experienced performance degradation in the Embodied setting, which the authors attribute to disorientation from ineffective navigation actions.

#### 4.3. Semantic Understanding Versus Metric Precision

Analysis of capability dimensions exposed a divergence between high-level semantic reasoning and fine-grained metric localization. While leading models achieved high accuracy in hierarchical classification (e.g., country-level inference), their performance decreased sharply at fine-grained distance thresholds (e.g., 1km accuracy). This indicates that MLLMs are capable of regional inference but struggle with precise coordinate regression. The substantial discrepancy between average and median errors across models further confirmed the prevalence of heavy-tailed geospatial prediction errors, including antipodal failures, which supported the design of the log-normalized GLS metric for robust assessment.

#### 4.4. Fine-grained Capability Analysis

Diagnostic evaluation across Foundational Perception, Spatial Awareness, and Common Sense capabilities provided further insights:

- **Foundational Perception:** Current MLLMs generally exhibited robust visual recognition. Most proprietary models achieved average scores above 70.0, with GPT-4o performing well in complex categories like Infrastructure (85.70%) and Vegetation (80.46%). Open-source models also showed competitive performance, such as InternVL3 in Terrain classification (94.03%). These results suggest that identifying "what is in the image" in high-quality street views is largely a solved problem.
- **Spatial Awareness:** A significant performance drop was observed when transitioning from perception to spatial reasoning. Average scores for Spatial Awareness were consistently lower than for Foundational Perception (e.g., GPT-4o dropped from 82.78% to 43.86%). Tasks requiring precise geometric inference, such as 2D Positioning and Yaw Estimation, proved challenging, with many models scoring below 20.0%. Gemini-2.0-Flash was an outlier, achieving the highest average spatial score (52.55%), suggesting that some efficient architectures might retain superior geometric grounding capabilities. The Cross-view Reasoning task was particularly difficult, indicating a bottleneck in maintaining a consistent 3D mental map across viewpoint changes.
- **Commonsense and Functional Reasoning:** In the Common Sense dimension, models generally performed well in Single-View identification (e.g., Gemini-2.0-Flash at 96.31%). However, performance degraded in Dual-View and Cross-View settings, which require spatial consistency and integration of evidence across views. This reinforced the finding that maintaining semantic consistency during viewpoint changes remains a challenge for embodied agents.

#### 4.5. Additional Analyses

Supplementary experiments addressed potential biases and robustness:

- **Bias Checking:** Evaluation of GPT-5 and o1 (other OpenAI models) showed them outperforming GPT-4o in geo-localization and capability dimensions, suggesting that GPT-4o's involvement in candidate annotation did not create a specific advantage for itself.
- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Stress Test:** A small OOD set of wilderness scenes resulted in substantial GLS drops for all tested models, indicating that geo-localization in environments with sparse visual cues remains a significant challenge.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ERGeoBench contributes to the field of multimodal large language models and embodied AI by providing a diagnostic and comprehensive evaluation framework for embodied geo-localization. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

First, **Advancement of Evaluation Methodologies**: The benchmark shifts the paradigm from static, passive geo-localization evaluation to an embodied, active, and interactive approach. By simulating human-like evidence acquisition through controllable camera actions (yaw, pitch, zoom), ERGeoBench offers a more ecologically valid assessment of MLLMs' capabilities in real-world environments. This moves beyond simple accuracy metrics to diagnose the underlying reasoning processes.

Second, **Identification of Key Challenges**: The research identifies specific areas where current MLLMs exhibit limitations. These include struggles with fine-grained metric localization, difficulties in maintaining spatial consistency across actively selected views, and challenges in precise geometric inference tasks within spatial awareness. These identified bottlenecks provide concrete directions for future research and development efforts aimed at improving MLLM capabilities.

Third, **Guidance for MLLM Development**: By explicitly decoupling evaluation into four capability dimensions—foundational perception, spatial awareness, commonsense reasoning, and geo-localization reasoning—ERGeoBench offers a detailed diagnostic tool. This can guide researchers and developers in designing and training MLLMs that are not only capable of visual recognition but also possess integrated spatial understanding and commonsense inference crucial for effective embodied agents. The observation that accurate localization depends on integrated perception and reasoning rather than isolated pattern matching highlights the need for holistic model development.

Fourth, **Foundation for Location-Aware Embodied Agents**: The benchmark provides a standardized platform for developing and testing MLLMs as embodied agents that can actively explore, learn from, and navigate complex environments. This is a foundational step towards building more sophisticated AI systems capable of tasks requiring contextual understanding, navigation, and interaction in the physical world.

Finally, **Ethical Considerations and Future Work**: The authors acknowledge the limitations of the current simulation, such as the absence of translational movement, obstacle-aware navigation, or dynamic scene interaction. This recognition provides a roadmap for future research, including incorporating continuous video streams, dynamic scenes, and real-world test suites. Furthermore, the paper addresses ethical considerations by highlighting privacy and misuse concerns associated with geo-localization technologies, particularly the potential for inferring sensitive personal locations. This proactive discussion encourages the integration of privacy-preserving evaluation and safeguards into future research and development, promoting responsible innovation in this domain.
