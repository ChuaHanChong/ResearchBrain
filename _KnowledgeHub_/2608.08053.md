# PhysX-CoT: Structured Physical Reasoning from a Single Image to Simulation-Ready 3D Assets

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysX-CoT turns single-image, simulation-ready 3D asset generation into an explicit structured physical reasoning process: a vision-language model emits an ordered, machine-parseable trajectory of part-level states (decomposition, 2D/3D boxes, relations, primitives, surface cues), factorizes geometry so boxes carry placement and local codes carry shape, and optimizes it with CoT-aligned GRPO, beating the closest baseline on geometry, scale, and physical attributes.

## Problem

- Prior single-image 3D generators optimize visual plausibility, leaving metric scale, part hierarchy, materials, collision geometry, and articulation that physics engines require unresolved or deferred.
- Existing simulation-ready generators predict physical outputs but keep image-to-part reasoning implicit, so intermediate grounding and relational states are never supervised, conditioned on, or verified.
- Serializing geometry in one global object frame couples where a part sits with what shape it takes, so position drift fractures or collapses thin, topologically complex parts.

## Method

- **PhysX-CoT**'s ==Structured Physical Chain-of-Thought==: an ordered parseable state trajectory of part count, semantics, 2D/3D boxes, inter-part relations, coarse primitives, and surface cues, each an SFT target, geometry condition, and reward target.
- ==Position-Factorized Local Geometry==: an axis-aligned 3D box in a 32-cubed voxel grid carries placement while ==run-length-encoded== local occupancy carries shape, cropped, encoded, then placed back by the box.
- ==CoT-aligned GRPO== over four sampled candidates with ==group-relative advantage==, clipped ratio, ==KL anchor==, and process rewards for localization, primitives, local/global voxel quality, physical validity, plus format penalties.

## Results

- Beats the closest full-task baseline: CD **0.041** vs **0.095**, F-score **0.480** vs **0.256**, PSNR **21.33** vs **19.10**, scale error **6.57%** vs **12.43%**, kinematic **0.73** vs **0.53**.
- Oracle states lift kinematics **0.73** to **0.92** and cut scale error to **4.12**, while shuffling state order drops 3D IoU **0.660** to **0.542** and parse validity **96.4** to **90.9**.
- In Unreal Engine 5 assets reach **94.1%** parse, **96.8%** collision, **92.0%** joint validity; local RLE uses **58%** fewer geometry tokens and runs about **30%** faster.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prompting a model to name reasoning fields changes almost nothing; intermediate states only help when separately supervised, parsed, and rewarded as verifiable objects.
> - Separating placement from local shape is an independently useful factorization: it improves geometry with or without structured reasoning, and shrinks token budgets substantially.
> - The ordering of an autoregressive state chain carries functional information, not just bookkeeping; token-matched unordered or parallel-head variants keeping the same fields degrade.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of three-dimensional (3D) assets from two-dimensional (2D) images represents a significant area of research at the intersection of computer vision, computer graphics, and artificial intelligence. Recent advancements in neural scene representations, such as neural radiance fields (NeRFs) and 3D Gaussian splatting, alongside generative models leveraging 2D diffusion priors, have enabled the creation of visually plausible 3D geometry from single images. These methods often focus on aesthetic fidelity and rendering quality, rapidly producing novel views or textured shapes.

However, a notable gap exists between visually plausible 3D content and physically usable 3D assets required for interactive virtual worlds, robotics, and embodied artificial intelligence. Existing approaches frequently overlook critical physical properties such as metric scale, part hierarchy, material properties, collision geometry, and kinematic articulation. An asset that appears correct visually may behave incorrectly or destabilize under physical simulation, which can invalidate training rollouts in robotic manipulation or policy learning scenarios.

Prior research has attempted to bridge this gap. Articulated modeling methods, such as those relying on datasets like SAPIEN, have focused on generating part-aware structures and kinematic annotations, sometimes using structured intermediates like part-connectivity graphs. More recently, methods like PhysX-3D and PhysX-Anything have directly aimed at generating physics-grounded 3D assets, incorporating scale, material, affordance, and kinematics from single images using Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

PhysX-CoT builds upon these advancements but critically addresses a limitation observed in previous VLM-based approaches, particularly PhysX-Anything. These systems tend to predict physical outputs without explicitly exposing or supervising the intermediate reasoning process that links visual evidence to the physical properties of each part. This "output-centric view" leads to two main problems:
1. **Where-Shape Coupling:** Geometry is often encoded in a shared, object-level coordinate frame, entangling part placement and local shape. This can lead to issues where prediction errors in position cause incorrect shape decoding or asset fragmentation in simulation.
2. **Implicit Intermediate States:** Human-like reasoning steps, such as decomposing an object into parts, grounding them in 2D and 3D space, identifying inter-part relations, and determining coarse geometry or surface cues, are typically not represented, supervised, or verifiable within the VLM's internal process.

PhysX-CoT positions itself as a refinement within this landscape by introducing an explicit, structured physical reasoning process. It aims to integrate concepts from process supervision and grounded reasoning by making the VLM's intermediate states machine-parseable, separately supervised, and optimizable with process-level rewards. This approach draws a connection between explicit reasoning traces, verifiable intermediate steps, and reward-based optimization techniques.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a method capable of generating simulation-ready 3D assets from a single image that are not only visually accurate but also physically usable in virtual environments for applications such as robotics and embodied AI.

The motivation for PhysX-CoT stems from several limitations identified in the current state of single-image to 3D asset generation:

1. **Bridging the Visual-Physical Gap:** Current 3D generative models often prioritize visual plausibility over physical accuracy. While high-fidelity reconstruction and rendering are achievable, the resulting assets frequently lack the metric scale, correct part hierarchy, physical material properties, collision geometry, and articulation necessary for realistic interaction within a physics engine. This renders them insufficient for interactive simulations or training embodied agents. The core motivation is to create a scalable bridge from abundant visual data to interactive virtual worlds where assets behave correctly.

2. **Addressing Implicit Reasoning in VLMs:** Existing VLM-based approaches for generating simulation-ready assets predict final physical outputs (geometry, articulation, physical attributes) but typically leave the underlying reasoning process implicit. This lack of transparency makes it challenging to understand how the model derives its predictions, diagnose errors, or systematically improve its physical understanding. The motivation is to expose this reasoning process to enable better supervision, conditioning, and verification.

3. **Mitigating "Where-Shape Coupling":** In output-centric methods, geometry is often represented in a global coordinate frame (e.g., as global voxels or serialized tokens). This design couples a part's placement (its "where") with its local shape, meaning that a single token stream must define both. If the predicted position of a part drifts, its shape can be decoded into the wrong region, leading to fractured, displaced, or unstable assets in simulation, especially for thin or topologically complex parts. A key motivation is to decouple these aspects for more robust geometry generation.

4. **Enabling Supervision and Verification of Intermediate States:** Human reasoning for physical understanding often involves intermediate steps like identifying individual parts, localizing them in 2D and 3D, determining their relationships, and inferring coarse geometric primitives and surface properties. Existing VLM systems generally do not represent, supervise, or verify these grounding and relational states explicitly. The motivation is to integrate these intermediate states as an "explicit structured physical reasoning process" that is machine-parseable and independently supervisable, rather than merely being a cosmetic natural-language rationale.

To address these motivations, PhysX-CoT sets forth the following key objectives:
- To recast single-image simulation-ready 3D generation as an explicit, ordered, and machine-parseable trajectory of part-level physical states, referred to as a Structured Physical Chain-of-Thought (CoT).
- To implement a position-factorized local geometry representation where a 3D bounding box specifies part placement ("where") and a local-frame code describes its shape, allowing for separate supervision and rewards.
- To develop a CoT-aligned Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework that optimizes for parse validity, grounding accuracy, local geometry, placement accuracy, and overall physical consistency through process-level rewards.
- To conduct rigorous evaluations, including oracle controls and state-order perturbations, to demonstrate that the exposed and ordered intermediate states are functionally utilized by the model, rather than being mere post-hoc rationalizations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysX-CoT approaches single-image, simulation-ready 3D asset generation by re-conceptualizing the Vision-Language Model (VLM) stage. It integrates an explicit structured reasoning process, factorizes geometry representation, and employs a specialized training objective, while maintaining compatibility with a pre-existing, frozen downstream physical decoder.

The process begins with an RGB input image *I* and an optional prompt *q*. The VLM's output, *Y*, comprises a Structured Physical Chain-of-Thought (*S*), an object-level physical description (*P*), and a set of local coarse-geometry sequences {*Gk*} for *K* individual parts. This *Y*, along with the input image *I*, is then passed to a physical decoder *Dphys* (reused from PhysX-Anything) to generate the final simulation-ready asset *A* (e.g., mesh/URDF).

#### 3.1. 1+K Round Generation Decomposition

The asset generation process is divided into a multi-round decomposition:
1. **Round 1 (Global Reasoning):** The VLM first predicts the overall Structured Physical CoT (*S*) and the object-level physical description (*P*).
2. **Rounds 2 to K (Part-Specific Geometry):** For each of the *K* parts identified in *S*, a part-specific prompt *qk* (constructed using information from *S*) is used to guide the VLM in generating its corresponding local coarse-geometry sequence *Gk*. This sequential generation ensures that the part geometry is conditioned on the previously reasoned physical structure.

#### 3.2. Structured Physical CoT Schema (S)

The core of PhysX-CoT is the Structured Physical CoT, an ordered, machine-parseable sequence of part-level states. This schema is defined as *S* = (*K*, *C*, {*sk*}), where:
- *K* denotes the total number of parts.
- *C* represents the semantic categories of the parts.
- {*sk*} is a sequence of states for each individual part *k*. Each part state *sk* is a tuple comprising:
 - *B2Dk*: A 2D bounding box for part *k*.
 - *B3Dk*: An axis-aligned 3D bounding box for part *k*. This carries the spatial placement information.
 - *Rk*: Inter-part relations, defining how part *k* connects to or interacts with other parts.
 - *Hk*: A coarse-geometry prior for part *k*, such as a primitive type (cuboid, cylinder, sphere).
 - *Mk*: Surface cues for part *k*, including properties like hardness, roughness, reflectivity, and transparency.
The object-level physical description *P* complements *S* by providing global information such as absolute scale, object category, material density, joint grouping, and overall object function.
These structured states serve multiple purposes during training: they are targets for supervision (SFT), conditions for generating local geometry, and reward targets during policy optimization.

#### 3.3. Position-Factorized Local Geometry

To address the "where–shape coupling" problem, PhysX-CoT introduces a position-factorized local geometry representation. For each part *k*:
- **Placement ("Where"):** The *B3Dk* (3D bounding box) explicitly carries the placement information within a 32x32x32 object voxel grid.
- **Shape ("Shape"):** The *Gk* token sequence describes the local shape *relative* to its *B3Dk*. Specifically, voxels occupied by the part within its *B3Dk* are mapped to local linear indices, which are then Run-Length Encoded (RLE) into *Gk*. This local encoding scheme adapts to each part's specific dimensions, avoiding resampling to a common cube and making the geometry tokens more compact (approximately 58% fewer tokens than global encoding).
This factorization allows placement and local shape to be supervised and rewarded independently, enhancing robustness. During reconstruction, the RLE-encoded *Gk* is decoded and then placed back into the global scene using the coordinates provided by *B3Dk*. Overlapping boxes are resolved at the coarse-voxel stage.

#### 3.4. CoT-Aligned Training

The training process involves two stages:
1. **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** The VLM (built on Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) is initially fine-tuned to learn the mapping from the input image to the structured output *Y* = (*S*, *P*, {*Gk*}). The SFT loss minimizes the negative log-likelihood of generating the target token sequence. Ground truth for *B3D* and *G* is derived by cropping and encoding part voxels from annotated data.
2. **Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO):** Starting from the SFT checkpoint, GRPO refines the model's policy, specifically tuning LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) on the LM projections. For each input, *N*=4 candidate outputs are sampled. Each candidate *i* receives a scalar sequence reward *ri*. A group-relative advantage *Ai* is computed as the difference between *ri* and the average reward of all *N* candidates. The policy is updated using a clipped policy-ratio objective, regularized by a KL divergence term to the frozen SFT reference policy.
 The scalar reward *ri* is a crucial component, aggregating multiple process-level terms and a penalty, clipped to [−2, 2]:
 - **Reward Terms (*Rm*):** Score localization accuracy, coarse primitive quality, local and global voxel quality, and physical/joint validity.
 - **Penalty (*P*):** Penalizes violations of format, range, and inter-state dependencies in the generated output, promoting parse validity.
 All rewards are computed using training-split annotations, ensuring that the optimization aligns with ground-truth physical properties.

#### 3.5. Inference and Asset Reconstruction

During inference, the model generates *S* and *P*. A dedicated parser extracts the states and bounding boxes, with mechanisms to reject or resample invalid outputs. Subsequently, each *Gk* is generated, decoded, and spatially positioned using its corresponding *B3Dk*. The complete set of structured outputs and the object-level description are then fed into the frozen physical decoder *Dphys* to reconstruct the final simulation-ready asset, including meshes, physical attributes, and joint structures, often in formats like URDF.

#### 3.6. Dataset

The model is trained on PhysX-CoTA, an augmented version of the PhysXNet dataset. PhysX-CoTA incorporates structured physical CoT content, providing 669,141 single-image samples across 32,047 objects, with object ID-based splits for training and testing. Open-ended fields within the CoT schema are proposed by the VLM and subsequently human-verified.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of PhysX-CoT encompasses both final asset quality metrics and process-level diagnostics, alongside generalization capabilities in simulation environments.

#### 4.1. Main Results on PhysX-CoTA (Table 1)

PhysX-CoT demonstrated enhanced performance across a range of geometry, scale, and physical-attribute metrics when compared against several baselines (URDFormer, Articulate-Anything, PhysXGen, and PhysX-Anything) under a unified protocol (retraining all learned baselines with the same backbone, data, and frozen decoder).
- **Geometry:** PhysX-CoT achieved a PSNR of 21.33 (compared to 19.10 for PhysX-Anything), a Chamfer Distance (CD) of 0.041 (vs. 0.095), and an F-score of 0.480 (vs. 0.256). The reduced CD and increased F-score indicate more accurate and complete geometric reconstruction.
- **Physical Attributes:** The model significantly improved physical realism, reducing absolute scale error to 6.57% (vs. 12.43%). Material similarity scored 17.09 (vs. 14.00), Affordance ranking 17.69 (vs. 15.17), Kinematic accuracy (VLM-judged) 0.73 (vs. 0.53), and Description similarity 21.80 (vs. 18.12). These improvements highlight the model's ability to infer and represent functional and material properties accurately.
- Qualitative comparisons (Figure 3) further illustrate that PhysX-CoT more reliably recovers thin/small parts, absolute scale, and joint structures compared to baselines.

#### 4.2. Simulation and Real-Image Generalization

- **In-the-Wild Generalization (Figure 4):** On real-world photographs without paired ground truth, PhysX-CoT demonstrated an ability to decompose objects into reasonable parts and recover fine structures, scale, and joints, suggesting effective transferability beyond controlled datasets.
- **Unreal Engine 5 (UE5) Simulation (Figure 5):** Assets generated by PhysX-CoT were imported into Unreal Engine 5 and assessed across six validity dimensions. The model achieved high validity rates: 94.1% parse, 96.8% collision, and 92.0% joint validity. This confirms that the generated assets are functionally compatible with physics engines, capable of rigid body interactions (e.g., toppling under wind) and robotic manipulation (e.g., grasping headphones). Limitations observed included challenges with occlusion, transparent/reflective materials, and highly non-rigid topologies.

#### 4.3. Process-Level Diagnostics (Table 4)

Beyond final asset metrics, direct evaluation of the predicted intermediate states against ground truth revealed consistent improvements:
- **Structured-State Accuracy:** Metrics such as 3D IoU, Rel. F1, Primitive type, Joint type, and Joint Axis error all improved from the baseline to the full PhysX-CoT model, with structured supervision contributing significantly. For instance, joint axis error decreased to 16.8 degrees for the full model.
- **Functional Dependence of CoT:**
 - **Oracle States:** Supplying ground-truth intermediate states significantly boosted final asset metrics (e.g., kinematic score rose from 0.73 to 0.92, scale error reduced from 6.57% to 4.12%), affirming the importance of accurate internal states.
 - **Perturbation:** Introducing perturbations to predicted 3D boxes degraded final metrics, causing, for example, scale error to inflate to 8.36%.
 - **State-Order Importance:** Shuffling, reversing, or removing the ordered sequence of states monotonically reduced quality (e.g., 3D IoU dropped from 0.660 to 0.542, parse validity from 96.4% to 90.9%). This indicates that the *ordered chain* of reasoning, not merely the structured fields, is functional. Token-matched unordered and parallel-head variants also showed degradation.
 - **Correlation:** A pre-decoding CoT score, not used as a reward, showed a Spearman correlation of ρ=0.455 with final asset quality, linking the internal reasoning quality to the fidelity of the output.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (Table 2 & 3)

The ablation study systematically evaluated the contribution of individual components:
- **Prompt-Only CoT:** Simply prompting the baseline VLM for structured CoT fields resulted in marginal improvements, suggesting that linguistic prompting alone is insufficient without explicit supervision and factorization.
- **Structured-CoT Supervision (PhysX-CoT-SFT):** Introducing supervised fine-tuning for the structured CoT states significantly improved most metrics over the PhysX-Anything baseline, underscoring the value of explicit process supervision.
- **Position-Factorized Local Geometry:** This component independently enhanced geometric quality (improved CD and F-score) and reduced scale error, confirming the benefits of decoupling "where" and "shape."
- **CoT-aligned GRPO:** The application of process rewards further refined the model, notably firming up kinematic quality and parse validity.
- **Reward Ablation (Table 3):** Removing specific reward terms demonstrated that each term primarily influenced its targeted competence (e.g., removing the format penalty reduced parse validity from 96.4% to 87.1%), validating the reward design.
The full PhysX-CoT model consistently achieved the best or tied-best performance across all metrics.

#### 4.5. Inference Cost

The use of compact local RLE for geometry representation led to approximately 58% fewer geometry tokens and a roughly 30% faster inference speed compared to the closest baseline, despite the multi-round generation process.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysX-CoT introduces a methodological advancement in the field of 3D asset generation from single images by emphasizing an explicit and verifiable reasoning process. This work demonstrates a shift from primarily generating visually plausible geometry to creating assets that are robust and functional within physical simulation environments.

The significance of this research lies in several aspects:
- **Enhanced Physical Realism and Usability:** By focusing on metric scale, part hierarchy, physical materials, collision geometry, and kinematic articulation, PhysX-CoT directly addresses the requirements for interactive virtual worlds, robotics, and embodied AI. The high validity rates observed in Unreal Engine 5 simulations indicate a practical step toward generating assets that can reliably interact with physics engines, which is crucial for applications requiring realistic physical behaviors.
- **Increased Interpretability and Controllability:** The explicit exposure of intermediate reasoning states as an ordered, machine-parseable Chain-of-Thought (CoT) enhances the interpretability of the generation process. This transparency allows for inspection, separate supervision, and verification of key decision points, which can facilitate debugging, refine model behavior, and offer greater control over the generated asset's properties.
- **Improved Geometric Robustness:** The introduction of position-factorized local geometry, which decouples part placement from its local shape, represents a structural improvement. This factorization helps mitigate issues associated with where-shape coupling in global coordinate systems, leading to more accurate and stable geometric outputs, particularly for complex objects with intricate or thin parts.
- **Novel Training Methodology:** The two-stage training approach combining Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with CoT-aligned Group-Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) offers a robust framework for learning complex physical reasoning. The design of process-level rewards, which target specific competences like localization, geometry quality, and physical consistency, allows for focused optimization and improvement of diverse physical attributes.
- **Efficiency Gains:** The adoption of a compact Run-Length Encoding (RLE) for local geometry tokens contributes to a reduction in the number of tokens required and yields faster inference times. This efficiency is beneficial for scalability and practical deployment in scenarios demanding rapid asset generation.
- **Advancing Embodied AI and Robotics:** The ability to automatically generate simulation-ready assets from single images can significantly accelerate the development of embodied AI and robotics. Researchers can leverage vast amounts of 2D visual data to populate virtual environments with physically accurate objects, enabling more realistic training and testing of robotic agents for manipulation, navigation, and interaction tasks.

While the paper acknowledges limitations such as reliance on a fixed decoder, coarse voxelization, and challenges with transparent, reflective, or non-rigid objects, it lays a foundation for future work. Expanding to broader datasets and incorporating multi-view inputs could further enhance the capabilities of such structured physical reasoning systems. Overall, PhysX-CoT represents a significant contribution towards making generated 3D content not just visually plausible, but genuinely physically functional for advanced computational applications.
