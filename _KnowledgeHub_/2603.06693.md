# Soft Equivariance Regularization for Invariant Self-Supervised Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Soft Equivariance Regularization (SER) is introduced as a method to enhance self-supervised learning in Vision Transformers by applying equivariance regularization to an intermediate spatial representation while maintaining invariance on the final embedding. This approach improves ImageNet-1k linear evaluation accuracy by up to +0.84%, boosts robustness by over 1% on ImageNet-C/P, and enhances spatially sensitive transfer tasks like COCO object detection by +1.7 mAP, with minimal computational overhead.

## Problem

- Enforcing strong invariance in self-supervised learning (SSL) can suppress transformation-dependent information, limiting robustness and performance on spatially sensitive tasks.
- Many existing invariance-plus-equivariance (Inv+Equiv) SSL methods apply both objectives to the final, often spatially collapsed, representation, leading to a trade-off where improved equivariance can degrade recognition accuracy.
- Prior explicit equivariant SSL methods often introduce architectural complexity through auxiliary modules or require learning per-sample transformation codes, increasing overhead.

## Method

- ==Soft Equivariance Regularization (SER)== decouples invariance and equivariance by applying a ==patch-wise NT-Xent loss== for equivariance to an intermediate spatial token map in Vision Transformers.
- Utilizes analytically specified ==feature-space actions== for geometric transformations (scaling, flips, rotations) directly on the token map, avoiding learned auxiliary modules.
- Employs a ==batch partitioning strategy== to allow for standard SSL augmentations (including cropping) for the invariance objective, while using cropping-free geometric augmentations for the equivariance objective.

## Results

- SER improved MoCo-v3 (ViT-S/16) by **+0.84%** Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1k linear evaluation (from **68.44%** to **69.28%**) and consistently enhanced DINO and Barlow Twins without architectural modifications.
- The method boosted robustness, with **+1.11%** average Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-C and **+1.22%** on ImageNet-P for MoCo-v3, and improved frozen-backbone COCO object detection by **+1.7 mAP**.
- Applying the layer-decoupling principle to existing methods like EquiMod and AugSelf also resulted in accuracy improvements (e.g., EquiMod improved from **68.95%** to **69.51%** Top-1), validating its general applicability.
- SER introduced minimal computational overhead, adding only **1.008x** training FLOPs compared to vanilla MoCo-v3.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An empirical trade-off exists when invariance and equivariance objectives are coupled on the same final representation, where deeper equivariance regularization can degrade ImageNet-1k linear evaluation accuracy.
> - Decoupling the layer at which equivariance is enforced (intermediate spatial representation) from the layer for invariance (final global embedding) is a generalizable design principle, improving SER and existing Inv+Equiv SSL methods.
> - Equivariance is more naturally and effectively encouraged on intermediate, spatially structured representations like ViT token maps than on a global embedding.
