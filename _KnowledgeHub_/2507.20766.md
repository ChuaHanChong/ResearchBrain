# Learning Only with Images: Visual Reinforcement Learning with Reasoning, Rendering, and Visual Feedback

> [!summary] Summary
> The Reasoning-Rendering-Visual-Feedback (RRVF) framework enables Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to acquire complex visual reasoning and code generation capabilities from raw images, circumventing the need for extensive image-text supervision. This approach achieves a 97.83% code execution rate on chart-to-code tasks, demonstrates strong generalization to unseen data, and allows a smaller policy model to outperform its larger teacher model.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) heavily depend on costly and time-consuming curated image-text datasets for training, leading to a significant data bottleneck.
- Existing MLLM training methods often struggle to foster deep visual reasoning, such as understanding complex spatial relationships and generative logic, beyond basic perception.
- Supervised learning in image-to-code generation faces challenges with "semantic equivalence," where valid code variants producing identical visual outputs are often incorrectly penalized.

## Method

- Introduces the ==Reasoning-Rendering-Visual-Feedback (RRVF) framework==, employing an iterative MLLM reasoner that generates code, which is then rendered by external tools.
- Implements a ==visual feedback mechanism== where a powerful MLLM compares the rendered output with the input image and provides natural language feedback to guide the next reasoning step.
- Utilizes Reinforcement Learning, specifically the GRPO algorithm, optimized with a ==hybrid reward function== derived from visual similarity, format correctness, and tool-use incentives, leveraging an ==MLLM-based visual judge==.

## Results

- Achieved a **97.83%** code execution rate and an overall score of **64.36** on the ChartMimic benchmark, outperforming all other open-source models and even the larger 72B model used for feedback.
- Demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization on the Plot2Code dataset, maintaining a **96.21%** execution rate and significantly surpassing Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) methods.
- Delivered competitive performance in the web-to-code task on the WebSight benchmark, achieving CLIP and GPT scores of **88.29** and **91.50** respectively, purely from visual feedback without ground-truth HTML.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The "Asymmetry of Verification" principle provides a powerful foundation for MLLM training, where easily verifiable visual outputs can guide complex generative tasks through reinforcement learning.
> - Visual feedback, generated by comparing a rendered image from MLLM-produced code with the original input, offers an effective and scalable reward signal for autonomous learning, eliminating dependence on image-text pairs.
> - A closed-loop reasoning, rendering, and feedback system enables MLLMs to iteratively self-correct and learn deep, transferable generative logic directly from raw pixel data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical and evolving challenge within the field of Artificial Intelligence: enhancing the deep visual reasoning capabilities of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) while reducing their heavy reliance on meticulously curated image-text supervision.

**Evolution of MLLMs and Visual Reasoning:**
The field of MLLMs has witnessed rapid advancements, with models like OpenAI o3, Gemini, and Claude demonstrating impressive performance across various visual tasks. Initially, MLLMs focused on basic visual perception and understanding, often mapping visual features to linguistic concepts. Subsequent research aimed at deeper visual reasoning, moving beyond simple object recognition to analyzing semantics, spatial relationships, and complex inter-object dependencies. This evolution highlighted a fundamental bidirectional dependency: robust visual perception is a prerequisite for sophisticated reasoning, yet sophisticated reasoning is often required to achieve robust perception.

**Limitations of Conventional MLLM Training:**
A significant bottleneck in MLLM development has been their dependence on large datasets of paired image-text data. These datasets, while effective for supervised learning of correlations, are expensive to create, limited in scope, and often struggle to capture the full complexity of human-like visual reasoning. They also suffer from the "semantic equivalence" issue, where multiple valid code representations might produce the same visual output, but only one is considered "ground truth." This limitation makes it difficult for MLLMs to learn generative logic directly from visual input and generalize to novel scenarios or unseen visual structures.

**Emergence of Tool Use and Reinforcement Learning:**
Recent trends in LLM and MLLM research have focused on empowering models with external tools and Reinforcement Learning (RL) to overcome some of these limitations. Works like OpenAI’s "Think with Images" demonstrated proactive interaction with visual content using OCR and zoom tools. Further, approaches like VRAG-RL employed RL to generate executable code for visual operations, showcasing the potential of integrating external tools and iterative refinement in MLLM reasoning. This paradigm shift, where models learn to act and receive feedback from their environment, is seen as crucial for advancing towards Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) by enabling autonomous exploration and learning.

**The "Asymmetry of Verification" Principle:**
This paper builds upon the "Asymmetry of Verification" principle, a relatively new concept in AI research (Wei, 2025). This principle posits that for many complex tasks, verifying a proposed solution is substantially easier than generating it from scratch. For instance, it's harder to write complex code than to check if given code produces the desired output. This asymmetry provides a powerful opportunity for RL-based training, where the ease of verification can be directly translated into a reward signal, guiding the model's learning process without explicit ground-truth generation labels.

**RRVF's Position:**
The "Reasoning-Rendering-Visual-Feedback" (RRVF) framework directly leverages these advancements. By focusing on learning "only with images" and employing RL guided by visual feedback, RRVF aims to circumvent the dependency on image-text supervision. It pushes the boundaries of autonomous learning for MLLMs, allowing them to infer generative logic and complex visual structures directly from raw pixel data. This work represents a significant step towards more self-sufficient and generalizable MLLMs, capable of deep visual reasoning without human-curated textual explanations, thereby aligning with the broader goal of developing more robust and intelligent AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to **enable Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to learn complex visual reasoning capabilities directly from raw images, thereby alleviating their heavy reliance on curated image-text supervision.**

The primary motivations driving this objective are:

1. **Addressing the Data Bottleneck:** Current state-of-the-art MLLMs achieve impressive performance but are fundamentally limited by the availability and quality of large, expensive, and meticulously curated image-text datasets. The cost and effort associated with creating such datasets hinder scalability and the exploration of novel reasoning tasks. The paper seeks a training paradigm that sidesteps this bottleneck.
2. **Enhancing Deep Visual Reasoning:** Beyond mere visual perception (object recognition, etc.), effective MLLMs need to perform sophisticated visual comprehension and inference, including analyzing underlying visual semantics, spatial-geometric relationships, and complex inter-object dependencies. The existing supervised learning approaches often struggle to cultivate this deep, compositional understanding, leading to models that might "mimic" rather than "reason."
3. **Leveraging the "Asymmetry of Verification":** A core motivation is the novel application of the "Asymmetry of Verification" principle. For tasks like image-to-code generation (e.g., converting a chart image to executable code or a web interface screenshot to HTML), verifying the visual fidelity of a rendered output against the source image is significantly easier than generating the correct, structured code from scratch. This relative ease of verification provides an ideal, robust, and automatic reward signal for Reinforcement Learning (RL) optimization, which is otherwise challenging to define for complex generative tasks.
4. **Promoting Autonomous Learning and Generalization:** By framing the problem as an RL task, the researchers aim to foster a closed-loop iterative learning process where the MLLM can self-correct and refine its outputs based on visual feedback. This approach is intended to lead to more robust, generalizable models that learn the underlying generative logic from raw pixels, rather than memorizing surface-level patterns from paired data. This self-improvement mechanism is crucial for advancing MLLMs towards truly autonomous cognitive abilities.
5. **Resolving Semantic Equivalence Issues:** In code generation tasks, multiple valid code snippets might produce the identical visual output. Supervised training typically relies on a single ground-truth code, which can penalize semantically equivalent but syntactically different correct solutions. By rewarding models based on visual fidelity, RRVF inherently resolves this semantic equivalence issue, as any program producing the correct visual output is rewarded.

In essence, RRVF aims to bridge the gap between image data and complex visual reasoning by creating a self-supervised learning loop that is both efficient and capable of developing a deeper, more transferable understanding of visual generative processes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The "Reasoning-Rendering-Visual-Feedback" (RRVF) framework introduces a novel closed-loop iterative process that enables MLLMs to learn complex visual reasoning solely from raw images. Its methodology is built upon three core components: an iterative visual reasoner, a visual feedback mechanism, and a final visual judge, all optimized via Reinforcement Learning.

**RRVF Framework Components:**

1. **Iterative Visual Reasoner (MLLM):**
 - The core MLLM acts as a visual reasoner, taking a single input image and engaging in a multi-turn dialogue.
 - In each turn, the MLLM generates a response comprising internal reasoning (within `<think>` tags) and an action.
 - For intermediate turns, the action is a code snippet (e.g., Python for charts, HTML for web interfaces) enclosed in a `<tool_call>` tag.
 - This code is then executed by an external rendering tool.
 - The resulting visual feedback (or a failure signal) is appended to the conversation history, informing the MLLM's subsequent reasoning steps.
 - The iterative cycle continues for a predefined maximum number of turns or until the model produces a final solution within an `<answer>` tag.

2. **Visual Feedback Mechanism:**
 - This component provides structured, descriptive guidance to the MLLM based on its generated code.
 - **Step 1: Rendering:** The MLLM's generated code is executed. For data charts, Python libraries (e.g., Matplotlib) are used. For web interfaces, Playwright renders the HTML and captures a screenshot. If the code is invalid, a failure signal is returned.
 - **Step 2: Comparison and Feedback Generation:** A separate, more powerful MLLM (termed the "qualitative assessor" or "visual judge") compares the rendered image with the original input image. It then articulates specific visual discrepancies (e.g., incorrect colors, missing elements, layout differences) in natural language. This natural language feedback serves as actionable information for the primary MLLM's next reasoning turn.

3. **Visual Judge:**
 - Distinct from the feedback mechanism, the Visual Judge provides a *quantitative score* for the final output, crucial for policy optimization in RL.
 - It renders the MLLM's final generated code and uses a powerful MLLM (e.g., GPT-4o) to evaluate the similarity between the generated and target images. This MLLM-based score becomes the primary reward signal for reinforcement learning.

**Reinforcement Learning Optimization:**

The iterative and goal-oriented nature of RRVF is inherently suited for Reinforcement Learning. The optimization process aims to learn an optimal policy (the MLLM's generation strategy) that produces accurate rendering code, thereby enhancing its visual understanding.

1. **Optimization Algorithm (GRPO):**
 - RRVF employs the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm, a computationally efficient alternative to PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization).
 - GRPO simplifies optimization by eliminating the need for a separate value function for advantage estimation.
 - It samples a group of candidate outputs (trajectories) from the current policy for each input query.
 - The policy is then optimized by maximizing a surrogate objective function that includes a clipping mechanism for stability and a KL regularization term to prevent excessive deviation from a reference policy.
 - The advantage function `A_i` for each sampled output `o_i` is derived as a normalized relative measure based on the rewards of the entire sampled group, providing a baseline.
 - The KL divergence term `D_KL(π_θ || π_ref)` ensures controlled policy updates, balancing exploration and stability.

2. **Hybrid Reward Design:**
 A comprehensive hybrid reward function guides the GRPO optimization, ensuring accuracy, format compliance, and effective tool utilization:
 - **Visual Similarity Reward (R_vision):** This is the primary reward, directly from the Visual Judge. It quantifies the fidelity between the final rendered image and the original input. This dense and accurate signal, computed by an MLLM judge, reduces reliance on image-text supervision.
 - **Format Correctness Reward (R_format):** This component penalizes structural and syntactic errors. A penalty is applied if reasoning, tool calls, or answer steps are improperly formatted, or if the generated code is non-executable.
 - **Tool-Use Reward (R_tool):** This incentivizes the model to leverage the feedback loop. A small reward is given for each successful tool call, encouraging iterative refinement. This reward is capped to prevent excessively long conversations and balance exploration with convergence.
 - **Total Reward (R):** The final reward is a weighted sum: `R = w_v R_vision + w_f R_format + w_t R_tool`. Hyperparameters `w_v`, `w_f`, and `w_t` (set to 0.2, 0.8, and 1.0 respectively in experiments) balance the contributions.

**Experimental Setup:**
- The policy model is initialized from Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-7B.
- The visual feedback and judgment (reward oracle) are provided by a more capable model, Qwen2.5-VL-Instruct-72B, deployed on a separate cluster to handle computational demands.
- Evaluations are conducted on image-to-code generation tasks across two domains: data charts (ChartMimic, Plot2Code) and web interfaces (WebSight).
- During inference, the model operates in a direct, single-turn mode, without iterative refinement or external tools, to rigorously assess its internalized capabilities.
- A "cold-start" procedure is implemented for the chart-to-code task to address initial format errors and facilitate tool-calling syntax learning, using a synthesized curriculum-based dataset.

This detailed methodology underscores RRVF's innovative approach to visual reasoning, transforming image-to-code generation into an autonomous learning problem driven by visual verification and reinforcement learning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive evaluations across diverse domains demonstrate the significant efficacy and robustness of the RRVF framework. The key findings are categorized by task and general observations:

#### 4.1. Chart-to-Code Task (ChartMimic and Plot2Code Benchmarks)

- **Superior Performance on ChartMimic (Table 1):**
 - **Execution Rate:** The RRVF-trained model achieves an exceptional code execution rate of 97.83%, surpassing all other evaluated models, including leading proprietary systems like OpenAI o3 and Gemini-2.5-Pro. This highlights the model's reliability in generating syntactically correct and executable code, a direct benefit of the RL process that penalizes invalid outputs.
 - **Overall Score:** RRVF achieves an Overall score of 64.36, securing the highest performance among all open-source models.
 - **Layout Understanding:** A strong Layout score of 80.97 indicates that the visual feedback loop effectively teaches the model to infer and replicate the underlying structural logic of data visualizations.
 - **Outperforming the Teacher Model:** Notably, the 7B policy model trained with RRVF surpasses its much larger 72B teacher model (used for visual feedback and judgment) in the Overall score (64.36 vs. 47.30 for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, which has a 72B counterpart as the judge). This is a profound finding, demonstrating that RRVF is more than knowledge distillation; it enables the policy model to actively explore and discover superior policies, bootstrapping its capabilities beyond the initial constraints of its feedback provider.

- **Exceptional Generalization on Plot2Code (Zero-Shot) (Table 2):**
 - **Execution Rate Stability:** On the unseen Plot2Code dataset, RRVF maintains a remarkably stable execution rate (96.21%), showing only a negligible decrease from its performance on ChartMimic. This starkly contrasts with the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) model, whose execution rate dramatically drops from 69.00% (ChartMimic) to 49.24% (Plot2Code).
 - **Enhanced Robustness and Reliability:** RRVF surpasses similarly sized models on primary metrics (Text, GPT-4o Score), underscoring its enhanced robustness and reliability even when exposed to a completely new domain.
 - This strong generalization ability is crucial evidence that RRVF learns fundamental, transferable principles of translating visual elements into programmatic logic, rather than merely memorizing dataset-specific code templates.

#### 4.2. Web-to-Code Task (WebSight Benchmark) (Table 3)

- **Strong Performance in Unstructured Domain:** The RRVF-trained model achieves strong performance in web interface generation with a CLIP score of 88.29 and a GPT score of 91.50, outperforming other similarly sized open-source models.
- **Learning from Raw Pixels:** This achievement is particularly significant as it's attained *without any access to ground-truth HTML*, relying entirely on the proposed visual feedback mechanism. This validates RRVF's capability to deconstruct and replicate complex layouts and component relationships from raw pixel information alone, even in domains characterized by high structural freedom.

#### 4.3. Training Analysis (Figure 3)

- **Policy Improvement:** The training curves show a steadily increasing reward score over training steps, confirming that the GRPO optimization effectively improves the model's policy.
- **Tool Usage Trend:** The number of tool calls exhibits a "dip-rise-plateau" trend. Initially, the model might struggle with tool mechanics (dip), then learns to effectively use the tool and iterative feedback (rise), eventually stabilizing at an optimal level of tool invocation (plateau). This aligns with observations in prior work on reinforcement learning with tool use, indicating a successful learning progression.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study (RRVF vs. SFT)

- The ablation study on ChartMimic directly compared RRVF with a standard Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) baseline.
- **RRVF's Superiority:** Despite SFT benefiting from direct access to ground-truth code, RRVF achieved a near-perfect execution rate (97.83% vs. 69.00% for SFT) and a dominant Layout score (80.97 vs. 63.60).
- **Robust and Compositional Reasoning:** This indicates that learning from the final visual outcome through RL cultivates a more robust and compositional reasoning ability in RRVF, in contrast to SFT's more surface-level mimicry of code patterns.

In summary, the main findings conclusively demonstrate that the RRVF framework successfully enables MLLMs to learn complex visual reasoning and code generation capabilities directly from raw image inputs. It achieves state-of-the-art performance among comparable open-source models, exhibits superior generalization, and critically, can even bootstrap capabilities beyond its teacher model, marking a significant advancement in autonomous visual learning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RRVF framework represents a significant advancement in the development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), with far-reaching implications for AI research and applications. Its significance stems from tackling a fundamental bottleneck in current MLLM training and offering a novel paradigm for learning.

**1. Reducing Reliance on Curated Data and Lowering Annotation Costs:**
Perhaps the most direct and impactful contribution is demonstrating that MLLMs can learn complex visual reasoning and generative logic *without* costly, laborious, and often bottlenecked image-text supervision. By shifting the learning paradigm to raw images and visual feedback, RRVF dramatically reduces the need for human annotation, making it more scalable and accessible to develop MLLMs for a wider range of tasks and domains. This democratizes MLLM development and accelerates research in areas where paired image-text data is scarce or non-existent.

**2. Validation of the "Asymmetry of Verification" Principle:**
The paper effectively showcases the practical utility of the "Asymmetry of Verification" principle for MLLM training. This principle provides a powerful theoretical underpinning for designing self-supervised learning systems where verification is inherently easier than generation. Its successful application in RRVF opens new avenues for designing reinforcement learning environments for other complex AI tasks where defining explicit reward functions or collecting ground-truth labels is challenging.

**3. Enhanced Robustness, Generalization, and Deeper Reasoning:**
The empirical results consistently show RRVF's superiority over traditional supervised fine-tuning (SFT) baselines, not just in raw performance but crucially in robustness and generalization. The model's ability to maintain high performance on unseen datasets (Plot2Code) and learn the "underlying generative logic" instead of just memorizing patterns is a testament to its deeper understanding. This leads to MLLMs that are more capable of transferring learned skills to novel scenarios, a critical step towards true intelligence.

**4. Enabling Self-Correction and Autonomous Skill Bootstrapping:**
The closed-loop iterative reasoning, rendering, and visual feedback mechanism empowers MLLMs with self-correction capabilities. This allows models to iteratively refine their outputs based on concrete visual discrepancies, mimicking a human-like trial-and-error learning process. The remarkable finding that a smaller 7B RRVF-trained model can outperform its larger 72B teacher model used for feedback highlights the potential for autonomous skill bootstrapping, where a model can learn to exceed the capabilities of its own feedback provider. This is a crucial step towards creating AI systems that can independently improve and innovate.

**5. Paving the Way for More Capable and Agentic MLLMs:**
By enabling MLLMs to learn from their interactions with a visual environment, RRVF pushes towards more "agentic" AI systems that can reason, act, and learn from feedback in a dynamic, goal-oriented manner. This paradigm aligns with the broader vision of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), where models can autonomously navigate and understand complex real-world environments.

**6. Broadening Application Scope:**
While currently focused on code reconstruction (chart-to-code, web-to-code), the principles underlying RRVF could be extended to other visual generative tasks. For example, it could impact areas like visual content creation, interactive design, scientific data visualization, and even robotics, where learning precise visual control or object manipulation from visual outcomes alone could be highly beneficial.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors acknowledge that the framework's current applicability is limited to code reconstruction tasks. Extending RRVF to more abstract reasoning tasks where "visual verification" might be less straightforward or more subjective remains an important future challenge. However, the foundational insights provided by RRVF, particularly regarding the power of visual feedback in reinforcement learning, are likely to inspire further research in these more complex domains.

In conclusion, RRVF offers a compelling new direction for MLLM training, addressing key limitations of existing methods. Its success in fostering robust, generalizable visual reasoning from raw images through an elegant RL framework signifies a substantial leap forward in developing more autonomous, capable, and intelligent AI systems.
