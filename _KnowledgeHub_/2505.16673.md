# R1-ShareVL: Incentivizing Reasoning Capability of Multimodal Large Language Models via Share-GRPO

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Nanyang Technological University, ByteDance, and collaborating institutions develop Share-GRPO, a reinforcement learning framework for multimodal large language models that addresses sparse reward and advantage vanishing problems by expanding question spaces through semantic transformations and sharing reward information across question variants during training, with their R1-ShareVL model achieving substantial improvements on mathematical reasoning benchmarks including 4.2-point gains on MathVista and 3.8-point gains on MathVerse compared to baseline GRPO when applied to Qwen2.5-VL models.

## Problem

- Existing reinforcement learning methods like GRPO suffer from sparse rewards when training multimodal large language models, making it difficult to obtain positive feedback for complex reasoning tasks
- Advantage vanishing occurs during training, where the model struggles to differentiate between different reasoning paths, limiting learning effectiveness

## Method

- Introduce ==Share-GRPO framework== that expands question spaces using ==Semantically Consistent Transformation (SCT)== through both offline textual rewrites and online multimodal transformations
- Implement ==shared advantage estimation== that aggregates reward information hierarchically across global and local levels to provide denser training signals
- Apply ==shared policy optimization== that leverages diverse reasoning trajectories from question variants to improve model learning

## Results

- R1-ShareVL achieves **4.2**-point improvement on MathVista and **3.8**-point improvement on MathVerse compared to GRPO baseline using Qwen2.5-VL-7B
- Method scales effectively to larger models, with Qwen2.5-VL-32B showing consistent performance gains across mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks
- Ablation studies confirm individual contributions of shared policy optimization and shared advantage estimation components, with the complete framework demonstrating complementarity with dynamic sampling techniques

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Information sharing principles from multi-agent reinforcement learning can be effectively adapted to address fundamental challenges in multimodal language model training
> - Question space expansion through semantic transformations creates multiple learning opportunities from single training examples while maintaining task consistency
> - Hierarchical advantage estimation at both global and local levels provides more stable and informative gradients for policy optimization

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** The paper directly addresses the rapidly growing field of MLLMs, which combine the power of large language models with the ability to process visual information. This is a very active area of research, driven by the potential to create AI systems that can understand and interact with the world in a more human-like way.
- **Reinforcement Learning for LLMs:** The application of reinforcement learning (RL) to fine-tune LLMs and MLLMs is a relatively recent trend. This approach allows models to learn from experience and optimize their performance on complex tasks, particularly those requiring reasoning and long-term planning. DeepSeek and Kimi's work has demonstrated the effectiveness of RL in incentivizing long-chain reasoning. This paper builds on this foundation.
- **Sparse Reward and Advantage Vanishing:** The paper identifies two key challenges that limit the effectiveness of RL in MLLMs: sparse rewards (difficulty in getting positive feedback) and advantage vanishing (difficulties in differentiating between different reasoning paths during training). These are known problems in RL, and this paper specifically addresses them in the context of MLLMs.
- **Information Sharing in Deep Learning:** The paper draws inspiration from the broader concept of information sharing, which has been successfully applied in various deep learning domains, including multi-modal learning and multi-agent RL. By adapting this principle to MLLM reasoning, the paper proposes a novel way to improve the learning process.
- **Benchmarking and Evaluation:** The paper contributes to the growing body of work focused on evaluating the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs. By providing results on multiple established benchmarks, the paper allows for comparison with other models and facilitates progress in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** To develop an effective reinforcement learning method (Share-GRPO) to improve the reasoning ability of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs).
- **Motivation:**
 - Existing RL methods, such as GRPO, suffer from sparse reward and advantage vanishing issues when applied to MLLMs, hindering their ability to effectively learn long-chain reasoning.
 - To address these limitations, the authors propose to introduce the concept of information sharing into MLLM reinforcement learning.
 - The goal is to create an RL approach that can mitigate sparse reward and advantage vanishing, leading to improved reasoning performance in MLLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Share-GRPO:** A novel RL approach designed to address the sparse reward and advantage vanishing issues in MLLMs.
- **Question Space Expansion:**
 - Semantically Consistent Transformation (SCT) is used to expand the question space for a given question.
 - Two types of transformation techniques are employed:
 - Offline Textual SCT: Rewrites the textual prompt using GPT-4o to generate semantically consistent variants.
 - Online Multimodal SCT: Applies visual transformations (e.g., rotation, noise injection) to the image and appends a transformation-specific prompt to the textual prompt.
- **Shared Advantage Estimation:**
 - Reward information is shared during advantage computation.
 - Advantages are estimated hierarchically at two levels:
 - Global Level: Aggregates responses across all variants of the same original question.
 - Local Level: Considers responses generated from each individual question variant.
- **Shared Policy Optimization:**
 - The policy model is optimized by sharing diverse reasoning trajectories across question variants.
 - A modified PPO loss function is used to update the policy.
- **Experimental Setup:**
 - Base models: Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-32B.
 - Training data: 52K multimodal data from MM-Eureka.
 - Training infrastructure: 8 NVIDIA H100 GPUs for the 7B model and 32 H100 GPUs for the 32B model.
 - Evaluation benchmarks: MathVista, MMStar, MMMU, MathVerse, MathVision, AI2D.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Superior Performance:** Share-GRPO outperforms the baseline and other state-of-the-art RL-based reasoning MLLMs on both mathematical and general reasoning benchmarks.
- **Effective Mitigation of Sparse Reward and Advantage Vanishing:** Share-GRPO provides denser rewards and higher valid advantage ratios compared to GRPO.
- **Significant Improvements on Reasoning Tasks:** R1-ShareVL achieves substantial improvements on challenging mathematical benchmarks such as MathVista and MathVerse.
- **Generalization Capability:** R1-ShareVL demonstrates generalization capability in enhancing reasoning across diverse tasks, including multi-discipline and general benchmarks.
- **Robustness and Scalability:** The method remains robust and consistently improves performance when scaling to larger models (i.e., Qwen2.5-VL-32B).
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablation studies confirm the individual contribution of each design component in Share-GRPO, including shared policy optimization and shared advantage estimation.
- **Complementarity with Dynamic Sampling:** Share-GRPO is complementary to dynamic sampling and achieves even higher accuracy when combined.
- **Qualitative Results:** Qualitative results demonstrate that Share-GRPO effectively enhances the model’s reasoning ability on complex mathematical problems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Addresses Key Limitations of RL in MLLMs:** By effectively mitigating the sparse reward and advantage vanishing issues, Share-GRPO makes RL a more viable approach for training high-performing MLLMs.
- **Advances the Field of MLLM Reasoning:** The paper contributes a novel method for improving the reasoning capabilities of MLLMs, which is crucial for enabling these models to solve complex real-world problems.
- **Promotes Information Sharing in MLLMs:** The introduction of information sharing into MLLM reinforcement learning is a significant contribution that could inspire new research directions in this area.
- **Potential Applications:** Improved MLLM reasoning can lead to advancements in a wide range of applications, including:
 - Mathematical and scientific problem-solving
 - Document understanding
 - Visual question answering
 - Robotics and autonomous systems
 - Education and tutoring systems
- **Reproducibility and Open Source:** The authors have made the code available, which promotes reproducibility and allows other researchers to build upon their work.

In conclusion, the paper presents a significant contribution to the field of MLLMs by introducing a novel reinforcement learning framework that addresses key limitations in existing approaches. The proposed Share-GRPO method demonstrates superior performance and has the potential to drive further advancements in MLLM reasoning and its applications.
