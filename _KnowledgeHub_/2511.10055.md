# Image Aesthetic Reasoning via HCM-GRPO: Empowering Compact Model for Superior Performance

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Alibaba Health developed HCM-GRPO, an enhanced reinforcement learning framework, along with a novel dataset, to improve image aesthetic reasoning in multimodal large language models. This approach enabled compact models to achieve over 20 points higher performance on image screening tasks compared to leading large-scale MLLMs, demonstrating superior physical and spatial understanding.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit unsatisfactory performance in image aesthetic reasoning, often failing to identify objective flaws like appearance deformation or illogical extensions in generated content.
- A scarcity of specialized datasets exists to effectively train and evaluate MLLMs on objective image quality assessment, particularly concerning verifiable physical transformations rather than subjective artistic preferences.
- The uncontrolled nature of AI-generated images necessitates a robust and automated post-generation screening mechanism to ensure quality and consistency in real-world applications.

## Method

- A large-scale image screening dataset was constructed, comprising over 128,000 samples and approximately 640,000 images, specifically designed to evaluate MLLMs on four objective aesthetic dimensions.
- A two-stage training methodology was implemented, starting with ==Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)== to establish basic spatial understanding, followed by reinforcement fine-tuning using the proposed ==HCM-GRPO== framework.
- The ==HCM-GRPO== (Hard Cases Mining with Dynamic Proportional Accuracy Reward in ==Group Relative Policy Optimization==) method was developed, incorporating a novel ==DPA reward function== and a strategy to identify and re-sample hard cases, thereby focusing learning on model weaknesses.

## Results

- Compact MLLMs fine-tuned with HCM-GRPO achieved an overall score of **64.74** on the image screening dataset, demonstrating over **20 points** higher performance than the best large-scale open-source and leading closed-source MLLMs (e.g., Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct at **39.74**, GPT4o at **34.62**).
- Hard Cases Mining proved effective, with the HCM-GRPO approach (InternVL3-2B) outperforming variants without hard case augmentation, confirming that targeted learning from difficult examples improves reasoning.
- The proposed methodology generalized well to public benchmarks, improving InternVL3-2B's accuracy on RealWorldQA by **2.62 points** and BLINK by **8.92 points**, indicating its robustness for broader physical reasoning and multi-image understanding tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Existing MLLMs, including large closed-source models, exhibit performance akin to random guessing on objective image aesthetic reasoning tasks, revealing a significant deficiency in their physical and spatial understanding.
> - Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), particularly with human-answer-guided Chain-of-Thought data, is crucial for equipping MLLMs with foundational instruction-following and basic spatial understanding, serving as an effective "cold start."
> - The combination of a dynamic proportional accuracy reward for multi-choice questions and a hard cases mining strategy within a reinforcement learning framework effectively focuses MLLM learning on challenging examples, leading to substantial performance improvements.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields: Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), AI-Generated Content (AIGC), and Reinforcement Learning (RL).

**Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs):** The paper acknowledges the extensive and growing research in MLLMs, covering foundational model development, evaluation dataset construction, and RL applications. This work contributes directly to the MLLM landscape by addressing a specific, yet critical, limitation: the unsatisfactory performance of current MLLMs in image aesthetic reasoning. While MLLMs have shown impressive general capabilities, their ability to perform nuanced, objective quality assessment of images, especially with fine-grained spatial and physical understanding, remains weak. By proposing both a specialized dataset and a tailored methodology, this paper aims to push the boundaries of MLLM capabilities in a practical, real-world task.

**AI-Generated Content (AIGC) and Image Generation:** The advent of sophisticated diffusion models has significantly advanced image generation capabilities. However, as the authors note, the inherent randomness in these generative processes often leads to "unpredictable results" and "unintended content." This necessitates a post-generation screening process to ensure quality and adherence to specific criteria. This paper directly tackles this crucial "last mile" problem in AIGC pipelines. Instead of focusing on *how* to generate better images, it focuses on *how to effectively screen* the generated images for aesthetic and physical inconsistencies, thus ensuring the utility and reliability of AIGC in applications like e-commerce. The created dataset, built upon AI-generated product images, specifically targets the issues arising from these generative models.

**Reinforcement Learning (RL) for LLMs/MLLMs:** Recent advancements have seen reinforcement learning, particularly techniques like Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) and related policy optimization methods, significantly enhance the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). Papers like DeepSeek-R1, DAPO, and VAPO exemplify this trend. This research extends these RL paradigms to the multimodal domain. It specifically adapts Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), a method known for its effectiveness in LLMs, to MLLMs for the image aesthetic reasoning task. The introduction of Hard Cases Mining (HCM) and Dynamic Proportional Accuracy (DPA) reward represents a methodological innovation tailored to the challenges of multimodal reasoning and objective evaluation, demonstrating how RL can be effectively applied to improve MLLM performance on complex, context-specific tasks. The work highlights that standard MLLMs struggle with this nuanced reasoning, even after instruction tuning, thus requiring more sophisticated RL fine-tuning.

In summary, this work addresses a practical challenge arising from the progress in AIGC, identifies a key weakness in current MLLM capabilities, and proposes an innovative RL-based solution to bridge this gap, thereby advancing both MLLM application and RL methodology for multimodal tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from a critical observation: despite significant advancements in image generation, the subsequent process of *screening* these generated images for quality and consistency is underdeveloped, and current MLLMs perform poorly in this specific task. The authors identify two primary reasons for this deficiency:

1. **Lack of Specialized Data:** There is a scarcity of comprehensive datasets specifically designed to evaluate and train MLLMs on image aesthetic reasoning, particularly for objectively verifiable physical space transformations rather than subjective artistic preferences.
2. **Weak Image Aesthetic Reasoning Ability in MLLMs:** Even state-of-the-art MLLMs, including powerful closed-source models like GPT4o and Qwen-VL-Max, exhibit performance akin to random guessing when tasked with identifying aesthetic and physical flaws in generated images. This indicates a fundamental gap in their ability to understand and reason about the physical world represented in images.

Against this backdrop, the paper sets forth several clear objectives:

- **Develop a Comprehensive Image Screening Dataset:** To overcome data scarcity, a primary objective is to collect and curate a large-scale dataset. This dataset, comprising over 128,000 samples and approximately 640,000 images, is specifically designed to assess image aesthetic reasoning across four objective dimensions: appearance deformation, physical shadow, placement layout, and extension rationality. The focus on real-world medicinal product images makes it highly relevant for e-commerce and healthcare applications.
- **Investigate Cost-Effective High-Quality CoT Data Acquisition:** Recognizing the labor-intensive nature of annotating Chains of Thought (CoT) data for complex reasoning tasks, the authors aim to explore efficient strategies, including purely manual, fully automated, and answer-driven approaches, to acquire high-quality CoT data at a reduced cost. This is crucial for enabling effective Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT).
- **Propose an Enhanced Reinforcement Learning Methodology:** The central methodological objective is to introduce an advanced RL framework, HCM-GRPO (Hard Cases Mining with Dynamic Proportional Accuracy Reward in Group Relative Policy Optimization). This method is designed to specifically enhance MLLMs' reasoning capabilities by focusing on challenging examples and providing more granular reward signals.
- **Empower Compact MLLMs for Superior Performance:** A key objective is to demonstrate that even smaller, more compact MLLMs, when effectively trained with the proposed dataset and methodology, can achieve superior performance in image aesthetic reasoning, outperforming much larger and more general-purpose open-source and closed-source MLLMs. This aims to challenge the reliance on increasingly larger models for specialized tasks.
- **Validate Generalizability:** To ensure broader applicability, the research aims to evaluate the proposed HCM-GRPO method on several public datasets, assessing its ability to generalize to real-world spatial reasoning and multi-image understanding tasks beyond the proprietary image screening dataset.

Ultimately, the motivation is to bridge the gap between advanced image generation and reliable image screening, thereby fostering more robust and trustworthy AI-generated content in practical applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a comprehensive two-stage approach to enhance image aesthetic reasoning in MLLMs, complemented by a novel dataset construction pipeline.

#### 3.1. Image Screening Dataset Construction

The foundation of this work is a new, large-scale image screening dataset, designed to objectively evaluate MLLMs' aesthetic reasoning. The construction involves a three-stage pipeline:

1. **Data Preparation:** Real-world medicine images are used as a base. A segmentation model extracts foreground regions (the medicine). These are then placed onto a white background, creating a "placeholder image." Concurrently, a diverse set of background images is curated as "reference images."
2. **Data Processing:** The placeholder image and a randomly selected reference image are combined to form an "initial image." A "mask image" is generated, crucial for controlling the subsequent generation process. This mask has a black half and a white half, with the target medicine region also white within the white half.
3. **Generation Phase:** State-of-the-art image generation models, FLUX.1-Fill-dev (an Inpaint DiT) and FLUX.1-Redux-dev (IP-Adapter), are employed. The reference image guides the IP-Adapter, while the initial image and mask image are fed into the Inpaint DiT. The Inpaint DiT "repaints" the white regions of the initial image, guided by the mask and reference, to generate new backgrounds and surrounding elements for the medicine. This process introduces realistic yet often flawed elements.

The resulting dataset comprises over 128,000 samples, totaling approximately 640,000 images (an original medicine image plus four generated variants per sample). The generated images intentionally exhibit issues categorized into four objective evaluation dimensions: **appearance deformation**, **physical shadow**, **placement layout**, and **extension rationality**.

#### 3.2. Dataset Division and Annotation

The dataset is split into training, testing, pseudo-label, and exploration sets.

- **Training and Testing Splits:** Manually annotated by human reviewers for ground-truth accuracy. Each sample receives a multiple-choice label (e.g., "AC", "N" for none). The testing set includes specific labels for each of the four aesthetic dimensions for nuanced evaluation.
- **Pseudo-Label Split:** Utilizes an automated annotation paradigm. Qwen-VL-Max is prompted to generate image descriptions and "weakly-supervised CoT data" (reasoning processes based on its descriptions). This aims to increase data diversity and volume economically.
- **Exploration Split:** Unlabeled, reserved for future research into unsupervised learning methods.

To acquire CoT data for supervised fine-tuning more effectively, the authors investigate multiple approaches:

- **Purely Manual Annotation:** Highest quality but prohibitively costly and slow.
- **Fully Automated Annotation:** Low cost, scalable, but prone to noise and hallucinations.
- **Answer-Driven Annotation:** A strategic trade-off. For the training split, human-annotated correct answers are used to guide Qwen-VL-Max to regenerate CoT data, ensuring consistency with human judgment while leveraging automation.

#### 3.3. Two-Stage Training Methodology

The core training methodology consists of two stages:

**Stage 1: Cold Start (Basic Spatial Understanding and Instruction Following via SFT)**

- **Motivation:** Existing MLLMs often lack the specific instruction-following and basic spatial understanding required for the image screening task, making direct application of RL inefficient.
- **Approach:** Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) is performed on the base MLLM using the acquired CoT data. This stage is further divided:
 1. **Continual Pre-training:** Utilizes image captions and weakly-supervised CoT data (from the pseudo-label split) to establish basic spatial understanding and general reasoning patterns.
 2. **Answer-Driven Fine-tuning:** Employs the higher-quality, human-answer-guided CoT data (from the training split) to refine instruction-following and strengthen task-specific reasoning.
- **Objective Function:** Standard cross-entropy loss for sequence generation. This stage ensures the model can produce responses in the desired structured CoT format.

**Stage 2: HCM-GRPO (Reinforcement Fine-Tuning and Hard Cases Mining)**

- **Foundation:** Builds upon the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) framework, which avoids a separate critic model by estimating baselines from group scores of sampled responses.
- **Dynamic Proportional Accuracy (DPA) Reward:** This is a novel reward function designed for multiple-choice questions with potentially multiple correct answers. Unlike a binary reward, DPA awards partial credit: `r_acc = len(response) / len(answer)` if the model's response is a subset of the correct options, and 0 otherwise. This provides a denser, more accurate feedback signal, crucial for guiding RL effectively.
- **Hard Cases Mining (HCM) Strategy:**
 1. **Hard Case Identification:** The SFT-trained model from Stage 1 is used to evaluate the entire training set. Samples answered incorrectly are identified as "hard cases."
 2. **Targeted Training:** The RL training process is structured:
 - Initial 3 epochs: General training on the complete dataset to establish a robust baseline.
 - Final 2 epochs: The training data is augmented by *re-introducing and oversampling* the previously identified hard cases alongside the full dataset. This forces the model to dedicate more learning capacity to its weaknesses, refining its reasoning on challenging examples.
- **GRPO Objective:** Maximizes the policy objective using the DPA reward, with an importance sampling coefficient and KL divergence penalty to maintain stability and prevent policy divergence.

#### 3.4. Experimental Settings and Baselines

- **Base Models:** InternVL3-1B and InternVL3-2B are used as compact base models for longitudinal comparison.
- **Baseline Models:** Various large-sized open-source (InternVL3.5-30B-A3B, Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, GLM-4.5V) and closed-source (GPT4o, Qwen-VL-Max, Qwen-VL-Plus) MLLMs are used for cross-sectional comparison.
- **Evaluation Metric:** The primary metric considers a response correct if *any* of the model's chosen options is among the correct ground-truth options, assuming that in a real-world scenario, if a model suggests multiple valid images, one would be selected.
- **Generalization Studies:** Performance is also evaluated on public datasets (RealWorldQA, MuirBench, BLINK) to assess generalization to real-world physical reasoning and multi-image understanding tasks.

This detailed methodology, encompassing data creation, annotation strategies, and a two-stage RL training paradigm with novel reward and mining components, forms a cohesive approach to address the challenging problem of image aesthetic reasoning in MLLMs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results highlight the significant challenges of image aesthetic reasoning for existing MLLMs and demonstrate the superior performance of the proposed HCM-GRPO method, particularly for compact models.

- **Current MLLMs Perform Poorly on Image Aesthetic Reasoning:** A critical finding is that state-of-the-art MLLMs, including powerful closed-source models like GPT4o and Qwen-VL-Max, exhibit very low performance on the proprietary image screening dataset. Their overall scores (e.g., GPT4o CoT: 34.62, Qwen-VL-Max CoT: 38.25) are close to the random guessing baseline of 34.27. This confirms the initial motivation that existing models lack specialized capabilities for this task. The best-performing large open-source model, Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, achieved 39.74 (Direct) and 35.47 (CoT).

- **Effectiveness of Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) with CoT Data:** The "cold start" SFT stage is crucial. Directly applying GRPO to original models without initial SFT is ineffective. However, SFT, especially with high-quality CoT data, significantly boosts the base model's performance. For InternVL3-2B, SFT improved the overall score from a baseline of 23.72 to 53.63, demonstrating its vital role in establishing foundational reasoning and instruction following.

- **Impact of CoT Data Quality:** Ablation studies on CoT data sources reveal that "answer-driven CoT data" (leveraging human-annotated answers to guide CoT generation) is substantially more effective than weakly-supervised CoT data or image caption data alone. Combining all available CoT data sources yielded the best SFT performance, suggesting a synergistic effect where diverse reasoning patterns and high-quality guidance complement each other.

- **Superiority of HCM-GRPO:**
 - **Significant Performance Gains:** The HCM-GRPO method, applied to a compact model like InternVL3-2B, achieved an overall score of 64.74. This represents a substantial improvement of over 20 points compared to the best large-scale open-source and leading closed-source MLLMs (e.g., 64.74 vs. Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct's 39.74).
 - **Outperforms Standard GRPO and DPA-GRPO:** Comparisons show that HCM-GRPO (combining DPA reward and hard cases mining) outperforms both the original GRPO (with binary reward) and DPA-GRPO alone. This validates the effectiveness of the proposed methodological enhancements, particularly the DPA reward for multi-choice scenarios and the strategic focus on challenging examples.
 - **Specific Dimension Performance:** HCM-GRPO-2B showed strong performance in "appearance deformation" (68.66) and "extension rationality" (74.51), but relatively lower scores in "physical shadow" (65.85) and "placement layout" (55.08). This aligns with human intuition that reasoning about complex physical interactions (shadows, floating objects) is more challenging than identifying basic appearance errors or illogical extensions.

- **Validation of Hard Cases Mining:** The ablation study on training data composition in Stage 2 confirms the efficacy of Hard Cases Mining. Augmenting the full training dataset with specifically identified hard cases (HCM-GRPO) yielded the highest performance (64.74 for InternVL3-2B), surpassing training only on easy or hard cases, or even merely adding random samples. This demonstrates that targeted learning from weaknesses is more effective, avoiding catastrophic forgetting while addressing model deficiencies.

- **Generalization to Public Datasets:** HCM-GRPO demonstrated its ability to generalize to other public datasets focused on real-world physical reasoning (RealWorldQA) and multi-image understanding (MuirBench, BLINK). On RealWorldQA, HCM-GRPO improved InternVL3-2B's accuracy by 2.62 points over the baseline. For MuirBench, it achieved the highest score of 63.43 (on InternVL3-1B), and on BLINK, it boosted InternVL3-2B's accuracy by 8.92 points. These results indicate broader applicability and robustness beyond the specific domain of product image screening.

In essence, the findings underscore that while generic MLLMs struggle with nuanced image aesthetic reasoning, a carefully designed two-stage fine-tuning approach, leveraging specialized data, effective CoT acquisition, and an enhanced reinforcement learning strategy like HCM-GRPO, can enable even compact models to achieve significantly superior performance on this challenging task.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries substantial significance and potential impact across several domains, including MLLM development, AI-Generated Content (AIGC) quality assurance, and real-world industrial applications.

- **Addressing a Critical Gap in MLLM Capabilities:** The most immediate impact is the successful demonstration that current MLLMs possess significant deficiencies in image aesthetic reasoning, a nuanced visual understanding task. By rigorously evaluating leading models and showing their near-random performance, the authors highlight a crucial area for future MLLM development beyond general-purpose understanding. The proposed HCM-GRPO method provides a concrete pathway to imbue MLLMs with these advanced reasoning skills.

- **Enabling Robust AIGC Quality Control:** With the proliferation of AIGC, particularly in commercial contexts like e-commerce, the need for reliable automated quality control is paramount. Generated images, despite their sophistication, often contain subtle or blatant errors in physical realism or consistency. This work offers a powerful solution for *screening* such content. By focusing on objective criteria (appearance deformation, physical shadow, placement layout, extension rationality) and achieving superior performance, the developed system can significantly enhance the trustworthiness and utility of AI-generated product images, minimizing errors before deployment.

- **Advancing Reinforcement Learning for Multimodal Tasks:** The introduction of HCM-GRPO, with its Dynamic Proportional Accuracy (DPA) reward and Hard Cases Mining strategy, is a significant methodological contribution to the field of reinforcement learning for MLLMs. The DPA reward, by providing a more granular feedback signal for multiple-choice questions, offers a valuable blueprint for designing more effective reward functions in complex reasoning tasks. Hard Cases Mining is a well-founded strategy, but its application and demonstrated efficacy within a multimodal RL context, especially for improving reasoning, is noteworthy. This methodology can serve as a reference for researchers applying RL to other challenging MLLM applications.

- **Empowering Compact Models and Efficiency:** A particularly impactful finding is that a much smaller model (e.g., InternVL3-2B) fine-tuned with HCM-GRPO can drastically outperform larger, generic MLLMs on this specific task. This challenges the "bigger is better" paradigm and underscores the importance of task-specific data and sophisticated fine-tuning techniques. For industry, this translates into significant advantages in terms of computational resources, deployment costs, and inference speed, making advanced MLLM capabilities more accessible and practical for real-world applications.

- **Valuable Dataset for Future Research:** The creation of a comprehensive, objectively evaluable image screening dataset with over 640,000 images, especially one built upon real-world medicinal products and designed to expose specific reasoning flaws, is a major contribution. This dataset fills a void and will be an invaluable resource for the MLLM and AIGC communities, facilitating further research into image aesthetic reasoning, multimodal perception, and robust quality control mechanisms.

- **Setting Future Research Directions:** The paper openly acknowledges limitations, such as struggles with highly complex physical rules (shadows, layout), and proposes clear directions for future work. These include enriching CoT data with more examples from challenging dimensions and exploring unsupervised knowledge transfer using the unlabeled exploration split. These open questions provide fertile ground for continued innovation in the field.

In essence, this research not only presents a highly effective solution to a challenging practical problem but also pushes the methodological boundaries of MLLMs and reinforcement learning, with broad implications for the development of more intelligent, reliable, and resource-efficient AI systems.
