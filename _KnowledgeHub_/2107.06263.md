# CMT: Convolutional Neural Networks Meet Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the University of Sydney and Huawei Noah's Ark Lab introduce CMT, a hybrid neural network architecture that effectively combines Convolutional Neural Networks and Vision Transformers. This approach establishes a new state of the art in accuracy-efficiency trade-offs for image classification, object detection, and segmentation, with CMT-S achieving 83.5% top-1 accuracy on ImageNet with 4.0B FLOPs.

## Problem

- Pure Vision Transformers (ViTs) incurred high computational costs, particularly due to the quadratic scaling of self-attention with input size.
- ViTs struggled with effectively capturing local spatial information and generating multi-scale features crucial for dense prediction tasks.
- Existing ViTs often lagged behind highly optimized CNNs in terms of accuracy-efficiency trade-off for similar computational budgets.

## Method

- A hierarchical architecture with a ==convolutional stem== for multi-scale feature generation and efficient initial processing.
- A novel ==CMT block== featuring a ==Local Perception Unit (LPU)== for explicit local feature extraction and an Inverted Residual Feed-forward Network (IRFFN) to enhance feature transformation with local learning.
- A ==Lightweight Multi-head Self-attention (LMHSA)== mechanism that uses depth-wise convolutions to reduce computational cost while retaining global context.

## Results

- On ImageNet, the CMT-S model achieved **83.5%** top-1 accuracy using **4.0B** FLOPs, surpassing EfficientNet-B4 (**82.9%** @ **4.2B** FLOPs) and DeiT-S (**79.8%** @ **4.6B** FLOPs).
- Ablation studies confirmed that each proposed component (CMT Stem, LPU, IRFFN) incrementally improved accuracy, with the hierarchical architecture alone boosting performance by **1.6%**.
- For object detection on COCO, CMT-S as a RetinaNet backbone yielded **44.3%** mAP, and as a Mask R-CNN backbone, it achieved **44.6%** AP_box and **40.7%** AP_mask, setting new performance levels for its efficiency class.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Systematically integrating convolutional operations and a hierarchical structure into Transformer designs effectively addresses the limitations of pure Vision Transformers, such as their lack of local inductive bias and inability to generate multi-scale features.
> - The combination of a Local Perception Unit (LPU) and an Inverted Residual Feed-forward Network (IRFFN) within the Transformer block enhances local feature learning and efficiency with minimal computational overhead.
> - Lightweight Multi-head Self-attention (LMHSA), using depth-wise convolutions for key and value projection, significantly reduces the quadratic complexity of attention, making the model more scalable for higher resolutions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of computer vision has been largely dominated by Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for decades, owing to their inherent inductive biases (translation equivariance and locality) which are well-suited for image processing. Architectures like ResNet and EfficientNet have pushed the boundaries of performance and efficiency in CNNs.

However, the remarkable success of Transformer architectures in Natural Language Processing (NLP), particularly their ability to capture long-range dependencies, inspired researchers to adapt them for vision tasks. The pioneering work, Vision Transformer (ViT) by Dosovitskiy et al. (2020), directly applied the Transformer architecture to images by treating image patches as tokens. While ViT showed promise, especially when pre-trained on massive datasets, it suffered from several drawbacks:
1. **High computational cost:** The self-attention mechanism in standard Transformers scales quadratically with input sequence length, making it computationally expensive for high-resolution images.
2. **Lack of inductive bias:** Pure ViTs lack the inherent local feature extraction and translation invariance that CNNs possess, requiring extensive data for training to generalize well.
3. **Single-scale features:** Original ViTs typically output single-scale feature maps, which are suboptimal for dense prediction tasks like object detection and semantic segmentation that require hierarchical, multi-scale representations.

In response to these limitations, a new wave of research emerged, aiming to bridge the gap between CNNs and Transformers. This included works like DeiT (data-efficient training), Swin Transformer (hierarchical architecture with shifted windows for locality and efficiency), PVT (pyramid vision transformer for multi-scale features), CvT (introducing convolutions to vision transformers), and CeiT (incorporating convolution designs). These models attempted to either make Transformers more efficient, incorporate CNN-like inductive biases, or generate multi-scale features.

CMT fits directly into this burgeoning landscape of **hybrid CNN-Transformer architectures**. It builds upon the lessons learned from both paradigms, explicitly acknowledging the strengths of CNNs in local feature extraction and efficiency, and the power of Transformers in modeling long-range dependencies. The paper proposes a systematic approach to combine these strengths, aiming to address the fundamental weaknesses of pure ViTs while surpassing the performance of leading CNNs. It distinguishes itself by carefully integrating convolutional operations *within* the Transformer block itself (LPU, IRFFN) and adopting a CNN-inspired stage-wise hierarchical architecture, rather than merely using convolutions as a pre-processing step or in parallel. This positions CMT as a significant advancement in the pursuit of highly performant and efficient universal backbones for computer vision.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the CMT research are clearly articulated in the abstract and introduction:

1. **Address the performance and computational cost gaps between Vision Transformers (ViTs) and Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs):** The authors note that despite ViTs' ability to capture long-range dependencies, they still lag behind high-performance CNNs (like EfficientNet) in terms of accuracy-efficiency trade-off and overall performance for similar computational budgets. The goal is to develop a network that not only outperforms canonical Transformers but also surpasses leading convolutional models.

2. **Develop a hybrid network that leverages the complementary strengths of CNNs and Transformers:** The core motivation is to combine the best of both worlds: Transformers' prowess in capturing global, long-range dependencies and CNNs' efficiency and effectiveness in extracting local, fine-grained information. The paper aims to mitigate the weaknesses of pure Transformer-based models by incorporating CNN-like components.

3. **Overcome specific limitations of existing Vision Transformers:** The authors identify three key weaknesses of current Transformer-based models like ViT:
 - **Ignoring 2D structure and local information:** Splitting images into fixed-size patches and processing them linearly loses valuable in-patch local information and 2D spatial context.
 - **Difficulty in extracting low-resolution and multi-scale features:** The fixed patch size design makes it challenging for Transformers to generate hierarchical, multi-scale features crucial for dense prediction tasks (e.g., object detection, segmentation).
 - **Quadratic computational cost of self-attention:** The `O(N^2C)` complexity of self-attention (where N is sequence length/number of patches) makes it prohibitive for high-resolution images, leading to GPU memory issues and inefficiency.

4. **Create a family of scalable models with superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off:** Beyond just a single model, the objective is to propose a systematic scaling strategy to generate a family of CMT models (CMT-Ti, CMT-XS, CMT-S, CMT-B, CMT-L) that offer a better trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency across various resource constraints.

In essence, the researchers are motivated to design a more **versatile, efficient, and performant** backbone for general visual recognition that addresses the practical shortcomings of pure Transformer architectures while pushing beyond the capabilities of traditional CNNs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The CMT (CNNs meet Vision Transformers) architecture is a novel hybrid network designed to synergistically combine the strengths of CNNs and Transformers. Its methodology can be broken down into three key aspects: overall architecture design, the specialized CMT block, and a principled scaling strategy.

#### 3.1. Overall Architecture

The CMT architecture adopts a **stage-wise, hierarchical design** reminiscent of modern CNNs (e.g., ResNet, EfficientNet), which is crucial for generating multi-scale feature maps. This contrasts with the flat, single-scale architecture of early ViTs.

1. **Convolutional Stem:** The input image first passes through a "CMT Stem," which is a stack of convolutional layers (one 3x3 stride-2 conv followed by two 3x3 stride-1 convs). This initial stem is designed for efficient, fine-grained local feature extraction and downsampling, preserving more local information than ViT's direct patch embedding. It produces features at a higher resolution (H/4 x W/4) compared to ViT's H/16 x W/16 first stage.
2. **Hierarchical Stages:** The architecture consists of four stages. Each stage begins with a "Patch Embedding" layer, which is a 2x2 convolutional layer with stride 2 followed by Layer Normalization (LN). This layer reduces the spatial resolution (2x downsampling) and increases the feature dimension (2x enlargement), facilitating the hierarchical representation learning.
3. **Stacked CMT Blocks:** Within each stage, several CMT blocks are stacked sequentially. These blocks are the core computational units responsible for feature transformation and learning both local and global dependencies.
4. **Global Average Pooling and Classifier:** The model concludes with a global average pooling layer (replacing the class token in ViT), a projection layer, and a 1000-way classification layer with softmax.

This stage-wise design ensures the generation of hierarchical feature maps (strides 4, 8, 16, and 32), which are essential for downstream dense prediction tasks like object detection and segmentation.

#### 3.2. The CMT Block

The proposed CMT block is an enhanced variant of the standard Transformer block, comprising three main components:

1. **Local Perception Unit (LPU):**
 - **Motivation:** Standard Transformers use absolute positional encodings and lack explicit mechanisms to capture local relationships within patches or maintain translation invariance.
 - **Approach:** LPU introduces a depth-wise convolution (DWConv) followed by a residual connection: `LPU(X) = DWConv(X) + X`.
 - **Benefit:** This unit effectively extracts local spatial information from the intermediate features with negligible computational cost and helps to restore local inductive bias, addressing the limitations of position embeddings and local relation modeling in pure ViTs.

2. **Lightweight Multi-head Self-attention (LMHSA):**
 - **Motivation:** The quadratic `O(N^2C)` complexity of standard self-attention (due to large N=H*W) is a major computational bottleneck, especially for high-resolution inputs.
 - **Approach:** LMHSA reduces the spatial size of the Key (K) and Value (V) tensors *before* the attention operation using a `k x k` depth-wise convolution with stride `k`. Additionally, it incorporates a learnable relative position bias `B` (which can be interpolated for different resolutions) to enhance positional awareness without breaking translation invariance.
 - **Benefit:** This significantly reduces the computational cost of the self-attention mechanism to `O(N^2C/k^2 + NC^2)`, making it more efficient for higher resolutions while still capturing global dependencies. The relative position bias improves performance and transferability.

3. **Inverted Residual Feed-forward Network (IRFFN):**
 - **Motivation:** The standard FFN in ViT consists of simple linear layers. Inspired by MobileNetV2's inverted residual block, which efficiently expands and then projects features, IRFFN aims to enhance feature transformation and local information extraction within the FFN.
 - **Approach:** IRFFN is structured as an expansion layer (1x1 conv), followed by a depth-wise convolution, and then a projection layer (1x1 conv), with batch normalization and GELU activation. Crucially, it re-positions the shortcut connection. The core part is `IRFFN(X) = Conv(F(Conv(X)))`, where `F(X) = DWConv(X) + X`.
 - **Benefit:** The depth-wise convolution within the IRFFN further reinforces local feature learning with minimal overhead, and the inverted residual structure, combined with proper normalization, improves gradient flow and representation ability.

The CMT block combines these components: `Y = LPU(X)`, `Z = LMHSA(LN(Y)) + Y`, `X = IRFFN(LN(Z)) + Z`.

#### 3.3. Complexity Analysis

The paper provides a detailed FLOPs analysis, demonstrating that the CMT block, particularly with the LMHSA's downsampling factor `k`, is significantly more computationally efficient than a standard Transformer block, especially for large input sizes (n).

#### 3.4. Scaling Strategy

Inspired by EfficientNet's compound scaling, CMT proposes a similar strategy to systematically scale model depth ($\alpha$), dimension ($\beta$), and input resolution ($\gamma$) using a compound coefficient $\phi$. The scaling is constrained such that `$\alpha \cdot \beta^{1.5} \cdot \gamma^2 \approx 2.5$`, ensuring that for a given $\phi$, the total FLOPs increase proportionally (approximately by $2.5^{\phi}$). This allows the generation of a family of models (CMT-Ti, CMT-XS, CMT-S, CMT-B, CMT-L) that balance computational cost and performance.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The CMT paper presents extensive experimental results across various computer vision tasks, consistently demonstrating the superiority of the proposed architecture in terms of accuracy-efficiency trade-off.

#### 4.1. ImageNet Classification

- **Superior Accuracy and Efficiency:** CMT models achieve higher top-1 accuracy on ImageNet with significantly fewer FLOPs and parameters compared to both existing Transformer-based models and high-performance CNNs.
 - **CMT-S** achieves **83.5% top-1 accuracy** with **4.0B FLOPs**. This is **3.7% higher** than DeiT-S (79.8% with 4.6B FLOPs) and **0.6% higher** than EfficientNet-B4 (82.9% with 4.2B FLOPs).
 - The paper highlights that CMT-S is **14x smaller in FLOPs** than DeiT (DeiT-B with 384x384 input resolution has 55.6B FLOPs) and **2x smaller in FLOPs** than EfficientNet-B7 (37B FLOPs) while achieving better or comparable accuracy.
- **Scalability:** The family of CMT models (CMT-Ti to CMT-L) consistently outperforms their counterparts across various scales, showcasing the effectiveness of the proposed compound scaling strategy. For instance, the largest model, CMT-L, reaches 84.8% top-1 accuracy, outperforming EfficientNet-B7.
- **Throughput:** CMT models also show competitive throughput (images/sec), indicating efficient inference speed.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Stage-wise Architecture:** Changing DeiT-S to a 4-stage hierarchical architecture (DeiT-S-4Stage) significantly improves performance by 1.6% (from 79.8% to 81.4%) with reduced FLOPs, validating the importance of multi-scale feature generation and CNN-like staging.
- **CMT Block Components:**
 - Adding the **CMT Stem** improves accuracy by 0.5% (from 81.4% to 81.9%).
 - Incorporating the **Local Perception Unit (LPU)** further boosts accuracy by 0.8% (from 81.9% to 82.7%). Removing the shortcut connection in LPU leads to a drop, emphasizing its importance.
 - Adding the **Inverted Residual Feed-forward Network (IRFFN)** provides another 0.6% gain (from 82.7% to 83.3%). Again, removing its shortcut connection degrades performance.
 - A final projection layer brings the CMT-S to its full 83.5%.
- **Normalization Function:** The choice of normalization (LayerNorm vs. BatchNorm) is crucial. While CMT retains LN for LMHSA and IRFFN, replacing all LNs with BNs or vice versa impacts convergence or performance, suggesting that a hybrid approach to normalization is beneficial.
- **Scaling Strategy:** The proposed compound scaling strategy (scaling depth, dimension, and resolution simultaneously) is shown to be significantly more effective than unidimensional scaling strategies, leading to better accuracy gains for both scaled-up and scaled-down models.

#### 4.3. Transfer Learning

- **Object Detection and Instance Segmentation (COCO):** CMT-S, used as a backbone for RetinaNet (object detection) and Mask R-CNN (instance segmentation), achieves state-of-the-art results.
 - For **RetinaNet**, CMT-S achieves **44.3% mAP**, outperforming Twins-PCPVT-S (43.0%) and PVT-M (42.0%) with comparable or less FLOPs.
 - For **Mask R-CNN**, CMT-S achieves **44.6% AP_box** and **40.7% AP_mask**, surpassing Twins-PCPVT-S (42.9% AP_box, 40.0% AP_mask) with similar FLOPs.
- **Other Vision Tasks (Fine-grained Classification):** CMT-S generalizes well to various fine-grained classification datasets (CIFAR10, CIFAR100, Stanford Cars, Flowers, Oxford-IIIT Pets). It consistently outperforms other Transformer-based models and achieves comparable performance to much larger EfficientNet-B7 (which has 9x more FLOPs) across these datasets, demonstrating its strong transfer learning capabilities and versatility.

In summary, the main findings strongly support the hypothesis that a thoughtful hybrid architecture combining CNN and Transformer principles can yield superior performance and efficiency across a wide range of vision tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "CMT: Convolutional Neural Networks Meet Vision Transformers" paper represents a significant contribution to the field of computer vision, with substantial potential impact in both research and practical applications.

1. **Bridging the CNN-Transformer Divide:** The most direct impact of CMT is its success in effectively bridging the gap between CNNs and Vision Transformers. It offers a principled design for a hybrid architecture that truly leverages the complementary strengths of both paradigms while mitigating their individual weaknesses. This work confirms that the future of powerful vision models likely lies in such hybrid designs, rather than pure reliance on one architecture type.

2. **State-of-the-Art Performance and Efficiency:** CMT models set new benchmarks for the accuracy-efficiency trade-off in visual recognition. Achieving higher accuracy than leading CNNs (like EfficientNet) and significantly outperforming pure Transformers (like DeiT) with fewer computational resources (FLOPs) is a major breakthrough. This means that high-performance vision models can now be developed and deployed more efficiently, making them accessible to a wider range of applications and hardware constraints.

3. **Versatile Backbone for Diverse Vision Tasks:** The demonstration of CMT's strong performance across various tasks—image classification, object detection, instance segmentation, and fine-grained classification—highlights its versatility as a universal backbone. Its ability to generate hierarchical, multi-scale features makes it particularly well-suited for dense prediction tasks that previously struggled with pure Transformer architectures. This positions CMT as a strong candidate for a foundational model in the computer vision ecosystem.

4. **Influence on Future Architectural Design:** The specific architectural innovations within the CMT block—the Local Perception Unit (LPU), Lightweight Multi-head Self-attention (LMHSA), and Inverted Residual Feed-forward Network (IRFFN)—provide concrete examples of how convolutional operations can be intelligently integrated into Transformer blocks. These components offer blueprints for future research into hybrid designs, inspiring novel ways to fuse inductive biases and attention mechanisms. The effectiveness of the CNN-style stem and stage-wise architecture also reinforces their importance even in Transformer-heavy models.

5. **Practical Implications for Deployment:** For industrial applications, the efficiency gains are paramount. Models like CMT, which offer high accuracy with significantly reduced FLOPs, are highly valuable for deployment on edge devices, mobile platforms, or in large-scale cloud services where computational cost and energy consumption are critical factors. Huawei Noah's Ark Lab's involvement and the provision of both MindSpore and PyTorch implementations underscore this practical orientation.

6. **Further Research Avenues:** CMT opens up several avenues for future research, including exploring different convolutional operations within Transformer blocks, investigating alternative lightweight attention mechanisms, refining scaling strategies for hybrid models, and applying CMT-like principles to other domains beyond computer vision. The careful ablation studies also provide valuable insights for architectural design choices.

In conclusion, CMT represents a major step forward in the evolution of neural network architectures for computer vision. By ingeniously combining the strengths of CNNs and Transformers, it delivers models that are not only highly performant but also remarkably efficient, thus paving the way for more powerful and deployable AI systems.
