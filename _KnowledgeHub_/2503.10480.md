# World Modeling Makes a Better Planner: Dual Preference Optimization for Embodied Task Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Fudan University researchers develop OpenMOSS, a dual preference optimization framework for embodied task planning that equips Large Vision-Language Models with world modeling capabilities, outperforming GPT-4o on the VoTa-Bench benchmark while reducing the need for human-annotated training data through automated tree search data collection.

## Problem

- Current LLM/LVLM-based approaches struggle with dependency constraints and planning efficiency in embodied tasks
- Existing methods lack understanding of physical interactions and dynamic environments
- Human annotation for training data is costly and time-consuming

## Method

- ==Introduce Dual Preference Optimization== (D²PO) framework that jointly optimizes state prediction and action selection
- Develop tree search mechanism for automated collection of training trajectories and preference pairs
- Create VoTa-Bench benchmark extending LoTa-Bench with egocentric visual information

## Results

- D²PO framework achieves higher success rates than GPT-4o on VoTa-Bench using only 7B parameters, with path-length weighted metrics showing more efficient action sequences than baseline methods
- Model demonstrates strong generalization to unseen environments and scenes
- Automated data collection eliminates need for manual annotation while maintaining performance

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - World modeling objectives significantly enhance planning capabilities compared to action-only optimization
> - Learning from both successful and unsuccessful trajectories improves performance over supervised fine-tuning
> - Physics-aware planning emerges from joint optimization of state prediction and action selection

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in embodied AI and robotics: enabling agents to perform complex tasks in dynamic, real-world environments using Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs). It fits into the broader research landscape by tackling the limitations of existing LLM/LVLM-based approaches to embodied task planning, which often struggle with:

- **Dependency Constraints:** Ensuring actions are performed in the correct order (e.g., picking up an object before placing it).
- **Planning Efficiency:** Avoiding unnecessary or redundant steps.
- **World Understanding:** Lacking the ability to model the dynamic nature of physical interactions.

The paper builds upon and contributes to the following areas:

- **Embodied Task Planning:** This is a core area of research within Embodied AI where agents are trained to complete tasks in simulated or real-world environments. This paper introduces a novel approach for generating action sequences in these settings.
- **Large Language Models and Vision-Language Models for Robotics:** The paper addresses limitations of using LLMs/LVLMs directly for robotics tasks. It proposes a method to improve the planning capabilities of these models specifically for the robotics domain.
- **World Models:** The paper integrates the concept of world models (models that learn to predict future states) into the training process to improve the agent's understanding of the environment and its ability to plan effectively.
- **Preference Learning:** The paper leverages Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), a technique used for aligning language models to human preferences, to train the agent to select actions and predict states that are more likely to lead to task success.
- **Reinforcement Learning:** The work offers an alternative to traditional reinforcement learning approaches that rely on reward functions. By using preference learning, the method avoids the need to design complex reward signals.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives of this research are:

- **To develop a framework that enables LVLMs to perform embodied task planning more effectively.** This includes improving their ability to handle dependency constraints, plan efficiently, and understand the dynamic nature of physical interactions.
- **To introduce a novel training framework (D²PO) that jointly optimizes state prediction and action selection through preference learning.** This framework aims to equip LVLMs with an "internal world model" that enhances their planning capabilities.
- **To create a method for automatically collecting training data for embodied task planning without human annotation.** This addresses the challenge of acquiring large, diverse datasets for training LVLMs in this domain.
- **To demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed approach through extensive experiments and comparisons with existing methods.** This includes evaluating the performance of the D²PO framework on a new multimodal benchmark (VoTa-Bench).

The motivation behind this research stems from the limitations of current LVLM-based approaches to embodied task planning. These approaches often struggle with fundamental issues such as dependency constraints, inefficient planning, and the inability to model the dynamic nature of physical interactions. The authors argue that by equipping LVLMs with an "internal world model," they can overcome these limitations and achieve better planning performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this paper consists of several key components:

- **Dual Preference Optimization (D²PO) Framework:** This framework jointly optimizes state prediction and action selection through preference learning. The model learns to predict the next state given the current state and action, and it learns to select actions that are more likely to lead to task success.
- **Tree Search Mechanism for Data Collection:** A tree search algorithm is used to automatically collect trajectories and construct stepwise preference data for training. This algorithm explores action sequences within a simulated environment and generates preference pairs for both action selection and state prediction.
- **VoTa-Bench Benchmark:** A new multimodal benchmark is created by extending the text-only LoTa-Bench to include egocentric visual information. This benchmark is used to evaluate the performance of the D²PO framework.
- **Experimental Evaluation:** The D²PO framework is evaluated on the VoTa-Bench benchmark and compared to existing methods, including zero-shot LVLMs, in-context learning, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and DPO (action selection only). The evaluation metrics include success rate (SR) and path-length weighted success rate (PL).

The approach is based on the idea that learning to model the world can enhance planning capabilities. By jointly optimizing state prediction and action selection, the D²PO framework enables LVLMs to understand environment dynamics and make more informed action selections. The tree search mechanism allows for the automatic collection of training data, eliminating the need for human annotation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The main findings and results of this research are:

- **The D²PO framework significantly outperforms existing methods and GPT-4o on the VoTa-Bench benchmark.** This demonstrates the effectiveness of the proposed approach for embodied task planning.
- **Incorporating world modeling objectives significantly enhances the model's planning capabilities.** The D²PO framework achieves superior performance compared to standard DPO, which only optimizes action selection.
- **Learning from mistakes improves performance.** The DPO and D²PO frameworks outperform SFT, which relies solely on successful trajectories.
- **The model develops physics-aware planning capabilities.** The improved path-length weighted success rate (PL) metrics indicate that the model learns to generate more efficient action sequences.
- **The D²PO framework generalizes well to unseen environments.** The model performs well on unseen scenes that were not part of the training environment.
- **A 7B-parameter model trained with D²PO outperforms GPT-4o.** This demonstrates the potential of the proposed approach to achieve strong performance with relatively small models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The significance and potential impact of this research are:

- **Advances the state-of-the-art in embodied task planning.** The D²PO framework provides a new and effective approach for enabling LVLMs to perform complex tasks in dynamic environments.
- **Reduces the need for human annotation in embodied AI.** The tree search mechanism for data collection allows for the automatic generation of training data, which can significantly reduce the cost and effort associated with developing embodied AI systems.
- **Enables more efficient and robust robots.** By equipping robots with better planning capabilities, they can perform tasks more efficiently and adapt to changing environments more effectively.
- **Wider applications of embodied AI.** By addressing the limitations of current approaches, this research can help to broaden the scope of applications for embodied AI, including robotics, assistive technology, and virtual assistants.
- **Potential for real-world applications:** By training robust models using this framework, these findings can extend to real-world applications where robots can function as assistive tools and augment human capabilities.

In conclusion, this paper presents a significant contribution to the field of embodied AI by introducing a novel framework (D²PO) that leverages world modeling and preference learning to improve the planning capabilities of LVLMs. The automatic data collection method and the extensive experimental results further enhance the value of this research. The findings have the potential to significantly impact the development of more efficient, robust, and adaptable robots for a wide range of applications.
