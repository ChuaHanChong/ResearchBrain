# PoseVLA: Universal Pose Pretraining for Generalizable Vision-Language-Action Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Pose-VLA introduces a decoupled pre-training framework that instills 3D spatial awareness in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models through a universal pose representation. This approach improves robotic manipulation generalizability, achieving up to 16.1% higher 3D grounding accuracy on Objectron and an average 14.0% higher success rate on RoboTwin 2.0 Hard scenarios compared to prior VLA models.

## Problem

- Existing VLA models suffer from a granularity mismatch, as VLM pre-training focuses on high-level semantics rather than fine-grained 3D state variations critical for robotic manipulation.
- There is a data heterogeneity gap: internet-scale visual datasets lack physical action grounding, while robotic demonstration datasets are scarce, narrowly distributed, and costly to acquire.
- Entangling high-level perception with sparse, embodiment-specific action supervision leads to feature collapse and low training efficiency in VLMs.

## Method

- **Pose-VLA** uses a decoupled learning paradigm that separates pre-training for universal 3D spatial priors in a camera-centric space from post-training for embodiment-specific alignment.
- Introduces 3D pose as a universal, ==discretized tokenized representation== for both object states and action trajectories, unifying feature spaces across diverse 3D datasets.
- Integrates auxiliary spatial priors, such as ==camera ray encodings== and ==depth maps==, into the VLM architecture to provide absolute geometric anchors and enhance 3D awareness.

## Results

- Achieved an AP15 of **87.3** on Objectron, an absolute **16.1%** improvement over Qwen3-VL, and **45.5** on SUN RGB-D, demonstrating robust 3D object localization and general indoor scene understanding.
- Outperformed prior methods in simulation, with **79.1%** average success on RoboTwin 2.0 Hard scenarios (**14.0%** higher than "pi"0) and **96.0%** overall on LIBERO, indicating enhanced robustness and cross-task generalization.
- Attained an **81.25%** average success rate on four challenging real-world tasks using limited demonstrations (100 per task), consistently matching or surpassing "pi"0.5 and confirming the importance of explicit 3D geometry.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly training VLMs on 3D spatial tasks with a pose-centric representation forms a robust geometric foundation that prevents feature collapse and enables fine-grained 3D reasoning for downstream robotic policies.
> - Leveraging large-scale, non-robotic 3D vision data, integrated via universal pose tokens, is a primary driver for spatial capabilities, significantly reducing the reliance on scarce robotic demonstration data.
> - Camera-centric, end-effector pose representations are more robust and align better with the learned geometric priors compared to base-frame or joint-angle representations for robotic actions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied intelligence has seen advancements with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which combine high-level semantic understanding from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with low-level control by action experts. Approaches like π and GR00T utilize dual-system architectures where VLMs provide semantic interpretation and generative action experts (e.g., using flow matching) handle action denoising.

Despite progress, a fundamental challenge persists in scaling vision-language representations to physically grounded, generalizable action policies. Existing VLAs often leverage off-the-shelf VLMs, which are frequently pre-trained on tasks such as Visual Question Answering (VQA) and image-text alignment. While these VLMs excel at semantic identification, they may not capture the subtle 3D state variations critical for robotic manipulation. Research has indicated that fine-tuning VLMs on auxiliary tasks like depth estimation or VQA does not consistently guarantee improved control performance. This discrepancy suggests a gap in transferring general semantic features to robust downstream policy performance.

This work identifies two specific misalignments contributing to this challenge:
1. **Granularity Mismatch:** VLM pre-training focuses on high-level semantics and categorical recognition, which contrasts with robotic manipulation's dependence on fine-grained 3D state variations (e.g., pose, contact geometry, relative motion).
2. **Data Heterogeneity Gap:** Internet-scale visual datasets lack physical action grounding, while robotic demonstration datasets are typically scarce, narrowly distributed, and costly to acquire. Existing VLAs struggle to effectively integrate these diverse data sources.

To address these limitations, the paper proposes a decoupled learning paradigm. Instead of entangling perception with embodiment-specific actions, Pose-VLA separates training into a pre-training phase for universal 3D spatial priors in a camera-centric space, followed by a post-training phase for embodiment-specific alignment. This approach aims to equip VLMs with transferable embodied priors that directly facilitate policy learning, making them geometrically aware and optimized for manipulation tasks. The concept of using pose as a "universal interface" aligns with growing efforts to extend VLMs to 3D understanding for robotic applications, moving beyond sequential detection and pose estimation to direct, open-vocabulary 3D grounding.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of Pose-VLA are to overcome the limitations of existing VLA models, specifically the feature collapse and low training efficiency that arise from entangling high-level perception with sparse, embodiment-specific action supervision. The work is motivated by two structural misalignments between current VLM pre-training and the requirements for robotic actions:

1. **Addressing Granularity Mismatch:**
 - **Motivation:** Standard VLM pre-training tasks (e.g., image-text alignment, VQA) emphasize categorical recognition and abstract semantics. Robotic manipulation, however, requires precise understanding of subtle 3D state changes, such as object pose, contact points, or relative movement, which dictate distinct action patterns. A VLM trained primarily on semantic tasks may recognize an object but fail to perceive the fine-grained physical state necessary for manipulation. This can lead to policies that are not robust to minor variations.
 - **Objective:** Pose-VLA aims to explicitly train the model to reason over fine-grained spatial supervision by representing object states and actions as 3D poses and temporal sequences. This forces the VLM to learn to distinguish subtle 3D variations.

2. **Bridging the Data Heterogeneity Gap:**
 - **Motivation:** Internet-scale visual datasets, while vast, lack physical action grounding. Conversely, robotic demonstration datasets, which provide grounded action data, are expensive to collect, limited in scale, and often narrowly distributed across specific embodiments and scenarios. Existing VLA models struggle to effectively leverage the benefits of large-scale general vision data while maintaining relevance to real-world control tasks.
 - **Objective:** Pose-VLA proposes using discrete pose tokens as a universal representation to create a common language across diverse 3D data sources. This enables the model to integrate large-scale non-robotic 3D datasets, which offer rich spatial experience, with limited robotic demonstrations. The goal is to transform the VLM into a geometrically-aware backbone that can efficiently adapt to new robotic policies with minimal fine-tuning data.

In summary, the key objective is to establish robust, transferable embodied priors within the VLM through a decoupled pre-training approach that centers on a universal pose representation. This aims to create a VLM capable of both semantic understanding and precise 3D spatial grounding, thereby facilitating more generalizable and data-efficient robotic manipulation policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Pose-VLA introduces a decoupled learning paradigm for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) modeling, separating training into a pre-training phase for universal 3D spatial priors and a post-training phase for embodiment alignment.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The model operates within an auto-regressive paradigm, predicting an output token sequence S given a multi-modal observation O and a language instruction L. The output S is defined as a structured sequence of tuples S = (τ1, τ2..., τT), where each tuple τt = {ct, bt, pt} is a fundamental unit for spatial representation:
- `ct`: Object category.
- `bt` ∈ R^2: 2D bounding box center in image coordinates.
- `pt` ∈ SE(3): Pose (translation and rotation) in the camera-centric frame.

This structure allows `τt` to serve as a versatile geometric primitive, enabling localization in static contexts and characterizing trajectory waypoints in temporal sequences.

#### 3.2. VLM Architecture

Pose-VLA builds upon the PaliGemma architecture, which uses SigLip as its visual encoder. To move beyond PaliGemma's VQA/detection-centric training and instill 3D awareness, Pose-VLA integrates auxiliary inputs:
- **RGB images:** Processed by the SigLip encoder.
- **Depth maps:** Provide metric depth information.
- **Camera intrinsics:** Encoded as raymaps to establish direct pixel-to-3D viewing direction correspondence.

These auxiliary inputs are integrated via additive fusion with RGB embeddings prior to the initial Transformer block, functioning similarly to positional encodings. A modality masking strategy is applied during training, where raymaps or depth values are randomly zero-padded, and sparse depth points are sampled for robustness to varying sensor availability and noise at inference time. The model's output is designed to predict actions directly within the camera frame, ensuring a shared camera-centric representation across heterogeneous data sources. The original PaliGemma's text and `<loc>` vocabularies are retained for `ct` and `bt`.

#### 3.3. Unified Pose Representation

A core component is the adoption of 3D pose as a universal representation. This unifies feature spaces across non-robotic 3D datasets and robotic manipulation data, constraining the model to estimate spatial coordinates directly from visual observations in the camera frame.

- **Object-Level Representation:**
 - Objects are characterized by rigid-body transformations (translation and rotation). These continuous parameters are discretized into specialized tokens.
 - **Rotation:** Uniform quantization of Euler angles is used, with a dedicated `<rot>` token for each principal axis.
 - **Translation:** Due to observed distributional differences (x, y axes concentrated around the origin; z-axis exhibiting a skewed depth distribution), a shared `<trans_xy>` token is used for x and y dimensions, and a distinct `<trans_z>` token for the depth dimension.
 - **Scale:** For 3D grounding benchmarks, object scale is also incorporated using a unified `<size>` token across all three spatial dimensions, as their statistics are similar.
 - This tokenization aims to be more efficient than decomposing numerical coordinates into individual digit tokens, ensuring all data is optimized within a shared real-world metric space.

- **Trajectory-Level Representation:**
 - Robot actions (gripper 6-DoF poses) directly reuse the discretized tokens established for object-level representations.
 - Action trajectories are represented as temporally ordered sequences of these unified pose tokens.
 - To align with the camera-centric observation space, all action trajectories are projected into the respective camera coordinate frame of each view using calibration parameters. This provides dense, grounded supervision in the image observation space and enhances 3D correspondence learning across diverse viewpoints.

#### 3.4. Adding Prior Conditioning

To enhance geometric awareness, auxiliary spatial priors are incorporated:
- **Camera Ray Encoding:** Camera rays `r = K^-1[u, v, 1]^T` (derived from the intrinsic matrix K) encode the viewing direction for each pixel. These ray maps are patchified, projected into the latent space, and fused with visual features, providing absolute geometric anchors.
- **Depth Prior Integration:** Depth maps D and their sparsity masks M are stacked as `[D, M]` and then patchified and embedded. Value normalization is avoided to maintain alignment with the metric space, and the configuration handles varying sparsity.

#### 3.5. Training Strategy

- **Pre-training Stage:**
 - The model is pre-trained using a next-token prediction loss, jointly supervising the generation of 2D localization tokens and 3D pose tokens.
 - The objective is `L_pre(θ) = - Σ log p_θ(s_k | ϕ(O), L, s_<k)`, where `ϕ(O)` represents the modality masking strategy (random zero-padding of raymaps/depth or sampling sparse depth points) to ensure robustness.
 - This phase instills coarse, semantically grounded spatial awareness, enabling the VLM to become a geometric reasoner.

- **Post-training Stage:**
 - A lightweight action expert is appended to map the pre-trained VLM representations to robot-specific commands, following previous work (e.g., π).
 - A flow matching objective is used to denoise action tokens, with the VLM backbone providing semantic and geometric conditioning through self-attention layers to the action expert.
 - The action expert is trained from scratch without inheriting initialization parameters from prior works, ensuring native grounding in Pose-VLA representations. During inference, the action expert directly maps implicit features to continuous actions, without explicit pose or camera trajectory estimation.

#### 3.6. Pre-training Datasets

The pre-training corpus comprises two main types of datasets:
1. **Spatial Grounding and 3D Perception (1.4M images, 6.5M 3D annotations):**
 - **General 3D Detection:** Omni3D (Objectron, SUN RGB-D, ARKitScene).
 - **6D Pose Estimation:** Omni6DPose (SOPE, ROPE).
 - **Manipulation-centric Perception:** BOP Challenge (YCB-V, LineMOD, HOT3D).
 - This mix of real and synthetic data aims for robust 3D geometry and lighting awareness.
2. **Robotic Trajectory Pre-training (1.55M trajectories):**
 - **AgibotWorld Beta (Real):** 1M trajectories from 100 isomorphic robots across 200 tasks and 87 atomic skills.
 - **InternData-A1 (Sim):** 550K trajectories across heterogeneous platforms (ARX, Aloha, Franka), spanning 4 embodiments and 227 scenes.
 - All end-effector poses are transformed into a unified camera-centric frame via calibration.

#### 3.7. Implementation Details

The model is optimized using AdamW with a cosine learning rate schedule (peak 5x10^-5, min 2.5x10^-6, 1,000-step warmup). Training is conducted on 16 NVIDIA H20 GPUs with bfloat16 precision, using a per-GPU batch size of 8. Both 3D spatial grounding and trajectory estimation tasks are completed within 2 days each, aiming for reduced GPU hours compared to previous VLA training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Pose-VLA encompassed 3D grounding benchmarks, simulation benchmarks (RoboTwin 2.0 and LIBERO), and real-world robotic tasks, supported by ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Evaluation in 3D Grounding Benchmarks

- **Metrics:** Average Precision (AP) with an IoU threshold of 0.15 (mAP@0.15), consistent with Qwen3-VL and Seed1.5-VL. Evaluation was conducted on SUN RGB-D and Objectron subsets of Omni3D.
- **Results:**
 - **Objectron:** Pose-VLA achieved an AP15 of 87.3, representing a 16.1% absolute improvement over Qwen3-VL (235B, Thinking) and outperforming closed-source models like Seed1.5-VL and Gemini-2.0-Pro. This indicates strong performance in handling diverse daily objects relevant for manipulation.
 - **SUN RGB-D:** Pose-VLA scored 45.5, outperforming all open-source variants (e.g., Qwen3-VL, VST-SFT) and comparable to Gemini Robotics-ER.
- **Analysis:** The performance on Objectron highlights robust capability in geometric localization for individual objects, while competitive results on SUN RGB-D demonstrate general indoor scene understanding. Qualitative results illustrated improved generalization to unseen scenarios (tabletop layouts, robotic workspaces) with precise localization and orientation estimation, which baselines struggled with. This supports the effectiveness of using auxiliary spatial priors and diverse 3D datasets.

#### 4.2. Evaluation in Simulation Benchmarks

Pose-VLA was evaluated on RoboTwin 2.0 and LIBERO, with RGB-only input to ensure fair comparison with baselines.

- **RoboTwin 2.0:**
 - **Setup:** Multi-task training on 2,500 demonstrations in clean scenes (50/task) and 25,000 in randomized scenes (500/task). All models were fine-tuned from pre-trained checkpoints for 80K steps with a batch size of 32.
 - **Metrics:** Average success rate over 100 evaluation trials per task in "Easy" and "Hard" scenarios.
 - **Results:** Pose-VLA achieved an average success rate of 79.1% in the "Hard" setting, outperforming π0 by 14.0% and the vanilla PaliGemma baseline by over 45%. In "Easy" scenarios, it achieved 79.91%. This indicated that spatial pre-training helps mitigate feature collapse.
- **LIBERO:**
 - **Setup:** Evaluation on four task suites (LIBERO-Spatial, -Object, -Goal, -Long), each with 10 tasks and 500 demonstrations.
 - **Metrics:** Success rates (%).
 - **Results:** Pose-VLA achieved an overall average success rate of 96.0%, surpassing π0 (94.1%) and ranking second only to π0.5 (96.8%). In the LIBERO-Long suite, which requires multi-stage reasoning, Pose-VLA attained 92.4%, tying with π0.5.
- **Analysis:** These simulation results suggest that integrating 3D spatial priors enhances robustness against visual perturbations and provides the structural consistency needed for long-horizon planning, leading to superior cross-task generalization.

#### 4.3. Evaluation in Real-world Tasks

- **Setup:** Dual-arm Xtrainer robot with RealSense D455 (head-mounted) and D405 (Eye-on-Hand) depth cameras.
- **Tasks:** Four challenging tasks: Stacking (nested bowls), Hanging (mug on peg), Long-horizon Interaction (drawer, pen, close drawer), and Deformable Object Manipulation (folding towel). 100 demonstrations per task were collected for embodiment alignment.
- **Metrics:** Success rate over 60 trials per task (20 trials per random seed).
- **Results:** Pose-VLA achieved an average success rate of 81.25%, outperforming vanilla PaliGemma (29.6%) and π0.5 (71.65%). It consistently matched or surpassed π0.5 across all tasks, with notable improvements in Task 2 (78.3% vs. 53.3%) and Task 3 (81.7% vs. 73.3%).
- **Analysis:** An ablation showed that removing depth information significantly reduced performance (e.g., a 15.0% drop in Task 3 from 81.7% to 66.7%), confirming the importance of explicit 3D geometry for precise spatial alignment and minimizing execution ambiguity in real-world settings.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Geometric Modalities on 3D Grounding (Table IV):**
 - **SUN RGB-D:** Integrating depth improved AP15 from 39.0 (RGB Only) to 45.1 (RGB+Depth). The full model (RGB+Raymap+Depth) achieved 45.5.
 - **Objectron:** Performance gains were less pronounced, with RGB Only at 86.4 and the Full Model at 87.3, attributed to the sparser depth context in Objectron.
 - **Conclusion:** The combination of Raymap and Depth demonstrated synergy for best overall performance, especially in complex indoor layouts.

- **Incremental Ablation on RoboTwin 2.0 (Table V):**
 - **+ 3D Data:** Incorporating non-robotic 3D grounding data alone improved the vanilla PaliGemma + Action-Expert baseline by +35.7% (Hard SR).
 - **+ 3D Data & Robotic Data:** Further adding robotic data provided additional gains of +2.9% (Hard SR).
 - **Action Space (Joint -> EE pose):** Shifting from joint angles to end-effector (EE) pose resulted in over +7.0% average gain, suggesting better leverage of learned geometric features.
 - **Action Space (Cam -> Base):** Predicting actions in the base frame led to a -2.4% drop in Hard scenarios, indicating that camera-centric representations offer more robustness.
 - **Conclusion:** Pre-training on 3D spatial tasks provides a robust geometric foundation, and EE-pose, camera-centric action representations align better with the learned priors.

- **Orthogonal Ablation on RoboTwin 2.0 (Table VI):**
 - **VLM Backbone (Qwen3PI vs Pose-VLA):** Qwen3PI, despite having state-of-the-art 3D grounding capabilities, lagged Pose-VLA by over 20% in success rate when only post-trained. This indicates the necessity of explicit pose-centric pre-training, not just a powerful VLM.
 - **Data Mixture:** "Robotic data only" significantly degraded results (68.3% Hard SR vs 79.1% for Full Model), while "3D data only" maintained competitive performance (76.2% Hard SR), suggesting generic 3D vision data is a primary driver of spatial capabilities.
 - **Tokenization (OpenVLA binning vs Pose-VLA tokens):** Reverting to OpenVLA's uniform binning for delta actions resulted in inferior performance (65.9% Hard SR vs 79.1% for Pose-VLA), validating Pose-VLA's non-uniform, global spatial tokenization for precision and data integration.

#### 4.5. T-SNE Visualization of VLM Features

T-SNE visualizations of Vision-Language (VL) features showed that Pose-VLA maintained well-separated clusters for target tasks, indicating superior inter-task discriminability and intra-task consistency. In contrast, PaliGemma-with-Expert exhibited significant overlap, suggesting representation collapse without specialized spatial pre-training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Pose-VLA presents a framework that re-centers Vision-Language-Action (VLA) learning around a universal pose representation, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of embodied intelligence:

1. **Addressing Core VLA Limitations:** The work directly tackles persistent challenges in VLA models, namely feature collapse and low training efficiency stemming from the misalignment between VLM pre-training (semantic focus) and robotic action requirements (fine-grained 3D state). By explicitly integrating 3D spatial priors, Pose-VLA enables VLMs to capture the subtle geometric variations critical for manipulation.

2. **Generalizable Robotic Control:** The introduction of discrete pose tokens as a universal interface allows for seamless integration of spatial grounding from diverse 3D datasets (both non-robotic and robotic). This decoupled pre-training paradigm establishes fundamental spatial and motion priors in a camera-centric space, leading to more robust and generalizable policies for downstream robotic tasks. The state-of-the-art results on RoboTwin 2.0 and competitive performance on LIBERO, alongside robust real-world generalization across varied objects with limited demonstrations (as few as 100 per task), substantiate this claim.

3. **Enhanced Data Efficiency:** By transforming the VLM into a geometrically-aware backbone, Pose-VLA significantly reduces the amount of embodiment-specific demonstration data required for policy adaptation. The ablation studies demonstrated that large-scale non-robotic 3D data, rather than solely robotic data, is a primary driver for the framework's spatial capabilities, offering an efficient pathway to acquire embodied priors. This lowers the barrier to deploying new robotic skills by alleviating the need for extensive, costly data collection.

4. **Unified Physical Vocabulary:** The consistent camera-centric observation space and the unified pose tokenization scheme create a "universal physical vocabulary" across perception and action. This allows the model to leverage rich 3D knowledge for precise robotic control, improving 3D grounding accuracy, robust execution in challenging scenarios, and multi-stage reasoning for long-horizon tasks.

5. **New Direction for VLM Pre-training:** The paper advocates for a shift from purely VQA-based VLM pre-training towards methods that instill explicit embodied awareness. By demonstrating that 3D pose pre-training establishes a vital geometric foundation for control, this research suggests a compelling direction for developing VLM backbones inherently grounded in the physical world, which could accelerate the development of more capable and adaptable general-purpose robots.

**Potential Impacts:**
- **Faster Development of Robotic Skills:** The reduced data requirements for new tasks can significantly speed up the deployment and adaptation of robotic systems in various environments.
- **Broader Generalization:** Robots trained with Pose-VLA could exhibit improved generalization to novel objects, scenes, and tasks, making them more versatile for real-world applications.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** This work contributes to foundational research in embodied AI by providing a method to bridge the gap between high-level language understanding and low-level physical interaction.

**Limitations and Future Work (as noted by authors):**
- **Computational Constraints:** Full potential of large-scale 3D data scaling and high-efficiency 3D annotation for robotic videos remains partially unexplored.
- **Deformable Object Representation:** Current pre-training focuses on rigid objects; explicit formulation of deformable states (e.g., 3D keypoints, dense grasp annotations) into pose tokens is a future step.
- **Dexterous Manipulation:** Extending spatial priors from parallel-jaw grippers to multi-fingered dexterous hands is an area for future development.
- **Real-world Scale:** Scaling real-world experiments to a broader diversity of behaviors in unstructured environments, transitioning from static tabletops to mobile and egocentric manipulation.
- **Semantic Reasoning Trade-off:** Acknowledging a trade-off where maximizing spatial priors may naturally impact the backbone's original semantic reasoning when facing complex semantic distractors, suggesting future work on semantic-preserving training paradigms.

In conclusion, Pose-VLA represents a methodical approach to equipping Vision-Language Models with the intrinsic 3D awareness required for robust and generalizable robotic control, marking a step towards more capable and adaptable embodied intelligent systems.
