# Quagmires in SFT-RL Post-Training: When High SFT Scores Mislead and What to Use Instead

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from FAIR at Meta and Virginia Tech found that high Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) scores often poorly predict subsequent Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) performance in Large Language Models. Their work introduces generalization loss on validation examples and Pass@large k accuracy as effective predictors for RLVR outcomes, enhancing predictability and optimizing resource use in LLM post-training workflows.

## Problem

- The common assumption that higher Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) performance reliably translates to superior final outcomes after Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) is often incorrect.
- This 'SFT metric trap' leads to substantial wasted computational resources and time, as expensive RL stages are often initiated from suboptimal SFT-trained models.
- Current SFT optimization methods, frequently carried out in isolation, lack the ability to consistently predict or improve downstream RL performance, creating inefficiencies in LLM development.

## Method

- The study empirically investigated the ==SFT-then-RLVR== (GRPO) paradigm across hundreds of Llama3-8B, Mistral-Nemo-12B, and Qwen3-4B-base models, conducting extensive evaluations on 7 math benchmarks using over 1M GPU hours.
- They systematically identified specific failure modes where SFT performance metrics diverge from actual RL outcomes through both 'dataset-level' and 'instance-level' experimental scenarios.
- Two new metrics were proposed and validated to reliably predict post-RL performance: generalization loss on validation examples during SFT and Pass@large k accuracy of the post-SFT model.

## Results

- Existing SFT `Pass@1` accuracy metrics demonstrated low predictive power for final RL outcomes (e.g., R² of **0.43** for Llama3-**8B**), failing to reliably rank which SFT models would achieve the best post-RL performance.
- The proposed combination of generalization loss and `Pass@large k` significantly improved RL outcome prediction for dataset-level scenarios, achieving a Spearman correlation of **0.97** and an R² of **0.94** for Llama3-**8B**.
- `Pass@large k` alone proved to be a robust predictor for instance-level scenarios (selecting optimal SFT datasets), showing Spearman correlation improvements of up to **0.28** (from **0.70** to **0.98** for Mistral-NeMo-**12B**) and R² improvements of up to **0.34** (from **0.58** to **0.92** for Llama3-**8B**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimizing SFT solely for high `Pass@1` accuracy can be misleading, as models appearing strong after SFT (especially from simpler or over-trained data) often underperform after the subsequent RLVR stage.
> - The 'SFT metric trap' stems from a misalignment: SFT focuses on fitting a specific data distribution, while RLVR aims to explore the solution space to distill a broad capability into a robust `Pass@1` solution.
> - Generalization loss during SFT signals overfitting, which limits a model's capacity for later RL improvement, whereas a high `Pass@large k` (the ability to generate multiple correct solutions) indicates strong underlying reasoning potential that RLVR can effectively compress.
