# Learning Robot Visual Navigation in Crowds via Intention-Aware Scene Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces iCrowdNav, a method for visual robot navigation in crowds that constructs intention-aware scene representations by integrating spatio-temporal environmental occupancy and 3D human pose information. The approach enables robots to learn more proactive and socially compliant navigation policies, demonstrating superior performance in simulations and successful zero-shot transfer to real-world crowded environments.

## Problem

- Existing Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) methods for crowd navigation often rely on simplified scene representations, neglecting rich visual cues from humans and the environment.
- Robots struggle to proactively infer subtle human motion intentions from basic observations like 2D positions and velocities, leading to suboptimal or overly conservative navigation.
- A lack of comprehensive understanding of social and structural environmental contexts often necessitates extensive manual tuning of reward functions for human-robot interaction patterns.

## Method

- **iCrowdNav** processes multi-timestep RGB-D images and detected 3D human poses through a feature extraction module to create '==intention-aware==' scene representations.
- A Spatio-Temporal Encoder uses a Lift & Splat technique to convert multi-view RGB-D into ego-centric Bird's-Eye View (BEV) features, capturing environmental occupancy and dynamics.
- An 'Intent-Interact Former' (I²Former) leverages attention mechanisms to infer human motion intentions from 3D poses and model complex human-robot interactions, which are then fused with BEV and robot proprioceptive states for DRL policy training.

## Results

- iCrowdNav achieved superior success rates (SR), shorter navigation times (NT), and lower time in private zones (TPZ) across diverse simulated crowd environments compared to state-of-the-art baselines, indicating safer and more efficient navigation.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contribution of both the Intent-Interact Former for intention inference and the spatio-temporal BEV encoder for scene awareness to the overall performance.
- The method demonstrated successful zero-shot sim-to-real deployment on a physical robot, exhibiting robust and socially compliant avoidance behaviors in real-world scenarios such as outdoor gyms, subway stations, and shopping malls.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Incorporating rich visual cues, specifically spatio-temporal BEV features and 3D human poses, is crucial for enabling robots to effectively infer human intentions and understand environmental constraints in crowded settings.
> - Attention-based mechanisms are effective for modeling both implicit behavioral intentions within individual pedestrian poses and interactions between the robot and surrounding pedestrians.
> - Learning navigation policies directly from intention-aware visual representations can facilitate proactive and socially compliant behaviors without explicit, finely tuned human-robot interaction reward terms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous robot navigation in dynamic, human-populated environments represents a significant challenge within robotics. Existing research in this domain has explored various approaches, including rule-based methods and Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL). While DRL methods have shown promise in learning complex crowd dynamics and interactions, many of them have traditionally relied on simplified scene representations. These simplifications often involve treating pedestrians as basic 2D points and overlooking detailed visual cues from both humans and the environment. This limited representational capacity can hinder a robot's ability to infer subtle human intentions and account for intricate environmental constraints, leading to suboptimal or overly conservative navigation policies.

The broader landscape of robot navigation increasingly emphasizes the need for comprehensive understanding of social contexts and rich environmental features. Approaches leveraging Bird's-Eye View (BEV) representations have gained traction in autonomous driving and general robotic navigation for their ability to consolidate multi-view observations into a spatially consistent map. Similarly, human motion prediction and social navigation research have identified the importance of human behavioral cues, such as gait patterns and body poses, in inferring intentions.

This work positions itself at the intersection of these trends by addressing the limitations of simplified scene representations in DRL-based crowd navigation. It aims to integrate rich visual information, specifically spatio-temporal environmental occupancy and 3D human pose data, into an "intention-aware" scene representation. By doing so, it seeks to enable DRL policies to make more informed, proactive, and socially compliant decisions, moving beyond basic collision avoidance to a more nuanced understanding of human-robot interactions in complex, real-world crowded settings. The paper leverages advancements in visual perception (e.g., 3D pose estimation, BEV feature extraction) and attention mechanisms (Transformers) to construct these richer representations, thereby contributing to the development of more robust and adaptive crowd navigation systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust vision-based robot navigation method capable of operating safely and efficiently in dense, dynamic crowd environments. This objective is driven by several key motivations stemming from the limitations of current approaches:

1. **Addressing Deficiencies in Existing Scene Representations:** Current DRL-based navigation methods frequently employ low-level state representations. For pedestrians, this often translates to simple 2D positions and velocities. For the environment, representations are commonly limited to 2D binary occupancy maps or single-line LiDAR scans. These simplified representations neglect a wealth of visual information available in raw images, such as subtle human behaviors (e.g., gestures, head turns, body poses) that can signal intent, and detailed spatial and semantic features of the environment. The authors contend that such omissions limit the generalizability and effectiveness of navigation policies, especially in complex real-world scenarios like constrained passages where rapid and accurate intention inference is crucial.

2. **Enhancing Human Intention Inference:** A significant motivation is the need for robots to infer human motion intentions proactively. Unlike environments with static obstacles, human crowds exhibit dynamic and reactive behaviors that do not adhere to deterministic rules. Therefore, merely observing positions and velocities is insufficient for foresighted decision-making. The research is motivated by the hypothesis that 3D human poses provide reliable visual cues for inferring motion tendencies, such as whether a pedestrian intends to walk straight, turn, or yield. Incorporating these cues is expected to enable more proactive and socially compliant navigation.

3. **Capturing Holistic Behavioral and Environmental Contexts:** The work seeks to capture a more comprehensive understanding of the social and structural environment. This involves recognizing that robot and human motion patterns are constrained by the physical environment (e.g., walls, furniture) and that human behavior is influenced by social norms. By integrating both spatio-temporal environmental features and human intention features, the goal is to create a scene representation that implicitly encodes these complex interactions, eliminating the need for extensive manual tuning of reward functions for human-robot interaction patterns.

4. **Enabling Vision-Based Navigation with Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A practical motivation is to develop a method that can learn effective navigation policies directly from egocentric visual observations (RGB-D cameras) and demonstrate its applicability in real-world settings without further retraining. This necessitates a representation that is compact, informative, and robust enough to bridge the simulation-to-reality gap, overcoming challenges like partial observability and the curse of dimensionality inherent in raw image-to-action mappings.

To achieve these, the central research question posed is: "How to learn scene representations from egocentric vision that better preserve visual cues for crowd navigation policy training?" The proposed solution involves augmenting standard Bird's-Eye View (BEV) representations with human pose features, thus forming "intention-aware scene representations" that facilitate more effective DRL policy learning for safe and efficient crowd navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, iCrowdNav, addresses vision-based robot navigation in crowded environments through a three-component architecture: a feature extraction module, a feature fusion module, and a Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) network. The system takes multi-timestep RGB-D images, detected pedestrian poses, and the robot's internal states as input.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The navigation problem is framed as a partially observed Markov Decision Process (POMDP). The robot's partial observation `o_t` at time `t` includes egocentric images and its internal states. The action `a_t` comprises linear and angular velocities. The objective is to optimize a navigation policy `π_θ` using DRL to maximize expected cumulative rewards, balancing navigation efficiency and safety. The total reward `r_t` is a sum of a navigation reward `r_t_nav` and a trajectory-smoothing reward `r_t_ω`. The navigation reward encourages progress towards the goal, penalizes collisions with obstacles/pedestrians, and provides a small penalty for proximity to private zones. The trajectory-smoothing reward penalizes high angular velocities to reduce jitter.

**B. Intention-Aware Scene Representations**
This core component is responsible for processing raw visual inputs and robot states into a rich, compact representation suitable for DRL. It consists of two main modules:

1. **Spatio-Temporal Encoder:** This module extracts occupancy features of the scene from multi-view RGB-D observations.
 - **Input Processing:** At each timestep `t`, multi-view RGB-D images from `N_c` cameras are fed into a pre-trained ResNet-18 to extract image features `e_t`. Corresponding depth images `d_t` are downsampled.
 - **Lift & Splat:** Image features `e_t` are lifted into 3D using the measured depth and known camera parameters. These 3D features are then projected and collapsed along the vertical dimension into a unified ego-centric Bird's-Eye View (BEV) coordinate frame, producing a local BEV feature map `x_t_bev`.
 - **Temporal Encoding:** To capture temporal dynamics, BEV representations from a window `τ` (e.g., `t-T` to `t`) are aggregated. Historical BEV features are aligned to the current frame `t` using ego-motion compensation. These aligned features are then processed by a 3D convolutional temporal encoder, pre-trained on the nuScenes dataset, to yield spatio-temporal BEV features `s_t_bev`.
 - **BEV Encoding:** Finally, a 2D convolutional BEV encoder with residual connections and pooling layers further processes `s_t_bev` to extract a scene representation `z_t_bev`.

2. **Intent-Interact Former (I²Former):** This attention-based module infers human motion intentions from 3D human poses and models human-robot interactions.
 - **Pose Detection and Lifting:** 2D human poses are first detected from RGB images using Ultralytics YOLO. These 2D poses are then lifted to 3D using camera intrinsics, extrinsics, and depth images, resulting in 3D joint coordinates `Θ_t` for `N_p` pedestrians, each with 17 joints. Occluded keypoints are zero-padded.
 - **Pose Encoder:** An MLP `ϕ_p` transforms the raw 3D joint coordinates into higher-dimensional embeddings `ˆΘ_t`.
 - **IntentFormer:** This sub-module employs multi-head self-attention (MHSA) to model relationships among all joints within each pedestrian's pose. This allows the system to infer implicit behavioral intentions (e.g., turning, yielding). It includes a Feed-Forward Network (FFN), residual connections, and LayerNorm (LN). Attention pooling (AttnPool) is applied at the end to adaptively weigh joint importance, yielding implicit intention features `f_t_ped` for each pedestrian.
 - **InteractFormer:** This sub-module uses multi-head cross-attention (MHCA) to model interactions between the robot and surrounding pedestrians. The robot's state embedding `e_t_robot` (derived from its internal states by an MLP `ϕ_sp`) serves as the query, attending to the pedestrians' intention features `f_t_ped`. This process helps the robot form a global understanding of surrounding intentions relative to its own state, producing interaction representations `z_t_interact`.

**C. Feature Fusion:**
To form the final DRL state embedding `z_t`, the robot's proprioceptive state `s_t` (goal direction, distance to goal, velocity) is first encoded by an MLP `ϕ_s` into `z_t_state`. This `z_t_state` is then concatenated with the `z_t_bev` (environmental occupancy) and `z_t_interact` (human-robot interaction) features. A fusion network `ϕ_f` (MLP) processes this concatenated vector to produce the complete DRL state embedding `z_t`.

**D. Deep Reinforcement Learning:**
The Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm is utilized for online training of the navigation policy. All modules (excluding the pre-trained RGB backbone and temporal encoder, which are frozen) are jointly optimized during DRL training. The reward function is designed to implicitly guide the robot towards safe, collision-free, and efficient navigation, relying on the rich scene representations to capture complex social contexts rather than explicit, finely tuned human-robot interaction reward terms.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated iCrowdNav through extensive simulation experiments and real-world deployment.

**A. Simulation Experiments**
Experiments were conducted using a Clearpath Dingo robot in Isaac Sim, featuring varied human-centric environments (SocNav-Gym) with pedestrians moving according to the Social Force Model (SFM).

1. **Crowd Navigation:**
 - **Environments:** Three test scenarios were used: an office lobby (7.0 m wide), a hospital corridor (4.0 m wide), and a warehouse (2.5 m wide), at both low (0.1 peds/m²) and high (0.2 peds/m²) crowd densities.
 - **Metrics:** Performance was assessed using Success Rate (SR), Navigation Time (NT), Path Length (PL), and Time in Private Zone (TPZ).
 - **Ablation Study:**
 - The full iCrowdNav method consistently outperformed ablated versions (without I²Former, and without the spatio-temporal BEV encoder) across all environments and densities.
 - Removing the I²Former resulted in lower SR and increased TPZ, indicating compromised intention inference.
 - Removing the BEV module led to reduced scene awareness, less flexible navigation, more frequent pauses, and higher intrusion into pedestrian space, suggesting its role in providing spatial and semantic environmental features.
 - **Comparison with Baselines:** iCrowdNav demonstrated superior performance compared to state-of-the-art methods: DRL-VO, SARL*-OM, ViNT, and DWA.
 - It consistently achieved the highest SR, particularly in dense and constrained environments.
 - It navigated more efficiently (shorter NT and PL) while maintaining the lowest TPZ, indicating safer and more socially compliant behavior.
 - Performance degradation in narrower, more crowded settings was slower for iCrowdNav compared to baselines.
 - Qualitative analysis of trajectories (Fig. 6) showed iCrowdNav initiating early avoidance maneuvers and respecting social space, unlike baselines which exhibited detours, inflexible navigation, or hesitation.

2. **Long-horizon Comparison:**
 - **Environments:** Two large-scale scenarios (hospital and office, >20m navigation) with topological maps were used to evaluate long-distance crowd navigation.
 - **Results:**
 - iCrowdNav again outperformed its ablated variants and all baselines.
 - In the office environment, it achieved an SR of 0.95 and significantly reduced TPZ (1.70s) compared to SARL*-OM (7.96s), indicating safer and less intrusive navigation.
 - In the hospital environment, it achieved an SR of 0.79 with the lowest TPZ (0.42s), demonstrating robust and safe navigation even in narrow passages over long distances.
 - The visual navigation baseline ViNT consistently showed poorer results across all metrics compared to iCrowdNav.

**B. Real-World Validation**
iCrowdNav was deployed on a physical robot with an onboard RTX 2060 GPU, achieving an inference rate of 15 Hz. Real-world experiments were conducted in diverse, complex scenes.

- **Outdoor Gym:** The robot actively adjusted its heading to yield space for non-cooperative pedestrians, demonstrating smooth and socially compliant avoidance behaviors.
- **Subway Station:** In dense crowds and constrained spaces, the robot reliably perceived limited free space and steered towards safer regions, avoiding both moving pedestrians and static obstacles.
- **Shopping Mall:** During a 109.49 m long-distance navigation task in a constrained environment, the robot rapidly adjusted its trajectory even when a pedestrian unexpectedly emerged from a blind corner, maintaining safe separation and stable motion at an average speed of 0.76 m/s.
- The real-world observations validated the robustness and applicability of the method, showcasing successful zero-shot sim-to-real deployment.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in this paper, iCrowdNav, contributes to the field of robotic navigation by proposing a method for visual crowd navigation that integrates intention-aware scene representations. This work addresses a critical challenge in deploying autonomous robots in human-populated dynamic environments, where effective perception of both environmental constraints and human behavior is crucial for safe and efficient operation.

The primary significance of iCrowdNav lies in its departure from oversimplified scene representations commonly used in Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) for crowd navigation. By explicitly encoding rich visual cues—specifically, spatio-temporal Bird's-Eye View (BEV) features for environmental context and 3D human poses for inferring intentions—the method aims to enable DRL policies to make more informed and proactive decisions. The design of the Intent-Interact Former (I²Former) is noteworthy, as it provides a mechanism for modeling both intra-pedestrian intentions from joint relationships and inter-agent interactions between the robot and pedestrians using attention mechanisms.

The reported experimental results, both in extensive simulations and real-world deployments, suggest that iCrowdNav offers a more robust and socially compliant navigation capability compared to several baseline methods. The consistent superiority in metrics such as success rate, navigation efficiency, and minimal intrusion into pedestrian private zones, particularly in dense and constrained environments, indicates a potential advancement in the state of the art for vision-based crowd navigation. The demonstration of zero-shot sim-to-real deployment is a significant practical impact, as it reduces the need for costly and time-consuming real-world data collection and retraining, thereby accelerating the development and deployment cycle of socially aware robots.

The potential impact of this work extends to various applications of service robots in public and private human-centric spaces, such as shopping malls, hospitals, offices, and airports. Robots equipped with iCrowdNav could operate more safely, efficiently, and with greater social acceptance, performing tasks like delivery, guidance, or assistance. By enabling robots to implicitly understand human intentions and environmental contexts from visual input, this research moves towards more intelligent and human-compatible autonomous systems. While the authors acknowledge limitations regarding severe occlusions and limited camera field-of-view in ultra-dense scenes, suggesting future work on multi-modal representations, the current approach represents a step in improving robot autonomy in complex social environments.
