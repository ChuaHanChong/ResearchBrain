# Beyond the Destination: A Novel Benchmark for Exploration-Aware Embodied Question Answering

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces EXPRESS-Bench, a new benchmark for Embodied Question Answering (EQA) that emphasizes exploration-aware evaluation, alongside the Fine-EQA model which combines frontier-based and goal-oriented exploration. The proposed system achieves superior performance on the new benchmark, reaching 40.55% correctness (C) and 16.22% exploration-answer consistency (E_path), while also improving accuracy and efficiency on existing EQA datasets.

## Problem

- Existing Embodied Question Answering (EQA) datasets suffer from biases, high ambiguity, and lack explicit support for genuinely active exploration, leading to models exploiting spurious correlations rather than true environmental interaction.
- Current EQA exploration strategies, such as frontier-based methods, are often inefficient in complex and cluttered environments, resulting in redundant visits or failure to conduct fine-grained exploration of task-relevant regions.
- Traditional EQA evaluation metrics primarily focus on answer similarity, failing to reliably assess whether an answer is grounded in the agent's actual observations or to detect model hallucinations and evaluate exploration efficiency.

## Method

- The authors introduce **EXPRESS-Bench**, a large-scale, high-quality EQA benchmark dataset built using the ==Habitat simulator== and ==HM3D==, providing full exploration trajectories and unique question-answer pairs to promote active exploration.
- They propose **Fine-EQA**, a ==two-stage hybrid exploration model== that integrates ==frontier-based exploration== for broad environmental coverage with ==goal-oriented exploration== for fine-grained investigation of task-relevant regions.
- A novel ==Exploration-Answer Consistency (EAC)== metric is developed to assess both answer correctness and its grounding in the agent's observed environment, incorporating exploration path efficiency to ensure faithful evaluation.

## Results

- EXPRESS-Bench, containing **777 trajectories** and **2,044 question-answer pairs**, demonstrated lower ambiguity (**9.7%**) compared to OpenEQA (**62.3%**) and HM-EQA (**35.6%**), enhancing evaluation reliability.
- Fine-EQA achieved the highest performance on EXPRESS-Bench, with **40.55%** correctness (C), **63.95%** correctness without grounding (C*), **16.22%** Exploration-Answer Consistency (E_path), and the lowest target distance (d_T) of **6.43m**.
- Fine-EQA outperformed existing methods on other datasets, achieving **43.27%** C' and **29.16%** E' on OpenEQA (vs. GPT-4V's **41.8%** C' and **7.5%** E'), and increasing accuracy to **56.0%** while reducing path length by **42%** on HM-EQA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid exploration strategy that combines broad frontier-based exploration with targeted, goal-oriented investigation of task-relevant regions significantly improves both answer accuracy and exploration efficiency in embodied tasks.
> - Explicitly evaluating the grounding of an agent's answer in its actual observations, rather than just answer similarity, is crucial for assessing true embodied intelligence and detecting unfaithful reasoning.
> - Creating high-quality, less ambiguous datasets with detailed ground truth exploration paths is essential for developing and reliably benchmarking EQA agents, pushing them towards genuine environmental interaction and reasoning.
