# Beyond the Destination: A Novel Benchmark for Exploration-Aware Embodied Question Answering

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces EXPRESS-Bench, a new benchmark for Embodied Question Answering (EQA) that emphasizes exploration-aware evaluation, alongside the Fine-EQA model which combines frontier-based and goal-oriented exploration. The proposed system achieves superior performance on the new benchmark, reaching 40.55% correctness (C) and 16.22% exploration-answer consistency (E_path), while also improving accuracy and efficiency on existing EQA datasets.

## Problem

- Existing Embodied Question Answering (EQA) datasets suffer from biases, high ambiguity, and lack explicit support for genuinely active exploration, leading to models exploiting spurious correlations rather than true environmental interaction.
- Current EQA exploration strategies, such as frontier-based methods, are often inefficient in complex and cluttered environments, resulting in redundant visits or failure to conduct fine-grained exploration of task-relevant regions.
- Traditional EQA evaluation metrics primarily focus on answer similarity, failing to reliably assess whether an answer is grounded in the agent's actual observations or to detect model hallucinations and evaluate exploration efficiency.

## Method

- The authors introduce **EXPRESS-Bench**, a large-scale, high-quality EQA benchmark dataset built using the ==Habitat simulator== and ==HM3D==, providing full exploration trajectories and unique question-answer pairs to promote active exploration.
- They propose **Fine-EQA**, a ==two-stage hybrid exploration model== that integrates ==frontier-based exploration== for broad environmental coverage with ==goal-oriented exploration== for fine-grained investigation of task-relevant regions.
- A novel ==Exploration-Answer Consistency (EAC)== metric is developed to assess both answer correctness and its grounding in the agent's observed environment, incorporating exploration path efficiency to ensure faithful evaluation.

## Results

- EXPRESS-Bench, containing **777 trajectories** and **2,044 question-answer pairs**, demonstrated lower ambiguity (**9.7%**) compared to OpenEQA (**62.3%**) and HM-EQA (**35.6%**), enhancing evaluation reliability.
- Fine-EQA achieved the highest performance on EXPRESS-Bench, with **40.55%** correctness (C), **63.95%** correctness without grounding (C*), **16.22%** Exploration-Answer Consistency (E_path), and the lowest target distance (d_T) of **6.43m**.
- Fine-EQA outperformed existing methods on other datasets, achieving **43.27%** C' and **29.16%** E' on OpenEQA (vs. GPT-4V's **41.8%** C' and **7.5%** E'), and increasing accuracy to **56.0%** while reducing path length by **42%** on HM-EQA.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid exploration strategy that combines broad frontier-based exploration with targeted, goal-oriented investigation of task-relevant regions significantly improves both answer accuracy and exploration efficiency in embodied tasks.
> - Explicitly evaluating the grounding of an agent's answer in its actual observations, rather than just answer similarity, is crucial for assessing true embodied intelligence and detecting unfaithful reasoning.
> - Creating high-quality, less ambiguous datasets with detailed ground truth exploration paths is essential for developing and reliably benchmarking EQA agents, pushing them towards genuine environmental interaction and reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied Question Answering (EQA) is an area of embodied intelligence that requires an agent to navigate a 3D environment, gather visual information, and perform multi-step reasoning to answer questions. This task differs from traditional Visual Question Answering (VQA), which relies on static images or pre-existing knowledge bases, by demanding active exploration and sequential decision-making. EQA is considered a challenging problem with implications for applications such as robotics, virtual assistants, and autonomous navigation.

Current EQA research faces several limitations. Existing EQA datasets, often manually created or rule-based, have been identified as costly, inflexible, and prone to biases. These biases can lead to "unfaithful question answering," where models exploit spurious correlations or prior knowledge embedded in the dataset to generate answers without actual exploration, thereby raising concerns about their credibility. Furthermore, ambiguity in question design and scene complexity in some datasets can result in non-unique answers, complicating performance evaluation. Common evaluation metrics, which primarily measure answer similarity, are often insufficient to assess answer reliability, detect model hallucinations, or evaluate exploration efficiency and quality.

Regarding exploration strategies, many existing EQA models utilize frontier-based exploration. While effective in open environments, this approach can be inefficient in cluttered or constrained spaces, leading to redundant visits to semantically important regions without acquiring new information. These methods often lack mechanisms for fine-grained exploration of task-relevant areas, which is necessary for gathering detailed environmental context for accurate reasoning.

This research aims to address these identified limitations by introducing an exploration-aware EQA task, a new benchmark dataset designed to promote active and rational exploration, a hybrid exploration model to improve efficiency, and a novel evaluation metric to ensure faithful assessment of both exploration and reasoning capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance Embodied Question Answering by developing a more robust framework that explicitly emphasizes and evaluates an agent's exploration capabilities alongside its reasoning abilities. To achieve this, the authors identify three key areas for improvement and propose corresponding solutions.

The detailed objectives and motivations are:

1. **To develop a high-quality, large-scale EQA benchmark dataset that supports active exploration and addresses limitations of existing datasets.**
 - **Motivation:** Existing datasets are often criticized for their biases, lack of support for active exploration, and inclusion of prior knowledge that allows models to answer questions without genuine interaction with the environment. Issues such as ambiguous questions or non-unique answers also hinder reliable evaluation. The researchers aim to create a benchmark that overcomes these issues by providing rich, fine-grained information, unique answers, and ground truth actions, enabling a more comprehensive assessment of an agent's dynamic exploration and reasoning.

2. **To propose an efficient and robust exploration framework for EQA agents.**
 - **Motivation:** Current EQA methods often suffer from inefficient exploration capabilities. Frontier-based exploration, while a common strategy, struggles in complex, cluttered environments and may lead to redundant exploration or failure to comprehensively investigate task-relevant areas. The goal is to develop a method that enhances navigation efficiency and enables fine-grained exploration of specific, task-relevant regions, thereby improving the agent's ability to gather necessary information effectively.

3. **To introduce a novel evaluation metric that ensures faithful assessment of EQA by measuring the alignment between an agent’s exploration process and its generated answers.**
 - **Motivation:** Existing evaluation metrics primarily focus on answer similarity and often fail to assess the "grounding" of an answer in the agent's actual observations. This can lead to inflated performance scores if models hallucinate responses that appear plausible but are not supported by the agent's environmental interaction. A more rigorous metric is needed to detect such ungrounded responses and provide a more accurate measure of an agent's true embodied intelligence.

In summary, the research is motivated by the recognized gap in current EQA systems regarding truly embodied reasoning, efficient exploration, and faithful evaluation. The authors seek to address these by constructing a new benchmark, proposing an advanced exploration model, and refining evaluation methodologies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces EXPRESS-Bench, a new benchmark dataset, the Fine-EQA exploration model, and the Exploration-Answer Consistency (EAC) metric.

#### 3.1. EXPRESS-Bench Dataset Construction

- **Simulator:** The dataset is constructed using the Habitat simulator, leveraging the HM3D dataset, which contains 3D reconstructions of real-world buildings rich in semantic information.
- **Dataset Generation Pipeline:** The process involves three stages:
 - **Stage 1: Trajectory Generation:** Random navigable initial and target positions are sampled within a scene, constrained to the same floor and a step length between 10 and 100. The shortest sequence of atomic actions (`move_forward`, `turn_left`, `turn_right`) is computed as ground truth. At each step, the agent's state (coordinates, orientation, first-person visual observation) is recorded, and a trajectory video is generated.
 - **Stage 2: Question-Answer Pair Generation:** The visual observation at the target location is fed into a multimodal large model (GPT-4o-mini), along with example question-answer pairs. The model generates open-ended questions and corresponding answers from a human perspective, simulating natural conversations in everyday home scenarios.
 - **Stage 3: Data Filtering:** Manual screening is performed to ensure relevance, address non-unique answers (e.g., by retaining questions where the target region is unique or the closest among similar regions), and refine contextual details. A curator performs a final check for consistency.
- **Dataset Statistics:** EXPRESS-Bench comprises 777 exploration trajectories and 2,044 question-trajectory pairs, categorized into seven types: state, knowledge, location, attribute, counting, existence, and object. The average trajectory involves 39.8 steps and covers 6.6 meters.

#### 3.2. Exploration-Answer Consistency (EAC) Metric

The EAC metric is designed to assess both the correctness and the grounding of an agent's answers.
- **Answer Scoring:**
 - **Correctness Score (σ_i):** A VLM (GPT-4o-mini) evaluates the model's response (A_i) against the given question (Q_i), the correct answer (A*_i), and the final environmental image (I_i) on a scale of 1 to 5.
 - **Grounding Evaluation Score (δ_i):** The VLM assesses the relevance of the model's response to the observed environment (I_i). A score of 1 indicates relevant observation and accurate description, 0.5 for relevant observation but incorrect description, and 0 for irrelevant observation or fabricated output.
 - The final answer score is computed as σ_i * δ_i.
- **Overall Metrics:**
 - **Correctness (C):** The average of (σ_i * δ_i) / 5 across all questions, expressed as a percentage.
 - **Exploration-Answer Consistency (E_path):** Measures the exploration efficiency by weighting the correctness C with the ratio of ground truth path length (l_i) to the agent's actual path length (p_i), i.e., C * (l_i / max(p_i, l_i)), expressed as a percentage.
 - **Target Distance (d_T):** The average geodesic distance from the agent's final exploration position to the target location, used to evaluate navigation performance.

#### 3.3. Fine-EQA Model

Fine-EQA is a two-stage EQA framework that integrates frontier-based and goal-oriented exploration strategies.
- **Environmental Representation:** The agent builds a 3D voxel map, projected onto a 2D planar map, to track exploration states and spatial occupancy.
- **Exploration Phase:**
 - **Frontier-Based Exploration (FBE):** This stage focuses on expanding environmental understanding. It constructs a global semantic map (M_sem) by integrating global semantic values (VLM confidence for scene) and local semantic values (VLM priority for sampled navigable points). Frontier points are identified, and composite weights are assigned based on semantic value, unexplored/unoccupied rates, and distance to the current position. The agent then samples the next exploration location probabilistically based on these weights.
 - **Goal-Oriented Exploration (GOE):** This strategy focuses on thoroughly exploring task-relevant regions.
 - **Functional Region Semantic Mapping:** A VLM classifies the current scene into functional regions (e.g., kitchen, bedroom) and creates a functional region semantic map (M_reg), merging spatially proximate points with identical semantic values.
 - **Task-Relevant Region Prioritization:** An LLM (GPT-4) analyzes the question to prioritize relevant functional regions.
 - **Masked Semantic Mapping:** A mask is applied to the global semantic map (M_sem) based on the functional region map (M_reg) and the highest-priority region (r). This creates a masked semantic map (M_masked) that directs the agent's attention to the prioritized region, while visited points within the mask have decreased semantic values to prevent redundant exploration. The agent selects the position with the highest semantic value in M_masked for exploration.
 - **Strategy Switching:** The agent alternates between FBE for coarse-grained exploration and GOE for fine-grained exploration, with a region-specific exploration limit. GPT-4o-mini determines when to stop exploration.
- **Question Answering Phase:** The VLM (GPT-4o-mini) integrates the latest visual information and the question to generate the final answer.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides several key findings regarding the proposed benchmark, model, and metric:

1. **EXPRESS-Bench Characteristics and Validity:**
 - EXPRESS-Bench is presented as the largest EQA dataset, containing 777 exploration trajectories and 2,044 question-trajectory pairs, which offers superior coverage compared to OpenEQA (152 trajectories).
 - The dataset exhibits a lower ambiguity rate (9.7%) compared to HM-EQA (35.6%) and OpenEQA (62.3%), addressing a limitation of previous benchmarks. This, along with the provision of complete exploration trajectories and clear target locations, enhances evaluation reliability and interpretability.

2. **Performance Comparison with State-of-the-art Methods (Table 2):**
 - **Non-Exploratory Agents:** Blind LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-V3, GPT-4) and Socratic Models (LLMs with image captions) showed limited performance, highlighting the necessity of active visual grounding. Socratic models demonstrated slight improvements, but Multi-Frame VLMs performed worse, suggesting that mere aggregation of frames is less effective than targeted information extraction.
 - **Active Exploration Agents:** Agents with active exploration capabilities generally outperformed non-exploratory models.
 - Random Exploration (RE) showed a high C* (correctness without grounding) but a significantly lower C (correctness with grounding) and poor E_path and d_T, indicating reliance on hallucinations and inefficiency.
 - Frontier-Based Exploration (FBE) and Goal-Orientated Exploration (GOE) improved upon RE, demonstrating better exploration efficiency (E_path) and navigation (d_T).
 - **Fine-EQA (Ours):** Achieved the highest performance across all key metrics: C (40.55%), C* (63.95%), E_path (16.22%), and the lowest d_T (6.43m). This indicates that the hybrid exploration strategy effectively enhances both answer accuracy and exploration efficiency.
 - **Human Agent Performance:** Human performance (83.99% C*) significantly surpassed all automated methods, underscoring the remaining gap and the challenging nature of the benchmark.

3. **Experiments on Other Datasets (Tables 3 & 4):**
 - **OpenEQA (A-EQA subset):** Fine-EQA outperformed the GPT-4V model from OpenEQA in C' (43.27% vs 41.8%) and substantially in exploration efficiency E' (29.16% vs 7.5%), primarily because its active exploration strategy avoids indefinite exploration and terminates efficiently.
 - **HM-EQA:** Fine-EQA achieved significant improvements over Explore-EQA in both accuracy (56.0% vs 50.4%) and reduced path length (54.267m vs 93.687m).

4. **Ablation Studies (Tables 5 & 6):**
 - **Module Ablation:** Removing either the FBE or GOE component from Fine-EQA led to a decline in overall performance (C, C*, E_path, d_T), with the FBE module showing a more pronounced impact, highlighting the importance of both components in the hybrid strategy.
 - **VLM Ablation:** Fine-EQA, when integrated with different VLMs (Janus-Pro-7B, Qwen-Vl-Plus, GPT-4o-mini), consistently outperformed other models, with GPT-4o-mini yielding the highest C scores.

5. **Effectiveness of Exploration and Answering (Figures 6, 7, Tables 7):**
 - **Qualitative Analysis (Exploration):** Visualizations of exploration trajectories demonstrated that Fine-EQA generated more strategic and efficient paths, focusing on task-relevant regions, in contrast to the less effective or redundant paths of RE, FBE, and GOE alone.
 - **Qualitative Analysis (Answer Faithfulness):** VLM confidence scores confirmed that Fine-EQA's final observation typically held the highest confidence for accurately answering the question, supporting the faithfulness and exploration-answer consistency of the module.
 - **Quantitative Reliability Study:** Fine-EQA exhibited the best performance across average confidence (ACE), normalized path length (NPL), and normalized path length-weighted confidence (WCE), further validating its faithfulness and efficiency.

6. **Performance by Question Type (Figures 9 & 10):** All models generally performed better on "knowledge" questions and showed larger performance gaps with human agents in "state," "existence," and "counting" categories. Fine-EQA demonstrated strong performance across most question types, ranking among the top models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several advancements in the field of Embodied Question Answering with implications for future development.

Firstly, the introduction of **EXPRESS-Bench** provides a larger and higher-quality benchmark dataset. By addressing issues of dataset bias, ambiguity, and the lack of explicit support for active exploration prevalent in previous datasets, EXPRESS-Bench offers a more reliable and challenging evaluation environment. This improved dataset can serve as a standardized tool for training and evaluating EQA agents, encouraging the development of models that genuinely explore and understand their environments rather than exploiting dataset artifacts.

Secondly, the proposed **Fine-EQA model** demonstrates an effective hybrid exploration strategy. By integrating frontier-based exploration for broad coverage with goal-oriented exploration for fine-grained investigation of task-relevant regions, Fine-EQA sets a new performance baseline. This approach can inform the design of more efficient and robust navigation and information-gathering strategies for embodied agents operating in complex and cluttered real-world environments.

Thirdly, the **Exploration-Answer Consistency (EAC) metric** introduces a more rigorous evaluation standard. By explicitly measuring the alignment between an agent's visual observations during exploration and its generated answers, the EAC metric can more faithfully assess an agent's reasoning capabilities and detect hallucinations. This shift towards grounded evaluation is critical for building trustworthy embodied AI systems, as it promotes accountability and reduces the risk of models providing plausible but unsupported responses.

The significant gap between human and Fine-EQA performance observed in the experiments highlights the ongoing challenges in EQA, particularly in complex reasoning and robust exploration under diverse conditions. This indicates fertile ground for future research, pushing the boundaries of multimodal understanding, spatial reasoning, and dynamic decision-making in embodied agents.

Collectively, this work contributes to the development of more capable and reliable embodied AI. The advancements in benchmarks, exploration strategies, and evaluation metrics have potential implications for a range of real-world applications, including robotics for domestic assistance, autonomous navigation, and virtual environments where agents must dynamically interact with their surroundings to gather information and respond intelligently.
