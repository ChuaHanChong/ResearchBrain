# O$^3$Afford: One-Shot 3D Object-to-Object Affordance Grounding for Generalizable Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The O³Afford framework introduces a one-shot approach for 3D object-to-object affordance grounding, enabling robots to understand complex interactions between object pairs from minimal training data. It combines vision foundation models, 3D point clouds, and large language models, achieving superior affordance prediction accuracy and robust real-world robotic manipulation across diverse tasks and unseen objects.

## Problem

- Most existing affordance grounding methods focus on single objects or simplistic 2D interactions, failing to capture complex 3D object-to-object functional relationships (e.g., pouring, cutting) crucial for real-world robotic tasks.
- Collecting and annotating large-scale datasets for precise object-to-object affordances is prohibitively expensive, leading to a significant data scarcity bottleneck for training robust models.
- Limitations of 2D affordance grounding, which lacks the precise geometric information needed for accurate 3D robotic manipulation and struggles with generalization across viewpoints and object categories.

## Method

- **Semantic Point Cloud Construction**: Leverages multi-view RGB-D observations and ==DINOv2==, a Vision Foundation Model, to distill robust 2D semantic features into 3D point clouds, creating semantically rich representations for interacting objects.
- **One-Shot Affordance Grounding**: Employs a novel ==joint-attention transformer decoder== that processes the semantically-enriched 3D point clouds, learning to predict per-point affordance scores from only one training example per affordance category.
- **Affordance-Based Manipulation with LLMs**: Integrates the predicted 3D affordance maps into an ==optimization-based robotic planning== framework, where Large Language Models (LLMs) automatically generate task-specific Python constraint functions to guide 6-DoF object transformations for manipulation.

## Results

- O³Afford significantly outperformed existing baselines in 3D affordance grounding across all metrics, achieving an aIOU of **26.19**, which is substantially higher than O2O-Afford's **14.31**, demonstrating superior accuracy in predicting interaction regions.
- The framework exhibited strong generalization capabilities, maintaining high accuracy even with significant occlusion (up to **50%** point cloud occlusion) and successfully predicting affordances for entirely novel object categories unseen during training.
- Achieved high success rates in robotic manipulation on a real Franka Research 3 robot, with approximately **80%** for common tasks and **50%** for complex tasks like hanging, significantly outperforming baselines which exhibited considerably poorer performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The synergy of pre-trained Vision Foundation Models (like DINOv2) with 3D point clouds provides robust, generalizable semantic and geometric features essential for understanding complex object interactions, even under occlusion.
> - A joint-attention transformer decoder can effectively learn intricate object-to-object functional relationships from a single demonstration, addressing the extreme data scarcity challenge in complex affordance grounding.
> - Integrating LLMs to generate task-specific constraints based on predicted 3D affordance maps bridges the gap between high-level task descriptions and low-level robot control, enabling more intuitive and generalizable manipulation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in O³Afford is strategically positioned at the confluence of several rapidly advancing fields: robotic manipulation, affordance grounding, few-shot learning, 3D vision, and the application of large foundation models (vision and language).

**Affordance Grounding:** The concept of affordance, initially introduced by James J. Gibson, refers to the potential actions an environment or object offers to an agent. In robotics, affordance grounding is crucial for enabling robots to understand how to interact with objects. Prior research in this area has largely focused on *single-object affordance*, predicting functional properties for a single object in isolation (e.g., a cup can be grasped, a door can be pushed). These methods often operate in 2D pixel space, which inherently limits their utility for robotic manipulation due to the lack of precise geometric information needed for physical interaction. While some works have extended to 3D affordance, they still primarily focus on individual objects.

**Object-to-Object (O2O) Interactions:** The authors correctly identify a significant gap: most real-world tasks involve interactions between *pairs* of objects (e.g., pouring from a teapot into a bowl, cutting an apple with a knife). Traditional single-object affordance models fail to capture these complex functional relationships that emerge from object pairings. The challenge for O2O affordance is the scarcity of annotated data, as collecting and labeling intricate interaction points between object pairs is significantly more arduous than for single objects. Existing O2O approaches, like O2O-Afford, have attempted to mitigate data scarcity through automatic extraction of contacts in simulation but are often limited to simpler affordance types (e.g., placing, fitting) and lack generalization to complex, skill-based interactions.

**Few-Shot/One-Shot Learning & Foundation Models:** A major trend in recent AI research is the emergence of powerful "foundation models," such as large language models (LLMs) like GPT-4o and vision foundation models (VFMs) like DINOv2 and CLIP. These models, trained on vast internet-scale datasets, exhibit remarkable zero-shot and few-shot generalization capabilities. They have demonstrated the ability to adapt to new tasks with minimal or no new training data. This paper leverages this paradigm, recognizing the potential of VFMs to provide robust, semantically rich features and LLMs for high-level reasoning and planning, thereby addressing the data scarcity issue in complex O2O affordance grounding. While VFMs have been explored for 2D affordance, the paper highlights that their utility is constrained by the limited geometric information in 2D images, motivating a 3D approach.

**3D Vision and Robotic Manipulation:** Grounding affordances in 3D representations, such as point clouds, is essential for robotic manipulation as it provides the necessary geometric context for precise interaction. O³Afford integrates 3D point cloud representations with the semantic power of VFMs, aiming to achieve robust generalization across varied geometries, unseen object instances, and novel object categories – a critical step towards practical, generalizable robotic systems. By combining this rich 3D affordance information with LLM-generated constraints for optimization-based planning, the work connects high-level semantic understanding with low-level robot control, pushing the boundaries of autonomous manipulation.

In essence, O³Afford innovates by:
1. **Shifting focus to complex 3D object-to-object affordances,** moving beyond single-object or simplistic O2O interactions.
2. **Pioneering one-shot learning for this challenging problem,** directly tackling the data scarcity bottleneck.
3. **Synergistically combining VFMs, 3D point clouds, and LLMs** to achieve robust semantic and geometric generalization, a comprehensive approach not fully explored in prior works.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **develop a generalizable solution for 3D object-to-object affordance grounding under extreme data constraints, specifically requiring only one training example per affordance category.** This core objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Bridging the Perception-Action Gap in Real-World Robotics:** Real-world robotic manipulation frequently involves intricate interactions between multiple objects (e.g., preparing food, assembling components). Current affordance grounding methods often fall short by focusing on single objects or simple contact-based interactions, failing to capture the rich functional relationships between interacting objects required for complex tasks like "pouring" or "cutting." The authors aim to enable robots to understand these more complex functional properties.

2. **Addressing Data Scarcity for Complex Interactions:** Collecting and annotating large-scale datasets for object-to-object affordances, especially those involving continuous interaction or precise spatial relationships, is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming. This data scarcity is a major bottleneck for training robust deep learning models. The motivation here is to overcome this limitation by developing a method that can learn effectively from minimal supervision (one-shot).

3. **Overcoming Limitations of 2D Affordance Grounding:** Prior work in 2D affordance, while demonstrating progress, provides limited geometric information. This restricts generalization across viewpoints and object categories and makes direct application in robotic manipulation challenging, as robots require precise 3D spatial understanding for successful physical interaction. The authors are motivated to leverage 3D representations to provide the necessary geometric context.

4. **Leveraging Advances in Foundation Models:** Recent breakthroughs in vision foundation models (VFMs) like DINOv2 have shown impressive zero-shot and few-shot generalization capabilities, providing powerful semantic features. Similarly, large language models (LLMs) offer unprecedented reasoning abilities based on commonsense knowledge. The authors are motivated to harness these powerful pre-trained models to extract robust, generalizable features and enable high-level task understanding, which can significantly enhance a robot's ability to reason about and execute complex interactions.

5. **Enhancing Generalizability and Robustness for Robotic Manipulation:** The ultimate motivation is to improve the practical applicability of robots by endowing them with generalizable perception and manipulation skills. This includes robustness to unseen object instances, novel object categories, and environmental challenges like occlusion – all crucial for deploying robots in unstructured real-world environments. By integrating 3D affordance representation with LLMs for constraint generation, the work aims to significantly enhance LLMs' capacity to comprehend and reason about object interactions for generating task-specific manipulation plans.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

O³Afford proposes a novel, one-shot 3D object-to-object affordance grounding framework built upon three main components: Semantic Point Cloud Construction, One-Shot Affordance Grounding, and Affordance-Based Manipulation with LLMs.

#### 3.1. Semantic Point Cloud Construction

The first step involves creating semantically rich 3D representations of the interacting objects. Inspired by methods like D³Field, the approach leverages Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), specifically DINOv2, to distill powerful 2D semantic features into 3D point clouds.
- **Multi-view RGB-D Observations:** The system takes multi-view RGB-D (color and depth) images of the scene containing the source and target objects.
- **DINOv2 Feature Extraction:** DINOv2, a self-supervised VFM, extracts high-level semantic features from the RGB images.
- **Feature Projection and Fusion:** These 2D semantic features are then projected onto the 3D point cloud geometry obtained from the depth images. A fusion mechanism, similar to D³Field, interpolates features and combines them across multiple views, assigning weights based on visibility and depth consistency. This process effectively encodes part-aware semantic information directly onto each point in the 3D point cloud of both the source and target objects, without requiring additional training for feature extraction.

#### 3.2. One-Shot Affordance Grounding

This module is designed to predict per-point affordance scores on the semantically-enriched point clouds, indicating the likelihood of interaction at each point. It employs a novel joint-attention transformer decoder.
- **Problem Formulation:** Object-to-object affordance grounding is framed as predicting affordance maps $A_s$ and $A_t$ for source $P_s$ and target $P_t$ point clouds, respectively. Each point has 3D coordinates and an $n$-dimensional semantic feature vector. The model learns from a single training example per affordance category (e.g., one "pouring" example, one "cutting" example).
- **Point Cloud Encoder:**
 - The concatenated point clouds of source and target objects, along with their DINOv2-derived features, are input.
 - **Farthest Point Sampling (FPS):** Used to select representative patch centers.
 - **k-Nearest Neighbors (kNN):** Forms local patches around these centers.
 - **PointNet:** Processes each local patch to aggregate local geometric information into compact, patch-level feature representations.
 - **Tokenization:** Produces tokenized input $Z$.
 - **Object Identity Embeddings:** One-hot embeddings are concatenated to distinguish between source and target object tokens, enabling the network to understand which object each token belongs to.
- **Joint-attention Transformer Decoder:**
 - Employs a multi-head cross-attention mechanism to capture complex contextual dependencies and complementary relationships between the source and target objects' features.
 - **Bidirectional Cross-Attention:** Computes attention for source features using target features as key/value, and vice-versa (e.g., $A_{src} = \text{CrossAttention}(Z_{src}, Z_{tgt}, Z_{tgt})$). This enables dynamic feature interaction across objects, crucial for understanding relational affordances.
 - **Per-Point Prediction:** The final patch-level embeddings are interpolated back to individual points using nearest-neighbor interpolation, creating dense point-level embeddings. These are then passed through a lightweight MLP projection head to output per-point affordance scores, normalized to $$.
- **Training:** Optimized using Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss against ground-truth affordance annotations.

#### 3.3. Affordance-Based Manipulation

The predicted 3D affordance maps are then used to guide robotic manipulation through an optimization-based planning framework, enhanced by Large Language Models (LLMs).
- **Constraint-Based Optimization:** Given source ($P_{src}, A_{src}$) and target ($P_{tgt}, A_{tgt}$) object point clouds and their affordance maps, the system optimizes a 6-DoF transformation $T$ for the source object to achieve the desired interaction. The objective is to minimize a weighted sum of constraint functions $S_i(P_{src}, A_{src}, P_{tgt}, A_{tgt}, T)$.
- **Constraint Generation with LLMs:** Instead of manually designing constraint functions, LLMs (e.g., GPT-4o) are utilized to automatically generate task-specific Python functions.
 - **Prompting:** The LLM is prompted with a high-level task description (e.g., "Pour from teapot into the bowl," "Cutting apple with knife").
 - **Function Output:** The LLM, leveraging its commonsense knowledge, generates Python functions that act as score terms $S_i$. These functions quantify how well the current pose $T$ satisfies task-relevant spatial relationships (e.g., alignment, contact, insertion) by considering the high-affordance regions identified by the grounding module.
 - **Solver Integration:** These LLM-generated score functions are then integrated into an off-the-shelf optimization solver to determine the optimal transformation $T$ for robot execution.

**Experiment Setup:**
- **Dataset:** A custom dataset generated in simulation using SAPIEN, as no high-quality O2O affordance dataset existed. Ground truth affordance maps are created via user-assigned contact points and distance-based propagation.
- **Robotic Platform:** Experiments conducted in both SAPIEN simulation and on a real-world Franka Research 3 robot, using Orbbec Femto Bolt RGB-D cameras for observation.
- **Tasks:** Five complex interaction tasks: pouring, inserting, pressing, hanging, and cutting, designed to evaluate various contact geometries and spatial relationships.
- **Training:** Crucially, the model is trained with only *one* pair of interacting objects for each affordance type, highlighting its one-shot learning capability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate O³Afford focused on three key questions: its effectiveness in affordance grounding, its generalization capabilities, and its impact on robotic manipulation.

#### 4.1. Affordance Grounding Performance

- **Quantitative Superiority:** O³Afford significantly outperformed existing baselines—O2O-Afford (a training-based 3D O2O method), IAGNet (a 2D image-based method adapted for 3D), and RoboPoint (a VLM-based approach)—across all four evaluation metrics: aIOU, SIMilarity, MAE, and AUC. For instance, O³Afford achieved an aIOU of 26.19, substantially higher than O2O-Afford (14.31), IAGNet (16.89), and RoboPoint (11.84). This demonstrates its superior accuracy in predicting fine-grained affordance regions.
- **Qualitative Precision:** Visual examples (Figure 2) illustrate that O³Afford's predictions are more precise and functionally relevant compared to baselines. RoboPoint, while capable of object localization, lacked the fine-grained precision for specific interaction regions. IAGNet suffered from overfitting due to the one-shot training paradigm, highlighting the challenges of 2D methods in such data-constrained scenarios.

#### 4.2. Generalization Analysis

O³Afford exhibited strong generalization capabilities beyond robust intra-class generalization under one-shot training.
- **Occlusion Generalization:** Evaluated under realistic occlusion scenarios (objects occluded by other objects, 10% to 50% point cloud occlusion), O³Afford showed the least performance drop, maintaining high accuracy even with significant visual obstruction (Figure 3). This robustness is attributed to DINOv2's robust semantic features and PointNet's ability to aggregate local geometric information, making the model less sensitive to missing global geometry.
- **Category-level Generalization:** The model successfully predicted affordance maps for entirely novel object categories that were unseen during training but exhibited similar semantic or geometric functionalities (e.g., scissors for cutting, a coat rack for hanging, a spray bottle for pouring, as shown in Figure 4). This crucial capability stems from DINOv2's ability to identify parts with similar semantics and the PointNet encoder's capacity to recognize similar local geometries, leading to clustering of functionally similar latent patches.

#### 4.3. Affordance-Guided Manipulation

The integration of O³Afford's predictions with LLM-generated constraints for robotic manipulation yielded impressive results in both simulation and the real world.
- **Simulation Performance:** Across five diverse tasks (pouring, hanging, pressing, inserting, cutting) and varying observation conditions (2, 3, or 4 camera views), O³Afford achieved the highest success rates (Table 2). It demonstrated superior robustness to partial observations (fewer views/occlusion) compared to baselines like 'ReKep' (keypoint-based planning) and a direct 'Baseline' (planning without affordance). The baseline planning approach performed worst due to its lack of understanding of object relationships, emphasizing the critical role of affordance. ReKep, while using keypoints, struggled with task-awareness and occlusion sensitivity.
- **Real-World Performance:** On a real Franka Research 3 robot, O³Afford maintained its strong performance (Table 3). For common tasks requiring two-object interaction, it achieved approximately an 80% success rate, significantly outperforming baselines which exhibited considerably poorer performance. In more complex tasks like hanging, O³Afford achieved about 50% success, whereas the baseline failed in all trials. Qualitative examples (Figure 5) illustrate that affordance representation leads to more feasible and coherent manipulation sequences, as the LLM-generated constraints leverage the learned affordance maps effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

O³Afford represents a significant advancement in robotic manipulation and AI, with broad implications and substantial potential impact:

1. **Enabling Generalizable Robotic Manipulation in Unstructured Environments:** By successfully demonstrating one-shot 3D object-to-object affordance grounding, O³Afford directly addresses a critical bottleneck for deploying robots in real-world, dynamic, and unstructured environments: the need for extensive, task-specific data. This work shows that robots can learn complex interaction skills from minimal demonstrations, drastically reducing the cost and effort of training.

2. **Advancing Semantic and Geometric Understanding for Robots:** The synergy between powerful Vision Foundation Models (DINOv2) for rich semantic features, 3D point cloud representations for precise geometry, and a novel joint-attention mechanism is a powerful combination. It allows robots to move beyond simple object recognition to a deeper understanding of *how* objects can interact functionally, considering both their form and their potential actions. This level of understanding is vital for autonomous agents.

3. **Enhancing Human-Robot Interaction and Task Specification:** The integration of Large Language Models (LLMs) for generating task-specific constraints is highly significant. It bridges the gap between high-level human instructions (e.g., "pour," "cut") and low-level robot control actions. This capability makes robots more intuitive to program and command, moving towards a future where users can describe tasks in natural language, and robots can autonomously translate these into executable plans based on their understanding of object affordances.

4. **Robustness to Real-World Challenges:** The demonstrated robustness to various levels of occlusion and generalization to entirely novel object categories are crucial practical contributions. Real-world scenes are often cluttered and contain objects the robot has never encountered. O³Afford's ability to maintain performance under such conditions vastly increases the reliability and adaptability of robotic systems.

5. **A Foundation for Future Research:** O³Afford establishes a robust framework that can inspire future research in several directions. The authors themselves suggest incorporating language instructions directly into the affordance prediction pipeline to enable open-vocabulary affordance grounding, a logical next step towards more versatile and intelligent robots. Furthermore, this approach could be extended to multi-object interactions, human-robot co-manipulation, or even cognitive robotics where understanding object functions is key to higher-level reasoning.

In conclusion, O³Afford pushes the frontier of robotic intelligence by providing a practical and generalizable solution for complex object-to-object interactions. By ingeniously combining the strengths of modern AI paradigms—one-shot learning, 3D vision, and foundation models—it significantly accelerates the journey towards more autonomous, capable, and human-friendly robotic systems.
