# Introducing HOT3D: An Egocentric Dataset for 3D Hand and Object Tracking

> [!summary] Summary
> HOT3D is an egocentric dataset for 3D hand-object tracking: 833 minutes, 3.7M multi-view RGB/monochrome images from Project Aria glasses and Quest 3 headsets, 19 subjects manipulating 33 scanned objects, with motion-capture ground-truth poses of hands, objects, and cameras.

## Problem

- Existing hand-object datasets rely on magnetic sensors, synthetic grasps, or tedious manual annotation that distort appearance or fail to scale to realistic interactions.
- Prior multi-view marker-based datasets like ARCTIC use only one true egocentric view (a helmet-mounted mock-up), not production AR/VR headsets.
- Existing datasets favor static or near-static grasps and simple pick-up scenarios, lacking dynamic, everyday kitchen/office/living-room interaction complexity.

## Method

- Dual-device ==egocentric== capture using ==Project Aria== research glasses (RGB and monochrome streams, ==SLAM== point clouds, eye gaze) and Quest 3 consumer VR headset (stereo monochrome).
- ==Marker-based motion-capture== pipeline using 3mm optical markers on hands and objects, tracked by ==OptiTrack== infrared cameras, fitted to ==UmeTrack== and ==MANO== hand models.
- In-house ==3D scanning== pipeline producing high-resolution ==PBR== object meshes and per-subject hand scans, plus curated onboarding sequences for model-free tracking benchmarks.

## Results

- Dataset totals **1.5M** multi-view frames (**3.7M** images) across **425** recordings, with **1.16M** frames fully annotated and passing visual quality inspection.
- Training split covers **13** subjects (**~1M** frames) and test split **6** subjects (**~0.5M** frames), plus **4117** curated 5-second clips (**2969** train, **1148** test) for challenge benchmarking.
- Objects were tracked over a combined **13 km** of travel distance, with per-object orientation distributions revealing usage-specific pose biases (e.g., bowls seen mostly upright).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining two real consumer/research headsets rather than mock-up rigs yields sensor data that matches deployment conditions, improving downstream model transfer.
> - Marker-based motion capture scales ground-truth quality without the appearance distortion magnetic sensors or synthetic rendering introduce.
> - Providing dual hand-pose formats (accurate UmeTrack, standardized MANO) lets a dataset serve both high-fidelity tracking and cross-dataset comparability needs simultaneously.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The research addresses a persistent challenge within computer vision: the accurate and robust understanding of 3D hand-object interaction, particularly from an egocentric perspective. This area is crucial for the advancement of augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), and artificial intelligence (AI) systems that require contextual understanding of human actions and dexterous manipulation.

Historically, research in 3D hand pose estimation and 6DoF object pose estimation has progressed largely independently. Early work in hand pose estimation often utilized custom monochrome or RGB-D datasets like NYU, ICVL, MSRA, and EgoDexter. More recently, with the shift towards AR/VR applications where depth sensors may be absent, RGB or monochrome datasets such as InterHand2.6M, FreiHAND, UmeTrack, and AssemblyHands have gained prominence. Similarly, 6DoF object pose estimation began with monochrome datasets, transitioning to RGB-D datasets like LM, YCB-V, and T-LESS, which are now unified under the BOP benchmark. Recent efforts have also focused on RGB-only object pose estimation using datasets like OnePose and HANDAL.

Datasets providing annotations for both hands and objects are less common. Many existing datasets capture hand-object interactions but only offer 2D annotations (e.g., bounding boxes, segmentation masks, action labels) or provide 3D hand pose without corresponding 6DoF object pose annotations. Early attempts at 3D joint hand-object pose datasets, such as Sridhar et al., often relied on manual annotation, which is resource-intensive and not scalable, or used intrusive methods like magnetic sensors (FHPA). Synthetic datasets like ObMan addressed scalability but introduced a reality gap.

More recent advancements have seen datasets like HO-3D, H2O, H2O-3D, DexYCB, and HOI4D utilize optimization procedures on multi-view RGB-D images for annotation, offering more automation. However, these often feature exocentric views, simpler scenarios, or focus on specific object types (e.g., YCB objects). ContactPose utilized thermal cameras and optimization for hand poses, but with static grasps and less realistic object appearances. The ARCTIC dataset notably used a marker-based motion-capture system for ground truth, similar to HOT3D, but primarily features exocentric views with only one egocentric camera using a mock-up device.

HOT3D positions itself in this landscape by addressing several limitations of prior work. It provides a large-scale, egocentric dataset with multi-view RGB/monochrome imagery directly from consumer-grade and research-prototype AR/VR headsets. Crucially, it offers high-quality, marker-based ground truth 3D poses for both hands and objects, along with accurate 3D object models featuring Physically Based Rendering (PBR) materials. Furthermore, it incorporates dynamic, non-trivial interaction scenarios across diverse environments, and includes multi-modal data such as eye gaze and scene point clouds, which are often absent in other datasets. This combination of scale, fidelity, egocentric perspective, and multi-modality from real devices aims to bridge gaps in existing datasets, thereby enabling more robust and realistic model development for 3D hand-object interaction understanding.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to accelerate advancements in computer vision research related to egocentric 3D hand and object tracking. This is achieved through the public release of HOT3D, a comprehensive dataset designed for training and evaluating methods in this domain.

The motivation for creating and releasing HOT3D stems from several factors:

Firstly, the paper identifies that current methods for understanding hand-object interaction lack the necessary accuracy and speed to reliably support emerging applications. These applications include transferring manual skills (e.g., from expert users to novices via AR glasses, or from humans to robots), enabling AI assistants to better interpret user actions, and introducing new input modalities for AR/VR users (e.g., turning physical surfaces into virtual keyboards or objects into control wands). Such functionalities necessitate robust, real-time, and precise understanding of 3D hand and object motion, shape, and contact.

Secondly, existing datasets, while valuable, present limitations that HOT3D aims to overcome. Many datasets either focus solely on hands or objects, provide only 2D annotations, utilize exocentric camera views (less relevant for AR/VR), or lack the scale and fidelity of 3D ground truth needed for advanced model training. The reliance on manual annotation in some datasets restricts their scalability, while synthetic datasets may not fully capture real-world complexities. HOT3D is motivated to fill these gaps by providing:
- **Large-scale, multi-view egocentric data**: Sourced directly from Meta's Project Aria and Quest 3 devices, reflecting real-world AR/VR usage scenarios. This supports development of methods that leverage multi-camera and temporal information.
- **High-quality ground truth**: Accurate 3D poses of hands, objects, and cameras obtained using a professional marker-based motion-capture system. This precision is critical for benchmarking and training high-performance models.
- **Detailed 3D models**: Comprehensive 3D mesh models of objects with PBR materials, enabling the synthesis of photo-realistic training images to augment real data and improve model generalization. Hand models are also provided in standard formats (UmeTrack, MANO).
- **Diverse and realistic interaction scenarios**: Beyond simple observations, the dataset includes scenarios resembling typical activities in kitchen, office, and living room environments, enhancing the dataset's ecological validity and supporting research on dynamic grasps and complex interactions.
- **Multi-modal information**: The inclusion of eye gaze information (from Project Aria) and 3D scene point clouds (from SLAM) motivates research into user intent prediction, foveated sensing, and broader contextual understanding.

Finally, a key motivation is to foster community engagement and accelerate progress by establishing a standardized benchmark. By making HOT3D publicly available and co-organizing public challenges (like those at ECCV 2024), the authors aim to provide a common platform for researchers to compare methods, identify limitations, and drive innovation in egocentric 3D hand and object tracking.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The creation of the HOT3D dataset involved a systematic approach to data collection, annotation, and organization to ensure high quality and utility for research in egocentric 3D hand and object tracking.

**Data Acquisition Environment and Devices:**
The dataset was collected in a professional motion-capture laboratory equipped with numerous infrared OptiTrack cameras and light diffuser panels to control illumination variability (Figure 4). Recordings were captured using two head-mounted devices from Meta:
1. **Project Aria**: A research prototype of light-weight AR/AI glasses. Aria recordings include one RGB camera (1408x1408 resolution, 30 fps, 110° FoV) and two global-shutter monochrome cameras (640x480 resolution, 30 fps, 150° FoV). Additionally, Aria provides 3D point clouds from its SLAM system and per-frame eye gaze information from two eye-tracking cameras (320x240 resolution, 10 fps). IMU data (accelerometer, gyroscope), barometer, and magnetometer data are also available. Privacy measures, such as EgoBlur, were applied to anonymize recordings.
2. **Quest 3**: A production VR headset. Quest 3 recordings include two global-shutter monochrome cameras (1280x1024 resolution, 30 fps, fisheye lenses) positioned at the front of the headset, suitable for capturing hand-object interactions.

All sensor streams from both devices were tightly calibrated and time-synchronized, with intrinsic and extrinsic camera parameters provided.

**Subjects and Objects:**
The dataset includes interactions from 19 diverse subjects, recruited to represent varying hand shapes and nationalities. Hand models for each participant were obtained through a custom 3D hand scanner and are provided in both UmeTrack and MANO formats.
Subjects interacted with 33 distinct rigid objects, which include common household and office items of diverse appearance, size, and affordances (Figure 2). These object models were obtained using an in-house 3D object scanner and feature high-resolution geometry with PBR materials (metallic, roughness, normal maps).

**Interaction Scenarios:**
Data was collected across four everyday scenarios to increase diversity beyond simple observations:
- **Simple inspection**: Subjects picked up, observed, and then put down objects.
- **Kitchen scenario**: Activities typical in a kitchen environment.
- **Office scenario**: Actions commonly performed in an office setting.
- **Living room scenario**: Interactions characteristic of a living room.
All scenarios were staged in the same lab with scenario-specific furniture. To enhance visual diversity, lighting conditions, furniture placement, and decorative elements were regularly randomized. The dataset comprises 425 recordings in total (199 from Aria, 226 from Quest 3), each approximately two minutes in length.

**Ground-Truth Annotation:**
High-quality 3D ground-truth annotations were obtained using the marker-based motion-capture system:
- **Markers**: Small (3 mm) optical markers with adhesive layers were attached to the surface of hands (19 markers per hand) and objects (approximately 10 markers per object). These markers were minimally intrusive to hand-object interactions.
- **Registration**: The 3D locations of the markers were semi-automatically registered to the 3D models of hands and objects obtained from scanners.
- **Pose Estimation**:
 - **Hand poses**: Calculated by fitting the participant’s UmeTrack hand model to the tracked optical markers, and provided in both UmeTrack and MANO formats.
 - **Object poses**: Estimated by aligning the tracked markers to their registered 3D locations within the object's model coordinate frame. Care was taken to ensure distinct marker constellations for reliable tracking.
 - **Wrist poses**: Represented as 3D rigid transformations.
 - **Camera poses**: The headset poses (and thus camera-to-world transformations) were also tracked by the motion-capture system. Aria's Machine Perception Services (MPS) provided 6DoF localization, which was then aligned to the OptiTrack coordinate frame.
- **Synchronization**: Data frames from all sources (egocentric cameras, motion capture) were synchronized using SMPTE timecode.
- **Quality Control**: All recordings underwent visual inspection. Frames with lower-quality or missing annotations were flagged, and a mask of valid frames is provided. Out of 1.5 million multi-view frames, 1.16 million are fully annotated and passed inspection.

**Dataset Organization and Splits:**
The dataset is structured to facilitate standardized benchmarking:
- **Training Split**: Includes recordings from 13 subjects, totaling 1 million multi-view frames. Ground-truth pose annotations for this split are publicly released.
- **Test Split**: Contains recordings from the remaining 6 subjects, comprising 0.5 million multi-view frames. Ground-truth annotations for the test split are withheld and accessible only via dedicated evaluation servers for public challenges.
- **Curated Clips**: To simplify benchmarking, 4117 curated clips (2969 from training, 1148 from test), each 150 frames (5 seconds) long, are provided. These clips are fully annotated and inspected for quality and are used in public challenges.
- **Object-Onboarding Sequences**: The dataset includes specific sequences to support model-free object tracking and 3D object reconstruction. These show all possible views of each object in two setups:
 1. A static object on a desk, both upright and upside-down, with full ground truth object poses.
 2. An object manipulated by hands. For these dynamic sequences, ground truth object poses are provided only for the first frame to define the canonical object space, simulating a challenging real-world scenario where initial pose might be known but continuous tracking is difficult. Hand poses are not provided for these onboarding sequences.

### 4. Main findings and results

As a dataset introduction paper, the main findings and results are primarily descriptive, detailing the characteristics, scale, and specific features of the HOT3D dataset itself.

**Dataset Scale and Diversity:**
- **Volume**: HOT3D comprises over 833 minutes of recordings, which translates to more than 3.7 million individual images (1.5 million multi-view frames).
- **Subjects**: The dataset features 19 diverse subjects, contributing to variability in hand shapes and interactions.
- **Objects**: It includes 33 distinct rigid objects, ranging from common household to office items, selected for their varied appearance, size, and affordances (e.g., a keyboard, coffee pot, bottle, toy, etc.).
- **Scenarios**: Interactions are captured across four distinct everyday scenarios: a general inspection task, and environments resembling a kitchen, an office, and a living room. This diversity in contexts aims to represent a wider range of real-world interactions.
- **Environmental Variability**: To enhance diversity, aspects such as lighting conditions, furniture arrangements, and decorative elements within the capture lab were regularly randomized.

**Ground-Truth Annotation Quality and Modalities:**
- **High-Fidelity 3D Poses**: The core of HOT3D's value lies in its high-quality ground-truth annotations for 3D poses of hands, objects, and cameras. These were obtained using a professional marker-based motion-capture system, providing millimeter-accurate data.
- **Hand Models**: Hand annotations are provided in both the UmeTrack and MANO formats, catering to different research preferences and offering varying levels of detail/accuracy.
- **Object Models**: The dataset includes high-resolution 3D mesh models for all 33 objects, complete with Physically Based Rendering (PBR) materials (metallic, roughness, normal maps). These models are suitable for photo-realistic rendering, which can be used to generate synthetic training data.
- **Multi-Modal Signals**: Beyond RGB/monochrome imagery, the dataset incorporates additional modalities from Project Aria, including:
 - **3D Point Clouds**: Generated from Aria’s SLAM system, providing a sparse representation of the static scene.
 - **Eye Gaze Information**: Directional data indicating where the user is looking.
 - **IMU Data**: Accelerometer, gyroscope, barometer, and magnetometer readings.
- **Annotation Reliability**: Out of the 1.5 million multi-view frames, 1.16 million frames are reported as fully annotated (i.e., ground-truth poses for all hands and objects are available) and have passed a visual quality inspection. A mask is provided to identify these valid frames.

**Interaction Dynamics and Object Statistics:**
- **Dynamic Interactions**: The dataset moves beyond static grasps, capturing dynamic interactions and manipulations. Statistics show that objects collectively traveled over 13 kilometers throughout the recordings, with individual objects exhibiting varying degrees of movement (e.g., the "white mug" showing extensive movement compared to a static "keyboard").
- **Object Orientation Statistics**: Analysis of object orientations (azimuth and elevation angles) reveals object-specific pose biases. For instance, objects like a "bowl" tend to be observed upright, while a "birdhouse toy" shows specific frontal and upright tendencies. These statistics could serve as valuable prior information for pose estimation methods.

**Dataset Structure for Benchmarking:**
- **Training/Test Splits**: The dataset is divided into training (13 subjects, 1M frames) and test (6 subjects, 0.5M frames) splits. Ground truth for the test set is reserved for evaluation servers, facilitating fair competition in challenges.
- **Curated Clips**: A set of 4117 curated 5-second clips (150 frames each), all fully annotated and visually inspected, are provided specifically for benchmarking purposes in public challenges.
- **Object-Onboarding Sequences**: Specialized sequences are included for both static and dynamically manipulated objects, supporting research on model-free object tracking and 3D object reconstruction from reference views.

In summary, the paper presents HOT3D as a large-scale, high-fidelity, multi-modal, egocentric dataset with comprehensive ground-truth annotations, designed to address the specific needs of advanced research in 3D hand and object tracking within realistic AR/VR contexts.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The HOT3D dataset is expected to contribute significantly to the advancement of research in computer vision, particularly in the fields of egocentric perception, 3D hand pose estimation, and 6DoF object pose estimation. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

**Acceleration of Research and Development:** By providing a large-scale, high-quality, and publicly available benchmark dataset, HOT3D is positioned to accelerate the development of new algorithms and models for understanding complex 3D hand-object interactions. The challenges co-organized with ECCV 2024 further emphasize this goal by fostering direct comparison and iteration on methods.

**Enabling Advanced AR/VR Applications:** The egocentric nature of HOT3D, recorded using actual head-mounted devices (Project Aria and Quest 3), directly supports the creation of next-generation AR/VR experiences. These include more natural and intuitive user interfaces, real-time contextual awareness for AI assistants, and novel input capabilities where physical objects or surfaces can be dynamically leveraged for interaction. The dataset's focus on dynamic grasps and realistic scenarios is crucial for transitioning research into practical applications.

**Improved Model Accuracy and Robustness:** The comprehensive and accurate ground-truth annotations, obtained through a professional marker-based motion-capture system, are critical for training and evaluating machine learning models. High-fidelity ground truth helps to minimize annotation noise, leading to the development of more precise and robust models for 3D hand and object tracking. The inclusion of 3D object models with PBR materials also enables researchers to generate photo-realistic synthetic data, which can augment real data and improve model generalization across varying conditions.

**Facilitating Multi-Modal Research:** The integration of multi-modal signals such as eye gaze, SLAM point clouds, and IMU data from Project Aria offers new research avenues. Eye gaze can be leveraged for predicting user intent, guiding attention-based models, or implementing foveated sensing techniques. Scene point clouds provide additional geometric context for environmental understanding, while IMU data can aid in robust egocentric camera pose estimation and motion analysis.

**Standardized Benchmarking and Comparison:** The clearly defined training and test splits, coupled with curated clips and the provision of evaluation servers for challenges, establish a standardized platform for benchmarking. This allows researchers to quantitatively compare the performance of different methods, identify their strengths and weaknesses, and objectively track progress in the field.

**Addressing Data Limitations:** HOT3D directly addresses several limitations of prior datasets, including scale, annotation quality, realism of interaction scenarios, and the egocentric perspective. It moves beyond datasets with only 2D annotations, exocentric views, or simplified/static interactions, providing a richer and more relevant data source for complex hand-object manipulation research. The inclusion of object-onboarding sequences also specifically supports advancements in model-free object tracking and 3D object reconstruction, which are challenging for novel objects without pre-existing 3D models.

In essence, HOT3D provides a foundational resource for developing vision-based systems that can accurately perceive and understand human interactions with the physical world from a first-person perspective. This capability is expected to be integral to the evolution of human-computer interaction, robotics, and contextual AI.
