# Leveraging the Value of Information in POMDP Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Colorado Boulder and Virginia Tech developed Value of Information Monte Carlo Planning (VOIMCP), a framework for Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes that adaptively prunes uninformative observation histories. The method consistently achieved higher cumulative rewards and greater search efficiency on benchmark problems with large observation spaces by constructing deeper search trees.

## Problem

- Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) face a "curse of history" where optimal decisions depend on exponentially growing action-observation histories.
- Problems with large observation spaces are particularly challenging for state-of-the-art POMDP planners, often leading to shallow search trees and myopic plans.
- Existing methods struggle to efficiently manage the computational burden of processing all possible observations, as not all observations provide equal actionable insights.

## Method

- An adaptive ==Value of Information (VOI)== framework allows agents to dynamically switch between computationally expensive ==closed-loop== and cheaper ==open-loop== execution based on a threshold parameter "kappa".
- The original ==POMDP== is structurally transformed into a =="VOI-POMDP"== by encoding the choice between open-loop and closed-loop reasoning as distinct meta-actions in an augmented action space.
- ==Value of Information Monte Carlo Planning (VOIMCP)==, an ==MCTS==-based algorithm, integrates this adaptive VOI reasoning by biasing its ==Upper Confidence Bound (UCB)== selection towards open-loop actions in low-VOI regions, with an annealing schedule for "kappa" to ensure asymptotic optimality.

## Results

- **VOIMCP** consistently achieved higher mean discounted cumulative rewards across Target Tracking, FieldVision RockSample, and Laser Tag benchmarks compared to **PO-UCT**, **I-UCB POMCP**, and Open-Loop baselines.
- The method demonstrated improved search efficiency by achieving the highest maximum tree depth and the lowest effective branching factor among closed-loop planners, enabling deeper exploration within budget constraints.
- Annealing the "kappa" parameter, which controls the VOI bias, provided small but consistent performance improvements (**2.52%** to **4.06%** increase in reward) over a fixed-"kappa" variant.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The expected performance gain from acquiring and reasoning about information (Value of Information) is not uniform across the belief space; observations vary in utility.
> - Dynamically balancing open-loop (ignoring observations) and closed-loop (fully reasoning over observations) execution significantly reduces the "curse of history" and enables deeper planning.
> - Heuristic proxies like observation entropy are insufficient for capturing true information value, and an explicit, adaptive VOI approach yields superior planning performance.
