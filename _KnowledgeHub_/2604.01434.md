# Leveraging the Value of Information in POMDP Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Colorado Boulder and Virginia Tech developed Value of Information Monte Carlo Planning (VOIMCP), a framework for Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes that adaptively prunes uninformative observation histories. The method consistently achieved higher cumulative rewards and greater search efficiency on benchmark problems with large observation spaces by constructing deeper search trees.

## Problem

- Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) face a "curse of history" where optimal decisions depend on exponentially growing action-observation histories.
- Problems with large observation spaces are particularly challenging for state-of-the-art POMDP planners, often leading to shallow search trees and myopic plans.
- Existing methods struggle to efficiently manage the computational burden of processing all possible observations, as not all observations provide equal actionable insights.

## Method

- An adaptive ==Value of Information (VOI)== framework allows agents to dynamically switch between computationally expensive ==closed-loop== and cheaper ==open-loop== execution based on a threshold parameter "kappa".
- The original ==POMDP== is structurally transformed into a =="VOI-POMDP"== by encoding the choice between open-loop and closed-loop reasoning as distinct meta-actions in an augmented action space.
- ==Value of Information Monte Carlo Planning (VOIMCP)==, an ==MCTS==-based algorithm, integrates this adaptive VOI reasoning by biasing its ==Upper Confidence Bound (UCB)== selection towards open-loop actions in low-VOI regions, with an annealing schedule for "kappa" to ensure asymptotic optimality.

## Results

- **VOIMCP** consistently achieved higher mean discounted cumulative rewards across Target Tracking, FieldVision RockSample, and Laser Tag benchmarks compared to **PO-UCT**, **I-UCB POMCP**, and Open-Loop baselines.
- The method demonstrated improved search efficiency by achieving the highest maximum tree depth and the lowest effective branching factor among closed-loop planners, enabling deeper exploration within budget constraints.
- Annealing the "kappa" parameter, which controls the VOI bias, provided small but consistent performance improvements (**2.52%** to **4.06%** increase in reward) over a fixed-"kappa" variant.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The expected performance gain from acquiring and reasoning about information (Value of Information) is not uniform across the belief space; observations vary in utility.
> - Dynamically balancing open-loop (ignoring observations) and closed-loop (fully reasoning over observations) execution significantly reduces the "curse of history" and enables deeper planning.
> - Heuristic proxies like observation entropy are insufficient for capturing true information value, and an explicit, adaptive VOI approach yields superior planning performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Decision-making in uncertain environments is a fundamental challenge across various fields, with Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) providing a principled mathematical framework for addressing such problems. POMDPs are applicable in domains such as robotics, resource management, human-computer interaction, and medical diagnosis. However, solving POMDPs, even approximately, is computationally intractable due to the "curse of dimensionality," which refers to the exponential scaling of complexity with state, action, and observation space sizes, and the "curse of history," where optimal decisions depend on the entire exponentially growing history of actions and observations.

Over the past decades, various practical solvers have been proposed, including sampling-based approximate methods like PO-UCT, DESPOT, and SARSOP. These methods have made progress in mitigating the curse of dimensionality, particularly in the state space, by employing particle filters and exploring subsets of histories. Nevertheless, problems characterized by large observation spaces continue to pose significant difficulties for state-of-the-art planners. In such scenarios, many planners tend to construct shallow search trees, which can lead to myopic plans that fail to account for long-term consequences.

A concept that has been explored in decision-making is the "value of information" (VOI), which quantifies the performance gain from acquiring and reasoning about information. While VOI theory has roots in fully observable sequential decision-making, its application to partially observable problems has been less extensively investigated. Existing work has explored VOI based on the difference between open-loop and closed-loop policies or for constructing macro-actions. Some approaches use heuristics like belief and observation entropy to guide information gathering, but these proxies may not always accurately capture the true VOI. This paper aims to integrate a more direct and adaptive form of VOI reasoning into POMDP planning to address the curse of history, particularly concerning the branching factor introduced by numerous observations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a planning framework that can dynamically prune parts of the action-observation histories in POMDPs while maintaining performance guarantees. This is motivated by the observation that in many POMDP problems, the value of information – the expected performance gain from fully reasoning about all possible observations – is not uniform across the belief space. There are often regions where observations provide little to no actionable insights, meaning that the computational effort spent on processing them may not yield a proportional benefit in decision quality.

Specifically, the authors aim to alleviate the "curse of history," which arises from the exponential growth of action-observation histories, especially in problems with large observation spaces. Current planners often struggle with these problems, leading to shallow search trees and suboptimal, short-sighted plans. By acknowledging that not all observations are equally valuable at every decision point, the motivation is to introduce a mechanism that allows a planning agent to selectively disregard uninformative observations. This selective processing is hypothesized to substantially reduce the effective branching factor on observations, thereby enabling deeper search within limited computational budgets.

The key motivations can be summarized as:
- **Addressing the Curse of History in Large Observation Spaces:** Develop a method to manage the exponential growth of possible observation histories, a critical bottleneck for POMDP solvers.
- **Exploiting Non-Uniform Value of Information:** Capitalize on the fact that the utility of observation information varies across the belief space.
- **Balancing Open-loop and Closed-loop Execution:** Formalize a trade-off between computationally cheaper open-loop execution (ignoring observations) and more expensive closed-loop execution (fully reasoning over observations).
- **Improving Computational Efficiency:** Enable planners to allocate computational effort more efficiently by avoiding unnecessary processing of low-VOI observations, thus allowing for deeper search and potentially better long-horizon policies.
- **Maintaining Performance Guarantees:** Ensure that this selective reasoning framework provides theoretical bounds on suboptimality relative to the optimal POMDP policy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology revolves around an adaptive Value of Information (VOI) reasoning framework, its encoding into a transformed POMDP, and its integration into a Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) algorithm.

**Adaptive Value of Information Framework:**
The paper begins by contrasting closed-loop and open-loop execution in POMDPs.
- **Closed-loop execution** involves performing a Bayesian belief update after every action and observation, resulting in a number of histories that grows as $(|A| \cdot |O|)^d$ over horizon $d$. This is generally optimal but computationally intensive.
- **Open-loop execution** ignores observations, planning based on expected beliefs, leading to a belief update that marginalizes over the observation space. This drastically reduces the number of histories to $|A|^d$ but is generally suboptimal, as indicated by Jensen's inequality ($V^{CL}_d \ge V^{OL}_d$).
The traditional "simple VOI" is defined as the difference $VOI_d(b) = V^{CL}_d(b) - V^{OL}_d(b)$, offering a static comparison.

This work introduces an **adaptive VOI** framework that allows an agent to dynamically switch between open-loop and closed-loop modalities based on the local value of information at each belief. A new value function, $\hat{V}^*_d(b)$, is defined recursively. At each step, the agent computes an "adaptive VOI" ($\hat{V}OI^\kappa_d(b) = \hat{V}^{CL}_d(b) - \hat{V}^{OL}_d(b)$). The choice between open-loop ($\hat{V}^{OL}_d(b)$) and closed-loop ($\hat{V}^{CL}_d(b)$) reasoning is made based on a threshold parameter $\kappa$:
$\hat{V}^*_d(b) = \begin{cases} \hat{V}^{OL}_d(b), & \text{if } \hat{V}^{OL}_d(b) \ge \hat{V}^{CL}_d(b) - \kappa|\hat{V}^{CL}_d(b)| \\ \hat{V}^{CL}_d(b), & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$
This criterion means that if the open-loop value is sufficiently close (within a $\kappa$ fraction) to the closed-loop value, the agent chooses the computationally cheaper open-loop path, effectively disregarding observations. This mechanism interpolates between purely open-loop ($\kappa$ large) and fully closed-loop ($\kappa$ small) behavior.

**Bounded Suboptimality:**
The framework provides theoretical guarantees on the suboptimality incurred by this adaptive reasoning. A theorem demonstrates that the regret $\rho_d(b) = |V^*_d(b) - \hat{V}^*_d(b)|$ is bounded linearly by $\kappa$, specifically $\rho_d(b) \le \kappa \frac{R_{max}}{1-\gamma} \frac{1-\gamma^d}{1-\gamma}$. This implies that the approximation error remains controlled as a function of the chosen $\kappa$.

**VOI-POMDP Representation:**
To integrate this adaptive VOI reasoning into standard planning algorithms, the authors introduce the "Value of Information POMDP" (VOI-POMDP). This is a structural transformation of the original POMDP where the choice between open-loop and closed-loop execution is encoded as distinct meta-actions.
- The action space $A$ is duplicated into $A_{OL}$ (open-loop actions) and $A_{CL}$ (closed-loop actions), forming an augmented action space $A'$.
- The observation space $O$ is augmented with a null observation $o_{null}$.
- For open-loop actions ($a_{OL}$), the observation function $Z'$ always yields $o_{null}$ with probability 1, and the belief update marginalizes over observations.
- For closed-loop actions ($a_{CL}$), the observation function and belief update behave as in the original POMDP.
The value function for the VOI-POMDP, $\hat{V}'_d(b)$, is shown to be equivalent to $\hat{V}^*_d(b)$ from the adaptive VOI framework, demonstrating that this transformation preserves the adaptive VOI value function. This representation allows standard POMDP solvers to automatically perform VOI reasoning by optimizing over the augmented action space.

**Value of Information Monte Carlo Planning (VOIMCP):**
Building on the VOI-POMDP, the authors propose VOIMCP, an MCTS-based algorithm inspired by PO-UCT. VOIMCP modifies the Upper Confidence Bound (UCB) selection policy to incorporate the $\kappa$-dependent bias:
- For open-loop actions ($ha_{OL}$), the selection metric is standard: $UCB_{VOI}(ha_{OL}) = \bar{Q}(ha_{OL}) + B_N(ha_{OL})$.
- For closed-loop actions ($ha_{CL}$), the exploitation term is deflated: $UCB_{VOI}(ha_{CL}) = \bar{Q}(ha_{CL}) - \kappa|\bar{Q}(ha_{CL})| + B_N(ha_{CL})$.
This deflation biases the search towards open-loop actions when the potential VOI (reflected in $\bar{Q}$) is insufficient relative to $\kappa$, effectively pruning observation branches in low-VOI regions.

**Annealing for Global Convergence:**
To ensure asymptotic optimality (convergence to $V^*_D$), the fixed $\kappa$ bias needs to vanish over time. An annealing schedule for $\kappa_N$ is employed, where $\kappa$ decays as a function of visitation counts and the confidence width. Specifically, $\kappa_i(t, s_i) \le \frac{1}{c_\kappa R_{max}} B_{t,s_i}$ for $c_\kappa > 1$. This ensures that as visitation counts increase and confidence intervals shrink, the VOI penalty becomes negligible, allowing the algorithm to recover the optimal POMDP policy.

**Convergence Analysis:**
The paper provides non-asymptotic convergence bounds for VOIMCP. Theorem 2 establishes that for a fixed $\kappa$, VOIMCP converges polynomially to the optimal VOI-value function $\hat{V}^*_D$. Theorem 3 demonstrates that with the annealing schedule, VOIMCP converges polynomially to the true optimal POMDP value function $V^*_D$. The proofs adapt existing MCTS analysis techniques by modeling the search tree as a hierarchy of $\kappa$-biased Multi-Armed Bandit problems and using backward induction.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of VOIMCP was conducted across three POMDP benchmark domains known for their large observation spaces: Target Tracking, FieldVision RockSample, and Laser Tag. VOIMCP was compared against three baselines: PO-UCT (standard MCTS), I-UCB POMCP (an information-guided variant using observation entropy), and an Open-Loop policy (a VOIMCP variant that only expands null observations). All algorithms used a Sequential Importance Resampling (SIR) particle filter for belief updates and random rollout policies for leaf node value estimation. Experiments were conducted over a range of computational budgets (100 to 1000 tree queries) averaged over 1000 trials.

The main findings are as follows:

- **Superior Cumulative Reward:** VOIMCP consistently achieved higher mean discounted cumulative rewards across all three benchmark problems compared to all baselines (Figure 1). This indicates that VOIMCP generates more performant policies under time-constrained online planning.

- **Improved Search Efficiency via Pruning:** The analysis of tree growth statistics (Figure 2) revealed the underlying mechanism for VOIMCP's performance.
 - **Higher Maximum Tree Depth:** VOIMCP consistently achieved the highest maximum tree depth. This suggests that by selectively pruning unnecessary branches, it can explore deeper into the planning horizon, leading to less myopic policies.
 - **Lower Effective Branching Factor:** Concurrently, VOIMCP maintained the lowest effective branching factor among the closed-loop planning algorithms. This indicates that the adaptive VOI framework successfully reduced the computational overhead associated with observation processing by avoiding the expansion of dense observation branches when information value was low.

- **Limitations of Heuristic Information Gathering:** The I-UCB POMCP baseline, which uses observation entropy as a heuristic for information gathering, was the worst-performing algorithm across all benchmarks. This suggests that observation entropy is an insufficient proxy for the true value of information, as it may prioritize observations that reduce uncertainty but do not necessarily contribute to reward maximization.

- **Insights from Open-Loop Performance:** The Open-Loop policy, which always ignores observations, sometimes outperformed PO-UCT and I-UCB POMCP (e.g., in FieldVision RockSample, and outperforming I-UCB in Target Tracking). This result highlights that in domains with large, noisy, or uninformative observation spaces, the computational cost of fully processing observations can outweigh the benefits. The Open-Loop policy avoids this cost by planning deeply but without reactivity to observations. VOIMCP effectively bridges this gap by adaptively switching between open-loop and closed-loop reasoning, leveraging the benefits of both when appropriate.

- **Effectiveness of Annealing:** Supplementary experiments confirmed that an annealing schedule for $\kappa$ (where $\kappa$ decays with visitation counts) provided small but consistent performance improvements (2.52% to 4.06% increase in reward) compared to a fixed-$\kappa$ variant. This supports the theoretical benefit of annealing for asymptotic optimality while also showing practical advantages.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a principled and empirically effective approach to enhance planning efficiency in Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes, particularly in challenging scenarios characterized by large observation spaces.

**Key Significance:**
- **Alleviating the Curse of History:** The central contribution is the development of a framework that directly addresses the "curse of history" by dynamically and selectively reducing the effective branching factor on observations. This allows planners to build deeper search trees within limited computational budgets, leading to more foresightful and less myopic policies.
- **Principled Value of Information Reasoning:** The adaptive VOI framework provides a formal mechanism for balancing the computational cost of closed-loop (information-rich) execution with the efficiency of open-loop (information-poor) execution. The introduction of the $\kappa$ parameter and the associated bounded regret theorem offers a controlled way to trade off computational efficiency with policy suboptimality.
- **Novel POMDP Transformation:** The VOI-POMDP representation is a structural innovation that embeds the meta-level choice of information processing directly into the problem dynamics. This allows existing POMDP solvers to leverage VOI reasoning with minimal modification, simplifying implementation and enabling broader adoption.
- **Improved Performance in Complex Domains:** The empirical results demonstrate that VOIMCP consistently outperforms established baselines in benchmark problems with highly complex observation spaces. This suggests its practical utility for real-world applications where traditional solvers struggle.
- **Theoretical Guarantees:** The derived non-asymptotic convergence bounds for VOIMCP, both for fixed $\kappa$ (converging to the adaptive VOI value) and with annealing (converging to the true optimal value), provide strong theoretical underpinnings for the proposed algorithm.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Robotic Autonomy:** Robotics applications, which often involve noisy and high-dimensional sensor data (observations), could significantly benefit from VOIMCP's ability to plan effectively with limited computational resources. This could lead to more robust and intelligent autonomous agents in complex, uncertain environments.
- **Better Resource Management and Medical Diagnosis:** In fields like resource management and medical diagnosis, where observations can be costly or ambiguous, the adaptive VOI framework could guide decision-making to only acquire and process information that is truly valuable, leading to more efficient and effective strategies.
- **Advancements in AI Planning:** This work contributes to the broader field of AI planning by offering a new perspective on managing uncertainty and computational complexity in POMDPs. The concept of adaptive information processing could inspire further research into dynamic computational allocation in other sequential decision-making frameworks.
- **New Research Directions:** The paper identifies several avenues for future work, including investigating the fundamental structures of POMDPs most amenable to VOI reasoning, developing more sophisticated tuning and annealing schemes for $\kappa$, and integrating adaptive VOI reasoning into other POMDP solution techniques. These directions highlight the foundational nature of this work and its potential to stimulate further innovations in the field.
