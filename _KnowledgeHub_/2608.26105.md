# VBVR-Pro: A Scalable and Verifiable Suite for Native Visual Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> VBVR-Pro is a closed-loop suite for native visual reasoning, where generated images and videos are the reasoning substrate itself. It supplies 300 procedural task generators rendering aligned video, image, and interleaved data (1.25M training instances), plus deterministic per-task verifiable scorers usable as RL rewards. Training nine open generators lifts benchmark scores by 0.290 on average and transfers to seven external benchmarks.

## Problem

- Existing visual reasoning benchmarks are evaluation-only, offering little training data; large synthetic sources stay in narrow symbolic settings, so whether learned skills transfer remains unclear.
- VLM-as-a-judge scoring is imprecise on counts and fine spatial detail, costly at RL rollout scale, and irreproducible, changing scores for 55-93% of samples at temperature zero.
- Image, video, and interleaved generators have never been trained and scored under one shared task distribution, so which generative substrate best supports reasoning stayed untested.

## Method

- **VBVR-Pro-Dataset**: 300 parameterized task generators with programmatic solvers, rendering every instance as aligned video, Last-Frame/Key-Frame/Multi-Frame images, and interleaved text, plus seed metadata for verification.
- Per-task ==verifiable reward scorers== extract semantic entities via HSV segmentation, contour detection, OCR and trajectory tracking, then aggregate weighted soft checks or multiplicative hard-constraint gates into a deterministic score.
- An RL baseline swaps Flow-SDE for ==Coefficients-Preserving Sampling (CPS)==, whose stochasticity parameter lets ==group-relative policy optimization (Flow-GRPO)== explore distinct semantic decisions without noise-driven visual corruption.

## Results

- Training raised overall score by **0.290** on average across nine models (**+0.401** in-domain, **+0.179** out-of-domain); the best video model lifted V-ReasonBench from **10.21** to **38.22**.
- Scorers reached above **0.60** per-vote human agreement versus **0.54** for GPT-5.5 and **0.52** for Gemini-3.1-Pro, with model-level Spearman correlation **0.95** in-domain and **1.00** out-of-domain.
- RLVR scored **0.548** overall against **0.508** for VLM-reward RL and **0.503** for SFT; removing intermediate visual states at inference collapsed performance from **0.638** to **0.099**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Task-space coverage beats instance count: diverse procedural generators transferred to real-world and embodied benchmarks sharing no visual appearance with the training distribution.
> - Intermediate visual states, not linguistic chains of thought, carry the computation; placeholder reasoning text barely hurt, while deleting intermediate images was catastrophic.
> - Deterministic rule-based rewards outperform VLM judges for RL, and useful exploration must perturb semantic decisions rather than pixels while preserving fidelity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements driven by large language models (LLMs), with language becoming a primary medium for studying machine reasoning. However, the authors argue that many forms of intelligence relevant to the physical world are inherently visual and involve spatial, temporal, and dynamic interactions, which may not be optimally captured through purely linguistic reasoning. This perspective motivates the emerging paradigm of "native visual reasoning," where visual generation itself serves as the medium for problem-solving. In this paradigm, visual states (images and videos) are considered primary substrates for reasoning, rather than merely inputs for language models or final rendered outputs.

Current progress in native visual reasoning is bottlenecked by several factors: the scarcity of scalable training tasks, the lack of reliable feedback mechanisms for evaluating open-ended visual outputs, and the difficulty in conducting controlled comparisons across different generative modalities (e.g., image, video, interleaved text-image generation). Existing benchmarks often focus on evaluation rather than providing training data, and large-scale synthetic datasets tend to simplify visual complexities. Furthermore, the prevalent "VLM-as-a-judge" paradigm for evaluating generated visual content, while convenient for open-ended tasks, often proves unreliable for visual reasoning problems requiring precise verification of counts, spatial relationships, or temporal consistency.

VBVR-Pro addresses these limitations by establishing a "closed-loop testbed" designed to make native visual reasoning trainable, verifiable, optimizable, and experimentally controllable. This work extends prior efforts like VBVR by expanding task coverage, enhancing evaluation reliability through verifiable reward scorers, and enabling systematic studies of different generative substrates. It positions itself as a foundational infrastructure for scaling research into vision-centric reasoning, complementing language-centric approaches and providing tools to systematically investigate the role of visual states in complex problem-solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work stems from the identified challenges in advancing native visual reasoning. While language models excel at various reasoning tasks, a significant portion of intelligence in the physical world is visual and dynamic. The authors propose that treating visual generation as the reasoning medium itself—where models construct, update, and optimize visual states to solve problems—could better capture these forms of intelligence. However, several critical impediments hinder systematic study and progress in this area:

- **Lack of Scalable and Trainable Task Sources:** Existing visual reasoning benchmarks are often designed for evaluation and offer limited training data. Conversely, large-scale synthetic datasets tend to oversimplify visual elements, raising questions about the transferability of learned skills. This creates a bottleneck for training robust visual reasoning models.
- **Absence of Reliable Feedback Mechanisms:** Evaluating open-ended visual outputs, especially for reasoning tasks that demand precise correctness (e.g., exact counts, fine-grained spatial or temporal relations), is challenging. The common "VLM-as-a-judge" paradigm is identified as problematic due to its inaccuracies, high cost for large-scale application (e.g., reinforcement learning), and lack of reproducibility. Reliable feedback is essential for both accurate evaluation and effective model optimization.
- **Unclear Efficacy of Different Generative Substrates:** It is not well understood which generative modality—video generation (for continuous temporal trajectories), image generation (for efficiency and condensed output), or interleaved generation (for integrated visual-textual reasoning)—best supports visual reasoning. Controlled comparisons under unified task distributions and evaluation protocols are needed to inform model design.

To overcome these challenges, VBVR-Pro sets forth the following key objectives:

1. **Task Scaling and Transferability:** To develop a large-scale, diverse, and procedurally generated suite of visual reasoning tasks. This suite aims to provide a trainable curriculum that teaches transferable visual reasoning skills, moving beyond superficial instruction-pattern fitting or memorization, and demonstrating effectiveness on external, unseen benchmarks.
2. **Verifiable and Optimizable Rewards:** To introduce a suite of verifiable reward scorers that provide accurate, reliable, reproducible, and cost-effective feedback for visual reasoning tasks. These scorers are intended to overcome the limitations of VLM-as-a-judge and serve as robust reward signals for large-scale multi-task reinforcement learning.
3. **Mechanism Study and Modality Comparison:** To enable controlled experimental studies across various generative modalities (image, video, interleaved) within a unified framework. This objective seeks to identify the strengths and weaknesses of each modality for different types of visual reasoning and to investigate the fundamental substrates (e.g., visual trajectories vs. linguistic chains of thought) underlying successful reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of VBVR-Pro integrates several methodological components to achieve its objectives:

- **VBVR-Pro-Dataset for Task Scaling:**
 - **Procedural Generation:** The dataset comprises 300 visual reasoning tasks, with 150 reimplemented and revised from the earlier VBVR suite and 150 newly designed tasks. Each task is instantiated via a procedural generator with randomized parameters (e.g., grid size, object count, appearance), ensuring visual diversity and variability.
 - **Task Design Principles:** Tasks are designed to be "vision-native," meaning solutions primarily rely on visual state transformations. They feature unambiguous outcomes for rigorous inspection, substantial visual diversity through randomization, and often require multi-step reasoning.
 - **Multi-Modality Support:** Each problem instance is simultaneously rendered into aligned visual modalities: video, image, and interleaved text-image.
 - **Video:** Captures continuous temporal trajectories.
 - **Image:** Employs three output regimes to preserve essential solution information compactly: "Last-Frame" for single-image solutions, "Key-Frame" for discrete steps (with path overlays for navigation), and "Multi-Frame" for continuous processes sampled uniformly.
 - **Interleaved Text-Image:** Pairs each output image with a textual reasoning step generated by Gemini-3.1-Pro-Preview, guided by task-specific prompts.
 - **Metadata:** Each instance includes metadata (seed, problem spec, solution, attributes) to facilitate verifiable evaluation and ensure proper data splitting.
 - **Scale:** The dataset provides 1.25 million training instances from 250 tasks, with a dedicated 50K instance subset for reinforcement learning. Fifty tasks are held out for out-of-domain evaluation.

- **Verifiable Reward Scorers:**
 - **Addressing VLM-as-a-Judge Limitations:** The paper systematically identifies failure modes of leading MLLMs as judges, including numerical imprecision, oversight of fine-grained details, and misunderstanding of task rules, alongside issues of cost and lack of reproducibility.
 - **Design and Implementation:** Verifiable reward scorers are developed for all 100 tasks in VBVR-Pro-Bench (50 in-domain, 50 out-of-domain). These are rule-based and operate on task-relevant semantic entities (e.g., color, shape, position, count, trajectories) extracted using classical computer vision methods (e.g., HSV segmentation, contour detection, OCR).
 - **Scoring Logic:** Scores are derived from sets of task-specific checks with calibrated weights, combining linearly for soft criteria or multiplicatively for hard constraints. The scorers are lightweight, deterministic, and provide interpretable error decompositions.
 - **Human Preference Alignment:** Scorers are validated through an arena-style human study, comparing their instance-level agreement and model-level ranking correlations with human judgments against multiple VLM-based judges.

- **Benchmarking and Analysis:**
 - **Model Selection and Training:** Nine open-source generative foundation models (image, video, interleaved) from diverse architectures (sparse/dense, latent/pixel space) are fine-tuned on VBVR-Pro-Dataset. Proprietary models are also benchmarked.
 - **Evaluation Protocol:** Models are evaluated on VBVR-Pro-Bench using the verifiable reward scorers, distinguishing between in-domain and out-of-domain task performance.
 - **Ablation Studies:** Interleaved text-image models are subjected to ablations (e.g., removing intermediate visual states vs. replacing reasoning text with placeholders) to ascertain the relative contributions of visual and textual reasoning trajectories.
 - **Counterfactual Diagnostics:** Inference-time interventions are performed on input modalities (rephrasing text, removing image, geometric transformations) and intermediate reasoning states (removing or contradicting text/image) to probe the causal use of information.
 - **Chain-of-Step Visualization:** The denoising process of diffusion models is visualized by decoding noisy latents to reveal intermediate reasoning behaviors, such as multi-path exploration and superposition-based exploration.
 - **Transferability Study:** The strongest VBVR-Pro-trained video model is evaluated on seven external, held-out visual reasoning benchmarks (including real-world and embodied scenarios). Nearest-neighbor analysis is used to determine if transfer is due to memorization or generalizable reasoning.

- **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards:**
 - **Algorithm:** Group-relative policy optimization methods (e.g., Flow-GRPO) are adapted. A key innovation is the use of Coefficients-Preserving Sampling (CPS) for exploration during RL, which is hypothesized to provide meaningful semantic variations without compromising visual fidelity, unlike conventional stochastic samplers.
 - **Training Pipeline:** A one-step-delayed rollout pipeline is implemented to overlap reward computation with GPU-side rollout generation and policy optimization, enhancing training efficiency. Reward computation for RLVR is CPU-based, while RLVLM uses GPU-resident VLM judges.
 - **Training Regimes:** Comparative experiments include Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Reinforcement Learning with VLM Rewards (RLVLM), and Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), all starting from the same base model and using the same RL training data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several findings across task scaling, evaluation, and model behavior:

- **Task Scaling and Transferability:**
 - Training on the VBVR-Pro-Dataset consistently improves the performance of all nine fine-tuned open-source models, achieving an average overall gain of 0.290. This includes significant gains on both in-domain (+0.401) and out-of-domain (+0.179) tasks, indicating the dataset facilitates both generalization within known tasks and transfer across task families.
 - The VBVR-Pro-trained video model (VBVR-Pro-Wan2.2-I2V-A14B) demonstrates improvements of up to 20 percentage points on seven diverse external benchmarks, many involving real-world visual problems, suggesting the acquisition of transferable visual reasoning capabilities rather than mere instruction-pattern fitting or memorization. Nearest-neighbor analysis supports that these gains do not stem from direct memorization of visually similar training examples.
- **Modality Comparison:**
 - The strongest interleaved text-image model (VBVR-Pro-SenseNova-U1) performs comparably to the strongest video model (VBVR-Pro-Wan2.2-I2V-A14B) on many in-domain tasks, suggesting that a sparse sequence of task-relevant visual states can be efficient for familiar visual reasoning problems.
 - Video generation maintains an advantage for tasks requiring persistent spatiotemporal state tracking and generalization of transition dynamics, particularly in out-of-domain and transformation tasks. Single-image generation is found to be substantially weaker for procedural and temporal reasoning.
 - Ablation studies reveal that removing intermediate visual states leads to a more significant performance degradation than replacing meaningful intermediate text with semantically uninformative placeholders. This suggests visual trajectories are a more critical substrate for reasoning in VBVR-Pro tasks.
- **Counterfactual Diagnostics and Reasoning Visualization:**
 - Models' performance is insensitive to semantic rephrasing of input text but heavily dependent on both task semantics and the specific visual state in the input image. This finding argues against instruction-template fitting.
 - Intervening on intermediate reasoning states shows that visual pathways are considerably more sensitive than textual pathways to disruptions (removing or corrupting intermediate images has a larger impact than equivalent textual interventions). This suggests visual states serve as the more consequential working representation during inference.
 - Chain-of-Step visualizations reveal that both video and image generators exhibit reasoning behaviors such as Multi-Path Exploration (exploring multiple solution paths) and Superposition-Based Exploration (overlaying plausible visual states during denoising) as reasoning unfolds.
- **Verifiable Rewards and Reinforcement Learning:**
 - Verifiable reward scorers achieve high agreement with human preferences (per-vote agreement > 0.60, outperforming all VLM judges including GPT-5.5 and Gemini-3.1-Pro) at substantially lower cost and with perfect reproducibility.
 - Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) significantly outperforms both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning with VLM Rewards (RLVLM), achieving an overall score of 0.548 compared to 0.503 (SFT) and 0.508 (RLVLM).
 - RLVR demonstrates sustained performance improvements throughout training, including on out-of-domain tasks, indicating that task-grounded verifiable feedback provides a strong signal for transferable visual reasoning. RLVLM exhibits smaller and less stable gains, including temporary performance drops attributed to VLM judges' unreliability in penalizing subtle visual fidelity issues.
 - Qualitative analysis of RLVR-trained models shows improvements in reasoning trajectories, including self-correction capabilities (revising initial incorrect predictions during denoising) and enhanced temporal consistency in executing multi-step spatial plans (e.g., maze navigation).
 - Coefficients-Preserving Sampling (CPS) proves effective for RL exploration, balancing stochasticity for semantic decision making with visual fidelity.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VBVR-Pro represents a significant contribution to the field of visual reasoning by establishing a comprehensive, closed-loop testbed. Its significance and potential impact are multi-faceted:

- **Foundational Infrastructure for Native Visual Reasoning:** By unifying procedurally generated visual reasoning environments, task-grounded verifiable rewards, reinforcement learning capabilities, and modality-controlled mechanism analyses, VBVR-Pro transforms native visual reasoning through generation into a more trainable, verifiable, and experimentally controllable research area. This infrastructure is critical for systematic progress.
- **Addressing Key Research Bottlenecks:** The work directly tackles the limitations of scalable training data, reliable evaluation, and controlled modality comparisons that have hindered the advancement of vision-centric reasoning.
- **Advancing Training and Evaluation Methodologies:**
 - The VBVR-Pro-Dataset provides a scalable curriculum of diverse, challenging, and procedurally generated tasks that are demonstrated to foster transferable visual reasoning skills, moving beyond simple data augmentation or memorization.
 - The development and validation of verifiable reward scorers offer a robust alternative to the often-unreliable VLM-as-a-judge paradigm. These scorers are accurate, reproducible, and cost-effective, making large-scale reinforcement learning for visual reasoning feasible and efficient.
- **Deepening Understanding of Reasoning Mechanisms:**
 - The extensive ablation and counterfactual studies provide empirical evidence supporting the hypothesis that visual trajectories serve as a "first-class substrate" for reasoning, often more consequential than explicit linguistic chains of thought in specific visual reasoning contexts. This challenges a purely language-centric view of AI reasoning.
 - The visualization of "Chain-of-Step" behaviors within diffusion models (e.g., multi-path and superposition-based exploration) offers qualitative insights into how generative models internally construct and refine solutions, revealing emergent reasoning capabilities.
 - Comparative studies of image, video, and interleaved generation clarify their respective strengths, guiding future model development tailored to task requirements (e.g., continuous video for spatiotemporal tracking, interleaved for efficiency).
- **Enabling Future Research:** By publicly releasing all data, models, scorers, and code, VBVR-Pro provides a valuable resource for the research community. This will facilitate further exploration into visual reasoning, model architectures, and learning paradigms, encouraging a shift towards greater attention to native visual reasoning as a complementary and potentially more appropriate paradigm for tasks rooted in the physical world. The demonstrated success of RL with verifiable rewards opens new avenues for optimizing generative models for complex, semantically-driven visual tasks.
