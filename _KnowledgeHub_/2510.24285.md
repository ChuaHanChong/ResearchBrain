# ViPER: Empowering the Self-Evolution of Visual Perception Abilities in Vision-Language Model

> [!summary] Summary
> ViPER, a self-evolutionary framework, iteratively enhances Vision-Language Models' fine-grained visual perception by autonomously generating high-quality training data and employing a two-stage reinforcement learning strategy. This approach enables average performance gains of 1.6-1.7% on comprehensive multimodal benchmarks and reduces hallucination rates in the resulting ViPER-trained models.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) possess limited capacity for fine-grained visual perception, hindering their utility in real-world applications.
- Acquiring vast amounts of precisely annotated data for fine-grained perception is costly and time-consuming, while existing data scaling methods yield diminishing returns.
- Current fine-tuning paradigms (SFT, RFT) often compromise VLM general capabilities or fail to enhance intrinsic visual understanding, frequently focusing on superficial textual reasoning or tool manipulation.

## Method

- ==ViPER== introduces a self-evolutionary, closed-loop framework that progressively refines VLM perceptual abilities through ==self-critiquing and self-prediction==.
- It employs a two-stage task formulation: =='Caption Self-Refining'== for holistic image reasoning and =='Visual-Operation Predicting'== for fine-grained understanding.
- The framework integrates a ==data synthesis module== using a VLM and a diffusion model to autonomously generate perception-intensive training data (Viper10K) for both image-level and instance-level reconstruction, alongside a tailored two-stage reinforcement learning strategy.

## Results

- ViPER-trained models (Qwen-Viper-3B/7B) achieved average performance gains of **1.7%** and **1.6%** respectively across seven comprehensive multimodal benchmarks.
- Demonstrated substantial improvements in fine-grained perception tasks, with gains of **4.4%** for Qwen-Viper-3B and **6.0%** for Qwen-Viper-7B.
- The models exhibited reduced hallucination rates, enhanced generalizability across domains (e.g., **3.6%** gain in Science & Technology for 7B), and validated the efficacy of the two-stage reinforcement learning strategy over mixed training approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A progressive coarse-to-fine learning strategy, moving from holistic scene understanding to instance-level perception, effectively enhances VLM visual abilities.
> - The reciprocal relationship between visual generation and self-critique profoundly improves VLM visual understanding, enabling a self-bootstrapping paradigm without relying on external annotations.
> - The self-synthesized training data effectively eliminates the dependency on expensive cold-start data and large-scale external data distillation for VLM enhancement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), especially Vision-Language Models (VLMs), have made significant strides, demonstrating impressive capabilities across various domains. However, as recognized by the authors, a critical bottleneck remains: their limited capacity for *fine-grained visual perception*. While VLMs, often built with a visual encoder and an LLM backbone, excel at "slow thinking" or complex textual reasoning (aided by methods like Chain-of-Thought), their performance on tasks requiring intricate visual understanding often falls short. This is particularly evident in real-world applications where precise object recognition, attribute identification, or spatial reasoning is paramount.

The existing approaches to enhance VLM performance have shown limitations:
- **Scaling Training Data and Knowledge Distillation:** Many methods involve distilling knowledge from superior models or expanding datasets. While this can provide some gains, it is computationally expensive, often yields only marginal improvements, and can compromise the model's generalization abilities.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with Tool Augmentation ("Thinking-with-Image"):** Another popular direction integrates RL to enable models to use external tools or engage in multi-step interactions (e.g., DeepEyes, Chain-of-Focus). While these methods improve system-level outcomes by allowing models to call visual tools or reason through iterative steps, they often introduce significant latency and can lead to superficial reasoning. The model might become proficient at manipulating tools rather than genuinely enhancing its underlying visual comprehension, failing to improve its intrinsic perceptual capabilities on downstream tasks.

ViPER directly addresses this fundamental gap. Instead of merely improving reasoning *around* visual input or scaling up data, it aims to *self-evolve* the core visual perception abilities of VLMs. It moves beyond text-centric reasoning and superficial tool manipulation by integrating generation and understanding in a closed-loop framework. This positions ViPER as a novel approach that seeks to make VLMs more autonomous in their perceptual learning, reducing reliance on expensive external supervision or data, and directly enhancing the visual encoder's capacity for detailed scene understanding. It is a significant step towards developing more genuinely "seeing" and "understanding" VLMs, rather than just "reasoning about" visual information.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome the critical bottleneck of limited fine-grained visual perception in Vision-Language Models (VLMs), which severely restricts their applicability in real-world scenarios. The authors identify several core challenges that motivate their novel approach:

1. **Scarcity of High-Quality Perceptual Data:** Training VLMs for fine-grained perception demands vast amounts of precisely annotated data, which is expensive and time-consuming to acquire. Existing methods for scaling data often lead to diminishing returns or computational inefficiencies.
2. **Limitations of Current Fine-Tuning Paradigms:**
 - **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** While effective for specific tasks, SFT can inadvertently compromise the VLM's general capabilities, leading to catastrophic forgetting or reduced versatility.
 - **Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT):** Existing RFT approaches in multimodal settings tend to prioritize textual reasoning and tool-use strategies, often neglecting or insufficiently enhancing the underlying visual perception capabilities of the model. They improve the *output* but not necessarily the *intrinsic visual understanding*.
3. **Lack of Self-Improvement Mechanisms:** Current VLMs largely rely on external data or human feedback for improvement. There is a need for frameworks that enable models to iteratively enhance their own capabilities in a more autonomous, self-bootstrapping manner.

Driven by these challenges, the key objectives of ViPER are:

- **Formulate a Progressive Learning Task:** Design a structured, two-stage task that guides VLMs from coarse, holistic scene understanding to fine-grained, instance-level perception. This coarse-to-fine progression is hypothesized to be a more effective learning trajectory.
- **Develop a Self-Bootstrapping Framework:** Create ViPER, an iterative framework that enables VLMs to evolve their perceptual abilities through a closed loop of self-critiquing and self-prediction. This includes autonomously generating high-quality training data.
- **Integrate Multi-Granularity Reconstruction and RL:** Synergistically combine image-level and instance-level visual reconstruction with a two-stage reinforcement learning strategy. This integration is designed to use the model's generative capacity to solidify its perceptual understanding.
- **Demonstrate Enhanced Performance and Generalizability:** Validate that the ViPER-trained models (Qwen-Viper series) achieve superior performance on perception-intensive tasks across various benchmarks, including those requiring fine-grained understanding, multi-image reasoning, and robustness against hallucinations, while preserving general capabilities.
- **Elucidate Generation-Understanding Reciprocity:** Provide empirical evidence for the hypothesis that the act of visual generation and self-critique can profoundly enhance the VLM's visual understanding, fostering a reciprocal relationship between these two cognitive processes.

By achieving these objectives, ViPER aims to fundamentally advance VLM capabilities, making them more robust, autonomous, and effective for demanding real-world applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ViPER introduces a novel self-evolutionary framework designed to progressively enhance the visual perception abilities of Vision-Language Models. This framework is characterized by a two-stage task formulation, a sophisticated data synthesis module, and a tailored two-stage reinforcement learning (RL) strategy.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation: A Coarse-to-Fine Progressive Process

ViPER structures visual perception learning into two distinct stages:

- **Stage 1: Caption Self-Refining:** This initial stage focuses on cultivating holistic image reasoning and static scene understanding, teaching the model to "see widely." The VLM is tasked with revising errors or biases in its own generated textual descriptions. For a given image (I) and an initial self-generated caption (Cg), the VLM identifies and outputs refinement actions (Rpred). The training objective is to minimize the discrepancy between Rpred and the ground-truth refinements (R), fostering self-reflection and correction in visual perception without external correction mechanisms.
- **Stage 2: Visual-Operation Predicting:** Building on the first stage, this phase targets fine-grained perception and dynamic change awareness, training the model to "focus accurately." The VLM predicts visual operations based on a pair of highly similar images: an original image (Iorig) and a reconstructed image (Irecon) that resulted from applying a specific editing operation. The VLM learns to infer the operation (Opspred) that transformed Iorig into Irecon, minimizing the discrepancy between predicted and actual operations (Ops). This enhances the model's ability to focus on key information and understand visual manipulations.

#### 3.2. ViPER: The Self-Evolutionary Framework

ViPER integrates data synthesis with a corresponding two-stage RL strategy, establishing a closed-loop, self-bootstrapping paradigm.

##### 3.2.1. The Data Synthesis Module

This module is central to ViPER's self-evolutionary nature, autonomously generating high-quality training data for both stages. It relies on a bidirectional vision-language mapping module comprising a VLM and a diffusion model.

- **First-stage Data Synthesis (Image-Level Reconstruction):**
 - The VLM generates an initial static description (caption) of an original image.
 - A diffusion model then reconstructs an image based *solely* on this description.
 - Due to inherent information loss in vision-to-text conversion, the reconstructed image will exhibit subtle discrepancies from the original.
 - These visual differences serve as "visual feedback," prompting the VLM to act as a critic, identifying and rectifying errors (e.g., object attributes, spatial relationships, omitted details) in its initial description.
 - This process allows the VLM to iteratively optimize its visual grounding by confronting the visual consequences of its own textual descriptions.

- **Second-stage Data Synthesis (Instance-Level Reconstruction):**
 - Leveraging the *refined caption* from the first stage, the VLM selects "hard entities" within the image.
 - It then generates corresponding visual operation instructions based on hand-crafted heuristic rules (detailed in Appendix A.2).
 - A diffusion model executes these instructions to edit the original image, producing a reconstructed version.
 - Crucially, the *instructions themselves* serve as the ground truth for the Visual-Operation Predicting task.
 - This concretizes instructional intent into observable visual variations, enabling the VLM to learn fine-grained visual reasoning from self-induced scene changes.

The generative model (diffusion model) is pivotal, externalizing the VLM's internal reasoning into a concrete visual snapshot, endowing it with a form of "image imagination." The VLM within the synthesis module is dynamically updated from training checkpoints, fostering a co-evolution of the model and its training data. This process constructs Viper10K, a 10K perception-intensive dataset (7K Caption Self-Refining, 3K Visual-Operation Predicting data).

##### 3.2.2. Two-stage Reinforcement Learning

To align with the progressive cognitive demands of the tasks, a phased RL approach is employed.

- **Self-Sourcing Strategy:** All training data is self-synthesized by the model undergoing training, eliminating distribution shifts from external sources and rendering the RL process free from any cold-start requirement.
- **Sequential Training:** The training proceeds sequentially: first, on the Caption Self-Refining data, followed by the Visual-Operation Predicting data.
- **Unified Reward Mechanism:** A consistent reward computation mechanism is applied across both stages. For each model output, it is split into text sequences, and the BGE-M3 model calculates semantic similarity with the ground truth. The reward function (Equation 7) combines format correctness (R_format) and semantic correctness (R_correct), with a higher weight on semantic accuracy.
- **Optimization Process:** A variation of the Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithm is adopted. It promotes policy diversity and avoids entropy collapse by decoupling lower and higher clipping ranges, and encourages bolder policy updates by removing the KL penalty term. The objective (Equation 8) maximizes expected rewards by optimizing the policy based on generated outputs and their corresponding reward scores.

This comprehensive methodology allows ViPER to iteratively refine a VLM's perceptual abilities by generating its own challenging training data and learning from the discrepancies, thereby establishing a powerful self-evolutionary paradigm.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research demonstrates the effectiveness of ViPER through extensive experiments, primarily by developing the Qwen-Viper series based on the Qwen2.5-VL family and evaluating them against a suite of diverse benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance Improvements

- **Consistent Gains:** Across seven comprehensive multimodal benchmarks (MMStar, RealWorldQA, MME-RW(en), BLINK, Mantis Eval, HallusionBench, CRPE (relation)), ViPER consistently produced average performance gains.
 - Qwen-Viper-3B achieved an average gain of **1.7%**.
 - Qwen-Viper-7B achieved an average gain of **1.6%**.
- **Benchmark Outperformance:** The Qwen-Viper models demonstrated superior performance over their base models (Qwen2.5-VL-3B/7B) and other strong open-source VLMs, including various InternVL and LLaVA-OneVision versions, showcasing its competitive edge.

#### 4.2. Fine-grained Perception Enhancement

- **Most Significant Impact:** The most pronounced improvements were observed in tasks requiring fine-grained perception, which was the primary target of ViPER.
 - Qwen-Viper-3B showed a substantial **4.4%** gain.
 - Qwen-Viper-7B achieved an impressive **6.0%** gain.
- **Broader Perceptual Strengthening:** Stable gains were also noted in Coarse Perception and Instance Reasoning, confirming that the framework enhances a broad spectrum of perceptual capabilities.

#### 4.3. Generalizability and Other Benefits

- **Cross-Domain Benefits:** Despite no direct training on knowledge-intensive tasks, the 7B model exhibited a significant **3.6%** average improvement in the Science & Technology domain. This suggests that enhanced visual perception enables more accurate integration of visual cues with parametric knowledge, highlighting the interconnectedness of VLM capabilities.
- **Reduced Hallucination:** Qwen-Viper models exhibited lower hallucination rates compared to base models, indicating that improved visual perception leads to more faithful processing of image information and mitigates the negative impact of linguistic priors.
- **Multi-Image Reasoning:** Performance gains on multi-image benchmarks (e.g., Mantis Eval) confirm that the perception enhancements are fundamental and generalizable, allowing for more effective understanding and reasoning across complex visual contexts.

#### 4.4. In-depth Analysis and Ablation Studies

- **Elimination of Cold-Start Dependency:** Experiments showed that incorporating a cold-start phase with external high-quality data did not improve performance. The reward curve of the direct two-stage RL without cold-start caught up and even surpassed the cold-start approach, converging to a marginally higher final reward. This strongly validates ViPER's self-bootstrapping efficacy and eliminates the dependency on expensive cold-start data.
- **Validation of Two-Stage RL Strategy:** Comparing the two-stage sequential RL with a mixed RL approach (where data from both stages were blended) confirmed the superiority of the phased training. The two-stage strategy significantly outperformed mixed training, underscoring the necessity of the coarse-to-fine progressive approach.
- **Emergence of "Thinking-with-Images":** During the Caption Self-Refining stage, the models spontaneously developed a "thinking-with-images" capability, evident from the high-frequency generation of visual operation verbs like "scan," "zoom in," and "focus on" in their chain-of-thought tokens.
- **Attention Shift for Fine-Grained Focus:** The Visual-Operation Predicting stage led to a significant increase in attention concentration on critical local information in attention heatmaps, effectively translating high-level reasoning into focused visual analysis.
- **Ablation Study:** The ablation study confirmed that both stages (Caption Self-Refining and Visual-Operation Predicting) are crucial, and their integration leads to the most significant gains. The first stage establishes a global context, which then primes and grounds the intensive local analysis of the second stage, fostering a more profound and integrated self-evolution.

In summary, ViPER not only achieves substantial improvements in fine-grained visual perception but also provides compelling evidence for its innovative self-evolutionary design, demonstrating a fundamental advancement in VLM training.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ViPER framework represents a significant advancement in the development of Vision-Language Models, offering several layers of significance and potential impact:

1. **Breaking the Fine-Grained Perception Bottleneck:** ViPER directly addresses a critical and long-standing limitation of VLMs: their struggle with fine-grained visual perception. By enabling models to "see widely" and "focus accurately" through a progressive, two-stage learning process, it equips them with a more robust and granular understanding of visual information, which is essential for real-world applications requiring precision.

2. **Pioneering a Self-Evolutionary Paradigm:** The most transformative aspect of ViPER is its self-bootstrapping, closed-loop training paradigm. By autonomously generating its own high-quality, perception-centric training data (Viper10K) and continually refining its capabilities through this self-synthesized data, ViPER significantly reduces reliance on scarce and expensive human-annotated datasets. This "learn from itself" approach makes VLM development more scalable, sustainable, and autonomous.

3. **Demonstrating Reciprocity Between Generation and Understanding:** ViPER provides compelling empirical evidence for the reciprocal relationship between a model's generative capacity and its perceptual understanding. The framework leverages image-level and instance-level reconstruction to use the act of "generation" (producing images from captions, or editing images based on instructions) as a mechanism for "understanding" (critiquing captions, inferring operations). This insight can fundamentally reshape how future multimodal AI systems are designed, fostering deeper, more integrated cognitive abilities.

4. **Enhancing VLM Robustness and Generalizability:** The improvements achieved by Qwen-Viper models extend beyond specific fine-grained tasks. The reduction in hallucination rates suggests that enhanced perception leads to more faithful processing of visual input, mitigating the negative influence of linguistic priors. Furthermore, the demonstrated gains on multi-image benchmarks indicate that the perceptual enhancements are fundamental and generalize to complex, out-of-domain visual reasoning scenarios.

5. **Efficiency and Resource Optimization:** By eliminating the need for expensive cold-start data and large-scale external data distillation, ViPER offers a more efficient and resource-friendly approach to VLM enhancement. This can democratize access to advanced VLM capabilities by lowering the computational and data annotation barriers.

6. **Implications for Embodied AI and World Models:** The ability of VLMs to self-evolve their visual perception is a crucial step towards building more capable embodied AI agents and more accurate "world models." Agents that can autonomously improve their understanding of their visual environment will be more effective in navigation, interaction, and complex task execution in dynamic real-world settings.

7. **Contribution to Reinforcement Learning in VLMs:** ViPER refines the application of reinforcement learning in multimodal contexts by tailoring a two-stage strategy and a reward mechanism specifically for perceptual tasks, avoiding the pitfalls of RFT approaches that prioritize superficial textual reasoning. The insights into the irrelevance of cold-start data and the benefits of phased training are valuable contributions to the RL community.

By open-sourcing its implementation codes and the Viper10K dataset, the authors facilitate further research and development in this promising direction, potentially catalyzing a new wave of autonomous and perceptually intelligent Vision-Language Models.
