# OpenVision: A Fully-Open, Cost-Effective Family of Advanced Vision Encoders for Multimodal Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> OpenVision introduces a family of fully open vision encoders that match or exceed OpenAI's CLIP performance on multimodal benchmarks while providing multiple parameter scales and resolution options, achieved through systematic analysis of training efficiency factors and progressive resolution training using open datasets and frameworks.

## Problem

- Existing multimodal systems heavily depend on proprietary vision encoders like CLIP, limiting transparency and reproducibility
- Current open-source vision encoders lag behind proprietary models in performance and parameter scale flexibility
- Known weaknesses exist in spatial understanding and object counting with current encoders

## Method

- Developed **OpenVision** fully open vision encoders by leveraging ==enhanced CLIP training== and ==Recap-DataComp-1B dataset==
- Implemented ==three-stage progressive resolution training== with ==auxiliary text decoder==
- Created multiple encoder variants optimized for different input resolutions and computational budgets

## Results

- OpenVision-B/16 and L/14 outperform CLIP counterparts on most multimodal benchmarks, scaling effectively from lightweight to high-capacity models while maintaining performance
- OpenVision-H/14 shows substantial gains in high-resolution VQA, OCR and retrieval tasks
- Full transparency with released training data, code and checkpoints enables reproducibility

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-quality synthetic captions and auxiliary text decoder are crucial for strong multimodal performance
> - Balanced allocation across resolutions during training improves efficiency and capabilities
> - Progressive resolution training enables both computational efficiency and high performance

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses a critical gap in the rapidly evolving field of multimodal learning: the lack of fully open and cost-effective vision encoders that can rival proprietary models like OpenAI's CLIP.

- **Dependence on Proprietary Models:** Multimodal foundation models have heavily relied on OpenAI's CLIP encoders as visual backbones. This reliance raises concerns about transparency, reproducibility, and limitations in parameter scales.
- **Open-Source Efforts:** The open-source community has made significant strides in creating open CLIP training frameworks, billion-scale datasets, and improved training methodologies. However, no fully open vision encoder has consistently matched or surpassed OpenAI's CLIP on multimodal benchmarks.
- **Filling the Gap:** This research introduces OpenVision, a family of vision encoders designed to address this gap. It builds upon existing open-source resources and incorporates key insights to improve encoder quality and training efficiency.
- **Significance of Openness:** By releasing fully open datasets, training recipes, and checkpoints, this work promotes transparency, reproducibility, and flexibility in multimodal research, enabling the community to move beyond the constraints of proprietary encoders.

This research aligns with the broader trend of open-source machine learning and the democratization of AI. By providing high-quality, fully open vision encoders, the authors enable researchers and practitioners to:

- Replicate and extend the results.
- Develop custom multimodal models without relying on proprietary technology.
- Deploy lightweight models on edge devices for real-world applications.
- Contribute to the collective knowledge and accelerate innovation in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are:

- **Develop a Fully Open Vision Encoder:** To create a family of vision encoders with fully disclosed architectures, training data, and training recipes.
- **Achieve Competitive Performance:** To match or surpass the performance of OpenAI's CLIP encoders on multimodal learning tasks.
- **Provide Cost-Effective Solutions:** To design encoders that are computationally efficient and can be trained with reasonable resources.
- **Enable Flexible Deployment:** To offer a range of encoder sizes to accommodate diverse deployment needs, from edge devices to high-capacity servers.

The motivation behind this research stems from the limitations of relying on proprietary vision encoders:

- **Lack of Transparency:** OpenAI's CLIP's training data and framework are undisclosed, hindering reproducibility and further research.
- **Limited Parameter Scales:** The availability of CLIP only in two parameter scales restricts the development of lightweight models and the exploration of higher-capacity encoders.
- **Known Weaknesses:** OpenAI's CLIP has documented weaknesses in spatial-relation understanding and object-counting hallucinations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a systematic approach to build and evaluate the OpenVision family of vision encoders:

- **Leveraging Existing Resources:** They build upon CLIPS (an enhanced CLIP training pipeline) and Recap-DataComp-1B (a re-captioned version of the DataComp-1B corpus).
- **Systematic Analysis:** They conduct a thorough analysis to identify key design elements that improve training efficiency and enhance encoder quality.
- **Three-Stage Training:** They employ a staged training approach with increasing resolution to improve efficiency and yield model variants capable of handling different input resolutions.
- **Multimodal Evaluation:** They benchmark the encoders on general VQA tasks by attaching a lightweight MLP projector and performing visual instruction tuning. They use two LLaVA setups: LLaVA-1.5 (keeping the vision encoder frozen) and Open-LLaVA-Next (fine-tuning the entire model).
- **Evaluation Benchmarks:** They report performance on a suite of tasks, including MME, GQA, ChartQA, POPE, TextVQA, OCR, SEED, MMVet, and SQA.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The key findings and results of this research are:

- **OpenVision achieves competitive performance:** OpenVision encoders match or surpass the performance of OpenAI's CLIP and Google's SigLIP on various multimodal benchmarks.
- **Superior multimodal performance:** OpenVision-B/16 and OpenVision-L/14 checkpoints significantly outperform their counterparts on most tasks under both MLLM setups.
- **Effective for high-resolution VQA:** The OpenVision-H/14 variant delivers substantial gains over OpenAI CLIP-L/14 in multimodal understanding, particularly in high-resolution VQA, OCR, and retrieval tasks.
- **Scalability and efficiency:** OpenVision encoders demonstrate flexibility in scaling model size and input resolution, making them suitable for diverse deployment environments.
- **Auxiliary decoder and synthetic captions are critical:** Ablation studies show that both the auxiliary text decoder and high-quality synthetic captions are crucial to the strong multimodal performance of OpenVision.
- **Progressive resolution training is efficient:** Progressive resolution training yields vision encoders that are both performant and computationally efficient for multimodal learning.
- **Fine-tuning improvements:** A balanced allocation across resolutions is more efficient for fine-tuning, as lower-resolution fine-tuning helps establish general visual representations, while high-resolution tuning refines fine-grained understanding capabilities.
- **High-quality captions are essential:** Ablation studies emphasize that high-quality synthetic captions from Recap-DataComp and a moderate learning rate are critical for maximizing the performance of this tiny multimodal models.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research has significant implications for the field of multimodal learning:

- **Democratization of AI:** By providing fully open and cost-effective vision encoders, this work empowers researchers and practitioners to develop custom multimodal models without relying on proprietary technology.
- **Accelerated Innovation:** The release of datasets, training recipes, and checkpoints fosters transparency, reproducibility, and flexibility, accelerating innovation in the field.
- **Edge Deployment:** The availability of lightweight OpenVision encoders enables the development of practical, high-quality edge-ready multimodal systems.
- **Improved Multimodal Performance:** The systematic analysis and design choices in OpenVision lead to improved performance on various multimodal tasks.

Potential impacts of this research include:

- **Advancement of Multimodal Foundation Models:** OpenVision provides a valuable resource for building and improving multimodal foundation models.
- **Development of New Applications:** The availability of lightweight encoders enables the creation of new applications in areas such as mobile AI, robotics, and IoT.
- **Increased Collaboration and Knowledge Sharing:** The open-source nature of this work promotes collaboration and knowledge sharing within the research community.

In conclusion, this paper makes a significant contribution to the field of multimodal learning by introducing OpenVision, a fully open and cost-effective family of vision encoders that rival proprietary models. This work has the potential to democratize AI, accelerate innovation, and enable the development of new applications in various domains.
