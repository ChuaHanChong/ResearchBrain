# Contrastive World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Contrastive World Models replaces Dreamer's pixel-reconstruction term in the RSSM objective with a Deep InfoMax-style InfoNCE bound that maximizes mutual information between state-action sequences and local patch features of future observations, deleting the pixel decoder. It matches Dreamer in clean DeepMind Control, and clearly outperforms Dreamer and a momentum-prediction baseline under moving distractors and natural-video backgrounds, while training faster per step.

## Problem

- Dreamer and Genie-style world models learn latents by pixel reconstruction, so high-entropy visual detail dominates the loss and crowds out control-relevant information.
- Earlier contrastive Dreamer variants underperformed reconstruction, and CSWM was scored only on latent ranking in deterministic domains, never on rewards or downstream control.
- JEPA-style latent world models such as LeWM stay validated at roughly 15M parameters on four visually clean stationary-background environments, leaving visual-nuisance robustness unshown.

## Method

- **Deep InfoMax** representation loss: an ==InfoNCE bound== scoring the ==global posterior state== concatenated with the action sequence against ==local patch encodings== of the future observation, negatives drawn across the batch.
- ==Log-bilinear score function== exp(c-transpose W x) with learned matrix W, averaged over all spatial patches, replacing the ==RSSM observation-likelihood term== entirely.
- Combined objective: same RSSM backbone and actor-critic, keeping the ==KL transition loss== and ==Gaussian reward head== weighted by lambda1 and lambda2, but ==dropping the pixel decoder== network.

## Results

- On default DeepMind Control (finger-spin, cheetah-run, walker-walk; **1M** env steps, **3** seeds) all three methods reach comparable asymptotic return, with InfoMax consistently faster per training step.
- With simple moving-ball distractors, InfoMax matches or exceeds Dreamer on **two of three** tasks, notably cheetah-run, while Momentum Prediction collapses on **all three** tasks.
- With full-color Kinetics video backgrounds, InfoMax beats both baselines on **every task**; Dreamer fails to make task progress and Momentum Prediction stays near random.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reconstruction is an entropy-weighted objective: it spends capacity on whatever dominates pixel variance, so it degrades exactly where backgrounds are rich.
> - BYOL-style momentum prediction without local-global structure collapses under distraction; the target is satisfied by encoding distractor motion instead of task dynamics.
> - Patch-level local-global mutual information is the load-bearing detail, not contrastive learning generally; evidence is small-scale RSSM on DeepMind Control only.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Position within the Broader Research Landscape

World models are latent models of environmental dynamics that allow reinforcement-learning agents to predict future states, rewards, and observations. They are used for planning, model-predictive control, and learning policies from imagined rather than exclusively real trajectories. Early work such as *World Models* separated visual encoding, memory, and control, while later Dreamer systems developed recurrent latent-state models that support end-to-end learning of value functions and policies from imagined rollouts.

A common design in this area is to train the world model partly through observation reconstruction. The encoder maps an image to a latent representation, and a decoder attempts to regenerate the image. This objective supplies a dense learning signal, but it also requires the latent state to retain information about every visually salient part of the observation. In environments containing moving backgrounds, lighting variation, camera noise, or unrelated objects, much of this information may be irrelevant to control. The model can therefore devote capacity to reconstructing nuisance factors rather than learning dynamics that matter for decision-making.

The paper places its contribution at the intersection of three research directions. The first is Dreamer-style model-based reinforcement learning, particularly the Recurrent State Space Model (RSSM), which combines deterministic recurrent features with stochastic latent states. The second is contrastive and mutual-information-based representation learning, including Contrastive Predictive Coding, Deep InfoMax, SimCLR, MoCo, and related methods. The third is latent-space predictive modeling, including JEPA-style methods that predict representations rather than pixels.

Prior attempts to use contrastive objectives in world models had mixed results. A contrastive Dreamer variant evaluated by Hafner et al. reportedly underperformed pixel reconstruction on most tasks available at the time. Other approaches, such as CSWM and more recent JEPA-based world models, avoid direct pixel generation but have been evaluated mainly in simple or visually clean settings. The present work therefore focuses on whether a mutual-information objective can improve robustness specifically when observations contain visual distractions.

### 2. Objectives and Motivation

The main objective is to replace pixel reconstruction in a Dreamer-style world model with a representation-learning objective that encourages latent states to preserve information predictive of the future without requiring them to reproduce complete images.

The paper is motivated by a distinction between **visual predictability** and **control relevance**. Pixel reconstruction treats all image content as part of the prediction target. A moving background or natural video may have high visual complexity and substantial pixel-level variation, even though it provides little information about the agent’s actions, rewards, or controllable state. In contrast, a representation objective can focus on whether the latent state and action sequence identify the correct future observation or future feature.

The proposed method is based on maximizing mutual information between a current latent state, the intervening actions, and local features of a future observation. Formally, the intended quantity is:

\[
I([s_t,a_{t:t+h}];o_{t+h}),
\]

where \(s_t\) is the current posterior state, \(a_{t:t+h}\) is the sequence of actions leading toward the future, and \(o_{t+h}\) is a future observation. Rather than using the whole future image as a reconstruction target, the method uses spatial patches from the encoder’s feature map. This local-global structure is adopted from Deep InfoMax: a global representation should retain information that helps identify local regions of a future observation.

The authors hypothesize that the proposed method will perform similarly to reconstruction-based learning in visually simple environments, where pixel reconstruction is already adequate. They expect the main benefits to emerge as visual complexity and distraction increase. A second objective is computational efficiency. Removing the pixel decoder should reduce the computation required for each training step while retaining the encoder and latent dynamics model.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Base World Model

The method is built on the RSSM architecture used in Dreamer. The environment is modeled as a partially observable Markov decision process with image observations \(o_t\), actions \(a_t\), rewards \(r_t\), and latent states \(s_t\).

The model contains four conceptual components:

1. A transition model predicts the next latent state from the previous state and action.
2. An observation encoder converts the current image into a lower-dimensional feature representation \(z_t\).
3. An update function combines the encoded observation with predicted state features to form the posterior state \(s_t\).
4. A reward model predicts rewards from the latent state.

In standard RSSM training, a decoder reconstructs \(o_t\) from \(s_t\). The objective combines observation reconstruction, reward prediction, and a KL divergence between the posterior state distribution and the action-conditioned prior transition distribution.

#### 3.2. Contrastive InfoMax Objective

Contrastive World Models remove the observation decoder. The replacement objective uses an InfoNCE-style lower bound on mutual information. For a given current state and action sequence, the method computes a compatibility score between the conditioning representation and local patches from a future observation.

The conditioning representation is the concatenation of the current posterior state \(s_t\) and the intervening action sequence \(a_{t:t+h}\). The future observation is passed through the same encoder, producing a spatial feature map with local features \(e_{m,n}(o_{t+h})\). A bilinear score is used:

\[
f_\theta(x,c)=\exp(c^\top W_\theta x),
\]

where \(c\) is the state-action conditioning vector and \(x\) is a future local feature.

For each positive pair, consisting of the correct conditioning sequence and its corresponding future observation, the objective compares the positive score with scores obtained using observations from other batch elements as negatives. The loss averages the log-softmax expression over time steps and spatial patches. The resulting objective encourages the state-action sequence to distinguish the correct future local features from features associated with other trajectories.

The full training objective contains three components:

- **InfoMax representation loss:** identifies future local features associated with the current state and action sequence.
- **Transition loss:** maintains consistency between posterior states inferred using future observations and prior states predicted from previous states and actions.
- **Reward loss:** trains the latent representation to predict rewards.

The transition and reward terms are weighted by coefficients \(\lambda_1\) and \(\lambda_2\). As in Dreamer, the learned latent dynamics support an actor-critic planner, which learns from imagined rollouts. Thus, the comparison between methods is intended to isolate the effect of the representation-learning objective rather than differences in the downstream control algorithm.

#### 3.3. Baselines and Evaluation

The study compares three methods:

- **Dreamer:** uses pixel reconstruction as the observation-learning objective.
- **InfoMax:** the proposed Contrastive World Model.
- **Momentum Prediction:** a BYOL- and MPR-like baseline that uses an online encoder and an exponentially moving-average target encoder. A prediction head is trained to match the target embedding of a future observation using cosine similarity, without explicit negative samples.

All methods use the same RSSM backbone, reward and transition objectives, and actor-critic planner. Each agent is trained for one million environment steps on three DeepMind Control Suite tasks: finger-spin, cheetah-run, and walker-walk. Results are averaged over three random seeds.

The experiments use three visual conditions:

1. **Default setting:** standard DeepMind Control Suite observations with stationary backgrounds.
2. **Simple distractor setting:** moving colored balls are added to the background.
3. **Natural video setting:** full-color video from the Kinetics dataset is used as the background. This is described as more difficult than related experiments that used grayscale backgrounds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Default Visual Setting

In the standard, distraction-free environment, all three methods eventually achieve comparable performance. This result indicates that pixel reconstruction remains sufficient when observations are visually simple and the task-relevant objects dominate the image. The InfoMax objective does not provide a clear performance advantage in this condition, consistent with the authors’ hypothesis.

Momentum Prediction is also competitive in the default setting. This suggests that a momentum-target representation objective can learn useful states when the observation contains relatively little nuisance variation.

#### 4.2. Simple Moving Distractors

The methods separate more clearly when moving distractors are added. InfoMax matches or exceeds Dreamer on two of the three tasks. The largest reported difference occurs on cheetah-run, where Dreamer reaches a substantially lower reward and appears to have difficulty separating the cheetah’s dynamics from the distractor motion.

The Momentum Prediction baseline performs poorly in this setting, remaining near its initial performance across all three tasks. The authors suggest that the baseline can satisfy its prediction objective by encoding predictable distractor motion rather than task-relevant information. The InfoMax formulation is intended to reduce this problem by requiring a global state-action representation to identify local features of the appropriate future observation. Distractors that move independently of the agent’s actions should be less consistently informative about the action-conditioned future.

#### 4.3. Natural Video Backgrounds

The strongest differences occur when natural video backgrounds are introduced. InfoMax substantially outperforms both Dreamer and Momentum Prediction on every evaluated task according to the reported learning curves.

Dreamer’s performance declines because the reconstruction objective is dominated by the high-entropy natural-video background. Since the decoder is required to reproduce the entire image, the model receives a strong learning signal from background variation that is not necessarily related to the task. The agent consequently makes limited task progress.

Momentum Prediction also plateaus early and remains close to random or initial performance. The paper interprets this as evidence that a prediction-only objective without the explicit local-global mutual-information structure can be vulnerable to representing nuisance variation.

InfoMax is more resilient because it does not need to reconstruct the background pixel by pixel. Instead, it learns features that help associate state-action histories with future encoded patches. The authors argue that this directs capacity toward information that is predictive of future observations in a way that is more compatible with controllable dynamics.

#### 4.4. Training Efficiency

The proposed method removes the pixel decoder altogether. With the encoder and dynamics model held at comparable parameter counts, the authors report consistently lower wall-clock time per training step than Dreamer across all three visual settings. The paper does not provide detailed hardware specifications or a numerical speedup, so the efficiency claim is qualitative rather than a fully quantified systems comparison.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Limitations

The paper’s primary significance is its demonstration that a world model can support downstream control without directly reconstructing images, and that this design may improve robustness to visual nuisance factors. This is relevant to real-world deployment, where observations commonly contain uncontrolled background motion, illumination changes, sensor artifacts, and unrelated objects.

The method also has implications for large-scale self-supervised pretraining. Because it does not require action-conditioned pixel reconstruction, the authors argue that the objective could potentially use state-action sequences paired with future observations in broader datasets. They identify action-free video pretraining, larger domains, and language-specified goals as possible extensions.

However, the evidence remains limited in scope. The experiments are small-scale, use only three DeepMind Control Suite tasks, average over three seeds, and rely on an older RSSM-based architecture. The paper does not evaluate transfer to new environments, real-world observations, longer-horizon planning, or transformer-based world models. It also does not provide ablations isolating the effects of patch size, prediction horizon, negative sampling, loss weights, or the use of action sequences. Because the natural-video setting is a benchmark construction rather than a physical environment, its relationship to real-world visual complexity requires further validation.

Overall, the study presents a focused alternative to pixel-reconstruction objectives in latent world models. Its results support the claim that mutual-information-based representation learning can be advantageous when visual observations contain substantial distractor information, while offering a potentially lower-computation training procedure. Further work is needed to determine whether these benefits persist at larger scales, across architectures, and in environments where accurate visual generation is itself necessary for planning.
