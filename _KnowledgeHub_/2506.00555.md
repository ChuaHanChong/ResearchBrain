# MMedAgent-RL: Optimizing Multi-Agent Collaboration for Multimodal Medical Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> MMedAgent-RL, a reinforcement learning-driven multi-agent framework, dynamically optimizes collaboration among a triage doctor, specialist agents, and an attending physician for multimodal medical reasoning. The framework improved diagnostic accuracy by an average of 23.6% over strong baselines across five medical VQA benchmarks, demonstrating robust performance even when presented with inconsistent specialist advice.

## Problem

- Single-agent Medical Large Vision-Language Models (Med-LVLMs) struggle to generalize across the diverse specialties required in medical practice.
- Existing multi-agent medical AI frameworks employ static, predetermined interaction sequences, limiting their adaptability and flexibility in complex or ambiguous diagnostic scenarios.
- Effectively integrating and weighing potentially unreliable or conflicting diagnostic judgments from multiple specialist agents remains a challenge.

## Method

- The **MMedAgent-RL** framework establishes a three-stage, RL-driven process: initial ==departmental triage==, specialist consultation, and evolving decisions by an attending physician, using Qwen2.5-VL as the core GP model.
- A "triage doctor" agent is optimized via Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) to dynamically assign patients to the most appropriate medical specialty based on multimodal input.
- Curriculum-based Entropy-Aware Reinforcement Learning (C-MARL) guides the "attending physician" agent to integrate specialist opinions by dynamically adjusting its exploration-exploitation balance based on the reliability of specialist inputs.

## Results

- MMedAgent-RL achieved an average performance gain of **23.6%** over strong baselines across five medical VQA benchmarks, attaining an average accuracy of **73.3%** on in-domain and **72.6%** on out-of-distribution datasets.
- The framework demonstrated robustness by maintaining performance and achieving significant gains (e.g., **23.0%** accuracy on hard OmniMedVQA samples where specialists failed) in scenarios with incorrect or conflicting specialist advice.
- Ablation studies confirmed C-MARL as the most impactful component, contributing an **18.6%** performance increase by effectively addressing the integration of potentially misaligned expert knowledge.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamic, reinforcement learning-driven multi-agent collaboration significantly enhances adaptability and robustness in complex medical reasoning compared to static interaction patterns.
> - Defining curriculum learning difficulty based on specialist agent reliability and dynamically adjusting entropy regularization effectively enables learning from both reliable and misleading expert inputs.
> - A structured, three-stage clinical workflow simulation (triage, specialist consultation, attending physician decision) can effectively synthesize diverse multimodal information and expert judgments for improved diagnostic outcomes.
