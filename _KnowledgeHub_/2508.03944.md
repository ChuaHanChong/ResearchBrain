# Constraint-Preserving Data Generation for Visuomotor Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> CP-Gen synthesizes thousands of robot demonstrations for visuomotor policy learning from a single expert trajectory, robustly adapting to diverse object geometries and poses. This approach enables an average 70% success rate on tasks involving novel object geometries in simulation and achieves 83% average success for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on challenging real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Large-scale robot demonstration datasets are expensive and time-consuming to collect, creating a bottleneck for imitation learning.
- Existing automated data generation methods struggle to adapt demonstrations to variations in object geometries, limiting generalization beyond pose changes.
- Achieving reliable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for visuomotor policies remains challenging due to the sim-to-real gap and the need for diverse training data.

## Method

- CP-Gen formulates robot skills using object-centric keypoint-trajectory constraints extracted from a single expert demonstration, segmenting it into free-space motion and skill segments.
- It generates new demonstrations by sampling novel object poses and non-uniform geometries, then optimizes robot joint configurations to satisfy the adapted keypoint-trajectory constraints for skill segments.
- A collision-free motion planner computes paths for free-space segments, and a Diffusion Policy is trained on the successfully generated and filtered simulations.

## Results

- In simulation, CP-Gen achieved an average 70% success rate on tasks with novel object geometries, outperforming MimicGen (37%) and DemoGen (44%) on these challenging variants.
- CP-Gen policies demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving an average 83% success rate across four complex real-world manipulation tasks with novel geometries, compared to MimicGen's 40%.
- Ablation studies confirmed that training policies on datasets with geometry diversity resulted in a 73% success rate on geometry generalization tasks, significantly higher than the 45% achieved without such diversity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing robot skills through keypoint-trajectory constraints relative to task-relevant objects naturally enables adaptation to diverse object geometries.
> - Explicitly incorporating geometry diversity into generated training data substantially improves policy generalization to unseen object shapes and sizes.
> - Generating structured, constraint-preserving data can achieve the generalization benefits of equivariant policy architectures without their potential expressivity limitations.
