# Constraint-Preserving Data Generation for Visuomotor Policy Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> CP-Gen synthesizes thousands of robot demonstrations for visuomotor policy learning from a single expert trajectory, robustly adapting to diverse object geometries and poses. This approach enables an average 70% success rate on tasks involving novel object geometries in simulation and achieves 83% average success for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer on challenging real-world tasks.

## Problem

- Large-scale robot demonstration datasets are expensive and time-consuming to collect, creating a bottleneck for imitation learning.
- Existing automated data generation methods struggle to adapt demonstrations to variations in object geometries, limiting generalization beyond pose changes.
- Achieving reliable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for visuomotor policies remains challenging due to the sim-to-real gap and the need for diverse training data.

## Method

- CP-Gen formulates robot skills using object-centric keypoint-trajectory constraints extracted from a single expert demonstration, segmenting it into free-space motion and skill segments.
- It generates new demonstrations by sampling novel object poses and non-uniform geometries, then optimizes robot joint configurations to satisfy the adapted keypoint-trajectory constraints for skill segments.
- A collision-free motion planner computes paths for free-space segments, and a Diffusion Policy is trained on the successfully generated and filtered simulations.

## Results

- In simulation, CP-Gen achieved an average 70% success rate on tasks with novel object geometries, outperforming MimicGen (37%) and DemoGen (44%) on these challenging variants.
- CP-Gen policies demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving an average 83% success rate across four complex real-world manipulation tasks with novel geometries, compared to MimicGen's 40%.
- Ablation studies confirmed that training policies on datasets with geometry diversity resulted in a 73% success rate on geometry generalization tasks, significantly higher than the 45% achieved without such diversity.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing robot skills through keypoint-trajectory constraints relative to task-relevant objects naturally enables adaptation to diverse object geometries.
> - Explicitly incorporating geometry diversity into generated training data substantially improves policy generalization to unseen object shapes and sizes.
> - Generating structured, constraint-preserving data can achieve the generalization benefits of equivariant policy architectures without their potential expressivity limitations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robot manipulation, particularly through imitation learning, has witnessed remarkable progress in recent years. Large-scale demonstration datasets, such as ALOHA Unleashed and DROID, have enabled robots to tackle complex tasks, demonstrating impressive dexterity and generalization. However, the immense cost in terms of human labor, operational time, and infrastructure required to collect such datasets remains a significant bottleneck, often involving months of continuous robot operation and multiple human operators.

This paper primarily addresses the challenge of **data scarcity and costly data collection** in imitation learning. It operates within two main lines of research:

1. **Automated Data Generation:** Prior work, exemplified by MimicGen, IntervenGen, DexMimicGen, Skill-Gen, and DemoGen, attempts to mitigate data scarcity by generating new demonstrations from a small set of human-provided "source" trajectories. These methods typically exploit the SE(3) equivariance of robot actions with respect to object poses. While effective for generalizing across different object poses, they fundamentally struggle to adapt demonstrations to **variations in object geometries**. For instance, a policy trained on data generated with a specific wine glass geometry might fail when presented with a taller, narrower one, even if the pose is similar. This limitation is a crucial gap that CP-Gen aims to fill.

2. **Equivariance in Policy Architectures:** A complementary approach focuses on embedding geometric structure (equivariance) directly into policy architectures to improve learning efficiency and generalization. This includes work on SE(2), SE(3), and SIM(3) equivariant feature representations and closed-loop policies [11–23, 28–32]. While these methods offer benefits like strong sample efficiency and reactivity, they can introduce bottlenecks in policy expressiveness, often assume uniform scaling for geometry variations, and may not generalize well to multi-object scenarios or complex symmetry-breaking changes. CP-Gen seeks to achieve similar benefits of generalization to varied geometries *through data generation*, thereby avoiding potential architectural limitations of explicit equivariance.

CP-Gen positions itself as a novel approach that bridges these two lines of research. It aims to achieve the broad generalization benefits of equivariant architectures without their inherent expressivity limitations, by focusing on **geometry-aware data generation** that goes beyond simple SE(3) transformations. Its key innovation lies in formulating robot skills using keypoint-trajectory constraints, which naturally adapt to object geometry changes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this research is to develop a data generation method, **Constraint-Preserving Data Generation (CP-Gen)**, that can effectively synthesize diverse and high-quality robot demonstrations from as little as a single expert trajectory.

The primary motivations driving this objective are:
- **Alleviate Data Collection Burden:** The astronomical cost and time associated with collecting large-scale robot demonstration datasets necessitate more efficient data generation techniques. CP-Gen aims to significantly reduce this burden.
- **Enable Geometry-Aware Generalization:** Existing automated data generation methods primarily focus on pose variations and fall short when confronted with changes in object geometry (e.g., aspect ratio, shape). CP-Gen seeks to overcome this by generating demonstrations that robustly adapt to novel object geometries, in addition to novel poses.
- **Facilitate Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A critical hurdle in deploying robot policies is the sim-to-real gap. By generating diverse, geometry-aware data in simulation, the authors aim to train visuomotor policies that can transfer directly to the real world without needing real-world fine-tuning.
- **Achieve Benefits of Equivariance without Architectural Bottlenecks:** The paper is motivated by the desire to achieve robust generalization across geometric variations, similar to equivariant policy architectures, but through data augmentation rather than direct architectural constraints. This aims to maintain policy expressiveness while benefiting from structured generalization.

In essence, CP-Gen's motivation is to make robot imitation learning more scalable, robust, and deployable by generating high-quality, diverse, and geometrically adaptive training data from minimal human input.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

CP-Gen operates in two main stages: Source Data Processing and New Data Generation, followed by policy training. The central idea revolves around formulating robot skills as keypoint-trajectory constraints, which enables geometry-aware adaptation.

#### 3.1. Source Data Processing

Given a single expert demonstration ($\tau_{src}$), this stage prepares the trajectory for data generation:
- **Demonstration Segmentation:** The expert trajectory is first segmented into alternating *free-space motion segments* ($\tau_{motion}$) and *robot skill segments* ($\tau_{skill}$). Skill segments represent interaction-heavy parts that require specific coordination with task-relevant objects, while motion segments are simpler point-to-point movements.
- **Keypoint Annotation:** For each skill segment, "actor keypoints" are manually annotated. These are specific 3D points on the robot gripper or the grasped object that are critical for successfully executing the skill (e.g., gripper tips for grasping, object stem for insertion).
- **Keypoint-Trajectory Constraint Extraction:** Each skill segment is then converted into a keypoint-trajectory constraint. This involves transforming the actor keypoints into the local frame of a task-relevant object (the "target object") at each timestep of the skill. This creates a "target keypoint-trajectory" expressed relative to the object. This object-centric representation is crucial for geometry awareness, as it anchors the skill's requirements to the object itself, rather than absolute world coordinates.

#### 3.2. New Data Generation

Once the source demonstration is processed, CP-Gen can generate thousands of new demonstrations for diverse scenes:
- **Sample Scene Variations:** For each new demonstration, CP-Gen samples novel pose and geometry transformations (e.g., non-uniform scaling, translation, rotation) for all task-relevant objects in the scene.
- **Skill Segment Adaptation:** For each skill segment in the new scene:
 - **Update Keypoint-Trajectory Constraint for New Geometry:** If an object's geometry is transformed, this transformation is applied to the extracted target keypoint-trajectory. If the robot grasps an object that has undergone a geometric change, the actor keypoints on the grasped object are also updated accordingly.
 - **Solve for Robot Configuration via Keypoint Matching:** For every timestep of the skill segment, an optimization problem is solved to determine the robot's joint configuration ($q^*_t$). This optimization minimizes the squared distance between the world-frame positions of the *current* actor keypoints (derived via forward kinematics from $q_t$) and the *updated target* keypoints (derived from the transformed target keypoint-trajectory and the new object pose). A temporal smoothness penalty is added to ensure fluid robot movements. The L-BFGS-B optimizer is used for this step.
- **Motion Planning:** For each free-space motion segment, a collision-free motion planner (e.g.,) is used to compute a path from the robot's current joint configuration to the first optimized joint configuration of the subsequent skill segment. This ensures that the robot can reach the starting pose of each skill without collisions.
- **Filtering:** Any generated demonstrations that fail (e.g., due to collisions or inability to reach the target) are filtered out using a task-specific success detector.

#### 3.3. Policy Training

Finally, a visuomotor policy (specifically, a Diffusion Policy (DDPM variant)) is trained on the successfully generated demonstrations. The policy takes robot proprioception (end-effector pose, gripper width) and visual observations (RGB images or depth maps with segmentation masks from wrist-mounted and third-person cameras) as input to predict robot actions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to answer four key questions, covering both simulation and real-world scenarios.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

Experiments were conducted on 8 single-arm Franka Panda tasks from the MimicGen benchmark, under two environment reset distributions:
- **Pose Only:** Varies only object poses (default MimicGen setting).
- **Geometry Generalization:** Introduces shape variations (non-uniform scaling) in addition to pose variations.

**Key Findings:**
- **Generalization to Novel Geometries (Q1, Q2):** CP-Gen significantly outperforms baselines on tasks with novel object geometries. On the Geometry Generalization variants (denoted TaskG), CP-Gen achieved an average success rate of 70%, drastically outperforming MimicGen (37%) and DemoGen (44%). This directly validates CP-Gen's ability to generate geometry-aware data and the efficacy of its keypoint-trajectory constraint formulation.
- **Performance on Pose-Only Variations (Q2):** On the default Pose Only tasks, CP-Gen achieved an average success rate of 88% (RGB) and 82% (D+S), comparable to DemoGen (87% RGB, 82% D+S) but significantly better than MimicGen (67% RGB, 59% D+S). The improved performance over MimicGen is attributed to CP-Gen's use of a collision-free motion planner and actor keypoints, which allow for more adaptive actions than MimicGen's simple SE(3) transformations.
- **Effect of Geometry Diversity (Q3 - Ablations):** Ablation studies confirmed that training policies on datasets generated with diverse object geometries substantially improves generalization to novel geometries (73% success on geometry tasks when trained with geometry diversity, compared to 45% when trained only with pose diversity).

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

CP-Gen's approach was evaluated on four challenging real-world manipulation tasks: Mug Cleanup, Hammer Cleanup, Mug Hanging, and Wine Glass Spiral Hanging, featuring multi-stage, non-prehensile, and tight-tolerance manipulation. Policies were trained entirely in simulation using CP-Gen generated data and then transferred zero-shot to a real Franka Panda robot.

**Key Findings:**
- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer (Q4):** CP-Gen policies achieved strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, with an average success rate of 83% across the four tasks, tested on novel object geometries. In contrast, MimicGen achieved only 40% success, highlighting its struggle with geometric variations in the real world. This demonstrates the robustness and practicality of CP-Gen's approach for real-world deployment.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Keypoint-Trajectory Constraints vs. Relative Pose:** CP-Gen's data generation process (which uses keypoint-trajectory constraints) consistently achieved higher demonstration success rates compared to MimicGen's relative pose formulation, both in pose-only (89% vs. 58%) and geometry generalization settings (62% vs. 23%). This validates the efficacy of the keypoint-trajectory constraint formulation in generating valid, adaptive demonstrations.
- **Impact of Geometry Diversity on Policy Generalization:** Training policies on datasets incorporating geometry diversity substantially improved their generalization capabilities to novel geometries (73% success rate on geometry tasks versus 45% when trained without geometry diversity).

### 5. Significance

CP-Gen represents a significant advancement in robot learning by addressing a critical gap in automated data generation: handling novel object geometries. By introducing a novel keypoint-trajectory constraint formulation, it enables the generation of highly diverse and geometrically adaptive demonstrations from a single expert trajectory. This radically reduces the manual effort and cost traditionally associated with collecting large datasets for imitation learning. The demonstrated success in zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for complex, tight-tolerance tasks is a strong indicator of its practical utility and robustness. This work paves the way for more generalizable and deployable robot policies in dynamic, unstructured environments where objects often vary in shape and size.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated Robot Deployment:** By minimizing data collection costs and enabling broader generalization, CP-Gen can significantly accelerate the development and deployment of robotic systems in various domains, from manufacturing and logistics to assistive robotics and household automation.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** Policies trained with CP-Gen data can handle a wider range of object variations, leading to more robust and autonomous robots that are less susceptible to changes in their environment.
- **Democratization of Robot Learning:** Reducing the entry barrier of data collection could make advanced robot manipulation more accessible to researchers and smaller organizations.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The keypoint-trajectory constraint idea provides a new framework for thinking about skill representation and adaptation, potentially inspiring further work in structured data generation and learned representations for manipulation.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors candidly discuss several limitations:
- **Manual Annotation:** The method currently requires manual annotation of robot skill segments and keypoints. Future work could integrate vision-language models [34–36] or task-specific keypoint detectors [37–40] to automate this process.
- **Fixed Skill Sequence:** CP-Gen assumes a fixed sequence of skills, limiting its ability to handle task-level variations. Incorporating task-level planning using large language models [47–50] or task and motion planning (TAMP) methods [51–53] could improve task generalization.
- **Simulation Fidelity:** The simulation environment and reward functions are manually defined, which could be augmented by foundation model-based success classifiers.
- **Robot Platform Specificity:** Current results are limited to a single-arm Franka Panda robot. Extending the method to multi-arm, mobile base, or legged systems is a natural next step.
- **Instance-Level Geometry Adaptation:** The method struggles with specific object instances within a category (e.g., different handle shapes for a mug) if the geometric transformation is non-trivial. Future research could explore neural descriptor fields to assist in these nuanced scenarios.

Despite these limitations, CP-Gen represents a substantial step forward in developing data-efficient and generalizable robot manipulation capabilities, addressing a core challenge in the field.
