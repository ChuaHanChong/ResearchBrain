# Learning to Reason under Off-Policy Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> LUFFY introduces a framework that enhances Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) by integrating off-policy guidance into Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). This approach enables LRMs to acquire new reasoning capabilities from stronger external policies, achieving state-of-the-art performance on math benchmarks, superior generalization on out-of-distribution tasks, and successfully training weaker foundation models where on-policy methods fail.

## Problem

- Existing Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) for Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) operates in an "on-policy" manner, limiting models to learning from their own self-generated outputs.
- This on-policy constraint restricts LRMs from acquiring genuinely new cognitive capacities or reasoning strategies that lie beyond their initial exploration capabilities.
- Models trained with on-policy RLVR often reach performance plateaus, unable to progress further due to a lack of foundational behaviors for advancement.

## Method

- ==LUFFY== extends Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) by integrating off-policy reasoning traces generated by stronger external models into the training process.
- It employs =="Mixed-Policy GRPO"== which incorporates both on-policy and off-policy rollouts for advantage computation, adaptively weighting the influence of external demonstrations based on the trained model's performance.
- =="Policy Shaping via Regularized Importance Sampling"== re-weights gradients for off-policy distributions, encouraging the model to learn from low-probability tokens and maintaining exploration to prevent =="entropy collapse"==.

## Results

- LUFFY achieved an average gain of **+6.4** points across six challenging math reasoning benchmarks and showed a **+6.2** points advantage on average over baselines for out-of-distribution tasks.
- It successfully trained weaker foundation models, such as LLaMA-3.1-8B, in scenarios where on-policy RLVR methods completely failed to make progress.
- Training dynamics revealed LUFFY maintains higher generation entropy throughout training, indicating continuous exploration, and dynamically balances imitation of off-policy traces with self-driven exploration.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Off-policy guidance allows Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) to transcend their initial cognitive boundaries by internalizing complex reasoning patterns from more capable external models.
> - Dynamically balancing imitation of external guidance with self-exploration is crucial for acquiring new skills and preventing models from becoming overly deterministic.
> - Off-policy learning enables the successful training of weaker foundation models that on-policy Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) methods cannot effectively optimize.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper "Learning to Reason under Off-Policy Guidance" directly addresses a critical and emerging limitation in the training of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). Recent breakthroughs, exemplified by models like OpenAI-o1, DeepSeek-R1, and Kimi-1.5, have showcased remarkable capabilities in complex reasoning, particularly through the generation of extensive Chains-of-Thought (CoT) and behaviors such as self-reflection. A key enabler for these advancements has been Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), where models learn by receiving explicit feedback on the correctness of their final answers.

However, the authors identify a fundamental constraint of existing RLVR approaches: their "on-policy" nature. This means models are primarily confined to learning from their *own* self-generated outputs. While effective for amplifying existing behaviors and refining current reasoning paths, this on-policy limitation inherently restricts the model's ability to acquire *genuinely novel cognitive capacities* or reasoning strategies that lie beyond its initial exploration capabilities. As the paper points out, models can quickly reach performance plateaus because they lack the foundational behaviors necessary for further advancement, akin to being trapped in an "echo chamber" of their existing knowledge.

LUFFY emerges as a significant contribution by bridging this gap. It introduces "off-policy guidance" to RLVR, a paradigm shift that allows LRMs to learn from high-quality reasoning traces generated by *stronger, external policies* (e.g., DeepSeek-R1). This approach moves beyond mere self-improvement to a form of "cognitive scaffolding," where a weaker model can observe and internalize reasoning patterns from a more capable "teacher" model. This is a novel application of off-policy learning principles, traditionally found in areas like Q-learning or Soft Actor-Critic, to the complex domain of LRM reasoning. By doing so, LUFFY aims to expand the reasoning repertoire of LRMs, enabling them to transcend the boundaries of their initial cognitive abilities and fostering the emergence of genuinely new reasoning skills. This positions LUFFY as a crucial step towards more adaptable, generalizable, and self-improving reasoning models, addressing a core challenge identified by leading researchers in the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to overcome the inherent limitations of "on-policy" Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in training Large Reasoning Models (LRMs). While current RLVR methods have successfully enabled sophisticated behaviors like multi-step reasoning and self-reflection, they are fundamentally constrained to learning from the model's own outputs. This restricts the model to amplifying existing behaviors rather than acquiring new, beyond-initial-capability reasoning abilities, leading to performance plateaus.

The authors' motivation stems from a critical question: "How can we empower LLMs to acquire reasoning behaviors surpassing their initial cognitive boundaries?" To address this, they propose and develop LUFFY, a framework designed to:
- **Integrate Off-Policy Guidance**: Allow LRMs to learn from external, high-quality reasoning traces generated by stronger models, exposing them to reasoning patterns otherwise inaccessible through self-exploration alone.
- **Balance Imitation and Exploration Dynamically**: Ensure that while the model learns from superior demonstrations, it also retains its capacity for self-driven exploration and avoids superficial, rigid imitation or "entropy collapse" (loss of exploratory capacity).
- **Achieve Robust and Generalizable Reasoning**: Develop a method that not only improves performance on in-distribution tasks but also enhances generalization to out-of-distribution reasoning challenges, and critically, enables the training of weaker foundation models where on-policy methods fail.

In essence, the core motivation is to enable LRMs to truly *learn* new ways of thinking and problem-solving, rather than merely refining their existing ones, thereby pushing the frontier of what these models can achieve in complex reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LUFFY (Learning to reason Under oFF-policY guidance) extends the RLVR paradigm by integrating off-policy reasoning traces into the training process. Its methodology centers on two key components: **Mixed-Policy GRPO** and **Policy Shaping via Regularized Importance Sampling**.

#### 3.1. Mixed-Policy GRPO

LUFFY builds upon Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), an effective on-policy RL method for LRMs that uses the reward scores of multiple sampled solutions from a query to estimate an advantage function, thus obviating the need for a separate value model.
- **Integration of Off-Policy Rollouts**: LUFFY incorporates off-policy reasoning traces (generated by a stronger model, $\pi_{\phi}$, like DeepSeek-R1) directly into the group of on-policy rollouts (generated by the model being trained, $\pi_{\theta_{old}}$).
- **Adaptive Advantage Computation**: The advantage $\hat{A}_i$ for a given trajectory is computed by normalizing its reward against the mean and standard deviation of rewards from *both* on-policy ($G_{on}$) and off-policy ($G_{off}$) rollouts. This design is crucial: when the trained model struggles (low on-policy rewards), the high-quality off-policy traces receive higher relative advantages, encouraging imitation. When the model performs well independently, its on-policy rollouts naturally receive higher advantages, promoting self-exploration.
- **Mixed Objective Function**: The overall objective, $J_{Mixed}(\theta)$, combines two terms: an on-policy objective (similar to GRPO/PPO) and a new off-policy objective. Both utilize importance sampling to calibrate gradients. The on-policy term uses $r_{i,t}(\theta) = \pi_{\theta}(\tau_{i,t}|\cdot) / \pi_{\theta_{old}}(\tau_{i,t}|\cdot)$, while the off-policy term introduces $\hat{r}_{j,t}(\theta, \phi) = \pi_{\theta}(\tau_{j,t}|\cdot) / \pi_{\phi}(\tau_{j,t}|\cdot)$.
- **Practical Implementation**: For computational efficiency and ease of integration with off-the-shelf datasets, the authors adopt $\pi_{\phi} = 1$ for the off-policy importance sampling denominator. This simplifies the computation while empirically facilitating the incorporation of diverse high-quality demonstrations without complex tokenization or recomputation. The clip operation, typically used in PPO for trust region enforcement, is omitted for off-policy rollouts due to the $\pi_{\phi} = 1$ assumption, as it would lead to imbalance.
- **Theoretical Guarantee**: The paper provides a theoretical convergence analysis (Theorem 1), demonstrating that the importance-weighted policy gradient estimator in LUFFY stabilizes and converges to a stationary point with a rate of $O(1/\sqrt{K})$, ensuring the method's theoretical soundness.

#### 3.2. Policy Shaping via Regularized Importance Sampling

A practical challenge with simply integrating off-policy guidance is the risk of "entropy collapse," where the model quickly converges to deterministic outputs, losing its capacity for exploration. This happens because the model tends to reinforce off-policy tokens that are *already likely* in its own distribution, neglecting crucial low-probability tokens that represent genuinely new reasoning capabilities.
- **Re-weighting Gradients**: To counter this, LUFFY introduces a "policy shaping" function, $f(\cdot)$, which modifies the off-policy importance sampling ratio $\hat{r}_{j,t}(\theta, \phi)$ to $f(\hat{r}_{j,t}(\theta, \phi))$. This function re-weights the gradient of off-policy distributions.
- **Enhancing Low-Probability Token Learning**: The chosen shaping function is $f(x) = x / (x + \gamma)$, where $\gamma$ is a small constant (empirically set to 0.1). This function is designed to amplify learning signals for actions (tokens) that have low probability under the model's current policy but are present in the high-quality off-policy traces. By doing so, it encourages the model to explore and internalize "unfamiliar yet effective decisions" from the off-policy guidance.
- **Maintaining Exploration**: This mechanism effectively acts as a regularizer, preventing premature convergence and maintaining higher policy entropy throughout training, which is critical for discovering deeper and more generalizable reasoning behaviors. An informal analysis suggests this regularization also helps reduce the variance of importance weights, leading to more stable training.

In summary, LUFFY's methodology is a sophisticated blend of traditional RL techniques and novel adaptations for LRMs. By carefully balancing the influence of high-quality external demonstrations with the model's own exploratory capacity, it aims to foster genuine learning and overcome the limitations of purely on-policy approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

LUFFY demonstrates significant improvements and addresses key limitations of prior RLVR methods for LRMs. The main findings are:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance on Math Benchmarks (In-Distribution)**: LUFFY, using Qwen2.5-Math-7B, achieved an average gain of **+6.4 points** across six challenging math reasoning benchmarks (AIME 24/25, AMC, MATH-500, Minerva, OlympiadBench) compared to previous RLVR methods. This establishes a new state-of-the-art for RLVR. Notably, it showed an +8.1 point advantage on the newly released AIME 25, suggesting strong generalization to nuanced reasoning. Compared to the authors' own On-Policy RL replication, LUFFY showed a +4.6 point average improvement.

- **Superior Generalization on Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Tasks**: LUFFY demonstrated an advantage of over **+6.2 points** on average over the best baseline (OpenReasoner-Zero) across three out-of-distribution tasks (ARC-c, GPQA*, MMLU-Pro), achieving an average score of 57.8. This indicates that LUFFY effectively leverages off-policy guidance for enhanced generalization, rather than merely overfitting to the training distribution.

- **Effectiveness Against Alternative Off-Policy Learning Methods**: LUFFY significantly outperformed other off-policy baselines such as Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), RL with SFT Loss, and SFT+RL. It showed +10.3 points improvement on OOD tasks compared to these alternatives, and was more efficient, requiring only 59% of the GPU hours and less off-policy data than SFT+RL. The analysis suggests that SFT often leads to rigid and superficial imitation, resulting in excessively long and inefficient generations, especially for incorrect outputs, whereas LUFFY strategically learns.

- **Successful Training of Weak Foundation Models**: A critical finding is LUFFY's ability to successfully train weaker foundation models, specifically LLaMA-3.1-8B, in scenarios where on-policy RLVR completely fails. On a "Hard" training dataset, on-policy RL's training rewards collapsed to zero, indicating an inability to obtain positive feedback. In contrast, LUFFY achieved stable reward improvements, demonstrating its robustness and capacity to overcome limitations imposed by the base model's initial capabilities. This directly addresses the paper's core motivation of moving beyond "amplifying existing behaviors."

- **Strategic Learning and Continuous Exploration (Training Dynamics)**:
 - **Adapting to External Guidance**: Initially, LUFFY's generation length increases, aligning with off-policy traces, indicating imitation. However, it then fosters self-driven exploration, leading to growing advantages in training rewards over On-Policy RL, showing a dynamic balance between imitation and exploration.
 - **Maintaining Exploration**: LUFFY consistently maintains higher generation entropy throughout training compared to On-Policy RL, which rapidly converges to near-zero entropy. This sustained high entropy allows for continuous exploration of less confident yet potentially superior policies, facilitating the discovery of novel cognitive behaviors and escaping local optima.

- **Impact of Policy Shaping**: Ablation studies confirmed that policy shaping and the removal of on-policy clipping both positively contribute to LUFFY's final performance. Policy shaping, in particular, prevents premature convergence and sustains performance gains in later stages of training by re-weighting gradients to emphasize learning from low-probability tokens, effectively acting as a regularizer against entropy collapse.

In summary, the results provide compelling evidence that LUFFY transcends the fundamental limitations of on-policy RLVR, demonstrating the great potential of utilizing off-policy guidance to build more capable, robust, and generalizable reasoning models across varying model capacities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of LUFFY marks a significant advancement in the field of Large Reasoning Models (LRMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), particularly within the Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Transcending On-Policy Limitations**: LUFFY fundamentally addresses the inherent "on-policy" limitation of previous RLVR methods. By enabling models to learn from high-quality off-policy reasoning traces, it allows them to acquire genuinely novel cognitive capacities that extend beyond their initial capabilities, rather than merely amplifying pre-existing behaviors. This is a crucial step towards true "self-improvement" in LRMs, as it allows for the internalization of sophisticated reasoning patterns from stronger "teacher" models.

- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness**: The consistent and substantial improvements on both in-distribution math benchmarks and, more critically, out-of-distribution tasks, highlight LUFFY's ability to foster more robust and generalizable reasoning skills. This suggests that the learned reasoning strategies are not brittle or overfit to specific training examples but represent deeper, more transferable knowledge.

- **Empowering Weaker Models**: The compelling demonstration that LUFFY can successfully train weaker foundation models (e.g., LLaMA-3.1-8B) in scenarios where on-policy RL completely fails is profoundly impactful. This finding suggests that off-policy guidance can democratize access to advanced reasoning capabilities, making it feasible to infuse complex reasoning into models with more limited initial capacities, which might be crucial for efficient deployment or specialized applications.

- **Strategic Learning vs. Rigid Imitation**: The detailed analysis of training dynamics, particularly the comparison with SFT, reveals that LUFFY engages in "strategic off-policy learning." It selectively internalizes valuable reasoning patterns, balancing imitation with exploration, rather than blindly mimicking the surface form of demonstrations. This leads to more efficient and effective reasoning (e.g., shorter, yet correct, generation lengths) and avoids the pitfalls of superficial imitation that can arise from pure supervised fine-tuning.

- **Advancing RL for LLMs**: LUFFY's methodological contributions, specifically Mixed-Policy GRPO and Policy Shaping via Regularized Importance Sampling, offer principled ways to integrate off-policy data into complex RL settings for LLMs. The theoretical convergence analysis provides a strong foundation for the approach. The emphasis on maintaining exploration through policy shaping is a critical design choice that addresses a common problem in RL when combining with imitation learning, preventing models from becoming overly deterministic.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Acceleration of LRM Development**: By providing a more effective and robust training paradigm, LUFFY could accelerate the development of highly capable reasoning models across various domains.
- **Broader Applicability**: While currently focused on math reasoning, the principles of off-policy guidance and dynamic imitation-exploration balance could be extended to other complex reasoning tasks, including scientific discovery, code generation, and strategic planning.
- **More Efficient Knowledge Distillation**: LUFFY offers a more nuanced approach to knowledge distillation from larger, more capable models to smaller ones, going beyond simple behavior cloning to truly imbue reasoning capabilities.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: The work opens avenues for future research into multi-trajectory off-policy guidance, multi-teacher learning, and the application of LUFFY's principles to broader modalities beyond text-based reasoning.

Despite current limitations (focus on math, 7B models due to resources, and one off-policy trajectory per question), LUFFY's compelling results strongly indicate its potential as a scalable and principled path towards building more general, capable, and truly self-improving reasoning models.
