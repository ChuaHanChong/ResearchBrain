# 4D-RGPT: Toward Region-level 4D Understanding via Perceptual Distillation

> [!summary] Summary
> This research from Purdue University and NVIDIA introduces 4D-RGPT, an MLLM designed for region-level 4D understanding of videos, enhanced by a Perceptual 4D Distillation (P4D) framework and Timestamp Positional Encoding (TPE). It improves performance on existing 4D VQA benchmarks by an average of 5.3% and achieves a 4.3% improvement on the newly created R4D-Bench, which features depth-aware dynamic scenes with region-level prompting.

## Problem

- Current Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) exhibit limited fine-grained spatial (3D) and temporal (4D) visual understanding, struggling to extract low-level 4D perceptual knowledge.
- Existing 3D and 4D Video Question Answering (VQA) benchmarks often lack region-level prompting, which is critical for evaluating MLLMs in applications requiring precise queries about specific visual areas.
- Prior methods for enhancing MLLMs with 3D knowledge typically introduce additional inference costs or face challenges in curating large-scale, well-annotated dynamic video data.

## Method

- The paper introduces **4D-RGPT**, a specialized MLLM architecture built on NVILA-Lite-8B, designed to directly capture latent 4D representations from video inputs with enhanced temporal perception.
- A Perceptual 4D Distillation (P4D) framework is proposed, which transfers 4D perceptual knowledge from a frozen expert 4D teacher model into **4D-RGPT** during training only, incurring no additional inference cost through latent and explicit distillation.
- Timestamp Positional Encoding (TPE) is integrated into the visual features of each input frame to provide explicit temporal cues, thereby enhancing the MLLM's temporal understanding for dynamic scenarios.
- R4D-Bench, a new benchmark for depth-aware dynamic scenes with region-level prompting, is curated through a hybrid automated and human-verified pipeline to facilitate comprehensive evaluation of 4D understanding.

## Results

- 4D-RGPT achieved an average performance improvement of **5.3%** over the baseline NVILA-Lite-8B model on six existing non-region-level 3D/4D VQA benchmarks, establishing a new state-of-the-art among open-source MLLMs.
- On the newly introduced R4D-Bench, 4D-RGPT demonstrated an average performance improvement of **4.3%** over baseline MLLMs, with a **4.4%** gain on the dynamic split, highlighting enhanced region-level 4D understanding capabilities.
- Ablation studies confirmed that P4D's dual-branch distillation (latent and explicit) yielded the best performance for 4D knowledge transfer, and TPE consistently improved temporal awareness across benchmarks without introducing additional processing burdens.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Perceptual 4D Distillation (P4D) effectively transfers comprehensive 4D knowledge (both abstract and explicit) from a specialized teacher to an MLLM without adding inference overhead, demonstrating superior efficiency and performance compared to alternative 4D integration strategies.
> - Explicit temporal cues, such as Timestamp Positional Encoding (TPE), are crucial for MLLMs to accurately interpret dynamic events and overcome inherent limitations in temporal perception.
> - Region-level grounding in 4D VQA benchmarks is essential for pushing MLLM capabilities towards practical, precise reasoning about specific objects and areas within complex, dynamic 3D environments over time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in integrating visual inputs with language models for various understanding tasks. However, a documented limitation of current MLLMs, including large proprietary models, is their performance on tasks requiring fine-grained spatial (3D) and temporal (4D) visual understanding. Specifically, their ability to reason over 3D structures and temporal dynamics from video inputs remains constrained by weak 4D perception and temporal understanding.

Existing research has attempted to enhance MLLMs' spatial and temporal reasoning. Some approaches rely on conventional Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) or Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigms, optimizing for text output using curated data. However, these methods often struggle with dynamic scenarios due to the difficulty of curating large-scale, well-annotated dynamic video data. More recently, several works have explored injecting 3D knowledge into MLLMs using external models to improve spatial understanding. Nevertheless, these approaches primarily focus on static videos or 3D understanding, often introducing additional modules into the MLLM architecture, which incurs extra inference costs.

Furthermore, current 3D and 4D Video Question Answering (VQA) benchmarks primarily emphasize static scenes or lack region-level prompting. Region-level understanding, which involves grounding language queries to specific visual regions, is critical for many practical applications, such as autonomous driving and industrial inspection, where precise targeting of user queries to visual areas is necessary. This gap in both MLLM capabilities and evaluation benchmarks highlights a need for models that can interpret complex 4D spatio-temporal information at a regional level without increasing inference burden.

This work positions itself within this landscape by addressing these identified limitations. It aims to enhance MLLMs' 4D perception and temporal understanding capabilities without incurring additional inference costs and to provide a benchmark specifically designed for region-level 4D VQA in dynamic scenes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to advance Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) to achieve effective Region-level 4D Understanding. This involves enabling MLLMs to answer questions that integrate depth information, temporal dynamics, and object interactions in 3D space over time, specifically when these queries are grounded to particular visual regions.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and gaps in current MLLM capabilities and evaluation benchmarks:

- **Limited 4D Perception and Temporal Understanding in MLLMs:** Current MLLMs, despite their advancements in general multimodal reasoning, often exhibit limitations in tasks requiring fine-grained spatial and temporal comprehension. They struggle with extracting low-level 4D perceptual knowledge, such as precise depth values and optical flow, which are crucial for understanding dynamic scenes and object movements in 3D space over time.
- **Need for Region-level Grounding:** For real-world applications like autonomous driving or industrial inspection, general video understanding is insufficient. Users require the ability to pose questions directly about specific visual regions (e.g., "What is the speed of *this* car?") rather than relying on ambiguous descriptions. Existing 3D and 4D VQA benchmarks frequently lack this region-level prompting capability, preventing comprehensive evaluation of MLLMs in such precise contexts.
- **Inefficiency of Prior Approaches:** Previous methods for enhancing MLLMs with 3D knowledge often involve integrating additional modules or external models into the MLLM architecture. While these might improve spatial understanding, they typically introduce additional inference burdens, making them less suitable for real-time or resource-constrained applications. There is a motivation to achieve enhanced 4D understanding without compromising inference efficiency.
- **Scarcity of Appropriate Training Data:** Training MLLMs for complex dynamic scenarios requiring 4D understanding is challenging due to the difficulty of curating large-scale, well-annotated dynamic video data. Existing methods often rely on self-curated data which may not fully capture the nuances of dynamic scenes.
- **Lack of Comprehensive Benchmarks:** Existing 3D/4D VQA benchmarks are often limited, either focusing on static scenes, lacking region-prompted questions, or not providing sufficient dynamic video data to truly challenge MLLMs in 4D understanding. This absence hinders systematic evaluation and progress in the field of region-level 4D VQA.

To address these motivations, the authors introduce three core components:
1. **4D-RGPT:** A specialized MLLM designed to directly capture 4D representations from video inputs with enhanced temporal perception.
2. **Perceptual 4D Distillation (P4D):** A training framework that transfers 4D perceptual knowledge from a frozen expert 4D teacher model into 4D-RGPT. This framework is designed to operate during training only, thereby incurring no additional inference cost.
3. **R4D-Bench:** A new benchmark explicitly designed for depth-aware dynamic scenes with region-level prompting, built through a hybrid automated and human-verified pipeline to facilitate comprehensive evaluation.

These objectives collectively aim to bridge the gap between current MLLM capabilities and the demands of precise, real-world region-level 4D understanding tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces 4D-RGPT, a specialized Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM), enhanced for region-level 4D understanding through a training-only framework called Perceptual 4D Distillation (P4D) and Timestamp Positional Encoding (TPE). A new benchmark, R4D-Bench, is also proposed for evaluation.

#### 3.1. 4D-RGPT Architecture

The 4D-RGPT is built upon a state-of-the-art open-source MLLM backbone, specifically NVILA-Lite-8B, which uses SigLIP as its vision encoder (E_V) and Qwen2 as its Large Language Model (LLM). The architecture is modified with training-only modules for 4D perception:

- **Latent 4D Representations:** To capture abstract 4D features, a lightweight 3-layer MLP called the 4D perception decoder (D_4DP) is introduced. It processes the hidden state features (F_hidden) from the MLLM's LLM component, after sampling and resizing, to decode latent 4D representations (^F_4D).
- **Explicit 4D Representations:** For interpretable, low-level 4D signals, training-only prediction heads (D_m) from the frozen 4D perception model are integrated. These heads take the latent 4D representations (^F_4D) and decode them into explicit 4D signals (^P_m) for various modalities (m ∈ M), including depth, optical flow, motion, and camray.
- **Timestamp Positional Encoding (TPE):** To provide explicit temporal cues, a sinusoidal positional encoding (p^(n)) is added directly to the visual features (E_V(I^(n))) of each input frame (I^(n)) before they are fed into the multimodal projector (E_P). This aims to enhance the MLLM's temporal perception for tasks requiring understanding of "when" or "how long" events occur.

#### 3.2. Perceptual 4D Distillation (P4D) Framework

The P4D framework is designed to distill comprehensive 4D knowledge from a frozen expert 4D perception model (teacher) into the 4D-RGPT (student) during the training phase, without adding any modules or overhead during inference. L4P is selected as the teacher model, utilizing its pre-trained weights and architecture, which consists of a ViT-based video encoder (E_4D) and specialized decoders (D_m) for various 4D modalities.

P4D employs a dual-branch distillation strategy:

- **Latent Distillation (LD):** This branch supervises the MLLM's internal latent 4D representations. A latent distillation loss (L_LD), defined as the Smooth-L1 distance, is used to align the student's latent 4D features (^F_4D) with the teacher's intermediate 4D embeddings (F_4D). This provides intermediate guidance on abstract 4D knowledge.
- **Explicit Distillation (ED):** This branch supervises the MLLM's explicit, interpretable 4D signals. An explicit distillation loss (L_ED), also based on the Smooth-L1 distance, aligns the student's predicted 4D signals (^P_m) for each modality (depth, flow, motion, camray) with the teacher's final 4D signals (P_m). This ensures accurate extraction of low-level 4D properties.

#### 3.3. Training Details

The 4D-RGPT is optimized end-to-end using a combined loss function: the standard cross-entropy Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) loss (L_SFT), the latent distillation loss (L_LD), and the explicit distillation loss (L_ED). The training data mixture includes several 3D/4D conversation datasets: RoboFAC, SAT, VSTI-Bench (training split), and Wolf. The training involves fine-tuning the multimodal projector (E_P) and the LLM, while keeping the vision encoder (E_V) frozen.

#### 3.4. R4D-Bench Benchmark Curation

To address the lack of region-level 4D VQA benchmarks, R4D-Bench is constructed using a hybrid automated and human-verified pipeline based on existing non-region-based 4D VQA benchmarks (STI-Bench and VLM4D).

The curation process involves:
1. **Keyword Extraction:** Qwen2.5-VL is used to identify object keywords from original non-region-prompted questions.
2. **Detect & Segment:** If ground truth segmentation masks are unavailable, GroundingDINO and SAM2 are used to generate 2D bounding boxes and segmentation masks for the identified objects in the first video frame.
3. **Set of Marks (SoM):** Numbered markers are overlaid on the detected objects in the first frame to create an annotated image, serving as an intermediate region-based visual input.
4. **Matching:** Qwen2.5-VL is then used to automatically match the objects referred to in the question to the marked regions, replacing object mentions with region tokens (e.g., "⟨R1⟩").
5. **Verification:** Human annotators manually verify and correct any mismatches, ensuring the quality and accuracy of the region grounding. Videos are trimmed if necessary to ensure regions of interest are visible from the first frame.

R4D-Bench comprises 1,517 region-prompted VQAs across 9 categories covering both static and dynamic aspects of 4D understanding. These categories include Dimension Measurement, 3D Video Grounding, Spatial Relation, Counting objects, Translational movement, Rotational movement, False Positive detection, Speed & Acceleration estimation, and Displacement & Path Length measurement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance of 4D-RGPT and the utility of its proposed components and the R4D-Bench benchmark.

#### 4.1. Performance on Non-region-based 4D VQA

4D-RGPT demonstrated improved performance on several existing non-region-level 3D/4D VQA benchmarks. Across six benchmarks (STI-Bench, VLM4D-real, MMSI-Bench, OmniSpatial, SAT, and VSTI-Bench), 4D-RGPT achieved an average improvement of 5.3% over the baseline NVILA-Lite-8B model. Notable gains were observed on VLM4D-real (+6.2%) and VSTI-Bench (+13.9%). The model's performance was competitive with proprietary MLLMs such as GPT-4o and established a new state-of-the-art among open-source MLLMs of similar model sizes.

#### 4.2. Performance on R4D-Bench

On the newly introduced R4D-Bench benchmark, 4D-RGPT achieved an average performance improvement of 4.3% over baseline MLLMs, with a 4.4% improvement on the dynamic split of the benchmark. This result indicates the model's enhanced capability in region-level 4D understanding, where depth perception and temporal dynamics are critical for answering questions about specific, often moving, regions. Qualitative examples illustrated instances where 4D-RGPT accurately perceived 4D dynamics to provide correct answers, while other MLLMs failed.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Alternative Strategies for 4D VQA:** The Perceptual 4D Distillation (P4D) framework was compared against alternative strategies for incorporating 4D knowledge. P4D outperformed direct Supervised Fine-Tuning (4D-SFT) and methods that directly concatenated 4D features (4D-Concat) or projected them as positional encodings (4D-PE). Crucially, P4D achieved these improvements without introducing additional inference costs, unlike 4D-Concat and 4D-PE, which require computing 4D features during inference.
- **Effectiveness of Perceptual 4D Distillation (P4D):**
 - Latent Distillation (LD) alone provided a 2.3% improvement on R4D-Bench over the Zero-shot baseline.
 - Explicit Distillation (ED) showed incremental improvements as more 4D modalities (depth, flow, motion, camray) were included, with depth and flow being particularly effective. ED alone improved R4D-Bench performance by 1.9%.
 - Combining both Latent and Explicit Distillation (LD+ED), as in the proposed P4D, yielded the best average performance, demonstrating the complementary benefits of both approaches in transferring comprehensive 4D knowledge. Visualizations confirmed that 4D-RGPT successfully learned to extract accurate 4D signals, such as depth maps, progressively during training.
- **Timestamp Positional Encoding (TPE):** MLLMs were found to struggle with temporal perception without explicit time cues, often resulting in performance close to random guessing on a controlled "TimeBench" experiment. Both explicit time marks (P4D+mark) and prompts (P4D+prompt) improved temporal understanding but required additional data preprocessing or introduced distractions. TPE, by encoding timestamps directly into visual features, consistently improved performance across benchmarks without these drawbacks, indicating its effectiveness in enhancing temporal awareness.
- **Architecture Design:** Ablation on trainable components indicated that tuning both the multimodal projector (E_P) and the LLM, while keeping the vision encoder (E_V) frozen, yielded the best performance (Tune-P+LLM). This suggests that E_P requires fine-tuning for TPE and P4D, and the LLM benefits significantly from the distillation process.

In summary, the main findings indicate that 4D-RGPT, equipped with P4D and TPE, effectively addresses the limitations of MLLMs in region-level 4D understanding, achieving performance improvements on both existing and the novel R4D-Bench benchmarks without increasing inference overhead.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes contributions to the field of multimodal AI and computer vision, particularly in advancing the capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) for complex spatio-temporal reasoning.

One significant aspect of this work is its direct focus on **region-level 4D understanding**. Many real-world applications, such as autonomous driving, robotics, and industrial inspection, require not just a general understanding of a scene but precise reasoning about specific objects or areas within dynamic environments. By enabling MLLMs to process region-level prompts in a 4D context, this research moves toward addressing a critical requirement for deploying these models in demanding applications where exact spatial and temporal information is paramount.

The introduction of **Perceptual 4D Distillation (P4D)** represents a novel approach to injecting complex 4D perceptual knowledge into MLLMs. A key advantage of P4D is its design as a training-only framework, which means it **incurs no additional inference cost**. This efficiency is a notable improvement over prior methods that often rely on external modules, which add computational burden during inference. This aspect is significant for practical deployment, especially in latency-sensitive applications. The dual-branch distillation (latent and explicit) ensures a comprehensive transfer of both abstract and interpretable 4D signals, which can lead to more robust and accurate models.

The development of **Timestamp Positional Encoding (TPE)** is also impactful. By providing explicit temporal cues without modifying input data or introducing extra modules, TPE addresses a documented weakness of MLLMs in temporal perception. This simple yet effective mechanism helps MLLMs accurately track events and infer durations, which is fundamental for any 4D reasoning task involving movement, speed, or acceleration.

Finally, the creation of **R4D-Bench** is a direct contribution to the research community. This new benchmark fills a gap in existing evaluation datasets by providing depth-aware dynamic scenes with region-level prompting. A robust and challenging benchmark is essential for driving future research and development in region-level 4D VQA. It allows for a more comprehensive and realistic evaluation of MLLMs' capabilities, fostering progress toward models that can truly understand complex, dynamic visual information in a granular way.

In summary, this work advances MLLMs' ability to reason about depth, temporal dynamics, and object interactions at a fine-grained, region-specific level. Its methodology for efficient knowledge transfer and its comprehensive evaluation benchmark contribute to overcoming current limitations, potentially accelerating the development of more capable and deployable multimodal AI systems for a wide range of real-world applications.
