# LUMOS: Language-Conditioned Imitation Learning with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LUMOS introduces a framework that integrates world models, imitation learning, and language conditioning to enable robots to execute complex, long-horizon tasks from natural language instructions. This approach effectively addresses distribution shift, yielding higher task success rates on benchmarks like CALVIN and demonstrating zero-shot transfer to real robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Enabling robots to understand and execute complex, long-horizon tasks specified by natural language commands.
- Traditional imitation learning methods struggle with distribution shift, where compounding errors lead to poor performance on longer tasks.

## Method

- A two-stage framework that first trains a world model on unlabeled play data to learn environment dynamics.
- An actor-critic agent is then trained within this learned world model, guided by an intrinsic reward function that matches expert demonstrations in the latent space.
- Incorporates a latent planning network and language alignment to enhance long-horizon performance and task comprehension.

## Results

- LUMOS achieved higher success rates on multi-stage, long-horizon tasks on the CALVIN benchmark compared to prior language-conditioned imitation learning approaches.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the intrinsic reward function and the latent planning network in improving performance and mitigating distribution shift.
- Learned policies successfully transferred zero-shot to real robots, executing complex, multi-stage manipulation tasks guided by natural language instructions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning policies entirely within a high-fidelity world model, combined with an intrinsic reward in latent space, effectively mitigates distribution shift for long-horizon imitation.
> - The latent planning network is crucial for enabling sequential decision-making over extended task horizons.
> - High-quality world models and robust latent state representations allow for effective zero-shot policy transfer from simulation to real robots.
