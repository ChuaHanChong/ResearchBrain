# LUMOS: Language-Conditioned Imitation Learning with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LUMOS introduces a framework that integrates world models, imitation learning, and language conditioning to enable robots to execute complex, long-horizon tasks from natural language instructions. This approach effectively addresses distribution shift, yielding higher task success rates on benchmarks like CALVIN and demonstrating zero-shot transfer to real robotic platforms.

## Problem

- Enabling robots to understand and execute complex, long-horizon tasks specified by natural language commands.
- Traditional imitation learning methods struggle with distribution shift, where compounding errors lead to poor performance on longer tasks.
- Learning policies via on-policy correction through direct real-world interaction is costly and impractical to scale across long-horizon manipulation tasks.

## Method

- A two-stage framework that first trains a world model on unlabeled play data to learn environment dynamics.
- An actor-critic agent is then trained within this learned world model, guided by an intrinsic reward function that matches expert demonstrations in the latent space.
- Incorporates a latent planning network and language alignment to enhance long-horizon performance and task comprehension.

## Results

- LUMOS achieved higher success rates on multi-stage, long-horizon tasks on the CALVIN benchmark compared to prior language-conditioned imitation learning approaches.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of the intrinsic reward function and the latent planning network in improving performance and mitigating distribution shift.
- Learned policies successfully transferred zero-shot to real robots, executing complex, multi-stage manipulation tasks guided by natural language instructions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Learning policies entirely within a high-fidelity world model, combined with an intrinsic reward in latent space, effectively mitigates distribution shift for long-horizon imitation.
> - The latent planning network is crucial for enabling sequential decision-making over extended task horizons.
> - High-quality world models and robust latent state representations allow for effective zero-shot policy transfer from simulation to real robots.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Enabling robots to understand and execute complex tasks specified by natural language commands represents a significant challenge in robotic learning. The paper "LUMOS: Language-Conditioned Imitation Learning with World Models" presents a framework that addresses this challenge by combining world models, imitation learning, and language conditioning.

#### 1.2. Research Context

LUMOS builds upon several research areas:

1. **Imitation Learning**: Traditional methods like behavior cloning struggle with distribution shift, where the agent encounters states during execution that differ from those in the training data, leading to a cascade of errors.

2. **World Models**: These are learned representations of environment dynamics that allow agents to "imagine" trajectories without interacting with the real world.

3. **Language-Conditioned Robotics**: Prior works have attempted to create robots that respond to natural language instructions, but many struggle with longer-horizon tasks that require sequential decision-making.

LUMOS differentiates itself by integrating these approaches into a cohesive framework that addresses their individual limitations. The use of a world model allows for on-policy learning in a simulated environment, reducing the need for extensive real-world data collection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

LUMOS learns to perform complex, long-horizon tasks specified by language instructions without requiring extensive real-world interactions. The framework addresses key limitations of traditional imitation learning methods, particularly the problem of distribution shift where small errors compound over time, leading to poor performance on longer tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Methodology

LUMOS employs a two-stage training process:

1. **World Model Learning**: A world model is trained on unlabeled play data, learning to predict future states based on actions and current states.

2. **Behavior Learning**: An actor-critic agent is trained within the learned world model using an intrinsic reward function that encourages the agent to match expert demonstrations in the latent space.

The framework uses a DreamerV2-style architecture for the world model, consisting of:
- An image encoder that maps observations to latent states
- A recurrent state-space model (RSSM) that learns transition dynamics
- A decoder that reconstructs observations from latent states

#### 3.2. World Model Learning

The world model is the foundation of LUMOS. It learns to represent the environment's dynamics and enables the agent to simulate trajectories without interacting with the real world. The model is trained on unlabeled play data, which consists of sequences of observations and actions collected from the environment.

The world model integrates several components:
- **Image encoder**: Transforms raw pixel observations into a compact latent representation
- **RSSM**: Consists of deterministic (h) and stochastic (z) components that together model the environment dynamics
- **Decoder**: Reconstructs observations from the latent representation

The training objective minimizes the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence between the predicted and actual latent states, encouraging the model to accurately predict future states based on current states and actions.

#### 3.3. Behavior Learning

Once the world model is trained, LUMOS uses it to learn a policy through an actor-critic framework. The key innovation in this stage is the introduction of:

1. **Latent Planning Network**: This network distills information about future trajectories into a "latent plan," which helps the agent achieve improved long-horizon performance. It consists of two components:
 - Plan proposal network: Encodes the current state and goal into a plan
 - Plan recognition network: Reconstructs a plan from an entire sequence

2. **Language Alignment**: Language instructions are aligned with the world model's latent features using a contrastive loss. This enables the agent to understand and execute tasks specified by natural language commands.

3. **Intrinsic Reward Function**: Defined in the latent space of the world model, this reward encourages the agent to match expert demonstrations:

```
r(s_t, a_t) = exp(-||z_t - z*_t||^2/σ) · exp(-||a_t - a*_t||^2/σ)
```

Where:
- z_t and a_t are the agent's latent state and action
- z*_t and a*_t are the expert's latent state and action
- σ is a temperature parameter

This reward function effectively reduces covariate shift by encouraging the agent to stay close to the expert's trajectory in latent space.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

LUMOS was evaluated on the CALVIN benchmark, which features long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks specified by language instructions. The results demonstrate that:

1. **LUMOS outperforms existing methods**: It achieves a higher success rate than previous language-conditioned robotic imitation learning approaches on multi-stage, long-horizon tasks.

2. **The intrinsic reward function is effective**: Ablation studies show that performance significantly degrades when the intrinsic reward is replaced with behavior cloning, indicating its crucial role in mitigating distribution shift.

3. **The latent planning network improves performance**: Removing this component reduces the agent's ability to complete longer-horizon tasks, demonstrating its importance for sequential decision-making.

4. **Language alignment enhances task performance**: The alignment between language instructions and latent state features contributes to the agent's ability to understand and execute language commands.

#### 4.2. Real-World Transfer

One of the most significant achievements of LUMOS is its ability to transfer learned policies from the world model to real robots without additional training or fine-tuning.

The success of this zero-shot transfer can be attributed to:

1. **High-quality world model**: The world model accurately captures the dynamics of the environment, allowing policies trained within it to transfer effectively to the real world.

2. **Latent state representations**: By operating in the latent space rather than directly on raw observations, LUMOS is more robust to the visual differences between the world model and reality.

The real-world experiments showcase LUMOS executing complex tasks such as picking up objects and placing them in designated locations, all guided by natural language instructions. Figure 4 demonstrates successful completion of tasks like manipulating a carrot.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Limitations and Future Work

Despite its achievements, LUMOS has several limitations:

1. **World Model Quality**: The performance of LUMOS depends significantly on the quality of the world model. If the model fails to capture important aspects of the environment dynamics, the learned policies may not transfer effectively to the real world.

2. **Computational Resources**: Training world models at higher image resolutions improves performance but requires substantial computational resources, potentially limiting accessibility.

3. **Data Requirements**: While LUMOS learns from offline data, the world model benefits from diverse and comprehensive datasets. Collecting such data can be challenging in some robotic domains.

Future research directions include:

1. **Improved World Model Architectures**: Developing more efficient and accurate world models that can be trained with less data and computational resources.

2. **Multi-task Learning**: Extending LUMOS to learn multiple tasks simultaneously and leveraging the shared structure between related tasks.

3. **Interactive Learning**: Incorporating human feedback to refine policies and correct errors during execution.

#### 5.2. Conclusion

LUMOS represents a significant advancement in language-conditioned robotic learning by combining world models, imitation learning, and latent planning. The framework addresses the limitations of traditional imitation learning methods, particularly the problem of distribution shift in long-horizon tasks.

By learning entirely in a world model and using an intrinsic reward function defined in latent space, LUMOS achieves robust performance on complex tasks specified by natural language instructions. Most importantly, the learned policies transfer successfully to real robots without additional training or fine-tuning.

LUMOS demonstrates that world models can serve as effective bridges between offline learning and real-world deployment, potentially accelerating the development of more capable and versatile robotic systems that can understand and execute natural language commands.
