# Belief-Conditioned One-Step Diffusion: Real-Time Trajectory Planning with Just-Enough Sensing

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from multiple institutions introduce Belief-Conditioned One-Step Diffusion (B-COD), a real-time system that simultaneously plans trajectories and manages sensor activation to minimize energy while maintaining localization accuracy. It achieves a 97.9% task completion rate with 42.3% of the sensing energy compared to an always-on baseline, operating with a consistent 10 ms planning cycle.

## Problem

- Autonomous robots face a trade-off between energy consumption from diverse sensor suites and maintaining sufficient localization accuracy for task success, with existing methods being energy-intensive or brittle.
- Classical belief-space planners are computationally expensive, struggle with high-dimensional belief spaces, and often rely on ad-hoc heuristics for sensor switching that overlook long-term value.
- Current data-driven diffusion models for planning implicitly assume continuous, accurate state estimation and constant sensor activity, lacking mechanisms for uncertainty management or energy-efficient sensing.

## Method

- Developed **Belief-Conditioned One-Step Diffusion (B-COD)**, a unified pipeline that integrates a ==belief raster representation==, a ==diffusion-based trajectory planner==, and a reinforcement learning-based sensor scheduler.
- Utilized a ==consistency distillation== approach to compress a multi-step diffusion teacher into a single-step student network, enabling real-time generation of a short-horizon trajectory and a calibrated ==CVaR-95== localization risk proxy.
- Employed a ==Constrained Soft Actor-Critic (SAC)== algorithm for online sensor scheduling, minimizing instantaneous power draw while ensuring the predicted localization error (CVaR-95) stays within a defined budget.

## Results

- B-COD achieved a **97.9%** goal-reach rate in marine trials, consuming **42.3%** of the sensing energy compared to an Always-ON baseline, and significantly outperforming heuristic and pure RL methods in energy efficiency and success.
- The system demonstrated real-time performance with a consistent **10.3 ± 0.6 ms** planning latency, which remains flat across varying workspace radii (25m-100m), out-scaling analytical belief planners by orders of magnitude.
- B-COD's predicted uncertainty (CVaR-95) is numerically calibrated (**6%** mean error) and context-aware, enabling the system to adaptively toggle sensors and autonomously recover from sensor faults without pre-programmed heuristics.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A diffusion planner, when conditioned on belief and sensor masks, can inherently provide a calibrated proxy for expected localization error from the spread of its denoising trajectories.
> - Compressing a multi-step diffusion model via consistency distillation can achieve real-time performance on embedded hardware while preserving the calibration of uncertainty estimates.
> - A fixed-shape, ego-centric belief raster representation allows neural networks to effectively process and condition on dynamic, high-dimensional pose belief states, making the system translation-equivariant and orientation-aware.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Autonomous robots often operate with diverse sensor suites, such as cameras, LiDARs, and GPS, to ensure reliable localization across various environments. However, continuously powering all sensors is energy-intensive and often impractical. Conversely, indiscriminately deactivating sensors can lead to rapid accumulation of localization uncertainty and subsequent task failure if the robot enters a poorly observed region. This trade-off between energy consumption, localization accuracy, and task success defines a significant challenge in robotics.

Classical belief-space planners address the motion planning aspect by propagating pose-belief covariance through analytical models and employing heuristic sensor switching strategies. While effective in idealized conditions with full sensor activation and accurate models, these methods typically exhibit brittleness in more complex scenarios, are computationally expensive at runtime, and struggle to manage high-dimensional belief spaces, often relying on ad-hoc heuristics that overlook the long-term value of information. Approaches based on reinforcement learning for sensor scheduling aim to learn policies for sensor activation, but they often demand extensive training data, sensitive reward shaping, and necessitate external safety mechanisms to maintain bounded pose error. Furthermore, these methods frequently decouple trajectory generation from sensor selection, leading to brittle systems that require re-engineering when environmental conditions change.

Data-driven methods, particularly diffusion models, have emerged as powerful tools for learning multi-modal trajectory distributions directly from demonstrations. Their advantages include avoiding reward-shaping complexities, capturing diverse motion behaviors, and straightforward conditioning on semantic maps. However, a fundamental limitation of these diffusion-based planners has been their implicit assumption of an accurate and continuously available state estimate, with all sensors perpetually active during their training and denoising processes. This work addresses the gap between the capabilities of diffusion-based planning and the practical demands of energy-efficient, uncertainty-aware robot operation by integrating real-time sensor selection directly into the diffusion planning framework. It seeks to leverage the inherent distributional output of diffusion models as a proxy for localization uncertainty, a largely overlooked aspect in prior research.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable autonomous robots to perform tasks in mapped environments with "just-enough sensing." This entails simultaneously generating short-horizon motion plans and determining the minimal subset of sensors required at each location to maintain localization uncertainty sufficiently low for task completion, all in real time.

The motivation for this objective stems from several practical and theoretical challenges in autonomous robotics:

- **Energy Efficiency:** Operating heterogeneous sensor suites continuously is costly in terms of energy consumption, which can severely limit mission duration for battery-powered autonomous systems. The goal is to reduce sensing energy expenditure without compromising performance.
- **Robustness to Uncertainty:** Robots must maintain adequate localization accuracy to successfully complete tasks and avoid hazards. Existing methods often rely on simplified uncertainty models or computationally intensive covariance rollouts, which can become inaccurate or infeasible in dynamic, partially observable environments. The paper aims to develop a robust mechanism for uncertainty management that directly informs sensor decisions.
- **Real-time Operation:** Many robot applications, especially in dynamic environments, require millisecond-level planning and decision-making cycles. Traditional belief-space planners often incur high computational costs, making them unsuitable for real-time deployment on embedded hardware. A key motivation is to achieve real-time performance that scales effectively with the environment's complexity.
- **Integration of Planning and Perception:** Prior approaches frequently separate motion planning from active perception or sensor scheduling. This decoupled architecture can lead to suboptimal decisions and reduced adaptability. The authors are motivated to create a unified framework where trajectory generation inherently informs sensor selection, ensuring coherent and context-aware operation.
- **Leveraging Diffusion Models:** Diffusion models naturally generate a distribution of trajectories rather than a single path. The intuition that the spread of this distribution might correlate with localization uncertainty motivates the repurposing of diffusion models to serve as both a planner and a sensing oracle, thereby eliminating the need for external, brittle covariance estimators.
- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Techniques:** The work is motivated to overcome the brittleness and runtime expenses of classical belief-space planners, the high data requirements and reward-shaping challenges of on-policy reinforcement learning, and the assumption of always-on, accurate state estimation in current diffusion-based planning approaches.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to deliver a planning system that is energy-efficient, robust to localization uncertainty, computationally efficient for real-time deployment, and provides a more integrated approach to robot autonomy.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed solution, Belief-Conditioned One-Step Diffusion (B-COD), integrates a belief representation, a diffusion-based trajectory planner, and a reinforcement learning-based sensor scheduler into a unified real-time pipeline. The core insight is that a diffusion planner, when explicitly conditioned on a pose-belief raster and a sensor mask, can inherently yield a calibrated proxy for expected localization error from the spread of its denoising trajectories.

The problem is formalized as minimizing total sensing energy while ensuring that the probability of the localization risk (measured by the square root of the trace of the pose covariance, $\sqrt{tr \Sigma_t}$) exceeding a user-specified budget ($\eta$) remains below a certain threshold ($\epsilon$), avoiding obstacles, and reaching the goal.

The methodology hinges on three key components:

1. **Belief Raster Representation:**
 - To allow neural networks to process dynamic and high-dimensional pose belief states, the robot's pose posterior is projected into a fixed-shape, five-channel image $\text{B}_t \in^{\text{H} \times \text{W} \times 5}$. This belief raster is ego-centric, its footprint adapting to the belief's spread, but then resampled to a preset resolution.
 - Each grid cell in the raster encodes five summary statistics: (i) belief mass, (ii-iii) sine and cosine of yaw (to represent orientation without discontinuities), (iv) planar spread (log-determinant of local positional covariance), and (v) circular variance (concentration of heading distribution). This representation is translation-equivariant, orientation-aware, and compact.

2. **Belief-Conditioned One-Step Diffusion (B-COD) Planner:**
 - B-COD learns the conditional distribution $p_\theta(\tau | \text{B}, \text{M}, \text{g}, \text{a})$, where $\tau$ is a short sequence of body-frame incremental poses, $\text{B}$ is the belief raster, $\text{M}$ is a co-cropped semantic map slice, $\text{g}$ is a binary goal mask, and $\text{a}$ is the vector of active sensors.
 - **Conditioning:** The belief, map, and goal are concatenated into a tensor and processed by a spatial encoder (ResNet). An embedding of the sensor mask $\text{a}$ is fused with these spatial features to create a comprehensive context vector $\text{C}$, ensuring the planner generates sensor-aware trajectories.
 - **Diffusion Teacher:** A multi-step denoising UNet is trained using a cosine forward process. The teacher learns to predict both the injected noise and waypoint-wise log-variances ($\hat{\sigma}_k$) for a short-horizon trajectory. The training loss combines a DDPM noise-reconstruction term with a diagonal Gaussian negative log-likelihood, ensuring that the predicted variances are calibrated to the empirical scatter of demonstrations.
 - **Consistency-Model Student:** To achieve real-time performance on embedded hardware, the multi-step teacher is compressed into a single-step student network via consistency distillation. The student is optimized to match both the mean path and the uncertainty (calibrated variances) of the teacher's reference samples. A ramped KL-divergence term transfers the calibrated variances, preserving their validity after compression.
 - **Uncertainty Proxy:** The waypoint log-variances from the student are condensed into a single scalar risk metric: the CVaR-95 ($u_{\text{CVaR}}$). This statistic, representing the 95th-percentile localization error across the trajectory, serves as a calibrated, differentiable proxy for expected localization error and is generated in a single forward pass alongside the trajectory.

3. **Online Sensor Scheduling with a Risk Budget:**
 - The sensor scheduling problem is formulated as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP). The state comprises the belief raster, the $u_{\text{CVaR}}$ scalar, the distance to the goal, and the previously active sensor mask.
 - The primary cost is the instantaneous power draw from activated sensors. A constraint is imposed, penalizing the system if the predicted $u_{\text{CVaR}}$ exceeds a maximum allowed threshold ($\eta_{\text{max}}$).
 - A Constrained Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm is employed to learn the scheduling policy. SAC, being off-policy and data-efficient, optimizes for energy consumption while adhering to the localization risk budget through a dual-gradient update mechanism for a Lagrange multiplier.

**Implementation Recipe:**
- Data generation uses a Unity-based simulator with 90 procedurally varied harbor scenes and an A\*+MPC oracle, generating 8.3 million belief-annotated short-horizon snippets under varied sensor subsets and noise.
- The diffusion teacher and student models are trained once on this corpus. The student model is exported via ONNX and TensorRT (FP16) for efficient inference on Jetson-class hardware, achieving approximate 10ms forward pass latency.
- The SAC policy is trained entirely in simulation. A shared CNN processes the belief image, and MLPs form the policy head.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of B-COD was evaluated through real-time marine trials on a SeaRobotics Surveyor Autonomous Surface Vehicle (ASV) in an open-air lake, with the objective of reaching waypoint goals while keeping the CVaR-95 localization error below a 2-meter budget and minimizing sensing energy. The system was benchmarked against various baselines.

**Key Finding #1: B-COD+SAC delivers near-perfect task completion at less than half the sensing cost of the Always-ON baseline.**
- As detailed in Table 1, B-COD achieved a goal-reach rate of 97.9%, closely matching the 100% of the Always-ON baseline, while consuming only 42.3% of its sensing energy. Collisions with B-COD were 0.9%, comparable to the 0.5% of the Always-ON baseline.
- In contrast, heuristic methods like Greedy-OFF conserved more energy (61.2%) but significantly sacrificed success (47.3% goal-reach) and increased collisions (22.3%). InfoGain-Greedy achieved a higher success rate (89.9%) but violated the risk budget eight times more often than B-COD. Random sensor masks performed considerably worse across all metrics.
- The Pure-RL baseline, which attempted to learn both motion and sensor toggles end-to-end, struggled with exploration in its high-dimensional action space, resulting in only 54.8% goal-reach and a 22.1% collision rate.
- DESPOT-Lite, an analytic POMDP planner, achieved respectable goal-reach (87.9%) and low CVaR violations (1.9%) but suffered from an average runtime of 565.3 ms, rendering it impractical for real-time operation on the ASV.

**Key Finding #2: B-COD’s variance is a calibrated, context-aware predictor of localization error.**
- The reliability curve (Figure 4) demonstrated numerical calibration, with a 6% mean error between the predicted 1-sigma uncertainty and the empirical planar error.
- Qualitative analysis of a representative lap (Figure 3) illustrates this context-awareness. In open water with only the IMU active (low energy), B-COD accurately predicted a wider uncertainty bound (e.g., 1.85m, tracking a ground truth 2.0m drift), deeming it acceptable due to the absence of immediate hazards. Conversely, when the ASV entered an obstacle-prone corridor at night, B-COD activated high-energy sensors like LiDAR and night camera, predicting a tighter bound (e.g., 0.45m, tracking a ground truth 0.46m drift) commensurate with the demand for precise localization. This demonstrated that $u_{\text{CVaR}}$ provides spatially discriminative and numerically reliable information for energy-certainty trade-offs.

**Key Finding #3: B-COD stays within a 10 ± 1 ms envelope and out-scales analytic belief planners.**
- Table 2 illustrates B-COD's computational efficiency. Its latency remained flat at 10.3 ± 0.6 ms across varying workspace radii (25m to 100m). This constant-time performance is attributed to its fixed-size belief raster processing and UNet architecture.
- In contrast, the InfoGain-Greedy baseline's runtime grew quadratically ($\Theta(R^2)$) with the workspace radius, reaching 23 ms at 100m. DESPOT-Lite's runtime ballooned to over 9000 ms for a 100m radius, making it several orders of magnitude too slow for real-time control. This highlights B-COD's practical advantage in large-scale environments.

**Key Finding #4: B-COD adapts online, re-allocating modalities to recover from faults.**
- During an experimental forced LiDAR outage in a critical corridor (Figure 5), B-COD's risk proxy immediately spiked from 0.6m to 1.8m. The SAC scheduler responded in the next cycle by activating both cameras and the EXO2 sonde (high energy), successfully reducing the risk back to 0.8m. Once the ASV cleared the obstacle, the risk proxy naturally dropped, and the scheduler reverted to an energy-saving IMU-only configuration. This demonstrated the system's ability to drive context-specific recovery sequences without reliance on pre-programmed heuristics.

**Ablation Studies:**
- Removing the belief raster from the planner (No-Belief Raster) significantly degraded goal-reach (68%), tripled collisions, and led the SAC to keep more sensors active, underscoring the importance of the belief representation.
- Replacing the CVaR-95 proxy with a naive mean of waypoint standard deviations ($\sigma$-Mean) resulted in a blurred risk signal, causing the policy to over-compensate (3 sensors active, 60% Always-ON energy) while still breaching the risk budget 9% of the time.
- Estimating risk from 20 Monte-Carlo samples (Sample-Spread) restored calibration but increased runtime to 84.1 ms, demonstrating the efficiency of the single-pass CVaR proxy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Belief-Conditioned One-Step Diffusion (B-COD) system represents a notable advancement in autonomous robot navigation, particularly for resource-constrained applications. Its primary significance lies in being the first planner to concurrently generate a short-horizon trajectory and derive a calibrated proxy for localization uncertainty from its own denoising process, all within a single, real-time forward pass.

**Key Contributions and Impact:**

- **Integrated Planning and Active Perception:** B-COD bridges a critical gap by unifying trajectory planning with active perception, enabling robots to reason about motion and sensing decisions coherently. This overcomes the limitations of decoupled systems that often lead to brittle performance and suboptimal resource allocation.
- **Energy Efficiency and Task Performance:** The system demonstrates substantial energy savings, reducing sensing energy consumption by over 50% compared to an always-on baseline, while maintaining a near-perfect goal-reach rate and low collision incidence. This has direct implications for extending mission endurance and operational range for autonomous platforms, especially in domains like marine, aerial, or long-term planetary exploration where power is a critical constraint.
- **Real-Time Capability and Scalability:** B-COD's ability to replan in approximately 10 ms, with a constant-time computational cost irrespective of the workspace size, makes it suitable for real-world, high-frequency control loops on embedded hardware. This addresses a major bottleneck faced by many traditional belief-space planners, whose computational requirements often scale poorly with environmental complexity.
- **Robust Uncertainty Management:** The novel use of a CVaR-95 proxy derived directly from the diffusion model's denoising spread provides a numerically calibrated and context-aware measure of localization risk. This allows the system to adaptively toggle sensors based on environmental features, anticipated uncertainty growth, and task criticality, leading to more resilient and intelligent decision-making, including autonomous fault recovery.
- **Reduced Reliance on Handcrafted Heuristics:** By leveraging learned representations and a principled CMDP formulation, B-COD minimizes the need for hand-engineered rules for sensor scheduling, leading to potentially more generalizable and robust behavior across varied conditions.
- **Research Catalyst:** The public release of code, trained models, and a novel, belief-annotated maritime dataset is a significant contribution that can accelerate further research in uncertainty-aware planning, active perception, and energy-efficient robotics within high-uncertainty domains.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

The authors acknowledge several limitations that guide future research:
- **Domain-Specific Retraining:** The current system requires retraining if new sensor modalities are added or swapped due to the learned sensor embedding. Future work could explore generic, shared sensor descriptors to enable plug-and-play adaptability.
- **Static Semantic Map Dependence:** B-COD relies on a pre-loaded static semantic map. This makes it vulnerable to unmapped environmental changes. Integrating online semantic SLAM or incremental map updating would enhance robustness to dynamic environments.
- **Binary Sensor Model:** The scheduler operates with discrete on/off sensor states. Extending the action space to incorporate graded sensor settings (e.g., variable scan rates, resolutions, duty cycles) could unlock finer energy-performance trade-offs.
- **Training Distribution Dependency:** The calibration of the uncertainty proxy is tied to the training data distribution. Performance might degrade in truly out-of-distribution scenarios. Addressing this could involve confidence-aware data augmentation, out-of-distribution detectors, or Bayesian recalibration techniques.

Despite these limitations, B-COD represents a substantial step toward autonomous robots that can intelligently manage their sensing resources, balancing mission objectives, energy constraints, and localization uncertainty in real-time, thereby fostering the development of more adaptive and sustainable robotic systems.
