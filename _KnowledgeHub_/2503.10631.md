# HybridVLA: Collaborative Diffusion and Autoregression in a Unified Vision-Language-Action Model

> [!summary] Summary
> HybridVLA introduces a unified Vision-Language-Action model that integrates both diffusion-based continuous action prediction and autoregressive reasoning within a shared Large Language Model backbone. This approach achieves state-of-the-art performance in robotic manipulation, demonstrating up to 19% higher success rates over prior methods in real-world tasks and strong generalization to unseen conditions.

## Problem

- Autoregressive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while leveraging VLM reasoning, quantize continuous actions, leading to a loss of precision in robotic manipulation.
- Existing diffusion-based VLA models often relegate the VLM to a feature extractor, thus failing to fully integrate the VLM's powerful token-level reasoning for continuous action prediction.
- A fundamental challenge exists in synergistically combining precise continuous control with robust semantic understanding derived from large language models within a unified framework.

## Method

- **HybridVLA** unifies diffusion and autoregressive action generation within a single Large Language Model (LLM) backbone, building on a pretrained VLM.
- A "==collaborative training recipe==" is used, involving a novel token sequence design (e.g., encapsulating ==diffusion tokens==) and joint optimization of diffusion and autoregressive losses to enable mutual reinforcement.
- During inference, a "==collaborative action ensemble==" adaptively fuses continuous diffusion-based actions with discrete autoregressive actions, based on autoregressive confidence, to balance precision and reasoning.

## Results

- Achieved a **74%** mean success rate across 10 RLBench simulation tasks, outperforming previous state-of-the-art autoregressive models (OpenVLA by **33%**) and diffusion models (CogACT by **14%**).
- Demonstrated robust performance in real-world robotic tasks, achieving **83%** mean success for single-arm and **71%** for dual-arm tasks, representing a **19%** improvement over prior VLA methods.
- Exhibited strong generalization capabilities, maintaining high performance across unseen manipulated objects, backgrounds, spatial positions, and lighting conditions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deeply integrating diffusion-based continuous action generation within the LLM's next-token prediction process enables full utilization of the VLM's pretrained reasoning for precise control.
> - A collaborative training recipe with hybrid objectives allows a single LLM backbone to simultaneously learn continuous action characteristics and semantic reasoning without negative interference.
> - Adaptive ensembling of continuous diffusion and discrete autoregressive actions, based on model confidence, leverages the complementary strengths of both paradigms for improved overall performance and robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the forefront of the rapidly evolving field of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** for robotic manipulation, aiming to build "foundation models" for generalist robots. The broader landscape of robotic policy design is moving beyond task-specific solutions towards intelligent agents capable of comprehending human instructions, reasoning about complex scenes, and executing generalized actions in dynamic, unstructured environments.

Historically, robotic manipulation policies have relied on traditional control methods or state-based reinforcement learning. More recently, the integration of visual observations for imitation learning gained prominence. The advent of **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, pre-trained on internet-scale image-text data, has brought significant breakthroughs in instruction following and common-sense reasoning. This success has naturally led to their extension into VLA models for predicting low-level robotic actions (e.g., end-effector poses).

Within the VLA paradigm, two primary approaches have emerged, each with distinct advantages and limitations:

1. **Autoregressive VLA Methods (e.g., RT2, ManipLLM, OpenVLA):** These methods emulate the reasoning of VLMs by quantizing continuous actions into discrete bins and predicting the next action token autoregressively. They excel at leveraging the vast common-sense reasoning acquired during VLM pretraining, enabling generalized manipulation skills. However, the inherent **quantization of actions disrupts continuity**, leading to a loss of precision crucial for intricate manipulation tasks.

2. **Diffusion-based VLA Methods (e.g., Pi0, CogACT, DiVLA):** Inspired by the success of diffusion models in generative AI, these approaches incorporate a diffusion head to predict continuous actions. They offer superior precision and can capture probabilistic action representations. A key limitation, however, is that this diffusion head often operates *independently* of the VLM, treating the VLM primarily as a feature extractor. Consequently, these methods **fail to fully leverage the VLM's pretrained reasoning capabilities through token-level generation**, as the diffusion process is not deeply integrated into the VLM's core next-token prediction mechanism.

HybridVLA directly addresses this architectural and functional disconnect. It proposes a novel unified framework that **elegantly integrates both diffusion-based continuous action prediction and autoregressive reasoning within a *single, shared Large Language Model (LLM) backbone***. This distinguishes it from prior diffusion-based VLA methods that append a separate diffusion head. By fostering a "collaborative" relationship between these two generation paradigms, HybridVLA aims to achieve the best of both worlds: the precision of continuous actions and the robust semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities derived from internet-scale pretraining. This approach represents a significant step towards creating more robust, generalized, and high-performing foundation models for robotic control. The model's large-scale pretraining on diverse robotic datasets (Open X-Embodiment, DROID, ROBOMIND) further aligns with the trend of developing generalist robot policies capable of zero-shot or few-shot generalization across tasks and embodiments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of HybridVLA is to develop a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that synergistically combines the strengths of both diffusion-based and autoregressive action generation paradigms. The authors are motivated by the inherent limitations of existing VLA approaches:

- **Bridging the Modality Gap for Precise Control:** Current autoregressive VLA models, despite their strong common-sense reasoning inherited from Vision-Language Models (VLMs), suffer from action quantization. This discretization of continuous actions (like end-effector poses) leads to a loss of precision, hindering the robot's ability to perform delicate and exact manipulations. The motivation is to overcome this by incorporating the continuous nature of diffusion-based actions.
- **Unlocking Full VLM Reasoning for Action Prediction:** While diffusion-based VLA models offer continuous action prediction, they typically relegate the VLM to a feature extraction role, attaching a separate diffusion head. This design prevents them from fully leveraging the VLM's powerful pretrained reasoning capabilities, which are primarily expressed through token-level generation. The authors are motivated to integrate diffusion denoising *into* the VLM's next-token prediction process, ensuring that the rich contextual understanding of the VLM directly informs and enhances the continuous action generation.
- **Achieving Mutual Reinforcement:** The core motivation is to move beyond simply concatenating or using separate modules for different action types. Instead, the authors seek a design where the continuous representation learned by diffusion modeling and the semantic understanding from autoregressive generation can "mutually reinforce" each other within a shared LLM backbone. This collaborative learning is expected to lead to more robust and versatile action predictions.
- **Enhancing Generalization and Robustness:** A fundamental goal of robotic manipulation is to enable robots to operate effectively in dynamic, unstructured real-world environments and generalize to unseen configurations. By combining the strengths of both paradigms and undergoing extensive large-scale pretraining on diverse robotic datasets, the authors aim to significantly improve the model's generalization capabilities across various tasks, objects, backgrounds, and lighting conditions.
- **Optimizing Performance and Practicality:** Ultimately, the objective is to achieve state-of-the-art manipulation performance in both simulation and real-world scenarios while maintaining a practical inference speed. The design considers the trade-offs between precision, reasoning, and computational efficiency.

In essence, HybridVLA aims to resolve the fundamental architectural challenge of integrating discrete reasoning with continuous control within a single, coherent large language model, thereby paving the way for more capable and generalist robot policies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HybridVLA introduces a unified framework that equips a single Large Language Model (LLM) with both diffusion and autoregressive action generation capabilities through a novel collaborative training recipe and inference mechanism.

#### 3.1. HybridVLA Architecture Overview

The model builds upon a pretrained VLM base, specifically **Prismatic VLMs**, utilizing either 7B LLAMA-2 or 2.7B Phi-2 as the LLM backbone. For vision encoding, it employs powerful combinations like DINOv2 and SigLIP (for 7B) or CLIP (for 2.7B) to extract rich semantic features from multi-view images. Language prompts are tokenized and fed into the LLM alongside projected visual and robot state features. The core innovation lies in how the LLM processes inputs and generates outputs to support two distinct yet integrated action prediction paradigms.

#### 3.2. Collaborative Training Recipe

This recipe is designed to overcome the challenge of combining continuous diffusion and discrete autoregressive action generation within a single LLM, which could otherwise lead to instability.

- **Token Sequence Formulation Design:** This is crucial for organizing multimodal inputs (vision, language, robot state) and the two types of action tokens within the LLM's embedding space.
 - **Robot State Integration:** The continuous robot state (e.g., 7-DOF end-effector pose) is mapped into the LLM's embedding space via a learnable MLP, rather than discretizing it. This avoids negative impacts on continuous diffusion prediction.
 - **Diffusion Token Encapsulation:** Special marker tokens, `<BOD>` (Beginning of Diffusion) and `<EOD>` (End of Diffusion), are introduced to encapsulate the diffusion tokens. This clarifies boundaries between diffusion and autoregressive generation and prevents interference (e.g., diffusion tokens directly predicting masked discrete tokens).
 - **Action Token Ordering:** Diffusion tokens are strategically placed *before* autoregressive tokens in the sequence. This ensures that autoregressive predictions (which follow the diffusion part) can explicitly condition on the continuous latent representations derived from the diffusion process. Crucially, because diffusion operates on noise, placing it first avoids information leakage of the ground truth autoregressive actions during training.
- **Hybrid Objectives:** Two distinct loss functions are combined to train the shared LLM backbone:
 - **Diffusion Loss (L_dif):** Mean Squared Error between the predicted noise and ground truth noise, following standard diffusion policy training.
 - **Autoregressive Loss (L_ce):** Cross-entropy loss for supervising the prediction of discrete action tokens.
 - **Joint Optimization:** By combining `L_hybrid = L_dif + L_ce`, gradients from both losses are jointly backpropagated to the shared LLM. This is the mechanism for "mutual reinforcement," allowing the LLM to learn both the continuous characteristics required for precise manipulation and the semantic reasoning derived from autoregressive generation.
- **Structured Training Stages:**
 - **Large-scale Pretraining:** HybridVLA is initially pretrained for 5 epochs on massive, diverse, cross-embodiment robotic datasets (Open X-Embodiment, DROID, ROBOMIND), comprising 760K trajectories and 33M frames. This stage uses single 2D observations to maximize data utilization from varied sources. This ensures the model acquires a broad foundation of robot manipulation skills and general understanding.
 - **Fine-tuning:** Subsequently, the model is fine-tuned on self-collected, task-specific simulation (RLBench) and real-world data, adapting to specific task requirements and potentially multi-view observations.

#### 3.3. Collaborative Action Ensemble (Inference)

During inference, HybridVLA concurrently generates actions using both diffusion and autoregressive methods and then adaptively fuses them for robust execution.

- **Autoregressive Actions:** Generated after the `<EOD>` token, crucially conditioning on the continuous action representations from the preceding diffusion tokens, leading to improved performance compared to independent autoregressive generation. The model also outputs a confidence score for these tokens.
- **Diffusion Actions:** Generated via a denoising process using DDIM, typically with 4 sampling steps. The noisy samples are iteratively fed into the LLM to predict noise, fully leveraging the LLM's contextual reasoning for continuous action prediction. A **KV cache** is employed before the diffusion tokens to accelerate sampling by reusing cached keys and values from the first pass, significantly improving inference speed.
- **Ensembled Actions:** Based on empirical observations that diffusion excels in intricate tasks and autoregression in semantic reasoning tasks, and that autoregressive token confidence is a reliable indicator of quality, an adaptive fusion mechanism is proposed:
 - If the mean confidence of autoregressive tokens (c_ar) exceeds a threshold `θ` (empirically 0.96), the final action is an average of the diffusion and autoregressive predictions: `a_t+1 = (a_d + a_ar) / 2`.
 - If `c_ar` is below `θ`, the model relies solely on the diffusion action: `a_t+1 = a_d`. This strategy leverages the strengths of each method based on contextual confidence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

HybridVLA consistently achieves state-of-the-art performance across a wide range of simulated and real-world robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating both enhanced precision and improved generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments (RLBench)

- **Superior Performance:** HybridVLA (7B) achieved a remarkable 74% mean success rate across 10 diverse RLBench tasks. This significantly surpasses previous state-of-the-art autoregressive models like OpenVLA (41%) by 33% and diffusion-based models like CogACT (60%) by 14%.
- **Effective VLM Integration:** Even the HybridVLA-dif (diffusion-only inference) variant, trained with the collaborative recipe, outperformed CogACT and Pi0 by 6% and 11% respectively. This highlights that HybridVLA's unique integration of diffusion *within* the LLM more effectively leverages the VLM's pretrained knowledge, unlocking greater potential for continuous action prediction.
- **Scalability:** HybridVLA (2.7B) also showed satisfactory results, indicating the method's effectiveness across different model sizes.
- **Inference Speed:** HybridVLA-dif (7B) achieved a competitive 9.4 Hz inference speed, comparable to prior diffusion-based methods, owing to reduced DDIM steps and the KV cache. HybridVLA (7B) with the ensemble ran at 6.1 Hz.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Validation of Collaborative Training Recipe (CTR):** Comparisons showed that training with the proposed hybrid objectives (HybridVLA-dif and HybridVLA-ar variants) significantly improved performance over training methods that relied solely on individual generation paradigms. This confirms that CTR effectively avoids negative interference and fosters mutual reinforcement between diffusion and autoregressive learning.
- **Importance of Large-scale Pretraining (LSP):** Models trained without LSP, even with pretrained VLM parameters, experienced a drastic drop in accuracy, underscoring the critical role of extensive pretraining on robot datasets for stable control.
- **Robot State Embedding (RSE):** Injecting robot state information was found to enhance the model's temporal consistency during action prediction.
- **Confidence Threshold for Ensemble:** An optimal confidence threshold of 0.96 for autoregressive tokens was empirically determined to ensure a stable and effective action ensemble. Lower or higher thresholds led to slight performance degradations.
- **KV Cache Impact:** The KV cache mechanism significantly boosted HybridVLA-dif's inference speed (from 5.0 Hz to 9.4 Hz) without compromising accuracy, demonstrating its practical value.
- **Denoising Steps:** Performance remained stable even when DDIM denoising steps were reduced from 30 to 4, allowing for faster inference without degradation.
- **Varying Strengths of Action Types:** Detailed analysis showed that diffusion-based predictions excelled in precise manipulation tasks (e.g., Phone on base, Close Laptop Lid), while autoregressive predictions performed better in tasks requiring rich semantic understanding (e.g., Sweep to dustpan, Water plants), justifying the ensemble approach.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

- **Outstanding Real-World Performance:** HybridVLA (7B) achieved mean success rates of 83% for single-arm tasks and 71% for dual-arm tasks. This represents a significant 19% improvement over previous SOTA VLA methods (e.g., Pi0 and CogACT in their respective domains).
- **Complex Task Proficiency:** The model demonstrated robust performance in tasks requiring precise positioning, rotation (e.g., Pour Water), and long-horizon multi-step actions (e.g., Open Drawer and Place Inside, Wipe Blackboard).
- **Dual-Arm Coordination:** HybridVLA successfully extended to 14-DOF dual-arm control, outperforming baselines in coordinated tasks like "Lift ball and place" and "Fold shorts," highlighting its ability to leverage VLM reasoning for complex bimanual coordination.

#### 4.4. Generalization Experiments

- **Robustness to Unseen Scenarios:** HybridVLA exhibited strong generalization capabilities across four critical unseen configurations:
 - **Unseen Manipulated Objects:** Smallest accuracy drop compared to baselines, indicating effective object-level semantic reasoning.
 - **Unseen Backgrounds:** Maintained satisfactory results, demonstrating inherited scene-level reasoning robustness.
 - **Unseen Spatial Positions (Height):** Achieved precise manipulation despite height variations, showcasing strong trajectory generalization.
 - **Unseen Lighting Conditions:** Maintained satisfactory performance, attributed to large-scale pretraining.
- **Synergy in Generalization:** The superior generalization performance is attributed to the collaborative model design, which combines continuous action generation with object- and scene-level semantic reasoning.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

HybridVLA represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic manipulation and embodied artificial intelligence, addressing a core architectural challenge in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key contributions:

1. **Unified and Harmonized VLA Architecture:** The most profound contribution is the successful integration of both continuous diffusion-based action generation and discrete autoregressive action prediction within a *single, shared Large Language Model (LLM) backbone*. This is a critical departure from previous approaches that often treated the diffusion head as a separate module, and it elegantly resolves the tension between precise continuous control and high-level semantic reasoning. This unification enables the LLM to learn a more coherent and comprehensive representation for robot control.

2. **Enhanced Performance and Robustness:** By enabling mutual reinforcement between the two action generation paradigms through a collaborative training recipe and an adaptive ensemble mechanism, HybridVLA achieves new state-of-the-art performance. The substantial improvements in success rates (14-19% over previous SOTA in simulation and real-world) across diverse and complex tasks, including challenging dual-arm coordination and long-horizon sequences, demonstrate its practical efficacy. This level of robustness is crucial for deploying robots in real-world, dynamic environments.

3. **Superior Generalization Capabilities:** The model's ability to generalize effectively to unseen objects, backgrounds, spatial positions, and lighting conditions is a major leap forward. This suggests that HybridVLA learns more abstract and transferable robotic policies, leveraging the strengths of both continuous trajectory generation and high-level semantic understanding. Improved generalization reduces the need for extensive task-specific data collection, a significant bottleneck in robotics.

4. **Practical Efficiency:** The HybridVLA-dif variant demonstrates that the benefits of collaborative training can be leveraged for high-speed inference (9.4 Hz) by relying solely on the diffusion process, while still outperforming previous diffusion-only VLA methods. This makes the technology more viable for real-time robotic applications where rapid decision-making is essential.

5. **Blueprint for Future Foundation Models:** HybridVLA offers a compelling architectural blueprint for building generalist robot foundation models. By showing how to effectively combine different learning paradigms within a unified large model, it provides a pathway toward creating more capable and adaptable AI agents for complex physical interactions. This work contributes to the broader vision of embodied intelligence, where AI systems can understand, reason, and act in the physical world akin to humans.

In the broader context, HybridVLA's success in fusing distinct yet complementary AI paradigms for robotics could inspire similar hybrid approaches in other domains of AI, pushing the boundaries of what unified multimodal models can achieve. The authors explicitly state their hope that this work "can promote the progress in the field of robot manipulation and open up a new paradigm for better providing foundation models in the field of embodiment intelligence," signifying its potential to contribute to the "healthy, controllable and sustainable development of the entire field."
