# World Action Verifier: Self-Improving World Models via Forward-Inverse Asymmetry

> [!summary] Summary
> The World Action Verifier (WAV) framework enhances action-conditioned world models by employing a self-improving cycle that leverages forward-inverse asymmetry to identify and rectify prediction errors. This approach resulted in a 2x improvement in sample efficiency for world model learning and an over-22% increase in average reward for downstream robotic manipulation policies compared to baseline methods.

## Problem

- Building robust, general-purpose world models requires reliability across a broad distribution of actions, including suboptimal and exploratory behaviors, which is difficult to achieve.
- Collecting large-scale, action-labeled robot interaction data is resource-intensive and often unsafe, limiting the coverage of necessary action distributions.
- Existing world model verification methods, such as those based on uncertainty estimation, often fail in under-explored data regions where new information is most needed.

## Method

- **WAV** decomposes world model prediction verification into two distinct problems: ==state plausibility==, checked by a ==subgoal generator==, and ==action reachability==, checked by a ==sparse inverse dynamics model==.
- The subgoal generator is trained on abundant action-free video data, while the sparse inverse dynamics model leverages the lower dimensionality of ==action-relevant features== from limited action-labeled data.
- A ==verification-guided self-improving cycle== prioritizes collecting new action-labeled interactions where the discrepancy between a plausible proposed future state and the world model's prediction is highest.

## Results

- **WAV** significantly reduced world model prediction error, achieving up to **2x** improvement in sample efficiency for world model learning across MiniGrid and robotic manipulation tasks.
- The sparse inverse dynamics model demonstrated superior robustness to state complexity, environmental stochasticity, and better out-of-distribution generalization compared to dense alternatives.
- Policies trained with **WAV**-improved world models achieved an average of over **22%** higher rewards across six robotic manipulation tasks compared to strong baselines, leading to more effective model-based planning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The method exploits two key asymmetries: the broader availability of action-free data for learning state plausibility and the lower dimensionality of action-relevant features for robust action inference.
> - Theoretical analysis demonstrates that sparse inverse verification is more robust and sample-efficient to learn than dense forward generation, especially in high-dimensional, stochastic environments with limited data.
> - The framework can identify and learn from compositional out-of-distribution transitions, enabling generalization beyond the initial training distribution.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models, which are action-conditioned forward dynamics models that predict future states given actions, are a significant area of research in robot learning. They offer the potential for scalable policy evaluation, optimization, and planning by enabling agents to simulate future outcomes. Recent advancements have shown that world models trained on a combination of action-labeled robot interactions and action-free internet videos can generate controllable dynamics and facilitate policy learning.

Despite these advancements, a core challenge remains in developing general-purpose world models robust enough for diverse downstream applications. A particular difficulty is "action following," which involves accurately predicting future states across a broad distribution of actions, including suboptimal, exploratory, or random actions. This contrasts with policy learning, which primarily focuses on optimal actions. Collecting robot interaction data that covers such a vast action space is often costly, slow, and potentially unsafe, leading to datasets where many action regimes are underrepresented.

Previous research has explored two main strategies to address this data scarcity:
1. **On-policy exploration:** Data is gathered by rolling out policies of interest. While effective for the specific policies being learned, the resulting world models often exhibit reduced performance outside these predefined policy sets, limiting their generality.
2. **Information-maximization (info-max) exploration:** This approach actively seeks interactions that maximize information gain, often by estimating the world model's prediction error before data collection. This process, referred to as world model verification, faces a practical limitation: such verification methods tend to be reliable in well-explored regions (where additional data is less critical) but become unreliable in under-explored regions (where verification is most needed). The challenge arises because the interactions most informative for exploration are precisely those where the least prior information is available for accurate verification.

This paper addresses this fundamental tension by proposing a framework designed to reliably verify world model predictions in these under-explored regimes. It introduces a novel approach that decomposes action-conditioned state prediction into independently verifiable factors, leveraging inherent asymmetries in data availability and feature dimensionality to overcome the limitations of existing verification methods. The work distinguishes itself by using an asymmetric forward-inverse cycle for self-improvement, contrasting with prior methods that primarily rely on the forward model's internal uncertainty or disagreement for verification.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the robustness and generalizability of world models for robot learning, particularly in their ability to accurately predict the consequences of diverse actions. The authors identify a central challenge: current world models struggle with "action following" across a wide spectrum of actions, especially those that are suboptimal or exploratory and thus underrepresented in training data. This limitation hinders their utility for scalable policy evaluation, optimization, and test-time planning.

The motivation for this work stems from the critical question: "How can we reliably verify the predictions of a world model in under-explored regimes?" Existing verification methods often fail in these crucial areas because they inherently share the blind spots of the world model they are trying to verify. To address this, the paper proposes a novel approach:
1. **Decomposition of Prediction:** Instead of directly verifying the entire action-conditioned forward prediction, the authors propose to decompose it into two complementary and more tractable factors:
 - **State Plausibility:** Verifying whether a predicted future state is visually realistic and consistent with the general environment dynamics.
 - **Action Reachability:** Verifying whether a transition from a current state to a future state is physically achievable given a specific action.
2. **Leveraging Asymmetries for Verification:** The decomposition is motivated by two key asymmetries that can make verification more tractable than direct forward prediction:
 - **Distribution Asymmetry (for State Plausibility):** Action-free video data, such as internet videos, is far more abundant than action-labeled robot interaction data. This extensive action-free data can be leveraged to train a robust prior model for state plausibility, enabling the generation of diverse and realistic future states without requiring specific action labels.
 - **Dimensionality Asymmetry (for Action Reachability):** Actions typically affect only a compact, lower-dimensional subset of state features (e.g., end-effector position, joint angles) rather than the entire high-dimensional visual scene. An inverse model focused on these action-relevant features can thus infer actions more reliably, even when the full state transition is complex or novel.

Therefore, the key objective is to leverage these asymmetries to build a "World Action Verifier (WAV)" framework. This framework aims to enable world models to autonomously identify and correct their own prediction errors, leading to self-improvement and more effective data acquisition in previously under-explored operational regimes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology is the World Action Verifier (WAV), a framework designed for self-improving world models by identifying and acting on their prediction errors. WAV operates in a semi-supervised learning setting, utilizing both a limited action-labeled robot interaction dataset (D_act) and a large action-free video dataset (D_vid).

The core of WAV lies in decomposing the verification of action-conditioned forward predictions into two sub-problems: state plausibility and action reachability. This decomposition enables the use of specialized components that leverage data and dimensionality asymmetries.

**Key Components of WAV:**
1. **World Model (f_θ):** This is the action-conditioned forward dynamics model, ˆs_{t+1} = f_θ(s_t, a_t), which predicts the next state given a current state and action. The goal of WAV is to iteratively improve the robustness and accuracy of this model. The world model is based on Dreamer-v3, utilizing a latent recurrent state-space model (RSSM) with convolutional encoders/decoders for visual observations and MLPs for proprioceptive states.
2. **Diverse Subgoal Generator (g_ϕ):** This component is responsible for verifying *state plausibility*. It is trained on both D_act and the much larger D_vid, leveraging the **distribution asymmetry** where action-free data is abundant. The subgoal generator learns a state transition prior, g_ϕ(s_{t+1} | s_t), which allows it to sample a diverse set of *K* plausible future subgoals (˜s_{t+1}^k) given a current state s_t. By focusing on generating visually plausible states without action constraints, it ensures that proposed futures are realistic.
3. **Sparse Inverse Model (h_ψ):** This component is designed to verify *action reachability*. It infers the action (ˆa_t) that could connect a current state (s_t) to a proposed future state (˜s_{t+1}). This model benefits from the **dimensionality asymmetry**: actions typically affect only a low-dimensional subset of state features. To exploit this, a learnable sparsity mask (M) is explicitly imposed on the inverse dynamics model: ˆa_t = h_ψ(M ⊙ s_t, M ⊙ ˜s_{t+1}). This sparsity encourages the model to focus on causally relevant features, making action inference more robust and generalizable, especially under novel scene configurations. The inverse dynamics model is based on CLAM architecture with a space-time Transformer for visual encoding and an L1 regularization term for sparsity in the latent action space.

**Goal-Oriented Exploration Cycle (Algorithm 1):**
WAV integrates these components into an iterative, self-improving exploration loop:
1. **Subgoal Sampling:** At the current state s_t, the diverse subgoal generator (g_ϕ) samples *K* plausible future subgoals {˜s_{t+1}^k}.
2. **Action Inference:** For each sampled subgoal ˜s_{t+1}^k, the sparse inverse model (h_ψ) infers the action ˆa_t^k that could lead from s_t to ˜s_{t+1}^k, based on the action-relevant subset of features.
3. **Forward Rollout:** The world model (f_θ) then uses the current state s_t and each inferred action ˆa_t^k to predict its own next state ˆs_{t+1}^k.
4. **Discrepancy Calculation:** A discrepancy measure ℓ(˜s_{t+1}^k, ˆs_{t+1}^k) is computed for each candidate, quantifying the difference between the proposed plausible subgoal and the world model's prediction. This measures the "surprise" or error of the world model in realizing the plausible, inverse-reachable subgoal.
5. **Action Selection:** The action (a_⋆) corresponding to the largest discrepancy score is selected. This prioritizes interactions where the world model's prediction deviates most significantly from a plausible, reachable outcome, indicating regions where the model is uncertain or inaccurate.
6. **Environment Interaction:** The selected action a_⋆ is executed in the real environment (or simulator) to obtain the true next state s_{new}.
7. **Model Update:** The new transition (s_t, a_⋆, s_{new}) is added to D_act, and both the world model (f_θ) and the sparse inverse model (h_ψ) are updated.

This goal-oriented cycle places the world model's forward prediction as the final verification step, making it more robust than action-oriented exploration which relies on the often less reliable forward model at the outset.

**Theoretical Justification:**
The paper provides a theoretical analysis (Proposition 3.1 and C.1) supporting the asymmetry claim. It demonstrates that, in a stylized linear-Gaussian setting, the expected error of a sparse inverse model can be significantly lower than that of a dense forward model due to:
- **Dimensionality:** The inverse model operates on a much lower-dimensional feature space (2d_z vs. d_s + d_a).
- **Stochasticity:** Forward prediction is more sensitive to environment noise (σ_s), while inverse verification is primarily affected by action-recovery ambiguity (σ_a).
- **Sample Size:** The forward estimator is less stable in low-data regimes.
Furthermore, it proposes that a sparse inverse model can generalize to out-of-support (OOS) compositional transitions, provided that the action-relevant subset of latent variables remains within the training support. This allows WAV to generate accurate pseudo-labels for novel experiences, effectively expanding the world model's action-labeled data coverage.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates WAV across nine tasks spanning three environments: MiniGrid (synthetic), RoboMimic (simulated robotic manipulation), and ManiSkill (simulated robotic manipulation). The findings address five key research questions:

**RQ1: Is learning an inverse dynamics model easier than learning a forward world model?**
The results indicate that inverse dynamics models (IDMs) are consistently easier to learn and more robust than action-conditioned forward world models (WMs), especially in low-data regimes. Figure 3 (Left) demonstrates that IDM accuracy consistently surpasses WM accuracy across varying sample sizes, with the performance gap being most pronounced when data is limited.

**RQ2: Do sparse IDMs generalize better than vanilla IDMs to unseen objects or interactions?**
Sparsity in the inverse dynamics model significantly improves generalization to more complex and out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios.
- **Robustness to Complexity:** As the number of objects (and thus state dimension) increases, WM performance degrades rapidly, while the sparse IDM maintains stable performance (Figure 3, Mid). This supports the dimensionality asymmetry hypothesis.
- **Robustness to Stochasticity:** In environments with increasing observation noise (noisy floor tiles), the WM shows sensitivity, whereas the sparse IDM's performance remains largely invariant (Figure 3, Right). This confirms the stochasticity asymmetry.
- **Compositional Generalization:** Figure 4 (Left) illustrates that the sparse IDM outperforms the vanilla IDM on tasks requiring compositional generalization, such as 'toggle' and 'swap' actions involving previously unseen object-action combinations. This suggests that enforcing sparsity encourages the model to learn more robust and task-relevant action inference.

**RQ3: How effective is forward–inverse asymmetry for self-improving the world model?**
WAV significantly improves world model learning quality.
- **Prediction Error:** On MiniGrid, WAV (referred to as "Ours") achieves prediction errors comparable to the Oracle method, outperforming other baselines (Random, Uncertainty, Progress, Vanilla IDM) (Figure 4, Mid).
- **Action Following:** WAV also demonstrates the highest Action Following Score (Figure 4, Right), indicating that the resulting world model better captures and distinguishes between the effects of different actions, preventing predictions from collapsing to visually plausible but action-agnostic states.
- **Robotic Manipulation:** In RoboMimic and ManiSkill tasks, WAV consistently yields world models with lower prediction errors across various data budgets, showing particularly large gains in low-data regimes (Figure 5 and Table 1 in Appendix A.2). The method's verification scores exhibit strong monotonic alignment with true prediction errors, indicating reliable sample ranking (Figure 10 in Appendix A.1).

**RQ4: Does this self-improvement translate into improved downstream policy learning?**
Yes, world models refined through WAV-guided exploration lead to improved performance in downstream imagination-based policy learning. Policies trained with WAV-improved world models achieve higher task rewards across RoboMimic and ManiSkill tasks compared to baselines, second only to the Oracle (Figure 7 in Appendix A.3). The gains are especially notable in tasks with complex or contact-rich dynamics, where accurate latent dynamics are crucial for effective policy refinement.

**RQ5: Can the learned world model adapt to out-of-distribution robotic manipulation settings with limited target-domain data under novel visual setups, objects, and interactions?**
WAV demonstrates stronger OOD adaptation capabilities.
- **Visual Shifts:** Under visual appearance changes (backgrounds, robot colors) but unchanged dynamics, WAV-adapted world models achieve lower prediction error and support policies with higher rewards compared to baselines, even with limited target-domain data (Figure 6).
- **Object and Interaction Shifts:** When facing novel objects and mixed-optimality demonstrations introducing diverse contacts and ambiguous interactions, WAV yields more robust adaptation. It achieves substantial improvements in both prediction error and downstream policy reward (approximately 22% improvement in reward on novel environments), suggesting its ability to efficiently acquire data crucial for learning complex OOD dynamics.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework, World Action Verifier (WAV), that exploits a fundamental asymmetry between forward and inverse dynamics. The central insight is that inferring the action responsible for a plausible state transition can be substantially more tractable than predicting the full future state resulting from an action, particularly in high-dimensional and stochastic environments.

The methodological contribution lies in decomposing the challenge of world model verification into two complementary factors—state plausibility and action reachability—and leveraging inherent data and dimensionality asymmetries to address them. By integrating a diverse subgoal generator from action-free video data and a sparse inverse model focused on action-relevant features within a goal-oriented self-improvement cycle, WAV provides a mechanism for world models to identify and correct their own prediction errors.

The empirical results consistently demonstrate that WAV offers significant advantages:
- **Enhanced Sample Efficiency:** The method achieves approximately 2x higher sample efficiency in exploration, reducing the amount of costly action-labeled robot interaction data required for robust world model training.
- **Improved World Model Quality:** WAV-trained world models exhibit lower prediction errors and better action-following capabilities, particularly in low-data regimes and when generalizing to increased scene complexity or environmental stochasticity.
- **Superior Downstream Policy Performance:** The improved world models translate directly into better performance for downstream imagination-based policy learning, with policies refined using WAV-enhanced models showing over 22% improvement in task rewards.
- **Robust OOD Adaptation:** The framework enables more effective adaptation to out-of-distribution settings, encompassing both visual shifts and more challenging object/interaction shifts, with limited target-domain data.

The significance of this work extends to the broader field of embodied AI and robot learning. By providing a reliable mechanism for self-improving world models, WAV contributes to overcoming a key bottleneck in scaling robot capabilities. The ability to autonomously identify and acquire informative interactions can reduce reliance on human-intensive data collection and expert-designed curricula, potentially leading to more generalizable and robust robotic systems. The findings suggest that the forward-inverse asymmetry is a valuable principle for developing world models that can learn effectively from sparse and diverse experiences.

While the current instantiation has computational overhead due to multiple inference passes, the authors identify promising future directions, including improving efficiency through shared representations or adaptive computation, and integrating richer generative priors and more expressive inverse models for longer-horizon and more complex embodied tasks. The work also highlights the importance of continued research into robust verification mechanisms, especially as reliance on learned world models increases in safety-sensitive applications.
