# Transferable Reward Learning by Dynamics-Agnostic Discriminator Ensemble

> [!summary] Summary
> A method called Dynamics-Agnostic Discriminator-Ensemble Reward Learning (DARL) learns state-action reward functions from expert demonstrations that remain robust and transferable across environments with varying dynamics. By employing mutual information minimization and a discriminator ensemble, DARL effectively mitigates dynamics and policy dependency in adversarial imitation learning, demonstrating superior performance and high reward consistency across diverse MuJoCo transfer tasks.

## Problem

- Reward functions recovered by existing adversarial imitation learning (AIL) methods are intrinsically coupled with specific training dynamics, hindering their transferability to environments with altered transition rules.
- AIL discriminators suffer from "policy dependency," where they catastrophically forget how to accurately evaluate early, suboptimal policies as they converge, leading to unreliable reward signals for training new policies from scratch.
- Current approaches to learning transferable rewards often rely on restrictive assumptions, such as requiring rewards to be state-only or needing partial knowledge of the reward function, limiting their real-world applicability.

## Method

- To achieve ==dynamics-agnostic reward learning==, **DARL** modifies the discriminator to operate on latent state-action embeddings, ==minimizing mutual information== between these embeddings and the next state.
- A ==discriminator ensemble==, constructed by saving historical discriminators during training, addresses ==policy dependency== by using the mean output of the ensemble as the reward signal, providing robust evaluation across policies at various learning stages.
- The ensemble incorporates ==normalization and clipping== for individual discriminator outputs to stabilize training and prevent the reinforcement learning agent from over-exploiting potentially unreliable early discriminator signals.

## Results

- DARL achieved at least **92%** reward consistency across all tested MuJoCo gravity-transfer tasks, significantly outperforming all baseline methods by providing more accurate directional feedback to agents.
- DARL policies demonstrated superior performance, achieving the highest normalized returns in **15 out of 16** transfer tasks across various MuJoCo environments (e.g., Ant, HalfCheetah) with altered dynamics.
- In action-dependent 'EnergyLim' environments, DARL was the only method to achieve positive returns in **3 out of 4** tasks, showcasing its effectiveness in inferring and utilizing action-dependent reward information.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly decoupling the learned reward from environment dynamics through mutual information minimization between state-action representations and future states is crucial for transferable reward functions.
> - Employing an ensemble of historical discriminators effectively mitigates policy dependency, allowing the learned reward to accurately guide policy learning from diverse starting points without catastrophic forgetting.
> - Transferable state-action dependent reward functions can be effectively learned, challenging the assumption that only state-only rewards are transferable, especially in tasks with significant action dependencies.
