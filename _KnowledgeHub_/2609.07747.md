# Dex-X: Learning Visual-Tactile Dexterous Manipulation From Human Videos with Simulated Interaction

> [!summary] Summary
> DEX-X learns visual-tactile dexterous manipulation from monocular human videos by treating simulation as a tactile completion engine: reconstructed hand-object trajectories are retargeted into simulation, where contact dynamics supply the missing fingertip forces for tactile-aware RL, then distilled into a point-cloud plus tactile student policy. The teacher reaches 65.9% average success across six simulated task categories; the deployed student hits 93% on real cube picking and 53% on table cleaning.

## Problem

- Human videos scale dexterous behavior data but carry no tactile or contact-force signal, which contact-rich grasping and sustained tool interaction critically depend on.
- Collecting tactile robot demonstrations needs instrumented gloves or custom sensing hardware, and teleoperated dexterous data collection stays costly, limiting task diversity and scale.
- Imitation and retargeting methods reproduce kinematics but lose stable contact over long horizons, while sim-to-real stays fragile under perception noise, actuation delay, contact-model error.

## Method

- ==Motion prior extraction==: FoundationPose object poses plus WiLoR hand poses refined by ==MANO temporal consistency== and ==penetration constraints==, then two-stage arm-then-hand ==retargeting== with planar translation and yaw augmentation.
- State expert training: PPO with ==asymmetric actor-critic== in IsaacLab, reference-conditioned reward mixing wrist and hand tracking, object tracking, fingertip-force, approach and ==no-slip contact shaping==, under broad domain randomization.
- ==DAgger distillation== into a student on an ==augmented contact point cloud==: depth scene points, six hand keypoints, 25 tactile points carrying fingertip force, encoded by a shared ==PointNet==.

## Results

- State expert averages **65.9%** across six simulated categories versus **21.9%** for ManipTrans, **5.2%** for kinematic retargeting, and **40.6%** for DAPG; tool use **74.3%** versus **35.0%**.
- Real cube picking reaches **28/30 (93.3%)**; removing vision drops it to **14/30**, removing tactile to **11/30**, proprioception only **8/30**; squeegee manipulation reaches **16/30**.
- Point cloud with force scores **44%** strict and **58%** relaxed success versus **32%** strict for depth; dropping force cuts relaxed to **45%**. Unseen thin cube: **23/30**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simulation can substitute for a missing sensor channel: physical contact dynamics recover supervision passive video never recorded, turning observation-only data into closed-loop interaction training.
> - Embedding tactile forces as typed points inside the scene point cloud lets one geometry encoder fuse touch with vision; scalar force matches richer 3D encodings.
> - Zero-shot generalization collapses under geometry shift, from 93% to roughly 25% on new shapes, so reference-conditioned policies stay bounded by demonstration diversity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of dexterous manipulation in robotics aims to enable robots to perform complex, fine-grained object interactions similar to humans. This capability typically requires sophisticated coordination of high-dimensional robot motions under dynamic contact conditions, utilizing multimodal sensory feedback such as vision and touch. Current approaches in reinforcement learning (RL) and imitation learning (IL) for dexterous manipulation often rely on either extensive interaction data collected in meticulously designed simulation environments or large datasets of robot demonstrations obtained through specialized teleoperation systems. These methods face limitations in scalability due to the high cost and complexity of data collection, especially for tactile information which often requires custom sensing hardware.

Human video demonstrations represent a compelling alternative data source, offering abundant and diverse examples of dexterous manipulation performed in natural settings. However, a significant challenge arises from the inherent lack of direct tactile feedback, particularly contact forces, in monocular human videos. This absence is critical because tactile information is essential for maintaining stable grasps and engaging in sustained, contact-rich interactions. Furthermore, transferring policies learned in simulation to real-world robotic hardware continues to be a challenge due to discrepancies in perception, actuation, and contact dynamics.

This work, DEX-X, positions itself within this landscape by addressing the fundamental question of whether robots can learn deployable visual-tactile dexterous manipulation policies directly from human video demonstrations without requiring robot-side data collection. It builds upon previous efforts in learning from human demonstrations by introducing simulation as a means to "complete" the missing tactile information. Existing works have explored motion retargeting, trajectory optimization, and RL over reconstructed human trajectories to bridge the human-to-robot embodiment gap. DEX-X extends these by leveraging retargeted human motion references to guide RL training, while using simulated contact interactions to explicitly provide force-level tactile supervision.

Regarding sim-to-real transfer, various studies have enabled the training of complex robot behaviors in simulation and their transfer to reality. However, robust transfer for dexterous manipulation remains challenging, particularly for behaviors sensitive to contact dynamics and tactile sensing. DEX-X contributes to this area by developing a distillation paradigm that transfers a privileged state-based policy to a deployable policy utilizing point-cloud observations, proprioception, and real fingertip tactile feedback, aiming for zero-shot transfer without real-world fine-tuning. This work is also situated within the growing area of visual-tactile manipulation, where tactile sensing augments visual observations. While prior research has investigated tactile representation learning and visual-tactile policy learning, DEX-X specifically focuses on deriving this critical tactile information from passive human videos through simulated physical interaction to enable autonomous, sustained tool-object-environment interaction.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to ascertain whether robots can acquire deployable visual-tactile dexterous manipulation policies directly from human video demonstrations, thereby circumventing the need for robot-specific data collection. This central aim is motivated by several considerations and addresses key challenges in robotic manipulation:

**Motivation for leveraging human video demonstrations:**
- **Abundance and Diversity:** Human videos are a vast and easily accessible resource, capturing a wide spectrum of dexterous manipulation behaviors performed in diverse, unstructured environments. This offers a scalable alternative to costly and time-consuming robot-centric data collection.
- **Naturalness of Behaviors:** Human demonstrations inherently reflect proficient and adaptable manipulation strategies, providing valuable priors for robot skill learning.

**Key challenges that the work seeks to overcome:**
1. **Absence of Tactile Information in Human Videos:** Human videos primarily provide visual observations, lacking explicit tactile feedback such as contact forces. Tactile information is crucial for various aspects of dexterous manipulation, including:
 - **Stable Grasping:** Maintaining a secure hold on objects, especially during dynamic movements or when object properties (e.g., slipperiness, fragility) are unknown.
 - **Sustained Contact-Rich Interaction:** Performing tasks like tool use or object manipulation where continuous physical contact and force regulation are necessary. Without this information, policies learned purely from visual cues may struggle with robust physical interaction.
2. **Challenges in Sim-to-Real Transfer for Dexterous Manipulation:** Policies trained exclusively in simulation often exhibit a "reality gap" when deployed on physical robots. This gap is attributable to:
 - **Perception Noise:** Differences in sensor noise and accuracy between simulation and real-world cameras or tactile sensors.
 - **Actuation Delays and Imperfections:** Discrepancies in motor response times and control fidelity.
 - **Inaccuracies in Contact Modeling:** Simplifications or errors in physics simulations that do not perfectly replicate real-world contact dynamics, especially critical for contact-rich tasks.

To address these challenges, the core insight of DEX-X is to conceptualize simulation as a "tactile completion engine." The framework aims to reconstruct human hand-object interactions within a physics simulation, allowing for the generation of physically grounded contact dynamics. This simulated interaction provides explicit tactile supervision (e.g., per-fingertip contact forces) that is absent in the original human videos. By doing so, the research aims to transform passive visual demonstrations into active, closed-loop visual-tactile policies capable of zero-shot transfer to real-world dexterous manipulation tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DEX-X is structured as a three-stage framework designed to learn visual-tactile dexterous manipulation policies from human video demonstrations and enable zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

**Stage 1: Motion Prior Data Extraction with Spatial Augmentation**
The initial stage focuses on processing human video demonstrations to extract robot-compatible motion references.
- **Hand-Object Motion Reconstruction:** From monocular human demonstration videos, hand-object interaction trajectories are reconstructed at 30 Hz. Object poses (position and rotation) are estimated using FoundationPose, while hand poses are initially estimated by WiLoR. These hand poses are subsequently refined using the MANO parametric model, incorporating temporal consistency constraints and penalties for hand-object interpenetration. The output comprises wrist trajectories, 3D MANO keypoint trajectories, and object pose trajectories.
- **Robot Embodiment Retargeting:** The reconstructed human trajectories are adapted to the robotic platform (Franka FR3 arm with Sharpa Wave dexterous hand) through a two-stage optimization process.
 - The first stage optimizes the arm trajectory to track the reconstructed human wrist pose, holding the robotic hand in a nominal configuration.
 - The second stage jointly optimizes the arm and hand to align robot hand keypoints with the reconstructed MANO targets. A weighting scheme prioritizes the thumb, index finger, and distal keypoints to ensure critical grasp points are accurately transferred.
- **Spatial Augmentation:** To enhance robustness and generalization, each demonstration is spatially augmented before retargeting. This involves applying a shared planar translation and yaw perturbation to the wrist, MANO keypoints, and object trajectory. This augmentation helps expose the policy to variations in initial object placement and hand-object configurations.

**Stage 2: State Expert Training**
Following the generation of robot-compatible motion priors, a privileged state-based expert policy is trained in simulation using reinforcement learning.
- **Reinforcement Learning Setup:** Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with an asymmetric actor-critic architecture is employed. The training leverages 4096 parallel environments in IsaacLab, operating at 30 Hz.
- **Tactile Feedback from Simulation:** The expert receives per-fingertip tactile feedback in the form of five scalar contact-force magnitudes, one for each fingertip. These force signals are averaged over the two most recent samples to mitigate transient contact spikes. To enhance robustness for real-world deployment, domain randomization is applied to tactile sensing, including latency, multiplicative force noise, and random signal dropouts for individual fingertips.
- **Observation Space:**
 - **Actor Observation:** A 557-dimensional vector comprising proprioceptive information, the next-step retargeted motion reference, object information (reference object pose, fingertip-to-object distances, BPS geometry encoding), tactile feedback (five scalar force magnitudes), and a noisy 6-DoF object pose estimate. Domain randomization is applied to the object pose observation (frame-wise noise, episode-wise bias, latency, dropouts) to simulate real-world perception errors.
 - **Critic Observation:** Augments the actor observation with 148 dimensions of privileged simulation state, including ground-truth object state, a five-step future horizon of target object states, and true fingertip-to-object distances.
- **Action Space and Control:** The policy outputs a 29-dimensional action vector, consisting of 7 arm joint-delta commands and 22 hand joint-position targets. Arm commands are scaled (0.2 rad/step) and applied as deltas, while hand actions are absolute joint-position targets. Actions are clipped, subjected to randomized delays (0-3 steps), low-pass filtered, and saturated to joint limits.
- **Reward Function:** A composite reward function guides learning, including terms for wrist tracking, hand tracking (absolute and wrist-relative), object tracking, contact shaping (fingertip-force, approach, no-slip), action regularization, task success, and collision penalties.

**Stage 3: Policy Distillation and Sim-to-Real Transfer**
The final stage involves distilling the trained state-based expert into a deployable visual-tactile student policy suitable for real-world execution.
- **Teacher-Student Distillation:** The expert policy is distilled into a single multi-task student policy using the DAgger algorithm. The student's observation is a reduced version of the teacher's, combined with a learned point-cloud feature. During training, a mixing coefficient balances the execution of teacher and student actions, gradually decaying the teacher's influence.
- **Augmented Point-Cloud Observation and Tactile Feedback (Student):** For real-world deployment, the student policy utilizes a unified point-cloud representation. This point cloud is constructed from depth camera data (1024 scene points), supplemented by six robot-hand keypoints and 25 tactile surface points (five per fingertip). Crucially, the tactile points embed fingertip force magnitudes, while a scalar type indicator distinguishes between scene, hand, and tactile points. This point-cloud is processed by a PointNet backbone to extract a 64-dimensional feature vector. The student's input thus combines proprioception, the motion reference, and this point-cloud feature.
- **Real-Time Deployment:** The distilled policy operates in a closed-loop manner at 30 Hz on the physical robot. It is conditioned on the retargeted motion reference, real-time proprioceptive data, depth camera observations, and actual fingertip tactile feedback. No additional real-world fine-tuning is performed.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of DEX-X addressed three primary research questions, demonstrating the framework's capabilities in simulation and zero-shot transfer to real-world tasks.

**Q1: Multi-Task Dexterous Manipulation in Simulation**
The state-based expert policy, trained in simulation, achieved an average success rate of 65.9% across six diverse task categories (pick-up, peg insertion, tool use, in-hand rotation, in-hand translation, bimanual handover). This performance significantly surpassed baseline methods:
- ManipTrans-style imitation baseline: 21.9% average success.
- Kinematic retargeting: 5.2% average success.
- DAPG-style PPO baseline (evaluated on five single-hand categories): DEX-X achieved 61.7% average success compared to DAPG's 40.6%.
The paper noted that imitation-based policies frequently experienced loss of stable contact, whereas DEX-X's integration of simulated fingertip force feedback enabled adaptive, closed-loop interaction.

**Q2: The Role of Scene and Tactile Representations**

- **Simulation Ablation (Student Policy):** Comparison of visual geometry and tactile feedback demonstrated the benefits of the point-cloud representation. Point-cloud scene observations with tactile feedback achieved 44% strict and 58% relaxed success criteria in simulation. In contrast, depth-based observations yielded 32% strict success. The removal of tactile feedback from the point-cloud policy reduced relaxed success from 58% to 45%, indicating that both explicit 3D geometry and tactile feedback contribute to manipulation performance. Different tactile encodings (scalar force, binary contact, 3D force) showed similar performance, leading to the adoption of scalar force magnitudes for consistency with real-world sensing.
- **Real-World Modality Ablation (Cube Picking):** Controlled experiments on a real robot for cube picking confirmed the importance of both visual and tactile observations:
 - Full Visual-Tactile Policy: 28/30 (93.3%) success.
 - No Vision: 14/30 (46.7%) success.
 - No Tactile: 11/30 (36.7%) success.
 - Proprioception Only: 8/30 (26.7%) success.
 Failure modes included missing the object during grasping and losing the object post-contact. These results underscore the necessity of multimodal sensing for robust real-world deployment.

**Q3: Real-World Deployment and Generalization**

- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The distilled visual-tactile policy was deployed on a 29-DoF dexterous hand-arm platform without any real-world fine-tuning. It achieved:
 - Cube picking: 28/30 (93.3%) success.
 - Cup pouring: 24/30 success.
 - Cup lifting: 22/30 success.
 - Squeegee manipulation (contact-rich tool use): 16/30 (53%) success.
 The lower success rate on squeegee manipulation was attributed to the increased difficulty of maintaining stable grasp and sustained contact over a longer interaction horizon.
- **Real-World Deployment Outcome Breakdown:** A detailed analysis on cube picking and squeegee tasks revealed that cube picking maintained high success across all execution stages. For squeegee manipulation, failures were predominantly observed during grasp acquisition and post-grasp collision/drop events.
- **Object Generalization:** The policy demonstrated zero-shot generalization to unseen object geometries on the cube-picking task:
 - Training Cube: 28/30 (93.3%) success.
 - Unseen Thin Cube: 23/30 (76.7%) success.
 - Unseen Square Cube and two toy ducks (larger geometry changes): 7-8/30 (23.3-26.7%) success.
 This indicates that the policy can transfer to novel object geometries, though performance degrades with increasing divergence from the training object's shape.
- **Sim-to-Real Alignment Analysis (Appendix B):** Comparisons of fingertip force patterns and wrist trajectories between simulation and real-world rollouts for the cube-picking task showed consistent force profiles, suggesting successful transfer of contact strategies. Wrist trajectories exhibited a mean sim-to-real tracking error of 2.83 cm, with systematic deviations attributed to arm compliance and camera calibration errors rather than fundamental differences in learned motion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents DEX-X, a framework that addresses critical challenges in scaling dexterous robot manipulation by effectively bridging human video demonstrations with deployable robotic skills. The primary significance of this work lies in its novel approach of utilizing simulation as a "tactile completion engine." By reconstructing human hand-object interactions in a physics simulation, DEX-X is able to infer and provide physically grounded contact dynamics, thereby generating essential tactile supervision that is absent in the original monocular human videos. This insight enables the learning of visual-tactile policies for contact-rich manipulation tasks, which is a key barrier for acquiring such skills from passive visual data alone.

The potential impact of DEX-X is multifaceted:

- **Scalable Skill Acquisition:** The framework offers a pathway for significantly scaling robot skill learning. By leveraging abundant and diverse human video data from the internet, it reduces the reliance on costly, complex, and robot-specific data collection, particularly for tactile demonstrations. This could accelerate the development of a wider range of dexterous manipulation capabilities for robots.
- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The demonstrated ability for zero-shot sim-to-real transfer of complex visual-tactile dexterous manipulation policies to a 29-DoF hand-arm platform is a notable achievement. This eliminates the need for extensive real-world fine-tuning, which is often a bottleneck in robot deployment.
- **Robust Real-World Performance:** The ablation studies confirm the importance of integrating both visual and tactile feedback for robust performance in real-world scenarios. The combined modalities enable the robot to adapt to dynamic contact conditions and uncertainties inherent in physical interaction.
- **Generalization Capabilities:** The observed zero-shot generalization to unseen object geometries suggests that the learned policies are not overly specialized to specific objects seen during training. This indicates a potential for broader applicability across variations in object shapes, although performance degradation with significant geometric changes highlights areas for further improvement.
- **Advancement in Visual-Tactile Integration:** This work contributes to the advancement of visual-tactile manipulation by demonstrating a practical method to derive crucial force-level tactile feedback from visually-only human demonstrations. This enhances the robot's ability to engage in sustained and stable interactions.

Despite these advancements, the paper identifies several limitations that also indicate avenues for future impact. Robust long-horizon interaction and broad generalization remain challenging, and performance is sensitive to perception errors, controller mismatch, and large shifts in object geometry or category. The deployed policy currently relies on retargeted motion references, suggesting future work could explore more autonomous task conditioning. Furthermore, the tactile representation is limited to per-fingertip force magnitudes, implying that incorporating richer tactile signals (e.g., pressure, shear, slip, contact patches) could enable more precise contact reasoning and force regulation. Addressing these limitations could further expand the impact of this approach, potentially leading to more versatile and adaptable dexterous robot manipulation systems.
