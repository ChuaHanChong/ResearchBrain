# Unifying Perception and Action: A Hybrid-Modality Pipeline with Implicit Visual Chain-of-Thought for Robotic Action Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> VITA, a framework from Nanjing University, integrates visual perception and action generation for robotic manipulation through a hybrid-modality pipeline and an implicit visual Chain-of-Thought. It demonstrates improved task completion rates on simulated benchmarks and robust real-world generalization, achieving low-latency action generation.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models using text-only Chain-of-Thought often lack the fine-grained visual details required for precise robotic action execution.
- Visual-guided VLA models struggle with a modality gap between dense visual observations and sparse robotic actions, and suffer from training instability due to competing optimization objectives.
- Traditional "predict-then-act" reasoning paradigms incur significant computational latency, hindering real-time robotic manipulation.

## Method

- Introduced **VITA**, a unified framework employing a hybrid-modality pipeline with a ==shared discrete latent== space for visual perception and action generation.
- Developed an "==implicit visual Chain-of-Thought==" where future frame prediction serves as an inductive bias, integrating visual dynamics directly into action generation.
- Implemented a progressive training regimen, starting with independent cross-modal vector quantization, followed by co-training of a VLM backbone, and efficient fine-tuning of only the action decoder.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance on simulated benchmarks, with a 4.73 average task completion rate on CALVIN and **96.7%** average success on LIBERO.
- Demonstrated robust real-world generalization, achieving an **80.5%** average success rate on six real-world tasks and maintaining performance on Out-of-Distribution scenarios.
- Showcased efficient inference with a latency of **60.6ms** and an average action-level throughput of **60 Hz**, making it suitable for real-time robotic control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A shared discrete latent space, achieved through cross-modal vector quantization, can effectively bridge the modality gap between dense visual data and sparse action commands.
> - Internalizing visual dynamics via an "implicit visual Chain-of-Thought" outperforms explicit visual prediction, acting as a powerful inductive bias for more robust and efficient action generation.
> - A progressive training regimen, starting with independent pre-training of modality-specific encoders and a shared codebook, is critical for stable learning and strong generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research on VITA is situated within the rapidly evolving field of **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models** for general-purpose robotic agents. This domain seeks to enable robots to understand human instructions, perceive complex environments, and execute precise actions across a wide range of tasks and robot embodiments. The paper acknowledges the significant advancements made by VLA models built upon Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning.

However, the authors identify critical limitations in existing approaches:
1. **Text-only CoT limitations**: While effective for high-level reasoning, text-only CoT often struggles to capture the fine-grained visual details and spatial topological features necessary for precise action execution in complex physical environments. Language descriptions alone can be insufficiently grounded and semantically ambiguous.
2. **Visual-guided VLA challenges**: A promising alternative involves leveraging visual dynamics (e.g., through future frame prediction) as a prior to guide action generation. However, current strategies face two inherent problems:
 - **Modality Gap**: There is an inherent disparity between high-dimensional visual observations (pixel-level details) and low-level, sparse robotic actions. Directly deriving precise actions from pixel predictions is challenging, as many visual details are irrelevant to the specific action.
 - **Training Instability and Competing Objectives**: The optimization objectives for visual prediction tasks (e.g., predicting future frames) and action generation tasks are often distinct and can compete, leading to unstable training and limiting the policy's ability to fully leverage learned visual dynamics. The "predict-then-act" paradigm, where images are predicted first and then actions generated, also introduces significant computational latency, making it unsuitable for high-frequency manipulations.

VITA's contribution to this landscape is its direct attempt to **address these two challenges simultaneously** by proposing a unified framework. It moves away from the explicit "predict-then-act" reasoning and instead introduces an "implicit visual Chain-of-Thought," inspired by how humans develop motor intuition without explicitly simulating future visual states. By establishing a shared discrete latent space and co-training vision and action generation, VITA aims to bridge the modality gap, stabilize training, reduce latency, and ultimately enable more robust and generalizable robotic policies. This positions VITA as a significant step towards more integrated and biologically-plausible VLA architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of the VITA project is to significantly advance the development of **general-purpose robotic agents** by overcoming critical limitations in current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. The authors identify several specific motivations and objectives that drive the design of VITA:

1. **Address the Modality Gap**: A primary motivation is to bridge the inherent semantic and structural discrepancy between high-dimensional visual observations (rich in pixel data) and low-level, sparse robotic action commands. Existing methods often struggle to directly align these disparate modalities, leading to inefficiencies and reduced precision.
2. **Mitigate Training Instability**: The competition between optimization objectives for visual prediction (e.g., future frame generation) and action generation tasks in current visual-guided VLAs causes training instability. VITA aims to develop a framework that unifies these objectives, fostering synergistic learning rather than competition.
3. **Reduce Computational Latency**: The "predict-then-act" paradigm, common in many visual CoT approaches, incurs significant computational overhead due to sequential image prediction before action generation. A key objective is to reduce this latency to enable real-time, high-frequency robotic manipulations, aligning more closely with human-like reactive control.
4. **Enhance Spatial Cognition and Dynamic Prediction**: The authors aim to improve VLA models' understanding of complex spatial environments and their ability to anticipate scene dynamics, which is crucial for grounded and robust action generation, especially in long-horizon tasks.
5. **Internalize Visual Chain-of-Thought (CoT)**: Inspired by human motor intuition, VITA seeks to internalize future frame prediction as an inductive bias for action generation, rather than treating it as an explicit, separate sub-task. This means the model learns to anticipate visual changes concurrently with deriving actions, thereby unifying forward and inverse dynamics.
6. **Develop a Scalable and General-Purpose Architecture**: The goal is to create an architecture that can effectively learn from extensive cross-modal data (human videos, robot operation videos) to achieve strong generalization across diverse tasks, environments, and robot embodiments, while discarding motion-irrelevant visual details.

In essence, VITA is motivated by the need for VLA models that are more integrated, efficient, and robust, reflecting a deeper understanding of the interplay between perception and action, similar to biological intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VITA (Vision-Integrated Trajectory Alignment) proposes a unified framework that fundamentally integrates visual perception and action generation through a shared latent space and an implicit visual Chain-of-Thought (CoT). Its methodology can be broken down into three main stages: Warmup, Co-train, and Fine-tune.

#### 3.1. Cross-modal Vector Quantization Framework

To overcome the modality gap between dense visual data and sparse action commands, VITA establishes a **modality-shared discrete latent space** via a vector quantization framework. This involves a shared codebook $C = \{c_k\}_{k=1}^K \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ where both visual and action features are quantized into discrete tokens.

- **Visual Auto-encoder and Quantization Module**: This module processes consecutive video frames to extract **motion-aware representations**. A DINOv2 encoder extracts spatial-temporal features from frames $I_t, I_{t+1}$, which are then concatenated and fed into an M-Former to yield a compact motion embedding $z_v$. This $z_v$ is quantized to $\hat{z}_v$ using the shared codebook. A visual decoder $D_v$ then reconstructs the future frame $\hat{I}_{t+1}$ from $\hat{z}_v$ and $f_t$. The visual reconstruction loss ($L_v$) is a combination of L1 and SSIM loss. Crucially, this quantizer learns rich motion priors from video data without any action supervision during this stage.
- **Action Auto-encoder and Quantization Module**: For action sequences $a_{t:t+H}$, each dimension (position, rotation, gripper force) is normalized. These sequences are then transformed into frequency-domain coefficients using Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT), flattened, and encoded by a lightweight MLP into an action representation $z_a$. This $z_a$ is then quantized to $\hat{z}_a$ using the **same shared codebook $C$**, ensuring semantic alignment with visual tokens. An action decoder $D_a$ (MLP followed by Inverse DCT) reconstructs the continuous action trajectory $\hat{a}_{t:t+H}$ from $\hat{z}_a$. The action encoding and quantization are supervised by an MSE loss ($L_a$).

The shared codebook is the core mechanism for structural consistency in the latent space, enabling implicit alignment without explicit cross-modal paired supervision in the warmup stage.

#### 3.2. VLM Backbone Architecture with Progressive Attention

The VLM backbone, initialized with PaliGemma (Gemma language backbone and SigLIP visual encoder), implements a two-stage reasoning process:

- **Progressive Attention Mechanism**: This mechanism coordinates two reasoning streams (textual and internal CoT). It applies bidirectional attention within input tokens (language instruction, current visual observation) and textual tokens (generated subtasks). A causal attention flow is established: `input -> textual -> cross-modal`, structuring action prediction as collaborative yet decoupled processes.
- **Textual Chain-of-Thought Reasoning**: The VLM backbone first decomposes high-level instructions into a sequence of symbolic subtasks (e.g., [GRASP], [LIFT], [MOVE]). These subtask tokens are generated by the model based on multimodal context but are *not explicitly supervised* with subtask labels during training; their generation is learned indirectly through the overall joint optimization.
- **Visual Chain-of-Thought Generation (Internal CoT)**: The concatenated input tokens and subtask embeddings are used by the VLM backbone to autoregressively generate a sequence of **visual-action hybrid modality latent tokens**. These tokens are indices into the shared codebook $C$. This single stream of tokens is then simultaneously routed to *both* the visual decoder $D_v$ (to predict future images $\hat{I}_{1:T}$) and the action decoder $D_a$ (to generate action trajectories $\hat{a}_{1:H}$). This simultaneous decoding realizes the **implicit visual CoT**: future scene prediction acts as an inductive bias for action generation, internalizing visual dynamics rather than being an explicit intermediate step.

#### 3.3. Progressive Training Regimen

- **Warmup Stage**: Visual and action modules ($E_v, D_v, E_a, D_a$) and the shared codebook $C$ are trained independently on diverse video and action datasets (human videos, robot videos, simulated robot data). No cross-modal paired data is required. This stage builds modality-agnostic latent representations.
- **Co-train Stage**: The VLM backbone, $D_v$, and $D_a$ are trained (with $C$ frozen). A mixed data stream is used:
 - **Video-only data**: If only video observations and instructions are available (no ground-truth actions), only the visual reconstruction loss $L_{co}$ (L1 + SSIM on future frames) is computed.
 - **Synchronized vision-action paired data**: If both are available, the model generates unified tokens, which are simultaneously decoded into future images and action trajectories. The loss $L_{co}$ combines visual reconstruction loss and action generation MSE loss. This mixed-objective approach integrates large-scale unlabeled video data with high-quality paired trajectories, ensuring visual prediction regularizes action generation by distilling task-relevant visual dynamics.
- **Fine-tune Stage**: For specific simulated or real-world tasks, only the action decoder $D_a$ is fine-tuned, while the VLM backbone and codebook $C$ remain frozen. This makes the fine-tuning process efficient and focuses on adapting the action policy.
- **Inference**: During deployment, the visual decoder $D_v$ is discarded (it's primarily for visualization and inductive bias during training), and only the VLM backbone and action decoder $D_a$ are used to generate actions from the hybrid latent tokens, ensuring low-latency operation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VITA demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across a comprehensive suite of simulated and real-world benchmarks, validating its proposed architecture and training methodology.

- **Superior Performance on Simulated Benchmarks**:
 - **CALVIN (ABC-D)**: VITA achieves a groundbreaking average completion rate of 4.73 tasks in a row, significantly surpassing previous baselines like UniVLA (4.41) and UP-VLA (4.08), representing 5.9% and 14.5% improvements respectively. This indicates superior action reasoning and sequential planning capabilities, especially for long-horizon, multi-step tasks.
 - **LIBERO**: VITA outperforms the strongest baseline, UniVLA, across all four challenging LIBERO suites (GOAL, SPATIAL, OBJECT, LONG), achieving an average success rate of 96.7%. On LIBERO-Long, the most challenging long-horizon suite, VITA registers a substantial 36.2% improvement over CoT-VLA, demonstrating enhanced modeling of complex task sequences.
 - **SimplerEnv**: VITA achieves average success rates of 57.4% on SimplerEnv-GoogleRobot (surpassing DeFI's 51.2%) and 71.5% on SimplerEnv-WidowX (outperforming UniVLA's 69.8%). Notably, it makes a significant breakthrough on challenging tasks like "Pick Carrot on Plate," improving from OpenVLA's 20.8% to 68.8%. These results highlight its strong foundation for sim-to-real transfer.

- **Robust Real-World Generalization**:
 - VITA achieves an impressive average success rate of 80.5% across six real-world tasks on a UR-5e robotic arm, significantly outperforming all baselines (e.g., Pi0 at 53.5%, OpenVLA at 43.4%, Octo at 29.8%).
 - Crucially, VITA maintains robust performance on challenging Out-of-Distribution (OOD) tasks ("Inverse Execution" at 66.9%, "Conditional Decision" at 71.3%), where baseline models exhibit marked performance drops (e.g., Octo's 48% decrease). This demonstrates VITA's superior task stability and cross-domain generalization.

- **Effectiveness of Internal CoT**:
 - Ablation studies reveal that VITA's "Textual-Internal CoT" strategy (96.7% on LIBERO, 71.5% on WidowX) substantially outperforms all other CoT variants, including "Textual-only CoT," "Visual-only CoT," and "Textual-Visual CoT." This confirms that internalizing visual prediction as an inductive bias, rather than an explicit prediction target, significantly enhances performance, especially for long-horizon and complex manipulation tasks.

- **Impact of Progressive Training Strategy**:
 - Ablations on training strategy underscore the importance of VITA's "warmup then co-train" regimen. Variants lacking the warmup stage or with partial initializations (e.g., "w/o warmup" at 45.2% on LIBERO) show significantly degraded performance compared to the full VITA model (96.7% on LIBERO), particularly on multi-step tasks. This validates the efficacy of establishing modality-shared representations before joint optimization.

- **Data and Training Efficiency**:
 - VITA demonstrates strong data efficiency, as its model fine-tuned on only 10% of the data surpasses OpenVLA fine-tuned on the full dataset on the CALVIN benchmark. It also achieves convergence in fewer fine-tuning iterations compared to baseline variants.

- **Inference Latency**:
 - VITA exhibits lower inference latency compared to "predict-then-act" paradigms like CoT-VLA. For a prediction horizon of 12, VITA's total latency is 60.6ms, significantly faster than CoT-VLA's 168.2ms. The system achieves an average action-level inference throughput of 60 Hz on an NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU, making it suitable for real-time robotic control.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VITA framework represents a significant advancement in the field of general-purpose robotics, offering a novel and effective approach to Vision-Language-Action (VLA) modeling. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Bridging Fundamental Modality Gaps**: VITA's core contribution lies in its ability to effectively bridge the inherent modality gap between high-dimensional visual observations and low-level robotic actions. By establishing a shared discrete latent space through cross-modal vector quantization, it provides a unified representation that allows for seamless integration of perception and control. This is a foundational step towards more holistic robotic intelligence.

2. **Robust and Stable Learning**: The "warmup then co-training" regimen, coupled with the implicit visual Chain-of-Thought (CoT), addresses the prevalent issues of training instability and competing optimization objectives. By internalizing visual dynamics as an inductive bias rather than an explicit prediction task, VITA enables the model to learn task-relevant visual information more efficiently, leading to more robust and generalizable policies.

3. **Enhanced Generalization and Real-World Applicability**: VITA's demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across diverse simulated benchmarks (CALVIN, LIBERO, SimplerEnv) and, critically, its exceptional performance on challenging real-world tasks, including out-of-distribution (OOD) scenarios, underscore its strong generalization capabilities. This makes VITA a promising candidate for deployment in complex, unstructured real-world environments, accelerating the adoption of generalist robots in various domains such as manufacturing, logistics, and assistive technology.

4. **Efficiency in Data, Training, and Inference**: The framework's ability to achieve high performance with less fine-tuning data and fewer iterations, combined with its significantly lower inference latency compared to "predict-then-act" models, translates into practical advantages. The ~60 Hz action-level inference throughput ensures VITA is viable for real-time and high-frequency robotic manipulations, a crucial requirement for many industrial and service robotics applications.

5. **New Paradigm for VLA Reasoning**: The concept of "implicit visual CoT" offers a novel alternative to explicit reasoning paradigms. By drawing inspiration from human motor intuition, VITA suggests a more integrated approach where perceptual anticipation directly informs motor execution. This could inspire future research into more biologically plausible and efficient architectures for embodied AI.

6. **Scalability through Hybrid Data Leverage**: VITA's progressive training regimen effectively leverages a vast array of hybrid datasets, including human demonstration videos (without action labels) and synchronized robot video-action data. This scalability in data utilization is essential for building powerful foundation models for robotics, enabling learning from the rich, diverse data available on the web.

While the paper acknowledges limitations in fully integrating visual and textual CoT for very complex textual reasoning, and suggests improvements for action sequence tokenization, VITA's successes lay a strong groundwork. Its integrated approach to perception and action, coupled with its robust performance and efficiency, positions it as a significant milestone towards developing truly general-purpose and intelligent robotic systems.
