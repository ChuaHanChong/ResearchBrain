# Occluding the Solution Space: Planner-Agnostic Adversarial Attacks on Tolerance-Aware Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for planner-agnostic adversarial attacks on robotic manipulation motion planning is presented, using strategically placed obstacles to occlude an entire task-level goal region. The method consistently achieves complete planning failure with fewer obstacles than baselines and demonstrates effectiveness in both high-fidelity simulation and real-world robotic deployments.

## Problem

- Existing adversarial attacks on robotic motion planning often rely on exact-pose objectives, which do not reflect the tolerance-aware nature of real-world manipulation tasks where a continuous goal region is acceptable.
- Prior adversarial methods frequently assume unrealistic "planner-in-the-loop" access to the victim planner, limiting their applicability to black-box security assessments.
- There is a need to systematically evaluate and quantify the intrinsic robustness of motion planning algorithms against physical environmental modifications under a more realistic, planner-agnostic threat model.

## Method

- A two-stage, planner-agnostic framework is introduced, comprising an offline ==kinematic occupancy heatmap construction== and an online ==adversarial occupancy maximization== phase.
- The offline stage pre-computes a "robustness prior" by sampling robot configurations and aggregating a heatmap of ==manipulability== and ==clearance-robustness scores== across the workspace.
- The online stage, given a specific task, utilizes a ==greedy algorithm== to strategically place a limited budget of geometric obstacles within a task-relevant corridor to maximize the occlusion of high-density regions in the pre-computed heatmap.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved complete planning failure (**0%** Planning Success Rate) across all **nine** classical planner-scene configurations with a maximum of **5** obstacles, significantly outperforming baselines that required substantially more obstacles.
- High effectiveness was demonstrated against VLA policies (OpenVLA and π_0.5_), with a **3**-obstacle budget reducing OpenVLA's PSR to **0%** and a **5**-obstacle budget achieving **0%** for π_0.5_, without policy-specific knowledge.
- Computational efficiency is a core benefit, with a one-time offline heatmap construction (approx. **1251 seconds**) enabling rapid online attack generation (approx. **1.27 seconds**), providing an amortized advantage over planner-in-the-loop baselines.
- The attack strategy successfully transferred to a real-world Rokae xMatePro7 manipulator, validating the effectiveness of the geometrically derived attack under real-world conditions with sensor noise and execution uncertainties.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adversarial attacks on manipulation tasks are more effective and realistic when targeting the occlusion of an entire feasible goal region, rather than merely obstructing a single path to an exact pose.
> - Effective planner-agnostic attacks can be generated by exploiting intrinsic geometric and kinematic vulnerabilities (spatial bottlenecks) of the robot, without requiring internal knowledge or iterative queries to the victim planner.
> - Kinematic bottlenecks represent a fundamental, shared physical vulnerability for diverse robotic planning approaches, including both classical sampling-based planners and modern end-to-end learned policies like VLAs.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper is situated within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on the robustness of motion planning algorithms to adversarial environmental modifications. Prior investigations into adversarial attacks on robotic systems have largely concentrated on vulnerabilities in perception systems, such as 3D point cloud processing or grasp detection and evaluation. These works often aim to mislead the robot's perception of objects or their manipulability, thereby invalidating the grasp target itself.

In contrast, direct adversarial attacks targeting the *motion planning* phase via environmental constraints are less explored. Existing work in this area has tended to focus on failures induced by corrupted state estimation or exploiting semantic vulnerabilities in high-level vision-language-model (VLM) based agents and vision-language-action (VLA) models. These approaches typically perturb inputs to the system rather than directly challenging the geometric feasibility for the planner.

A closely related study by Wu et al. addressed environmental modifications to disrupt motion planners. However, this prior work is characterized by two limitations: it relies on a planner-in-the-loop approach, which grants the adversary oracle-like access to the victim planner, and it uses rigid exact-pose objectives that do not account for the inherent task-level tolerances in many manipulation scenarios.

This paper positions itself as addressing these limitations by proposing a framework that shifts the evaluation paradigm from exact-pose objectives to task-level feasibility over goal regions, acknowledging that successful manipulation often involves reaching a continuous manifold of valid configurations rather than a single point. Furthermore, it introduces a planner-agnostic threat model, where the adversary operates without direct knowledge of or access to the victim's internal planning logic. This work thus contributes to the underexplored area of evaluating and quantifying the intrinsic robustness of planning algorithms against strategically placed physical obstacles, under a more realistic and generalizable adversarial model.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to evaluate and quantify the intrinsic robustness of robotic manipulation motion planning systems against adversarial environmental modifications. The authors identify two critical limitations in existing approaches to adversarial attacks on motion planning that motivate their work:

1. **Restrictive Exact-Pose Objectives**: Current adversarial evaluations commonly enforce highly restrictive success criteria, such as requiring the robot to track specific end-effector pose targets within tight tolerances. The authors argue that this perspective confounds true task-level feasibility. In many practical manipulation tasks, operational success depends on reaching *any* configuration within a feasible goal *region* (e.g., a continuous grasp manifold), rather than a singular, exact pose. A planning failure, from a task perspective, occurs only if the robot cannot reach any part of this acceptable goal region. Consequently, an effective adversarial attack should aim to occlude this entire solution space, rather than merely obstructing a singular path that might lead to an exact pose.

2. **Reliance on Planner-in-the-Loop Queries**: Many existing adversarial approaches, particularly those directly perturbing planning, assume an adversary with unrealistic oracle-like access to the victim planner. This means the adversary can iteratively query the target planning system to optimize its attack. This dependency renders the threat model impractical for black-box security assessments, where internal system access is not available. The authors contend that a practical adversarial framework must be planner-agnostic, meaning it should operate with limited information about the victim system, typically only public robot kinematics and static scene observability.

To address these limitations, the paper sets out to achieve the following key objectives:

- **Formulate a Task-Level Adversarial Threat Model**: Develop a threat model for tolerance-aware manipulation where the attack objective shifts from targeting exact-pose configurations to occluding the entire feasible goal region, thus reflecting real-world task requirements.
- **Propose a Planner-Agnostic Attack Framework**: Develop a two-stage methodology that efficiently generates geometric constraints (adversarial obstacles) without requiring iterative queries to the victim planner or knowledge of its internal logic. This framework aims to exploit intrinsic geometric vulnerabilities rather than over-fitting to a specific algorithm.
- **Characterize Robot Capabilities Offline**: Create a mechanism to understand the robot's intrinsic workspace capabilities and identify critical spatial bottlenecks, encoding the density of valid manipulation trajectories and robustness priors without invoking a specific planner.
- **Optimize Obstacle Placement Online**: Develop an efficient method for strategically deploying a limited budget of obstacles, subject to explicit geometric constraints, to maximize the occlusion of the task-level solution manifold.
- **Validate Efficacy and Efficiency**: Demonstrate that the proposed method reliably induces planning failures, performs effectively across diverse simulation and real-world scenarios, and is computationally efficient compared to planner-in-the-loop baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method is a two-stage, planner-agnostic framework designed to generate adversarial obstacles that occlude the most critical workspace volume for tolerance-aware manipulation.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The work considers motion planning for a manipulator to reach a continuous goal region G ⊂ SE(3), which induces a feasible goal set Q_G in joint space. The planning task is to find a collision-free path from an initial configuration q_init to any q_end ∈ Q_G.
The adversary's objective is to force a task failure by strategically inserting a set of M obstacles, O(θ) = {o_1(θ_1)..., o_M(θ_M)}, into the environment E, resulting in a perturbed environment E_θ := E ∪ O(θ). An attack is successful if no valid path can be found in E_θ.
Insertion constraints are defined:
- **Cardinality Budget (C1)**: A maximum of M obstacles.
- **Geometric Bounds (C2)**: Obstacles are standard geometric primitives (e.g., boxes, spheres) with a maximum size.
- **Placement Feasibility (C3)**: Obstacles must be within a valid search region S ⊂ E_free and maintain a minimum clearance from q_init and G to prevent trivial infeasibility.
The adversary's capabilities are restricted:
- **Known Kinematics (A1)**: Access to the robot's kinematic model and collision geometry.
- **Scene Observability (A2)**: Perception of the nominal scene E and task specification G.
- **No Oracle Access (A3)**: Cannot query the victim planner or simulate the full planning process during attack generation.

#### 3.2. Offline: Kinematic Occupancy Heatmap Construction

This stage aims to characterize the robot's intrinsic capabilities and identify "spatial bottlenecks" in the workspace.
1. **Workspace Voxelization**: The 3D workspace is discretized into a voxel grid V with a resolution of δ (e.g., 0.02 m). Voxels not occupied by static geometry (E) form V_free. A heatmap H is initialized to zero for all v ∈ V_free.
2. **Robust Configuration Sampling**: A large set of collision-free joint configurations q are sampled (e.g., N_off = 15,000). Each valid sample is assigned a weight w(q) based on two quality metrics:
 - **Manipulability Score (s_man)**: Based on the Yoshikawa index, m(q) = sqrt(det(J(q)J(q)^T)), where J(q) is the geometric Jacobian. This score is saturated (s_man(q) = m(q)/(m(q) + κ) with κ = 0.01) to prioritize regions where the robot has high control authority, identifying configurations far from kinematic singularities.
 - **Clearance-Robustness Score (s_clr)**: Computes the minimum distance between the robot's occupied volume B(q) and static obstacles E, normalized by a safety margin d_safe (s_clr(q) = min(d_clr(q)/d_safe, 1) with d_safe = 0.1 m). This prioritizes configurations in open, high-clearance areas.
 The combined weight is w(q) = s_man(q)^γ1 * s_clr(q)^γ2, with γ1 = γ2 = 1.
3. **Swept-Volume Aggregation**: For each weighted sample q, the volume occupied by the robot's kinematic links, B(q), is projected onto the voxel grid. The weight w(q) is accumulated into H(v) for all voxels v ∈ Voxelize(B(q)) ∩ V_free. The resulting heatmap H represents the density of robust trajectories within the workspace. The discrete workspace volume V is defined by the bounding box of the robot’s swept volume across valid samples, with padding.

#### 3.3. Online: Adversarial Occupancy Maximization

In this stage, given q_init and G for a specific task, the adversary places obstacles.
1. **Task-Relevant Candidate Selection**: A candidate set X_cand for obstacle placement is defined. This set is restricted to a task-aligned corridor connecting the start position (x_start) to the goal region (G) with a radius r_corr (e.g., 0.3 m). Voxels within d_safe (e.g., 0.1 m) of q_init or G are pruned to avoid trivial attack scenarios (Constraint C3).
2. **Budgeted Maximum-Coverage Optimization**: Obstacle placement is formulated as a submodular maximization problem. The objective is to select a set of M obstacle centers C ⊆ X_cand such that the sum of heatmap values H(v) for all voxels v covered by obstacles centered at c ∈ C is maximized. Constraints include the obstacle budget |C| ≤ M and a minimum separation d_min (e.g., 0.05 m) between obstacle centers to ensure spatial diversity.
3. **Greedy Attack Generation**: Since the optimization is NP-hard, a greedy algorithm (Algorithm 1) is employed, which provides a (1 − 1/e) approximation. In each iteration, the algorithm selects the candidate center c* that maximizes the marginal heatmap coverage of previously uncovered voxels. An obstacle (Primitive(c*, s) with s = 0.05 m) is added to the set, the covered voxels are updated, and candidate centers too close to c* are removed to enforce spatial diversity. This iterative process continues until the budget M is exhausted.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates the proposed planner-agnostic adversarial framework in both high-fidelity simulation and real-robot deployments using a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda (simulation) and a Rokae xMatePro7 (real-world).

#### 4.1. Attack Performance Against Classical Planners (RQ1)

- **Overall Effectiveness**: The proposed method consistently resulted in lower Planning Success Rates (PSR) across all tested scene-planner combinations (RRT, PRM*, BKPIECE) compared to baselines (Random, PFA, PFA++). This advantage became more pronounced with an increased obstacle budget.
- **Budget Impact**: At a budget of 1 obstacle, the method showed improvements in difficult settings (e.g., reducing PRM* PSR in Scene 3 to 0.712 from near 1.0 for baselines). With 3 obstacles, its advantage was amplified, and with 5 obstacles, the method achieved complete occlusion (PSR = 0) across all nine scene-planner configurations. Baselines showed notable residual success even at higher budgets.
- **Plan Quality Degradation**: Before complete planning failure (PSR = 0), the method consistently induced degradation in the quality of successful plans. This was evidenced by:
 - **Decreased Clearance Margin (CMR)**: Indicating remaining feasible paths were narrower.
 - **Decreased Singularity Margin (SMR)**: Suggesting planners were forced into less dexterous, near-singular postures.
 - **Increased Joint Deviation Score (JDS)**: Implying longer and more complex detours.
 - **Increased Planning Time (PT)**: Reflecting harder search scenarios.

#### 4.2. Attack Efficiency on Classical Planners (RQ2)

- **Obstacle Budget for Failure**: The method required substantially fewer obstacles to achieve specified PSR thresholds (e.g., 80%, 60%, 0%) compared to baselines. For instance, to completely block RRT in Scene 1 (PSR = 0%), the proposed method required 4.1 obstacles, whereas Random needed 50.8 and PFA needed 12.4. Across all tested configurations, the method never required more than 5 obstacles to achieve absolute planning failure. This suggests that obstacles placed based on kinematic occupancy can effectively paralyze the manipulator with a limited budget.

#### 4.3. Transferability to VLA-based Policies (RQ3)

- **Black-Box Effectiveness**: The method demonstrated high effectiveness against two VLA policies, OpenVLA and π_0.5_, even though obstacle generation was performed without access to the policies' network architecture, training data, or gradients, relying solely on robot kinematic occupancy.
- **Performance Comparison**: For OpenVLA, a single obstacle reduced the PSR to 0.340 (comparable to PFA's 0.364), and three obstacles achieved complete occlusion (PSR=0), outperforming PFA (0.188 residual success). For π_0.5_, a 3-obstacle budget yielded a PSR of 0.142, which was lower than PFA (0.426) and PFA++ (0.390). Complete occlusion was achieved with 5 obstacles.
- **Shared Vulnerability**: These results suggest that kinematic bottlenecks represent a shared physical vulnerability across both classical sampling-based planners and end-to-end learned policies, as both operate within the same geometric and kinematic feasibility constraints.
- **Qualitative Failure Modes**: VLA policies exhibited progressive, cascading failures (slowdown, hesitation, contact, oscillation, deadlock) when the task became infeasible, distinct from the explicit failure returns of classical planners. This indicates a potential lack of explicit geometric feasibility reasoning in current VLA policies under adversarial occlusion.

#### 4.4. Computational Efficiency (RQ4)

- **Offline/Online Paradigm**: The framework shifts the main computational burden to a one-time offline heatmap construction, which took approximately 1251 seconds. This enabled rapid online attack generation, requiring approximately 1.27 seconds.
- **Comparison to Baselines**: This contrasts with planner-in-the-loop attacks like PFA, which incurred their entire computational cost (around 1224 seconds) during the online phase for each task query. PFA++ could be even more computationally intensive (e.g., ~3978 seconds with PRM*). The one-time offline computation provides an amortized advantage, allowing the heatmap to be reused for diverse tasks and goal regions on the same robotic platform.

#### 4.5. Real-World Robotic Deployments (RQ5)

- **Sim-to-Real Transfer**: The framework's attack strategy was successfully deployed on a physical Rokae xMatePro7 manipulator. The planning scene was constructed from real-world RGB-D sensing, and adversarial obstacles were physically instantiated as rigidly mounted box primitives.
- **Consistent Behavior**: The physical deployments showed results consistent with simulations. A single obstacle forced the planner into suboptimal, narrower paths, increasing complexity. With increased obstacle budgets (three and five), the planner consistently failed to find a feasible solution within the allocated time and safely aborted. This validated the effectiveness of the geometrically derived attack in real-world conditions, accounting for sensor noise and execution uncertainties.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces a framework for adversarial attacks on robotic manipulation planning that addresses limitations in existing approaches, contributing to the field in several ways:

- **Realistic Threat Model**: The proposed planner-agnostic and tolerance-aware threat model offers a more realistic and generalizable approach to evaluating the robustness of robotic manipulation systems. By considering task-level feasibility over goal regions and operating without oracle access to victim planners, the framework provides a more practical method for black-box security assessments.
- **Enhanced Robustness Evaluation**: The framework provides a systematic and efficient method for quantifying the intrinsic robustness of motion planning algorithms. By effectively occluding the solution space with a minimal number of obstacles, it exposes fundamental vulnerabilities that are independent of specific planner implementations.
- **Diagnostic Capabilities**: Beyond generating attacks, the framework can serve as a diagnostic tool. The kinematic occupancy heatmap and the resulting obstacle placements can identify critical workspace regions where motion feasibility is concentrated and where it rapidly collapses under bounded perturbations. This information can provide concrete signals for developers to improve the robustness of robot planning systems.
- **Broad Applicability**: The planner-agnostic nature and computational efficiency of the online attack generation make the framework suitable for large-scale robustness benchmarking across various planning algorithms, including both classical sampling-based planners and emerging end-to-end learned policies. Its amortized computational advantage, stemming from the one-time offline computation, supports near real-time safety evaluations.
- **Identification of Shared Vulnerabilities**: The findings demonstrate that kinematic bottlenecks constitute a fundamental, shared physical vulnerability for diverse robotic planning approaches, regardless of whether they are classical or learning-based. This insight is significant for developing more robust and secure robotic systems.

In summary, this work advances the understanding and evaluation of robotic manipulation system robustness, offering both an effective attack methodology and a diagnostic tool with broad implications for robot safety and security. Future work is suggested to extend the framework to dynamic environments.
