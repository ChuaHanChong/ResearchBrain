# TaF-VLA: Tactile-Force Alignment in Vision-Language-Action Models for Force-aware Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Tactile-Force Alignment in Vision-Language-Action (TaF-VLA) grounds high-resolution tactile data directly in physical interaction forces, overcoming the traditional 'force-blindness' of VLA models. This approach achieved an average success rate of 64.8% across contact-rich manipulation tasks, substantially outperforming vision-only baselines which reached 37.1%.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while generalist, are 'force-blind' due to their reliance on visual modalities, limiting their capability in contact-rich tasks requiring precise force regulation.
- Existing methods integrating tactile sensors often treat tactile inputs as mere auxiliary visual textures, failing to extract the critical contact force information necessary for physically-aware manipulation.
- A scarcity of large-scale, synchronized visuotactile and ground-truth force datasets hinders the training of models that can align these distinct modalities for physically intelligent robots.

## Method

- Developed an automated TaF-Device to construct a TaF-Dataset of over 10 million synchronized tactile-force pairs, capturing diverse contact dynamics across multiple Vision-based Tactile Sensors (VBTSs).
- Introduced the TaF-Adapter, which implicitly aligns tactile sequences with force signals in a shared latent space using a Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) and temporal encoding to capture history-dependent dynamics.
- Integrated the pre-trained and frozen TaF-Adapter into a VLA backbone by interleaving the force-aligned tactile representations with global visual-language features and proprioception to condition action generation.

## Results

- TaF-VLA achieved an average success rate of 64.8% on contact-rich manipulation tasks, nearly doubling the performance of vision-only baselines (37.1%) and significantly outperforming tactile-vision-aligned methods (42.8%).
- The TaF-Adapter demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization to unseen tactile sensors, maintaining a 60.3% success rate, and proved to be a plug-and-play module that improved existing robotic policies (ACT and Diffusion Policy) by 6.7% to 33.3%.
- TaF-VLA showed improved data efficiency, matching or surpassing baseline performance with significantly fewer training demonstrations (100 episodes compared to the baseline's 200 episodes).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly grounding tactile observations in physical interaction forces, rather than simply aligning tactile with visual data, is critical for enabling force-aware manipulation in VLA models.
> - Incorporating temporal context into tactile encoding is essential for distinguishing nuanced interaction states, such as static deformation versus incipient slip, enhancing robustness.
> - An implicit latent alignment strategy with discrete force representations provides greater robustness to sensor variations and noise compared to explicit force regression, facilitating cross-sensor generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The current research stands at the intersection of several rapidly evolving fields in robotics and artificial intelligence: Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, tactile sensing, and physically-grounded AI.

**VLA Models and their "Force-Blindness":** Recent years have seen significant advancements in VLA models, exemplified by architectures like RT-1, RT-2, and π
0.5, which leverage large-scale visual and language representations to enable generalist robotic manipulation. These models excel at understanding high-level instructions and visual scenes, demonstrating impressive generalization across diverse tasks. However, a critical limitation, termed "force-blindness" by the authors, persists: their predominant reliance on visual modalities inherently lacks the physical intuition necessary for contact-rich tasks requiring precise force regulation, physical reasoning, and interaction with delicate or deformable objects. Visual observations are susceptible to occlusion and cannot directly perceive interaction forces, friction, or compliance, which are vital for robust and safe manipulation.

**Bridging the Sensory Gap with Tactile Sensing:**
- **Global Force Feedback:** Previous attempts to address the physical gap have often incorporated global force feedback from wrist-mounted Force/Torque (F/T) sensors or joint torque sensors. While useful for general load measurement, these sensors are expensive, fragile, and provide low-dimensional, spatially aggregated data. They fail to capture the detailed, distributed pressure patterns and local contact geometry crucial for nuanced interactions.
- **Vision-based Tactile Sensors (VBTSs):** VBTSs have emerged as a powerful alternative, offering high-resolution, dense feedback on surface deformation, geometry, texture, local pressure distribution, and slippage. Their visual nature makes them highly compatible with modern vision-based learning methods.
- **Limitations of Prior Tactile Integration:** Existing approaches to integrate VBTS into VLA architectures typically treat tactile inputs as mere "auxiliary visual textures," aligning them with language and scene images in a "tactile-vision alignment" paradigm. The paper argues that this strategy overlooks the unique mechanical interaction nature of tactile sensing, failing to extract the critical contact force information for force-aware manipulation. Without explicit grounding in physical forces, these models cannot truly understand *how* an object is being grasped or manipulated dynamically.

**The Proposed Paradigm Shift:** This paper proposes a fundamental "paradigm shift from tactile-vision alignment to tactile-force alignment." It aims to explicitly ground high-dimensional tactile observations in physical interaction forces. This distinguishes it from prior work by:
1. **Explicit Force Grounding:** Moving beyond treating tactile data as just "more vision" to directly connecting tactile representations with physical force measurements.
2. **Temporal Dynamics:** Building upon insights from force estimation literature that emphasize the history-dependent nature of contact (e.g., viscoelastic hysteresis, slip), the paper integrates temporal modeling into tactile encoding.
3. **Generalist Policy Integration:** Synthesizing these advancements into a unified framework for generalist VLA policy learning, enabling force-aware control across a wide array of tasks.

By addressing the scarcity of aligned tactile-force data, the representation disparity between visual-tactile and force signals, and the architectural challenges of integrating such multi-modal information into VLA backbones, this work seeks to push the boundaries of physically intelligent robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **overcome the inherent "force-blindness" of current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and enable robust, force-aware manipulation in contact-rich tasks.** The authors argue that while VLA models have made significant strides as generalists in robotic manipulation, their over-reliance on visual modalities fundamentally limits their ability to interact effectively with the physical world in scenarios demanding precise force regulation or an understanding of interaction dynamics.

This overarching goal is motivated by several critical observations and challenges:

- **Inadequacy of Vision-Only Policies:** For tasks involving fragile objects, precision assembly, or deformable materials, visual information alone is insufficient. Vision cannot perceive direct contact forces, friction, or compliance, and suffers from occlusion, leading to failures in force-sensitive scenarios (e.g., dropping objects due to insufficient grip force, damaging fragile items, or misaligning parts).
- **Limitations of Global Force Sensors:** While wrist-mounted F/T sensors provide total interaction load, they are expensive, fragile, difficult to scale, and critically, provide low-dimensional, spatially aggregated data. This aggregated information loses the detailed, localized pressure patterns and contact geometry that are essential for truly understanding complex manipulation.
- **Misinterpretation of Tactile Data in Prior Work:** Vision-based Tactile Sensors (VBTSs) offer high-resolution feedback on local mechanical interactions. However, existing VLA models often treat tactile inputs as merely "auxiliary visual textures," attempting a "tactile-vision alignment." The authors argue that this approach fundamentally misunderstands the modality of touch, failing to extract the crucial contact force information that tactile data intrinsically represents. This semantic gap prevents policies from developing a true understanding of physical dynamics.

To address these motivations, the paper sets out to tackle three fundamental challenges:

- **Q1: Scarcity of Tactile Data for Alignment:** VLA models require vast datasets. There is a severe lack of large-scale datasets providing synchronized visuotactile and ground-truth force data, which is essential for training models to align these distinct modalities.
- **Q2: Representation Disparity:** Bridging the gap between high-dimensional, geometric visuotactile inputs and low-dimensional, dynamic force signals is non-trivial. The design choice is whether to pursue explicit force regression (which might lack cross-sensor generalization) or implicit latent alignment, and how to construct a robust representation that captures history-dependent dynamics while being insensitive to sensor noise and hardware variations.
- **Q3: Policy Integration:** Architecturally, how can this novel tactile-force-aligned representation be effectively integrated into large-scale VLA backbones without causing modality collapse, diluting the physical signal, or disrupting the existing visual-language reasoning capabilities?

By resolving these challenges, the authors aim to empower VLA models with a genuine "physical intuition," enabling them to perform robust, force-aware manipulation through cross-modal physical reasoning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The development of TaF-VLA proceeds through three interconnected stages, addressing the identified challenges systematically.

#### 3.1. Tactile-Force Data Acquisition Device and TaF-Dataset (Addressing Q1)

To overcome the scarcity of synchronized tactile-force data, the authors designed and built an **automated data acquisition device, the TaF-Device**.
- **Design Principle:** The device features a parallel actuation structure that ensures identical forces are applied simultaneously to both the tactile sensor and ground-truth force sensors. This kinematic coherence is achieved by linking corresponding Degrees of Freedom (DoFs) with identical motor-gear pairs, eliminating relative phase errors and ensuring precise spatial and temporal alignment of sensor readings. The chassis is 3D-printed, modular, and utilizes off-the-shelf components for ease of fabrication and replication.
- **System Architecture:** It comprises a compact structural chassis, five Dynamixel servomotors for actuation, and a custom gear-driven transmission mechanism. The transmission generates Z-axis translation, Yaw rotation, and Roll/Pitch inclinations. A compliant element in the Z-axis prevents overload. The system integrates three sensing modalities: multiple Vision-based Tactile Sensors (VBTSs, including custom-built and GelSight Mini), an ATI Axia80 6-axis Force/Torque (F/T) sensor, and a 12x12 piezoresistive matrix force sensor. Data collection is synchronized to the VBTS's 30 Hz frequency.
- **TaF-Dataset Construction:**
 - **Automated Protocol:** The device performs a "press-and-perturb" strategy, dynamically varying Z-axis pressure while simultaneously driving Pitch, Roll, and Yaw motors. This generates continuous, diverse contact trajectories, efficiently exploring the full state space from static indentation to incipient slip. Human intervention is only required for swapping indenters and VBTS units.
 - **Contact Diversity:** To maximize robustness, the dataset incorporates: 1) modulated Z-axis torque for continuous pressure variations; 2) randomized rotational velocities for intricate shear forces and torsional moments; and 3) a diverse library of over 60 distinct indenters (rigid, deformable, varied patterns and curvatures) to generalize deformation features.
 - **Scale:** The **TaF-Dataset** is a large-scale real-world multi-modal repository, comprising over **10 million synchronized tactile-force pairs** collected across six distinct VBTS units.
- **Force-aware Manipulation Dataset Construction:** In addition to the TaF-Dataset for alignment pre-training, a separate dataset of over 10,000 force-aware episodes across 20+ diverse scenarios (fragile object handling, tool use, dynamic surface interaction) was curated for VLA policy training. This dataset was collected using a high-fidelity master-puppet teleoperation system and augmented with force-grounded language annotations generated by an LLM-based prompting framework.

#### 3.2. TaF-Adapter: Tactile-Force Alignment (Addressing Q2)

The **TaF-Adapter** is the core component bridging the representation gap. It proposes an implicit alignment strategy, mapping tactile sequences and force signals into a shared latent space, a paradigm shift from direct force regression or tactile-vision alignment.
- **Force Quantization:** Instead of continuous regression, force and pressure signals are quantized using a **Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE)**. Two separate MLP encoders project sequences of spatial pressure maps (12x12) and 6-axis F/T vectors into latent spaces. These are then quantized to their nearest neighbors in two dedicated, learnable codebooks (C' for pressure, C'' for F/T). This discretization mitigates high-frequency sensor noise and captures compact, semantically interpretable force primitives. A reconstruction mechanism (decoders gψ', gψ'') prevents codebook collapse by enforcing signal recovery from quantized embeddings.
- **Temporal Tactile Encoding:** For tactile inputs, a sequence of tactile images ({I_tac}) from a receding horizon (N=5) is processed. A Vision Transformer (ViT) extracts per-frame features, which are then fed into a causal Transformer encoder along with a learnable summary token (s). The output embedding corresponding to (s) serves as the holistic representation of the temporal tactile event, capturing history-dependent dynamics crucial for distinguishing states like static deformation from incipient slip.
- **Cross-Modal Alignment:** A symmetric **InfoNCE objective** is used for contrastive learning. This objective aligns the tactile representation (z*) from the tactile encoder with its corresponding ground-truth quantized force code (c*) in a shared latent space. It maximizes similarity between positive pairs while minimizing similarity with negative (unpaired) force codes from other time steps.
- **Total Loss:** The TaF-Adapter is trained with a combined loss: L_total = L_NCE + λ1 * L_quant + λ2 * L_recon.

#### 3.3. Force-aware VLA Policy Integration (TaF-VLA, Addressing Q3)

The pre-trained and *frozen* TaF-Adapter is then seamlessly integrated into a state-of-the-art VLA backbone to form the **TaF-VLA policy**.
- **VLA Backbone:** The authors build upon the π
0.5
architecture, a flow-matching-based generalist policy.
- **Integration:** The VLA backbone receives global visual inputs (third-person image I_global, wrist-mounted image I_wrist) and a language instruction (l), which are processed by a VLM to produce a unified conditioning vector (φ). Simultaneously, the TaF-Adapter processes the current sliding window of VBTS tactile signals to extract the force-aligned tactile latent representation (z_tac).
- **Action Generation:** These force-aligned tactile tokens (z_tac) are interleaved with the global visual–language features (φ) and robot proprioception (q_t) to condition the VLA's action expert. The policy then uses a flow-matching objective to generate end-effector actions (a_t), effectively conditioning its execution on both semantic instructions and real-time tactile feedback. This design enables the model to adjust manipulation behaviors in real-time based on local contact dynamics while adhering to high-level task directives.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive real-world experiments demonstrate that TaF-VLA significantly advances force-aware robotic manipulation.

- **1. Performance Benchmarking (E1): TaF-VLA Outperforms Baselines on Contact-Rich Tasks**
 - TaF-VLA achieved an average success rate of **64.8%** across seven force-critical daily manipulation tasks. This represents a substantial improvement, roughly doubling the performance of state-of-the-art vision-only baselines (e.g., π
0.5
at 37.1%).
 - It also significantly outperformed existing tactile-vision-aligned methods like FreeTacMan (42.8%) by an average of **22 percentage points**.
 - The performance gains were most pronounced in tasks requiring dynamic force modulation or precise contact regulation, such as "Tweezer Weight Pick" (TaF-VLA: 33.3%; vision-only baselines: 0%), "Power Bank Extraction" (TaF-VLA: 66.7%), "Whiteboard Erasing" (TaF-VLA: 60.0%), and "Jelly Slicing" (TaF-VLA: 53.3%). In these scenarios, vision-only policies frequently failed due to an inability to infer stiffness, friction, or micro-slip cues.
 - For tasks primarily dominated by geometric constraints (e.g., "Tube Insertion"), where visual servoing is largely sufficient, the performance gap was smaller, but TaF-VLA still showed improvements.

- **2. Cross-Sensor Robustness and Plug-and-Play Capability (E2):**
 - **Cross-Sensor Generalization:** The TaF-Adapter exhibited strong zero-shot generalization to *unseen* VBTS units (sensors not encountered during pre-training). For instance, on an unseen "Custom-built B" sensor, TaF-VLA maintained a robust mean success rate of 60.3%, whereas an explicit force prediction baseline suffered a catastrophic drop to 30.0%. This suggests that implicit latent alignment, combined with discrete representations, is more robust to sensor variations than explicit force regression.
 - **Plug-and-Play:** Integrating the *pre-trained* TaF-Adapter into existing policies (ACT and Diffusion Policy) consistently led to performance gains (ranging from 6.7% to 33.3% absolute improvement), demonstrating its generality as a module for injecting physical tactile intuition. ACT + TaF-Adapter even surpassed the dedicated tactile-vision-aligned FreeTacMan in force-critical scenarios.

- **3. Pre-trained TaF-Adapter Improves Data Efficiency (E3):**
 - TaF-VLA achieved equivalent or superior performance using significantly less training data compared to the vision-only baseline (π
0.5
). For example, TaF-VLA matched π
0.5
's best performance with only **100 demonstrations** while the baseline required **200 episodes**. This indicates that the force-aligned tactile codebook provides valuable structured physical priors, reducing the need for extensive visual-language-action demonstrations.

- **4. Ablation Studies (E4): Validation of Design Choices:**
 - **Necessity of Temporal Context ("No History"):** Reducing the receding horizon window from N=5 to N=1 (single frame) led to substantial performance degradation (e.g., "Whiteboard Erasing" dropped from 60.0% to 26.7%). This confirms that capturing history-dependent dynamics is crucial for disambiguating interaction states like incipient slip.
 - **Impact of Codebook Capacity ("Small Codebook"):** Reducing the VQ-VAE codebook size decreased success rates, demonstrating that sufficient capacity is needed to preserve representational granularity and distinguish fine-grained interaction states.
 - **Benefits of Decoupled Modalities ("Shared Codebook"):** Forcing both pressure maps and 6-DoF force vectors into a single shared codebook performed poorly, underscoring that these modalities have fundamentally different latent distributions and require separate encoding for optimal performance.
 - **Quality of Tactile Data ("w/ Noise"):** Artificially corrupting ground-truth force labels with Gaussian noise significantly degraded performance, highlighting the critical importance of a stable and low-disturbance data acquisition pipeline (like the TaF-Device) for learning meaningful alignments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The TaF-VLA framework represents a significant step forward in the field of robotic manipulation, offering several key contributions and promising future impacts:

**1. Bridging the Physical-Semantic Gap in VLA Models:** This work fundamentally addresses the "force-blindness" of current VLA models. By explicitly grounding high-dimensional tactile observations in physical interaction forces, TaF-VLA enables robots to move beyond purely semantic understanding to acquire genuine physical intuition. This is crucial for expanding the capabilities of generalist robots into real-world scenarios where physical interaction is paramount.

**2. Enabling Robust Force-Aware Manipulation:** TaF-VLA empowers robots to perform a wider array of complex, delicate, and safety-critical tasks that have historically been challenging for vision-only systems. Examples include handling fragile objects, precision assembly, deformable object manipulation, and tool use where fine-grained force regulation is essential. This capability has immense potential for applications in manufacturing, healthcare (e.g., surgery, delicate patient handling), and service robotics.

**3. Generalizable Tactile Perception and Plug-and-Play Integration:** The design of the TaF-Adapter, particularly its implicit latent alignment with discrete representations and temporal context, fosters strong cross-sensor generalization. This "hardware-agnostic" approach makes tactile perception more practical for diverse robotic deployments, reducing the need for extensive re-calibration or re-training for every new sensor. Its demonstrated "plug-and-play" capability allows easy integration into existing VLA frameworks, making it a valuable module for enhancing current robotic policies.

**4. Scalable and High-Fidelity Data Resource:** The development of the automated TaF-Device and the resulting TaF-Dataset (over 10 million synchronized tactile-force pairs) addresses a critical bottleneck in physically-grounded AI research. This large-scale, high-fidelity multimodal dataset serves as a valuable public resource, enabling further research and development in tactile representation learning and robot control.

### 6. Advancing Physically-Grounded AI and Future Research Directions

 - The explicit use of force-aware language instructions pushes the frontier of language-conditioned manipulation, allowing for fine-grained control over physical interactions that were previously intractable.
 - The insights into the importance of temporal context and decoupled discrete force representations for robustness are crucial for designing future tactile-aware systems.
 - The limitations identified by the authors (e.g., pre-contact errors, inference frequency, generalization to highly different sensor modalities, and architectural designs for deeper tactile integration) open clear avenues for future research, such as developing faster reactive control loops, exploring alternative sensor technologies, and unifying vision-tactile-language-action backbones for more holistic environmental understanding.

In conclusion, TaF-VLA represents a significant methodological and empirical contribution to robot learning. By effectively bridging the gap between high-level visual-language understanding and low-level physical interaction, it brings generalist robots closer to human-like dexterity and intuition in manipulating the physical world, laying a strong foundation for future advancements in embodied artificial intelligence.
