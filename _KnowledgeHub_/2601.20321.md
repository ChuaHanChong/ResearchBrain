# TaF-VLA: Tactile-Force Alignment in Vision-Language-Action Models for Force-aware Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Tactile-Force Alignment in Vision-Language-Action (TaF-VLA) grounds high-resolution tactile data directly in physical interaction forces, overcoming the traditional 'force-blindness' of VLA models. This approach achieved an average success rate of 64.8% across contact-rich manipulation tasks, substantially outperforming vision-only baselines which reached 37.1%.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, while generalist, are 'force-blind' due to their reliance on visual modalities, limiting their capability in contact-rich tasks requiring precise force regulation.
- Existing methods integrating tactile sensors often treat tactile inputs as mere auxiliary visual textures, failing to extract the critical contact force information necessary for physically-aware manipulation.
- A scarcity of large-scale, synchronized visuotactile and ground-truth force datasets hinders the training of models that can align these distinct modalities for physically intelligent robots.

## Method

- Developed an automated TaF-Device to construct a TaF-Dataset of over 10 million synchronized tactile-force pairs, capturing diverse contact dynamics across multiple Vision-based Tactile Sensors (VBTSs).
- Introduced the TaF-Adapter, which implicitly aligns tactile sequences with force signals in a shared latent space using a Vector Quantized Variational Autoencoder (VQ-VAE) and temporal encoding to capture history-dependent dynamics.
- Integrated the pre-trained and frozen TaF-Adapter into a VLA backbone by interleaving the force-aligned tactile representations with global visual-language features and proprioception to condition action generation.

## Results

- TaF-VLA achieved an average success rate of 64.8% on contact-rich manipulation tasks, nearly doubling the performance of vision-only baselines (37.1%) and significantly outperforming tactile-vision-aligned methods (42.8%).
- The TaF-Adapter demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization to unseen tactile sensors, maintaining a 60.3% success rate, and proved to be a plug-and-play module that improved existing robotic policies (ACT and Diffusion Policy) by 6.7% to 33.3%.
- TaF-VLA showed improved data efficiency, matching or surpassing baseline performance with significantly fewer training demonstrations (100 episodes compared to the baseline's 200 episodes).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly grounding tactile observations in physical interaction forces, rather than simply aligning tactile with visual data, is critical for enabling force-aware manipulation in VLA models.
> - Incorporating temporal context into tactile encoding is essential for distinguishing nuanced interaction states, such as static deformation versus incipient slip, enhancing robustness.
> - An implicit latent alignment strategy with discrete force representations provides greater robustness to sensor variations and noise compared to explicit force regression, facilitating cross-sensor generalization.
