# Efficient Vocabulary-Free Fine-Grained Visual Recognition in the Age of Multimodal LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel framework called NeaR enables efficient vocabulary-free fine-grained visual recognition by combining MLLM-generated labels with nearest-neighbor refinement and CLIP model fine-tuning, achieving comparable or better performance than direct MLLM inference while significantly reducing computational costs and inference time across multiple benchmark datasets.

## Problem

- Direct use of MLLMs for fine-grained visual recognition is computationally expensive and time-consuming
- Traditional FGVR methods require large expertly annotated datasets which are often unavailable
- Existing approaches struggle with vocabulary-free settings where categories aren't predefined

## Method

- Generate initial labels using ==MLLMs on a small training set==
- Refine labels using ==K-nearest neighbors in CLIP embedding space== and ==GMM-based noise detection==
- ==Fine-tune CLIP model== using refined labels, with ==label filtering== applied to reduce complexity and vocabulary size at inference time

## Results

- Outperforms direct MLLM inference and baseline methods across multiple datasets
- Achieves significant reduction in computational costs compared to direct MLLM queries
- Shows robustness across different MLLMs (GPT-4V, GeminiPro, open-source models) and effectiveness with limited training data and imbalanced class distributions

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Similar images likely share similar labels (manifold assumption), enabling MLLM-generated labels to be effectively refined using neighbor information
> - Knowledge can be distilled from expensive MLLMs to more efficient CLIP models
> - Label filtering helps manage vocabulary size while maintaining performance

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

- **Fine-Grained Visual Recognition (FGVR):** FGVR is a well-established area in computer vision, focusing on distinguishing between highly similar categories. Traditionally, it relies on large, expertly annotated datasets, a significant limitation in many real-world scenarios, such as medical imaging and biodiversity studies, due to privacy concerns, annotation costs, or the lack of available experts.
- **Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) for Vision:** Recent advances in MLLMs have shown potential for various vision-language tasks, including FGVR. Their ability to leverage both visual and textual information enables zero-shot learning, making them attractive for scenarios where labeled data is scarce.
- **Vocabulary-Free FGVR (VF-FGVR):** This paper tackles a specific challenge within FGVR: the "vocabulary-free" setting, where the set of fine-grained labels is not predefined or known. This aligns with real-world situations where the categories of interest are unknown beforehand. This paper also acknowledges the need for sustainable AI systems which will have impacts in the future.
- **Cost and Efficiency Concerns:** While MLLMs offer promising zero-shot capabilities, directly querying them for each test image is computationally expensive and time-consuming, hindering their practical scalability. This paper directly addresses these limitations.
- **Related Work:** The paper acknowledges related work in several areas:
 - Traditional FGVR methods.
 - Foundation Models for VF-FGVR.
 - Prompt Tuning.
 - Learning with Noisy Labels (LNL).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective:** The primary objective is to develop an efficient and effective method for VF-FGVR that overcomes the limitations of relying solely on direct MLLM queries.
- **Motivation:** The work is motivated by the following:
 - The need for FGVR in domains where labeled data is scarce.
 - The potential of MLLMs for zero-shot FGVR.
 - The impracticality of directly querying MLLMs for every test input due to high costs and inference times.
 - The need for sustainable AI systems that are efficient with computational resources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **NeaR (Nearest-Neighbor Label Refinement):** The core of the paper is the proposed NeaR method. It addresses the VF-FGVR challenge by leveraging MLLMs to create a weakly supervised training dataset and then fine-tuning a downstream CLIP model for efficient inference.
- **Key Steps:** The methodology involves several key steps:
 1. **MLLM-Based Label Generation:** Querying an MLLM with a simple prompt to generate a label for each image in a small, unlabeled training set.
 2. **Candidate Set Construction:** For each image, constructing a candidate set of labels based on the labels generated for its K-nearest neighbors in the CLIP embedding space. This leverages the manifold assumption.
 3. **Noisy Sample Detection:** Using a Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM) to partition the training data into "clean" and "noisy" samples based on their cross-entropy loss.
 4. **Label Refinement:** Refining the MLLM-generated labels using the candidate sets, clean posterior probability and CLIP model class probabilities. For "noisy" samples, the labels are refined based on the candidate set.
 5. **Fine-Tuning CLIP:** Fine-tuning a CLIP model using the refined labels.
 6. **Label Filtering:** Incorporating a label filtering mechanism to truncate the label space at inference time, reducing the complexity and improving classification performance. The label filtering mechanism uses the labels that are both predicted by CLIP on the training set and by the candidate sets.
- **Underlying Principles:**
 - **Manifold Assumption:** Similar images are likely to have similar labels.
 - **Learning with Noisy Labels:** The method explicitly addresses the noisy nature of MLLM-generated labels.
 - **Knowledge Distillation:** Transferring knowledge from the MLLM to a more efficient CLIP model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **Superior Performance:** NeaR consistently outperforms various baselines, including direct MLLM inference, contemporary VF methods, and simple CLIP fine-tuning strategies (CoOp).
- **Cost-Effectiveness:** NeaR achieves comparable or better performance than direct MLLM inference at a significantly reduced cost and inference time.
- **Robustness:** The method is shown to be robust across different MLLMs, including proprietary models (GPT-4o, GeminiPro) and open-source models (LLaMA-3.2, Qwen2).
- **Ablation Studies:** Ablation studies demonstrate the importance of the key components of NeaR: candidate set construction and label filtering.
- **Imbalanced Data Handling:** The method demonstrates resilience to class imbalance in the training data.
- **Performance with Varying Shots:** Performance improves as the number of shots increases.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Efficient VF-FGVR:** The paper presents a significant advancement in efficient VF-FGVR, making it more practical for real-world applications.
- **Bridging the Gap:** It effectively bridges the gap between the zero-shot capabilities of MLLMs and the need for computationally efficient solutions.
- **Broader Applicability:** The proposed method has potential applications in various domains where labeled data is limited, such as medical imaging, biodiversity conservation, and e-commerce.
- **Sustainability in AI:** By reducing the reliance on expensive MLLM queries, NeaR contributes to the development of more sustainable AI systems.
- **Future Directions:** This research opens several avenues for future work:
 - Exploring different prompting strategies for MLLM label generation.
 - Investigating alternative methods for candidate set construction.
 - Developing more sophisticated label refinement techniques.
 - Applying the method to other vision-language tasks.
 - Exploring various values for κ to analyze performance in VF-FGVR tasks.
