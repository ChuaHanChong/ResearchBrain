# Spatial-TTT: Streaming Visual-based Spatial Intelligence with Test-Time Training

> [!summary] Summary
> Spatial-TTT equips Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) with the ability to process and reason about 3D spaces from continuous video streams using a test-time training framework. It integrates a hybrid architecture and spatial-predictive mechanism, achieving state-of-the-art performance, including a 12.3 percentage point improvement on MindCube-Tiny and robust object counting over 120-minute videos.

## Problem

- Current MLLMs struggle with precise 3D geometric understanding and spatial reasoning, often degrading on tasks requiring it due to their reliance on 2D semantic-level training data.
- Processing long-horizon, streaming visual observations (videos) with MLLMs incurs prohibitive computational and memory costs from the quadratic complexity of standard self-attention mechanisms.
- Existing methods for enhancing spatial awareness in MLLMs are limited to single images or short video clips, failing to scale to continuous, unbounded visual streams encountered in real-world applications.

## Method

- **Spatial-TTT** introduces a framework that integrates ==Test-Time Training (TTT)== into MLLMs, allowing a subset of model parameters to continually adapt during inference for streaming visual-based spatial intelligence.
- A hybrid TTT architecture combines TTT layers with standard self-attention layers, uses large chunk updates, and incorporates parallel sliding window attention for efficient, approximately linear-complexity processing of long video streams.
- A spatial-predictive mechanism uses depth-wise 3D spatiotemporal convolutions to inject inductive biases, encouraging fast weights to capture geometric correspondence and temporal continuity for coherent memory updates; the Muon update rule stabilizes these updates.

## Results

- Spatial-TTT-2B achieved an overall average score of **64.4** on VSI-Bench, outperforming all proprietary and open-source baselines, and attained **76.2%** accuracy on MindCube-Tiny, surpassing Gemini-3-pro by **12.3** percentage points.
- The model demonstrated robust performance on streaming spatial sensing tasks (VSI-SUPER-Recall and VSI-SUPER-Count) over long-horizon videos (up to 120 minutes), significantly outperforming baselines in continuous object counting.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the spatial-predictive mechanism, dense scene-description supervision, and hybrid TTT architecture are critical components, each contributing to improved performance, and Spatial-TTT exhibited approximately linear scaling of resource usage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time training, when combined with a hybrid architecture and spatial-predictive mechanisms, can effectively build a compact and adaptive spatial memory for MLLMs to process and reason over long streaming videos.
> - Injecting spatiotemporal inductive biases via 3D convolutions into TTT layers is crucial for enhancing the model's ability to capture geometric correspondence and temporal continuity, leading to improved metric-level 3D spatial reasoning.
> - Dense, scene-level spatial descriptions provide a significantly richer supervisory signal than sparse spatial QA data, enabling the model to learn more effective fast-weight update dynamics for accumulating and organizing global 3D spatial evidence persistently.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses challenges in developing spatial intelligence within Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), particularly concerning their ability to process and reason over long-horizon, streaming visual observations. Current MLLMs have demonstrated proficiency in 2D visual understanding, but their performance tends to degrade on tasks requiring precise 3D geometric understanding and spatial reasoning. This limitation is primarily attributed to their training data, which often consists of 2D semantic-level image-text pairs lacking explicit 3D geometric priors.

While recent research has explored methods to enhance MLLMs with spatial awareness—such as augmenting inputs with geometric cues, utilizing 3D spatial VQA data, or incorporating 3D feature alignment—these approaches are typically confined to single images or short video clips (e.g., 16 or 32 frames). Scaling these methods to the long, continuous video streams encountered in real-world applications (e.g., embodied robotics, autonomous driving, augmented reality) presents significant computational challenges. Naively extending input sequences leads to prohibitive costs due to the quadratic complexity of standard self-attention mechanisms, while aggressive temporal subsampling risks losing fine-grained spatial details critical for accurate 3D reasoning.

This paper proposes an alternative by integrating Test-Time Training (TTT) into MLLMs. TTT is a paradigm where a subset of model parameters (fast weights) are continually updated during inference using unlabeled test data. While TTT has shown promise in language modeling and other areas, its application to enhancing the visual capabilities of MLLMs for streaming spatial intelligence has received limited attention. Spatial-TTT positions itself within this gap, aiming to leverage TTT's efficiency for long-context processing while specifically adapting it to capture and organize 3D spatial evidence from continuous visual streams. It seeks to provide a method for online, adaptive memory of spatial information, which is a departure from fixed-parameter inference common in most MLLM applications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for developing Spatial-TTT are detailed as follows:

1. **Enabling Streaming Spatial Understanding:** The fundamental motivation is to equip MLLMs with the capability to perceive, understand, and reason about 3D spaces from continuous streams of visual observations. Human spatial perception relies on progressively updating spatial evidence from ongoing visual input, a critical ability for applications like embodied robotics, autonomous driving, and augmented reality. Existing MLLMs struggle with this due to their reliance on static or short-context inputs.

2. **Addressing Limitations of Current MLLMs in 3D Spatial Intelligence:** Current MLLMs, despite their advancements in 2D vision-language tasks, lack inherent 3D geometric priors. They typically degrade in performance on tasks requiring precise 3D spatial alignment and reasoning. This gap necessitates methods that can imbue MLLMs with a robust understanding of 3D structure and geometric relationships.

3. **Overcoming Computational and Memory Bottlenecks for Long Videos:** Processing long-horizon video streams with traditional Transformer architectures incurs significant computational and memory costs, largely due to the quadratic complexity of self-attention. Existing solutions, such as temporal subsampling, often sacrifice crucial fine-grained spatial details. A key objective is to develop a mechanism that can efficiently handle unbounded video streams without prohibitive resource demands or loss of critical information.

4. **Developing a Compact, Adaptive Spatial Memory:** The paper aims to create a system that can selectively maintain, progressively update, and reason over spatial memory from extended video inputs. This requires a method for continuously accumulating 3D evidence over time, acting as a dynamic, non-linear memory that adapts to evolving scenes and viewpoints. Test-Time Training (TTT) is identified as a suitable paradigm for this adaptive memory.

5. **Injecting Spatial-Temporal Inductive Biases:** Standard TTT mechanisms often use point-wise linear projections for keys, queries, and values, which do not inherently account for the local neighborhood structure and continuity present in visual data. A specific objective is to enhance TTT layers with a spatial-predictive mechanism that encourages the model to capture geometric correspondence and temporal continuity across frames, making the memory updates more spatially coherent.

6. **Providing Richer Supervision for Fast-Weight Update Dynamics:** The effectiveness of TTT depends on how well the model learns to update its "fast weights" to retain useful information. Existing spatial datasets often provide sparse and local supervision, which offers weak gradient signals for learning effective fast-weight update dynamics for global 3D memory. A motivation is to construct a dataset with dense, scene-level spatial descriptions to provide richer supervision, guiding the fast weights to memorize and organize global 3D spatial signals in a structured manner.

In summary, the motivation is to advance MLLMs towards practical streaming visual-based spatial intelligence by overcoming architectural, data, and learning challenges through an adaptive, test-time training approach.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Spatial-TTT is a framework designed for streaming visual-based spatial intelligence using test-time training (TTT). It combines architectural modifications, a novel spatial-predictive mechanism, and a tailored training strategy to handle long-horizon video streams and enhance 3D spatial understanding.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Test-Time Training (TTT)

Traditional neural networks operate with fixed parameters after training. TTT introduces on-the-fly parameter adaptation during inference. A designated subset of parameters, termed "fast weights" (W), is continually updated as each test sequence is processed. These fast weights function as an adaptive memory encoding contextual information. At each timestep, TTT alternates between two operations:
1. **Update Operation:** Fast weights (W) are modified by performing a gradient descent step on a self-supervised loss function, associating the current key-value pair (k_t, v_t) with the memory (W).
2. **Apply Operation:** The updated network processes the current query (q_t) using the refined fast weights (W_t) to produce an output (o_t) enriched with information from preceding tokens encoded in W_t.

#### 3.2. Overall Framework

Spatial-TTT builds upon this TTT paradigm with several key components:

#### 3.2.1. Hybrid TTT Architecture

- **Interleaving TTT and Self-Attention Layers:** To balance the efficiency of TTT with the semantic reasoning ability of pretrained MLLMs, a hybrid architecture is adopted. TTT layers are interleaved with standard self-attention "anchor layers" at a 3:1 ratio (75% TTT, 25% self-attention). Anchor layers retain full attention access over the entire context, preserving pretrained cross-modal alignment and visual semantics, while TTT layers handle long-range temporal dependencies with sublinear memory growth.
- **Large Chunk Updates:** Unlike conventional TTT implementations that use small chunks, Spatial-TTT employs large chunk sizes for visual tokens, approximately aligned with multiple video frames. This improves parallelism, hardware efficiency, and maintains spatial coherence within update units.
- **Parallel Sliding Window Attention (SWA):** A challenge with large chunks in TTT is the causal constraint preventing intra-chunk token interaction during fast-weight updates. To address this and preserve spatiotemporal continuity, SWA operates in parallel with the TTT branch within each TTT layer. SWA utilizes a fixed-size window (w) that is not smaller than the chunk size (b), ensuring complete causal structure within the chunk. The layer output combines contributions from both the TTT branch and the SWA branch.
- **Fast-Weight Network:** The fast-weight network (f_W) is implemented as a bias-free SwiGLU-MLP to enhance nonlinearity and expressiveness.

#### 3.2.2. Spatial-Predictive Mechanism

- **3D Spatiotemporal Convolution:** To inject spatiotemporal inductive bias and make memory updates more spatially coherent, standard point-wise linear projections for Q, K, V in the TTT branch are replaced. Instead, lightweight depth-wise 3D spatiotemporal convolutions are applied to aggregate local neighborhood context for visual tokens. Visual tokens from videos are reshaped into a spatiotemporal grid, and convolution kernels (initialized with Dirac delta) aggregate information within a local 3D neighborhood. This encourages fast weights to learn predictive mappings between spatiotemporal contexts rather than isolated tokens.
- **Muon Update Rule:** To improve the stability and effectiveness of TTT updates, the Muon update rule (Equations 6 and 7 in the paper) is adopted instead of vanilla gradient descent. Muon accumulates momentum and orthogonalizes gradients via Newton-Schulz iterations, and L2-normalizes weights, enhancing the learning dynamics.

#### 3.3. Bridging Sparse Spatial QA with Dense Supervision

- **Dense Scene-Description Dataset:** Recognizing that existing spatial datasets often provide sparse, local supervision (e.g., short answers to specific questions), the authors construct a dense scene-description dataset from SceneVerse annotations. This dataset requires the model to generate comprehensive 3D scene descriptions, formatted as coherent scene walkthroughs. These descriptions include:
 - **Global Context:** Identifying scene type and functional setting.
 - **Objects and Counts:** Enumerating object categories and precise counts.
 - **Object Relations:** Describing spatial layouts and pairwise relationships.
This dense, high-coverage supervision provides richer gradient signals, encouraging fast weights to capture structured, scene-level spatial information and retain persistent global 3D evidence over long video streams.

#### 3.4. Spatial-Aware Progressive Training

A two-stage progressive training strategy is employed:
1. **Stage 1: Initializing with Global 3D Awareness:** The hybrid TTT architecture is initially trained on the dense scene-description dataset. This stage aims to teach fast weights to retain comprehensive scene-level information through chunk-by-chunk memory updates. A sliding window annealing strategy is used, where the SWA window size (w) linearly decreases from a maximum value (covering the entire sequence) to the chunk size (b) over the first stage. This gradually shifts responsibility for cross-chunk information propagation to the TTT layers.
2. **Stage 2: Tuning Streaming Spatial Reasoning:** The model is then fine-tuned on a large-scale spatial VQA dataset (2M samples) covering diverse tasks (e.g., object relative direction/distance, spatial counting, route planning, room size estimation). In this stage, the SWA window size is fixed at the chunk size (w=b), focusing TTT layers on cross-chunk spatial information aggregation. This enables the model to selectively retain task-relevant spatial evidence and retrieve accumulated knowledge for reasoning.

#### 3.5. Inference Mechanism

During inference, a dual KV cache mechanism ensures constant-memory streaming:
- **Sliding Window KV Cache:** A fixed-length cache for local context modeling in SWA. Earliest entries are discarded when the cache exceeds its window size.
- **TTT Pending KV Cache:** Accumulates key-value pairs for fast-weight updates. When its length reaches the chunk size, these pairs are used for one fast-weight update, and the cache is cleared.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of Spatial-TTT was conducted across several spatial intelligence benchmarks, demonstrating its capabilities in general spatial understanding and streaming spatial sensing.

#### 4.1. General Spatial Understanding Results on VSI-Bench

Spatial-TTT-2B achieved an overall average (Avg.) score of 64.4 on VSI-Bench, outperforming all proprietary and open-source baselines, despite its compact 2B parameter count.
- **Numerical Questions:** Spatial-TTT-2B attained the highest score on Absolute Distance (47.8) and remained competitive on Object Count (70.8), Object Size (71.7), and Room Size (65.9). These results suggest improved metric grounding and scene-scale estimation.
- **Multiple-Choice Questions:** The model exhibited strong performance in tasks requiring consistent geometric understanding across egocentric views, such as Relative Direction (73.0) and Route Plan (47.4), indicating enhanced capabilities in directional reasoning and navigation planning.

#### 4.2. Results on MindCube Benchmark

On MindCube-Tiny, a diagnostic subset assessing fine-grained spatial capabilities, Spatial-TTT-2B achieved an accuracy (ACC) of 76.2. This performance surpassed all baseline models, including the strongest proprietary baseline (Gemini-3-pro, 63.9% average) and the strongest open-source spatial model (MindCube-3B, 51.7% average), by 12.3 and 24.5 percentage points, respectively. It attained the best performance in the ACROSS and AMONG subsets, indicating improved spatial reasoning under viewpoint changes and occlusions.

#### 4.3. Streaming Spatial Sensing Results (VSI-SUPER-Recall and VSI-SUPER-Count)

For tasks requiring continual spatial sensing under long-horizon videos, Spatial-TTT-2B showed robust performance.
- **VSI-SUPER-Recall (VSR):** Spatial-TTT-2B achieved competitive performance across video lengths (10-120 minutes), with scores of 38.3 (10min), 35.0 (30min), 28.3 (60min), and 30.0 (120min).
- **VSI-SUPER-Count (VSC):** The model significantly outperformed all baselines, demonstrating the ability to count objects across extended sequences. Its scores were 31.8 (10min), 45.6 (30min), 36.2 (60min), and 38.4 (120min).
- **Comparison with Baselines:** Long-video understanding models (e.g., MovieChat, Flash-VStream) consistently underperformed, suggesting that long-context modeling alone is insufficient. General-purpose and spatial intelligence models (e.g., Qwen3-VL, Cambrian-S) showed performance degradation or out-of-memory errors on longer sequences (beyond 60 minutes), indicating limitations in processing extended inputs. Spatial-TTT maintained stable performance as video length increased, attributed to its online updates for incremental integration of new observations.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study and Analysis

An ablation study on VSI-Bench validated the contribution of each proposed component:
- **Spatial-Predictive Mechanism:** Removing the depth-wise 3D spatiotemporal convolution (w/o SP-Mechanism) led to a drop in average score from 64.4 to 62.1, with a larger decline on Numerical questions (from 64.0 to 60.7). This supports the importance of injecting local spatiotemporal inductive bias for stable fast-weight updates and metric-level spatial reasoning.
- **Dense Scene-Description Supervision:** Training without the dense scene-description dataset (w/o Dense Data) reduced the overall average score from 64.4 to 61.3, with declines of 3.0 points on Numerical and 3.3 points on Multiple-Choice subsets. This indicates that dense, scene-level supervision provides stronger signals for learning effective online update dynamics and retaining globally useful 3D evidence.
- **Hybrid TTT Architecture:** Eliminating the self-attention anchor layers (w/o Hybrid Arch) caused the largest degradation, with the average score dropping from 64.4 to 53.9, particularly on Multiple-Choice tasks (from 64.8 to 52.4). This highlights the necessity of the hybrid architecture to retain pretrained cross-modal alignment and global-context reasoning while scaling to long videos.

#### 4.5. Efficiency Analysis

Comparisons of peak memory usage and theoretical TFLOPs revealed that Spatial-TTT-2B exhibits approximately linear scaling with input length, consistent with its linear-complexity attention mechanism.
- At 1024 frames, Spatial-TTT achieved over 40% reduction in both TFLOPs and memory compared to Qwen3-VL-2B (a general-purpose MLLM), whose resource usage scales quadratically.
- Spatial-MLLM-4B (a geometry-augmented spatial VLM) exhibited super-linear scaling and ran out of memory beyond 256 frames, demonstrating its impracticality for streaming scenarios. This efficiency advantage positions Spatial-TTT for scalable streaming spatial intelligence.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Spatial-TTT represents an advancement in the field of multimodal large language models, particularly in their capacity for streaming visual-based spatial intelligence. The work introduces a framework that directly addresses critical limitations of current MLLMs, such as their lack of robust 3D geometric understanding and their inability to efficiently process and reason over long-horizon video streams.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Efficient Long-Context Processing for Spatial Data:** By integrating Test-Time Training (TTT) with a hybrid architecture (interleaving TTT and self-attention layers, large chunk updates, and parallel sliding-window attention), Spatial-TTT demonstrates a method for processing unbounded visual streams with approximately linear complexity. This addresses the computational bottlenecks of quadratic attention, making streaming spatial understanding feasible for extended durations without prohibitive memory or computational costs.
- **Enhanced 3D Spatial Awareness:** The introduction of a spatial-predictive mechanism using depth-wise 3D spatiotemporal convolutions enables TTT layers to capture geometric correspondence and temporal continuity. This inductive bias helps fast weights learn more structured and coherent representations of 3D scenes, leading to improved metric-level spatial reasoning and planning.
- **Improved Fast-Weight Update Dynamics through Dense Supervision:** The creation and utilization of a dense scene-description dataset provide richer, scene-level supervision compared to sparse spatial QA data. This encourages the model to learn more effective fast-weight update dynamics, enabling it to accumulate and organize global 3D spatial evidence persistently over time.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** Spatial-TTT achieves state-of-the-art performance across multiple challenging video spatial benchmarks, including VSI-Bench, MindCube, VSI-SUPER-Recall, and VSI-SUPER-Count. This empirical validation indicates its effectiveness in various spatial understanding tasks, from general spatial reasoning to continual object counting and temporal recall over very long videos.
- **Scalability for Real-World Applications:** The demonstrated ability of Spatial-TTT to maintain stable performance as video length increases, coupled with its efficient resource utilization, suggests its potential for integration into real-world applications requiring continuous spatial awareness. These include embodied AI (e.g., robots navigating complex environments), autonomous driving (e.g., long-range scene perception and situational awareness), and augmented/virtual reality devices (e.g., persistent environmental mapping and interaction).

**Potential Impact:**

The methodology presented in Spatial-TTT offers a promising direction for building more robust and scalable MLLMs with persistent spatial memory. It moves beyond static, short-clip visual understanding towards dynamic, streaming spatial intelligence, aligning MLLM capabilities more closely with human-like perception of the physical world. Future research could explore further optimizations of TTT, novel inductive biases for spatial reasoning, and broader applications in dynamic 3D environments. This work contributes to the foundational understanding and practical implementation of spatial intelligence in AI systems.
