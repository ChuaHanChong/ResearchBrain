# Detecting Data Contamination from Reinforcement Learning Post-training for Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University researchers introduce Self-Critique, an entropy-based method for detecting data contamination in the reinforcement learning (RL) post-training phase of large language models (LLMs). The approach quantifies "policy collapse" by measuring the similarity of token-level entropy sequences between an initial and a self-critique response, improving contamination detection by 19% (average AUC 0.70) on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct compared to prior baselines.

## Problem

- Existing data contamination detection methods for LLMs primarily focus on pre-training and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stages, leaving the RL post-training phase unaddressed.
- Traditional likelihood-based detection metrics are ineffective for RL-trained models, as RL optimizes for reward rather than direct likelihood maximization of ground-truth responses.
- A lack of dedicated benchmarks to simulate and evaluate contamination specifically within the RL post-training phase hindered research in this area.

## Method

- Introducing Self-Critique, an ==entropy-based== detection method that compares token-level entropy sequences of an initial model response with a subsequent "self-critique" response for the same problem.
- Developing RL-MIA, a new benchmark designed to simulate and evaluate data contamination purely within the RL post-training phase, using mathematical and logical reasoning datasets.
- The method measures the similarity between entropy sequences; higher similarity indicates a struggle to deviate from a memorized path, signaling contamination.

## Results

- Self-Critique consistently outperformed baselines, achieving an average AUC of **0.70** on Qwen2.5-**7B**-Instruct (**19%** improvement) and **0.64** on DeepSeek-Math-**7B**-Instruct (**19%** improvement).
- Likelihood-based methods performed near random guessing (AUC 	 **0.50**), while entropy-based variants of existing probing mechanisms showed improved performance, confirming entropy's relevance.
- The method demonstrated robustness across various RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO, DAPO) and alignment paradigms (PPO, DPO, TDPO, RTO), maintaining its superior performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning post-training induces "policy collapse" for contaminated data, causing models to converge to overly stable and specific reasoning paths, even when prompted for alternatives.
> - Token-level entropy distributions provide a sensitive signal for detecting RL-induced policy collapse and memorization, outperforming traditional likelihood-based metrics in this context.
> - Active probing, specifically prompting the model for a "self-critique" or an alternative reasoning path, effectively exposes the path dependencies caused by RL contamination.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Data contamination poses a challenge to the validity of performance evaluations for Large Language Models (LLMs). This occurs when test samples from benchmarks inadvertently appear in a model's training data, leading to inflated and potentially misleading performance metrics. Previous research on data contamination detection has predominantly concentrated on the pre-training and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stages of LLM development. These stages typically involve likelihood-based objectives, where models are trained to maximize the probability of observed data. This creates identifiable signals, such as unusually low perplexity, that existing detection methods leverage.

However, the increasing adoption of Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training, particularly methods like Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), for enhancing LLM reasoning capabilities has introduced a new, largely unaddressed vulnerability: data contamination during the RL phase. This phase operates on a reward-maximization principle rather than direct likelihood maximization. The policy is guided by sparse reward signals to find successful reasoning paths, which often decouples the model's behavior from the likelihood-based signals that traditional detectors rely upon. Consequently, RL-phase contamination represents a distinct challenge requiring specialized detection approaches. This work positions itself as the first systematic study to address this specific gap, proposing a method designed to identify contamination signals inherent to the reward-driven training process.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to conduct the first systematic study of data contamination detection specifically within the Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training phase of Large Language Models (LLMs) and to propose a novel method for addressing this challenge.

The motivation for this work stems from several interconnected observations and recognized limitations in the current LLM evaluation landscape:

- **Critical Research Gap:** While data contamination detection methods exist for pre-training and Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) stages, there is a lack of specialized methods for the RL post-training phase. This is deemed a significant oversight given the increasing reliance on RL, particularly techniques like Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR), for advancing LLM reasoning abilities. The absence of such methods creates a vulnerability in ensuring the reliability of LLM evaluations.
- **Inapplicability of Existing Methods:** Traditional contamination detection techniques, largely based on likelihood metrics (e.g., perplexity, token probabilities), are ineffective for RL post-training. Pre-training and SFT objectives are rooted in Maximum Likelihood Estimation (MLE), directly training models to assign high probabilities to observed data. In contrast, RL post-training optimizes for reward maximization, decoupling the model's policy from direct token-level likelihoods of ground-truth responses. This fundamental shift renders likelihood-based signals unreliable for identifying memorization in an RL context.
- **Identification of a New Signal: Policy Collapse and Entropy:** The authors posit that RL post-training induces a phenomenon known as "policy collapse." For samples that consistently yield high rewards, the model tends to converge to a narrow, highly specific reasoning path, resulting in overly stable outputs. This phenomenon is hypothesized to manifest as distinctive patterns in token-level entropy distributions. While RL generally causes entropy sequences to become sparse, a simple passive check of entropy is insufficient because policy collapse can also occur on clean samples.
- **Need for Active Probing:** To differentiate between general policy collapse and policy collapse indicative of contamination, an active probing mechanism is motivated. The core hypothesis is that when an LLM is asked to generate an *alternative* reasoning path for a problem, contaminated samples (which the model has memorized and optimized a specific high-reward path for) will struggle to deviate significantly from their initial response, exhibiting highly similar entropy curves. Conversely, for clean samples, the model is expected to show greater flexibility and generate more distinct entropy patterns when prompted for an alternative. This "self-critique" probing strategy aims to expose the underlying path dependencies induced by RL training.
- **Lack of Suitable Benchmarks:** A practical motivation was the absence of a benchmark specifically designed to isolate and simulate data contamination purely within the RL phase. This necessitated the creation of RL-MIA to enable rigorous evaluation of detection methods for this unique scenario.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to secure the integrity of LLM evaluations in the era of RL post-training, by developing an effective, specialized detection mechanism that leverages the unique training dynamics of reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces **Self-Critique**, an entropy-based detection method, and **RL-MIA**, a new benchmark, to address data contamination in the RL post-training phase of LLMs.

#### 3.1. Self-Critique Detection Method

Self-Critique operates on the principle that RL post-training induces a strong dependence on specific reasoning paths for contaminated samples due to reward maximization. The method quantifies this path dependency by comparing the token-level entropy sequences of an initial response with a subsequent "self-critique" response generated for the same problem. The detection process involves three main steps in a black-box setting (querying the model without access to internal states or training data):

1. **Initial Response Generation:**
 - For a given problem `q`, an initial prompt `P1` is constructed by embedding `q` into a chat template.
 - The model `M` generates an initial response `r1` using a deterministic decoding strategy (e.g., greedy decoding) to obtain its most confident output.
 - The token-level entropy sequence `E1 = {Ht(r1)}` is computed for `r1`. Token-level entropy `Ht` at each decoding step `t` is calculated as `Ht = - Σ_v∈V p_θ(v | x_<t) log p_θ(v | x_<t)`, where `p_θ(v | x_<t)` is the probability of token `v` given preceding tokens `x_<t`.

2. **Self-Critique Response Generation:**
 - A self-critique prompt `P2` is created by augmenting the original problem `q` with an instructional meta-prompt `I_critique` and the text of the initial response `r1`. The `I_critique` instructs the model to generate a different reasoning path or an alternative solution, conditioned on `r1`.
 - The model `M` then generates a second response `r2` using the augmented prompt.
 - The token-level entropy sequence `E2 = {Ht(r2)}` is computed for `r2`.

3. **Similarity Score Calculation:**
 - The contamination score `Score_Self-Critique(q)` is determined by the similarity between the two entropy sequences, `E1` and `E2`.
 - A length-aware (penalized) cosine similarity is used: `cos_penalized(A, B) = cos(pad(A), pad(B)) × (min(|A|, |B|) / max(|A|, |B|))`.
 - `pad(A)` and `pad(B)` refer to zero-padding the shorter sequence to match the length of the longer one. The multiplicative length ratio penalizes cases where response lengths significantly differ.
 - A higher similarity score indicates that the model struggled to deviate from its initial reasoning path, suggesting memorization and thus a higher likelihood of contamination.

#### 3.2. RL-MIA Benchmark Construction

To facilitate the evaluation of RL-phase contamination detection methods, the authors developed **RL-MIA (Reinforcement Learning Membership Inference Attack)**. The benchmark simulates controllable contamination by selectively incorporating a subset of test data into the RL post-training process.

- **Datasets:** Problems are drawn from four sources:
 - **AIME 2024 and AIME 2025:** Mathematical reasoning benchmarks. AIME 2024 allows for testing robustness to potential prior pre-training exposure, while AIME 2025 is post-cutoff and less likely to be pre-trained on.
 - **Knights & Knaves (K&K) and SAT:** Synthetically generated logical reasoning datasets, chosen for their controlled nature to ensure any detected training signal is attributable solely to RL post-training.
- **Contamination Simulation:**
 - For AIME24 and AIME25, 50% of each benchmark's items are injected into the OpenR1-Math-46K corpus, which serves as the base RL post-training data.
 - For K&K and SAT, the provided training portions form the contaminated split, with additional synthesized items serving as clean, held-out samples.
- **Models:** Experiments primarily used Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and DeepSeek-Math-7B-Instruct. Additional experiments were conducted on Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-7B-Math, and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. RL training was implemented using the VeRL framework on NVIDIA A100 GPUs.

#### 3.3. Baseline Methods

Self-Critique was compared against several representative baselines, adapted to operate on model responses rather than input text for fairness in the RL post-training context:

- **Likelihood-based:** Perplexity (PPL), Min-K% Prob, Min-K%++. These assume memorized text exhibits distinct log-probability properties.
- **Probing with other metrics:**
 - Recall: Prefixes text with non-member content and measures relative change in log-likelihood.
 - CDD (Consistency-based Data Detector): Measures output consistency under stochastic sampling via average token-level edit distance.
- **Entropy-based baselines (newly introduced):**
 - Entropy-Temp: Uses stochastic sampling (like CDD) but measures entropy.
 - Entropy-Noise: Injects a non-member prefix (like Recall) but measures entropy.

#### 3.4. Evaluation Metrics

The primary metric used was the **Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC)**, which is threshold-independent and indicates the probability of a detector ranking a contaminated sample higher than a clean one (0.50 for random guess). The F1 score at the Youden threshold was also reported as a threshold-specific reference.

#### 3.5. Ablation Studies

Further analyses included:
- Investigating dual-stage contamination (pre-training and RL) on GSM8K to differentiate signals.
- Testing Self-Critique's robustness across different RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO, DAPO).
- Evaluating the impact of Top-K entropy approximation (using only top K token probabilities).
- Examining the effect of different sampling strategies (greedy vs. temperature).
- Assessing sensitivity to meta-instruction phrasing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across multiple models and datasets in the RL-MIA benchmark yielded several key findings regarding data contamination detection in RL post-training.

1. **Self-Critique Outperforms Baselines:**
 - Self-Critique consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to all baseline methods. On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, it achieved an average AUC of 0.70, representing a 19% improvement over the best non-Self-Critique baseline. Similarly, on DeepSeek-Math-7B-Instruct, it achieved an average AUC of 0.64, also a 19% improvement.
 - Self-Critique also led on most individual dataset AUCs across these primary models and showed strong performance on Qwen2.5-7B-Math and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.
 - Likelihood-based baselines (PPL, Min-K%, Min-K%++) generally performed near random guessing (AUC ≈ 0.50) and exhibited instability, confirming their inadequacy for RL-phase contamination detection.
 - Among existing methods, Recall, which employs a probing mechanism, showed relatively better performance, suggesting that actively eliciting signals is more effective than relying on intrinsic text properties alone.

2. **Entropy as a Relevant Signal:**
 - When existing probing mechanisms (from CDD and Recall) were adapted to use entropy as the core metric (Entropy-Temp and Entropy-Noise), these variants generally outperformed their likelihood-based counterparts (CDD and Recall respectively). This indicates that token-level entropy is a sensitive indicator of RL-induced changes and is better suited for detecting contamination in the reward-driven setting.
 - Self-Critique's specific self-critique probing mechanism, when combined with entropy, yielded further notable gains (e.g., +13% to +16% AUC over other entropy-based baselines), suggesting its alignment with the policy's dependence on high-reward paths.

3. **Dual-Stage Contamination Analysis:**
 - In scenarios with dual pre-training and RL-phase contamination (using GSM8K), Self-Critique's performance significantly improved (AUC increasing up to 0.88 from 0.59) when evaluated on subsets of data with lower estimated pre-training contamination (filtered using a likelihood-based detector like PPL).
 - This result demonstrates Self-Critique's ability to isolate and effectively detect RL-specific memorization signals when pre-training-induced signals are attenuated, confirming the distinct nature of RL-phase contamination.
 - The PPL-based detector's performance remained close to random guess in these filtered scenarios, further highlighting the specialized efficacy of Self-Critique.

4. **Robustness to RL Algorithms and Alignments:**
 - Self-Critique maintained its status as the most reliable detector across various RL algorithms (PPO, GRPO, DAPO) and across different RLHF alignment paradigms (PPO, DPO, TDPO, RTO).
 - It achieved the highest AUC for each algorithm and the highest average AUC, suggesting that the policy collapse leading to path dependence is a fundamental, algorithm-agnostic characteristic of RL training, regardless of the reward structure (sparse, implicit, token-level).

5. **Efficiency and Robustness of Implementation:**
 - **Top-K Entropy Approximation:** An ablation study showed that approximating token-level entropy using only the Top-K probabilities (even with K as low as 3) did not significantly harm performance. This indicates that most probability mass concentrates on a small set of tokens, making the approach efficient and practical without compromising accuracy.
 - **Sampling Strategy:** The optimal performance was achieved when both the initial and self-critique responses were generated using greedy (deterministic) sampling, as this best revealed the sharp policy distributions resulting from RL-induced entropy collapse by eliminating randomness.
 - **Meta-Instruction Sensitivity:** Self-Critique demonstrated high stability across different paraphrased meta-instructions for the self-critique prompt, with very low standard deviations in AUC. This confirms that the detection signal is driven by the underlying policy collapse rather than specific prompt phrasing.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research addresses a previously overlooked yet critical aspect of Large Language Model (LLM) evaluation: data contamination in the Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training phase. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Reliability of LLM Evaluation:** By providing the first systematic study and an effective method for detecting RL-phase contamination, this work contributes to more trustworthy and valid evaluations of LLMs. As RL post-training (e.g., RLVR) becomes increasingly central to improving LLM reasoning, ensuring that reported performance is not artificially inflated by memorized benchmark data is crucial for scientific integrity and fair comparison of models.
- **Addressing a Critical Research Gap:** The paper identifies and fills a significant void in the field of data contamination detection. Prior methods were largely ineffective in the RL context due to the shift from likelihood-based to reward-based optimization. This research highlights the unique challenges of RL-induced memorization and offers a specialized solution.
- **Novel Detection Mechanism:** The proposed Self-Critique method, leveraging the concept of "policy collapse" and active entropy-based probing, introduces a new paradigm for detecting contamination specific to reward-driven training. This approach moves beyond traditional likelihood metrics, which are demonstrated to be inadequate in this setting.
- **Development of a Specialized Benchmark (RL-MIA):** The creation of the RL-MIA benchmark is a significant contribution. It provides a controlled environment for researchers to simulate and evaluate contamination purely within the RL phase, enabling rigorous testing and development of future detection methods. This benchmark is open-sourced, fostering further research.
- **Deeper Understanding of RL Dynamics:** The findings related to policy collapse and its manifestation in token-level entropy patterns offer insights into the internal dynamics of LLMs undergoing RL post-training. This contributes to a broader understanding of how models learn and generalize or memorize in reward-driven environments.
- **Facilitating Transparent Research and Development:** By providing tools and methodologies to detect contamination, this work enables researchers and developers to ensure the transparency and honesty of their model evaluations. This can help prevent misleading claims about model capabilities and promote more robust LLM development practices.
- **Practical Implications for LLM Deployment:** As LLMs are increasingly deployed in real-world applications where reasoning capabilities are paramount, ensuring the integrity of their training and evaluation is essential. Detection methods like Self-Critique can serve as validation tools to audit models for unintended memorization, especially in high-stakes reasoning tasks.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The paper lays the groundwork for subsequent investigations into RL-specific data contamination. It suggests avenues for future work, such as generalizing detection across diverse domains, scaling to larger models, and exploring other reward-aware detection mechanisms.

In summary, this research significantly advances the field of trustworthy LLM evaluation by pioneering contamination detection in the RL post-training phase, providing both a novel methodological solution and a dedicated benchmark.
