# World Simulation with Video Foundation Models for Physical AI

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA developed Cosmos-Predict2.5 and Cosmos-Transfer2.5, a new generation of world foundation models, to advance simulation fidelity for Physical AI. These models demonstrate improved realism, prompt alignment, and control across applications like synthetic data generation, robot policy learning, and autonomous driving simulation, often achieving comparable or superior performance with fewer parameters than previous models.

## Problem

- Training embodied agents for Physical AI is costly, time-consuming, and poses safety risks in real-world environments.
- Existing video generative models often lack the precise control over object dynamics, interactions, and physical consistency needed for robust Physical AI applications.
- Prior open-source video generative models designed for Physical AI required advancements in data quality, architectural design, and training methodologies.

## Method

- A refined 7-stage video curation pipeline, processing over 200 million videos, was developed to generate high-quality general and domain-specific datasets for Physical AI.
- The core model architecture, built on flow-matching, unifies **Text2World**, **Image2World**, and **Video2World** generation, incorporating a Physical AI-specialized VLM ([**Cosmos-Reason1**]) as the text encoder.
- A multi-stage training paradigm includes progressive resolution, Supervised Fine-tuning (==SFT==), Model Soup merging, Reinforcement Learning (RL) with a VLM-based ==reward model==, and timestep distillation for accelerated inference.

## Results

- Cosmos-Predict2.5-14B achieved human preference scores on par with Wan2.2 27B-A14B using half the parameters, and Cosmos-Transfer2.5-2B outperformed its **3.5x** larger predecessor (Cosmos-Transfer1-7B) on control adherence and video quality.
- Robot policies augmented with synthetic data from Cosmos-Transfer2.5-2B achieved 24 out of 30 successes in real-robot tasks, a significant improvement over baseline policies (1/30 and 5/30 successes).
- Multi-view driving models demonstrated up to a **2.3x** boost in visual quality (FVD/FID scores) and up to 60% improvement in 3D-cuboid and lane detection metrics, indicating enhanced consistency and adherence to control signals.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Rigorous, VLM-based data curation is essential for creating high-fidelity and physically consistent world models tailored for Physical AI tasks.
> - Combining a unified model architecture with a progressive training strategy, SFT, model merging, and RL post-training effectively enhances both general and specialized Physical AI simulation capabilities.
> - Replacing generic text encoders with a Physical AI-specialized VLM provides richer context grounding and finer control, leading to improved human preferences and benchmark performance, even with reduced model size.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Physical AI systems, which are embodied agents designed to interact with the physical world, faces challenges during training due to the cost, time, and safety risks associated with real-world deployment. High-fidelity world simulators are proposed as a solution, offering a safe and efficient environment for agents to acquire perception and control skills before physical implementation. This paper contributes to the field of world models, specifically focusing on the paradigm of modeling world dynamics directly in pixel space through high-fidelity video prediction.

Existing world models operate within two primary paradigms: learning predictive models in abstract, latent representation spaces or modeling dynamics directly in pixel space. This work adopts the latter, employing video generative models that simulate future observations frame-by-frame, extendable with various control signals such as camera pose, action sequences, and spatially dense inputs like world scenario maps. An emerging third direction explores native 3D and 4D representations.

While numerous video generative models, both closed-source (e.g., Sora, Kling, Runway) and open-source (e.g., Wan, LTX, Hunyuan), have demonstrated advancements in general-purpose video generation, many often lack the precise, fine-grained control over object dynamics, interactions, and physical consistency required for Physical AI applications. Previous work by NVIDIA, specifically [Cosmos-Predict1] and [Cosmos-Transfer1], introduced the first open-source video generative models tailored for Physical AI. This current research builds upon these foundations, aiming to enhance their capabilities through improved data, architecture, and training methodologies. The work seeks to advance a simulation-first ecosystem for Physical AI, fostering innovation and deployment by providing accessible, open-source tools.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and introduce the next generation of world foundation models, [Cosmos-Predict2.5] and [Cosmos-Transfer2.5], designed to significantly enhance world simulation fidelity for Physical AI. The motivation stems from the inherent difficulties of training embodied agents directly in physical environments, which can be slow, expensive, and unsafe. A robust world simulator can serve as a crucial proxy, enabling agents to learn and refine skills in a virtual setting before real-world deployment.

The specific objectives for [Cosmos-Predict2.5] include:
- **Improving Simulation Fidelity**: Substantially enhancing the realism and accuracy of world simulations across diverse Physical AI domains.
- **Architectural and Training Enhancements**: Building upon its predecessor, [Cosmos-Predict1], through three key improvements:
 - **Data Quality**: Strengthening the data filtering pipeline to produce higher-quality pre-training datasets and curating specialized post-training data specific to Physical AI.
 - **Model Unification**: Simplifying the model architecture to unify Text2World, Image2World, and Video2World generation capabilities within a single model.
 - **Training Recipe Optimization**: Improving the training process by leveraging model merging techniques, a novel reinforcement learning algorithm for post-training, and replacing the T5 text encoder with [Cosmos-Reason1], a Physical AI-specialized vision-language model, to provide richer text grounding and finer control over world generation.

Beyond the core model, the research aims to demonstrate broad applicability and benefits across various Physical AI downstream applications. These include enabling more efficient synthetic data generation for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model training, improving action-conditioned video world generation for policy validation, enhancing coherent multi-view video world generation for autonomous driving simulation, and supporting camera-controllable multi-view video generation for robotic manipulation.

Furthermore, the project seeks to extend these capabilities with [Cosmos-Transfer2.5], a control-net style framework designed for diverse visual world-translation tasks such as enhancing the photorealism of physical simulator outputs, augmenting real-world videos, and converting semantic world scenarios into realistic sensory inputs for Physical AI agents. A significant motivation is to deliver higher fidelity with a more compact model size compared to its predecessor, [Cosmos-Transfer1], and enable robust long-horizon video generation and closed-loop simulation.

Finally, a key objective is to accelerate research and deployment in Physical AI by openly releasing the source code, pre-trained checkpoints, and curated benchmarks under the NVIDIA Open Model License. This aims to lower barriers to adoption and foster community-driven innovation in embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology employed in this research spans data curation, model architecture design, a multi-stage training paradigm, and specialized infrastructure.

**Data Curation:**
The research utilized a refined and scaled video curation pipeline, processing over 200 million raw videos from proprietary and open internet sources to yield approximately 200 million high-quality trainable clips. This pipeline involves seven stages: shot-aware video splitting, GPU-based transcoding, video cropping, multi-stage filtering (aesthetic, motion, OCR, perceptual quality, semantic artifacts, and VLM-based filtering), video captioning using a Qwen2.5-VL-7B vision-language model, semantic deduplication, and sharding. A strong emphasis was placed on removing unrealistic content and generating multi-length, context-aware captions.
Complementing the general dataset, domain-specific data was curated for Robotics, Autonomous Driving, Smart Spaces, Human Dynamics, and Physics. Each domain followed a tailored curation process with adjusted filters and specialized captioning prompts from a larger VLM model to ensure relevance and quality for Physical AI tasks. For example, robotics data included multiple camera views and task-centric descriptions, while autonomous driving data covered diverse geographic regions, traffic conditions, and maneuvers across seven synchronized camera views.

**Model Architecture:**
The core of [Cosmos-Predict2.5] is built on a flow-matching (FM) formulation, which replaces the Elucidated Diffusion Model (EDM) used in [Cosmos-Predict1]. FM trains a denoising network to predict the velocity of a diffusion trajectory, offering a more direct training target and improved sample quality. The training process incorporates a shifted logit-normal distribution for timestep sampling, biasing towards higher noise levels to address redundancy in high-resolution content and improve signal reconstruction.
The denoising network largely reuses the DiT architecture from [Cosmos-Predict1], but with a key modification: the removal of absolute positional embeddings in favor of only relative positional embeddings, enhancing generalization to varying resolutions and sequence lengths. Auxiliary models include:
- **Visual Tokenizer**: WAN2.1 VAE, a causal variational autoencoder, compresses video sequences with a 4×8×8 rate, allowing for the generation of 93 frames (24 latent frames) at 16 fps.
- **Text Encoder**: [Cosmos-Reason1], a decoder-only Vision-Language Model (VLM) specialized for Physical AI, replaces the T5 encoder. It concatenates activations across multiple blocks to produce richer, context-aware embeddings, guiding video generation through cross-attention layers.
[Cosmos-Predict2.5] operates in three modes: Text2World (text-guided generation), Image2World (text + reference image conditioning), and Video2World (text + video sequence conditioning), employing a frame-replacement strategy for temporal consistency in image/video-conditioned modes.

**Training Paradigm:**
A progressive training strategy was adopted, starting with Text2Image at 256p resolution, then progressively introducing Image2World and Video2World tasks and increasing resolution to 480p and 720p. Finally, the Text2World task was added. During training, a masking scheme differentiates conditional inputs, and a modified scheduler ensures 5% of training samples are drawn from the highest 2% of the noise distribution to reduce transition artifacts. The AdamW optimizer was used with specific learning rates and a linearly decaying scheduler with warmup.

**Post-training Techniques:**
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)**: Separate models were fine-tuned on curated, high-quality Physical AI datasets categorized into object permanence, high motion, complex scenes, driving, and robotic manipulation. These domain-specific models were subsequently merged using a Model Soup approach to unify their strengths, augmented by a cooldown stage with 4K videos for enhanced visual detail and motion.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: The VLM-based reward model VideoAlign was used to align model outputs with human preferences for text alignment, motion quality, and visual quality. An advantage-based gradient accumulation over denoising trajectories was employed, with diffusion loss regularization to mitigate reward hacking.
- **Timestep Distillation**: The rCM hybrid forward-reverse joint distillation framework was adopted to accelerate inference, enabling high-fidelity sample generation in as few as 4 steps.

**Infrastructure:**
The training infrastructure leverages FSDP2 for sharding model weights, gradients, and optimizer states, with optimizations like asynchronous distributed checkpointing. Flexible Context Parallelism using a Ulysses-style approach allows efficient handling of long video sequences and dynamic disabling for image iterations. Selective Activation Checkpointing balances memory usage and computational efficiency. An Elastic Reward Service, built for RL post-training, supports VideoAlign and other reward functions, dynamically scaling and using CUDA IPC for efficient data transfer.

**Applications-Specific Methodologies:**
- **[Cosmos-Transfer2.5]**: This control-net style model, built on [Cosmos-Predict2.5], generates world simulations conditioned on multiple spatial control inputs (edges, blur, segmentation, depth). Its architecture distributes control blocks more evenly than its predecessor, [Cosmos-Transfer1]. It was trained on curated control-condition data from Physical AI domains, leveraging tools like Video Depth Anything and SAMv2 for depth and segmentation map generation.
- **Robot Policy Learning**: [Cosmos-Transfer2.5] was used to augment robot policy training by generating diverse, visually altered synthetic videos from real human demonstrations. This involved global edge control, robot-pixel-specific blur control, and text prompts refined by an LLM to vary object appearance, scene backgrounds, and lighting.
- **Driving Simulation**: [Cosmos-Predict2.5] was extended to [Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B/auto/multiview] for multi-view generation, re-purposing the latent temporal dimension for multiple views. For controlled generation, [Cosmos-Transfer2.5-2B/auto/multiview] utilizes "world scenario maps" (projected HD maps and dynamic objects) as control inputs across seven camera views, trained on the RDS-HQ dataset.
- **Multi-view Generation with Camera Control**: [Cosmos-Transfer2.5-2B/robot/multiview] synthesizes videos from multiple target viewpoints given a reference view and camera trajectories. It tokenizes source and target videos, incorporates camera parameters as Plücker raymaps, and trains on datasets like Agibot, MultiCamVideo, and SynCamVideo.
- **Synthetic Data for VLA Training**: [Cosmos-Predict2.5-14B] was post-trained on real robot demonstration videos with natural language instructions. It generates videos of robots executing unseen commands, from which pseudo-action sequences are extracted using a latent action model or inverse-dynamics model, creating synthetic VLA training data.
- **Action-Conditioned World Generation**: [Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B/robot/action-cond] was developed to generate future frames conditioned on a single image and a sequence of robot actions. An action embedder MLP maps actions to tensors, which are added to the timestamp embeddings of the DiT modules for autoregressive generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several findings regarding the performance and capabilities of [Cosmos-Predict2.5] and [Cosmos-Transfer2.5]:

**[Cosmos-Predict2.5] Core Performance:**
- **Benchmarking**: On the PAI-Bench benchmark for physical AI generation, the post-trained [Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B] and [Cosmos-Predict2.5-14B] models achieved performance comparable to the larger Wan2.2 27B-A14B model in Text2World generation and exhibited leading performance in Image2World generation.
- **Human Evaluation**: Human preference studies indicated that the post-trained [Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B], despite being considerably smaller, was preferred over Wan2.2 5B and performed comparably to Wan2.1 14B. The [Cosmos-Predict2.5-14B] model demonstrated a stronger human preference over Wan2.1 14B and achieved performance on par with Wan2.2 27B-A14B, despite having half the parameters. This suggests that the model size increase from 2B to 14B yielded noticeable quality benefits in human perception.
- **Qualitative Results**: Sample videos indicated the model's capacity to generate realistic, high-quality, and physically coherent world simulations across driving, industrial, robotics, and general physics scenarios.

**Training Process Effectiveness:**
- **Supervised Fine-tuning (SFT)**: Domain-specific SFT significantly improved model performance on targeted domains. A model merging approach (Model Soup) successfully combined these specialized capabilities while maintaining performance on general tasks.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: RL post-training, guided by the VideoAlign reward model, demonstrably improved the quality of generated videos, showing increases in text alignment, motion quality, and visual quality scores, and was preferred by human evaluators.
- **Timestep Distillation**: Distilled models were capable of generating high-fidelity samples in only 4 inference steps, achieving quantitative results similar to the larger teacher models.

**[Cosmos-Transfer2.5] Capabilities:**
- **Controlled Generation**: [Cosmos-Transfer2.5-2B] outperformed [Cosmos-Transfer1-7B] on the PAIBench-Transfer benchmark in both adherence to control inputs and overall video quality, despite being 3.5 times smaller. This was attributed to a stronger base model and refined, Physical AI-focused training data.
- **Long Video Generation**: Evaluation using the Averaged Relative Normalized Dover Score (RNDS) showed that [Cosmos-Transfer2.5-2B] exhibited less error accumulation and maintained higher fidelity over long video sequences compared to [Cosmos-Transfer1-7B].

**Applications-Specific Results:**
- **Robot Policy Learning**: In real-robot experiments, a diffusion policy augmented with synthetic data generated by [Cosmos-Transfer2.5-2B] achieved 24 out of 30 successes across 10 novel test-time scenarios. This significantly outperformed policies trained solely on base demonstrations (1/30 successes) or with standard image augmentations (5/30 successes), demonstrating improved robustness and generalization to unseen visual conditions.
- **Driving Simulation**: The multi-view [Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B/auto/multiview] model achieved a significant boost (up to 2.3x) in FVD/FID scores (visual quality metrics) compared to [Cosmos-Predict1-7B-Sample-AV] while maintaining competitive temporal and cross-camera consistency. [Cosmos-Transfer2.5-2B/auto/multiview] also showed substantial improvement (up to 60%) in 3D-cuboid and lane detection metrics on generated videos, indicating better adherence to control signals.
- **Multi-view Generation with Camera Control**: [Cosmos-Transfer2.5-2B/robot/multiview] generated more coherent videos across multiple viewpoints with significantly better cross-view consistency than its single-view counterpart, while maintaining comparable camera trajectory accuracy.
- **Synthetic Data Generation for VLA Training**: The post-trained [Cosmos-Predict2.5-14B/robot/gr00tdream-gr1] model achieved the highest instruction-following scores on the DreamGen GR1 benchmark, demonstrating strong generalization, particularly in object and environment domains, over other competing models.
- **Action-Conditioned World Generation**: [Cosmos-Predict2.5-2B/robot/action-cond] demonstrated superior performance across various quantitative metrics (PSNR, SSIM, Latent L2, FVD) on the Bridge dataset compared to its predecessor. An ablation study indicated that incorporating action conditioning via time embeddings yielded the best performance.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents [Cosmos-Predict2.5] and [Cosmos-Transfer2.5] as foundational video world models that address critical challenges in Physical AI development. The demonstrated enhancements in simulation fidelity, prompt alignment, and control capabilities hold significant implications for various domains.

The ability to generate high-quality, diverse, and physically coherent synthetic videos reliably means that Physical AI systems, such as robots and autonomous vehicles, can be trained and evaluated in virtual environments more effectively. This reduces the reliance on costly, time-consuming, and potentially hazardous real-world data collection and experimentation. Specifically, the models enable:
- **Improved Synthetic Data Generation**: Providing rich, labeled data for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model training, which can lead to more generalizable and robust embodied intelligence.
- **Enhanced Policy Evaluation and Closed-Loop Simulation**: Offering a robust platform for validating robot policies and autonomous driving systems in simulated environments that closely mirror real-world complexities, including unseen scenarios and adversarial visual perturbations. This is crucial for iterating on policy designs quickly and safely.
- **Advanced Robotic Manipulation**: The multi-view and camera-controllable generation capabilities can provide richer scene representations for robots, enabling better perception, planning, and control for complex tasks that require reasoning about occluded objects or multiple viewpoints.
- **Realistic Autonomous Driving Simulation**: The multi-view world generation, conditioned on world scenario maps, allows for the creation of consistent and realistic driving scenarios, which is vital for training and testing perception and planning modules of autonomous vehicles.

By releasing the source code, pre-trained checkpoints, and curated benchmarks, NVIDIA aims to foster an open-source, simulation-first ecosystem for Physical AI. This initiative has the potential to:
- **Lower Barriers to Entry**: Make advanced world simulation tools accessible to a broader community of researchers and developers.
- **Accelerate Research and Development**: Enable faster iteration, specialization, and innovation in robotics, autonomous systems, and embodied reasoning.
- **Promote Reproducibility**: Facilitate the verification and extension of research findings, driving collective progress in the field.

In summary, [Cosmos-Predict2.5] and [Cosmos-Transfer2.5] represent a step in bridging the gap between simulation and real-world deployment for Physical AI. Their capabilities contribute to making embodied intelligence more scalable, safer, and ultimately more effective in real-world applications.
