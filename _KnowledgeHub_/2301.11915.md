# Understanding Self-Supervised Pretraining with Part-Aware Representation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers investigated the capability of self-supervised pretraining methods to learn part-aware representations, finding that contrastive learning and Context AutoEncoder (CAE) effectively capture fine-grained semantic parts, often surpassing supervised baselines on part-level tasks. A combination of these paradigms, as seen in iBOT, yielded robust representations across both object and part levels.

## Problem

- Prior research on understanding self-supervised learning (SSL) primarily focused on augmentation strategies, loss functions, or scaling laws, lacking a deep, quantitative insight into the semantic granularity of learned representations.
- It was unclear if SSL methods, particularly contrastive learning (CL) and masked image modeling (MIM), implicitly or explicitly learn robust part-aware features, despite qualitative observations from Vision Transformers (ViTs) suggesting such capabilities.
- A comprehensive empirical framework was needed to compare and understand the part-awareness capabilities of various state-of-the-art SSL models against fully-supervised baselines across diverse tasks.

## Method

- Proposed and empirically validated two hypotheses: ==contrastive learning== operates as a =='part-to-whole' task== where encoders learn part information and projectors infer whole objects, while ==masked image modeling== acts as a 'part-to-part' task focusing on contextual inter-part relationships.
- Developed a comprehensive empirical evaluation framework using a diverse suite of object-level (e.g., image classification, object retrieval) and ==part-level recognition tasks== (e.g., patch retrieval, part classification, part segmentation).
- Compared representative self-supervised models (MoCo v3, DINO, BEiT, MAE, CAE, iBOT) against a fully-supervised DeiT baseline, all utilizing a frozen ViT-B encoder architecture for evaluation.

## Results

- Self-supervised methods, especially modern CL and CAE, significantly outperformed the supervised DeiT model on part-level recognition tasks (e.g., patch retrieval, part classification, part segmentation).
- The iBOT model, combining contrastive learning and masked image modeling, achieved top or near-top performance across almost all object-level and part-level tasks, even surpassing DeiT in some object-level benchmarks like **ADE20K object segmentation**.
- Contrastive learning's encoded features yielded higher performance on part retrieval, while its projected features showed better object-level retrieval, quantitatively supporting the 'part-to-whole' operational mechanism.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Self-supervised pretraining methods consistently learn strong part-aware representations, often outperforming supervised models on fine-grained part-level tasks, demonstrating a unique strength in capturing local semantics.
> - Contrastive learning's encoder learns rich part information which its projector then generalizes to a more holistic, object-level representation, supporting the 'part-to-whole' hypothesis.
> - Masked image modeling, particularly CAE, excels in learning part-aware features through 'part-to-part' prediction, and the choice of reconstruction target in MIM (e.g., RGB vs. discrete tokens) significantly influences the level of features learned.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated within the rapidly evolving and increasingly critical field of self-supervised representation pretraining (SSP) in computer vision. SSP aims to train powerful visual encoders from vast amounts of unlabeled data, addressing the bottleneck of expensive human annotations. The landscape is currently dominated by two main paradigms: **Contrastive Learning (CL)** and **Masked Image Modeling (MIM)**.

- **Contrastive Learning (CL):** Methods like SimCLR, MoCo, BYOL, and DINO learn representations by maximizing the agreement between different augmented views of the same image, while often pushing away representations of different images or distinct views.
- **Masked Image Modeling (MIM):** Inspired by masked language modeling in NLP (e.g., BERT), methods like MAE, BEiT, and CAE learn by reconstructing masked portions of an image from visible patches.

Prior research on understanding self-supervised learning (SSL) has primarily focused on various aspects:
- **Augmentation strategies:** The role of different data augmentations in CL (Saunshi et al., 2022).
- **Loss functions:** Analyzing the properties and variants of contrastive loss (Chen et al., 2022b).
- **Scaling laws:** Investigating how model size, data volume, and training duration impact MIM performance (Xie et al., 2022b).
- **Attention mechanisms:** Qualitative observations in ViT-based SSL models (DINO, iBOT) showing attention heads attending to semantic regions or object parts.

This work explicitly positions itself as **complementary** to these existing understanding efforts. Instead of focusing on *how* SSL models learn (i.e., the mechanisms of augmentations or loss functions), this paper investigates *what* representations are learned, specifically asking: **Do self-supervised methods learn part-aware representations?**

The motivation for this specific line of inquiry stems from two key observations:
1. **Nature of SSL pretext tasks:** Random views in CL and masked/visible patches in MIM frequently depict *portions* or *parts* of objects rather than entire objects.
2. **Emergent properties of ViTs:** Previous works (DINO, iBOT) on self-supervised Vision Transformers (ViTs) have noted that different attention heads can attend to distinct semantic regions or parts of an object.

By focusing on the capability of learning part-aware representations, this paper delves into a crucial aspect of interpretability and effectiveness for downstream tasks, particularly those requiring fine-grained understanding (e.g., part segmentation, pose estimation, fine-grained classification). It builds upon qualitative observations to provide a quantitative empirical study, thereby offering a deeper semantic understanding of SSL model behavior beyond mere performance metrics. The inclusion of models like iBOT, which combines both CL and MIM, also reflects the contemporary trend towards hybrid SSL approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to gain a deeper **understanding of self-supervised pretraining (SSP) methods by investigating their capability to learn part-aware representations.** This objective is driven by several key motivations and leads to specific hypotheses about the operational mechanisms of CL and MIM.

The primary motivations for this study are:
- **Observation of Pretext Tasks:** The authors note that the common data manipulations in self-supervised learning naturally emphasize object parts. In **contrastive learning**, random cropping (generating "views") often isolates a portion of an object (e.g., a dog's head, leg, or tail). Similarly, in **masked image modeling**, the "masked patches" and "visible patches" inherently represent different parts of an object. This fragmented input suggests that the learning process might inherently foster an understanding of these constituent parts.
- **Emergent ViT Properties:** Previous qualitative studies on Vision Transformers (ViTs) pre-trained with self-supervised methods (e.g., DINO, iBOT) have shown that different attention heads within these models tend to focus on distinct semantic regions or parts of an object. This qualitative evidence provides a strong foundation for a more rigorous, quantitative investigation into part-aware learning.

Based on these motivations, the paper formulates specific explanatory hypotheses for the two main SSP paradigms:
- **Contrastive Learning (CL) as a "Part-to-Whole" Task:** The authors propose that in CL, the encoder learns a representation of a *part* of an object (from a random crop), and a subsequent projection layer then "hallucinates" or infers the representation of the *entire object* from this part. This allows different projected parts from the same object to align, effectively making the projected representations more "whole-object-aware."
- **Masked Image Modeling (MIM) as a "Part-to-Part" Task:** For MIM, the hypothesis is that the model learns to predict the masked patches (one part of an object) from the visible patches (another part of the same object). This process, which involves understanding contextual relationships between different parts, inherently trains the encoder to capture *part-level* semantics.

The overarching goal is to empirically validate these explanations and compare the part-aware capabilities of various state-of-the-art self-supervised models against a fully-supervised baseline. By doing so, the authors aim to provide a novel perspective on the internal workings of SSP models, shedding light on *what kind of semantic information* they primarily encode within their representations. This understanding is crucial for better model design, selection, and application, especially for tasks requiring fine-grained visual comprehension.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a mixed-methods approach, combining qualitative analysis with extensive quantitative empirical comparisons to understand part-aware representation learning in self-supervised models.

#### 3.1. Qualitative Analysis and Hypotheses Formulation

The paper first introduces its core hypotheses:
- **Contrastive Learning (CL) as Part-to-Whole:** Using a typical CL pipeline (Figure 3), the authors explain that random crops provide views of object parts. The encoder generates a "part representation," which is then mapped by a projector to a "whole object representation" to maximize agreement with other views. This implies the encoder itself becomes skilled at capturing part information, which the projector then generalizes. This is illustrated with patch retrieval results (Figure 4), showing encoded representations retrieving similar parts, while projected representations retrieve patches related to the whole object.
- **Masked Image Modeling (MIM) as Part-to-Part:** Taking CAE (Context AutoEncoder) as an example (Figure 5), MIM is described as predicting masked patches (one part) from visible patches (another part), often utilizing positional information. This direct interaction between parts trains the encoder to understand inter-part relationships.

#### 3.2. Empirical Evaluation Framework

The study conducts a comprehensive empirical comparison using a set of representative models and a diverse suite of downstream tasks, categorized into object-level and part-level recognition.

- **Models Under Study:**
 - **Supervised Baseline:** DeiT (Touvron et al., 2020) - a Vision Transformer trained with full supervision, serving as a critical reference point.
 - **Contrastive Learning (CL):** MoCo v3 (Chen et al., 2021), DINO (Caron et al., 2021).
 - **Masked Image Modeling (MIM):** BEiT (Bao et al., 2021), MAE (He et al., 2021), CAE (Chen et al., 2022a).
 - **Combined CL + MIM:** iBOT (Zhou et al., 2021).
 All models used a ViT-B encoder architecture, and publicly available checkpoints were utilized, trained for their respective standard epochs (e.g., 300 for DeiT, up to 1600 for MAE/CAE). Frozen encoders were used for all evaluations to isolate the learned representations.

- **Evaluation Tasks:**
 - **Object-Level Recognition Tasks:** Designed to assess the ability of models to capture high-level, whole-object semantics.
 - **Image Classification:** Linear probing and attentive probing on ImageNet (Deng et al., 2009).
 - **Object Retrieval:** Mean Average Precision (AP) on CIFAR10 (Krizhevsky et al., 2009) and cropped objects from COCO (Lin et al., 2014).
 - **Object-level Semantic Segmentation:** Linear evaluation (4x upsampling + 1x1 conv layer) on ADE20K (Zhou et al., 2019) with 150 object classes.
 - **Part-Level Recognition Tasks:** Specifically designed to measure the capability of models to learn fine-grained, part-aware representations.
 - **Patch Retrieval:** AP on cropped part patches from CUB-200-2011 (Wah et al., 2011) and COCO. Queries are single patches, retrieved based on cosine similarity of their representations. Visualizations on ImageNet also provided (Figures 2, 6, 8).
 - **Part Classification:** Linear probing and attentive probing on part patches from CUB-200-2011 and COCO, classifying them into corresponding part categories.
 - **Part Segmentation:** Linear evaluation (similar to object segmentation) on ADE20K-Part (209 part classes), Pascal-Part (Chen et al., 2014, 193 part categories), and LIP (Gong et al., 2017, 19 human part labels).

- **Experimental Details:**
 - For part retrieval and classification, patches were cropped centered at keypoints and resized to 224x224 to maintain consistent input resolution for the frozen ViT encoders (detailed in Appendix A.3).
 - For segmentation tasks, standard training protocols (learning rate, iterations, batch size) were kept consistent across models for fair comparison.
 - Metrics included Top-1 accuracy, Average Precision (AP), Mean Intersection over Union (mIoU), Mean Accuracy (mAcc), and Pixel Accuracy (aAcc).

This methodical approach ensures that the comparisons are robust and directly address the research questions by evaluating representations across different levels of granularity (object vs. part) and across a variety of common computer vision tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive empirical evaluation yielded several key findings that substantiate the authors' hypotheses and provide a deeper understanding of self-supervised pretraining's ability to learn part-aware representations.

#### 4.1. Object-Level vs. Part-Level Recognition Performance

- **Supervised Superiority at Object-Level (mostly):** The fully-supervised DeiT model generally outperformed most self-supervised methods on object-level recognition tasks (ImageNet classification, object retrieval, and ADE20K object segmentation). This suggests that explicit object-level supervision provides an advantage for understanding whole objects.
- **Self-Supervised Superiority at Part-Level:** Conversely, the majority of self-supervised methods (especially those based on contrastive learning and CAE within MIM) significantly outperformed DeiT on part-level recognition tasks (patch retrieval, part classification, and part segmentation). This is a central finding, directly supporting the claim that self-supervised pretraining is strong at learning part-aware representations. For instance, while DeiT surpassed DINO and CAE in ADE20K object segmentation, DINO and CAE showed better performance in ADE20K part segmentation.

#### 4.2. Insights into Contrastive Learning (CL)

- **Part-to-Whole Hypothesis Confirmed:** CL methods like MoCo v3 and DINO showed higher performance on part retrieval tasks when using their *encoded representations* compared to their *projected representations*. The projected representations, intended for alignment, yielded lower part-level retrieval scores but generally better object-level retrieval scores. This empirically validates the "part-to-whole" explanation: the encoder learns rich part information, which the projector then transforms into a more holistic, object-level representation.
- **Strong Part-Awareness from Encoder:** The encoded features from CL methods were highly effective for part-level tasks, often surpassing DeiT, indicating that the pretext tasks indeed encourage the encoder to capture detailed local semantics.

#### 4.3. Insights into Masked Image Modeling (MIM)

- **CAE Excels in Part-Awareness:** The Context AutoEncoder (CAE) consistently demonstrated strong capabilities in learning part-aware representations, often outperforming both DeiT and CL methods on part-level tasks (e.g., part retrieval, part classification, and part segmentation). This aligns with the "part-to-part" hypothesis, where explicit prediction of masked part representations from visible parts fosters fine-grained understanding.
- **Varied MIM Performance:** Other MIM methods, like BEiT and MAE, generally performed worse than CAE, DeiT, and CL methods on both object-level and most part-level tasks.
 - **MAE's Low-Level Bias:** MAE, while weaker in classification/segmentation, performed exceptionally well in human pose estimation (Appendix A.6), a task requiring middle-level features. Its patch retrieval visualizations often showed similar hues, suggesting a bias towards learning lower-level features (e.g., color, texture) due to its RGB reconstruction target, rather than high-level semantics. This indicates that the choice of reconstruction target in MIM significantly influences the nature of learned features.
 - **BEiT's Pretraining Quality:** BEiT's inferior performance was attributed partly to lower overall representation quality (indicated by lower ImageNet linear probing scores).

#### 4.4. Power of Combination (CL + MIM)

- **iBOT's Superiority:** The iBOT model, which combines both contrastive learning and masked image modeling, consistently achieved the best or near-best performance across almost all object-level and part-level tasks. Notably, iBOT even surpassed the supervised DeiT on some object-level tasks (e.g., ADE20K object segmentation) and demonstrated superior fine-grained understanding across all part segmentation benchmarks. This highlights that combining the strengths of both paradigms can lead to representations that are robust and semantically rich at multiple granularities.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Visualizations

- Patch retrieval visualizations (Figures 2, 6, 8) qualitatively supported the quantitative findings. Self-supervised methods (MoCo v3, DINO, CAE, iBOT) retrieved patches that were semantically more correlated to the query part compared to DeiT, which often retrieved patches covering broader object regions or less precise parts.

In summary, the results strongly indicate that self-supervised pretraining, particularly with modern CL and MIM (like CAE), is highly effective at learning part-aware representations, often surpassing supervised methods in this aspect. The combination of these paradigms, as seen in iBOT, yields the most versatile and powerful representations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes significant contributions to the field of computer vision by providing a novel and empirically-backed understanding of what self-supervised learning (SSL) models actually learn. Its potential impact spans theoretical understanding, future research directions, and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Enhanced Understanding of SSL

- **Semantic Granularity:** The most significant contribution is demonstrating that SSL methods, while often designed for broader visual understanding, implicitly or explicitly excel at learning **part-aware representations**. This shifts the "understanding SSL" narrative beyond just invariance/equivariance to augmentations or loss function properties, adding a crucial dimension of semantic granularity.
- **Validation of Hypotheses:** The empirical validation of the "part-to-whole" explanation for contrastive learning and "part-to-part" for masked image modeling provides intuitive and mechanistic insights into how these complex systems operate at a semantic level. It explains the role of different network components (encoder vs. projector) in CL and the type of information flow in MIM.

#### 5.2. Guidance for Future Research and Model Design

- **Hybrid SSL Architectures:** The outstanding performance of iBOT, which combines contrastive learning and masked image modeling, strongly suggests that hybrid approaches are a highly promising direction. Future research could focus on designing more sophisticated ways to integrate these paradigms to leverage their complementary strengths for multi-level semantic understanding.
- **Target-Aware MIM:** The observation regarding MAE's performance (better at pose estimation, low-level features) linked to its RGB reconstruction target highlights the importance of the chosen reconstruction target in MIM. Future MIM designs can strategically choose targets (e.g., DALL-E tokens, higher-level semantic tokens) to guide the model towards learning specific levels of features (low, middle, or high-level semantics) depending on the desired downstream task.
- **Explicit Part-Awareness:** The findings could inspire the development of new SSL pretext tasks or architectural modifications that explicitly encourage or regularize for part-aware representation learning, rather than relying solely on implicit emergence. This could involve multi-scale part-level supervision or attention mechanisms tailored for part detection.
- **Understanding Supervised vs. Self-Supervised Discrepancies:** The paper delineates a clear functional difference: supervised models are strong at "whole object" understanding due to explicit labels, while SSL excels at "part-level" understanding due to its pretext tasks. This understanding can help choose the right pretraining strategy for specific tasks. For instance, tasks requiring fine-grained detail (e.g., medical image analysis, fault detection in industrial images) might benefit immensely from SSL.
- **Scaling with In-the-Wild Data:** The preliminary exploration with Long-seq-MAE on COCO (Appendix) suggests that pretraining on more diverse, in-the-wild datasets could further enhance part representation learning, as these datasets naturally contain more complex scenes with multiple objects and their parts. This is an exciting avenue for future work.

#### 5.3. Practical Implications and Applications

- **Fine-Grained Recognition:** Models pretrained with SSL methods, especially those validated here, would be excellent candidates for fine-grained image classification (e.g., bird species, car models) and other tasks requiring meticulous attention to small visual cues and object parts.
- **Semantic Segmentation and Pose Estimation:** The strong performance in part segmentation and human pose estimation (demonstrated by MAE in Appendix) shows that these SSL pre-trained encoders are highly suitable as backbones for dense prediction tasks that require detailed spatial and semantic understanding of object components.
- **Robustness to Occlusion:** Learning from masked patches (MIM) inherently equips models with robustness to occlusion, as they learn to infer missing information from visible parts. This could lead to more robust vision systems in real-world scenarios.

In conclusion, this paper significantly advances our comprehension of self-supervised representation learning by focusing on the often-overlooked dimension of part-awareness. It not only explains *how* these models achieve their performance but also *what specific semantic capabilities* they develop, thereby offering invaluable insights for future research and guiding the application of these powerful techniques to a broader range of complex computer vision problems.
