# MedVR: Annotation-Free Medical Visual Reasoning via Agentic Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> MedVR introduces an annotation-free framework that enables medical Vision-Language Models to perform visual reasoning by interactively manipulating images, mimicking human clinical workflows. The framework achieves state-of-the-art performance across six medical Visual Question Answering benchmarks and demonstrates strong out-of-domain generalization.

## Problem

- Existing medical Vision-Language Models (VLMs) rely on text-only reasoning, which limits fine-grained visual analysis and leads to visual hallucinations.
- Developing visually-grounded medical AI is hindered by the high cost and scarcity of fine-grained expert annotations for intermediate visual grounding steps.
- Current reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) paradigms struggle to emulate human clinicians' dynamic interaction with images to form and validate hypotheses.
- The discrepancy between text-only reasoning in VLMs and the need for direct visual evidence in medical diagnostics.

## Method

- **MedVR** is an end-to-end reinforcement learning framework that trains a VLM agent to ==interleave== textual deliberation with image manipulation tools, specifically a 'Zoom-in' function.
- Entropy-guided Visual Regrounding (EVR) leverages the model's intrinsic predictive uncertainty (token-level entropy) to adaptively guide visual exploration, initiating new reasoning paths from points of high spatial uncertainty.
- Consensus-based Credit Assignment (CCA) generates dynamic pseudo-labels for salient image regions by aggregating the visual footprints of successful reasoning trajectories from the EVR ensemble, solving the credit assignment problem without ground-truth spatial annotations.

## Results

- MedVR achieved state-of-the-art results across a comprehensive suite of six public medical Visual Question Answering (VQA) benchmarks, including OmniMedVQA (**96.8%**) and Modality-Specific VQA tasks, outperforming existing VLMs.
- Demonstrated strong out-of-domain (OOD) generalization, achieving SOTA or highly competitive results on PMC-VQA, MedXQA, and PathVQA, suggesting transferable reasoning capabilities.
- Ablation studies confirmed the necessity of both EVR and CCA, with the full MedVR model showing a powerful synergy between uncertainty-aware exploration and self-generated supervision, and EVR correlating strongly with improved localization quality.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A VLM's intrinsic token-level entropy during tool-command generation serves as a reliable, annotation-free indicator of spatial uncertainty, effectively guiding visual exploration.
> - High-confidence, self-generated pseudo-labels for visual grounding can be effectively derived from the collective agreement across multiple successful reasoning trajectories, overcoming the need for human supervision.
> - Interleaving textual deliberation with interactive visual tools, orchestrated by reinforcement learning, empowers medical VLMs to mitigate hallucinations and achieve more robust, verifiable reasoning akin to human clinical practice.
