# SRPO: Self-Referential Policy Optimization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> SRPO (Self-Referential Policy Optimization) enhances Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation by addressing reward sparsity, generating dense, progress-wise rewards using the model's own successful trajectories and latent world representations from V-JEPA 2. The method achieved a 99.2% success rate on the LIBERO benchmark, a 103% relative improvement over its one-shot SFT baseline, and demonstrated strong generalization on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation rely heavily on expert demonstrations, leading to demonstration bias and limited generalization to novel situations.
- Acquiring diverse and high-quality expert demonstrations is expensive, time-consuming, and often impractical for a wide range of tasks and environments, hindering scalability.
- Reinforcement Learning (RL) post-training in VLA domains faces severe reward sparsity, as agents typically receive only binary success/failure feedback, leading to inefficient learning from failed trajectories and high computational costs.

## Method

- **SRPO** introduces a ==self-referential paradigm== that leverages the model's own successful trajectories within the current training batch to generate dense, ==progress-wise rewards== for all trajectories, including failures.
- It extracts robust ==latent world representations== for trajectories using a pre-trained large-scale video-pretrained ==latent world model (V-JEPA 2)==, which captures transferable behavioral patterns.
- Successful trajectory representations are ==clustered== to form stable reference points, and failed trajectories are assigned a reward based on their behavioral similarity (==L2 distance==) to the closest successful cluster center.

## Results

- SRPO achieved a new state-of-the-art success rate of **99.2%** on the LIBERO benchmark, representing a **103%** relative improvement over its initial one-shot Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) baseline, reaching this performance in just **200** RL steps.
- The method demonstrated substantial robustness and generalization on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark (featuring seven perturbation dimensions), achieving a **167%** performance improvement over its one-shot SFT base model and surpassing full-shot SFT policies.
- Qualitative and quantitative analysis showed that SRPO's latent world representation-based reward provides smoother, more monotonic progress curves compared to pixel-level and ImageBind methods, leading to more stable and efficient training across various metrics like Spearman Correlation (**0.998**) and Monotonicity (**0.992**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dense, progress-wise reward signals can be automatically generated for VLA-RL without external expert demonstrations or manual engineering by referencing a model's own successful trajectories.
> - Latent world representations from pre-trained world models provide a task-agnostic and robust metric for measuring behavioral progress, enabling effective reward shaping that is transferable across environments and less sensitive to perceptual variations than pixel-level methods.
> - Integrating this self-referential reward mechanism into a policy optimization framework allows VLA models to efficiently learn from previously uninformative failed trajectories, accelerating training and fostering exploration beyond initial demonstration data.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen significant advancements with Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which leverage large-scale pre-trained vision-language models to enable embodied agents to understand and execute complex instructions. However, a major bottleneck for current VLA systems is their heavy reliance on expert demonstrations. This reliance leads to two critical problems:
1. **Demonstration Bias:** VLA models tend to overfit the specific examples in the training dataset, limiting their ability to generalize to novel situations or surpass human-level performance. They are confined to the state-action distributions observed in demonstrations.
2. **Scalability and Cost:** Acquiring diverse and high-quality expert demonstrations is expensive, time-consuming, and often impractical for a wide range of tasks and environments.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a crucial post-training strategy to overcome these limitations by allowing models to learn through interaction with the environment. Among RL methods, group-based optimization approaches, particularly Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), have gained traction for VLA fine-tuning due to their simplicity and effectiveness.

However, existing VLA-RL methods, including GRPO, face a severe challenge: **reward sparsity**. In robotic tasks, success is often a binary outcome (0 or 1), meaning the agent receives informative feedback only upon task completion. This "sparse reward" problem has several drawbacks:
- **Inefficient Learning:** Failed trajectories, which constitute the majority of early learning attempts, provide no specific guidance on *why* they failed or *how close* they were to success. This wastes valuable interaction data.
- **High Computational Cost:** Multi-turn trajectory rollouts in VLA domains are computationally expensive. Wasting information from failed trajectories exacerbates this cost, making training inefficient.
- **Scalability Limitations:** While some approaches try to provide denser feedback through "process supervision" or "hand-crafted task decompositions," these often require expert demonstrations or task-specific engineering, reintroducing the very scalability issues that RL aims to solve.

SRPO directly addresses these fundamental challenges by introducing a **self-referential paradigm** for generating dense, progress-wise rewards. Instead of relying on external demonstrations, hand-crafted rules, or sparse binary outcomes, SRPO leverages the model's own successful trajectories *within the current training batch* as a dynamic reference. This shifts the paradigm from requiring external labels to efficiently extracting learning signals from internal successes. The innovation of using **latent world representations** from pre-trained world models further distinguishes SRPO, offering a robust, transferable, and task-agnostic way to measure behavioral similarity and progress, avoiding the generalization issues of pixel-level methods or the fine-tuning demands of domain-specific models. By tackling reward sparsity and dependence on external supervision, SRPO aims to push VLA models towards more autonomous, efficient, and robust real-world robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Self-Referential Policy Optimization (SRPO) are:

1. **Mitigate Reward Sparsity in VLA-RL:** The most critical motivation is to overcome the severe reward sparsity prevalent in current VLA-RL methods. Relying solely on binary success indicators (0/1 rewards) leads to inefficient learning, especially given the high computational cost of robotic trajectory rollouts. SRPO aims to provide a dense, informative reward signal for *all* trajectories, including failures, to maximize learning from every interaction.

2. **Eliminate Reliance on Expert Demonstrations and Manual Reward Engineering:** Existing approaches for denser rewards (e.g., process supervision, hand-crafted reward models) often require expensive external expert demonstrations or tedious, task-specific engineering. This creates scalability limitations and contradicts the goal of autonomous learning. SRPO is designed to be fully self-referential, generating progress-wise rewards without any external expert data or manual intervention, thus promoting true autonomous learning.

3. **Enhance Training Efficiency:** By providing progress-wise rewards for failed attempts, SRPO aims to convert otherwise wasted information from unsuccessful trajectories into valuable learning signals. This more efficient utilization of data is expected to significantly accelerate the VLA-RL training process, reducing the number of required environment interactions and computational resources.

4. **Improve Generalization and Robustness:** Traditional VLA models suffer from demonstration bias, leading to poor generalization. SRPO's self-referential nature, combined with the exploratory capabilities of RL, is designed to enable the policy to explore a broader action space and discover novel strategies beyond initial demonstrations. The use of transferable latent world representations also aims to make the progress assessment robust to diverse environments and perturbations, thereby improving generalization and real-world applicability.

5. **Achieve State-of-the-Art Performance:** Ultimately, the framework is motivated to push the performance boundaries of VLA models in robotic manipulation, demonstrating superior success rates compared to existing supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and RL-based methods on challenging benchmarks.

6. **Develop a Task-Agnostic and Transferable Reward Mechanism:** A core challenge in creating dense rewards is their domain specificity. SRPO aims to introduce a reward mechanism based on latent world representations that can naturally capture progress patterns across various environments and tasks, making it highly transferable and generalizable without requiring domain-specific fine-tuning.

In essence, SRPO strives to build a more autonomous, efficient, and capable VLA-RL system by intelligently generating its own dense reward signals from within its own learning process, thereby unlocking new levels of performance and generalization for robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SRPO introduces a novel VLA-RL framework centered on self-referential learning and latent world representations to address reward sparsity. The methodology can be broken down into three key components: problem formulation, world progress reward modeling, and self-referential policy optimization.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation and Key Components

The agent's task is to learn a policy $\pi_{\theta}(a_t|o_t, l)$ that selects an action $a_t$ at time $t$ given observation $o_t$ and a language goal $l$, to maximize cumulative reward. Observations $o_t$ are derived from environment states $z_t$, which are not directly accessible. The environment provides a sparse, terminal reward $R(z_{0:T}, l)$ (0 for failure, 1 for success). SRPO's core idea is to shape this sparse reward into a dense $\hat{R}(o_{0:T}, S)$ for failed trajectories, using a set $S$ of successful trajectories as a reference.

#### 3.2. World Progress Reward Modeling

This is the central innovation for generating dense rewards:
1. **Latent World Representation Extraction:** For both unsuccessful and successful trajectories in a given batch, visual observations ($o_{0:T}$) are encoded into robust, compressed latent world representations. This is achieved using a pre-trained large-scale video-pretrained latent world model, specifically **V-JEPA 2 (Assran et al., 2025)**, as an encoder $W$. This yields a latent embedding $h_i = W(o^{(i)}_{0:T})$ for each trajectory $i$. The advantage of latent representations over raw pixels is their ability to capture transferable behavioral progress patterns, overcoming generalization limitations of pixel-level models.

2. **Self-Referential Success Trajectory Clustering:** The latent representations of the *successful trajectories* within the current batch are gathered into a set $S$. To provide robust and diverse references, these successful trajectory representations are then clustered using the **DBSCAN algorithm (Ester et al., 1996)**. This clustering generates a set of representative cluster centers $C$. Clustering allows for multiple successful strategies to be recognized and provides a more stable reference than individual noisy trajectories.

3. **Behavioral Similarity and Progress-Wise Reward Calculation:** For each *failed trajectory* in the batch, its latent representation $h_i$ is compared to the cluster centers $C$ of the successful trajectories. The behavioral similarity is quantified as the L2 distance $d_i = \min_j(\Vert h_i - h_j \Vert_2)$ to the closest cluster center $h_j \in C$. A smaller distance indicates greater progress towards a successful behavioral pattern.
 Finally, the reward $g_i$ is calculated as:
 $g_i = \begin{cases} 1.0 & \text{for success trajectory} \\ \phi\left(\frac{d_i - \bar{d}}{\sigma_d}\right) & \text{for failed trajectory} \end{cases}$
 where $\phi(\cdot)$ is a sigmoid activation function (scaled by a coefficient $\alpha=0.8$ based on hyperparameter analysis) that transforms the normalized distance into a reward in the range (0, 1). $\bar{d}$ and $\sigma_d$ are the mean and standard deviation of distances for all failed trajectories, providing normalization. This formulation assigns higher rewards to failed trajectories that are "closer" in the latent world representation space to a successful trajectory. The choice of trajectory-level rewards over fine-grained shaping avoids potential pitfalls of overly detailed, hand-crafted dense signals that might lead to suboptimal policies.

#### 3.3. Self-Referential Policy Optimization

SRPO integrates this novel reward mechanism into a policy optimization framework inspired by GRPO.
1. **Advantage Estimation:** Instead of relying solely on sparse outcome rewards for advantage estimation, SRPO uses the newly computed progress-wise rewards ($g_i$) for both successful and failed trajectories. The advantage $\hat{A}_i$ for each trajectory is then calculated by normalizing these rewards: $\hat{A}_i = \frac{g_i - \mu_g}{\sigma_g}$, where $\mu_g$ and $\sigma_g$ are the mean and standard deviation of the progress rewards across the current group of trajectories.

2. **Clipped Surrogate Objective:** Similar to PPO, SRPO utilizes a clipped surrogate objective function $L_{CLIP}$ to ensure stable policy updates. This objective computes a loss based on the ratio of new to old policy probabilities, clipped to prevent excessively large updates, and weighted by the advantage estimate.

3. **Overall SRPO Objective:** The final SRPO objective $L_{SRPO}(\theta)$ maximizes the expected clipped surrogate objective over time steps and trajectories, with an additional KL divergence regularization term $\omega(\theta) = \beta D_{KL}(\pi_{\theta} \Vert \pi_{ref})$ to maintain policy stability and prevent large deviations from a reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$, typically the policy before the update).

This self-referential approach, combined with the robust world model-based progress reward, enables SRPO to efficiently learn from *all* collected trajectories, providing dense, transferable, and automatically generated feedback to guide policy improvement.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SRPO demonstrates significant improvements across several key aspects of VLA-RL: performance, generalization, training efficiency, novel exploration, and real-world applicability.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance on LIBERO Benchmark (RQ1)

- **Superior Success Rate:** SRPO achieves a new state-of-the-art success rate of **99.2%** on the LIBERO benchmark (average across Goal, Spatial, Object, Long suites). This represents a remarkable **103% relative improvement** over its initial one-shot Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) baseline of 48.9%.
- **Efficiency in Post-Training:** The method reaches this performance in just **200 RL steps**, demonstrating the efficacy of online post-training through environmental interaction.
- **Reward Design Superiority:** SRPO significantly outperforms other RL-based methods like SimpleVLA-RL (96.9%) and RLinf (98.0%) that rely on sparse outcome rewards. It also surpasses methods using manually designed process rewards (e.g., TGRPO at 80.7%), confirming that self-reference-based rewards derived from pre-trained world models provide more effective learning signals than task-specific or heuristic alternatives.
- **Limited Input Modalities, High Performance:** Notably, SRPO achieves SOTA performance using only third-person visual observations and language instructions, outperforming several baselines that utilize additional modalities such as multiple camera views, proprioception, or 3D data.

#### 4.2. Strong Generalization Capabilities on LIBERO-Plus Benchmark (RQ2)

- **Robustness to Perturbations:** SRPO shows substantial robustness on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark (which introduces seven dimensions of perturbations like camera changes, robot-init variations, language, light, background, noise, and layout), achieving a **167% performance improvement** over its one-shot SFT base model.
- **Outperforming Full-Shot SFT:** SRPO post-training, even from a one-shot SFT baseline, surpasses the performance of *full-shot SFT* policies (OpenVLA*-Full at 51.1%) across all seven perturbation dimensions, demonstrating its ability to overcome the diversity limitations of limited demonstration data. When trained with augmented data, SRPO achieves 82.1% total success, outperforming OpenVLA-OFT+ (79.5%) which also uses augmented data and additional input modalities.

#### 4.3. Improved Reward Shaping via Latent World Representation (RQ3)

- **Qualitative Superiority:** Compared to pixel-level and ImageBind-based progress rewards, SRPO's latent world representation-based reward produces smoother, more monotonic, and physically plausible progress curves, especially for long-horizon and multi-sub-task scenarios (Figure 3). Pixel-level methods are sensitive to perceptual changes, while ImageBind often exhibits erratic, non-monotonic trends.
- **Quantitative Validation:** On a benchmark of successful and failed trajectories, SRPO significantly outperforms pixel-level and ImageBind methods across five metrics: Spearman Correlation (0.998 vs. 0.125/0.957), Monotonicity (0.992 vs. 0.498/0.837), MMD (0.615 vs. 0.274/0.356), JS Divergence (0.572 vs. 0.548/0.408), and Standardized Mean Difference (188.799 vs. 2.100/18.111) (Table 3). This confirms SRPO provides more reasonable progress rewards for intermediate failure states and better distinguishes between success and failure.
- **Training Effectiveness:** The SRPO-based reward enables stable and efficient learning, consistently outperforming baselines (Figure 4), which either converge slowly (pixel-level) or plateau prematurely (ImageBind).

#### 4.4. Enhanced Training Efficiency (RQ4)

- **Faster Convergence:** SRPO achieves its results in significantly fewer training steps compared to traditional SFT, which requires tens of thousands of steps.
- **Superior to GRPO:** SRPO demonstrates a steeper efficiency slope than GRPO (Figure 5), particularly for long-horizon tasks. This is because SRPO effectively extracts valuable learning signals from near-successful trajectories, which GRPO essentially discards, leading to more efficient policy guidance.

#### 4.5. Motivation for Novel Trajectory Exploration (RQ5)

- **Expanded Action Space:** SRPO enables the policy to explore previously unreachable regions and generate more dispersed trajectories compared to full-shot SFT policies (Figure 6). This indicates a propensity for spatial exploration beyond merely fitting demonstration paths.
- **Discovery of Novel Strategies:** Even when initialized with only a single successful demonstration, online RL fine-tuning with SRPO allows the agent to discover novel strategies, including diverse spatial paths and grasping positions, that were not present in the original limited demonstration data (Figure 7). This highlights SRPO's ability to autonomously acquire new motor skills and affordance understanding.

#### 4.6. Real-World Transferability of Reward Shaping (RQ6)

- **Successful Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The proposed latent world progress rewarding method successfully generalizes to real-world robotic tasks on an X-ARM 7 robot.
- **Policy Backbone Agnostic Improvement:** When applied to both diffusion-based (π0) and autoregressive (π0-FAST) VLA policy backbones, SRPO-enhanced offline RL demonstrates substantial performance improvements over SFT baselines (average gains of +66.8% for π0 and +86.7% for π0-FAST) (Figure 8). This confirms the method's efficacy in adapting to perceptual variations and enabling more efficient policy optimization in physical systems without domain-specific expert demonstrations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Self-Referential Policy Optimization (SRPO) represents a significant advancement in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic manipulation, with profound implications and potential impact across several domains:

1. **Towards More Autonomous and Generalist Robots:** SRPO's ability to learn effectively without relying on expert demonstrations or hand-crafted reward engineering is a crucial step towards truly autonomous robots. This paradigm shift reduces the dependence on human intervention, making it feasible to train robots for a much wider array of complex tasks where demonstrations are scarce or impossible to collect. It moves VLA models closer to being "generalist" in the sense that they can adapt and improve themselves through interaction, rather than being confined by pre-recorded data.

2. **Increased Efficiency and Scalability of Robotic Learning:** By transforming sparse binary rewards into dense, progress-wise signals, SRPO dramatically improves the training efficiency of VLA-RL. This means robots can learn complex manipulation skills in fewer environmental interactions and less computational time. This enhanced efficiency makes advanced robotic capabilities more accessible and scalable, reducing the economic and computational barriers to deploying VLA agents in diverse real-world applications.

3. **Robustness and Generalization in Challenging Environments:** The framework's demonstrated superior generalization capabilities on the LIBERO-Plus benchmark, which introduces various perturbations, highlights its potential for deploying robots in unstructured and dynamic real-world environments. The latent world representation-based reward mechanism, being task-agnostic and robust to perceptual variations, enables policies to perform reliably even when faced with unseen scenarios, lighting conditions, or object layouts. This significantly addresses the "sim-to-real gap" challenge.

4. **Novel Exploration and Skill Discovery:** SRPO encourages policies to explore beyond the confines of initial demonstration data, leading to the discovery of novel and more efficient manipulation strategies. This capability for self-improvement and "out-of-distribution" exploration is vital for robots to handle unforeseen situations and continually refine their skills, potentially surpassing human-designed solutions.

5. **A New Paradigm for Reward Modeling:** The introduction of self-referential learning with latent world representations provides a powerful and generalizable approach to reward modeling. This method can potentially inspire similar techniques in other complex RL domains where reward sparsity is an issue. By leveraging pre-trained world models (like V-JEPA 2), SRPO effectively transforms raw perceptual data into semantically meaningful progress signals, paving the way for more sophisticated and intuitive reward functions.

6. **Impact on Open-Source Robotics and AI:** The association with "OpenMOSS" and `siiRL` suggests that SRPO is intended to be an open-source contribution. This can accelerate research and development in the broader robotics and AI community by providing a robust, efficient, and readily available framework for VLA-RL, fostering further innovation and application.

In conclusion, SRPO offers a transformative approach to VLA-RL, addressing core limitations of current systems. Its self-referential, efficient, and robust learning paradigm has the potential to accelerate the development of highly capable and autonomous robotic agents, pushing the boundaries of what embodied AI can achieve in real-world manipulation tasks.
