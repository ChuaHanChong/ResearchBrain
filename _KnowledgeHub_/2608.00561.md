# Through the LENS: Local Geometric Decomposition of Vision-Language Model Representations

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework named LENS decomposes Vision-Language Model representations into local, low-rank geometric neighborhoods using Mixture of Factor Analyzers, which revealed architecture-specific cross-modal fusion patterns and enabled causal steering of VLM behavior with improved cross-modal semantic retrieval compared to global feature methods.

## Problem

- The internal mechanisms by which Vision-Language Models (VLMs) integrate visual and linguistic information within their shared residual streams remain largely unexplored.
- Current interpretability methods, like Sparse Autoencoders, often rely on identifying global linear features, potentially overlooking locally low-dimensional representational structures crucial for multimodal interactions.
- A precise understanding of how the 'modality gap' is bridged within VLMs, and methods for semantically grounding mixed visual and linguistic components, were lacking.

## Method

- **LENS** (Local Explanation of Neighborhood Subspaces) applies ==Mixture of Factor Analyzers (MFA)== to decompose VLM activations into local, low-rank Gaussian neighborhoods.
- The pipeline extracts multimodal activations from VLM intermediate layers, trains independent MFA models per layer, and employs a judge VLM for automated, cross-modal semantic labeling of these components.
- Causal evaluation procedures, including ==scoped activation steering== and ==cross-modal coefficient-space retrieval==, are used to assess the functional impact and semantic alignment of the identified neighborhoods.

## Results

- LLaVA-1.5-7B showed a monotonic increase in mixed components from **0%** at layer 8 to **5.7%** at layer 29, while Qwen3-VL-8B exhibited a non-monotonic fusion pattern, peaking at **5.7%** mixed components at layer 9.
- MFA centroid steering achieved higher concept alignment and fluency, for example, outperforming VL-SAE by a factor of **5.7** in Vis→Vis steering at LLaVA layer 8, demonstrating effective causal control and cross-modal concept transfer.
- Cross-modal retrieval in the full MFA coefficient space (`b(x)`) significantly improved R@1 scores, increasing from **14.9%** (raw) to **48.6%** for Qwen3-VL Image–Written retrieval and from **27.1%** to **34.7%** for LLaVA Image–Text retrieval.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLMs exhibit distinct, architecture-dependent cross-modal fusion trajectories, such as progressive late fusion in LLaVA-1.5 and an early mixing with re-segregation pattern in Qwen3-VL-8B.
> - The local geometric neighborhoods discovered by LENS are causally effective units, enabling manipulation of VLM outputs within and across modalities, outperforming global feature-based steering baselines.
> - The MFA coefficient space provides a shared semantic coordinate system, significantly enhancing cross-modal retrieval performance and revealing latent semantic correspondences not visible in raw activations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language models (VLMs) have demonstrated advanced capabilities in tasks such as captioning, visual question answering, and instruction following, leading to their adoption in high-stakes applications like medical diagnosis and industrial quality control. Despite their performance, the mechanisms by which these models integrate visual and linguistic information, particularly in their shared residual streams, remain largely unexplored. This lack of understanding hinders comprehensive analysis and debugging.

Current mechanistic interpretability methods frequently address polysemanticity—where individual neurons encode multiple concepts—using techniques like Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs). SAEs represent activations as sparse combinations of globally learned linear features or directions. These methods have been applied to vision encoders, aligned across vision architectures, and utilized for interpreting internal representations in large language models. VL-SAE is an extension that learns a unified concept space for visual and linguistic representations. However, approaches relying on globally shared linear features may fail to capture representational structures that are high-dimensional globally but possess low-dimensional local geometry. Additionally, previous research has identified a "modality gap," indicating that image and text embeddings often occupy distinct geometric regions. The precise manner in which this separation is bridged within the fused VLM residual stream is not well understood.

This work builds upon recent advancements in unimodal language models, where Mixtures of Factor Analyzers (MFA) have been used to model activations as Gaussian neighborhoods with locally low-dimensional structures, enabling concept localization and causal steering. The current research extends this local-geometric approach to VLMs, addressing the challenges inherent in multimodal contexts. These challenges include identifying regions where modalities mix despite inherent cross-modal separation, grounding these mixed components with semantic labels derived from both image patches and text contexts, and enabling modality-specific causal interventions. By focusing on local, low-rank neighborhoods, this study aims to provide interpretable and steerable units for analyzing cross-modal fusion, thereby advancing the mechanistic understanding of VLM internal representations beyond global feature detection.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limited understanding of how Vision-Language Models (VLMs) process and integrate information from disparate modalities. While VLMs are increasingly deployed in critical applications, the intricate mechanisms of cross-modal fusion within their shared residual streams are not well characterized. Existing interpretability methods, predominantly focused on identifying global linear features, may overlook the local, nuanced interactions crucial for understanding multimodal representations.

The specific objectives and motivations for introducing LENS (Local Explanation of Neighborhood Subspaces) are detailed as follows:

1. **Identify and Characterize Cross-Modal Interaction Regions**: A central goal is to develop tools capable of pinpointing where visual and linguistic modalities converge and interact within a VLM's internal representations. This involves characterizing these regions in terms of their semantic content and tracking their prevalence across different layers of the model. Current methods often either treat modalities separately or identify global features, which may obscure the fine-grained interactions.
2. **Overcome Limitations of Global Feature Methods**: Traditional interpretability methods that rely on globally shared linear features (e.g., Sparse Autoencoders) may be insufficient for capturing representations that exhibit high dimensionality globally but low dimensionality locally. The research aims to explore whether a local geometric decomposition, such as that offered by Mixture of Factor Analyzers (MFA), can reveal these intricate, locally low-dimensional structures where multimodal information is blended.
3. **Ground Representations Semantically Across Modalities**: Extending a local-geometric approach to VLMs presents challenges, including the need to interpret neighborhoods that contain both image patches and text contexts. The study aims to develop a robust methodology for automatically assigning concise semantic descriptions to these mixed-modality neighborhoods, drawing evidence from both visual and linguistic inputs. This grounding is essential for making the discovered representations human-interpretable.
4. **Enable Causal Control and Modality-Specific Interventions**: Beyond descriptive analysis, a key objective is to determine if the identified local neighborhoods act as causal levers on VLM behavior. This involves developing and evaluating intervention techniques that can causally steer model outputs by targeting specific component centroids. Crucially, the research seeks to enable and test modality-specific interventions—for instance, verifying if a concept primarily localized in one modality can be accessed and controlled by intervening on representations of the other modality. This addresses whether the "units" of fusion are functionally active and controllable.
5. **Establish Shared Coordinate Systems for Cross-Modal Comparison**: The research aims to evaluate whether the MFA decomposition can induce a shared coordinate system where image and text activations can be directly compared. This objective seeks to identify if the local geometry reveals semantic alignments across modalities that might not be apparent in raw activation spaces or global feature representations.

By addressing these objectives, the research seeks to provide a more granular and causally effective framework for interpreting and understanding the complex process of cross-modal fusion in state-of-the-art VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces LENS (Local Explanation of Neighborhood Subspaces), a method designed to decompose Vision-Language Model (VLM) activations into local low-rank Gaussian neighborhoods. The approach comprises three main stages: multimodal activation extraction, Mixture of Factor Analyzers (MFA) training, and automated cross-modal labeling. These stages are complemented by two causal evaluation procedures: scoped activation steering and cross-modal coefficient-space retrieval.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: Mixture of Factor Analyzers (MFA)

MFA models an activation space as *K* local, low-rank Gaussian neighborhoods. Each data sample *x* ∈ ℝ<sup>*d*</sup> is assumed to be generated by one of *K* components. Conditioned on a discrete latent variable *ω* = *k*, the sample *x* is modeled as:
*x* = *μ*<sub>*k*</sub> + *W*<sub>*k*</sub>*z*<sub>*k*</sub> + *ε*, where *z*<sub>*k*</sub> ~ *N*(0, *I*<sub>*q*</sub>) and *ε* ~ *N*(0, *Ψ*).
Here, *μ*<sub>*k*</sub> ∈ ℝ<sup>*d*</sup> is the centroid of the *k*-th neighborhood, *W*<sub>*k*</sub> ∈ ℝ<sup>*d*×*q*</sup> is the factor-loading matrix whose columns span the *q*-dimensional local subspace (*q* ≪ *d*), *z*<sub>*k*</sub> is the latent factor, and *Ψ* ∈ ℝ<sup>*d*×*d*</sup> is a diagonal covariance matrix for coordinate-wise independent noise. The component covariance is *C*<sub>*k*</sub> = *W*<sub>*k*</sub>*W*<sub>*k*</sub><sup>⊤</sup> + *Ψ*. The marginal density is a weighted sum of Gaussian distributions. The posterior probability *R*<sub>*k*</sub>(*x*) represents the responsibility of component *k* for generating *x*. The local coordinate of *x* within component *k*'s subspace is given by *ẑ*<sub>*k*</sub>(*x*) = *W*<sub>*k*</sub><sup>⊤</sup>*C*<sub>*k*</sub><sup>−1</sup>(*x* - *μ*<sub>*k*</sub>).

#### 3.2. The LENS Pipeline

1. **Multimodal Activation Extraction**:
 - VLMs (LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct) are used to process image-caption pairs from the CC3M dataset.
 - Per-token residual-stream activations are extracted from selected intermediate layers (e.g., LLaVA layers 8, 16, 29; Qwen3 layers 9, 18, 27).
 - A fixed square input resolution (336x336 for LLaVA, 448x448 for Qwen3) is used to ensure deterministic patch geometry.
 - **Stratified Modality Sampling**: To balance the representation of modalities, a maximum of 10 positions are sampled per modality (image and text) for each image-caption pair. This prevents overrepresentation of vision tokens.
 - **Metadata Storage**: Each extracted activation is tagged with its source modality, and additional metadata (spatial patch index for image tokens, windowed token context for text tokens) is stored for downstream labeling.

2. **MFA Training**:
 - A separate MFA model is trained independently for each target layer.
 - The principal hyperparameters are the number of components *K* (set to 8,192) and the local latent rank *q* (set to 10).
 - **Initialization**: Activations are first clustered using K-means on a 256-dimensional random orthonormal projection. The full-space centroids *μ*<sub>*k*</sub> are then initialized as the mean of original activations assigned to these projected clusters. Factor-loading matrices *W*<sub>*k*</sub> are initialized with entries from *N*(0,1), shared diagonal noise *Ψ* as *I*<sub>*d*</sub>, and mixing weights *π*<sub>*k*</sub> uniformly as 1/*K*.
 - **Optimization**: Training minimizes the negative log-likelihood using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 10<sup>-3</sup>, gradient clipping at norm 1.0, and a batch size of 1024.

3. **Automated Cross-Modal Labeling**:
 - **Component Modality Classification**: Components are classified as text-dominated, vision-dominated, or mixed. A component is deemed mixed if neither modality accounts for at least *τ* = 0.8 of its hard-assigned activations (where hard assignment is based on maximum responsibility).
 - **Top Activations**: For selected components, the top 25 activations by responsibility *R*<sub>*k*</sub>(·) are retained as examples.
 - **Visual Patch Extraction**: For each top image activation, the corresponding source image is resized, and a fixed-size window centered on the patch's spatial coordinate is cropped.
 - **Vision-Language Judge-based Synthesis**: A judge VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct) is employed to generate a concise natural-language description for each component. The judge is provided with text snippets and cropped image patches from the top activations, ordered by responsibility, guided by a strict system prompt designed to span both modalities.

#### 3.3. Causal Evaluation Interventions

1. **Scoped Activation Steering**:
 - **Intervention**: During text generation, hidden states *x* at the target layer are interpolated towards a component centroid *μ*<sub>*k*</sub> using the formula *x'* = (1 - *α*)*x* + *αμ*<sub>*k*</sub>, where *α* ∈ [0.15, 0.8] controls the intervention strength.
 - **Scope**: Interventions can be applied to image activations only, text activations only, or both, allowing for modality-specific causal control.
 - **Evaluation**: A Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct-AWQ judge assesses concept alignment (CS) and fluency (FL) of the generated continuations on a {0, 1, 2} scale. The harmonic mean of these average scores is reported. The median best score over an *α* sweep is used for each component.
 - **Baselines**: Compared against DiffMeans (additive steering using global linear directions from concept labels) and VL-SAE (steering along unit directions derived from VL-SAE features), and a non-interventional prompting reference.

2. **Cross-Modal Coefficient-Space Retrieval**:
 - **Shared Representation**: The MFA framework induces a shared coordinate system.
 - **Representations**:
 - **Responsibility vector `r(x)`**: `[R_1(x), ..., R_K(x)]` indicates the activity of each neighborhood.
 - **Full MFA coefficients `b(x)`**: Stacks `R_k(x)` and `R_k(x)ẑ_k(x)` for each component, encoding both neighborhood activity and within-neighborhood position. This forms a representation in `ℝ^(K(1+q))`.
 - **Application**: Cosine similarity in `r`-space or `b`-space is used for cross-modal retrieval without additional contrastive training.
 - **Dataset**: ImageNet (1,000 classes) is used to create natural images, rendered class labels, and textual class labels for controlled comparison.
 - **Tasks**: Image–Text, Written–Text (rendered label vs. text label), and Image–Written (natural image vs. rendered class name) retrieval.
 - **Metric**: Bidirectional R@1 is reported, with 95% confidence intervals from bootstrap resampling.
 - **Baselines**: Raw residual-stream activations, `r(x)`, standard VL-SAE, and aligned VL-SAE representations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated LENS on LLaVA-1.5-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct, examining cross-modal structural patterns, causal steering capabilities, and cross-modal retrieval performance.

#### 4.1. Cross-Modal Structure via Local Geometry

- **Architecture-Dependent Fusion Trajectories**: LENS revealed distinct patterns of cross-modal fusion tied to the VLM architecture.
 - **LLaVA-1.5-7B** exhibited a monotonic increase in mixed components with depth. The number of mixed components increased from 0 at layer 8, to 55 (0.6%) at layer 16, and 472 (5.7%) at layer 29. This indicates that visual and textual neighborhoods are initially separated and progressively interleave in later layers, consistent with a progressive late-fusion mechanism.
 - **Qwen3-VL-8B** displayed a non-monotonic fusion trajectory. It had 464 mixed components (5.7%) at layer 9, decreased to 61 (0.7%) at layer 18, and then rose again to 128 (1.5%) at layer 27. This pattern of early mixing, partial re-segregation, and late recombination aligns with Qwen3-VL's DeepStack design, which injects multi-level visual features into early LLM layers.
- **Semantic Content of Mixed Neighborhoods**: The identified mixed components demonstrated semantic coherence across modalities. For example, a LLaVA component activated on both image patches of rockets and text contexts related to "space exploration," while a Qwen3-VL component showed high responsibility for images of polar bears and text snippets mentioning "polar bears."
- **Robustness**: Ablation studies confirmed that these depth-dependent fusion trajectories remained stable across variations in component counts (*K*), local latent ranks (*q*), and modality-purity thresholds (*τ*), suggesting they are robust architectural signatures rather than artifacts of hyperparameter choices.

#### 4.2. Cross-Modal Activation Steering

- **Performance Against Baselines**: MFA centroid steering generally achieved higher best-sweep scores compared to the global-direction baselines (DiffMeans and VL-SAE) in most early- and middle-layer conditions. For instance, at LLaVA layer 8 in a vision-to-vision steering setting, MFA obtained a harmonic mean score of 1.307, outperforming DiffMeans (0.094) and VL-SAE (0.227) by a factor of 5.7 compared to VL-SAE. This suggests that local geometric neighborhoods provide more effective causal handles for concept manipulation.
- **Cross-Modal Concept Transfer**: Steering effects were observed to transfer across modalities. At LLaVA layer 8, steering vision tokens towards a text-dominated centroid resulted in a Vis→Text score of 1.244, comparable to the same-modality Vis→Vis result. Conversely, steering text tokens toward a vision-dominated centroid yielded a Text→Vis score of 0.921. This indicates that concepts primarily localized in one modality can be causally accessed through interventions on the other.
- **Depth-Dependent Performance Shifts**: The relative steering performance shifted with depth. At early layers of both models, vision-dominated components generally outperformed text-dominated components in most conditions. This pattern reversed at middle layers, where text-dominated components often showed better performance.
- **Human Evaluation**: A human evaluation of 80 generated outputs (balanced across methods) rated concept alignment and fluency. MFA achieved the highest overall median human-evaluation score (1.64), followed by prompting (1.43), VL-SAE (0.31), and DiffMeans (0.30). Prompting slightly outperformed MFA on LLaVA-1.5 (1.84 vs. 1.81), while MFA demonstrated stronger performance on Qwen3-VL-8B (1.43 vs. 0.91). Human ratings showed a moderate correlation with VLM-judge scores (Spearman’s *ρ* = 0.727, Pearson’s *r* = 0.747), supporting the automated evaluation.
- **Late-Layer Steering Challenges**: Steering at late layers (e.g., LLaVA layer 29, Qwen3-VL layer 27) proved less robust, with scores generally dropping. In these layers, DiffMeans occasionally outperformed MFA for text-targeted scopes. Interventions strictly on vision tokens largely failed, suggesting that visual information is already deeply assimilated into the language model's flow, rendering manipulation at original visual token positions less effective.

#### 4.3. Cross-Modal Retrieval in Coefficient Space

- **Enhanced Semantic Correspondences**: The MFA coefficient space, particularly the full MFA coefficients `b(x)`, exposed semantic correspondences that were weakly expressed by raw residual-stream activations. For Qwen3-VL at layer 27, Image–Written R@1 significantly increased from 14.9% for raw activations to 48.6% for `b(x)`. Similarly, at LLaVA layer 29, Image–Text retrieval improved from 27.1% (raw) to 34.7% (`b(x)`). This indicates that the local coordinates capture finer-grained semantic alignment.
- **Intermediate Depth Advantage**: In several middle-layer conditions, raw-activation R@1 dropped to very low levels (0.4%–0.7%, compared to a random baseline of 0.1%), whereas `b(x)` maintained retrieval performance above 27%. This suggests that cross-modal content can remain locally aligned even when global vector similarities provide little retrieval signal.
- **Preservation of Model Differences**: The coefficient space preserved architecture-specific differences in representation. LLaVA consistently performed below 5% on Image–Written retrieval, while Qwen3-VL showed substantially higher performance. This demonstrates that LENS reveals inherent architectural characteristics rather than normalizing them away.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces LENS, a novel framework that significantly contributes to the mechanistic interpretability of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). By decomposing VLM activations into local, low-rank Gaussian neighborhoods using a Mixture of Factor Analyzers (MFA), the study offers a more granular understanding of cross-modal fusion processes than global feature-based approaches.

One of the primary contributions is the revelation of **architecture-dependent fusion trajectories**. The observation that LLaVA-1.5 exhibits progressive late fusion, while Qwen3-VL-8B demonstrates an early mixing, partial re-segregation, and late recombination pattern, provides empirical evidence for distinct internal processing strategies across VLM designs. This finding is stable across various hyperparameter settings, highlighting robust architectural signatures. Such insights can inform future VLM architecture development, guiding engineers to design models with more predictable and interpretable fusion characteristics.

The study also establishes that these local geometric neighborhoods are **causally effective units** for manipulating model behavior. The scoped activation steering experiments demonstrate that interpolating activations towards MFA centroids can redirect VLM generation within and across modalities. MFA consistently outperforms established baselines like DiffMeans and VL-SAE in most evaluated conditions, particularly for vision-targeted interventions. The ability to causally transfer concepts across modalities (e.g., steering visual outputs using text-dominated centroids) suggests that these neighborhoods represent truly integrated multimodal concepts. This causal control capability is crucial for debugging, fine-tuning, and aligning VLMs with desired behaviors or safety constraints. The moderate correlation between human and VLM-judge evaluations further supports the validity of the automated steering metrics, providing confidence in the interpretability of these interventions.

Furthermore, the MFA coefficient space provides a **shared semantic coordinate system** that enhances cross-modal retrieval. By encoding both neighborhood activity and within-neighborhood position, the `b(x)` representation significantly improves retrieval performance (e.g., from 14.9% to 48.6% R@1 for Qwen3-VL Image–Written retrieval) compared to raw activations. This indicates that LENS can uncover latent semantic correspondences that are not easily accessible through global similarity measures, which could have implications for multimodal search, data retrieval, and understanding concept alignment in VLMs.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Interpretability and Transparency**: LENS offers a powerful tool for dissecting the "black box" of VLMs, providing human-interpretable semantic labels for cross-modal interaction regions. This can increase transparency in how VLMs combine information, fostering trust and accountability, especially in high-stakes applications.
- **Targeted Debugging and Bias Mitigation**: By identifying specific components responsible for certain concepts, LENS could enable targeted debugging of VLM failures or biases related to multimodal understanding. Interventions could be designed to reinforce or diminish specific conceptual associations.
- **Improved VLM Design**: Understanding the depth-dependent fusion patterns and the causal efficacy of local neighborhoods can guide the development of new VLM architectures, potentially leading to models that are more efficient in their multimodal integration or more amenable to control.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: This work lays the groundwork for future mechanistic interpretability research, particularly in tracing how specific neural circuits (e.g., attention heads, MLP layers) contribute to the formation, merging, and separation of these multimodal neighborhoods.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The study acknowledges limitations, including its focus on two specific open VLMs and the CC3M/ImageNet datasets. Future work should broaden the evaluation to a wider array of architectures, datasets, and tasks to confirm generalizability. While LENS identifies *what* and *where* fusion occurs, it does not delineate the specific computational circuits responsible for forming these neighborhoods; this remains an area for future mechanistic investigation. The observed decrease in steering reliability at late layers and the sensitivity to local latent rank also warrant further exploration. Finally, applying this framework to analyze concept-specific modality gaps and consistency for safety-relevant concepts could have significant implications.
