# Focal Self-attention for Local-Global Interactions in Vision Transformers

> [!summary] Summary
> The Focal Transformer presents a novel focal self-attention mechanism that efficiently captures both fine-grained local details and coarse-grained global context by attending to nearby tokens at original resolution and distant tokens after pooling. This approach improves performance over Swin Transformer on ImageNet classification (e.g., Focal-Tiny at 82.2% vs. Swin-Tiny at 81.2%), COCO object detection, and ADE20K semantic segmentation.

## Problem

- Standard self-attention in Vision Transformers has quadratic computational complexity with respect to token count, hindering its application to high-resolution images or dense prediction tasks.
- Existing efficient attention mechanisms either use coarse global attention (losing fine details) or restrict to fine-grained local attention (missing long-range dependencies), creating a trade-off between detail and context.
- Pyramidal approaches like PVT progressively reduce spatial resolution but apply uniform attention at every stage, failing to distinguish between near and far token contexts.

## Method

- Introduces ==focal self-attention==, a mechanism that processes nearby tokens at fine granularity and distant tokens at progressively coarser granularity through pooling.
- The mechanism combines ==fine-grained local self-attention== within a window with ==coarse-grained global self-attention== to summarized tokens outside the window.
- Integrates this attention mechanism into a hierarchical ==Focal Transformer== architecture with four stages, enabling efficient multi-scale processing of high-resolution images.

## Results

- Achieves higher ImageNet Top-1 accuracy compared to Swin Transformer variants of similar sizes (e.g., Focal-Tiny: **82.2%** vs. Swin-Tiny: **81.2%**).
- Outperforms Swin Transformer as a backbone for object detection and instance segmentation on COCO, yielding improvements like **44.8** box AP for Focal-Tiny with RetinaNet (vs. Swin-Tiny: **43.9**).
- Establishes state-of-the-art results for semantic segmentation on ADE20K, with Focal-XL achieving **55.4** mIoU.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual information inherently follows a focal pattern, where nearby details are crucial, while distant context can be effectively summarized at a coarser level.
> - The model learns to dynamically balance between local fine-grained attention and global coarse-grained attention across different layers and stages of the network.
> - Ablation studies confirm that both the fine-grained local and coarse-grained global components of focal attention are necessary for achieving optimal performance across tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Vision Transformers (ViTs) have rapidly gained prominence in computer vision since their introduction, demonstrating impressive performance on various tasks like image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation. However, the standard self-attention mechanism in Transformers has a significant limitation: its computational complexity scales quadratically with the number of tokens, making it challenging to process high-resolution images or perform dense prediction tasks efficiently.

Existing approaches to address this limitation typically fall into two categories:
1. Using coarse-grained global self-attention that summarizes or downsamples tokens
2. Restricting attention to fine-grained local self-attention within a limited window

However, these approaches create a trade-off: global self-attention loses fine detail, while local self-attention misses long-range dependencies. The paper introduces focal self-attention, a novel mechanism that combines the best of both worlds by attending to nearby tokens at fine granularity while attending to distant tokens at progressively coarser granularity. This approach effectively balances computational efficiency with the ability to model both local details and global context.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. How Focal Self-attention Works

The focal self-attention mechanism is based on a simple but powerful insight: visual information typically follows a focal pattern where nearby context is more important at fine detail, while distant context can be effectively summarized at a coarser level. This mimics human visual perception, where we focus on details at the center of our gaze while maintaining peripheral awareness at lower resolution.

The core idea of focal self-attention involves three levels of token interactions:

1. **Fine-grained local self-attention**: Each token attends to nearby tokens at the original resolution, capturing detailed local context.

2. **Coarse-grained global self-attention**: Tokens at greater distances are first summarized through pooling operations before being attended to, reducing computational cost while maintaining global awareness.

3. **Window-based implementation**: For efficiency, tokens are grouped into windows, and the same set of surrounding tokens is used for all queries within a window.

The focal self-attention operation can be formally expressed as:

Given a query token at position (i, j), it attends to:
- Fine-grained tokens within a local window
- Summarized (pooled) tokens from regions outside that window
- The token itself with a special self-attention mechanism

This approach significantly reduces computational complexity compared to standard self-attention while maintaining the ability to capture both local details and global context.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Focal Transformer Architecture

The Focal Transformer is built on this novel attention mechanism and follows a hierarchical structure similar to other vision transformer variants:

The architecture consists of four stages:

1. **Stage 1**: Processes tokens at 1/4 of the original image resolution
2. **Stage 2**: Processes tokens at 1/8 of the original image resolution
3. **Stage 3**: Processes tokens at 1/16 of the original image resolution
4. **Stage 4**: Processes tokens at 1/32 of the original image resolution

Each stage contains:
- A patch embedding layer to adjust resolution and feature dimensions
- Multiple Focal Transformer blocks, each containing:
 - Layer normalization
 - Multi-head focal self-attention
 - MLP blocks with GELU activation

This hierarchical design allows the model to process visual information at multiple scales, enabling it to handle high-resolution images efficiently while capturing multi-scale features.

#### 3.2. Implementation of Focal Self-attention

The implementation of focal self-attention involves several key components:

1. **Window partitioning**: The feature map is divided into non-overlapping windows of size M×M.

2. **Local-to-global attention spans**: For each query window, the algorithm defines:
 - A fine-grained local attention region around the window
 - Multiple levels of coarse-grained attention regions at increasing distances
 - Each level uses different pooling rates to summarize tokens

3. **Relative position encoding**: To model spatial relationships, the Focal Transformer uses learnable relative position biases that are added to the attention scores.

4. **Efficient implementation**: The operation is implemented efficiently using window-wise operations and matrix multiplications, making it compatible with modern GPU architectures.

The authors introduce a window-wise approach where all queries within a window share the same set of keys and values, improving computational efficiency. This is combined with window shifting between consecutive layers to enable connections across window boundaries.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Experimental Results

The Focal Transformer demonstrates exceptional performance across various computer vision benchmarks:

#### 4.2. Image Classification on ImageNet

The Focal Transformer variants consistently outperform their Swin Transformer counterparts with similar model sizes:

For example:
- Focal-Tiny: 82.2% Top-1 accuracy (vs. Swin-Tiny: 81.2%)
- Focal-Small: 83.5% Top-1 accuracy (vs. Swin-Small: 83.0%)
- Focal-Base: 83.9% Top-1 accuracy (vs. Swin-Base: 83.3%)

#### 4.3. Object Detection and Instance Segmentation on COCO

When used as a backbone for object detection and instance segmentation, Focal Transformer consistently outperforms Swin Transformer across different detection frameworks:

- With RetinaNet, Focal-Tiny achieves 44.8 box AP (vs. Swin-Tiny: 43.9)
- With Mask R-CNN, Focal-Tiny achieves 43.7 box AP and 41.0 mask AP (vs. Swin-Tiny: 42.0 and 39.8)

#### 4.4. Semantic Segmentation on ADE20K

For semantic segmentation using UperNet, Focal Transformer achieves state-of-the-art results:
- Focal-Base: 50.5 mIoU
- Focal-Large: 53.1 mIoU
- Focal-XL: 55.4 mIoU

These results demonstrate that the Focal Transformer's ability to capture both local details and global context is particularly beneficial for dense prediction tasks.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

The authors conduct comprehensive ablation studies to understand the contribution of different components:

Key findings from the ablation studies:

1. **Window-only baseline**: Using only local window attention achieves 80.1% Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet.
2. **Adding local focal attention**: Extending attention to surrounding windows improves to 81.4%.
3. **Adding global focal attention**: Adding coarse-grained global attention further improves to 81.6%.
4. **Full focal attention**: Combining both local and global focal attention achieves the best performance at 82.2%.

These results clearly demonstrate that both local and global components of focal attention are essential for optimal performance, confirming the effectiveness of the hybrid approach.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

#### 5.1. Comparison with Other Approaches

The Focal Transformer differs from previous vision transformer approaches in several key ways:

1. **Vs. standard ViT**: The standard Vision Transformer applies global self-attention to all tokens uniformly, which is computationally expensive and struggles with high-resolution images.

2. **Vs. local-window approaches (Swin)**: Models like Swin Transformer restrict attention to local windows with occasional window shifting, but miss direct long-range dependencies.

3. **Vs. pyramidal approaches (PVT)**: These models reduce spatial resolution progressively but apply uniform attention at each stage, not distinguishing between near and far contexts.

The Focal Transformer's unique contribution is its principled approach to handling tokens at different distances differently, attending to nearby tokens at fine granularity and distant tokens at coarse granularity. This approach efficiently balances computational complexity with the ability to model both local details and global context.

#### 5.2. Conclusion and Impact

The Focal Transformer presents a significant advancement in vision transformer architecture by introducing the focal self-attention mechanism that effectively captures both fine-grained local details and coarse-grained global context. The experimental results demonstrate that this approach leads to state-of-the-art performance across a range of computer vision tasks.

Key impacts and potential future directions include:

1. **Efficiency for high-resolution tasks**: The focal attention mechanism makes transformer-based approaches more viable for high-resolution images and dense prediction tasks.

2. **General-purpose visual backbone**: The Focal Transformer demonstrates excellent performance as a backbone for various vision tasks, suggesting it could serve as a universal architecture for computer vision applications.

3. **Inspiration for future attention mechanisms**: The principle of treating different spatial regions with different granularity could inspire further innovations in attention mechanisms for vision and beyond.

4. **Broader applications**: The methodology could potentially extend to other domains like video processing, 3D vision, and multi-modal learning, where both local details and global context are important.

By bridging the gap between local and global visual modeling, the Focal Transformer advances the state of the art in vision transformers and provides a promising direction for future research in efficient attention mechanisms.
