# RoboWM-Bench: A Benchmark for Evaluating World Models in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboWM-Bench introduces a manipulation-centric benchmark to evaluate the physical executability of video world models for robotic tasks, revealing a significant discrepancy between visual plausibility and actual robot action success. The benchmark demonstrates that videos depicting human-hand manipulation achieve higher success rates than robotic ones, and that fine-tuning with task-specific data substantially improves performance.

## Problem

- Existing video world models often generate visually realistic predictions that lack physical plausibility or executability by embodied robotic agents.
- Current benchmarks for video world models primarily focus on perceptual metrics like visual fidelity and temporal coherence, overlooking embodiment-grounded physical consistency.
- Real-world robot validation for world models is resource-intensive and inherently limits task diversity and reproducibility across research efforts.

## Method

- **RoboWM-Bench** is a manipulation-centric benchmark that evaluates ==video world models== on their ability to generate ==physically executable actions== in a simulated robotic environment.
- A multi-stage methodology: video generation → conversion of predicted behaviors into executable robot action sequences (via ==human-centric retargeting== or ==robot inverse dynamics models==) → high-fidelity ==real-to-sim validation==.
- Includes diverse manipulation tasks (rigid, articulated, deformable, long-horizon, bimanual) within a reproducible ==LeHome simulation framework==, with success evaluated at step and final task levels.

## Results

- **Human-hand manipulation videos** achieved consistently higher execution success rates than robotic manipulation videos across tasks.
- Fine-tuning **Cosmos-Finetune** on a small robotic manipulation dataset improved embodied executability but limitations remain in 3D spatial reasoning and object localization (grasping failures).
- RoboWM-Bench showed **greater discriminative power** than perceptual plausibility metrics, revealing significant variations in execution accuracy even at high perceptual scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial gap exists between the visual realism of generated videos and their physical executability by robots, indicating that visual plausibility alone is an insufficient metric for robot learning applications.
> - Biases in large-scale pretraining datasets, which predominantly feature human interactions, lead to world models performing better on human-hand manipulation videos compared to robotic ones.
> - Even limited, task-specific fine-tuning data for robotic manipulation can significantly enhance the embodied executability of world models by improving dynamic consistency and structural priors for robot arms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of large-scale video generation models, often referred to as video world models (e.g., Sora, Veo, Wan), has enabled the synthesis of increasingly realistic and temporally coherent future predictions. These advancements have opened avenues for their application in various domains, including robotics, by potentially serving as a scalable source of supervision for robot learning.

Within the robotics community, there is a growing interest in leveraging these models to generate embodied interactions and dynamics that are relevant to robot–environment interactions. This has led to the exploration of robotics-oriented world models for tasks such as video-based robot planning, data generation for robot learning, embodied interaction prediction, and interactive simulation. As these video world models are increasingly envisioned as simulators, planners, or data generators for embodied AI, their evaluation must extend beyond perceptual quality to assess physical consistency and control feasibility.

Existing benchmarks for video world models primarily focus on metrics related to visual fidelity, semantic consistency, and temporal coherence. Examples include VBench, EvalCrafter, and T2VEval. More recent efforts have expanded evaluation to include physical plausibility, with benchmarks like PAI-Bench and VBench-2.0 providing diagnostics on whether generated videos preserve coherent dynamics or possess "intrinsic faithfulness."

However, a gap exists in the systematic evaluation of whether the behaviors depicted in predicted videos can be translated into *executable actions* that accomplish an intended task by a robotic agent. While some studies have initiated embodiment-grounded evaluation, sometimes involving real-world robot validation, these approaches often face challenges regarding cost, scalability, and reproducibility due to the specific hardware requirements of physical experiments. This research introduces a benchmark designed to address this specific gap by providing a scalable, simulation-based, and embodiment-grounded evaluation framework focused on the executability of actions derived from generated videos.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce RoboWM-Bench, a manipulation-centric benchmark designed for the embodiment-grounded evaluation of video world models. The benchmark aims to systematically assess whether predicted behaviors from generated human-hand and robotic manipulation videos can be converted into executable actions that successfully complete manipulation tasks in physically grounded simulation environments.

The motivation for developing RoboWM-Bench stems from several key observations and needs within the field:

- **Discrepancy between Visual Realism and Physical Executability:** Recent video world models have achieved significant progress in generating visually plausible and temporally coherent videos. However, for applications in embodied robotics, perceptual realism alone is often insufficient. Generated interactions must also be physically consistent, dynamically feasible, and, crucially, *executable* by robotic agents. Current evaluation metrics, which often emphasize visual quality or general physical plausibility, do not directly measure whether the depicted actions are viable for robotic control.
- **Need for Action-Level Task Completion Assessment:** Existing benchmarks offer valuable assessments of visual quality and high-level physical plausibility. However, they generally do not provide a systematic framework to evaluate the operational question of whether a predicted behavior can be translated into a sequence of low-level actions that can achieve a specific manipulation task. This gap hinders the development of world models that are genuinely useful for downstream robotic control and learning.
- **Scalability and Reproducibility Challenges of Real-World Evaluation:** While real-world robot validation offers the most direct assessment of executability, it is inherently resource-intensive, time-consuming, and difficult to standardize across different research groups and hardware setups. This limits its scalability for broad comparisons of multiple world models across diverse tasks. A simulation-based approach, when validated for fidelity, can offer a more accessible, reproducible, and scalable alternative.
- **Diverse Manipulation Task Coverage:** To provide a comprehensive assessment, an evaluation benchmark needs to cover a wide array of manipulation scenarios, encompassing different object properties (rigid, articulated, deformable), interaction dynamics, temporal horizons (short-term vs. long-term), and coordination requirements (single-arm vs. bimanual). This diversity is necessary to diagnose the strengths and weaknesses of world models in handling the complexities of robotic manipulation.
- **Guidance for Physically Grounded World Modeling:** By identifying specific failure modes related to execution, such as inaccurate contact prediction or non-physical geometric distortions, RoboWM-Bench aims to provide fine-grained diagnostic feedback. This feedback can guide researchers in developing physically grounded world models that are better aligned with the requirements of robotic manipulation, fostering progress towards more capable embodied AI systems.

In essence, the benchmark is motivated by the need for an operational and diagnostic evaluation framework that moves beyond aesthetic judgments of generated videos to directly measure their utility for physical task completion by robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

RoboWM-Bench proposes a manipulation-centric benchmark for evaluating video world models through embodiment-grounded execution. The methodology involves a pipeline that converts generated human-hand and robotic manipulation videos into executable action sequences, which are then validated in high-fidelity simulation environments.

#### 3.1. Benchmark Overview

The core pipeline of RoboWM-Bench operates as follows:
1. **Scene Initialization:** An initial scene observation and a task description are provided. Real-world scenes are reconstructed in simulation via a real-to-sim pipeline.
2. **Video Generation:** A video world model, conditioned on the initial observation and task description, generates a future manipulation video, either depicting human-hand or robotic interaction.
3. **Video-to-Action Conversion:** The predicted video behaviors are converted into executable robot action sequences using distinct pathways for human-hand and robotic videos.
4. **Embodied Execution:** The extracted action sequences are executed in corresponding high-fidelity simulation environments (simulation-native or real-to-sim reconstructed).
5. **Evaluation:** Executability is assessed using both step-level verification and final task-level success rates.

#### 3.2. High-Fidelity Real-to-Sim Framework

To ensure reproducible and scalable evaluation, RoboWM-Bench utilizes open-source simulation environments, specifically building on the LeHome simulation engine, which supports household manipulation with rigid, articulated, and deformable objects.

- **Simulation Environments:** The benchmark includes both simulation-native scenarios and real-to-sim reconstructions of real-world scenes.
- **Real-to-Sim Pipeline:** This modular pipeline reconstructs real-world scenes in simulation:
 - **Backgrounds:** Reconstructed using 4D Gaussian representations to maintain visual realism and spatial consistency.
 - **Interactive Objects:** Rigid geometries are obtained through 3D segmentation and reconstruction. Articulated and deformable object pairs are constructed following established methods.
 - **Pose Estimation:** Object poses are estimated using pose estimation models, and camera poses are calibrated with FEEPE, with results averaged for stability.

#### 3.3. Embodied Video-to-Action Execution

The benchmark employs two distinct video-to-action pathways:

- **Human-Centric Retargeting:** For human-hand manipulation videos, the approach estimates 3D human hand poses and retargets them to robot end-effector actions.
 - **3D Hand Pose Estimation:** HaMeR is used to reconstruct 3D hand poses from videos.
 - **Control Component Derivation:** Recovered keypoints are used to derive three robot control components:
 - **End-effector Position:** Defined as the midpoint between the thumb and index fingertips.
 - **Orientation:** A plane is fitted through thumb and index finger keypoints, and the x-axis is defined by the line connecting the projections of the two fingertips on this plane, with the z-axis being the plane normal. This aims for stable end-effector poses while preserving local human–object interaction geometry.
 - **Gripper Opening:** Uses the minimum distance between the thumb tip and all other fingertips, rather than just the thumb–index distance.
 - **Motion Stabilization:** Trajectory smoothing and temporal denoising are applied to stabilize retargeted motion signals.

- **Robot-Centric Execution:** For robotic manipulation videos, an Inverse Dynamics Model (IDM) recovers action sequences.
 - **IDM Architecture:** An IDM takes two consecutive image frames and predicts the intermediate joint-space action chunk.
 - **Two-Stage Training:**
 - **Pretraining:** Large-scale simulation data with a Franka arm and paired RGB observations and joint-space actions are used. Background masking is applied to focus on robot motion.
 - **Finetuning:** The IDM is finetuned on a smaller set of real-world trajectories collected with a physical Franka arm, without background masking, to adapt to real observations.

#### 3.4. Manipulation Task Suite with Diverse Complexity

RoboWM-Bench features a diverse task suite covering:
- **Object Properties:** Rigid (e.g., object pickup, trash disposal), articulated (e.g., drawer opening, faucet rotation), and deformable (e.g., towel folding).
- **Interaction Regimes:** Contact precision, spatial reasoning, kinematic constraints, non-rigid interactions.
- **Temporal Horizons:** Short-horizon (e.g., Push Button) to long-horizon compositional tasks (e.g., assembling a hamburger).
- **Coordination Requirements:** Single-arm and bimanual interactions (e.g., object handover, collaborative towel folding).

#### 3.5. Evaluation of Embodied Executability

Executability is evaluated at two levels:
- **Step-Level Verification:** Predetermined key action nodes (e.g., contact events, stable end-effector configurations) are checked for satisfaction of required interaction and dynamical constraints during execution.
- **Task-Level Success:** A trajectory is considered successful only if all key nodes pass their checks and the final task objective is achieved. This hierarchical approach enables both fine-grained failure diagnosis and holistic assessment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted using RoboWM-Bench evaluated state-of-the-art video world models across various human-hand and robotic manipulation tasks, yielding several key findings.

#### 4.1. Embodied Executability of Video World Models

The evaluation of task-level and step-level execution success rates revealed consistent trends:

- **Human-Hand vs. Robotic Videos:** Human-hand videos consistently achieved higher execution success rates compared to robotic manipulation videos. This difference is attributed to potential biases in large-scale video pretraining datasets, which typically contain a greater prevalence of human interactions. Additionally, generated human hands tended to maintain more stable geometry during interaction, whereas robotic manipulators in generated videos were prone to structural distortions that led to execution failures.
- **Impact of Task Complexity:** Execution success rates generally decreased as task complexity increased. Short-horizon tasks, such as "Push Button," were more frequently completed successfully. In contrast, longer-horizon tasks like "Put in Drawer" suffered from error accumulation across multiple interaction stages. Deformable-object manipulation tasks, such as "Fold Towel," presented particular challenges, exhibiting lower success rates in human-hand scenarios.
- **Effect of Manipulation-Specific Fine-tuning:** Fine-tuning video world models on manipulation-specific data demonstrated a notable improvement in robotic executability. The "Cosmos-Finetune" variant substantially outperformed its pretrained counterpart, indicating that even a limited dataset (50 trajectories per task) could enhance the generation of executable robotic behaviors. While fine-tuning mitigated robot-geometry artifacts and improved joint articulation, issues such as inaccurate 3D object localization and unstable grasps persisted as common failure factors.

#### 4.2. Qualitative Analysis of Execution Failures

Qualitative analysis provided insights into the discrepancies between visual plausibility and embodied executability:

- Generated interactions could appear visually plausible in video form but nevertheless fail under physical execution. For instance, in a "Put on Plate" task, a predicted video might show fingers merely touching an object, with the object subsequently being lifted. Such interactions are physically implausible and led to execution failures.
- In tasks like "Open Drawer," predicted motions might resemble closing the drawer or fail to establish a proper grasp, which was accurately reflected by the simulated execution outcome.
- For robotic manipulation videos, failures often stemmed from geometric distortions of the generated robot arm. The end effector might appear to reach its target in the video, but the recovered joint configuration could yield an inaccurate end-effector pose under the robot’s actual forward kinematics.

#### 4.3. Perceptual Plausibility vs. Embodied Executability

A comparison between execution accuracy in RoboWM-Bench and perceptual plausibility scores from PAI-Bench highlighted a crucial distinction:

- The same predicted videos often obtained near-saturated scores on PAI-Bench (a metric for perceptual plausibility) across different world models. This indicated that PAI-Bench provided less discriminative outcomes regarding task completion.
- In contrast, RoboWM-Bench's execution-based evaluation yielded a much wider range of outcomes, more effectively differentiating the capabilities of various models.
- This discrepancy illustrated that actions appearing visually plausible could still be physically infeasible, a nuance that perceptual domain scores did not reliably capture but became evident during embodied execution. This finding underscored the complementary role of RoboWM-Bench.

#### 4.4. Reliability Analysis of RoboWM-Bench

The reliability of the benchmark's key interfaces was validated:

- **Real-to-Sim Execution Consistency:** By replaying real-world robotic trajectories in their corresponding reconstructed simulation environments, it was found that the simulation consistently reproduced both successful and failed task outcomes (100% consistency across tasks). This indicated that the reconstructed environments preserved the task-relevant geometry and interaction constraints necessary for accurate evaluation.
- **Video-to-Action Replay Success:**
 - The human-hand pose tracking and retargeting pipeline achieved near-perfect replay success (average 97.1%). Remaining failures occurred in highly contact-sensitive scenarios (e.g., cylindrical objects in "Stack Cups" or "Pour Water") where minor discrepancies in contact points could destabilize grasps.
 - For robotic videos, the IDM trained with large-scale simulation pretraining followed by real-world finetuning (IDM *Sim+Real*) achieved substantially higher replay success (average 95.7%) compared to an IDM trained solely on real-world trajectories (IDM *Real*, average 71.4%). This result demonstrated the value of scalable simulation data for improving inverse-dynamics prediction. Remaining failures typically involved near-boundary contact cases where small prediction errors could compromise grasp stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

RoboWM-Bench introduces a significant advancement in the evaluation of video world models, particularly for their application in robotic manipulation. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Embodiment-Grounded Evaluation:** RoboWM-Bench is specifically designed to assess "physical executability," a critical but often underexplored dimension of video world model performance in robotics. It moves beyond purely perceptual or high-level physical plausibility metrics to directly measure whether generated behaviors can be converted into actionable commands that achieve tasks in a simulated physical environment.
- **Standardized, Reproducible, and Scalable Protocol:** By leveraging high-fidelity real-to-sim simulation environments and standardized task suites, the benchmark offers a reproducible and scalable evaluation protocol. This addresses the limitations of real-robot experiments, which are often costly and difficult to generalize across different research settings, thereby enabling broader and more frequent comparisons of world models.
- **Diagnostic Framework for World Model Development:** The benchmark's two-level evaluation (step-level and task-level success) provides a fine-grained diagnostic framework. This allows researchers to identify specific failure modes, such as issues with spatial reasoning, contact prediction, handling deformable objects, or geometric distortions of robot components. Such detailed feedback is crucial for guiding the development of more physically consistent and capable world models.
- **Revealing Discrepancies between Visual and Physical Plausibility:** A key finding is the clear discrepancy between visually plausible video generation and actual physical executability. This highlights that models performing well on perceptual metrics may still be inadequate for embodied tasks, underscoring the necessity of execution-based evaluation for robotics.
- **Guiding Physically Grounded AI:** By emphasizing the importance of physically consistent interactions, RoboWM-Bench contributes to the broader goal of developing "physically grounded" AI systems. It provides a means to ensure that world models generate not just plausible-looking futures but futures that respect the laws of physics and are amenable to robotic control.
- **Catalyst for Advanced Robot Learning:** The benchmark can serve as a catalyst for advancing robot learning by providing a reliable method to evaluate potential sources of scalable supervision, such as predicted videos. Understanding the executability of generated content can inform the development of more effective robot policies and data generation techniques.
- **Broader Impact on Safer AI:** By revealing cases where visually plausible generated videos may not correspond to executable manipulation behaviors, RoboWM-Bench supports the safer and more reliable development of embodied AI systems. This diagnostic capability can help prevent the deployment of models whose outputs might appear correct but would fail in real-world physical interactions.
