# RoboWM-Bench: A Benchmark for Evaluating World Models in Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboWM-Bench introduces a manipulation-centric benchmark to evaluate the physical executability of video world models for robotic tasks, revealing a significant discrepancy between visual plausibility and actual robot action success. The benchmark demonstrates that videos depicting human-hand manipulation achieve higher success rates than robotic ones, and that fine-tuning with task-specific data substantially improves performance.

## Problem

- Existing video world models often generate visually realistic predictions that lack physical plausibility or executability by embodied robotic agents.
- Current benchmarks for video world models primarily focus on perceptual metrics like visual fidelity and temporal coherence, overlooking embodiment-grounded physical consistency.
- Real-world robot validation for world models is resource-intensive and inherently limits task diversity and reproducibility across research efforts.

## Method

- **RoboWM-Bench** is a manipulation-centric benchmark that evaluates ==video world models== on their ability to generate ==physically executable actions== in a simulated robotic environment.
- A multi-stage methodology: video generation → conversion of predicted behaviors into executable robot action sequences (via ==human-centric retargeting== or ==robot inverse dynamics models==) → high-fidelity ==real-to-sim validation==.
- Includes diverse manipulation tasks (rigid, articulated, deformable, long-horizon, bimanual) within a reproducible ==LeHome simulation framework==, with success evaluated at step and final task levels.

## Results

- **Human-hand manipulation videos** achieved consistently higher execution success rates than robotic manipulation videos across tasks.
- Fine-tuning **Cosmos-Finetune** on a small robotic manipulation dataset improved embodied executability but limitations remain in 3D spatial reasoning and object localization (grasping failures).
- RoboWM-Bench showed **greater discriminative power** than perceptual plausibility metrics, revealing significant variations in execution accuracy even at high perceptual scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A substantial gap exists between the visual realism of generated videos and their physical executability by robots, indicating that visual plausibility alone is an insufficient metric for robot learning applications.
> - Biases in large-scale pretraining datasets, which predominantly feature human interactions, lead to world models performing better on human-hand manipulation videos compared to robotic ones.
> - Even limited, task-specific fine-tuning data for robotic manipulation can significantly enhance the embodied executability of world models by improving dynamic consistency and structural priors for robot arms.
