# AutoMate: Specialist and Generalist Assembly Policies over Diverse Geometries

> [!summary] Summary
> AutoMate proposes a simulation-based framework for learning robotic assembly policies that handle a wide range of geometrically diverse parts, demonstrating robust zero-shot transfer of both specialist and generalist policies to real-world perception-initialized tasks. The system achieves an average real-world success rate of 86.50% for specialist policies and 84.50% for generalist policies across 20 distinct assemblies without requiring real-world adaptation.

## Problem

- Traditional robotic assembly relies on highly customized engineering and fixtures, lacking adaptability for diverse parts in 'high-mixture' manufacturing settings.
- Prior research often focused on specialist policies for a limited number of parts (1-20), lacking generalizability and robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for contact-rich assembly tasks.
- Developing generalist policies capable of assembling many diverse parts and achieving zero-shot sim-to-real transfer from perception-initialized tasks has been largely unexplored.

## Method

- **AutoMate** develops a comprehensive dataset of ==100 geometrically diverse, interpenetration-free assemblies== compatible with parallelized simulation environments and real-world 3D printing.
- Specialist policies are learned using a novel approach that combines ==disassembly for demonstration generation==, ==reinforcement learning with an imitation objective==, and advanced trajectory matching via ==Dynamic Time Warping (DTW)== or ==Signature Transforms==.
- Generalist policies are trained by encoding part geometries with a ==PointNet autoencoder==, distilling knowledge from specialists via ==Behavior Cloning== and ==DAgger==, and fine-tuning with ==curriculum-based reinforcement learning==.

## Results

- Specialist policies trained with AutoMate's DTW-based approach achieved an **81.50%** mean success rate in simulation across 100 diverse assemblies, significantly outperforming RL-only baselines (**38.45%**).
- A generalist policy (trained on 20 assemblies) achieved an **80.42%** mean success rate in simulation, with curriculum-based RL fine-tuning being critical for its performance.
- Both specialist and generalist policies demonstrated robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, achieving real-world mean success rates of **86.50%** and **84.50%** respectively, often matching or exceeding simulation performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Generating demonstrations through assembly-by-disassembly, combined with a novel imitation reward using Dynamic Time Warping, is highly effective for learning contact-rich specialist assembly policies in simulation.
> - A generalist policy can be effectively learned by distilling knowledge from multiple specialist policies, with an additional curriculum-based RL fine-tuning proving crucial for high success rates.
> - Extensive randomization during simulation training, coupled with advanced real-world perception and physics-based grasp optimization, enables robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for diverse, perception-initialized assembly tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic assembly is a long-standing challenge, critical for manufacturing and increasingly relevant for domestic robotics. Traditionally, industrial robotic assembly relies on highly customized engineering, precise fixtures, and stiff controllers for repetitive motions. This approach is costly, time-consuming, and lacks the adaptability needed for "high-mixture settings"—scenarios involving a diverse range of parts and requiring flexible assembly strategies.

The broader robotics landscape has seen tremendous progress in other areas, such as locomotion and general manipulation, largely driven by advancements in large models (e.g., vision-language-action models) and robust sim-to-real transfer methods. These methods leverage fast, high-fidelity simulators to train policies efficiently and then transfer them to physical robots with minimal real-world adaptation. However, as the authors highlight, these successes have been "extremely nascent for contact-rich tasks in industrial settings, including assembly."

Prior research in robotic assembly often focused on:
- **Specialist Policies:** Training robots to assemble a small number of specific parts (typically 1-5, occasionally up to 15-20). This approach lacks generalizability.
- **Simulation Limitations:** Simulation-based development of generalist policies (unified policies for many diverse parts) and their zero-shot transfer to reality has been largely unexplored.
- **Sim-to-Real Gaps:** Many sim-to-real efforts for assembly either involved large parts, required specialized fixtures, relied on human demonstrations, or necessitated real-world policy adaptation, failing to achieve truly "zero-shot" transfer for complex, perception-initialized tasks. Recent work like IndustReal started addressing zero-shot transfer but on a smaller scale of tasks.

AutoMate directly addresses these gaps by aiming to:
- **Scale Up:** Develop policies that can handle a "wide range" of assemblies (100 distinct parts) rather than just a few.
- **Generalize:** Create both "specialist" (part-specific) and "generalist" (unified) policies.
- **Bridge Sim-to-Real:** Achieve robust "zero-shot sim-to-real transfer" without real-world adaptation, and critically, for "perception-initialized assembly" (where the robot must first perceive and grasp the parts).
- **Integrate Diverse Techniques:** Synthesize algorithms from different fields (manufacturing engineering, character animation, time-series analysis) to create a robust solution.

By providing the "first simulation-based framework for learning specialist and generalist policies over a wide range of assemblies" and the "first system demonstrating zero-shot sim-to-real transfer over such a range," AutoMate significantly pushes the boundaries of robotic assembly, moving it closer to the "large-model paradigm" seen in other areas of robotics. It emphasizes scalability, adaptability, and practical deployment, charting a path toward more flexible and autonomous manufacturing systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind AutoMate stems from the significant limitations of current robotic assembly systems, particularly in high-mixture settings where adaptability to diverse parts and poses is paramount. Traditional industrial robots, designed for repetitive, precise motions, are ill-suited for the inherent variability and contact-rich nature of assembling a wide array of geometrically diverse objects. The authors are inspired by the tremendous progress in other areas of robotics, where large models and robust sim-to-real techniques have proven transformative, and aim to bring these advantages to industrial assembly.

The key objectives of the AutoMate project are multi-faceted and ambitious:

1. **Develop a Comprehensive Dataset and Simulation Environments:** To facilitate scalable research, a core objective was to create a dataset of 100 geometrically diverse, interpenetration-free assemblies that are compatible with both simulation and real-world 3D printing. This is coupled with parallelized simulation environments to enable efficient policy learning for a broad spectrum of realistic assembly problems.
2. **Innovate Learning Methods for Specialist Policies:** The authors aimed to develop a novel, simulation-based approach for learning highly effective part-specific (specialist) policies. This objective specifically sought to overcome the challenges of generating demonstrations and effectively guiding reinforcement learning (RL) in contact-rich assembly tasks by synthesizing techniques like assembly-by-disassembly, RL with an imitation objective, and advanced trajectory matching (Dynamic Time Warping and Signature Transforms).
3. **Establish Learning Methods for Generalist Policies:** Beyond individual part specialists, a critical objective was to develop a unified (generalist) assembly policy capable of solving multiple, diverse assembly tasks. This involved addressing how to effectively represent varied part geometries to a neural network and distill knowledge from multiple specialist policies into a single, more generalized policy, using techniques like geometric encoding, policy distillation (Behavior Cloning, DAgger), and curriculum-based RL fine-tuning.
4. **Demonstrate Robust Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A paramount objective was to achieve seamless, zero-shot transfer of both specialist and generalist policies from simulation to the real world, without requiring any real-world adaptation or human demonstrations. This included demonstrating effectiveness in perception-initialized assembly tasks, where the robot must autonomously identify, grasp, and assemble parts. This objective aims to validate the practical utility and robustness of the developed learning framework.

In essence, AutoMate seeks to provide a generic and robust learning framework that enables robots to adaptively assemble a diverse range of parts, bridging the gap between advanced simulation and real-world industrial applicability, and laying groundwork for the large-model paradigm in industrial robotics.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AutoMate is presented as a learning framework and system comprising four main parts, each addressing a critical aspect of robotic assembly for diverse geometries:

**A. Dataset and Environments**
The foundation of AutoMate is a meticulously curated dataset of **100 interpenetration-free assemblies**, derived from the Fusion 360 Gallery and Assemble Them All datasets. These assemblies consist of two parts (a plug and a socket) and are selected for geometric diversity, graspable surfaces, insertion requirements, and top-down assembly compatibility.
- **Mesh Preprocessing:** A key novelty is the extensive preprocessing of CAD models to ensure simulation compatibility and real-world feasibility. This includes scaling parts to a manipulable size (10 cm longest edge), reorientation, translation, and crucially, **depenetration** and **clearance generation** (0.5 mm radial clearance) to prevent unstable dynamics in physics simulators. Chamfering is optionally applied for realism, and meshes are subdivided for stable SDF-based contact simulation.
- **Parallelized Simulation Environments:** Ready-to-use environments for all 100 assemblies are provided in a robotics simulator (NVIDIA Isaac Sim,). These environments feature a Franka Panda robot, randomized initial poses for the plug (in gripper) and socket (on tabletop), and utility functions for key algorithms.

**B. Learning Specialist Policies**
Training policies for contact-rich assembly is challenging. AutoMate proposes a novel simulation-based approach combining three distinct algorithms:
1. **Assembly-by-Disassembly for Demonstration Generation:** Instead of difficult human demonstrations or motion planning for assembly (a narrow passage problem), AutoMate generates demonstrations by performing disassembly.
 - **Grasp Optimization:** For each assembly, a physics-based grasp optimization procedure identifies a high-performing grasp. This involves sampling 100 kinematic/geometry-feasible grasps and evaluating them over 1000 simulated disassembly trials (randomizing assembly pose, executing grasp, lifting plug, checking if still held).
 - **Path Reversal:** From 100 successful disassembly trials using the best grasp, only the **end-effector position paths** are recorded and reversed to create assembly demonstration paths. Velocities and accelerations are *not* reversed, acknowledging their non-physical nature when simply inverted.
2. **Reinforcement Learning with Imitation Objective:**
 - **Baseline RL:** Uses Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with an asymmetric actor-critic architecture. The actor observes joint angles, current/goal end-effector pose, and relative target pose (with noise added to socket-related observations for sim-to-real robustness). The critic has privileged information (joint/end-effector velocities, plug pose). Actions are incremental pose targets for a task-space impedance controller. The baseline reward penalizes distance-to-goal (SDF-based), simulation error (SAPU), and rewards task difficulty (SBC), with a bonus for success.
 - **Imitation Augmentation:** A novel imitation reward term ($R_I$) is added to the baseline reward: $R_t = \omega_B R_B_t + \omega_I R_I_t$. This term encourages the robot to mimic the generated disassembly paths.
3. **Trajectory Matching for Imitation Reward:** To define $R_I$, the method needs to quantify how well the robot's current path segment matches a demonstration path. This is challenging due to disparate velocities and sampling rates.
 - **Dynamic Time Warping (DTW):** A dynamic programming algorithm that computes the minimum-cost alignment between two time series, robust to variations in speed and sampling. For imitation, DTW calculates the distance between a windowed segment of the robot's end-effector path and segments of all reversed disassembly paths, selecting the minimum cost for the reward.
 - **Signature Transforms:** An alternative mathematical technique that represents trajectories as a collection of path integrals, inherently invariant to translation and reparameterization. The L2 norm between signatures of the robot's full trajectory and demonstration path segments is used as the cost.

**C. Learning Generalist Policies**
To create a policy that can assemble *multiple* diverse parts, three techniques are combined:
1. **Geometric Encoding:** A **PointNet autoencoder** is pretrained on point clouds sampled from plug and socket meshes. The encoder learns compact latent representations ($z_p, z_s$) of object geometry, which are then concatenated and fed as input to the generalist policy network. This allows the policy to reason about different part shapes.
2. **Policy Distillation:** Knowledge from trained specialist policies is transferred to a single generalist policy.
 - **Behavior Cloning (BC):** The generalist policy is initially trained via supervised learning to imitate state-action pairs collected from successful rollouts of *all* specialist policies.
 - **DAgger:** Iteratively reduces covariate shift by deploying the current generalist policy, querying the specialist policies for expert actions on the visited states, and adding these new (state, expert action) pairs to the training dataset for further BC.
3. **Curriculum-based RL Fine-tuning:** The distilled generalist policy undergoes a final RL fine-tuning phase using the PPO algorithm. A **sampling-based curriculum (SBC)** is crucial here, gradually increasing the difficulty of initial-pose randomization (by increasing the lower bound of initial plug height) as the policy improves, ensuring robust learning.

**D. Sim-to-Real Transfer**
AutoMate aims for **zero-shot transfer** without real-world adaptation. The robustness is built into the simulation training through:
- **Extensive Randomization:** Broad initial-pose randomization (Table II) and observation noise (Table V) during training.
- **State-of-the-Art Perception:** A real-world system using a wrist-mounted RGB-D camera, advanced segmentation (Segment Anything), and a state-of-the-art 6D pose estimator (FoundationPose) to accurately estimate part poses from real-world images. This replaces simulated ground-truth poses for deployment.
- **Standardized Control:** Identical control gains and action scales are used for all assemblies, avoiding task-specific tuning.
- **Optimized Grasps:** Physics-based grasp optimization ensures robust real-world grasping.

The overall approach meticulously integrates advanced machine learning, robotics control, and computer vision techniques, leveraging large-scale simulation to tackle the long-standing challenges of flexible and robust robotic assembly.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The AutoMate project presents a comprehensive set of findings, demonstrating the effectiveness of its proposed framework in both simulation and real-world deployments.

**A. Evaluations of Specialist Policies (Simulation)**

1. **Trajectory-Matching Effectiveness:**
 - Comparison of four approaches: IndustReal (RL-only), State-based Matching, Dynamic Time Warping (DTW), and Signature Transform.
 - IndustReal showed the lowest success rates (38.45% mean over 100 assemblies), highlighting the necessity of imitation.
 - State-based matching improved performance significantly (59.11%), but was still insufficient.
 - **DTW and Signature Transforms consistently yielded the highest success rates**, with DTW slightly outperforming (81.50% vs. 77.46% mean). This validates the core methodological innovation of using advanced trajectory matching.
2. **Overall Performance vs. Baselines:**
 - Compared AutoMate (DTW-based) against naive control baselines (Go to Goal, Top Down, Follow Trajectory) and the state-of-the-art RL-only baseline (IndustReal).
 - Naive baselines performed poorly (15-21% mean success), lacking robustness to errors and contact.
 - IndustReal showed improvement (38.45%), but **AutoMate significantly outperformed all baselines (81.50% mean success rate)** across all 100 assemblies.
3. **Performance Across Diverse Assemblies:**
 - AutoMate achieved $\approx$80% success rates or higher for 80 out of 100 assemblies, and 90% or higher for 55 assemblies.
 - Challenging assemblies often involved small-diameter plugs, sockets with limited contact surfaces, or complex internal features.
4. **Robustness to Randomization and Noise:** Specialist policies demonstrated high robustness to in-distribution initial-pose randomization and observation noise. Out-of-distribution performance moderately degraded, but could be effectively improved through policy fine-tuning on the expanded ranges.

**B. Evaluation of Generalist Policy (Simulation)**

1. **Policy Distillation Approach:**
 - Four approaches for training a generalist policy over 20 diverse assemblies: Behavior Cloning (BC), BC + DAgger, BC + DAgger + RL fine-tuning, and BC + DAgger + RL fine-tuning with Sampling-Based Curriculum (SBC).
 - BC alone performed poorly (28.84% mean), and BC + DAgger showed only marginal improvement (31.06%), primarily due to covariate shift.
 - BC + DAgger + RL fine-tuning achieved substantial improvement (52.85%), highlighting the value of RL-based refinement.
 - **BC + DAgger + RL fine-tuning with SBC was the most effective approach (80.42% mean success rate)**, underscoring the critical role of curriculum learning during fine-tuning.
 - An RL policy trained from scratch with SBC achieved a lower success rate (48.43%), demonstrating the value of distillation from specialists.
2. **Scaling Law:** The performance of the generalist policy showed high success rates (approx. 80%) when trained from 10-20 specialists. However, performance steeply dropped for generalists trained on 30-40 specialists (to 55% and 30% respectively) and remained low for more specialists (approx. 20%). This indicates a current limitation in scaling generalist policies to a very large number of diverse tasks.

**C. Sim-to-Real Transfer (Real World)**

1. **Specialist Policies Transfer:**
 - 20 specialist policies were deployed zero-shot to the real world over 200 trials.
 - Real-world mean success rate: 86.50% ($ \pm $ 16.52%).
 - Corresponding simulation mean success rate: 90.65% ($ \pm $ 13.07%).
 - Real-world performance was comparable to simulation, and for 11/20 assemblies, real-world success rates were even *better*, suggesting that the simulated training conditions were sufficiently adverse.
2. **Generalist Policies Transfer:**
 - The generalist policy (trained on the 20 assemblies) was deployed zero-shot to the real world over 200 trials.
 - Real-world mean success rate: 84.50% ($ \pm $ 21.79%).
 - Corresponding simulation mean success rate: 80.42% ($ \pm $ 20.93%).
 - Real-world generalist performance was *better* than simulation for 16/20 assemblies and within 2.0% of real-world specialist performance on average. Qualitatively, the generalist exhibited smoother motions.
3. **Perception-Initialized Assembly:**
 - Demonstrated an end-to-end workflow (perception, grasping, insertion) for 5 distinct assemblies.
 - Specialist policies achieved 90.0% mean success rate, within 4.0% of isolated policy evaluations.
 - Generalist policies achieved 86.0% mean success rate, within 10.0% of isolated policy evaluations.
 - These results validate the integration of 6-DOF pose estimation, grasp optimization, and the learned policies for practical, unconstrained assembly tasks.

In summary, AutoMate successfully demonstrates a robust and scalable framework for robotic assembly, with its novel specialist policy learning methods (especially DTW for imitation) and an effective generalist policy distillation strategy proving crucial. Critically, it achieves strong zero-shot sim-to-real transfer, showcasing the practical utility of the entire system for complex, contact-rich manipulation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The AutoMate project represents a significant advancement in the field of robotic assembly, offering a comprehensive framework that addresses several long-standing challenges and opens new avenues for future research and industrial applications.

**Key Significance:**

1. **First-of-its-Kind Scalability for Assembly:** AutoMate is the first simulation-based framework to enable the learning of both specialist and generalist policies over a *wide range* (100) of diverse assemblies. This addresses the critical need for adaptability in high-mixture manufacturing settings, moving beyond the traditional limitation of policies being tailored for only a handful of parts.
2. **Robust Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer at Scale:** A major breakthrough is the demonstration of robust zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for this broad range of assemblies, including perception-initialized tasks. This eliminates the costly and time-consuming process of real-world data collection or adaptation, a bottleneck for many robotic applications. The fact that real-world performance often matches or even slightly exceeds simulation performance underscores the effectiveness of their domain randomization and robust training strategies.
3. **Novel Integration of Diverse Algorithms:** The work successfully synthesizes techniques from seemingly disparate fields—assembly-by-disassembly (manufacturing engineering), RL with imitation (character animation), and dynamic time warping/signature transforms (time-series analysis)—into a cohesive and powerful solution. This interdisciplinary approach provides a generic and robust methodology for contact-rich tasks.
4. **Comprehensive Dataset and Environments:** The release of the 100 interpenetration-free, 3D-printable assembly models and their corresponding parallelized simulation environments is a substantial contribution to the research community. This standardized benchmark will greatly accelerate future research in robotic assembly, enabling fair comparisons and scalable experimentation.
5. **Foundation for Large Models in Industrial Robotics:** By demonstrating the feasibility of learning generalized policies and their transfer to reality, AutoMate takes a crucial step towards realizing the "large-model paradigm" for industrial robotics. This vision involves training highly capable, generalized models that can perform a vast array of tasks, similar to how large language models have transformed NLP.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Manufacturing:** The framework could revolutionize flexible manufacturing, allowing robots to handle product variations and diverse part portfolios without extensive re-engineering, specialized fixtures, or reprogramming. This could lead to faster product development cycles, reduced costs, and increased automation in industries with high-mixture production.
- **Robotics Research:** The open-sourced dataset, environments, and algorithmic implementations will serve as a valuable resource, fostering innovation in areas like contact-rich manipulation, generalizable skill learning, and robust sim-to-real transfer. It sets a new benchmark for what is achievable in robotic assembly.
- **Skill Transfer and Generalization:** The insights into specialist-to-generalist distillation and the scaling laws provide critical guidance for developing more capable robotic agents. Future work can build upon these findings to create generalists that scale to an even wider range of tasks.
- **Perception-Initialized Autonomy:** The successful demonstration of perception-initialized assembly, from haphazard part placement to precise insertion, is a significant step towards fully autonomous robotic systems that can operate in unstructured or semi-structured environments, reducing reliance on precise fixturing or human intervention.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**

The authors acknowledge current limitations, such as the specialist policies struggling with some irregular geometries or sudden slips, and the generalist policy's performance degrading when scaling beyond 20 specialists. They propose exciting future work, including:
- Developing accelerated sequence planners for multi-part assemblies.
- Generalizing trajectory-matching approaches to SE(3) transforms for complex reorientations.
- Distilling generalist policies from an even larger number of specialists using more powerful architectures and model capacities.

In conclusion, AutoMate represents a landmark achievement in robotic assembly. By rigorously addressing the challenges of data generation, policy learning, and sim-to-real transfer at an unprecedented scale, it provides a robust framework that pushes the boundaries of autonomous manipulation and offers a clear vision for the future of intelligent robotic systems in industrial settings.
