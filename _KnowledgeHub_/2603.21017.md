# Dreaming the Unseen: World Model-regularized Diffusion Policy for Out-of-Distribution Robustness

> [!summary] Summary
> The Dream Diffusion Policy (DDP) integrates a diffusion policy with a predictive diffusion world model to enhance robotic manipulation robustness under severe out-of-distribution (OOD) visual disturbances. This system dynamically shifts from relying on corrupted external vision to its internal predictive "imagination," achieving up to a 73.8% success rate on OOD MetaWorld tasks and an 83.3% average success rate across real-world OOD tasks, significantly outperforming existing baselines.

## Problem

- Visuomotor policies, particularly diffusion models, fail catastrophically when observations deviate from the training distribution due to unexpected disturbances like object displacements or occlusions.
- Existing OOD mitigation strategies often introduce architectural complexity, are computationally expensive, or aggressively alter learned actions, potentially compromising skill fidelity.
- Traditional world model integrations are often weakly coupled, limiting their ability to actively substitute corrupted visual inputs and sustain control under severe OOD shifts.

## Method

- A **Dream Diffusion Policy (DDP)** framework that co-optimizes a ==diffusion policy== and a ==diffusion world model==, both sharing a ==3D visual encoder==, to learn unified geometric and dynamic representations.
- An inference mechanism dynamically shifts between physical reality and internal "imagination," detecting OOD events via a ==real-imagination discrepancy== and bypassing corrupted visual inputs.
- When in OOD mode, the policy generates actions conditioned on ==autoregressively forecasted latent dynamics== from the world model, performing localized physical recoveries upon subtask completion to realign with physical objects.

## Results

- DDP achieved a **73.8%** average success rate on MetaWorld tasks and an **83.3%** average success rate in real-world tasks under OOD conditions, drastically outperforming state-of-the-art baselines like DP3 (**0.8%** and **3.3%** respectively).
- The policy demonstrated robust performance under severe visual occlusion, seamlessly completing tasks like 'Stack Blocks' and 'Pour Tea' by relying entirely on autoregressive imagination.
- Continuous reliance on **100%** imagined latents after initial observation maintained a **61.4%** success rate in MetaWorld and **76.7%** in real-world tasks, validating the stability of the internal world model for control.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Deep, co-optimized integration of a diffusion world model actively regularizes the diffusion policy, enabling it to generate actions from internally imagined latent states when real observations are corrupted.
> - The "real-imagination discrepancy" serves as an effective, intuitive detector for out-of-distribution events, allowing the system to switch from physical perception to internal prediction.
> - Recursive imagination, sustained by the world model's autoregressive forecasting, enables coherent and continuous control trajectories even in the complete absence of reliable external visual input.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has seen advancements with visuomotor policies, particularly those based on diffusion models, which excel at generating complex, high-dimensional motor commands from visual observations. These policies model action generation as a conditional denoising process and have demonstrated dexterity and robustness in various intricate tasks.

However, a fundamental limitation of standard visuomotor imitation learning is its dependence on continuous, uncorrupted visual data. In real-world environments, unexpected disturbances such as object displacements, occlusions, or sensor noise can push observations into out-of-distribution (OOD) states. Under such severe covariate shifts, conventional policies often fail catastrophically due to a lack of explicit physical reasoning or mechanisms to adapt to unseen states.

Existing approaches to mitigate OOD challenges include:
- **Domain randomization:** Expanding training diversity by varying environmental parameters.
- **Test-time adaptation:** Adjusting policy parameters during deployment to new conditions.
- **Dataset expansion:** Augmenting training data with synthetic spatial perturbations.
- **Large pretrained visual models:** Leveraging general visual representations for robustness.
- **Representational and physical priors:** Incorporating explicit knowledge about objects or physics.
- **Inverse recovery policies:** Learning specific policies to recover from failure states.

While these methods offer some mitigation, they often introduce architectural complexity, can be computationally expensive, or may aggressively alter the expert-derived actions during adaptation, potentially compromising the fidelity of learned skills. Moreover, many still assume the presence of a continuous visual stream or cannot reconstruct missing visual information effectively.

World models, which learn internal representations to predict environmental dynamics, have emerged as a crucial component for planning, sample-efficient learning, and predictive reasoning in embodied AI. Diffusion-based world models, specifically, have shown promise in forecasting complex physical dynamics. However, their integration into control policies often treats them as weakly coupled auxiliary modules, which limits their ability to actively substitute corrupted visual inputs and sustain control under severe OOD shifts.

This work addresses the gap by proposing a tighter, active integration of a diffusion world model into a diffusion policy. It aims to enable a policy to dynamically switch from relying on potentially corrupted external vision to its internal predictive "imagination," thereby enhancing resilience to severe OOD disturbances, analogous to how humans might rely on kinesthetic intuition when visual cues are unreliable.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust visuomotor control framework that can effectively operate under severe out-of-distribution (OOD) disturbances, such as unexpected object displacements or visual corruptions, which typically cause catastrophic failures in conventional diffusion policies.

The key motivations driving this work are:
1. **Addressing the brittleness of existing diffusion policies:** While diffusion policies demonstrate high dexterity in visuomotor control, their performance degrades significantly when environmental observations deviate from the training distribution. This vulnerability limits their deployment in unstructured and dynamic real-world settings.
2. **Drawing inspiration from human cognitive strategies:** The authors observe that humans often rely on internal intuition and predictive capabilities when external visual information is unreliable or chaotic. This paper seeks to translate this principle into robotic control, endowing a diffusion policy with a similar "internal intuition" or predictive capability.
3. **Enhancing the role of world models:** Traditional integrations of world models with control policies often treat them as passive or weakly coupled components. The motivation is to elevate the world model from a mere auxiliary module to an active, tightly integrated part of the policy that can dynamically substitute corrupted visual inputs with imagined latent states, thereby sustaining control.
4. **Enabling active recovery from OOD states:** Instead of merely attempting to adapt to OOD observations, the goal is to develop a mechanism that can detect OOD anomalies, actively bypass the corrupted visual stream using internal predictions, and then trigger a localized physical recovery to bring the environment or the robot back to an in-distribution (ID) state.
5. **Preserving high-fidelity skills:** Existing mitigation strategies often involve complex architectural changes or aggressive alterations of expert-derived actions during adaptation, which can compromise the high-fidelity skills learned via imitation learning. This work aims to preserve these skills by providing a mechanism to navigate OOD situations without overwriting the core policy.

In summary, the key objective is to create a Dream Diffusion Policy (DDP) that, through co-optimization with a diffusion world model and a shared 3D visual encoder, can achieve robust state-prediction capabilities. This capability allows the policy to detect discrepancies between real and imagined states, abandon corrupted visual streams, rely on autoregressively forecasted latent dynamics for action generation, and subsequently realign with physical reality, thus enabling resilient robotic manipulation in dynamic and unpredictable environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The Dream Diffusion Policy (DDP) framework integrates a diffusion policy with a predictive diffusion world model through a shared 3D visual encoder. The approach consists of a co-optimization training pipeline and a multi-stage inference mechanism for OOD robustness.

#### 3.1. Action and Latent Embedding Chunking

To manage temporal dependencies and mitigate compounding errors, both the policy and world model utilize chunking:
- **Action Chunking:** The Diffusion Policy predicts an action sequence over a horizon `H`, but only executes a sub-sequence of length `N` (an action chunk).
- **Latent Embedding Observation Chunking:** The World Model operates over the same `N`-step chunk, predicting `N` future latent states conditioned on `M` historical observations and the `N`-step planned action chunk. For continuous OOD rollouts, a sliding window is used, where the last `M` predicted latents become the historical condition for the next cycle.

#### 3.2. Shared 3D Visual Encoder (Eψ)

A shared 3D encoder is central to the DDP. It processes raw historical observations `X_0...M-1`, extracting unified geometric latent embeddings. This involves:
- **PointNet:** Processes point clouds to extract spatial features.
- **MLP:** Encodes proprioceptive states (e.g., end-effector pose).
- **Concatenation:** Combines features from both streams into a unified context `O_0...M-1`. This shared representation is designed to capture robust geometric priors essential for both policy execution and world model prediction.

#### 3.3. Diffusion Policy (πθ)

Building on Diffusion Policy (DP3), action generation is framed as a conditional denoising task.
- **Input:** The encoded history context `O_0...M-1` serves as a global condition.
- **Architecture:** A 1D Conditional U-Net.
- **Conditioning:** The context is injected into the U-Net's residual blocks via Feature-wise Linear Modulation (FiLM).
- **Process:** Gaussian noise `ϵ_p` is added to ground-truth actions `a_0...H-1` to create a noisy trajectory `a^k_p` at a random timestep `k_p`. The policy learns to predict `ϵ_p`.
- **Objective:** Standard Behavior Cloning (BC) via noise matching: `L_BC = E[||ϵ_p - π_θ(a^k_p, k_p, FiLM(O_0...M-1))||^2_2]`.

#### 3.4. Diffusion World Model (Wϕ)

Based on prior diffusion world model frameworks, `W_ϕ` predicts `N`-step future latent embeddings `O_M...M+N-1`.
- **Input:** Future target latents `O_M...M+N-1` (extracted by `E_ψ`) are corrupted with noise `ϵ_w` at timestep `k_w`.
- **Conditioning:** Unlike sampling-based "prefix-fixing," DDP uses direct feature-level integration. The historical latent `x_obs` is repeated across the temporal horizon and concatenated channel-wise with noisy samples, ensuring continuous access to historical structural context. The executed action sequence `a_M-1...M+N-2` also serves as a global condition, injected via FiLM.
- **Objective:** Minimizing noise prediction error: `L_WM = E[||ϵ_w - W_ϕ(C_wm, k_w, FiLM(a_M-1...M+N-2))||^2_2]`, where `C_wm` is the concatenated input.

#### 3.5. Training Objective

The Diffusion Policy and World Model are jointly optimized end-to-end.
- **Combined Loss:** `L_total = L_BC + λL_WM`, where `λ` balances the two objectives.
- **Regularization:** The world model acts as a geometric and dynamic regularizer, embedding environmental kinematics and dynamics into the shared representation.
- **Stabilization:** Exponential Moving Average (EMA) is applied to model weights to stabilize convergence and enhance inference robustness.

#### 3.6. Inference Stage (Robustness Mechanism)

DDP dynamically shifts between physical reality and internal "imagination" upon detecting OOD states.
- **Subtask Definition:** For long-horizon tasks, a sequence of subtasks `S = {S_1..., S_J}` is defined, each targeting an object `C_j`. Completion triggers are simple expert heuristics.
- **Real-Imagination Discrepancy (OOD Detector):** Anomalies are detected by comparing the real observation latent `O_t_real = E_ψ(X_t_real)` with the World Model's prediction `O_t_pred`.
 - `D_R-I(t) = ||O_t_real - O_t_pred||^2_2`.
 - An OOD event is flagged if `D_R-I(t)` exceeds a threshold `τ_diff`.
 - A 6D pose estimator (e.g., FoundationPose) identifies displaced objects `C_disp` by comparing current poses to expected ID states.
- **Simplified Object Tracking under OOD:** Upon detecting displacement and once the object settles, the 6D pose estimator provides its new resting pose `p_C_j`. A static spatial displacement `∆p_C_j = p_C_j - p_exp_C_j` is computed and applied as a compensatory tracking action `a_track_j` for the end-effector.
- **Recursive Imagination for Action Generation:** When in OOD mode, the system bypasses corrupted physical observations.
 - The policy generates action chunks `(a_0...a_N)` conditioned *entirely on the previously predicted latent embedding* (internal imagination).
 - The Diffusion World Model uses this imagined latent and the newly predicted actions to forecast the next latent state.
 - This autoregressive loop sustains a coherent trajectory purely within the agent's internal imagination.
- **Target-Specific Recovery:**
 - For an imagined subtask targeting a displaced object `C_j`, once a completion trigger `T_end(S_j)` is met, an inverse spatial translation `a_rec_j = -∆p_C_j` is applied to realign the imagined trajectory with the physical object.
 - `C_j` is removed from `C_disp`.
 - `D_R-I` is re-evaluated. If below `τ_diff`, the environment is considered recovered, and the policy reverts to the real visual latent `O_t_real` for standard ID execution. Otherwise, imagination continues.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Dream Diffusion Policy (DDP) was extensively evaluated across simulated (MetaWorld, Adroit) and real-world (Franka Panda) robotic manipulation tasks, demonstrating its robustness to out-of-distribution (OOD) disturbances.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Benchmarks:** 10 MetaWorld tasks (general manipulation) and 3 Adroit tasks (complex dynamical systems).
- **OOD Setup:** Dynamic, mid-task object displacements moving targets outside trained ID boundaries.
- **Baselines:** DP3 and FlowPolicy (state-of-the-art diffusion policies).

**Key Findings:**
- **Base Policy Performance (ID vs. OOD):** Standard base policies (DP3, FlowPolicy, DDP without OOD inference mechanism) achieved high success rates in-distribution (ID) (e.g., DDP 89.5% average). However, under OOD conditions, their success rates plummeted (e.g., DP3 0.8%, FlowPolicy 6.7%, DDP 1.3% on MetaWorld), confirming their brittleness.
- **DDP OOD Robustness:** The tracking-augmented DDP significantly outperformed baselines under OOD conditions, achieving a 73.8% average success rate on MetaWorld. In contrast, applying the identical tracking mechanism to DP3 and FlowPolicy resulted in much lower OOD success rates (23.9% for DP3, 5.4% for FlowPolicy). This indicates that simple tracking alone is insufficient; the tight coupling with the Diffusion World Model is crucial for robust task completion.
- **Qualitative Results (MetaWorld):** Visual sequences on MetaWorld Assembly and Push tasks showed DDP successfully navigating OOD shifts. For instance, in Assembly, DDP tracked a displaced peg and successfully inserted a ring using imagination. In Push, DDP tracked a shifted puck, executed actions via imagination, and used a localized recovery action to push it to the target.
- **World Model Imagination Analysis (ID):** DDP demonstrated resilience even when replacing real observations with imagined latents during ID execution.
 - On MetaWorld, DDP maintained near-baseline accuracy at 10% (92.3%) and 50% (89.4%) imagined latent substitution rates. Even at 100% open-loop imagination (after initial observation), DDP achieved a 61.4% success rate.
 - On the complex Adroit benchmark, imagination even improved success rates from a 77.8% baseline to 95.2% at 10% substitution, suggesting the world model acts as a temporal smoother for high-DoF control.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Benchmark:** Franka Emika Panda robot with Robotiq 2F-85 gripper. Three tasks: Press Button (easy), Pour Tea (medium), Stack Blocks (hard).
- **OOD Setup:** Target objects abruptly moved mid-task entirely outside the trained ID zone.
- **Visual Processing:** Intel RealSense L515 for depth, D455 for tracking (FoundationPose++ module).

**Key Findings:**
- **OOD Detection:** The D_R-I discrepancy successfully identified OOD anomalies in real-time across all tasks. Sudden object shifts caused `D_R-I` to spike above task-specific thresholds, reliably triggering OOD mode.
- **DDP OOD Robustness:** The tracking-augmented DDP achieved an 83.3% average success rate across real-world OOD tasks, drastically outperforming the DP3 tracking ablation (3.3%).
- **Qualitative Results:**
 - **Stack Blocks:** DDP tracked OOD shifts for each block, completed remaining motions via imagination, and performed a precise physical recovery. The DP3 ablation failed to pick the displaced block.
 - **Pour Tea:** DDP tracked the shifted cup and executed pouring actions from imagination. DP3 accidentally dropped the teapot.
 - **Press Button:** DDP recovered to press the target precisely. DP3 ablation failed by either under-pressing or over-pressing.
- **Open-loop Imagination:** DDP maintained a 76.7% average success rate when relying entirely on 100% imagined latents after the first observation chunk, confirming the stability and reliability of its internal world model for continuous real-world control.
- **Robustness to Visual Occlusion:** When the camera was blocked mid-task, DDP seamlessly bypassed the visual corruption using autoregressive imagination to complete tasks like Stack Blocks and Pour Tea, whereas the DP3 baseline failed when blinded.

In summary, the results consistently demonstrate that DDP's deep integration of a diffusion world model enables a robust response to severe OOD disturbances, surpassing baselines that lack this active predictive imagination, both in simulated and physical environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Dream Diffusion Policy (DDP) contributes to the field of robotic control by addressing a critical vulnerability of visuomotor policies: their brittleness under out-of-distribution (OOD) disturbances. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Enhanced OOD Robustness:** DDP demonstrates a significant improvement in robotic manipulation robustness against severe OOD conditions, such as unexpected object displacements and complete visual occlusions. This capability is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic and unpredictable real-world environments.
- **Novel Integration of World Models:** The paper proposes a deep and active integration of a diffusion world model with a diffusion policy via a shared 3D visual encoder. This co-optimization allows the world model's predictive dynamics to directly regularize and guide policy inference, moving beyond traditional weakly coupled auxiliary roles.
- **Intuitive OOD Detection and Recovery:** The introduction of the real-imagination discrepancy (D_R-I) as an OOD detector provides a transparent and intuitive mechanism for identifying anomalies. Coupled with subtask-aware execution, recursive imagination, and localized physical recovery, DDP offers a structured approach to bypass corrupted vision and safely regain control.
- **Validation of Internal Imagination:** The experiments, particularly those involving high rates of imagined latent substitution and open-loop imagination, confirm that the DDP's internal world model can generate stable and reliable representations for continuous control, even in the absence of real visual input. This capability is analogous to human reliance on internal kinesthetic intuition.
- **Preservation of Learned Skills:** Unlike methods that heavily adapt or alter policy actions, DDP's approach of dynamically substituting visual input with imagination aims to preserve the high-fidelity skills initially learned from expert demonstrations, focusing instead on robust navigation through OOD states.
- **State-of-the-Art Performance in OOD Scenarios:** DDP significantly outperforms leading diffusion policy baselines in OOD conditions across both simulated and real-world tasks, establishing a new benchmark for resilience in these challenging scenarios.

#### 5.2. Potential Impact

- **Safer and More Reliable Robotic Systems:** By enabling robots to recover from unexpected environmental changes or sensor failures, DDP can contribute to the development of safer and more reliable robotic systems for applications in manufacturing, logistics, service robotics, and hazardous environments.
- **Reduced Reliance on Perfect Sensors:** The ability to operate effectively under visual occlusion or when visual streams are corrupted could lessen the strict requirements for continuous, perfect sensor data, potentially reducing system costs and complexity.
- **Advancement in Embodied AI:** The work furthers the understanding and implementation of cognitive capabilities like "imagination" in artificial agents, pushing the boundaries of embodied AI towards more intelligent and adaptable robots.
- **New Research Directions:** DDP's limitations also highlight promising avenues for future research, such as developing integrated tracking modules, enabling OOD recovery from the very beginning of a task, handling compounding OOD events, and incorporating other sensory modalities like tactile feedback.
- **Foundation for Complex Tasks:** The framework could serve as a foundation for more complex manipulation tasks, especially those requiring multi-object interaction or long-horizon planning, by integrating with higher-level reasoning mechanisms like language-conditioned control or large language models.

#### 5.3. Limitations and Future Work

The authors acknowledge several limitations:
1. **Reliance on External Tracking:** DDP currently relies on external tracking modules (e.g., FoundationPose), which can introduce errors and accumulate drift, particularly in multi-stage tasks.
2. **Initial In-Distribution Requirement:** Tasks must begin in an In-Distribution (ID) state to properly anchor the initial observation and allow the system to build its predictive context.
3. **Compounding OOD Events:** The current inference mechanism may struggle with multiple simultaneous OOD events or low-level action disruptions like gripper slippage.

Future work aims to address these limitations by:
- Employing meta-reinforcement learning to adaptively fine-tune the world model online.
- Integrating lifelong learning mechanisms to combine and preserve prior knowledge.
- Incorporating low-level tactile feedback to enhance resilience to contact-rich disruptions.
- Extending DDP to more complex multi-object manipulation tasks.
- Exploring integration with language-conditioned methods or large language models for higher-level reasoning and planning capabilities.
