# X-GuideAR: An Augmented Reality Framework to Mitigate Radiation Exposure during Fluoroscopic Guidance

> [!summary] Summary
> X-GuideAR, an augmented reality framework, integrates DeepDRR previews and X-ray augmentation to guide fluoroscopically-assisted orthopedic screw placement, aiming to mitigate radiation exposure. Preliminary phantom studies showed a 62.3% reduction in X-ray usage and improved screw trajectory quality, allowing for placement of larger diameter screws with adequate bony containment, without significantly increasing surgical duration.

## Problem

- Conventional fluoroscopic guidance in orthopedic surgeries, especially complex procedures like S2AI screw placement, results in significant radiation exposure for patients and surgical teams due to repeated X-ray acquisitions ("fluoro-hunting").
- Existing advanced guidance systems (e.g., iCT, robotics) are costly and require specialized equipment or extensive training, hindering their widespread adoption.
- Current augmented reality solutions often do not adequately reduce radiation during the initial C-arm positioning (fluoro-hunting) or lack real-time anatomical feedback for precise tool alignment.

## Method

- The **X-GuideAR** framework integrates ==Spatial Awareness==, ==DeepDRR Generation==, ==X-ray Augmentation==, and Visualization/Interaction modules, wirelessly communicating to provide an AR-based surgical copilot.
- It employs ==DeepDRR== previews to enable radiation-free "fluoro-hunting," guiding C-arm positioning to acquire essential anatomical views with minimal initial X-ray exposure.
- Real-time augmented X-ray images, displayed on a ==head-mounted display==, project virtual surgical tool trajectories to guide precise cannula manipulation and screw placement after initial X-ray views are secured.

## Results

- X-GuideAR reduced the number of X-rays required for S2AI screw placement by an average of **62.3%** (from **19.25 to 7.25** per trajectory) in phantom trials with an expert surgeon.
- The framework allowed for the placement of significantly larger maximum safe screw diameters (e.g., **12.95 mm** for 60-mm screws vs. **5.9 mm** in the control group), indicating improved bony containment and trajectory quality.
- The average surgical duration using X-GuideAR (**267.25 seconds** per trajectory) was comparable to conventional guidance (**254.5 seconds**), showing no significant increase in procedural time.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining realistic, fast DeepDRR previews with augmented reality can effectively reduce radiation exposure during the C-arm positioning (fluoro-hunting) phase of fluoroscopically-guided surgery.
> - Real-time augmentation of acquired X-rays with projected virtual tool trajectories improves trajectory awareness and precision during screw placement, diminishing the need for additional intra-operative X-rays.
> - An AR framework leveraging conventional fluoroscopy and pre-operative CT, rather than costly robotic or iCT systems, offers a practical and adoptable approach to enhance safety and efficiency in orthopedic procedures.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Orthopedic surgeries, particularly those involving screw placement, frequently rely on fluoroscopic guidance to ensure accurate positioning and minimize invasiveness. However, this conventional approach, which often involves a process termed "fluoro-hunting" to acquire specific anatomical views, leads to repeated X-ray exposures, posing long-term radiation risks to both patients and surgical teams. The increasing complexity of procedures like S2 alar-iliac (S2AI) screw placement, which necessitates multiple non-orthogonal views to navigate intricate anatomical regions and avoid neurovascular structures, exacerbates this radiation exposure.

To address the limitations of conventional fluoroscopy, several computer-assisted methods have been developed. These include volumetric guidance, imageless navigation, and robotic surgery. For S2AI screw placement specifically, techniques range from free-hand methods, which are radiation-free but highly surgeon-skill dependent, to patient-specific templates that improve accuracy but prolong pre-operative planning. Intra-operative computed tomography (iCT) offers real-time 3D imaging and enhanced navigation accuracy but increases patient radiation exposure and cost. Robotic assistance provides precision and reduced radiation but is costly and has limited accessibility. Despite their benefits, the widespread adoption of many of these advanced techniques is constrained by the need for specialized equipment, extensive training, or reliance on expert surgical skill, leaving fluoroscopy as a primary guidance method in clinical practice.

Recent advancements in augmented reality (AR) have shown promise in enhancing surgical guidance by improving anatomical understanding and reducing radiation exposure during spinal and pelvic surgeries. Existing AR applications have demonstrated reduced mental workload for tool alignment and anatomical interpretation, as well as reductions in radiation during the intervention phase once anatomy is visualized. However, the initial phase of "fluoro-hunting" for optimal views often remains a significant source of radiation exposure, as view selection still largely depends on surgeon technique.

To mitigate radiation during fluoro-hunting, Digitally Reconstructed Radiographs (DRRs) have been explored. Conventional DRRs, generated from 3D CT data, are often computationally intensive and may lack the detail of real fluoroscopic images for real-time intra-operative use. DeepDRR, a deep learning-enhanced method, addresses these limitations by providing faster and more realistic synthetic X-ray previews. While some systems leverage DRRs and robotic C-arms to reduce X-ray acquisition, they may not extend radiation reduction to the tool alignment and intervention phases.

Head-mounted display (HMD) based AR systems have further advanced spatial awareness in the operating room, offering real-time, hands-free visualization of fluoroscopic images. Challenges in HMD-based AR systems include accurate and robust surgical scene tracking, with recent progress in inside-out tracking offering sub-millimeter accuracy without line-of-sight constraints. While some AR systems have been introduced for screw insertion without fluoroscopy, they often lack real-time anatomical feedback, which can impact accuracy and surgeon confidence. Registration-based navigation systems also aim to reduce radiation but require additional training, specialized equipment, and may increase surgical duration, often still necessitating intra-operative fluoroscopy for verification.

This work, X-GuideAR, positions itself within this landscape by integrating advancements in HMD-based AR, robust tracking, and realistic DeepDRR generation to address the specific gap of reducing radiation exposure during both the fluoro-hunting and tool alignment phases of fluoroscopically guided orthopedic procedures, without requiring costly robotic systems or sacrificing real-time anatomical feedback. It aims to offer a surgeon-friendly and more adoptable paradigm for safer and more efficient surgeries, with a focus on S2AI screw placement.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce X-GuideAR, an augmented reality (AR) framework designed to mitigate radiation exposure for both patients and surgical teams during fluoroscopically guided orthopedic screw placement. The specific use case highlighted is S2AI screw placement.

The key motivations and detailed objectives are:

- **Mitigate Radiation Exposure during Fluoro-hunting:** A significant motivation stems from the current practice of "fluoro-hunting," where surgeons frequently acquire multiple X-ray images from various angles to identify optimal anatomical views for screw placement. This repetitive process is a major source of ionizing radiation exposure. X-GuideAR aims to reduce this by generating synthetic X-ray previews (DeepDRRs) that enable radiation-free guidance for C-arm positioning, thereby accelerating the identification of essential views and minimizing the number of actual X-ray scans.
- **Enhance Accuracy and Safety of Screw Placement:** The precision required for procedures like S2AI screw placement, which involves navigating complex anatomy and avoiding critical neurovascular structures, often relies on extensive intra-operative X-ray verification. The system is motivated by the need to provide enhanced visual guidance that reduces the dependence on repeated X-rays for tool alignment. Once key anatomical views are acquired, X-GuideAR augments these real X-rays with a preview of the drilling trajectory, facilitating precise screw placement with minimal additional radiation.
- **Develop a Surgeon-Friendly and Adoptable System:** Current advanced guidance systems, such as iCT and robotics, often entail high costs, specialized equipment, or extensive training, limiting their widespread adoption. The motivation for X-GuideAR is to provide an accessible and intuitive AR-based "surgical copilot" system that can be readily integrated into existing clinical workflows, leveraging pre-operative CT imaging and conventional fluoroscopy while substantially reducing intra-operative radiation.
- **Improve Workflow Efficiency:** The paper seeks to streamline the surgical process by reducing the time spent on fluoro-hunting and tool alignment. By providing immediate feedback through realistic X-ray previews and augmented trajectories, the system aims to make the procedure more efficient, potentially reducing overall surgical duration.

The three key contributions of this work are explicitly stated as:
1. Minimizing radiation exposure by enabling prompt C-arm fluoro-hunting for essential views using realistic DeepDRR previews.
2. Improving trajectory awareness and workflow efficiency by augmenting fluoroscopic images with predicted insertion outcomes.
3. Validating the safety and feasibility of X-GuideAR through experiments with an experienced orthopedic surgeon.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The X-GuideAR framework is designed as an AR-based surgical copilot system, specifically implemented for S2AI screw placement. It comprises four wirelessly communicating core modules: Spatial Awareness, DeepDRR Generation, X-ray Augmentation, and Visualization and Interaction.

**I. Spatial Awareness Module:**
This module continuously tracks and maintains the spatial relationships between critical surgical components: the patient, surgeon (via HMD), X-ray device (C-arm), and surgical tool.

- **X-ray Source Calibration:** The C-arm, typically used in orthopedic surgeries, is tracked by rigidly attaching retro-reflective markers to its gantry. The pose of the C-arm source, crucial for DeepDRR generation, is determined through a one-time calibration procedure. This involves modeling the C-arm source as a pinhole camera, obtaining its intrinsic matrix (K), and solving a Perspective-n-Point (PnP) problem using a calibration marker with four retro-reflective spheres placed within the C-arm's imaging frustum. The transformation from the C-arm gantry marker to the X-ray source is averaged over multiple scans.
- **Surgical Tool and Insertion Pathway:** X-GuideAR utilizes a novel 3D-printed cannula designed to improve pelvic screw placement by mitigating K-wire deflection. The tool undergoes a one-time pivot calibration using a Polaris system and reflective markers to accurately align and validate the K-wire's trajectory, yielding the 3D coordinate of the tool tip (P_tip) and its normal vector (n_tool) in the tool frame.
- **Pre- and Intra-operative Image Alignment:** Pre-operative CT scans, used for planning, need to be accurately aligned with the patient's anatomy under the intra-operative tracking system. If the patient tracking marker is not attached during the CT scan, this alignment (registration) is established through 2D/3D registration between intra-operative X-ray images and the pre-operative CT scans.

**II. DeepDRR Generation Module:**
This module leverages the DeepDRR framework, which uses deep learning models to generate fast, realistic, and detailed synthetic X-ray previews from 3D CT data. The generation process uses a projection matrix derived from the kinematic chain established by the spatial awareness module, linking the CT data, patient tracking, C-arm tracking, and calibrated X-ray source.

**III. X-ray Augmentation Module:**
This module overlays virtual surgical tool trajectories onto real-time or acquired X-ray images. By knowing the C-arm's pose and the surgical tool's calibrated pose and trajectory, points along the cannula's axis (P_tip + s*n_tool) can be projected onto the X-ray image plane, providing a real-time preview of tool alignment without additional radiation exposure.

**IV. Visualization and Interaction Module:**
Developed in Unity, this module provides the user interface for the surgeon, displayed on a head-mounted display (HMD) and an external monitor.
- **3D Guidance System (HMD):** Displays the X-ray field-of-view and the preview trajectory of the surgical tool in 3D, providing spatial awareness directly in the surgeon's field of view.
- **Graphical User Interface (Workstation):** Visualizes DeepDRR previews, displays real-time X-ray augmentations, and enables user interaction for system control (e.g., selecting fluoro-hunting views, enabling/disabling 3D guidance).

**S2AI Procedure Workflow with X-GuideAR:**
1. **Fluoro-hunting:** The surgeon moves the C-arm while observing real-time DeepDRR previews on the monitor/HMD. Once a satisfactory anatomical view (AP, inlet, or tear-drop) is achieved, the C-arm is fixed.
2. **X-ray Acquisition:** A single X-ray scan is taken. If acceptable, its pose is recorded to define an imaging frustum.
3. **Repeat for Views:** Steps 1 and 2 are repeated for all required anatomical views (AP, inlet, tear-drop), creating three frustums.
4. **Screw Navigation:** The surgeon adjusts the entry point and pivots the cannula, guided by virtual trajectories projected onto the acquired X-ray views.
5. **Drilling:** Once satisfactory alignment is confirmed with the augmented views, the surgeon advances the drill through the cannula sheath.
6. **Verification:** Post-drilling X-rays may be taken to confirm pathway quality.
7. **Screw Insertion:** S2AI screws are inserted along the K-wire into the established pathway.

**Experimental Setup:**
A preliminary phantom study was conducted involving an expert orthopedic surgeon performing S2AI screw placement.
- **Phantom Preparation:** Four distinct radio-opaque male pelvic models (Sawbones) were used. For the X-GuideAR trials, CT scans were acquired with retro-reflective markers rigidly affixed to the phantom, isolating registration errors. The pelvis was secured prone and draped to simulate a clinical scenario.
- **Hardware:** A Siemens Healthcare Cios Fusion C-arm, a Microsoft HoloLens 2 HMD (for inside-out tracking and 3D guidance), a 3D-printed cannula, orthopedic drill bits, and an orthopedic drill were used. A server with dual NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPUs facilitated DeepDRR generation, and a Magewell frame grabber streamed real-time X-rays.
- **Calibration:** A one-time calibration of the Cios Fusion was performed using an NDI Polaris system (9 X-rays, reprojection error of 1.74 mm). The NDI tracker was only for calibration, not the phantom study. The pivot calibration of the cannula yielded a 0.48 mm RMS error.
- **Study Design:** Four trials were performed: two control groups (conventional fluoroscopic guidance) and two experimental groups (X-GuideAR guidance). Two assistants were involved: one for C-arm positioning and one for data recording.
- **Control Group Procedure:** The surgeon directed C-arm positioning for X-rays at each view, guided the drill, and took post-drilling X-rays for verification.
- **X-GuideAR Group Procedure:** The surgeon used DeepDRR previews to guide C-arm positioning for each view (AP, inlet, teardrop), taking one X-ray for acceptance. Then, augmented X-rays guided cannula manipulation and drilling. Post-drilling X-rays were used for verification.

**Post-operative Assessments:**
Post-operative CT scans (Brainlab Loop-X) of all phantoms were taken. Pencil leads were inserted into the drill pathways for visualization. 3D Slicer was used for segmentation and annotation. A 2mm-thick cortical shell model was generated. Virtual 60mm and 90mm S2AI screws were simulated along the annotated pathways to determine the maximum allowable screw diameter without cortical breach, excluding the first 2 cm of the trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The preliminary phantom study, involving an expert orthopedic surgeon performing eight S2AI trajectories across four trials, yielded several findings regarding radiation exposure, surgical duration, and screw trajectory quality.

**Radiation Exposure Reduction:**
- **Overall X-ray Reduction:** X-GuideAR reduced the number of X-rays required for the S2AI procedure by an average of 62.3% compared to the conventional fluoroscopic guidance workflow.
 - **Control Group Average:** 19.25 X-rays per trajectory.
 - **X-GuideAR Group Average:** 7.25 X-rays per trajectory.
- **View-Specific Reduction:**
 - **AP View:** Reduced from an average of 5.25 X-rays (control) to 2.25 X-rays (X-GuideAR).
 - **Inlet View:** Reduced from an average of 8 X-rays (control) to 2.25 X-rays (X-GuideAR).
 - **Tear-drop View:** Reduced from an average of 6 X-rays (control) to 2.75 X-rays (X-GuideAR).
- **Optimized X-GuideAR Workflow:** In three out of four X-GuideAR trajectories (Trial 2 right; Trial 3 left and right), the workflow was further refined to use only three X-rays total (one per required view) to establish the pathway, with additional imaging reserved only for drill-trajectory quality assurance.
- **Fluoroscopy Time:** The average fluoroscopy time per trajectory with X-GuideAR was 4.5 seconds.

**Surgical Duration:**
- X-GuideAR did not significantly increase the average surgical duration compared to the regular workflow.
 - **Control Group Average:** 254.5 seconds per trajectory.
 - **X-GuideAR Group Average:** 267.25 seconds per trajectory.
- **Operational Phase Durations (X-GuideAR):** Fluoro-hunting took an average of 123 seconds, cannula manipulation 84 seconds, and drilling 68.25 seconds.
- **Variability:** Pathway creation on the left side consistently took longer than on the right, potentially due to anatomical access.

**Screw Trajectory Quality and Bony Containment:**
- **Maximum Safe Screw Diameter:** X-GuideAR trajectories allowed for larger maximum safe screw diameters.
 - **Control Group Average:** 5.9 mm for 60-mm-long screws and 3.34 mm for 90-mm-long screws.
 - **X-GuideAR Group Average:** 12.95 mm for 60-mm-long screws and 7.95 mm for 90-mm-long screws.
- **Implication for Clinical Sizes:** The X-GuideAR results indicate that standard clinical S2AI implant sizes (7.5–9.5 mm) could be accommodated with adequate bony containment, allowing for the placement of larger diameter screws compared to conventional guidance.
- **Cortical Proximity:** Visualizations of virtual 9mm x 60mm screws demonstrated that X-GuideAR pathways maintained safer distances from critical anatomical structures like the sciatic notch and extended more medially into the iliac wall, reducing the risk of nerve and vessel injuries.

**Reprojection Error:**
The C-arm calibration using the Perspective-n-Points algorithm resulted in a reprojection error of 1.74 mm. The pivot calibration for the surgical tool (cannula) had a 0.48 mm RMS error.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development and preliminary evaluation of X-GuideAR suggest several significant implications for orthopedic surgery, particularly in fluoroscopically guided procedures like S2AI screw placement.

**Reduction in Radiation Exposure:** The most direct and substantial impact is the demonstrated 62.3% reduction in X-ray usage. This finding is consistent with or surpasses reductions reported by other advanced systems (e.g., Killeen et al. reported ~66% with robotic C-arms, Silva et al. ~63%), while X-GuideAR avoids the need for complex and costly robotic X-ray systems. This significantly mitigates the long-term health risks associated with ionizing radiation for both patients and the surgical team, addressing a critical safety concern in modern operating rooms. The reduced average fluoroscopy time per trajectory also contributes to this benefit.

**Improved Surgical Precision and Safety:** The ability of X-GuideAR to facilitate the placement of larger diameter screws, with average maximum safe diameters of 12.95 mm for 60-mm screws and 7.95 mm for 90-mm screws, indicates enhanced bony containment compared to conventional methods (5.9 mm and 3.34 mm, respectively). This improvement in trajectory quality allows for the use of standard clinical implant sizes (7.5–9.5 mm) with a higher margin of safety, potentially leading to improved fixation strength, stability, and overall surgical outcomes. The system's ability to guide screws away from critical structures like the sciatic notch further reduces the risk of neurovascular injury, which is a common concern in S2AI procedures. These results suggest comparable trajectory safety to established robotic guidance and intra-operative CT-based navigation, without their associated high costs or increased patient radiation exposure.

**Enhanced Workflow and Potential for Broader Adoption:** X-GuideAR did not significantly increase the average surgical duration, despite the initial learning curve associated with new tools and the AR HMD. The system's integration of DeepDRR previews for radiation-free fluoro-hunting and augmented X-rays for tool guidance streamlines the surgical process. By leveraging existing pre-operative CT imaging and conventional fluoroscopy, rather than requiring expensive robotic platforms or intra-operative CT systems, X-GuideAR offers a more practical and potentially widely adoptable solution for medical centers. Its surgeon-friendly design, which maintains dynamic, real-time anatomical feedback consistent with current clinical practice, is expected to enhance surgeon confidence and accelerate its integration into routine surgical workflows.

**Usability and Learning Curve:** While an initial learning curve was observed, particularly for cannula manipulation and drilling, the reduction in X-ray usage between trials suggests that familiarity with the system can lead to further efficiencies. The spatial awareness provided by the HMD, including 3D visualization of the X-ray field-of-view and tool trajectory, contributes to improved tracking robustness and user understanding. This suggests that the benefits could be even more pronounced for less-experienced clinicians or trainees.

**Bridging Pre- and Intra-operative Data:** The system's ability to bridge the kinematic chain between pre-operative CT and the intra-operative scenario creates future potential for seamless integration of detailed surgical plans into the operating room environment.

**Limitations and Future Directions:** The study acknowledges limitations, including evaluation by a single expert surgeon and a limited number of trials on a single phantom model. These factors restrict the assessment of usability, performance variability across different skill levels, and generalizability to diverse patient anatomies and soft-tissue conditions. Future research should involve a larger sample size, multiple surgeons with varying expertise, and more anatomically diverse phantoms or cadaveric specimens to strengthen the generalizability and clinical relevance of these findings. Further end-to-end analysis could validate the significance of the systematic and human-related error sources identified.

In conclusion, X-GuideAR represents a promising advancement in AR-based surgical guidance, demonstrating the potential to significantly improve radiation safety, surgical precision, and efficiency in orthopedic procedures, while offering a more accessible and adoptable solution within existing clinical practices.
