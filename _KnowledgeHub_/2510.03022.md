# HumanoidExo: Scalable Whole-Body Humanoid Manipulation via Wearable Exoskeleton

> [!summary] Summary
> HumanoidExo, a system developed by researchers from National University of Defense Technology and Midea Group, provides scalable whole-body humanoid manipulation data via a wearable exoskeleton and motion retargeting. This system enables a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to acquire new skills like walking solely from exoskeleton data and achieve 80% success rates in complex manipulation tasks using significantly fewer real-robot demonstrations.

## Problem

- A critical bottleneck in humanoid policy learning is the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and reliable real-world whole-body data.
- Existing data collection methods like simulation-to-real transfer and human videos face a substantial 'embodiment gap' due to physical differences.
- Direct robot teleoperation, while high-fidelity, is costly, not scalable, and poses risks of hardware damage and operator fatigue.

## Method

- HumanoidExo, a novel hardware-software system, comprises a custom-designed wearable exoskeleton, a back-mounted LiDAR sensor for torso tracking, and visual cameras for efficient whole-body data acquisition.
- A sophisticated motion retargeting pipeline fuses human arm joint movements from the exoskeleton and lower-body pose from LiDAR to generate kinematically feasible robot trajectories.
- HE-VLA, a hybrid policy learning method, combines a pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for visuomotor control with a Reinforcement Learning (RL) component for maintaining dynamic balance and robustness.

## Results

- Augmenting 5 real teleoperated demonstrations with 195 HumanoidExo demonstrations boosted success rates in a manipulation task from 5% to 80%, performing comparably to a policy trained on 200 solely real teleoperated demonstrations.
- The Unitree G1 robot successfully acquired a new walking skill solely from HumanoidExo data, achieving a 100% success rate in navigating to a table for a manipulation task, without any real-robot walking demonstrations.
- Policies trained with HumanoidExo data demonstrated strong generalization to novel objects and environments and remarkable robustness to physical disturbances, recovering autonomously when dragged away from its workspace.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct mapping of human joint movements to robot joint movements, combined with precise motion retargeting, effectively minimizes the embodiment gap for whole-body humanoid control.
> - Exoskeleton-generated data can substantially augment scarce real-robot demonstrations, leading to comparable or superior policy performance and generalization with significantly less actual robot interaction.
> - A hybrid learning approach that integrates imitation learning with dynamic balance control via reinforcement learning enables robots to acquire complex, multi-modal skills, including locomotion, from human demonstrations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of humanoid policy learning is rapidly evolving, driven by the ambition to create general-purpose humanoid robots capable of diverse tasks, from locomotion to complex manipulation and language-conditioned tasking. This progress is significantly propelled by initiatives from major technology companies, such as Nvidia's GR00T and Figure AI, which aim to develop foundational models for humanoids.

However, a critical bottleneck in this advancement is the acquisition of large-scale, diverse, and reliable real-world data. Existing approaches to data collection each face substantial limitations:
- **Simulation-to-real transfer**: While cost-effective, it suffers from a significant "embodiment gap" where simulated robot dynamics inevitably mismatch real-world physics, making direct policy transfer challenging.
- **Learning from web-scale human videos (e.g., EgoMimic, Vid2Robot)**: These methods are rich in potential data, but the morphological and kinematic differences between humans and robots create a severe embodiment gap, making direct mapping of human actions to robot policies difficult.
- **Direct teleoperation**: This approach provides high-fidelity, real-world data but is inherently difficult to scale. It typically requires a costly one-to-one human-to-robot setup, demanding highly skilled operators, leading to fatigue, and incurring significant risks of damaging expensive hardware during live sessions. These factors severely limit the volume and diversity of data that can be collected.

Against this backdrop, "HumanoidExo" positions itself as a novel and integrated solution designed to overcome these challenges, specifically for *whole-body humanoid* data. Previous works have explored "in-the-wild" data collection using various equipment like wearable gloves (e.g., DexCap), low-cost exoskeletons for arm manipulation (e.g., AirExo), or universal manipulation interfaces (UMI, DexUMI). However, these efforts primarily focused on conventional robotic arms or upper-body manipulation, and often did not address the complex dynamics of whole-body humanoid control.

HumanoidExo stands out by being the **first system designed for in-the-wild, whole-body policy learning on a humanoid robot**. It directly addresses the embodiment gap by carefully mapping human joint movements to robot joint movements and by incorporating lower-body tracking. Furthermore, it tackles the scalability and cost issues by offering an efficient way to generate large-scale datasets from human demonstrations, minimizing the reliance on costly and risky direct robot teleoperation. By integrating a custom wearable exoskeleton with sophisticated motion retargeting and a hybrid policy learning model (HE-VLA), this work pushes the boundaries of how diverse and voluminous data can be acquired and leveraged for more capable and generalist humanoid robots. It offers a practical bridge between the rich expressiveness of human motion and the specific kinematic and dynamic constraints of humanoid robots.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to **alleviate the critical bottleneck in humanoid policy learning: the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and reliable whole-body humanoid data**. The authors are motivated by the inherent difficulties and high costs associated with collecting real-world data directly from humanoid robots, as well as the limitations of existing proxy data sources like simulations or human videos due to the embodiment gap.

Specifically, the key objectives are to:
- **Develop an efficient and scalable data collection system**: Design a system that can capture natural human motion for whole-body humanoid tasks without the constraints and costs of direct robot teleoperation.
- **Minimize the embodiment gap**: Create a methodology to effectively transfer human motion data to kinematically feasible whole-body humanoid trajectories, bridging the morphological and kinematic differences between humans and robots.
- **Enhance humanoid robot performance**: Enable humanoid policies to generalize to novel environments, learn complex tasks with minimal real-robot data, and acquire new skills, thereby significantly improving their capabilities in dynamic, real-world scenarios.
- **Facilitate the development of general-purpose humanoids**: Provide a practical solution that contributes to building more versatile and capable humanoid robots, crucial for the advancement of foundational models in robotics.

The motivation stems from the recognized limitations of current data collection paradigms. Simulation data often lacks fidelity, leading to performance degradation in the real world. Human video data, while abundant, requires complex and often imperfect cross-domain translation. Direct teleoperation, while offering high-quality data, is expensive, resource-intensive, physically demanding for operators, and carries a high risk of hardware damage. HumanoidExo aims to circumvent these problems by offering a safe, scalable, and effective alternative for generating high-quality training data for humanoid robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The HumanoidExo system integrates novel hardware and software components to facilitate scalable whole-body humanoid manipulation. The methodology is structured around bridging the embodiment gap, efficient data acquisition, and a robust policy learning framework.

#### 3.1. Bridging the Embodiment Gap

A core principle of HumanoidExo is to address the embodiment gap by favoring joint space control over Cartesian space control. While end-effector tracking (e.g., from VR headsets) can work for Cartesian control, it struggles with the multiple solutions of inverse kinematics for high-DoF robot arms, leading to potential collisions and difficulties in confined spaces. Direct mapping of human joint movements to robot joint movements is deemed more practical and precise for complex whole-body control.

#### 3.2. Data Acquisition Hardware

The system comprises:
- **Custom-designed, lightweight, and flexible wearable exoskeleton**: This exoskeleton is designed to capture all seven joints of the human arm without impeding natural movements. It features two extra Degrees of Freedom (DoF) at the Glenohumeral (GH) joint for improved ergonomics.
- **Back-mounted LiDAR sensor (Livox Mid-360)**: This sensor tracks the operator's torso, providing a 6D pose for recording base movements such as walking, squatting, and bending. It utilizes a LiDAR-odometry module based on FAST-LIO for real-time estimation of spatial position and orientation, offering superior adaptability and robustness compared to vision-based or external localization systems.
- **Visual Data Acquisition**: A primary head-mounted camera (Realsense D455) and two supplementary wrist-mounted fisheye cameras (mounted on Dexmo force-feedback gloves) capture operational perspectives. To minimize the visual embodiment gap, the same camera models are used on both the exoskeleton and the target robot (Unitree G1), and the field of view of wrist cameras is calibrated to match. Discrepancies in head camera position and actuation are acknowledged but empirically shown not to hinder learning.

#### 3.3. Motion Retargeting Pipeline

The system generates kinematically feasible, whole-body trajectories by fusing data from the exoskeleton and LiDAR.
- **Upper-Body Alignment**:
 - **Upper Arm Pose Alignment**: The exoskeleton's base, fixed to the wearer's torso, serves as the reference. Rotational joints leading to the upper arm attachment point are analyzed, and a Denavit-Hartenberg (DH) parameter table is constructed. Forward kinematics computes the relative pose (quaternion), which is then mapped to the first three joints of the robot's arm via an iterative inverse kinematics method.
 - **Forearm Pose Alignment**: A motor at the exoskeleton elbow directly maps the human elbow's bending angle to the robot's forearm.
 - **Wrist Pose Alignment**: The human wrist is modeled as a spherical joint. Inertial Measurement Units (IMUs) on the forearm and Dexmo glove capture wrist rotation. A quaternion-based calculation determines the wrist's orientation relative to the forearm, which is then mapped to the final three joints of the robot's arm using iterative inverse kinematics.
- **Lower-Body Alignment**: LiDAR odometry provides base translational velocity, base rotational velocity, and height, which serve as commands for the robot's balance control algorithm.

#### 3.4. HE-VLA: A Whole-Body Humanoid Policy Learning Method

HumanoidExo-VLA (HE-VLA) is a hybrid approach combining imitation learning with reinforcement learning:
- **Vision-Language-Action (VLA) Model**: DexVLA, a pre-trained VLA model, is leveraged for foundational whole-body motion control. This model learns visuomotor policies from expert demonstrations, mapping visual observations to actions. The pre-training dataset for DexVLA does *not* include data from the Unitree G1 robot used in the experiments, highlighting its transferability.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) for Motion Balance**: To address the stability risks of relying solely on imitation learning for whole-body control, an RL-based component is introduced. This PPO-based actor-critic controller maintains dynamic balance while executing VLA-generated commands for base speed, yaw rate, and target torso height. It receives observations including base angular velocity, gravity, and joint states, and outputs desired lower-body joint targets. A PD controller applies torques to drive locomotion, while upper-body joints follow the operator.
 - **Curriculum Learning**: A curriculum scales the admissible joint range to smoothly expand motion from static to fully expressive, increasing robustness.
 - **Squatting Control**: A reward function tracks a target base height and shapes knee flexion.
 - **Symmetry Losses**: To improve sample efficiency and reduce bias, both original and mirrored samples (x-z plane reflection) are used, with auxiliary actor/critic symmetry losses encouraging consistent predictions.

#### 3.5. Robot Configuration

The target experimental platform is the **Unitree G1 humanoid robot**, standing 1.3m tall with 29 DoF in its body and two Inspire-Hand end-effectors, each with 12 DoF (6 active, 6 passive), totaling **41 active degrees of freedom**. The policy model outputs target joint angles for the entire robot body.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across three challenging real-world tasks demonstrate the effectiveness of HumanoidExo data in enhancing humanoid robot policy learning, particularly in generalization, data efficiency, and skill acquisition.

#### 4.1. Generalizable Manipulation with HumanoidExo (PlaceToy Task)

- **Data Efficiency**: For the tabletop manipulation task (PlaceToy), training with only five real teleoperated demonstrations resulted in a mere 5% success rate. However, augmenting these five demonstrations with 195 HumanoidExo demonstrations dramatically boosted the success rate to approximately 80%, performing comparably to a policy trained on 200 *solely* real teleoperated demonstrations. This highlights the substantial utility and efficiency of HumanoidExo data as a substitute or augmentation for scarce real-robot data.
- **Object & Environment Generalization**: When tested with novel objects and in entirely new environments (out-of-domain scenarios), policies trained with a mixed dataset (teleoperated + HumanoidExo data) consistently achieved higher average success rates compared to those trained solely on teleoperated data. This indicates that HumanoidExo data effectively improves the policy's ability to generalize to unseen conditions.

#### 4.2. Exoskeleton Data Brings New Skill (Walk & PlaceToy Task)

- **Skill Acquisition**: HumanoidExo data proved capable of teaching the robot entirely new skills. In the "Walk & PlaceToy" task, where the robot needed to walk to a table, stop, and then manipulate a toy, the policy was trained with 195 HumanoidExo demonstrations and only five teleoperated demonstrations for the *stationary manipulation part* (i.e., no walking data from the real robot). The robot achieved a 100% success rate on the walking portion in every trial, consistently navigating to the table and stopping correctly before manipulating. This unequivocally demonstrates that the robot acquired the complex skill of walking solely from HumanoidExo data, without any real-robot walking demonstrations.
- **Robustness to Disturbance**: A striking finding was the policy's remarkable robustness to physical disturbances. When the Unitree G1 robot was manually dragged away from its workspace during the "Walk & PlaceToy" task, the policy consistently recovered by autonomously walking the robot back to the table, even from distances far exceeding any encountered in the training data. This suggests that HumanoidExo data instills strong generalization capabilities against significant real-world perturbations.
- **Generalization to New Environment (Locomotion)**: The walking skill acquired from HumanoidExo data transferred perfectly to a new, visually different environment. While a slight decrease in manipulation success was observed, the locomotion capability remained robust, showcasing the strong generalization of learned mobile skills.

#### 4.3. HumanoidExo for Whole-Body Dexterous Manipulation (PlaceLaundry Task)

- For the complex "PlaceLaundry" task, involving squatting, grasping deformable objects (clothes) from a basket, and placing them into a washing machine, the results reinforced previous findings. A model trained with a mixed dataset (5 teleoperated + 195 HumanoidExo) achieved 80% success rates for both seen and unseen clothes, performing comparably to a model trained on 200 *solely* teleoperated demonstrations. In contrast, the model with only 5 teleoperated demonstrations achieved a mere 5% success rate. This validates HumanoidExo data as a strong, viable substitute for teleoperated data even for complex whole-body dexterous manipulation tasks, and potentially more effective for task generalization.

In summary, the results empirically validate that HumanoidExo is a crucial addition to real-robot data, enabling humanoid policies to generalize to novel environments, learn complex whole-body control from limited real-robot demonstrations, and acquire new skills (like walking) solely from exoskeleton-generated data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "HumanoidExo" system represents a significant advancement in the field of humanoid robotics, offering a powerful paradigm for generating large-scale, high-quality datasets that address the most critical bottleneck hindering the development of capable, general-purpose humanoid robots.

The key significance and potential impact of this work include:

1. **Breaking the Data Bottleneck**: By providing an efficient, scalable, and cost-effective method for collecting diverse whole-body humanoid data, HumanoidExo directly tackles the long-standing challenge of data scarcity. This can dramatically accelerate research and development in humanoid control, moving beyond reliance on expensive and risky direct teleoperation or imperfect simulation-to-real transfers.
2. **Bridging the Embodiment Gap Effectively**: The meticulous design of the exoskeleton, coupled with sophisticated motion retargeting for both upper and lower body (joint space control, IMU-based wrist tracking, LiDAR odometry), significantly minimizes the inherent differences between human and robot kinematics. This allows for more direct and high-fidelity transfer of human motor skills to robot policies.
3. **Enabling Generalist Humanoids**: The demonstrated ability to generalize to novel environments and objects, learn complex skills from limited real-robot data, and even acquire entirely new motor skills (like walking) from exoskeleton data alone, is transformative. This capability is essential for creating truly generalist humanoid robots that can adapt to unstructured, dynamic, and unknown real-world scenarios.
4. **Enhancing Data Efficiency**: The finding that a small number of real-robot demonstrations, when augmented with HumanoidExo data, can achieve performance comparable to or better than much larger datasets of pure real-robot data, highlights an unprecedented level of data efficiency. This drastically reduces the experimental burden and cost associated with training humanoid policies.
5. **Increasing Robustness and Safety**: The integrated reinforcement learning component for balance control, combined with the comprehensive training data, results in policies that are remarkably robust to physical disturbances. This is crucial for safe deployment of humanoids in real-world environments, minimizing the risk of falls and damage.
6. **Empowering Human-Centric Robot Learning**: HumanoidExo democratizes robot data collection by making it more accessible and less demanding for human operators. This human-in-the-loop approach leverages human intuition and dexterity to teach robots complex tasks, potentially opening avenues for non-expert users to train robots.
7. **Contribution to Robot Foundation Models**: As the field moves towards large-scale robot foundation models (e.g., VLA models), the ability to generate vast amounts of diverse, high-quality whole-body data via HumanoidExo will be invaluable. It provides the necessary training material to unlock the full potential of these models for general-purpose humanoid control.

In conclusion, HumanoidExo is not just an incremental improvement but a fundamental step towards overcoming critical barriers in humanoid robotics. Its practical, scalable, and effective solution for data acquisition promises to accelerate the development of autonomous, robust, and versatile humanoid robots capable of interacting intelligently and safely within human environments.
