# HumanoidExo: Scalable Whole-Body Humanoid Manipulation via Wearable Exoskeleton

> [!summary] Summary
> HumanoidExo, a system developed by researchers from National University of Defense Technology and Midea Group, provides scalable whole-body humanoid manipulation data via a wearable exoskeleton and motion retargeting. This system enables a Unitree G1 humanoid robot to acquire new skills like walking solely from exoskeleton data and achieve 80% success rates in complex manipulation tasks using significantly fewer real-robot demonstrations.

## Problem

- A critical bottleneck in humanoid policy learning is the scarcity of large-scale, diverse, and reliable real-world whole-body data.
- Existing data collection methods like simulation-to-real transfer and human videos face a substantial 'embodiment gap' due to physical differences.
- Direct robot teleoperation, while high-fidelity, is costly, not scalable, and poses risks of hardware damage and operator fatigue.

## Method

- HumanoidExo, a novel hardware-software system, comprises a custom-designed wearable exoskeleton, a back-mounted LiDAR sensor for torso tracking, and visual cameras for efficient whole-body data acquisition.
- A sophisticated motion retargeting pipeline fuses human arm joint movements from the exoskeleton and lower-body pose from LiDAR to generate kinematically feasible robot trajectories.
- HE-VLA, a hybrid policy learning method, combines a pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model for visuomotor control with a Reinforcement Learning (RL) component for maintaining dynamic balance and robustness.

## Results

- Augmenting 5 real teleoperated demonstrations with 195 HumanoidExo demonstrations boosted success rates in a manipulation task from 5% to 80%, performing comparably to a policy trained on 200 solely real teleoperated demonstrations.
- The Unitree G1 robot successfully acquired a new walking skill solely from HumanoidExo data, achieving a 100% success rate in navigating to a table for a manipulation task, without any real-robot walking demonstrations.
- Policies trained with HumanoidExo data demonstrated strong generalization to novel objects and environments and remarkable robustness to physical disturbances, recovering autonomously when dragged away from its workspace.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Direct mapping of human joint movements to robot joint movements, combined with precise motion retargeting, effectively minimizes the embodiment gap for whole-body humanoid control.
> - Exoskeleton-generated data can substantially augment scarce real-robot demonstrations, leading to comparable or superior policy performance and generalization with significantly less actual robot interaction.
> - A hybrid learning approach that integrates imitation learning with dynamic balance control via reinforcement learning enables robots to acquire complex, multi-modal skills, including locomotion, from human demonstrations.
