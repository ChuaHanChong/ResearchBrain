# Whole-Body Coordination for Dynamic Object Grasping with Legged Manipulators

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shenzhen University, Shenzhen MSU-BIT University, HKUST (Guangzhou), and CUHK introduce DQ-Bench, the first benchmark for dynamic object grasping with quadruped robots, and DQ-Net, a whole-body coordination framework. DQ-Net achieves a 41.0% grasp success rate on Level 4 random 3D high-speed tasks, outperforming adapted baselines by 25%, and demonstrates strong generalization to unseen objects.

## Problem

- Legged manipulators primarily focus on static object grasping, failing to address the complexities of continuous object motion in dynamic environments.
- Existing dynamic grasping benchmarks and methods are typically for fixed-base manipulators, overlooking the whole-body coordination and locomotion challenges of mobile robots.
- A critical gap exists in the absence of a standardized, reproducible benchmark for evaluating dynamic object grasping with quadruped robots.

## Method

- Development of DQ-Bench, the first benchmark for dynamic object grasping by quadruped robots, featuring physically realistic motion, diverse objects, and varied terrain and task difficulties.
- Introduction of **DQ-Net**, a ==teacher-student knowledge distillation== framework, designed for whole-body coordination and dynamic grasping using accessible perceptual inputs.
- DQ-Net integrates a ==Grasp Fusion Module== (GFM) for dynamic grasp pose selection and employs dual-view temporal modeling within its student policy for robust perception.

## Results

- DQ-Net's student policy achieved a **41.0%** Grasp Success Rate (GSR) on Level 4 tasks (random 3D high-speed motion), outperforming the adapted VBC-D baseline by **25%**.
- The framework consistently demonstrated superior One-Shot Success Rate (OSSR) and required fewer Timesteps to Completion (TSC) across all difficulty levels, indicating higher efficiency and decisiveness.
- DQ-Net exhibited robust generalization capabilities, maintaining superior performance on unseen objects and across varying motion complexities compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Dynamically fusing pre-generated grasp candidates with real-time object features and motion cues (via GFM) significantly enhances grasp pose robustness and enables precise end-effector alignment.
> - Explicitly modeling object velocity and temporal visual sequences allows for predictive whole-body anticipation, improving the robot's responsiveness and ability to track moving targets.
> - A teacher-student knowledge distillation paradigm effectively transfers complex control strategies from a privileged simulation environment to a perception-limited student, facilitating robust deployment on real-world mobile manipulators.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of mobile manipulation, particularly with legged robots, has seen significant advancements in recent years, largely due to improvements in locomotion control and the integration of robotic arms. However, the existing body of work, as highlighted by the authors, predominantly concentrates on scenarios where target objects remain static. While robust for stationary tasks like picking and placing, these methods fall short in dynamic environments common in logistics, search and rescue, or human-robot collaboration, where objects are continuously moving.

**Existing Limitations Identified by the Authors:**
- **Static Grasping Focus:** Most prior research on quadruped manipulators assumes static targets, neglecting the complexities of real-time perception and agile control required for dynamic objects.
- **Fixed-Base Dynamic Grasping:** Benchmarks and methods for dynamic grasping exist (e.g., DGBench, GAP-RL), but they typically involve fixed-base manipulators on tabletops, lacking the challenges of mobile locomotion and whole-body coordination.
- **Lack of Quadruped-Specific Benchmarks:** A critical gap is the absence of a standardized, reproducible benchmark specifically designed to evaluate dynamic object grasping with whole-body quadruped robots across varying motion complexities and terrains.
- **Computational and Perceptual Challenges:** High-performance grasping networks often demand high-quality visual inputs and computational resources, making them less suitable for real-time deployment on mobile platforms with limited resolution and strict latency requirements. They also output static grasp poses, lacking the temporal consistency needed for continuous motion planning.

**Contribution to the Landscape:**
This paper directly addresses these limitations by introducing **DQ-Bench**, the first benchmark tailored for dynamic object grasping with quadruped robots. This benchmark fills a crucial void by providing a standardized platform for evaluating algorithms under physically realistic dynamic conditions, including non-planar terrains and 6-DoF target motion. Concurrently, the paper proposes **DQ-Net**, a novel framework designed to overcome the perception and control challenges inherent in dynamic whole-body quadruped manipulation. By combining a teacher-student learning paradigm with a unique Grasp Fusion Module and dual-view temporal modeling, DQ-Net aims to provide a robust and efficient solution for operating in dynamic, perception-limited scenarios, pushing the boundaries of what legged manipulators can achieve in complex, real-world tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the disparity between the advanced mobility and adaptability of quadrupedal robots and their limited capability in dynamic manipulation tasks. While quadruped-arm systems show great promise for real-world applications in unstructured environments, their current research focus on static object grasping severely restricts their utility in many practical scenarios.

The core objectives of this work are:

1. **To establish a standardized benchmark for dynamic quadrupedal grasping:** The authors aim to introduce **DQ-Bench**, a novel and comprehensive benchmark. This benchmark is designed to systematically evaluate algorithmic generalization and decision efficiency for dynamic object grasping with whole-body quadruped robots. It features realistic dynamics, diverse objects (seen/unseen), multi-level task difficulties (varying object motion, velocity, height, and terrain complexity), and comprehensive evaluation metrics (GSR, OSSR, TSC).
2. **To develop a robust and efficient framework for dynamic object grasping with quadruped robots:** The paper proposes **DQ-Net**, a whole-body control framework. Its key goal is to infer optimal grasp configurations from limited perceptual cues, enabling stable and efficient dynamic object grasping. This framework is explicitly designed to address challenges such as maintaining whole-body stability, precisely controlling the end-effector in the presence of continuous relative motion, and overcoming the computational and temporal limitations of traditional grasp estimation methods.
3. **To demonstrate superior performance and generalization:** Through extensive experiments on the proposed DQ-Bench, the authors aim to show that DQ-Net significantly outperforms existing baselines in terms of grasp success rate, one-shot success rate, and responsiveness, while also demonstrating strong generalization to unseen objects and varying motion complexities.

In essence, the research is motivated by the need to bridge the gap between static manipulation capabilities and the demands of dynamic real-world environments, thereby unlocking the full potential of legged mobile manipulators.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces a two-pronged approach: first, defining a challenging benchmark (DQ-Bench) and second, proposing a novel solution framework (DQ-Net) to address the challenges presented by the benchmark.

#### 3.1. DQ-Bench: Dynamic Grasping Benchmark for Quadruped Robots

DQ-Bench is designed as the first comprehensive and reproducible evaluation platform for dynamic object grasping with whole-body quadruped control.

- **Environment Setup:** Built on the physics-based simulation platform Isaac Gym, DQ-Bench employs a movable floating platform to generate physically realistic object motion (linear, arc, random 2D/3D trajectories). This avoids unnatural object dynamics. It also features uneven terrain to test robot adaptability.
- **Objects for Grasping:** Uses diverse objects from the YCB dataset, split into "seen" (training/testing) and "unseen" (testing only) sets to assess generalization.
- **Multi-Level Task Design:** Four progressively challenging levels:
 - **Level 1:** Low-speed, fixed 2D trajectories.
 - **Level 2:** High-speed, fixed 2D trajectories.
 - **Level 3:** High-speed, random 2D trajectories.
 - **Level 4:** High-speed, random 3D trajectories (including z-axis motion).
 Objects' initial height and terrain variations are randomized at each episode.
- **Evaluation Metrics:**
 - **Grasp Success Rate (GSR):** Overall effectiveness.
 - **One-Shot Success Rate (OSSR):** Decisiveness and quality of initial grasp attempt.
 - **Time Steps to Completion (TSC):** Grasping efficiency and responsiveness.

#### 3.2. DQ-Net: Whole-Body Dynamic Grasping Framework

DQ-Net is a teacher-student knowledge distillation framework designed for whole-body coordination and dynamic grasping using accessible perceptual inputs.

- **Overall Architecture:**
 - **Low-Level Policy (LLP):** A pre-trained and frozen reinforcement learning policy that converts high-level commands (target end-effector poses, base velocities) into executable joint torques for the robot's legs and arm. It handles whole-body motion tracking.
 - **High-Level Teacher Policy (HLP-Teacher):** Trained in simulation with privileged information (ground-truth object pose, velocity, point cloud features) to learn robust grasping and motion control.
 - **High-Level Student Policy (HLP-Student):** Learns to imitate the teacher using only onboard sensory inputs (depth maps, segmentation masks from dual cameras, proprioception). This policy is lightweight and suitable for real-world deployment.

- **High-Level Teacher Policy Network with Grasp Fusion Module (GFM):**
 - **Grasp Memory Construction:** A pre-trained grasp pose prediction network (e.g., Contact-GraspNet) generates top-K grasp candidates for an object, stored as relative transformations in the object's local frame within a memory bank.
 - **Dynamic Fusion Stage:** At each timestep, the object's point cloud features and current pose form a query vector. These queries are matched against the memory bank of pre-generated grasp candidates using an attention mechanism. This dynamic fusion produces a refined and robust grasp pose representation ($g_t$).
 - **Policy Network Input:** The fused grasp ($g_t$), robot proprioception ($b_t$), point cloud features ($f_p$), and privileged object pose ($x_t$) and velocity ($v_t$) are fed into an MLP to generate high-level actions.

- **High-Level Student Policy Network: Temporal Modeling with Dual-View Fusion:**
 - **Input:** Dual-perspective visual observations from wrist-mounted and base-mounted cameras. Each view provides three consecutive frames, each containing a target mask (obtained from a pre-trained Track-SAM) and a depth map. Proprioceptive data is also included.
 - **Dual-Stream Visual Modeling (DSM):** Both visual streams are processed by a shared CNN encoder. Proprioceptive state is encoded and concatenated with visual features.
 - **Temporal Modeling:** Two separate Transformer encoders (one for each view) process the feature sequences, capturing time-aware attention.
 - **Output:** Fused features from the Transformers are concatenated and decoded by an MLP action head to generate high-level actions for the low-level policy.
 - **Knowledge Distillation:** The student policy is trained to imitate the teacher by minimizing the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between their action outputs, enabling robust control in perception-limited scenarios.

- **Training Details:** A staged training approach is used: LLP is trained first and frozen, then the HLP-Teacher is trained using reinforcement learning with a modified reward function (including a yaw-angle penalty for stability), and finally, the HLP-Student is trained via knowledge distillation (DAgger) using the teacher's outputs as labels. Experiments are conducted in 5,000 steps per task level, with a 4-frame visual observation delay to simulate real-world latency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experiments conducted on the DQ-Bench benchmark demonstrate the superior performance and robustness of DQ-Net compared to existing baselines.

- **Superior Grasping Performance under Varying Motion Complexity:**
 - DQ-Net (student policy) consistently achieved the highest Grasp Success Rate (GSR) across all four difficulty levels. For instance, at Level 4 (random 3D high-speed motion), DQ-Net's student policy achieved a GSR of 41.0%, significantly outperforming the adapted baseline VBC-D (16.0%) by 25%.
 - Ablation studies showed that both the Grasp Fusion Module (GFM) and explicit object velocity input contribute substantially to DQ-Net's performance. Removing GFM ("DQ-Net w/o GFM") or velocity input ("DQ-Net w/o vel") led to performance degradation, but these variants still surpassed VBC-D.
 - The baseline VBC, designed for static objects, showed near-zero success rates in dynamic scenarios, validating the authors' premise about the limitations of existing methods.

- **Enhanced One-Shot Success Rate (OSSR) and Responsiveness (TSC):**
 - DQ-Net consistently demonstrated superior OSSR across all difficulty levels, indicating its ability to make more decisive and accurate grasp predictions in a single attempt, which is crucial for dynamic tasks. Its OSSR at Level 4 was 38.5%, compared to VBC-D's 15.2%.
 - In terms of efficiency, DQ-Net required significantly fewer Timesteps to Completion (TSC) than VBC-D across all levels, suggesting faster and more agile grasping. DQ-Net's TSC at Level 4 was 34.32 steps, compared to VBC-D's 41.56 steps.

- **Strong Generalization to Unseen Objects:**
 - When tested on unseen objects (categories not present during training), DQ-Net consistently outperformed VBC-D, demonstrating robust generalization capabilities. This highlights its ability to adapt to novel object geometries and physical attributes, even under the most challenging Level 4 conditions.
 - Interestingly, the "DQ-Net w/o vel" variant sometimes performed marginally better on *certain* unseen object categories than the full DQ-Net. The authors suggest this might be due to the full model potentially overfitting to known motion patterns, whereas relying more on geometric priors (via GFM) can enhance generalization to novel object dynamics.

- **Effectiveness of Transformer-based Student Policy:**
 - The proposed Transformer-based student policy, with its dual-view and temporal modeling capabilities, achieved consistently higher grasp success rates compared to a CNN-based student baseline (e.g., 41.04% vs. 36.14% at Level 4).
 - Crucially, the Transformer-based policy accomplished this with a smaller model size (5.37M parameters vs. 8.43M for CNN-based), faster convergence, and higher rewards during training, underscoring its efficiency and architectural advantages.

- **Qualitative Results and Behavioral Differences:**
 - Visualizations revealed that DQ-Net achieved superior end-effector pose alignment just before contact, precisely matching the gripper with the target, unlike VBC-D which often suffered from misalignment. This precision is attributed to the dynamic integration of grasp priors from GFM.
 - DQ-Net exhibited more predictive motion anticipation, adjusting its gait and body posture proactively to the object's future trajectory. In contrast, VBC-D showed delayed reactions and lacked anticipatory behavior. This predictive capability stems from DQ-Net's enhanced ability to model object motion dynamics using velocity cues and GFM's memory retrieval.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in the field of mobile manipulation, particularly for legged robots operating in dynamic and unstructured environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Advancing Mobile Manipulation with Legged Robots:** By introducing DQ-Bench, the paper provides the first standardized and reproducible evaluation platform for dynamic object grasping with whole-body quadruped control. This is a critical enabler for systematic research, allowing for fair comparison and accelerating progress in this challenging subfield of robotics.
- **Enabling Real-World Dynamic Applications:** DQ-Net demonstrates that quadruped manipulators can move beyond static pick-and-place tasks to perform robust grasping of moving objects. This capability is transformative for applications such as:
 - **Logistics and Warehousing:** Automated sorting and handling of items on moving conveyor belts.
 - **Search and Rescue:** Grasping and manipulating dynamic debris or objects in disaster zones, where terrains are often uneven and targets may be unstable.
 - **Human-Robot Collaboration:** Safely and efficiently exchanging objects with humans in motion, fostering more natural and fluid interactions.
 - **Space Exploration and Inspection:** Operating in dynamic, unpredictable environments where traditional fixed-base robots are impractical.
- **Addressing Perception and Control Challenges:** The teacher-student framework, coupled with the Grasp Fusion Module and dual-view temporal modeling, offers a practical solution to the complexities of real-time perception and whole-body control in dynamic settings. By relying on lightweight visual inputs (masks, depth) and proprioception for the student policy, DQ-Net proposes a path towards robust deployment on real hardware with limited sensor suites.
- **Promoting Generalization and Robustness:** The demonstrated ability of DQ-Net to generalize to unseen objects and maintain performance across varying motion complexities highlights its potential for deploying robots in open-world scenarios where encountering novel objects and unpredictable dynamics is common.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The limitations identified by the authors and their proposed future work—such as real hardware deployment, handling deformable/articulated objects, multi-object/collaborative tasks, and online learning for the GFM—lay out a clear roadmap for subsequent investigations, promising further advancements in autonomous, adaptive mobile manipulation.

In conclusion, this work provides both a foundational benchmark and a high-performing solution that will undoubtedly catalyze further research and development in making quadrupedal manipulators truly agile and effective in the dynamic, complex environments of the real world.
