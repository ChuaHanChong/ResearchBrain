# OneSearch-V2: The Latent Reasoning Enhanced Self-distillation Generative Search Framework

> [!summary] Summary
> Kuaishou Technology researchers developed OneSearch-V2, an enhanced generative search framework for e-commerce that integrates latent reasoning and refined preference alignment to deepen query understanding and personalize item retrieval. This system achieved substantial gains in online business metrics, including a +3.98% increase in item Click-Through Rate (CTR) and +3.45% in Gross Merchandise Value (GMV), without increasing inference latency.

## Problem

- Prior generative search systems struggled with insufficient understanding of complex, ambiguous, or long-tail e-commerce queries under strict latency constraints.
- Inefficient exploitation of latent user intents and personalized reasoning over user context due to reliance on shallow matching and historical co-occurrence patterns.
- Fragile reward systems prone to sampling bias, reward hacking, and distributional bias, hindering timely adaptation to new queries and user preferences.

## Method

- A ==thought-augmented query understanding== module that uses LLMs to generate compact, keyword-based ==Chain-of-Thought (CoT)== reasoning and calibrate preferences based on user profiles.
- A ==reasoning-internalized self-distillation== training pipeline transfers explicit CoT reasoning into the model's parameters using ==information-asymmetric teacher-student training==, stabilized with ==R-Drop== and ==Adversarial Perturbation (FGM)==.
- A behavior feedback preference alignment optimization system that leverages composite rewards and a ==Token-Position Marginal Advantage (TPMA-GRPO)== mechanism for fine-grained credit assignment.

## Results

- Online A/B tests on Kuaishou's platform showed OneSearch-V2 increased item CTR by **+3.98%**, PV conversion rate by **+2.90%**, and GMV by **+3.45%**.
- The framework demonstrated improved performance for challenging segments, including long-tail queries (**+5.37%** CTR) and cold items (**+6.16%** CTR).
- Manual evaluation confirmed enhanced user experience, with a **+1.37%** increase in page good rate and **+1.65%** in query-item relevance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Internalizing LLM-generated reasoning through self-distillation, where the same model acts as teacher and student with input-level asymmetry, enables advanced reasoning capabilities without adding online inference costs.
> - The Token-Position Marginal Advantage (TPMA) mechanism provides a granular and hierarchically gated approach to preference learning, significantly improving credit assignment for individual tokens within generated sequences.
> - Combining R-Drop, FGM, and focal loss effectively mitigates representation instability and long-tail class imbalance during information-asymmetric self-distillation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is situated within the evolving field of generative retrieval (GR) and its application to search and recommendation systems, particularly for large-scale e-commerce platforms. Traditional search systems often employ multi-stage cascaded architectures involving recall, relevance, and ranking modules. Generative Retrieval presents an alternative paradigm that aims for end-to-end joint optimization and enhanced computational efficiency by directly generating item identifiers. Representative works in this area include OneRec for video recommendation, OneSug for query suggestion, OneLoc for local services, and MTGR for advertisement. In the context of e-commerce search, where both query-item relevance and user-item collaborative preferences are crucial, OneSearch-V1 was previously developed as a generative model designed to optimize final objectives directly.

The integration of large language models (LLMs) is a significant trend in search and recommendation. While LLMs can be directly applied or distilled into smaller models for specific tasks, a more recent focus is on incorporating LLMs to imbue generative retrieval models with reasoning capabilities. A key challenge, however, is the computational cost and latency associated with LLM inference, especially for online deployment. This paper addresses the question of how to equip generative search models with reasoning capabilities while maintaining practical inference costs. It also builds upon research in latent reasoning and self-distillation, which aims to internalize reasoning without explicit token generation, and preference alignment techniques that use reinforcement learning to optimize models based on user feedback.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The development of OneSearch-V2 is motivated by identified limitations in the preceding OneSearch-V1 framework, particularly as user preferences diversify and search queries become more complex. The three primary challenges that OneSearch-V2 aims to address are:

1. **Insufficient understanding of complex queries**: OneSearch-V1, which takes raw queries as input and generates items directly, struggles with ambiguous head queries (e.g., "indoor fitness equipment") and diverse long-tail queries (e.g., negation-type or question-type queries) that require deeper semantic understanding and reasoning beyond shallow semantic matching. These complex queries constitute a significant portion of page views but have a disproportionately low conversion rate on the Kuaishou Mall platform, indicating a "which to retrieve" dilemma for head queries and a "what can be retrieved" dilemma for tail queries.

2. **Inefficient exploitation of latent user intents and overfitting to narrow historical preferences**: OneSearch-V1's reliance on periodic updates and log-fitting objectives based on historical co-occurrence patterns leads to shallow matching. It fails to uncover potential user intentions that require reasoning over user-specific context (e.g., avoiding allergenic flowers for a user allergic to them, even if such items historically convert). While LLMs can perform such personalized intent reasoning using explicit Chain-of-Thought (CoT), the computational expense of generating additional tokens at test time is prohibitive for online systems.

3. **Fragile reward system with distributional bias**: OneSearch-V1's multi-stage, periodically updated preference reward system prevents timely adaptation to new queries and user intents. The reward model, primarily trained on historical user behavior logs, is susceptible to inefficient sampling and potential "reward hacking," leading to overfitting to narrow historical preferences and reinforcing long-tail distributional biases.

To overcome these limitations, OneSearch-V2 aims to develop a generative search framework with three innovations: (1) enhancing deep query understanding, (2) internalizing latent reasoning capabilities without incurring additional inference costs, and (3) establishing a robust, adaptive preference alignment system that mitigates reward hacking and addresses personalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

OneSearch-V2 is a latent reasoning enhanced self-distillation generative search framework comprising three key innovations: a thought-augmented complex query understanding module, a reasoning-internalized self-distillation training pipeline, and a behavior preference alignment optimization system.

#### 3.1. SID Tokenization

The paper first discusses the choice between unimodal and multimodal Semantic ID (SID) tokenization for e-commerce generative retrieval. Given that search engines must align unimodal queries with potentially multimodal item contents, OneSearch-V1 transforms multimodal information into a unimodal representation by extracting core keywords using Qwen-VL. This approach was validated experimentally, with unimodal methods (including Keyword Hierarchical Quantization Embedding (KHQE)) outperforming multimodal counterparts in recall and MRR, suggesting that mitigating cross-modal disparities and enhancing salient information are crucial. KHQE, specifically, demonstrated superior core attribute extraction and hierarchical representation, allowing for real-time query processing.

#### 3.2. Thought-Augmented Query Understanding Module

This module leverages LLMs to generate compact, keyword-based Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning for query-user pairs, which is then used to augment query understanding during training.

- **Keyword-based CoT Extraction**: A three-step reasoning pipeline is formulated:
 1. **Query Analysis**: An LLM performs intent understanding (e.g., product search), hierarchical category identification, attribute recognition (explicitly stated attributes), and topic recommendation (candidate topics satisfying constraints).
 2. **Keyword Extraction**: For product search intents, keywords are extracted from the analysis, refined (synonym merging, redundancy removal), and ranked by item popularity.
 3. **Preference Calibration**: User profiles and historical behavioral signals (past queries, interacted items) are used by the LLM to filter or augment the keyword set, aligning it with individual user preferences.
- **Training Paradigm Refinement**: The resulting ⟨query, keywords⟩ and ⟨query, user, keywords⟩ tuples constitute a semantic alignment corpus. Four CoT tasks are incorporated into the Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) Stage 1 of OneSearch-V1, guiding the model to acquire richer query knowledge and explore preference-aware item topics. This process is performed asynchronously for online deployment, with cached CoTs reused to minimize latency.

#### 3.3. Reasoning-Internalized Self-Distillation Training Pipeline

This mechanism aims to transfer the explicit reasoning capability (derived from keyword-based CoTs) into the generative model's parameters, enabling latent reasoning without additional inference costs or architectural modifications.

- **Self-Distillation Formulation**: The teacher and student share the same model weights ($M_𝜃$). The teacher receives an input prompt augmented with keyword-based CoTs ($x^{(T)}$), while the student receives the prompt without keywords ($x^{(S)}$). The distillation objective uses KL divergence ($L_{KL}$) to encourage the student's output logits to match the teacher's, with the teacher's logits detached from the computational graph. The base training objective combines cross-entropy loss with the distillation signal: $L_{base} = L_{CE}(z^{(S)}, y) + 𝛼_{KL} \cdot L_{KL}$.
- **Mitigating Representation Instability**: To address the challenges arising from information asymmetry:
 - **Prediction Consistency via R-Drop**: R-Drop regularization is applied, performing two forward passes with independent dropout masks and minimizing their divergence ($L_{R-Drop}$) to enforce prediction stability.
 - **Input Robustness via Adversarial Perturbation**: Fast Gradient Method (FGM) perturbs the shared embedding layer along its gradient direction, smoothing the loss landscape and preventing sharp decision boundaries for semantically ambiguous queries.
- **Total Optimization Objective**: The student objective combines these elements: $L_{SDFT} = L_{CE} + 𝛼_{KL} \cdot L_{KL} + 𝛼_R \cdot L_{R-Drop} + L_{adv}$, where $L_{adv}$ uses focal loss (instead of standard cross-entropy) to mitigate long-tail class imbalance.

#### 3.4. Behavior Feedback Preference Alignment Optimization System

This system replaces the previous hybrid ranking framework (which relied on a separately trained reward model) with direct user interaction feedback, supporting streaming updates and flexible reward composition.

- **Composite Reward Design**: For each generated SID sequence ($o_i$), a scalar reward ($R_i$) is computed by aggregating three signals:
 - **Relevance Reward ($R_{Rel}$)**: Categorizes items into tiers (Excellent, Related, Mismatch, Irrelevant) based on query-item relevance.
 - **Posterior Conversion Reward ($R_{CTR}$)**: Uses calibrated posterior CTR as a dense feedback signal, clipped to a bounded range.
 - **Click and Order Score ($R_{C&O}$)**: Directly rewards SIDs corresponding to clicked or purchased items with constant values ($V_c, V_o$), reflecting preference hierarchy.
 - The final composite item-level reward is $R_{item}(o_i) = R_{C\&O}(o_i) + R_{CTR}(o_i) + R_{FR}(o_i)$.
- **Token-Position Marginal Advantage (TPMA)**: To address imprecise credit assignment in standard GRPO, TPMA-GRPO decomposes sequence-level rewards into position-level marginal contributions and gates gradient flow based on prefix correctness.
 - **Prefix Reward ($R_{i,l}$)**: Measures maximum cumulative match against ground-truth SIDs at each token position $l$, with higher marginal contributions for coarser-grained initial tokens.
 - **Position-level Advantage ($\hat{A}_{i,l}$)**: Normalizes marginal contributions independently across rollouts for each position.
 - **Prefix Gate ($g_{i,l}$)**: Modulates gradient magnitude based on prefix accuracy, opening fully if the prefix is perfectly matched and closing if incorrect, promoting hierarchical learning.
 - **Combined Advantage ($\hat{A}_{final}_{i,l}$)**: Integrates the position-level advantage with a group-normalized item-level advantage ($\hat{A}_{item}_i$) to balance structural prefix matching and business conversion signals.
- **TPMA-GRPO Loss**: The final loss function is $L_{TPMA} = - \frac{1}{G} \sum_{i=1}^{G} \frac{1}{L} \sum_{l=1}^{L} g_{i,l} \cdot r_{i,l} \cdot \hat{A}_{final}_{i,l}$, where $r_{i,l}$ is the per-token importance ratio. Clipping operations are omitted, as the prefix gate inherently provides regularization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The effectiveness of OneSearch-V2 was assessed through comprehensive offline evaluations, online A/B tests, and ablation studies.

#### 4.1. Offline Performance

- **Query Understanding and Self-Distillation**: The keyword-based CoT mechanism and reasoning-internalized self-distillation significantly improved performance. Self-distillation alone contributed the most substantial improvement (+1.17% order HR@10, +1.67% click HR@10). The combination of R-Drop, FGM, and focal loss further enhanced recall and ranking, achieving an average improvement of +2.04% in HR@10 and +0.62% in MRR@10 for order behaviors compared to the baseline.
- **Behavior Feedback Preference Alignment**: The composite reward design and the position- and item-level combined alignment formulation (TPMA) achieved optimal performance compared to standard GRPO and a baseline adaptive reward system (PARS). The final OneSearch-V2 model, integrating all innovations, demonstrated superior recall and ranking performance across all metrics, with significant gains for both order and click behaviors.
- **Valid SID Rate**: Nearly every innovation contributed to improvements in the valid SID rate, with OneSearch-V2 achieving 98.90% for click and 99.15% for order, indicating semantic coherence and diverse, relevant item candidate generation.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **CoT Task Augmentation**: Incorporating four CoT tasks consistently improved performance for both head and tail queries. However, explicit CoT reasoning (where the GR model generates CoT context before SIDs) substantially degraded query understanding, suggesting that explicit reasoning during inference can be detrimental for small-scale models. Using keyword-based CoTs as input augmentation (+ RAG) enhanced generation performance but incurred prohibitive latency for online deployment, motivating the self-distillation approach.
- **Self-Distillation Components**: Individually, R-Drop, FGM, and focal loss each improved the baseline. When applied on top of self-distillation, their combined effect yielded larger improvements than their sum, indicating that they synergistically address multifaceted representation instability.
- **Self-Distillation vs. Alternatives**: Self-Distill (S) consistently outperformed Base (T) (teacher model with keyword augmentation) across all metrics, even though the self-distilled student never observed keywords at inference time. This confirms that reasoning capabilities are internalized into the model weights. The proposed self-mode distillation (shared weights, input-level asymmetry) outperformed alternative strategies like special-token distillation, CODI-style hidden-state alignment, EMA-mode, and joint-mode, suggesting its efficacy for the generative search setting. Further analysis of CODI-style approaches indicated that supervision granularity and potential loss incompatibility were limiting factors.

#### 4.3. Online A/B Testing

Rigorous online A/B tests on the Kuaishou mall search platform demonstrated statistically significant improvements (P-value < 0.05) across key business metrics for all OneSearch-V2 variants compared to OneSearch-V1.

- **OneSearch-V2_RAG (V1 + CoT tasks)**: Showed consistent improvements, including +0.52% Item CTR, +0.77% PV CTR, +1.04% Buyer volume, and +1.07% Order volume.
- **OneSearch-V2_Reason (V2_RAG + self-distillation)**: Further amplified performance gains, with +2.59% Item CTR, +1.42% PV CTR, +1.50% Buyer volume, and +1.57% Order volume.
- **OneSearch-V2 (Full Model)**: Achieved the most pronounced improvements:
 - **+3.98%** in Item CTR
 - **+1.17%** in PV CTR
 - **+2.90%** in PV CVR
 - **+2.07%** in Buyer volume
 - **+2.11%** in Order volume
 - **+3.45%** in GMV (Gross Merchandise Value)
- **Industry Impact**: Most industries experienced CTR increases, with particularly pronounced gains in categories with extensive but ambiguous head queries (e.g., Clothing, Shoes, Cosmetics), averaging 3.98%.
- **Manual Evaluation**: Manual assessments confirmed gains in search experience quality, with +1.37% in page good rate, +0.55% in item quality, and +1.65% in query-item relevance for the full OneSearch-V2 model.

#### 4.4. Further Analysis

- **Online Gains Across Segments**: OneSearch-V2 demonstrated consistent CTR improvements across various user segments (low-U, middle-U, high-U), query frequency categories (top, middle, long-tail), and item popularity levels (cold, warm, hot). Long-tail queries showed the highest improvement (+5.37%), followed by top queries (+5.01%), indicating strong performance in handling ambiguous or rare queries. Cold items benefited most significantly, with a 6.16% CTR improvement, supporting merchant satisfaction and platform ecosystem health.
- **Flexible Optimization**: TPMA-GRPO demonstrated flexibility for adaptive training, exemplified during a shopping festival where assigning a higher relevance reward to emerging merchant items resulted in higher positions for those items, showing adaptability to evolving business objectives.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

OneSearch-V2 contributes to the field of generative retrieval by addressing key challenges in large-scale e-commerce search. Its primary significance lies in enhancing query understanding and personalized reasoning while maintaining practical deployment efficiency. The framework demonstrates that explicit reasoning capabilities of large language models can be effectively internalized into smaller generative models through self-distillation, eliminating the need for additional inference costs or serving latency. This approach mitigates common search system issues such as information bubbles and long-tail sparsity, without relying on a separate reward model.

The statistically significant improvements observed in online A/B tests across multiple business metrics, including Item CTR, Buyer volume, and Order volume, suggest a direct positive impact on commercial operations. The framework's ability to boost performance for challenging segments, such as long-tail queries, low-activity users, and cold items, indicates its potential to improve search equity, user satisfaction, and support the growth of new merchants and products. The demonstrated flexibility of the behavior feedback preference alignment system, allowing real-time intervention and adaptive training for dynamic optimization objectives, offers a practical advantage for industrial deployment.

The open-sourcing of the code and data cases aims to facilitate further research in generative retrieval, particularly in areas like beyond-logs training strategies for long-tail queries, unified SID tokenization for diverse content modalities, and efficient online learning mechanisms for future agentic search systems.
