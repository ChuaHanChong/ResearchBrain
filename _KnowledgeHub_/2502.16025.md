# FeatSharp: Your Vision Model Features, Sharper

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers developed FeatSharp, a method to generate sharper, more detailed high-resolution features from low-resolution vision encoders by combining joint bilateral upsampling with tile-guided attentional refinement. The approach achieves state-of-the-art multi-view consistency fidelity, improves semantic segmentation mIoU on ADE20k by +1.66%, and boosts object detection AP on COCO 2017, while maintaining computational efficiency.

## Problem

- State-of-the-art vision encoders, particularly Vision Transformers, operate at low resolutions, leading to a loss of fine-grained details critical for many perception tasks.
- Running these encoders at genuinely high resolutions is computationally prohibitive due to the quadratic scaling of self-attention mechanisms.
- Many Vision Transformers are inflexible to resolution changes, with learned position embeddings degrading feature quality if input resolution is simply increased without specific design or finetuning.

## Method

- FeatSharp combines an extended ==Joint Bilateral Upsampling (JBU)== stack with a novel ==tile-guided attentional refinement== module to upsample features and reintroduce fine-grained details.
- It processes overlapping image tiles with the original low-resolution vision encoder to generate a ==tiled mosaic== of local features, which is then fused with global JBU-upsampled features using a 2D local attention transformer block.
- A ==learnable de-bias buffer== and ==PHI-S normalization== are incorporated to reduce inherent encoder noise and standardize feature distributions, ensuring consistent and coherent output features.

## Results

- FeatSharp consistently achieved the highest multi-view consistency fidelity across all tested foundation models, demonstrating superior robustness and semantic coherence of its upsampled features.
- It delivered substantial performance improvements on downstream tasks, including an mIoU gain of **+1.66%** on ADE20k for semantic segmentation and higher Average Precision (AP) on COCO 2017 for object detection, particularly for small objects.
- When used to provide high-resolution teacher targets for agglomerative models like RADIO, FeatSharp-enhanced teachers led to an average Multi-Task Learning Gain of +**0.39%** across diverse benchmarks, improving student model performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tiling strategies, common in Vision-Language Models for high-resolution input, can be effectively leveraged to re-evaluate low-resolution encoders on local patches, thereby recovering lost fine-grained details.
> - Local attention within a transformer block is sufficient and computationally efficient for coherently fusing global context with detailed local information from tiled features, avoiding the quadratic cost of full high-resolution inference.
> - Feature normalization (PHI-S) and the removal of systematic biases (learned de-bias module) are crucial for maintaining the fidelity, multi-view consistency, and overall quality of upsampled features.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

FeatSharp addresses a critical limitation in the current landscape of computer vision: the inflexibly low resolution of features produced by many state-of-the-art vision encoders, especially Vision Transformers (ViTs) trained with contrastive losses (e.g., CLIP). While Vision Foundation Models (VFMs) have become ubiquitous for tasks like transfer learning, information retrieval, and powering Vision-Language Models (VLMs), their typical operational resolution of 224x224px or 336-448px often leads to a loss of fine-grained details crucial for many perception tasks (e.g., semantic segmentation, object detection, depth estimation). Running these models at genuinely high resolutions is computationally prohibitive due to the quadratic scaling of self-attention in Transformers, and many ViTs struggle with resolution changes due to their reliance on learned position embeddings.

The research landscape has seen several attempts to tackle this issue:
- **Resolution-Flexible ViTs:** Efforts like DINOv2 demonstrate some robustness to position embedding interpolation, and approaches like AM-RADIO, FlexiViT, and RO-ViT aim to create intrinsically resolution-flexible backbones, often through distillation. However, these often still rely on or are constrained by low-resolution teachers.
- **Feature Upsampling:** Prior works like deconvolutional networks, resize-convolution, and pixel-adaptive methods (CARAFE, SAPA, LiFT) directly upsample features. A prominent recent work, FeatUp, introduces learned upsampling algorithms, either using Joint Bilateral Upsampling (JBU) guided by raw pixels or a computationally intensive implicit network. FeatUp's JBU variant, while good at edge preservation, tends to over-smooth object interiors and cannot introduce new details not present in the original low-resolution features.
- **Feature Denoising:** Research like ViT-Denoiser noted the inherent noisiness of ViT features and leveraged multi-view consistency to clean them, a concept also central to FeatUp and FeatSharp.
- **Tiling in VLMs:** A growing trend in VLMs (e.g., LLaVA, InternVL, NVLM, Eagle) is to handle high-resolution inputs by processing an image as a mosaic of lower-resolution tiles, aggregating local features. This approach effectively introduces fine-grained details by re-evaluating the encoder on local patches.
- **Agglomerative Models:** Models like RADIO, Theia, and UNIC focus on distilling knowledge from multiple, diverse VFMs. RADIO, in particular, emphasizes resolution flexibility.

FeatSharp uniquely positions itself by integrating and improving upon these diverse threads. It builds on FeatUp's JBU upsampling, but critically **introduces tile-guided attentional refinement** inspired by VLMs, enabling it to not only coherently upsample but also to **recover or introduce fine-grained details** that were lost or too small for the original low-resolution inference. By doing so, FeatSharp offers a *cheap, coherent, and detail-rich* upsampling solution that can serve as a drop-in enhancement for existing vision systems and provides significantly richer target features for the training of next-generation agglomerative models like RADIO. This work pushes the frontier of making high-quality, high-resolution features accessible without the quadratic cost of full high-resolution ViT inference.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind FeatSharp stems from the inherent limitations of current state-of-the-art vision encoders, particularly Vision Transformers (ViTs) like CLIP. These models, despite their powerful representational capabilities, are predominantly trained and operate at relatively low input resolutions (e.g., 224x224px or 336-448px). This fixed, low-resolution processing leads to two major problems:
1. **Loss of Fine-Grained Details:** Many downstream computer vision tasks, such as semantic segmentation, object detection (especially for small objects), and depth perception, critically depend on fine-grained visual information. Low-resolution feature maps simply cannot capture these details, leading to less precise predictions and limiting performance.
2. **Inflexible and Expensive High-Resolution Inference:** Attempting to run these ViTs at higher resolutions is problematic. It is computationally very expensive due to the quadratic scaling of attention mechanisms, making it infeasible for many applications. Furthermore, many ViTs are "inflexible" to resolution changes; their learned position embeddings mean that simply increasing input resolution can degrade feature quality unless the model is specifically finetuned or designed for it.

Given these challenges, the **key objectives** of the FeatSharp research are:
- **To develop a novel method for coherently and cheaply upsampling the feature maps of low-resolution vision encoders.** The method should produce high-resolution features that maintain the semantic consistency of the original model.
- **To ensure that the upsampled features can recover and introduce fine-grained details** that would otherwise be lost due to the original low input resolution. This goes beyond simple interpolation.
- **To demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach on core perception tasks**, such as semantic segmentation and object detection, showcasing tangible performance improvements.
- **To prove the utility of FeatSharp within agglomerative model training frameworks**, specifically using RADIO. The goal is to provide richer, high-resolution teacher targets for distillation, thereby enhancing the learning process of resolution-flexible student models.

In essence, FeatSharp aims to unlock the full potential of existing low-resolution vision models by making their features sharper, more detailed, and resolution-agnostic, without the need for expensive retraining or full high-resolution inference.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

FeatSharp introduces a novel approach to feature upsampling that combines the strengths of existing techniques while addressing their limitations. The core methodology can be broken down into several key components:

#### 3.1. Foundation on FeatUp's Joint Bilateral Upsampling (JBU)

FeatSharp builds upon the JBU formulation from prior work (FeatUp). JBU is a learned upsampling algorithm that leverages a high-resolution guidance signal (typically raw image pixels) and a low-resolution feature map to produce a higher-resolution feature map. It uses spatial and range kernels to enforce smoothness in visually similar regions while preserving sharp edges. FeatSharp extends FeatUp's typical 2x upsampling stack to handle arbitrary integer upsampling factors by prime factorization. While effective at edge preservation, JBU's reliance on raw pixels means it tends to over-smooth object interiors and cannot introduce entirely new details not already present in the low-resolution features.

#### 3.2. Feature Normalization with PHI-S

To ensure consistent feature distributions and improve training stability across diverse vision encoders, FeatSharp adopts the PHI-S (Progressive Homogenization for Identity-invariant Sampling) normalization method. Unlike raw features (which have varied distributions) or LayerNorm (which can alter the original feature space), PHI-S standardizes backbone features without distortion, allowing the model to effectively learn and retain the original distribution properties. This is crucial for maintaining compatibility with the frozen backbone and for stable distillation.

#### 3.3. Tile-Guided Attentional Refinement (The FeatSharp Module)

This is the central innovation of FeatSharp. Inspired by tiling strategies used in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to handle high-resolution inputs, FeatSharp introduces a mechanism to inject fine-grained local details into the upsampled feature map.
1. **Tiled Feature Generation:** The input image is divided into a grid of overlapping or non-overlapping tiles (e.g., 2x2, 4x4). Each tile is then resized to the *native input resolution of the frozen vision encoder* and processed independently by that encoder. These "tiled features" contain rich local details that might be entirely missed by a single low-resolution pass over the whole image.
2. **Mosaic Reconstruction:** The individual tile feature maps are then stitched back together to form a higher-resolution "tiled mosaic" feature map. While this mosaic provides high detail, it inherently suffers from discontinuities and representation issues at tile boundaries due to the independent processing of each tile.
3. **FeatSharp Module for Fusion:** The JBU-upsampled feature map (from the global low-resolution image) and the newly generated tiled mosaic feature map are concatenated along their channel dimension. This combined tensor is then passed through a specialized **FeatSharp module**.
 - The FeatSharp module consists of a single transformer block utilizing **2D local attention** (sliding window attention) instead of global attention. This design choice is critical for computational efficiency, preventing the quadratic scaling cost associated with full high-resolution ViTs.
 - Following the local attention, a SwiGLU MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) is applied.
 - After processing, the output is sliced to retain only the first C channels, where C is the original feature dimension. This effectively refines the JBU-upsampled features by leveraging the detailed information from the tiled mosaic.
- **Key Benefit:** This tile-guided approach enables FeatSharp to introduce *new details* that were too small for the base encoder to detect at its native low resolution when processing the full image. The attentional refinement block coherently integrates these details while resolving tile boundary inconsistencies.

#### 3.4. Denoising with a Learnable Bias Buffer

Drawing inspiration from ViT-Denoiser, FeatSharp incorporates a learnable bias buffer, `g`, at the output of the frozen vision encoder. This buffer is designed to learn and negate fixed position artifacts or "noise" that the encoder inherently produces. By removing these systematic biases, `g` improves the overall multi-view consistency and coherence of the features. The learned bias is applied consistently to both the global low-resolution features and all individual tile features.

#### 3.5. Training Objective and Computational Complexity

FeatSharp is trained using the **multi-view consistency algorithm**, similar to FeatUp. The objective is to minimize the Mean Squared Error (MSE) between the upsampled-transformed-downsampled features and different low-resolution views of the same image. This encourages the upsampled features to be robust and semantically consistent across various transformations.

Regarding complexity, while FeatSharp requires more evaluations of the base vision model (one for the global image plus `u^2` for `u x u` tiles), its overall cost for high-resolution feature generation is demonstrably lower than running the original ViT at the equivalent high resolution. This is because the FeatSharp module itself uses linear-scaling local attention, whereas the base ViT's global attention scales quadratically with input resolution.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

FeatSharp demonstrates significant improvements across various quantitative and qualitative metrics, validating its effectiveness in producing sharper and more detailed vision model features.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Visualizations

- **PCA Features (Figure 1):** Visualizations using PCA (Principal Component Analysis) clearly show that FeatSharp produces substantially more legible, coherent, and detailed feature maps compared to traditional bilinear upsampling and the baseline FeatUp (JBU-only). For models like SAM, which have thick internal feature edges, FeatUp tends to blur interiors, whereas FeatSharp maintains sharpness and detail. FeatSharp's outputs visually align much closer with "Real 4x" features obtained by actually running scale-equivariant models (like DINOv2 or RADIO) at higher resolutions.
- **Adapter Features (Figure 8, Figure 9):** When used to train agglomerative models like RADIO, FeatSharp-enhanced teachers result in student adapter features that are smooth, detailed, and globally consistent. In contrast, other tiling methods (e.g., "Tile" or "S2") introduce noticeable grid patterns and mode-switching artifacts at tile boundaries, and bilinear upsampling results in high-frequency noise at higher resolutions.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Performance

- **Multi-View Consistency Fidelity (Figure 6, Table 7, Table 8, Table 9):** FeatSharp consistently achieves the highest fidelity scores across all tested foundation models (DFN CLIP, DINOv2-L, PaliGemma, RADIOv2.5-L, SAM-H, SigLIP, ViT). This metric, which measures how well upsampled features can be warped and downsampled back to match low-resolution ground truths, indicates that FeatSharp produces more robust and semantically consistent features. The addition of the learned de-bias module further improves fidelity across all models.
- **Semantic Segmentation (ADE20k - Figure 7):** On semantic segmentation, FeatSharp significantly outperforms both baseline methods (native featurizer at 1x or 2x input size) and FeatUp. For instance, with the strong RADIOv2.5-L model, FeatSharp 2x upsampling combined with 2x input size improves mIoU from RADIO's published 51.47% to 53.13%, a substantial gain. FeatSharp also benefits models that are typically inflexible to resolution changes, like the CLIP family.
- **Object Detection (COCO 2017 - Table 1):** Integrated into a Detectron2 pipeline for object detection, FeatSharp demonstrates clear superiority. It achieves higher Average Precision (AP) scores compared to baselines, FeatUp, SAPA, and ReSFU, particularly for small objects, across both RADIOv2.5-L and SigLIP2-SO400M-512 backbones. This highlights its ability to capture fine details relevant for precise object localization.
- **Agglomerative Models (RADIO Training - Table 2):** When used to provide high-resolution teacher targets for RADIO training, FeatSharp leads to the highest average MTL (Multi-Task Learning) Gain (+0.39%) across a suite of diverse benchmarks (classification, dense prediction, 3D retrieval, VLM tasks). This indicates that the richer, sharper features from FeatSharp-enhanced teachers enable the student RADIO model to learn more effectively, outperforming even the original RADIOv2.5-L on most tasks.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **De-bias Module (Table 7):** Confirming its utility, the learned de-bias module consistently improved multi-view consistency fidelity across all models and upsampling factors.
- **FeatSharp Architecture (Table 8, Table 9, Figure 12):** Extensive ablations on input feature selection (bilinear, FeatUp JBU, or tiles) and residual pathways show that combining JBU with tiled features ("JBU + Tiles") generally offers the best balance of fidelity, detail, and noise reduction, especially for models less adept at resolution scaling.
- **Local Attention Window Size (Figure 13):** Ablations showed optimal local attention window sizes typically around 3x3 or 5x5, indicating that local context is sufficient for effective refinement.
- **Attention/MLP Necessity (Table 10):** Studies confirmed that the attention block, in conjunction with the MLP, is crucial for achieving the highest fidelity, particularly with longer training recipes, proving its role beyond a simple linear layer.

#### 4.4. Throughput Analysis

- **Empirical Throughput (Figure 14):** Empirical measurements demonstrate that FeatSharp's computational cost scales approximately linearly with the upsampling factor (in terms of time per token), which is significantly more efficient than the quadratic scaling of running a full ViT at high resolutions. FeatSharp typically becomes faster than the base ViT for upsampling factors above ~3.3x, making it a practical solution for high-resolution feature generation.

In summary, FeatSharp convincingly demonstrates its ability to generate sharper, more detailed, and coherent high-resolution features from low-resolution vision encoders. It provides substantial performance uplifts across various downstream tasks and improves the training of advanced foundation models, all while maintaining computational efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FeatSharp represents a significant advancement in the practical deployment and further development of Vision Foundation Models (VFMs). Its potential impact spans several key areas:

- **Enhanced Performance for Existing Vision Systems:** FeatSharp provides a lightweight, drop-in extension that can immediately upgrade the feature quality of existing vision systems reliant on pretrained, low-resolution vision encoders (e.g., CLIP, DFN CLIP, SigLIP, ViT). This means applications in semantic segmentation, object detection, and depth perception can achieve higher precision and recover fine-grained details without the prohibitively expensive process of retraining the base model or running it at full high-resolution inference. This can lead to more accurate autonomous driving systems, finer-grained medical image analysis, and improved robotics perception.

- **Improved Training of Next-Generation VFMs:** The work's demonstration of using FeatSharp to provide richer, high-resolution teacher targets for agglomerative models like RADIO is particularly impactful. This allows the distillation process to leverage far more detailed information, enabling student models (FeatSharp-RADIO) to learn superior, intrinsically resolution-flexible representations. This paradigm shift can accelerate the development of more capable and robust VFMs that can operate across a wider range of resolutions without sacrificing performance.

- **Cost-Effective High-Resolution Feature Generation:** One of the most compelling aspects of FeatSharp is its computational efficiency. By leveraging tiling and local attention, it avoids the quadratic scaling cost associated with running standard Vision Transformers at truly high resolutions. This makes high-resolution feature maps accessible for scenarios where computational resources are constrained, opening up new possibilities for real-time applications or large-scale data processing that require fine details.

- **Bridging the Resolution Gap for Inflexible Models:** For models that are inherently inflexible to resolution changes (e.g., many CLIP-family models due to learned position embeddings), FeatSharp offers a viable pathway to unlock their potential for high-resolution tasks. It effectively "sharpens" their features without demanding a complete architectural overhaul or extensive finetuning of the original backbone.

- **Foundation for Multimodal AI:** As Vision-Language Models (VLMs) become increasingly sophisticated, the ability to process and understand high-resolution visual inputs with fine-grained detail is paramount. FeatSharp's approach, partly inspired by VLM tiling strategies, offers a robust method to generate such detailed visual features, which can then be fed into multimodal understanding pipelines, leading to more accurate and nuanced visual reasoning.

- **Open Science and Reproducibility:** The commitment to releasing the training code via NVIDIA Labs (NVlabs) ensures that the research can be reproduced, built upon, and integrated into the broader AI community, fostering further innovation in feature upsampling and VFM development.

In conclusion, FeatSharp addresses a core challenge in modern computer vision with an elegant, effective, and efficient solution. By making vision model features sharper and more detail-rich, it paves the way for higher-performance vision systems, more capable foundation models, and more practical applications across a multitude of domains.
