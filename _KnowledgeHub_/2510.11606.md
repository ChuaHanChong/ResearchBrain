# ExpVid: A Benchmark for Experiment Video Understanding & Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai AI Laboratory, Institute of Science Tokyo, and Nanjing University developed EXPVID, the first benchmark for scientific experiment video understanding and reasoning, leveraging JoVE videos and peer-reviewed papers. The benchmark evaluates Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) across perception, procedural understanding, and scientific reasoning tasks, revealing that proprietary models like GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5 significantly outperform open-source counterparts in complex scientific contexts.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) lack systematic evaluation on complex, real-world scientific experiment videos, especially for fine-grained and long-horizon tasks.
- Existing video understanding benchmarks focus on general human actions or outcome-focused medical scenarios, failing to address 'wet-lab' scientific procedures, while knowledge-driven/scientific benchmarks rely on static images or text, neglecting dynamic experimental videos' temporal and visual complexity.
- A critical gap exists in systematically evaluating MLLMs' ability to perceive experimental manipulations, check procedural fidelity, and connect observations to scientific conclusions.

## Method

- The team curated **EXPVID** using step-by-step experiment videos from JoVE, accompanied by ASR transcripts and peer-reviewed papers, ensuring high-quality visual and textual content.
- They designed a ==three-level hierarchical task structure== to evaluate MLLMs: fine-grained perception (Level-1), procedural understanding (Level-2), and scientific reasoning (Level-3) with 10 distinct tasks.
- A ==vision-centric annotation pipeline==, combining ==LLM assistance== with rigorous multi-disciplinary expert validation, was employed to generate over 7,800 high-quality, vision-dependent QA pairs.

## Results

- GPT-5 achieved the highest average score in Level-3 scientific reasoning tasks at **56.4%**, with the best open-source model scoring only **39.6%**, indicating a substantial performance gap.
- Gemini-2.5-Flash (with 'think') led Level-1 perception tasks with **60.2%** accuracy, surpassing the human baseline of **37.6%** and the best open-source InternVL3-78B at **50.9%**.
- Ablation studies confirmed that visual inputs are indispensable, with performance significantly degrading (near zero for Level-3 tasks) without frames, while some models effectively leveraged up to **256** frames for improved accuracy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Proprietary MLLMs like GPT-5 and Gemini-2.5 consistently outperform open-source models and human baselines, especially in higher-order procedural understanding and scientific reasoning.
> - Current MLLMs struggle with fine detail disambiguation, tracking subtle state changes over time, and robustly linking visual experimental procedures to their broader scientific outcomes and discoveries.
> - While scaling improves performance in open-source models, 'thinking' strategies (e.g., chain-of-thought) do not consistently enhance video-grounded reasoning and can sometimes lead to reliance on logical priors rather than visual evidence.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) marks a significant advancement in AI, enabling models to process and reason over diverse data types, including text, images, and video. These models hold immense promise for revolutionizing various domains, and scientific discovery is a particularly compelling application area. The authors note the "tempting" prospect of delegating parts of the scientific workflow to AI, such as perceiving experimental manipulations, checking procedural fidelity, and connecting observations to conclusions. However, a critical gap exists in systematically evaluating MLLMs' true capabilities in these complex, real-world scientific contexts.

Current video understanding benchmarks, while advancing the field, largely fall short in addressing the unique challenges of scientific experimentation. They typically focus on:
- **General actions or activities:** Datasets like ActivityNet or Hollywood in Homes evaluate common human actions but lack the fine-grained precision, specialized tools, and domain-specific knowledge required for laboratory work.
- **Medical computer vision scenarios:** Benchmarks like SCI-VID and SFE, while scientific, often concentrate on outcome recognition (e.g., medical image diagnosis) rather than the understanding of entire experimental *procedures*.

Knowledge-driven and scientific benchmarks (e.g., ChemBench, MathVision, MMMU) require models to integrate discipline-specific knowledge, but often rely on static images or text, neglecting the temporal and visual complexities inherent in dynamic experimental videos. Benchmarks for long video understanding (e.g., MLVU, LVBench, VRBench) address temporal reasoning but remain "agnostic to domain-specific scientific knowledge and experimental contexts."

ExpVid directly addresses these limitations by introducing the *first* benchmark specifically designed for "scientific experiment video understanding and reasoning." It carves out a unique niche by:
1. **Focusing on "wet-lab" settings:** These environments present distinct challenges like visually subtle operations, small and occluded tools, and fine-grained material states.
2. **Emphasizing "fine-grained and long-horizon" understanding:** Unlike coarse action recognition, ExpVid demands understanding of precise manipulations and dependencies across extended experimental workflows.
3. **Integrating published scientific context:** By curating videos from peer-reviewed journals (JoVE) and pairing them with papers, ExpVid necessitates reasoning that links visual procedures to published scientific conclusions, a capability crucial for scientific AI.

In essence, ExpVid bridges the gap between general video understanding, domain-specific knowledge reasoning, and the practical demands of real-world scientific experimentation. It positions itself as a crucial tool for pushing MLLMs beyond superficial understanding towards becoming genuinely intelligent and trustworthy partners in accelerating scientific discovery.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind ExpVid stems from the recognized potential of MLLMs to transform scientific discovery, contrasted with the inadequacy of existing evaluation tools to truly gauge this potential. The authors articulate several key objectives:

1. **Systematically Evaluate MLLMs on Authentic Scientific Experiment Videos:** The primary objective is to create a benchmark that rigorously tests MLLMs on footage of real-world laboratory experiments, particularly "wet-lab" procedures common in biology, chemistry, and medicine. This is driven by the observation that current benchmarks overlook the "fine-grained and long-horizon nature of authentic laboratory work."
2. **Bridge the Gap in Existing Benchmarks:** The paper explicitly states the goal to "bridge this gap" by moving beyond general video activities or outcome-focused medical scenarios. ExpVid aims to capture the distinct challenges of wet-lab work, which include visually subtle operations, small tools, and complex state changes over time.
3. **Develop a Hierarchical Evaluation Framework:** To mirror the multi-faceted scientific process, a key objective is to design a task hierarchy that assesses MLLMs across different levels of cognitive complexity:
 - **Fine-grained Perception:** Recognizing individual elements like tools, materials, quantities, and precise actions.
 - **Procedural Understanding:** Comprehending the sequence, completeness, and dependencies of steps within a procedure.
 - **Scientific Reasoning:** Connecting the full experimental process to its broader scientific context, including conclusions, analyses, and discoveries.
4. **Ensure Vision-Centricity and Scientific Rigor:** The benchmark aims to create tasks where visual grounding is indispensable for correct answers, preventing models from relying solely on textual cues or prior knowledge. This is achieved through a "vision-centric annotation pipeline" and validation by multi-disciplinary experts, ensuring domain fidelity and the scientific accuracy of questions and distractors.
5. **Chart a Roadmap for Future MLLM Development:** Beyond evaluation, ExpVid is intended to "chart a roadmap for developing MLLMs capable of becoming trustworthy partners in scientific experimentation." By highlighting specific strengths and weaknesses of current models, the benchmark aims to guide research towards addressing crucial limitations for practical scientific AI applications.

In essence, the motivation is to provide a much-needed diagnostic tool that accurately measures how well MLLMs can truly "understand and reason" about the intricacies of scientific experiments from video, thereby accelerating their development into reliable AI assistants for researchers.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ExpVid's construction is a meticulous, multi-stage process involving data curation, multi-level task design, and a robust vision-centric annotation pipeline with expert validation.

#### 3.1. Experiment Data Curation

1. **Collection Source:** Videos, Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) transcripts, and corresponding peer-reviewed papers are collected from **JoVE (Journal of Visualized Experiments)**. JoVE is ideal as it publishes step-by-step experimental protocols in video format, featuring "exo-view recordings" with detailed narrations, providing high-quality visual content and associated textual descriptions. The paired peer-reviewed papers are crucial for higher-level reasoning tasks.
2. **Filtering for Quality and Relevance:**
 - **ASR Transcript Scoring:** DeepSeek-R1 (an LLM) is used to score transcripts on five criteria: Continuity, Alignment, Clarity, Integrity, and Focus (0-5 scale). Only transcripts with an overall score of at least 4 and no dimension below 3.5 are retained (37.2% of initial collection).
 - **Video Duration Constraints:** Videos are filtered to the interquartile range (378–728 seconds) to remove outliers and standardize length.
 - **Content Exclusion:** Videos primarily featuring computer-screen displays or lacking actual laboratory footage are manually excluded.
 - **Discipline Balancing:** Multi-disciplinary experts select 30 top-ranked experiments from each of 13 disciplines, resulting in a balanced dataset of 390 videos (averaging 1,026 words in ASR transcripts).
3. **Preprocessing into a Three-Level Hierarchy:** Videos are segmented to evaluate MLLMs across different temporal scales:
 - **Level-1 (Action-level Clips):** Approximately 10,000 clips (average 8 seconds) are created by segmenting ASR transcripts by punctuation and aligning sentences with video. This is for perception-oriented tasks.
 - **Level-2 (Stage-level Segments):** Approximately 3,500 segments (average 48 seconds) are generated by using DeepSeek-R1 to identify semantically coherent stages (e.g., preparation, main procedures) within each ASR transcript, with paragraphs constrained to 20-60 seconds. Step-level operation descriptions are extracted to form "segment step lists" and "full step lists" for procedural understanding.
 - **Level-3 (Full Procedure Videos):** The full experiment videos (average ~8 minutes) are preserved, with concluding slides/figures removed to ensure reliance on procedural content for long visual context and structural reasoning tasks.

#### 3.2. Task Hierarchy in ExpVid

ExpVid defines a three-level task hierarchy, mirroring the scientific process (Figure 1 in paper):

1. **Level-1: Fine-grained Perception:** Evaluates visual grounding of essential elements in short clips (MCQ tasks):
 - **Material Recognition:** Identify experimental materials.
 - **Tool Recognition:** Identify tools used.
 - **Quantity Recognition:** Determine numerical attributes (e.g., dosage, temperature).
 - **Operation Recognition:** Recognize specific actions performed.
2. **Level-2: Procedural Understanding:** Assesses reasoning about logical and temporal order across multiple steps within stage-level clips:
 - **Step Ordering:** Reorder perturbed step sequences.
 - **Sequence Generation:** Identify ordered steps present in a clip from candidates.
 - **Completeness Verification:** Detect missing steps.
 - **Step Prediction:** Predict the next step in a sequence.
3. **Level-3: Scientific Reasoning:** Requires integrating visual evidence with domain knowledge from the accompanying paper (fill-in-the-blank questions):
 - **Experimental Analysis:** Infer crucial conclusions from experimental data.
 - **Scientific Discovery:** Reason over the entire experiment, abstracting broader insights, significance, or potential applications.

#### 3.3. Vision-centric Annotation with Knowledge Guidance

The annotation pipeline employs a semi-automatic approach combining LLM assistance and human expert validation:

1. **Automated Generation (LLM-assisted):**
 - **Fine-grained Perception:** DeepSeek-R1 extracts candidate entities (materials, tools, quantities, operations) from ASR sentences. A Qwen2.5-VL captioner verifies visual visibility. Distractors for MCQs are generated by DeepSeek-R1 based on task-specific rules to ensure scientific plausibility, visual/functional similarity, and common confusion, forcing reliance on visual cues.
 - **Procedural Understanding:** Step lists (from ASR) are used. DeepSeek-R1 generates plausible but incorrect permutations for Step Ordering. For Sequence Generation, Completeness Verification, and Step Prediction, step lists are embedded into question templates.
 - **Scientific Reasoning:** MinerU extracts key sections (Introduction, Results, Discussion) from peer-reviewed papers. GPT-5 summarizes findings as anchors.
2. **Expert Validation and Refinement:** PhD-level experts in relevant disciplines use an online annotation platform to rigorously review, refine, and validate all generated QA pairs.
 - **Principles:** Questions must be solvable *only* through visual observation and reasoning across the full experiment, not from background knowledge or textual shortcuts. Distractors are carefully balanced.
 - **Level-1 Validation:** Experts verify visibility of targets and plausibility/confusability of distractors.
 - **Level-2 Validation:** Experts check consistency between step lists and video segments, correcting errors and ensuring completeness.
 - **Level-3 Validation:** Experts ensure fill-in-the-blank questions demand genuine reasoning over full workflows, avoid textual shortcuts, and have unambiguous answers.
 - **Iterative Process:** Low-quality annotations are returned for revision until standards are met. This iterative, human-in-the-loop process yields 7,800 QA pairs across 10 tasks in 13 disciplines.

This sophisticated methodology ensures that ExpVid is not only comprehensive and challenging but also scientifically accurate and truly vision-centric, fulfilling its purpose as a diagnostic benchmark for MLLMs in scientific experimentation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of 19 leading MLLMs (both open-source and proprietary) on ExpVid revealed several key insights into their capabilities and limitations:

1. **Proprietary Models Lead, Especially in Higher-Order Reasoning:** Frontier closed-source models, notably GPT-5 and the Gemini-2.5 series, consistently outperformed open-source models and human baselines (general knowledge, non-expert).
 - **Level-1 (Perception):** Gemini-2.5-Flash (with "think") achieved an average of 60.2%, compared to the human baseline of 37.6% and the best open-source InternVL3-78B at 50.9%. This indicates a notable lead in fundamental visual perception.
 - **Level-2 (Procedural Understanding):** GPT-5 led with an average of 57.5%, significantly above the human average of 42.1% and InternVL3-78B's 41.9%. The gap widened for more complex tasks within this level (e.g., Sequence Generation, Step Prediction).
 - **Level-3 (Scientific Reasoning):** GPT-5 achieved the highest average of 56.4% (55.4% for Experimental Analysis, 57.4% for Scientific Discovery). The best open-source model, Intern-S1, scored only 39.6%, highlighting a substantial gap (nearly 17 points) in advanced scientific reasoning capabilities.

2. **Identified Challenges for MLLMs:** While MLLMs excel at coarse-grained recognition, they struggle significantly with:
 - **(i) Disambiguating fine details:** Visually similar tools and materials, especially under occlusion.
 - **(ii) Tracking state changes over time:** Understanding how quantities and material states evolve across multiple steps.
 - **(iii) Linking experimental procedures to scientific outcomes:** Connecting visual evidence from the full experiment to its published conclusions and implications.
 - **Uneven Capabilities:** Within Level-2, models performed highest on Step Ordering (rearranging given information) but significantly lower on Completeness Verification (identifying missing information) and Step Prediction (forecasting future actions). This indicates brittleness and a lack of holistic procedural understanding.

3. **Scaling Effects in Open-Source Models:** A clear positive correlation was observed between model scale and performance in open-source models (e.g., InternVL family from 8B to 78B parameters). Larger models generally exhibited improved capabilities across all three levels, validating scaling as a crucial factor for advancing experiment video understanding in the open-source ecosystem.

4. **Effect of "Thinking" Strategies:** The use of "thinking" (e.g., chain-of-thought prompting) did not consistently improve results and sometimes even degraded performance. Analysis of error cases suggested that "thinking" models could drift from video-grounded reasoning, relying more on logical priors or "reasonable" workflows rather than faithfully interpreting the actual visual sequence. "NoThinking" models often remained more directly video-grounded.

5. **Critical Role of Visual Inputs:** Ablation studies confirmed that visual inputs are indispensable. Performance was consistently boosted with frames, and some tasks became "unsolvable without visual cues." For Level-3 reasoning, accuracy was near zero without frames and increased with more frames. However, models benefited differently from extended temporal context: some (e.g., InternVL3.5) saturated early, while others (e.g., MiMo-VL, Kimi-VL) continued to improve with up to 256 frames, indicating varying abilities to leverage long-video data.

These findings collectively underscore the significant progress of MLLMs in certain aspects of video understanding but also pinpoint critical areas where current models fall short, particularly in the nuanced, long-horizon, and knowledge-intensive reasoning demanded by scientific experimentation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ExpVid represents a significant contribution to the fields of multimodal AI and scientific discovery, offering a new frontier for evaluating and advancing AI capabilities. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **First-of-its-Kind Benchmark:** ExpVid is the first benchmark systematically evaluating MLLMs on scientific experimental footage across a comprehensive three-level hierarchy (fine-grained perception, procedural understanding, scientific reasoning). This fills a critical gap identified in the existing literature, moving beyond general video tasks or outcome-focused scientific benchmarks.
2. **Diagnostic Tool for MLLM Capabilities:** By meticulously breaking down scientific understanding into hierarchical tasks, ExpVid provides a powerful diagnostic tool. It precisely identifies where current MLLMs excel (e.g., coarse recognition) and where they struggle significantly (e.g., disambiguating fine details, tracking state changes, linking procedures to scientific outcomes, higher-order reasoning), particularly highlighting the persistent performance gap between proprietary and open-source models.
3. **Roadmap for Future AI Research:** The identified limitations serve as a clear roadmap for future research directions. To enable MLLMs to become "trustworthy partners in scientific experimentation," future work must focus on:
 - Improving fine-grained visual grounding and disambiguation.
 - Developing robust capabilities for tracking temporal state changes.
 - Enhancing models' ability to integrate visual experimental evidence with complex scientific domain knowledge for higher-level reasoning.
 - Exploring more effective "thinking" or reasoning strategies that are genuinely video-grounded rather than relying on abstract priors.
4. **Acceleration of Scientific Discovery:** By pushing the boundaries of MLLM capabilities in interpreting complex experimental procedures, ExpVid directly contributes to the long-term vision of accelerating scientific discovery. MLLMs capable of perceiving, verifying, and reasoning about real experiments could drastically reduce the time and effort involved in experimental design, execution, troubleshooting, and analysis, making science more efficient and accessible.
5. **Foundation for Trustworthy AI in Science:** The emphasis on "trustworthy assistants or agents" is crucial. By ensuring vision-centric tasks and rigorous expert validation, ExpVid fosters the development of AI systems that can reliably interpret scientific processes, rather than just generating plausible but ungrounded narratives. This is essential for AI adoption in high-stakes scientific domains.
6. **Curated High-Quality Data:** The benchmark's reliance on peer-reviewed JoVE videos, coupled with a sophisticated semi-automatic annotation pipeline and multi-disciplinary expert validation, ensures a high-quality, scientifically rigorous dataset that is suitable for training and evaluating advanced MLLMs.

While the paper acknowledges limitations (e.g., primary focus on wet-lab experiments, lack of granular insight into chain-of-thought reasoning for Level-3 tasks), ExpVid establishes a foundational benchmark. It provides both a challenge and a guiding light for the MLLM community to develop AI that can truly engage with the complexities of scientific inquiry, moving beyond simple recognition to deep procedural and scientific understanding.
