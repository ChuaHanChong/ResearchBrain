# Geometric-Mean Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Geometric-Mean Policy Optimization (GMPO) stabilizes reinforcement learning for large language models (LLMs) by employing a geometric mean for token-level reward aggregation. This approach yielded an average 4.1% improvement in Pass@1 accuracy over GRPO on mathematical reasoning benchmarks and showed gains in multimodal and Mixture-of-Experts settings, demonstrating more stable training and enhanced policy exploration.

## Problem

- Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) for LLMs suffers from training instability due to its arithmetic mean objective being highly susceptible to outlier importance-weighted rewards.
- Extreme importance sampling ratios can lead to aggressive and unstable policy updates, hindering consistent learning and model performance.
- Clipping mechanisms used in GRPO to mitigate instability often restrict policy exploration, potentially limiting the discovery of optimal reasoning paths.

## Method

- GMPO replaces the arithmetic mean of token-level rewards in GRPO's objective function with a ==geometric mean== to inherently suppress the influence of extreme importance-weighted rewards.
- It applies ==token-level clipping==, rather than sequence-level, which provides a more stable range of ==importance sampling ratios== and avoids discarding valuable update signals.
- GMPO utilizes a significantly wider clipping range, leveraging the robustness of the geometric mean to encourage greater policy exploration without reintroducing instability.

## Results

- GMPO improved average Pass@1 accuracy by **4.1%** (**63.4%** vs. **59.3%**) over GRPO on five mathematical reasoning benchmarks using DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B, outperforming several state-of-the-art methods.
- The method demonstrated consistent performance gains across different model scales, including a **2.1%** improvement on MATH500 with a Qwen-32B Mixture-of-Experts model and a **1.4%** gain in multimodal reasoning on Geometry3K.
- GMPO exhibited significantly higher token entropy, more stable gradient norms, and lower KL divergence throughout training compared to GRPO, confirming its superior stability and exploration capabilities.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The geometric mean inherently offers a narrower objective value range and less sensitivity to outliers than the arithmetic mean, leading to more stable policy updates.
> - GMPO's gradient updates are more balanced because each token's gradient is weighted by the geometric mean of all importance sampling ratios in a sequence, making it robust to individual outlier tokens.
> - GMPO maintains higher token entropy and a smaller KL divergence from the pre-trained model during training, indicating enhanced exploration capabilities while preserving stability and preventing premature convergence.
