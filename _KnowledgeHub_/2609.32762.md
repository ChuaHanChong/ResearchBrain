# An Empirical Study on What Matters for Viewpoint-Generalizable Policies in Visual Imitation Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> VGP is a controlled empirical study and resulting recipe for visuomotor policies that generalize across camera viewpoints. It keeps dense DINOv2 patch tokens with cross-view attention and grounds every image, proprioception, and action token in the robot base frame via 4D rotary positional encodings, feeding a flow-matching DiT head. It averages 63.5 on randomized-camera tasks versus 44.1 with sinusoidal encodings, and transfers zero-shot sim-to-real.

## Problem

- End-to-end pixel-to-action policies encode no robot-observation structure, so they overfit training camera poses and degrade sharply when viewpoint shifts, especially with the robot out of frame.
- Point-cloud and voxel policies are viewpoint-robust by construction but lack large-scale pretraining and must downsample dense observations, losing fine image detail to reach tractable control rates.
- Image-based fixes like Plücker camera conditioning or canonical-view reprojection tell the policy where the camera is, not where observed pixels lie relative to the robot.

## Method

- ==Dense token retention==: all ==DINOv2== patch tokens are kept and cross-attended by a ==flow-matching Diffusion Transformer== action head, instead of compressing the scene via spatial softmax or max pooling.
- ==Cross-view encoder==: DINOv2 finetuned following Depth Anything 3, freezing early within-view layers and alternating within-view and global cross-view self-attention over the final eight blocks to fuse cameras.
- Robot-frame ==4D RoPE==: image tokens lifted to 3D via depth and extrinsics, proprioception at the gripper position, action tokens at gripper positions integrated from noised action chunks, plus a temporal index.

## Results

- 4D RoPE lifts randomized-camera Cabinet normalized opening from **39.5** to **64.4** and the task average from **44.1** to **63.5**, beating Plücker raymaps (**43.4**) and canonical views (**48.9**).
- Global compression collapses randomized-camera averages to **37.4** (spatial softmax) and **39.7** (max pooling); Diffusion Policy scores **12.4**, while encoder swaps only move averages between **57.2** and **63.5**.
- VGP beats point-cloud DP3 on Cabinet (**64.4** vs **43.9** randomized), keeps its edge on out-of-distribution cameras (**51.4** vs **45.6** canonical views), and wins zero-shot real-world microwave opening.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Viewpoint robustness comes from telling the policy where observed pixels sit relative to the robot, not where the camera is; geometry-grounded token positions beat camera-pose conditioning.
> - Compressing the scene into a global vector works with a fixed camera but discards the spatial structure needed across viewpoints; keep dense tokens for the action head.
> - Positional encoding and token density matter more than encoder choice, but the recipe depends on calibrated depth and extrinsics and degrades as depth noise grows.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Visual imitation policies learn robot actions from camera images. A camera shift, however, can make a familiar scene look unfamiliar and can leave the robot itself out of frame. That brittleness is a practical deployment bottleneck when cameras cannot be fixed in the training position. The authors keep detailed image patches and tell the policy where those observed patches, the gripper, and proposed actions lie relative to the robot. This differs from merely telling the policy where the camera is. Trained on randomized simulation cameras, the policy was transferred zero-shot to a real robot under random camera configurations.

Randomized simulation cameras, training on those views, and zero-shot transfer to a real robot under random camera configurations. Inset: Cabinet normalized-opening scores with fixed and randomized cameras. Diffusion Policy scores 56.1 and 33.2, and the paper's policy scores 69.1 and 64.4.

### 2. Keep the scene; ground the action

The authors frame this as a controlled study of existing design ingredients, not a new architecture. Their policy, AGP, combines three ingredients: dense image tokens, a cross-view-finetuned DINOv2 encoder, and robot-frame 4D rotary positional encoding (4D RoPE). The key idea is that a policy may not need the camera's location as such. Instead, it needs the location of what the camera sees relative to the robot.

The vision encoder produces dense patch tokens from calibrated RGB views. Depth and camera pose lift the center pixel of each patch into a 3D position in the robot-base frame. Each image, proprioception, and action token receives a spatiotemporal position: robot-frame x, y, z plus a history-time index. A shared Diffusion Transformer (DiT) action head cross-attends from proprioception and noised-action tokens to the image tokens.

Future actions have no known location at inference. The policy therefore estimates future gripper positions by accumulating the translational components of its current noisy action chunk. As denoising proceeds, these estimates approach the future gripper path. 4D RoPE means rotary positional embeddings applied independently to the x, y, z, and time coordinates. It is the proposed geometric grounding, not a guarantee of viewpoint invariance in every deployment setting.

Calibrated RGB views enter a DINOv2-initialized visual encoder with within-view and cross-view attention, producing dense image tokens. Image, proprioception, and action tokens receive (x, y, z, h) positions. The DiT cross-attends from proprioception and action tokens to image tokens and self-attends over the action chunk.

### 3. Which ingredients survive the test?

Cabinet is a simulated articulated-object opening task. Its metric, normalized opening, is the method's opened joint angle relative to the expert demonstrator, not task success rate. Both comparisons below change one component relative to AGP, with cameras randomized during training and evaluation. Values are means ± standard deviations over three seeds.

The cleanest mechanism test replaces 4D RoPE with image-space sinusoidal positions. On randomized-camera Cabinet, AGP scores 64.4 ± 0.8 normalized opening, versus 39.5 ± 3.5 with the sinusoidal positions. The paper's rationale is that a fixed image patch index can correspond to different robot-relative 3D positions as the camera moves.

Compression points the same way. With spatial softmax instead of dense tokens, randomized-camera Cabinet scores 33.4 ± 0.2, compared with AGP's 64.4 ± 0.8. Compression also removes spatial detail the action head can use. Cross-view attention helps, but the authors report that encoder changes matter less than compression and positional encoding. These ablations do not isolate 4D RoPE as the only cause of all performance gains.

### 4. From simulation to a real robot

The authors trained on 2,358 simulated microwave-opening demonstrations from five microwave objects, with visual and camera randomization. They then evaluated on a Franka robot. Its camera poses were unknown during data generation but within the randomized simulation distribution. The test used three camera configurations and 30 trials per policy. AGP outperformed Plücker Raymaps and Canonical Views on both grasp success and normalized opening.

### 5. What the geometry buys

Preserving local visual evidence and placing it in the robot's frame gives the action head a more consistent relationship between what it sees and where it acts as the camera changes. The study measures this as improved task performance. Deployment still depends on calibrated RGB-D input. The real-world transfer demonstration covers one object category and camera poses within the simulation training distribution.
