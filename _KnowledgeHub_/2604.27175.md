# Global Sampling-Based Trajectory Optimization for Contact-Rich Manipulation via KernelSOS

> [!summary] Summary
> Global-MPPI, a framework for global sampling-based trajectory optimization in contact-rich robotic manipulation, integrates Kernel Sum-of-Squares for global exploration with Model Predictive Path Integral for local refinement and a graduated non-convexity strategy. This approach consistently achieved faster convergence and lower final costs with reduced variance compared to existing sampling-based methods on challenging manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks present highly non-convex and non-smooth optimization landscapes due to high-dimensional, underactuated systems and hybrid contact dynamics.
- Existing sampling-based optimization methods (e.g., MPPI, CEM) are local in nature, often converging to suboptimal solutions, especially as task complexity and dimensionality increase.
- Direct application of global optimization techniques like KernelSOS to contact-rich problems is hindered by severe non-smoothness, sparse information content, difficulty in discovering remote local minima, and computational burden from large sample requirements.

## Method

- The Global-==MPPI== framework integrates a graduated non-convexity (GNC) strategy, KernelSOS for global exploration, and MPPI for local refinement within a receding-horizon control paradigm.
- A general GNC strategy utilizes Log-Sum-Exp (LSE) smoothing to iteratively create a sequence of surrogate problems with gradually increasing non-convexity, aiding coarse-to-fine exploration and improving objective smoothness for KernelSOS.
- KernelSOS constructs a global non-parametric surrogate model from samples to identify promising regions, employing a data-driven linear-to-quadratic auto-calibration strategy for kernel parameters based on an empirical Bayes approach; MPPI then refines these global candidates locally.

## Results

- On the PushT task, Global-MPPI demonstrated faster convergence, achieved lower final costs, and exhibited reduced variance compared to Predictive Sampling, vanilla MPPI, and DIAL-MPC.
- For dexterous in-hand manipulation, Global-MPPI again achieved the fastest convergence and the lowest final cost, reaching DIAL-MPC's best performance in approximately half the iterations.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of local refinement, automatic calibration, and the GNC component, showing that their removal led to higher final costs, increased variance, or compromised convergence speed and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified framework combining global exploration via KernelSOS and local refinement via MPPI effectively leverages the strengths of both, overcoming limitations of purely local methods while mitigating scalability challenges of global solvers in high-dimensional, non-smooth spaces.
> - Graduated non-convexity with Log-Sum-Exp smoothing provides a function-agnostic mechanism to smooth optimization landscapes iteratively, thereby facilitating more effective global exploration and making global optimization techniques more applicable to non-smooth contact-rich problems.
> - Automating the calibration of KernelSOS kernel parameters using an empirical Bayes approach eliminates the need for manual tuning, significantly enhancing the robustness and practical applicability of global optimization in dynamic contact-rich environments.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Contact-rich robotic manipulation, encompassing tasks such as pushing, grasping, and in-hand manipulation, presents significant challenges in modern robotics. These problems typically involve high-dimensional, underactuated systems with hybrid contact dynamics, resulting in optimization landscapes that are both highly non-convex and non-smooth. Consequently, the computation of long-horizon optimal plans and control policies for such tasks remains a core research challenge.

Sampling-based optimization methods have gained traction for addressing these problems due to their ability to handle non-smooth, non-linear dynamics without requiring gradient information, and their compatibility with GPU-accelerated parallel simulation for efficient evaluation of numerous candidate trajectories. Popular methods in this category include Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI), Cross-Entropy Method (CEM), and Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy (CMA-ES). However, a recognized limitation of these methods is their fundamentally local nature. This locality often leads to convergence to suboptimal solutions or task failures, particularly as the complexity, planning horizon, and dimensionality of tasks increase. While some approaches incorporate entropy regularization to enhance exploration, their underlying mechanisms remain primarily local.

Alternative lines of research include learning-based methods (e.g., deep reinforcement learning), which demonstrate robustness but require extensive tuning and data, limiting generalization. Model-based optimal control frameworks often rely on explicit contact models but are inherently local and demand high-quality initial guesses. Global optimization formulations, such as mixed-integer programming, can offer optimality guarantees but do not scale effectively with problem dimension or the number of contact modes.

More recent zero-order methods, like Bayesian optimization (BO), incorporate global exploration by fitting surrogate models, but the optimization of their acquisition functions can still be non-convex. Kernel Sum-of-Squares (KernelSOS) is a relatively new global optimization technique that constructs a global surrogate of the cost landscape from samples and minimizes it via a Semidefinite Program (SDP), offering global optimality guarantees within the surrogate space. Previous applications of KernelSOS have been in domains like optimal control and estimation. However, its direct application to high-dimensional, long-horizon, contact-rich problems has been challenging due to the severe non-smoothness of the cost landscapes, the sparse information content (e.g., in regions without contact), the difficulty in discovering remote local minima, and the computational burden associated with the large number of samples often required for accurate global approximation, leading to intractable SDPs.

This paper positions itself by addressing the limitations of local sampling-based methods in contact-rich settings while overcoming the specific hurdles that have historically prevented KernelSOS from being effectively applied to these problems. It integrates the global exploration capabilities of KernelSOS with the local refinement of MPPI, complemented by a novel graduated non-convexity strategy. This combined approach aims to provide a robust framework for global trajectory optimization in the presence of complex, non-smooth, and non-convex contact dynamics, thereby advancing the state of the art in contact-rich robotic manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a global sampling-based trajectory optimization framework, termed Global-MPPI, specifically designed for contact-rich manipulation problems. The motivation stems from several critical challenges inherent to these tasks and the limitations of existing optimization methodologies:

1. **High-Dimensional, Non-Convex, and Non-Smooth Landscapes**: Contact-rich manipulation involves systems with high degrees of freedom, long planning horizons, and complex hybrid contact dynamics. These characteristics generate cost landscapes that are highly non-convex and non-smooth, often featuring sharp, asymmetric basins and discontinuities. Such landscapes pose a significant challenge for efficient optimization, as local methods are prone to becoming trapped in suboptimal minima.
2. **Limitations of Local Sampling-Based Methods**: While sampling-based methods like MPPI are capable of handling non-differentiable dynamics, their inherent local nature means they typically converge to the nearest local minimum. This becomes a substantial impediment in contact-rich scenarios where the globally optimal solution may be far from the initial guess or surrounded by numerous suboptimal local minima. The effectiveness of these methods diminishes as task complexity, planning horizon, and system dimensionality increase.
3. **Challenges in Applying Global Optimization Techniques**: General global optimization methods, particularly those leveraging convex relaxation, often do not scale to the high-dimensional and combinatorial nature of contact-rich problems. Furthermore, advanced global sampling methods like KernelSOS, despite their theoretical guarantees for global optimality within a surrogate space, face practical difficulties in contact-rich settings. These difficulties include:
 - **Sensitivity to Non-Smoothness**: KernelSOS's theoretical underpinnings and practical performance rely on a degree of smoothness in the objective function, which is violated by discontinuous contact dynamics.
 - **Hyperparameter Tuning**: Effective application of KernelSOS requires careful calibration of kernel parameters, which can vary significantly across a long-horizon task with changing contact modes, making manual tuning impractical.
 - **Computational Scalability**: To accurately approximate highly non-convex landscapes and identify globally promising regions, KernelSOS may demand a prohibitively large number of samples. This, in turn, can lead to computationally intractable Semidefinite Programs (SDPs), hindering its application to real-world problems.

To address these motivations, the authors established the following key objectives for Global-MPPI:
- To create a unified framework that seamlessly integrates global exploration with local refinement, leveraging the strengths of both KernelSOS and MPPI.
- To introduce a general mechanism, independent of specific loss functions, for iteratively smoothing the optimization landscape, thereby improving the exploration capabilities of KernelSOS in non-smooth environments.
- To develop an automatic and continuous calibration method for KernelSOS kernel parameters, eliminating the need for manual tuning and improving robustness across varied contact scenarios.
- To validate the proposed method's performance on high-dimensional, long-horizon contact-rich manipulation tasks using a GPU-accelerated physics simulator, demonstrating its ability to robustly uncover high-quality solutions, achieve faster convergence, and yield lower final costs compared to existing baseline methods.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Global-MPPI is presented as a global sampling-based trajectory optimization framework specifically designed for contact-rich problems. It operates within a receding-horizon control paradigm and consists of three integrated components: a graduated non-convexity (GNC) strategy, KernelSOS for global exploration, and MPPI for local refinement. The framework aims to balance global exploration with local convergence by operating in a coarse-to-fine manner.

The problem is formulated as a finite-horizon trajectory optimization task (Equation 1), seeking to minimize a cumulative cost function $J(u)$ subject to initial state conditions, contact-implicit dynamics $f_{dyn}(·)$, and control input constraints. The single-shooting formulation is employed, where state trajectories are rolled out from the initial condition, simplifying the problem to finding an optimal control sequence $u$.

The methodological details of each component are as follows:

1. **Graduated Non-Convexity via Log-Sum-Exp Smoothing (GNC-LSE)**:
 - **Purpose**: To tackle non-convex and non-smooth optimization landscapes. GNC solves a sequence of surrogate problems with gradually increasing non-convexity, facilitating coarse-to-fine exploration. It also helps KernelSOS by providing smoother objective functions.
 - **Mechanism**: A general GNC strategy based on Log-Sum-Exp (LSE) smoothing is proposed. The LSE-smoothed surrogate function $J_{lse}(u, \sigma_{lse})$ (Equation 2) is defined using a temperature parameter $\lambda$ and a GNC smoothing parameter $\sigma_{lse}$.
 - **Process**: Large $\sigma_{lse}$ values produce a strongly smoothed, nearly convex surrogate, while smaller values progressively recover the original non-smooth cost function. $\sigma_{lse}$ is gradually reduced across restart phases, with solutions from one stage warm-starting the next. This process aims to enlarge the basin of attraction around low-cost solutions and improve robustness to initialization and sampling variability. LSE smoothing also places greater weight on global minima.

2. **Kernel Sum-of-Squares (KernelSOS) for Global Optimization**:
 - **Purpose**: To identify globally promising regions of the smoothed cost landscape. KernelSOS generalizes classical Sum-of-Squares optimization by using kernel functions to construct a global non-parametric surrogate model.
 - **Mechanism**: Given a finite number of samples $N$ and their corresponding smoothed cost evaluations, KernelSOS constructs a kernel matrix $K_\sigma$ using a chosen positive-definite kernel $k_\sigma$. It then formulates and solves a Semidefinite Program (SDP) (Equation 3) to find an approximate global minimum $c^*_{ksos}$ of the surrogate function $f(u) = c + v(u)^T R^{-1} B R^{-T} v(u)$. The relaxation gap between $c^*_{ksos}$ and the true global optimum is guaranteed to asymptotically approach zero under certain conditions.
 - **Auto-Calibration for KernelSOS**: To address the challenge of selecting appropriate kernel parameters $\sigma_{ksos}$, a data-driven linear-to-quadratic auto-calibration strategy based on an empirical Bayes approach is introduced. This method estimates the kernel parameter by minimizing the negative log-marginal likelihood (NLL) (Equation 4) with respect to a linear Gaussian process surrogate model. A two-stage process is used: a coarse grid search over a candidate set followed by local gradient-based refinement. This approach aims to eliminate manual tuning and provide a statistically principled method for kernel parameter estimation.

3. **Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) for Local Refinement**:
 - **Purpose**: To efficiently refine the global solution candidate proposed by KernelSOS. While KernelSOS performs global exploration, its accuracy may be limited in high-dimensional settings with sparse samples. MPPI, a local solver, is effective for refining solutions within a basin of attraction.
 - **Mechanism**: MPPI is well-suited because its sampling-based update rule can be interpreted as a stochastic gradient-descent step on the LSE-smoothed objective (Equation 5). By selecting appropriate step size parameters, the standard MPPI update rule (Equation 8) is recovered.
 - **Integration**: MPPI is initialized with the global solution candidate from KernelSOS and operates on the LSE-smoothed surrogate function, aligning naturally with the GNC scheme. It relies solely on cost evaluations, making it suitable for contact-rich environments without requiring explicit analytic models or gradients.

**Final Algorithm and Implementation Details (Algorithm 1)**:
The Global-MPPI algorithm operates iteratively, alternating between sampling for global search, KernelSOS for finding a global candidate, and MPPI for local refinement. The sampling radius $\Delta$ and the LSE smoothing parameter $\sigma_{lse}$ are gradually reduced across restart stages. Control trajectories are parameterized by cubic splines to reduce dimensionality and enforce smoothness. Input constraints are handled by clipping. The algorithm terminates upon achieving a predefined tolerance for objective improvement. For efficiency, sampling, rollouts, function evaluation, LSE smoothing, and MPPI updates are performed on a GPU, while KernelSOS runs on the CPU using a custom interior-point SDP solver optimized for rank-one structure.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The Global-MPPI framework was evaluated on two contact-rich benchmark tasks using the MuJoCo physics simulator on a workstation equipped with an Intel i9 CPU and an RTX 4090 GPU. The performance was compared against Predictive Sampling, vanilla MPPI, and DIAL-MPC, with all methods tested under consistent environmental conditions and initial states.

**A. PushT Task**
The PushT task involves a robotic pusher manipulating a T-shaped object to a target pose in a planar environment. This task is characterized by discontinuous contact dynamics, frictional interactions, and frequent contact mode switches, leading to a highly non-smooth and non-convex optimization landscape. The cost function penalizes both translational and rotational errors between the object's current and target poses. The control sequence was a 12-dimensional vector, parameterized by six control points for a cubic spline.

- **Performance Comparison (Fig. 3, left panel)**: Global-MPPI demonstrated faster convergence, achieved lower final costs, and exhibited reduced variance compared to all baseline methods. MPPI and DIAL-MPC, being local optimization methods, frequently became trapped in poor local minima, resulting in higher costs. Predictive Sampling, while more robust to local minima, converged at a slower rate and with higher variance.
- **Ablation Study (Fig. 4, left panel)**: Removing local refinement or automatic calibration from Global-MPPI resulted in higher final costs and increased variance. The removal of the GNC component led to a slightly lower final cost but compromised convergence speed and stability. This suggests GNC plays a role in stabilizing and accelerating the optimization process.
- **Trajectory Visualization (Fig. 5)**: Visualizations confirmed that Global-MPPI produced shorter, more efficient robot trajectories that successfully moved the object to the target region. In contrast, local methods like MPPI and DIAL-MPC failed to move the object to the target region, while Predictive Sampling achieved the target but via less efficient, higher-cost trajectories due to its reliance on random exploration.

**B. Dexterous In-Hand Manipulation**
This task involved a 16-DoF LEAP hand rotating and stabilizing a cube to a desired target orientation, characterized by intermittent multi-point contacts and highly nonlinear dynamics. The cost function solely penalized orientation error. The control sequence had a dimensionality of 64, parameterized by four control points.

- **Performance Comparison (Fig. 3, right panel)**: Global-MPPI again achieved the fastest convergence and the lowest final cost, demonstrating robust performance in this high-dimensional, contact-rich problem. Predictive Sampling showed limited improvement and plateaued at a significantly higher cost due to inefficient exploration in the high-dimensional action space. MPPI often converged to suboptimal local minima or failed to maintain stable object manipulation. DIAL-MPC showed improved performance compared to the PushT task but still exhibited higher final costs and slower convergence than Global-MPPI, which reached DIAL-MPC's best performance in approximately half the iterations.
- **Ablation Study (Fig. 4, right panel)**: While all ablated Global-MPPI variants achieved similar final costs, the full Global-MPPI converged notably faster, reaching its final cost within approximately 30 iterations. Automatic calibration remained an important factor, while the impact of removing local refinement or GNC on the *final cost* was less pronounced for this specific task, though they still contributed to faster convergence.

In summary, the experimental results consistently demonstrate that Global-MPPI robustly uncovers high-quality solutions, achieving faster convergence and lower final costs with reduced variance across different challenging contact-rich manipulation tasks when compared to existing sampling-based methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of Global-MPPI represents a significant contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for tasks involving frequent and complex contact interactions. Its primary significance lies in providing a unified trajectory optimization framework that effectively addresses the long-standing challenge of performing global optimization over highly non-convex and non-smooth landscapes, which are characteristic of contact-rich problems.

The key innovations contributing to this significance include:
- **Effective Integration of Global and Local Optimization**: By combining KernelSOS for global exploration with MPPI for local refinement, Global-MPPI leverages the strengths of both paradigms. This hybrid approach enables the framework to overcome the limitations of purely local sampling-based methods, which are prone to getting trapped in suboptimal solutions, while also mitigating the accuracy and scalability challenges of global solvers in high-dimensional spaces.
- **General Graduated Non-Convexity Strategy**: The introduction of a general, function-agnostic GNC strategy based on Log-Sum-Exp smoothing is crucial. This mechanism allows the optimization landscape to be progressively smoothed, transitioning from a more convex surrogate to the original non-smooth objective. This gradual transition facilitates more effective global exploration, enlarges the basins of attraction for low-cost solutions, and improves the ability of KernelSOS to construct accurate surrogates, thus making global methods more applicable to non-smooth problems.
- **Automated Kernel Parameter Calibration**: The proposed data-driven, empirical Bayes approach for automatically calibrating KernelSOS parameters eliminates the need for extensive manual tuning. This significantly enhances the robustness and practicality of KernelSOS for dynamic environments where optimal kernel parameters might vary over time or across different contact modes.
- **Demonstrated Performance on Challenging Tasks**: The empirical validation on the PushT task and dexterous in-hand manipulation, both characterized by high dimensionality, non-smooth dynamics, and long horizons, substantiates the efficacy of Global-MPPI. The consistent achievement of faster convergence, lower final costs, and reduced variance compared to state-of-the-art baselines indicates a notable advancement in the ability to find high-quality solutions for complex manipulation problems.

The potential impact of Global-MPPI is multifaceted. It offers a more reliable and efficient approach for planning complex manipulation sequences, which could lead to advancements in robot dexterity and autonomy in industrial, service, and even domestic applications. The sampling-based nature, requiring only cost evaluations, also allows for integration with complex and highly accurate physics simulators, bridging the gap between simulation and potential real-world deployment. The open-sourcing of the implementation is expected to facilitate further research and development in this domain.

Despite its advancements, the paper acknowledges certain limitations. The cubic scaling of SDP computational cost with the number of samples poses a challenge for real-time and very large-scale applications. Future work on GPU-accelerated SDP solvers and more efficient sampling strategies could address this. Additionally, the reliance on physics simulators introduces a sim-to-real gap, which, while partially mitigated by the receding-horizon MPC framework, necessitates comprehensive real-world validation for practical implementation. These limitations define important directions for future research to expand the applicability and robustness of this framework.
