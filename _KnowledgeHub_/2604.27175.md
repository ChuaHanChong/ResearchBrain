# Global Sampling-Based Trajectory Optimization for Contact-Rich Manipulation via KernelSOS

> [!summary] Summary
> Global-MPPI, a framework for global sampling-based trajectory optimization in contact-rich robotic manipulation, integrates Kernel Sum-of-Squares for global exploration with Model Predictive Path Integral for local refinement and a graduated non-convexity strategy. This approach consistently achieved faster convergence and lower final costs with reduced variance compared to existing sampling-based methods on challenging manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Contact-rich robotic manipulation tasks present highly non-convex and non-smooth optimization landscapes due to high-dimensional, underactuated systems and hybrid contact dynamics.
- Existing sampling-based optimization methods (e.g., MPPI, CEM) are local in nature, often converging to suboptimal solutions, especially as task complexity and dimensionality increase.
- Direct application of global optimization techniques like KernelSOS to contact-rich problems is hindered by severe non-smoothness, sparse information content, difficulty in discovering remote local minima, and computational burden from large sample requirements.

## Method

- The Global-==MPPI== framework integrates a graduated non-convexity (GNC) strategy, KernelSOS for global exploration, and MPPI for local refinement within a receding-horizon control paradigm.
- A general GNC strategy utilizes Log-Sum-Exp (LSE) smoothing to iteratively create a sequence of surrogate problems with gradually increasing non-convexity, aiding coarse-to-fine exploration and improving objective smoothness for KernelSOS.
- KernelSOS constructs a global non-parametric surrogate model from samples to identify promising regions, employing a data-driven linear-to-quadratic auto-calibration strategy for kernel parameters based on an empirical Bayes approach; MPPI then refines these global candidates locally.

## Results

- On the PushT task, Global-MPPI demonstrated faster convergence, achieved lower final costs, and exhibited reduced variance compared to Predictive Sampling, vanilla MPPI, and DIAL-MPC.
- For dexterous in-hand manipulation, Global-MPPI again achieved the fastest convergence and the lowest final cost, reaching DIAL-MPC's best performance in approximately half the iterations.
- Ablation studies confirmed the importance of local refinement, automatic calibration, and the GNC component, showing that their removal led to higher final costs, increased variance, or compromised convergence speed and stability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A unified framework combining global exploration via KernelSOS and local refinement via MPPI effectively leverages the strengths of both, overcoming limitations of purely local methods while mitigating scalability challenges of global solvers in high-dimensional, non-smooth spaces.
> - Graduated non-convexity with Log-Sum-Exp smoothing provides a function-agnostic mechanism to smooth optimization landscapes iteratively, thereby facilitating more effective global exploration and making global optimization techniques more applicable to non-smooth contact-rich problems.
> - Automating the calibration of KernelSOS kernel parameters using an empirical Bayes approach eliminates the need for manual tuning, significantly enhancing the robustness and practical applicability of global optimization in dynamic contact-rich environments.
