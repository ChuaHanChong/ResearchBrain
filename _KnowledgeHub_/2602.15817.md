# Solving Parameter-Robust Avoid Problems with Unknown Feasibility using Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Feasibility-Guided Exploration (FGE), developed at MIT and MIT Lincoln Laboratory, introduces a reinforcement learning framework to simultaneously identify the maximal feasible operating region for a system and learn a robust policy that guarantees safety within that region. FGE consistently achieved superior safety rates and expanded coverage of safe operating conditions across diverse environments, including high-dimensional and image-based tasks, outperforming existing robust RL and curriculum learning methods by making over 50% more challenging parameters safe.

## Problem

- Traditional optimal safe controller synthesis methods like Hamilton-Jacobi analysis are computationally intractable for high-dimensional systems.
- Current deep RL approaches optimize expected returns over user-defined initial condition distributions, which often mismatches worst-case safety objectives and can lead to critical failures in low-probability regions.
- Robust optimization requires a priori knowledge of a feasible set of initial conditions, which is difficult to determine, making the problem ill-posed if an infeasible set is assumed.

## Method

- The ==Feasibility-Guided Exploration== (**FGE**) framework iteratively estimates the feasible parameter set, optimizes a robust policy via saddle-point finding, and employs explicit exploration to expand the feasible set estimate.
- A conservative classifier is learned using variational inference over a mixture of empirically observed safe parameters and on-policy samples to robustly estimate the unknown feasible parameter set.
- An 'explore distribution' actively seeks parameters with uncertain or estimated infeasibility, guiding policy improvement and facilitating the discovery of new safe parameters to overcome learning stagnation.

## Results

- FGE consistently demonstrated higher safety rates and the largest 'Coverage Gain vs DR' across all tested environments, making over **50%** more challenging parameters safe than the next best existing method while maintaining low 'Coverage Loss vs DR'.
- Case studies, such as in the Dubins and Hopper environments, showed FGE's ability to efficiently allocate samples to rare and difficult conditions, leading to successful safety achievements where other methods failed to concentrate samples.
- FGE successfully scaled to high-dimensional state spaces (Lander9D with 9D parameters) and RGB image observations, maintaining superior safety rates in these complex scenarios compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The problem of concurrently identifying a maximal feasible operating region and learning a robust safe policy can be effectively addressed through an integrated iterative framework.
> - Learning a conservative feasibility classifier using a mixture of reliable safe observations and policy rollouts provides a robust way to estimate the boundaries of the unknown feasible parameter set.
> - Active, goal-directed exploration of uncertain or infeasible regions is essential for efficiently discovering new safe operating conditions and expanding the known feasible region, which prevents negative feedback loops in learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses a fundamental challenge in applying reinforcement learning (RL) to optimal safe controller synthesis. Traditional optimal safe controller synthesis aims to determine the maximal set of initial conditions from which a system can be guaranteed safe indefinitely. Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ)-based optimal control analysis is a powerful tool for this but is limited by the curse of dimensionality, making it computationally intractable for high-dimensional systems. Recent advancements in deep RL have shown promise in approximating optimal control solutions for high-dimensional nonlinear dynamics. However, existing RL approaches typically optimize expected returns over a user-defined distribution of initial conditions, which fundamentally mismatches the worst-case safety objective. This can lead to policies that perform well on frequently sampled states but fail critically in low-probability regions that are still part of the operating safe set.

A natural alternative, robust optimization, attempts to minimize cost under the worst-case initial condition within a specified set of interest. This aligns with worst-case safety but requires *a priori* knowledge of a feasible candidate set of initial conditions, which is itself a non-trivial problem whose exact solution is computationally infeasible. If the specified set is infeasible, the robust problem offers no meaningful solution, as all policies would appear equally suboptimal.

This research distinguishes itself from existing literature in several ways:
- **Saddle-point problems:** While saddle-point problems have been applied in RL for game-theoretic equilibria, this work introduces the additional complexity of an unknown and optimizable domain (the feasible parameter set) for one of the players.
- **Robust Reinforcement Learning:** Approaches like Domain Randomization (DR) train policies on randomized environments, and methods like Robust Adversarial Reinforcement Learning (RARL) use adversarial disturbances. However, RARL implicitly assumes feasibility under the worst-case disturbance, a premise not adopted in this work. This research instead seeks to identify the largest possible *feasible subset* of the parameter space rather than assuming universal feasibility.
- **Curriculum Learning and Unsupervised Environment Design (UED):** These fields also modify initial state distributions, usually to improve learning efficiency or maximize regret. While FGE shares some mechanisms with these areas, its specific focus on parameter-robust *avoid* problems allows it to leverage problem-specific structures, such as a feasibility classifier, which differentiates it from general RL or UED tasks.
- **Safe Reinforcement Learning:** Current safe RL methods typically add safety constraints to an existing task objective, often leading to constrained optimization problems. This work, in contrast, focuses exclusively on a safety objective, aiming solely to maximize the set of parameters under which safety can be guaranteed, without a separate task objective.

By addressing the simultaneous identification of a feasible operating region and learning a robust policy within that region, this work contributes a novel formulation to the field of safe and robust control synthesis in high-dimensional systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the limitations of current deep reinforcement learning applications in safety-critical optimal control. As detailed in the broader research landscape, traditional RL's focus on expected returns over user-defined distributions creates a misalignment with the goal of optimal safe controller synthesis, which demands worst-case safety over an entire operating region. When RL policies are trained on arbitrary or narrow distributions, they may perform well on common scenarios but fail in low-probability, safety-critical situations that are still within the intended operating domain.

A robust optimization framework, which seeks to minimize cost under the worst-case initial condition, offers a more appropriate perspective for safety-critical applications. However, this approach critically depends on being able to define a *feasible* set of initial conditions (or parameters) for which safety is actually achievable. Determining this feasible set itself is a challenging problem, equivalent to the goal of HJ-based analysis, and is computationally intractable to solve exactly *a priori*. If the chosen parameter set includes conditions under which no policy can guarantee safety, the robust optimization problem becomes ill-posed, yielding no meaningful guidance for policy learning.

To address this gap, the key objectives of this work are twofold:
1. **To formally define and tackle the novel "parameter-robust avoid problem with unknown feasibility."** This problem formulation explicitly acknowledges that the set of initial conditions (parameters) for which safety can be achieved is unknown and must be discovered.
2. **To simultaneously (i) identify a maximal subset of parameters (Θ* ⊆ Θ) where safety is achievable, and (ii) learn a control policy (π) that satisfies the safety constraints over this identified feasible subset.** This joint optimization seeks to maximize the coverage of safe operating conditions while ensuring that the learned policy is robust to the worst-case within that discovered region.

The paper motivates its specific problem formulation (Equation 5) by transforming the traditional HJ optimal safe control problem into a sum-over-time objective (Equation 3) with a negative indicator reward, which is compatible with standard RL algorithms. This transformation ensures that J(π, θ) = 0 indicates a safe policy for parameter θ. The robust objective is then formulated to maximize the *size* of the feasible subset Θ' (denoted as |Θ'|) such that a policy exists that guarantees safety (i.e., min_{θ∈Θ'} J(π, θ) = 0). This approach avoids the pitfalls of assuming a universally feasible parameter space, thereby enabling meaningful policy learning even in complex, partially unknown safety domains.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, **Feasibility-Guided Exploration (FGE)**, is an iterative framework designed to concurrently identify a maximal feasible parameter region and learn a robust safe policy within that region. It integrates three main components: estimating the feasible parameter set, robust policy optimization via saddle-point finding, and explicit exploration to expand the feasible set estimate.

#### 3.1. Estimating the Feasible Parameter Set (Θ*)

FGE addresses the challenge of asymmetric feasibility labels (safe observations are reliable, unsafe observations might just indicate a suboptimal policy) by learning a conservative classifier. This classifier is designed to have zero false positives (never marking an infeasible parameter as feasible) while minimizing false negatives.
The classifier, `q_ψ(f|θ)`, is trained using variational inference (minimizing KL divergence) to approximate a carefully constructed mixture distribution `p_mix(f, θ)`. This `p_mix` combines two data sources:
1. **`D_f` (Feasible Dataset):** A buffer of parameters for which safe behavior has been empirically observed. These provide reliable positive labels (`f=1`).
2. **On-policy samples:** Trajectories collected by the current policy `π`, under an arbitrary sampling distribution `ρ(θ)`. These samples provide potentially noisy labels (`f=0` if unsafe, `f=1` if safe).
The mixture coefficient `α` controls the weighting between these sources. The resulting classifier `ϕ(θ) = 1{q_ψ(f=1|θ) ≥ β}` (for threshold `β`) has theoretical guarantees regarding its false positive rate (zero) and a controllable false-negative rate, especially when `ρ` is chosen to concentrate density on regions predicted as infeasible. In practice, `ρ` is taken as the rollout distribution of `θ` under the current policy.

#### 3.2. Feasible Robust Optimization with Saddle-Point Finding

To solve the robust optimization problem `max_π min_{θ∈Θ'} J(π, θ)` over the estimated feasible set `Θ'`, FGE employs a saddle-point finding approach adapted from online learning. This involves an alternating optimization scheme:
- **Policy Update:** The policy `π` is updated using a variant of Follow-The-Regularized-Leader (FTRL) with a quadratic regularizer. This approach aims for sublinear regret, promoting stability in policy updates by keeping successive policies close. The FTRL update is approximated using deep RL (specifically PPO) by treating the sum of past rewards as an expectation over a replay buffer of worst-case parameters. The reward function is `r_θ(s) = -1{h_θ(s) > 0}`, where `h_θ(s) > 0` signifies an unsafe state.
- **Parameter (Adversary) Update:** The "adversary" selects the worst-case parameter `θ` from the current estimated feasible set `Θ'`. Since an exact best-response `argmin_{θ∈Θ'} J(π, θ)` is intractable, an approximate worst-case `θ` is selected. This is achieved by sampling a batch of `θ` from the estimated `Θ'` and using a learned neural network estimator of `J` to identify parameters where the current policy performs poorly. These worst-case parameters are then added to a rehearsal buffer `D_θ,t`.
This saddle-point formulation, though relying on approximations of theoretical conditions (e.g., convex-concave objective, exact oracles), aims to improve stability and convergence compared to gradient descent-ascent methods.

#### 3.3. Expanding the Feasible Set Estimate

A crucial aspect of FGE is its mechanism for actively expanding `D_f`. Relying solely on the policy to stumble upon new safe parameters can lead to a negative feedback loop: a weak policy fails often, `D_f` remains small, and training is restricted. FGE breaks this cycle with an explicit "explore distribution."
This distribution actively seeks parameters whose feasibility is uncertain or currently estimated as infeasible by the classifier `ϕ`. Parameters are drawn by rejection sampling `θ ~ p(. | ϕ(θ) = 0)`, guiding the policy to improve in unexplored or challenging regions, thus facilitating the discovery of new safe parameters and the expansion of `D_f`.

#### 3.4. Feasibility-Guided Exploration (Overall Framework)

The complete FGE algorithm (Algorithm 2) interleaves these components:
1. **Trajectory Collection:** Rollouts are performed using a mixed reset distribution (Algorithm 3), blending a base distribution (`p_base`), the exploration distribution (`p_explore`) for uncertain regions, and a rehearsal distribution (`p_rehearse`) for past worst-case feasible parameters.
2. **Policy Update:** An on-policy RL algorithm (e.g., PPO) updates the policy and value function based on collected trajectories.
3. **Feasible Buffer Update:** Newly discovered safe parameters from rollouts are added to `D_f`.
4. **Classifier Training:** The feasibility classifier `q_ψ` is updated using `D_f` and recent rollouts. A policy-conditioned classifier `p_π_ϕ` is also trained to estimate policy performance given a parameter.
5. **Best-Response and Rehearsal Buffer Update:** An approximate best-response `θ*` (a worst-case feasible parameter) is identified using `p_π_ϕ` and added to `D_θ,t`.

This cyclical process aims to adaptively explore the parameter space, learn the boundaries of the feasible set, and train a robust policy within that expanding region.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates FGE against a range of baseline methods across a suite of challenging tasks, demonstrating its effectiveness in solving parameter-robust avoid problems with unknown feasibility.

**Performance Benchmarking:**
- **Environments:** FGE was tested on "ToyLevels," two MuJoCo environments ("Hopper," "HalfCheetah"), a driving scenario ("Dubins"), a high-dimensional "FixedWing" aircraft simulator, and two "Lander" environments (Lander2D, Lander9D) featuring RGB image-based observations and high-dimensional parameter spaces.
- **Baselines:** The comparison included methods from Robust RL (Domain Randomization (DR), RARL), Curriculum Learning (VDS, PLR), and Unsupervised Environment Design (PAIRED, FARR, ACCEL, SFL), all instantiated with PPO as the underlying RL algorithm.
- **Metrics:** Evaluation focused on "Safety Rate" (conditioned on a parameter being feasible), "Coverage Gain vs DR" (measuring improvement over DR on difficult parameters), "Coverage Loss vs DR" (measuring performance degradation on easy parameters compared to DR), and "Unique Coverage" (parameters made safe only by the method).

**Key Results:**
1. **Superior Safety Rates and Coverage:** FGE consistently achieved high safety rates across all tasks. Performance profiles (Fig. 7a) indicated that FGE had the most runs with safety rates above any given threshold, dominating all other baselines. FGE demonstrated the highest "Coverage Gain vs DR" across all environments, making over 50% more challenging parameters safe than the next best existing method. Simultaneously, it maintained a low "Coverage Loss vs DR," indicating that its improvements on hard parameters did not compromise performance on easier ones.
2. **Efficient Sampling and Exploration:** Case studies illuminated FGE's mechanism for performance gains.
 - In the **Dubins** environment (Fig. 8), FGE effectively allocated significantly more samples to rare and difficult conditions (e.g., an "overtake" maneuver requiring squeezing through a tight gap) than VDS, enabling it to achieve safety where VDS failed.
 - In the **Hopper** environment (Fig. 9, 10), the feasibility classifier and explore distribution guided FGE to concentrate a third of its samples on a narrow gap between platforms, a region initially difficult to solve. This focused exploration eventually led to safe navigation in that region, while other methods' heuristics failed to concentrate samples as efficiently.
3. **Scalability to High-Dimensionality:** FGE proved capable of scaling to complex scenarios, including those with RGB image observations (Lander2D, Lander9D) and high-dimensional (9D) parameter spaces (Lander9D). It maintained the highest safety rates in these challenging environments (Fig. 11).

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Importance of Exploration:** Removing the explicit "explore distribution" from FGE significantly reduced its coverage gain, underscoring its critical role in discovering new feasible parameters (Fig. 12).
- **Role of Rehearsal Buffer:** The "rehearsal buffer" was found to be critical for training stability. Its removal led to a smaller drop in coverage gain but increased coverage loss and destabilized training (Fig. 12, Fig. 13).
- **Feasibility Classifier Effectiveness:** The proposed classifier, leveraging mixture distributions, outperformed density-based methods (Planar Flows, Neural Spline Flows) in terms of coverage gain (Fig. 14).
- **Robustness to Hyperparameters:** The classifier's performance was found to be "fairly robust" to variations in the mixing fraction `α` (Fig. 22).
- **Comparison to RND:** Integrating Random Network Distillation (RND), a traditional exploration technique, with DR yielded only slight improvements and did not match FGE's performance (Fig. 20).

**Complementary to Safe RL:** When FGE was combined with existing safe RL algorithms (PPO-Lagrangian, SauteRL) on a constrained task, it significantly expanded the Pareto frontier of reward versus safety rate compared to using DR. This indicates FGE's potential to complement and enhance the safety performance of current safe RL methods over varied parameter spaces (Fig. 23).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a significant advancement in addressing a fundamental challenge at the intersection of optimal control, safe systems, and reinforcement learning. By introducing and providing a solution framework for the "parameter-robust avoid problem with unknown feasibility," the work offers several layers of significance and potential impact:

1. **Enhanced Safety Guarantees:** The primary impact lies in its ability to synthesize robust safe controllers that operate effectively across a wider, unknown range of operating conditions. By explicitly identifying the maximal feasible parameter region and learning a policy for it, FGE moves beyond average-case performance to provide more comprehensive worst-case safety guarantees within the discovered operating envelope. This is crucial for safety-critical applications where catastrophic failures in low-probability scenarios are unacceptable.

2. **Addressing Intractability:** The methodology directly tackles the computational intractability of determining feasible sets *a priori* in high-dimensional systems, a long-standing bottleneck in traditional HJ-based optimal control. FGE's approach of concurrently identifying the feasible set and optimizing the policy provides a practical pathway to robust control synthesis where exhaustive *a priori* analysis is not feasible.

3. **Efficient Learning in Complex Domains:** The "Feasibility-Guided Exploration" mechanism is a key innovation. By proactively seeking parameters whose feasibility is uncertain, it enables efficient exploration, preventing learning stagnation caused by weak policies restricting the known safe region. This intelligent exploration leads to significantly larger coverage of safe operating conditions, as demonstrated by the empirical results.

4. **Scalability to Realistic Systems:** The demonstrated ability of FGE to scale to high-dimensional state spaces, RGB image observations, and complex multi-dimensional parameter spaces (e.g., Lander9D) suggests its applicability to real-world, high-fidelity systems in domains like autonomous driving, aerospace, and robotics. This scalability broadens the scope of problems that can be tackled with formal safety considerations.

5. **Complementary Framework:** The finding that FGE can synergistically enhance existing safe RL algorithms (by improving the initial state distribution for training) indicates its potential to serve as a foundational component within broader safe and robust AI systems. It offers a method to significantly improve the robustness dimension of safe RL without requiring a fundamental redesign of underlying constrained optimization algorithms.

6. **Bridging Theoretical and Practical Gaps:** The research attempts to bridge theoretical insights from online learning and saddle-point problems with practical deep RL implementations, even acknowledging that some theoretical assumptions (e.g., convex-concavity, exact oracles) are approximated. While full theoretical guarantees under these approximations remain an open challenge, the empirical benefits observed suggest a promising direction for future research in robust optimization and learning.

In summary, FGE provides a principled and empirically effective method for simultaneously discovering the boundaries of safe operation and learning controllers that respect these boundaries across parameter variations. This has substantial implications for developing more reliable and trustworthy autonomous systems.
