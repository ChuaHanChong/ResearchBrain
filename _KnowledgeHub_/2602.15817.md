# Solving Parameter-Robust Avoid Problems with Unknown Feasibility using Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Feasibility-Guided Exploration (FGE), developed at MIT and MIT Lincoln Laboratory, introduces a reinforcement learning framework to simultaneously identify the maximal feasible operating region for a system and learn a robust policy that guarantees safety within that region. FGE consistently achieved superior safety rates and expanded coverage of safe operating conditions across diverse environments, including high-dimensional and image-based tasks, outperforming existing robust RL and curriculum learning methods by making over 50% more challenging parameters safe.

## Problem

- Traditional optimal safe controller synthesis methods like Hamilton-Jacobi analysis are computationally intractable for high-dimensional systems.
- Current deep RL approaches optimize expected returns over user-defined initial condition distributions, which often mismatches worst-case safety objectives and can lead to critical failures in low-probability regions.
- Robust optimization requires a priori knowledge of a feasible set of initial conditions, which is difficult to determine, making the problem ill-posed if an infeasible set is assumed.

## Method

- The ==Feasibility-Guided Exploration== (**FGE**) framework iteratively estimates the feasible parameter set, optimizes a robust policy via saddle-point finding, and employs explicit exploration to expand the feasible set estimate.
- A conservative classifier is learned using variational inference over a mixture of empirically observed safe parameters and on-policy samples to robustly estimate the unknown feasible parameter set.
- An 'explore distribution' actively seeks parameters with uncertain or estimated infeasibility, guiding policy improvement and facilitating the discovery of new safe parameters to overcome learning stagnation.

## Results

- FGE consistently demonstrated higher safety rates and the largest 'Coverage Gain vs DR' across all tested environments, making over **50%** more challenging parameters safe than the next best existing method while maintaining low 'Coverage Loss vs DR'.
- Case studies, such as in the Dubins and Hopper environments, showed FGE's ability to efficiently allocate samples to rare and difficult conditions, leading to successful safety achievements where other methods failed to concentrate samples.
- FGE successfully scaled to high-dimensional state spaces (Lander9D with 9D parameters) and RGB image observations, maintaining superior safety rates in these complex scenarios compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The problem of concurrently identifying a maximal feasible operating region and learning a robust safe policy can be effectively addressed through an integrated iterative framework.
> - Learning a conservative feasibility classifier using a mixture of reliable safe observations and policy rollouts provides a robust way to estimate the boundaries of the unknown feasible parameter set.
> - Active, goal-directed exploration of uncertain or infeasible regions is essential for efficiently discovering new safe operating conditions and expanding the known feasible region, which prevents negative feedback loops in learning.
