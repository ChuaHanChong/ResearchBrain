# HOVER: Versatile Neural Whole-Body Controller for Humanoid Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> HOVER introduces a neural whole-body controller for humanoid robots that unifies diverse control modes into a single policy, leveraging distillation from large-scale human motion data. The system demonstrates superior performance compared to individually trained specialist controllers and multi-mode reinforcement learning baselines in over 15 control configurations, showcasing robust real-world deployment and seamless mode transitions on a Unitree H1 robot.

## Problem

- Humanoid control relies on specialized policies for specific tasks (e.g., locomotion, manipulation), which limits versatility and transferability across different operational modes.
- Developing new controllers for each task is repetitive and time-consuming, hindering efficient expansion of robot capabilities.
- Existing methods struggle to enable seamless, instantaneous transitions between different control modes during dynamic operations.

## Method

- A multi-mode policy distillation framework is employed to train a unified neural controller (**HOVER**) by mimicking an 'oracle' motion imitator, which is trained on large-scale human motion data.
- A comprehensive and atomic command space is designed, encompassing kinematic position tracking, local joint angle tracking, and root tracking, with a one-hot masking vector to define specific control modes.
- Human motion data from the AMASS dataset is retargeted to humanoid-feasible motions, providing a rich foundation for the oracle policy trained via goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (PPO).

## Results

- HOVER consistently outperformed specialist policies trained for prior work's control modes and other useful modes, showing superior generalization in both simulation (outperforming in at least 7 of 12 metrics) and real-world Unitree H1 experiments (outperforming in 11 of 12 metrics).
- Compared to a multi-mode RL baseline, HOVER achieved consistently lower tracking error across all 32 evaluated metrics and eight distinct control modes, demonstrating the efficacy of the distillation approach.
- Successfully deployed on a 19-DOF Unitree H1 robot, HOVER executed accurate tracking of complex dynamic motions, demonstrated robustness to abrupt command mode switches, and supported teleoperation with randomized masking of command inputs.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Full-body kinematic motion imitation offers a common abstraction for acquiring general-purpose motor skills, which can be adapted to various control modes.
> - Policy distillation from an oracle trained on full-body human kinematics provides stronger motor priors, leading to a more competent and generalizable whole-body controller across diverse modes than direct multi-mode RL.
> - A unified and dynamically maskable command space allows a single neural policy to handle arbitrary combinations of control objectives, significantly enhancing versatility without requiring task-specific retraining.
