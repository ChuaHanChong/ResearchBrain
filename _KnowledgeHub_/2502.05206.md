# Safety at Scale: A Comprehensive Survey of Large Model and Agent Safety

> [!summary] Summary
> A comprehensive survey systematically categorizes safety threats, defense strategies, and evaluation benchmarks across six major large AI model types, including vision, language, multimodal, diffusion, and agentic systems. The work synthesizes current research, highlights limitations in attack understanding and defense development, and calls for coordinated global efforts towards more robust AI safety.

## Problem

- Large AI models, while capable, face diverse and escalating safety risks such as adversarial attacks, data poisoning, and prompt manipulation upon widespread deployment.
- Existing AI safety research is fragmented, often focusing on narrow model categories or specific threat types, lacking a unified and comprehensive overview of the field.
- Unaddressed safety issues in AI systems can lead to unintended behaviors, privacy breaches, dissemination of harmful content, and a decline in public trust.

## Method

- Developed a systematic, ==two-level taxonomy== to categorize safety threats and defense strategies across six major large model types: ==VFMs==, ==LLMs==, ==VLPs==, ==VLMs==, ==DMs==, and large-model-powered ==Agents==.
- Reviewed **574** technical papers, summarizing key ideas of attacks and defenses for each model category and organizing them by ==ten distinct attack types==.
- Conducted a quantitative analysis to illustrate temporal trends in safety research, distribution across model types, and the focus on various attack and defense strategies.

## Results

- Systematically cataloged **ten** distinct attack types (e.g., adversarial, jailbreak, backdoor, memory, tool-calling) and corresponding defense mechanisms across six major large AI model categories.
- Observed a significant surge in AI safety research since 2023, with LLMs, DMs, and Agents garnering the most attention, along with an imbalance where attack studies (**~60%**) outweigh defense studies (**~40%**).
- Identified critical open challenges and future directions, advocating for diagnostic attack research, improved multi-level safety evaluation, development of practical defenses, and enhanced global collaboration to ensure safe AI development.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Current attack research often focuses on merely 'breaking' models rather than serving as diagnostic tools to understand fundamental vulnerabilities, especially in cross-modal and agentic systems.
> - Existing safety evaluation metrics, particularly over-reliance on Attack Success Rate (ASR), are insufficient for comprehensive and realistic assessment, necessitating multi-level and dynamic adversarial evaluations.
> - Defense strategies are limited by issues like 'fake alignment' and a lack of practical, generalizable, efficient, and black-box compatible solutions, requiring a shift towards proactive and detection-focused approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The paper addresses the rapidly evolving field of Artificial Intelligence, particularly the "era of large models," which include Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), Large Language Models (LLMs), Vision-Language Pre-training (VLP) models, Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Diffusion Models (DMs), and large-model-powered Agents. These models have demonstrated extensive capabilities across diverse applications, from conversational AI to autonomous driving, fundamentally reshaping the AI landscape. However, their widespread deployment also introduces significant safety concerns regarding robustness, reliability, and ethical implications. The research landscape has seen a growing focus on identifying and mitigating these risks through various attack methodologies, defense strategies, and evaluation benchmarks.

This survey positions itself as a comprehensive review within this expanding domain. While numerous existing surveys have explored specific aspects of AI safety, often focusing on a narrow range of model types (e.g., only LLMs or VLMs) or particular threat categories (e.g., only jailbreak attacks), this work distinguishes itself by providing a broader and more integrated perspective. It covers a wider array of large model categories and a more extensive spectrum of safety threats. The authors aim to bridge the gaps in existing literature by offering a unified overview of the current state of safety research, highlighting overarching methodologies, and identifying systemic challenges rather than focusing on isolated technical details. This comprehensive scope is intended to serve as a foundational reference for researchers and practitioners navigating the complex safety landscape of large-scale AI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation for this comprehensive survey stems from the dual nature of advancements in large AI models. While these models have exhibited unprecedented capabilities in learning and generalization, leading to their integration into a wide array of commercial and societal applications, their widespread deployment also exposes them to significant safety risks. These risks encompass vulnerabilities to adversarial attacks, data poisoning, backdoor infiltration, prompt manipulation (jailbreak and prompt injection), energy-latency exploitation, and data/model extraction. The emergence of autonomous agents, powered by these large models, introduces further complex and cascading safety threats. The authors emphasize that failure to address these issues can lead to unintended system behaviors, privacy breaches, the dissemination of harmful content, and a general erosion of public trust in AI. Therefore, ensuring the safety of these models is presented as paramount for responsible AI development and usage.

Against this backdrop, the paper establishes several key objectives:
1. **Systematic Threat Taxonomy:** To develop and present a comprehensive taxonomy that categorizes the diverse safety threats relevant to various large models, including Vision Foundation Models, Large Language Models, Vision-Language Pre-training models, Vision-Language Models, Diffusion Models, and large-model-powered Agents.
2. **Defense Strategy Review:** To review and summarize the existing defense strategies developed to counter each identified type of attack, alongside commonly used datasets and benchmarks for safety research.
3. **Identification of Open Challenges:** To identify and discuss the current limitations and open challenges in large model safety research, focusing on the need for more comprehensive safety evaluations, scalable and effective defense mechanisms, and sustainable data practices.
4. **Advocacy for Collaboration:** To highlight the critical necessity of collective efforts from the research community and international collaboration to address these challenges effectively.
5. **Resource for Stakeholders:** To serve as a valuable reference for researchers and practitioners, thereby fostering the continuous development of robust defense systems and platforms for safeguarding AI models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors adopted a structured and multi-faceted methodology to conduct their comprehensive survey.

**Scope Definition:** The survey’s scope was defined to include six major categories of large models that are widely studied and prominent in current AI research: Vision Foundation Models (VFMs), Large Language Models (LLMs), Vision-Language Pre-training (VLP) models, Vision-Language Models (VLMs), Diffusion Models (DMs), and large-model-powered Agents. This selection aims to provide broad coverage across different modalities and levels of autonomy.

**Information Gathering:** The initial step involved a keyword-based search. This targeted specific model types (e.g., "Vision Transformer safety," "LLM jailbreak") and threat types (e.g., "adversarial attacks," "data poisoning," "backdoor attacks," "prompt injection," "energy-latency attacks," "data extraction," "model extraction," "agent safety") to identify relevant research papers from prominent AI/ML conferences and journals.

**Filtering and Selection:** Following the initial search, the retrieved papers underwent a manual filtering process. This step focused on excluding non-safety-related papers and those that were non-technical in nature, ensuring that the survey concentrates on actionable research in attacks and defenses. In total, 574 technical papers were reviewed for this survey.

**Categorization and Taxonomy Development:** For each selected paper, its proposed method or framework was systematically categorized. This involved a two-level taxonomy:
- **Category:** Differentiating attacks and defenses based on the threat model (e.g., white-box, gray-box, black-box scenarios) or specific subtasks (e.g., detection, purification, robust training/tuning, robust inference).
- **Subcategory:** Offering a more detailed classification based on the specific techniques employed within each category.
- **Organization by Model and Threat Type:** Within each of the six main model categories, the reviewed works were further classified into attacks and defenses. The authors identified ten distinct attack types: adversarial, backdoor, poisoning, jailbreak, prompt injection, energy-latency, membership inference, model extraction, data extraction, and agent-specific attacks. When backdoor and poisoning attacks exhibited significant similarities for a particular model category, they were combined for clarity. Defense strategies were reviewed immediately following their corresponding attack types.

**Granularity of Review:** To maintain clarity and facilitate comprehension given the broad scope, the authors adopted a concise approach to presenting individual papers. For each reviewed work, only its key ideas, objectives, and general approaches were highlighted, while intricate technical details and exhaustive experimental analyses were intentionally omitted. This high-level perspective aims to provide an accessible overview of the methodologies without overwhelming the reader.

**Visualization of Trends:** The methodology also included a quantitative analysis of the surveyed literature, presented graphically in Figures 1, 2, and 3. These figures illustrate temporal trends in safety research, the distribution of surveyed papers across different model types, and the proportional focus on various attack and defense strategies. This analysis revealed a significant surge in safety research since 2023, with LLMs, DMs, and Agents garnering the most attention, and a notable imbalance between the number of attack studies (~60%) and defense studies (~40%).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey systematically categorized and analyzed a wide range of safety research across six major types of large AI models, identifying prevalent attack vectors, existing defense mechanisms, and crucial evaluation benchmarks.

#### 4.1. Model-Specific Safety Landscapes

- **Vision Foundation Models (VFMs):** The survey details adversarial attacks such as patch-level, position embedding, and attention manipulation for Vision Transformers (ViTs), alongside black-box transfer-based and query-based methods. Backdoor attacks for ViTs include patch-level, token-level, multi-trigger, and data-free approaches. Defenses involve adversarial training, detection (patch-based, attention-based), robust architectural modifications, and adversarial purification. For the Segment Anything Model (SAM), prompt-agnostic, universal, and transfer-based adversarial attacks are identified, with limited defenses primarily focused on adversarial tuning. Backdoor and poisoning attacks against SAM leverage visual triggers and unlearnable examples. Key datasets for VFMs include ImageNet and CIFAR-10/100.

- **Large Language Models (LLMs):** LLMs face adversarial attacks at character and word levels (white-box) and sentence-level (black-box, e.g., in-context, induced, LLM-assisted, tabular attacks). Jailbreak attacks are categorized into hand-crafted (scenario-based, encoding-based) and automated methods (prompt optimization, LLM-assisted), as well as fine-tuning-based approaches. Prompt injection attacks also utilize hand-crafted and automated techniques. Backdoor attacks leverage data poisoning (prompt-level, multi-trigger), training manipulation, and parameter modification. Energy-latency attacks, model extraction, and data extraction attacks (e.g., prefix attacks, RAG extraction) are also detailed. Defenses include adversarial detection (input filtering, robust inference), jailbreak defenses (input rephrasing/translation/filtering, output filtering/repetition, ensemble methods, and defenses against fine-tuning attacks), and prompt injection defenses (input rephrasing, adversarial fine-tuning). Safety alignment via Human Feedback (RLHF: PPO, DPO, KTO, SFT), AI Feedback (RLAIF: PPO), and Social Interactions are discussed, alongside the challenge of deceptive alignment.

- **Vision-Language Pre-training (VLP) Models:** These models are vulnerable to white-box adversarial attacks (invisible and visible perturbations) and black-box attacks (sample-specific and universal perturbations). Backdoor and poisoning attacks involve visual and multi-modal triggers, as well as targeted poisoning. Defenses include adversarial detection (one-shot, stateful), standard adversarial training frameworks, and adversarial prompt tuning (textual and multi-modal) and contrastive tuning. Datasets like MS-COCO and Flickr30K are common.

- **Vision-Language Models (VLMs):** Adversarial attacks on VLMs target specific tasks, cross-prompt scenarios, or chain-of-thought processes (white-box), with gray-box attacks focusing on encoders and black-box attacks using transfer-based or generator-based methods. Jailbreak attacks for VLMs include white-box (target-specific, universal, hybrid) and black-box (transfer-based, manually-designed, system prompt leakage, red teaming) approaches. Energy-latency and prompt injection attacks also exist. Backdoor and poisoning attacks occur during tuning-time or test-time via various triggers. Defenses involve jailbreak detection and prevention strategies, such as adaptive prompting, image-to-text transformation, and reinforcement learning-based suffix generation.

- **Diffusion Models (DMs):** Adversarial attacks perturb text prompts or latent space (white-box), exploit CLIP text encoders (gray-box), or use textual adversarial techniques at character, word, or sentence levels (black-box). Jailbreak attacks aim to bypass internal (safety alignment) and external (safety filters) mechanisms via gradient-based optimization (white-box), text encoder exploitation (gray-box), or hand-crafted/LLM-assisted prompts (black-box). Backdoor attacks are achieved through training manipulation (visual triggers) or data poisoning (text-text and text-image pair triggers). Privacy attacks encompass membership inference, data extraction (explicit/surrogate conditions), and model extraction (LoRA-based). Intellectual property protection methods include learning/editing prevention and data attribution for natural data, watermarking for generated data, and model watermarking/attribution. Defenses against jailbreaks involve concept erasure (finetuning-based, close-form solution, pruning-based) and inference guidance. Backdoor defenses focus on detection and removal.

- **Large Model Powered Agents:** Agents are highly susceptible to Indirect Prompt Injection (malicious instruction, jailbreak). Memory attacks include backdoors in short-term (contextual, demonstration) and long-term (RAG-based) memory, as well as general memory poisoning. Tool-calling attacks exploit web browsing, internal reasoning, tool selection, and the Model Context Protocol (MCP). VLM agents face attacks via targeted adversarial perturbations, environmental context manipulation, and visual interface exploitation. Multi-Agent Systems are vulnerable to propagation and infiltration attacks. Embodied agents face adversarial perturbations, jailbreak, backdoor, and red teaming attacks. The survey also identifies "Agentic Attacks," where agents autonomously discover and exploit vulnerabilities, and "Agentic Defenses" that leverage collaborative reasoning. Defenses cover privilege management, instruction detection/removal, authentication, prompt engineering, secure design patterns, context extension, knowledge consolidation, privacy protection, and agent-based guardrails.

#### 4.2. Open Challenges

The survey identifies several limitations and gaps in current research:
- **Attack Research:** A call to shift the focus of attack research from merely "breaking" models to understanding fundamental vulnerabilities as diagnostic tools. Specific questions are raised about inherent LLM vulnerabilities, cross-modal vulnerability propagation, the lack of language capabilities in current diffusion models for visual generation, the extent of data memorization in large models, and the increasing vulnerabilities of agents as their capabilities grow.
- **Safety Evaluation:** Critiques the over-reliance on Attack Success Rate (ASR) as a sole metric, advocating for multi-level, fine-grained metrics. It highlights that static evaluations create a false sense of safety and that adversarial evaluations are a necessity, not an option, especially for commercial APIs. Challenges in open-ended evaluation for tasks like jailbreaking are also noted.
- **Defense Research:** Emphasizes that "safety alignment" is not a panacea due to issues like "fake alignment." There is a pressing need for more practical defenses that are generalizable, efficient, black-box compatible, and continually adaptable. The lack of proactive defenses and the overlooked role of detection in current defense pipelines are also raised. Furthermore, ensuring safety for embodied agents and addressing the profound challenges of "Safe Superintelligence" (via oversight systems, safety switches, expert models, adversarial alignment, and safety consciousness) are presented as critical future directions.
- **Call for Collective Action:** The report concludes by advocating for a defense-oriented shift in research, the provision of dedicated safety APIs by commercial models, the development of open-source safety platforms, and enhanced global collaborations across academia, industry, and governments to address AI safety challenges effectively.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This comprehensive survey holds significant implications for the advancement of AI safety research and practice. Its primary significance lies in providing a structured and panoramic view of the current landscape, consolidating fragmented knowledge across diverse large model architectures and threat types. By offering a detailed taxonomy of attacks and defenses, coupled with a summary of relevant datasets and benchmarks, the paper serves as an invaluable resource for both new and experienced researchers entering or working within the field.

The potential impact extends beyond academic understanding:
- **Guiding Future Research:** The identification of numerous open challenges in attack research, safety evaluation, and defense development provides a clear roadmap for future investigations. Emphasizing the diagnostic purpose of attacks, the need to understand fundamental vulnerabilities, and the complexity of cross-modal interactions will likely steer research toward more systemic and foundational solutions rather than superficial fixes.
- **Fostering Robust Defenses:** The call for defense-oriented research and for more practical, generalizable, efficient, and adaptable defense mechanisms directly addresses critical gaps in the current safety landscape. This emphasis could stimulate the development of robust, real-world deployable safeguards that keep pace with the rapid evolution of large models.
- **Improving Safety Evaluation:** The critique of static evaluations and over-reliance on single metrics like ASR is crucial for developing more comprehensive and realistic safety assessment protocols. Advocating for dynamic, adversarial, and open-ended evaluation frameworks will lead to more trustworthy assessments of AI system robustness.
- **Promoting Responsible AI Development:** By highlighting the challenges of deceptive alignment and the profound implications of agentic attacks and the pursuit of Safe Superintelligence, the survey reinforces the ethical imperative for responsible AI development. It underscores that technical advancements must be coupled with robust safety engineering and a deep understanding of societal impacts.
- **Encouraging Collaboration and Standardization:** The explicit call for dedicated safety APIs, open-source platforms, and global collaborations is a powerful advocacy for a more unified and transparent approach to AI safety. Such initiatives could accelerate knowledge sharing, establish universal safety standards, and facilitate coordinated efforts to tackle complex, transnational AI risks.

In essence, this survey functions as a critical synthesis, aiming not only to inform but also to galvanize the global AI community towards a more coordinated, proactive, and comprehensive strategy for ensuring the safety and trustworthiness of large-scale AI systems as they become increasingly integrated into the fabric of society.
