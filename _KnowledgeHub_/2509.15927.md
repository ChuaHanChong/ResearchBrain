# Enhancing Generative Auto-bidding with Offline Reward Evaluation and Policy Search

> [!summary] Summary
> AIGB-Pearl, a generative auto-bidding method from Alibaba and Tsinghua University, enhances performance and generalization by integrating offline reward evaluation and a theoretically sound, constrained policy search. This approach achieved a +3.00% Gross Merchandise Volume (GMV) improvement in real-world A/B tests on a large e-commerce platform.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) in auto-bidding suffers from training instability and out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization issues due to reliance on bootstrapped value estimates.
- Existing generative auto-bidding (AIGB) methods are imitation-based, inherently limited to replicating observed trajectories and lacking explicit mechanisms for performance improvement beyond static offline data.
- Current generative auto-bidding models struggle with unreliable extrapolation and lack a suitable reward signal to guide them toward generating novel, higher-performing trajectories.

## Method

- **AIGB-Pearl** introduces a ==trajectory evaluator==, a neural network trained to estimate the true quality of generated trajectories, providing an explicit reward signal for the generative model.
- It employs a KL-Lipschitz-constrained score-maximization objective for the generative planner, ensuring safe and efficient exploration beyond the offline dataset with theoretical guarantees against OOD issues.
- The method integrates practical innovations like synchronous coupling for enforcing Lipschitz continuity in the generative planner and incorporates LLM embeddings with pairwise losses for a more accurate and robust trajectory evaluator.

## Results

- AIGB-Pearl achieved a +**3.00%** GMV, +**2.20%** BuyCnt, and +**1.89%** ROI improvement in real-world A/B tests on a large E-commerce platform (TaoBao) compared to the DiffBid baseline.
- The approach demonstrated enhanced generalization capabilities, yielding a +**3.32%** GMV uplift for unseen advertisers and robust performance even with multi-modal offline data.
- Ablation studies confirmed that both the KL constraint (+**1.09%** GMV) and the Lipschitz constraint (+**1.81%** GMV) individually contributed to performance gains and maintained plausible trajectory generation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The true trajectory quality in auto-bidding is Lipschitz continuous, a theoretical property leveraged to bound evaluator errors and ensure safe exploration when generating OOD trajectories.
> - By constraining the generative planner's exploration using KL divergence and Lipschitz conditions, it is possible to achieve performance improvements while maintaining fidelity to the offline data and mitigating OOD risks.
> - Combining a stable generative model with an explicit reward evaluator and theoretically sound policy optimization offers a robust solution for offline sequential decision-making, overcoming the limitations of both traditional offline RL instability and purely imitation-based generative approaches.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work addresses the problem of auto-bidding in online advertising, a critical component for advertisers seeking to optimize performance in dynamic and competitive auction environments. Auto-bidding is typically formulated as an offline sequential decision-making problem, where policies are learned from historical data due to the inherent safety concerns and high costs associated with online exploration in real-world advertising systems.

Historically, offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) methods have been widely adopted for auto-bidding. These methods aim to maximize cumulative advertising performance over a bidding episode while adhering to a budget. Offline RL employs conservative policy search schemes to mitigate the "out-of-distribution" (OOD) problem, which arises when a learned policy attempts to generalize beyond the data it was trained on. However, offline RL methods are often prone to training instability, primarily due to their reliance on bootstrapped value estimates, which can lead to erroneous ground truth during training.

Recent advancements have introduced generative models to offline decision-making, specifically in auto-bidding through AI-Generated Bidding (AIGB). AIGB models auto-bidding as a trajectory-generation task, using a generative model to approximate the conditional trajectory distribution from offline data. This approach avoids the bootstrapping issues prevalent in RL, leading to more stable training and generally superior performance. Despite these advantages, existing AIGB methods face a limitation: they inherently imitate trajectories from the static offline dataset. This imitation-based learning lacks an explicit mechanism to improve generation quality beyond the observed data by leveraging performance feedback. Consequently, AIGB's conditional generation in extrapolation regimes (i.e., attempting to generate trajectories with performance better than anything seen in the training data) can become unreliable, potentially leading to performance degradation or risky outcomes, and lacks theoretical guarantees for such extrapolation.

This paper positions itself as an advancement that integrates the strengths of generative planning with policy optimization, directly addressing the exploration and feedback limitations of existing AIGB methods. It seeks to combine the stability of generative models with the performance-seeking capabilities of RL, specifically in the context of safe and effective exploration beyond static offline data.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the performance and generalization capabilities of AI-Generated Bidding (AIGB) in auto-bidding by enabling continuous improvement in generation quality through explicit reward guidance and exploration beyond the static offline dataset.

The motivation for this objective stems from several identified limitations of existing state-of-the-art auto-bidding approaches:

1. **Performance Bottleneck in Current AIGB:** While AIGB methods, such as DiffBid, have shown superior performance and training stability compared to offline RL by modeling auto-bidding as a trajectory generation task, they are fundamentally imitation-based. They learn to replicate trajectories from the offline dataset but lack the inherent ability to proactively explore novel trajectories or improve their generation quality based on performance feedback. This reliance on static data creates a performance bottleneck, particularly when attempting to extrapolate to conditions (e.g., higher desired returns) not adequately represented in the training data.

2. **Unreliable Extrapolation:** In the absence of explicit reward signals or a mechanism to evaluate the quality of generated trajectories, AIGB's attempts to generate trajectories beyond the observed performance range (extrapolation regime) can be unreliable. Such undirected exploration could lead to suboptimal or even risky trajectory generations, which is particularly problematic in risk-sensitive domains like online advertising where monetary losses or campaign failures are significant concerns.

3. **Absence of Reward Signals for Generative Models:** A core challenge identified is the lack of a suitable reward signal within the AIGB framework to guide the generative model towards improved performance. Without a mechanism to assess the "quality" of generated (and potentially unseen) trajectories, it is infeasible to guide the model to explore and improve.

4. **Lack of Dedicated Offline RL for Generative AIGB:** Integrating offline RL principles into AIGB is non-trivial. There is no existing offline RL algorithm specifically designed for generative models in auto-bidding that provides theoretical guarantees for safe generalization and effectively mitigates OOD issues. Such theoretical grounding is crucial for ensuring the reliability and safety of any deployed auto-bidding system.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes a novel method, AIGB-Pearl (Planning with EvaluAtor via RL), with the following core objectives:

- **Construct a Trajectory Evaluator:** To provide an explicit reward signal by learning to assess the quality of generated trajectories. This evaluator would overcome the limitation of AIGB's inability to determine the performance of novel trajectories.
- **Design a Provably Sound Policy Optimization Scheme:** To drive the generative planner's exploration beyond the offline dataset. This involves formulating a KL-Lipschitz-constrained score-maximization objective. The aim is to ensure that exploration is both safe and efficient, mitigating OOD problems and providing theoretical guarantees on sub-optimality.
- **Develop a Practical Algorithm:** To implement the proposed constrained score maximization, including techniques like synchronous coupling to satisfy the necessary Lipschitz conditions for the generative model, thereby ensuring model regularity and reliability.
- **Ensure Training Stability:** Unlike traditional offline RL which suffers from instability due to bootstrapping, AIGB-Pearl aims to maintain the training stability characteristic of generative models while incorporating policy optimization.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to develop a generative auto-bidding method that not only leverages the stability and expressive power of generative models but also continuously learns and improves its performance by reliably exploring beyond historical data, with a strong theoretical foundation for safety and generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, AIGB-Pearl (Planning with EvaluAtor via RL), integrates generative planning with policy optimization by introducing a trajectory evaluator and a constrained score-maximization scheme.

#### 3.1. Problem Statement and Trajectory Definition

The auto-bidding problem is framed as a Markov Decision Process (MDP) aiming to maximize cumulative reward (Gross Merchandise Volume, GMV) within a budget. Bidding decisions are made by recalibrating a scaling factor `a` at fixed intervals over a finite episode. A trajectory `$\tau$` is defined as a sequence of states `[$s_1, \dots, s_T$]`, where each state includes time, cost ratio, and static advertiser features. Trajectory quality `y($\tau$)` is the normalized cumulative reward `$(\sum r_t) / B$`. The problem operates in an offline setting, learning from a static dataset `D` of historical trajectories.

#### 3.2. AIGB-Pearl Overview

AIGB-Pearl consists of two main components: a **trajectory evaluator** and a **generative planner**.
1. **Evaluator:** A neural network `$\hat{y}_\phi(\tau)$` trained to estimate the true trajectory quality `y($\tau$)` from the offline dataset `D` via supervised learning.
2. **Planner:** A conditional generative model `p_$\theta$($\tau$|$y$)` (a Causal Transformer) that generates trajectories `$\tau$` conditioned on a desired quality `y`. During training, the planner aims to maximize the scores provided by the evaluator.

#### 3.3. Addressing Evaluator Reliability and OOD Problem

A central challenge is ensuring the evaluator's reliability, particularly when the planner generates trajectories outside the offline data distribution (OOD). To mitigate OOD issues and ensure safe exploration, AIGB-Pearl incorporates theoretical analysis and constraints:

- **Lipschitz Continuity of Trajectory Quality (Theorem 1):** The paper theoretically demonstrates that the true trajectory quality `y($\tau$)` is `$\sqrt{T}R_m$`-Lipschitz continuous with respect to the Frobenius norm of the trajectory, where `T` is episode length and `R_m` is maximum ROI. This property is crucial for bounding evaluator errors.
- **Evaluator Bias Bound (Theorem 2):** By leveraging the Lipschitz property of `y($\tau$)` and enforcing Lipschitz regularity on the trained evaluator `$\hat{y}_\phi(\tau)$`, the paper derives an upper bound on the gap between the planner's true performance `J($\theta$)` and its evaluator-predicted score `L($\theta$)`. This bound depends on:
 - `$\delta_D$`: Evaluator's bias on the training dataset.
 - `k$\sqrt{T}R_m$`: Lipschitz constant of the evaluator.
 - `W_1(p_$\theta$($\tau$|$y^*$), p_$\theta$($\tau$|$y$))`: Planner's generation sensitivity to the condition `y`.
 - `W_1(p_$\theta$($\tau$|$y$), p_D($\tau$|$y$))`: Planner's imitation error on the offline dataset `D`.

- **KL-Lipschitz-Constrained Score Maximization (Eq. 8):** To control the Wasserstein terms in the bias bound, the planner's objective is formulated as:
 - **Score Maximization:** `max_$\theta$ L($\theta$)`
 - **KL Constraint (Eq. 8a):** `E_{y~p_D(y)}[D_{KL}(p_D(\tau|y)||p_$\theta$(\tau|y))] $\leq$ $\delta_K$`. This constraint ensures the planner maintains fidelity to the offline dataset, bounding imitation error.
 - **Lipschitz Constraint (Eq. 8b):** `Lip_{W_1}(p_$\theta$($\tau$|$y$)) $\leq$ $L_p$`. This constraint limits the planner's sensitivity to changes in the condition `y`, bounding generation sensitivity.
 This objective ensures exploration within a theoretically certified neighborhood of high-quality offline trajectories where the evaluator remains accurate.

- **Sub-optimality Gap Bound (Theorem 3):** The paper further provides a theoretical bound on the sub-optimality gap `J($\theta$*) - J($\hat{\theta}$)` (difference between optimal performance and AIGB-Pearl's performance). This bound shows how factors like evaluator error `$\delta_D$`, Lipschitz constants `k, L_p`, and behavior cloning error `$\delta_K$` influence the ultimate performance.

#### 3.4. Practical Algorithm Design

- **Lipschitz Trajectory Evaluator (Section 3.2.1, Appendix E.1):**
 - **Architecture:** Processes trajectories `$\tau$`.
 - **Training Loss (Eq. 11):** Includes a mean squared error (MSE) term for fitting ground truth `y($\tau$)` and a Lipschitz regularization term `$\beta_1$ * max(0, |$\hat{y}_\phi(\tau_1)$ - $\hat{y}_\phi(\tau_2)$| - $\sqrt{T}R_m ||\tau_1 - \tau_2||_F$).
 - **Enhancements:**
 - **LLM Embedding:** Advertiser-specific textual features (e.g., product titles) are processed by a pre-trained LLM (QWen2.5-1.5B-Instruct) to generate an embedding, which is then used as an additional positional encoding for the evaluator, enhancing its representational capacity.
 - **Pair-wise Loss:** A Bradley-Terry (BT) loss (Eq. 58) is added to the MSE, penalizing incorrect relative rankings between trajectory pairs, improving ranking accuracy. The overall evaluator loss (Eq. 59) combines pointwise, pairwise, and Lipschitz regularization.

- **Lipschitz Planner with Synchronous Coupling (Section 3.2.2):**
 - **Architecture:** Implemented using a Causal Transformer, generating states auto-regressively `p_$\theta$($s_{t+1}$|$s_{1:t}, y$)` as Gaussian distributions.
 - **Training Loss (Eq. 12):**
 - Negative expected evaluator score `- E_{$\tau$~p_$\theta$($\tau$|$y^*$)}[$\hat{y}_\phi(\tau)$]`.
 - Conditional behavior cloning term `-$\beta_2$ E_{( $\tau$,y)~p_D}[log p_$\theta$($\tau$|y)]` for the KL constraint.
 - Lipschitz penalty `$\beta_3$ * E_{y_1,y_2~D$\cup${y*}}[max(0, $\hat{W}_1(y_1,y_2;\theta)$ - $L_p|y_1 - y_2|$)]`.
 - **Synchronous Coupling for Wasserstein Distance (Eq. 13):** Instead of computing the true Wasserstein distance `W_1`, an upper bound `$\hat{W}_1$` is used. This upper bound is estimated by generating two trajectories (`$\tau_1, \tau_2$`) conditioned on `y_1` and `y_2` respectively, using the *same sequence of Gaussian noise*. This synchronous coupling reduces variance and provides a tighter upper bound, helping to enforce the Lipschitz constraint effectively.

The overall algorithm alternates between training the evaluator (with Lipschitz regularization) and training the planner (with score maximization, KL constraint, and Lipschitz penalty enforced via synchronous coupling).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on both simulated and real-world advertising systems, evaluating AIGB-Pearl against various offline RL and generative auto-bidding baselines.

#### 4.1. Overall Performance

- **Simulated Experiments (Table 1):** AIGB-Pearl consistently outperformed all baselines in terms of Gross Merchandise Volume (GMV) across four different budget levels. For example, at a budget of 1.5k, AIGB-Pearl achieved a +4.62% relative improvement in GMV compared to the most competitive baseline, DiffBid.
- **Real-world A/B Tests (Table 2):** In experiments involving 6,000 advertisers over 19 days on a large E-commerce platform (TaoBao), AIGB-Pearl demonstrated superior performance. It achieved a +3.00% improvement in GMV, +2.20% in BuyCnt (total impressions won), and +1.89% in Return on Investment (ROI) compared to DiffBid, while maintaining Cost fluctuations within a ±2% tolerance. Similar improvements were observed against other baselines like USCB, DT, and MOPO.
- **TargetROAS Bidding Problem (Appendix F.3, Table 8):** For the more complex TargetROAS auto-bidding problem, AIGB-Pearl achieved a +5.1% improvement in GMV compared to DiffBid in real-world A/B tests with 300,000 advertisers over 22 days.

#### 4.2. Generalization Ability

- **Unseen Advertisers (Table 3):** AIGB-Pearl was evaluated on a set of 4,000 advertisers whose data was not used in the offline dataset for training. In these real-world experiments, AIGB-Pearl consistently delivered better results in GMV (+3.32% over DiffBid, +3.08% over DT), BuyCnt, and ROI, while maintaining Cost fluctuations within 2%. This indicates enhanced generalization capabilities compared to existing AIGB methods.
- **Multi-modal Offline Data (Appendix F.7, Table 11):** The method's robustness was tested with an offline dataset generated by nine distinct bidding policies (multi-modal distribution). AIGB-Pearl still outperformed DiffBid by +4.9% in GMV, suggesting its performance is not highly sensitive to the specific distribution of the offline dataset.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study (RQ2)

- **Contribution of Constraints (Table 4):**
 - The KL constraint contributed a +1.09% improvement in GMV.
 - The Lipschitz constraint contributed a +1.81% improvement in GMV.
 This demonstrates the individual effectiveness of both constraints in enhancing AIGB-Pearl's performance.
- **Trajectory Visualization (Fig. 3):** Visualizations showed that AIGB-Pearl generated plausible and effective trajectories. In contrast, an ablated variant without the KL and Lipschitz constraints produced trajectories that significantly deviated from optimal behavior, exhibiting pathological issues such as excessive budget consumption, backward-trending pacing, or under-utilization of available budgets.

#### 4.4. Lipschitz Value Examination (RQ3)

- The empirical Lipschitz constants for the evaluator and planner were examined (Fig. 4 & 5). The Lipschitz constant of the evaluator `$\hat{y}_\phi(\tau)$` was `~2.2`, close to the theoretical value of `$\sqrt{T}R_m = 1.62$`. The planner's Lipschitz constant `~0.56` was also close to its set target `L_p = 0.50`. This indicates that the proposed Lipschitz regularization and synchronous coupling successfully enforced the desired Lipschitz properties on both models.

#### 4.5. Evaluator Accuracy Examination (RQ4)

- **Accuracy Metrics (Table 5):** The trajectory evaluator demonstrated reasonable accuracy on both training and out-of-distribution (OOD) data (via 5-fold cross-validation). In real-world experiments, it achieved an OOD Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of `1.2 +/- 0.03` and an OOD Area Under the Curve (AUC) of `75.1% +/- 0.2%` for ranking accuracy. The AUC is substantially above the 50% random-chance level.
- **Effectiveness of Enhancements (Appendix F.6, Table 9 & 10):**
 - **LLM Embedding:** Incorporating LLM embeddings led to faster convergence and improved both absolute (lower SMAPE) and ranking (higher AUC) accuracy of the evaluator.
 - **Hybrid Point-wise and Pair-wise Losses:** Combining both loss types resulted in the best overall evaluator accuracy (lower SMAPE and higher AUC), indicating benefits for both absolute value prediction and relative ranking.

#### 4.6. Training Stability (Appendix F.5)

- Learning curves for cumulative rewards, online rate, bad case rate, and cost rate demonstrated that AIGB-Pearl exhibited significantly smoother and more consistent learning progress with lower variance across multiple random seeds, compared to offline RL methods that rely on bootstrapping. This supports the claim of improved training stability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents AIGB-Pearl, a novel approach that significantly advances generative auto-bidding by integrating reward evaluation and policy optimization. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Performance and Generalization:** AIGB-Pearl demonstrates consistent state-of-the-art performance in both simulated and real-world advertising environments, including complex scenarios like TargetROAS. The observed real-world GMV uplifts (e.g., +3% on TaoBao) represent substantial economic value for large-scale advertising platforms. Its proven ability to generalize effectively to unseen advertisers, a critical aspect in dynamic commercial settings, further highlights its practical utility.

- **Bridging Generative Models and Reinforcement Learning:** The work effectively addresses a key limitation of prior generative auto-bidding methods (AIGB) by enabling them to explore beyond static offline datasets and learn from performance feedback. It marries the stable training characteristics of generative models with the performance-seeking capabilities of reinforcement learning, offering a robust paradigm for offline sequential decision-making.

- **Theoretical Guarantees for Safety and Reliability:** A significant contribution is the introduction of a provably sound KL-Lipschitz-constrained score-maximization objective. This theoretical framework, coupled with the derived sub-optimality bound, provides explicit guarantees for safe and efficient exploration beyond the offline data. In risk-sensitive domains like online advertising, where suboptimal decisions can lead to considerable financial losses, these theoretical underpinnings are crucial for building trustworthy and deployable AI systems.

- **Practical Algorithm Design and Innovations:** The methodology includes practical innovations such as the synchronous coupling technique for enforcing Lipschitz continuity in the generative planner and the integration of LLM embeddings and pairwise losses for a more accurate trajectory evaluator. These designs contribute to the robustness, efficiency, and real-world applicability of the proposed solution. The demonstrated improved training stability over traditional offline RL further enhances its appeal for industrial deployment.

- **Broader Applicability:** While developed for auto-bidding, the principles of combining generative planning with a theoretically constrained, evaluator-guided policy search could extend to other offline sequential decision-making problems in various domains (e.g., robotics, recommendation systems, healthcare) where data is static, exploration is costly or unsafe, and reliable generalization is paramount. The framework for bounding OOD generalization errors and ensuring model regularity through Lipschitz constraints offers a generalizable approach to mitigate risks in critical applications.
