# AnyGrasp: Robust and Efficient Grasp Perception in Spatial and Temporal Domains

> [!summary] Summary
> AnyGrasp, developed by researchers at Shanghai Jiao Tong University, introduces a unified system for robust, 7-DoF robotic grasp perception that operates effectively in both static and dynamic environments. The system achieved a 93.3% success rate on unseen objects in bin-picking, comparable to human performance, and demonstrated over 900 Mean Picks Per Hour, showcasing its efficiency and generalization capabilities.

## Problem

- Most 6-DoF robotic grasping methods are designed for static scenes, with dynamic object grasping largely unexplored or relying on computationally intensive, sparse predictions.
- Current deep learning-based grasping systems often require high-precision depth cameras or struggle with sim-to-real transfer when trained on simulated data, limiting their robustness to noise from common, low-cost commercial sensors.
- Robotic grasping frequently lacks the promptness, accuracy, flexibility, and temporal continuity observed in human grasping, particularly regarding stable grasp planning and implicit collision avoidance in cluttered environments.

## Method

- **AnyGrasp**'s unified deep learning framework combines a ==geometry processing module== for dense, ==7-DoF spatial grasp prediction== and a ==temporal association module== for continuous grasp tracking of moving objects.
- The geometry processing module incorporates a ==stable score== to prioritize grasps closer to the object's center of gravity and is trained to ==implicitly avoid obstacles== by penalizing collision-prone grasp candidates.
- The system trains on an extended real-world subset of the ==GraspNet-1Billion== dataset with novel annotations for stable scores and temporal association, enabling robustness to real-world ==sensor noise== and dynamic capabilities.

## Results

- AnyGrasp achieved a **93.3%** average attempt-centric success rate when clearing bins with over **300** unseen objects, demonstrating human-comparable performance (**93.9%** for humans) in static bin-picking.
- The system delivered high efficiency, achieving over **900 Mean Picks Per Hour (MPPH)** on a single UR5 arm, significantly outperforming previous state-of-the-art methods.
- It successfully performed dynamic grasping, attaining a **75.5%** success rate in catching swimming robot fish in a challenging underwater environment, which includes continuous movement and noisy point clouds.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training robotic grasping models with real-world depth data provides superior robustness against sensor noise and better generalization to unseen objects, outperforming models trained on large-scale simulated data.
> - Integrating human-inspired principles, such as awareness of an object's center-of-gravity and implicit obstacle avoidance during grasp planning, enhances grasp stability and overall success rates.
> - Dense, analytically generated grasp pose annotations and diverse training scene compositions are critical for developing robust grasp perception models that generalize well to varied real-world scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic grasping is a fundamental problem in robotics, crucial for enabling prehensile manipulation. The broader research landscape in visual-guided grasping has seen various approaches, each with its strengths and limitations, which AnyGrasp seeks to address and overcome.

Historically, grasping methods often assumed full knowledge of object 2D or 3D models and contact dynamics, which is impractical in unstructured real-world environments. Simplifications, such as reducing grasp perception to a planar (4-Degrees of Freedom, DoF) detection problem, are common but neglect critical orientations, limiting subsequent manipulation. More sophisticated sampling-evaluation methods generate 6-DoF grasp poses but are computationally intensive, leading to sparse predictions and being largely confined to static scenes.

More recently, the field has seen the rise of end-to-end deep learning networks for 6-DoF grasp pose detection,–, which directly predict dense grasp poses from scene inputs. AnyGrasp builds directly upon this advancement, specifically using the architecture of GSNet as its core "geometry processing module."

However, a significant gap remains in addressing the dynamic nature of human grasping. Most existing methods for 6-DoF grasping are tailored for static scenes. Dynamic grasping without prior object information is largely unexplored, with current approaches either relying on simple nearest-neighbor tracking or generating sparse future candidates, often failing to maintain consistency in the object's coordinate system. AnyGrasp directly tackles this by proposing a "spatially and temporally unified methodology."

Another critical discussion point in the landscape revolves around training data. While large-scale simulated datasets (e.g., DexNet, EGAD, Acronym) have become popular due to ease of annotation, the authors of AnyGrasp argue that models trained on simulation often require high-precision depth cameras for effective sim-to-real transfer. In contrast, AnyGrasp champions the use of real-world data, even if smaller in scale, to achieve robustness against noise from common, low-cost commercial depth sensors. This challenges a prevailing trend in the community and provides empirical evidence for a different data collection philosophy.

Furthermore, AnyGrasp explicitly incorporates human-inspired grasping principles: awareness of an object's center-of-mass (COG) for stability and obstacle avoidance, which are observed in human visually-guided grasping behavior. This integration aims to bridge the "ability gap between robots and humans" in terms of promptness, accuracy, flexibility, and temporal continuity.

In summary, AnyGrasp positions itself at the forefront of robotic grasping research by unifying dense 7-DoF spatial grasp perception with temporal tracking capabilities, explicitly optimizing for real-world noise resilience, and demonstrating human-comparable performance in both static and dynamic challenging scenarios.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind AnyGrasp stems from the observation that while human grasping is remarkably "prompt, accurate, flexible, and continuous across spatial and temporal domains," few existing robotic grasping methods fully encapsulate all these properties. The primary objective is to enable robots with these human-like grasping abilities, specifically for parallel grippers.

The key objectives can be broken down as follows:

1. **Robust and Efficient Grasp Perception:** To develop a system, AnyGrasp, capable of generating grasp poses that are highly successful across a wide variety of objects and challenging scenarios, while maintaining high operational speed. This includes robustness against inherent noise from low-cost depth sensors.
2. **7-DoF Grasp Generation:** To produce dense, accurate, 7-Degrees of Freedom (position, orientation, and gripper width) grasp configurations. This moves beyond simpler 4-DoF or 6-DoF approaches to provide comprehensive grasp information for manipulation.
3. **Spatial Continuity (Dense Grasp Prediction):** To enable "spatial-continuous learning" where the model can efficiently generate abundant, high-quality grasp candidates across the entire scene from a single feed-forward pass. This contrasts with slower, sampling-based methods that provide sparse predictions.
4. **Temporal Continuity (Dynamic Grasp Tracking):** To address the largely unexplored problem of dynamic grasping by enabling continuous, target-consistent tracking of grasp poses for moving objects. This ensures smooth and effective interaction with dynamic scenes.
5. **Integration of Human Grasping Principles:** To incorporate awareness of the object's Center of Gravity (COG) for improved grasping stability and to make the model "obstacle-aware," allowing it to implicitly avoid collisions and accelerate grasp pose selection. These aspects are inspired by human visual attention during grasping.
6. **Leveraging Real-World Data:** To demonstrate that directly training with real-world perception data, even from a relatively small dataset (144 objects), yields superior performance and robustness against sensor noise compared to training with large-scale simulated data, especially when using low-cost commercial sensors. This aims to guide future data collection strategies in the community.
7. **High Throughput and Generalization:** To achieve a high Mean Picks Per Hour (MPPH) rate for bin-picking tasks and to demonstrate strong generalization ability to a large number of unseen objects with diverse properties, mimicking real-world logistics or household scenarios.
8. **Demonstrate Challenging Dynamic Task:** To showcase the dynamic grasping capability by successfully catching fast-moving, small, and difficult-to-grasp objects in an adverse environment (robot fish in water).

In essence, the motivation is to bridge the existing gap between human and robotic grasping capabilities, making robots more versatile and efficient in unstructured, dynamic environments by providing a comprehensive, robust, and fast grasp perception system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AnyGrasp’s methodology is built upon a spatially and temporally unified deep learning framework, primarily designed for parallel grippers. It consists of two main modules: a geometry processing module for dense spatial grasp prediction and a temporal association module for dynamic grasp tracking. The approach also emphasizes a specific strategy for data collection and annotation.

#### 3.1. Grasp Representation

A grasp pose $G$ is represented as a 7-DoF configuration: $G = [R \ t \ w]$, where $R \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ is the gripper orientation, $t \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 1}$ is the center of the grasp, and $w \in \mathbb{R}$ is the minimum gripper width.

#### 3.2. Geometry Processing Module

- **Foundation:** This module is based on GSNet, an end-to-end 3D convolutional network. It takes a single-view point cloud as input.
- **Dense Grasp Prediction (Spatial-Continuous Learning):** Instead of sampling and evaluating, the network directly estimates grasp quality across the entire 6D pose space.
- **Architecture:** A 3D convolutional backbone extracts geometric features from the point cloud. An MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) predicts an objectness mask and a graspable probability heatmap. Farthest Point Sampling (FPS) is used to select $M$ seed points. For each seed point, another MLP determines the best view direction through Graspable Probabilistic View Selection (PVS). A cylinder grouping module gathers local features along this view.
- **Parameter Decomposition:** GSNet decomposes the grasp pose into a grasp point, view, in-plane rotation, approach depth, and width.
- **Modifications for AnyGrasp:**
 - **Extended Approach Depths:** An additional approach depth of 0.5 cm is added to accommodate smaller objects, bringing the total to 5 approach depths.
 - **Center of Gravity (COG) Awareness (Stable Score):** Inspired by human grasping, a "stable score" is incorporated. This score is the normalized perpendicular distance from the gripper plane to the assumed COG of the grasped object (calculated assuming uniform density from its 3D mesh). The network learns to predict this score. During inference, the grasp quality score is multiplied by `(1 - stable_score)` to prioritize grasps closer to the COG.
 - **Implicit Obstacle Awareness:** The network is trained to implicitly recognize and penalize grasp candidates that lack sufficient space for gripper pre-shaping (collision with scene obstacles), setting their grasp quality score to zero. This is enabled by the network perceiving the whole scene.

#### 3.3. Temporal Association Module

- **Purpose:** To enable smooth grasp pose tracking for moving objects by identifying correspondence between grasp poses across different time steps.
- **Feature Vector Generation:** For each of the $M$ predicted grasp poses, a unique feature vector of size $C$ (256) is generated. This vector concatenates:
 - **Geometric Features:** Seed features and grasp features extracted from the geometry processing module.
 - **Color/Texture Features:** RGB information grouped along the grasp direction using cylinder grouping, then processed by an MLP.
 - **Grasp Pose Parameters:** The 7-DoF parameters themselves.
- **Correspondence Score:** Cosine similarity is used to calculate the association score between any two grasp poses' feature vectors across different frames.
- **Dynamic Tracking:** During inference, feature vectors from the current frame are compared with those stored in a temporal buffer from the previous frame to identify corresponding grasp poses. For dynamic grasping, the system can predict a "future grasp pose" by calculating moving momentum from the historical poses in the buffer.

#### 3.4. Training Data and Annotation

- **Dataset:** AnyGrasp trains on an extended version of GraspNet-1Billion. While GraspNet-1Billion originally provided simulated data, AnyGrasp emphasizes using *real-world perception data* (268 scenes, 144 unique objects) captured by a depth camera to ensure robustness to real-world noise.
- **Dense Supervision Strategy:** Grasp poses and their qualities are analytically generated using object 3D meshes and then projected onto real-world point clouds with manually annotated 6D object poses. Collision detection is also performed.
- **Novel Annotations for AnyGrasp:**
 - Additional 0.5 cm approach depth labels.
 - Stable score labels (perpendicular distance to COG) for each grasp pose.
 - **Temporal Association Labels:** Generated by exploiting the 256 different viewpoints per static scene in the GraspNet-1Billion dataset. Pairs of grasp poses on the *same object* from *adjacent viewpoints* are considered "associated" if their SE(3) distance in the object's coordinate system is below a threshold. This effectively simulates continuous movement for training.

#### 3.5. Loss Functions

- **Geometry Processing:** Uses softmax loss for objectness classification and smooth-L1 loss for regression of graspable heatmaps and grasp pose parameters (from GSNet).
- **Temporal Association:** Employs supervised contrastive learning. It pulls feature vectors of "same class" grasp poses (those with SE(3) distance $\leq \sigma$) closer in the embedding space and pushes features of different classes apart.

#### 3.6. Training Details

- Input point clouds are down-sampled. $M=1024$ seed points, $C=256$ feature vector size, $K=16$ points for cylinder grouping.
- Trained on Nvidia GTX 2080 Ti GPU with Adam optimizer and poly learning rate decay.
- Sequential training: Geometry module trained first, then its weights frozen for joint training of the temporal association module.
- Extensive data augmentation: Random flipping, rotation, translation, and random object removal during temporal module training.

#### 3.7. Detection Post-processing

- **Explicit Collision Detection:** A crucial safety step where the top-100 predicted grasp poses undergo a secondary collision check based on the partial-view point cloud, simplifying the gripper as three cubes. This supplements the network's implicit obstacle awareness.
- **Gripper-Centering Process:** Adjusts the grasp pose to ensure both fingertips contact the object surface simultaneously, preventing the object from being pushed away by sequential contact. This is done by translating the gripper along its closing direction to equalize distances from fingertips to estimated contact points.

This comprehensive methodology, combining advanced neural network architectures with tailored real-world data strategies and post-processing, allows AnyGrasp to achieve its stated objectives of robust and efficient grasping in both static and dynamic environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

AnyGrasp demonstrates significant performance improvements across both static and dynamic grasping scenarios, validated through extensive real-robot experiments and comparative analyses.

#### 4.1. Static Scene Grasping (Bin-Picking)

- **Overall Performance and Generalization:** AnyGrasp achieved a **93.3% average attempt-centric success rate** when clearing bins with over 300 *unseen* objects, showcasing remarkable generalization across diverse shapes, materials, and sizes (ranging from 1.5 cm³ to 36x4x11.5 cm³). The object-centric completion rate was 99.8%.
- **Human-Level Performance:** The success rate is **on par with human subjects (93.9% average)** using the same end-effector configuration (parallel gripper with soft rubber fingertips) and an open-loop grasping strategy (no tactile feedback adjustments). While AnyGrasp showed slight variations across object categories (81.5% to 100%), human performance was more consistently high (91.4% to 96.6%).
- **Efficiency (MPPH):** AnyGrasp reported **over 900 Mean Picks Per Hour (MPPH)** on a single UR5 arm system. This is a dramatic improvement over previous state-of-the-art methods (e.g., Dex-Net 4.0 achieved 300 MPPH on a dual-arm system). The perception system itself predicts grasps in 100ms, with total decision time under 200ms, making robot arm speed the primary bottleneck. Humans, for comparison, achieved 1,000-1,200 MPPH on average (up to 1,500 MPPH maximum).
- **Robustness to Depth Sensor Noise:** AnyGrasp demonstrated robustness against noise from different commercial depth sensors (Intel RealSense D415 vs. D435), with the D435 showing larger depth variance (up to ±5mm error). The model trained on real-world data maintained high performance even with the noisier D435, validating the effectiveness of its training strategy.
- **Challenging Adversarial Objects:** On a test set of 62 adversarial 3D-printed objects (known for complex shapes and smooth surfaces, making grasping harder), AnyGrasp’s accuracy decreased but still significantly outperformed DexNet 4.0. Human subjects, however, maintained stable performance.
- **Fragile Object Grasping:** AnyGrasp successfully demonstrated cleaning thin fragments of a broken clay pot (less than 3mm thick), a challenging scenario due to the small size, thinness, and noisy depth perception, which previous algorithms have not shown success in.

#### 4.2. Dynamic Scene Grasping (Robot Fish Catching)

- **Performance:** AnyGrasp achieved an average **75.5% success rate** in catching swimming robot fish in a tank across five trials. This highlights its ability to handle dynamic, unstructured, and challenging environments.
- **Challenges Addressed:** The task presented multiple difficulties: low friction in water, small fish size requiring high accuracy, continuous fish movement, and noisy underwater point clouds due to light reflection/refraction.
- **Comparison to Heuristic Baseline:** AnyGrasp significantly outperformed a simple nearest-grasp-pose heuristic method (62.5% success rate), which tended to lag the moving target and took 12.7% longer for successful grasps.
- **Failure Analysis:** Common failure modes included: (i) slippage due to low water friction (~50% of failures), (ii) inaccurate future pose prediction (due to sudden changes in fish speed), (iii) poor grasp quality (gripper pushing fish away), and (iv) correspondence switching (when two similar fish are close).

#### 4.3. Training Data Analysis

- **Real vs. Simulated Data:** A crucial finding was the **superiority of training with real-world data** over simulated data (even with Gaussian noise augmentation). Models trained on simulated data showed a "large performance gap" on the GraspNet-1Billion benchmark, especially on novel objects and for high-score grasps. In real-world bin-picking, the simulated-trained model performed much worse and often failed to generate any high-quality grasps when few objects remained. This strongly advocates for prioritizing real-world data collection.
- **Dense Annotation Importance:** Downsampling the *density of grasp annotations per object* by 10x or 50x led to performance degradation comparable to decreasing the *number of training images*. This suggests that dense, automatically generated grasp annotations are highly valuable and a good trade-off in data collection efforts.
- **Scene Diversity Importance:** Downsampling the *number of training scenes* showed the largest performance degradation, with the model failing to converge when only 2 training scenes were used. This indicates that diverse scene compositions are critical for robust model training and generalization.
- **Grasp Pose Tracking vs. 6D Pose Tracking:** The paper argues that grasp pose tracking is more advantageous for deformable objects and more robust to partial occlusion and large depth noise compared to general 6D object pose tracking, as it focuses on relevant local features for grasping.

In summary, AnyGrasp delivers state-of-the-art performance in robotic grasping, demonstrating human-level accuracy and high efficiency in static bin-picking, alongside robust capabilities in novel dynamic grasping scenarios. Its empirical findings on training data highlight critical considerations for future dataset design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AnyGrasp represents a significant stride in robotic manipulation, offering a unified and robust solution for grasp perception that addresses several long-standing challenges in the field. Its potential impact spans both academic research and practical robotic applications.

#### 5.1. Advancing Robotic Grasping Capabilities

- **Unified Spatial-Temporal Perception:** AnyGrasp is presented as the "first unified system for fast, accurate, 7-DoF and temporally-continuous grasp pose detection." This integration is crucial for moving beyond static pick-and-place tasks to more complex, human-like interactions with dynamic and unstructured environments.
- **Human-Comparable Performance:** Achieving a 93.3% success rate on unseen objects, comparable to human subjects, sets a new benchmark for generalization and reliability in real-world bin-picking. This level of performance makes the system viable for industrial and logistical applications.
- **High Throughput:** The reported 900+ MPPH on a single-arm system demonstrates remarkable efficiency, making AnyGrasp highly suitable for high-volume automation tasks where speed is critical.

#### 5.2. Practicality and Robustness for Real-World Deployment

- **Robustness to Sensor Noise:** A key contribution is the empirical validation that training on real-world data leads to superior robustness against noise from low-cost commercial depth sensors. This is highly significant for practical deployment, as it reduces the need for expensive, high-precision sensors often required by simulation-trained models. This finding challenges the prevailing trend of relying solely on massive simulated datasets and encourages a re-evaluation of data collection strategies.
- **COG Awareness and Obstacle Avoidance:** The incorporation of stable score (COG awareness) and implicit obstacle awareness, inspired by human grasping, directly contributes to more stable and safer grasps, reducing failure rates and collisions in cluttered environments. This adds an important layer of "intelligence" to the grasp perception process.

#### 5.3. Impact on Research Methodology and Dataset Design

- **Empirical Evidence for Real-World Data:** The detailed analysis comparing real-world and simulated data training provides compelling evidence for prioritizing real-world data for models intended for real-world deployment with typical sensors. This could influence future research directions and data collection efforts in the robotics community.
- **Insights into Annotation Density and Scene Diversity:** The findings on the importance of dense grasp pose annotations and diverse training scenes offer valuable guidance for designing more effective and efficient datasets, optimizing annotation efforts without sacrificing model performance, especially for generalization to novel objects.
- **Grasp Tracking vs. Object Pose Tracking:** The argument for grasp pose tracking's superiority over general 6D object pose tracking for deformable objects and noisy partial views offers a strategic insight for tackling complex manipulation challenges.

#### 5.4. Future Directions and Open Contributions

- **Open-Source Library:** The commitment to releasing the AnyGrasp library and example code significantly contributes to reproducible research and provides the community with a powerful, human-level performance tool. This can serve as a strong baseline for further developments in the field.
- **Motivation for Future Work:** The paper explicitly acknowledges limitations, such as the fragility to occlusion during closed-loop grasping (due to reliance on visual perception) and the need for tactile feedback. This openly invites future research into multimodal perception and in-hand adjustment for truly robust, human-like closed-loop manipulation. Extending the capabilities to different robotic hands (e.g., multi-fingered grippers) is also highlighted.

In conclusion, AnyGrasp pushes the boundaries of robotic grasp perception by offering a fast, accurate, and robust system for both static and dynamic environments. Its practical implications, particularly concerning data strategy and real-world deployment, are substantial, potentially accelerating the adoption of intelligent robotic manipulation in a wider range of applications, from logistics and manufacturing to service robotics in unstructured settings.
