# Robot Trains Robot: Automatic Real-World Policy Adaptation and Learning for Humanoids

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University introduced the 'Robot Trains Robot' (RTR) framework, integrating a compliant robotic arm teacher, automated learning infrastructure, and a three-stage reinforcement learning pipeline to facilitate efficient and safe real-world policy adaptation and learning for humanoid robots. This system enabled a humanoid to double its walking speed in 20 minutes and learn a swing-up motion from scratch in 15 minutes.

## Problem

- High safety risks and potential for damage during real-world exploration for inherently unstable humanoid robots.
- Difficulty in designing effective real-world reward functions due to the inaccessibility of crucial internal states and contact forces on physical humanoids.
- Poor learning efficiency and extensive human intervention required for frequent manual resets and data collection in real-world humanoid training.

## Method

- A teacher-student hardware system featuring a 6-DoF robotic arm that provides active, compliant support, perturbation, and crucial sensory feedback to a humanoid student via elastic ropes and an F/T sensor.
- An automated learning infrastructure incorporating a dynamic curriculum, real-time reward signal generation from teacher measurements, automatic failure detection, and autonomous resets, minimizing human intervention.
- A three-stage reinforcement learning pipeline for sim-to-real adaptation, involving dynamics-conditioned policy training, universal latent optimization in simulation, and efficient real-world refinement of a dynamics-encoded latent variable using PPO.

## Results

- The humanoid robot successfully doubled its zero-shot walking speed, precisely tracking treadmill velocity, with only 20 minutes of real-world training.
- A complex swing-up motion, difficult to simulate, was learned from scratch in 15 minutes of real-world interaction, demonstrating the system's flexibility beyond adaptation.
- RTR's latent-based adaptation achieved superior stability metrics and data efficiency compared to direct policy fine-tuning or residual policies, exhibiting lower torso roll/pitch and reduced reliance on arm support.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - An active, compliant robotic arm can function as an effective 'teacher,' providing safe support, informative reward signals, and task-relevant perturbations crucial for real-world humanoid learning.
> - Optimizing a dynamics-aware latent variable, conditioned via FiLM layers, offers a data-efficient and stable method for adapting simulation-trained policies to real-world humanoid dynamics.
> - Full automation of the learning loop, including curriculum design, failure detection, and automatic resets, is essential for achieving practical and high-throughput real-world reinforcement learning on complex robots.
