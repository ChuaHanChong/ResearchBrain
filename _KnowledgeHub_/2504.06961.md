# Two by Two: Learning Multi-Task Pairwise Objects Assembly for Generalizable Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Shanghai Qi Zhi Institute and partner institutions introduce 2BY2, a large-scale dataset for pairwise object assembly along with a two-step SE(3) pose estimation network, enabling robots to perform complex assembly tasks across 18 different categories while achieving improvements of 0.046 in translation RMSE and 8.97 in rotation RMSE compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Existing datasets focus mainly on geometric fragments or factory parts, lacking semantic and functional relationships needed for daily object assembly
- Robots struggle to generalize assembly skills across diverse everyday objects and tasks

## Method

- Created 2BY2 dataset with annotated 3D object pairs covering 18 fine-grained assembly tasks
- Developed two-step network architecture using SE(3) equivariant features to predict poses for base and fitting objects

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art performance across all 18 tasks in the 2BY2 dataset
- Demonstrated strong generalization ability across different assembly scenarios
- Successfully validated approach through real-world experiments using UR5 robotic arm
- Provided new benchmark dataset for evaluating 3D assembly methods

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Breaking down assembly into two steps (base and fitting object) mirrors human approach and improves accuracy
> - Combining geometric features at different scales helps capture both overall shape and fine details
> - Including pose and symmetry annotations enables better understanding of object relationships

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a critical gap in the field of robot manipulation and 3D assembly: the lack of datasets and benchmarks that adequately represent the complexities of everyday object interactions. Existing datasets primarily focus on geometric fragments or factory parts, neglecting the semantic and functional relationships crucial for robots to perform daily tasks effectively.

**Position within the Landscape:**

- **Dataset Contribution:** The 2BY2 dataset directly addresses the limitation of existing datasets (AutoMate, JoinABLe, Neural Shape Mating, Breaking Bad, Factory, RLBench, RoboSuite) by providing a large-scale, annotated dataset specifically designed for daily pairwise object assembly. This contribution aligns with the growing recognition in the robotics community that high-quality, diverse datasets are essential for training and evaluating robot learning algorithms.
- **Focus on Semantic and Functional Relationships:** The work moves beyond purely geometric assembly by emphasizing the importance of understanding functional and spatial relationships between objects (e.g., understanding that a lid needs to cover a container, or a plug needs to fit into a socket). This semantic understanding is crucial for robots to generalize to novel objects and tasks.
- **Assembly Methods:** The proposed two-step SE(3) pose estimation method leverages equivariant geometric features, which fits into the trend of using geometric deep learning and equivariant neural networks for robot manipulation. These methods aim to improve sample efficiency and generalization by incorporating geometric priors into the network architecture.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

- **Objective 1:** Create a large-scale, annotated dataset (2BY2) specifically for daily pairwise object assembly. This dataset should cover a diverse range of tasks reflecting real-life scenarios and include pose and symmetry annotations.
- **Objective 2:** Develop a novel two-step SE(3) pose estimation method that can effectively leverage the 2BY2 dataset for accurate and generalizable object assembly.
- **Objective 3:** Demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method through quantitative evaluation on the 2BY2 dataset and qualitative evaluation through real-world robot experiments.

**Motivation:**

The primary motivation stems from the need to enable robots to perform complex 3D assembly tasks in unstructured, everyday environments. The authors argue that existing benchmarks and datasets are insufficient for this purpose, as they fail to capture the intricacies of real-world object interactions. By introducing the 2BY2 dataset and the associated pose estimation method, the authors aim to facilitate the development of robots capable of assisting humans in a wide range of daily activities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

- **Dataset Creation:** The 2BY2 dataset was constructed using 3D meshes from various sources (3D Warehouse, SAPIEN PartNet-Mobility, Google SketchUp 3D Challenge, and Objaverse). Meshes were manually paired, cleaned, annotated, and scaled. Crucially, the dataset includes pose and symmetry annotations for each object pair and is divided into 18 fine-grained tasks categorized into Lid Covering, Inserting, and High Precision Placing.
- **Two-Step Pairwise Network Architecture:** The core of the methodology is a two-step network designed to mimic human assembly processes. The network has two branches:
 - **Branch B:** Predicts the pose of the "base" object (e.g., the vase) using a two-scale Vector Neuron DGCNN encoder to extract SE(3) equivariant features.
 - **Branch A:** Takes the transformed base object and the "fitting" object (e.g., the flower) as input. It extracts SE(3) equivariant and SO(3) invariant features, fuses them, and then predicts the pose of the fitting object.
- **Two-Scale Vector Neuron DGCNN Encoder:** This encoder extracts SE(3) equivariant and SO(3) invariant features from point cloud inputs. The two-scale architecture allows the network to capture geometric features at different scales, improving its ability to understand both the overall shape and fine-grained details.
- **Training and Evaluation:** The network is trained in two steps, independently training Branch A and Branch B. This approach minimizes the impact of pose prediction errors from Branch B on the training of Branch A. Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) is used to evaluate the translation and rotation accuracy of the predicted poses.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** The proposed method achieves state-of-the-art performance across all 18 tasks in the 2BY2 dataset, demonstrating a significant improvement over existing shape assembly methods. The average improvement is 0.046 in translation RMSE and 8.97 in rotation RMSE.
- **Generalization Ability:** The method exhibits strong generalization across multiple tasks within the 2BY2 dataset, including the comprehensive "All" task. This indicates the network's ability to handle diverse object shapes and assembly scenarios.
- **Real-World Validation:** Real-world robot experiments using a UR5 robotic arm further validate the practical applicability and generalizability of the method for complex 3D assembly tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

- **Advancement of Robot Manipulation:** The 2BY2 dataset and the proposed pose estimation method represent a significant step forward in enabling robots to perform complex 3D assembly tasks in real-world environments.
- **Benchmark for Future Research:** The 2BY2 dataset serves as a valuable benchmark for evaluating and comparing future 3D assembly methods. Its focus on daily object interactions and comprehensive annotations will drive research towards more practical and generalizable solutions.
- **Potential Applications:** The research has the potential to impact a wide range of applications, including:
 - **Domestic Robots:** Robots capable of assisting humans with household tasks such as furniture assembly, appliance repair, and object arrangement.
 - **Industrial Automation:** Robots that can automate assembly processes in manufacturing settings.
 - **Assistive Robotics:** Robots that can help individuals with disabilities perform daily tasks.

**Overall, this research provides a valuable contribution to the field of robot manipulation by addressing the limitations of existing datasets and developing a novel pose estimation method that exhibits strong performance and generalization ability.** The 2BY2 dataset is expected to become a widely used benchmark, driving future research in this area. Further improvements on the grasping and motion trajectory of the robot can further assist its usability in the real world.
