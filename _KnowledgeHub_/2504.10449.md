# M1: Towards Scalable Test-Time Compute with Mamba Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> M1 introduces a hybrid linear RNN reasoning model based on the Mamba architecture, addressing the scalability limitations of Transformers for complex reasoning tasks. It achieves competitive reasoning performance, demonstrates over 3x faster inference throughput than similarly sized Transformers, and effectively translates this speed into higher accuracy on mathematical benchmarks when leveraging test-time compute strategies like self-consistency.

## Problem

- Transformer-based Large Language Models (LLMs) suffer from quadratic computational complexity and linear memory requirements, hindering scalable reasoning tasks.
- These limitations restrict the efficient handling of long Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning sequences and large batch sizes during inference.
- There was a need to evaluate if alternative architectures, specifically Mamba, could maintain strong performance on specialized reasoning tasks compared to Transformers.

## Method

- A hybrid linear RNN reasoning model, M1, was developed using the ==Mamba architecture== with **6** interleaved attention layers among its **28** total layers.
- A three-stage training process was implemented: ==distillation from a Transformer== using reverse KL divergence, followed by multi-round supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on general math and reasoning-specific datasets.
- The model underwent a final reinforcement learning (RL) phase using the ==GRPO loss function==, with adaptations to remove the KL penalty and add an entropy bonus for policy refinement.

## Results

- M1-3B achieved competitive reasoning scores, for instance, scoring **23.5%** on AIME25 and **47.3%** on OlympiadBench, often matching or slightly exceeding state-of-the-art distilled Transformer reasoning models.
- The model demonstrated over a **3x** speedup in inference throughput compared to a Llama-3.2-3B Transformer at large batch sizes and was at least **2x** faster for long sequence generation.
- Under a fixed time budget, M1 achieved comparable or higher reasoning accuracy than Transformer models by enabling a greater number of samples for self-consistency, effectively converting speed into enhanced performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hybrid Mamba architectures can achieve reasoning performance comparable to or slightly surpassing specialized distilled Transformer models on complex mathematical benchmarks.
> - Mamba's memory efficiency enables significant inference speedups (over 3x at large batch sizes and 2x for long sequence generation) compared to similarly-sized Transformer models.
> - The increased inference throughput of Mamba models can be directly leveraged to enhance reasoning accuracy by allowing more extensive test-time compute (e.g., self-consistency voting) within the same time budget.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research paper, "M1: Towards Scalable Test-Time Compute with Mamba Reasoning Models," is situated at the intersection of several critical and rapidly evolving areas in large language model research: architectural innovation, efficient inference, and advanced reasoning capabilities.

**Challenges with Transformer Architectures:** The dominant paradigm for LLMs, the Transformer architecture, has undeniably achieved remarkable success, particularly in complex reasoning tasks through techniques like Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting. However, the paper highlights a fundamental limitation: Transformers suffer from quadratic computational complexity and linear memory requirements with respect to sequence length. This poses significant hurdles for applications requiring long reasoning chains, large batch sizes, or extensive test-time computation (e.g., self-consistency voting), making them computationally expensive and memory-intensive. The increasing demand for longer contexts and more sophisticated multi-step reasoning further exacerbates these scaling issues.

**Emergence of Alternative Architectures:** In response to Transformer limitations, the research community has actively explored alternative architectures. This includes a resurgence of RNN-based models (e.g., RWKV, XL-STM), linear attention mechanisms, and notably, State Space Models (SSMs). Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2024), a key innovation by co-author Tri Dao, has emerged as a particularly promising SSM. It offers linear-time sequence modeling with strong performance, competitive with Transformers, especially in long-context scenarios, due to its selective state spaces and memory-efficient design. This paper explicitly builds on Mamba, positioning itself as a direct exploration of its utility in a domain traditionally dominated by Transformers.

**Scaling Test-Time Compute for Reasoning:** A parallel trend in LLM research involves boosting model performance not by increasing model size, but by scaling "test-time compute." Techniques like CoT (Wei et al., 2023), self-consistency (Wang et al., 2023), and verification models (Cobbe et al., 2021) have shown that allowing models to "think longer" or "try multiple paths" during inference can significantly improve reasoning accuracy. Recent studies even suggest that smaller models with intelligent inference-time compute can outperform larger models with less inference-time scaling (Snell et al., 2024). This paper directly engages with this concept, proposing that more efficient base architectures like Mamba can enable even greater benefits from these test-time scaling strategies, as they allow more computation within a fixed time/resource budget.

**Knowledge Distillation and Cross-Architecture Transfer:** The paper also fits within the broader context of knowledge distillation. Distillation is a well-established technique for transferring knowledge from larger, more capable "teacher" models to smaller, more efficient "student" models, often within the same architecture (e.g., Transformer-to-Transformer). A more challenging frontier is *cross-architecture distillation*, where knowledge is transferred from one type of model (e.g., Transformer) to a fundamentally different one (e.g., RNN, SSM). This paper directly tackles cross-architecture distillation, aiming to transfer the robust reasoning capabilities of large Transformer-based models (like DeepSeek-R1) to a Mamba-based architecture. This is a crucial step for demonstrating the viability of Mamba as a substitute or enhancement for Transformers in complex tasks.

In summary, this work contributes to the active pursuit of more efficient and scalable LLM architectures, specifically by demonstrating that Mamba models can be effectively trained and perform competitively on complex mathematical reasoning tasks. It bridges the gap between architectural innovation and practical deployment, showing how the inference speedups offered by Mamba can be directly translated into improved reasoning accuracy through test-time scaling, challenging the notion that only Transformer-based models can achieve state-of-the-art reasoning performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind the M1 research is to overcome the inherent limitations of transformer-based Large Language Models (LLMs) when applied to complex reasoning tasks, particularly those requiring extensive test-time computation. The paper sets forth several key objectives:

1. **Address Scalability Challenges of Transformers in Reasoning:** The primary motivation stems from the quadratic computational complexity and linear memory requirements of Transformers. These limitations severely restrict their ability to handle long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning sequences and large batch sizes efficiently during inference. Since effective reasoning, especially in mathematics and programming, often benefits from generating longer thought processes and/or multiple parallel attempts, these architectural constraints become a significant bottleneck.

2. **Evaluate Mamba Architectures for Reasoning Tasks:** While linear hybrid RNN models, including those based on Mamba, have shown promise as general language models, their effectiveness on specialized reasoning tasks was an open question. The authors explicitly aim to determine if Mamba-based models can maintain strong performance in scenarios requiring long CoTs for challenging math problems, or if their performance diminishes compared to Transformers.

3. **Develop an Efficient Training Pipeline for Mamba Reasoning Models:** The paper seeks to propose and validate a multi-stage training process capable of endowing Mamba-based architectures with robust reasoning capabilities. This includes leveraging knowledge distillation from existing high-performing Transformer models, followed by supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on mathematical and reasoning-specific datasets, and finally, performance boosting through reinforcement learning (RL). A key aspect is to achieve this with a relatively low training token budget compared to the trillion-token pre-training of some baseline models.

4. **Demonstrate Superior Inference Efficiency:** A core objective is to showcase that the Mamba-based M1 model can achieve significantly faster inference throughput compared to similarly-sized Transformer models. This speedup is crucial for making test-time compute scaling (e.g., self-consistency) economically viable and practically feasible.

5. **Translate Inference Speed into Enhanced Reasoning Accuracy:** Beyond mere speed, the research aims to prove that M1's increased throughput can be converted into tangible gains in reasoning accuracy under a fixed computational budget. By generating more samples (via self-consistency) or longer reasoning chains within the same time, M1 should demonstrate superior performance compared to a Transformer that is constrained by its slower generation.

6. **Open-Source and Advance Research in Efficient Reasoning Models:** By open-sourcing the code and pre-trained checkpoints, the authors aim to contribute to the broader research community, providing a robust baseline and framework for further development in efficient, non-Transformer reasoning models.

In essence, the motivation is to break the dependency on computationally expensive Transformers for state-of-the-art reasoning, proposing Mamba as a viable, more scalable, and efficient alternative that can leverage test-time compute strategies more effectively.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The M1 reasoning model is developed through a meticulously designed multi-stage training pipeline, combining distillation, supervised fine-tuning (SFT), and reinforcement learning (RL). This approach aims to efficiently transfer and enhance reasoning capabilities within a Mamba-based architecture.

**Model Architecture:**
M1 is a **hybrid linear RNN reasoning model** built on the Mamba architecture. Specifically, it uses a Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct model as the initial distillation target. For its Mamba layers, the SSM state size is set to 16. The model also incorporates 6 interleaved attention layers among its 28 total layers, indicating a hybrid structure that potentially leverages the strengths of both Mamba (for long-range dependencies and efficiency) and attention (for certain local interactions or expressiveness).

**Three-Stage Training Process:**

1. **Stage 1: Distillation**
- **Goal:** To transfer foundational language model capabilities from a pre-trained Transformer to a Mamba-based architecture and initialize its weights efficiently.
- **Framework:** The distillation adapts the `MAMBAINLLAMA` framework (Wang et al., 2024a). This framework proposes distilling hybrid Transformer-Mamba models by reusing weights from the Transformer's attention layers.
- **Mechanism:** Linear projections for Query (Q), Key (K), Value (V), and Output (O) in the Transformer are used to initialize corresponding projections for Context (C), Branch (B), Input (X), and Output (O) in the Mamba layers, respectively.
- **Key Adaptation:** The authors introduce two additional linear layers to project from `head_dim * kv_head` to `head_dim * n_head`. This is because the original Transformer used Grouped Query Attention (GQA) to reduce KV cache size, but Mamba does not use a KV cache. This expansion is hypothesized to increase the expressiveness of B and X in Mamba.
- **Training Details:** The distillation involves replacing attention layers with Mamba layers in a single step and then fine-tuning the entire model. The loss function is the **reverse KL divergence** (D_KL(p(·; θ) ∥ p(·; θ_T))), chosen for its mode-seeking properties, aligning the student model's probability distribution with the teacher's. Training uses the Axolotl framework, applying chat templates, masking user prompts, and computing loss only on assistant outputs. Data packing is employed to merge sequences, speeding up training for a maximum sequence length of 8192. An AdamW optimizer with a learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁵ is used.

2. **Stage 2: Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**
- **Goal:** To imbue the distilled model with strong mathematical and reasoning-specific problem-solving abilities.
- **First SFT Round (General Math):** The model is initially fine-tuned on a large set of general math problems from the **OpenMathInstruct-2** dataset (Toshniwal et al., 2024) for two epochs. The same chat template, masking, and loss computation strategy as distillation are applied.
- **Second SFT Round (Reasoning-Specific Data):** Subsequently, the model is further fine-tuned on a mixed reasoning dataset totaling 10 billion reasoning tokens. This dataset includes:
 - OpenR1-Math-220k
 - OpenThoughts-114k-math (both generated from DeepSeek-R1)
 - ServiceNow-AI-R1-Distill
 - Magpie-Reasoning-250K (generated from R1 distilled Qwen 32B and Llama 70B models)
 - The training length is extended to 24,576 tokens to cover 99% of the data items. This stage runs for five epochs with the same optimizer but a reduced peak learning rate of 6 × 10⁻⁶.

3. **Stage 3: Reinforcement Learning (RL) Training**
- **Goal:** To further refine the model's mathematical reasoning capabilities, particularly for verifiable tasks.
- **Method:** The model is integrated into a popular RL pipeline, `VeRL` (Sheng et al., 2024), and trained using the **GRPO (Generalized Proximal Policy Optimization)** loss function.
- **Key Adaptations:**
 - The KL penalty term, typically present in PPO/GRPO, is removed as it was found to destabilize training empirically.
 - An entropy bonus is added to encourage a more diverse policy.
- **RL Parameters:** Batch size of 128, PPO batch size of 64 (determining 2 PPO iterations). Each sequence generates 8 samples, with a maximum generation length of 32k.
- **Optimization:** Adam optimizer with a learning rate of 1 × 10⁻⁶ for 50 steps, selecting the checkpoint with the highest critic reward.
- **Prompting:** The prompt "Let’s think step by step and output the final answer within \boxed{}" is appended to each question during both training and evaluation to elicit chain-of-thought reasoning.

This comprehensive, multi-stage approach aims to leverage the strengths of different training paradigms – efficient architectural initialization, large-scale supervised learning for knowledge acquisition, and RL for policy refinement – to create a highly capable and efficient Mamba-based reasoning model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The M1 paper presents several compelling findings demonstrating the effectiveness and efficiency of their hybrid Mamba reasoning model:

1. **Competitive Reasoning Performance:**
- M1-3B achieves reasoning scores comparable to or even slightly surpassing state-of-the-art distilled Transformer reasoning models (specifically, DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B) despite having more parameters (3B vs 1.5B) but offering significantly faster inference.
- **Key benchmark results (pass@1):**
 - **AIME25:** M1-3B (23.5%) vs. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (23.0%)
 - **AIME24:** M1-3B (28.9%) vs. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (28.8%)
 - **MATH500:** M1-3B (82.1%) vs. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (82.8%)
 - **OlympiadBench:** M1-3B (47.3%) vs. DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B (43.3%)
- M1-3B also significantly outperforms larger non-reasoning math Transformer models (e.g., Qwen2.5-Math-7B-Instruct) on several benchmarks.

2. **Significant Inference Speedup:**
- **Batch Size Scaling:** M1 achieves over a **3x speedup** compared to a Transformer model of the same size (Llama-3.2-3B) when served using the highly optimized vLLM inference engine, particularly at large batch sizes (e.g., batch size 512, decoding length 4096). This advantage is attributed to Mamba's memory efficiency, which transforms memory access advantages into speed gains during the memory-bound decoding phase.
- **Long Sequence Generation:** When fixing the batch size (e.g., 128) and increasing the generation length, M1 consistently generates at least **2x faster** than Llama-3.2-3B, with speedups increasing for longer sequences (e.g., 2.64x faster for the longest sequence length tested). This is crucial for applications requiring extensive chain-of-thought.

3. **Translation of Speed to Accuracy via Test-Time Scaling:**
- **Majority Voting (Self-Consistency):** Under a fixed time budget, M1's higher throughput allows it to generate more samples for self-consistency voting (Maj@k) compared to Transformers.
 - On AIME25, M1 nearly matches DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B in accuracy for larger sample sizes (e.g., Maj@32: M1-3B 34.6% vs DeepSeek 35.0%), but achieves this in significantly less time due to its speedup.
 - When plotting accuracy against actual generation time, M1 achieves comparable or even slightly higher accuracy than DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B within the same time budget. For instance, to reach ~40% AIME25 accuracy with majority voting, M1 requires about half the time of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B.
- **Longer Sequences:** M1 also benefits from scaling the maximum generation length. When comparing accuracy against generation time, M1 consistently achieves higher accuracy for the same amount of time spent generating at most evaluated sequence lengths, highlighting its efficiency in leveraging longer reasoning paths.

4. **Impact of Training Stages:**
- Each stage of the proposed training pipeline (Distillation, SFT on general math, SFT on reasoning data, and RL) incrementally boosts M1's performance.
- The most significant gains were observed from fine-tuning on reasoning-specific datasets, leading to a +29% accuracy boost on MATH500 and +17% on AIME24.

5. **Importance of RL Training Length:**
- Increasing the maximum sequence length used during RL training (GRPO) demonstrably improves model performance. For example, on AIME25, increasing the maximum GRPO training length from 4096 to 24576 boosts accuracy from below 10% to 23%. This suggests that Mamba's efficiency can enable more effective RL training by allowing longer thought processes during learning.

These findings collectively establish M1 as a strong contender in reasoning tasks, not only matching the performance of specialized Transformer models but also offering significant practical advantages in terms of inference speed and cost, especially when test-time computation is leveraged.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The M1 research presents a significant advancement in the field of large language models, particularly for applications requiring complex reasoning and efficient deployment. Its potential impact spans several key areas:

1. **Validation of Mamba for Reasoning Tasks:** This paper provides compelling evidence that the Mamba architecture, and more broadly, hybrid linear RNN models, can achieve state-of-the-art performance on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks. This is crucial because it dispels the notion that only Transformer-based models possess the necessary expressiveness or inductive biases for such tasks. It significantly expands the architectural options available for developing highly capable reasoning agents.

2. **Enabling Scalable Test-Time Compute:** One of the most critical impacts of M1 is its ability to make "test-time compute" strategies, such as self-consistency, majority voting, and generating long chains of thought, far more practical and economically viable. By achieving a 3x speedup over Transformers, M1 allows users to perform significantly more inference-time computation within the same time or resource budget. This means smaller, more efficient Mamba models can potentially outperform larger, slower Transformers, particularly in scenarios where generating multiple samples or longer reasoning paths is beneficial. This could democratize access to advanced reasoning capabilities by reducing the computational cost.

3. **Cost Reduction in LLM Deployment:** For companies and researchers deploying LLMs for reasoning tasks, inference speed directly translates to cost. M1's superior throughput means lower GPU hours are needed for inference, leading to substantial cost savings. This is particularly relevant for high-volume applications or those requiring real-time reasoning. It also eases the pressure on memory resources, especially with large batch sizes, which is a common bottleneck in current LLM serving.

4. **Acceleration of Reinforcement Learning for Reasoning:** The paper highlights that Mamba's efficiency can accelerate RL training, which is often bottlenecked by the generation of long sequences. By enabling faster generation during RL rollouts, M1 (and future Mamba-based models) could lead to more efficient and effective RL training paradigms, allowing models to explore longer reasoning paths during learning and potentially unlock even more sophisticated reasoning abilities with less training time.

5. **Advancement in Cross-Architecture Knowledge Distillation:** The successful demonstration of distilling reasoning capabilities from a Transformer to a Mamba-based model, particularly through a multi-stage process involving reasoning-specific SFT and RL, is a significant technical achievement. It contributes to the growing body of work on cross-architecture knowledge transfer, opening avenues for leveraging the vast knowledge embedded in large Transformer models to bootstrap entirely new, more efficient architectures.

6. **Open-Source Contribution:** The decision to open-source the code and pre-trained checkpoints (github.com/jxiw/M1) is a vital contribution to the community. It provides a concrete and reproducible foundation for other researchers and developers to build upon, experiment with, and integrate into their own projects, accelerating further innovation in efficient reasoning models.

In conclusion, M1 represents a crucial step towards making advanced reasoning capabilities more scalable, affordable, and accessible. It strongly advocates for architectural diversity in LLM development, demonstrating that the future of powerful AI models is not solely tied to ever-larger Transformers but also to the intelligent design and efficient deployment of alternative, specialized architectures like Mamba.
