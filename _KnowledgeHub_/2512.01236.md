# PSR: Scaling Multi-Subject Personalized Image Generation with Pairwise Subject-Consistency Rewards

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Zhejiang University and Huawei Inc. developed a scalable framework for multi-subject personalized image generation, introducing a novel data generation pipeline and a reinforcement learning (RL) strategy with Pairwise Subject-Consistency Rewards (PSR). Their method achieved state-of-the-art performance on a new comprehensive benchmark, PSRBench, demonstrating superior subject consistency, semantic alignment, and aesthetic quality in generating images with multiple specified subjects.

## Problem

- Existing personalized image generation models struggle to maintain subject consistency and adhere to complex textual prompts in multi-subject scenarios.
- A significant lack of high-quality, large-scale multi-subject personalized image datasets hinders the training of robust generative models.
- Current training strategies, like supervised fine-tuning, optimize at a global image level, failing to ensure fine-grained, subject-specific consistency when multiple subjects are present.

## Method

- A scalable ==multi-subject== data generation pipeline was developed, leveraging single-subject personalization models and Large Language Models (LLMs) to synthesize diverse and consistent training data.
- A novel Pairwise Subject-Consistency Reward (PSR) was introduced within a reinforcement learning (RL) framework to explicitly optimize for subject identity preservation in multi-subject outputs.
- A scalable frame-wise positional encoding scheme was devised to extend single-subject models, enabling them to handle varying numbers of input reference images without imposing fixed spatial biases.

## Results

- The 'Ours (PSR)' model achieved state-of-the-art performance on the PSRBench benchmark, with overall scores of **0.673** for subject consistency, 1.124 for aesthetic preference, and **0.783** for semantic alignment.
- The RL stage with PSR led to a substantial improvement in subject consistency (from **0.559** to **0.673** compared to SFT-only) and restored overall aesthetic quality, demonstrating the effectiveness of the targeted reward mechanism.
- A user study showed that images generated by 'Ours (PSR)' were preferred across all metrics (subject consistency, semantic alignment, and aesthetic preference) over outputs from other state-of-the-art methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly calculating consistency rewards at a pairwise subject level during RL fine-tuning significantly improves subject identity preservation in generated images, surpassing global image-level optimization.
> - A data generation pipeline that synthesizes multi-subject data by re-contextualizing single-subject instances can overcome data scarcity and produce high-quality, consistent training examples.
> - Employing a virtual temporal offset for positional encoding, instead of spatial offsets, enables a single-subject model to generalize effectively to multi-subject inputs without introducing unwanted spatial priors.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of image generation has made substantial progress, particularly in personalized image generation, which focuses on producing images that faithfully represent given subjects while adhering to textual instructions. Initially, research in this domain concentrated on single-subject personalization models, such as FLUX.1 Kontext and Qwen-Image-Edit, demonstrating capabilities in generating novel scenes for a single target subject. These early approaches often involved test-time fine-tuning or zero-shot methods, with the latter gaining prominence for its ability to personalize for arbitrary subjects without per-concept model fine-tuning.

More recently, the research landscape has begun to explore multi-subject generation, where models must handle multiple reference images and maintain the consistency of several subjects within a single generated image. Existing efforts in this area, including UNO and OmniGen, have shown initial promise but encounter significant limitations. These limitations primarily manifest as poor subject consistency, where generated subjects may deviate from their references or even be omitted, and limited adherence to complex textual prompts, leading to incorrect attribute assignments or spatial relationships.

The authors attribute these shortcomings to two primary factors: the scarcity of high-quality, large-scale multi-subject personalization datasets and the absence of refined post-training strategies capable of addressing the nuanced challenges of multi-subject scenarios. Current multi-subject datasets often suffer from consistency issues (e.g., X2I-subject-driven focusing on faces, or UNO's T2I-generated pairs lacking inherent consistency). Similarly, most training strategies for multi-subject models are limited to supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which optimizes at a global image level and struggles with fine-grained, subject-specific consistency.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as an effective paradigm for aligning generative models with specific objectives, such as semantic alignment and aesthetic preference, in various visual generation tasks. However, its application to the specific challenges of multi-subject personalized generation, particularly for enhancing subject consistency and complex text adherence, remained an open challenge prior to this work.

This paper positions itself as an effort to bridge these gaps by introducing a scalable data generation pipeline and an RL-based post-training strategy to advance multi-subject personalized image generation, alongside a comprehensive benchmark for its evaluation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind this research stems from the recognized limitations of existing personalized image generation models when extended from single to multiple subjects. While single-subject models have achieved notable effectiveness, their multi-subject counterparts frequently exhibit degraded performance, specifically concerning subject consistency and adherence to textual prompts.

The authors identify two principal causes for these limitations:
1. **Absence of High-Quality Multi-Subject Datasets:** Existing datasets for multi-subject personalization are either limited in scale, lack diversity, or suffer from inconsistent subject appearances. For instance, some datasets are heavily biased towards human faces, while others, constructed using Text-to-Image (T2I) models, inherently contain discrepancies in subject consistency, hindering effective model training.
2. **Lack of Refined Post-Training Strategies:** Current training approaches, predominantly supervised fine-tuning (SFT), optimize at a global image level. This global optimization objective proves insufficient for ensuring fine-grained subject consistency and precise control over individual subjects as dictated by complex textual prompts in multi-subject scenarios.

Based on these identified shortcomings, the research sets forth several key objectives:

- **Develop a Scalable Multi-Subject Data Generation Pipeline:** The primary objective is to create a robust and scalable pipeline capable of synthesizing diverse and high-quality multi-subject training data. This pipeline aims to leverage the strengths of powerful single-subject generation models to overcome the data scarcity issue without introducing consistency artifacts inherent in direct T2I generation. The goal is to generate data with an arbitrary number of subjects.
- **Equip Single-Subject Models with Multi-Subject Knowledge:** The research aims to adapt existing single-subject personalization models to acquire the capability of synthesizing multi-image and multi-subject scenarios effectively. This involves developing an encoding scheme that can generalize to varying numbers of input reference images.
- **Enhance Subject Consistency and Text Controllability:** A critical objective is to design and integrate refined post-training strategies that specifically target and improve both subject consistency across generated images and the model's adherence to intricate textual prompts. This includes addressing issues where attributes are incorrectly assigned or specific scene details are overlooked.
- **Establish a Comprehensive Evaluation Benchmark:** Recognizing the limitations of existing evaluation protocols, the research aims to introduce a new, fine-grained benchmark. This benchmark would allow for a more accurate and comprehensive assessment of model performance across different aspects of multi-subject personalization, including subject consistency, aesthetic quality, and semantic alignment, and across various challenging scenarios.

By addressing these objectives, the authors aim to significantly advance the state of multi-subject personalized image generation, enabling the creation of more consistent, controllable, and high-fidelity multi-subject visual content.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed methodology involves a two-stage training paradigm: an initial supervised fine-tuning (SFT) stage followed by a reinforcement learning (RL) stage incorporating novel reward mechanisms. This is supported by a scalable data generation pipeline and evaluated using a newly introduced benchmark.

#### 3.1. Dataset and Benchmark Construction

The paper introduces a scalable multi-subject data generation pipeline to address the lack of high-quality datasets. It consists of two stages:

- **Stage 1: Multi-subject paired image generation.**
 - An LLM (Qwen3-32B) generates diverse `T_t2i` (Text-to-Image instructions) and `T_sub` (subject-driven instructions) for a sampled set of `n` categories from Objects365.
 - A state-of-the-art T2I model (FLUX.1-schnell, or Qwen-Image for four-subject scenes) synthesizes multi-subject images (`I_out`) based on `T_t2i`.
 - GroundingDINO, an object detection model, detects and crops single-object instances (`I_crop`) from `I_out`.
 - A single-subject personalization model (FLUX.1 Kontext) generates new reference images (`I_ref`) for each subject using `I_crop` and `T_sub`. This process yields approximately 350K multi-subject synthetic image pairs.
- **Stage 2: Multi-subject paired instruction generation.**
 - This stage focuses on creating diverse and high-quality instructions for seven predefined multi-subject personalization tasks: Attribute, Background, Action, Position, Complex, Three, and Four subjects.
 - Recaptioning strategies, utilizing an MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL), are employed to describe the generated images (`I_out`) while specifically using pronouns to refer to subjects and avoiding textual leakage of appearance information.
 - For tasks requiring specific attributes or actions, a "re-editing" step is introduced. LLMs generate task-specific editing instructions, which are then used by an editing model to modify `I_out` into `I*out`, followed by MLLM-based instruction generation.

To address limitations in existing evaluation methods, the paper introduces **PSRBench**, a comprehensive and multi-dimensional benchmark.
- It comprises seven subsets corresponding to the personalization tasks: Attribute, Background, Action, Position, Complex, Three, and Four, each with 50 evaluation samples.
- Evaluation is performed across three dimensions:
 - **Subject Consistency:** GroundingDINO is used to detect and crop subjects in both input references and generated images, followed by DINO scores to measure similarity between corresponding subject pairs.
 - **Semantic Alignment:** An MLLM (Qwen2.5-VL) evaluates image-text alignment based on task-specific prompt templates. For the "Position" subset, grounding-based evaluation compares the relative spatial relations of detected subject centers.
 - **Aesthetic Preference:** HPSv3 is used to evaluate image quality.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture and Training

The multi-subject personalization model is built upon FLUX.1 Kontext and trained using LoRA.

- **Preliminary: Flow Matching and GRPO.** The model leverages Flow Matching for image generation, minimizing an objective related to noise and data distribution. For the RL stage, Flow-GRPO is adopted, which transforms the ODE-based sampling into an SDE form to incorporate stochasticity for policy optimization.

- **Scalable Frame-wise Positional Encoding.** In the initial SFT stage, the goal is to enable a single-image personalization model to handle multi-image, multi-subject scenarios. This is achieved by extending the positional encoding scheme from FLUX.1 Kontext. Instead of spatial offsets along height and width dimensions (which can introduce biases), a *virtual temporal offset* `PO_i = (i, h, w)` is used for the `i`-th input image to distinguish its latent tokens. This avoids imposing strong spatial priors. A multi-image joint training strategy samples varying numbers of input images (2, 3, or 4) with specific probabilities to enhance generalization.

- **Pairwise Subject-Consistency Rewards (PSR).** The SFT stage, operating at a global image level, is augmented by an online RL stage using a novel Pairwise Subject-Consistency Reward (PSR) combined with other rewards.
 - **Subject Decoupling:** An open-vocabulary object detector (GroundingDINO) is applied to both the generated output (`I_out`) and input references (`I_ref`) to extract subject-specific crops (`I_i_dec` and `I_i_gt` respectively).
 - **PSR Definition:** The `R_PSR` is calculated as the average DINO feature similarity between each pair of corresponding subject crops (`f(I_i_dec, I_i_gt)`).
 - **Overall Reward:** To prevent reward hacking and enhance overall quality, `R_PSR` is combined with other rewards:
 - `R_s`: Semantic reward computed by Qwen2.5-VL, evaluating image-text alignment.
 - `R_h`: Aesthetic preference reward from HPSv3.
 - The total reward is `R = w1 * R_PSR + w2 * R_s + w3 * R_h`, with empirically determined weights (w1=0.4, w2=0.4, w3=0.2).
 - The RL stage uses a smaller learning rate and trains over 28 diffusion timesteps, optimizing the model (initialized from SFT) with this multi-reward objective. All images are resized to 512x512 pixels.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented extensive experimental evaluations to demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed PSR approach for multi-subject personalized image generation.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance on PSRBench

- **Subject Consistency (Table 1):** The "Ours (PSR)" model achieved an overall subject consistency score of 0.673, outperforming all baseline models, including OmniGen2 (0.587) and XVerse (0.587). The improvements were particularly notable in challenging scenarios such as the "Complex" (0.713), "Three" (0.615), and "Four" (0.571) subsets, where it significantly surpassed previous best results. This indicated enhanced robustness in handling consistency when multiple subjects and complex interactions are involved. The "Ours-SFT" model, trained only with supervised fine-tuning, showed a lower overall score of 0.559, highlighting the contribution of the RL stage.
- **Aesthetic Preference (Table 2):** "Ours (PSR)" achieved the highest overall aesthetic preference score of 1.124. While "Ours-SFT" initially showed a lower aesthetic score (0.794) compared to FLUX.1 Kontext (0.870), attributed to a resolution mismatch during SFT, the PSR-based reinforcement learning stage effectively mitigated this issue, restoring and improving overall perceptual quality.
- **Semantic Alignment (Table 3):** "Ours (PSR)" demonstrated the best overall semantic alignment score of 0.783, showing strong performance on subsets like "Position" (0.468) and "Four" (0.866), outperforming other state-of-the-art models. This indicated superior text controllability.

#### 4.2. Comparisons with Additional Baselines

- **DreamBench (Table 5):** On DreamBench, "Ours (PSR)" achieved state-of-the-art performance on the DINO metric (0.529), surpassing the second-best method by 0.23, and competitive results on the CLIP-T metric.
- **MS-Diffusion and MOSAIC (Table 6):** Further comparisons against MS-Diffusion and MOSAIC showed that "Ours (PSR)" consistently outperformed these baselines across all metrics (Subject Consistency, Aesthetic Score, and Semantic Alignment) and all PSRBench subsets.

#### 4.3. User Study (Table 7)

A user study involving 100 random test cases and five participants showed that "Ours (PSR)" received the highest average scores for Subject Consistency (0.92), Semantic Alignment (0.80), and Aesthetic Preference (0.82) compared to OmniGen2, XVerse, and Qwen-Image-Edit-2509, indicating a preference for the generated images.

#### 4.4. Qualitative Analysis (Figure 4, 7)

Qualitative results on both PSRBench and DreamBench indicated that the proposed approach exhibited superior performance in subject preservation and text adherence. For instance, the model consistently maintained the original appearance of subjects while accurately following complex semantic instructions, such as specific object attributes or positional constraints. In contrast, other methods often failed to maintain high consistency or even omitted subjects, and sometimes struggled with precise attribute or action assignments. The ability to handle "non-standard" subjects (e.g., an unconventional candle) was also demonstrated.

#### 4.5. Ablation Study (Table 4)

An ablation study on the scalable frame-wise positional encoding revealed its effectiveness. The proposed encoding scheme ("w/ ours") consistently achieved superior semantic alignment scores across 2, 3, and 4-subject settings, with a significant lead on the "Position" subset (0.508 compared to the next best 0.469). This supported the hypothesis that avoiding fixed spatial layouts (present in "w/ h-w", "w/ w", "w/ h" schemes) enables the model to better modify subject positions according to textual prompts without pre-imposed spatial biases.

#### 4.6. Failure Cases (Figure 5-C)

The paper acknowledged a failure case where identity preservation struggled to be maintained for small subjects, indicating an area for future improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of personalized image generation by addressing critical limitations in multi-subject scenarios, thereby holding several significant implications and potential impacts:

- **Advancement in Multi-Subject Personalized Generation:** The work directly tackles the core challenges of poor subject consistency and limited text adherence in multi-subject image generation. By presenting a methodology that achieves superior performance across various metrics and complex scenarios, it represents an advancement in the capability of generative models to handle intricate multi-entity compositions.
- **Scalable Data Generation:** The proposed data generation pipeline, leveraging existing single-subject models and LLMs, offers a scalable solution to the long-standing problem of data scarcity for multi-subject personalization. This pipeline enables the construction of diverse, high-quality datasets with arbitrary numbers of subjects, which is crucial for training robust models. This method could reduce the reliance on costly manual data collection.
- **Enhanced Controllability and Fidelity:** The introduction of Pairwise Subject-Consistency Rewards (PSR) within a multi-reward reinforcement learning framework significantly enhances the model's ability to maintain subject identity while adhering to complex textual instructions, including specific attributes, actions, and spatial relationships. This increased controllability and fidelity lead to more semantically faithful and visually appealing generated images.
- **Comprehensive Evaluation Standard:** The development of PSRBench provides a fine-grained and multi-dimensional benchmark for evaluating multi-subject personalization. This benchmark addresses shortcomings of previous evaluation protocols, offering a more rigorous and accurate assessment of model performance across subject consistency, semantic alignment, and aesthetic preference. It sets a new standard for future research and comparisons in this domain.
- **Robustness to Realistic Inputs:** By performing subject decoupling on reference images that may contain backgrounds, the methodology demonstrates improved robustness in extracting and preserving subject identity from realistic inputs, moving beyond idealized settings.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work establishes a solid foundation for future research in controllable and scalable multi-subject personalized generation. The components of the proposed system—the data generation strategy, the novel positional encoding, and the RL-based fine-tuning with targeted rewards—can serve as building blocks for developing even more advanced and generalized multi-subject generative AI.
- **Potential Applications:** The advancements in generating consistent and controllable multi-subject images have potential implications across various industries. These include film production (e.g., character consistency in virtual sets), personalized marketing, content creation, and interactive design, where the ability to accurately render multiple specific subjects in diverse scenes according to detailed prompts is highly valuable.
