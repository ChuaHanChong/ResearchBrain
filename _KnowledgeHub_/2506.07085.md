# State Entropy Regularization for Robust Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> A study from Technion and MILA Institute formally characterizes State Entropy Regularization's (SER) robustness in reinforcement learning, demonstrating its theoretical equivalence to solving specific reward-robust problems and its empirical effectiveness against spatially correlated perturbations like unexpected obstacles. The research differentiates SER's protection against global state occupancy adversaries from Policy Entropy Regularization's local benefits, while also outlining its limitations for general kernel uncertainty and risk-averse scenarios.

## Problem

- Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies trained in controlled nominal environments often exhibit degraded performance when deployed in real-world settings due to model misspecification or environmental variations.
- Existing regularization techniques, such as policy entropy, primarily induce robustness against local perturbations, but may fail catastrophically when faced with spatially localized or 'global' disruptions like large obstacles.
- There was a lack of formal theoretical understanding regarding the specific robustness properties conferred by state entropy regularization and how it differs from policy entropy in mitigating various types of uncertainties.

## Method

- The research provides a formal proof demonstrating that ==state entropy== regularization exactly solves a specific type of reward-robust RL problem, characterizing the induced Log-Sum-Exp (LSE)-based uncertainty set and the nature of the adversarial reward.
- A non-trivial lower bound on performance under transition (kernel) uncertainty is derived for state entropy regularization, including a comparative analysis with the combined use of state and policy entropy.
- Empirical evaluations are conducted across discrete (MiniGrid) and continuous (Mujoco) control tasks, comparing state entropy regularization against baselines under various spatially correlated and uniform perturbations, as well as reward uncertainty.

## Results

- State entropy regularization significantly improved agent performance in environments with spatially correlated perturbations, such as large obstacles blocking optimal paths in MiniGrid and Mujoco tasks, outperforming unregularized and policy entropy-regularized agents.
- The theoretical analysis confirmed that state entropy regularization is equivalent to solving a reward-robust RL problem, providing protection against adversarial reward perturbations dependent on the global state occupancy distribution.
- Empirical results demonstrated that the robustness benefits of state entropy regularization are sensitive to the number of rollouts used for policy evaluation, especially in continuous state spaces, due to statistical and behavioral factors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State entropy regularization (SER) confers robustness against adversaries that can perturb rewards by exploiting global state visitation patterns, contrasting with policy entropy regularization (PER) which addresses local, state-specific perturbations.
> - The regularization strength parameter (temperature α) in SER tunes the conservativeness of the induced reward uncertainty set, transitioning from an ℓ∞\ell_\inftyℓ∞-like constraint for small α to an ℓ1-like constraint for large α.
> - Entropy regularization, including SER, cannot solve all general kernel-robust RL problems and can arbitrarily degrade performance in risk-averse settings (e.g., measured by CVaR) because it does not inherently prioritize low-variance trajectories.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Reinforcement Learning (RL) has achieved notable successes in controlled synthetic environments. However, its widespread deployment in real-world scenarios continues to face challenges stemming from model misspecification, noisy observations, and limited data. Policies trained in a nominal environment may exhibit degraded performance when confronted with deviations in dynamics or reward structures during deployment. This issue has catalyzed the field of robust RL, which aims to ensure reliable policy performance despite uncertainties or variations in the environment's parameters.

A common approach in machine learning, the connection between regularization and robustness, has been extended to RL. Regularization techniques have been demonstrated to induce robustness against adversarial perturbations to reward functions and transition kernels. Policy entropy regularization, in particular, has been extensively studied in this context, establishing a link between encouraging stochasticity in action selection and robustness to certain types of perturbations.

More recent research has explored the regularization of the RL objective with the entropy of the state distribution induced by a policy, sometimes in conjunction with policy entropy. Empirical evidence suggests that state entropy regularization can lead to improved exploration and sample efficiency. However, a formal theoretical understanding of the effects of state entropy regularization, particularly its relationship with robustness, has been largely absent from the literature. This paper addresses this gap by providing a comprehensive theoretical and empirical characterization of the robustness properties conferred by state entropy regularization, distinguishing it from the more established policy entropy regularization and elucidating the specific types of uncertainties it can mitigate.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to formally investigate the theoretical guarantees of state entropy regularization in the context of robust reinforcement learning. Specifically, the authors aim to determine whether state entropy regularization, similar to policy entropy, acts as a robustness mechanism, and if so, to characterize the specific types of robustness it provides.

The motivation for this investigation stems from an observed difference in how policy entropy and state entropy intuitively influence agent behavior. Policy entropy encourages stochasticity in action choices at each state, often leading to variations along a single dominant trajectory. This characteristic makes it effective for smoothing out small, localized, or uniform perturbations. However, if the primary trajectory is severely disrupted—for instance, by a large obstacle—policies regularized with policy entropy may exhibit catastrophic failure due to their limited divergence from the original path.

In contrast, state entropy regularization aims to increase the coverage and uniformity of state visitation across the entire state space. This approach encourages the agent to discover and utilize multiple distinct high-reward paths. The underlying hypothesis is that by fostering a broader distribution of state occupancies, state entropy regularization could confer stronger robustness, especially against spatially localized or "catastrophic" perturbations that might block a single optimal path. This distinction suggests that policy entropy might protect against a locally informed adversary, while state entropy could offer robustness against globally informed perturbations that exploit spatial correlations.

The specific contributions of the paper, detailing how these objectives are addressed, are summarized as follows:

1. **Theoretical Analysis of Reward-Robustness**: The work provides a formal proof that state entropy regularization exactly solves a specific type of reward-robust RL problem. It characterizes the induced uncertainty set over reward functions and describes the nature of the adversarial reward. The analysis contrasts this with policy entropy, highlighting that policy entropy provides protection against local adversaries, while state entropy guards against adversaries exploiting global state visitation patterns. The paper further examines how the regularization strength, represented by the temperature parameter $\alpha$, influences the conservativeness of the induced uncertainty set, showing an interpolation between $\ell_\infty$-like uncertainty in the low-$\alpha$ limit and $\ell_1$-like uncertainty as $\alpha$ increases.
2. **Theoretical Analysis of Kernel Uncertainty**: The paper derives a non-trivial lower bound on performance under transition (kernel) uncertainty when using state entropy regularization. A comparative analysis reveals that the addition of policy entropy regularization weakens this lower bound, suggesting a structural advantage for using state entropy alone in this specific analytical context.
3. **Limitations of Entropy Regularization**: The research investigates inherent limitations. It establishes an impossibility result, demonstrating that entropy regularization (whether policy, state, or both) cannot solve all general kernel-robust RL problems. Building on this, it shows that entropy regularization can arbitrarily degrade performance in risk-averse settings (quantified by Conditional Value at Risk - CVaR) because it does not prioritize low-variance trajectories.
4. **Practical Considerations**: The paper examines the practical sensitivity of state entropy regularization's robustness benefits. It demonstrates that these benefits are more susceptible to the number of rollouts used for policy evaluation compared to other regularization forms, suggesting a statistical and behavioral component to this sensitivity.
5. **Empirical Validation**: The theoretical findings are supported by empirical evaluations across both discrete (MiniGrid) and continuous (Mujoco) control tasks. The experiments illustrate that state entropy regularization improves performance under spatially correlated perturbations, such as obstacle placement, without significantly degrading performance under smaller, more uniform perturbations. The empirical studies also confirm the dependence of robustness benefits on the rollout budget.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a combination of theoretical analysis and empirical experimentation to characterize the properties of state entropy regularization.

**Theoretical Framework**:
The paper frames its analysis within the standard Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework, defined by a tuple $(S, A, P, r, \gamma)$, where $S$ is the state space, $A$ is the action space, $P$ is the transition kernel, $r$ is the reward function, and $\gamma$ is the discount factor. Robust RL problems are defined as minimizing the expected return over an uncertainty set $\mathcal{P} \times \mathcal{R}$ of possible transition kernels and reward functions.

The study focuses on three types of entropy regularization objectives:
- **Policy Entropy Regularization**: $J_\alpha(\pi, H_A) = \mathbb{E}_{\rho^\pi_P}[r(s,a) + \alpha H_A(\pi(\cdot|s))]$
- **State Entropy Regularization**: $J_\alpha(\pi, H_S) = \mathbb{E}_{\rho^\pi_P}[r(s,a)] + \alpha H_S(d^\pi_P)$
- **State-Action Entropy Regularization**: $J_\alpha(\pi, H_{S \times A}) = \mathbb{E}_{\rho^\pi_P}[r(s,a)] + \alpha H_{S \times A}(\rho^\pi_P)$

where $d^\pi_P$ is the discounted state distribution and $\rho^\pi_P$ is the state-action occupancy distribution. The paper primarily analyzes the latter two forms, with a focus on state entropy.

**Reward-Robustness Analysis (Section 3.1)**:
The authors prove a duality between state entropy regularization and a reward-robust RL problem. This is achieved by reformulating the robust objective (minimizing expected reward over an uncertainty set) and using a convex conjugate argument. Specifically, the entropy regularization term is interpreted as introducing a soft constraint on the difference between the nominal and perturbed reward functions. The Lagrangian is formulated, and KKT conditions are applied to find the worst-case reward function. This analysis explicitly characterizes the LSE-based uncertainty set $\tilde{\mathcal{R}}^\pi(\epsilon, \alpha)$ and the form of the adversarial reward, which depends on the state occupancy distribution $d^\pi_P$. This is contrasted with policy entropy regularization, whose adversarial reward depends only on the local policy $\pi(\cdot|s)$.

To understand the effect of the regularization strength $\alpha$, the paper analyzes the limiting behavior of the induced uncertainty sets as $\alpha \to 0$ and $\alpha \to \infty$. This involves taking limits of the LSE function, demonstrating an interpolation between $\ell_\infty$-like (for small $\alpha$) and $\ell_1$-like (for large $\alpha$) constraints on the adversarial reward.

**Lower Bound under Kernel Uncertainty (Section 3.2)**:
For transition (kernel) uncertainty, the methodology involves transforming the problem of minimizing return under a perturbed kernel $\tilde{P}$ into a problem of minimizing return under an adversarial reward. This transformation uses importance sampling and a logarithmic transformation of the reward function. By leveraging the previously established duality for reward-robustness, a weak duality (lower bound) is derived for worst-case performance under kernel uncertainty. The induced uncertainty set is expressed in terms of the ratio of state distributions $d^\pi_P(s) / d^\pi_{\tilde{P}}(s)$. The paper then compares this bound to one obtained when both state and policy entropy regularization are applied, demonstrating that the latter leads to a strictly looser bound due to the properties of chain rule for entropy.

**Limitations of State Entropy Regularization (Section 4)**:
- **Impossibility Result for General Kernel Robustness (Section 4.1)**: A theoretical argument based on a symmetrization principle is used. By considering an MDP with a constant reward function, it is shown that any regularization function relying solely on the nominal kernel and policy must be zero if it is to characterize a kernel robust return. This leads to the conclusion that entropy regularization, regardless of type, cannot generally solve arbitrary kernel robust RL problems without incorporating reward information.
- **Degradation of Risk-Averse Performance (Section 4.2)**: The authors utilize the Conditional Value at Risk (CVaR) as a measure of risk-averse performance. A counterexample MDP (Figure 7) is constructed where, despite entropy regularization, the policy's CVaR can be arbitrarily worse than an unregularized optimal policy. The intuition is that entropy regularization does not inherently prioritize low-variance trajectories, which are critical for good risk-averse performance.
- **Sample Sensitivity (Section 4.3)**: Two explanations are provided:
 - **Statistical**: For continuous state spaces, k-nearest neighbor (k-NN) estimators for differential entropy (commonly used) have a convergence rate of $O(n^{-1/D})$, which is slower than the $O(n^{-1/2})$ rate for expected return or policy entropy. This implies that state entropy estimation is statistically more demanding in high-dimensional settings.
 - **Behavioral**: In settings with a low number of rollouts per entropy estimate, maximizing empirical state entropy can lead to deterministic policies that favor long, single trajectories to visit more states, rather than encouraging diverse, stochastic behavior across multiple paths, thereby undermining robustness. A toy MDP (Figure 3) illustrates this phenomenon.

**Empirical Evaluation (Section 5)**:
- **Environments**: Experiments are conducted on discrete MiniGrid environments and continuous Mujoco environments (Pusher, Ant).
- **Algorithms**: A2C is used for MiniGrid, and PPO for Mujoco experiments. State entropy regularization is implemented by adding an intrinsic reward term based on a k-NN entropy estimator, following prior work. Policy entropy regularization is also used in conjunction with state entropy for training stability, as is common practice.
- **Perturbation Types**:
 - **Spatially Correlated Transition Perturbations**: Large walls or obstacles are introduced to block optimal paths in MiniGrid, Pusher, and Ant.
 - **Uniformly Distributed Small-Scale Perturbations**: Randomly scattered obstacle tiles in MiniGrid.
 - **Reward Uncertainty**: Goal locations are randomized at evaluation time in MiniGrid and Pusher, resembling $\ell_1$-bounded reward uncertainty.
- **Baselines**: Policies trained without regularization, with policy entropy regularization, and, for some tasks, with established robust RL methods like PRMDP and NRMDP.
- **Ablation Studies**: The number of rollouts used for state entropy estimation and the regularization temperature $\alpha$ are varied to analyze their impact on robustness.
- **Metrics**: Success rate and average return are used to evaluate performance in nominal and perturbed environments. Confidence bounds (96%) are reported across multiple seeds.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides a detailed characterization of state entropy regularization, both theoretically and empirically:

**Theoretical Findings**:
1. **Reward-Robustness Duality (Theorem 3.1)**: State entropy regularization is theoretically equivalent to solving a reward-robust RL problem. This implies protection against an adversary that can perturb rewards to minimize performance within an uncertainty set defined by a Log-Sum-Exp (LSE) function. The paper elucidates that the adversarial reward for state entropy regularization depends on the global state occupancy distribution $d^\pi_P(s)$, enabling robustness against spatially structured perturbations. This contrasts with policy entropy regularization, which protects against adversaries with local, state-specific knowledge (dependent on $\pi(\cdot|s)$).
2. **Regularization Strength and Uncertainty Set (Theorem 3.2)**: The regularization strength $\alpha$ monotonically controls the conservativeness of the induced reward uncertainty set. As $\alpha \to 0$, the uncertainty set approaches an $\ell_\infty$-like constraint (max difference in reward is bounded). As $\alpha \to \infty$, it approaches an $\ell_1$-like constraint (average difference in reward is bounded). This demonstrates a tunable aspect of state entropy's robustness profile.
3. **Kernel Uncertainty Lower Bound (Theorem 3.3)**: State entropy regularization yields a non-trivial lower bound on performance under transition kernel uncertainty. The uncertainty set in this context is expressed in terms of the ratio of state distributions $d^\pi_P(s) / d^\pi_{\tilde{P}}(s)$. A notable finding is that the combined use of state and policy entropy regularization results in a strictly looser lower bound compared to using state entropy alone, suggesting a structural advantage for state entropy in this specific analytical setting.
4. **Limitations on General Kernel Robustness (Theorem 4.1)**: Entropy regularization, in any form (policy, state, or both), cannot fully solve general kernel-robust RL problems. The theoretical result shows that any regularizer based solely on the nominal kernel and policy, without dependency on the reward function, cannot universally characterize the robust return against kernel uncertainty.
5. **Degradation of Risk-Averse Performance (Theorem 4.2)**: Entropy regularization can arbitrarily degrade performance in risk-averse settings (e.g., as measured by CVaR). This is because entropy maximization does not inherently prioritize trajectories with low variance, which are crucial for minimizing downside risk.

**Empirical Findings**:
1. **Robustness to Spatially Correlated Perturbations**:
 - In MiniGrid environments, when large walls block optimal paths, state entropy regularization significantly outperforms both unregularized and policy entropy-regularized agents (Figure 4b).
 - In Mujoco's Pusher and Ant environments, introducing obstacles that fully block optimal trajectories demonstrates that state entropy regularization leads to substantially more robust performance, allowing agents to bypass these "local catastrophes" (Figures 5a, 6c). It also outperforms PRMDP and NRMDP baselines, which are primarily designed for action-based adversarial perturbations.
2. **Robustness to Uniform Perturbations**: When MiniGrid environments are perturbed with uniformly scattered small obstacles, both policy and state entropy regularization improve robustness compared to the unregularized baseline. Policy entropy exhibits a slight advantage in this setting, but state entropy regularization does not degrade performance (Figure 4c).
3. **Reward Robustness**: In tasks where the goal location is randomized (MiniGrid and Pusher), state entropy regularization yields more substantial gains in performance than policy entropy regularization. This effect is particularly pronounced when the new goal is far from the nominal location, highlighting state entropy's benefit in encouraging broader state space coverage (Figures 4d, 5b).
4. **Sensitivity to Rollout Budget**: The robustness benefits of state entropy regularization are sensitive to the number of rollouts used for policy evaluation. With a small number of rollouts, state entropy regularization can lead to worse performance than policy entropy, possibly due to encouraging deterministic behavior rather than diverse stochasticity (Figure 5b). Nominal performance, however, remains largely unaffected by the rollout budget.
5. **Influence of Regularization Temperature**: Empirical evaluation shows that increasing the state entropy regularization coefficient improves robust performance up to a plateau, aligning with the theoretical characterization of expanding uncertainty sets (Figure 5c).
6. **Interplay with Policy Entropy**: While policy entropy is used to stabilize training, experiments suggest that state entropy is the primary driver of the observed robustness. A policy initially trained with both regularizations can maintain its robust performance even after continued training with only state entropy, despite a decrease in policy entropy (Appendix B.5).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research provides a foundational theoretical and empirical analysis of state entropy regularization, establishing its role as a mechanism for robustness in reinforcement learning. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

**Theoretical Advancements**:
- **Filling a Theoretical Gap**: The paper provides the first formal theoretical characterization of state entropy regularization's robustness properties, addressing an existing gap in the literature where its benefits were primarily empirical. This foundational understanding is crucial for the principled development and application of entropy-based regularization techniques.
- **Distinguishing Robustness Types**: It formally distinguishes the types of robustness conferred by state entropy versus policy entropy regularization. By demonstrating that state entropy protects against adversaries that exploit global state occupancy, while policy entropy addresses local variations, the research offers a more nuanced understanding of how different regularization forms induce specific robustness guarantees. This distinction can guide researchers and practitioners in selecting appropriate regularization strategies based on the expected nature of environmental uncertainties.
- **Characterizing Uncertainty Sets**: The detailed characterization of the reward uncertainty sets induced by state entropy regularization, including their interpolation between $\ell_\infty$-like and $\ell_1$-like constraints, provides a concrete mathematical framework for understanding and controlling the conservativeness of robust policies.

**Practical Implications**:
- **Addressing Spatially Correlated Perturbations**: The findings highlight state entropy regularization as a valuable tool for enhancing robustness to spatially correlated or "catastrophic" perturbations, such as unexpected obstacles or blocked paths. These types of variations are common in real-world scenarios, including transfer learning, robot manipulation, and autonomous navigation, where changes can significantly alter optimal trajectories. Traditional robust RL methods often focus on bounded, local perturbations, making state entropy a complementary approach for a distinct class of challenges.
- **Guidance for Algorithm Design**: The analysis of limitations (e.g., general kernel robustness, risk aversion) and practical sensitivities (e.g., rollout budget) provides critical insights for algorithm designers. It emphasizes that state entropy regularization is not a universal solution but a tool with specific strengths and weaknesses. This guidance can inform the development of hybrid approaches that combine state entropy with other techniques to achieve broader robustness.
- **Improved Exploration and Generalization**: By encouraging broader state space coverage, state entropy regularization contributes to more diverse policy behavior, which can lead to better exploration and, consequently, policies that generalize better to perturbed environments. This is particularly relevant in sparse reward settings or environments with many suboptimal paths.

**Future Research Directions**:
- The identified sensitivity to rollout budget for state entropy estimation suggests a need for more sample-efficient and robust entropy estimators, especially in high-dimensional continuous spaces.
- Further investigation into how state entropy regularization interacts with training stability, potentially reducing reliance on policy entropy for this purpose, could lead to more streamlined algorithms.
- Developing methods that integrate the benefits of state entropy (for spatially correlated robustness) with solutions for general kernel robustness or risk-averse performance could lead to more comprehensive robust RL frameworks.
- Exploring the tightness of the derived kernel uncertainty lower bound could lead to a deeper theoretical understanding.

In conclusion, this work significantly advances the theoretical understanding of state entropy regularization in robust RL, offering both formal guarantees and empirical evidence for its effectiveness against specific types of environmental variations. While acknowledging its limitations, the research positions state entropy regularization as an important component in the toolkit for developing more robust and reliable RL agents for real-world applications.
