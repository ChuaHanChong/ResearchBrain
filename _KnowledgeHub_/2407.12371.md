# HIMO: A New Benchmark for Full-Body Human Interacting with Multiple Objects

> [!summary] Summary
> HIMO is a 3.3K-sequence, 4.08M-frame MoCap dataset of full-body humans manipulating multiple household objects, paired with fine-grained temporal-segment text annotations. Alongside it, a dual-branch conditional diffusion model with a mutual-interaction module generates coordinated human-object motion for both whole-prompt and segmented timeline-controlled synthesis.

## Problem

- Existing 4D human-object interaction datasets like GRAB, BEHAVE, InterCap and ARCTIC only capture interaction with a single object, ignoring common multi-object manipulation.
- Prior text-driven HOI datasets provide one flat caption per sequence, lacking temporal segmentation that would allow fine-grained timeline control of composite actions.
- The KIT Whole-body dataset covers multi-object manipulation but represents the body as a humanoid rather than a realistic SMPL-X model with finger detail.

## Method

- Hybrid MoCap capture combining an Optitrack optical system with inertial PNS gloves to record 41-marker body motion and dexterous finger poses despite severe occlusion.
- **HIMO-Gen**: a ==dual-branch conditional diffusion model== with a ==mutual-interaction module== of stacked ==cross-attention Transformer== blocks fusing human and object motion features.
- ==Object-pairwise distance loss== constraining relative inter-object geometry, combined with an ==auto-regressive== **HIMO-SegGen** pipeline conditioning each new clip on prior frames.

## Results

- HIMO-Gen beats MDM, priorMDM and IMoS on all metrics for 2-object sequences, e.g. FID **1.48** versus MDM's **6.85** and IMoS's **7.59**.
- On 3-object sequences HIMO-Gen improves R-Precision to **0.535** and MM-Dist to **5.09**, both best among baselines though behind real motion (**0.699**).
- Ablations show removing the mutual-interaction module drops R-Precision from **0.637** to **0.471**, and conditioning HIMO-SegGen on **10** past frames outperforms 1, 5, or 20 frames.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Cross-branch mutual attention between human and object streams is critical for coordinated multi-object motion, not just larger model capacity.
> - Explicit relative-distance constraints between interacting objects transfer as a useful inductive bias whenever multiple rigid bodies must move consistently.
> - Temporal segmentation of long interaction sequences enables generalization to novel action compositions unseen during training, beyond the exact demonstrated sequences.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The generation of human-object interactions (HOIs) is a fundamental capability for advancements in digital avatars, video games, augmented/virtual reality (AR/VR), robotics, and embodied artificial intelligence. Existing research and datasets in this domain have primarily focused on scenarios where humans interact with a single object. Datasets such as GRAB, BEHAVE, ARCTIC, and CHAIRS have contributed to modeling 4D full-body or hand-object interactions, sometimes with associated textual descriptions.

However, the manipulation of multiple objects is a ubiquitous aspect of daily human activities and represents a more complex and practical scenario for real-world applications. The scarcity of datasets that capture full-body human interaction with multiple objects, particularly with detailed textual descriptions and temporal segmentation, has limited the development of synthesis models for such complex interactions. While some datasets like the KIT Whole-body dataset involve multiple objects, they often use simplified humanoid models instead of more realistic representations like SMPL-X. Capturing accurate 4D HOIs involving multiple objects is technically challenging due to factors such as subtle finger movements, severe occlusions, and the precise tracking of various objects in dynamic spatial and temporal relationships.

This work addresses this identified gap by introducing HIMO, a large-scale motion capture (MoCap) dataset specifically designed for full-body humans interacting with multiple objects. By providing fine-grained textual descriptions and temporal segmentation, HIMO aims to serve as a benchmark for synthesizing more complex, multi-step, and multi-object human interactions, thereby expanding the capabilities of HOI generation beyond single-object limitations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The motivation behind developing HIMO stems from several factors:
- **Real-world Complexity**: Daily human activities frequently involve the manipulation of multiple objects in sequence or in combination. Examples include pouring tea from a teapot into a teacup or cutting an apple on a knifeboard with a knife. Existing datasets primarily focusing on single-object interactions fall short of representing this real-world complexity.
- **Application Requirements**: Advanced applications in robotics, AR/VR, and embodied AI require models capable of generating and understanding multi-object interactions for more realistic simulations and intelligent agent behaviors. The limitations of current datasets hinder progress in these areas.
- **Lack of Benchmarking Data**: The absence of a large-scale, high-quality dataset featuring full-body human interactions with multiple objects, combined with detailed annotations, has impeded the development and evaluation of advanced HOI synthesis models.
- **Fine-grained Control**: For complex, multi-step interactions, a simple, single-text prompt is often insufficient. The ability to control the timeline of interactions through segmented descriptions is essential for synthesizing flexible and composable HOI sequences.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of this research are:
1. **To collect and curate a large-scale 4D MoCap dataset**, named HIMO, capturing full-body human interactions with multiple daily objects, ensuring high precision and diversity in human and object motions.
2. **To provide detailed textual annotations** for each HOI sequence, describing the interaction order, patterns, and involved body parts, along with **temporal segmentation labels** that decompose long sequences into atomic interaction steps.
3. **To establish two novel benchmarking tasks**:
 - **HIMO-Gen**: Text-driven HOI synthesis where the model generates synchronized human and object motions conditioned on a single, comprehensive text prompt and initial states.
 - **HIMO-SegGen**: Text-driven HOI synthesis with fine-grained timeline control, where the generation is conditioned on a series of segmented text prompts.
4. **To propose a methodology** capable of addressing these novel tasks, including a dual-branch conditional diffusion model with a mutual interaction module for coordinated HOI synthesis and an auto-regressive generation pipeline for smooth transitions between segmented HOI clips.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research presents a comprehensive methodology covering data acquisition, post-processing, and the development of a novel synthesis framework.

#### 3.1. The HIMO Dataset

- **Hybrid Capture System**:
 - An **optical MoCap system (Optitrack)** with 20 PrimeX-22 infrared cameras captured full-body movements at 120fps. Subjects wore MoCap suits with 41 reflective markers. This system was chosen for its precision over multi-view RGB camera systems.
 - **Inertial gloves (Noitom Perception Neuron Studio - PNS)** were employed to capture dexterous finger movements, addressing severe occlusions common in hand-object interactions. Regular re-calibration maintained data quality.
 - **Spatial alignment** was achieved by integrating wrist rotation data from locating boards on hands with body movements.
 - **Temporal synchronization** of Optitrack and PNS gloves was ensured using a Tentacle Sync device.
 - A Kinect camera was used to record front-view RGB videos (1080x768), although the primary focus was on motion data.
- **Capturing Objects**:
 - 53 common household objects from ContactDB and Sketchfab were selected, 3D printed, and scaled.
 - 3-6 reflective markers (12.5 mm diameter) were securely attached to each object to track its rigid body motion.
 - A post-calibration process compensated for the bias between the tracked marker centroid and the actual object centroid.
- **Recording Process**:
 - 34 subjects participated, performing predefined tasks involving combinations of 2 or 3 objects.
 - Objects were initially placed on a 74 cm high table, and subjects started in a rest pose.
 - Each interaction task was performed three times with variations, including casual operations to enhance sequence complexity.
 - The collection yielded 3,376 4D HOI sequences, totaling 9.44 hours and 4.08 million 3D HOI frames (2,486 sequences with 2 objects, 890 with 3 objects).

#### 3.2. Data Postprocessing

- **SMPL-X Parameter Fitting**: The human body and finger articulations were represented using the expressive SMPL-X parametric model. An optimization algorithm fit the SMPL-X parameters (global orient, body pose, finger poses, root translation, shape) to the MoCap data. The objective function included a joint position loss (E_j), a motion smoothing term (E_s), and a regularization term (E_r), with empirically set weights.
- **Textual Annotations**: Fine-grained textual descriptions were manually annotated for each motion sequence. Annotators detailed the interaction procedure, including the manipulation order ("First..., then..., finally..."), interaction patterns ("picking up," "rotating," "pouring"), and involved body parts (e.g., "left hand," "right hand"). An annotation tool facilitated this process.
- **Temporal Segment Annotations**: Long HOI sequences were temporally segmented, and corresponding texts were semantically aligned. This allowed for decomposing complex operations into simpler atomic HOI steps, with each sequence containing an average of 2.60 segments.

#### 3.3. Text-driven HOI Synthesis Framework

The paper introduces two novel tasks, HIMO-Gen and HIMO-SegGen, and proposes a framework to address them.

- **Data Representation**:
 - **Human Motion (H)**: Represented by SMPL-X parameters: global joint positions (P ∈ R⁵²ˣ³), global joint rotations (Q ∈ R⁵²ˣ⁶ in 6D continuous format), and translation (t ∈ R³).
 - **Object Motions (O)**: Relative rotation (R ∈ R⁶) and global translation (T ∈ R³).
 - **Object Geometries (G)**: Encoded using a Basis Point Set (BPS) representation, sampling 1,024 points from object mesh surfaces.
- **HIMO-Gen Framework (Whole Text Prompt Synthesis)**:
 - **Dual-branch Conditional Diffusion Model**: (Figure 4)
 - Comprises two parallel diffusion-based branches for human and object motion generation.
 - **Conditions**: Textual description (L), initial human motion (H₀), initial object motions (O₀), and object geometries (G).
 - **Text Encoding**: CLIP encoder followed by a linear layer provides semantic embeddings, which are concatenated with timestep features extracted by an MLP.
 - **Human Branch**: Noised human motion (H_t) concatenated with a masked (zero-padded) representation of the initial human state (H₀).
 - **Object Branch**: Noised object motion concatenated with masked initial object poses and embedded object geometry (G).
 - These representations are fed into linear layers to obtain motion embeddings, then passed through a mutual interaction module.
 - **Mutual Interaction Module**: (Figure 4c)
 - A stack of 8 Transformer blocks, each with 4 attention heads, designed to fuse features between the human and object branches.
 - Each Transformer block contains a self-attention layer and a mutual attention layer. The mutual attention layer allows queries from one branch to attend to key-value pairs from the other branch, facilitating information exchange and ensuring coordination between human and object movements.
 - **Losses Formulation**:
 - **Joint Position Loss (L_pos)**: L2 loss on human joint positions.
 - **Joint Velocity Loss (L_vel)**: L2 loss on human joint velocities.
 - **Interpenetration Loss (L_pen)**: A signed distance field (SDF)-based loss to prevent collisions between the human body and objects.
 - **Object-Pairwise Loss (L_dis)**: A novel L2 loss designed to maintain consistency between the generated and ground-truth relative distances between interacting objects throughout the sequence. This loss helps generate plausible object movements.
 - The overall optimization objective is a weighted sum of these losses: L = λ_vel L_vel + λ_pos L_pos + λ_pen L_pen + λ_dis L_dis (weights: λ_vel = λ_pos = λ_pen = 1, λ_dis = 0.1).
- **HIMO-SegGen Framework (Segmented Text Prompts Synthesis)**:
 - **Problem Formulation**: Generates HOI sequences conditioned on a series of atomic text prompts (L = {l_1, l_2..., l_m}), with a focus on smooth transitions.
 - **Auto-regressive Generation Pipeline**: (Figure 5)
 - The HIMO-Gen model is trained on segmented motion-text pairs.
 - The model is modified to condition the generation on the last few frames of a previously generated motion clip.
 - During inference, the model iteratively generates subsequent HOI clips by taking the last frames of the current clip as a condition for the next, aiming to create a smooth, composite HOI sequence.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted demonstrate the capabilities of the HIMO dataset and the proposed synthesis framework.

**Quantitative Comparisons (Tables 2 and 3):**
- **HIMO-Gen (2-objects)**: The proposed HIMO-Gen framework consistently outperformed baseline methods (IMoS, MDM, priorMDM) across most metrics on the 2-object partition. Specifically, it achieved higher R-Precision (0.6369 vs. 0.5013-0.6052 for baselines), significantly lower FID (1.4811 vs. 6.8457-7.8517), and lower MM-Dist (3.6491 vs. 7.2509-8.7402), indicating superior generation quality and relevance to text prompts. Diversity was comparable to the best baseline.
- **HIMO-Gen (3-objects)**: For the more complex 3-object partition, HIMO-Gen continued to show improvements, with higher R-Precision (0.5350 vs. 0.4662-0.5137) and lower MM-Dist (5.0866 vs. 5.8900-7.7702) compared to baselines. FID was competitive but not distinctly superior (4.7712 vs. 4.5713-4.9902).
- **HIMO-SegGen (2-objects)**: HIMO-SegGen demonstrated slightly higher R-Precision (0.6404), better MM-Dist (3.6077), and higher MModality (2.0495) compared to HIMO-Gen, suggesting improved fine-grained control and variability in generated motions, though with a higher FID (4.2004).

**Qualitative Results (Figures 6, F, G):**
- Visualizations illustrate that the HIMO-Gen framework can synthesize plausible and realistic human-object interaction sequences for both 2-object and 3-object scenarios.
- HIMO-SegGen successfully generates composite HOI sequences with smooth transitions between segments, guided by consecutive text prompts.
- Qualitative comparisons against baselines indicate that the proposed methods maintain greater consistency and semantic accuracy in the synthesized HOIs.

**Generalization Experiments (Figures B and C):**
- **Unseen Object Geometries**: The trained model demonstrated an ability to generalize to object meshes of the same category but with different geometries, producing plausible interactions, albeit with some minor imperfections in human-object contact.
- **Novel HOI Compositions**: The HIMO-SegGen model showed capability in generating novel HOI compositions by combining segmented text prompts that were not explicitly present in the training set (e.g., "Knocks on the beer with a hammer" followed by "Grabs the beer to observe"), highlighting the flexibility offered by temporal segmentation.

**Ablation Studies (Tables 4 and 5):**
- **HIMO-Gen Ablations**:
 - Removing the **mutual interaction module (w/o IM)** led to a significant drop in R-Precision (0.4710 vs. 0.6369) and higher FID/MM-Dist, confirming its importance for feature fusion and coordination between human and object motions.
 - Removing the **object-pairwise loss (w/o L_dis)** resulted in notably higher FID (2.8694 vs. 1.4811) and MM-Dist (4.9342 vs. 3.6491), indicating its role in constraining plausible object movements and improving realism.
 - A **consecutive generation scheme** (generating human motion conditioned on generated object motion) led to a substantial performance degradation across all metrics, supporting the benefit of simultaneous, mutually interacting generation.
- **HIMO-SegGen Ablations (Conditioned Frames)**:
 - Experimentation with the number of past frames used for conditioning revealed that **10 frames** yielded the best performance in R-precision, FID, and MM-Dist. Conditioning on more frames (e.g., 20 frames) did not provide additional performance gains and sometimes led to a decline, potentially due to reduced focus on relevant past information.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HIMO dataset and the associated research make several contributions to the field of human-object interaction synthesis:

- **Novel Dataset**: HIMO is presented as a large-scale, high-quality 4D MoCap dataset for full-body human interaction with *multiple* objects, directly addressing a gap in existing HOI datasets that primarily focus on single-object interactions. This dataset is designed to support more complex, realistic interaction scenarios.
- **Enhanced Annotation Scheme**: The inclusion of fine-grained textual descriptions and temporal segmentations for HOI sequences is a key innovation. This allows for unprecedented levels of control in motion generation, enabling timeline-based manipulation and the composition of complex, multi-step interactions.
- **New Benchmarking Tasks**: The introduction of HIMO-Gen (text-driven HOI synthesis) and HIMO-SegGen (segmented text-driven HOI synthesis for timeline control) provides standard tasks for evaluating models capable of handling multi-object interactions with varying degrees of granularity.
- **Effective Methodology**: The proposed dual-branch conditional diffusion model, integrated with a mutual interaction module and a novel object-pairwise loss, offers a robust approach for synthesizing coordinated and plausible human-multiple-object interactions. The auto-regressive pipeline for HIMO-SegGen demonstrates an effective strategy for generating smooth, composable HOI sequences.
- **Demonstrated Capabilities**: The experimental results confirm the framework's ability to synthesize realistic and coordinated HOIs, generalize to variations in object geometries, and generate novel compositions of actions, showcasing the potential of the dataset and model.

**Potential Impact**:
The HIMO dataset and framework could have a significant impact on several areas:
- **Digital Avatars and Virtual Characters**: Facilitating the creation of more lifelike and interactively capable digital humans in virtual environments.
- **AR/VR Applications**: Enabling more immersive and intuitive user experiences where virtual objects interact realistically with human users.
- **Robotics**: Providing valuable data and models for training robots to perform complex manipulation tasks involving multiple tools or objects, enhancing their learning from demonstration and planning capabilities.
- **Embodied AI**: Advancing research into intelligent agents that can understand, predict, and generate complex interactions within multi-object environments.
- **Future Research**: By making the data, code, and models publicly available, HIMO is positioned to serve as a foundational benchmark, stimulating further research into sophisticated HOI modeling, control, and generation.

**Limitations (as acknowledged by the authors)**:
- The HIMO dataset primarily focuses on small household objects, lacking interactions with larger objects like chairs or suitcases.
- While RGB videos are captured, the appearances of humans in MoCap suits and objects with reflective markers are not natural, limiting its direct applicability for tasks requiring realistic visual appearance.
- The dataset and model development did not prioritize facial expressions, focusing instead on body movements, finger gestures, and object motions.
