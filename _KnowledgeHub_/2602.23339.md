# Retrieve and Segment: Are a Few Examples Enough to Bridge the Supervision Gap in Open-Vocabulary Segmentation?

> [!summary] Summary
> Aravanis et al. introduce a retrieval-augmented test-time adapter for open-vocabulary segmentation that uses a few pixel-annotated visual examples to enhance textual class descriptions, achieving up to 18.4% mIoU improvement over zero-shot baselines and significantly narrowing the performance gap to fully supervised methods.

## Problem

- Traditional semantic segmentation models are limited by the high cost of dense pixel-level annotation and their inability to generalize to novel categories.
- Open-vocabulary segmentation (OVS) models, while leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs), suffer from a supervision mismatch due to VLMs' coarse image-level training.
- The semantic ambiguity of natural language can hinder precise pixel-level localization required for accurate segmentation in OVS.

## Method

- The **Retrieve and Segment** (RNS) method proposes a few-shot learning paradigm, augmenting textual class prompts with a small 'support set' of pixel-annotated visual examples.
- RNS develops a retrieval-augmented ==test-time== adapter that performs learned, per-query fusion of textual and dynamically retrieved visual support features to train a lightweight linear classifier for each test image.
- The approach incorporates strategies for robust operation under partial visual or textual support using pseudo-labeling, and supports continually expanding support sets.

## Results

- RNS consistently outperformed zero-shot and other few-shot OVS methods, showing up to a +**18.4%** mIoU improvement over zero-shot DINOv3.txt with only one visual support image per class.
- The method demonstrated robustness to partial visual or textual support, with graceful performance degradation and effective use of pseudo-labeling for classes lacking direct visual examples.
- With 20 support images per class, RNS reduced the performance gap to fully supervised segmentation by **11.5%** mIoU on average and surpassed state-of-the-art OVS methods by **14.1%** mIoU on various datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Even a minimal number of pixel-annotated visual examples (e.g., one per class) provides substantial performance gains in open-vocabulary segmentation, effectively bridging the zero-shot to few-shot gap.
> - A learned, per-query fusion mechanism for textual and visual support features is more effective and robust across diverse support scenarios than fixed or heuristic fusion strategies.
> - Dynamically retrieving relevant visual support and using class relevance weighting are crucial for adapting to specific test image contexts and handling classes with incomplete information.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Traditional semantic segmentation models rely on extensive, densely pixel-level annotated datasets for a fixed set of categories. While these models achieve high accuracy and precise mask localization, their scalability is limited by the high cost of manual annotation and their inability to recognize novel categories outside of their training distribution.

Open-vocabulary segmentation (OVS) emerged as an approach to address these limitations by leveraging the zero-shot recognition capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). VLMs, pre-trained on large-scale image-text datasets, learn a shared embedding space that allows them to compare images and text, enabling the recognition of arbitrary categories specified by text prompts. Extending this image-level recognition to pixel-level prediction has been an active area of research. However, a significant performance gap persists between current OVS methods and fully supervised segmentation models.

This performance gap is attributed primarily to two challenges:
1. **Supervision Mismatch:** VLMs are typically trained with coarse image-level supervision, which contrasts with the fine-grained, pixel-level predictions required for semantic segmentation.
2. **Semantic Ambiguity of Natural Language:** While natural language offers flexibility for open-vocabulary recognition, it can lack the precision often necessary for accurate pixel-level localization and disambiguation.

Existing OVS methods can be broadly categorized into:
- **Training VLMs for Segmentation:** Approaches that involve weakly supervised or fully supervised training of VLMs for dense prediction, sometimes hindering open-vocabulary performance on unseen categories.
- **Training-free VLM Tweaks:** Methods that modify VLM inference processes to enhance spatial sensitivity without additional training.
- **VLM+VM Hybrids:** Systems that combine VLM semantics with the localization capabilities of specialized vision models (VMs) like DINO or SAM, typically by localizing regions first and then classifying them using VLMs.

This paper positions itself within the context of these challenges by proposing a few-shot learning paradigm for OVS. It introduces the use of a small set of pixel-annotated visual examples (visual support) to augment the textual class descriptions, aiming to bridge the supervision gap. This approach integrates aspects of few-shot segmentation, which adapts models to novel classes using limited examples, and retrieval-augmented models, which dynamically expand knowledge bases for improved predictions. Unlike some prior retrieval-based methods that rely on generated examples or hand-crafted fusion strategies, this work focuses on learned, per-query fusion of real visual and textual support, aiming for a more robust and adaptable solution within an open-world, continually expanding scenario.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to narrow the performance discrepancy between zero-shot open-vocabulary segmentation (OVS) and fully supervised semantic segmentation, while preserving the open-vocabulary generalization capability. This is pursued by addressing the limitations of current OVS approaches, specifically the coarse supervision of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and the inherent semantic ambiguity of natural language.

The key motivations and detailed objectives of the work are:

1. **Introduce a Few-Shot Setting for OVS:** The core motivation is that while zero-shot OVS is flexible, it lacks the fine-grained supervision often needed. The authors propose augmenting textual class prompts with a small "support set" of pixel-annotated visual examples. This few-shot setting aims to provide more precise, pixel-level guidance without incurring the high costs of full supervision.
2. **Develop a Retrieval-Augmented Test-Time Adapter (RNS):**
 - **Learned Per-Query Fusion:** A significant objective is to move beyond previous methods that rely on late, hand-crafted fusion of textual and visual information. The proposed RNS aims for a learned, per-query (per-test-image) fusion mechanism. This is motivated by the hypothesis that a learned fusion can achieve stronger synergy between modalities, adapting dynamically to the specific context of each test image rather than applying a fixed, global rule.
 - **Lightweight and Efficient Adaptation:** The authors leverage the strong, generalizable features of large-scale pre-trained VLMs. This allows them to avoid retraining the entire backbone network. Instead, the objective is to train a lightweight, linear classifier per test image using only a few support examples, making the adaptation process computationally efficient (sub-second inference time per image on modern GPUs) and memory-minimal (only compact visual prototypes are stored).
3. **Support Continually Expanding Support Sets:** Real-world scenarios often involve new data and evolving categories. The method is motivated to be compatible with a dynamic environment where new visual examples can be added to the support set at any time without compromising the open-vocabulary nature or requiring significant re-engineering. This contrasts with models that require retraining for vocabulary expansion.
4. **Handle Diverse Support Scenarios:** The research aims to develop a robust method that performs effectively under various practical conditions:
 - **Full Support:** Both textual descriptions and pixel-annotated visual examples are available for all classes.
 - **Partial Visual Support:** Textual descriptions are available for all classes, but visual examples are missing for some.
 - **Partial Textual Support:** Visual examples are available for all classes, but textual descriptions are missing for some (e.g., novel categories or specialized domains).
 - **Only Textual Support (Zero-Shot):** No visual support images are provided, serving as a baseline and a fallback mode.
 This broad applicability is a key motivation for its real-world utility.
5. **Applicability to Fine-Grained Tasks:** The dynamic adaptability and ability to incorporate specific visual examples make the method suitable for fine-grained tasks such as "personalized segmentation," where the goal is to segment particular instances of an object (e.g., "my plate" instead of "plate").

In summary, the motivation is to develop a flexible, efficient, and high-performing OVS system that can leverage sparse, pixel-level visual supervision to significantly enhance segmentation accuracy, adapt to evolving data, and operate robustly across a spectrum of support availability, while retaining the inherent open-vocabulary strengths of VLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Retrieve and Segment (RNS), is a retrieval-augmented test-time adapter designed to enhance open-vocabulary segmentation by fusing textual and visual support features.

#### 3.1. Task Formulation

The task is Open-Vocabulary Segmentation (OVS), where the goal is to assign a semantic class to each pixel of an input image `I`. The set of classes `C` is arbitrary and defined at test time, specified either by a textual example (e.g., class name) or by a small set of pixel-annotated visual examples. The method considers various support settings:
- **Full-support:** All classes have a class name and at least one annotated image.
- **Partial-visual-support:** Some classes lack visual examples but all have class names.
- **Partial-textual-support:** Some classes lack class names but all have visual examples.
- **Only-textual-support:** The standard zero-shot segmentation setting, where only class names are available.

#### 3.2. Zero-Shot Segmentation with VLMs

This serves as the baseline. An input image `I` is processed by a VLM's vision encoder to obtain patch-level features `X ∈ R^(n×d)`. Each class name `c ∈ C` is processed by the VLM's text encoder to yield a textual class feature `t_c ∈ R^d`. Pixel-level predictions `P_jc` are computed by taking the dot product similarity between patch features `x_j` and textual class features `t_c`, followed by a softmax over classes. The low-resolution prediction is then upsampled to the original image resolution.

#### 3.3. Full Textual and Visual Support (RNS Core Mechanism)

When both textual and visual support are available, RNS constructs two sets of support features and uses them for test-time adaptation:

- **Visual Support Features:** For each support image `I_i` with ground-truth segmentation `Y_i`, patch-level features `X_i` are extracted. `Y_i` is downsampled and normalized to patch-level labels `P_i ∈^(n×C)`. Per-image visual class features `v_i_c` are computed by pooling patch features `x_i_j` using `P_i_jc`. The collection of all such `v_i_c` across all support images forms the visual support feature set `V`.
- **Fused Support Features:** To combine modalities, textual class features `t_c` are fused with aggregated visual class features `v_c`. `v_c` is obtained by summing all `v_i_c` for class `c` from images containing `c`. The fused class feature `f_cλ` is then computed as `λ * t_c + (1 - λ) * v_c`, where `λ ∈` is a mixing coefficient. RNS uses a set of mixing coefficients `Λ` to capture diverse information. The set of all `f_cλ` forms the fused support feature set `F`.
- **Support Feature Set Maintenance:** Both `V` and `F` are designed to be dynamically expandable. When new support images arrive, the relevant feature sets are updated efficiently.
- **Test-Time Adaptation:** For a given test image `I_q`, a lightweight linear classifier `g_θ` is trained specifically for this image.
 - **Retrieval:** The `k` nearest neighbors of each test image patch feature `x_q_j` are retrieved from the visual support feature set `V`. These retrieved features form `V_r`.
 - **Visual Support Loss (L_v):** `g_θ` is trained using a cross-entropy loss on `V_r`. Each training sample (retrieved feature) is weighted by a class relevance weight `w_c`. `w_c` is estimated by the softmax similarity between the global average pooled image-level feature `x_q` and the textual class feature `t_c`. This weighting prioritizes classes relevant to the test image.
 - **Fused Support Loss (L_f):** `g_θ` is also trained on the fused class features `f_cλ` corresponding to classes `C_r` that appear in `V_r`. This loss also uses the class relevance weights `w_c`.
 - **Total Loss:** The overall loss is `L = L_v + β_f L_f`. After training for a fixed number of steps, `g_θ` is applied to the test image's patch features to generate predictions, which are then upsampled.

#### 3.4. Partial Visual Support

This setting handles classes `C_d` for which no visual examples are provided but class names exist.
- **Pseudo-labeling:** For classes in `C_d` that are predicted to be present in the test image `I_q` (via zero-shot prediction), pseudo-labels are generated. These pseudo-labels are used to compute visual class features `v_c` for these "unseen" classes within the test image itself.
- **Fused Feature Pseudo-labeling:** The fused class features `f_cλ` derived from these pseudo-labeled `v_c` are themselves pseudo-labeled using their similarity to textual class features.
- **Pseudo-Label Loss (L_p):** An additional loss term, `L_p`, based on Kullback-Leibler divergence, is introduced. This loss leverages the pseudo-labels of `f_cλ` to guide `g_θ` for classes without direct visual support.
- **Extended Total Loss:** The total loss becomes `L = L_v + β_f L_f + β_p L_p`.

#### 3.5. Partial Textual Support

When class names are missing for some classes, their textual class features `t_c` cannot be formed directly. To ensure these classes still participate in the fusion and loss, their missing `t_c` are replaced by the average textual class feature across all classes that *do* have class names. If no textual support is available at all, the mixing coefficients `Λ` are set to `{0}` and class relevance weights `w_c` are set to `1` (effectively operating in a visual-only mode).

#### 3.6. Region-Proposal Predictions

For improved accuracy, especially at object boundaries, RNS can leverage external region proposals (e.g., from SAM). Instead of individual patch features, patch features are pooled within each region mask to create region-level features `x_q_r`. The classifier then predicts labels for these regions, which are mapped back to the full-resolution masks.

#### 3.7. Implementation Details

The method employs OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 or DINOv3.txt ViT-L/16 as VLM backbones, often combined with SAM 2.1 for region proposals. Hyperparameters such as `k=4` (nearest neighbors), `τ=0.1` (temperature), `β_f=1.5`, `β_p=0.2`, and `Λ = {0.9, 0.8, 0.6, 0.4, 0.2, 0.0}` are fixed across experiments. The linear classifier `g_θ` is trained using Adam optimizer for 700 steps in full-batch mode for each test image.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted across six OVS benchmarks (PASCAL VOC, PASCAL Context, COCO Object, COCO-Stuff, Cityscapes, ADE20K) and fine-grained datasets (PASCAL Context-59, FoodSeg103, CUB) with two VLM backbones (OpenCLIP ViT-B/16 and DINOv3.txt ViT-L/16) yielded the following main findings:

#### 4.1. Full Textual and Visual Support

- **Consistent Outperformance:** RNS consistently surpassed all tested competitors (zero-shot, kNN-CLIP, FREEDA, and their variants) across varying numbers of support images per class (B), both VLM backbones, and both patch-level and region-level input feature granularities.
- **Significant Improvement over Zero-Shot:** RNS demonstrated substantial performance gains over zero-shot segmentation. For instance, with just one support image per class (B=1), it achieved a +7.3% mIoU improvement on OpenCLIP and a +18.4% mIoU improvement on DINOv3.txt, highlighting the efficacy of even minimal visual support.
- **Effective Modality Fusion:** Textual priors provided significant value when visual support was sparse (low B), while visual information became more dominant and crucial as support density increased.
- **Scalability with Better Representations:** The performance gap between RNS and competitors widened when using stronger backbones like DINOv3.txt, indicating that RNS effectively leverages advanced visual representations.
- **Benefits of Region Proposals:** Utilizing SAM region proposals consistently boosted segmentation performance compared to direct patch-level predictions, albeit at a higher computational cost during inference.

#### 4.2. Partial Visual Support

- **Robustness to Missing Support:** RNS exhibited a graceful degradation in performance as the fraction of classes lacking visual examples increased. It continued to benefit from the available visual support even for small fractions of supported classes.
- **Crucial Role of Pseudo-Label Loss:** The pseudo-label loss (Equation 12 in the paper) was found to be critical. Removing it resulted in a steep performance drop, underscoring its importance in compensating for missing visual support by leveraging pseudo-labels for unseen classes.
- **Superiority over Competitors:** Unlike RNS, kNN-CLIP and FREEDA showed significant performance drops and often fell below zero-shot performance when visual support was partially missing, suggesting their methods were less robust to incomplete support sets.

#### 4.3. Partial Textual Support

- **Resilience to Missing Text:** RNS maintained its leading performance even as the fraction of classes lacking textual descriptions increased, experiencing only mild degradation.
- **Learned Fusion Advantage:** The results indicated that RNS's learned joint training on visual and textual support was superior to heuristic late fusion approaches employed by competitors like kNN-CLIP and FREEDA, which degraded steeply or gained minimally without full textual information. RNS effectively utilizes text as an auxiliary signal for fusion and relevance weighting without making the model fragile.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Importance of Class Relevance Weights:** Removing class relevance weights (`w_c`) led to a performance reduction across all few-shot settings, confirming their role in effectively suppressing the impact of irrelevant retrieved classes.
- **Benefits of Multiple Mixing Coefficients:** Using a single mixing coefficient `λ` for fused features, instead of a set `Λ`, particularly harmed performance in the low-shot regime, demonstrating the value of capturing diverse fusion strategies.
- **Efficacy of Retrieval:** Experiments confirmed that the retrieval mechanism is crucial for RNS. Replacing the retrieved visual support features with a random subset or semantically distant features led to significant performance degradation, highlighting that RNS's retrieval effectively identifies relevant support features.

#### 4.5. Closed-Set Comparisons

- **Superiority to Offline Baselines:** RNS outperformed linear classifiers trained offline on pixel-level annotations in low-shot settings, suggesting that its test-time adaptation and objective are more effective for few-shot support utilization. It also demonstrated greater robustness to fixed training settings compared to offline baselines, which often required extensive hyperparameter tuning.
- **Complementary Strengths:** The best performance was achieved when combining a pre-trained backbone with RNS, indicating the complementary gains from textual supervision and the efficacy of test-time training on dynamically relevant support.

#### 4.6. Personalized Segmentation

- Qualitative results demonstrated that RNS's dynamic support expandability enabled personalized segmentation. By adding a few examples of a specific object instance to the support set, the model could differentiate that instance from its broader generic class, even for partially occluded objects. Some failure cases were observed, often attributed to insufficient contextual information.

#### 4.7. Bridging the Gap to Fully Supervised Methods

- With 20 support images per class (B=20), RNS significantly narrowed the performance gap to fully supervised segmentation, reducing it to 11.5% mIoU on average. This represented a 34% mIoU improvement over the zero-shot baseline.
- RNS surpassed state-of-the-art OVS methods, such as CAT-Seg, by 14.1% mIoU, while utilizing considerably fewer pixel-level annotations. This improvement was particularly evident on fine-grained and out-of-domain datasets (e.g., CUB and Food), where many OVS methods trained on COCO-Stuff showed weaker generalization.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a notable advancement in the field of open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVS), primarily by introducing a robust and adaptable few-shot learning framework. Its significance and potential impact span several areas:

#### 5.1. Narrowing the Supervision Gap in OVS

The most direct impact is the significant reduction of the performance gap between zero-shot OVS and fully supervised semantic segmentation. By demonstrating that substantial improvements can be achieved with only a few pixel-annotated examples, RNS offers a practical pathway to more accurate OVS without the prohibitively high cost of dense, large-scale annotation required by traditional methods. This makes high-quality segmentation more accessible for a wider range of applications and novel categories.

#### 5.2. Novel and Robust Multi-Modal Fusion

RNS introduces a retrieval-augmented, test-time adaptation strategy that performs learned, per-image fusion of textual and visual support features. This approach is demonstrated to be more effective and robust than prior methods relying on fixed or hand-crafted fusion heuristics. This learned fusion mechanism provides a more flexible way to combine complementary information from different modalities, adapting to the specific context of each test image and leading to more accurate and less ambiguous segmentations. This could influence the design of future multi-modal learning architectures, particularly for dense prediction tasks.

#### 5.3. Practicality and Adaptability for Open-World Scenarios

The method's ability to handle continually expanding support sets and operate effectively under partial visual or textual support scenarios is highly significant for real-world deployment. In dynamic environments where new object categories or instances emerge, RNS can be updated efficiently without requiring full model retraining. This "open-world" readiness extends to fine-grained tasks like personalized segmentation, where specific instances can be taught to the model with minimal examples, enabling highly customized applications.

#### 5.4. Computational Efficiency

By utilizing strong, pre-trained VLM features and training only a lightweight, per-image linear classifier, RNS achieves high performance with efficient test-time training. This avoids the extensive computational resources and time required for retraining large backbone models, making it a more practical solution for inference in scenarios with limited computational budgets or for on-the-fly adaptation.

#### 5.5. Foundations for Future Research

RNS provides a strong baseline and a new direction for research in several areas:
- **Few-shot Learning for Dense Prediction:** It reinforces the potential of few-shot learning to bridge supervision gaps in complex vision tasks.
- **Retrieval-Augmented Models:** It highlights the power of dynamic retrieval for knowledge expansion and context-specific adaptation in vision-language models.
- **Adaptive Fusion Strategies:** The success of learned, per-query fusion could inspire further investigation into more sophisticated and adaptive multi-modal integration techniques.
- **Ethical Implications:** By enabling segmentation with fewer examples, it could facilitate research in domains where data annotation is scarce or sensitive, potentially democratizing access to advanced segmentation capabilities.

In summary, RNS represents a step towards more flexible, accurate, and scalable open-vocabulary segmentation. Its innovations in learned multi-modal fusion and dynamic support management are likely to influence both academic research and practical applications of computer vision, particularly in scenarios demanding adaptability and efficiency.
