# Reinforce to Learn, Elect to Reason: A Dual Paradigm for Video Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> The RLER framework from the National University of Defense Technology and Sun Yat-sen University introduces a dual paradigm to enhance video reasoning in large multimodal models by explicitly linking evidence production during training with evidence-aligned verification during inference. It achieved an average accuracy improvement of 6.3% over its base model (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) across eight diverse video benchmarks, including outperforming GPT-4o on VideoMME.

## Problem

- Existing large multimodal models (LMMs) for video reasoning often perform inference in a single, unverified pass, leading to outputs not explicitly grounded in visual evidence.
- This single-pass approach makes LMMs susceptible to minor perturbations and prone to generating unreliable or uninterpretable conclusions, particularly in complex video scenarios.
- Current methods to improve LMM reasoning lack systematic mechanisms to enforce evidence consistency across multiple reasoning traces or to verify chains of thought against key visual evidence.

## Method

- **RLER**-Training utilizes ==Group Relative Policy== Optimization (GRPO) with five task-driven rewards to shape the model's outputs for structured, verifiable, and evidence-centric generation, strengthening reasoning capacity.
- RLER-Inference, a train-free orchestrator, generates diverse candidate reasoning traces and parses them for canonical answers, cited key-frame indices, reasoning text, and confidence proxies.
- It employs an evidence-aligned scoring mechanism, robust aggregation (including outlier trimming and evidence weighting), and a referee-style self-check to elect the most reliable and evidence-supported answer.

## Results

- RLER delivered an average accuracy improvement of **6.3%** over its base model (Qwen2.5-VL-**7B**) across eight diverse video benchmarks, including video reasoning, general video understanding, and long video understanding tasks.
- It achieved state-of-the-art results among open-source LMMs and notably exceeded the proprietary GPT-4o entry on the VideoMME benchmark.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all custom reward components during training and components of the inference pipeline (diversity, evidence weighting, referee self-check) are crucial for achieving robust accuracy and grounding.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The framework successfully shifts video reasoning from an 'answer-driven' to an 'evidence-driven' paradigm by explicitly integrating evidence production during training and evidence-based verification during inference.
> - RLER-Training effectively teaches LMMs to generate structured, machine-parsable evidence, including specific key frame citations and concise reasoning, which directly informs and strengthens the inference process.
> - An 'aha moment' of self-correction emerges during training, where models identify and re-evaluate flaws in their reasoning, a capability mirrored and leveraged by the referee-style self-check in the inference stage.
