# Latent Bridge: Feature Delta Prediction for Efficient Dual-System Vision-Language-Action Model Inference

> [!summary] Summary
> Latent Bridge accelerates inference for dual-system Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by predicting intermediate VLM feature representations, allowing the computationally intensive VLM backbone to be called less frequently. This method achieves a 1.65-1.73x net per-episode speedup and reduces VLM calls by 50-75%, while retaining 95-100% of the VLA's task success rate across various manipulation benchmarks.

## Problem

- Dual-system Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models suffer from high inference latency due to the computationally expensive VLM backbone, which must execute at every control step.
- The necessity of frequent VLM calls limits the real-time deployment of these models for robotic manipulation tasks requiring control frequencies of 10-50Hz.
- Existing VLM acceleration techniques offer limited per-step speedups and do not address the fundamental bottleneck of executing the full VLM backbone at every timestep.

## Method

- A lightweight model, **Latent Bridge**, is introduced to predict the temporal deltas (changes) in VLM output features, enabling the VLM backbone to be skipped for intermediate control steps.
- **Latent Bridge** is instantiated in two forms: a ==feature-space bridge== for VLAs like **GR00T** and a ==KV-cache bridge== for interleaved VLAs like π0.5, demonstrating architectural generalization.
- A three-stage task-agnostic training pipeline (sync data collection, R0 bridge training, and ==DAgger refinement==) minimizes distribution shift and autoregressive prediction errors.

## Results

- Latent Bridge reduced VLM calls by 50-**75%** and achieved net per-episode wall-clock speedups of **1.73x** for GR00T and **1.65x** for π0.5 on LIBERO suites.
- The method retained an average task success rate of **94.54%** for GR00T and **96.92%** for π0.5 across LIBERO, RoboCasa, and ALOHA sim benchmarks, demonstrating strong performance retention.
- Ablation studies confirmed that learned delta prediction is essential, outperforming a feature caching baseline (34.25-**56.38%** SR retention) and traditional VLM acceleration methods (**1.08x** speedup).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - VLM output features exhibit high temporal similarity between consecutive timesteps, suggesting that a smaller model can predict their changes (deltas) between full VLM calls.
> - Operating on predicted feature deltas rather than directly accelerating the VLM backbone allows for entirely skipping VLM calls on intermediate steps, offering a distinct efficiency gain.
> - Autoregressive prediction in representation space benefits from DAgger-style refinement to mitigate distribution shift, especially for long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent a paradigm for generalist robotic manipulation, integrating pre-trained vision-language understanding with action prediction capabilities. These models typically ground language instructions in visual observations to generate motor commands. The VLA landscape broadly consists of two architectural categories: single-system and dual-system designs.

Single-system VLAs, such as RT-2 or OpenVLA, autoregressively generate action tokens directly through a large language model (LLM). This approach can be bottlenecked by the sequential decoding nature of the LLM. In contrast, dual-system VLAs, exemplified by GR00T, π$_{0.5}$, and Octo, decompose inference into two stages:
1. A large Vision-Language Model (VLM) backbone processes observations to produce a feature representation.
2. A separate, typically lightweight, action head decodes these features into actions.

Dual-system VLAs have demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across various manipulation benchmarks. However, this architecture introduces a fundamental latency bottleneck: the VLM backbone, which is the computationally intensive component, must execute at every control step to provide updated features for the action head. This requirement limits real-time deployment, especially for control frequencies between 10-50Hz.

Existing VLM/VLA acceleration techniques primarily focus on optimizing the VLM's forward pass through methods such as KV cache compression, speculative decoding, visual token pruning, layer skipping, and weight quantization. While these methods can offer per-step speedups (e.g., 1.08–1.62× reported), they still require the full VLM backbone to execute at every step, making them complementary to but distinct from approaches that aim to reduce the frequency of VLM calls. The reported speedups for these methods are often on single-system VLAs, and their benefit on dual-system architectures is constrained by Amdahl's Law, as they only accelerate a fraction of the total inference cost.

Latent Bridge addresses this specific bottleneck by proposing a method to *skip* VLM backbone calls on intermediate steps entirely, rather than just accelerating them. It leverages the observation that VLM outputs exhibit high temporal similarity between consecutive timesteps, suggesting that a smaller model could predict the temporal changes (deltas) in these features. This positions Latent Bridge as an orthogonal approach that can be composed with existing VLM acceleration techniques, potentially leading to cumulative benefits. The work builds upon the concept of prediction in representation space, akin to frameworks like LeCun's JEPA, but applies it specifically for accelerating VLA inference rather than for general representation learning or planning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance the inference efficiency of dual-system Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models without significantly compromising task performance. This objective is driven by a critical bottleneck identified in current state-of-the-art dual-system VLA architectures:

1. **Computational Bottleneck of the VLM Backbone:** In dual-system VLAs (e.g., GR00T, π$_{0.5}$), a large VLM backbone is responsible for processing visual observations and language instructions to generate a high-dimensional feature representation. This process is computationally expensive and constitutes the dominant portion of the overall inference time.
2. **Real-Time Deployment Limitations:** For robotic manipulation tasks requiring precise, real-time control (typically 10-50Hz), the high latency associated with executing the VLM backbone at every control step severely limits the practical deployment of these models on physical robots.
3. **Temporal Redundancy of VLM Outputs:** A key observation motivating this work is that the feature representations produced by the VLM backbone often exhibit significant temporal redundancy. That is, the VLM outputs at consecutive timesteps change slowly, implying that a full VLM re-computation at every step might be unnecessary. This redundancy suggests an opportunity for a more efficient mechanism to approximate or update these features between full VLM calls.

Based on these motivations, the specific objectives are:
- To develop a lightweight model, termed "Latent Bridge," capable of predicting the *deltas* (changes) in VLM output features between consecutive timesteps.
- To enable the action head of a VLA to operate on these predicted features, thereby allowing the expensive VLM backbone to be invoked only periodically (e.g., every 2 to 4 steps) rather than at every control step.
- To demonstrate that this approach generalizes across architecturally distinct dual-system VLA designs, specifically by instantiating Latent Bridge on both feature-space (GR00T) and KV-cache (π$_{0.5}$) based VLAs.
- To achieve significant reductions in VLM call frequency and overall inference latency, quantified by net per-episode speedup, while retaining a high percentage of the original VLA's task success rate (e.g., 95-100%).
- To develop a task-agnostic training pipeline for Latent Bridge that minimizes distribution shift issues arising from autoregressive prediction and can transfer across diverse benchmarks without modification.
- To evaluate the approach across a range of manipulation benchmarks, including LIBERO suites, RoboCasa kitchen tasks, and ALOHA sim, to validate its effectiveness and generalization capabilities.

By addressing the VLM backbone bottleneck through feature delta prediction, the research aims to make powerful dual-system VLA models more practical for real-world robotic applications requiring low-latency control.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Latent Bridge is designed to predict VLM output deltas, enabling the action head to operate on approximated features while the VLM backbone is called periodically. The methodology involves a specific problem formulation, two distinct architectural instantiations, a three-stage training pipeline, and optional enhancements.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

A dual-system VLA is defined by a policy $\pi(a_t | o_t) = A(V(o_t))$, where $V$ is the VLM backbone and $A$ is the action head. The VLM backbone produces features $z_t = V(o_t)$ at each control step $t$, which the action head then decodes into action $a_t = A(z_t, q_t)$ (conditioned on proprioceptive state $q_t$).

Latent Bridge aims to approximate $VLM$ features without running $V$. A VLM call period $f$ is defined such that the backbone $V$ executes once every $f$ control steps (i.e., at $t = 0, f, 2f, \ldots$). For the $f-1$ intermediate steps, the bridge $B$ predicts the next feature $\hat{z}_{t+t'}$ based on the previous predicted feature $\hat{z}_{t+t'-1}$:
$\hat{z}_{t+t'} = \hat{z}_{t+t'-1} + B(\hat{z}_{t+t'-1}, s_t, q_{t+t'}, a_{t+t'-1})$ for $t' = 1, \ldots, f-1$.
Here, $\hat{z}_t := z_t$ (the fresh VLM feature) on VLM steps. $s_t$ represents stable intermediate-layer VLM features cached from the last VLM call, providing visual context. The action head then produces $a_{t+t'} = A(\hat{z}_{t+t'}, q_{t+t'})$. This autoregressive chaining means that for $t' \geq 2$, the bridge's input is its own previous prediction. A baseline, "feature caching," corresponds to $B = Id$ (predicting $\Delta_t = 0$), where stale VLM features are reused.

#### 3.2. Latent Bridge Architecture

Two distinct instantiations of Latent Bridge are developed to generalize across VLA designs:

- **Feature-space bridge (for GR00T):** For VLAs that use a single feature vector interface between the VLM backbone and the action head.
 - **Prediction:** The bridge $B$ predicts one-step feature deltas $\Delta_t$ via residual prediction: $\hat{z}_{t+1} = \hat{z}_t + \Delta_t$, where $\Delta_t = B(\hat{z}_t, s_t, q_t, a_{t-1})$.
 - **Components:** $B$ is a Transformer with cross-attention (DiT blocks). It includes self-attention over input features $\hat{z}_t$, cross-attention to stable context $s_t$ (reused from the last VLM call), and AdaLN conditioning on proprioceptive state $q_t$ and previous action $a_{t-1}$.
 - **Initialization:** A zero-initialized output projection ensures the untrained bridge starts at the feature caching baseline ($\Delta_t = 0$).
 - **Image-only processing:** The bridge operates exclusively on image tokens, copying text tokens from the last VLM cache. This is justified by the temporal invariance of text tokens.
 - **Stable vs. dynamic layers:** Stable intermediate VLM layers are used for $s_t$ to provide scene context, while the bridge predicts the final action-head input $z_t$.

- **KV-cache bridge (for $\pi_{0.5}$):** For VLAs like $\pi_{0.5}$ that use an interleaved design where the action expert cross-attends to the VLM backbone's per-layer KV cache.
 - **Prediction:** The bridge $B_{KV}$ predicts pre-RoPE key and value deltas, $\{\Delta K_l, \Delta V_l\}_{l=1}^L$, for all $L=18$ layers simultaneously.
 - **Input:** $B_{KV}(\Delta e_t, e_t, c_{KV_{t-1}}, q_t, a_{t-1})$, where $e_t = \text{SigLIP}(o_t)$ is a fresh vision embedding, $\Delta e_t = e_t - e_{t-1}$ captures visual changes, and $c_{KV_{t-1}}$ is the most recent per-layer KV cache.
 - **Architecture:** A shared DiT backbone with 18 lightweight per-layer output heads. SigLIP is run at every bridge step to provide fresh visual context at low cost. RoPE is handled by predicting pre-RoPE deltas and re-applying RoPE.

#### 3.3. Training Pipeline

A three-stage task-agnostic training pipeline is employed:

1. **Stage 1: Sync data collection:** The pretrained VLA is rolled out in "sync mode" (VLM at every step) in a simulator. Tuples $(z_t, z_{t+1}, s_t, q_t, a_{t-1})$ are recorded.
2. **Stage 2: R0 bridge training:** An initial "round-0" bridge is trained offline on the sync-only dataset.
 - **Loss function:** A combined MSE and cosine loss, restricted to image tokens: $L = \|\hat{z}_{t+1} - z_{t+1}\|^2 + \alpha (1 - \frac{\hat{z}_{t+1} \cdot z_{t+1}}{\|\hat{z}_{t+1}\| \|z_{t+1}\|})$ (with $\alpha=1.0$).
3. **Stage 3: DAgger refinement (R1):** One round of DAgger (Dataset Aggregation) refinement is applied.
 - The R0 bridge is deployed in simulation at period $f$, while a VLM oracle runs in parallel to provide ground-truth features at every control step. The robot acts according to the bridge's policy.
 - This generates DAgger pairs $(\hat{z}_{t+t'-1}, z_{t+t'})$ for each bridge step. The bridge is then retrained on a mixed dataset of sync and DAgger data, resuming from R0 weights with a reduced learning rate. DAgger mitigates distribution shift during autoregressive chaining.

#### 3.4. Optional Enhancements (for long-horizon tasks)

- **Phase-aware VLM scheduling:** Dynamically adjusts the VLM call period $f$ based on the magnitude of the previous action's translation. Higher motion phases result in smaller $f$ (more frequent VLM calls), while lower motion allows larger $f$.
- **LoRA action head adaptation:** Applies Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to the action head to increase its tolerance to potential noise in bridge predictions. This involves training the action head on a mix of bridge and sync features with Gaussian noise.

#### 3.5. Inference

At deployment with VLM call period $f$:
- **VLM steps ($t \pmod f = 0$):** The full VLM backbone runs. $z_t$, $s_t$, and KV pairs are cached.
- **Bridge steps ($t \pmod f \neq 0$):** The Latent Bridge predicts deltas from the most recent representation ($\hat{z}_t$) – either the fresh VLM feature (first bridge step) or its own previous output (subsequent steps). The action head then operates on the predicted $\hat{z}_t$. This replaces a 46–63 ms VLM call with a 2–6 ms bridge computation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were conducted on two dual-system VLAs: GR00T-N1.6-3B and π$_{0.5}$, across various benchmarks including four LIBERO suites (Spatial, Object, Goal, LIBERO-10), 24 RoboCasa kitchen tasks, and the ALOHA sim transfer-cube task. Evaluation used 3 random seeds × 20 episodes per task, or 50 episodes per seed for ALOHA. Latency profiling was performed on a single NVIDIA A100 80GB GPU with bfloat16 precision and `torch.compile`.

#### 4.1. Main Results on LIBERO

Table 1 (from the paper) provides a summary of results on LIBERO suites.
- **Performance Retention:** Latent Bridge achieved average success rate (SR) retention of 94.54% for GR00T and 96.92% for π$_{0.5}$ compared to synchronous (Sync) VLA execution. Specifically, GR00T showed 95.83% (Spatial), 97.83% (Object), 95.33% (Goal), and 89.17% (LIBERO-10) SR. For π$_{0.5}$, SRs were 99.00% (Spatial), 97.67% (Object), 97.33% (Goal), and 93.67% (LIBERO-10).
- **VLM Call Reduction & Speedup:** Latent Bridge reduced VLM calls by 50-75% (using $f=2-4$ for GR00T and $f=4$ for π$_{0.5}$). This resulted in a net per-episode wall-clock speedup of 1.73× for GR00T and 1.65× for π$_{0.5}$. The average per-step latency for GR00T dropped from 90 ms (Sync) to 49 ms, and for π$_{0.5}$ from 76 ms to 46 ms.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - **Feature Caching ($\Delta_t=0$):** This baseline, which simply reuses stale VLM features, performed significantly worse, collapsing to average SRs of 34.25% (GR00T) and 56.38% (π$_{0.5}$). This highlights the necessity of learned delta prediction.
 - **Token-Pruning Baselines (FastV, VLA-Cache):** FastV yielded a net slowdown (0.95× speedup) and VLA-Cache provided only a modest 1.08× speedup. These methods still execute the full VLM backbone, with speedups limited by Amdahl's Law on dual-system VLAs (affecting only 60-70% of per-step cost), unlike Latent Bridge which skips the backbone entirely on bridge steps.

#### 4.2. Cross-Benchmark Evaluation

Latent Bridge demonstrated generalization beyond LIBERO:
- **RoboCasa (GR00T):** On 24 kitchen tasks (zero-shot transfer to a different robot embodiment), Latent Bridge (at $f=3$) retained 95.38% of the sync success rate (63.16% vs 66.22%). Feature caching achieved only 75.17% retention.
- **ALOHA sim (π$_{0.5}$):** On the bimanual transfer-cube task, Latent Bridge (at $f=3$) retained 97.73% of sync SR (86.00% vs 88.00%), outperforming caching which retained 52.27%.
These results indicate that the approach transfers across different embodiments, action spaces (7-DoF vs 14-DoF), and task distributions.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

- **Bridge Architecture Components (π$_{0.5}$):**
 - Removing the fresh vision embedding ($\Delta e_t$) degraded Goal SR by 11.16 percentage points (pp) and LIBERO-10 by 28.34 pp.
 - Removing stable intermediate VLM features ($s_t$) as cross-attention context degraded Goal SR by 5.16 pp and LIBERO-10 by 11.00 pp. This indicates both visual input and stable context are crucial, especially for more complex tasks.
 - A smaller 19M-parameter bridge achieved comparable SR to a 148M bridge, suggesting capacity was not the limiting factor.
- **DAgger Training Progression:**
 - R0 (sync-only training) provided a strong baseline, recovering 93–96% of sync SR on easier LIBERO suites.
 - R1 (+DAgger refinement) delivered consistent gains: +4pp on π$_{0.5}$ and +4–13pp on GR00T, with the largest improvements observed on long-horizon tasks where autoregressive drift is more pronounced.
 - For GR00T LIBERO-10, the optional enhancements (Stage 2 LoRA adaptation, Stage 3 phase-aware scheduling) further improved R1 SR from 67.17% to 89.17% (+34.84pp total gain).
- **VLM Call Period ($f$) vs. Performance (π$_{0.5}$):**
 - Spatial, Object, and Goal suites maintained above 94.5% SR even up to $f=8$.
 - LIBERO-10, a long-horizon suite, showed a steeper degradation (94.0% to 84.5% at $f=8$) due to error compounding.
 - The default $f=4$ provides 75% VLM savings with less than 1pp average SR drop.
- **Case Study (Why Caching Fails):** On a LIBERO-Spatial task, feature caching led to KV cosine similarity dropping to approximately 0.91, causing the action head to go off-trajectory and fail. Latent Bridge, by contrast, maintained cosine similarity at approximately 0.99, enabling successful task completion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Latent Bridge represents a contribution to the field of efficient robotic control using Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. Its significance and potential impact stem from several key aspects:

- **Addressing a Core Bottleneck:** The work directly targets the computational bottleneck of large VLM backbones in dual-system VLA models, which is a limiting factor for real-time robotic deployment. By proposing to *skip* VLM calls rather than just accelerate them, it offers a distinct and effective strategy for efficiency.
- **Significant Inference Speedup:** The demonstrated 1.65–1.73× net per-episode speedup, alongside a 50–75% reduction in VLM calls, translates directly into more responsive and higher-frequency robotic control. This can enable the deployment of advanced VLA models in scenarios where latency is critical, such as precise manipulation tasks in dynamic environments.
- **High Performance Retention:** Achieving 95–100% task performance retention across diverse and challenging benchmarks (LIBERO, RoboCasa, ALOHA sim) is a critical validation of the method's practical utility. It indicates that the efficiency gains do not come at a significant cost to the robot's capabilities.
- **Architectural Generalization:** Instantiating Latent Bridge on two architecturally distinct VLAs (GR00T's feature-space interface and π$_{0.5}$'s KV-cache interface) demonstrates its adaptability and broad applicability within the dual-system VLA paradigm. This suggests that the approach is not specific to a single VLA model.
- **Robust Training Pipeline:** The proposed three-stage, task-agnostic DAgger training pipeline effectively mitigates distribution shift during autoregressive prediction, which is a common challenge in sequence prediction tasks. Its ability to transfer across benchmarks without modification simplifies the deployment process.
- **Complementary to Existing Methods:** Latent Bridge is orthogonal to existing VLM acceleration techniques (e.g., pruning, quantization). This implies a potential for synergistic integration, where further speedups might be achieved by applying existing methods to the VLM backbone when it *is* called, in addition to skipping calls via the bridge.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Broader Real-World Robotic Deployment:** The increased inference efficiency could facilitate the deployment of advanced VLA models on real robots, moving them from research labs to more practical applications in manufacturing, logistics, and service robotics.
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** Faster inference enables robots to react more quickly and fluidly to environmental changes, contributing to more robust and capable autonomous systems.
- **Cost-Effectiveness:** Reducing the computational load could potentially lower the hardware requirements or energy consumption for deploying VLA-powered robots, making them more economically viable.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **DAgger Requires Online Simulation:** The DAgger refinement stage necessitates online interaction with a simulator, which might limit its direct applicability for real-robot deployment scenarios where simulator access is restricted or imperfect. Future work could explore offline training methods for the bridge, potentially with augmented data generation.
- **VLA-Checkpoint Specificity:** The bridge is trained for a specific VLA checkpoint, requiring retraining if the base VLA model is updated. While bridge training is lightweight, this adds an overhead for frequently updated VLA models.
- **Architectural Constraints for Interleaved VLAs:** For interleaved architectures like π$_{0.5}$, the action expert still traverses all transformer layers, imposing a floor on latency. Future research could investigate "suffix-level bridging" or consistency distillation to accelerate the action expert itself, potentially pushing speedups beyond the current 2×.
- **Minor Episode Length Increase:** Bridge-driven rollouts are slightly longer than sync rollouts on successful episodes. This effect is accounted for in reported speedups but indicates a minor policy deviation.

Overall, Latent Bridge offers a pragmatic and effective solution to a fundamental challenge in robotic control with modern VLA models, contributing to their transition from research prototypes to deployable systems.
