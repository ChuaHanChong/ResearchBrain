# Self-supervised perception for tactile skin covered dexterous hands

> [!summary] Summary
> Meta AI researchers develop Sparsh-skin, a self-supervised learning framework for magnetic tactile sensors that enables full-hand touch perception on robotic hands, achieving 41% improvement over prior work across force estimation, pose detection, and manipulation tasks while requiring significantly less labeled training data.

## Problem

- Existing vision-based tactile sensors have limitations in response time, form factor, and coverage
- Magnetic skin sensors are difficult to interpret and lack general-purpose models for extracting meaningful representations
- Existing self-supervised learning approaches, adapted from computer vision, are not well-suited to the unique characteristics of noisy magnetic flux tactile signals

## Method

- ==Self-supervised learning== framework, **Sparsh-skin**, specifically designed for magnetic tactile signals
- Novel ==tokenization== and ==masking== strategies adapted for temporal tactile data
- ==Self-distillation== approach using ==student-teacher networks== to handle noisy signals

## Results

- **56% improvement** over end-to-end learning across downstream tasks
- Accurate force and pose estimation achieved with superior sample efficiency, requiring significantly less labeled data than end-to-end training for comparable performance
- Higher success rates on plug insertion tasks compared to vision-only and end-to-end visuo-tactile policies

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Block masking of contiguous sensor regions preserves semantic meaning better than random masking
> - Combining tactile signals with joint position data improves representation quality
> - Self-distillation outperforms traditional contrastive learning for tactile signals

## Detailed Report

### 1. Broader Research Landscape

This work addresses a critical challenge in robotics: enabling dexterous manipulation through effective tactile perception. The paper situates itself within the broader research landscape by:

- **Highlighting the limitations of existing tactile sensing approaches:** The introduction contrasts vision-based tactile sensors (e.g., DIGIT, GelSight) with magnetic skin-based sensors (e.g., uSkin, ReSkin). While vision-based sensors offer high-resolution data, they often suffer from slow response times, bulky form factors, and limited coverage. Magnetic skin sensors, on the other hand, provide faster response times, flexibility in form factor, and potential for full-hand coverage, but they are challenging to interpret and model.
- **Addressing the gap in general-purpose models for magnetic tactile sensors:** The paper identifies a lack of robust, general-purpose models for magnetic skin sensors as a key obstacle to their wider adoption. Existing research in tactile representation learning has largely focused on vision-based sensors, leveraging advancements in computer vision. This paper specifically targets the underexplored area of representation learning for magnetic tactile data.
- **Building on self-supervised learning (SSL) for tactile learning:** The work recognizes the potential of SSL to learn useful representations from unlabeled data, which is particularly relevant in robotics where acquiring labeled data can be difficult and expensive. However, the authors argue that existing SSL approaches, often adapted from computer vision, may not be well-suited for the unique characteristics of magnetic flux signals.

By addressing these limitations and building upon the promise of SSL, this research contributes to the growing field of tactile robotics and aims to unlock the potential of magnetic skin sensors for dexterous manipulation. This work also addresses a gap in the community by planning to open source the code, datasets, and models associated with this research. This will help advance research by making the technology accessible to others.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The key objectives of this research are:

- **Develop Sparsh-skin:** A general-purpose tactile representation model for magnetic skin sensors covering a dexterous robot hand.
- **Improve tactile representation learning:** Revisit tokenization, masking, and learning algorithm choices for temporal magnetic tactile signals to improve downstream task performance.
- **Create a dataset:** Release a dataset containing diverse in-hand interactions with the Allegro hand sensorized with Xela sensors, along with labeled datasets, metrics, and task designs for evaluating learned representations.
- **Achieve state-of-the-art performance:** Demonstrate that pre-trained Sparsh-skin representations outperform end-to-end training and prior work in terms of both performance and sample efficiency on downstream tasks.

The motivation behind this work stems from the need for more robust and generalizable tactile perception systems for robotic manipulation. The authors recognize the limitations of existing tactile sensing approaches and the potential of magnetic skin sensors to overcome these limitations. They aim to bridge the gap between sensor technology and practical application by developing a self-supervised learning framework that can extract meaningful representations from raw tactile data and enable robots to perform complex manipulation tasks with greater dexterity and efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of the paper revolves around the Sparsh-skin framework, which leverages self-supervised learning to train a tactile encoder model. The methodology involves the following key steps:

1. **Data Collection:** A dataset of approximately 4 hours of random play data was collected using an Allegro robot hand sensorized with Xela uSkin sensors. The hand performed various atomic manipulation actions with 14 household objects.
2. **Sensor Tokenization and Preprocessing:** Raw Xela signals are preprocessed using baseline subtraction and resampling to a consistent frequency. The tactile data is then tokenized into chunks of 100ms, concatenated with sensor position data obtained from forward kinematics, and projected through a linear layer.
3. **Self-Supervised Learning:** The Sparsh-skin model is trained using self-distillation, a technique that involves a student and a teacher network. The student network receives a corrupted version of the tactile signal, while the teacher network receives privileged information about the same data sample. The student network is trained to predict the data representation produced by the teacher network.
4. **Data Corruption Strategy:** A block masking strategy is employed to corrupt the input signals to the student network. This involves masking sensor data from contiguous blocks, including sensors from neighboring sensor islands, to avoid data augmentations that change the semantic meaning of the signal.
5. **Online Probes:** Reconstruction and object identification tasks are used as online probes to monitor the quality of the learned representations during training.
6. **Downstream Task Evaluation:** The learned Sparsh-skin representations are evaluated on four downstream tasks: force estimation, joystick state estimation, pose estimation, and policy learning (plug insertion). These tasks are chosen to represent a range of relevant problems in tactile perception and manipulation.

The authors carefully consider and address potential pitfalls in their approach. The use of self-distillation avoids issues with noisy magnetic flux, and tokenization of time-series data incorporates important temporal context. The design of online probes ensures meaningful representation learning and offers a mechanism to check the progress of SSL.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate the effectiveness of the Sparsh-skin framework:

- **Outperformance on downstream tasks:** Sparsh-skin significantly outperforms end-to-end training and prior work on the downstream tasks, achieving over 41% improvement compared to prior work and over 56% compared to end-to-end learning in terms of performance and sample efficiency.
- **Sample efficiency:** Sparsh-skin exhibits high sample efficiency, achieving comparable or better performance with significantly less labeled data compared to end-to-end training.
- **Effective force estimation:** Sparsh-skin accurately predicts normal and shear forces, even with limited training data.
- **Accurate state estimation:** Sparsh-skin accurately estimates the state of a joystick and the pose of an object being manipulated.
- **Improved policy learning:** Policies trained with Sparsh-skin representations achieve higher success rates on the plug insertion task compared to vision-only policies and end-to-end trained visuo-tactile policies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Sparsh-skin research has several significant implications:

- **Enabling dexterous manipulation:** By providing a robust and generalizable tactile perception system, Sparsh-skin paves the way for robots to perform more complex and dexterous manipulation tasks in unstructured environments.
- **Accelerating robotics research:** The open-sourcing of code, datasets, and models will facilitate further research in tactile robotics and accelerate the development of new algorithms and applications.
- **Advancing self-supervised learning in robotics:** Sparsh-skin demonstrates the effectiveness of self-supervised learning for tactile perception and provides a valuable framework for future research in this area.
- **Practical applications:** The demonstrated capabilities have potential applications in various domains, including manufacturing, healthcare, and assistive robotics. The ability to precisely estimate forces and object poses through touch can enable robots to perform delicate assembly tasks, assist surgeons in minimally invasive procedures, and provide personalized assistance to individuals with disabilities.

The Sparsh-skin project represents a significant step towards creating foundation models for full-hand tactile representations. This progress will unlock previously difficult high-dexterity robotics tasks. Despite limitations (spatial data corruption strategy, pose estimation task only designed for static hands, and limited generalization assessment for manipulation policy), this work provides a strong foundation for future research and development in tactile robotics. Future research directions may include improving the model design by explicitly handling temporal correlation learning, extending the pose estimation task to more dynamic scenarios, and exploring methods for improving the generalization of manipulation policies across diverse tactile feedback signals.
