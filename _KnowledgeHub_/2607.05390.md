# Deform360: A Massive Multi-view Visuotactile Dataset for Deformable World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Brown University and Columbia University introduce Deform360, a massive multi-view visuotactile dataset featuring 198 diverse deformable objects, enabling systematic benchmarking of 2D pixel-space and 3D geometric-space world models. The dataset facilitates improved 3D tracking using tactile feedback, reducing error by approximately five times, and supports preliminary real-world robot planning demonstrations for deformable object manipulation.

## Problem

- Accurate modeling of deformable objects is challenging due to their high degrees of freedom, complex material properties, and frequent occlusion of local deformations during interaction.
- A scarcity of diverse, large-scale, real-world datasets with multi-view observations and ground-truth 3D states limits the development and systematic evaluation of world models for deformable objects.
- Existing datasets often lack object diversity, rely on synthetic data, or miss high-fidelity annotations, comprehensive multi-view coverage, or synchronized tactile sensing capabilities.

## Method

- The **Deform360** dataset was collected using ==41 surround-view cameras== and ==bimanual tactile-equipped UMI grippers== to capture dense, synchronized visuotactile interactions with 198 diverse deformable objects.
- A novel ==markerless 3D tracking pipeline== was developed, combining ==per-frame 3D Gaussian Splatting== for dynamic geometry reconstruction with ==multi-view 2D-to-3D particle lifting==.
- The particle tracking was refined using a ==physics-informed objective function==, incorporating ==shape==, ==local rigidity (ARAP)==, ==Laplacian regularization==, and a ==tactile loss== to ensure physical plausibility and accuracy under occlusions.

## Results

- The 3DGS models achieved high reconstruction fidelity (average PSNR of **27.66 dB**), and tactile-enhanced tracking reduced Chamfer distance error by approximately **five times** compared to purely visual tracking.
- A transformer-based model achieved an **88.67%** accuracy in predicting local tactile contact events from visual observations, highlighting the dataset's ability to capture visuotactile coupling.
- Benchmarking showed PhysTwin's superior performance in frame generalization (**CD 0.014**), ParticleFormer's better future prediction in episode generalization (**CD 0.051**), and Cosmos's strong zero-shot image quality generalization (**PSNR 25.042**) for unseen objects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Tactile sensing significantly improves 3D tracking accuracy, particularly during occlusions by robotic grippers, demonstrating its critical role in robust state estimation for deformable objects.
> - 3D geometric-space world models with explicit physical priors (e.g., PhysTwin) excel in generalizing dynamics to novel interaction sequences within known objects, especially in low-data scenarios.
> - 2D pixel-space world models, leveraging large-scale pre-training (e.g., Cosmos), demonstrate superior zero-shot visual generalization to entirely unseen object categories, though they may not always strictly follow robot commands in long-horizon predictions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation often relies on accurate prediction of object dynamics, a process referred to as world modeling. While significant progress has been made in this area, accurately modeling deformable objects presents a challenge. Deformable bodies possess a high number of degrees of freedom and complex material properties. Furthermore, local deformations resulting from contact are frequently occluded by robotic end-effectors or the object itself, making observation difficult. Tactile sensing has emerged as a complementary modality to address these occlusion issues and provide direct contact feedback.

Current approaches to modeling deformable object dynamics typically fall into two categories:
1. **2D Pixel Space Models**: These models predict dynamics in the 2D image space, often through video generation techniques.
2. **3D Geometric Space Models**: These models represent objects using explicit 3D geometry, such as meshes or particle systems, and predict their dynamics in 3D space.

A comprehensive understanding of the relative strengths and limitations of these two paradigms remains incomplete, primarily due to a scarcity of diverse, large-scale, real-world datasets that provide the necessary multi-view observations and ground-truth 3D states. Existing datasets often exhibit limitations in object diversity, rely on synthetic data, or lack high-fidelity annotations, multi-view coverage, or tactile sensing capabilities. This gap hinders the development and systematic evaluation of generalizable world models for deformable object manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research were to:

1. **Address the Data Scarcity Problem**: Develop and release a large-scale, diverse, and high-fidelity visuotactile dataset of real-world deformable object interactions. This dataset, named Deform360, aims to provide an extensive resource for training and evaluating world models that can handle the complexities of deformable objects.
2. **Enable Systematic Model Evaluation**: Facilitate a systematic comparison between 2D pixel-space world models and 3D geometric-space world models. By providing ground-truth 3D states and diverse interaction scenarios, the dataset is intended to allow for a rigorous analysis of the trade-offs between structured physical priors (typical in 3D models) and scalability (often seen in 2D models).
3. **Develop a Robust Annotation Pipeline**: Create a novel markerless 3D tracking pipeline capable of extracting dense geometry and motion information from the collected multi-view visuotactile data, even under challenging conditions such as occlusions caused by grippers.
4. **Demonstrate Real-World Applicability**: Provide initial demonstrations of how world models trained on Deform360 can be utilized for practical robotic planning tasks involving deformable objects in real-world environments.
5. **Benchmark Visuotactile Coupling**: Investigate the coupling between visual observations and physical contact events by evaluating the feasibility of predicting tactile contacts solely from visual inputs.

The motivation for these objectives stems from the identified limitations in the current research landscape. The absence of a sufficiently rich and diverse dataset has impeded progress in understanding and modeling the dynamics of deformable objects, which is a fundamental challenge for advanced robotic manipulation. By providing such a resource and a comprehensive benchmark, the authors aim to accelerate research towards more robust and generalizable deformable object-centric world models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research approach involved two main components: the creation of the Deform360 dataset and the development of an annotation pipeline to extract high-fidelity 3D dynamics.

#### 3.1. Deform360 Dataset Collection

The Deform360 dataset was designed for dense, synchronized visuotactile recording of complex physical interactions.

#### 3.2. Visuotactile Capture System

The data collection rig incorporated:
- **41 surround-view cameras**: Capturing RGB videos at 720x1280 resolution and 30 frames per second (FPS), ensuring 360-degree observability of global object dynamics. Cameras were calibrated with intrinsic and extrinsic matrices.
- **Bimanual tactile-equipped UMI grippers**: These grippers provided tactile signals synchronized with the visual streams at 30 Hz, offering data on contact forces and local material response, crucial for capturing occluded deformations.
- **Robot proprioception**: The 6D pose and openness of the grippers were recorded for precise action reasoning.

#### 3.3. Object Taxonomy and Diversity

The dataset features 198 distinct daily-life objects, totaling 1,980 interaction sequences. Objects were categorized based on their physical material responses and topological dimensionality:
- **1D Deformables (28 objects)**: Ropes, cables, wires, varying in stiffness and thickness.
- **2D Deformables (98 objects)**: Fabrics, cloths, garments, bags, paper-like materials.
- **3D Volumetric Deformables (72 objects)**: Plush toys, stuffed animals, foam objects, and squeezable volumetric items.
These objects span 17 semantic categories.

#### 3.4. Interaction Protocol and Scale

Data collection involved tactile-equipped UMI grippers performing a wide range of manipulation primitives, including unimanual actions (poking, squeezing) and bimanual maneuvers (stretching, folding, twisting). Each object had 5 unimanual and 5 bimanual episodes. The dataset comprises 74,850 raw videos, 23.3 million frames across all views, and 215.7 hours of cumulative multi-view footage, with an average episode duration of 10.34 seconds.

#### 3.5. Annotation Pipeline

A novel markerless 3D tracking pipeline was developed to generate high-fidelity particle annotations for deformable objects, addressing the limitations of existing dynamic reconstruction methods (e.g., temporal inconsistency).

#### 3.6. Visuotactile Preprocessing

- **Multi-view calibration**: ArUco grids were used for precise metric-scale 3D reconstruction.
- **Video undistortion**: Raw RGB videos were undistorted.
- **Gripper tracking**: ArUco markers on the grippers were tracked across multiple views, and their 6D pose and openness were aggregated using RANSAC for robustness.

#### 3.7. Dynamic Geometry Reconstruction

- **Per-frame 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)**: High-fidelity 3D dynamic geometry of interacting objects was reconstructed for each frame. Object masks, generated by segmentation models, were applied to filter RGB videos, ensuring 3DGS optimized solely for the object. This provided detailed geometry without requiring explicit deformation fields.

#### 3.8. Markerless Particle Tracking

- **Multi-view 2D-to-3D lifting**: CoTracker3 was used to track up to 1,600 mask-filtered grid points in 2D across all camera views, generating persistent 2D trajectories. These 2D tracks were then lifted into 3D space using the depth maps rendered from the optimized 3DGS models.
- **Multi-view fusion and refinement**: Single-view 3D estimates, often noisy, were fused into a unified velocity field using RANSAC. To ensure physical plausibility, especially under occlusions, the tracking results were refined by minimizing a physics-informed objective function ($L_{track}$) composed of:
 - **Shape loss ($L_{shape}$)**: Bidirectional Chamfer distance to the target 3DGS point cloud, ensuring geometric alignment. Particles influenced by tactile sensors were excluded from this loss to avoid conflicts.
 - **Local rigidity loss ($L_{local}$)**: Enforced As-Rigid-As-Possible (ARAP) constraints to preserve local metric structure.
 - **Laplacian regularization ($L_{lap}$)**: Encouraged spatial smoothness in the velocity field.
 - **Tactile loss ($L_{tactile}$)**: Incorporated reduced-order physics from tactile sensors, acting as a soft regularizer to enforce localized no-slip conditions around activated taxels within the contact set.
This optimization process produced warped point clouds by integrating optimized per-frame velocities from initial particle positions.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments evaluated the dataset's utility for learning and benchmarking deformable dynamics, encompassing perception quality, visuotactile coupling, world model performance, and real-world robotic planning.

#### 4.1. Evaluation of Reconstruction and Tracking Quality

- **3DGS Reconstruction Fidelity**: The 3DGS models achieved high reconstruction fidelity across all object categories, with a global average PSNR of 27.66 dB, SSIM of 0.96, and LPIPS of 0.0708. 3D volumetric objects showed the highest PSNR (30.00 dB).
- **Tactile-enhanced Tracking**: The integration of tactile feedback significantly improved tracking accuracy, particularly when parts of the object were occluded by the gripper during prehensile manipulations. Visuotactile tracking resulted in a Chamfer distance error of $2.71 \times 10^{-5}$ m$^2$, which was approximately five times lower than the $1.41 \times 10^{-4}$ m$^2$ error from purely visual tracking. This indicates the critical role of tactile cues in maintaining tracking accuracy through occlusions.

#### 4.2. Visuotactile Contact Prediction

- The study investigated the ability to predict local tactile contact events from visual observations. A transformer-based encoder was trained to map visual streams and robot actions to a binary contact/no-contact signal.
- The model achieved a mean accuracy of 88.67% (compared to 50.31% random guessing) and an F1-score of 0.8909 across 36 synchronized camera views. This demonstrated the dataset's utility in capturing the coupling between visual surface changes and underlying contact physics.

#### 4.3. World Model Benchmarking

A systematic comparison of state-of-the-art 2D video models (Cosmos-Predict 2.5) and 3D particle models (ParticleFormer, PGND, PhysTwin) was performed across three generalization settings.

#### 4.4. Per-episode (Frame Generalization)

- Models were trained on initial frames of an episode and evaluated on subsequent frames within the same episode.
- **PhysTwin**, a physics-based approach, demonstrated superior performance in both reconstruction and future prediction (CD of 0.014, track error of 0.025, PSNR of 26.574 for prediction). This indicated that explicit physical and structural priors were beneficial for accurate dynamics modeling in low-data scenarios.
- Learning-based models (PGND, ParticleFormer) showed higher errors. Cosmos was not included due to insufficient per-episode data for stable post-training.

#### 4.5. Multi-episode (Episode Generalization)

- Models were trained on a subset of interaction episodes for a given object and tested on unseen episodes of the same object. PhysTwin was excluded as it requires per-episode registration, limiting its cross-episode generalization without manual intervention.
- **Cosmos** achieved superior reconstruction performance (PSNR of 27.748, LPIPS of 0.034), indicating its ability to preserve visual textures in 2D space.
- **ParticleFormer** outperformed in future prediction tasks (CD of 0.051, track error of 0.079, PSNR of 25.203), suggesting that its explicit 3D structural priors allowed for more robust generalization of object dynamics to novel interaction sequences, despite Cosmos's visual synthesis capabilities.

#### 4.6. Multi-object (Object Generalization)

- Models were trained on a set of objects and tested on entirely unobserved objects (zero-shot generalization). PhysTwin was not applicable due to its reliance on specific object geometries.
- **Cosmos** demonstrated better zero-shot generalization in image quality metrics (PSNR of 25.042, LPIPS of 0.037), outperforming 3D counterparts. This was attributed to its large-scale pre-training, which enabled it to leverage visual diversity for novel object dynamics.
- A failure mode for Cosmos involved not strictly following robot commands in long-horizon predictions, though the resulting dynamics often remained physically reasonable. Current 3D world models, lacking massive pre-training, exhibited limitations in this zero-shot setting.

#### 4.7. Real-world Robot Planning

- A preliminary demonstration involved deploying models learned from Deform360 in a Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for robot planning tasks on deformable objects.
- The experiments were conducted using a different robot setup (xArm) in a different lab environment, indicating potential for zero-shot transfer.
- **PhysTwin-based representations** were successfully used within the MPC framework to plan robot actions towards a goal state.
- Cosmos was not deployed for real-world planning due to sensitivity to appearance differences across environments and the difficulty of designing effective reward functions directly on generated videos.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Deform360 provides a foundational benchmark for research in deformable object manipulation and world modeling, addressing a significant data gap in the field. Its large scale, diversity, multi-view coverage, and synchronized visuotactile data offer a unique resource for developing and evaluating advanced robotic perception and control systems.

The systematic benchmarking reveals critical insights into the trade-offs between different world modeling paradigms:
- **Structural priors in 3D models** (e.g., PhysTwin, ParticleFormer) are beneficial in low-data regimes and for robust generalization to novel interaction sequences.
- **Scalability and pre-training in 2D video models** (e.g., Cosmos) enable strong zero-shot visual generalization to entirely new object categories.

The demonstration of real-world robot planning using models trained on Deform360 highlights the dataset's practical utility, suggesting a path toward building generalizable, physically grounded world models for complex robotic manipulation tasks. The insights gained regarding the limitations of current models, particularly concerning action alignment in video models and the need for scalable 3D pre-training, can guide future research directions. Ultimately, Deform360 is intended to inspire the development of more sophisticated world models that combine the strengths of both 2D visual fidelity and 3D physical reasoning.
