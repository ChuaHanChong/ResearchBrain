# Q-VGM: Q-Guided Value-Gradient Matching for Flow-Matching VLA Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Q-Guided Value-Gradient Matching (Q-VGM) is an off-policy reinforcement learning method designed to fine-tune flow-matching vision-language-action (VLA) policies. It generates denoising-time velocity corrections by leveraging first-order value-gradient information from an off-policy critic, leading to an average success rate increase from 75.0% to 92.5% on the LIBERO benchmark and an average 27.5 percentage point improvement on real-robot tasks.

## Problem

- Efficiently fine-tuning expressive flow-matching VLA policies using reinforcement learning is challenging due to the lack of tractable action likelihoods.
- Direct Q-function backpropagation through the multi-step denoising process of large VLA models is numerically unstable and leads to long gradient paths.
- Existing value-based methods either only refine inference without policy updates or fail to provide direct value-aware supervision to the intermediate velocity field of flow policies.

## Method

- Develops an action-sensitive, off-policy Cal-QL critic that provides stable action-space value gradients for clean action chunks, effectively compressing VLA features into a 2048-dimensional RL Token (RLT).
- Constructs Q-guided velocity corrections by performing Q-gradient ascent on a one-step clean-action estimate at each denoising step, anchored to the behavior policy's distribution.
- Trains the policy's residual velocity to match these Q-gradient-derived corrections, using a denoising-time gate to modulate guidance strength and circumventing direct Q-backpropagation through the flow model.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **92.5%** on the LIBERO benchmark (40 tasks), a significant improvement from the initial **75.0%** (SFT baseline) and outperforming all comparative methods.
- Demonstrated an **87.2%** average success rate on RoboTwin **2.0** (10 bimanual tasks), an increase from **76.4%** (SFT baseline), with substantial gains on complex coordination tasks like "lift pot" (+16 percentage points).
- Showcased real-robot applicability with an average success rate improvement of **27.5** percentage points across two tabletop manipulation tasks (e.g., Pick Peach from **45.0%** to **75.0%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action-space critic gradients can be effectively translated into denoising-time velocity corrections for flow policies, providing direct supervision to the intermediate velocity field without requiring tractable action likelihoods.
> - Adapting optimal control frameworks (VGG-Flow) allows for connecting terminal action-space Q-gradients to intermediate velocity field adjustments, bypassing the instability of direct Q-backpropagation in large VLA models.
> - An action-sensitive critic, achieved through repeated action conditioning in its architecture and an ensemble approach, is crucial for generating reliable and useful action-space gradients for policy fine-tuning.
