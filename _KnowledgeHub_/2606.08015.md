# Q-VGM: Q-Guided Value-Gradient Matching for Flow-Matching VLA Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> Q-Guided Value-Gradient Matching (Q-VGM) is an off-policy reinforcement learning method designed to fine-tune flow-matching vision-language-action (VLA) policies. It generates denoising-time velocity corrections by leveraging first-order value-gradient information from an off-policy critic, leading to an average success rate increase from 75.0% to 92.5% on the LIBERO benchmark and an average 27.5 percentage point improvement on real-robot tasks.

## Problem

- Efficiently fine-tuning expressive flow-matching VLA policies using reinforcement learning is challenging due to the lack of tractable action likelihoods.
- Direct Q-function backpropagation through the multi-step denoising process of large VLA models is numerically unstable and leads to long gradient paths.
- Existing value-based methods either only refine inference without policy updates or fail to provide direct value-aware supervision to the intermediate velocity field of flow policies.

## Method

- Develops an action-sensitive, off-policy Cal-QL critic that provides stable action-space value gradients for clean action chunks, effectively compressing VLA features into a 2048-dimensional RL Token (RLT).
- Constructs Q-guided velocity corrections by performing Q-gradient ascent on a one-step clean-action estimate at each denoising step, anchored to the behavior policy's distribution.
- Trains the policy's residual velocity to match these Q-gradient-derived corrections, using a denoising-time gate to modulate guidance strength and circumventing direct Q-backpropagation through the flow model.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **92.5%** on the LIBERO benchmark (40 tasks), a significant improvement from the initial **75.0%** (SFT baseline) and outperforming all comparative methods.
- Demonstrated an **87.2%** average success rate on RoboTwin **2.0** (10 bimanual tasks), an increase from **76.4%** (SFT baseline), with substantial gains on complex coordination tasks like "lift pot" (+16 percentage points).
- Showcased real-robot applicability with an average success rate improvement of **27.5** percentage points across two tabletop manipulation tasks (e.g., Pick Peach from **45.0%** to **75.0%**).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Action-space critic gradients can be effectively translated into denoising-time velocity corrections for flow policies, providing direct supervision to the intermediate velocity field without requiring tractable action likelihoods.
> - Adapting optimal control frameworks (VGG-Flow) allows for connecting terminal action-space Q-gradients to intermediate velocity field adjustments, bypassing the instability of direct Q-backpropagation in large VLA models.
> - An action-sensitive critic, achieved through repeated action conditioning in its architecture and an ensemble approach, is crucial for generating reliable and useful action-space gradients for policy fine-tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are an emerging paradigm in robotic control, designed to integrate multimodal reasoning with continuous action generation. These models, conditioned on visual observations, robot proprioception, and language instructions, aim to execute diverse manipulation skills and adapt to new environments. Flow-matching policies, in particular, have gained traction in continuous control within VLA architectures due to their ability to model multimodal action distributions by learning a velocity field that transforms noise into action chunks through iterative denoising.

While imitation learning has been a primary method for fine-tuning VLAs, reinforcement learning (RL) offers an alternative for task-aware post-training adaptation. However, applying RL to flow-matching VLAs presents specific challenges. Standard policy-gradient methods typically require a tractable action likelihood, which is not directly available in iterative denoising processes used by flow policies. Approaches that attempt to recover approximate likelihoods often introduce stochastic flow variants or surrogate objectives. Furthermore, policy-gradient methods primarily utilize zeroth-order reward information, potentially leading to lower sample efficiency compared to value-gradient methods that can leverage first-order signals (∇_A Q).

Existing value-based methods to address these challenges fall into several categories:
- **Direct Q-maximization:** This approach integrates a critic-value objective into the policy loss, propagating gradients through the entire denoising chain. While it connects the critic to the flow policy, it can lead to long and unstable gradient paths, especially problematic for large VLA models.
- **Test-time Q-selection or Q-guidance:** These methods use a critic solely during inference to select or refine sampled actions. The underlying policy remains unchanged, limiting the extent of improvement.
- **Q-guided action distillation:** This approach updates the policy by treating critic-improved actions as supervised action labels. However, this method converts value improvement into terminal action cloning, thereby not directly supervising the intermediate velocity field, which is central to flow policies.

The core challenge that Q-VGM addresses is the lack of a direct mechanism for value-aware supervision of the intermediate velocity field in flow-matching policies. The critic evaluates clean, executable action chunks, while the flow policy operates on noisy intermediate states. Therefore, value improvement needs to be expressed as a denoising-time velocity correction rather than a terminal action label. Q-VGM positions itself by adapting existing optimal control frameworks for flow models, specifically VGG-Flow, to this off-policy VLA fine-tuning context, thereby circumventing the limitations of prior methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an off-policy reinforcement learning method, Q-Guided Value-Gradient Matching (Q-VGM), that can efficiently fine-tune expressive flow-matching VLA policies by effectively exploiting first-order gradient information from a learned Q-function.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges inherent in applying RL to flow-matching VLAs:
- **Difficulty in exploiting first-order information:** While value-gradient methods can offer better sample efficiency by utilizing the action-space gradient ∇_A Q, flow policies do not provide tractable action likelihoods required by policy-gradient methods.
- **Numerical instability of direct Q-backpropagation:** Directly backpropagating the value through the multi-step denoising process of flow policies is numerically unstable, particularly at the scale of VLA models, which often involve billions of parameters. Existing methods like Diffusion-QL face this stability issue.
- **Limitations of existing value-based methods:**
 - Test-time Q-selection/guidance improves inference but does not update the policy, leaving the core policy capabilities unchanged.
 - Q-guided action distillation treats critic-improved actions as terminal labels, neglecting the internal flow structure and failing to supervise the intermediate velocity field. This overlooks the fundamental aspect of how flow policies operate.
- **Lack of denoising-time value-aware supervision:** The critic operates on clean action chunks, whereas the flow policy generates actions through a sequence of noisy intermediate states. There is a gap in translating terminal value improvement into a useful correction for these intermediate states.

To address these, Q-VGM aims to:
1. **Exploit critic gradients without unstable backpropagation:** By leveraging VGG-Flow, Q-VGM seeks to transform the value gradient into a denoising-time value-gradient field, avoiding direct backpropagation through the full denoising chain.
2. **Operate without action likelihoods:** The proposed method does not require explicit action likelihoods, which are unavailable for iterative denoising processes.
3. **Provide direct velocity-field supervision:** It formulates policy improvement as an optimal-control problem over the denoising process, generating velocity corrections that directly supervise the intermediate velocity field of the flow policy.
4. **Enable practical few-shot initialization and learn-from-experience:** The method is designed to start from a few-shot supervised fine-tuned (SFT) π_0.5 VLA and then leverage self-generated rollout data to substantially improve task performance without requiring additional expert supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Q-VGM fine-tunes a pretrained flow-matching VLA using an off-policy critic. The methodology consists of two main stages: training an action-sensitive off-policy critic and constructing value-gradient-guided velocity corrections for policy fine-tuning.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries

The base policy used is π_0.5, a flow-based VLA model that predicts an action chunk A_k given an observation o_k, language instruction ℓ, and proprioceptive state p_k. The VLM backbone provides conditioning context c_k, and a flow action expert generates the action chunk. In fine-tuning, the VLM backbone is frozen. The flow path is defined as x_t = (1-t)A + tϵ, where t=0 corresponds to clean actions and t=1 to Gaussian noise. The action expert learns a velocity field v_θ(x_t, t, c_k) via conditional flow matching. Inference involves discretizing the flow ODE from noise to a clean action.

The work frames policy improvement as a KL-regularized objective, aiming to find a policy p*(x_0) ∝ p_base(x_0) · exp(r(x_0)/λ). For flow models, this is equivalent to adding a residual velocity h to the base dynamics, solving a stochastic optimal control problem where the optimal residual velocity h*(x_t, t) is proportional to the gradient of a denoising-time value function V(x_t, t), specifically h*(x_t, t) = β ∇_x_t V(x_t, t), with a terminal condition ∇_x_0 V(x_0, 0) = -∇_x_0 r(x_0). Q-VGM instantiates this framework with r(x_0) = Q_ψ(o, x_0).

#### 3.2. Action-Sensitive Chunk Critic

The critic, Q_ψ(o, x_0), is designed to provide action-space value gradients ∇_A Q(s, A) for clean action chunks.
- **State Representation:** To manage the high dimensionality of VLA features, the VLA prefix tokens are compressed into a 2048-dimensional RL Token (RLT) readout vector z_rl, concatenated with projected proprioception to form the critic state c(s). The VLA backbone and RLT encoder are frozen during critic and policy training.
- **Chunk-level Value Learning:** The critic operates on temporally extended action chunks. An ensemble Cal-QL critic is trained offline on chunk-level transitions (s_t, A_t, R_t, s_{t+Ha}, A_{t+Ha}) from a replay buffer. The Bellman backup uses the next action chunk from the dataset, ensuring fully off-policy learning. The ensemble mean Q(s, A) is used for action ranking and gradient computation.
- **Action-Sensitive Architecture:** To ensure the critic remains responsive to local action changes, the action chunk A is re-injected at every hidden layer of each critic head. This repeated conditioning helps in generating useful ∇_A Q(s, A).

#### 3.3. Q-Guided Value-Gradient Matching

This component translates the critic's action-space value gradients into velocity corrections for the flow policy.
- **Look-forward Clean-Action Estimate:** For each denoising step x_k at time t_k, a one-step Euler projection under the frozen base velocity, ˆA_k^base = x_k - t_k sg[v_base(x_k, t_k, s)], estimates the clean action. This anchors the estimate to the behavior policy's distribution, keeping it within the critic's training support.
- **Constructing the Velocity Correction:**
 1. Starting from ˆA_k^base, J projected Q-gradient ascent steps are performed: ˆA_k,j+1 = Π_A[ˆA_k,j + α clip_G(∇_A Q(s, A) | _{A=ˆA_k,j})]. Π_A clips to valid action ranges, and clip_G bounds gradient magnitude.
 2. A "keep-best" mechanism selects the highest-valued candidate among all iterates, j* = argmax_j∈{0...,J} Q(s, ˆA_k,j). This mechanism ensures that if gradient ascent fails to improve value, the original base prediction is retained.
 3. The resulting velocity correction is calculated as h_k^eff = (ˆA_k^base - ˆA_k,j*)/t_k, which represents a shift in the flow towards the critic-improved action. This process implicitly provides a per-sample adaptive effective β, controlling guidance strength.
- **Residual Velocity Matching:** A denoising-time gate s(t_k) = (1-t_k)^p modulates the correction strength, focusing guidance where the clean-action approximation is more reliable (i.e., closer to t=0). The action expert's residual velocity is trained to match this h_eff using the loss: L_align = Σ_k s(t_k) ||(v_θ(x_k, t_k, s) - v_base(x_k, t_k, s)) - sg[h_k^eff]||^2. Gradients only flow through the local prediction v_θ, while other components are stop-gradiented. At inference, the fine-tuned policy (v_θ = v_base + h_θ) amortizes the critic guidance, eliminating test-time search.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The efficacy of Q-VGM was evaluated across three distinct environments: LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0, and real-robot tabletop tasks. The experiments followed a paradigm of few-shot SFT initialization, followed by fixed SFT-policy rollouts for critic training, and subsequent offline policy fine-tuning without additional environment interaction. All critic-based baselines utilized the same SFT checkpoint, rollout data, RLT features, and Cal-QL critic.

#### 4.1. LIBERO

On the LIBERO benchmark, comprising 40 tabletop tasks with a 7-DoF EEF action space, Q-VGM demonstrated consistent improvements over the few-shot SFT initialization and all baselines.
- **Overall Performance:** The average success rate across 40 tasks increased from an initial 75.0% (π_0.5 few-shot SFT) to 92.5%.
- **Task Categories:** Significant gains were observed across Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long suites. Notably, LIBERO-Long, a challenging suite, saw its success rate improve from 56.0% to 84.0%.
- **Comparison to Baselines:**
 - Test-time Q Selection and Q Guidance improved success to 82.0% and 85.0%, respectively, but they do not modify the policy.
 - Q-Improved Action Distillation reached 86.3%, indicating that while amortizing critic-improved actions is beneficial, Q-VGM's velocity-level value-gradient matching is more effective.
 - Diffusion-QL, which relies on direct Q-backpropagation, performed worse than the SFT policy, achieving 69.5%, suggesting instability for large VLA models.

#### 4.2. RoboTwin 2.0

Evaluations on RoboTwin 2.0, involving 10 bimanual manipulation tasks with a 14-DoF dual-arm qpos action space, further supported Q-VGM's effectiveness.
- **Overall Performance:** The average success rate increased from 76.4% (SFT baseline) to 87.2%, surpassing all comparative methods.
- **Task-Specific Gains:** Substantial absolute gains were observed on tasks with lower initial SFT performance, such as "lift pot" (+16 percentage points), "place shoe" (+16 percentage points), and "handover mic" (+16 percentage points), indicating the method's ability to address coordination and contact-rich failure modes.
- **Baseline Comparison:** Test-time methods provided moderate improvements but plateaued around 80%, whereas Q-VGM demonstrated greater potential.

#### 4.3. Real-Robot Evaluation

A small-scale real-robot study on two tabletop tasks (Pick Peach and Stack Bowls) using a 7-DoF arm confirmed the method's real-world applicability.
- **Results:** Starting from a π_0.5 SFT baseline (30 demonstrations per task), Q-VGM improved the success rate by +30.0 percentage points on Pick Peach (from 45.0% to 75.0%) and +25.0 percentage points on Stack Bowls (from 35.0% to 60.0%), leading to an average improvement of +27.5 percentage points.

#### 4.4. Ablation and Analysis

Ablation studies on LIBERO investigated the contribution of key components:
- **Critic-Side Ablations:**
 - Replacing the RLT encoder with a ResNet encoder caused a significant drop to 82.5%, highlighting the importance of rich VLA-derived state features.
 - Removing per-layer action injection reduced success to 87.5%, indicating the necessity of repeated action conditioning for action sensitivity.
 - Using a single critic head instead of an ensemble lowered success to 89.5%, suggesting benefits of ensembling for stable action gradients.
- **Policy-Side Ablations:**
 - Disabling "keep-best" selection resulted in 88.5%, demonstrating its role in adaptively preserving the base action when gradient ascent overshoots.
 - Applying velocity alignment at all denoising steps (s(t)=1) rather than gated guidance (s(t)=(1-t)^p) yielded 86.0%, supporting the focused application of guidance.
 - Removing the frozen-base velocity anchor for look-forward estimation reduced success to 86.5%, confirming that anchoring stabilizes velocity targets.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Q-Guided Value-Gradient Matching (Q-VGM) method represents a contribution to the field of robotic control, particularly for fine-tuning flow-matching VLA policies.

- **Addressing Fundamental Challenges:** The work directly addresses the long-standing challenges of efficiently improving flow-matching VLA policies with RL. It circumvents the numerical instability of direct Q-backpropagation through complex denoising chains and the lack of tractable action likelihoods, which have been significant obstacles for applying RL to these models.
- **Novel Application of Optimal Control:** By adapting the VGG-Flow framework to conditional robotic action flows, Q-VGM offers a novel approach to translate action-space critic gradients into denoising-time velocity corrections. This allows for direct, value-aware supervision of the intermediate velocity field, which is critical for flow-matching policies, unlike previous methods that treated value improvement as terminal action cloning.
- **Practical Fine-Tuning Paradigm:** Q-VGM enables a practical "few-shot initialization then learn-from-experience" paradigm. This allows for leveraging a small amount of initial expert data (few-shot SFT) and then refining the policy using self-generated rollout data, reducing the need for extensive expert supervision or complex online interaction.
- **Demonstrated Effectiveness:** The consistent and substantial performance improvements observed across diverse benchmarks—including simulated tasks (LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0) and real-robot experiments—underscore the method's robustness and generalizability. The gains achieved on complex bimanual manipulation tasks and real-world scenarios indicate its potential for practical robotic applications.
- **Enhanced Robot Capabilities:** By increasing average success rates significantly (e.g., from 75.0% to 92.5% on LIBERO, 40.0% to 67.5% on real-robot tasks), Q-VGM contributes to more reliable and capable general-purpose robot control, especially in tasks requiring dexterous and long-horizon manipulation.

**Limitations:**
The primary limitation identified by the authors is the reliance on the learned critic Q(s, A) for action-space value gradients. The reliability of ∇_A Q(s, A) is highest within the distribution of actions supported by offline rollouts. Critic errors outside this region can lead to misleading velocity corrections. While the method includes gradient clipping and a "keep-best" mechanism to mitigate this, a more robust solution could involve adaptively bounding path-space deviations.
A broader limitation is the scalability and generalization of Q-learning as task horizons and environment diversity increase. Future work could explore combining world models with value estimation to shorten effective TD horizons via multi-step rollouts while still utilizing value gradients for matching.
