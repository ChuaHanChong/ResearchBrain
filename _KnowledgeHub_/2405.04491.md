# TorchDriveEnv: A Reinforcement Learning Benchmark for Autonomous Driving with Reactive, Realistic, and Diverse Non-Playable Characters

> [!summary] Summary
> TorchDriveSim is a lightweight differentiable 2D driving simulator written entirely in PyTorch, with configurable kinematic models, heterogeneous agents, traffic lights, and differentiable infraction metrics. Its benchmark extension TorchDriveEnv wraps it in a Gym interface and calls a behavioural API (INITIALIZE, DRIVE) to populate scenes with reactive, realistic, diverse NPCs, adding separate train and validation scenarios. Standard RL baselines learn but never drive infractions to zero.

## Problem

- Existing autonomous-vehicle simulators lack realistic traffic behaviour and are hard to modify as training or testing engineering requirements change over time.
- Multi-agent driving benchmarks drive non-ego agents by rule-based heuristics or log replay, so those agents are non-reactive or lack behavioural diversity.
- Most RL benchmarks train and test in the same environment, and prior simulators combine neither data-driven scenario generation nor train-validation generalization splits.

## Method

- **TorchDriveSim**: pure-PyTorch batched 2D simulator with differentiable rendering, swappable kinematic models (bicycle, unconstrained, teleporting), and differentiable collision, off-road, and wrong-way metrics.
- **TorchDriveEnv**: Gym-standard step/reset wrapper calling ==INITIALIZE== (conditional ==diffusion==-based, traffic-light-aware NPC placement) and ==DRIVE== (learned human-like behaviour) endpoints, offloading heavy NPC models behind an API.
- Benchmark task definition: continuous steering-acceleration action space, 3x3x64x64 egocentric birds-eye observation stack, end conditions on collision/off-road/traffic-light, and reward combining movement plus waypoint bonuses minus a heading-smoothness penalty.

## Results

- Across ==A2C==, ==PPO==, ==SAC==, and ==TD3== baselines trained to roughly **0.8-0.9 million** timesteps, SAC and PPO were the two most performant algorithms on every training and validation benchmark.
- Adding reactive NPCs sharply raised difficulty: multi-agent settings gave markedly lower return and episode horizon than single-agent, with returns near **200** versus about **300** single-agent.
- Policies trained multi-agent transferred well to single-agent settings, while single-agent-trained policies failed to generalize to multi-agent; all agents retained non-trivial collision and off-road rates.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Training against reactive, diverse simulated traffic yields policies that transfer to simpler settings, whereas ego-only training produces controllers that break once other agents react.
> - Average return hides safety failures: high-reward policies still collided and left the road, so benchmarks need infraction-explicit metrics alongside cumulative reward.
> - Serving expensive generative behaviour models behind an API keeps the local simulator lightweight and differentiable, decoupling scenario realism from a researcher's available compute.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development and deployment of autonomous vehicles (AVs) heavily relies on simulation for training, testing, and validation due to the high costs, time consumption, and safety risks associated with real-world testing. Over the past decade, advancements in deep learning and AV simulation have significantly contributed to the progress of the AV industry. Consequently, academic and industry researchers have focused on creating more realistic and efficient simulators to solve complex problems in autonomous control and computer vision.

Despite these efforts, many existing AV simulators present several technical limitations. These often include a lack of realistic traffic behavior, difficulty in modification to meet evolving engineering requirements, and an insufficient integration with modern computation graphs and deep learning frameworks. Simulators commonly employ rule-based systems or replay historical data for non-ego agents (NPCs), which can limit the realism, reactivity, and diversity of simulated traffic interactions. While some benchmarks address multi-agent environments or incorporate expert data for behavioral cloning, many do not fully capture the complexity of real-world dynamic interactions or provide robust generalization testing capabilities.

The research presented here addresses these gaps by introducing TorchDriveSim and its benchmark extension, TorchDriveEnv. TorchDriveSim aims to provide a lightweight, differentiable, and easily modifiable 2D simulator built entirely in PyTorch, which natively supports deep learning paradigms. TorchDriveEnv extends this by integrating with a state-of-the-art behavioral simulation API to generate reactive, realistic, and diverse NPCs, a feature often lacking or less sophisticated in other simulators. Furthermore, TorchDriveEnv distinguishes itself by including both training and validation environments, which explicitly facilitate the assessment of generalization capabilities, a critical aspect of reinforcement learning benchmarks like MetaWorld and ProcGen, but often absent in AV-specific contexts with data-driven scenario generation. This work positions itself as a tool to bridge the gap between efficient simulation and the need for realistic, complex, and generalizable AV controller development.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to introduce TorchDriveSim, a lightweight 2D driving simulator, and its benchmark extension, TorchDriveEnv, as a comprehensive platform for autonomous driving research using reinforcement learning. The motivation stems from several identified limitations in existing autonomous vehicle (AV) simulators and benchmarks.

**Motivation for TorchDriveSim:**
Existing AV simulators are often described as lacking realism in traffic behavior and flexibility for modification as training and testing requirements evolve. Additionally, many do not fully leverage advancements in computation graphs and deep learning. To address these, TorchDriveSim was motivated by the need for a simulator that is:
- **Efficient and Accessible:** Programmed entirely in Python and built on PyTorch, providing native support for deep learning frameworks.
- **Differentiable:** Capable of producing a computation graph that includes state transition functions, kinematic models, and observations via differentiable rendering. This feature can facilitate gradient-based optimization in control and learning.
- **Customizable:** Allows for easy modification of kinematic models and rendered observations to test various factors in learned vehicle behavior.
- **Comprehensive in Metrics:** Includes differentiable metrics for collisions, off-road infractions, and wrong-way driving.
- **Scalable:** Provides first-class support for batch processing.
- **Modular:** Defined by a collection of wrappers that can alter simulator behavior.
- **Supportive of Diversity:** Facilitates diverse traffic environments through heterogeneous agent types (vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists) and includes control information like traffic lights.

**Motivation for TorchDriveEnv:**
Building upon TorchDriveSim, TorchDriveEnv was motivated by the need for a reinforcement learning (RL) benchmark suite that offers:
- **Realistic External Agent Behavior:** Unlike many replay-based or rule-based approaches, it integrates with a state-of-the-art commercial behavioral simulation API to provide reactive, realistic, and diverse Non-Playable Characters (NPCs). This aims to create more challenging and representative interaction scenarios.
- **Standardized RL Environment:** Adheres to the OpenAI Gym environment standard with `step` and `reset` functions, ensuring quick and easy integration with existing RL libraries.
- **Generalization Testing:** Includes distinct validation and training environments to allow users to verify the generalization capabilities of learned controllers, addressing a common shortcoming in RL benchmarks where training and testing often occur in the same environment.
- **Scenario Customization:** Provides an environment labeling tool for users to define their own unique AV scenarios, including static initial conditions and waypoint information, which can then be combined with dynamic NPC behavior.

In essence, the overarching objective is to provide a user-friendly, flexible, and realistic simulation and benchmark environment that directly supports the development and rigorous evaluation of advanced autonomous driving algorithms, particularly those leveraging deep reinforcement learning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology involves the development of two interconnected software packages: TorchDriveSim, a core simulation engine, and TorchDriveEnv, a benchmark suite built on top of it, specifically designed for reinforcement learning.

**TorchDriveSim (Simulator)**
TorchDriveSim is a lightweight, 2D driving simulator developed entirely in PyTorch. Its design prioritizes differentiability, modularity, and realism for AV research.

- **Core Architecture:** The simulator is structured around a `simulator` class which initializes a static background, including road and lane markings represented by a rendered triangle mesh. It also incorporates control elements like traffic lights and yield signs, each with an internal state.
- **Agents:** Agents (cars, pedestrians, bicycles) are represented as rigid rectangles. Each agent possesses a static state (e.g., length, width), a dynamic state (x, y, orientation, speed), and a 'present mask' flag indicating activity.
 - **Kinematic Models:** The default kinematic model for non-ego vehicles is the bicycle model, where actions are steering and acceleration. Other models, such as an unconstrained model (action defines state change) and a teleporting model (action directly sets next state), are also provided and can be customized.
 - **Interactions and Infractions:** The simulator detects collisions when agents overlap. Overlap or non-overlap with bounding boxes or lane markings identifies other infractions like off-road or wrong-way driving. All these metrics are designed to be fully differentiable.
- **Differentiability:** Crucially, TorchDriveSim produces a computation graph that includes state transition functions based on kinematic models and observation information derived from differentiable rendering. This allows for gradient propagation through the simulation.
- **Modularity with Wrappers:** The system uses a modular collection of wrappers that can modify simulator behavior. Examples include controlling subsets of agents, removing agents that exit designated areas, or monitoring and visualizing infractions. These wrappers are composable, allowing for flexible configuration.
- **Batch Processing:** The simulator provides first-class support for batch processing to enhance efficiency in model training and evaluation.

**Generative Models of Vehicle Behaviour (NPCs)**
To achieve realistic, reactive, and diverse traffic behavior, TorchDriveEnv integrates TorchDriveSim with an external, state-of-the-art commercial API for behavioral simulation. This integration abstracts complex AV models and their computational requirements behind a simple API layer.

- **API Endpoints:** Two primary API endpoints are utilized:
 - `INITIALIZE`: Called during simulator initialization, this endpoint populates the scenario with randomized but realistic NPCs. It leverages recent work in conditional diffusion generative modeling and is traffic-light state-aware. Users can manually set initial conditions for some NPCs, and additional agents are then conditionally sampled based on these placements.
 - `DRIVE`: Invoked during the environment's `step` function, this endpoint provides driving behaviors for each initialized NPC, leveraging advances in models of human-realistic vehicle behaviors.
- **Purpose:** This approach enables complex, data-driven NPC behavior without requiring local high-performance computing resources for the behavioral models themselves.

**TorchDriveEnv (Benchmark Environment)**
TorchDriveEnv bundles TorchDriveSim with the API-based behavioral models to define scenarios for training and testing AV algorithms. It follows the OpenAI Gym standard for environment construction.

- **Scenario Definition:** All maps in the benchmark are derived from the CARLA simulator. Scenarios are configured such that the user controls only the ego agent, while all other agents (NPCs) are controlled by the `DRIVE` API. The ego agent's initial position is randomized along scenario-specific waypoints.
- **Action Space:** The ego agent's action space is continuous, defined by a steering angle (b ∈ (−0.3, 0.3)) and acceleration (c ∈ (−1.0, 1.0)).
- **Observation Space:** The observation space is an egocentric birds-eye view rendered as a 3 × 3 × 64 × 64 tensor. The first index captures historical frames (current and previous two time-steps).
- **End Conditions:** Simulation halts upon: ego agent going off-road, traffic light violation by the ego agent, collision with another agent, or reaching a maximum number of timesteps.
- **Reward Function:** The reward structure is designed to guide learning:
 - `Movement Bonus`: Awarded for traveling at least 0.5 meters within a time-step.
 - `Waypoint Bonus`: Awarded when the agent reaches within two meters of the next waypoint, which is then popped from the queue.
 - `Smoothness Penalty`: Penalizes based on the cosine similarity between headings at current and next time-steps, encouraging smoother driving.
 - Specific coefficients (α1=1, α2=10, β1=0.05) are used for the benchmark results.
- **Training and Validation Scenarios:** The benchmark includes distinct training and validation environments for both single-agent (ego-only) and multi-agent settings, facilitating the assessment of generalization.
- **Scenario Editor:** A provided environment labeling tool allows users to define custom scenarios.

**Benchmark Evaluation:**
The paper evaluates TorchDriveEnv using common reinforcement learning baselines (A2C, PPO, SAC, TD3) integrated via the stable-baselines library. Performance is reported using metrics such as cumulative reward (return), infraction rates (collision, traffic light, off-road percentages), average time-horizon, and average number of waypoints achieved across multi-agent and single-agent training and validation environments.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates common reinforcement learning (RL) baselines (A2C, PPO, SAC, and TD3) within TorchDriveEnv, reporting metrics for both training and validation environments across single-agent (ego-only) and multi-agent settings. The key findings are derived from the analysis of collision rates, traffic light violations, waypoint achievement, off-road infractions, cumulative reward (return), and episode horizon (length).

1. **Increased Difficulty in Multi-Agent Environments:**
 - The inclusion of reactive external agents (NPCs) significantly increased the difficulty of the environment for the learning agents.
 - In multi-agent training and validation scenarios, RL agents consistently achieved lower cumulative rewards and shorter episode horizons compared to their performance in single-agent environments.
 - Conversely, infraction rates (collisions, traffic light violations, off-road incidents) were generally higher in multi-agent environments, indicating that agents struggled more to maintain safe and compliant driving behavior.

2. **Algorithm Performance:**
 - Among the tested algorithms, Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) and Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) generally exhibited the most performant behavior across all benchmarks, often achieving higher returns and longer horizons while showing relatively lower infraction rates compared to A2C and TD3.

3. **Generalization Capabilities:**
 - **Multi-agent trained agents:** Agents trained in multi-agent environments demonstrated better generalization. They were observed to function reasonably well when evaluated in both single-agent and multi-agent validation environments. This suggests that exposure to complex, reactive NPC interactions during training leads to more robust policies.
 - **Single-agent trained agents:** Agents trained exclusively in single-agent (ego-only) environments did not generalize effectively to multi-agent settings. When evaluated in multi-agent validation scenarios, these agents exhibited significantly worse performance, characterized by substantially lower episode horizons and cumulative rewards, and higher infraction rates. This outcome underscores the importance of multi-agent simulation for developing AV controllers that can handle realistic traffic interactions.

4. **Persistent Infractions:**
 - Despite the best-performing algorithms (SAC and PPO) achieving notable improvements in cumulative reward, even these models maintained non-trivial rates of collision and off-road infractions in the multi-agent environments. This indicates that while agents learned to navigate and achieve waypoints, completely eliminating unsafe behaviors remained a significant challenge, even after millions of timesteps of training.

In summary, the benchmark results demonstrate that TorchDriveEnv is a challenging environment, particularly with reactive NPCs. The findings highlight the critical need for multi-agent simulation to train generalizable and robust AV controllers and suggest that current standard RL baselines still struggle to achieve high performance while simultaneously ensuring safety in complex, interactive driving scenarios.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TorchDriveEnv, alongside its underlying simulator TorchDriveSim, offers several significant contributions to the field of autonomous driving research and has the potential for considerable impact.

**Scientific Significance:**
- **Differentiable Simulation:** The development of TorchDriveSim as a lightweight, differentiable simulator built entirely in PyTorch is significant. Differentiability allows for the application of gradient-based optimization techniques, potentially enabling more efficient and novel approaches to control, perception, and planning in AVs, moving beyond traditional model-free or model-based RL.
- **Realistic and Reactive NPC Behavior:** The integration with a commercial behavioral simulation API to generate reactive, realistic, and diverse Non-Playable Characters (NPCs) is a key advancement. This addresses a major limitation in many existing simulators that rely on rule-based or simple replay methods, which often fail to capture the complexity and unpredictability of real-world multi-agent interactions. The benchmark's results confirm that multi-agent training is crucial for developing robust AV policies.
- **Generalization Benchmark:** The inclusion of distinct training and validation environments for both single-agent and multi-agent scenarios provides a robust framework for assessing the generalization capabilities of learned controllers. This is critical for developing AVs that can reliably perform in unseen situations and environments, moving research beyond policies that are merely effective in their training environments. The paper explicitly demonstrates the poor generalization of single-agent trained policies to multi-agent settings, reinforcing the value of this validation approach.
- **Accessibility and Modifiability:** Being entirely in Python and based on PyTorch, TorchDriveEnv is designed for ease of use and modification. This accessibility lowers the barrier to entry for researchers and practitioners, allowing for rapid iteration and customization of kinematic models, agent types, traffic control patterns, and reward functions to explore a wide range of AV problems.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Accelerated AV Research and Development:** By providing a highly customizable, efficient, and realistic simulation platform, TorchDriveEnv can accelerate the development and testing of novel reinforcement learning algorithms for autonomous driving. Researchers can more quickly iterate on policy designs and evaluate their robustness in complex, dynamic environments.
- **Improved AV Safety and Reliability:** The emphasis on realistic NPC interactions and generalization testing directly contributes to the development of safer and more reliable AV systems. Training agents in environments with diverse and reactive traffic can lead to controllers that are better equipped to handle unexpected real-world scenarios, thereby reducing the "simulation to reality gap." The finding that even top RL algorithms struggle to eliminate infractions suggests a clear direction for future research in safety-critical AI.
- **Standardization for Benchmarking:** TorchDriveEnv's adherence to the OpenAI Gym standard promotes its adoption as a common benchmark, allowing for standardized comparisons of different RL and AV algorithms. This fosters reproducibility and enables clearer progress tracking within the research community.
- **Enabling Future Research Directions:** The paper explicitly outlines future research avenues, such as incorporating expert-trained imitation learning models, RL algorithms with pre-trained feature encoders, and leveraging TorchDriveSim's differentiable dynamics. This indicates that the platform is not just a benchmark but also a foundational tool for exploring advanced topics in intelligent control and learning. The challenge of balancing reward maximization with zero infractions highlights a critical area for ethical and safety-focused AI research.

In summary, TorchDriveEnv serves as a valuable resource for the autonomous driving research community, providing a platform that emphasizes realism, generalization, and flexibility, thereby facilitating the development of more advanced and robust autonomous driving solutions.
