# UniRecGen: Unifying Multi-View 3D Reconstruction and Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> The UniRecGen framework unifies multi-view 3D reconstruction and generation to create complete, consistent 3D object models from sparse, unposed multi-view images. It establishes new state-of-the-art performance on Toys4K and GSO datasets for 3D reconstruction and significantly improves camera pose and depth estimation.

## Problem

- Deterministic 3D reconstruction methods often produce geometrically incomplete models and lack global structural priors.
- Stochastic 3D generation models struggle to maintain strict fidelity to input-specific details and multi-view consistency, often leading to geometric 'hallucinations'.
- Previous 2D-to-3D unification approaches, which rely on lifting 2D information, suffer from 'representation gaps' and consistency artifacts.

## Method

- A two-stage modular pipeline of **UniRecGen** first trains a ==feed-forward reconstruction module==, then a ==reconstruction-guided controllable generation module==.
- Utilizes ==branch repurposing== in the reconstruction stage to canonicalize camera-centric geometry predictions into an object-centric space, making them compatible with generative priors.
- Employs ==latent-augmented view conditioning== in the generation stage, enriching multi-view conditioning signals with view-dependent geometric context from reconstruction outputs and refined camera tokens.

## Results

- Achieved state-of-the-art 3D reconstruction performance on Toys4K (**Chamfer-L2 0.0175, F-Score 0.7695**) and GSO (**Chamfer-L2 0.0163, F-Score 0.7818**) datasets, outperforming prior methods like ReconViaGen.
- Demonstrated superior camera pose estimation (**ATE 0.0151** on GSO) and improved depth prediction accuracy (**Abs Rel 0.0033** on GSO) compared to baselines.
- Generated 3D meshes with higher structural fidelity and superior multi-view consistency from sparse inputs, showing robustness and effective generalization to real-world environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Native 3D unification of reconstruction and generation, by addressing disparate learning dynamics and representation incompatibility, effectively combines input fidelity with generative completeness.
> - Canonicalizing reconstruction outputs via a targeted 'branch repurposing' strategy enables seamless integration with object-centric generative models while preserving pre-trained priors.
> - Enhancing multi-view generative conditioning with latent-augmented geometric context from the reconstruction module significantly improves 3D shape modeling performance and consistency.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of 3D object modeling from sparse observations presents a challenge in balancing reconstruction fidelity with generative plausibility. Historically, two distinct paradigms have addressed this: deterministic reconstruction and stochastic generation.

Deterministic reconstruction methods, exemplified by feed-forward regression models, prioritize efficiency and direct alignment with input 2D observations. However, these methods often produce geometrically incomplete models and may lack semantic consistency due to their reliance on local features and an absence of global structural priors.

Conversely, stochastic generation methods, such as 3D diffusion models, leverage object-centric priors to synthesize plausible and detailed geometries. While capable of generating rich details, these approaches frequently struggle with maintaining strict faithfulness to input-specific fine-grained details, which can result in geometric "hallucinations" and deviations from the actual object shape. They also face challenges in ensuring multi-view consistency.

A recent trend in the research landscape involves attempting to unify these paradigms by aligning the latent spaces of 2D generative models with 3D reconstruction systems. Works such as VIST3A and Gen3R aim to make 2D generation "3D-aware" through model stitching or adapter-based regularization. However, these methods remain predominantly tethered to the 2D domain, often inheriting limitations such as a "representation gap" and consistency artifacts inherent in 2D-to-3D lifting processes.

Within 3D generation, advancements have progressed from GANs to diffusion models, supporting various 3D representations like point clouds, voxel grids, NeRFs, SDFs, and 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS). Latent diffusion approaches have been employed to reduce computational cost. Score distillation sampling from pretrained 2D diffusion models has also been used, but it often entails costly test-time optimization and can yield inconsistent geometry. Generating high-fidelity 3D assets from *unposed* multi-view RGB images remains an underexplored area, as it demands precise geometry and strong multi-view consistency without known camera poses. Existing pose-free reconstruction methods like PF-LRM are limited by their representation, while others like ReconViaGen employ multi-stage pipelines.

In multi-view 3D reconstruction, traditional methods like Structure-from-Motion (SfM) and Multi-View Stereo (MVS) are sensitive to camera pose accuracy and struggle with sparse inputs. Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) offer high-quality synthesis but can overfit sparse views and require per-scene optimization. Feed-forward architectures, particularly those inspired by 3DGS, directly regress Gaussian parameters and offer speed and generalization but rely on local features, leading to incomplete reconstructions and artifacts in occluded regions. The dependency on external pose estimation remains a bottleneck, although some pose-free methods exist (e.g., DUSt3R, MASt3R, VGGT), they often produce point clouds lacking surface topology or completeness.

UniRecGen positions itself as a native 3D unification framework, aiming to bridge the gap between deterministic reconstruction and stochastic generation. It conceptualizes these two areas not as competing methodologies but as complementary dimensions of the same 3D inference problem, specifically addressing the creation of complete and consistent 3D models from sparse, unposed observations. This approach seeks to overcome the limitations of previous attempts at unification by operating directly in 3D and addressing fundamental incompatibilities between the two paradigms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to achieve high-fidelity 3D object modeling from sparse, unposed multi-view images by integrating 3D reconstruction and 3D generation into a single, cooperative system. The motivations for this unification stem from the inherent limitations of each paradigm when used in isolation, as well as the challenges in combining them effectively:

1. **Addressing Incompleteness in Reconstruction:** Deterministic feed-forward reconstruction methods, while efficient and aligned with input data, often fail to produce geometrically complete 3D models. They tend to reconstruct only visible regions and lack the global structural priors necessary to infer unobserved parts of an object accurately, leading to models with holes or missing components.

2. **Improving Fidelity and Multi-View Consistency in Generation:** Stochastic 3D generation models, particularly diffusion-based approaches, can synthesize plausible and detailed geometries by leveraging strong object-centric priors. However, they frequently struggle with maintaining strict fidelity to the specific fine-grained details present in the input images. This can result in "hallucinations" where the generated geometry deviates from the actual object, and they may also lack robust multi-view consistency across different input perspectives.

3. **Overcoming Limitations of 2D-to-3D Lifting Approaches:** Previous efforts to unify generation and reconstruction have often involved aligning 2D generative models with 3D reconstruction systems (e.g., VIST3A, Gen3R). These methods are fundamentally rooted in the 2D domain and typically suffer from a "representation gap" and consistency artifacts when lifting 2D information to 3D. The motivation is to move beyond these 2D-centric approaches towards a native 3D unification that inherently understands and operates within 3D space.

4. **Resolving Disparate Learning Dynamics:** A significant technical challenge in unifying these two paradigms is their distinct learning dynamics. Reconstruction models employ deterministic regression, while diffusion models utilize stochastic iterative denoising. A naive joint training approach would likely lead to slow convergence and prohibitive computational overhead due to the need for each module to adapt to the fluctuating and noisy outputs of the other. The research aims to develop a stable training strategy for such a combined system.

5. **Addressing Representation and Coordinate Incompatibility:** Another core technical challenge lies in the incompatibility of 3D representations and coordinate systems. Feed-forward reconstruction typically operates in camera-relative spaces using explicit geometric features. In contrast, diffusion-based generation often occurs in a compact latent space, where features are mapped to and interpreted within a canonical, object-centric coordinate system. This misalignment impedes effective information flow and structural collaboration between the two modules. The objective is to establish a shared, unified coordinate and representation space.

By addressing these motivations and technical challenges, UniRecGen aims to develop a framework that combines the strengths of both reconstruction (input fidelity) and generation (completeness and plausibility) to produce high-fidelity, complete, and multi-view consistent 3D models from sparse, unposed image inputs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

UniRecGen proposes a unified framework that integrates feed-forward multi-view geometry estimation (reconstruction) with controllable 3D generation. The methodology is designed to address the challenges of disparate learning dynamics and representation/coordinate incompatibility through a two-stage modular pipeline.

#### 3.1. Overall Pipeline and Modular Design

The system operates as a two-stage modular pipeline to ensure stable training and allow for interchangeability of sub-modules:
1. **Stage I: Generation-compatible Feed-Forward Reconstruction:** The reconstruction module is trained first to establish a stable geometric anchor that maintains fidelity to the input observations.
2. **Stage II: Reconstruction-guided Controllable Generation:** The generation module is subsequently trained as a prior-driven refiner, leveraging the canonical output from Stage I to recover unobserved regions and synthesize fine-grained details.

This disentangled cooperative learning approach allows the system to first ground itself in visible evidence and then infer missing structures using global shape priors.

#### 3.2. Generation-compatible Feed-Forward Reconstruction

This stage focuses on canonicalizing feed-forward geometry predictions to make them compatible with object-centric generative priors. The chosen base model for reconstruction is VGGT, a scalable feedforward multi-view representation that jointly regresses camera parameters and scene geometry.

The primary challenge addressed here is the mismatch between VGGT's camera-centric, reference-frame outputs (camera poses $g_{ref}$, depth maps $D_{ref}$, point maps $P_{ref}$) and the canonical, object-centered space required by 3D generative frameworks. The paper explores three strategies and adopts "branch repurposing":

- **Branch Repurposing (Our Approach):** This strategy repurposes only the point map head of the VGGT model to output point maps ($P_{can}$) directly in canonical space. The camera head ($g_{ref}$) and depth head ($D_{ref}$) continue to operate in the original reference frame. This approach is based on three insights:
 1. Maintaining $g_{ref}$ and $D_{ref}$ in the reference frame provides strong regularization, preserving the pre-trained priors of VGGT.
 2. Supervising only $P_{can}$ in canonical space provides dense gradient signals, facilitating effective adaptation.
 3. More accurate depth predictions from $D_{ref}$ can be transformed into canonical space through similarity alignment with the predicted $P_{can}$. This involves aligning depth-derived 3D points (obtained from $g_{ref}$ and $D_{ref}$) with $P_{can}$ using a weighted Procrustes analysis to compute a similarity transformation $T \in \text{Sim}(3)$ (rotation, translation, uniform scaling). This process refines the canonical point cloud, yielding better geometry than using $P_{can}$ alone.

#### 3.3. Reconstruction-guided Controllable Generation

In this stage, the canonicalized 3D reconstruction (point cloud) from Stage I is used as a conditioning signal for controllable 3D generation. Hunyuan3D-Omni, a latent generative paradigm supporting point-cloud conditioning, is utilized as the generative backend. The paper identifies limitations when directly applying the existing Hunyuan3D-Omni with VGGT outputs:
1. VGGT reconstructions can contain outliers and noise, which are not typical conditioning inputs for a model trained on clean data.
2. Point cloud conditioning alone provides limited geometric details and lacks continuous surface information.
3. Hunyuan3D-Omni is generally limited to single-view conditioning, failing to exploit rich multi-view image information.

To address these, UniRecGen further trains the Hunyuan3D-Omni model to leverage both point-cloud and multi-view images as conditioning signals. The joint conditioning signal `cond` is modified to include multi-view conditioned features alongside the original control embedding $\beta$: $cond = [F_{MV_1}, F_{MV_2}, \dots, F_{MV_N}, \beta]$.

The paper investigates two multi-view integration strategies for $F_{MV_i}$ and adopts "latent-augmented view conditioning":

- **Latent-Augmented View Conditioning (Our Approach):** Instead of sampling sparse features, this approach enriches the full set of DINO tokens ($F_{D_i}$) by injecting view-dependent geometric context. VGGT latent tokens ($F_{V_i}$) and refined camera tokens ($\hat{t}_{cam_i}$) are projected via learnable Multi-Layer Perceptrons (MLPs) to serve as geometric embeddings, which are then added to the DINO features: $F_{MV_i} = F_{D_i} + MLP_{view}(F_{V_i}) + MLP_{cam}(\hat{t}_{cam_i})$.
 - This strategy preserves the dense semantic priors within DINO tokens (critical for Hunyuan3D-Omni's synthesis quality) while integrating global structural coherence and multi-scale spatial context from VGGT tokens, enabling the diffusion model to better leverage multi-view information.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Data Preparation:** Trained on a filtered subset of 40K high-quality 3D models from Objaverse-XL, with 50 rendered viewpoints per object. Evaluated on 100 objects each from Google Scanned Objects (GSO) and Toys4K, using a randomly selected 4-view subset from 24 synthesized views with camera perturbations.
- **Network Training:**
 - **Stage I:** VGGT training recipe, omitting tracking loss, 80K steps with 1x10^-5 learning rate.
 - **Stage II:** Reconstruction module frozen. Hunyuan3D-Omni generator trained for 80K steps with 1x10^-5 learning rate using a flow-matching objective. Ground-truth point clouds are aligned to the predicted canonical geometry via a similarity transform before encoding to reduce supervision ambiguity.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental results demonstrate that UniRecGen achieves improvements in 3D object reconstruction and generation from sparse, unposed images compared to existing methods.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Results

- **3D Object Reconstruction (Table 1):** On both the Toys4K and GSO datasets, UniRecGen consistently produced lower (better) Chamfer-L2 distances and higher (better) Precision, Recall, F-Score, Normal Consistency, and Voxel IoU metrics. For example, on Toys4K, UniRecGen achieved a Chamfer-L2 of 0.0175, compared to the next best ReconViaGen at 0.0281. Similarly, the F-Score was 0.7695 for UniRecGen versus 0.6105 for ReconViaGen, and IoU was 0.8030 versus 0.7229. Similar trends were observed on the GSO dataset.
- **Multi-view Camera Pose Estimation (Table 2):** UniRecGen demonstrated superior accuracy in camera pose estimation. On GSO, it achieved an Absolute Trajectory Error (ATE) of 0.0151, compared to 0.0190 for ReconViaGen and 0.0799 for the original VGGT. Relative Pose Error (RPE) for translation ($RPE_t$) and rotation ($RPE_r$) were also lower (better) for UniRecGen across both datasets.
- **Depth Estimation (Table 3):** UniRecGen showed superior depth prediction accuracy, with lower Absolute Relative Error (Abs Rel) and Root Mean Square Error (RMSE) values on both GSO and Toys4K. On GSO, UniRecGen recorded an Abs Rel of 0.0033 and RMSE of 0.0057, outperforming ReconViaGen (Abs Rel 0.0051, RMSE 0.0081) and VGGT (Abs Rel 0.0512, RMSE 0.0686).

#### 4.2. Qualitative Results

- **Multi-view Consistent 3D Object Generation (Fig. 5):** Qualitative comparisons showed that UniRecGen generated 3D meshes with higher structural fidelity and superior multi-view consistency from sparse inputs compared to state-of-the-art baselines like TRELLIS-M/S, Hunyuan3D-MV, LucidFusion, and ReconViaGen. Other methods often exhibited geometric artifacts, incompleteness, or less faithful alignment with the input.
- **Generalization to Real-world Environments (Fig. 6):** The framework demonstrated robustness and maintained superior multi-view consistency and mesh quality when applied to real-world sparse-view inputs, indicating effective generalization beyond synthetic datasets.
- **Additional Qualitative Results (Fig. 7):** Further examples illustrated UniRecGen's capability to produce high-fidelity 3D object models that align well with both input images and ground-truth shapes.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies (on reduced experimental scale)

- **Canonical Alignment Strategy (Table 4, Fig. 3):**
 - "Direct Supervision Transferring" resulted in performance degradation in camera pose and depth due to disruption of pre-trained priors.
 - "Explicit Transformation Prediction" suffered from poor global SE(3) alignment (higher ATE) despite moderate depth metrics, suggesting weak gradient signals for global pose regression.
 - "Branch Repurposing (Ours)" achieved a balance, yielding the best camera alignment (lowest ATE, $RPE_t$, $RPE_r$) while maintaining comparable depth accuracy. Qualitatively, this strategy resulted in better geometric quality and canonical object space alignment.
- **Multi-view Conditioning Strategy (Table 5, Fig. 4):**
 - "Point-Guided Feature Sampling" resulted in minor artifacts and inconsistencies, attributed to the loss of dense image context during sparse point-based indexing of features.
 - "Latent-Augmented View Conditioning (Ours)" achieved better 3D shape modeling performance across most geometric metrics (e.g., Chamfer-L2 of 0.0259 vs. 0.0260, F-Score of 0.7428 vs. 0.7208). Qualitatively, it preserved dense image context more effectively, leading to improved input alignment and consistency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The UniRecGen framework represents an advance in the field of 3D object modeling, particularly for scenarios involving sparse and unposed multi-view image inputs. By proposing a native 3D unification of reconstruction and generation, the work addresses a fundamental tension between input fidelity and generative completeness that has characterized previous approaches.

The significance of this work can be understood through its technical contributions and potential implications:

1. **Bridging Paradigmatic Gaps:** UniRecGen's core contribution is its ability to seamlessly integrate deterministic 3D reconstruction and stochastic 3D generation. This is achieved by systematically addressing two key technical challenges: disparate learning dynamics (through a modular design and disentangled cooperative learning) and representation/coordinate incompatibility (through canonicalization and latent-augmented multi-view conditioning). This integration provides a pathway for leveraging the strengths of both, resulting in models that are both faithful to observed inputs and plausible in unobserved regions.

2. **Enhanced 3D Object Modeling Quality:** The consistent quantitative and qualitative improvements over existing state-of-the-art methods demonstrate the effectiveness of UniRecGen. The framework produces 3D meshes with higher structural fidelity, superior multi-view consistency, and greater geometric completeness from limited input data. This directly addresses the shortcomings of reconstruction-only methods (incompleteness) and generation-only methods (lack of input fidelity and consistency).

3. **Robustness to Unposed and Sparse Inputs:** A key practical advantage of UniRecGen is its ability to operate on unposed multi-view images. Many existing 3D reconstruction and generation techniques rely on accurate camera pose information, which is often unavailable in real-world scenarios. By building upon pose-free reconstruction foundations and canonicalizing outputs, UniRecGen offers a more robust solution for practical applications where precise camera metadata is not guaranteed.

4. **Modular and Extensible Design:** The proposed modular pipeline offers practical benefits beyond performance. It allows for stable training, provides interpretable intermediate outputs, and facilitates future development. As stronger reconstruction or generation models emerge, sub-modules can potentially be swapped without necessitating a complete retraining of the entire system, promoting long-term utility and adaptability.

5. **Foundation for Integrated 3D Vision Systems:** This work lays a foundation for more integrated and versatile 3D vision systems. By demonstrating effective unification, UniRecGen suggests a blueprint for future developments that could span various computer graphics and computer vision tasks. The ability to generate high-fidelity 3D assets from sparse visual data could impact numerous fields.

Potential areas of impact include:

- **3D Content Creation:** Enabling faster and more automated creation of high-quality 3D models for games, movies, and virtual/augmented reality applications.
- **Robotics:** Improving environmental perception and object manipulation capabilities by providing more complete and accurate 3D representations of objects from sparse sensor data.
- **Digital Archiving and Cultural Heritage:** Facilitating the digital preservation of real-world objects with greater detail and completeness from limited photographic documentation.
- **E-commerce and Product Visualization:** Generating detailed 3D models of products from a few images for online display and interactive experiences.

Looking ahead, the authors indicate plans to extend the framework to scene-level generation and incorporate texture synthesis, which would further broaden its applicability and impact on creating comprehensive 3D environments and assets.
