# Detecting Twenty-thousand Classes using Image-level Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> Detic (Detector with Image Classes) trains object detectors for 21,000 classes by effectively integrating image-level supervision, addressing the vocabulary gap between classification and detection datasets. It achieved a 2.4 mAP gain for all classes and an 8.3 mAP gain for novel classes on the open-vocabulary LVIS benchmark, and generalized to new datasets without fine-tuning.

## Problem

- Bridging the significant vocabulary gap between image classification (21K classes) and object detection (typically <1K classes) due to costly bounding box annotations.
- Addressing the limitations of existing weakly-supervised object detection (WSOD) methods that struggle with "chicken-and-egg" prediction-based label assignments for novel or rare classes.
- Improving detection performance for "long-tail" and novel categories where very few or no bounding box annotations are available.

## Method

- Employs a ==mixed supervision strategy==, co-training a standard two-stage detector with both traditional bounding-box-labeled data and large-scale image-level labeled classification datasets.
- Introduces a ==non-prediction-based classification loss== for image-labeled data, which applies the loss to fixed object proposals (e.g., the largest RPN proposal) instead of error-prone, model-predicted assignments.
- Integrates ==pre-trained CLIP embeddings as classification weights==, aligning region features with language embeddings to facilitate open-vocabulary detection.

## Results

- Achieved a **2.4 mAP** gain for all classes and an **8.3 mAP** gain for novel classes on the LVIS open-vocabulary benchmark, significantly outperforming prior methods, including a **7.7-point** gain on `mAP_novel` over ViLD-text.
- Trained the first object detector on the full **21,000 classes** of ImageNet-21K, demonstrating strong generalization to new, unseen datasets like Objects365 and OpenImages without fine-tuning.
- The 21K-class detector reached **70-80%** of the performance of dataset-specific oracle models trained with full bounding box annotations on those respective datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Modern Region Proposal Networks (RPNs) already possess strong generalization capabilities for localization, allowing innovation to focus primarily on the classification sub-problem of object detection.
> - Simple, non-prediction-based assignment strategies for image-level labels, such as selecting the largest proposal ("max-size"), provide a more robust and effective training signal than complex prediction-based methods for weakly-supervised learning.
> - Large-scale image-level supervision, especially when combined with vision-language models like CLIP, can effectively bridge the performance gap for novel and rare classes, substituting for costly bounding box annotations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper, Detic (Detector with Image Classes), addresses a critical bottleneck in the field of object detection: the limited vocabulary size of current detectors compared to the much larger vocabularies handled by image classifiers. This limitation primarily stems from the high cost and difficulty of collecting bounding box annotations, which are essential for traditional object detection training. The paper positions itself at the intersection of several active research areas in computer vision:

- **Weakly-Supervised Object Detection (WSOD):** This field aims to train object detectors using less granular supervision than bounding boxes, typically image-level labels (e.g., "this image contains a cat"). Prior WSOD methods often rely on complex, prediction-based assignment schemes to infer bounding box locations from image labels. These schemes, however, suffer from a "chicken-and-egg" problem: they require good initial detections to assign labels accurately, but good detections require ample labeled data. Detic innovates here by proposing *non-prediction-based* assignment, which is simpler and more robust, particularly for novel or rare classes where initial predictions are inherently weak.

- **Open-Vocabulary Object Detection (OVOD) / Zero-Shot Object Detection:** This area focuses on enabling detectors to recognize objects from classes that were not present in the training set with bounding box annotations. Current approaches often leverage language models (like GloVe or CLIP embeddings) to transfer knowledge from text to vision, or use knowledge distillation techniques (like ViLD). Detic builds upon these by incorporating CLIP embeddings but significantly enhances OVOD performance by integrating large-scale image-level supervision more effectively than prior distillation-based methods. It shows that direct co-training with image-labeled data can outperform complex distillation or ensembling strategies.

- **Large-Vocabulary Object Detection and the Long-Tail Problem:** As datasets grow to hundreds or thousands of classes (e.g., LVIS), the "long-tail" problem emerges, where a few frequent classes have abundant data, while many rare classes have very few samples. This imbalance severely degrades detector performance on rare classes. Detic directly tackles this by using image-level supervision to boost performance for rare and novel classes, effectively closing the performance gap between frequent and rare categories.

- **Proposal Network Generalization:** The paper benefits from the observation that modern Region Proposal Networks (RPNs) already possess a strong ability to generalize and localize objects, even for unseen classes. This allows Detic to focus its innovation on the *classification* sub-problem of detection, rather than redesigning the entire localization pipeline.

In essence, Detic provides a simple, yet highly effective, framework that synergistically combines the strengths of large-scale image classification datasets, state-of-the-art detection architectures, and powerful vision-language models (CLIP) to overcome the vocabulary limitations of traditional object detection. It represents a significant step towards real-world, adaptable object detectors that can recognize a vast and diverse range of objects.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of Detic can be summarized as follows:

- **Bridging the Vocabulary Gap:** The most fundamental motivation is to address the stark disparity in vocabulary size between image classification datasets (e.g., ImageNet with 21K classes and 14M images) and object detection datasets (e.g., LVIS with 1K classes and 120K images). Traditional object detectors are inherently limited by the availability of expensive bounding box annotations, restricting their applicability to a narrow set of predefined categories. Detic aims to expand this vocabulary significantly.

- **Leveraging Abundant Weak Supervision:** The authors recognized that while bounding box annotations are scarce, image-level labels are much easier and cheaper to collect, resulting in massive classification datasets. A key objective was to devise an effective mechanism to leverage this abundant "weak supervision" from image classification data to enhance object detectors.

- **Overcoming Limitations of Prior WSOD Methods:** Existing weakly-supervised object detection techniques often rely on model predictions to assign image labels to proposals. This creates a challenging "chicken-and-egg" problem, especially for novel or rare classes where initial model predictions are unreliable. A crucial objective for Detic was to sidestep these complex and error-prone prediction-based assignment schemes with a simpler, more robust alternative.

- **Improving Open-Vocabulary and Long-Tail Detection:** The project aimed to not only expand the detector's vocabulary but also to achieve superior performance on open-vocabulary benchmarks (detecting unseen classes) and for rare classes within existing large-vocabulary datasets. The goal was to close the performance gap for object categories with few samples or no box annotations.

- **Enabling Generalization to New Datasets Without Fine-tuning:** A highly ambitious objective was to train a single, large-vocabulary detector that could generalize effectively to entirely new datasets with disjoint label spaces, without requiring any specific fine-tuning. This would represent a significant leap towards truly general-purpose object recognition systems.

The motivation stems from the practical need for object detectors that can handle a much wider range of concepts in the real world, without incurring prohibitively high annotation costs. By decoupling the localization and classification sub-problems and focusing on improving the latter with readily available data, Detic seeks to make large-vocabulary object detection more accessible and performant.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Detic (Detector with Image Classes) proposes a straightforward yet highly effective methodology to integrate image-level supervision into object detection. The core idea revolves around decoupling the localization and classification tasks and primarily using image-level labels to boost the classification component of a detector.

**Problem Setup and Architecture:**
Detic operates within a standard two-stage object detection framework (e.g., CenterNet2, Faster R-CNN with a ResNet/Swin-B backbone). The first stage, a Region Proposal Network (RPN), generates object proposals. The second stage then takes features from these proposals to perform classification and bounding box regression. The paper explicitly states that the RPN and bounding box regressors are not the current performance bottleneck, as modern RPNs demonstrate good generalization. This allows Detic to focus its innovation on the classification head. For open-vocabulary capabilities, Detic utilizes pre-trained CLIP embeddings as the classification weights, aligning region features with language embeddings.

**Mixed Supervision Strategy:**
Detic trains the detector using a mixed-batch approach, combining images from two types of datasets:
1. **Detection Datasets (D_det):** These datasets provide traditional bounding box and class labels. For images from D_det, Detic uses standard detection losses, including RPN loss, bounding box regression loss, and classification loss, training all components of the detector.
2. **Image Classification Datasets (D_cls):** These datasets provide only image-level class labels (e.g., ImageNet-21K, Conceptual Captions). For images from D_cls, Detic *only trains the classification sub-problem*. The localization losses (RPN and regression) are not computed for these images.

**Non-Prediction-Based Classification Loss for Image-Labeled Data:**
This is the central innovation of Detic. Unlike prior weakly-supervised methods that try to assign image labels to proposals based on model predictions (which can be unreliable for novel classes), Detic proposes a fixed, non-prediction-based scheme:

- **Key Insight:** Prediction-based assignment leads to a "chicken-and-egg" problem where accurate labels require good initial predictions, which are lacking for rare/novel classes. A fixed assignment, even if imperfect, offers a more consistent and reliable training signal.
- **Variants Explored:**
 - `L_image-box`: Uses the entire image as a single "proposal" and applies the classification loss to its corresponding RoI features.
 - `L_max-object-score`: Selects the proposal with the highest "objectness" score (as predicted by the RPN) and applies the classification loss to its features.
 - `L_max-size` (Default and Best Performing): Selects the proposal with the largest bounding box area. This approach proved most effective because larger proposals are more likely to encompass the target object, even if they are coarse, and their selection is stable during training.
- **Loss Function:** For the selected proposal, a Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss is applied for all ground-truth image-level labels present in the image. The classification parameters (W) are shared between the detection and classification data, allowing the image-level supervision to directly enhance the detector's classifier.

**Training Details:**
- **Sampling Ratio:** Mini-batches from D_det and D_cls are typically sampled in a 1:1 ratio.
- **Resolution Adaptation:** Smaller input resolutions (e.g., 320x320) are used for image-level labeled images (like ImageNet) because they are often more object-centric and smaller than detection dataset images. This also allows for larger batch sizes.
- **Training Schedule:** Models are often first trained on base-class-only detection data and then fine-tuned with additional image-labeled data for an extended schedule.
- **Large-Vocabulary Training (21K classes):** To handle the massive 21K classes of ImageNet-21K efficiently, Detic employs a modified Federated Loss strategy, uniformly sampling a subset of classes (e.g., 50) at each iteration for classification score computation and backpropagation.

This methodology results in a system that is simple to implement, compatible with various detector architectures, and robust to the challenges of open-vocabulary and long-tail detection, making it a highly practical approach for scaling object recognition.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Detic demonstrates significant advancements across open-vocabulary, long-tail, and large-vocabulary object detection benchmarks:

- **Superior Performance on Open-Vocabulary LVIS:**
 - Detic achieved a notable **2.4 mAP gain** for all classes and a substantial **8.3 mAP gain** for novel classes on the open-vocabulary LVIS benchmark when using ImageNet-L for image-level supervision, compared to a strong box-supervised baseline.
 - Crucially, Detic's performance for novel classes closely matched or even exceeded that of a fully-supervised detector that had access to box annotations for all classes, despite Detic not using novel-class box labels. This suggests that image-level supervision can effectively substitute for expensive box annotations in many cases.
 - **Outperformance of Prediction-Based Methods:** Detic's non-prediction-based methods consistently and significantly outperformed prior complex prediction-based weakly-supervised detection techniques (e.g., Self-training, WSDDN, DLWL*, YOLO9000). For example, it provided a ~4.2 mAP gain on novel classes over the best prediction-based method with ImageNet-L, and ~2.6 mAP with Conceptual Captions. This validates the simplicity and robustness of Detic's approach.
 - Among the non-prediction-based variants, the **`max-size` loss** consistently yielded the best performance, indicating its effectiveness in reliably covering objects and providing consistent training signals.

- **State-of-the-Art Comparisons:**
 - **Open-Vocabulary LVIS (vs. ViLD):** Detic (with IN-L) achieved a 7.7-point gain on `mAP_novel` compared to ViLD-text and outperformed ViLD's ensemble model with a single model, demonstrating superior efficiency and performance over distillation-based methods.
 - **Open-Vocabulary COCO (vs. OVR-CNN):** Using the same detector, training recipe, and data as OVR-CNN, Detic significantly outperformed it by 5 mAP on the open-vocabulary COCO benchmark, further solidifying its state-of-the-art status.

- **Training a 21K-Class Detector and Generalization:**
 - For the first time, Detic successfully trained a detector using the full **21,000 classes of the ImageNet-21K dataset** with a Swin-B backbone.
 - This large-vocabulary detector demonstrated remarkable **generalization capabilities to new datasets (Objects365, OpenImages) *without any fine-tuning***. It achieved 21.5 mAP on Objects365 and 55.2 mAP50 on OpenImages, significantly improving over baselines trained only on LVIS.
 - The performance of Detic's 21K-class model reached 70%-80% of dataset-specific oracle models that were trained with full bounding box annotations on those respective datasets, highlighting its strong transfer learning ability.

- **Ablation Studies and Robustness:**
 - **Classifier Agnosticism:** Detic's benefits were observed across different types of classifiers (trained classifier, FastText, OpenCLIP), although CLIP embeddings yielded the best performance. This shows its general applicability beyond a single language model.
 - **Orthogonality to Pretraining:** Detic provided consistent gains regardless of the backbone's pretraining data (ImageNet-1K or ImageNet-21K), suggesting its approach is complementary to advances in pretraining.
 - **Standard LVIS Benchmark:** On the standard LVIS benchmark (all classes supervised with boxes), Detic achieved 41.7 mAP for both all classes and rare classes (`mAP_rare`), effectively closing the performance gap for categories with very few samples.
 - **Architectural Flexibility:** Detic also generalized well to Transformer-based detectors, demonstrating improvements when applied to Deformable-DETR.

In summary, the main findings show that Detic's simple, non-prediction-based use of image-level supervision provides substantial, consistent, and broad improvements in object detection, particularly for scaling vocabulary, enhancing open-vocabulary capabilities, and addressing the long-tail problem.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The research presented in Detic has profound significance and substantial potential impact across several dimensions of computer vision and its applications:

- **Democratization and Scalability of Object Detection:** Detic fundamentally changes how large-vocabulary object detectors can be trained. By effectively leveraging readily available image-level labeled data, it significantly lowers the barrier to entry for expanding detector vocabularies. This moves beyond the prohibitively expensive and time-consuming process of collecting millions of bounding box annotations, making it feasible to train detectors for tens of thousands of classes.

- **Reduced Annotation Burden:** The ability to achieve strong performance for novel classes without requiring any bounding box annotations for those classes directly translates to a massive reduction in annotation costs. This is critical for practical applications where new objects or domains constantly emerge, allowing for rapid deployment and adaptation of detectors.

- **Enhanced Open-World Recognition:** Detic significantly pushes the boundaries of open-vocabulary object detection. Its improved generalization capabilities mean that a single detector can potentially recognize a vast array of objects, including those it has never explicitly seen with box-level supervision. This is a crucial step towards truly "open-world" vision systems that can understand and interact with their environments more comprehensively.

- **Addressing the Long-Tail Problem Effectively:** The notable improvement in performance for rare classes (`mAP_rare`) on benchmarks like LVIS indicates that Detic provides a powerful solution for the long-tail problem. In real-world scenarios, where data distributions are inherently imbalanced, this ability to robustly detect infrequent objects is invaluable for tasks ranging from inventory management to surveillance.

- **Simplicity and Reproducibility:** The elegance and simplicity of Detic's "max-size" non-prediction-based loss are significant. It is easy to implement, compatible with various modern detector architectures (two-stage and Transformer-based), and robust across different data sources. This simplicity enhances its accessibility and encourages wider adoption and further research, as evidenced by the authors' commitment to open-sourcing their code.

- **Foundational Research for General AI:** The training of a detector with 21,000 classes that generalizes to unseen datasets without fine-tuning represents a landmark achievement. This capability forms a stronger foundation for developing more general and versatile AI systems that can reason about a broader spectrum of visual concepts, akin to human perception.

- **Broadening Real-World Applications:** The practical implications are vast. Industries like autonomous driving, robotics, augmented reality, e-commerce, and content moderation can benefit immensely from detectors that can reliably identify a much wider range of objects without continuous, expensive re-training. For example, a robot could be deployed in a new environment and recognize novel tools or objects based on prior image-level exposure.

In conclusion, Detic is not just an incremental improvement; it presents a paradigm shift in how large-vocabulary object detectors can be trained and deployed. Its simplicity, effectiveness, and scalability are poised to significantly accelerate progress in making object recognition models more robust, adaptable, and practically useful in diverse real-world scenarios.
