# Always Skip Attention

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Adelaide University and CSIRO found that the self-attention mechanism in Vision Transformers requires skip connections to prevent catastrophic training failure, attributing this to its intrinsic ill-conditioning. They introduced Token Graying, a pre-conditioning method that improved ViT accuracy on ImageNet-1K by up to 0.3% with minimal computational cost.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers (ViTs) catastrophically fail to train if skip connections are removed from the self-attention block, an observation not shared by other network components or CNNs.
- A lack of theoretical understanding explaining why the self-attention mechanism, unlike other layers, is uniquely dependent on skip connections for stable training.
- The ongoing need to develop effective methods for improving the conditioning of input tokens in ViTs to enhance performance and stability.

## Method

- Empirically demonstrated that removing skip connections from the self-attention block in ViTs leads to catastrophic training failure, while removal from Feedforward Networks (FFNs) results in only a modest performance drop.
- Developed a theoretical framework showing that the ==condition number of a linear self-attention output embedding== (without skip connections) is bounded by the cube of the input condition number, and proved that ==skip connections regularize== this ill-conditioning.
- Proposed ==Token Graying (TG)== methods, SVDTG and the computationally efficient DCTTG, to pre-condition input tokens by adjusting singular values or Discrete Cosine Transform coefficients.

## Results

- Removing skip connections from the self-attention block resulted in a catastrophic **22%** accuracy loss on CIFAR-10 for ViT-Tiny and complete training failure on Tiny-ImageNet, whereas FFNs experienced only a modest **2%** drop.
- The computationally efficient DCT Token Graying (DCTTG) consistently improved Top-1 accuracy by **0.2-0.3%** across various ViT architectures (e.g., ViT-Base to **81.3%** from **81.0%**) in supervised and self-supervised settings on ImageNet-1K.
- DCTTG achieved these performance improvements with negligible computational overhead, adding less than **1%** to the training time, making it practical for real-world application.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The self-attention mechanism is intrinsically ill-conditioned, with its output embedding's condition number bounded by the cube of the input's, making it uniquely reliant on skip connections for stable optimization.
> - Skip connections serve as fundamental regularizers for self-attention, critically stabilizing gradient flow and signal propagation, rather than merely offering a performance benefit.
> - Pre-conditioning input tokens through methods like DCT Token Graying can further improve ViT performance and conditioning, complementing the role of skip connections.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is situated at the intersection of several critical and rapidly evolving areas within deep learning: Vision Transformers (ViTs), the self-attention mechanism, skip connections, and the optimization theory of deep neural networks.

**Vision Transformers (ViTs)** have revolutionized computer vision, displacing traditional Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) in many tasks due to their ability to capture long-range dependencies and excellent scaling properties. The paper acknowledges ViT's impressive performance across diverse applications, including object detection, semantic segmentation, video understanding, and visual-language learning.

At the heart of ViTs is the **self-attention mechanism**, which dynamically weights interactions between tokens. Understanding its fundamental properties and limitations is crucial for designing more effective and efficient transformer architectures. This paper specifically investigates the stability and conditioning of this mechanism during training.

**Skip connections (or residual connections)**, famously introduced by He et al. in ResNets, have been indispensable for training very deep neural networks by mitigating vanishing gradients and improving optimization. They have become a de facto component in modern deep architectures, including transformers. While their empirical necessity is well-established, the *precise theoretical reasons* for their critical role in specific transformer components have remained less explored or debated. Prior work by He et al. attempted to train deep transformers *without* skip connections, achieving comparable performance but at a substantial computational cost (five times more iterations), leaving the underlying reasons unresolved. This paper directly addresses this gap by offering a theoretical characterization.

Furthermore, the paper engages with the broader field of **neural network optimization and conditioning**. Poor conditioning of the network Jacobian is known to be detrimental to gradient descent training, impeding convergence and stability. By linking skip connections to regularization of conditioning, the authors contribute to a deeper understanding of the interplay between architectural design and optimization dynamics.

In summary, this work provides a novel theoretical explanation for a critical empirical observation within ViTs, specifically regarding the self-attention mechanism's unique dependency on skip connections. It distinguishes this dependency from other architectural components and previous network types, thereby advancing the foundational understanding of transformers and guiding future architectural innovations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The paper is motivated by a "curious empirical result" observed in modern Vision Transformers and aims to provide a robust theoretical and practical understanding of this phenomenon. The key objectives are:

1. **To empirically highlight a catastrophic failure mode in ViTs**: The primary objective is to demonstrate that the self-attention mechanism in ViTs *catastrophically fails to train* unless it is used in conjunction with a skip connection. This is presented in stark contrast to other elements of a ViT, like the Feedforward Network (FFN), which can still perform reasonably (albeit suboptimally) without skip connections, and older architectures like CNNs, where skip connections are beneficial but not strictly essential for basic training. This unobserved phenomenon in prior literature provides a strong impetus for the research.
2. **To theoretically characterize the fundamental ill-conditioning of self-attention**: The core theoretical objective is to explain *why* the self-attention mechanism is intrinsically ill-conditioned. The authors posit that this inherent ill-conditioning is the root cause of its unique and critical dependence on skip connections for regularization, which in turn ensures stable and convergent gradient descent training. This involves making simplifying assumptions about the relationship between output embedding conditioning and Jacobian conditioning to enable practical analysis.
3. **To propose an effective complementary regularization method**: Building on the insights gained from analyzing self-attention's conditioning issues, the paper aims to develop a simple yet effective method, called Token Graying (TG), to further improve the conditioning of input tokens. This method is designed to complement existing skip connections, enhancing ViT performance in both supervised and self-supervised learning settings. The ultimate goal is to offer practical tools derived from a deeper theoretical understanding.

The overarching motivation is to move beyond empirical observations of skip connection utility in transformers and provide a *principled, theoretical explanation* for their necessity, particularly for the self-attention block. This deeper understanding is crucial for guiding the design of more robust, efficient, and performant ViT architectures in the future.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a multi-faceted methodology, combining empirical observations, theoretical analysis, and novel algorithmic development, followed by extensive experimental validation.

**A. Empirical Observation and Problem Definition:**
The research begins by empirically demonstrating the differential impact of removing skip connections from various ViT components.
- **ViT-Tiny Experiments:** They train ViT-Tiny models on datasets like CIFAR-10 and Tiny-ImageNet.
 - Removing skip connections from the FFN block leads to a modest performance drop (e.g., 2% on CIFAR-10).
 - Crucially, removing skip connections from the Self-Attention Block (SAB) results in a *catastrophic performance drop* (e.g., 22% on CIFAR-10) and even *failure to train* on larger datasets like Tiny-ImageNet, with training loss diverging (Fig. 1c, Fig. 2).
- **Condition Number Analysis:** They measure the condition number of the output embeddings of SAB and FFN blocks, with and without skip connections, in a pre-trained ViT-Tiny model. This reveals that the SAB output embedding without skip connections is significantly ill-conditioned (condition number ~e^6), compared to other configurations (~e^3) (Fig. 1b).
- **Contrast with CNNs:** To highlight the uniqueness of this phenomenon in transformers, they conduct a similar experiment on ConvMixer, a modern CNN architecture. They find that removing skip connections in ConvMixer causes negligible performance change (Fig. 6 in Appendix), reinforcing the idea that SAB introduces an extreme conditioning issue unique to ViTs.

**B. Theoretical Analysis:**
To explain the empirical findings, the authors develop a theoretical framework, making two simplifying assumptions:
1. The condition of the network Jacobian is bounded by the most ill-conditioned sub-block Jacobian.
2. The condition of the output embedding of a sub-block serves as a proxy for the condition of its Jacobian.
- **Ill-Conditioning of Self-Attention (Proposition 4.1):** They theoretically demonstrate that for a simplified linear self-attention mechanism, the condition number of the SAB output embedding (without skip connection) is bounded by the *cube* of the input matrix's condition number (κ(X)^3). This explains why applying linear self-attention across multiple layers quickly leads to severe ill-conditioning. In contrast, FFNs only bound the condition number by a linear factor of the input condition number (κ(X)). The empirical analysis (Fig. 3) extends this observation to the more common softmax self-attention.
- **Skip Connections as Regularizers (Proposition 4.2):** They prove that the addition of a skip connection (i.e., `X * M + X` versus `X * M`, where M is the self-attention transformation) significantly reduces the upper bound on the condition number. This demonstrates theoretically how skip connections regularize the condition of the self-attention output embeddings, enhancing stability and performance.

**C. Proposed Solution: Token Graying (TG):**
To further improve conditioning beyond skip connections, the authors propose Token Graying, a method to pre-condition input tokens.
- **SVD Token Graying (SVDTG):** This method uses Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) to decompose the input token matrix X (`UΣV^T`). It then amplifies the non-maximal singular values (`˜Σ ← ˜Σ^ϵ`) before reconstructing the token (`˜X = U˜ΣV^T`). The goal is to increase the minimal singular value relative to the maximal one, thereby reducing the condition number (`σ_max / σ_min`).
- **DCT Token Graying (DCTTG):** Recognizing the high computational cost of SVD, they propose an approximation using the Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT). DCT, computationally more efficient (`O(nd log(nd))` vs. `O(nd min(n,d))`), transforms tokens into frequency components. DCTTG manipulates these components in a similar way to SVDTG, intuitively targeting better conditioning.

**D. Experimental Validation of Token Graying:**
The effectiveness of SVDTG and DCTTG is validated through extensive experiments:
- **Supervised Learning:** Evaluated on various ViT architectures (ViT-Tiny, ViT-Base, Swin-S, CaiT-S, PVT V2 b3) and datasets (Tiny-ImageNet, ImageNet-1K). Metrics include Top-1/Top-5 classification accuracy and condition numbers of SAB output embeddings throughout training.
- **Self-supervised Learning:** Tested on MAE (Masked Autoencoders) pre-training and subsequent fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K, measuring classification accuracy.
- **Computational Efficiency:** Training times for SVDTG and DCTTG are compared to standard ViT training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The paper presents several significant findings that collectively provide a deeper understanding of Vision Transformer mechanics and offer practical improvements.

1. **Catastrophic Failure of Self-Attention Without Skip Connections**: The most striking empirical finding is that removing skip connections from the Self-Attention Block (SAB) leads to a near-catastrophic performance drop (e.g., ~22% accuracy loss on CIFAR-10 for ViT-Tiny) and complete failure to train on larger datasets like Tiny-ImageNet. This is in stark contrast to removing skip connections from the Feedforward Network (FFN), which results in only a modest performance decrease (~2%). This observation is highlighted as a novel empirical result not previously reported in literature.

2. **Intrinsic Ill-Conditioning of the Self-Attention Mechanism**:
 - **Poor Conditioning without Skips**: Empirically, the SAB output embedding without skip connections exhibits significantly higher condition numbers (approaching e^6) compared to other ViT block configurations with skip connections (around e^3). This poor conditioning directly correlates with slow convergence and eventual divergence during training (Fig. 2).
 - **Theoretical Basis**: Proposition 4.1 mathematically demonstrates that for linear self-attention, the condition number of the output embedding (without skip connections) is bounded by the *cube* of the input matrix's condition number. This polynomial growth rapidly leads to ill-conditioning across layers. FFNs, in contrast, show a linear dependency on the input condition number.
 - **Uniqueness to Transformers**: Experiments with ConvMixer (a CNN-based architecture) show that removing skip connections has a negligible impact on performance, further reinforcing that this extreme conditioning issue is specific to the self-attention mechanism in ViTs (Fig. 6 in Appendix).

3. **Skip Connections as Crucial Regularizers**:
 - **Empirical Regularization**: The presence of skip connections significantly improves the condition number of SAB output embeddings, maintaining them at a much lower level (~e^3) during training (Fig. 1b, Fig. 3).
 - **Theoretical Regularization**: Proposition 4.2 proves that adding a skip connection to the self-attention transformation (i.e., `X + SA(X)`) drastically lowers the bound on the condition number compared to `SA(X)` alone. This demonstrates their fundamental role in stabilizing the gradient flow and ensuring signal propagation.

4. **Effectiveness of Token Graying (TG) for Pre-conditioning**:
 - **Improved Conditioning**: Both SVDTG and DCTTG successfully reduce the condition numbers of SAB output embeddings throughout the training process, indicating better token conditioning (Fig. 4, Fig. 5).
 - **Performance Gains in Supervised Learning**: DCTTG consistently improves Top-1 and Top-5 classification accuracy across various ViT architectures (ViT-S, ViT-B, Swin-S, CaiT-S, PVT V2 b3) on the ImageNet-1K dataset. For instance, ViT-Base + DCTTG achieved 81.3% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming the vanilla ViT-Base at 81.0% (Table 4). Similar marginal but consistent improvements were seen across other models.
 - **Performance Gains in Self-Supervised Learning**: DCTTG also boosts performance in self-supervised pre-trained models. For an MAE pre-trained ViT-Base model, DCTTG led to a 0.2% improvement in both Top-1 (83.2% vs. 83.0%) and Top-5 accuracy during fine-tuning on ImageNet-1K (Table 5).
 - **Computational Efficiency of DCTTG**: While SVDTG is computationally expensive (increasing ViT-Base training time from 0.723 to 4.552 days on ImageNet-1K), DCTTG offers a computationally efficient alternative, with negligible overhead (0.732 days for ViT-Base + DCTTG), making it practical for real-world application (Table 1).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This paper makes several significant contributions that could have a substantial impact on the field of deep learning, particularly concerning Vision Transformers.

1. **Fundamental Theoretical Insight into ViTs**: The most profound impact is the provision of a robust theoretical and empirical explanation for *why* skip connections are not just beneficial but *catastrophically essential* for the self-attention mechanism in Vision Transformers. By identifying the intrinsic ill-conditioning of self-attention (bounded by the cube of input condition number) as the root cause, the paper offers a foundational understanding of a critical architectural component. This moves beyond mere empirical observation to a principled explanation, significantly advancing our knowledge of how transformers learn.

2. **Guidance for Future ViT Design**: The central insight—that SABs are intrinsically ill-conditioned—opens "brand new lines of inquiry for the vision community for more effective and efficient ViT design." Future transformer architectures could be designed with explicit mechanisms to manage or regularize the conditioning of attention blocks, rather than relying solely on empirically discovered shortcuts. This could lead to more stable, robust, and potentially deeper transformer models. For example, architectural modifications that inherently improve attention conditioning or specialized initialization schemes could emerge.

3. **Practical Performance Enhancement**: The proposed Token Graying (TG) methods, particularly the computationally efficient DCTTG, offer a simple yet effective way to further improve ViT performance. While the accuracy gains (typically 0.2-0.3%) might appear marginal, such consistent improvements across diverse ViT architectures and learning paradigms (supervised and self-supervised) are valuable in highly competitive benchmarks. DCTTG's low computational overhead makes it a practical addition to the ViT training toolkit.

4. **Broader Implications for Deep Learning Optimization**: The study reinforces the critical importance of matrix conditioning for stable and efficient training in general deep neural networks. It provides a concrete, high-impact example of how conditioning issues can manifest and how architectural design choices can effectively mitigate them. This understanding can inspire similar analyses in other complex deep learning models beyond transformers.

5. **Reconciliation of Prior Work**: The paper helps to contextualize and potentially reconcile findings from previous work, such as, which struggled to train deep transformers without skip connections. This prior work hypothesized "signal propagation" issues, which this paper reinterprets as a manifestation of extreme ill-conditioning, thus providing a deeper underlying mechanism.

In conclusion, "Always Skip Attention" provides a crucial piece of the puzzle in understanding Vision Transformers, solidifying the theoretical basis for a ubiquitous architectural element. Its findings are not only scientifically significant but also offer immediate practical utility and chart new directions for future research in transformer architecture design.
