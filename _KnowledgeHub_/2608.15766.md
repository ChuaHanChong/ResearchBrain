# Tac4Loco: Learning Spatiotemporal Plantar Pressure Representations for Humanoid Locomotion

> [!summary] Summary
> Tac4Loco is a tactile-perceptive humanoid locomotion policy that feeds multi-array plantar pressure, quantized into a shared sim-to-real ordinal representation, through a dual-branch spatiotemporal encoder fused with augmented proprioception in an asymmetric actor-critic. It raises slope survival from 22% to 77.9% and enables zero-shot transfer to foam and gravel terrain.

## Problem

- Proprioception-only policies infer plantar support only indirectly and reactively, after instability already begins to appear in joint states.
- Exteroceptive terrain perception predicts contact before touchdown but cannot verify whether the foot is actually supported afterward.
- Prior tactile-augmented locomotion work reduces foot sensors to binary contact, total force, or center of pressure, discarding spatial pressure topology.

## Method

- ==Topology-preserving ordinal quantization== maps simulated contact forces and calibrated FSR insole readings into shared non-uniform load bins, closing the sim-to-real tactile gap.
- ==Contact-conditioned terrain-orientation estimation== derives fore-aft/medial-lateral sole inclination plus support confidence from kinematics and pressure spread, forming augmented proprioception.
- **Tac4Loco**'s ==dual-branch encoder== pairs a state-conditioned spatial cross-attention branch over pressure tokens with a temporal convolutional branch over an eight-packet pressure-proprioception history, fused for an ==asymmetric actor-critic PPO== policy.

## Results

- Tac4Loco lifts survival from 71.7% to **96.5%** on random support-height terrain and from 22.0% to **77.9%** on slopes versus a proprioception-only baseline.
- Real-world G1 trials show completion gains such as 0/10 to **10/10** on a 15-degree-up/9-degree-down slope transition and 1/10 to **8/10** on lateral 9-degree ascent.
- Ablations show removing the pressure encoder hurts V-trench survival and lateral drift while removing terrain-orientation estimation hurts slope survival, confirming complementary contributions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Absolute force is an unstable sim-to-real interface for FSR arrays; converting to shared ordinal load levels preserves relative spatial structure that generalizes better than raw magnitude.
> - Treating tactile sensing as post-contact posterior feedback complements rather than replaces anticipatory exteroceptive perception, addressing distinct failure modes.
> - Spatial topology and temporal load-transfer history serve complementary roles, so summary statistics (total force, center of pressure) alone can hide contact configurations that matter for control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Humanoid robot locomotion on complex, unstructured terrains remains a significant challenge in robotics. Current approaches to achieving robust locomotion primarily fall into two categories: proprioceptive adaptation and exteroceptive terrain perception.

Proprioceptive methods, often employing reinforcement learning with techniques like domain randomization, curriculum learning, or temporal policy architectures, infer terrain and contact conditions indirectly from the robot's internal states (e.g., joint angles, IMU data). While these methods demonstrate robustness, their reactive nature means that terrain and contact properties are only observed after they manifest in the robot's motion, potentially leading to instability on highly uncertain surfaces. Such methods implicitly infer plantar support, which the authors argue is insufficient.

Exteroceptive methods, typically utilizing vision sensors (depth cameras, lidar) to generate elevation maps or depth images, provide a preview of the terrain for anticipatory adaptation and foothold selection. However, these methods are susceptible to issues such as occlusion, latency, calibration errors, and depth noise. Crucially, vision-based perception cannot directly verify the actual foot loading conditions *after* touchdown, which can differ significantly from pre-contact predictions due to surface compliance, friction, or unexpected foot placement errors.

A third category involves the use of foot-mounted tactile or pressure sensors to obtain direct contact information. Existing work in this area has often simplified plantar contact information, reducing it to binary contact states, total ground reaction forces, centers of pressure, or low-dimensional tactile features. The authors contend that these summary statistics overlook the spatial topology of plantar pressure—details such as contact shape, localized pressure peaks, edge loading, and load redistribution across the foot sole. This spatial topology provides a more direct and comprehensive characterization of the realized contact state. For instance, two distinct contact patterns (e.g., full sole contact vs. two localized patches) could yield similar total forces and centers of pressure, but imply different support characteristics.

Tac4Loco addresses this identified gap by proposing a framework that directly incorporates multi-array plantar pressure, preserving its spatial and temporal topology, as a primary source of post-contact feedback. This approach aims to complement existing proprioceptive and exteroceptive strategies by providing detailed, real-time information about the actual foot–ground interaction, thereby enhancing locomotion robustness on diverse and uncertain terrains.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a humanoid locomotion policy that leverages detailed, real-time plantar pressure information to achieve robust adaptation to complex and uncertain foot–ground interactions. The authors identify several motivations and specific objectives:

1. **Address limitations of existing perception methods:**
 - Proprioceptive methods infer contact indirectly, leading to inherent reactivity and potential instability in highly dynamic or uncertain contact conditions.
 - Exteroceptive (vision-based) methods provide anticipatory information but cannot verify actual contact post-touchdown, leaving a critical gap in real-time support feedback.
 - Prior tactile integration methods often oversimplify plantar data, losing crucial spatial and temporal details about how the foot is actually supported.

2. **Incorporate direct, topology-preserving plantar pressure feedback:** The core motivation is to move beyond summary statistics of plantar contact and instead utilize the full spatial and temporal distribution of pressure across the foot sole. This detailed information is hypothesized to provide a more accurate and immediate characterization of the realized contact state, enabling the robot to adapt to variations in foot placement, ground properties, and transient dynamics.

3. **Bridge the sim-to-real gap for tactile sensing:** Plantar pressure sensors, particularly Force Sensing Resistor (FSR) arrays, exhibit non-linear responses and element-dependent heterogeneity, making direct force replication from simulation to reality challenging. A key objective is to develop a robust representation that can be consistently applied across both simulated and physical environments, mitigating these sensor discrepancies.

4. **Develop a spatiotemporal representation for plantar pressure:** Locomotion involves dynamic load transfer and evolving contact patterns. The framework aims to extract both instantaneous spatial load distribution and its temporal evolution to fully inform the locomotion policy.

5. **Integrate tactile feedback with augmented proprioception:** The objective is not to replace proprioception but to complement it. The proposed system aims to fuse learned tactile representations with standard proprioceptive data, augmented with terrain estimation cues, to create a comprehensive observation space for the policy.

6. **Enable zero-shot deployment on unseen and challenging terrains:** A practical objective is to demonstrate that policies trained with this detailed tactile feedback can transfer directly to real-world scenarios, including terrains with compliant or granular properties that were not explicitly included in the simulation training. This would validate the generalizability of the learned contact-aware behaviors.

In summary, the motivation for Tac4Loco stems from the belief that direct, detailed, and topology-preserving plantar pressure feedback is crucial for robust humanoid locomotion on challenging, human-centered environments, and the framework aims to achieve this by developing novel representations and integration strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Tac4Loco is an end-to-end tactile-perceptive humanoid locomotion controller based on reinforcement learning (RL). Its core approach involves treating multi-array plantar pressure as direct, post-contact feedback to complement proprioception, providing immediate information on foot–ground interaction.

The methodology can be broken down into several key components:

**A. Problem Formulation:**
The locomotion problem is modeled as a discrete-time Markov Decision Process (MDP). At each control step, the actor receives a deployable observation `o_t` and samples an action `a_t`.
- **Observation `o_t`**: Comprises four components:
 - `b_t`: Deployable proprioceptive observation (IMU, commanded velocity, joint states, previous action, gait phase).
 - `P_t`: Current ordinal plantar pressure observation.
 - `H_t`: Recent pressure evolution and compact motion context.
 - `e_t`: Contact-conditioned terrain-orientation feature.
- **Action `a_t`**: Residual joint-position targets relative to a nominal standing pose.

**B. Plantar Pressure Sensing and Quantization:**
To bridge the sim-to-real gap inherent in FSR arrays (nonlinear response, element-dependent heterogeneity), the authors developed a shared, topology-preserving ordinal representation for plantar pressure.
- **Hardware:** Each foot is equipped with a 60-element FSR insole, digitized by a 12-bit ADC at 50 Hz.
- **Quantization Scheme:** Instead of absolute force, the system uses ordinal load levels. A quadratic ADC-to-load map `m(a)` is fitted from calibration loads for physical sensors. Simulated contact forces (`F_sim`) and real forces (`F_real`) are then quantized into discrete ordinal levels `q_i,t ∈ {0, ..., 30}` using a non-uniform binning operator `Q(F)`. This allows for higher resolution at lower loads (50g steps withing) and lower resolution at higher loads (100g steps within (1000, 2000]g). This shared ordinal alphabet is designed to absorb inter-element response discrepancies while retaining spatial load gradients.
- **Sim-to-Real Augmentation:** During training, dynamic artifacts such as force-domain gain, asymmetric hysteresis, zero-order hold, and level-domain perturbations with structured dropouts are randomized to further improve deployment robustness.

**C. Plantar Pressure Observation Construction:**
From the ordinal taxel pressures `q_i,t`, two types of observations are constructed:
- **Spatial Plantar Pressure Observation (`P_t`):** The quantized values for each foot are arranged into a topology-preserving grid, preserving the physical layout of sensors. `P_t = [P_L,t, P_R,t]` represents the bilateral spatial observation.
- **Temporal Pressure–Proprioceptive History (`H_t`):**
 - Each spatial observation `P_t` is compressed into a 21-dimensional spatial descriptor `φ(P_t)` (e.g., regional pressure proportions, contact indicators, load-balance ratio, CoP differences).
 - This descriptor `φ(P_t)` is fused with a 16-dimensional compact proprioceptive context `C_t` (base angular velocity, projected gravity, leg joint-state statistics) to form a single-frame packet `s_t`.
 - `H_t` is an eight-packet sequence `[s_t-κ5, ..., s_t-κ1, s_t-2, s_t-1, s_t]`, employing a near-dense, far-sparse sampling scheme to capture both rapid transients and extended temporal context.

**D. Contact-Conditioned Terrain-Orientation Estimation (`e_t`):**
This component provides a compact physical cue about the realized support under each foot.
- **Orientation Estimates:** Bilateral terrain-orientation estimates `θ_L,t, θ_R,t` (fore–aft and medial–lateral sole inclinations) are derived from robot base orientation and leg kinematics.
- **Support Confidence:** `c_L,t, c_R,t` are derived from the number and spatial spread of active pressure locations. Estimates are updated only under reliable contact; otherwise, the previous estimate is retained with decaying confidence.
- **Caution Cue:** An overall caution cue `η_t` is derived from bilateral confidences.
- **Augmented Proprioception:** `e_t = [θ_L,t, θ_R,t, c_L,t, c_R,t, η_t]` is concatenated with the base proprioceptive observation `b_t` to form `b_aug,t = [b_t, e_t]`.

**E. Dual-Branch Plantar Pressure Encoder:**
A neural network architecture jointly encodes `P_t` and `H_t` into complementary tactile features.
- **State-conditioned Spatial Branch (Fig. 3a):**
 - Ordinal pressure maps `P_t` are normalized, tokenized by a CNN, and augmented with spatial-position and foot-identity embeddings to form `T_p,t`.
 - A multi-head attention (MHA) mechanism conditions `T_p,t` on the augmented observation `b_aug,t`. `b_aug,t` is projected into a query token, while `T_p,t` serves as keys and values, allowing the current locomotion state to retrieve relevant plantar regions.
 - The attention output is fused with mean- and max-pooled token features and projected into the spatial latent `z_sp,t`.
- **Temporal Pressure–Proprioceptive Branch (Fig. 3b):**
 - A 1D temporal convolutional layer processes the eight-packet history `H_t` into a feature sequence `U_t`.
 - `U_t` is summarized using its latest feature, temporal mean, and latest-to-earliest difference to characterize current state, persistent context, and recent change.
 - These summaries are fed into an MLP to obtain the temporal latent `z_tp,t`.
- **Fusion:** The spatial `z_sp,t` and temporal `z_tp,t` latents are combined: `z_p,t = [z_sp,t, z_tp,t]`.

**F. Policy Learning and Training Details:**
- **Actor Input:** The combined plantar pressure representation `z_p,t` is fused with `b_aug,t` to form the actor input `x_π,t = [b_aug,t, z_p,t]`.
- **Asymmetric Actor–Critic:** The actor uses only deployable observations (`x_π,t`), while the critic additionally receives privileged simulator states (e.g., base linear velocity, foot–ground contact quantities, local height map, unperturbed pressure readings) for value estimation.
- **Optimization:** The policy is optimized using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with a terrain curriculum.
- **Reward Function:** Rewards are structured into four groups: (i) task tracking (velocity), (ii) motion regularization (torso tilt, joint limits, actions), (iii) gait and contact shaping (footfalls, swing clearance, slip, landings, residual motion), and (iv) plantar support alignment (sole normal aligning with force-weighted ground-contact normal).
- **Robustness Augmentation:** During training, plantar pressure observations are perturbed with sensor-rate variations, sensitivity shifts, noise, and temporary dropouts to enhance real-world deployment robustness.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research conducted extensive evaluations in simulation and real-world deployment on a Unitree G1 humanoid robot.

**A. Simulation Evaluation on Complex Terrains:**
Experiments were conducted in MJLab using a Unitree G1 model on various terrains: flat, gently undulating (GU), random support-height (RSH), slopes (SP), and V-trenches (VT). Three policy variants were compared against Tac4Loco: "Proprio-only" (baseline), "w/o Pressure Enc." (without spatial/temporal pressure latents), and "w/o Terrain Est." (without terrain-orientation feature `e_t`). Evaluation metrics included survival rate, linear/yaw velocity tracking errors, and directional regulation (drift angle), and step length.

1. **Overall Task Execution (Table I):**
 - Tac4Loco maintained 100% survival on flat, gently undulating, and V-trench terrains.
 - Significant improvement in survival rates was observed on challenging terrains: from 71.7% to 96.5% on RSH terrain and from 22.0% to 77.9% on slopes.
 - Linear velocity tracking error was reduced across all terrain groups, and yaw tracking generally improved or remained comparable.

2. **Directional Regulation (Table II):**
 - Tac4Loco consistently reduced the drift angle on flat, gently undulating, and random support-height terrains, indicating improved preservation of the commanded translational direction.

3. **Support Metrics (Fig. 5):**
 - Tac4Loco exhibited longer step lengths on RSH terrain, slopes, and the V-trench. This, combined with improved survival and velocity tracking, suggests more consistent and less disrupted progression under difficult support conditions.

**B. Ablation Study (Table III):**
The ablation study isolated the contributions of the dual-branch plantar pressure encoder and the contact-conditioned terrain-orientation feature.
- **Removing Terrain-Orientation Feature (`w/o Terrain Est.`):** This primarily degraded locomotion on continuously varying or inclined support. Increased velocity-tracking error on GU terrain and reduced survival/step length on slopes indicated that this feature is crucial for adapting gait to the realized support surface orientation.
- **Removing Pressure Encoder (`w/o Pressure Enc.`):** This led to degradation under localized, asymmetric, and discontinuously changing support. Lower survival and greater lateral drift in the V-trench suggested failures due to deviating from the intended path. On RSH terrain, increased step-interval variation indicated weaker regulation of changing support patterns.
- **Complementary Roles:** The results demonstrated that the terrain-orientation feature aids velocity regulation and progression over continuously varying support directions, while the dual-branch pressure encoder provides spatial and temporal contact structure essential for heading regulation and support-transition control under asymmetric or locally uncertain contact.

**C. Real-World Deployment Validation:**
Tac4Loco was deployed on a Unitree G1 robot with physical plantar pressure insoles, using the same ordinal pressure representation as in simulation. Pressure-specific training augmentations were disabled during deployment.

1. **Real-World Validation Results (Table IV):**
 - **Static Structural Terrains (Fig. 6A):** Tac4Loco achieved higher completion rates than the proprioception-only baseline across configurations with abrupt support-height changes (e.g., 9° ramp edge, lateral 9° ascent, 15° up + 9° down slope transition, 9° V-trench). The baseline often failed to climb ramps, exhibited abrupt drops, or struggled with directional control. Tac4Loco demonstrated smoother transitions and improved stability due to immediate haptic feedback after touchdown.
 - **Deformable and Granular Terrains (Fig. 6B):** Tac4Loco demonstrated zero-shot transferability to unseen compliant foam platforms and gravel roads. The proprioception-only baseline struggled significantly on compliant foam due to unreliable inference of plantar support from leg joint states. Tac4Loco maintained higher completion rates, effectively adapting to continuous variations in support force and contact area on foam, and fragmenting/time-varying loading on gravel, by explicitly modeling multi-array plantar pressure.

**D. Qualitative Observations:**
The proprioception-only baseline showed distinct failure modes, such as inability to climb, abrupt drops, or gradual drifting. Tac4Loco, in contrast, managed smooth downward transitions, maintained commanded velocity and direction, and demonstrated robust behavior on terrains where support geometry evolves post-touchdown (compliant deformation, granular rearrangement).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents Tac4Loco, a framework that advances humanoid locomotion by directly integrating detailed spatiotemporal plantar pressure information. The significance and potential impact of this work are multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced Locomotion Robustness:** By providing direct, post-contact feedback on foot–ground interaction, Tac4Loco addresses a critical limitation of previous proprioceptive and exteroceptive approaches. The ability to directly observe the spatial topology and temporal evolution of plantar pressure allows the robot to react more effectively to unexpected contact conditions, leading to significantly improved stability and task completion rates on diverse and challenging terrains (slopes, uneven surfaces, V-trenches, terrain edges).

2. **Robust Sim-to-Real Transfer for Tactile Sensing:** The development of a topology-preserving ordinal quantization scheme for plantar pressure is a notable contribution. This method effectively bridges the notorious sim-to-real gap for noisy and heterogeneous tactile sensors, making it practical to train complex tactile-perceptive policies in simulation and deploy them on physical hardware without extensive real-world data collection. This approach has implications for broader robotic applications requiring robust tactile perception.

3. **Novel Spatiotemporal Representation and Encoder Architecture:** The dual-branch encoder, explicitly decoupling state-conditioned spatial topology and temporal pressure–proprioceptive history, provides a comprehensive representation of contact. This architecture allows the policy to leverage both instantaneous contact patterns and their dynamic evolution, which is crucial for dynamic tasks like locomotion. The ablation study clearly demonstrates the complementary benefits of these branches.

4. **Zero-Shot Adaptation to Unseen Terrains:** The successful zero-shot deployment of Tac4Loco on compliant (foam) and granular (gravel) terrains, which were not present in the simulation training, indicates a high degree of generalizability. This capability is vital for humanoid robots operating in unstructured, human-centric environments where surface properties can be highly variable and unpredictable. It suggests that learning with direct contact information can lead to more fundamental and transferable adaptive behaviors.

5. **Foundation for Advanced Multi-Modal Perception:** The work lays a foundation for future integration of plantar pressure with other sensing modalities. The authors explicitly mention future work exploring multi-modal integration with exteroceptive visual sensing. Combining terrain anticipation from vision with the post-contact reactivity provided by detailed tactile feedback could lead to even more capable and agile humanoid robots.

In summary, Tac4Loco represents a step towards more contact-aware and robust humanoid locomotion, with implications for robotics research in general, particularly in areas requiring fine-grained interaction with complex and uncertain environments.
