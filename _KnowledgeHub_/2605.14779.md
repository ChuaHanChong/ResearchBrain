# Peng's Q($λ$) for Conservative Value Estimation in Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Conservative Peng’s Q(λ) (CPQL) is a model-free offline reinforcement learning algorithm that leverages a multi-step operator to achieve conservative value estimation. The method consistently outperforms single-step baselines across D4RL benchmarks while mitigating over-pessimism and demonstrating robustness to hyperparameters.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) struggles with distributional shift, leading to extrapolation errors and suboptimal policies when learned policies query out-of-distribution (OOD) state-action pairs.
- Most model-free offline RL algorithms for policy evaluation primarily rely on single-step Temporal-Difference (TD) updates, underutilizing the sequential information available in complete trajectories.
- Conservative methods like CQL, while addressing overestimation, can suffer from over-pessimism by excessively penalizing both in-distribution and OOD actions, potentially leading to suboptimal learned policies.

## Method

- The authors propose Conservative Peng’s Q(λ) (**CPQL**), a model-free offline multi-step RL algorithm that adapts the Peng’s Q(λ) (PQL) operator for conservative value estimation.
- CPQL integrates the PQL operator into a conservative Q-learning objective, replacing the standard Bellman operator with the PQL operator for iterative Q-value updates.
- The algorithm computes multi-step targets by recursively backing up rewards and future Q-values along sampled trajectories, incorporating the λ parameter to blend estimates from the behavior and target policies.

## Results

- CPQL achieves the highest performance in **22** out of **29** D4RL benchmark tasks across MuJoCo locomotion, Adroit manipulation, and AntMaze navigation, consistently outperforming single-step offline RL algorithms.
- The algorithm demonstrates reduced sensitivity to the conservatism parameter (α) compared to CQL, maintaining high performance across a wider range of α values.
- CPQL pre-training significantly benefits offline-to-online learning, enabling online fine-tuning to avoid initial performance drops and achieve faster, more robust adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The fixed point of the PQL operator in an offline setting naturally acts as an implicit behavior regularization, biasing value estimates towards the behavior policy without requiring explicit behavior policy estimation or auxiliary networks.
> - This implicit regularization effectively mitigates over-pessimism common in conservative offline RL methods, enabling the learned policy to achieve performance at least equal to the behavior policy.
> - The choice of the λ parameter in CPQL allows for a balance between the influence of the behavior and target policies, theoretically reducing the sub-optimality gap compared to single-step CQL.
