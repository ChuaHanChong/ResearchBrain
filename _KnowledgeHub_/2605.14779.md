# Peng's Q($λ$) for Conservative Value Estimation in Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Conservative Peng’s Q(λ) (CPQL) is a model-free offline reinforcement learning algorithm that leverages a multi-step operator to achieve conservative value estimation. The method consistently outperforms single-step baselines across D4RL benchmarks while mitigating over-pessimism and demonstrating robustness to hyperparameters.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) struggles with distributional shift, leading to extrapolation errors and suboptimal policies when learned policies query out-of-distribution (OOD) state-action pairs.
- Most model-free offline RL algorithms for policy evaluation primarily rely on single-step Temporal-Difference (TD) updates, underutilizing the sequential information available in complete trajectories.
- Conservative methods like CQL, while addressing overestimation, can suffer from over-pessimism by excessively penalizing both in-distribution and OOD actions, potentially leading to suboptimal learned policies.

## Method

- The authors propose Conservative Peng’s Q(λ) (**CPQL**), a model-free offline multi-step RL algorithm that adapts the Peng’s Q(λ) (PQL) operator for conservative value estimation.
- CPQL integrates the PQL operator into a conservative Q-learning objective, replacing the standard Bellman operator with the PQL operator for iterative Q-value updates.
- The algorithm computes multi-step targets by recursively backing up rewards and future Q-values along sampled trajectories, incorporating the λ parameter to blend estimates from the behavior and target policies.

## Results

- CPQL achieves the highest performance in **22** out of **29** D4RL benchmark tasks across MuJoCo locomotion, Adroit manipulation, and AntMaze navigation, consistently outperforming single-step offline RL algorithms.
- The algorithm demonstrates reduced sensitivity to the conservatism parameter (α) compared to CQL, maintaining high performance across a wider range of α values.
- CPQL pre-training significantly benefits offline-to-online learning, enabling online fine-tuning to avoid initial performance drops and achieve faster, more robust adaptation.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The fixed point of the PQL operator in an offline setting naturally acts as an implicit behavior regularization, biasing value estimates towards the behavior policy without requiring explicit behavior policy estimation or auxiliary networks.
> - This implicit regularization effectively mitigates over-pessimism common in conservative offline RL methods, enabling the learned policy to achieve performance at least equal to the behavior policy.
> - The choice of the λ parameter in CPQL allows for a balance between the influence of the behavior and target policies, theoretically reducing the sub-optimality gap compared to single-step CQL.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) addresses the challenge of learning effective policies from static datasets without further interaction with the environment. A central issue in offline RL is the distributional shift, where the learned policy’s state-action visitation distribution deviates from that of the behavior policy used to collect the data. This shift can lead to querying out-of-distribution (OOD) state-action pairs, resulting in extrapolation errors and suboptimal policy performance.

Existing model-free offline RL approaches primarily tackle this problem through various mechanisms. Conservative Q-learning (CQL) penalizes the Q-function for OOD actions to ensure conservative value estimates. Other methods introduce policy constraints to keep the learned policy close to the behavior policy (e.g., TD3+BC), quantify uncertainty using ensembles, or design algorithms that avoid querying OOD actions altogether (e.g., IQL). Many of these methods, particularly for policy evaluation, predominantly rely on single-step Temporal-Difference (TD) learning updates (e.g., Bellman operator), even though offline datasets often consist of complete trajectories.

In contrast, online RL has a history of developing multi-step operators (e.g., Peng’s Q(λ) (PQL), Retrace, Tree-backup) that leverage temporally extended trajectories. These multi-step approaches have been shown to improve learning efficiency and offer a more forward-looking perspective. However, the application and effectiveness of such multi-step operators in the specific context of offline RL, especially when combined with conservative value estimation, have remained largely unexplored. This paper aims to bridge this gap by investigating the utility of multi-step learning within the offline RL framework, particularly focusing on how it can mitigate challenges like over-pessimistic value estimation while maintaining performance.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel model-free offline multi-step reinforcement learning algorithm that effectively addresses the challenges of distributional shift and over-pessimistic value estimation inherent in offline RL. The authors identify several specific questions guiding their work:
1. **Can multi-step learning be utilized for value estimation in offline RL?** Traditional model-free offline RL algorithms often treat trajectories as fragmented single-step transitions. The authors hypothesize that leveraging the multi-step nature of offline trajectories could provide richer information about the behavior policy and help prevent the selection of OOD actions.
2. **What is a suitable multi-step operator for offline RL?** Given various multi-step operators developed in online RL, the research aims to identify one that is particularly well-suited for the offline setting. The Peng’s Q(λ) (PQL) operator is selected for its ability to fully leverage whole trajectories without requiring importance sampling or explicit behavior policy estimation, which are known sources of instability and mismatch issues in offline settings.
3. **Can the multi-step operator enhance performance in offline RL?** The paper seeks to theoretically and empirically demonstrate that integrating a multi-step operator, specifically PQL, with conservative value estimation can lead to improved performance compared to existing single-step baselines.

The motivation stems from several limitations of current offline RL methods:
- **Reliance on single-step updates:** Most model-free offline RL algorithms for policy evaluation use single-step TD updates, underutilizing the sequential information available in full trajectories.
- **Over-pessimism in conservative methods:** While methods like CQL address overestimation due to OOD actions, they can suffer from over-pessimism, leading to suboptimal policies by excessively penalizing both in-distribution and OOD actions. Existing solutions to over-pessimism often introduce additional complexity, such as estimating behavior policies or auxiliary networks, which can incur further drawbacks like distribution mismatches or increased training times.
- **Lack of multi-step theoretical and empirical evidence:** There has been no prior work in offline RL that theoretically and empirically demonstrates the effectiveness of conservative value estimation with a multi-step operator.

By proposing Conservative Peng’s Q(λ) (CPQL), the authors aim to overcome these limitations. They motivate CPQL as a method that naturally induces implicit behavior regularization through the PQL operator's fixed point characteristics, which lie closer to the behavior policy's value function. This design is intended to simultaneously mitigate over-pessimism, achieve performance at least equal to the behavior policy, and provide near-optimal performance guarantees, thereby offering a more balanced and robust approach to conservative value estimation in offline RL.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed algorithm, Conservative Peng’s Q(λ) (CPQL), is a model-free offline multi-step RL algorithm that adapts the Peng’s Q(λ) (PQL) operator for conservative value estimation.

**PQL Operator Adaptation for Offline RL:**
The foundation of CPQL lies in reinterpreting the PQL operator for offline settings. In online RL, the fixed point of the PQL operator, $Q^{\pi_\beta, \pi}_{\lambda}$, satisfies $Q^{\pi_\beta, \pi}_{\lambda} = (\lambda T^{\pi_\beta} + (1 - \lambda) T^\pi) Q^{\pi_\beta, \pi}_{\lambda}$. This implies that the fixed point is the value function of a mixture policy that interpolates between the behavior policy $\pi_\beta$ and the target policy $\pi$. While this property might be a "bias" in online RL aiming for optimality, in offline RL with a fixed behavior policy $\hat{\pi}_\beta$, this characteristic is leveraged as an implicit behavior regularization. The fixed point of the PQL operator converges to $Q_{\lambda\hat{\pi}_\beta+(1-\lambda)\pi}$, meaning it inherently biases value estimates towards the behavior policy. This property is key to mitigating Q-value overestimation caused by distributional shift.

**CPQL Objective Function:**
CPQL integrates this PQL operator into a conservative Q-learning framework. It replaces the standard Bellman operator in the CQL objective with the PQL operator. The iterative Q-value update in CPQL is defined as:
$Q_{k+1} \in \text{argmin}_Q \left[ \frac{1}{2} E_{s,a,s' \sim \mathcal{D}} \left( Q(s,a) - T^{\hat{\pi}_\beta,\pi_k}_{\lambda} Q_k(s,a) \right)^2 + \alpha \left( E_{s \sim \mathcal{D}, a \sim \pi_k(\cdot|s)} [Q(s,a)] - E_{s,a \sim \mathcal{D}} [Q(s,a)] \right) \right]$
Here, $\mathcal{D}$ is the offline dataset, $T^{\hat{\pi}_\beta,\pi_k}_{\lambda}$ is the PQL operator, $\pi_k$ is the current target policy (greedy with respect to $Q_k$), and $\alpha \geq 0$ is the conservatism parameter. The first term is a standard TD error with the PQL target, and the second term is the conservatism penalty adapted from CQL.

**Theoretical Analysis:**
The paper provides three main theoretical guarantees for CPQL:
- **Theorem 1 (Lower Bound on State Value Function):** It is proven that for a sufficiently large conservatism parameter $\alpha$, the state value of the mixture policy learned by CPQL, $V_{\lambda\hat{\pi}_\beta+(1-\lambda)\pi}$, lower-bounds the true state value of that policy. This ensures conservative estimation and mitigates overestimation bias, even when considering sampling errors.
- **Theorem 2 (Comparison to Behavior Policy):** The mixture policy $\lambda\hat{\pi}_\beta + (1-\lambda)\hat{\pi}$ (where $\hat{\pi}$ is the learned policy) is guaranteed to achieve performance greater than or equal to that of the behavior policy $\hat{\pi}_\beta$ in the actual MDP. This implies that CPQL can improve upon the data-collecting policy without necessarily converging to the optimal policy, addressing the problem of over-pessimism.
- **Theorem 3 (Sub-Optimality Gap):** CPQL reduces the sub-optimality gap between the optimal policy and the learned mixture policy compared to CQL (which is the $\lambda=0$ case). The theorem highlights that the choice of $\lambda$ can balance the influence of the behavior policy and the target policy, controlling the magnitude of terms related to policy divergence and density ratios, thus offering a more effective reduction of the sub-optimality gap.

**Algorithm Details (Algorithm 1):**
CPQL is implemented using a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) style architecture.
1. **Initialization:** Two critic networks ($Q_{\theta_1}, Q_{\theta_2}$) and an actor network ($\pi_\phi$) are initialized, along with their target counterparts.
2. **Multi-step Target Computation:** For each sampled partial trajectory of length $n$, the target Q-function is recursively computed backward from $s_n$ to $s_0$ using the PQL update rule. This involves computing $bQ_i$ using $r_i$, $Q(s_{i+1}, \pi_\phi(s_{i+1}))$, and the $bQ_{i+1}$ from the next step, incorporating the $\lambda$ parameter. The minimum of the two target Q-values is used for stability.
3. **Critic Update:** The critic networks are updated by minimizing a loss function comprising two parts:
 - A mean-squared error term between $Q(s,a)$ and the constructed PQL target $y$.
 - A conservatism penalty, adopted from CQL, which encourages lower Q-values for OOD actions by minimizing the difference between the expected Q-value under the learned policy and the expected Q-value under the data distribution. The log-sum-exp method is used for this penalty.
4. **Actor Update:** The actor network is updated via gradient ascent to maximize the Q-values predicted by the critics under the learned policy, with an entropy regularization term.
5. **Target Network Update:** The target networks are soft-updated using an exponential moving average.

The paper notes that $\alpha_{td}=0$ for entropy bonus in the target Q-function update (Line 7) to avoid amplifying numerical scale and simplifying value estimations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides extensive empirical validation on the D4RL benchmark, encompassing MuJoCo locomotion, Adroit manipulation, and AntMaze navigation tasks.

**i. Performance Comparison with Single-Step Offline Baselines:**
CPQL exhibits strong performance across various tasks and datasets. In the MuJoCo locomotion tasks (Table 1), CPQL consistently shows performance improvements across all dataset types (Random, Medium, Medium-Replay, Medium-Expert, Expert). For Adroit manipulation tasks, CPQL outperforms existing algorithms in "door-human" and "door-cloned" datasets and achieves high performance in "pen-human" and "pen-cloned" tasks. In Antmaze navigation tasks, known for sparse rewards, CPQL demonstrates competitive or superior performance. Overall, CPQL achieves the highest performance in a significant majority (22 out of 29) of the evaluated tasks, which indicates its effectiveness in leveraging trajectories and the PQL operator to mitigate over-pessimistic value estimation.

**ii. Sensitivity to the Conservatism Parameter (α):**
A key observation (Figure 1) is that CPQL is less sensitive to the choice of the conservatism parameter $\alpha$ compared to CQL. While CQL's performance can fluctuate significantly with small changes in $\alpha$, CPQL maintains high performance across a wider range of $\alpha$ values. This suggests that CPQL mitigates over-conservatism, allowing the learned policy to explore promising actions more effectively without being excessively penalized. The theoretical analysis (Theorem 3) supports this by showing how an appropriate $\lambda$ can reduce the sub-optimality gap, contributing to the robustness observed.

**iii. Comparison with Other Multi-Step Operators:**
An ablation study (Figure 2) compares CPQL with conservative variants of other multi-step operators (Uncorrected N-step, Retrace, Tree-backup) combined with CQL. While these alternative multi-step methods sometimes show faster initial learning, their performance tends to drop after an early peak. Retrace, which relies on accurate behavior policy estimation, suffers from degradation due to estimation inaccuracies. Tree-backup, designed for discrete action spaces, leads to unstable updates in continuous environments. The Uncorrected n-step return can overly restrict OOD action exploration. In contrast, CPQL demonstrates stable and competitive performance without requiring additional components or suffering from similar instabilities.

**iv. Contribution to Offline-to-Online Learning Framework:**
CPQL also demonstrates benefits in offline-to-online learning (Figure 3, 4). When the Q-function pre-trained by CPQL is used to initialize an online PQL agent, it helps avoid or quickly recover from the typical performance drop observed at the start of online fine-tuning. This leads to faster adaptation and more robust performance improvements. This is attributed to two factors: CPQL provides a well-trained Q-function from the offline phase, reducing the need for extensive online exploration; and CPQL's method of reducing the influence of the learned policy on Q-value estimation prevents Q-value degradation at the online phase's outset, enabling a smoother transition than, for example, CQL-to-SAC, where larger $\alpha$ values can lead to more severe performance drops.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces Conservative Peng’s Q(λ) (CPQL) as the first multi-step Q-learning algorithm for model-free offline reinforcement learning. The paper makes several contributions to the field:

- **Novel Integration of Multi-step Learning and Conservatism:** CPQL demonstrates that multi-step operators, specifically PQL, can be effectively adapted to offline RL for conservative value estimation. This addresses a gap where most prior offline RL methods relied on single-step updates, underutilizing available trajectory data.
- **Mitigation of Over-pessimism with Implicit Regularization:** A core insight is that the fixed point of the PQL operator in offline settings naturally biases value estimates towards the behavior policy, inducing an implicit behavior regularization. This mechanism allows CPQL to mitigate the common issue of over-pessimistic value estimation in conservative methods without requiring complex additional estimation procedures or auxiliary networks, which often lead to increased complexity or instability.
- **Robust Performance and Theoretical Guarantees:** The empirical results on the D4RL benchmark indicate that CPQL consistently outperforms existing single-step offline RL algorithms across a wide range of tasks and dataset qualities. Furthermore, the theoretical analysis provides guarantees for CPQL, demonstrating that it lower-bounds the true value function, achieves performance at least equivalent to the behavior policy, and reduces the sub-optimality gap compared to conventional CQL. The algorithm also exhibits reduced sensitivity to the conservatism parameter, contributing to its practical applicability.
- **Enhanced Offline-to-Online Learning:** CPQL contributes to the offline-to-online learning framework by providing a pre-trained Q-function that enables online agents to bypass initial performance drops and achieve more robust and efficient fine-tuning. This facilitates a smoother transition from offline pre-training to online interaction.

**Potential Impact:**
The introduction of CPQL has the potential to influence the design of future offline RL algorithms by advocating for the adoption of multi-step learning. Its ability to balance conservatism and performance, coupled with robustness to hyperparameters, could lead to more reliable and widely applicable offline RL solutions. In scenarios requiring an efficient transition from existing datasets to real-world deployment, its benefits for offline-to-online learning could be particularly impactful.

**Limitations:**
The paper acknowledges two limitations. First, CPQL incurs additional computational cost due to multi-step backups, although the authors state that this overhead is small in practice. Second, performance may degrade on very low-quality datasets, where single-step updates might be preferable. However, CPQL's design allows it to reproduce single-step TD learning as a special case.
