# Multimodal Perception for Goal-oriented Navigation: A Survey

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey paper from Tongji University and Peking University introduces a framework of "inference domains" to systematically categorize and analyze multimodal perception approaches for goal-oriented navigation. It reviews approximately 200 articles, revealing common computational patterns across tasks like PointNav and ObjectNav, while highlighting challenges in sim-to-real transfer and balanced multimodal integration.

## Problem

- The diverse and rapidly evolving field of embodied navigation lacks a unified framework to systematically categorize and compare multimodal approaches.
- Researchers face difficulty in identifying common computational foundations and recurring patterns across seemingly disparate goal-oriented navigation tasks (e.g., PointNav, ObjectNav, ImageNav, AudioGoalNav).
- A comprehensive overview is needed to clarify the historical progression, current state, and future directions of multimodal perception in autonomous navigation.

## Method

- Introduces a novel categorization framework based on six "inference domains" (latent map-based, implicit representation learning, graph-based, linguistic, embedding-based, and diffusion model-based).
- Systematically applies this inference domain framework to analyze methods across four major goal-oriented navigation tasks: ==Point Goal==, Object Goal, Image Goal, and Audio Goal Navigation.
- Reviews approximately 200 relevant articles to establish foundational concepts, compare datasets and metrics, and identify emerging trends and challenges.

## Results

- Categorized and analyzed numerous multimodal navigation methods within six distinct inference domains, demonstrating their application and evolution across PointNav, ObjectNav, ImageNav, and AudioGoalNav tasks.
- Identified the adaptive nature of latent maps (from geometric to semantic/acoustic) and the task-specific specializations within implicit representation learning approaches.
- Highlighted the increasing utility of linguistic integration for tasks with higher semantic complexity and the promise of diffusion models for generative environmental modeling and trajectory synthesis, particularly in conditions of partial observability.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Common computational patterns and architectural paradigms (e.g., recurrent and transformer networks) support a variety of navigation tasks, despite diverse goal specifications and sensory inputs.
> - The complexity of multimodal perception for navigation has evolved from modular, geometric reasoning to sophisticated end-to-end deep learning architectures integrating semantic and contextual understanding.
> - Persistent challenges include bridging the sim-to-real gap, achieving robust multimodal representation learning and integration, and developing systems capable of human-guided scene generalization.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied navigation, which involves enabling autonomous agents to traverse complex and unknown environments to reach specified targets, is a fundamental area of research within autonomous systems and artificial intelligence. Historically, this field has progressed from basic geometric path planning to more sophisticated approaches that integrate multiple sensory modalities. Early navigation systems primarily focused on geometric reasoning, often relying on explicit mapping and planning components.

The broader research landscape has witnessed a significant shift towards multimodal reasoning, where agents leverage diverse information sources such as visual (RGB, depth, LiDAR), linguistic (natural language instructions), and acoustic (sound sources) data. This evolution is closely intertwined with advancements in deep learning, which has facilitated the transition from modular, handcrafted systems to end-to-end architectures capable of learning complex perception-to-action policies. The increasing complexity of navigation tasks, from simple point-goal navigation to object, image, and audio-goal navigation, further necessitates the integration of high-level semantic understanding with low-level perception. This survey positions itself within this evolving landscape by providing a structured overview of multimodal navigation, emphasizing the common computational foundations that underpin seemingly disparate approaches and tasks, thereby seeking to unify the understanding of this diverse field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this survey is to provide a comprehensive analysis of multimodal navigation approaches through the unifying perspective of "inference domains." This organizational framework aims to clarify how autonomous agents perceive, reason about, and navigate environments by integrating visual, linguistic, and acoustic information.

The authors detail several key motivations and contributions for undertaking this survey:
1. **Organization by Inference Domains:** The survey aims to categorize navigation methods based on their primary environmental reasoning mechanisms across different inference domains. This approach is intended to reveal cross-task insights, expose the historical progression from explicit to implicit representations, and facilitate direct comparison of approaches sharing computational foundations.
2. **Analysis of Shared Computational Foundations:** A central motivation is to systematically analyze how common computational principles and architectural patterns support a variety of navigation tasks, despite their varying goal specifications (e.g., coordinates in PointNav, visual targets in ImageNav, semantic categories in ObjectNav, sound sources in AudioGoalNav).
3. **Identification of Recurring Patterns and Strengths:** The authors aim to identify recurring patterns, distinctive strengths, and limitations across different navigation paradigms. This includes understanding the evolution from modular approaches to modern end-to-end deep learning architectures.
4. **Examination of Multimodal Integration Challenges and Opportunities:** The survey seeks to explore the challenges and opportunities associated with integrating multimodal perception to enhance navigation capabilities, thereby identifying emerging trends and future research directions.
5. **Extensive Literature Review:** To achieve these objectives, the authors review approximately 200 relevant articles, providing a detailed understanding of the current state of the field.

By addressing these points, the survey intends to serve as a comprehensive reference and a catalyst for future innovation in embodied AI navigation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this survey is structured around a novel categorization framework based on "inference domains," which are defined as the main environmental reasoning mechanisms employed during navigation. This framework provides a unified lens to analyze methods across diverse navigation tasks.

The survey outlines its approach as follows:

1. **Categorization with Inference Domains:** The core methodological contribution is the identification and analysis of six primary inference domains:
 - **Latent map-based methods:** These construct and maintain explicit environmental representations, such as 2D occupancy grids or 3D semantic maps, integrating geometric and semantic information for path planning.
 - **Implicit representation approaches:** These leverage end-to-end learning to develop navigation capabilities without explicit mapping, typically using recurrent neural networks or transformers to maintain an internal state.
 - **Graph-based methods:** These represent environments as relational graphs with nodes corresponding to locations, objects, or regions, enabling reasoning about spatial relationships via graph neural networks.
 - **Linguistic domain approaches:** These utilize large language models (LLMs) to incorporate common-sense knowledge and semantic reasoning, often through prompt-based decision-making.
 - **Embedding-based methods:** These employ pretrained visual-language models (e.g., CLIP) to establish semantic connections between visual observations and language descriptions, supporting zero-shot generalization.
 - **Diffusion model-based domain:** This emerging domain leverages denoising diffusion probabilistic models for generative modeling, including synthesizing trajectory sequences, generating semantic maps, and developing unified exploration policies.

2. **Task-Specific Analysis:** The survey systematically applies this inference domain framework to four major goal-oriented navigation tasks:
 - **Point Goal Navigation (PointNav):** Navigating to a specific 3D coordinate.
 - **Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav):** Locating and reaching a specified object category.
 - **Image Goal Navigation (ImageNav):** Navigating to a destination defined by a reference image.
 - **Audio Goal Navigation (AudioGoalNav):** Localizing and moving towards sound sources.
 Each of these sections categorizes and discusses relevant approaches based on their primary inference domain.

3. **Preliminary Concepts:** Before diving into the task-specific analyses, the survey establishes foundational concepts in Section 2, including:
 - **Historical Development:** A chronological overview of navigation tasks, illustrating the progression from simpler to more complex and multimodal paradigms.
 - **Formal Definition of Navigation Tasks:** A mathematical framework applicable across modalities, defining the agent's state space, observation space, action space, and task-specific goal space.
 - **Navigation Datasets:** A comparison of widely used simulation environments and datasets, such as Habitat-Matterport 3D (HM3D), Gibson, Matterport3D, AI2-THOR, ScanNet, Replica, and SoundSpaces. This includes details on scale, complexity, visual fidelity, and audio-visual capabilities.
 - **Evaluation Metrics:** A standardized set of metrics used to assess navigation performance, encompassing success rates (SR, SPL, SoftSPL), distance and efficiency (DTG, DTS, NE), multi-goal navigation (PR, PPL), and specialized audio navigation metrics (SNE, DSPL, SNA, SWS).

4. **Review Scope:** The analysis is based on a review of approximately 200 research articles, providing a broad overview of the current state of the art in goal-oriented navigation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The survey's main findings and results are organized by the identified inference domains and their applications across various goal-oriented navigation tasks, followed by cross-task insights.

#### 4.1. Inference Domains Across Navigation Tasks

The survey categorizes and analyzes numerous approaches within each of the six inference domains:

1. **Latent Map-Based Inference Domain:**
 - **PointNav:** Approaches like ANM, LSP-Unet, UPEN, and MoDA construct explicit spatial representations (e.g., occupancy grids or semantic maps) from egocentric observations. These maps are then used for path planning, obstacle avoidance, and frontier-based exploration, often integrating uncertainty estimation.
 - **ObjectNav:** Methods such as Sem-EXP, PEANUT, L2M, PONI, and Distance Map extend geometric maps by incorporating semantic object labels and likelihood distributions. They often predict target probabilities in unexplored areas and leverage potential functions or geodesic distance metrics to guide exploration. Advanced approaches like 3D-Aware and RNR-Map utilize detailed 3D maps and neural radiance fields.
 - **ImageNav:** MANav, memoNav, Mod-IIN, MIGN, FeudalNav, and LiteVLoc employ memory modules with state embedding networks or graph attention networks to build spatial memory structures. These systems integrate visual features within spatial structures to match goal images and plan paths.
 - **AudioGoalNav:** VAR, AV-WaN, and AFP integrate visual geometry with sound localization cues to construct spatial-acoustic maps. These maps represent sound propagation patterns alongside visual geometry, supporting path planning for sound sources.

2. **Implicit Representation Learning Inference Domain:**
 - **PointNav:** This domain includes methods like visual odometry (VO, MPVO, IMN-RPG) for pose estimation, auxiliary task learning (SplitNet, Attention Fusion, Efficient Learning) to enhance representations, and policy-based approaches (DD-PPO, DD-PPO Depth, Ego-Localization, RobustNav, iSEE, ENTL) that use recurrent or transformer architectures for end-to-end control. These methods learn latent representations of the environment without explicit mapping.
 - **ObjectNav:** VTNet, DRL, DITA, RIM, OMT, Habitat-web, PIRLNav, HiNL, OVRL, and OVON jointly learn environmental representations and action policies. They often leverage CNNs, RNNs, and Transformers to process visual data, integrate memory modules, and combine reinforcement learning with imitation learning. Transformer-based models and large pre-trained encoders (SigLIP) are becoming common.
 - **ImageNav:** EmerNav, NUENav, FGPrompt, SLING, and SimView use correspondence networks or Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) to match observations with goal images implicitly. They focus on visual reasoning and fine-grained feature fusion without constructing explicit maps.
 - **AudioGoalNav:** ORAN, CATCH, SDM, SAAVN, SAVi, AVLEN, and CAVEN encode spatial-acoustic understanding directly within neural networks. They address static and dynamic sound sources, incorporate confidence-aware policy distillation, and leverage memory components for handling multiple concurrent or sporadic sound events. Some integrate language for semantic audio-visual navigation.

3. **Graph-Based Inference Domain:**
 - **ObjectNav:** Ion, HOZ, and MJOLNIR-o construct relational graphs where nodes represent objects, zones, or scenes, and edges capture spatial or semantic relationships. Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs) are used to process features and inform hierarchical navigation decisions.
 - **ImageNav:** TSGM uses a dual-memory system with a topological graph for spatial representation and semantic features for visual recognition. Nodes represent distinct locations, and edges capture navigability, supporting hierarchical decision-making.

4. **Linguistic Inference Domain:**
 - **ObjectNav:** DDN, L3mvn, and SGM leverage Large Language Models (LLMs) to integrate common-sense knowledge about object-environment relationships and spatial layouts. These models use LLM-generated descriptions, prompt-based reasoning, and masked modeling to predict unobserved regions and guide exploration.
 - **AudioGoalNav:** RILA introduces a zero-shot semantic audio-visual navigation framework that uses LLMs for environmental reasoning. It combines pre-trained vision-language models with an "Imaginative Assistant" and a "Reflective Planner" for frontier-based exploration and sophisticated audio localization.

5. **Embedding-Based Inference Domain:**
 - **ObjectNav:** EmbCLIP, ZSEL, COWs, ZSON, VLFM, and AKGVP utilize pre-trained vision-language models (e.g., CLIP, BLIP-2) to establish semantic connections between visual observations, text descriptions, and image goals. This enables zero-shot generalization to unseen objects and environments through unified embedding spaces and similarity-based reasoning, often integrated with frontier-based exploration.
 - **AudioGoalNav:** AVLMaps extends audio-visual navigation by integrating natural language understanding within a 3D spatial mapping framework. It uses multimodal localization modules (visual, object, area, audio) leveraging models like NetVLAD, SuperPoint, open-vocabulary segmentation, and AudioCLIP to create unified spatial representations for complex target specifications.

6. **Diffusion Model-Based Inference Domain:**
 - **ObjectNav:** DAR and T-Diff leverage denoising diffusion probabilistic models for generative modeling. DAR generates plausible semantic maps of unexplored areas with LLM-biased guidance, while T-Diff generates temporally coherent trajectory sequences, reconceptualizing navigation as a generative modeling challenge.
 - **ImageNav:** NOMAD introduces a diffusion-based policy for both exploration and goal-directed behaviors, using goal-masking and a conditional U-Net to capture multimodal action distributions. This unified framework learns spatial relationships and object placement distributions.

#### 4.2. Cross-Task Insights

The survey highlights common computational patterns across tasks:
- **Latent Maps:** Evolve in complexity from geometric (PointNav) to semantic (ObjectNav), visual features (ImageNav), and acoustic propagation patterns (AudioGoalNav), adapting to task-specific information needs while maintaining explicit spatial modeling.
- **Implicit Representations:** Show task-specific specializations (e.g., visual odometry for PointNav, object relationship modeling for ObjectNav, visual correspondence for ImageNav, audio-visual integration for AudioGoalNav), yet share core recurrent and transformer architectures.
- **Graph-Based Methods:** Nodes and edges take on different semantics (object-scene relationships in ObjectNav, topological locations in ImageNav), indicating adaptability in abstracting environmental information.
- **Linguistic Integration:** Language becomes increasingly valuable with higher semantic complexity (e.g., prominent in ObjectNav and AudioGoalNav, less in PointNav), used for reasoning about relationships, spatial layouts, and semantic properties of targets.
- **Embedding Balance:** The balance between pre-trained knowledge and task-specific adaptation varies. Direct transfer of CLIP embeddings is effective for ObjectNav, but AudioGoalNav requires careful integration with spatial reasoning due to larger sensory gaps.
- **Diffusion Models:** Demonstrate significant promise for tasks requiring semantic prediction of unobserved regions and generative environmental modeling, particularly in ObjectNav, indicating their utility for high semantic complexity and partial observability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This survey contributes to the field of embodied AI navigation by providing a structured and comprehensive analysis of multimodal approaches. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Unified Understanding:** By introducing and applying the framework of "inference domains," the survey offers a unified perspective that transcends individual navigation tasks. This framework reveals common computational patterns and underlying principles across diverse multimodal navigation methods, fostering a more coherent understanding of the field's advancements. This can help researchers identify fundamental components that are transferable across different navigation challenges.

2. **Guidance for Research:** The systematic comparison of strengths and limitations across various inference domains and navigation paradigms provides valuable insights for future research. It highlights effective strategies for integrating visual, linguistic, and audio information, and points towards promising avenues for enhancing navigation capabilities. For instance, the discussion on emerging diffusion models suggests a new paradigm for generative environmental modeling in tasks with high semantic complexity.

3. **Identification of Gaps and Challenges:** The survey explicitly outlines critical challenges that persist in embodied navigation.
 - **Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The gap between simulated and real-world environments remains a significant hurdle. While visual fidelity in simulations has improved, discrepancies in physical dynamics, sensor noise, and acoustic properties limit the direct transferability of learned policies. Addressing this requires robust domain adaptation techniques and more realistic simulation of environmental complexities.
 - **Multimodal Representation and Integration:** Achieving truly balanced and optimal fusion of multiple sensory modalities is an ongoing challenge. Many current approaches prioritize one modality, with others playing a supplementary role. Developing representations that leverage the complementary strengths of visual, auditory, and linguistic information without bias, and effectively grounding linguistic concepts in physical environments, is crucial.

4. **Direction for Future Work:** The authors propose concrete directions for future research aimed at addressing current limitations and advancing the field:
 - **Human-Guided Scene Generalization:** Beyond automated techniques like domain randomization, integrating strategic human interaction can provide complementary support for generalizing to novel environments. Future systems that can recognize their limitations and strategically request human assistance, combining linguistic communication with latent maps for spatial representation and diffusion models for environmental completion, are envisioned.
 - **Unified Multimodal Representation Learning:** Emphasizing the development of truly balanced and integrated multimodal representations, rather than prioritizing single modalities. This includes research into joint embedding spaces, cross-modal attention mechanisms that dynamically weight modalities based on reliability, and the creation of multimodal foundation models specifically trained for embodied navigation tasks.

In conclusion, this survey serves as both a detailed reference and a forward-looking guide. It clarifies the state of the art, underscores the evolution from geometric to semantic reasoning, and highlights the critical role of multimodal integration. The insights provided are intended to inspire innovations towards developing more robust, generalizable, and human-capable autonomous navigation systems.
