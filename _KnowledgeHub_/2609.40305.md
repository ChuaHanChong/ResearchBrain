# Looped Diffusion Transformer

> [!summary] Summary
> Looped-DiT scales text-to-image diffusion by repeatedly running a shared group of middle Transformer blocks within each denoising step, adding computational depth without adding parameters. Deep supervision on every intermediate loop and self-modulating attention (Gated or Exclusive Self-Attention) stabilize the looping. A 260M-parameter model beats a 6.5x larger model across six benchmarks at 4.9x lower inference compute, and deeper loops self-correct earlier errors.

## Problem

- Scaling text-to-image models has relied on more parameters or more denoising steps, both raising deployment cost, while chain-of-thought reasoning lengthens sequences and adds inference compute.
- Naive looping of shared Transformer blocks in diffusion models fails to improve quality reliably, staying below baseline at shallow depths and degrading beyond the trained loop count.
- Supervising only the final loop starves earlier loop states of direct gradients, and unregulated softmax attention updates progressively erase spatial information, with position-probe R² falling from 0.865 to 0.562.

## Method

- **Looped MMDiT** architecture: a pixel-space ==MiniT2I== backbone split into pre-loop, looped, and post-loop block groups (6/5/6), with the middle five ==shared blocks== repeated four times per denoising step.
- **Deep Supervision** decodes every intermediate loop output through the shared post-loop blocks and applies the ==flow-matching clean-image loss==, weighted ==Final + Mean==, adding no parameters or inference overhead.
- **Self-Modulating Attention**, applied only inside looped blocks, scales head outputs via a learned token-dependent ==Gated Attention== or a parameter-free ==Exclusive Self-Attention== projection that removes the self-value component.

## Results

- Looped-DiT B/16 with 0.26B parameters averages **71.5** across six benchmarks, beating 1.7B InternVL-U (**69.0**) and 6.6B Uni-CoT (**66.8**), and leads PRISM at **67.0** and CoReBench at **53.5**.
- Looped-DiT B/32 scores **59.1** versus **55.2** parameter-matched, **57.7** deeper, **58.6** wider, and **58.1** training-compute-matched baselines; at matched FLOPs, extra loop depth beats extra denoising steps.
- Ablations: naive looping gives **56.2**, XSA alone **58.5**, and Final + Mean supervision plus XSA **59.1**; XSA adds **+1.6** when looped versus **-0.1** non-looped; adaptive exit reaches the plateau at **2.70** average loops.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Weight-shared iterative depth is a parameter-efficient scaling axis for diffusion models that can beat both larger models and spending the same compute on more denoising steps.
> - Repeating identical blocks needs explicit regulation: intermediate-step supervision plus update-magnitude control stop recurrent updates from overwriting token information such as spatial position.
> - Latent looping and textual chain-of-thought are complementary: loops excel at constraint resolution and self-correction, while CoT rewriting better infers implied content from rules and examples.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Position in the Broader Research Landscape

Text-to-image generation has generally improved through three forms of scaling: increasing model parameters, increasing training data and compute, and increasing the number of denoising evaluations during inference. Larger diffusion Transformers can represent more complex relationships, but their memory and computation requirements make deployment more difficult. Increasing denoising steps can improve quality, but it directly increases generation latency.

This paper investigates a different scaling axis: **repeatedly applying the same Transformer blocks within each denoising step**. This approach is related to Universal Transformers, recursive or looped Transformers, latent-reasoning models, and parameter sharing methods such as ALBERT. In these systems, shared blocks are reused multiple times to increase effective computational depth without proportionally increasing the number of learned parameters.

The work also connects to recent research on latent reasoning. In language models, recurrent computation can refine hidden states without producing explicit chain-of-thought tokens. The authors examine whether a similar process can occur in text-to-image models, where intermediate hidden representations may progressively resolve visual constraints, such as object counts, spatial arrangements, and relationships between entities.

The paper differs from most efficient diffusion work, which attempts to reduce the number of denoising steps through consistency models, distillation, or one-step generation. Instead, it asks whether some computation can be moved inside each denoising step, allowing the model to perform multiple internal refinement passes. It is also related to Elastic Looped Transformers, which study variable loop depths for visual generation, but the present work focuses specifically on open-ended text-to-image generation, intermediate supervision, and visual reasoning behavior.

### 2. Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective is to determine whether looped computation can improve text-to-image generation while keeping the parameter count fixed. The authors focus on three questions.

First, they ask whether repeatedly applying shared Transformer blocks improves image quality and text-image alignment more efficiently than increasing model width or depth. A looped model can have fewer unique parameters while performing a similar number of effective block applications as a larger conventional Transformer.

Second, they investigate how inference computation should be allocated. Diffusion models already perform iterative computation through denoising steps. The paper compares adding more denoising steps with increasing the number of internal loop iterations. This tests whether hidden-state refinement is a more productive use of computation than repeatedly evaluating the model at additional noise levels.

Third, the authors examine whether looped computation supports behavior analogous to latent visual reasoning. Generating an image from a complex prompt requires more than identifying individual words. The model may need to infer implied content, satisfy multiple constraints simultaneously, preserve spatial information, and correct errors that become visible only after other elements have been represented. The authors hypothesize that repeated processing of image and text tokens can support these operations without explicit reasoning tokens or external annotations.

Initial experiments showed that simply looping a standard Transformer was unreliable. Performance improved at first but then saturated or declined when the model was run for more loops than used during training. A ridge-regression probe also found that spatial position information became progressively less linearly decodable from image-token representations. The proposed method therefore addresses two problems: insufficient training signals for intermediate loop states and excessive or redundant attention updates that can overwrite useful information.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

Looped-DiT is built on MiniT2I, a compact pixel-space Multimodal Diffusion Transformer based on MMDiT. The model processes image and text tokens through modality-specific pathways with joint attention between them.

The 17 Transformer blocks are divided into three groups:

- Six blocks in a pre-loop stage.
- Five shared blocks in the looped stage.
- Six blocks in a post-loop stage.

The middle five blocks are applied four times during training. Consequently, each denoising forward pass applies 32 effective block operations while retaining only 17 unique parameterized blocks. At inference, the loop depth can be changed without retraining. Two variants are evaluated at 512-by-512 resolution: B/16, with 1,024 image tokens, and B/32, with 256 image tokens. Both contain approximately 260 million parameters.

#### 3.2. Deep Supervision

In a standard looped model, only the final loop output is supervised. Earlier loop states receive learning signals indirectly through all subsequent loop applications. The authors argue that this creates a long optimization path and leaves intermediate states weakly trained.

Looped-DiT therefore decodes every intermediate loop state through the shared post-loop blocks. Each output is trained against the same clean image target using a flow-matching objective. The final training configuration uses a “final plus mean” weighting scheme: the three intermediate losses are averaged and added to the final-loop loss. These intermediate decoding paths are used only during training, so they add training computation but no inference parameters or inference-time overhead.

#### 3.3. Self-Modulating Attention

The second component is Self-Modulating Attention, which regulates how strongly attention updates the residual representation during repeated applications of the shared blocks. The authors study two variants.

**Gated Attention** uses a learned, token-dependent scalar gate for each attention head. The gate controls the magnitude of each head’s contribution before it is written to the residual stream.

**Exclusive Self-Attention (XSA)** uses a parameter-free projection that removes the component of the attention output aligned with the token’s own value vector. This suppresses direct self-updates and constrains the magnitude and direction of repeated attention modifications.

Both mechanisms are applied only inside the looped blocks. They are intended to reduce redundant updates and preserve information, particularly spatial information encoded in image tokens.

#### 3.4. Training and Evaluation

The models are trained directly in pixel space using flow matching. Training data consist of CC12M for pretraining and a mixture of BLIP3o-60K, DALL-E 3 data, and ShareGPT-4o-Image data for fine-tuning. The authors note that this is a relatively limited data setup compared with several competing models.

Evaluation uses six benchmarks: GenEval, DPG-Bench, PRISM-Bench, T2I-CoReBench, SpatialGenEval, and TIIF-Short. These measure object composition, dense-prompt adherence, instruction following, spatial reasoning, and complex relational constraints. Comparisons include non-looped models with matched parameters or computation, larger and wider Transformers, additional denoising steps, and explicit textual chain-of-thought prompt rewriting.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

#### 4.1. Benchmark Performance and Parameter Efficiency

The 260-million-parameter Looped-DiT B/16 obtains the following scores: 87.4 on GenEval, 87.0 on DPG-Bench, 67.0 on PRISM, 53.5 on T2I-CoReBench, 54.6 on SpatialGenEval, and 79.7 on TIIF-Short. Its mean score is 71.5.

This average exceeds the 69.0 reported for the 1.7-billion-parameter InternVL-U model. Thus, in the authors’ comparison, Looped-DiT achieves a 2.5-point higher mean score with approximately 6.5 times fewer parameters. It also obtains the highest reported score among the listed models on five of the six individual benchmarks; MiniT2I-B/16 is marginally higher on GenEval.

The results suggest that parameter count alone does not determine the effectiveness of the model’s computation. Reusing blocks allows the model to perform more transformations without storing a separate parameter set for every effective layer.

#### 4.2. Matched-Compute Comparisons

Using the smaller B/32 setup, the non-looped MiniT2I baseline scores 55.2 on the four-benchmark average. A deeper model with approximately 473 million parameters scores 57.7, while a wider model with approximately 489 million parameters scores 58.6. The looped model, with 260 million parameters and the same effective depth as the deeper alternative, scores 59.1.

When training computation is also approximately matched by adding deep supervision to the deeper model, Looped-DiT still scores higher, 59.1 versus 58.1. These comparisons indicate that the reported gains are not explained solely by adding more effective layers or increasing forward-pass computation.

#### 4.3. Loop Depth Versus Denoising Steps

Under matched inference budgets, increasing loop depth generally produces larger gains than allocating the same computation to additional denoising steps. The authors compare one-pass and four-loop configurations while adjusting the number of denoising steps to equalize per-image computation. Across DPG-Bench, PRISM, CoReBench, and SpatialGenEval, four-loop inference consistently performs better.

This finding suggests that internal representation refinement and external diffusion-time refinement contribute differently to generation quality. The authors interpret loop depth as a complementary inference-time scaling axis rather than merely another way to increase model evaluations.

#### 4.4. Evidence of Iterative Refinement

Qualitative examples show that later loops can add missing objects, correct object counts, reorganize spatial layouts, and remove extraneous elements. Quantitatively, performance rises consistently as loop depth increases from one to four.

The paper describes this as behavior suggestive of latent visual reasoning, although the evidence is behavioral and representational rather than a mechanistic demonstration. The experiments do not establish that the model performs reasoning in the same sense as explicit symbolic or textual reasoning.

Looping and textual chain-of-thought rewriting also provide partly complementary benefits. Looping is more effective on relational, procedural, and spatial constraint tasks, while textual rewriting produces larger gains when the prompt requires inferring implied visual content. Combining both methods gives the highest overall scores.

#### 4.5. Ablation Results

Looping alone improves the four-benchmark average from 55.2 to 56.2. Deep supervision raises performance further, with the final-plus-mean weighting reaching 57.5 without attention modulation. XSA increases the score to 58.5 when used with looping, and the complete configuration reaches 59.1.

XSA provides a larger improvement for the looped model than for non-looped baselines. This supports the authors’ explanation that repeated attention updates are a particular source of instability in recursive computation. Measurements show that XSA produces smaller relative attention updates and better preserves the linear decodability of token positions.

The paper also introduces adaptive looping. A lightweight gating network predicts whether another loop is likely to reduce reconstruction error. By changing a threshold, inference can stop at different loop depths without retraining. Adaptive looping achieves higher performance than fixed-loop inference at matched average loop counts, indicating a possible way to allocate more computation only to difficult prompts.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Limitations

Looped-DiT presents parameter sharing as a distinct method for scaling text-to-image computation. Its main practical implication is that image-generation systems may gain quality and compositional reliability without a proportional increase in model parameters. This could be relevant for deployment environments where memory capacity is more restrictive than raw arithmetic throughput.

The approach also provides a framework for studying internal iterative refinement in generative models. Deep supervision makes intermediate states usable, while self-modulating attention helps prevent repeated computation from erasing spatial or semantic information. Adaptive looping could further support variable-compute generation.

However, the evidence is limited to approximately 260-million-parameter pixel-space models at 512-by-512 resolution. It is not yet clear whether the findings transfer to billion-parameter systems, latent-space diffusion models, higher resolutions, or different Transformer partitions. The authors use a fixed six-five-six block split and train primarily at four loops, leaving the broader architecture and training-depth design space unexplored. Deep supervision also increases training computation by approximately 1.54 times relative to compute-matched deeper or wider baselines. Finally, the claim of latent reasoning remains provisional: progressive correction is consistent with iterative reasoning, but the paper does not provide a complete causal or mechanistic account of the computations performed across loops.
