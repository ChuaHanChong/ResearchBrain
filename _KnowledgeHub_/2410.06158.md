# GR-2: A Generative Video-Language-Action Model with Web-Scale Knowledge for Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at ByteDance Research developed GR-2, a generalist robot agent that combines large-scale video pre-training with robot action generation using a GPT-style transformer model. The system achieved a 97.7% success rate on multi-task tabletop manipulation, an average of 79.0% on industrial bin-picking, and established new state-of-the-art on the CALVIN benchmark for language-conditioned tasks.

## Problem

- Developing robotic systems capable of understanding natural language instructions and performing a wide range of manipulation tasks.
- Enabling robots to generalize effectively to unseen environments, novel objects, and new tasks with limited specific training data.
- Addressing the challenge of generating precise robot action trajectories from observations, where multiple valid actions may exist for a given state.

## Method

- A **GR-2** ==GPT-style transformer== architecture that processes tokenized representations of text instructions, video frames, and robot actions.
- A two-stage training process involving large-scale ==video-language pre-training== on **38 million** diverse video clips, followed by ==fine-tuning== on robot-specific video-language-action data.
- Integration of a ==conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE)== within the fine-tuning stage to generate diverse and robust action trajectories.

## Results

- Achieved a **97.7%** success rate on simple multi-task tabletop manipulation and maintained **75%** success with only **50 demonstrations** per task, showing data efficiency.
- Demonstrated an average success rate of **79.0%** on industrial bin-picking tasks, a significant improvement over its predecessor GR-1 (**35.9%**), including with unseen objects and in cluttered environments.
- Established a new state-of-the-art on the CALVIN benchmark, achieving **98.6%** success for completing one task and an average task completion length of **4.64** for longer sequences.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large-scale video pre-training on diverse human activity data enables strong generalization capabilities for subsequent robot manipulation tasks.
> - A unified GPT-style transformer architecture can effectively integrate language, video, and robot action modalities for complex control.
> - The use of a conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE) helps in generating robust action trajectories by addressing the one-to-many mapping inherent in robot control.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Developing robotic systems capable of understanding natural language instructions and performing a wide range of manipulation tasks remains a significant challenge in artificial intelligence and robotics. GR-2 (Generalist Robot Agent 2) represents a substantial advancement in this field by combining video pre-training with robot action generation through a unified model architecture.

GR-2, developed by researchers at ByteDance Research, builds upon foundation model principles that have proven successful in other domains like natural language processing and computer vision. By pre-training on a massive dataset of 38 million video clips (over 50 billion tokens) and then fine-tuning on robot-specific data, GR-2 can generalize to unseen environments, objects, and tasks with remarkable efficiency.

### 2. Model Architecture and Training

GR-2 employs a GPT-style transformer architecture that processes tokenized representations of text instructions, video frames, and robot actions. The model architecture includes:

1. A frozen text encoder for processing language instructions
2. A VQGAN model for tokenizing image frames
3. A core transformer decoder that predicts future video frames during pre-training and both video frames and robot actions during fine-tuning

The model is trained in an autoregressive manner, learning to predict subsequent tokens based on previous ones. A key innovation in GR-2 is the integration of a conditional Variational Autoencoder (cVAE) to generate action trajectories during fine-tuning, which helps handle the one-to-many mapping from observations to possible actions.

### 3. Two-Stage Training Process

GR-2's training unfolds in two distinct stages:

**Video-Language Pre-training:**
In this initial stage, GR-2 learns to understand the relationship between language instructions and video sequences. The model is trained to predict future video frames given a text description and previous frames. This pre-training occurs on a diverse dataset including:

- Howto100M: Instructional videos with narrations
- Ego4D: First-person view of everyday activities
- Something-Something V2: Basic object interactions
- EPIC-KITCHENS: Kitchen activities
- Kinetics-700: Human actions in varied contexts
- Robot datasets (RT-1, Bridge): Robot manipulation videos

The pre-training objective can be formulated as:

$$L_{pretrain} = -\mathbb{E}_{(x,y)\sim D_{pretrain}}[\log p_\theta(y|x)]$$

where $x$ represents the input (text and initial frames) and $y$ represents the target future frames.

**Video-Language-Action Fine-tuning:**
During fine-tuning, GR-2 learns to map language instructions and visual observations to robot actions. The fine-tuning dataset consists of robot manipulation trajectories paired with language instructions. The model is trained to predict both future video frames and action trajectories:

$$L_{finetune} = \lambda_{video}L_{video} + \lambda_{action}L_{action}$$

where $L_{video}$ is the video prediction loss and $L_{action}$ is the action prediction loss, with $\lambda_{video}$ and $\lambda_{action}$ serving as balancing coefficients.

### 4. Performance on Multi-Task Manipulation

GR-2 demonstrates exceptional performance on multi-task tabletop manipulation. The model was evaluated on 105 different tabletop tasks across various conditions:

As shown in Figure 2, GR-2 achieves a 97.7% success rate on simple tabletop tasks with 400 demonstrations per task. Even with just 50 demonstrations per task, GR-2 achieves a 75% success rate, demonstrating its data efficiency. The model also shows robust performance in challenging scenarios:

- With distractors (irrelevant objects): 80.0% success rate
- With unseen backgrounds: 71.4% success rate
- In completely unseen environments: 71.7% success rate
- With unseen manipulation tasks: 53.3% success rate

These results highlight GR-2's generalization capabilities across diverse conditions. Adding data augmentation (GR-2 w/ DA) further improves performance in unseen scenarios, pushing the average success rate in unseen conditions to 75.3%.

### 5. Bin-Picking Performance

GR-2 excels in industrial bin-picking tasks, which require handling novel objects in cluttered environments:

As illustrated in Figure 3, GR-2 significantly outperforms its predecessor GR-1, achieving an average success rate of 79.0% compared to GR-1's 35.9%. This improvement is consistent across all testing conditions:

- Seen objects: 82.0% vs. 49.0%
- Unseen objects: 77.0% vs. 38.0%
- Cluttered environments with seen objects: 75.0% vs. 24.0%
- Cluttered environments with unseen objects: 78.0% vs. 26.0%

The substantial performance gap demonstrates GR-2's enhanced ability to generalize to new objects and handle complex, cluttered environments, which is crucial for practical industrial applications.

### 6. CALVIN Benchmark Results

GR-2 establishes a new state-of-the-art on the CALVIN benchmark, a standardized evaluation framework for language-conditioned robot manipulation:

GR-2 achieves superior performance across all metrics on the CALVIN benchmark:
- 98.6% success rate for completing one task
- 96.1% for two consecutive tasks
- 93.1% for three consecutive tasks
- 90.1% for four consecutive tasks
- 85.9% for five consecutive tasks

The model's average task completion length of 4.64 is significantly higher than competing methods like RT-1, MT-ACT, HULC, RoboFlamingo, and even GR-1. This means GR-2 can successfully execute longer sequences of tasks without failing, demonstrating its reliability in extended operation scenarios.

### 7. Model Scaling Effects

The research team conducted experiments to understand how model scaling affects performance:

GR-2 was tested in four sizes:
- GR-2-S: Small (1.1B parameters)
- GR-2-B: Base (1.9B parameters)
- GR-2-L: Large (3.5B parameters)
- GR-2-XL: Extra Large (6.8B parameters)

As shown in Figure 5, larger models consistently achieve lower validation loss during training. The performance improvements are particularly evident in real-robot experiments, where success rates scale from 39% with GR-2-S to 60% with GR-2-XL. This scaling trend suggests that further performance gains could potentially be achieved by increasing model size even further.

### 8. Video Generation Capabilities

A distinctive feature of GR-2 is its ability to generate high-quality prediction videos that align with real-world executions:

As demonstrated in Figure 6, GR-2 can accurately predict future video frames that closely match the ground truth. This capability serves two purposes:

1. It indicates that the model has a good understanding of how actions affect the environment
2. It provides interpretable visualizations of the model's planned actions

The close alignment between predicted and ground truth videos suggests that GR-2 is effectively "replaying" trajectories in its predicted video space, indicating that the model has learned meaningful representations of both the visual world and robot actions.

### 9. Real-World Deployment

For real-world deployment, GR-2 incorporates a Whole-Body Control (WBC) algorithm that ensures smooth and accurate execution of the generated action trajectories. The system setup includes:

- Kinova Gen3 robot arm
- Robotiq 2F-85 gripper
- Two cameras (static and end-effector mounted)

The WBC algorithm formulates trajectory following as an optimization problem:

```python
def whole_body_control(desired_trajectory, current_state):
    # Objective function combines position, orientation, and gripper tracking
    def objective(action):
        return position_cost(action) + orientation_cost(action) + gripper_cost(action)

    # Find optimal action through optimization
    optimal_action = minimize(objective, initial_guess)
    return optimal_action
```

This algorithm ensures that the robot can accurately execute the complex manipulation trajectories generated by GR-2, contributing to the high success rates observed in real-world experiments.

### 10. Conclusion

GR-2 represents a significant advancement in generalist robot manipulation by combining large-scale video pre-training with efficient robot action generation. The model demonstrates exceptional performance across various evaluation metrics:

1. High success rates (97.7%) on multi-task tabletop manipulation
2. Strong generalization to unseen environments, objects, and tasks
3. Superior performance in industrial bin-picking tasks
4. State-of-the-art results on the CALVIN benchmark
5. Effective scaling properties with increasing model size

The model's ability to generate accurate video predictions alongside action trajectories provides both interpretability and a means to debug the model's understanding. The successful real-world deployment using the Whole-Body Control algorithm demonstrates the practical applicability of GR-2 in robotics systems.

GR-2's pre-training on diverse video data followed by fine-tuning on robot-specific data shows how foundation models from adjacent fields can be adapted to robotics, potentially reducing the data requirements for learning new tasks. This approach paves the way for more capable, general-purpose robotic systems that can understand natural language instructions and perform diverse manipulation tasks in varied environments.
