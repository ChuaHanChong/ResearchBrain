# In-Context Compositional Q-Learning for Offline Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> In-context Compositional Q-Learning (ICQL) develops an offline reinforcement learning framework that leverages linear Transformers and in-context learning to infer adaptive local Q-functions for complex, compositional tasks. This approach yielded average performance increases of 8.8% on MuJoCo, 6.3% on Adroit, and 16.4% on Kitchen D4RL benchmarks, while generating Q-value estimates that more closely align with online reinforcement learning methods.

## Problem

- Offline Reinforcement Learning (RL) faces the distributional shift problem, leading to unreliable Q-value estimates and suboptimal policies when queried for state-action pairs not sufficiently represented in the static dataset.
- Existing offline RL methods often rely on a single, global Q-function, which is inefficient and insufficient for tasks with inherent compositional structures or distinct local dynamics.
- Global function approximators struggle to generalize to novel, high-performing behaviors or guarantee optimality with biased or sparse datasets, as knowledge from one subtask may not directly transfer to another.

## Method

- **ICQL** reframes offline Q-learning as a ==contextual inference== problem, defining local Q-function approximations that adapt to specific state regions rather than a single global function.
- A linear Transformer-based in-context learning (ICL) module is employed to dynamically infer context-dependent weights for these local Q-functions, leveraging a small, retrieved set of state-similar transitions.
- The framework integrates with existing offline RL algorithms (e.g., IQL, TD3+BC) by using expectile regression for critic training and advantage-weighted regression for policy optimization, all based on locally estimated Q-values.

## Results

- ICQL achieved average performance gains of **8.8%** over CQL on MuJoCo, **6.3%** over IQL on Adroit, and a notable **16.4%** over DT on Kitchen D4RL benchmarks.
- The framework produced Q-value estimates with a higher correlation (e.g., **0.69**) to online RL values (SAC) compared to baseline offline methods (e.g., IQL's **0.29** correlation) on the Walker2d-Medium dataset.
- Ablation studies confirmed that state-similar retrieval, optimal context length (around 20 transitions), and the linear Transformer architecture are critical for effective and computationally efficient local Q-function inference, outperforming random retrieval and other in-context modeling choices.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Many complex RL tasks exhibit compositional structures, making locally adaptive Q-functions more effective than a single global Q-function for accurate value estimation and policy learning.
> - In-context learning with linear Transformers can effectively infer these local Q-functions by processing relevant contextual information, offering a flexible mechanism for addressing localized dynamics without explicit subtask labels.
> - Theoretical analysis demonstrates that accurate local Q-function approximations, under realistic assumptions about feature representation and context coverage, can lead to bounded policy sub-optimality.
