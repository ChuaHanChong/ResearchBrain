# Fleet-DAgger: Interactive Robot Fleet Learning with Scalable Human Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at UC Berkeley introduced Interactive Fleet Learning (IFL), a framework where multiple robots learn from multiple human supervisors to continuously improve their policies. They developed Fleet-DAgger algorithms, including the C.U.R. method, which prioritize robots for human intervention, achieving up to 8.8x higher Return on Human Effort (ROHE) and increased task successes in large-scale simulations and physical experiments.

## Problem

- Efficiently scaling human supervision for continually improving robot policies in large commercial and industrial robot fleets (N robots, M humans, N > M).
- Existing interactive learning methods primarily focus on single-robot, single-human scenarios and do not address the complex supervisor allocation problem for multi-robot fleets.
- Lack of a formal framework and standardized benchmark for multi-robot, multi-human interactive learning.

## Method

- Formalized Interactive Fleet Learning (IFL), defining the supervisor allocation problem and introducing Return on Human Effort (ROHE) as a metric for human supervision efficiency.
- Developed **Fleet-DAgger**, a family of algorithms for IFL that utilize interactive imitation learning with various priority functions for human-to-robot allocation, including the novel C.U.R. (Constraint-Uncertainty-Risk) algorithm.
- Created the open-source Interactive Fleet Learning Benchmark (IFLB) on NVIDIA Isaac Gym, featuring environments for complex robotic tasks and a modular architecture for algorithm evaluation.

## Results

- C.U.R. consistently outperformed baselines in large-scale simulations (100 robots, 10 humans), achieving higher cumulative successes and up to **8.8x** greater ROHE across Humanoid, Anymal, and AllegroHand tasks.
- In real-world experiments with 4 ABB YuMi robots and 2 human supervisors, C.U.R. demonstrated higher ROHE, more successful task completions, and fewer hard resets compared to baseline methods.
- Sensitivity analyses revealed that both very short and very long human intervention times negatively impact ROHE, and that an optimal number of human supervisors exists beyond which returns diminish due to superfluous interventions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Intelligent supervisor allocation is critical for maximizing both immediate fleet task throughput and long-term policy improvement, significantly impacting overall system performance and human efficiency.
> - The Return on Human Effort (ROHE) metric effectively quantifies the trade-off between fleet performance gains and human intervention costs, serving as a robust objective for IFL algorithm optimization.
> - Hierarchical prioritization of robots based on constraint violations, uncertainty, and risk (C.U.R.), combined with an initial learning period, allows for more effective collection of valuable teleoperation data and reduces unnecessary hard resets.
