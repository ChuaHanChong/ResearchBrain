# Mixpert: Mitigating Multimodal Learning Conflicts with Efficient Mixture-of-Vision-Experts

> [!summary] Summary
> Mixpert applies the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) paradigm to the vision encoder of Multimodal Large Language Models, mitigating domain conflicts by using specialized experts for different visual tasks like charts, documents, or general images. This approach achieves consistent performance gains across diverse benchmarks while maintaining high computational efficiency during inference.

## Problem

- Single vision encoders in Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) experience performance degradation due to conflicting optimization objectives when simultaneously handling diverse visual domains.
- Using multiple independent vision encoders to address domain conflicts significantly increases computational costs, memory requirements, and deployment complexity.
- A challenge existed in achieving the specialization benefits of multiple encoders while maintaining the efficiency and joint optimization advantages of a single, integrated system.

## Method

- Mixpert integrates a ==Mixture-of-Vision-Experts (MoE) paradigm== into the MLLM's vision encoder, comprising a shared shallow component and multiple domain-specific experts (e.g., Document, Math, Chart, OCR, General, Versatile).
- A ==lightweight two-layer MLP dynamic router== analyzes features from the shared component and employs a "score-difference" strategy to determine which specialized expert receives the input.
- A =="Disentangled Vision Expert Tuning" strategy== is used for training, where experts are initialized from a jointly trained MLLM and then fine-tuned on domain-specific data with frozen shared components and LLM parameters, followed by separate router training.

## Results

- Mixpert achieved consistent performance gains across diverse benchmarks, including **+0.6%** on ChartQA and DocVQA, **+31** points on OCRBench, and **+34** points on MME when integrated with LLaVA-OV.
- The model demonstrates high efficiency, adding only **237.1M** total parameters, activating just **1.3M** additional parameters during inference, and incurring negligible FLOPs (**0.001G**) overhead.
- The dynamic routing mechanism proved highly accurate in classifying visual inputs to specific domains, achieving, for example, **95.2%** for OCR, **94.0%** for Math, and **93.7%** for General images.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Applying the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) paradigm to the vision encoder, specifically on deeper layers and the projector, can effectively mitigate domain conflicts in MLLMs without substantial computational overhead.
> - A training strategy that disentangles expert specialization (fine-tuning on domain-specific data with frozen shared components) from router training preserves joint learning benefits while enabling deep domain expertise.
> - A dynamic routing mechanism with a "score-difference" strategy effectively directs diverse visual inputs to the most appropriate specialized expert, providing robust handling for ambiguous cases.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Introduction

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have emerged as powerful systems capable of understanding and reasoning about both visual and textual information. These models typically combine a vision encoder with a large language model, allowing them to process images alongside text. However, a fundamental challenge arises when these models encounter diverse visual tasks - from analyzing complex charts and documents to understanding general photographs or mathematical diagrams.

The research presented in "Mixpert: Mitigating Multimodal Learning Conflicts with Efficient Mixture-of-Vision-Experts" addresses a critical limitation in current MLLM architectures. When a single vision encoder is tasked with handling multiple visual domains simultaneously, it often experiences performance degradation due to conflicting optimization objectives - a phenomenon known as domain conflicts. While using multiple specialized encoders can address this issue, it introduces significant computational overhead and deployment complexity.

Mixpert introduces an innovative solution that applies the Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) paradigm specifically to the vision encoder component, creating specialized experts for different visual domains while maintaining efficiency and joint optimization benefits.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

#### 2.1. The Problem of Domain Conflicts in Multimodal Models

Domain conflicts in multimodal learning represent a fundamental challenge where optimizing for one visual task can degrade performance on another. The authors demonstrate this through carefully designed experiments comparing different training strategies:

1. **Joint Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Training on diverse visual tasks simultaneously provides foundational capabilities and common knowledge acquisition but results in suboptimal performance on specific domains.

2. **Task-specific SFT**: Fine-tuning exclusively on individual domains improves performance for those specific tasks but loses the benefits of cross-domain knowledge transfer.

3. **Joint SFT + Task-specific SFT**: Sequential training that first establishes broad capabilities then specializes, showing the need for both approaches.

The research reveals that while joint optimization is crucial for acquiring robust foundational capabilities, it creates conflicts when dealing with diverse visual characteristics. For example, processing detailed financial charts requires different visual attention patterns compared to understanding general photographs or reading dense documents.

Traditional solutions involve using multiple independent vision encoders, each specialized for different domains. While effective, this approach significantly increases computational costs, memory requirements, and deployment complexity. The challenge becomes: how to achieve the specialization benefits of multiple encoders while maintaining the efficiency and joint optimization advantages of a single encoder system.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Mixpert restructures the traditional vision encoder into a sophisticated mixture-of-experts framework consisting of several key components:

#### 3.1. Shared Component Architecture

The **shared component** ($g_s(\cdot)$) comprises the shallow layers of the vision encoder and serves as the foundation for extracting common visual features. This component processes all input images regardless of their domain, capturing fundamental visual patterns and representations that are universally beneficial across different tasks.

#### 3.2. Multi-Expert Structure

The deeper layers of the vision encoder and the projector are organized into domain-specific experts, each tailored for particular visual characteristics:

- **Document Expert**: Specializes in processing text-heavy images, forms, and structured documents
- **Mathematical Expert**: Focuses on mathematical diagrams, equations, and geometric figures
- **Chart Expert**: Optimized for graphs, plots, and data visualizations
- **OCR Expert**: Dedicated to optical character recognition tasks and text extraction
- **General Expert**: Handles everyday photographs and common visual scenes
- **Versatile Expert**: Acts as a fallback for ambiguous or uncategorized inputs

#### 3.3. Dynamic Routing Mechanism

The routing system employs a lightweight two-layer MLP that analyzes features from the shared component to determine the most appropriate expert for each input. The router predicts probability scores for each domain type and implements a sophisticated "score-difference" strategy:

```
d = s^(1) - s^(2)
if d < τ:
    route to versatile expert
else:
    route to highest-scoring expert
```

Where $s^{(1)}$ and $s^{(2)}$ are the highest and second-highest routing scores, and $τ$ is a predefined threshold. This approach ensures robust handling of ambiguous cases by routing uncertain classifications to the versatile expert.

#### 3.4. Methodology and Training Approach

Mixpert employs a unique training strategy called "Disentangled Vision Expert Tuning" that optimizes each expert independently while preserving shared knowledge:

#### 3.5. Phase 1: Expert Initialization and Specialization

1. **Initialization**: All experts begin with weights from a jointly supervised fine-tuned MLLM, ensuring they start with comprehensive foundational capabilities.

2. **Selective Freezing**: The shared component and LLM parameters remain frozen during expert training, preserving common knowledge while allowing specialization.

3. **Domain-Specific Training**: Each expert is fine-tuned exclusively on its corresponding domain data, enabling deep specialization without interference from other domains.

#### 3.6. Phase 2: Router Training

The routing mechanism is trained using a dedicated dataset with domain labels, learning to classify visual inputs accurately across the predefined categories. The router achieves high classification accuracy:

- Chart: 89.2%
- Document: 91.3%
- OCR: 95.2%
- Math: 94.0%
- General: 93.7%

#### 3.7. Training Benefits

This disentangled approach offers several advantages:
- **Simplified Data Balancing**: No need to carefully balance datasets across domains during training
- **Reduced Training Complexity**: Experts can be trained independently and in parallel
- **Preserved Joint Benefits**: Shared components maintain cross-domain knowledge while experts specialize
- **Modular Development**: New experts can be added without retraining existing components

#### 3.8. Technical Innovations and Efficiency

Mixpert introduces several technical innovations that distinguish it from existing approaches:

#### 3.9. Sparse Activation Pattern

Unlike traditional multiple encoder methods that activate all encoders simultaneously, Mixpert activates only one expert per input, dramatically reducing computational requirements during inference while maintaining specialization benefits.

#### 3.10. Plug-and-Play Integration

The modular design enables seamless integration into existing MLLM architectures without requiring extensive modifications or complete retraining. This compatibility across different model families (LLaVA-OV, InternVL2, Qwen2-VL) demonstrates broad applicability.

#### 3.11. Robust Routing Strategy

The score-difference routing mechanism provides superior handling of edge cases and ambiguous inputs compared to simple threshold-based or direct routing approaches. This robustness ensures reliable performance across diverse real-world scenarios.

#### 3.12. Joint Optimization Benefits

By maintaining shared components and enabling joint pre-training, Mixpert preserves the foundational knowledge acquisition benefits that are lost in purely independent multi-encoder approaches.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive experimental evaluation demonstrates Mixpert's effectiveness across multiple dimensions:

#### 4.1. Architecture Optimization

Through systematic ablation studies, the authors determined that restructuring only the projector and final two layers of the Vision Transformer as MoE components provides optimal performance-efficiency trade-offs. While applying MoE to all layers yields maximum performance gains (+1.5% on ChartQA, +1.7% on MathVista), the selected configuration reduces additional parameters by 1.9B while maintaining substantial improvements.

#### 4.2. Performance Improvements

Mixpert consistently delivers significant performance gains across diverse benchmarks:

**LLaVA-OV Integration:**
- ChartQA: +0.6% improvement
- DocVQA: +0.6% improvement
- OCRBench: +31 points
- MME: +34 points
- Notable gains on MathVerse and MathVista

**Cross-Architecture Compatibility:**
When integrated with other state-of-the-art models:
- InternVL2-8B: +0.7% on ChartQA and DocVQA, +61 on MME
- Qwen2-VL-7B: Consistent performance improvements across benchmarks

#### 4.3. Efficiency Analysis

The efficiency metrics demonstrate Mixpert's practical advantages:
- **Total Additional Parameters**: 237.1M
- **Activated Parameters During Inference**: Only 1.3M additional
- **Additional FLOPs**: Negligible (0.001G)
- **Memory Overhead**: Minimal compared to multiple encoder approaches

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Mixpert represents a significant advancement in multimodal AI with several important implications:

#### 5.1. Immediate Impact

The research directly addresses one of the most pressing challenges in MLLM development - the trade-off between specialization and efficiency. By demonstrating that domain conflicts can be effectively mitigated with minimal computational overhead, Mixpert makes high-performance multimodal systems more accessible for practical deployment.

#### 5.2. Methodological Contributions

The application of MoE principles specifically to vision encoders, rather than language models, opens new research directions. The disentangled expert tuning strategy provides a template for training specialized components while preserving shared knowledge.

#### 5.3. Scalability and Extensibility

The modular architecture supports easy extension to new visual domains by adding specialized experts without disrupting existing functionality. This scalability is crucial as multimodal applications continue to diversify.

#### 5.4. Industrial Applications

The efficiency characteristics make Mixpert particularly valuable for resource-constrained environments and large-scale deployments where computational costs significantly impact feasibility.

#### 5.5. Future Research Directions

Mixpert establishes a foundation for several promising research areas:
- Adaptive expert allocation based on input complexity
- Cross-modal MoE architectures spanning vision and language
- Automated domain discovery and expert creation
- Integration with emerging multimodal tasks and modalities

The work demonstrates that sophisticated specialization can be achieved without sacrificing the fundamental advantages that make joint learning valuable, providing a pathway toward more capable and efficient multimodal AI systems.
