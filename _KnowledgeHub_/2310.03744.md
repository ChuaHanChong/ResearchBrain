# Improved Baselines with Visual Instruction Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> LLaVA-1.5 presents an enhanced Large Multimodal Model from UW-Madison and Microsoft Research that achieves state-of-the-art performance across 11 diverse visual benchmarks. This work establishes a strong, reproducible open-source baseline by demonstrating that a simple architectural update and careful instruction tuning can yield robust generalization with significantly less training data and computational resources than comparable models.

## Problem

- Ambiguity exists regarding the optimal design and training strategies for developing truly general-purpose Large Multimodal Models (LMMs).
- Many LMMs struggle with balancing performance on academic, short-form VQA benchmarks and general, long-form conversational responses.
- The lack of systematic, controlled studies and accessible, reproducible open-source baselines hinders broader research and comparison in LMM development.

## Method

- Enhances the ==LLaVA== framework with a ==two-layer MLP== for the vision-language connector, replacing the original linear projection.
- Introduces response format prompting and full LLM fine-tuning to balance short-form VQA answers with long-form responses, integrating additional academic-task-oriented datasets (VQA, OCR, region-level data) to broaden specialized visual capabilities.
- Develops a novel ==grid-splitting and feature-merging== approach with global context (LLaVA-1.5-HD) to efficiently scale to higher input resolutions without expensive pre-training.

## Results

- LLaVA-1.5 achieves state-of-the-art performance across **11 diverse** benchmarks (VQAv2, GQA, LLaVA-Bench) with remarkable efficiency: training on only **1.2 million** public data points in ~**1 day** on a single 8-A100 node.
- LLaVA-1.5-HD, with increased input resolution (**448x448**), further improves performance, particularly on fine-grained perception tasks, and reduces hallucination.
- The model exhibits emergent properties such as generalization to novel response formats (e.g., JSON output) and multilingual multimodal capabilities (e.g., in Chinese and Spanish) despite English-only visual instruction tuning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Simple architectural improvements, such as a two-layer MLP connector and precise response formatting prompts, combined with high-quality visual instruction tuning on strong pre-trained components, achieve state-of-the-art results with significantly less training data and computational resources than previously thought necessary.
> - Insufficient input resolution can contribute to model hallucination, and scaling to higher visual inputs can mitigate this, suggesting a direct link between visual information quality and model reliability.
> - LLaVA-1.5 exhibits compositional capabilities, such as emergent multilingual multimodal understanding derived from text-only multilingual data combined with English visual instruction tuning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) has recently seen explosive growth, driven by the success of Large Language Models (LLMs) and the increasing availability of large-scale image-text datasets. LMMs are envisioned as key building blocks for general-purpose AI assistants that can understand and reason across different modalities, particularly vision and language.

The prevailing paradigm in LMM development is "visual instruction tuning." This involves taking a pre-trained visual encoder (like CLIP's Vision Transformer) and a pre-trained Large Language Model (LLM, like LLaMA or Vicuna) and connecting them through a lightweight projection layer. The entire system is then fine-tuned on multimodal instruction-following data, enabling it to respond to diverse visual queries in a conversational manner. Early pioneers in this space include LLaVA and MiniGPT-4, which demonstrated impressive capabilities in visual reasoning and instruction following.

The current research landscape is characterized by several ongoing efforts:
- **Scaling Up:** Researchers are exploring improvements by scaling up various components: pre-training data, instruction-following data, visual encoders, and base language models.
- **Architectural Diversity:** While LLaVA uses a simple linear projection connector, other models like InstructBLIP and Qwen-VL introduce more complex "visual resamplers" (e.g., Q-former) to process visual tokens more efficiently or effectively.
- **Benchmark Development:** To better understand and evaluate LMM capabilities, numerous benchmarks have been proposed, covering tasks like Visual Question Answering (VQA), image captioning, and conversational visual reasoning.
- **Downstream Applications:** LMM architectures are being adapted for specific tasks and domains, including region-level and pixel-level understanding, biomedical applications, and image generation.

Despite this rapid progress, a significant challenge remains: understanding the optimal "recipe" for training LMMs towards truly general-purpose assistants. Discrepancies in performance across benchmarks (e.g., LLaVA excelling in conversational tasks, while InstructBLIP performs better on traditional VQA) highlight that architectural choices, training data mixtures, and fine-tuning strategies have nuanced impacts. Often, state-of-the-art models leverage proprietary or in-house data, making reproducibility and systematic comparison difficult. This paper steps into this landscape by providing a systematic, controlled study within the LLaVA framework, aiming to demystify effective design choices and establish strong, publicly reproducible baselines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation of this research is to conduct the first systematic study of LMM design choices within a controlled setting, specifically using the LLaVA framework. The authors aim to identify the most effective and efficient components for training LMMs, addressing the existing ambiguity about what truly constitutes the "best recipe" for general-purpose assistants.

Key objectives include:
1. **Investigate LLaVA's Architecture:** To understand why LLaVA, despite its architectural simplicity, achieves commendable proficiency, especially in conversational visual reasoning. The authors hypothesize that its simple fully-connected vision-language connector is surprisingly powerful and data-efficient.
2. **Establish Stronger, More Feasible Baselines:** To improve upon the original LLaVA model by incorporating simple yet effective modifications, specifically focusing on generalizability across diverse visual tasks, including academic-task-oriented VQA that LLaVA previously struggled with.
3. **Address Multitask Balancing Problem:** To resolve the challenge of balancing short-form answers (common in VQA benchmarks) and long-form conversational responses (crucial for general assistants). This involves investigating the role of response formatting prompts and LLM fine-tuning.
4. **Explore Open Problems in LMMs:** To conduct early explorations into critical areas for LMM development, including:
 - **Scaling to Higher Resolution Inputs:** How to efficiently process high-resolution images to improve detailed perception.
 - **Compositional Capabilities:** Understanding how LMMs generalize to tasks requiring a combination of learned skills.
 - **Data Efficiency:** Investigating the minimum amount of data required to maintain strong performance.
 - **Model Hallucination:** Gaining insights into the causes and mitigation strategies for LMM hallucination, particularly concerning input resolution and data granularity.
5. **Promote Accessibility and Reproducibility:** To offer an improved, open-source, and easily reproducible baseline (LLaVA-1.5) that uses only publicly available data and is computationally affordable, thereby making state-of-the-art LMM research more accessible to the broader community.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors' approach is rooted in systematically enhancing the original LLaVA framework, focusing on impactful yet simple modifications and rigorous empirical evaluation.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries: The LLaVA Framework

LLaVA's core architecture consists of a pre-trained visual encoder (CLIP-ViT), a pre-trained large language model (LLaMA/Vicuna), and a single linear projection layer connecting the visual features to the language model's embedding space. The training follows a two-stage protocol:
1. **Vision-Language Alignment Pre-training:** Aligning visual features with the LLM's word embedding space using image-text pairs (e.g., 600K pairs for original LLaVA).
2. **Visual Instruction Tuning:** Fine-tuning the model on multimodal instruction-following data to enable conversational capabilities.

The paper highlights that while original LLaVA excelled in conversational tasks, it fell short on academic VQA benchmarks requiring short answers, sometimes defaulting to "yes" for yes/no questions due to data distribution.

#### 3.2. Enhancements for LLaVA-1.5

The authors introduce several modifications to create LLaVA-1.5:

- **Response Format Prompting:** To address the "multitask balancing problem" (short vs. long answers), a critical finding is that prior models like InstructBLIP struggled because of ambiguous prompts and not fine-tuning the LLM itself. LLaVA-1.5 proposes appending a clear response formatting prompt (e.g., "Answer the question using a single word or phrase.") to VQA questions when short answers are desired. Critically, the entire LLM is fine-tuned, not just a visual resampler, allowing it to adapt its output format. This negates the need for complex data conversions like those used by some prior works.
- **MLP Vision-Language Connector:** Instead of a single linear layer, LLaVA-1.5 uses a two-layer Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) for the vision-language connector. Inspired by improvements in self-supervised learning, this simple change enhances the representation power of the connection.
- **Academic Task-Oriented Data Inclusion:** LLaVA-1.5 integrates additional publicly available academic VQA datasets beyond LLaVA's original conversational data. These include:
 - VQA datasets: VQAv2, OKVQA, A-OKVQA.
 - OCR datasets: OCRVQA, TextCaps.
 - Region-level VQA datasets: Visual Genome, RefCOCO.
 These datasets are incorporated with specific response formatting prompts where appropriate (e.g., for multiple-choice questions).
- **Additional Scaling:**
 - **Higher Resolution Input:** The vision encoder is swapped to CLIP-ViT-L-336px to allow for 336x336 pixel input resolution, enabling the LLM to perceive more visual details.
 - **Additional Knowledge Source:** The GQA dataset is added.
 - **Language Model Scaling:** The base LLM is scaled up to a 13B parameter model (Vicuna-13B v1.5).
 - **General Conversational Data:** ShareGPT data, a text-only conversational dataset, is incorporated to enhance the LLM's general conversational and writing capabilities.

#### 3.3. Scaling to Higher Resolutions (LLaVA-1.5-HD)

To go beyond the fixed resolution of pre-trained CLIP models (e.g., 336x336), LLaVA-1.5-HD employs a novel strategy:
- **Grid Splitting:** Images are divided into smaller 224x224 patches, each encoded independently by the CLIP encoder.
- **Feature Merging:** The features from these patches are combined into a single, larger feature map.
- **Global Context:** To mitigate artifacts from splitting and merging, features from a downsampled (224x224) version of the original image are additionally concatenated, providing the LLM with overall global context.
This approach allows flexible scaling to arbitrary resolutions without requiring expensive positional embedding interpolation or massive high-resolution pre-training.

#### 3.4. Computational Cost

Despite the improvements, LLaVA-1.5 maintains high computational efficiency. It uses merely 1.2M publicly available data points and completes full training (pre-training and instruction tuning) in approximately 1 day on a single 8-A100 node. This is a significant advantage over models that require hundreds of millions or billions of image-text pairs and multiple compute nodes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of LLaVA-1.5 reveals several significant findings:

- **State-of-the-Art Performance:** LLaVA-1.5 achieves state-of-the-art results across a broad range of 11 benchmarks, encompassing both academic-task-oriented datasets (e.g., VQAv2, GQA, VizWiz, SQA-IMG, TextVQA) and instruction-following LMM benchmarks (e.g., POPE, MME, MMBench, SEED-Bench, LLaVA-Bench, MM-Vet). This demonstrates its robust generalization capabilities across diverse visual understanding tasks and response formats.
- **Remarkable Data and Computational Efficiency:** A standout finding is that LLaVA-1.5 achieves SoTA performance with magnitudes smaller pre-training and instruction tuning data compared to other leading methods like InstructBLIP (129M pre-training data) and Qwen-VL (1.4B pre-training data). The total instruction tuning data used is only 665K samples, and it trains within ~1 day on a single 8-A100 node, making it highly resource-efficient and accessible.
- **Impact of Design Choices:**
 - The **MLP vision-language connector** significantly improves LLaVA's multimodal capabilities over the original linear projection.
 - **Response format prompting** combined with **fine-tuning the LLM** is crucial for balancing short-form and long-form answers, enabling LLaVA-1.5 to perform well on VQA benchmarks without sacrificing conversational fluency.
 - The inclusion of **academic-task-oriented VQA, OCR, and region-level data** is vital for enhancing specialized visual capabilities.
- **Higher Resolution Scaling (LLaVA-1.5-HD):** Scaling the input resolution to 448x448 with LLaVA-1.5-HD further improves overall performance, particularly on tasks requiring fine-grained perception (e.g., OCR, detailed descriptions). The "global context" mechanism (concatenating features of a downsampled image) effectively recovers the model from potential "split-and-merge" artifacts, guiding it to relevant regions.
- **Data Efficiency Insights:** Ablation studies on data efficiency show that randomly downsampling LLaVA-1.5's training data mixture by up to 50% does not significantly decrease performance (retains over 98% of full dataset performance). This suggests potential for further data compression or more sophisticated sampling strategies, reinforcing the "less-is-more" principle observed in NLP.
- **Emerging Properties:**
 - **Format Instruction Generalization:** LLaVA-1.5 generalizes to novel response format instructions not explicitly seen during training, such as generating JSON outputs or structured prompts for image generation. It can also adapt its response granularity (brief vs. detailed) within a conversation.
 - **Multilingual Multimodal Capability:** Despite being trained solely on English visual instructions, LLaVA-1.5 exhibits multilingual instruction-following capabilities (e.g., in Spanish, Japanese, Chinese). This is attributed to the text-only multilingual ShareGPT data, demonstrating a compositional learning effect. Quantitatively, it outperforms Qwen-VL-Chat on MMBench-CN (Chinese benchmark), highlighting this emergent property.
- **Ablation on LLM Choices:** The choice of the base LLM significantly impacts the final LMM's performance. Vicuna-v1.5 (LLaMA-2-based) generally outperforms LLaMA-1-based variants (Vicuna-v1.1, v1.3), underscoring the importance of a capable and well-aligned base language model for multimodal tasks.
- **Rethinking Hallucination:** A profound finding is the link between input resolution and hallucination. When the input resolution is insufficient to discern details, the model may learn to hallucinate. Conversely, scaling to higher resolutions significantly reduces hallucination, suggesting that perceived hallucination might sometimes be a consequence of insufficient visual input rather than inherent data errors alone. This highlights the need for a balance between data granularity and model perceptual capability.
- **Compositional Capabilities:** LLaVA-1.5 demonstrates that capabilities learned from independent tasks can compose to improve performance on more complex, combined tasks without explicit joint training. For instance, ShareGPT data improved general language capabilities, while academic VQA data improved visual groundness in conversations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work presented in "Improved Baselines with Visual Instruction Tuning" has several profound significances and potential impacts on the research and development of Large Multimodal Models:

- **Establishing Accessible and Reproducible Baselines:** By providing LLaVA-1.5 as an open-source model trained exclusively on publicly available data with minimal computational resources (1 day on a single 8-A100 node), the authors dramatically lower the barrier to entry for LMM research. This makes state-of-the-art LMM development more accessible to academic researchers and smaller institutions, fostering broader participation and innovation.
- **Challenging Current Paradigms:** The paper critically questions the common belief that LMMs necessitate massive-scale vision-language alignment pre-training on hundreds of millions or billions of image-text pairs, as done by models like InstructBLIP and Qwen-VL. LLaVA-1.5's success with significantly less pre-training data (558K) suggests that high-quality visual instruction tuning, combined with a strong pre-trained vision encoder and LLM, can be surprisingly effective. It also implicitly questions the absolute necessity of complex visual resamplers (like Q-former) when a simpler MLP connector, paired with effective instruction tuning, yields superior results.
- **Advancing LMM Understanding:** The systematic study provides invaluable insights into key design choices and emergent properties:
 - The power of simple architectural components (MLP connector).
 - The critical role of precise response formatting prompts in instruction tuning.
 - The relationship between input resolution, detailed perception, and model hallucination, suggesting that some "hallucinations" might stem from insufficient visual information rather than just data noise.
 - Demonstrations of compositional capabilities (e.g., multilingual visual conversation from English visual data + multilingual text-only data), opening avenues for more efficient training by leveraging transfer learning across modalities and tasks.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** The findings offer clear directions for future LMM development:
 - **Data Efficiency:** The revelation that performance is largely maintained even with significant data subsampling suggests that researchers could focus on data quality and diversity over sheer quantity, or explore sophisticated data compression/sampling techniques.
 - **Hallucination Mitigation:** The connection between resolution and hallucination provides a concrete direction for improving LMM reliability.
 - **Compositionality:** Further understanding the mechanisms behind compositional capabilities could lead to more robust and generalized LMMs without explicitly training on all possible task combinations.
- **Catalyst for Innovation:** LLaVA-1.5, as a high-performing, reproducible, and resource-efficient baseline, is likely to become a reference point for future research, enabling faster iteration and comparative studies. Its open-source nature facilitates community contributions and specialized adaptations.

**Limitations Acknowledged by Authors:**
Despite its strengths, the paper also acknowledges several limitations:
- **Prolonged Training for High-Resolution Images:** While efficient, training with full image patches at higher resolutions (e.g., LLaVA-1.5-HD) can still be computationally intensive.
- **Lack of Multi-Image Understanding:** The current model cannot process multiple images simultaneously due to architecture design and lack of relevant training data.
- **Limited Problem-Solving Capabilities:** While strong in instruction following, its deeper problem-solving abilities in highly specialized fields remain constrained by the base LLM and training data.
- **Residual Hallucination:** Despite significant reductions, LLaVA-1.5 is not entirely immune to hallucination or disseminating misinformation, necessitating caution in critical applications.

Overall, this paper represents a significant step forward in making advanced LMM research more transparent, reproducible, and accessible, while simultaneously pushing the boundaries of what is achievable with efficient architectural designs and instruction tuning strategies.
