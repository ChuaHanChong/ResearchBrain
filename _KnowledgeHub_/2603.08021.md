# AffordGrasp: Cross-Modal Diffusion for Affordance-Aware Grasp Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> AffordGrasp presents a diffusion-based framework that synthesizes physically stable and semantically accurate human grasping poses from object point clouds and textual instructions. The framework enhances grasp quality and semantic alignment by integrating object affordances and a distribution adjustment module, outperforming prior methods on various benchmarks and showing successful deployment on a real robot.

## Problem

- Traditional grasp generation methods rely solely on 3D object geometry, limiting their ability to produce grasps aligned with semantic intent or interaction context.
- Existing semantic grasping approaches struggle with a significant modality gap between raw 3D geometry and natural language, hindering fine-grained geometric-semantic alignment.
- Current diffusion pipelines for grasp synthesis often lack explicit spatial and instruction-driven constraints, leading to physically invalid or semantically inconsistent grasps.

## Method

- **AffordGrasp** uses a ==self-looping annotation engine== and ==Affordance Generator== to predict point-wise affordance probabilities on object surfaces, explicitly bridging the language-geometry modality gap.
- A ==cross-modal latent diffusion model (LDM)== is conditioned on textual instructions, object point clouds, and predicted affordance maps to synthesize grasp poses.
- A ==Distribution Adjustment Module (DAM)== refines the diffusion model's latent grasp representations, integrating contextual and physical constraints to ensure physical plausibility and semantic alignment.

## Results

- AffordGrasp achieved state-of-the-art performance across HO-3D, OakInk, GRAB, and AffordPose benchmarks, demonstrating improvements in physical plausibility (e.g., reduced penetration volume), grasp stability, diversity, and semantic accuracy (up to **80.08%** on OakInk).
- The framework exhibited strong zero-shot generalization capabilities, significantly outperforming baselines on out-of-domain datasets like HO-3D and AffordPose in terms of penetration volume and semantic accuracy.
- Successful real-world deployment with a ShadowHand robot demonstrated the system's ability to generate motions consistent with varied language instructions for the same object, meeting requirements for physical environments.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling object affordances provides a crucial intermediate representation that effectively bridges the modality gap between natural language instructions and 3D object geometry for fine-grained semantic grounding.
> - Combining a conditional latent diffusion model with a dedicated refinement module (DAM) allows for both broad exploration of grasp space and precise adherence to physical and semantic constraints during synthesis.
> - An automated, self-looping annotation pipeline can scalably enrich existing datasets with consistent, fine-grained language and affordance labels, addressing data scarcity for complex human-object interaction understanding.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human grasp synthesis is critical for advancements in augmented reality (AR), virtual reality (VR), robotics, and embodied artificial intelligence (AI). Traditional approaches to grasp generation primarily rely on 3D object geometry, which limits their ability to produce grasps that align with a user's semantic intent or interaction context. For instance, holding a teacup by its handle versus its rim requires distinct semantic understanding beyond just its shape.

Recent efforts have begun to integrate textual instructions with 3D object representations, often employing diffusion models. However, these methods face challenges, including a significant modality gap between raw 3D geometry and natural language, which hinders fine-grained geometric-semantic alignment. This gap can lead to physically invalid or semantically inconsistent grasps. Additionally, current diffusion pipelines often lack explicit spatial and instruction-driven constraints, potentially resulting in inappropriate contact or unrealistic poses. Approaches leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for semantic grounding also encounter issues with consistency and controllability due to error propagation and context dependencies in multi-turn question answering.

AffordGrasp addresses these limitations by proposing a framework that explicitly bridges the modality gap through an affordance-aware representation and a dual-conditioning diffusion process. It aims to enhance precision, physical stability, and semantic consistency in generated human grasps, thereby advancing the capabilities of human-robot interaction and synthetic environments beyond purely geometric considerations.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objective of this work is to generate human grasping poses that are both physically stable and semantically faithful to user-specified textual instructions, with high precision. The authors identify several motivations and challenges that drive their research:

1. **Bridging the Modality Gap:** Existing semantic grasping approaches struggle with the large discrepancy between 3D object representations (like point clouds) and natural language instructions. This gap makes it difficult for models to achieve fine-grained geometric-semantic alignment, such as distinguishing "grasp the handle" from "hold the rim" for the same object. AffordGrasp aims to reduce this gap by introducing object affordances as a complementary guidance mechanism for cross-modal fusion.
2. **Addressing Lack of Explicit Constraints:** Current semantic grasp generation methods often lack explicit spatial or semantic constraints during the generation process. This absence can lead to physically invalid hand poses, interpenetration with the object, or grasps that are semantically inconsistent with the provided instruction. The motivation is to integrate mechanisms that enforce physical plausibility and semantic alignment.
3. **Improving Data Annotation Scalability and Consistency:** Enhancing existing hand-object interaction datasets with fine-grained, structured language labels that capture interaction intent is crucial for training robust semantic grasp generation models. However, manual annotation is labor-intensive, and VLM-based annotation can suffer from inconsistencies. The authors are motivated to develop a scalable and consistent automated annotation pipeline to enrich datasets.
4. **Enhancing Grasp Quality and Diversity:** Beyond semantic accuracy, the goal is to generate physically stable and diverse grasping poses that are representative of natural human interactions. This includes minimizing object penetration, ensuring stability during simulated lifting, and offering a variety of plausible grasps for a given instruction.

In summary, the key contributions of AffordGrasp are:
- Introducing AffordGrasp, a diffusion-based framework for generating precise, physically stable, and semantically meaningful grasps without requiring test-time adaptation.
- Proposing object affordance as a complementary guidance mechanism to bridge linguistic semantics and geometric representations.
- Developing a Distribution Adjustment Module (DAM) to maintain diffusion sampling stability while enforcing physical and semantic constraints.
- Establishing new state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks for grasp quality, semantic accuracy, and diversity.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AffordGrasp is a diffusion-based framework designed to synthesize human grasp poses from an object point cloud and a textual instruction. It operates through three main integrated components: an Affordance Generator, a Cross-Modal Diffusion Model, and a Distribution Adjustment Module (DAM).

#### 3.1. Affordance Generator

The Affordance Generator's role is to bridge the gap between language and geometry by predicting point-wise affordance probabilities on the object's surface. Given an object point cloud ($P_g$) and a textual instruction ($I$), it outputs an affordance map ($P_a$) that highlights semantically relevant regions for interaction.

- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** To overcome the limited availability of diverse affordance-annotated datasets (only AffordPose provides such labels), the authors implement a self-looping annotation engine. Initially, a network (based on LASO architecture) is trained on AffordPose. This trained model then generates pseudo-labels for unlabeled datasets like OakInk and GRAB, expanding the training data and enhancing geometric diversity.
- **Addressing Class Imbalance:** Affordance prediction often faces class imbalance (e.g., many non-affording points versus few affording points). This is mitigated by optimizing the model using a combined objective of Focal Loss and Dice Loss.

#### 3.2. Text and Affordance Guided Grasp Generation

This component utilizes a cross-modal latent diffusion model (LDM) conditioned on the triplet $C = \{I, P_g, P_a\}$.

- **Feature Extraction:**
 - The language instruction ($I$) is encoded using RoBERTa.
 - The object point cloud ($P_g$) and the predicted affordance map ($P_a$) are processed by two independent PointNet encoders.
 - The resulting features ($f_I, f_{pg}, f_{pa}$) are fused into a unified conditioning vector ($f$).
- **Latent Hand Representation:** Ground-truth hand mesh vertices are encoded into a compact latent representation using a pre-trained autoencoder (specifically, one based on FastGrasp for its reconstruction fidelity and compression ratio).
- **Conditional Diffusion Model:** A conditional diffusion model (U-Net) is trained to learn the distribution of these latent hand embeddings, conditioned on the fused feature vector ($f$). The model iteratively denoises a noisy latent representation to reconstruct realistic and semantically consistent hand meshes, guided by both linguistic and geometric cues. The training objective minimizes the difference between the predicted noise and the actual noise added during the forward diffusion process.
- **Hand Mesh Reconstruction:** The denoised latent representation is decoded into MANO parameters, which are then used by a differentiable MANO layer to reconstruct the 3D hand mesh.

#### 3.3. Distribution Adjustment Module (DAM)

The DAM is a lightweight fusion framework designed to refine the latent grasp representation predicted by the diffusion module, ensuring closer alignment with contextual and physical constraints.

- **Input and Refinement:** The DAM takes the intermediate latent hand pose representation (derived from the diffusion model's noise prediction) and integrates it with the conditioning feature ($f$).
- **Architecture:** It fuses global object features and affordance features with the latent hand feature to create a spatial representation. This spatial representation interacts with the instruction embedding via a multi-head attention (MHA) module. A dual residual mechanism is employed to preserve both instruction semantics and the original hand representation.
- **Training:** During DAM training, the diffusion model remains frozen. The DAM refines the diffusion model’s output, optimizing it with a combined loss that includes reconstruction losses (for MANO parameters and hand mesh vertices) and physical constraint losses (consistency, contact map, and penetration). These physical losses penalize object-object interpenetration and promote consistent contact patterns.

#### 3.4. Inference

The inference process begins by sampling random noise, which is then iteratively denoised by the conditional diffusion model (using DDIM for efficiency) to synthesize a coarse grasp pose in the latent space. This intermediate latent output is then passed through the DAM for refinement, producing an optimized latent vector. Finally, this refined latent vector is decoded into MANO parameters and subsequently into a 3D hand mesh, yielding a grasp pose that respects both linguistic plausibility and physical feasibility. This two-stage approach mitigates cross-modal challenges by narrowing the gap between language and geometry.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluates AffordGrasp across four instruction-augmented benchmarks: HO-3D, OakInk, GRAB, and AffordPose.

#### 4.1. Automated Labeling for Dataset Enrichment

- **Process:** An automated pipeline was developed to generate instruction annotations for OakInk and GRAB datasets. This involved cold-start training of a classifier (using PointBERT) on AffordPose for initial language labels, followed by iterative refinement through high-confidence pseudo-labeling, human validation, and retraining. Large language models (Qwen) were then used to generate task-oriented instructional text.
- **Validation:** The semantic classifier achieved an accuracy of 98% on both validation and test sets of the AffordPose dataset, suggesting effective and consistent annotation. The affordance prediction model, validated against state-of-the-art 3D affordance learning methods, demonstrated high Intersection Over Union (IoU), Area Under the Curve (AUC), and Similarity (SIM) scores across various object categories and actions, with low Mean Absolute Error (MAE).

#### 4.2. Evaluation Metrics

Grasp poses were assessed using four criteria:
1. **Physical Plausibility:** Measured by hand-object mutual penetration volume (lower is better) and contact ratio (higher is better).
2. **Grasp Stability:** Quantified by gravitational displacement of the object’s center of mass in a physics simulation (lower displacement indicates greater robustness).
3. **Diversity:** Assessed by the entropy of K-means cluster assignments (higher entropy indicates more diverse distributions) and average cluster size (larger size reflects better coverage of grasp space) of 3D hand keypoints.
4. **Semantic Accuracy (ACC):** Evaluated by a classifier that determines if the generated hand pose aligns with the corresponding semantic affordance instruction (higher percentage indicates better alignment).

#### 4.3. Grasp Generation Performance

- **In-Domain Evaluation (OakInk, GRAB):** AffordGrasp demonstrated superior performance across all metrics (penetration volume, simulation displacement, contact ratio, entropy, cluster size, and semantic accuracy) compared to state-of-the-art baselines like TTA, FastGrasp, D-VQVAE, and a ControlNet-based approach. For example, on OakInk, AffordGrasp achieved a penetration volume of 7.31 (vs. 7.88 for FastGrasp) and an ACC of 80.08% (vs. 78.05% for FastGrasp). On GRAB, it achieved a penetration volume of 3.06 (vs. 4.61 for FastGrasp) and an ACC of 62.50% (comparable to FastGrasp's 61.50% but with better physical metrics).
- **Out-of-Domain Evaluation (HO-3D, AffordPose):** When trained on GRAB and tested on HO-3D and AffordPose (zero-shot settings), AffordGrasp again significantly outperformed baselines, demonstrating strong generalization capabilities. For example, on HO-3D, it achieved a penetration volume of 7.38 (vs. 12.55 for TTA) and an ACC of 72.00% (vs. 66% for TTA). On AffordPose, it had a penetration volume of 10.36 (vs. 19.41 for TTA) and an ACC of 69.71% (vs. 42.56% for TTA).
- **Qualitative Results:** Visualizations (Figure 5) illustrate that AffordGrasp generates grasps with reduced object penetration and improved semantic alignment compared to other methods.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

- **Excluding Object Affordances:** Removing object affordances led to a slight improvement in displacement distance but increased volume intrusion. This indicates that affordances contribute to capturing spatial relationships and minimizing collisions, while the observed displacement reduction when affordances are excluded might be due to objects becoming immobilized by increased penetration.
- **Excluding DAM:** Removing the Distribution Adjustment Module (DAM) resulted in larger cluster sizes, suggesting the diffusion model, without DAM, generates a broader range of poses but with weaker conditioning.
- **Whole Pipeline:** The full AffordGrasp pipeline, incorporating both object affordances and the DAM, showed a balance across metrics, with improved thematic coherence, adherence to physical constraints, and object contact rates. The DAM specifically enhanced the concentration and quality of output distribution.

#### 4.5. Downstream Applications and Real Robot Test

- **Simulation:** Generated grasps from AffordGrasp were successfully used as reference poses in the RaiSim physics simulator, guiding D-Grasp and CrossDex frameworks for dynamic grasp trajectories. In simulation, AffordGrasp achieved a comparable success rate to CrossDex (92.96% vs. 92.90%) with lower MPJPE (0.161) and FOL (0.235), indicating improved physical consistency and stability.
- **Real Robot:** Real-world experiments using a ShadowHand robot demonstrated that AffordGrasp generates motions consistent with varied language instructions for the same object, meeting essential requirements for successful grasping in physical environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AffordGrasp presents a significant advancement in the field of human grasp synthesis, particularly in its ability to generate high-precision, physically stable, and semantically faithful grasps from textual instructions.

- **Enhanced Realism and Control:** By explicitly modeling object affordances and integrating them into a dual-conditioning diffusion process, the framework provides a more robust mechanism for aligning linguistic semantics with 3D geometric representations. This leads to more realistic and physically plausible hand-object interactions, crucial for applications where natural and intuitive interactions are desired.
- **Improved Generality and Robustness:** The method's strong performance across diverse in-domain and out-of-domain datasets, including zero-shot generalization, indicates its potential for broad applicability in various scenarios without extensive re-training.
- **Scalable Data Enrichment:** The automated annotation pipeline addresses a key bottleneck in data-driven approaches by enabling scalable and consistent enrichment of existing datasets with fine-grained language labels. This can accelerate research and development in semantic interaction understanding.
- **Applications in AR/VR and Embodied AI:** The ability to synthesize instruction-aligned grasps directly from text is highly relevant for creating more immersive and interactive AR/VR experiences, as well as for developing more intelligent and dexterous robotic systems that can interpret and execute human commands more effectively. The successful validation in physics simulations and on a real robot underscores its practical utility.

The paper acknowledges a limitation regarding the explicit incorporation of physical priors such as gravity or friction, suggesting future work could integrate physics-based reasoning to further enhance realism. Nevertheless, AffordGrasp offers a foundational framework for advancing human grasp synthesis and interaction understanding, contributing to a future where AI systems can perform more natural and semantically aware physical interactions.
