# MoLe-VLA: Dynamic Layer-skipping Vision Language Action Model via Mixture-of-Layers for Efficient Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> MoLe-VLA is a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that dynamically skips layers in its underlying MLLM backbone, achieving significantly enhanced computational efficiency for robot manipulation tasks. The model reduces LLM computational costs by approximately 5.6x and improves mean success rates by up to 10.2% in simulation and 3.3% in real-world robot experiments, enabling more practical deployment on resource-constrained platforms.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have very high computational and storage demands, preventing their real-time deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms that require high control frequencies (e.g., 50-1000 Hz).
- Existing MLLM efficiency methods like early-exit often discard crucial final layers that encode semantic information, while Mixture-of-Depths (MoD) can lead to token-wise inconsistencies.
- Achieving a balance between computational efficiency and maintaining the model's cognitive ability for accurate task understanding and action generation in VLA models remains a challenge.

## Method

- ==Introduces MoLe (Mixture-of-Layers)==, a dynamic layer-skipping mechanism for the MLLM backbone, treating each LLM layer as a potential 'expert' and dynamically activating only a subset of layers.
- Develops Spatial-Temporal Aware Router (STAR), a lightweight router that combines modality-specific spatial features from visual inputs and temporal dependencies from textual inputs to make intelligent layer selection decisions.
- Proposes Cognitive self-Knowledge Distillation (CogKD), a teacher-student framework with an adaptive mimicking loss based on 'Tokens of Interests' (ToIs) and a Reverse-KL divergence term, to mitigate 'cognitive collapse' from layer skipping and preserve task-critical information.

## Results

- MoLe-VLA (MoLe-CogAct) achieved a **60.8%** mean success rate across ten RLBench tasks, outperforming CogAct (**57.2%**) while requiring approximately **5.6x** less computational cost (**985.8** GFLOPs vs. **1935.8** GFLOPs).
- MoLe-OpenVLA demonstrated a **10.2%** improvement in success rate over the original OpenVLA (from **45.4%** to **55.6%**) by utilizing only **50%** of LLM layers.
- In real-world robot experiments on a Franka Research 3 arm, MoLe-VLA achieved a **70.0%** mean success rate, surpassing CogAct's **66.7%**, and reaching an **80%** success rate in the precision-demanding 'pour water' task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit significant redundancy across layers in robotics tasks, suggesting that not all layers are equally critical for every inference.
> - The 'Shallow Brain Hypothesis' from neuroscience, which describes a balance between deep hierarchical and shallow parallel processing, can inspire dynamic network architectures.
> - Effective layer skipping in VLA models requires a router that understands both spatial visual context and temporal linguistic instructions, and a mechanism to mitigate 'cognitive collapse' due to reduced model capacity.
