# Enhancing Vision-Based Policies with Omni-View and Cross-Modality Knowledge Distillation for Mobile Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A knowledge distillation framework enhances lightweight, single-view RGB-based policies for mobile robots by transferring robust navigation capabilities from an omni-view depth-based teacher. This method improves scene transferability and navigational success, achieving a 72.0% success rate with an approximate 23% increase over baselines, without requiring expensive depth sensors or extensive onboard computation during deployment.

## Problem

- Vision-based policies for lightweight mobile robots face a "trilemma" of poor scene transferability, limited onboard computational resources, and high sensor hardware costs.
- Monocular RGB policies struggle with generalization to unseen environments and variations, while omnidirectional or depth sensors, which offer better robustness, are expensive and computationally demanding.
- Existing knowledge distillation methods often don't fully address the gap between information-rich teachers (e.g., omni-view, multi-modal) and resource-constrained students (e.g., single-view RGB).

## Method

- A knowledge distillation framework is proposed that enables a single-view RGB student policy to learn from an omni-view depth-based teacher policy.
- The teacher policy is trained on expert demonstrations using omnidirectional depth images (derived from RGB using DepthAnythingV2), ensuring rich, appearance-invariant input.
- The student policy, operating with monocular RGB input and an LSTM for temporal fusion, is trained with a composite loss including both action imitation and a contrastive loss to align its latent embeddings with the teacher's.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a **72.0%** navigational success rate and 8.49m moving distance on unseen environments, marking an approximate **23%** increase in SR and **20%** in MD over RGB baselines without distillation.
- Visual embedding similarity and attention maps demonstrated that the student policy learned more consistent, task-relevant features across varied scenes compared to other RGB-based methods.
- The approach maintained low onboard inference times (around 20 ms) on a lightweight mobile robot (NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX) without relying on real-time depth estimation or high-capacity models during deployment, circumventing latency issues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning latent embeddings of a resource-constrained student with those of an information-rich teacher through contrastive loss effectively transfers complex spatial and appearance-invariant representations.
> - End-to-end training that optimizes the visual encoder using both task-relevant action loss and feature-alignment contrastive loss yields superior performance compared to methods that freeze pre-trained encoders.
> - Distilling knowledge from an omni-view teacher provides the student with implicit omnidirectional context, even when operating with a single-view camera, enhancing navigational robustness.
