# Enhancing Vision-Based Policies with Omni-View and Cross-Modality Knowledge Distillation for Mobile Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> A knowledge distillation framework enhances lightweight, single-view RGB-based policies for mobile robots by transferring robust navigation capabilities from an omni-view depth-based teacher. This method improves scene transferability and navigational success, achieving a 72.0% success rate with an approximate 23% increase over baselines, without requiring expensive depth sensors or extensive onboard computation during deployment.

## Problem

- Vision-based policies for lightweight mobile robots face a "trilemma" of poor scene transferability, limited onboard computational resources, and high sensor hardware costs.
- Monocular RGB policies struggle with generalization to unseen environments and variations, while omnidirectional or depth sensors, which offer better robustness, are expensive and computationally demanding.
- Existing knowledge distillation methods often don't fully address the gap between information-rich teachers (e.g., omni-view, multi-modal) and resource-constrained students (e.g., single-view RGB).

## Method

- A knowledge distillation framework is proposed that enables a single-view RGB student policy to learn from an omni-view depth-based teacher policy.
- The teacher policy is trained on expert demonstrations using omnidirectional depth images (derived from RGB using DepthAnythingV2), ensuring rich, appearance-invariant input.
- The student policy, operating with monocular RGB input and an LSTM for temporal fusion, is trained with a composite loss including both action imitation and a contrastive loss to align its latent embeddings with the teacher's.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a **72.0%** navigational success rate and 8.49m moving distance on unseen environments, marking an approximate **23%** increase in SR and **20%** in MD over RGB baselines without distillation.
- Visual embedding similarity and attention maps demonstrated that the student policy learned more consistent, task-relevant features across varied scenes compared to other RGB-based methods.
- The approach maintained low onboard inference times (around 20 ms) on a lightweight mobile robot (NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX) without relying on real-time depth estimation or high-capacity models during deployment, circumventing latency issues.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Aligning latent embeddings of a resource-constrained student with those of an information-rich teacher through contrastive loss effectively transfers complex spatial and appearance-invariant representations.
> - End-to-end training that optimizes the visual encoder using both task-relevant action loss and feature-alignment contrastive loss yields superior performance compared to methods that freeze pre-trained encoders.
> - Distilling knowledge from an omni-view teacher provides the student with implicit omnidirectional context, even when operating with a single-view camera, enhancing navigational robustness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-based policies are an area of active research in robotics, providing a streamlined approach for tasks such as manipulation, locomotion, and navigation by directly mapping raw image data to control actions. Imitation learning (IL) is a widely adopted paradigm for training such visuomotor policies due to its sample efficiency and straightforward formulation.

However, the application of these policies to lightweight mobile robots introduces several challenges. Monocular vision policies often exhibit poor generalization to unseen environments, struggling with variations in illumination or object textures that were not present in the training data. Mobile robots also typically operate with limited onboard computational resources, which conflicts with the preference for large field-of-view (FOV) image inputs or high-capacity image encoders that provide more comprehensive visual information. While omnidirectional sensing can improve obstacle awareness and navigation, processing the increased data volume demands more resources. The use of depth sensors (e.g., RGB-D cameras, LiDAR) offers appearance-invariant observations, improving scene transferability, but these sensors are often costly and bulky, restricting their use in low-cost, lightweight platforms. Alternatively, high-capacity monocular depth networks can generate depth images without additional hardware, but they introduce significant computational overhead, exacerbating the resource limitation.

Knowledge distillation has emerged as a method to transfer knowledge from a complex or data-rich teacher model to a simpler, more efficient student model. In robot policy learning, this typically involves training a student to replicate a teacher's actions. Recent advancements extend this by distilling intermediate embeddings, allowing students with partial or less informative observations to learn representations from teachers with richer information (e.g., full observability, additional modalities). This approach has been applied in quadruped locomotion for transferring inaccessible information and in robot manipulation or monocular depth estimation for transferring knowledge from information-rich to less informative inputs.

This work addresses the aforementioned challenges by proposing a knowledge distillation framework that specifically targets the "trilemma" of scene transferability/safety, onboard resource limitations, and robot hardware cost. It builds upon the principles of imitation learning and knowledge distillation by integrating cross-modality (RGB to depth) and omni-view information transfer. The objective is to enable a lightweight monocular RGB-based policy (student) to achieve performance comparable to policies leveraging omnidirectional depth inputs (teacher), thereby circumventing the need for expensive depth sensors or extensive computational resources for real-time depth estimation during deployment. The research positions itself within the domain of enhancing the robustness and efficiency of vision-based policies for resource-constrained mobile robots through intelligent knowledge transfer.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from a "trilemma" encountered when deploying vision-based policies on lightweight mobile robots:
1. **Limited Scene Transferability and Safety Performance:** Visuomotor policies, especially those relying on monocular cameras, often exhibit poor generalization to new environments or variations in visual conditions (e.g., illumination, textures). This can lead to unpredictable actions and compromised safety. Policies with large FOV inputs or depth information tend to perform better in terms of scene transfer and obstacle avoidance, but these inputs are not always practical.
2. **Restricted Onboard Computational Resources:** Mobile robots typically have limited power and computational capabilities. This constrains the complexity of the vision models and the volume of data that can be processed in real-time. High-capacity image encoders or processing omnidirectional inputs can provide richer visual representations but demand substantial computational power.
3. **Sensor Hardware Cost:** Depth sensors (e.g., RGB-D cameras, LiDAR), while providing appearance-invariant observations that improve scene transfer, are often expensive and bulky. This limits their adoption in low-cost, lightweight mobile robot applications. While monocular depth networks can mitigate the hardware cost, they introduce computational demands.

This trilemma leads to the core question: Is it possible for a policy trained with a low-cost, single-view RGB camera to achieve performance levels close to those of policies utilizing depth images and omnidirectional views?

To address this question and overcome the identified limitations, the key objectives of this research are:
- To propose a knowledge distillation framework that enhances vision-based policies trained through imitation learning.
- To enable a lightweight student policy, relying solely on single-view RGB input, to inherit robust, appearance-invariant, and information-rich knowledge from an omni-view depth-based teacher policy.
- To improve the navigational success rate and collision avoidance capabilities of the monocular student policy, thereby enhancing its scene transferability.
- To develop a solution that does not require depth sensors or multi-camera omnidirectional systems during online deployment, reducing hardware costs and computational load.
- To validate the practical effectiveness and feasibility of the proposed approach through extensive experimentation in both simulated and real-world environments, particularly on lightweight mobile robot systems with limited computation resources.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method involves a knowledge distillation framework designed to transfer robust and information-rich knowledge from an omni-view depth-based teacher policy to a lightweight monocular RGB-based student policy. The process is divided into three main stages: expert data collection, teacher policy training, and student policy training with knowledge distillation.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The visuomotor policy maps a temporal sequence of image observations and goal states to control actions. The image sequence and goal states are encoded into a joint latent embedding, $z_t$, which the policy then uses to produce 2D velocity commands, $a_t = [v_{tx}, v_{ty}, \omega_t]$. The goal state $g_t$ is a 2D point in the robot's local coordinate frame.

**B. Expert Policy for Data Generation ($\pi_E$)**
An expert policy, $\pi_E$, is initially trained to collect demonstration data. This expert policy is state-based, utilizing relative positions of obstacles ($p_{t_{obs}}$) and the relative goal position ($p_{t_{goal}}$) in the local frame as input, $s_t = [p_{t_{obs}}, p_{t_{goal}}]$. The state vector is processed by a two-layer Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) before being fed into actor and critic networks. The expert policy is trained using the Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) algorithm for 5 million environment steps. The reward function, $r_t = r_{t_{dist}} + r_{t_{obst}} + r_{t_{bound}}$, incentivizes approaching the goal while penalizing collisions with obstacles and boundaries.

**C. Teacher Policy Training ($\pi_T$)**
The teacher policy, $\pi_T$, is trained using the expert demonstrations collected by $\pi_E$. Its input consists of omnidirectional depth images. Since the target lightweight robot is equipped with RGB cameras, omnidirectional RGB images (obtained by concatenating inputs from a multi-camera setup) are first converted to depth images using DepthAnythingV2 (DATv2).
- **Architecture:** The omnidirectional depth images are divided into four separate parts, each processed by a shared ResNet image encoder. The resulting embeddings are concatenated, then combined with an embedding of the goal point. This combined embedding is linearly projected to a fused embedding, $z_1$. Finally, $z_1$ is processed by an MLP network to output actions.
- **Training:** $\pi_T$ is trained with a vanilla imitation loss, minimizing the L2 discrepancy between its output action and the expert's action: $L_T = \|\pi_{T,\theta_T}(z_{t1}) - \pi_E(s_t)\|^2$. The image encoder, goal state encoder, and action network are trained end-to-end.

**D. Student Policy Training ($\pi_S$)**
The student policy, $\pi_S$, operates with a single-view RGB image sequence from a monocular camera.
- **Architecture:** A shared ResNet image encoder processes the RGB image sequence to generate image embeddings. Concurrently, the goal point state is encoded by an MLP. At each time step, the image embedding and goal point embedding are concatenated, forming a series of fused embeddings. These fused embeddings are then passed to a Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) recurrent module for temporal fusion, producing a spatially and temporally rich embedding, $z_2$. A final MLP regresses the action output from $z_2$. This temporal aggregation helps to compensate for the limited information of a single static image.
- **Training:** The student policy is trained using a composite loss function: $L_S = \lambda_0 L_{act} + \lambda_1 L_{con}$.
 - **Action Loss ($L_{act}$):** This component minimizes the L2 discrepancy between the student's output action and the expert's action: $L_{act} = \|\pi_{S,\theta_S}(z_{t2}) - \pi_E(s_t)\|^2$.
 - **Contrastive Loss ($L_{con}$):** To overcome the generalization limitations of single-view RGB input, an additional contrastive loss aligns the student's latent embeddings ($z_2$) with those of the teacher ($z_1$). The InfoNCE contrastive loss is used:
 $L_{con} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N -\log \frac{\exp(\text{sim}(z_{i1}, z_{i2})/\tau)}{\sum_{j=1}^N \exp(\text{sim}(z_{i1}, z_{j2})/\tau)}$.
 Here, $\tau$ is a temperature parameter, and $\text{sim}(z_1, z_2)$ is cosine similarity. Positive pairs are defined as embeddings from spatially close robot states ($z_{i1}, z_{i2}$), while negative pairs are from spatially distant states ($z_{i1}, z_{j2}$). This loss encourages $z_2$ to be similar to $z_1$ for corresponding states and dissimilar for non-corresponding states. This mechanism enriches the student's embedding with both omni-view contextual information and appearance-invariant depth information implicitly encoded in $z_1$. The LSTM module further aids in narrowing the semantic gap between $z_1$ and $z_2$.
- **Training Details:** The hyperparameters $\lambda_0$ and $\lambda_1$ balance the loss components; $\lambda_0$ is fixed at 1, and $\lambda_1$ decays linearly from 0.9 to 0.1, shifting focus from embedding alignment to action learning. The temperature parameter $\tau$ is 0.1. The image encoder is a ResNet initialized from ImageNet-1K. The student is trained end-to-end using DAgger to improve generalization.

The knowledge distillation is conceptualized as both a regularization mechanism (forcing the student to learn robust intermediate representations, preventing overfitting) and a data augmentation strategy (treating single-view RGB as an "augmented" version of the teacher's rich input).

**E. Experimental Setup**
- **Task:** Robot navigation to a goal point (static or moving) while avoiding collisions, using onboard camera perception.
- **Data Collection:** 50,000 (state, image, action) tuples collected by rolling out $\pi_E$ in 4 simulated Gazebo environments (4m x 4m, random obstacle density 0.05-0.20) and a real-world lab environment. Omni-directional images are formed by concatenating images from 4 calibrated onboard RGB cameras (110° FOV, 30 Hz).
- **Policy Training:** Teacher trained with batch size 64, learning rates $1 \times 10^{-5}$ (encoder) and $1 \times 10^{-4}$ (action network). Student trained with batch size 32, same learning rates. Embeddings $z_1, z_2$ are 1024-dimensional.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from evaluations in both simulated and real-world environments, focusing on scene transfer performance and mobile robot task performance.

**A. Scene Transfer Performance**

1. **Visual Embedding Similarity:**
 - The study used distinct simulation environments with identical layouts but varying visual appearances to assess scene transferability. Embedding similarity was quantified using the average cosine similarity of visual embeddings at the same position across different scenes.
 - Depth-image and RGB-D-based encoders (e.g., MultiMAE) demonstrated high embedding similarities, indicating consistent features across scenes.
 - Among RGB-based encoders, the proposed knowledge distillation framework achieved the highest mean embedding similarity, suggesting more consistent features across different scenes compared to other RGB-based methods.
 - t-SNE visualizations (Figure 5) showed that the embeddings from the proposed method were more consistent across different scenes, with a distribution that aligned with the robot's spatial movements and preserved meaningful spatial variation. In contrast, embeddings from pre-trained models (e.g., DINOv2) exhibited less structured scattering.

2. **Visual Attention Maps:**
 - Full Gradient (FG) attention maps were generated to analyze the focus of the encoders.
 - Policies trained with the proposed knowledge distillation method exhibited attention maps that concentrated on task-relevant features, such as nearby obstacles, aligning with human intuition (Figure 6d).
 - Policies trained without knowledge distillation, directly on RGB input, were observed to be distracted by irrelevant features in the visual input, potentially leading to less robust representations (Figure 6c).

**B. Mobile Robot Task Performance**

1. **Action Error (AE):**
 - Action Error (AE) quantifies the discrepancy between expert and student outputs. Table I presents AE results for various methods under different input modalities and camera views.
 - Policies utilizing omnidirectional depth images yielded the lowest AE in both training and testing scenes (e.g., Resnet18 Depth Omni: 0.0012 train, 0.0037 test).
 - Omni-view input generally reduced AE compared to single-view setups when the input modality was the same, indicating improved policy performance.
 - Using depth as an input modality resulted in lower AE compared to RGB when the view type was the same, suggesting enhanced imitation performance.
 - The proposed omni-view and cross-modality distillation method (Ours-Rnet18 RGB Single: 0.0030 train, 0.0070 test; Ours-Rnet50 RGB Single: 0.0021 train, 0.0067 test) achieved a lower AE than other RGB-based encoders, with performance approaching that of omni-view depth input policies. This was observed for both Resnet18 and Resnet50 backbones.

2. **Navigational Performance:**
 - Evaluations were conducted on navigational success rate (SR), defined as reaching the goal within 0.3m without collision, and moving distance (MD) before collision. Onboard inference time and parameter count were also reported (Table II).
 - The proposed method (Ours-Rnet50 RGB Single) demonstrated improved performance among RGB-based encoders, achieving a Success Rate (SR) of 72.0% and a Moving Distance (MD) of 8.49m (±0.25). This represents an approximate 23% increase in SR and a 20% increase in MD compared to a Resnet50 RGB Single baseline without distillation.
 - While policies using depth as input showed strong performance in simulation (e.g., Resnet50 Depth Omni: 80.0% SR, 8.90m MD), they introduced significant additional latency in real-world deployment (25.7 ms for single-view DATv2, 55.0 ms for omni-view DATv2), alongside increased CPU and GPU load (39% GPU, 19% CPU increase).
 - High-capacity models like DINOv2 exhibited long inference times (over 130 ms), rendering them unsuitable for real-time onboard control.
 - In contrast, the proposed distillation method enhanced policy performance without relying on an external depth estimation module during deployment, maintaining a low onboard inference time (Ours-Rnet50: 24.10 ms mean, 31.45 ms 99% percentile encoder time).
 - Trajectory visualizations (Figure 7) illustrated that policies trained with raw RGB images without distillation often collided, while those with depth input or the proposed distillation method enabled safe navigation and target tracking. Single-view depth input policies still exhibited minor collisions when obstacles moved out of the field of view.

3. **Comparisons with Other Methods:**
 - A comparison with other literature methods focused on enhancing visuomotor policies (Table III) demonstrated that the proposed method (Ours-Rnet50) achieved lower Action Error (AE), higher Success Rate (SR), and greater Moving Distance (MD) (72.0% SR, 8.49m MD) compared to baselines like RoboSaGA, VISARL, and Zhang et al..
 - Specifically, the proposed method showed a 16.1% SR increase over the strongest baseline (Zhang et al.). The improved performance was attributed to the joint distillation of intermediate embeddings and action outputs, allowing the visual encoder to be optimized with gradients from both task-relevant action loss and feature-relevant contrastive loss. This contrasts with methods that freeze pre-trained encoders or rely solely on contrastive learning without end-to-end task-specific optimization. The incorporation of omni-view information in the teacher was also identified as a factor contributing to performance on robots with omnidirectional mobility.

4. **Real-world Experiments:**
 - Real-world experiments validated the effectiveness and practicality of the approach on a lightweight mobile robot with limited computational resources (NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX). Figure 8 shows typical robot trajectories in a real-world setting.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of robotics by addressing critical limitations in deploying vision-based policies on resource-constrained mobile platforms. The proposed omni-view and cross-modality knowledge distillation framework offers a method to enhance the capabilities of lightweight monocular policies, circumventing a "trilemma" of poor scene transferability, limited onboard computation, and high hardware costs.

The significance of this work lies in several key aspects:
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** By distilling knowledge from an information-rich, appearance-invariant omni-view depth policy, the monocular RGB student policy gains improved scene transferability and navigational safety. This suggests that low-cost vision systems can achieve performance closer to that of more advanced sensor setups, making robot deployment more robust in varied and unseen environments.
- **Cost-Effectiveness and Resource Efficiency:** The method eliminates the need for expensive depth sensors or multi-camera omnidirectional systems during online deployment. The student policy, operating solely on single-view RGB input, maintains a low onboard inference time (approximately 20 ms), avoiding the high latency and computational burden associated with real-time depth estimation modules or large-capacity vision models. This facilitates the development and widespread adoption of more affordable and energy-efficient mobile robots.
- **Effective Knowledge Transfer Mechanism:** The use of contrastive learning to align intermediate latent embeddings, in conjunction with action imitation, provides a robust mechanism for transferring complex spatial and appearance-invariant representations. This approach demonstrates that end-to-end training where the visual encoder is optimized alongside the action network, using both task-specific and feature-alignment losses, can yield superior results compared to freezing pre-trained encoders.
- **Practical Applicability:** Extensive validation in both simulation and real-world environments, particularly on a lightweight mobile robot with limited computational resources, confirms the practical effectiveness and feasibility of the approach. This indicates that the method is ready for consideration in real-world applications requiring autonomous navigation.

The potential impact includes enabling a new generation of mobile robots that are more adaptable, safer, and economically viable for a wider range of applications, from logistics and exploration to service robotics. The framework provides a pathway for leveraging advanced, data-rich training environments and models to train efficient and effective policies for resource-limited hardware.
