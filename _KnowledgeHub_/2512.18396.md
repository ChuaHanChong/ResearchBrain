# AOMGen: Photoreal, Physics-Consistent Demonstration Generation for Articulated Object Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> AOMGen generates photorealistic, physically consistent demonstrations for articulated object manipulation from one real scan plus one demonstration. It reconstructs the scene with part-level segmented 3D Gaussian Splatting, recovers joint motion by supervising Gaussian edits with the robot arm's contact-point trajectory, then swaps in same-category assets with contact-mapped geometry optimization and relit materials. Fine-tuning VLA policies on this data lifts success from 0% to 88.7%.

## Problem

- Real demonstration collection for fine-grained articulated manipulation is expensive and labor-intensive, and covers few scenarios, limiting the kinematically rich data such policies need.
- Physics simulators give aligned state-action data but look visually unrealistic, while video world models look real yet lack supervision over physical realism and action executability.
- Prior single-demo augmentation handles only simple grasp-and-place, fixes object appearance and geometry, and uses single-view input, so it cannot generalize to novel articulated instances.

## Method

- Scene Reconstruction: part-level segmented ==3D Gaussian Splatting== built from ==SAM2== multi-view masks, with ==ICP== against sampled robot URDF points aligning the Gaussian field to the real-world coordinate frame.
- **AOMotion** recovers articulated motion without recorded joint values via motion-score keyframe extraction, contact-point detection, bounding-box edge-pair scoring for joint axis and center, and trajectory-supervised part optimization.
- Articulated Object Replacement maps contact points through ==NOCS-style normalization==, runs two-stage geometry optimization for scale, offset and motion range, then relights assets using ==DiffusionLight== plus Gaussian inpainting.

## Results

- Simulator replay of generated trajectories succeeds **98%** on average across five replacement assets: microwave **98%**, tool box **96%**, computer **96%**, drawer **100%**, cabinet **100%**.
- Fine-tuning on **150** generated samples raises success from **0%** to **88.66%** for the tested flow-matching VLA and **81.34%** for OpenVLA, evaluated over **30** trials each.
- Scaling assets to **0.6-0.9** keeps success at **65-85%** (microwave) and **80-90%** (drawer); mixed-object training reaches **65%** on an unseen object versus **15%** for single-object data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A recorded robot arm trajectory is a free physical supervisor: it can pin down unobserved joint axes and motion of the manipulated object.
> - Category-level asset swapping, not just spatial trajectory augmentation, is what buys generalization; mixed-instance synthetic data unlocks unseen objects that single-instance data cannot.
> - Splitting synthesis into geometry-consistent contact optimization plus separate appearance relighting keeps physical plausibility and visual realism from trading off against each other.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation has made progress through Vision-Language-Action (VLA) and world-model methods, which aim to improve generalization across various tasks. A significant challenge in this domain is the reliance on extensive, high-quality real-world demonstration data. This data collection is particularly demanding and costly for fine-grained manipulation tasks involving articulated objects, due to their complex kinematic constraints and the need for diverse and precise demonstrations.

Current approaches to address data scarcity primarily fall into two categories:
- **Physics-based simulation:** Platforms like Isaac Gym and Genesis can efficiently generate large datasets with state-action alignment, reducing manual annotation and hardware costs. However, simulated environments often suffer from a "Sim-to-Real" gap, exhibiting insufficient visual realism compared to real-world scenes, which hinders direct transfer of policies to physical robots.
- **Video-driven world models:** Methods like DreamGen and ORV learn directly from large-scale real-world videos, offering higher visual fidelity and diversity than purely simulation-based approaches. Nevertheless, these models frequently lack sufficient supervision over physical realism and action executability, potentially leading to physically inconsistent interactions.

More recent data synthesis methods, such as DemoGen and R2RGen, attempt to generate additional robot manipulation demonstrations from limited real-world examples, including augmented end-effector trajectories and 3D visual observations. However, these methods exhibit several limitations: they are typically restricted to simpler tasks like grasping and placement, struggle with fine-grained articulated manipulation, are limited to fixed object appearances and geometries, and often rely on single-view inputs, which reduces visual realism in multi-view contexts.

AOMGen positions itself within this landscape by seeking to overcome the limitations of prior simulation-based and video-driven methods, as well as existing data synthesis techniques. It aims to provide a scalable data generation framework that produces both visually realistic and physically consistent demonstrations specifically for *articulated* object manipulation. By doing so, AOMGen addresses the core problem of data scarcity and the inherent challenges of Sim-to-Real transfer and physical consistency for complex robotic tasks. It leverages 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for high-fidelity reconstruction and editing, a technique that has shown promise in reducing visual discrepancy between real and simulated environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop AOMGen, a scalable data generation framework capable of synthesizing photorealistic and physically consistent demonstration data for category-level articulated object manipulation. This framework is designed to operate from a single real-world scan and demonstration, supplemented by a library of digital assets.

The motivation behind AOMGen stems from several critical challenges in training effective robotic manipulation policies:
- **High Cost and Labor Intensity of Real Data Collection:** Successful VLA and world-model methods for robotic manipulation necessitate substantial amounts of high-quality real-world demonstration data. Collecting this data is expensive, labor-intensive, and inherently constrained in the diversity of scenarios it can cover.
- **Complexity of Articulated Object Manipulation:** Fine-grained manipulation of articulated objects is particularly data-hungry due to their complex kinematic constraints. Learning effective control policies for such objects requires richer and more precise demonstrations than for rigid objects.
- **Limitations of Existing Simulation Approaches:** While physics-based simulations can generate large datasets efficiently, they often fail to achieve sufficient visual realism, leading to a significant Sim-to-Real gap that impedes policy transfer to the real world.
- **Deficiencies of Video-Driven World Models:** Although video-driven world models offer higher visual fidelity, they frequently lack reliable physical consistency and action executability, which can result in unrealistic or impossible interactions in generated data.
- **Inadequacies of Prior Data Synthesis Methods:** Existing methods for synthesizing robot manipulation data are typically limited to simple grasping and placement tasks, cannot handle fine-grained articulated manipulation, struggle to generalize to novel objects or poses, and often provide only single-view inputs.

To address these motivations, AOMGen sets out to achieve the following specific objectives:
- **Scalable Data Generation:** To generate a diverse corpus of training data from a minimal real-world input (a single static scan and one demonstration).
- **Photorealism:** To synthesize synchronized multi-view RGB data that is visually realistic, leveraging techniques like 3D Gaussian Splatting and transferring scene lighting and materials.
- **Physical Consistency:** To ensure that the generated demonstrations are interaction-accurate and physically plausible, including precise action commands and state annotations for joints and contacts. This involves accurately recovering articulated motions and adapting interactions to new objects.
- **Category-Level Generalization:** To enable the framework to vary camera viewpoints, object styles (by replacing the original object with new instances from the same category), and object poses, thereby significantly expanding data diversity and supporting generalization to unseen objects and layouts.
- **Improved VLA Model Performance:** To demonstrate that fine-tuning VLA policies with AOMGen-generated data leads to increased success rates and enhanced robustness when tested on unseen objects and layouts.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AOMGen is a framework designed to generate photorealistic and physically consistent demonstration data for articulated object manipulation from a single real-world scan and demonstration. The pipeline consists of two core modules: Scene Reconstruction and Motion Recovery, and Articulated Object Replacement with Pose Generalization.

#### 3.1. Scene Reconstruction and Motion Recovery

This module focuses on creating a high-fidelity 3D representation of the initial scene and accurately recovering the dynamic motions of both the robotic arm and the articulated object.

##### 3.1.1. Scene Reconstruction

- **Initial Reconstruction:** Multi-view images of the manipulation scene are captured. COLMAP is used to generate a sparse scene reconstruction and estimate camera poses.
- **Part-level Segmented 3DGS:** A 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) model is built. To enable fine-grained editing, each Gaussian point is augmented with a feature vector (`f_D`) learned from multi-view 2D masks generated by SAM2. This allows for the segmentation of the articulated object into its `Part_move` (movable part) and `Part_static` (static parts), and the robot arm, based on the end-effector's position.
- **Coordinate Alignment:** The reconstructed 3DGS coordinate system is aligned with the real-world coordinate frame. This is achieved by sampling point clouds from the robot's URDF (known in the real-world frame) and applying Iterative Closest Point (ICP) to align these with the segmented robot points in the 3DGS scene, yielding a transformation matrix `T_gs→real`.

##### 3.1.2. Motion Recovery (AOMotion)

Motion recovery is performed for both the robotic arm and the articulated object.
- **Robotic Arm Motion:** The 3DGS representation of the robotic arm is segmented into its individual links. Using the collected demonstration data, which includes robot arm joint states, Forward Kinematics is applied to calculate the rotation transformation matrix `T_l^t` for each link `l` at time `t`.
- **Articulated Object Motion (AOMotion submodules):** This is a supervised method based on real-world interaction, designed to recover the articulated object's motion without direct motion recording.
 - **Keyframe Extraction:** From the dynamic manipulation video, SAM2 generates masks for the movable part (`M_t^dynamic`) and the robot arm (`M_t^robot`) at each frame `t`. To isolate the movable part's motion, overlapping robot arm masks are removed from the movable part masks. A `MotionScore` is calculated based on pixel changes between consecutive processed masks. This score is smoothed using a Savitzky-Golay filter to reduce noise. A dynamic threshold (`μ`) derived from the 20th quantile and standard deviation of motion scores is used to determine `start_frame` and `end_frame` of the interaction.
 - **Contact Point Detection:** The position of the end-effector at `start_frame` (`v_ee`) is used to detect the contact points (`PC_r` on the robot arm and `PC_move` on the movable part) by minimizing distances between their respective point clouds.
 - **Articulated Object Modeling:** The goal is to accurately determine the joint direction and center. Bounding boxes for `Part_move` and `Part_static` are constructed. A scoring scheme is devised to identify adjacent edge pairs between the two parts, considering parallelism and distance. This is refined by a criterion that assumes the interaction point is typically opposite the joint, using `v_ee` and `PC_move`. The joint direction is derived from the identified movable part edge. The joint center is determined by averaging the midpoints of the K closest point pairs between the two parts, constrained to points near the identified edges.
 - **Movable Part Motion Recovery:** The motion of the movable part, described by physical parameters, is supervised by the trajectory of the robot arm's contact points (`T_raj_r`). An optimization function minimizes the difference between the actual contact point trajectory and the intersection point of `T_raj_r` with the movable part's surface (which is rotated or translated by `θ_t` based on joint type).

#### 3.2. Articulated Object Replacement with Pose Generalization

This module replaces the original articulated object with a new instance from the same category (`AO_new`) and allows for pose generalization, ensuring both physical consistency and visual realism.

##### 3.2.1. Physical Interaction Adaptation

A two-stage optimization process is used to adapt the geometry of `AO_new` to ensure consistent physical interaction with the original robot arm trajectory.
- **Contact Point Mapping:** An NOCS-inspired method is used to map the contact point `PC_move` from the original object to `PC_map` on `AO_new`. Both movable parts are normalized to a unit cube, `PC_move`'s relative position is projected onto the new object's surface, and then denormalized.
- **Stage 1 Optimization:** A rough estimate of `AO_new`'s geometric features (`g` = scaling, initial motion parameter, XY-axis offset) is obtained by minimizing the difference between `PC_r,t` (robot contact point) and `R_t(PC_map|g)` (transformed contact point on `AO_new`), assuming constant speed.
- **Stage 2 Optimization:** This stage refines `g` by minimizing the difference between `PC_r,t` and the intersection point of `T_raj_r` with `Surf(Part_move(g))`. This accounts for non-uniform movable part motion and potential sliding between the end-effector and the surface. The optimized parameters `g*` are then applied to the `AO_new` USD asset.

##### 3.2.2. Visual Enhancement

To enhance visual realism:
- **Lighting Transfer:** DiffusionLight extracts lighting from the real environment, which is then baked onto the material of `AO_new` in Blender.
- **Gaussian Inpainting:** This technique is applied to fill any holes in the Gaussian representation caused by the object replacement, maintaining visual coherence.

##### 3.2.3. Pose Generalization

The pose of the replacement object (`AO_new`) can be arbitrarily transformed (`T_ao`) to expand data diversity.
- The original end-effector trajectory `τ` is divided into three stages (`τ_1, τ_2, τ_3`) based on `start_frame` and `end_frame`.
- The interaction segment (`τ_2`) is directly transformed by `T_ao` (`τ'_2 = T_ao * τ_2`).
- The pre- and post-interaction segments (`τ_1, τ_3`) are modified by linearly interpolating translation and spherically interpolating rotation between the original start/end poses and the new transformed poses.
- Inverse kinematics are then used to compute the corresponding robot joint angles for the new trajectory.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to validate the physical consistency of generated data, its applicability for VLA training, and its ability to enhance VLA model robustness.

#### 4.1. Simulator Replay (Physical Consistency)

To verify the physical realism of the generated data (Question 1), the robotic arm trajectories and articulated object poses produced by AOMGen were replayed in Isaac-Sim.
- The optimized USD assets and their corresponding poses were imported into the simulator, and the robot arm's joint states were replayed.
- Across 50 data samples for each replacement object (microwave oven, tool box, computer with rotational joints; drawer, cabinet with prismatic joints), the tasks were successfully completed with high success rates.
- Success rates ranged from 96% (Tool Box, Computer) to 100% (Drawer, Cabinet), with an average success rate of 98%.
- This demonstrates that the data generated by AOMGen adheres to physical laws and provides a fundamental guarantee for the effectiveness of subsequent model training.

#### 4.2. VLA Training (Model Performance Improvement)

To assess whether AOMGen data can be directly used for VLA training (Question 2), two VLA models (`π_0.5` and `OpenVLA`) were fine-tuned and evaluated.
- Three training conditions were compared for each replacement object: no fine-tuning (original model), fine-tuning with 50 AOMGen data samples, and fine-tuning with 150 AOMGen data samples.
- During inference, simulation assets were used, with 3DGS rendered images applied as textures to a "background wall" to simulate the real operating scene, reducing the Sim-to-Real gap. Each evaluation involved 30 trials.
- Results showed a notable improvement in success rates as the number of generated demonstrations increased.
- With fine-tuning, `π_0.5` achieved a success rate of 88.66%, and `OpenVLA` reached 81.34%.
- This indicates that fine-tuning with AOMGen-generated articulated object manipulation data enables VLA models to acquire the capability to manipulate the corresponding objects effectively.

#### 4.3. Robustness Analysis (Generalization Capabilities)

The robustness of the VLA models, enhanced by AOMGen's generalization strategies, was evaluated (Question 3). This included implicit robustness to environmental differences (3DGS vs. simulator), explicit robustness to pose variations, and further analyses on scale and unseen object generalization.
- **Pose Generalization:** The VLA models were tested under various initial object position configurations, demonstrating robustness to pose variations which validated the pose generalization strategy.
- **Scale Generalization:** Optimized USD assets for the microwave oven and drawer were scaled between 0.6x and 0.9x.
 - `π_0.5` showed success rates from 65% to 85% for the microwave oven and 80% to 90% for the drawer across different scales.
 - `OpenVLA` showed success rates from 55% to 70% for the microwave oven and 60% to 80% for the drawer.
 - These results suggest that simple scaling of the generated data enables the model to handle objects of different sizes.
- **Articulated Object Generalization (Unseen Objects):** VLA models were fine-tuned using either single-object data or mixed data (from different replacement articulated objects). They were then tested on an entirely unseen object from the same category (20 trials).
 - Models fine-tuned with single-object data achieved a 15% success rate on unseen objects, indicating limited generalization.
 - Models fine-tuned with mixed data, however, achieved a 65% success rate on unseen objects of the same category.
 - This finding demonstrates AOMGen's capability to replace objects of the same category and generate diverse data, significantly enhancing the model's generalization ability to novel objects.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

AOMGen presents a framework that addresses a key bottleneck in the development of robotic manipulation systems: the scarcity and cost of high-quality, real-world demonstration data, particularly for articulated objects. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Scalable and Efficient Data Generation:** By synthesizing diverse, photorealistic, and physically consistent manipulation data from a single real-world scan and demonstration, AOMGen offers a highly scalable and cost-effective alternative to extensive real-world data collection. This efficiency can accelerate research and development in robot learning.
- **Enhanced Generalization for Robotic Policies:** The framework's ability to systematically vary camera viewpoints, object styles (via category-level replacement), and object poses enables the generation of highly diverse training corpora. This directly contributes to improving the generalization capabilities of VLA models, making them more robust to novel objects, poses, and environmental variations, as demonstrated by the improved success rates on unseen objects and scaled versions.
- **Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap:** AOMGen leverages 3D Gaussian Splatting for high visual fidelity, ensuring that generated data is photorealistic. Simultaneously, its meticulous approach to motion recovery and physical interaction adaptation ensures consistency with physical laws. This dual focus helps to mitigate the long-standing Sim-to-Real gap, allowing policies trained on synthetic data to perform effectively in real-world or near-real-world scenarios.
- **Advancement in Articulated Object Manipulation:** The paper introduces novel methods for part-level segmentation, joint modeling, and motion recovery for articulated objects without direct motion recording. This is crucial for handling the complexities of such objects and represents a step forward in generating data for fine-grained manipulation tasks.
- **Broadened Applicability of VLA Models:** By providing a robust and scalable method for data generation, AOMGen can facilitate the training of more capable and general-purpose robotic manipulation policies. This could expand the practical applications of VLA models in various domains, from industrial automation to service robotics, where interacting with diverse articulated objects is common.
- **Reduced Resource Dependence:** The framework's ability to leverage readily available digital assets in conjunction with minimal real-world data reduces the reliance on specialized hardware setups and extensive human supervision, making advanced robot learning more accessible.

In conclusion, AOMGen offers a comprehensive solution for generating high-quality synthetic data for articulated object manipulation, addressing critical limitations of current methods and demonstrating a direct positive impact on the performance and robustness of advanced VLA robotic policies.
