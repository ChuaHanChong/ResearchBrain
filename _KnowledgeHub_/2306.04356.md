# Fine-Grained Visual Prompting

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers propose Fine-Grained Visual Prompting (FGVP) using semantic segmentation masks to enhance Vision-Language Model performance on instance-level tasks in a zero-shot manner. Their "Blur Reverse Mask" strategy significantly improves accuracy for referring expression comprehension and part detection, achieving state-of-the-art zero-shot results and an average 3.4% accuracy increase over prior methods in referring expression comprehension.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) demonstrate robust image-level understanding but exhibit diminished performance in instance-level tasks requiring precise spatial localization and recognition, such as referring expression comprehension and part detection.
- Existing visual prompting techniques rely on coarse markers (e.g., bounding boxes, circles) which introduce ambiguity and irrelevant pixels, leading to sub-optimal guidance for VLMs.
- Traditional methods like cropping regions of interest, while improving localization, discard valuable global contextual information essential for understanding inter-object relationships.

## Method

- A **Fine-Grained Visual Prompting (FGVP)** framework was developed that leverages precise ==semantic segmentation masks== to highlight target instances directly on the full image, preserving global context.
- The ==Segment Anything Model (SAM)== was integrated to automatically generate these fine-grained masks in a zero-shot manner, either from detector-generated bounding box proposals or from a grid-wise set of keypoints for proposal-free scenarios.
- A systematic exploration of various visual prompt designs, including contouring, colorful masking, grayscale reversal, and blurring of background areas, identified the **Blur Reverse Mask** as the most effective strategy.

## Results

- The "Blur Reverse Mask" consistently achieved superior performance across various object-label matching and referring expression comprehension benchmarks, outperforming traditional cropping and other coarse prompting methods.
- FGVP attained state-of-the-art zero-shot performance in referring expression comprehension, improving average accuracy by **3.4%** over previous methods like RedCircle and up to **12.5%** on specific subsets of RefCOCO+.
- The method demonstrated superior zero-shot part detection accuracy on the PACO dataset, with the Blur Reverse Mask achieving **18.3%** accuracy using a proposal-free SAM-based mask generation, surpassing other coarse prompts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Fine-grained visual prompts, particularly those using semantic masks, are more effective than coarse markers at guiding VLM attention to specific instances, overcoming limitations in spatial localization.
> - The "Blur Reverse Mask" strategy, which blurs the image content outside the target instance, leverages VLMs' implicit understanding of photographic composition (e.g., Bokeh effect) learned from web-scale training data.
> - Generalist segmentation models like SAM can be effectively chained with VLMs to create robust zero-shot pipelines for instance-level tasks, eliminating the need for task-specific fine-tuning or extensive ground-truth annotations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

The field of artificial intelligence has seen significant advancements with the development of Vision-Language Models (VLMs), such as CLIP, ALIGN, and Flamingo. These models, trained on vast datasets of image-text pairs, have demonstrated robust zero-shot transfer capabilities, particularly in image-level visual perception tasks. Their ability to generalize to unseen categories without task-specific fine-tuning has made them foundational backbones for various applications, including open-vocabulary detection, visual grounding, and image editing.

Despite these successes, a notable limitation of off-the-shelf VLMs is their diminished performance in instance-level tasks that demand precise spatial localization and recognition. These tasks, such as referring expression comprehension and part detection, often require a fine-grained understanding of objects and their relationships within an image, which current VLMs struggle with due to the presence of weakly related background noise. Previous approaches to address this often involve cropping regions of interest, which provides a zoomed-in view of a single object but discards valuable global contextual information essential for understanding inter-object relationships.

In response to this challenge, visual prompting has emerged as a technique to guide VLMs' attention towards specific targets while preserving global context. Prior works like CPT and ReCLIP have explored incorporating coarse visual cues, such as colorful boxes or circles, directly onto images. However, the exploration of visual prompting designs has been less extensive compared to language prompting. Existing coarse markers frequently introduce ambiguity and irrelevant pixels, leading to sub-optimal performance.

This research addresses this gap by systematically exploring more fine-grained visual prompting designs, specifically leveraging semantic segmentation masks and their variations. It aims to enhance the inherent image-text understanding of VLMs for instance-level tasks in a zero-shot, training-free manner. By focusing on pixel-level precision, this work seeks to improve upon existing coarse prompting methods and overcome the spatial localization limitations of current VLMs, thereby contributing to the broader efforts to make VLMs more versatile and effective across a wider range of visual perception tasks. The work also integrates recent advancements in generalist segmentation models, like Segment Anything Model (SAM), to facilitate the generation of these fine-grained prompts, establishing a novel pipeline for zero-shot instance-level analysis.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to systematically investigate and propose effective fine-grained visual prompting strategies to enhance the zero-shot performance of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on instance-level visual perception tasks.

This overarching objective is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing VLM Limitations in Instance-Level Tasks**: While VLMs demonstrate strong image-level understanding and zero-shot transfer, their performance is limited when precise spatial localization and recognition of individual instances are required. Tasks such as referring expression comprehension and part detection, which involve identifying specific objects or parts within an image based on textual descriptions, often result in sub-optimal accuracy. This limitation stems from the VLMs' sensitivity to background noise and their difficulty in distinguishing between target instances and weakly related regions.
2. **Overcoming Coarse Prompting Inadequacies**: Existing visual prompting techniques, which typically employ coarse markers like colorful bounding boxes or circles, are often insufficient for accurately highlighting target instances. These coarse cues frequently encompass irrelevant pixels or fail to precisely delineate the object boundaries, thereby introducing noise and ambiguity into the VLM's input. This leads to sub-optimal performance, indicating a need for more precise and informative visual prompts.
3. **Systematic Exploration of Visual Prompt Designs**: Unlike language prompting, visual prompting designs have been less thoroughly explored. The authors are motivated to conduct a comprehensive study of various visual marking formats beyond simple boxes and circles, including the use of semantic masks, different line styles, colors, grayscale effects, and blurring techniques. This systematic investigation aims to identify the most effective visual cues for guiding VLM attention.
4. **Developing a Zero-Shot Framework for Fine-Grained Marking**: A significant motivation is to enable fine-grained visual prompting in practical scenarios where ground-truth annotations are unavailable and task-specific training is undesirable. The researchers aim to leverage the capabilities of a generalist segmentation model, such as Segment Anything Model (SAM), to automatically generate precise pixel-level masks for target instances, thereby establishing a viable zero-shot pipeline that does not require additional detector- or task-specific training.
5. **Preserving Global Context while Enhancing Localization**: Traditional methods like cropping regions of interest often discard crucial global contextual information, which is vital for understanding relationships between objects in tasks like referring expression comprehension. The motivation here is to design visual prompts that can effectively direct VLM attention to the target instance while retaining the overall spatial coherence and background context of the image. The discovery that blurring outside the target mask (Blur Reverse Mask) could align with VLM's implicit knowledge of "Bokeh" photography further motivated this direction.

By addressing these motivations, the research aims to significantly advance the application of off-the-shelf VLMs to complex instance-level tasks, making them more robust and accurate without the need for extensive retraining.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this research centers on a systematic exploration and development of fine-grained visual prompting (FGVP) strategies to enhance the zero-shot performance of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) on instance-level tasks. The approach encompasses a comprehensive evaluation of various visual prompt designs, the integration of a generalist segmentation model for mask generation, and the construction of zero-shot pipelines for referring expression comprehension and part detection.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

The general zero-shot framework takes an image $I$, $N$ box proposals $\Phi$, and $M$ caption texts $T$. The objective is to identify the best matching image-text pairs. Unlike traditional methods that crop regions based on $\Phi$, this work introduces visual prompting (VP) to mark regions on the full image, thus preserving global context. The prompted image input $I_{\Phi}$ is generated as $I_{\Phi} = \text{VP}(I, \Phi)$. A VLM, specifically CLIP, then computes cosine similarities $S$ between each prompted image proposal and text.

For referring expression comprehension, post-processing techniques like "Relations" (considering spatial relations between proposals) and "Subtraction" (using negative captions to penalize scores) are discussed. For part detection, a Hungarian algorithm is used for bipartite matching between image proposals and part labels.

#### 3.2. Visual Prompting Design Exploration

A key aspect of the methodology is the systematic organization and investigation of various visual prompt formats. These are categorized based on their shape (box, circle, mask) and marking style (line-based, color-based, grayscale reverse, blur reverse):

- **Coarse Prompting**: Includes traditional methods like `Crop` (extracting cutouts) and `Keypoint` (small circle at box center). Box-based (`Box`, `Colorful Box`, `Grayscale Reverse Box`, `Blur Reverse Box`) and Circle-based (`Circle`, `Colorful Circle`, `Grayscale Reverse Circle`, `Blur Reverse Circle`) prompts primarily utilize detector-generated proposal boxes or inscribed ellipses.
- **Fine-Grained Visual Prompting (FGVP)**: The core contribution, utilizing semantic masks. This includes `Contour` (line around mask), `Mask` (colorful mask), `Grayscale Reverse Mask` (grayscale background), and `Blur Reverse Mask` (blurring outside the mask).

The "reverse-based" prompts are considered negative marking, aiming to reduce the impact of weakly related background information by modifying areas *outside* the target.

#### 3.3. Fine-Grained Mask Generation

To enable fine-grained visual prompting, the methodology addresses how to obtain precise semantic masks in a zero-shot context:

1. **With Detector Proposals**: For tasks where object proposals (bounding boxes) are provided by a pre-trained detector (e.g., MAttNet, UNINEXT), the Segment Anything Model (SAM) is employed. SAM takes the bounding boxes as input prompts and generates corresponding class-agnostic semantic masks: $M = \text{SAM}(I, \Phi)$. These masks are then used by the FGVP strategy.
2. **Without Detector Proposals**: For tasks like zero-shot part detection where no initial box proposals are available, a novel approach is introduced. SAM is prompted directly with a grid-wise set of keypoints $G$ covering the image. This generates an exhaustive set of masks for potential objects/parts. Non-Maximum Suppression (NMS) is then applied to filter out duplicate proposals, yielding the semantic masks: $M = \text{NMS}(\text{SAM}(I, G))$. Bounding boxes are subsequently derived from these generated masks.

#### 3.4. Identification of "Blur Reverse Mask"

Through extensive ablation studies using ground-truth annotations, the `Blur Reverse Mask` [D4] was identified as exceptionally effective. This specific prompt involves applying a Gaussian blur to all content *outside* the target instance mask. The rationale for its effectiveness is posited to be its alignment with photography techniques like "Bokeh," where subjects are highlighted by blurring the background. VLMs, having been trained on web-scale datasets containing such images, may possess prior knowledge that benefits from this visual cue, which preserves spatial coherence while reducing background interference.

#### 3.5. Ablation Studies and Hyperparameter Tuning

The methodology includes detailed ablation studies to evaluate individual visual prompts and optimize hyperparameters:

- **Prompting Performance**: Comparison of all prompt types (Table 2) under ground-truth annotations and detector-generated proposals.
- **Blur Kernel Standard Deviation**: Tuning the standard deviation of the Gaussian blur kernel for blur-based prompting (Fig. 5), finding an optimal value of 100.
- **Line-Based Prompts**: Ablation on line thickness and color (Fig. A8, A9).
- **Color-Based Prompts**: Ablation on transparency and color (Fig. A10, A11).
- **Image Preprocessing**: Evaluation of different strategies to transform rectangular images into square VLM inputs (e.g., cropping, padding, resizing, blurring) (Fig. A12, A13). The study suggests that blur-based preprocessing (Blur-Padding/Blur-Resize) is superior as it retains more spatial relevance.
- **Mask Preciseness Robustness**: Investigating the impact of mask expansion or shrinking on performance, demonstrating that precise masks are critical (Fig. A14, A15, A16).

#### 3.6. Implementation Details

The experiments utilize CLIP backbones (ViT-B/32, ViT-L/14@336px, RN50x16) and SAM-ViT-H for mask generation. All experiments are conducted on 8 NVIDIA V100 GPUs. For part detection on PACO, object cropping precedes part detection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings demonstrating the efficacy of Fine-Grained Visual Prompting (FGVP), particularly the Blur Reverse Mask, across various instance-level visual perception tasks.

#### 4.1. Superiority of Blur Reverse Mask

A comprehensive ablation study evaluating individual visual prompts revealed the consistent superior performance of the `Blur Reverse Mask` [D4].

- **Ground-Truth Annotations**: When using ground-truth bounding boxes and semantic masks, the `Blur Reverse Mask` demonstrated the highest overall performance across object-label matching (COCO, PACO) and referring expression comprehension (RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, RefCOCOg) datasets (Table 2, left side). For instance, it achieved 67.8% accuracy on COCO, 43.3% on PACO, and 52.8%, 58.0%, 63.5% on RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg respectively, outperforming `Crop` (which yielded 70.9% on COCO, but significantly lower on other datasets).
- **Detector Proposals**: When applied to proposal boxes generated by a detector for referring expression comprehension, the `Blur Reverse Mask` continued to exhibit consistent superiority (Table 2, right side). It achieved 52.8%, 55.4%, and 57.8% accuracy on RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg, surpassing `Crop` (45.3%, 46.4%, 56.4%) and other box/circle-based prompts.
- **Optimal Blur Parameter**: An ablation study on the standard deviation of the Gaussian blur kernel for `Blur Reverse Mask` indicated that a value of 100 achieved the best overall performance across multiple datasets (Fig. 5), demonstrating that a relatively strong blur of the background is beneficial.

#### 4.2. State-of-the-Art Performance in Zero-Shot Referring Expression Comprehension (REC)

FGVP achieved state-of-the-art zero-shot results on the referring expression comprehension task across the RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg benchmarks.

- **Individual Prompting**: Using a single `Blur Reverse Mask` with a ViT-L model, FGVP surpassed previous works such as RedCircle by an average of 3.4% accuracy in the authors' codebase and showed even more significant improvements (5.5% to 8.1%) when implemented within the CPT codebase (Table 3).
- **Ensemble and Post-processing**: When integrated into the ReCLIP codebase, which employs ensembles of multiple models and prompts, along with spatial relations ("R") and score subtraction ("S") post-processing techniques, FGVP consistently delivered improved performance. The improvements ranged from approximately 3% on average to a maximum of 12.5% on the RefCOCO+ testA subset, indicating that the gains from FGVP are orthogonal to existing ensemble and post-processing methods. For example, FGVP with P | D1 | D3 | D4 and RS post-processing achieved 59.6%, 65.0%, 52.0% on RefCOCO val/testA/testB, 60.0%, 66.8%, 49.7% on RefCOCO+ val/testA/testB, and 63.3%, 63.4% on RefCOCOg val/test respectively, outperforming RedCircle's best ensemble results.

#### 4.3. Superior Part Detection Accuracy

FGVP demonstrated superior performance in the zero-shot part detection task on the PACO dataset.

- **Without Box Proposals**: Employing the proposed pipeline that generates masks directly from grid-wise keypoints using SAM (without prior box proposals), the `Blur Reverse Mask` achieved an accuracy of 18.3% on PACO, surpassing other coarse prompts like `Crop` (16.5%) and `Circle` (17.4%) (Table 4). This indicates the effectiveness of the fine-grained, proposal-free approach for dense object/part detection.
- **Hyperparameter Influence**: Ablation studies on grid size and NMS threshold for mask generation showed that a larger grid size and higher NMS threshold could further improve performance by including more proposals (Fig. 6).
- **Referring as Part Detection**: The application of this proposal-free approach to referring expression comprehension (viewing each instance as a "part" of the image) also yielded strong results, with `Blur Reverse Mask` surpassing certain coarse prompting methods even with their provided box proposals (Table 4).

#### 4.4. Ablations on Prompting Properties and Preprocessing

- **Mask Preciseness**: Experiments showed that precise masks are crucial. Both expanding and shrinking the mask relative to the ground truth led to a decrease in performance, highlighting the importance of accurate pixel-level delineation for FGVP (Fig. A15, A16).
- **Image Preprocessing**: Blur-based image preprocessing strategies (e.g., `Blur-Padding`, `Blur-Resize`), which transform rectangular images into square inputs for CLIP while blurring padded areas, demonstrated better overall performance compared to `Crop` or `OV-Seg` (grayscale masked background) methods (Fig. A13). This further supports the hypothesis that blurred backgrounds are beneficial.

#### 4.5. Limitations in Inference Cost

While effective, FGVP incurs higher inference costs due to the integration of the segmentor (SAM) for mask production. Table 5 indicates that FGVP can take significantly longer (e.g., 27.47 minutes for RefCOCO with mask-filter and SAM-base) compared to `Crop` (4.49 minutes) or `RedCircle` (4.00 minutes), which do not rely on a segmentor. Disabling mask-filter post-processing can improve speed but with some minor performance degradation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research on Fine-Grained Visual Prompting (FGVP) offers several significant contributions and has the potential for considerable impact within the fields of computer vision and natural language processing, particularly concerning Vision-Language Models (VLMs).

#### 5.1. Addressing a Critical VLM Limitation

The most significant impact of this work is its direct addressal of a key limitation of contemporary VLMs: their sub-optimal performance in instance-level tasks requiring precise spatial localization and recognition. By enabling VLMs to effectively process fine-grained visual cues, this research extends their utility beyond image-level understanding, unlocking their potential for more complex applications like referring expression comprehension and part detection, without requiring extensive fine-tuning.

#### 5.2. Novel Visual Prompting Paradigm

The introduction and systematic exploration of fine-grained visual prompts, especially the `Blur Reverse Mask`, represents a novel paradigm in visual prompt engineering. Unlike prior coarse methods, FGVP leverages the pixel-level precision of semantic masks, demonstrating that the *quality* of the visual prompt significantly impacts VLM performance. The discovery of the `Blur Reverse Mask`'s exceptional effectiveness, potentially owing to VLMs' implicit understanding of photographic techniques like "Bokeh," provides a new guiding principle for future prompt design.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Zero-Shot Capabilities

FGVP establishes robust and effective zero-shot pipelines for challenging tasks like referring expression comprehension and part detection. This capability drastically reduces the dependency on large, task-specific annotated datasets and extensive training for each new application. The ability to perform part detection using only image inputs and grid-wise keypoints, without requiring pre-computed object proposals, further expands the scope of zero-shot learning. This can accelerate research and development by making advanced VLM capabilities more accessible and adaptable to novel scenarios.

#### 5.4. Synergistic Integration of Foundational Models

The methodology effectively integrates the Segment Anything Model (SAM), a powerful generalist segmentation model, with VLMs. This demonstrates a successful synergy between different foundational models, showcasing how advancements in one domain (segmentation) can directly enhance capabilities in another (multimodal understanding). This approach could inspire similar integrations to address other complex multimodal challenges.

#### 5.5. Insights into VLM Mechanics

The empirical success of the `Blur Reverse Mask` offers valuable insights into the internal workings of VLMs. The hypothesis that VLMs implicitly "understand" visual composition principles like "Bokeh" due to their web-scale training data suggests that carefully designed visual prompts can tap into these latent representations. This understanding can guide the development of more intuitive and effective prompting strategies in the future.

#### 5.6. Future Research Directions

The paper identifies several avenues for future research:
- **Inference Efficiency**: The current computational cost associated with integrating SAM poses a limitation for real-time applications. Future work could focus on optimizing the inference speed of SAM or exploring more efficient mask generation techniques.
- **Sophisticated Prompt Coupling**: Exploring the combined optimization of visual prompts with specifically designed language prompts could unlock even deeper image-text alignment comprehension abilities within VLMs.
- **Broader Task Applicability**: The proposed method could be adapted for other zero-shot transfer tasks, such as referring image segmentation, further extending its impact across different computer vision problems.

#### 5.7. Broader Societal Implications

As with any advanced AI technology, the potential impact extends to societal considerations. The authors acknowledge that the method relies on statistical properties learned from training datasets, which may contain biases. Careful consideration and further research are necessary to mitigate potential negative societal impacts that could arise from the deployment of such systems in real-world applications.
