# DITTO: Offline Imitation Learning with World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DITTO (Offline Imitation Learning with World Models) introduces an algorithm that learns expert policies from high-dimensional pixel observations entirely offline by training an agent within a learned world model, leveraging a non-adversarial intrinsic reward based on latent space divergence. The method consistently recovers or exceeds expert performance across all tested Atari environments and a continuous control task, effectively mitigating policy-induced covariate shift without direct environment interaction.

## Problem

- Covariate shift, where an agent's policy deviates from the expert's distribution, remains a central challenge in imitation learning (IL), leading to compounding errors.
- Traditional IL methods often struggle with high-dimensional observations (e.g., raw pixels) and typically require online interaction, which is impractical or unsafe in many real-world applications.
- Adversarial imitation learning approaches (e.g., GAIL) frequently suffer from instability issues like discriminator overfitting and mode collapse, especially when dealing with high-dimensional observations.

## Method

- **DITTO** employs a two-stage process: first, a ==recurrent state-space model (RSSM)== learns environment dynamics and encodes high-dimensional observations into a compact ==latent space== from available trajectory data.
- Second, an ==actor-critic imitation agent== is trained entirely within this learned ==world model=='s latent space, without access to the real environment or extrinsic rewards.
- The agent is optimized using a novel ==non-adversarial intrinsic reward==, formulated as a ==modified dot product== between the agent's current latent state and the closest expert latent state, promoting ==mode-seeking== behavior, and trained with ==REINFORCE== gradients on λ-returns.

## Results

- DITTO consistently recovered or exceeded expert performance across all **five** Atari environments (MsPacman, Qbert, BeamRider, Pong, Breakout) and the BipedalWalker continuous control task.
- The algorithm matched or outperformed baselines (Behavior Cloning, Dream-BC, Dream-GAIL) in both **sample efficiency** and **asymptotic performance**, while D-GAIL exhibited adversarial collapse in some cases.
- The quality of the data used to train the world model had minimal impact on subsequent imitation learning outcomes, suggesting that broad state space coverage is more crucial than policy optimality for dynamics learning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Minimizing on-policy latent divergence within a learned world model is a reliable predictor of generalization performance in the true environment and effectively mitigates covariate shift in offline settings.
> - Standard off-policy metrics like expert action prediction accuracy can be negatively correlated with true environment performance, indicating their inadequacy for evaluating generalization in sequential decision-making.
> - Leveraging a fixed, non-adversarial intrinsic reward based on latent space similarity offers greater stability and robustness for offline imitation learning compared to complex adversarial objectives.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Imitation learning (IL) is a policy learning paradigm that infers agent behavior directly from expert demonstrations, circumventing the need for explicit reward function design. A foundational challenge in IL is **covariate shift**, where the agent's policy induces a state distribution that diverges from the expert's training distribution, leading to compounding errors. Traditional Behavior Cloning (BC) trains an agent to predict expert actions from observations, but its inability to account for the sequential nature of decision problems makes it susceptible to covariate shift. Theoretical work by Ross & Bagnell (2010) established a quadratic regret bound for BC, while subsequent interactive online learning approaches (Ross et al., 2011) achieved linear regret by training the agent under its own distribution with expert corrections, thereby mitigating distribution mismatch. However, online learning is often impractical or unsafe in real-world scenarios such as robotics.

Inverse Reinforcement Learning (IRL) methods attempt to learn a reward function from expert demonstrations, then optimize this reward with on-policy Reinforcement Learning (RL), which helps address covariate shift. A subset of IRL approaches, including Generative Adversarial Imitation Learning (GAIL) (Ho & Ermon, 2016), frame IL as a divergence minimization problem between expert and learner state distributions, often using adversarial training. While GAIL can bound the expected return difference, it is known to suffer from instability issues like discriminator overfitting and collapse, especially with high-dimensional observations (Wang et al., 2019; Brock et al., 2019). Efforts to stabilize adversarial training or use intrinsic rewards (Wang et al., 2019; Reddy et al., 2020; Ciosek, 2022) have been explored, but often involve online learning or complex reward shaping.

Concurrent advancements in model-based RL have introduced **world models (WMs)**, which learn environment dynamics and can facilitate policy learning entirely within a learned latent space (Ha & Schmidhuber, 2018). Approaches like Dreamer (Hafner et al., 2020) and Dreamerv2 (Hafner et al., 2021) have demonstrated successful policy training in WMs, enabling offline policy learning and generalization to real environments, even physical robots (Wu et al., 2022). This paradigm offers a potential solution to the online learning constraint of IL by allowing "on-policy" learning within a simulated environment.

This work contributes to the intersection of offline imitation learning and world models. Previous work by Rafailov et al. (2021) also uses model-based rollouts for on-policy latent trajectories but relies on adversarial reward learning. DITTO differentiates itself by proposing a non-adversarial, fixed distance function in the world model's latent space as the intrinsic reward, aiming for greater stability and robustness while operating entirely offline and from high-dimensional pixel observations. The paper addresses the recognized gap in offline imitation learning that consistently recovers expert performance from pixels, positioning itself as a method for scaling IL to complex, partially observable environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an offline imitation learning algorithm capable of handling high-dimensional observations, facilitating learning entirely offline, and mitigating policy-induced covariate shift. This aims to bridge the gap between the theoretical benefits of on-policy learning for covariate shift mitigation and the practical constraints of real-world scenarios where online interaction with the environment or an expert is infeasible or unsafe.

The key motivations are:
1. **Addressing High-Dimensional Observations:** Many real-world applications, particularly in robotics, involve raw pixel data as observations. Traditional IL methods, especially those reliant on explicit state representations or discriminator-based rewards, often struggle with the complexity and dimensionality of pixel inputs. The goal is to develop a method that can learn directly from pixels.
2. **Enabling Offline Learning:** The ability to learn solely from a pre-recorded dataset of expert demonstrations, without any further interaction with the environment, is crucial for safety-critical applications or environments where online data collection is expensive or time-consuming. This contrasts with many IL methods that require online interaction for effective policy improvement and covariate shift reduction.
3. **Mitigating Policy-Induced Covariate Shift:** This remains a central challenge in IL. When a learner policy deviates from the expert policy, it generates states not present in the training data, leading to compounding errors. The motivation is to allow the agent to learn under its own induced distribution, even in an offline setting, to generalize effectively at test time.
4. **Developing a Robust and Stable Imitation Signal:** Prior adversarial imitation learning approaches, such as GAIL, have demonstrated instability and sensitivity to hyperparameters, often failing with high-dimensional observations. The motivation is to develop a simpler, more robust reward signal for imitation that avoids these issues. This involves leveraging the learned latent space of a world model as a natural measure of trajectory divergence, without requiring an additional learned reward model or discriminator.
5. **Achieving Expert-Level Performance in Challenging Environments:** The work is motivated to demonstrate that these objectives can be met by achieving or exceeding expert performance on complex, pixel-based Atari benchmarks, which have historically been difficult for offline imitation learning algorithms.

In essence, the research aims to create a framework where the benefits of on-policy learning can be realized offline, within a learned world model, using a stable and intuitive latent divergence metric to guide policy acquisition from high-dimensional, limited expert data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DITTO algorithm operates in a two-stage process: first, learning a world model from available trajectory data, and second, training an imitation agent within this learned world model.

#### 3.1. World Model Learning

The world model (WM) is trained on a dataset `D` composed of sequences of observations and actions `{(x_t, a_t)}`. The architecture adapts the recurrent state-space model (RSSM) from Hafner et al. (2021), comprising:
- **Image Encoder:** A convolutional neural network (CNN) that processes raw pixel observations `x_t` into a compact representation.
- **Recurrent State-Space Model (RSSM):** A recurrent neural network that learns the environment's transition dynamics in a latent space. It predicts a sequence of deterministic recurrent states `h_t`. Each `h_t` is used to parameterize a stochastic prior state `hat{z}_t` (predicting the next state without current observation) and a stochastic posterior state `z_t` (predicting the current state with current observation). The full model state `hat{s}_t` is the concatenation of `h_t` and `z_t`.
- **Decoder:** A transposed CNN that reconstructs observations `hat{x}_t` from the full model state `hat{s}_t`.

The WM components are jointly trained using a modified Evidence Lower Bound (ELBO) objective (Equation 11) that includes an image reconstruction term and a KL-divergence term between the posterior and prior states. **KL-balancing** (Hafner et al., 2021) is applied to the KL-divergence, using stop-gradient operations and a parameter `delta`, to control the regularization rates of the prior and posterior towards each other, promoting faster updates for the prior.

#### 3.2. Imitation Agent Training

Once the world model is trained, the imitation agent, comprising an actor and a critic, is trained entirely within the WM's latent space. This training is performed without access to the real environment or extrinsic rewards.

##### 3.2.1. Intrinsic Reward Formulation

A core aspect of DITTO is its novel intrinsic reward `r_int_t`. Initially, the paper presents a general form `r_int_t(z_pi_t) = 1 - min_{z_E in D_E} d(z_pi_t, z_E)`, which rewards the agent for matching its latent state-action pairs `z_pi_t` to the closest expert latent state-action pair `z_E` in the expert dataset `D_E` using an arbitrary distance function `d`.

For practical implementation and computational efficiency, the objective is modified. Instead of finding the minimum distance across the entire `D_E`, the agent's rollouts are compared to expert latent trajectories from the same time-step. Specifically, the agent starts from a randomly sampled expert latent state `z_E_t` and unrolls its policy `pi` for a fixed horizon `H` in the WM, generating a sequence of agent latents `z_pi_t:t+H`. The intrinsic reward at each step `t` is then computed as:
`r_int_t(z_E_t, z_pi_t) = 1 - d(z_E_t, z_pi_t) = (z_E_t * z_pi_t) / max(||z_E_t||, ||z_pi_t||)^2` (Equation 8).
This formulation utilizes a modified dot product, normalized by the maximum of the squared norms, which the authors found empirically outperformed L2 and cosine similarity metrics. This reward strategy shifts the learning from general distribution matching to **mode seeking**, where states frequently visited by the expert receive greater reward.

The theoretical underpinning for this reward is established through a corollary of Ciosek (2022) (detailed in the supplementary material), proving that maximizing this intrinsic reward bounds the total variation divergence between the expert and learner latent-state distributions, and consequently, their extrinsic reward difference in the true environment.

##### 3.2.2. Agent Architecture and Optimization

The agent employs a standard stochastic actor-critic architecture:
- **Actor (`pi_theta`):** A multi-layer perceptron (MLP) that observes Markovian recurrent states `hat{s}_t` from the world model and outputs a distribution over actions `a_t`.
- **Critic (`v_psi`):** An MLP that predicts the expected discounted sum of future intrinsic rewards.

The critic is trained to regress the **λ-target** `V_lambda_t` (Sutton & Barto, 2005) (Equation 14), which blends n-step returns with value function bootstrapping. The critic's loss minimizes the squared error between its predictions and the `lambda`-targets (Equation 15). A slowly updating target network is used for stability.

The actor is optimized to maximize the discounted sum of rewards predicted by the critic. It uses **REINFORCE gradients** (Williams, 2004), with the critic's value predictions subtracted from the `lambda`-targets for variance reduction. An **entropy regularization term** is added to the actor's loss (Equation 16) to encourage exploration and prevent premature policy collapse.

#### 3.3. Algorithm Flow (Algorithm 1 Summary)

1. **World Model Learning:**
 - Initialize WM parameters `phi`.
 - Iteratively draw mini-batches of transition sequences from the dataset `D`.
 - Compute RSSM components (recurrent, prior, posterior states).
 - Update `phi` using the ELBO loss (Equation 11).
2. **Agent Training:**
 - Initialize actor and critic parameters `theta`, `psi`.
 - Iteratively draw expert latent state sequences `hat{s}_E_tau` from the encoded expert demonstrations `hat{D}_E`.
 - Generate agent trajectories `(hat{s}_pi_tau, a_tau)` for a horizon `H` in the world model by sampling actions from the current actor policy.
 - Compute intrinsic rewards `r_int_tau` (Equation 8) between agent and expert latents.
 - Compute `lambda`-returns `V_lambda_tau`.
 - Update the critic using the `lambda`-targets (Equation 15).
 - Update the actor using the REINFORCE-based loss (Equation 16).

#### 3.4. Baselines and Evaluation Setup

DITTO is evaluated against:
- **Behavior Cloning (BC):** A standard end-to-end BC model trained from pixels using a CNN and MLP.
- **Dream-BC (D-BC):** A BC agent trained on world model latent states, using maximum-likelihood estimation with an entropy regularization term.
- **Dream-GAIL (D-GAIL):** A GAIL agent (Ho & Ermon, 2016) adapted to the world model setting. It trains on-policy in the world model using the adversarial objective, optimizing its learned adversarial reward with the same actor-critic formulation as DITTO.

Experiments are conducted on 5 Atari environments (MsPacman, Qbert, BeamRider, Pong, Breakout) and a continuous control environment (BipedalWalker) from pixels. Expert demonstrators are strong PPO agents (Schulman et al., 2017). World models are trained on 1000 episodes from pre-trained policies (PPO or A2C), while agent policies are trained with varying numbers of expert episodes (`N_E`). Evaluation metrics include extrinsic reward in the true environment, latent distance from the expert, and expert action prediction accuracy.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate DITTO's performance in offline imitation learning from high-dimensional pixel observations, particularly in Atari environments and a continuous control task.

#### 4.1. On-Policy Metrics and Covariate Shift

A key finding concerns the relationship between imitation metrics and true environment performance. The paper investigates how different metrics predict generalization performance in the true environment (using extrinsic reward, which the imitation learner does not access during training).
- **Latent Distance:** DITTO explicitly minimizes the latent distance from the expert in the world model. This on-policy latent divergence measure was found to be predictive of the agent's performance (extrinsic reward) in the true environment. DITTO achieved the lowest latent distance, correlating with its superior performance.
- **Expert Action Prediction Accuracy:** Counter-intuitively, standard expert action prediction accuracy (an off-policy metric) was found to be negatively correlated with the final extrinsic return in the true environment. This suggests that metrics solely evaluating performance within the expert's distribution are insufficient for predicting real-world generalization, as they do not account for policy-induced covariate shift and the sequential nature of decision-making. This supports the paper's hypothesis that evaluating policies under their own induced distribution (even if simulated in a WM) is crucial.

#### 4.2. Performance Across Environments

DITTO consistently recovered or exceeded expert performance across all tested Atari environments and the BipedalWalker continuous control environment.
- **Sample Efficiency and Asymptotic Performance:** DITTO matched or outperformed all baselines (BC, D-BC, D-GAIL) in terms of both sample efficiency (requiring fewer expert episodes to achieve high performance) and asymptotic performance (reaching higher final scores).
- **Atari Environments:**
 - In MsPacman and Qbert, most model-based methods (DITTO, D-BC, D-GAIL) achieved expert performance with minimal data, indicating these environments might be easier for latent-space imitation.
 - D-GAIL, while generally performing better than standard pixel-based GAIL reported in prior works, exhibited adversarial collapse twice in MsPacman, highlighting persistent instability issues associated with adversarial training.
 - DITTO consistently achieved or exceeded expert performance across all five Atari games, including BeamRider, Pong, and Breakout, which often presented more challenges for baselines.
- **Continuous Control (BipedalWalker):** DITTO also demonstrated strong performance in the pixel-based BipedalWalker environment, suggesting its applicability beyond discrete-action games.

#### 4.3. World Model Data Quality

An unexpected result was that the quality of the data used to train the world model (1000 episodes from either a high-performing PPO agent or a lower-performing A2C agent) had minimal impact on the WM's performance or the subsequent imitation learning outcomes. The authors hypothesize that this is because both PPO and A2C datasets provided similar broad coverage of the environment's state space, which appears more critical for dynamics learning than the optimality of the generating policy. The quality of the *imitation episodes* (the expert data `N_E`) remained crucial for the agent's final performance.

#### 4.4. Training Horizon Ablation

An ablation study on the training time horizon `H` for model-based rollouts indicated that both DITTO and D-GAIL achieved maximum performance at similar horizons (e.g., H=15). This suggests that `H` is an environment-specific hyperparameter affecting how far into the future the agent plans within the world model.

Overall, the findings indicate that DITTO, by leveraging world models and a latent divergence reward, effectively addresses the challenges of offline, high-dimensional imitation learning, demonstrating consistent expert-level performance without direct environment interaction or adversarial training complexities.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DITTO algorithm represents a notable advancement in the field of imitation learning, particularly in its capacity to address several long-standing challenges simultaneously. Its significance and potential impact stem from the following aspects:

1. **Scaling Imitation Learning to Real-World Constraints:** DITTO directly tackles three critical obstacles for real-world deployment of imitation learning: high-dimensional observations (e.g., pixels), offline learning capabilities, and mitigation of policy-induced covariate shift. By demonstrating consistent recovery of expert performance in complex pixel-based Atari environments and a continuous control task entirely offline, DITTO offers a blueprint for applying IL in practical scenarios where online interaction is unsafe, costly, or impossible (e.g., robotics, autonomous driving in critical situations).
2. **Robust and Stable Policy Learning Framework:** The proposed use of a fixed, natural divergence measure in the world model's latent space as an intrinsic reward contributes to a more stable and robust policy learning process. This approach bypasses the complexities and instabilities commonly associated with adversarial training objectives, as seen in GAIL, which often suffer from mode collapse or discriminator overfitting. The empirical observation that DITTO avoids adversarial collapse, unlike D-GAIL (even in the world model setting), highlights this benefit.
3. **Leveraging World Models for Covariate Shift Mitigation:** The work reinforces the utility of world models not just for model-based reinforcement learning, but specifically for imitation learning to tackle covariate shift. By enabling on-policy learning in a simulated environment, DITTO allows the agent to generate data under its own induced distribution and learn to correct deviations from expert behavior, thereby improving generalization performance in the true environment. The finding that latent distance is predictive of extrinsic reward, while action prediction accuracy is not, underscores the importance of this on-policy evaluation in the latent space.
4. **Conceptual Simplicity and Generality:** The core idea of using a simple distance function in a learned latent space as a dense reward signal for imitation is conceptually straightforward and domain-agnostic. This contrasts with methods requiring explicit reward engineering or complex adversarial architectures, potentially making DITTO easier to implement and adapt across various tasks and observation modalities.
5. **Benchmarking and Baseline Contribution:** By adapting existing IL algorithms (BC, GAIL) to the world model setting (D-BC, D-GAIL) and demonstrating DITTO's superior performance, the paper provides valuable new baselines and insights into offline imitation learning from pixels. This expands the understanding of how world models can enhance other IL approaches.

The successful implementation of offline imitation learning from pixels, consistently achieving expert-level performance, contributes to the ongoing effort to make AI agents learn complex behaviors with minimal supervision and direct interaction, thereby accelerating the deployment of autonomous systems in challenging real-world domains.
