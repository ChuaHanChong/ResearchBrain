# Reliable and Scalable Robot Policy Evaluation with Imperfect Simulators

> [!summary] Summary
> SureSim introduces a framework that integrates imperfect simulation with limited real-world trials to provide statistically rigorous and cost-effective evaluation of robot policies. It provides finite-sample valid confidence intervals on real-world policy performance, achieving an average reduction of 20-25% in hardware evaluation effort compared to real-only methods.

## Problem

- Real-world robot policy evaluation is costly, time-consuming, and lacks statistical rigor due to the reliance on a small, statistically insufficient number of hardware trials.
- Physics-based simulators offer scalability but their inherent 'sim-to-real gap' prevents direct, statistically sound inferences about real-world performance from simulation data alone.
- There is a need for methods that provide finite-sample valid confidence intervals on individual robot policy performance, robustly combining imperfect simulation with scarce real-world data.

## Method

- **SureSim** formalizes policy evaluation as a ==Prediction-Powered Inference== (**PPI**) problem, leveraging a small dataset of paired real-world/simulation outcomes with a large dataset of additional simulation predictions.
- It employs a meticulous `real2sim` pipeline to create corresponding simulation environments for real-world setups, ensuring a strong correlation between paired data.
- The framework utilizes a uniform **PPI** estimator and the ==Waudby-Smith and Ramdas== (**WSR**) algorithm to compute finite-sample valid and tighter confidence intervals for mean real-world policy performance.

## Results

- SureSim reduced the required real-world hardware evaluation effort by an average of 20-**25%** compared to traditional real-only methods for achieving similar statistical confidence bounds.
- The uniform PPI variant of SureSim efficiently tightened confidence interval widths, reducing them by **14.4%** for a diffusion policy when incorporating 700 additional simulations.
- Extensive Sim2Sim experiments confirmed that SureSim consistently provides provably valid confidence interval coverage, unlike control variate methods which often showed miscoverage.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Prediction-Powered Inference (PPI) effectively combines imperfect simulation with limited real-world data to yield statistically valid and tighter confidence intervals on robot policy performance.
> - The effectiveness of simulation augmentation is directly tied to the correlation between real and simulated outcomes; low correlation (large sim-to-real gap) diminishes the inferential benefits.
> - Finite-sample valid statistical methods are essential for reliable robot policy evaluation, as methods relying on asymptotic guarantees can exhibit miscoverage in practical, data-limited scenarios.
