# DreamWAM: Beyond RGB Future Prediction for World Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DreamWAM enhances World Action Models (WAMs) by predicting future states using multiple structured representations —appearance, motion, geometry, and semantics—instead of solely RGB. This approach yields policies with increased robustness and generalization capabilities when facing unseen visual conditions in robotic manipulation, without requiring non-RGB inputs during deployment.

## Problem

- Most World Action Models (WAMs) define and predict future states exclusively in raw RGB observation space.
- Relying solely on RGB prediction can entangle task-relevant state transitions with visually salient but action-irrelevant variations (e.g., texture, illumination, background).
- This entanglement hinders the robustness and generalization of policies, making them susceptible to failures under visual distribution shifts.

## Method

- **DreamWAM** introduces a structured world modeling approach that represents future states using four complementary views: appearance (RGB), motion, geometry, and semantics.
- It employs a hybrid integration methodology within a ==VideoDiT-ActionDiT== architecture, using ==joint latent denoising== for RGB and motion, and ==gated residual modeling== for geometry and semantics.
- The system is trained with these heterogeneous future views but maintains an RGB-only deployment interface by deactivating non-RGB pathways at inference time.

## Results

- Achieved improved average success rates on the LIBERO-Plus out-of-distribution benchmark, increasing from **51.36%** (Fast-WAM) to **63.44%** (DreamWAM-uncond) and from **69.16%** (Fast-WAM-Joint) to **75.47%** (DreamWAM).
- Demonstrated enhanced performance on real-world robotic manipulation tasks, improving the average success rate from **90.8%** (Fast-WAM-Joint) to **96.7%** on standard tasks, and from **55.6%** to **74.4%** under unseen visual perturbations.
- Ablation studies confirmed that all three auxiliary views (motion, geometry, semantics) contribute to performance improvements, with motion providing the most consequential signal.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Predicting the future through complementary views beyond RGB (motion, geometry, semantics) significantly enhances policy robustness and generalization against visual perturbations.
> - The method of injecting diverse future representations into the WAM is crucial; VAE-aligned motion latents benefit from joint latent denoising, while heterogeneous features like geometry and semantics require gated residual connections to avoid interference.
> - The structured future supervision is internalized by the policy during training, leading to greater resilience to visual changes even when future imagination is not performed at test time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the field of robotic manipulation, specifically focusing on World Action Models (WAMs). WAMs represent a class of robot policies that learn to act by predicting how the observed world will evolve in response to actions. Unlike traditional Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that often map current observations directly to actions, WAMs incorporate future prediction as both a rollout mechanism and an inductive bias for learning scene dynamics and task progress.

Most existing WAMs primarily define and predict this future state in raw RGB observation space. While RGB images contain rich visual information, the authors argue that relying solely on RGB prediction can entangle task-relevant state transitions (e.g., object displacement, contact) with visually salient but action-irrelevant variations (e.g., texture, illumination, background, viewpoint). This entanglement can hinder the robustness and generalization of policies, especially when visual conditions change from the training environment.

The paper builds upon recent advancements in WAMs, which have focused on improving future prediction through stronger video backbones, tighter world-action coupling, and more efficient generation mechanisms (e.g., Fast-WAM, Motus, WorldVLA). DreamWAM distinguishes itself by addressing a more fundamental question: *what* features or representations should a WAM prioritize when imagining the future? It connects with a growing line of research that injects auxiliary world knowledge into VLA and video models, such as using depth for geometric reasoning (e.g., DepthVLA, GeoVLA, PointVLA), semantic features (e.g., DreamWorld), or motion information (e.g., Motus, VideoJAM). DreamWAM extends this by combining multiple complementary non-RGB views within a coupled VideoDiT-ActionDiT WAM architecture, aiming for more robust action learning while maintaining an RGB-only deployment interface.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to enhance the robustness and generalization capabilities of World Action Models (WAMs) by reformulating how they represent and predict future world states. The authors identify a core limitation in current WAMs: their prevalent reliance on predicting future states exclusively in RGB observation space.

The motivation for this work stems from the following observations:

- **Entanglement of Information in RGB:** While RGB images are rich in information, they conflate task-relevant state changes (e.g., an object moving to a target location) with visually variable but action-irrelevant nuisance factors (e.g., changes in texture, lighting, background, or camera viewpoint). For a robotic policy, the critical information often pertains to object identities, their relative spatial configurations, and their dynamics, rather than every pixel detail of the scene.
- **Lack of Robustness under Visual Shifts:** This entanglement makes RGB-only WAMs susceptible to failures when encountering visual distribution shifts in deployment environments, even if the underlying manipulation task remains semantically identical. A policy trained to predict pixel-perfect future frames might struggle when the background changes or lighting shifts, as the "correct" future RGB frame differs significantly, despite the action required for task completion remaining the same.
- **Beyond Pixel-Perfect Reproduction:** The authors argue that a "useful anticipation" or "dream" of an interaction for a robot should capture the essence of what is involved, how it changes, and where that change occurs, rather than a perfect pixel rendering. This suggests a need for a richer, structured representation of the future that explicitly focuses on action-relevant attributes.

To address these issues, the key objective is to:

- **Introduce a structured world modeling approach for future prediction:** This involves representing future states through multiple, complementary views beyond RGB, specifically appearance (RGB), motion, geometry, and semantics.
- **Develop a novel architecture (DreamWAM):** This architecture should effectively integrate these heterogeneous future views during training to inform action learning, while maintaining an efficient and standard RGB-only deployment interface.
- **Demonstrate improved robustness and generalization:** The ultimate goal is to validate that this multi-view future representation leads to more robust robotic policies that perform reliably across unseen visual perturbations and in real-world environments, compared to RGB-only baselines. The paper explicitly aims to show that such robustness depends "not only on predicting the future, but on representing it in a form that matters for action."

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DreamWAM reforms the future prediction mechanism within a World Action Model by incorporating structured world representations beyond RGB appearance. The method is built upon the two-expert VideoDiT-ActionDiT architecture of Fast-WAM.

**Structured Future Views:**
DreamWAM represents future states (Y<sub>1:H</sub>) through four complementary views:
1. **Appearance (z<sub>rgb</sub>):** The standard future RGB sequence encoded by a Wan2.2 video VAE.
2. **Motion (z<sub>mot</sub>):** Dense optical flow between adjacent future frames, estimated by a frozen RAFT model, visualized as RGB, and then encoded by the same video VAE. This explicitly models image-space temporal change.
3. **Geometry (f<sub>geo</sub>):** Geometry-oriented features extracted from future frames using a frozen Depth Anything V3 (DA3-Base) model. These features are aligned with the VideoDiT grid and capture depth structure.
4. **Semantics (f<sub>sem</sub>):** Patch-level features extracted from future frames using a frozen DINOv2 ViT-B/14 model. These features are aligned and normalized, aiming to preserve object- and region-level consistency.

All non-RGB views (motion, geometry, semantics) are precomputed offline from training videos and serve as training targets. They are not used as additional policy observations during deployment.

**Heterogeneous Future Modeling:**
To integrate these diverse views, DreamWAM employs two distinct pathways:

- **Joint Latent Denoising for RGB and Motion:**
 - RGB and motion latents (z<sub>rgb</sub> and z<sub>mot</sub>) share a similar dense spatiotemporal structure and are compatible with the video VAE latent space.
 - During training, they are perturbed at the same flow-matching timestep and concatenated along the latent channel dimension (x<sub>joint</sub><sup>t</sup> = Concat<sub>C</sub>[x<sub>rgb</sub><sup>t</sup>, x<sub>mot</sub><sup>t</sup>]).
 - The VideoDiT is trained to jointly denoise both views in a shared stream. This allows explicit modeling of temporal change alongside appearance.

- **Gated Residual Modeling for Geometry and Semantics:**
 - Geometry (f<sub>geo</sub>) and semantic (f<sub>sem</sub>) features differ in source and representation from VAE latents. Directly concatenating them into the denoising stream could interfere with the VideoDiT's pretrained video-generation prior.
 - Instead, DreamWAM uses lightweight gated residual branches to modify selected VideoDiT layers during training.
 - For each feature view *j* and layer *ℓ*, the update is: h<sub>ℓ</sub> = B<sub>ℓ</sub>(h<sub>ℓ-1</sub>) + Σ<sub>j</sub> g<sub>j</sub><sup>ℓ</sup>(h<sub>ℓ-1</sub>, c) ⊙ R<sub>j</sub><sup>ℓ</sup>(h<sub>ℓ-1</sub>), where R<sub>j</sub><sup>ℓ</sup> produces a view-specific residual, and g<sub>j</sub><sup>ℓ</sup> is a learned gate that controls its magnitude.
 - This additive path ensures the original backbone state (h<sub>ℓ-1</sub>) remains primary, while feature-level supervision is applied as controlled corrections. A prediction head P<sub>j</sub>(h<sub>L</sub>) predicts the aligned future feature during training.

**World-Action Coupling:**
The system uses a two-expert VideoDiT-ActionDiT architecture. The VideoDiT processes the multi-view future-state representation, while the ActionDiT denoises the action trajectory. These experts exchange information through shared attention at paired layers. This mechanism allows the ActionDiT to condition on the enriched multi-view future representation learned by the VideoDiT during training, without directly consuming the raw optical-flow, depth, or semantic teacher features. The additional future views are thus not isolated auxiliary tasks but directly influence action learning.

**Training Objective and Inference:**
- The overall training objective (L) is a weighted sum of:
 - Flow matching losses for RGB (L<sub>FM</sub><sup>rgb</sup>) and motion (L<sub>FM</sub><sup>mot</sup>).
 - Prediction losses for geometry (L<sub>pred</sub><sup>geo</sup>) and semantics (L<sub>pred</sub><sup>sem</sup>).
 - Flow matching loss for action (L<sub>FM</sub><sup>act</sup>).
 - An ℓ1 penalty on gate activations (L<sub>gate</sub>) to regularize their magnitude.
- **Crucially, at inference (deployment), all non-RGB supervision pathways are inactive.** This means the motion input is disabled (zero-filled channels), and the residual branches, external target encoders (RAFT, DA3, DINOv2), and feature-prediction heads are removed. DreamWAM reverts to the RGB-only deployment interfaces of Fast-WAM, outputting only the denoised action chunk.

**Target Construction Details (from Appendix):**
- **RAFT motion latent:** RAFT estimates flow fields between adjacent frames, which are then converted to color-coded RGB visualizations. This RGB flow video is encoded by the Wan2.2 VAE, resulting in z<sub>flow</sub> ∈ R<sup>48x3x14x28</sup>.
- **DA3 geometry latent:** DA3-Base extracts depth maps per camera view, which are then log-transformed. Local region descriptors (aligned with 14x28 video latent grid) are projected to 8 channels using PCA. Temporal aggregation (first frame, average frames 2-5, average frames 6-9) yields z<sub>depth</sub> ∈ R<sup>8x3x14x28</sup>.
- **DINOv2 semantic latent:** DINOv2 ViT-B/14 extracts 768-dimensional patch tokens, projected to 8 dimensions via PCA, resized to 14x28, and temporally aggregated similarly to depth features, yielding z<sub>DINO</sub> ∈ R<sup>8x3x14x28</sup>.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DreamWAM was evaluated across various manipulation tasks in simulation (LIBERO, LIBERO-Plus) and on a real robot, comparing against matched RGB-only baselines, primarily Fast-WAM and Fast-WAM-Joint.

#### 4.1. LIBERO (In-distribution Performance)

On the standard LIBERO benchmark, DreamWAM demonstrated consistent improvements:
- **No-rollout setting:** DreamWAM-uncond improved the average success rate from 97.30% (Fast-WAM) to **98.40%**.
- **Joint inference setting:** DreamWAM improved the average success rate from 98.00% (Fast-WAM-Joint) to **98.90%**.
These results indicate that structured future supervision enhances the policy itself, and this benefit is complementary to test-time imagination. DreamWAM also achieved the highest overall average among compared methods in its joint configuration.

#### 4.2. LIBERO-Plus (Out-of-distribution Robustness)

LIBERO-Plus evaluates generalization under seven unseen visual perturbations (camera viewpoint, robot appearance, language, lighting, background, image noise, object layout). No LIBERO-Plus data was used for training.
- **No-rollout setting:** DreamWAM-uncond significantly improved the average success rate across perturbations from 51.36% (Fast-WAM) to **63.44%**.
- **Joint inference setting:** DreamWAM improved the average success rate from 69.16% (Fast-WAM-Joint) to **75.47%**.
DreamWAM showed improvements across all seven perturbation dimensions in both inference modes. The substantial gains under distribution shifts, particularly in the no-rollout setting, suggest that the structured future supervision is internalized by the policy, leading to greater resilience to visual changes even when future imagination is not performed at test time. The reduced gap between no-rollout and joint variants post-DreamWAM training (12.02 points vs. 17.80 points for baselines) indicates that some action-relevant future structure typically provided by online imagination is already learned and internalized.

#### 4.3. Real-World Robot Evaluation

Evaluations on an AgileX PiPER dual-arm robot corroborated the simulation findings:
- **Standard tasks:** DreamWAM improved the average success rate from 90.8% (Fast-WAM-Joint) to **96.7%** across four multi-step tabletop tasks.
- **Unseen visual perturbations:** On the Strawberry Selection task, under changes in lighting, background, and object layout, DreamWAM achieved an average success rate of **74.4%**, a significant improvement over Fast-WAM-Joint's 55.6%.
This pattern of larger gains under visual perturbations compared to in-distribution performance mirrors the LIBERO-Plus results, suggesting improved resilience to varied visual conditions rather than just better in-distribution fitting. A qualitative example showed Fast-WAM-Joint failing due to an inaccurate gripper-to-plate spatial relation, which DreamWAM's multi-view supervision (motion, geometry, semantics) aims to address.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study

The ablation study investigated the complementarity of the three beyond-RGB views and the efficacy of different injection pathways.
- **Complementary Views:** Under their optimized routing, all three auxiliary views (motion, geometry, semantics) individually contributed to improving the RGB-only baseline. Motion was identified as the most consequential signal; removing it led to the largest performance degradation, suggesting it provides a crucial temporal scaffold for other feature-level signals.
- **Representation-matched Injection:**
 - Full denoising (channel concatenation, 'D') was found preferable for VAE-aligned motion latents.
 - Gated residual injection ('R') was found preferable for heterogeneous geometry and semantic features.
 - The "all-denoise" control (D/D/D for all three views) performed worse than the RGB-only baseline, while the proposed "hybrid" (D/R/R) routing achieved the best overall result (98.90%). This highlights that the gains are not merely from adding more supervision but from correctly matching the type of future view to a compatible integration pathway within the pretrained VideoDiT backbone, minimizing interference.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DreamWAM research presents several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotic control and world modeling:

- **Rethinking Future Representation in WAMs:** The work's primary significance lies in identifying and demonstrating that the *representation* of the predicted future is a core design variable in World Action Models, moving beyond the traditional reliance on solely RGB pixel predictions. By explicitly formulating future imagination through complementary views of appearance, motion, geometry, and semantics, DreamWAM shifts the focus from pixel-perfect generation to action-relevant state preservation.

- **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The most impactful finding is the consistent and substantial improvement in policy robustness under unseen visual perturbations, both in simulation (LIBERO-Plus) and on a real robot. This addresses a critical challenge in robotics: deploying policies in varied and unpredictable real-world environments. By guiding the WAM to learn about the dynamics, spatial organization, and semantic structure of interactions, DreamWAM enables policies to generalize better to changes in lighting, background, object layouts, and other visual shifts, even when the task semantics remain constant.

- **Practical Deployment Strategy:** A key strength of DreamWAM is its ability to achieve these robustness gains without altering the RGB-only deployment interface of the underlying WAM. All beyond-RGB supervision pathways are disabled at inference, meaning the policy at test time still operates with the familiar RGB input. This "training-time rich, deployment-time lean" approach makes DreamWAM highly practical and compatible with existing robotic systems and infrastructure, reducing computational overhead and complexity during operation.

- **Insights into World-Action Learning:** The ablation studies provide valuable insights into how different types of future information should be integrated into a WAM. The finding that VAE-aligned motion benefits from joint latent denoising, while heterogeneous geometry and semantic features are better incorporated via gated residual branches, suggests a blueprint for designing more effective multi-modal world models. It underscores that the *method* of injection is as important as the *quantity* of auxiliary supervision.

- **Catalyst for Future Research:** This work opens avenues for future research in several directions. It encourages further exploration of other action-relevant representations, such as temporally consistent metric 3D information, contact-aware targets, or even force/torque predictions. The success of DreamWAM suggests that investing in richer, more structured internal "dreams" for robots can lead to more capable and adaptable autonomous agents, paving the way for more reliable and generalizable robotic systems in complex, dynamic environments. It reinforces the idea that an embodied agent's perception should be intrinsically linked to its actionable understanding of the world.
