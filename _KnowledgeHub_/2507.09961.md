# Text-Driven Causal Representation Learning for Source-Free Domain Generalization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from the Chinese Academy of Sciences, University of Electronic Science and Technology of China, University of Surrey, Shenzhen University, and University of Rochester introduced Text-Driven Causal Representation Learning (TDCRL), a framework integrating causal inference into Source-Free Domain Generalization (SFDG). It learns robust, domain-invariant features by mitigating domain-specific confounders through text-simulated visual representations from VLMs, achieving state-of-the-art performance on various benchmarks.

## Problem

- Traditional Domain Generalization (DG) methods are resource-intensive, requiring extensive, costly collection and annotation of diverse multi-source image datasets.
- Existing Source-Free Domain Generalization (SFDG) approaches, while leveraging Vision-Language Models (VLMs), struggle with domain-specific confounders that lead to overfitting styles and poor generalization to unseen target domains.
- The powerful theoretical framework of causal inference, effective in traditional DG for explicit confounder removal, had not yet been successfully applied within the SFDG setting.

## Method

- The method efficiently generates diverse text embeddings by combining class names with ==augmented style word vectors==, encoded by CLIP's text encoder, to simulate rich visual data without actual source images.
- A ==Causal Intervention Network (CIN)== is designed to perform a =="do-operation"== on these features, explicitly intervening on style components using a ==learned confounder dictionary== to disentangle domain-invariant features from spurious style correlations.
- A classifier is trained on the intervened, domain-invariant features, with the entire model (CIN and classifier) optimized jointly using a contrastive loss for intervention and a cross-entropy loss for classification.

## Results

- TDCRL achieved state-of-the-art average Top-1 accuracy across **four** challenging benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, DomainNet) using various CLIP backbones, notably reaching **84.6%** with ViT-L/14, outperforming prior SFDG and many source-dependent DG methods.
- Ablation studies empirically confirmed the crucial role of the causal intervention module, demonstrating accuracy improvements of up to **3.4%** and showing effective removal of domain styles in both text and image features.
- The framework exhibits enhanced computational efficiency, requiring less training time than comparable SFDG methods, and t-SNE visualizations confirm its ability to merge domain-specific feature clusters into cohesive, domain-invariant representations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Causal intervention, traditionally complex for image data, can be effectively implemented in a source-free manner using VLM-driven text prompts, allowing precise control over style components to isolate category-specific information.
> - The causal intervention network, trained exclusively on text embeddings, successfully transfers its ability to remove domain-specific style information from *image* embeddings during inference, enhancing cross-modal transferability.
> - Explicitly modeling and intervening on domain-specific confounders through a theoretically grounded causal framework significantly improves out-of-distribution generalization, moving beyond purely empirical solutions in SFDG.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a critical challenge in deep learning: **domain shift**, where a model trained on one data distribution (source domain) performs poorly when tested on a different distribution (target domain). This problem is central to the field of **Domain Generalization (DG)**, which aims to develop models that can generalize to unseen target domains without access to their data during training.

Historically, DG methods have primarily fallen into four categories:
1. **Domain Alignment:** Minimizing discrepancies between source domains using statistical measures (e.g., MMD, KL divergence) or adversarial learning.
2. **Meta-Learning:** Simulating domain shifts during training to learn meta-knowledge that enhances generalization.
3. **Data Augmentation:** Diversifying training samples through various transformations to improve model robustness.
4. **Self-Supervised Learning:** Exploiting intrinsic data structures to learn useful representations without explicit labels.

A significant limitation of traditional DG is its reliance on *multiple source domains* for training. Collecting and annotating large image datasets from diverse domains is often prohibitively expensive, time-consuming, and impractical in real-world scenarios.

The emergence of **Vision-Language Models (VLMs)**, such as CLIP, has revolutionized various vision tasks. CLIP, in particular, aligns image and text embeddings into a shared feature space, enabling powerful zero-shot capabilities. This advancement has paved the way for **Source-Free Domain Generalization (SFDG)**, an exciting new paradigm that eliminates the need for large, diverse image datasets. SFDG methods leverage VLMs to simulate visual representations using text prompts, drastically reducing data demands.

Current SFDG methods generally fall into two categories:
1. **Prompt Tuning-based Strategies:** Learning various style word vectors and combining them with class names to generate text embeddings, then directly training a classifier. Examples include PromptStyler, PromptTA, and BatStyler.
2. **Adapter-based Strategies:** Using data augmentation to create varied style word vectors and text embeddings, often incorporating modules to remove domain-specific features based on metrics like entropy. An example is DPStyler.

However, a key limitation of existing SFDG methods is their struggle with **domain-specific confounders**. These are factors related to the style or context of a domain that are spuriously correlated with class labels, causing models to overfit to learned styles and hindering generalization to significantly different unseen test environments.

This paper positions **TDCRL** as a novel solution by integrating **causal inference** into the SFDG setting. Causal inference, which focuses on uncovering true cause-and-effect relationships beyond mere correlations, has been successfully applied in traditional DG to identify and remove domain-specific confounders, leading to more robust and interpretable models. TDCRL is the *first* method to explicitly bring causal learning into the source-free domain, addressing the critical gap of confounder removal in text-simulated visual representations. By leveraging the ease of "intervening" on styles through simple text prompt modifications (e.g., "cat in a sketch style" vs. "cat in a cartoon style"), TDCRL simplifies complex style transformations often required in image-based causal DG, offering a theoretically sound and computationally efficient approach to learning domain-invariant features in a source-free manner.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to develop a highly effective and efficient method for **Source-Free Domain Generalization (SFDG)** that can robustly generalize to unseen target domains. This objective is driven by several key motivations stemming from the limitations of existing approaches:

1. **Address the Resource-Intensive Nature of Traditional DG:** Traditional Domain Generalization (DG) methods require access to and annotation of extensive image datasets from multiple source domains. This process is costly, time-consuming, and often impractical in real-world applications where data diversity is limited or sensitive. The motivation is to overcome these practical barriers by moving towards a "source-free" paradigm.

2. **Enhance Generalization Capabilities of SFDG Methods:** While recent Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP enable SFDG by simulating visual data with text prompts, existing SFDG approaches (e.g., prompt tuning, adapter-based methods) often struggle with domain-specific confounders. These confounders lead to models that may overfit to the styles present in the *simulated* training data, performing poorly when encountering vastly different styles in unseen target domains. The key objective is to improve the robustness and generalization performance of SFDG models by mitigating these confounders.

3. **Introduce Causal Inference to the SFDG Setting:** Causal inference has proven highly effective in traditional DG for identifying and isolating domain-invariant features by explicitly removing the influence of domain-specific confounders. However, this powerful framework had not yet been explored in the SFDG context. A core motivation is to bridge this gap, leveraging the theoretical guarantees of causal learning to extract truly domain-invariant features from text-simulated representations. This involves adapting causal intervention mechanisms, which are often complex for image-based data, to the more flexible text-driven environment.

4. **Develop a Theoretically Grounded and Mechanistic Approach:** Many existing DG/SFDG methods rely on empirical strategies. This research aims to provide a clear and effective mechanism, grounded in causal learning theory (specifically, the do-operation), to ensure robust generalization. The objective is to establish a theoretical equivalence that demonstrates how intervening on style components can isolate category-specific information, leading to better predictions.

5. **Achieve Scalability and Efficiency:** By using text prompts and a streamlined causal intervention process, the goal is to develop an SFDG method that is not only robust but also scalable and computationally efficient, reducing training time and infrastructure requirements compared to both traditional DG and some existing SFDG methods.

In summary, the primary objective is to develop **TDCRL (Text-Driven Causal Representation Learning)** as the first method to integrate causal inference into the source-free domain generalization setting, thereby learning robust, domain-invariant features and achieving state-of-the-art performance with high efficiency.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TDCRL operates through a novel two-step framework, leveraging pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) like CLIP to achieve source-free domain generalization with a strong foundation in causal inference.

**Step 1: Text Embedding Generation**
The first stage focuses on efficiently generating diverse text embeddings that simulate visual representations, circumventing the need for actual source domain images.
1. **Predefined Domain Words:** The method begins with a small set of *D* predefined domain words (e.g., "sketch", "cartoon", "photo"). These words represent distinct visual styles.
2. **Style Word Vector Augmentation:** To enhance diversity beyond these *D* initial words, a data augmentation strategy is employed. *M* new "style word vectors" (where *M* > *D*, e.g., M=80) are generated for each epoch using a mixup strategy. Each new vector `s_m` is a weighted combination of the initial `D` word vectors, with weights `w_d` sampled from a beta distribution, plus a Gaussian noise term `ϵ` to introduce additional variability. This process creates a richer set of style representations.
3. **Text Prompt Generation:** For each of the *K* class names and *M* generated style word vectors, a text prompt is constructed using a template: "a [class_k] in a [S_m] style." The placeholder `S_m` is replaced by the augmented style word vector `s_m`.
4. **CLIP Text Encoding:** These prompts are then tokenized and fed into CLIP's pre-trained text encoder `T(·)` to generate `M × K` text embeddings, denoted as `f_cls_k_s_m`. These embeddings serve as the training samples, effectively simulating visual data that inherently mixes category information (`f_cls_k`) with domain-specific style information (`f_s_m`).

**Step 2: Causal Representation Learning**
The second, and core, stage introduces causal inference to disentangle domain-invariant features from the generated text embeddings.
1. **Causal Analysis and Intervention:** The paper theoretically establishes that by applying a causal "do-operation" to `f_cls_k_s_m` (intervening on its style component), one can model `P(Y | do(f_cls_k_s_m))`, which is equivalent to `P(Y | f_cls_k)`. This means that by controlling for style variations, the model can learn the direct causal relationship between category features and labels, ensuring domain invariance.
2. **Confounder Dictionary (Z):** To implement this intervention, a "confounder dictionary" `Z = {z_n}_N` is constructed. This dictionary comprises *N* (e.g., N=6) style intervention vectors. Each `z_n` is derived from a subset of the initial `D` predefined domain words by creating a generic prompt "a object in a [domain_n] style" and encoding it with CLIP's text encoder `T(·)`. Each `z_n` represents a distinct style to which features can be causally intervened.
3. **Causal Intervention Network (g):** A multi-layer linear neural network, `g(·)`, is designed to perform the actual intervention. It takes two inputs: an input embedding (either a text embedding `f_cls_k_s_m` during training or an image embedding `f_img` during inference) and a style intervention vector `z_n` from the confounder dictionary. The network's role is to transform the input embedding such that its original style information is "replaced" or "intervened" by the style `z_n`.
4. **Training the Causal Intervention Network (`g`):** `g(·)` is trained using a contrastive learning framework with the InfoNCE loss (`L_g`). For each input `f_cls_k_s_m` and intervention `z_n`, `g`'s output `g(f_cls_k_s_m, z_n)` is optimized to be similar to a "ground truth" embedding `F[k, n, :]` while being dissimilar to other styles `F[k, j, :]` (where `j ≠ n`). `F[k, n, :]` represents the ideal embedding for "a [class_k] in a [domain_n] style," generated directly by CLIP's text encoder. This ensures `g` learns to transform features according to the target style `z_n` while preserving class information.
5. **Training the Classifier (`C`):** A linear classifier `C(·)` is trained to predict labels from the *intervened* features. As per the causal theory, `P(Y | do(f_cls_k_s_m))` is approximated by `E_z [P(Y | f_cls_k_z)]`, which is further approximated by `C(1/N * Σ_n g(f_cls_k_s_m, z_n))`. The sum over `z_n` represents the aggregate effect of all style interventions, effectively averaging out domain-specific confounders. `C(·)` is trained with standard cross-entropy loss (`L_C`).
6. **Overall Training Objective:** The entire model (both `g` and `C`) is optimized jointly using a combined loss: `min_g,C E[L_C + λL_g]`, where `λ` is a balance hyperparameter.

**Inference:**
During inference, a target image is first encoded using CLIP's visual encoder to obtain its feature representation `f_img`. This `f_img` is then fed into the causal intervention network `g(·)` along with each `z_n` from the confounder dictionary. The average of these intervened features, `1/N * Σ_n g(f_img, z_n)`, represents the domain-invariant feature, which is then passed to the trained classifier `C(·)` for final label prediction.

This methodology combines the data-efficient nature of VLM-based SFDG with the robust feature disentanglement capabilities of causal inference, offering a powerful solution to out-of-distribution generalization.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

TDCRL consistently demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across diverse domain generalization benchmarks and various CLIP backbones, validating its effectiveness in learning domain-invariant features.

**A. Comparisons with State-of-the-Art Methods:**
- **Superior Average Accuracy:** TDCRL achieves the highest average Top-1 accuracy across all four benchmark datasets (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, DomainNet) and all tested CLIP visual backbones (ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14).
 - **ResNet-50:** TDCRL reached 75.5% average accuracy, outperforming the next best source-free method, PromptTA (74.9%), and even source-dependent methods like SPG (75.2%).
 - **ViT-B/16:** TDCRL achieved 81.7% average accuracy, surpassing PromptTA (80.8%) and SPG (80.8%).
 - **ViT-L/14:** TDCRL scored 84.6% average accuracy, again outperforming PromptTA (83.9%).
- **Outperformance on Individual Datasets:** TDCRL shows dominant performance on most individual datasets. For instance, with ViT-L/14, it scored 99.0% on PACS, 84.6% on VLCS, 89.0% on OfficeHome, and 65.6% on DomainNet, consistently setting new benchmarks.
- **Closing the Gap with Source-Dependent Methods:** A significant observation is that TDCRL, as a source-free method, often matches or even surpasses the performance of traditional DG methods that utilize actual source domain data. This highlights the power of combining VLMs with causal inference to negate the need for expensive multi-source data.
- **Stability:** TDCRL exhibits low standard deviations across runs (e.g., ±0.1 to ±0.3), indicating high stability and consistency in its performance, which is crucial for reliable deployment.

**B. Ablation Studies and Analysis:**
- **Crucial Role of Causal Intervention (CI):** Removing the causal intervention module (`g` network trained with `L_g`) leads to a notable decrease in accuracy across all backbones (0.7% to 3.4% drop). This empirically confirms that causal intervention is essential for mitigating style confounders and learning truly domain-invariant features. The impact was more pronounced on smaller models (ResNet-50), suggesting CI is particularly beneficial when raw embeddings are less robust.
- **Cross-Modal Transferability:** Analysis of style classification loss and MMD distance demonstrates that the causal intervention network (CIN), trained on text embeddings, successfully transfers its ability to remove style information from *image* embeddings. After intervention, the style classification loss significantly increases (meaning the model struggles to classify styles, indicating their removal), and MMD distances between domain pairs drastically decrease (indicating enhanced domain invariance).
- **Robustness to Loss Function Choices:** TDCRL's performance remains robust when using different loss functions for `L_g` (InfoNCE vs. L2) and `L_C` (cross-entropy vs. ArcFace), with minimal variations in accuracy (average absolute change around 0.5% or less). This suggests that the core causal intervention mechanism is stable and effective, regardless of minor variations in optimization objectives.
- **Sensitivity Analysis of Hyperparameters (τ and λ):** The model shows robustness to changes in the temperature hyperparameter `τ` (for InfoNCE loss) and the balance hyperparameter `λ` (balancing `L_g` and `L_C`). Accuracy typically peaks within a reasonable range and then gradually declines, indicating that the method is not overly sensitive to precise hyperparameter tuning.
- **Feature Visualization (t-SNE):** Visualizations of "dog" category features from the PACS dataset using t-SNE provide compelling evidence. Without intervention, features cluster distinctly by domain (Art, Cartoon, Photo, Sketch). After causal intervention, these domain-specific clusters merge into a single, cohesive cluster, with domain boundaries significantly blurred. This visually confirms that TDCRL effectively removes domain-specific confounders and promotes domain-invariant feature learning.
- **Optimal Network Depth for `g(·)`:** An ablation on the number of layers in `g(·)` showed that 3 layers offered the best balance between accuracy and efficiency. Performance gains diminish beyond 3 layers, while inference speed slightly decreases.
- **Optimal Confounder Dictionary Size (N):** The study on the number of confounders `N` in `Z` indicated that `N=6` provides an optimal balance, yielding strong generalization without excessive computational cost. Marginal gains were observed with larger `N`, suggesting that `N=6` already captures sufficient style diversity for effective interventions.
- **Efficiency:** TDCRL is shown to be more computationally efficient in terms of training time (255 minutes) compared to PromptStyler (511 minutes) and DPStyler (384 minutes), while also achieving higher performance gains (+2.2% over CLIP's zero-shot performance). Inference speed remains competitive.

In summary, the findings robustly demonstrate that TDCRL not only achieves state-of-the-art performance in SFDG but also provides strong empirical and visual evidence for the efficacy of its causal inference framework in disentangling domain-invariant features and ensuring cross-modal transferability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TDCRL represents a significant advancement in the field of domain generalization, particularly for scenarios where access to diverse source domain data is limited. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

1. **Pioneering Causal Inference in SFDG:** TDCRL is the first method to successfully integrate causal inference into the Source-Free Domain Generalization (SFDG) setting. This marks a new research direction, offering a theoretically grounded framework to explicitly address and eliminate domain-specific confounders in VLM-driven generalization. It bridges the gap between the practical advantages of VLMs and the robustness benefits of causal learning.

2. **Reduced Resource Demands for Robust AI:** By eliminating the need for expensive and time-consuming collection and annotation of large, diverse source domain image datasets, TDCRL makes robust AI deployment more accessible and practical. This has a profound impact on fields with scarce or sensitive data, such as medical imaging, robotics, or specialized industrial applications, where acquiring diverse training data is a major bottleneck.

3. **Enhanced and Guaranteed Generalization:** The causal intervention mechanism provides a clear, mechanistic approach to learning truly domain-invariant features. This offers stronger theoretical guarantees for generalization to unseen target domains, moving beyond purely empirical approaches. The empirical results confirm this, showing TDCRL consistently outperforms existing SFDG methods and even many source-dependent DG methods.

4. **Leveraging VLM Capabilities for Disentanglement:** The work effectively harnesses the powerful pre-trained knowledge of Vision-Language Models (CLIP) not just for zero-shot transfer but for sophisticated text-driven causal interventions. The ability to simulate diverse styles through simple text prompt modifications simplifies the complex style transformations often required in image-based causal DG, enhancing computational efficiency.

5. **Improved Model Interpretability and Robustness:** By disentangling domain-specific features from category-relevant features, TDCRL contributes to building more robust and potentially more interpretable AI models. Understanding *why* a model generalizes (or fails to) is crucial for trust and debugging, and causal models offer a pathway towards this.

6. **Catalyst for Future Research:** This pioneering work opens new avenues for research at the intersection of VLMs, causal inference, and domain generalization. Future studies could explore more complex causal graphs, dynamic intervention strategies, or applications to other cross-modal or multimodal learning scenarios under domain shift. It could inspire causal approaches in other VLM-based tasks susceptible to spurious correlations.

7. **Broad Applicability Across AI Domains:** The demonstrated state-of-the-art performance across diverse benchmarks (PACS, VLCS, OfficeHome, DomainNet) and various visual backbones (ResNet-50, ViT-B/16, ViT-L/14) suggests wide applicability. TDCRL can be a valuable tool for deploying AI systems in dynamic environments where visual styles, textures, or lighting conditions vary unpredictably, ensuring consistent performance without requiring continuous re-training.

In conclusion, TDCRL offers a novel, effective, and efficient solution to a critical problem in deep learning. By integrating causal inference into source-free domain generalization, it significantly reduces data requirements while boosting generalization capabilities, promising a substantial impact on the development and deployment of robust AI systems in real-world scenarios.
