# Toward Humanoid Brain-Body Co-design: Joint Optimization of Control and Morphology for Fall Recovery

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at The Chinese University of Hong Kong, Shenzhen introduced RoboCraft, an iterative framework for the brain-body co-design of humanoid robots, specifically optimizing their morphology and control policy for fall recovery. This approach achieved an average performance improvement of 44.55% in fall recovery across seven different humanoid robot models.

## Problem

- Existing co-design methods are typically limited to simpler robots, struggling with the high complexity, degrees of freedom, and rigid-body dynamics of humanoids.
- Humanoid fall recovery, a critical capability, often focuses solely on control policy optimization for a fixed morphology, overlooking potential performance gains from physical design changes.
- Evaluating novel morphologies in co-design is computationally intensive, as it often requires training a new control policy from scratch for each design, limiting scalability.

## Method

- ==The RoboCraft== framework implements an iterative bi-level optimization approach that jointly optimizes both the morphology (body) and control policy (brain) of humanoid robots for fall recovery.
- A "Control Policy Update" phase incorporates knowledge transfer through inter-design pretraining and intra-design finetuning to efficiently evaluate morphology performance.
- A "Design Update" phase systematically searches for improved morphologies within a defined parameter space using various optimization algorithms, guided by a priority buffer of top-performing designs.

## Results

- The RoboCraft framework delivered an average performance improvement of **44.55%** in fall recovery across seven different public humanoid robot models.
- Specific robots demonstrated significant gains, with Bez1 improving by **145%** and Sigmaban by **106%** in fall recovery performance.
- An ablation study confirmed the necessity of control policy finetuning, showing that finetuned policies consistently yielded higher improvements for evolved morphologies compared to using only a static pretrained policy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Morphological optimization can contribute substantially to performance gains, with some robots (e.g., OP3-Rot, Sigmaban) showing over 90% of improvements attributed to body changes rather than solely control adjustments.
> - Knowledge transfer mechanisms, including pretraining a shared control policy and then finetuning it, are crucial for achieving computational efficiency and scalability in co-design for complex humanoids.
> - Optimized morphologies tend to evolve towards taller structures and lower passive resistance, which directly enhances fall recovery by facilitating upright balance and controller action.
