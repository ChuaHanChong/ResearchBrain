# WaveForward: An Omnidirectional Passive Wheeled Quadruped Robot with Casters

> [!summary] Summary
> The WaveForward robot, an omnidirectional passive wheeled quadruped, leverages 2-DOF casters on each leg and an asymmetric actor-critic reinforcement learning framework to achieve multi-directional movement. It demonstrated an 89.1% reduction in Cost of Transport compared to point-foot trotting, alongside high-speed capabilities up to 4.1 m/s.

## Problem

- Existing passive wheeled-legged robots lack omnidirectional mobility, being restricted by non-holonomic constraints to mainly forward/backward motion due to single-DOF wheels.
- Actuated wheeled-legged robots offer enhanced mobility but incur increased complexity, weight, and cost due to their active wheel components.

## Method

- Introduces **WaveForward**, a passive wheeled quadruped robot featuring 2-DOF casters on each of its four legs to enable omnidirectional movement.
- Develops a control framework based on asymmetric actor-critic ==reinforcement learning== (PPO) that dynamically adjusts the caster base posture using actuated leg joints.
- Implements a caster base posture adjustment strategy that aligns the caster swivel axis and deflection angle with commanded velocities to generate directional propulsive force.

## Results

- Demonstrated omnidirectional mobility through successful slalom navigation in real-world tests and achieved high-speed motion up to **4.1** m/s on flat terrain.
- Reduced the mechanical Cost of Transport (COT) by **89.1%** compared to traditional point-foot trotting of the same robot, consuming 41 Wh over a **1.1** km trajectory versus 91 Wh.
- Validated the capability to smoothly switch between different propulsion modes, including four-wheel, front-wheel, and rear-wheel drive, by dynamically controlling caster engagement.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Propulsive force in passive casters can be generated through a geometric-constraint coupling involving swivel-axis inclination, caster deflection, and no-slip conditions, requiring both non-zero inclination and deflection.
> - An asymmetric actor-critic reinforcement learning framework can effectively control strongly underactuated systems by allowing the critic to access privileged information about passive joint states during training.
> - Dynamically adjusting the caster base posture using actuated leg joints provides a viable method for achieving omnidirectional propulsion in passive wheeled systems.
