# Dynamic Neural Koopman Distillation for Real-Time Robot Control Using Diffusion Models

> [!summary] Summary
> A novel Dynamic Neural Koopman Distillation framework transforms iterative diffusion model inference into a single forward pass, enabling real-time, low-latency robot control while preserving multimodal trajectory generation. This approach achieved up to 172x speedup in simulation and 37x on a Kinova manipulator, maintaining strong control performance across locomotion and obstacle avoidance tasks.

## Problem

- Diffusion models generate expressive, multimodal robot trajectories but are too slow for real-time, high-frequency closed-loop control due to iterative denoising.
- Current fast sampling or one-step distillation methods often compromise either speed, multimodal expressivity, or ability to handle state-dependent nonlinear robot dynamics.
- Static Koopman distillation approaches lack the flexibility to model the state-dependent, dynamic nature of robot motion generation effectively.

## Method

- Developed the Dynamic Neural ==Koopman== (DNK) Distillation framework to compress the iterative diffusion process into a single, fast forward pass.
- Integrated a Factorized Dynamic Koopman (FDK) layer to enable state-dependent latent dynamics, adapting the linear transition based on the robot's current condition.
- Employed a comprehensive training objective with teacher-fidelity, control-oriented, and structural regularization terms to preserve multimodality and ensure training stability.

## Results

- Demonstrated substantial reduction in inference latency, achieving up to **172x** speedup in simulated D4RL tasks and **37x** speedup (to **4.08** ms) on a physical Kinova manipulator.
- Maintained or improved control performance compared to the original diffusion teacher, achieving normalized returns of **0.8885** on HalfCheetah and **1.0973** on Walker2d, and **100%** success rate on a real-world obstacle avoidance task.
- Showcased enhanced robustness and multimodal trajectory generation, with lower intra-run variability and effective candidate diversity, outperforming existing one-step distillation methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly modeling the latent denoising process with state-dependent dynamics, rather than a static global operator, is essential for effective diffusion distillation in robotics.
> - Factorizing the Koopman latent transition using modal projection/reconstruction matrices and sample-dependent modal gains provides a flexible and efficient parameterization for complex robot behaviors.
> - Decoupling multimodal trajectory generation from immediate action selection via an external critic enables real-time control while preserving the teacher's distributional diversity.
