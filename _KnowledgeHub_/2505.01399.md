# Physics-Conditioned Grasping for Stable Tool Use

> [!summary] Summary
> Purdue University researchers developed a framework that selects robotic grasps based on predicted interaction forces and torques during tool use, addressing mechanical instability. This approach achieved a 17.5% improvement in real-world task success compared to baselines and consistently reduced peak induced wrist torque by up to 17.6% in simulation.

## Problem

- Robotic tool use often fails due to mechanical instability (slip, rotation) caused by interaction forces exceeding grasp limits, rather than semantic misunderstanding.
- Current grasp selection metrics, relying on geometry or quasi-static assumptions, do not account for dynamic forces, inertial impulses, and lever-arm amplification that generate significant wrist torque.
- There is a limited coupling between grasp selection and the predicted interaction wrench induced by a specific task trajectory, meaning a grasp stable for static lifting may fail under active tool-object interaction.

## Method

- Introduced inverse Tool-use Planning (iTuP), a framework that conditions grasp selection on the predicted interaction wrench (forces and torques) derived from a task-conditioned trajectory.
- Developed a principled, physics-based method to quantify destabilizing effects of task-induced wrench using three penalties: interaction torque, slip, and alignment deviation, derived from rigid-body mechanics.
- Created the ==Stable Dynamic Grasp Network (SDG-Net)==, a learned surrogate model that approximates these trajectory-conditioned wrench costs in real-time, enabling efficient evaluation of numerous candidate grasps.

## Results

- SDG-Net consistently reduced peak induced wrist torque by up to **17.6%** and slip magnitude in simulation, with failure cases largely correlating with high torque levels above an empirical instability threshold of approximately **6.9** Nm.
- In real-world hardware experiments, iTuP achieved an overall task success rate of **77.5%**, representing a **17.5%** improvement over a compositional VLM baseline, with greater gains in dynamic and lever-arm-dominated tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that SDG-Net's wrench-conditioned scoring was responsible for the reduced torque-induced instabilities and improved success rates, demonstrating its contribution to mechanical feasibility independent of semantic reasoning.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Mechanical robustness in robotic tool use is significantly improved when grasp selection is explicitly conditioned on the predicted dynamic wrench (forces and torques) that will be transmitted through the grasp during a task.
> - The separation of semantic reasoning (tool identification and interaction points) from mechanical feasibility (grasp stability under predicted forces) allows for more reliable systems that leverage advanced language models while ensuring physically sound actions.
> - Lever-arm amplification and misalignment between the gripper and interaction normal are critical factors in generating destabilizing torques and tangential forces, making grasps that mitigate these effects more stable.
