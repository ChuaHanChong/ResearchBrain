# Reviving DSP for Advanced Theorem Proving in the Era of Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DSP+ proposes an inference-only neuro-symbolic framework for automated theorem proving that achieves performance comparable to state-of-the-art models. It accomplishes this without extensive reinforcement learning-based training or fine-tuning, instead relying on careful coordination of existing reasoning models and symbolic provers.

## Problem

- The prevailing approach to automated theorem proving (ATP) relies on large language models (LLMs) trained with reinforcement learning (RL), which demands vast synthesized data, computational resources, and human effort.
- Prior inference-only neuro-symbolic frameworks, such as the original DSP, were largely surpassed by these resource-intensive, RL-trained models.
- The high computational cost of current ATP methods limits their accessibility and widespread adoption for advanced mathematical reasoning.

## Method

- **DSP+** introduces an enhanced, inference-only ==neuro-symbolic== framework that operates in three pipelined phases: Draft, Sketch, and Proving.
- The Draft phase uses a reasoning model to generate a concise natural language proof outline, while the Sketch phase autoformalizes this draft into formal subgoals with explicit hypothesis hints and uses error line masking for robustness.
- The Proving phase integrates an LLM-based step prover with a symbolic tree search engine (Aesop in Lean 4) for efficient tactic application and subgoal completion.

## Results

- Achieved an **83.6%** pass rate on miniF2F-test in ensemble, closely approaching DeepSeek-Prover-V2-**671B** and outperforming Kimina-Prover-Preview-**72B** at similar or fewer sample budgets, and uniquely solved the previously unproven `imo_2019_p1` problem.
- Demonstrated significantly higher inference token efficiency (e.g., 3.6k + 0.8k average tokens for **80.7%** on miniF2F) compared to existing methods while maintaining competitive performance.
- Enabled the identification of eight wrongly formalized statements within the miniF2F dataset, highlighting its utility in dataset quality assurance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art automated theorem proving performance can be achieved through inference-only neuro-symbolic orchestration of existing models, challenging the reliance on extensive reinforcement learning training.
> - Fine-grained coordination between a natural language proof draft, a formal sketch with explicit hypothesis hints and error masking, and a robust symbolic proving phase with tree search is crucial for successful formalization.
> - The neuro-symbolic approach yields human-comprehensible proof patterns and is effective in identifying errors in formal mathematics datasets.
