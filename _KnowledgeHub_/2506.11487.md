# Reviving DSP for Advanced Theorem Proving in the Era of Reasoning Models

> [!summary] Summary
> DSP+ proposes an inference-only neuro-symbolic framework for automated theorem proving that achieves performance comparable to state-of-the-art models. It accomplishes this without extensive reinforcement learning-based training or fine-tuning, instead relying on careful coordination of existing reasoning models and symbolic provers.

## Problem

- The prevailing approach to automated theorem proving (ATP) relies on large language models (LLMs) trained with reinforcement learning (RL), which demands vast synthesized data, computational resources, and human effort.
- Prior inference-only neuro-symbolic frameworks, such as the original DSP, were largely surpassed by these resource-intensive, RL-trained models.
- The high computational cost of current ATP methods limits their accessibility and widespread adoption for advanced mathematical reasoning.

## Method

- **DSP+** introduces an enhanced, inference-only ==neuro-symbolic== framework that operates in three pipelined phases: Draft, Sketch, and Proving.
- The Draft phase uses a reasoning model to generate a concise natural language proof outline, while the Sketch phase autoformalizes this draft into formal subgoals with explicit hypothesis hints and uses error line masking for robustness.
- The Proving phase integrates an LLM-based step prover with a symbolic tree search engine (Aesop in Lean 4) for efficient tactic application and subgoal completion.

## Results

- Achieved an **83.6%** pass rate on miniF2F-test in ensemble, closely approaching DeepSeek-Prover-V2-**671B** and outperforming Kimina-Prover-Preview-**72B** at similar or fewer sample budgets, and uniquely solved the previously unproven `imo_2019_p1` problem.
- Demonstrated significantly higher inference token efficiency (e.g., 3.6k + 0.8k average tokens for **80.7%** on miniF2F) compared to existing methods while maintaining competitive performance.
- Enabled the identification of eight wrongly formalized statements within the miniF2F dataset, highlighting its utility in dataset quality assurance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - State-of-the-art automated theorem proving performance can be achieved through inference-only neuro-symbolic orchestration of existing models, challenging the reliance on extensive reinforcement learning training.
> - Fine-grained coordination between a natural language proof draft, a formal sketch with explicit hypothesis hints and error masking, and a robust symbolic proving phase with tree search is crucial for successful formalization.
> - The neuro-symbolic approach yields human-comprehensible proof patterns and is effective in identifying errors in formal mathematics datasets.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Automated theorem proving (ATP) has seen remarkable progress recently, largely driven by the application of large language models (LLMs) and advanced training paradigms. The prevailing trend in the current research landscape, as acknowledged by the authors, involves "leveraging reinforcement learning (RL)-based large-scale training" to enhance LLMs' theorem proving capabilities. Prominent examples of this trend include models like DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B, Kimina-Prover-Preview-72B, AlphaProof, BFS-Prover, Goedel-Prover, and STP-Prover. These systems often require vast amounts of synthesized formal statements and proofs, coupled with significant computational resources and human effort, to achieve their impressive results on benchmarks such as miniF2F.

This paper positions itself as a direct, yet contrasting, response to this dominant paradigm. It "revisits" and "revives" the "Draft, Sketch, and Prove (DSP)" framework, which was initially proposed as an inference-only approach mimicking human informal-to-formal reasoning. While the original DSP was state-of-the-art at its publication time, it was subsequently "significantly outperformed by current top solutions" that utilized large-scale RL training.

DSP+ distinguishes itself by demonstrating that "even without any training," meaning *without additional model training or fine-tuning for the overall framework*, careful neuro-symbolic coordination of *existing* off-the-shelf reasoning models and tactic step provers can achieve comparable, and in some cases, superior performance. This work challenges the assumption that ever-larger models and more extensive RL training are the sole path to progress in ATP. Instead, it advocates for an "efficient, complementary approach" that capitalizes on the "overlooked potential of existing reasoning models" through intelligent orchestration and fine-grained integration of neural and symbolic components. By doing so, DSP+ proposes a more resource-efficient and accessible pathway to advanced theorem proving, potentially democratizing access to high-performance ATP without the prohibitive computational costs associated with large-scale RL.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to demonstrate that an enhanced version of the Draft, Sketch, and Prove (DSP) framework, dubbed DSP+, can achieve state-of-the-art or comparable performance in automated theorem proving, critically, **without any additional model training or fine-tuning for the framework itself**. This stands in stark contrast to the prevailing trend of deep reinforcement learning-based approaches that demand substantial computational resources and data.

The key motivations driving this work are:
- **Re-evaluating Underestimated Potential:** The authors "surprisingly find" that the original DSP framework, despite being surpassed by newer RL-trained models, holds underestimated potential. They aim to prove that with careful design, its core principles remain highly effective.
- **Efficiency and Accessibility:** By relying on inference-only coordination of existing models, DSP+ seeks to offer a significantly more budget-friendly approach in terms of computational resources and time, making advanced theorem proving more accessible. This addresses the resource intensity of large-scale training.
- **Synergy of Neuro-Symbolic AI:** The paper is motivated by the belief that "classical reasoning patterns" and the "synergy of reasoning models, step provers, symbolic search, and careful neuro-symbolic coordination" can provide a powerful alternative or complement to purely neural, large-scale training methods. It seeks to prove the efficacy of integrating human-like informal-to-formal reasoning with formal verification systems.
- **Developing a Flexible Framework:** Beyond performance, the authors aim to develop a modular and efficient system that allows for flexible model coordination and ensemble settings, facilitating further research and deployment within the theorem proving community.
- **Improving Proof Comprehensibility and Debugging:** A practical motivation is to generate proof patterns that are comprehensible to human experts, which can, in turn, facilitate the identification of formalization errors in datasets, as demonstrated by the discovery of issues in miniF2F.

In summary, the core objective is to challenge the single-minded pursuit of ever-larger, RL-trained models by showcasing a highly effective, resource-efficient, and human-interpretable neuro-symbolic alternative.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DSP+ is an improved, "fine-grained and integrated neuro-symbolic" version of the Draft, Sketch, and Prove framework, designed to leverage the strengths of large language models (LLMs) and symbolic theorem provers. It operates in three distinct, yet interconnected, phases:

#### 3.1. Draft Phase with Thinking and Conciseness

- **Input:** The formal statement of the theorem to be proven.
- **LLM Role:** A "reasoning model" (e.g., QwQ-32B, DeepSeek-R1) is prompted to generate a natural language proof draft. Reasoning models are chosen for their strong mathematical reasoning capabilities, often enhanced by "thinking tokens" (internal reasoning processes).
- **Enhancements:**
 - **Conciseness:** The draft model is specifically prompted to produce "concise outputs," focusing only on key formulas and steps, avoiding excessive verbosity or "lost in the middle" effects for subsequent phases.
 - **Filtering:** Thinking tokens generated by the LLM are filtered out, and references to human-written proofs are removed, ensuring the draft is purely model-generated intuition.
 - **No Human Proofs:** Unlike the original DSP, DSP+ finds that human informal proofs are not necessary, and in some cases, might even hinder performance for modern reasoning models.

#### 3.2. Sketch Phase with LLM Hints and Error Line Masking

- **Input:** The concise natural language proof draft from the Draft phase.
- **LLM Role:** A "sketch model" (e.g., DeepSeek-V3-0324) autoformalizes this informal draft into a hierarchy of subgoals in formal language (Lean 4 syntax), but without providing the full proof for each subgoal.
- **Enhancements:**
 - **Explicit Hypothesis Specification:** The sketch model is instructed to explicitly specify potential supporting hypotheses for each subgoal using a `prove_with [...]` format (a pseudonym for Lean's `sorry` augmented with hypothesis usage). This helps narrow down the search space for the proving phase, as cluttered subgoal contexts can degrade performance.
 - **Error Line Masking:** To handle syntactically invalid output from the sketch model, an automatic iterative repair process is introduced:
 - If a subgoal statement is syntactically wrong, that line and all subsequent dependent lines are commented out.
 - If a proof line *within* a subgoal raises an error, only that line and subsequent dependent lines are replaced with `sorry`.
 This "masking" strategy aims to retain as much of the valid sketch as possible, sidestepping full re-execution of the sketch phase.

#### 3.3. Proving Phase with Step Prover and Tree Search

- **Input:** The (potentially masked) formal sketch with unproven subgoals.
- **Methods:** This phase combines symbolic search with an LLM-based step prover for heuristic guidance.
 - **Step Prover Integration:** A "step prover" (e.g., BFS-Prover) predicts the next tactic given a proof state. This is "tightly integrated" with a symbolic search engine (Aesop in Lean 4). This "built-in integration" (via Lean Copilot framework) allows the step prover's proposed tactics to be incorporated directly into Aesop's internal search logic, avoiding complex state-to-theorem conversions or external parsing.
 - **Tree Search (Aesop):** Aesop, a highly customizable proof search engine, explores the tactic tree. Its search space is configured to include both tactics proposed by the step prover and a set of common, efficient tactics (e.g., `linarith`, `simp`).
 - **Hypothesis Handling:** The proving phase intelligently handles hypotheses: it attempts to prove subgoals using *only* the hypotheses hinted by the sketch model (`clear * -...`) and also using *all* available hypotheses. If either attempt succeeds, the proof for that subgoal is considered complete.

#### 3.4. Implementation and System Design

- **Lean 4 (v4.17.0-rc1):** The chosen formal language and theorem prover.
- **Default Configuration:** QwQ-32B (draft), DeepSeek-V3-0324 (sketch), BFS-Prover (proving).
- **Ensemble Setting:** To boost performance and explore LLM generation diversity, DSP+ can execute multiple "workflow attempts" per problem using different combinations of reasoning models. An ensemble setting further combines results from different model configurations.
- **Modularity:** Designed for easy replacement of reasoning models by updating prompt templates and serving models via vLLM.
- **Efficiency:** Features a pipelined and buffered process where phases load results from a buffer and pass processed results to the next, sharing buffered results across different model combinations to avoid reprocessing.

This comprehensive methodology allows DSP+ to effectively bridge the gap between human-like intuitive reasoning and formal verification, making it both powerful and relatively efficient.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

DSP+ demonstrates remarkable performance and efficiency, positioning it as a strong competitor to current state-of-the-art (SOTA) RL-based theorem provers, despite its inference-only nature.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **miniF2F-test:**
 - DSP+ (default: QwQ-V3-BFS) achieves 79.5% pass rate with 1024 workflow attempts.
 - DSP+ (R1-V3-BFS) reaches 80.7% with 1024 attempts, demonstrating its competitive edge.
 - The ensemble setting further boosts performance to 83.6%, closely approaching DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (88.9% at 8192 sample budget), while outperforming Kimina-Prover-Preview-72B (80.7% at 8192 sample budget).
- **ProofNet-test:**
 - DSP+ (default) solves 32.8% of problems.
 - The ensemble setting improves this to 33.9%, which is on par with DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (33.6% at 128 sample budget) for the same sample budget.
- **PutnamBench:**
 - DSP+ (default) solves 24 out of 644 problems.
 - The ensemble setting solves 25 out of 644 problems.
- **miniF2F/IMO Subset:**
 - DSP+ achieves 40% and 45% (ensemble) accuracy on IMO problems, comparable to Kimina-Prover-Preview-72B (40%) and DeepSeek-Prover-V2-671B (50%) at significantly higher sample budgets.

#### 4.2. Efficiency in Inference Tokens

- A key finding is DSP+'s efficiency. For example, DSP+ (R1-V3-BFS) achieves 80.7% accuracy on miniF2F-test using `3.6k + 0.8k` average inference tokens per pass (total over passes), while Kimina-Prover-Preview-72B achieves the same 80.7% accuracy but requires `10k` tokens per pass and 8192 passes, indicating a much higher total token expenditure for Kimina-Prover. This highlights DSP+'s ability to achieve comparable results with fewer computational resources.

#### 4.3. Key Achievements and Qualitative Insights

- **Unprecedented IMO Problem Solution:** DSP+ uniquely solves `imo_2019_p1`, an International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) problem in miniF2F that had not been solved by any prior work, showcasing its advanced reasoning capabilities.
- **Human-Comprehensible Proofs:** DSP+ generates proofs with `have` statements that are "comprehensible by human experts," improving readability and alignment with human proof conventions compared to some other automated provers like BFS-Prover.
- **Dataset Error Discovery:** Through its nuanced proof generation and verification process, DSP+ facilitated the identification of eight "wrongly formalized statements" in the miniF2F dataset. This practical outcome underscores the system's robustness and its utility beyond just proving, extending to dataset quality assurance.
- **Ablation Studies Insights:**
 - **Synergy (RQ1):** All DSP+ components (Draft, Sketch, Proving) contribute significantly to overall performance, with removing any component leading to a performance drop. The ensemble setting further boosts accuracy by leveraging the diversity of different model combinations.
 - **Effectiveness of Enhancements (RQ2):** The "explicit specification of subgoal hypotheses" and "error line masking" in the Sketch phase are crucial optimizations, with their removal causing a "significant drop in accuracy and sample efficiency." Interestingly, direct prompting for "conciseness" in the draft was less impactful than allowing "no format," which proved more informative for sketch models. Also, providing human informal proofs did *not* improve performance for DSP+, differentiating it from the original DSP.
 - **Robustness Across LLMs (RQ3):** DSP+ maintains strong performance with different underlying LLMs (e.g., DeepSeek-R1 for draft, QwQ-32B for sketch), demonstrating its general applicability. Reasoning models generally perform better in the draft phase due to their "thinking tokens."

These findings collectively affirm DSP+'s effectiveness, efficiency, and robustness, validating the authors' core hypothesis that neuro-symbolic coordination can indeed compete with resource-intensive RL-based training in ATP.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The work on DSP+ carries several significant implications and potential impacts for the field of automated theorem proving and broader AI research:

1. **Validation of Neuro-Symbolic Approaches:** The most profound significance lies in demonstrating the competitive power of a carefully designed, inference-only neuro-symbolic framework. In an era dominated by large-scale, RL-trained models, DSP+ provides compelling evidence that sophisticated coordination of existing, off-the-shelf models can achieve comparable, if not superior, results. This validates the "classical reasoning patterns" and suggests that architectural ingenuity and integration strategies can be as impactful as raw model scale and extensive training.

2. **Increased Efficiency and Accessibility:** By achieving high performance with significantly fewer inference tokens and without requiring additional training or fine-tuning, DSP+ offers a far more resource-efficient solution. This reduces the barriers to entry for researchers and practitioners, making advanced theorem proving more accessible to those without access to massive computational resources. This efficiency is crucial for real-world deployment and iterative development cycles.

3. **Complementary Research Direction:** DSP+ presents a vital complementary research direction to the prevailing RL-based training trend. Instead of viewing these approaches as mutually exclusive, DSP+ suggests a future where both paradigms can co-exist and potentially reinforce each other. Its findings might inspire hybrid approaches that combine the strengths of large-scale training for general reasoning with the structured precision of symbolic methods and inference-time coordination.

4. **Improved Interpretability and Debuggability:** The design of DSP+, which mimics human informal-to-formal reasoning and generates human-comprehensible proof patterns at the `have` statement level, greatly enhances the interpretability of generated proofs. This is a crucial advantage for formal verification, where understanding *why* a proof holds (or fails) is as important as its correctness. The ability to identify "wrongly formalized statements" in existing datasets, as demonstrated, further highlights its utility in debugging and quality assurance for formal mathematics.

5. **Potential for Data Generation and Benchmarking:** The paper notes that DSP+ "can also serve in the pipeline for generating high-quality cold-start data for model training, as indicated in DeepSeekProver-V2." This indicates a potential symbiotic relationship where DSP+ can generate diverse and correct proofs to further train and improve future generations of large, RL-trained models, creating a virtuous cycle of improvement. Moreover, its rigorous evaluation on challenging benchmarks provides valuable insights into the strengths and weaknesses of current ATP systems.

6. **Catalyst for Diversified Approaches:** By providing both strong results and open-sourcing its code, DSP+ encourages the theorem proving community to "have more diversified and synergized approaches for advanced theorem proving." This can foster innovation beyond singular, large-model-centric solutions.

**Limitations:**
Despite its strengths, the authors acknowledge limitations, primarily the "underexplored design space" of DSP+. Finding the absolute optimal configuration for its various components and ensemble settings remains an open challenge. Furthermore, specific failure cases in each phase (e.g., drafting novel proofs, formalizing complex concepts, handling trivial-but-hard subgoals) point to ongoing research challenges in achieving truly general and robust automated mathematical reasoning. The impact of different Lean 4 versions on proof validity also highlights the subtle complexities of working with evolving formal systems.

In conclusion, DSP+ is a significant contribution that reasserts the power of neuro-symbolic integration and intelligent system design in the competitive landscape of automated theorem proving. It offers an efficient, interpretable, and complementary path forward, with broad implications for both research and practical applications in formal mathematics.
