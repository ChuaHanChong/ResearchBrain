# DexTele: A Dual-Arm Dexterous Teleoperation System Based on Motion Retargeting and Adaptive Force Control

> [!summary] Summary
> A dual-arm teleoperation system named DexTele achieves precise motion retargeting across varied robotic platforms without platform-specific training, and implements adaptive force control for compliant grasping of diverse objects. It demonstrates robust cross-platform performance with low errors and significantly improved grasping success rates.

## Problem

- Existing motion retargeting systems lack cross-platform generalization, necessitating extensive data collection and retraining for each new robotic platform.
- Traditional dexterous grasping methods, relying on fixed force thresholds or simple position control, are inadequate for safe and stable manipulation of objects with diverse properties (e.g., fragile, deformable, or slippery).
- Integrating accurate motion reproduction with adaptive and compliant grasping within a unified teleoperation framework remains a challenge.

## Method

- **DexTele** employs a vision-based motion retargeting module utilizing a dual-stream ==Spatial Attention Gated Graph Convolutional Network (SAG-GCN)== to map human movements to robot joint angles in a latent space.
- An ==adaptive grasping module== combines ==Vision-Language Model (VLM)== inference for semantic target force estimation with ==Model Predictive Control (MPC)==-based online optimization for dynamic force regulation.
- Human and robot skeletons are represented as weighted graphs, and the retargeting network uses ==Spatial Basic Blocks (SBB)== and ==Gated Residual Blocks (GRB)== to capture and fuse distinct arm and hand motions.

## Results

- The motion retargeting module consistently achieved lower errors across multiple robotic platforms (RMC-DA, YuMi, Unitree H1), yielding an MPJPE of **0.0882m** and a Quaternion Distance of **0.1550 rad** on the CSL-Daily dataset.
- The adaptive grasping module maintained force deviation and oscillation within **10%** for both rigid and deformable objects, and improved the average grasping success rate from **5.13** to **9.13** out of **10** trials.
- The system operates in real-time, achieving approximately **10 frames per second (FPS)** during teleoperation, with the retargeting module processing human pose in **0.02 seconds** per frame.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Formulating cross-platform motion retargeting as a graph-based cross-topology mapping problem using latent space optimization enables generalization across diverse robot kinematics without requiring paired human-robot datasets.
> - Integrating semantic understanding from a VLM allows the system to intelligently infer appropriate grasping forces based on object characteristics, moving beyond static force thresholds.
> - The combination of VLM-inferred target forces with MPC-based online optimization provides a robust framework for adaptive and compliant grasping, ensuring stable interaction with varied objects.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic teleoperation allows human operators to control robots remotely for complex tasks, with motion retargeting being a critical component for translating human movements to robotic platforms. Traditional motion retargeting systems often employ direct mappings or rely on paired human-robot datasets for training supervised models. While these approaches can achieve accuracy in specific scenarios, they typically exhibit limitations in cross-platform generalization. Expanding these methods to new robotic platforms often necessitates extensive data collection and retraining, thereby restricting their applicability and increasing deployment costs. This challenge is particularly pronounced when dealing with diverse robotic architectures, which can lead to inaccuracies and reduced reliability.

In the context of dexterous hand teleoperation, replicating human gestures accurately is essential, but equally important is the ability of the robot hand to adapt to the interaction characteristics of different objects. Existing hand control strategies frequently utilize position control or employ fixed force thresholds. These methods are generally sufficient for basic tasks but often lack the flexibility required for safe and stable manipulation of varied objects, such as those that are fragile, deformable, or prone to slippage. Such limitations can result in unstable grasps, object damage, or failure to perform the task effectively.

Previous research has explored various avenues to address these issues. For motion capture, methods range from high-accuracy but bulky and costly systems (e.g., VR headsets, data gloves, marker-based optical systems) to more accessible vision-based, non-contact approaches. While some studies have focused on kinematics-based arm retargeting for cross-platform adaptation, these often face limitations in high-fidelity reproduction. For dexterous grasping, recent advancements combine machine learning with model predictive control (MPC) to predict and regulate forces, often relying on offline training or specific sensor feedback. However, these methods sometimes lack intelligent perception of target forces or task-specific adaptability across a wide range of objects.

This work addresses the combined challenges of cross-platform motion retargeting and adaptive grasping within a unified teleoperation system. It aims to overcome the generalization deficiencies of existing retargeting methods by proposing a graph-based approach that does not require paired human-robot datasets for new platforms. Concurrently, it seeks to enhance dexterous grasping by moving beyond fixed-threshold control to an intelligent strategy that integrates semantic reasoning and dynamic optimization, thereby enabling compliant and adaptive manipulation of diverse objects. The proposed DexTele system positions itself as a development towards more versatile, robust, and user-friendly remote manipulation capabilities.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a dual-arm dexterous teleoperation system, termed DexTele, which can effectively address two critical challenges: achieving precise motion retargeting with cross-platform generalization and enabling compliant grasping through adaptive force control.

**Motivation for Cross-Platform Motion Retargeting:**
Existing motion retargeting systems often suffer from a lack of generalization across different robotic platforms. Many are designed for a specific robot architecture, requiring the collection of new paired human-robot datasets and retraining of supervised mapping models when a new robot is introduced. This process is time-consuming, resource-intensive, and limits the scalability and practicality of teleoperation systems. The heterogeneity in kinematic structures and degrees of freedom between human operators and various robots further complicates accurate and natural motion reproduction. The motivation is to overcome these constraints by developing a retargeting method that can generate accurate robot motions from human input without requiring platform-specific paired data, thereby enabling seamless deployment across a diverse range of robotic systems. The goal is to reformulate cross-platform retargeting as a graph-based cross-topology mapping problem, utilizing a latent space optimization that relies primarily on human motion data.

**Motivation for Adaptive Grasping:**
In dexterous teleoperation, the robot hand's ability to safely and stably interact with a wide variety of objects is paramount. Traditional grasping methods, which often employ simple position control or rely on fixed force thresholds, are inadequate for handling objects with diverse properties (e.g., varying materials, shapes, masses, and fragility). These methods can lead to issues such as crushing fragile objects, failing to secure slippery items, or exhibiting instability when external disturbances occur. The lack of adaptability to object characteristics compromises the reliability and safety of manipulation tasks. The motivation is to develop an intelligent grasping strategy that can dynamically adjust grasping forces based on object properties and real-time interaction, moving beyond static control schemes. This requires integrating semantic understanding of objects with dynamic force regulation to ensure stable, safe, and foresightful manipulation in complex, multi-object environments.

**Overall Motivation:**
The overarching motivation is to create a unified teleoperation system that is both practical and reliable. By combining a generalized motion retargeting module with an adaptive grasping module, the researchers aim to develop a system that not only accurately translates human intent to robot actions across multiple platforms but also enables the robot to interact compliantly and safely with a broad spectrum of objects. This addresses fundamental limitations in current teleoperation technologies, paving the way for more versatile and efficient remote manipulation capabilities in various applications.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

DexTele is a dual-arm dexterous teleoperation system that integrates a vision-based motion retargeting module with an adaptive grasping module. The system's workflow begins with motion capture, where FrankMocap is used to acquire 3D human arm and hand motion data from standard RGB camera images. This data is then separated into independent arm and hand motions, which are processed by the respective modules.

**A. Motion Retargeting Module:**
The motion retargeting module is designed to translate human arm and hand movements to robot joint angles, aiming for cross-platform generalization.

1. **Motion Retargeting Problem Statement:** The problem is formulated as a latent-space optimization task. An encoder maps a sequence of human skeletal graphs into a latent vector, and a decoder then produces robot joint angles. These angles are converted to a retargeted trajectory via forward kinematics. The objective is to minimize a composite retargeting loss function, subject to joint angle limits. The loss function includes terms for end-effector position, end-effector orientation, arm normal vector, dynamics, fingertip orientation, and finger angle, each weighted to ensure accuracy, naturalness, and physical plausibility.
2. **Motion Retargeting Architecture:** The core of the retargeting network is an end-to-end encoder-decoder architecture based on a Spatial Attention Gated Graph Convolutional Network (SAG-GCN).
 - **Dual-Stream Design:** The encoder and decoder both feature a dual-stream input-output design. The first two layers independently process arm and hand motions to account for their distinct scale differences (coarse-grained arm movements, fine-grained hand movements). A third layer is dedicated to fusing these features, coordinating arm-hand motions for enhanced accuracy.
 - **Graph Representation:** Both human and robot skeletons are represented as weighted graphs, Gk = (Vk, Ek, Wk), where Vk represents joint nodes (with features including position and quaternion rotation), Ek defines connectivity, and Wk is a dynamic attention matrix. Edge features encode local geometry.
 - **Normalization:** To address scale differences, human joint positions are normalized using their geometric center and global scale, while rotations remain unchanged for physical consistency.
 - **Spatial Basic Block (SBB):** This block functions as a feature extractor. It processes node features and edge attributes through concatenation and message propagation, efficiently encoding skeletal topology for real-time retargeting.
 - **Gated Residual Block (GRB):** This block incorporates an attention mechanism and a gated residual structure. Attention weights, derived from spatial-pose similarity, guide the aggregation of neighborhood information. A gating vector controls the fusion ratio between the aggregated message and residual features, enhancing feature selectivity, mitigating noise accumulation, and improving stability in deep feature propagation.

**B. Adaptive Grasping Module:**
This module enables compliant grasping by combining Vision-Language Model (VLM) inference with Model Predictive Control (MPC)-based force regulation.

1. **VLM-Driven Target Force Inference:**
 - When the dexterous hand initiates a grasp, an external camera captures an image of the robot's interaction with the object.
 - This image, along with pre-defined recognition commands, is fed into a VLM (specifically, the Doubao-seed-1-6-vision-250815 model).
 - The VLM performs rapid semantic interpretation, outputting the object category (e.g., "water bottle") and a recommended target grasping force (e.g., "300g").
 - This provides a knowledge-driven a priori estimate for the required force, circumventing the need for manual calibration or fixed thresholds and offering an initial quantitative reference for adaptive control.

2. **Joint Angle-Force Prediction Model Construction:**
 - To evaluate grasp performance during closed-loop optimization, a data-driven model is constructed to predict forces acting on the robotic hand from joint angle information.
 - A Random Forest Regressor is trained on historical motion-force data, where the input consists of concatenated commanded and actual joint angles, and the output is the predicted six-dimensional force.
 - This model serves as a differentiable surrogate for the MPC optimizer, enabling rapid force predictions and inference of force responses within the command space without explicit dynamic modeling or reliance on real-time force sensor readings during optimization.

3. **Adaptive Force Optimization Based on MPC Principles:**
 - During grasp execution, joint commands are continuously refined using an MPC-inspired online optimization module, integrating the force prediction model into a gradient-based iterative loop.
 - At each control cycle, the optimization problem minimizes the squared difference between the predicted force (from the trained model using current joint angles) and the VLM-inferred target force. A regularization term penalizes large variations from the previous joint command, balancing responsiveness and smoothness.
 - The joint angles are treated as differentiable variables and updated through gradient descent using the Adam optimizer.
 - This rolling optimization approach ensures smooth convergence of the output force to the target value, preserving motion continuity while adaptively compensating for force deviations to achieve stable and controllable grasping.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents extensive experiments to validate DexTele's capabilities in motion retargeting and adaptive grasping across multiple robotic platforms.

**A. Motion Retargeting Performance:**

1. **Experimental Setup:**
 - **Robots:** RMC-DA (physical and virtual), YuMi (virtual), and Unitree H1 (virtual). These robots feature varying degrees of freedom and dexterous hands.
 - **Datasets:** Sign (high-quality human pose) and a custom dataset derived from CSL-Daily (complex upper-body movements), processed by FrankMocap for 3D human poses.
 - **Training:** PyTorch Geometric with Adam optimizer.

2. **Arm Motion Retargeting:**
 - **Metrics:** Mean Per Joint Position Error (MPJPE), Quaternion Distance (Quat), Velocity Error (VE), and Acceleration Error (AE).
 - **Comparison:** Evaluated against NLO, VMR, and ATP baselines on both Sign and CSL-Daily datasets.
 - **Results:** The proposed method consistently achieved lower errors across all arm metrics on both datasets. For instance, on the CSL-Daily dataset, it yielded MPJPE of 0.0882m, Quat of 0.1550 rad, VE of 0.0388 m/s, and AE of 0.9421 m/s², outperforming all baselines, indicating higher positional accuracy, improved rotational consistency, and smoother motion dynamics.

3. **Hand Motion Retargeting:**
 - **Metrics:** Finger joint angle errors (Fin Angle), computed using the three-point method.
 - **Comparison:** Same datasets and baselines as arm motion.
 - **Results:** The proposed method demonstrated the lowest finger joint angle errors across all fingers and datasets (e.g., 0.1495 rad average on CSL-Daily dataset), confirming precise and consistent retargeting of diverse hand motions.

**B. Ablation Experiments on Motion Retargeting:**
An ablation study on the CSL-Daily dataset investigated the contributions of the dual-stream architecture and its components.
- **Single Graph:** Using a single-stream architecture increased MPJPE to 0.1333m, Quat to 0.1821 rad, and Fin Angle to 0.1947 rad, indicating interference between local and global features.
- **Without GRB:** Removal of the Gated Residual Block led to MPJPE of 0.1513m, Quat of 0.1939 rad, and Fin Angle of 0.2020 rad, suggesting weakened feature fusion and attention.
- **Without SBB:** Replacing the Spatial Basic Block with a linear structure resulted in MPJPE of 0.1566m, Quat of 0.2035 rad, and Fin Angle of 0.2239 rad, highlighting the importance of SBB for modeling nonlinear dependencies.
- **Without Hand/Arm Stream:** Retaining only one stream reduced accuracy in overall coordination, demonstrating the necessity of separately modeling and integrating arm and hand motions.
These results underscore the importance of the dual-stream architecture, SBB, and GRB for coherent and natural motion retargeting.

**C. Multi-Robot Motion Retargeting Experiments:**
The cross-platform generalization capability was evaluated on YuMi, Unitree H1, and RMC-DA.
- **Visualization:** Figure 6 visually demonstrated consistent human motion reproduction quality across all three platforms in both simulated and real environments.
- **Quantitative Evaluation:** All platforms exhibited low positional (MPJPE ranging from 0.0877m to 0.1024m) and rotational errors (Quat ranging from 0.1422 rad to 0.1735 rad), along with smooth motion dynamics (low velocity and acceleration errors).
- **Real-time Performance:** The system achieved approximately 10 frames per second (FPS) during teleoperation, with FrankMocap processing human pose in 0.08s and the retargeting module in 0.02s per frame. These findings confirm the robustness and applicability of the proposed method across multiple robot platforms in real-time.

**D. Adaptive Grasping Performance Evaluation:**

1. **Adaptive Grasping Performance:**
 - **VLM:** The Doubao-seed-1-6-vision-250815 model had an average inference latency of 0.2–0.3s.
 - **Object Grasping:** The system was validated on eight daily objects with varying properties (e.g., water bottle, beer cans, paper box, sponge block).
 - **Results:** The VLM accurately inferred target grasping forces for each object. During execution, both force deviation and oscillation remained within 10% for both rigid and deformable objects. All objects were grasped securely without slippage or damage.
 - **Force Curve Analysis (Water Bottle):** Visualization showed that with the adaptive module, individual finger forces converged smoothly to the target force, unlike the uncontrolled forces observed without the module. This indicates precise, low-oscillation, and safe compliant grasping.

2. **Grasping Success Rate Evaluation:**
 - The Adaptive Grasping Module (AGM) was evaluated against a baseline without AGM across eight object categories over 10 trials each.
 - **Results:** Incorporating the AGM substantially improved the average grasping success rate from 5.13 to 9.13 out of 10 trials. For deformable objects (e.g., paper cups, plush toys), adaptive force regulation prevented excessive compression. For slippable items (e.g., plastic mangoes, sponge blocks), rapid adjustment reduced slippage. This demonstrated enhanced grasp stability and safety.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DexTele system represents a contribution to the field of robotic teleoperation by addressing two fundamental challenges: cross-platform generalization in motion retargeting and adaptive force control for dexterous grasping.

**Significance of Motion Retargeting:**
The proposed vision-based motion retargeting module, utilizing SAG-GCN with a dual-stream architecture and latent optimization, significantly enhances the versatility of teleoperation systems. By effectively modeling human-robot topology and processing arm and hand motions independently before fusion, it enables precise motion retargeting across diverse robot platforms without the need for platform-specific paired training datasets. This reduces the barriers to deploying teleoperation systems on new hardware, as it primarily relies on human motion data for training. The vision-based, non-contact approach further contributes to user comfort and reduces hardware costs compared to traditional marker-based or haptic-glove systems. This generalization capability is crucial for expanding the applicability of teleoperation in various dynamic and multi-robot environments.

**Significance of Adaptive Grasping:**
The adaptive grasping module integrates semantic understanding from Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with dynamic force control via Model Predictive Control (MPC). This approach allows the system to intelligently infer appropriate grasping forces for target objects based on their visual properties and semantically relevant information. Coupled with gradient-based online optimization, it facilitates compliant, stable, and safe grasping of a wide range of objects, including those that are fragile, deformable, or prone to slippage. This moves beyond the limitations of fixed-threshold or position-controlled grasping, which often fail in complex manipulation scenarios. The ability to adapt force dynamically based on real-time feedback and object characteristics is essential for robust manipulation tasks that mimic human dexterity.

**Potential Impact:**
The integrated DexTele system has the potential for impact across several domains:
- **Industrial Automation:** Remote operation of complex assembly or maintenance tasks in hazardous or inaccessible environments, improving worker safety and operational efficiency.
- **Healthcare:** Development of more dexterous and intuitive remote surgical robots or rehabilitation assistance systems, enabling precise manipulation in delicate procedures or personalized therapeutic interventions.
- **Logistics and Service Robotics:** Enhanced capability for handling diverse items in warehouses, retail, or home settings, leading to more flexible and robust automation solutions.
- **Research and Development:** Provides a robust and generalizable framework for further research in human-robot interaction, compliant manipulation, and embodied AI, serving as a platform for developing and testing new algorithms for intelligent robotic control.

While the paper acknowledges current limitations, such as constraints from pose estimation algorithms and the focus on upper-body motion, the demonstrated cross-platform generalization and adaptive grasping capabilities represent a step towards more practical and reliable robotic teleoperation systems. Future advancements in these areas could further extend the system's utility and broaden its impact.
