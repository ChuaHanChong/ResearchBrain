# VisRL: Intention-Driven Visual Perception via Reinforced Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> VisRL introduces a reinforced reasoning framework that enables large multimodal models (LMMs) to perform intention-driven visual perception by learning to focus on relevant image regions using only task-level rewards, significantly reducing dependence on costly intermediate bounding box annotations. The framework shows strong performance across diverse benchmarks, often outperforming methods trained with more supervised data.

## Problem

- Traditional visual perception systems process entire scenes uniformly, lacking the adaptive, intention-driven focus observed in human perception.
- Large Multimodal Models (LMMs) often exhibit "hallucination" and lack explicit mechanisms for dynamically focusing on specific image regions based on a user's intent.
- Existing Visual Chain-of-Thought (CoT) methods require extensive, densely annotated bounding boxes for intermediate reasoning steps, creating a prohibitive annotation bottleneck that limits scalability and generalization.

## Method

- VisRL introduces a reinforcement learning framework using ==Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)== to train large multimodal models (LMMs) for intention-driven visual perception.
- The approach models visual reasoning as a two-step sequence: generating a ==bounding box for focus==, then generating a response from the cropped region and original input.
- It employs an ==iterative, self-evolutionary DPO process== that begins with a small supervised fine-tuning warm-up and then primarily uses a larger dataset of unannotated image-question-answer pairs, applying task-level reward signals.

## Results

- VisRL consistently achieved strong performance on comprehensive benchmarks (MME, MMBench, POPE), outperforming or being comparable to state-of-the-art methods while using significantly less densely-labeled data (30k vs 376k+).
- The framework showed substantial average accuracy gains on Visual Chain-of-Thought benchmarks, ranging from **23.78%** to **49.07%** across **11** datasets and various LMM architectures compared to base models.
- VisRL improved intermediate bounding box prediction accuracy, achieving 0.437 Top-1 Accuracy@0.5 IoU on LLaVA-1.5-7B, surpassing VisCoT (0.378) despite VisCoT using substantially more bounding box-annotated data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Large Multimodal Models can learn to perform intention-driven visual perception, including precise region focusing, primarily from task-level rewards, significantly reducing the need for dense intermediate bounding box annotations.
> - An iterative, self-evolutionary Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) framework effectively generates high-quality preference data and progressively refines the model's visual reasoning capabilities.
> - A two-stage reinforcement learning process, first optimizing bounding box generation (RL1) and then jointly optimizing both bounding box and response (RL2), is beneficial for robust visual Chain-of-Thought reasoning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Visual understanding is a cornerstone of artificial intelligence, enabling machines to interpret and interact with the visual world. Traditionally, visual perception systems processed entire scenes uniformly, without considering the specific intent or goal of a user. However, human perception is inherently "intention-driven," meaning humans selectively focus on different parts of a scene based on their current objectives. For example, a person looking for a remote in a room will focus on different areas (tables, couches) than someone looking for a clock (walls). This adaptive focus is crucial for efficient and context-aware understanding.

The advent of Large Multimodal Models (LMMs), such as LLaVA and Qwen-VL, has significantly advanced the field by integrating visual and language modalities. These models can encode visual signals into tokens and process them alongside text tokens to generate responses, enabling powerful capabilities in various tasks. However, despite their impressive performance, current LMMs often suffer from limitations like "hallucination" (generating factually incorrect or non-existent information) and lack explicit mechanisms for intention-driven visual perception. They typically operate in a "one-pass end-to-end" manner, which might not fully capture the sequential, iterative nature of human visual reasoning.

To address these limitations, recent research has explored "Visual Chain-of-Thought" (Visual CoT) frameworks. Inspired by Chain-of-Thought reasoning in large language models, these methods introduce explicit, interpretable reasoning steps. A common approach involves the model first predicting a "focus region" (e.g., a bounding box) based on a query, then cropping the image to this region, and finally using the cropped input, along with the original image and query, to derive the final answer. This paradigm improves interpretability and leverages the multi-turn capabilities of LMMs.

However, a significant bottleneck for existing Visual CoT methods is their heavy reliance on supervised training with densely annotated intermediate reasoning steps, specifically bounding boxes. Generating these precise annotations for every possible intention-region pair is combinatorially complex and prohibitively expensive, severely limiting the scalability and generalizability of these models. Some approaches attempt to circumvent this by incorporating external tools (e.g., specialized detection models) or knowledge bases, but these methods don't enhance the intrinsic visual reasoning capabilities of the LMMs themselves. Furthermore, while some recent works have explored Reinforcement Learning (RL) for bounding box generation, they often still rely on ground truth bounding boxes to provide reward signals, thus not fully escaping the annotation dependency.

VisRL directly tackles this core challenge. It proposes a novel approach that leverages Reinforcement Learning to optimize the entire visual reasoning process using *only* reward signals derived from task success or failure, without requiring explicit bounding box annotations during its primary training phase. This positions VisRL as a significant step towards more scalable, flexible, and human-like intention-driven visual perception, moving beyond the limitations of dense supervised annotation and external dependencies.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of VisRL is to enable large multimodal models (LMMs) to perform intention-driven visual perception in a scalable and human-like manner, significantly reducing the reliance on costly, dense annotations for intermediate reasoning steps.

The key motivations driving this research are:

- **Emulating Human Perception:** Human visual understanding is inherently adaptive and goal-oriented. People selectively focus on relevant areas of a scene based on their immediate intentions. The authors aim to imbue LMMs with a similar adaptive focus, allowing them to dynamically determine the most pertinent regions of an image given a specific query or task.
- **Addressing Annotation Bottleneck:** Current state-of-the-art visual reasoning frameworks, particularly those employing "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) strategies like Visual CoT, rely heavily on supervised learning with explicit annotations for intermediate steps (e.g., bounding boxes for focused regions). This poses a severe scalability challenge because the number of possible intention-region pairs grows combinatorially, making comprehensive annotation practically impossible. VisRL seeks to overcome this by eliminating the need for such dense, manual annotations.
- **Promoting Scalability and Generalization:** By moving beyond a supervised annotation paradigm, the authors aim to develop a framework that can learn from more readily available, large-scale, unannotated data (simple image-question-answer pairs). This would enable models to generalize more effectively across diverse tasks and out-of-distribution scenarios, as they learn to reason about visual focus intrinsically rather than memorizing specific annotated regions.
- **Fostering Intrinsic Reasoning:** Unlike methods that rely on external tools or models to perform sub-tasks (like object detection), VisRL aims to enhance the inherent capabilities of the LMM itself to perform intention-driven focus selection and subsequent reasoning. This allows the model to learn a more unified and self-contained reasoning process.
- **Aligning with Human Learning:** Humans learn to perceive and focus not through meticulously labeled datasets but through trial-and-error interactions with their environment, gradually refining their ability to adaptively select relevant information. VisRL's reinforcement learning approach, driven by task rewards, mirrors this more natural learning process.

In essence, VisRL is motivated by the desire to make LMMs smarter, more adaptable, and more efficient in visual reasoning by enabling them to learn how and where to focus their attention based on context and feedback, rather than explicit, pre-defined instructions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VisRL proposes a novel reinforcement learning (RL) framework to optimize intention-driven visual perception in large multimodal models, primarily addressing the challenge of extensive bounding box annotations. The core idea is to train the model using task-level reward signals rather than direct supervision on intermediate steps.

#### 3.1. Preliminary: Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)

VisRL leverages Direct Preference Optimization (DPO), an RL algorithm that simplifies the training pipeline compared to traditional RL methods requiring separate reward model training and policy model training. DPO directly optimizes the policy model using pairwise preference data. Given an input and two outputs (one preferred, one undesirable), DPO aims to maximize the probability of the preferred output while minimizing that of the undesirable one, using a straightforward objective function that avoids the complexities of sampling from an RL environment or training a separate reward function. This simplicity makes DPO an attractive choice for optimizing complex generative processes like visual reasoning.

#### 3.2. Method Overview and Iterative Process

The visual reasoning process in VisRL is framed as a two-step sequence:
1. **Focus Selection:** Based on a given query (Q) and original image (I), the model generates a bounding box (B) representing the critical focus area.
2. **Response Generation:** The cropped image region (I_s) corresponding to the bounding box, along with the original image and question, is fed into the multimodal model to produce the final response (R).

VisRL adopts an iterative DPO framework. It starts with an initial model (M_0_RL) and then cycles through data generation and model optimization phases. In each iteration `k`, the improved model (M_k_RL) is used to collect better data, and this better data, in turn, further refines the model, leading to a self-evolutionary learning process.

#### 3.3. Data Generation Pipeline

The crucial aspect of VisRL is its data generation pipeline, which enables reward-driven learning without dense bounding box annotations.

- **SFT Warm-up:** Before the RL training, a small amount of data (30,000 samples, much less than typical CoT datasets) from the VisCoT dataset is used for Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT). The primary purpose of this warm-up is not to teach complex reasoning, but merely to enable the model to *output bounding boxes in the required format*. This initial step ensures the model has the basic structural capability to generate the intermediate reasoning step.
- **RL Data Source:** For the main RL training phase, VisRL utilizes a larger set of 180,000 simple image-question-answer pairs from nine diverse datasets, which *do not contain any bounding box annotations*.
- **Self-Evolution (Actor-Critic Approach):** The data generation process itself is designed as a self-evolutionary loop, using different versions of the model as an "actor" and a "critic":
 - **Sample Generation (Actor):** For each (Q, I) pair, the *currently updated model* (M_k_RL, starting with M_SFT) samples two distinct bounding boxes, B1 and B2, to represent different reasoning paths.
 - **Diversity Controller:** To ensure exploration and avoid redundant or highly similar bounding boxes, a diversity controller is introduced. If the Intersection over Union (IoU) between B1 and B2 exceeds a threshold (T), B2 is re-sampled from a region outside B1, with a similar area, promoting wider exploration of potential focus regions.
 - **Rewarding (Critic):** The *original model* (M_org, the model before any SFT or RL training) acts as a critic. It scores both the generated bounding boxes (s_b) and the final responses (s_r) based on the ground truth answer, providing a reward signal for each reasoning path. This uses the inherent capability of the base LMM to judge correctness without requiring explicit bbox labels.
 - **Data Filtering:** After generating multiple candidate paths (2N per (Q,I) pair), a filtering mechanism is applied. It selects "win" and "lose" paths based on predefined score thresholds (T_b_max, T_r_max for win; T_b_min, T_r_min for lose). This filtering ensures that only questions of appropriate difficulty (where both good and bad paths can be distinguished) are included in the preference dataset, thus improving learning efficiency. The most representative win and lose paths (those with maximum/minimum scores for both bbox and response) are then selected to form the preference pairs for DPO.

#### 3.4. Reinforced Visual Reasoning (Step-Level DPO)

VisRL employs a two-stage, step-level DPO strategy to optimize the visual reasoning process:

- **Stage 1: Bounding Box Optimization (RL1):** In this stage, the model is primarily optimized to generate accurate bounding boxes. Given an input (Q, I), the DPO objective focuses on maximizing the probability of the preferred bounding box (B_win) and minimizing that of the undesirable one (B_lose), based on the scores derived during data generation. This stage refines the model's ability to localize relevant visual information.
- **Stage 2: Joint Bounding Box and Response Optimization (RL2):** After Stage 1, the model (now π_ˆθ) has improved its bounding box prediction. Stage 2 then jointly optimizes both the bounding box generation and the final response generation. The DPO objective considers the entire CoT sequence: given the question, original image, and the preferred cropped sub-image (I_s_win), it maximizes the probability of the preferred final response (R_win) while penalizing the undesirable one (R_lose). This ensures that the model learns to generate both the correct focus region and the correct answer based on that focus.

By iteratively generating preference data and applying this two-stage DPO, VisRL enables the LMM to learn intention-driven visual perception from task-level feedback, effectively bypassing the need for extensive, hand-annotated intermediate bounding boxes for the bulk of its training.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

VisRL demonstrates significant improvements across various benchmarks and LMMs, showcasing its effectiveness in enabling intention-driven visual perception with reduced annotation dependency.

#### 4.1. Comprehensive Benchmark Performance

Evaluations on comprehensive benchmarks like MME, MMBench, and POPE (for hallucination detection) consistently show VisRL outperforming or achieving second-best performance against state-of-the-art baselines.
- VisRL achieves these strong results while utilizing significantly less densely-labeled data (30k samples with bounding boxes for initial SFT warm-up, plus 180k unannotated QA pairs for RL) compared to supervised methods like VisCoT (which uses 376k dense-labeled samples).
- For instance, when based on LLaVA-1.5 with CLIP-ViT-L-14-336, VisRL outperforms VisCoT by 5.00% on MME and 1.74% on POPE, highlighting its superior generalization and hallucination reduction capabilities.
- The results also demonstrate VisRL's broad applicability, generalizing effectively across different base LMMs (e.g., LLaVA, LLaVA-NeXT).

#### 4.2. Performance on Visual Chain-of-Thought Benchmarks

On the Visual CoT benchmark, which specifically assesses the model's ability to focus on critical regions and perform multi-step reasoning, VisRL shows substantial gains across 11 diverse datasets (e.g., DocVQA, TextVQA, Flickr30k, CUB) and various LMM architectures (LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, Llama-3.2-Vision, MiniCPM-o, PaliGemma2, Yi-VL, Qwen2.5-VL).
- The paper notes that even supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with bounding box annotations can sometimes lead to performance decline on certain datasets, suggesting that data-driven SFT alone may struggle with generalization.
- In contrast, VisRL's RL phases (RL1 for bounding box optimization and RL2 for joint optimization) consistently yield comprehensive enhancements. Improvements range from a minimum of 23.78% (for Llama-3.2-Vision-11B) to a remarkable 49.07% (for PaliGemma2-10B) in average accuracy compared to their respective base models without CoT. This underscores the power of reward-driven learning in acquiring essential visual perception skills.

#### 4.3. Enhanced Detection Ability

Beyond overall task performance, VisRL significantly improves the accuracy of intermediate bounding box predictions, which is crucial for CoT reasoning.
- Measured by Top-1 Accuracy@0.5 IoU (Intersection over Union), VisRL (SFT+RL1+RL2) on LLaVA-1.5-7B achieved a 0.437 average accuracy, notably outperforming VisCoT (0.378), despite VisCoT using significantly more (438k vs 30k) bounding box annotated data for its training.
- This improvement extends even to datasets not directly included in the RL training phase, such as DocVQA, where RL1 alone boosted accuracy by 39.71%, and RL2 further improved it by 73.68%. This robust generalization capability is a key strength.

#### 4.4. Performance Across Multiple RL Iterations

The iterative nature of VisRL's training process demonstrates continuous improvement.
- Performance (average accuracy across 11 datasets) consistently increases with each subsequent iteration of data generation and VisRL training.
- For example, LLaVA-1.5-7B, after four iterations of VisRL, even surpassed the performance of the larger Llama-3.2-Vision-11B model, highlighting the potential for substantial capability gains through this self-evolutionary learning approach. This suggests that the quality of self-generated preference data and the model's capabilities are intertwined and improve synergistically.

#### 4.5. Qualitative Results (Visualization)

Visualizations confirm that VisRL produces more accurate bounding box predictions and better final responses compared to baselines like VisCoT. The green bounding boxes (VisRL) consistently align better with the ground truth (blue) and lead to more precise answers than the red bounding boxes (VisCoT), even for complex or fine-grained visual queries.

#### 4.6. Ablation Studies

Ablation studies provided crucial insights into the effectiveness of different components of VisRL:
- **Importance of Two-Stage RL:** Training with RL2-Only (joint optimization without prior RL1 bounding box optimization) showed promising results compared to SFT, but still resulted in performance drops on certain benchmarks compared to the original model. This confirms the necessity of Stage 1 (RL1) to independently optimize bounding box detection before joint optimization in Stage 2 (RL2), indicating a staged learning process is beneficial for complex multimodal tasks.
- **Ineffectiveness of Direct Response Fitting:** Attempts to directly optimize the final response (without CoT) using traditional SFT, DPO, KTO, or PPO methods proved less effective, often leading to performance degradation on some benchmarks. This validates VisRL's core hypothesis that incorporating explicit visual CoT reasoning steps, optimized via RL, is superior for intention-driven visual perception.
- **Effectiveness of Data Generation Pipeline:**
 - The self-evolutionary data generation pipeline, using the SFT-trained model as an actor and the original base model as a critic, was found to be highly effective. It maximizes the proportion of "win-positive, lose-negative" preference pairs (64.64%), which are ideal for DPO training.
 - Successive iterations of VisRL training further increased the quality of generated data and the number of valid preference pairs, demonstrating the positive feedback loop.
 - Replacing the internal critic with GPT-4o was costly without significant quality gains. Using the SFTed model itself as a critic led to a drastic reduction in data volume and quality.
 - Crucially, omitting the bounding box critics or the diversity controller from the data generation pipeline significantly reduced the proportion of useful preference pairs, underlining their importance for creating high-quality, diverse training data.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

VisRL introduces a paradigm shift in training large multimodal models for intention-driven visual perception, moving away from a reliance on expensive, densely annotated data towards a more scalable and human-like learning approach. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Overcoming the Annotation Bottleneck:** This is VisRL's most significant contribution. By demonstrating that LMMs can learn sophisticated visual reasoning, including precise region focusing, primarily from task-level rewards (i.e., correct final answers) without requiring explicit bounding box annotations for the majority of the training data, VisRL opens the door to leveraging vast amounts of readily available, unannotated image-question-answer pairs. This dramatically lowers the data annotation cost and effort, making advanced multimodal AI more accessible and scalable.
- **Enhanced Generalization and Robustness:** The reward-driven learning, especially through the iterative self-evolution process, enables VisRL to acquire more fundamental and generalizable visual perception capabilities. The consistent outperformance across diverse benchmarks, including out-of-distribution datasets and hallucination detection, indicates that the model learns robust reasoning strategies rather than merely memorizing annotated patterns. This leads to more reliable and trustworthy LMMs.
- **More Human-like Learning:** The reinforcement learning paradigm, akin to learning through trial-and-error, aligns more closely with how humans develop perceptual skills. This approach could inspire future research into more biologically plausible and efficient AI learning mechanisms for complex tasks.
- **Intrinsic Model Capability Enhancement:** Unlike approaches that rely on external tools or specialized models, VisRL focuses on improving the intrinsic visual reasoning capabilities of the LMM itself. This leads to a more unified and coherent model that can perform intention-driven perception without external dependencies, potentially simplifying deployment and improving efficiency.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** VisRL establishes a solid foundation for further exploration in several directions:
 - **Online Reinforcement Learning:** The iterative self-improvement loop suggests potential for online learning systems that continuously refine their visual perception abilities.
 - **More Complex Reasoning:** The step-level DPO framework could be extended to optimize even more intricate multi-step reasoning processes, incorporating diverse intermediate modalities (e.g., masks, spatial coordinates, textual thoughts).
 - **Resource-Efficient Training:** By drastically reducing the need for human-annotated data, VisRL contributes to making the development of powerful multimodal AI models more resource-efficient, promoting broader participation in the field.
- **Improved Interpretability (Retained CoT Benefit):** While moving away from supervised bounding box training, VisRL retains the "Chain-of-Thought" paradigm, meaning the model still explicitly outputs its focus region. This preserves the interpretability benefits of CoT, allowing users to understand *why* the model arrived at a particular answer by inspecting its intermediate visual reasoning steps.

In conclusion, VisRL represents a significant leap towards developing more intelligent, scalable, and human-like large multimodal models capable of sophisticated intention-driven visual perception, fundamentally altering the reliance on costly, dense annotations in the field.
