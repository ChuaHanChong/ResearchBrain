# Continual Hand-Eye Calibration for Open-world Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework for continual hand-eye calibration allows robotic systems to sequentially learn new manipulation scenes while retaining accurate calibration knowledge from previously encountered environments. This approach achieved an average accuracy of 98.4% and a total forgetting rate of 1.6% on a simulated robotic manipulation dataset, enabling robots to maintain precise spatial understanding across diverse scenes.

## Problem

- Deep learning models for hand-eye calibration suffer from catastrophic forgetting when adapting to new, open-world scenes, losing acquired knowledge of previous environments.
- Existing continual learning methods for visual localization often create spatially redundant replay buffers or fail to preserve fine-grained pose precision, leading to either 'Open-world Scene Forgetting' or 'Pose Precision Degradation'.
- The inability to maintain accurate calibration across diverse, continuously changing environments hinders the practical deployment and autonomy of robotic manipulation systems.

## Method

- A ==Spatial-Aware Replay Strategy (SARS)== uses a ==hybrid distance metric== and ==online Poisson disk sampling== with ==density-based replacement== to create a geometrically diverse and maximally informative replay buffer.
- A ==Structure-Preserving Dual Distillation (SPDD)== mechanism decomposes localization knowledge into a ==coarse topological prior== and ==fine metric offset== components for separate distillation from a teacher model.
- The framework integrates **SARS** and **SPDD** with a ==Scene Coordinate Regression (GLACE)== backbone, sequentially training on new scene data while regularizing with replay samples and distilled knowledge from past scenes.

## Results

- The proposed method consistently demonstrated superior localization accuracy and significantly reduced total forgetting rates compared to existing continual learning baselines across i7Scenes, i12Scenes, and a simulated robotic manipulation dataset.
- On i7Scenes, it achieved **74.9%** average accuracy with a **6.65** TFR, and on i12Scenes, it reached **91.8%** average accuracy with an **8.1** TFR, outperforming baselines like Buff-CS and GDR.
- For robotic manipulation, the framework attained **98.4%** average accuracy under **1 cm/1°** criteria and a **1.6%** TFR on the Sim dataset, validating its effectiveness for precision-demanding tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed that SARS provided substantial accuracy gains (up to **24.9%** on i12Scenes), and SPDD further enhanced performance (additional **2.9%** on i7Scenes), highlighting the necessity of both components.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Geometrically diverse and representative replay samples, selected based on joint position and orientation, are essential for effectively combating catastrophic forgetting in continual visual localization tasks.
> - Decomposing and separately preserving coarse scene layout and fine pose precision through a dual distillation strategy improves knowledge retention over monolithic distillation methods.
> - Combining a spatial-aware replay buffer with structured knowledge distillation enables robust continual adaptation, allowing a single model to accumulate multi-scene calibration capability without losing past expertise.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic manipulation in open-world environments necessitates precise spatial understanding, which is frequently facilitated by visual localization-based hand-eye calibration. This process involves estimating the rigid transformation between a camera and a robot, allowing for accurate mapping of visual observations to robot actions. Traditional methods for hand-eye calibration often rely on closed-form solutions or nonlinear optimization, while more recent learning-based approaches, such as those utilizing Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) for keypoint detection or differentiable rendering, directly estimate camera poses from images.

A prominent category of learning-based visual localization methods is Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR). Models like DSAC, ACE, and GLACE implicitly encode scene geometry into neural networks to achieve accurate camera pose estimation within a fixed environment. However, a significant limitation of these methods is their susceptibility to catastrophic forgetting when adapted to new, continuously changing "open-world" scenes without complete retraining. This issue restricts their practical deployment in dynamic robotic applications where environments are not static.

The field of continual learning (CL) addresses the problem of catastrophic forgetting, enabling models to acquire knowledge from sequential data streams while retaining performance on previously learned tasks. Existing CL strategies typically fall into replay-based, regularization-based, or parameter-isolation categories. While some CL methods have been applied to visual localization, their efficacy in the context of hand-eye calibration for diverse, open-world scenes remains an area of active investigation. For instance, Buff-CS introduced a replay buffer strategy targeting scene coverage, but it relies on computationally intensive 3D pseudo-labels. Another approach by Bahadir et al. formulated continual hand-eye calibration by partitioning a single scene into robot-centered regions, which primarily addresses viewpoint changes within a fixed environment rather than adaptation across distinct, open-world scenes.

This research paper positions itself within this landscape by recognizing the gap in robust continual learning solutions for visual localization-based hand-eye calibration that can handle sequential adaptation across fundamentally different open-world scenes. It aims to develop a unified framework that overcomes catastrophic forgetting while preserving both coarse scene-level understanding and fine-grained pose precision, aspects critical for practical robotic manipulation. The work builds upon the foundation of SCR methods (specifically GLACE) and integrates novel continual learning components tailored to the geometric nature of the task.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a continual hand-eye calibration framework that enables a single robotic calibration model to sequentially learn and adapt to new, open-world manipulation scenes without experiencing catastrophic forgetting of previously encountered environments. This capability is intended to support persistent robotic manipulation tasks without requiring manual recalibration whenever the robot moves to a new setting or revisits an old one.

The motivation for this research stems from the practical limitations of current deep learning-based hand-eye calibration models when deployed in dynamic, real-world scenarios. The authors identify two specific, coupled challenges that hinder effective continual adaptation:

- **Open-world Scene Forgetting:** Deep learning models, after training on a new scene (e.g., a bedroom), tend to forget the calibration knowledge acquired from prior scenes (e.g., a kitchen). This phenomenon leads to a loss of coarse scene-level or region-level geometric association, making the robot unable to accurately localize itself if it returns to a previously learned environment. Existing replay-based continual learning strategies often suffer from constructing replay buffers that are spatially redundant or fail to comprehensively cover the pose space of past scenes. This can result in the model remembering only a narrow subset of viewpoints while forgetting under-represented regions, thus failing to meet the requirements of open-world applications where robots must operate in diverse and changing environments.

- **Pose Precision Degradation:** Even if a model retains a coarse awareness of a scene, it may lose the fine-grained precision necessary for accurate camera pose estimation. This can manifest as the model correctly identifying a "kitchen scene" but estimating the camera pose inaccurately within that scene, potentially confusing coordinate systems or leading to large translation and rotation errors. Traditional knowledge distillation methods, which often treat the model's prediction as a monolithic output, may not adequately preserve these distinct levels of geometric knowledge (coarse scene layout vs. fine pose details). This degradation in precision is critical in robotic manipulation, where sub-centimeter and sub-degree accuracy can be essential for task success.

To address these challenges, the research is motivated to propose a framework that incorporates:
1. A method for constructing a geometrically uniform and maximally informative replay buffer.
2. A mechanism for knowledge distillation that explicitly decomposes and preserves both coarse topological scene layout and fine metric pose precision during continual learning.

The overarching goal is to enable robust and autonomous robotic operation in continuously evolving environments by ensuring that a robot's hand-eye calibration remains accurate across all encountered scenes, past and present.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed continual hand-eye calibration framework integrates two main components: a Spatial-Aware Replay Strategy (SARS) and a Structure-Preserving Dual Distillation (SPDD) mechanism. The framework builds upon a Scene Coordinate Regression (SCR) backbone, specifically GLACE, which estimates camera poses from images.

**A. Problem Definition**
The problem is defined as sequential camera pose estimation, where the camera pose (translation `p` and orientation `q`) is estimated with respect to a unified robot base frame. An open-world deployment is modeled as a sequence of scene tasks `T = {T1, T2..., TN}`. During training for the `i`-th scene, the model accesses the current dataset `Di` and a replay memory `M` of samples from prior scenes. The objective is to minimize the average pose estimation error across all observed scenes, balancing plasticity (adaptation to new scenes) and stability (retention of old knowledge). The authors identify "Open-world Scene Forgetting" (loss of coarse geometric association) and "Pose Precision Degradation" (loss of fine-grained pose accuracy) as the two primary failure modes.

**B. Spatial-Aware Replay Strategy (SARS)**
To combat "Open-world Scene Forgetting" caused by redundant samples in conventional replay buffers, SARS constructs a geometrically diverse replay buffer.
- **Hybrid Distance Metric:** SARS defines a distance `D(Sa, Sb)` between two samples based on their camera positions (`∥p_a - p_b∥₂`) and orientations (`λ ⋅ Ang(q_a, q_b)`), where `Ang` is the geodesic angular difference between quaternions and `λ` is a weighting factor. This ensures that samples are considered close only if they have similar positions and viewing directions.
- **Online Poisson Disk Sampling:** For each new incoming training sample `S_new`, its minimum distance `d_min` to all existing samples in the buffer `M` is calculated. If `d_min` is less than a predefined exclusion radius `r`, `S_new` is considered redundant and rejected. If `d_min ≥ r`, the sample occupies a new region of the pose space and is accepted.
- **Density-Based Replacement:** If the buffer reaches its capacity `N_b`, instead of random eviction, SARS removes the sample `S_rm` from the densest local neighborhood (i.e., the sample that has the smallest minimum distance to any other sample in the buffer). This strategy ensures that the buffer maintains a maximally dispersed distribution of camera poses, maximizing geometric coverage under a fixed memory budget.

**C. Structure-Preserving Dual Distillation (SPDD)**
To address "Pose Precision Degradation," SPDD decomposes the localization knowledge into distinct components for separate distillation. This approach is based on a GLACE-inspired cluster-center parameterization.
- **Localization Output Decomposition:** The underlying GLACE model outputs a logit vector `z` (encoding coarse probabilistic assignment over fixed cluster centers `C`) and an offset tensor `d` with associated scale weights `w` (for fine refinement). The final 3D scene coordinate prediction `x̂` is a combination of these.
- **Active Centers:** To prevent distilling noise from geometrically irrelevant cluster centers, distillation is restricted to a set `A` of `M_a` "active" centers, which are the top `M_a` centers ranked by the teacher model's activation probability.
- **Topological Prior Distillation (`L_prior`):** This loss preserves the coarse scene layout. It applies KL divergence between the re-normalized softmax distributions (over active centers `A`) of the teacher and student models. A temperature `τ` is used in the softmax. This helps the student retain the correct probabilistic assignment to scene regions.
- **Metric Offset Distillation (`L_metric`):** This loss maintains fine pose precision. It uses an L1 loss on the effective coordinate offsets (`d/w`) predicted by the student and teacher models for the active centers. This directly regularizes the local metric refinement stage.
- **Output Consistency Constraint (`L_out`):** An additional L1 loss on the final predicted 3D scene coordinates `x̂` of the student and teacher is included as a global safeguard, ensuring end-to-end consistency.
- **Total Distillation Loss (`L_SPDD`):** The three distillation losses (`L_prior`, `L_metric`, `L_out`) are combined with weighting coefficients (`α`, `β`, `γ`).

**D. Training Procedure**
The continual learning process proceeds sequentially through the scene tasks.
- **First Scene (`T1`):** The model is trained from scratch using the standard reprojection-based pose loss (`L_pose`) from GLACE. SARS populates the initial replay buffer `M` with samples from `D1`.
- **Subsequent Scenes (`Ti ≥ 2`):**
 1. A frozen copy of the model from the end of `T(i-1)` is designated as the teacher model `T`.
 2. The student model `S` is initialized from `T`.
 3. During training, `S` learns from the new scene data `Di` using `L_pose`.
 4. In each iteration, a mini-batch of replay samples is drawn from `M`, and the `L_SPDD` loss is computed against the teacher `T`.
 5. The total training loss is `L_total = L_pose + L_SPDD`.
 6. After `S` converges on `Ti`, `M` is updated with new samples from `Di` using SARS, and the trained `S` becomes the teacher for the next stage.

This synergistic approach ensures that SARS provides geometrically representative replay samples, while SPDD effectively preserves the structured geometric knowledge during adaptation, enabling the model to continually accumulate multi-scene calibration capability.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research evaluated the proposed continual hand-eye calibration framework on two public continual-localization benchmarks (i7Scenes and i12Scenes) and a simulated robotic manipulation dataset (Sim dataset). The framework was compared against several continual learning baselines, including Fine-tune, iCaRL, Buff-CS, GEC, and GDR, all adapted to the GLACE visual localization backbone. Joint training, where all scenes are learned simultaneously, served as an upper bound.

**Overall Performance:**
The proposed method consistently demonstrated superior performance in terms of localization accuracy and total forgetting rate (TFR) across all datasets compared to existing continual learning baselines.

**Results on i7Scenes (Table I, Fig. 3 & 5):**
- **Accuracy:** The proposed method achieved a final average accuracy of 74.9%, substantially outperforming the strongest baselines: iCaRL (65.2%), Buff-CS (66.1%), GDR (59.6%), and GEC (26.5%).
- **Forgetting Rate:** It exhibited the lowest TFR of 6.65, indicating less forgetting compared to iCaRL (11.37), Buff-CS (13.03), and GDR (12.25). This represents approximately a 50% reduction in forgetting compared to Buff-CS.
- **Scene-Specific Gains:** Significant improvements were observed on challenging scenes like Office, Redkitchen, and Stairs, where baselines suffered more severe forgetting. For instance, on Stairs, the proposed method achieved 56.2% accuracy, while Buff-CS and GDR reached 31.3% and 32.7% respectively.

**Results on i12Scenes (Table II, Fig. 3 & 4):**
- **Accuracy:** The advantage was more pronounced on the larger i12Scenes dataset, with the proposed method achieving 91.8% final accuracy, compared to Buff-CS (83.1%), GDR (78.7%), iCaRL (66.0%), and GEC (13.5%).
- **Forgetting Rate:** The TFR for the proposed method was 8.1, which was again the lowest among all continual-learning methods.
- **Scaling:** The results indicate that the framework scales effectively to longer scene sequences and larger spatial coverage, narrowing the gap to joint training to 5.1 percentage points. Consistent improvements were observed across most forgetting-prone scenes.

**Results on Simulation Dataset (Table III, Fig. 3 & 6):**
- **Accuracy:** On the simulated robotic manipulation dataset, under stricter 1 cm/1° criteria, the proposed method achieved 98.4% average accuracy, closely approaching the joint-training upper bound of 99.0%.
- **Forgetting Rate:** It recorded a TFR of only 1.6%, demonstrating robust anti-forgetting capabilities in a robotic context.
- **Robotic Manipulation Relevance:** The performance validates the framework's effectiveness in the hand-eye calibration domain for robotic manipulation scenarios, outperforming competitive baselines like GEC (95.0%) and GDR (95.3%) across all three scenes.

**Forgetting Rate Analysis (Fig. 4, 5, 6):**
Visualizations of per-scene accuracy evolution demonstrated that the proposed method's accuracy curves degraded more slowly and remained flatter as the number of learned scenes increased. This indicates a reduction in both immediate post-update forgetting and cumulative long-term drift, particularly evident on difficult scenes across all datasets.

**Qualitative Visualization (Fig. 7, 8, 9):**
Qualitative results, presented as overlays of rendered 3D models from estimated camera poses onto query images, showed accurate alignment of contours with real scene structures. This confirmed the precise pose estimation maintained by the proposed method across the diverse indoor environments of i7Scenes and i12Scenes, as well as the simulated robotic manipulation environments (Bedroom, Kitchen, Living).

**Ablation Study (Table IV):**
- **Effect of SARS:** Incorporating SARS into the iCaRL baseline significantly improved average accuracy by 6.8% on i7Scenes and 24.9% on i12Scenes. This highlights the critical role of geometrically diverse replay samples for anti-forgetting in visual localization tasks, especially with larger spatial extents.
- **Effect of SPDD:** Adding SPDD on top of SARS further enhanced performance, yielding additional gains of 2.9% on i7Scenes and 0.9% on i12Scenes. This indicates that while spatial replay provides the dominant benefit, structured distillation contributes to refining and preserving internal localization knowledge, mitigating pose precision degradation. The study concludes that both representative pose-space memory and structured knowledge preservation are necessary for effective continual hand-eye calibration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework for continual hand-eye calibration that addresses a fundamental challenge in deploying visual localization-based robotic systems in dynamic, open-world environments. By effectively mitigating catastrophic forgetting, the work offers several significant contributions and potential impacts:

- **Enabling Persistent Robotic Manipulation:** The core significance lies in enabling robots to maintain accurate hand-eye calibration across diverse and sequentially encountered scenes without requiring manual recalibration or retraining from scratch. This is crucial for autonomous systems operating in real-world settings like flexible manufacturing, warehouse logistics, service robotics, and domestic assistance, where environments change frequently and unpredictably. The ability to adapt to new scenes while retaining knowledge of old ones reduces operational downtime and increases robot autonomy.

- **Advancement in Continual Learning for Robotics:** The proposed Spatial-Aware Replay Strategy (SARS) and Structure-Preserving Dual Distillation (SPDD) introduce novel mechanisms specifically tailored to the geometric and metric nature of visual localization. SARS's use of Poisson disk sampling in joint position-orientation space offers a more efficient and representative way to construct replay buffers for pose estimation tasks, addressing the limitations of general-purpose replay strategies. SPDD's decomposition of localization knowledge into coarse scene layout and fine pose precision, followed by separate distillation, provides a more nuanced and effective approach to knowledge retention than monolithic output distillation. This contributes to the broader field of continual learning by demonstrating how task-specific knowledge structures can be leveraged for improved performance.

- **Improved Robustness and Reliability:** The demonstrated consistent performance across multiple challenging datasets (i7Scenes, i12Scenes, and a simulated robotic manipulation environment) indicates that the framework enhances the robustness and reliability of visual localization models. Maintaining high accuracy and low forgetting rates, even under sequential learning of many distinct scenes, implies that robots can operate with greater confidence in their spatial awareness over prolonged periods and across varied workspaces.

- **Practical Applicability:** The validation on a simulated robotic manipulation dataset directly demonstrates the framework's relevance to real-world robotic tasks. The stringent accuracy requirements (1 cm/1°) met in this simulation suggest that the method could be applicable to tasks requiring high precision, such as assembly, grasping in cluttered environments, or human-robot collaboration.

- **Foundation for Future Work:** The paper acknowledges limitations, such as current evaluation being restricted to indoor and simulated scenes. This provides a clear direction for future research, including extending the framework to real-robot deployment in outdoor, larger-scale, or less structured environments. This work lays a foundation for developing more generalizable and robust continual learning systems for perception-driven robotics.

In summary, this research advances the state of the art in continual learning for robotic vision by providing a robust solution to the catastrophic forgetting problem in hand-eye calibration. Its impact could facilitate the widespread deployment of intelligent robots capable of autonomously adapting to and operating effectively in the complex and dynamic environments of the real world.
