# RoboMemArena: A Comprehensive and Challenging Robotic Memory Benchmark

> [!summary] Summary
> RoboMemArena introduces a comprehensive benchmark designed to evaluate embodied memory in robotic systems, featuring multimodal annotations and a diverse set of challenging long-horizon tasks, including real-world scenarios. The accompanying PrediMem Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, which incorporates hierarchical memory and a predictive coding objective, demonstrates improved task success rates and enhanced sensitivity to critical state transitions.

## Problem

- Existing robotic memory benchmarks often lack multimodal annotations, such as visual keyframes and language instructions, necessary for training comprehensive memory representations.
- Many current benchmarks have limited task coverage and structural complexity, focusing on short-term memory or including tasks that do not genuinely require memory, thus failing to rigorously test long-term memory.
- There is a prevalent absence of real-world evaluation in existing benchmarks, creating a simulation-to-reality gap that hinders the practical validation of memory mechanisms in physical robots.

## Method

- The authors developed RoboMemArena, a large-scale benchmark with 26 simulated and 5 real-world tasks, featuring an automated pipeline for generating multimodal annotations, including subtask instructions and physically-grounded keyframes.
- RoboMemArena includes diverse memory-dependent task categories such as multi-object transferring, occlusion, counting, and sequencing, all designed to expose failure modes of reactive policies.
- They propose PrediMem, a dual-system Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model that uses hierarchical memory (a long-term keyframe bank and a recent sliding window buffer) and integrates a predictive coding head to enhance sensitivity to task dynamics and critical state transitions.

## Results

- PrediMem achieved a 38.5% Task Success Rate (TSR) and 55.2% Cumulative Success Rate (CSR) on RoboMemArena, outperforming the best baseline (MemER with 27.3% TSR and 49.1% CSR).
- Ablation studies confirmed the predictive coding head's contribution, with its removal reducing PrediMem's TSR to 32.3%, and the keyframe bank's necessity, as its elimination dropped TSR to 17.7%.
- In real-world experiments on five memory tasks, PrediMem achieved an average success rate of 52%, outperforming baselines and successfully completing the longest-horizon 'Imitate Human to Make Breakfast' task.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RoboMemArena exposes a significant memory gap, with 68.9% of its subtasks genuinely requiring historical information for successful completion, revealing limitations of current reactive and memory-augmented baselines.
> - The predictive coding objective, by forcing the model to anticipate future visual features, makes learned hidden states more sensitive to subtle state transitions, thereby improving keyframe selection and overall performance in memory-intensive tasks.
> - Both an explicit keyframe bank for preserving task-critical past events and scaling up the high-level planning model (S2) are crucial for robust performance on long-horizon, memory-dependent robotic tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of intelligent robotic systems capable of performing complex, long-horizon tasks in dynamic and partially observable environments necessitates effective memory mechanisms. Recent advancements in robot foundation policies have driven research into endowing these models with memory capabilities to manage longer task sequences and more intricate scenarios. This paper positions itself within this evolving landscape by addressing identified limitations in existing robotic memory benchmarks.

Previous benchmarks for robotic manipulation primarily focus on local observability, often failing to isolate memory as a critical bottleneck. While some recent memory-oriented benchmarks have emerged, the authors identify three specific gaps:
1. **Lack of Multimodal Annotations for Memory Formation**: Current benchmarks often do not provide the rich, multimodal annotations—such as visual keyframes and corresponding language instructions—that are essential for training and evaluating comprehensive memory representations in robots, akin to human memory.
2. **Limited Task Coverage and Structural Complexity**: Many existing benchmarks concentrate on short-term memory, lack diverse task scenarios, exhibit low structural complexity, and, in some instances, include tasks that do not genuinely require memory. This limits their utility for evaluating robust, long-term memory.
3. **Absence of Real-World Evaluation**: Most benchmarks are confined to simulated environments, leading to a disparity between simulated memory effectiveness and practical execution in physical robotics. This gap hinders the validation of memory mechanisms in real-world applications.

RoboMemArena directly addresses these identified shortcomings by introducing a benchmark designed from the ground up to evaluate embodied memory comprehensively. By offering multimodal annotations, a wide range of long-horizon and memory-dependent tasks, and support for real-world evaluations, this work aims to provide a more rigorous testing ground for advanced robotic memory systems. Simultaneously, the paper proposes a specific Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, PrediMem, which is designed to effectively leverage the features of this new benchmark, thereby contributing both a new evaluation standard and a corresponding solution architecture to the field.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop and introduce RoboMemArena, a large-scale, comprehensive, and challenging benchmark specifically designed for evaluating embodied memory in robotic systems. This objective is driven by several motivations stemming from the limitations observed in existing robotic memory benchmarks:

1. **To address the deficiency in multimodal memory annotations**: Existing benchmarks lack granular annotations that facilitate the formation of multimodal memory representations. Robots, like humans, benefit from memory components that integrate visual information (e.g., keyframes) and linguistic cues (e.g., subtask instructions). The motivation is to provide these critical annotations natively within the benchmark, enabling supervised learning of memory formation.
2. **To overcome limited task coverage and structural complexity**: Many current benchmarks are characterized by tasks that are short-horizon, possess low structural complexity, and offer limited diversity. Furthermore, some tasks do not genuinely necessitate memory for successful completion. The motivation here is to create a benchmark with a broad suite of tasks that are inherently long-horizon, require reasoning over extended periods, and explicitly depend on memory for successful execution across various challenging scenarios. This includes tasks involving object transfer between similar containers, object occlusion, precise counting of actions, and sequential execution where later steps depend on earlier outcomes.
3. **To bridge the gap between simulation and real-world performance**: The efficacy of robotic memory in simulated environments does not consistently translate to physical robot deployment. The lack of corresponding real-world evaluation setups in existing benchmarks restricts their practical relevance. A key motivation is to complement the large-scale simulated benchmark with a set of challenging real-world memory tasks, allowing for the physical validation of memory mechanisms and facilitating the transfer of learned policies to real-world robots.

In addition to the benchmark, a secondary objective is to propose PrediMem, a novel dual-system Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. The motivation for developing PrediMem is to demonstrate an effective approach to tackling the memory challenges posed by RoboMemArena. Specifically, PrediMem aims to leverage hierarchical memory management—combining recent observations with keyframe-based long-term memory—and a predictive coding mechanism to enhance sensitivity to task dynamics and critical state transitions, thereby improving robotic performance in memory-intensive tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research employs a two-pronged approach: the development of the RoboMemArena benchmark and the design of the PrediMem memory-augmented VLA model.

#### 3.1. RoboMemArena Benchmark Methodology: Task Setting

RoboMemArena comprises 26 simulated tasks categorized into four memory-dependent types to expose different failure modes for reactive policies:
- **Multi-Object Transferring:** Tasks requiring the robot to remember source-target mappings when containers are visually similar.
- **Multi-Object Occlusion:** Tasks where objects are placed into containers that subsequently become visually obscured, necessitating recall of contents and locations. This category is the most extensive with 11 tasks.
- **Multi-Object Counting:** Tasks demanding a specific number of repetitions for an action, requiring the robot to track counts despite similar visual states.
- **Multi-Object Sequence:** Tasks where subsequent actions depend on prior subtask outcomes or object references, emphasizing temporal reasoning.
Additionally, the benchmark includes five real-world memory tasks (e.g., Pour Bottle ×2, Brush Plates with Swap, Transfer Objects, Shell Game, Imitate Human to Make Breakfast (IHMB)) evaluated on an AgileX Cobot Mobile Aloha Platform. These tasks cover counting, occlusion, sequential execution, hidden-target tracking, and human demonstration imitation.

#### 3.1.2. Automated Data Generation Pipeline

The benchmark dataset is generated through a three-stage automated pipeline designed for scalability and fine-grained annotation:
1. **Stage 1: VLM-Driven Task Decomposition:** A Vision-Language Model (VLM) takes a high-level natural language instruction and the current RGB observation to propose an ordered sequence of executable subtasks. Prompts are crafted to elicit memory demands. Manual refinement is applied to unsuitable decompositions before execution.
2. **Stage 2: AnyGrasp-Based Autonomous Generation:** Each decomposed subtask is autonomously executed using AnyGrasp, a 6-DoF grasp-pose estimator that operates on point-cloud input. The estimated poses are translated into action trajectories via predefined primitives. A post-condition checker is integrated to retry failed subtasks with updated grasp poses, ensuring high success rates and closed-loop execution.
3. **Stage 3: Multi-Conditioned Keyframe Extraction:** Keyframes are extracted from continuous trajectories by combining two physically grounded conditions:
 - **Physical interaction anchors (K_phys):** Frames where the gripper state transitions (open to close, or close to open).
 - **Kinematic inflections (K_kin):** Frames where the end-effector linear velocity magnitude drops below a threshold or the cosine similarity between consecutive velocity vectors falls below a certain angle, indicating changes in motion phases.
 This method aims to capture information-bottleneck frames efficiently, providing compact, event-focused temporal supervision without dense video storage.

#### 3.1.3. Evaluation Protocol

To assess performance on long-horizon memory tasks, two metrics are reported:
- **Task Success Rate (TSR):** A binary metric where a task is deemed successful only if all stage-level verification predicates (encoding object location, containment, visibility, etc.) are satisfied.
- **Cumulative Success Rate (CSR):** Measures the fraction of completed verification stages for each task, providing a granular measure of progress.

#### 3.2. PrediMem Model Methodology

PrediMem is a dual-system VLA framework incorporating hierarchical memory and predictive coding:
- **Dual-System Architecture:** It consists of a high-level planner (System 2, S2) and a low-level execution policy (System 1, S1), asynchronously coupled.
- **Memory Bank (M_t):** M_t combines a long-term keyframe buffer (M_key_t) with a recent sliding window buffer (M_rec_t) of fixed horizon (e.g., 5 frames).
- **S2 (High-Level Planner):** Built upon Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct. S2 takes the current observation and the aggregated memory bank (M_t) as input to predict the current subtask instruction and decide whether the current frame should be stored as a keyframe in M_key_t. The vision tower of Qwen3 is frozen, while other modules are fine-tuned.
- **S1 (Low-Level Execution Policy):** A VLA actor that predicts fresh subtask-conditioned action chunks from the current observation.
- **Predictive Coding Head:** An auxiliary, training-only component integrated into S2. It aims to enhance the model's sensitivity to keyframes and its ability to capture future dynamics by predicting the visual feature representation of the subsequent frame (ˆZ_t+1) from the current frame's hidden features (h_t).
 - The predictive loss (L_Pre) is formulated as a combination of Mean Squared Error and cosine distance between the predicted feature and the stop-gradient ground truth feature (L_Pre = MSE(ˆZ_t+1, sg(Z_t+1)) + (1 - cos(ˆZ_t+1, sg(Z_t+1)))).
- **Total Training Loss:** The S2 training objective combines a next-token prediction loss (L_text) for subtask generation and keyframe decisions with the predictive coding loss (L_S2 = L_text + 0.1 * L_Pre). The S1 policy is trained using a flow-matching objective.
- **Inference:** During inference, the predictive coding head is removed, allowing PrediMem to operate with the architecture and computational cost of a standard dual-system framework while retaining the improved capabilities for dynamic understanding and keyframe selection. S2 runs at a lower frequency (1.06 Hz) to update subtasks and keyframe decisions, while S1 executes actions at a higher frequency (3.40 Hz) based on the freshest subtask.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on RoboMemArena aimed to evaluate the benchmark itself and the performance of the PrediMem framework, yielding several insights.

#### 4.1. Benchmark Characteristics and Memory Gap Exposure (Q1, Q2)

RoboMemArena features 26 tasks with an average trajectory length of 1,076 steps per task. A significant characteristic is its high proportion of memory-dependent subtasks, with 104 out of 151 subtasks (68.9%) requiring historical information for correct execution, which is noted as the highest among compared robotic memory benchmarks. The benchmark includes multimodal memory-related annotations, supports scalable generation, and incorporates real-world evaluation.

Experiments revealed a substantial memory gap for existing VLAs:
- **Reactive policies (π_0.5)** achieved an average Task Success Rate (TSR) of 21.5% and Cumulative Success Rate (CSR) of 38.7%. These policies struggled with tasks requiring recall of past states (e.g., drawer-based tasks), often entering loops or forgetting global constraints.
- **Memory-augmented baselines (HiF-VLA, MemoryVLA, MemER)** showed varying improvements. MemER, which uses keyframe retrieval and a dual-system design, performed best among the baselines with 27.3% TSR and 49.1% CSR. However, its performance was still limited by its perception of task dynamics and keyframe selection.
- **PrediMem** outperformed all baselines, achieving 38.5% TSR and 55.2% CSR. This suggests that its hierarchical memory input and explicit subtask management, combined with enhanced sensitivity to state transitions, address critical memory challenges.
- **Closed-source VLMs (e.g., GPT-5.4, frozen Qwen3-VL-8B)** demonstrated poor generalization to robotic memory tasks, with GPT-5.4 achieving only 8.7% TSR. This finding indicates that VLMs require training on robotic data for effective memory management in embodied systems.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies (Q3)

Ablation studies on PrediMem components provided insights into their contributions:
- **Predictive Coding Head:** Removing the predictive coding head reduced PrediMem's TSR to 32.3% and CSR to 49.0%. This component was found to be more crucial for tasks involving subtle state transitions, such as occlusion, counting, and sequential execution. The predictive coding objective, by forcing the model to predict future visual representations, appears to make the learned hidden states more sensitive to these transition points, thereby improving keyframe selection. An optimal weight of 0.1 for the predictive loss (L_Pre) was identified. Qualitative analysis using t-SNE visualized that predictive coding resulted in more discriminative and compact clusters of keyframe representations.
- **Keyframe Bank:** Eliminating the keyframe bank resulted in a significant performance drop, reducing TSR to 17.7% and CSR to 41.6%. This highlights the necessity of explicitly preserving task-relevant events beyond the short-term recent-frame window, particularly for occlusion and sequential tasks where decisions depend on past, no longer visible, information.

#### 4.3. Scaling Studies (Q4)

- **Memory Bank Scaling:**
 - **Recent-frame buffer:** A window size of 3-5 frames for the recent buffer was found to be sufficient for most short-term changes. Smaller windows lacked temporal evidence, while larger ones introduced redundancy and increased latency.
 - **Keyframe buffer:** Performance consistently improved with increased keyframe buffer capacity. The uncapped keyframe bank yielded the best results, as it allowed the preservation of early decision-critical observations essential for long-horizon tasks.
- **S2 Model Scaling:** Experiments with different Qwen3 backbone sizes (1.7B, 4B, 8B parameters) showed a consistent improvement in performance with larger models. Scaling up the S2 model from Qwen3-1.7B (19.9% TSR) to Qwen3-8B (38.5% TSR) across all tasks suggests that larger models offer enhanced reasoning and memory capabilities beneficial for complex memory systems.

#### 4.4. Real-World Experiments (Q5)

On the five real-world memory tasks, PrediMem achieved an average success rate of 52%, outperforming π_0.5 (20%) and MemER (40%). Notably, for the longest-horizon task, "Imitate Human to Make Breakfast," PrediMem was the only method that succeeded. These results indicate PrediMem's effectiveness in complex real-world scenarios, demonstrating the practical importance of its memory mechanism design.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents RoboMemArena as a notable contribution to the field of robotic intelligence, specifically addressing the evaluation of embodied memory. The significance stems from its comprehensive design that tackles several limitations of prior benchmarks.

Firstly, RoboMemArena’s **multimodal memory-related annotations**, which include both subtask instructions and physically grounded keyframe extractions, provide richer supervision for training and evaluating advanced memory systems. This detailed annotation can facilitate the development of more human-like memory representations in robots, bridging a known gap in current approaches.

Secondly, the benchmark's **diverse and challenging task suite**, comprising 26 tasks across four memory-dependent categories and explicitly designed to include a high proportion of history-dependent subtasks (68.9%), provides a rigorous testing ground for long-horizon planning and execution. This emphasis on tasks that genuinely require memory ensures that advancements measured on RoboMemArena directly contribute to more capable and autonomous robotic systems.

Thirdly, the inclusion of **paired real-world memory tasks** for physical evaluation addresses the critical sim-to-real gap. This aspect allows for direct validation of memory mechanisms in physical robots, moving beyond purely simulated performance metrics and fostering the development of policies that are robust in real-world environments.

Beyond the benchmark itself, the introduction of **PrediMem**, a dual-system VLA with a novel predictive coding objective, demonstrates a tangible approach to utilizing such a benchmark. PrediMem's architectural design, particularly its hierarchical memory management and the inference-time-free predictive coding head, represents an advance in creating models sensitive to critical state transitions. The experimental results, showing PrediMem's superior performance over baselines and the impact of its core components, offer **valuable insights into memory management, model architecture, and scaling laws** for complex robotic memory systems.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Accelerated Research:** RoboMemArena is poised to accelerate research into robotic memory, offering a standardized platform that encourages the development of more sophisticated memory architectures and learning algorithms.
- **More Capable Robots:** By fostering the creation of robots that can better understand, retain, and utilize past experiences, this work contributes to the development of more intelligent, autonomous, and general-purpose robots capable of operating in complex, partially observable environments.
- **Bridging AI and Robotics:** The findings underscore the necessity of training Vision-Language Models on specific robotic data for effective memory management, highlighting an important direction for integrating advanced AI models into embodied systems.
- **Practical Applications:** The real-world validation suggests direct applicability of these memory-augmented approaches to practical robotic tasks, particularly those requiring long-horizon planning and adaptation to dynamic conditions.
