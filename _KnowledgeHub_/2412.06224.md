# Uni-NaVid: A Video-based Vision-Language-Action Model for Unifying Embodied Navigation Tasks

> [!summary] Summary
> Uni-NaVid presents a unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of addressing four distinct embodied navigation tasks by processing ego-centric video and natural language instructions. The model employs a novel online visual token merging mechanism to achieve a 5 Hz inference speed, demonstrating state-of-the-art performance in simulation and strong real-world generalization.

## Problem

- Embodied navigation research is fragmented, with task-specific models developed for each objective (e.g., VLN, ObjectNav), limiting robotic versatility and deployment.
- High inference latency of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) and Large Language Models (LLMs) hinders their real-time application in continuous, long-horizon robotic tasks.
- Existing generalist embodied AI models often struggle with the sim-to-real gap and lack sufficient integration of diverse, uniformly formatted real-world data.

## Method

- **Uni-NaVid**, a unified ==Vision-Language-Action (VLA)== model that takes ego-centric RGB video and natural language instructions as input to output low-level robotic actions.
- A novel ==online visual token merging== mechanism that efficiently compresses visual history into current, short-term, and long-term tokens for the LLM, maintaining low latency.
- An extensive dataset of 3.6 million unified multi-task navigation samples from the ==Habitat simulator==, jointly trained with 2.3 million open-world video QA/captioning samples.

## Results

- Uni-NaVid achieves state-of-the-art or comparable performance on VLN (e.g., **+25.7%** SR on R2R), ObjectNav, EQA, and the newly introduced language-described human following benchmark.
- The model maintains an average inference speed of **5 Hz** (**0.2 seconds** per inference) on long video sequences, showcasing its efficiency for real-time robotic control.
- Successful zero-shot deployment on a real Unitree GO2 robotic dog confirms strong real-world generalizability and the ability to execute complex, compositional navigation tasks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Joint training across multiple distinct navigation tasks (VLN, ObjectNav, EQA, Human Following) yields synergistic performance improvements.
> - An online visual token merging mechanism can drastically reduce computational latency, enabling real-time (5 Hz) inference for long video histories without compromising performance.
> - Combining large-scale multi-task simulation data with open-world video understanding datasets enhances sim-to-real transfer and generalizability for embodied agents.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied navigation is a cornerstone of intelligent robotics, empowering autonomous agents to interact with and traverse physical environments based on human commands. The broader research landscape in this field has seen significant advancements, yet it faces several critical challenges that Uni-NaVid seeks to address.

Historically, embodied navigation research has largely focused on developing specialized solutions for individual tasks. This includes:
- **Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN):** Agents interpret natural language instructions to navigate complex routes.
- **Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav):** Robots locate and approach specific objects.
- **Embodied Question Answering (EQA):** Agents navigate to find information needed to answer questions about their environment.
- **Human Following:** Robots track and follow designated individuals.

The proliferation of task-specific models has led to a fragmented research landscape where each solution often requires unique architectures, specialized modules, and dedicated datasets. This siloed approach limits the versatility of robotic agents, prevents the leveraging of synergistic relationships between different navigation competencies, and complicates real-world deployment where robots are expected to perform a multitude of tasks.

Recent trends in Embodied AI have shifted towards developing more generalist models, often leveraging the powerful generalization capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Vision-Language Models (VLMs). However, these efforts encounter their own set of hurdles:
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Many generalist approaches, particularly those using imitation learning (IL) or reinforcement learning (RL), are heavily reliant on simulation environments. The discrepancies in rendering quality and environmental diversity between simulators and the real world often result in poor generalization.
- **Computational Efficiency:** While LLMs offer powerful reasoning capabilities, their high inference latency poses a significant challenge for real-time robotic control, particularly in dynamic, long-horizon tasks. Many LLM-based approaches simplify the problem by adopting discretized modeling (e.g., relying on pre-defined graphs), which can sacrifice output flexibility and introduce additional deployment complexities.
- **Data Heterogeneity:** Training a truly versatile agent requires integrating diverse datasets from various navigation tasks, which often come in disparate formats and settings.

Uni-NaVid directly tackles these challenges by proposing a unified, video-based VLA model. It differentiates itself by:
1. **Unification:** It aims to integrate four distinct, yet commonly demanded, navigation tasks within a *single* end-to-end framework.
2. **Efficiency:** It addresses the critical issue of LLM inference latency in long-horizon tasks through a novel online token merging mechanism, making it practical for real-world deployment.
3. **Data Integration:** It demonstrates how collecting and uniformly formatting a massive, diverse dataset across these tasks, supplemented by open-world VQA data, can foster synergistic learning and improve sim-to-real transfer.
4. **Continuous Environment:** Unlike some LLM-based approaches that discretize the environment, Uni-NaVid operates in continuous environments, outputting low-level actions, which is more practical for real-world robotics.
5. **Novel Benchmark:** It introduces a new language-guided human-following benchmark, enriching the dataset landscape for dynamic, multi-person scenarios.

In essence, Uni-NaVid represents a significant step towards creating general-purpose embodied agents that are not only versatile across tasks but also efficient and robust enough for practical real-world applications, bridging the gap between advanced VLM capabilities and the stringent demands of robotic deployment.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation behind Uni-NaVid stems from the inherent limitations of current embodied navigation systems, which are largely designed for single-purpose tasks. As intelligent robots become more ubiquitous and are expected to perform a wider array of functions, the demand for versatile navigation agents that can seamlessly switch between, and even combine, different objectives becomes paramount. The authors identify that existing fragmented approaches hinder the natural integration of diverse navigation tasks and prevent the exploitation of potential synergies between them. Furthermore, the computational intensity of modern Vision-Language Models (VLMs), while powerful, poses a significant barrier to their real-time deployment on robotic platforms.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of the Uni-NaVid project are:

1. **Unify Diverse Embodied Navigation Tasks:** The core objective is to develop a single, comprehensive Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of addressing multiple, distinct navigation paradigms. Specifically, this includes Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN), Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav), Embodied Question Answering (EQA), and Human Following. The aim is to move away from task-specific architectures towards a generalist agent.
2. **Enable End-to-End, Low-Level Action Generation:** To ensure practicality and flexibility in continuous environments, Uni-NaVid is designed to directly ingest natural language instructions and raw RGB video streams, and subsequently output low-level robotic actions in an end-to-end manner. This bypasses the need for explicit graph-based planning or high-level abstract commands, which can limit maneuverability in dynamic, real-world settings.
3. **Enhance Performance through Task Synergy:** A fundamental hypothesis is that training on multiple related navigation tasks concurrently will lead to synergistic improvements, where knowledge gained from one task (e.g., object recognition for ObjectNav) benefits another (e.g., scene understanding for VLN or EQA).
4. **Achieve High Inference Efficiency for Real-time Operation:** A critical practical goal is to overcome the computational latency often associated with large language models when processing extensive video streams. The authors aim to achieve a high inference speed (e.g., 5 Hz) to facilitate non-blocking, responsive robot navigation in dynamic real-world environments.
5. **Demonstrate Strong Generalizability and Real-world Applicability:** Beyond simulated environments, a key objective is to validate Uni-NaVid's robustness and generalization capabilities in novel, unseen real-world settings, confirming its practical utility. This includes handling diverse instructions and environments in a zero-shot manner.
6. **Create a New Language-Described Human Following Benchmark:** To address a gap in existing datasets for human following in crowded, multi-person environments with textual descriptions, a specific objective was to construct a new benchmark to train and evaluate this challenging task.

By pursuing these objectives, Uni-NaVid aims to establish a new paradigm for embodied navigation, fostering the development of more intelligent, versatile, and practically deployable robotic agents.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Uni-NaVid's methodology is centered on building an efficient, unified Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model capable of end-to-end processing of visual observations and language instructions to generate low-level robotic actions across diverse navigation tasks. The approach combines a novel architectural design, a comprehensive data collection strategy, and a robust training regimen.

#### 3.1. Model Architecture

Uni-NaVid comprises three main components: a vision encoder, an innovative online token merge mechanism, and a large language model (LLM).

- **Vision Encoder:** The model utilizes EVA-CLIP, a powerful pre-trained vision encoder, to extract frame-wise visual features from ego-centric RGB video streams. These features are represented as visual tokens, providing rich spatial and semantic information about the environment.
- **Online Visual Token Merging (Key Innovation):** This mechanism is central to Uni-NaVid's efficiency, tackling the challenge of progressively increasing visual token counts from long video histories, which typically leads to high computational latency in LLM-based approaches. Inspired by human memory models, it categorizes visual tokens based on their temporal relevance:
 - **Current Visual Tokens (X_curr):** The most recent frame, retaining comprehensive fine-grained visual information.
 - **Short-Term Visual Tokens (X_short):** Recent historical frames (up to 64 steps), capturing immediate temporal context.
 - **Long-Term Visual Tokens (X_long):** Distant historical frames, retaining coarse but structured temporal information.
 To reduce token redundancy, grid pooling is applied at different resolutions for each category: highest resolution for current, moderate for short-term, and lowest for long-term tokens. Crucially, this merging operates in an *online* manner. When a new frame arrives, only the new current frame and the oldest short-term frame (which transitions to long-term) undergo pooling. Long-term tokens are further merged by combining adjacent tokens with high cosine similarity (threshold $\tau=0.95$), preventing linear growth and ensuring a compact history representation. This strategy significantly reduces the total number of visual tokens processed by the LLM, achieving an inference speed of 5 Hz (0.2 seconds per inference).
- **Cross-Modality Projector:** An MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron) projector (P_V) aligns the merged visual tokens into a feature space compatible with the LLM's input representation.
- **Large Language Model (LLM):** Vicuna-7B serves as the backbone LLM. It takes a concatenated sequence of:
 - Projected visual observation tokens ($E^V_T$) from the current, short-term, and long-term memory.
 - A navigation task indicator `_NAV_` to specify the task type (crucial for distinguishing navigation from QA).
 - Language observation tokens ($E^L_T$) encoded from the natural language instruction.
 The LLM then infers a sequence of four low-level actions (`FORWARD`, `TURN-LEFT`, `TURN-RIGHT`, `STOP`) for a future horizon, enabling foresight prediction and supporting asynchronous robot deployment.

#### 3.2. Data Collection and Training Strategy

To train Uni-NaVid for multi-task mastery, the authors employ a two-pronged data strategy:

- **Extensive Multi-Task Navigation Data (3.6 Million Samples):** The largest multi-task navigation dataset to date was collected within the Habitat simulator (HM3D, MP3D) across 861 diverse scenes, ensuring a unified input/output format.
 - **Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN):** 2.4M samples from VLN-CE R2R and RxR datasets, covering diverse trajectories from ground truth and DAGGER-collected expert actions. Augmented with low-level action descriptions.
 - **Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav):** 483k successful navigation samples from HM3D ObjectNav, using a "Search for a/an [Object]" template.
 - **Embodied Question Answering (EQA):** 240k video-action samples (GT trajectories) and 10k video-answering samples from MP3D-EQA.
 - **Human Following:** A novel, self-built benchmark based on Habitat 3.0, comprising 544k video-action samples. This benchmark introduces textual descriptions for avatars (e.g., "Follow the man in the blue t-shirt") and includes distracting human avatars in crowded environments, addressing a critical gap in existing datasets.
 - **Uniformity:** All data are formatted as egocentric RGB video, natural language instructions, and corresponding four future actions. The robot embodiment settings (height, radius) are varied during collection to prevent overfitting. ChatGPT was used for instruction augmentation to enhance linguistic diversity.
- **Joint Training with Open-World Video Data (2.3 Million Samples):** To imbue Uni-NaVid with open-world knowledge and improve sim-to-real generalization, it is co-tuned with real-world video-based question-answering (VQA) and video captioning data from publicly available datasets (LLaMA-VID, PANDM).
- **Two-Stage Training Process:**
 1. The cross-modality projector is pre-trained using Image QA data (same as LLaMA-VID).
 2. Both the projector and the LLM (Vicuna-7B) are fine-tuned jointly using the 2.3M VQA/captioning samples and the 3.6M multi-task navigation samples.
 Training is performed for one epoch on a cluster of 40 NVIDIA H800 GPUs. The vision encoder (EVA-CLIP) and LLM (Vicuna-7B) are pre-loaded with their default pre-trained weights.

This comprehensive methodology, integrating architectural innovation with large-scale, diverse data collection and a multi-stage training strategy, allows Uni-NaVid to achieve both versatility across navigation tasks and high efficiency for real-world robotic deployment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Uni-NaVid demonstrates compelling performance across a broad spectrum of embodied navigation tasks and video understanding benchmarks, showcasing its versatility, efficiency, and generalization capabilities.

#### 4.1. State-of-the-Art Performance Across Individual Tasks

- **Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN):** Uni-NaVid achieves state-of-the-art (SOTA) or SOTA-comparable performance on the VLN-CE R2R and RxR benchmarks, using only RGB video as input. Notably, it significantly outperforms previous VLM-based approaches like NaVid, achieving a +25.7% increase in Success Rate (SR) on R2R. It maintains consistent strong performance across both R2R (simpler trajectories) and RxR (more diverse, longer paths), demonstrating effective leveraging of detailed instructions. Even when trained without RxR data, it still surpasses prior methods, highlighting its robust learning.
- **Object Goal Navigation (ObjectNav):** On the Habitat Matterport 3D (HM3D) ObjectNav dataset, Uni-NaVid attains the best performance in terms of Success Rate (SR) and Success Weighted by Path Length (SPL), even without using odometry. Crucially, for open-vocabulary object goal navigation (HM3D-OVON) in a zero-shot manner, Uni-NaVid outperforms existing zero-shot methods and even some fine-tuned approaches, indicating strong generalization to unseen object categories.
- **Embodied Question Answering (EQA):** Uni-NaVid outperforms mainstream EQA methods on MP3D-EQA, despite operating in continuous environments (which is generally more challenging than discrete, landmark-based settings). When provided with ground truth navigation trajectories, its performance further improves, showcasing its strong understanding of navigation history. On the more challenging OpenEQA benchmark, Uni-NaVid achieves competitive results comparable to powerful commercial models like GPT-4V without requiring explicit scene captions or Socratic prompting.
- **Human Following:** On the newly proposed language-described human following benchmark, Uni-NaVid significantly outperforms strong baselines like PoliFormer and IBVS in Success Rate (SR) and Following Rate (FR), while maintaining a low Collision Rate (CR). This holds true even when baselines are given ground-truth bounding box information, underscoring Uni-NaVid's ability to effectively interpret instructions, track the correct human, and predict their movements. Performance is consistent across different environments (HSSD, MP3D).
- **Video Understanding (Auxiliary Task):** Uni-NaVid also demonstrates strong performance on standard video question answering (ScanQA, MSVD-QA, MSRVTT-QA, ActivityNet-QA) and video captioning benchmarks. Despite the aggressive token merging employed for efficiency, its results are comparable to state-of-the-art methods, validating the effectiveness of the token merging strategy and its robust open-world understanding capabilities.

#### 4.2. Synergistic Benefits of Multi-Task Learning

Ablation studies confirm that joint training across multiple navigation tasks yields synergistic improvements. Comparing single-task training to multi-task training, consistent performance gains are observed across all four navigation tasks (VLN, ObjectNav, EQA, Human Following), with VLN, ObjectNav, and EQA showing particularly significant boosts. This validates the hypothesis that shared knowledge and representation learning across diverse tasks enhance overall navigation intelligence.

#### 4.3. Effectiveness of Online Visual Token Merging

The online visual token merging mechanism is crucial for Uni-NaVid's efficiency. It achieves an average inference speed of 5 Hz (0.2 seconds per inference), which is significantly faster and more stable compared to other VLM-based approaches that suffer from increasing latency with longer video sequences. Ablation studies further demonstrate that both short-term and long-term memory components, derived from the token merging, contribute positively to performance, especially for tasks heavily reliant on historical context like VLN.

#### 4.4. Real-World Generalizability and Compositional Capabilities

Real-world deployment on a Unitree GO2 robotic dog, in a zero-shot setting (i.e., in novel environments and with novel instructions not seen during training), confirms Uni-NaVid's effectiveness and impressive robustness. It successfully executes complex, long-horizon navigation tasks (e.g., navigating across multiple rooms, searching for out-of-vision objects). Crucially, Uni-NaVid demonstrates an impressive ability to handle *compositional tasks*, where instructions involve a sequence of multiple navigation objectives (e.g., "Follow the man until you see a sofa. Turn right and search for a TV, stop by the TV. Finally, what is the color of the sofa?"). This capability highlights the model's capacity for aligning current navigation progress with multi-step instructions and reasoning about the environmental state.

Overall, the main findings highlight Uni-NaVid's success in unifying diverse embodied navigation tasks within an efficient, end-to-end VLA framework, demonstrating SOTA-level performance in simulation and strong real-world generalizability.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Uni-NaVid research represents a significant leap forward in the field of embodied AI, particularly for robotic navigation, and holds substantial potential for broad impact across both research and practical applications.

#### 5.1. Advancing General-Purpose Embodied Navigation

The most profound significance of Uni-NaVid lies in its successful unification of multiple distinct embodied navigation tasks (VLN, ObjectNav, EQA, Human Following) within a single, end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model. This moves the field away from fragmented, task-specific solutions towards truly general-purpose agents. This holistic approach means a robot equipped with Uni-NaVid could potentially handle a wide array of human commands in varied environments without requiring extensive retraining or specialized modules for each new task. This is a crucial step towards truly intelligent and autonomous robots that can operate flexibly in complex, human-centric environments.

#### 5.2. Enabling Real-time Robotic Deployment

A critical bottleneck for deploying sophisticated LLM-based embodied agents in the real world has been their computational latency. Uni-NaVid's innovative online visual token merging mechanism effectively addresses this by enabling high-speed inference (5 Hz). This rapid processing, coupled with its ability to predict a sequence of actions (foresight prediction), makes Uni-NaVid highly practical for real-time robotic control, ensuring non-blocking and responsive navigation. This efficiency is a game-changer for moving advanced VLM capabilities from simulation to tangible, responsive robotic systems.

#### 5.3. Bridging the Sim-to-Real Gap and Leveraging Data Synergy

The work demonstrates that combining large-scale synthetic multi-task navigation data with open-world video understanding data (VQA and captioning) can effectively enhance scene understanding and improve sim-to-real transfer. This data synergy helps the model acquire robust visual and linguistic knowledge that generalizes well to real-world complexities, mitigating the long-standing challenge of simulation fidelity. The collection of 3.6 million navigation samples across diverse tasks, uniformly formatted, provides a powerful resource for future research.

#### 5.4. Introduction of a Novel Benchmark

The creation and release of a new language-described human following benchmark, based on Habitat 3.0 and featuring textual descriptions and multi-person scenarios, fills a crucial gap in the embodied AI community. This benchmark will undoubtedly stimulate further research into dynamic environments and complex human-robot interaction, areas where current datasets are often limited.

#### 5.5. Potential Applications and Future Directions

The capabilities demonstrated by Uni-NaVid open doors for a multitude of advanced robotic applications:
- **Home Service Robots:** Robots that can follow complex instructions, find objects, answer questions about their environment, and even follow family members.
- **Robotic Guide Dogs:** Providing assistance to visually impaired individuals by interpreting instructions and navigating dynamic environments.
- **Industrial/Logistics Robots:** Agents capable of dynamic navigation, object retrieval, and answering queries within warehouses or factories.
- **Mobile Manipulation:** The foundational navigation skills learned by Uni-NaVid could be extended to more complex mobile manipulation tasks, where the robot needs to move to a location before interacting with objects.

The authors openly acknowledge limitations and propose future work, including the need for more general-purpose navigation benchmarks, architectures for even longer-horizon tasks (e.g., navigating across buildings), and integration with advanced motion planning techniques. Their commitment to releasing the full source code and the new human-following benchmark underscores a dedication to fostering collaborative progress in the embodied AI community.

In conclusion, Uni-NaVid marks a significant milestone in embodied AI, offering a robust, efficient, and versatile framework for robotic navigation. Its emphasis on unification, real-time performance, and multi-task learning lays crucial groundwork for the development of the next generation of highly capable and adaptable intelligent robots.
