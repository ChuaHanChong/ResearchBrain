# REPAIR-Bench: A Benchmark for Robot Error Perception And Interaction Recovery

> [!summary] Summary
> REPAIR-Bench introduces an integrated, multimodal benchmark designed for robot error perception and interaction recovery within human-robot interaction contexts. It enables the development of systems that can detect interaction failures, classify their specific type, and predict user-preferred recovery strategies by leveraging visual and linguistic cues, showing improved performance over baselines for detection and classification, and effective prediction of recovery actions using fine-tuned language models.

## Problem

- Prior work often treated robot failures as isolated events, neglecting the impact of longitudinal user adaptation or cross-trial history on user perception and response.
- Existing failure detection methods largely used binary success/failure outcomes, lacking fine-grained multi-class categorization, while recovery strategies were typically researcher-defined or rule-based rather than predicting user-preferred recovery actions based on interaction context.
- A lack of integrated benchmark frameworks existed that connected the entire failure lifecycle (detection, understanding, and recovery decision-making) for end-to-end evaluation.

## Method

- Developed **REPAIR-Bench**, a unified benchmark on the RFM-HRI dataset, featuring three tasks: failure detection, multi-class failure classification, and user-preferred recovery strategy prediction.
- Utilized multimodal features, including visual (facial action units, head pose) and linguistic data, with ==hierarchical recurrent neural networks== (HRNN) to model cross-session user adaptation for detection and classification tasks.
- Implemented and fine-tuned Small Language Models (**Mistral-7B** and **Gemma-9B**) using ==QLoRA== for predicting user-preferred recovery strategies from interaction transcripts and failure labels.

## Results

- The hierarchical HRNN achieved a macro **F1-score of 0.80** for window-level failure detection and a **median absolute error of 2.97 seconds** for failure transition localization, outperforming a standard GRU baseline by **0.15 F1-points**.
- For multi-class failure classification, the HRNN attained a macro **F1-score of 0.44**, showing robust performance for 'Success' (**F1=0.93**) and moderate detection for 'Speech' and 'Search' failures.
- QLoRA fine-tuning improved Mistral-7B's **Hit@5** for recovery prediction from **0.68 to 0.76** and enabled Gemma-9B to achieve a comparable **Hit@5 of 0.68**, demonstrating the method's effectiveness for generating user-aligned recovery actions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Hierarchical recurrent models effectively leverage cross-session interaction history, improving robot failure detection and classification accuracy over non-hierarchical baselines by modeling user adaptation.
> - Multimodal visual cues (facial AUs, head pose) provide sufficient signal for multi-class robot failure classification, though distinguishing some failure types (e.g., comprehension, timing) remains challenging.
> - QLoRA fine-tuning significantly enhances the capability of Small Language Models to accurately predict user-preferred recovery strategies from interaction context, demonstrating the importance of domain-specific adaptation for such tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Human-Robot Interaction (HRI) research increasingly addresses the critical challenge of robot failures, particularly as robots are deployed in high-stakes environments such as emergency departments. Prior work in HRI has recognized the importance of understanding user responses to robot failures to build robust and trustworthy systems. Existing efforts have focused on recognizing task failures through user behavior, identifying multimodal markers (e.g., gaze shifts, acoustic changes, nonverbal cues, facial action units), and leveraging techniques like representation learning, time-series analysis, and alignment strategies for error detection. Datasets such as ERR@HRI and Response-to-Errors have contributed to this area by providing multimodal data for analyzing user reactions to robot-initiated interaction ruptures and conversational failures. Some research has also extended beyond technical failures to address social breakdowns and repair attempts.

However, the authors identify several gaps in the existing landscape. Specifically, much of the prior work:
1. **Treats failures as independent events**, neglecting the potential for cross-trial history or accumulation effects on user perception and response.
2. **Emphasizes binary failure detection** (success/failure) rather than supporting more nuanced multi-class failure categorization.
3. **Relies on researcher-defined or rule-based recovery strategies**, instead of predicting user-preferred recovery actions based on interaction context.
4. **Lacks integrated benchmark frameworks** that connect the entire lifecycle of failure—detection, understanding, and recovery decision-making—within a single evaluation setting.

REPAIR-Bench addresses these limitations by providing a unified, multimodal benchmark framework designed to facilitate research across these interconnected aspects of robot failure management. Its focus on longitudinal user adaptation, fine-grained failure classification, and user-centered recovery prediction distinguishes it from previous approaches and aims to advance the development of more adaptive and trustworthy HRI systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to introduce REPAIR-Bench, a comprehensive benchmark designed to facilitate research into robot error perception and interaction recovery, particularly within healthcare HRI contexts. This benchmark aims to enable the development of robot systems that can not only detect failures but also understand their nature and respond with user-preferred recovery strategies.

The motivation for REPAIR-Bench stems from several critical needs:
- **Enhancing Robot Trustworthiness in High-Stakes Environments:** Robots are being developed for applications such as emergency departments, where failures can have significant consequences. Effective handling of these failures is crucial for maintaining user trust, ensuring task completion, and promoting long-term acceptance of robots.
- **Modeling Longitudinal User Adaptation:** Prior research often overlooks how users adapt their behavior or expectations across repeated interactions, especially when failures accumulate. The benchmark seeks to incorporate cross-trial history to understand how users' reactions evolve over time.
- **Moving Beyond Binary Failure Detection:** Current systems largely focus on a binary "success/failure" outcome. However, knowing *what type* of failure occurred (e.g., speech, timing, comprehension, search) provides critical contextual information necessary for designing appropriate, tailored system responses.
- **Predicting User-Preferred Recovery Strategies:** Instead of robots relying on predefined or rule-based recovery actions, the motivation is to enable systems to infer and apply recovery strategies that align with user expectations. This user-centered approach is intended to restore user confidence and trust more effectively after interaction breakdowns.
- **Creating an Integrated Evaluation Framework:** The authors aim to bridge the gap between failure detection, classification, and recovery decision-making. By integrating these stages into a single benchmark, REPAIR-Bench provides a standardized framework for end-to-end evaluation, enabling systematic investigation of the entire failure lifecycle in HRI.
- **Leveraging Multimodal User Behavior:** The work is motivated by the understanding that nonverbal cues often precede verbal complaints and can serve as early indicators of failure. By focusing on visual behavioral signals (facial action units, head pose, gaze) and linguistic features, the benchmark aims to improve the timeliness and accuracy of failure detection and classification.
- **Addressing Specific Challenges in Medical HRI:** Building on the RFM-HRI dataset, which captures interactions in a medical context (crash-cart item retrieval), the benchmark specifically aims to support the development of adaptive recovery behaviors suitable for safety-critical healthcare settings.

In summary, the key objectives are to provide the HRI and Medical HRI communities with a standardized, multimodal framework for evaluating robot failures and for building transparent, adaptive, and trustworthy recovery systems by addressing the identified gaps in prior research concerning longitudinal effects, fine-grained classification, and user-centered recovery.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

REPAIR-Bench is built upon the RFM-HRI dataset and introduces three novel tasks spanning the lifecycle of human-robot interaction failures. The methodology involves extensive data preprocessing, feature extraction, and the development of specialized baseline models for each task.

**A. Dataset and Preprocessing**
- **Base Dataset:** REPAIR-Bench utilizes the RFM-HRI dataset, which comprises 214 interaction trials from 41 participants in both laboratory and hospital settings. The dataset captures interactions with a semi-autonomous medical crash-cart robot designed for item retrieval.
- **Failure Conditions:** The original study included five trials per participant: four experimentally induced robot failure conditions (speech failure, timing failure, comprehension failure, search failure) and one error-free success condition.
- **Data Modalities:** Each trial includes synchronized video, audio, and task transcripts. Participants also completed post-interaction surveys reporting emotional reactions (Self-Assessment Manikin, SAM) and preferred recovery strategies (e.g., apology, clarification, step-by-step guidance, escalation).
- **Feature Extraction:**
 - **Visual:** Facial Action Units (18 intensity features), gaze estimates, and 3D head pose (pitch, yaw, roll) were extracted using Google MediaPipe. 3D facial landmarks (468 keypoints per frame) were also obtained.
 - **Audio/Linguistic:** Time-stamped speech transcripts were generated using the Whisper Automatic Speech Recognition (ASR) model.
- **Sample Exclusions:** 30 samples were excluded due to duplicated/near-identical transcripts or insufficient linguistic evidence for reliable failure localization/characterization, resulting in a final analysis set of 184 samples.
- **Temporal Failure Annotations:** For failure trials, precise onset and offset timestamps were annotated, defining the ground truth for temporal localization tasks. Onset was defined by explicit robot utterances corresponding to the failure condition.
- **MediaPipe Feature Preprocessing:** Frames with failed face detection were excluded, and missing values were linearly interpolated. A bidirectional second-order Butterworth filter (6 Hz) was applied to reduce high-frequency noise. Features were then normalized by range per feature per sample. Features with near-zero variance (e.g., depth coordinates from pose/landmarks, full 468-point facial landmarks) were excluded, resulting in 21 channels per frame (18 AU + 3 head pose).
- **Transcript Preprocessing:** For recovery modeling, timestamps were removed, and natural language dialogue was preserved. A supervised instruction-tuning dataset was constructed with structured prompts containing the robot's role, transcript, success/failure label, candidate strategies, and gold-standard user-preferred recovery strategies as comma-separated outputs.

**B. Task 1: Failure Detection**
- **Definition:** To detect the temporal moment of robot failure inference based on the user’s visual behavioral reaction, accounting for cross-trial dependencies.
- **Inputs:** Sequential visual features (facial AUs, head pose) extracted in sliding windows. Each window generates summary statistics (mean, min, max, std dev) as a fixed-dimensional feature vector.
- **Outputs:** (i) A binary label per window (pre-failure/post-failure state) and (ii) a single temporal prediction (in seconds relative to trial onset) for the transition moment.
- **Ground Truth:** The failure offset (end of the robot's failed response) was used as the ground-truth transition moment.
- **Architecture:** A two-level Hierarchical Recurrent Neural Network (HRNN) with Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) cells was the best-performing model.
 - **Window Level:** A single-layer GRU processes window feature vectors, producing per-window logits for pre/post-failure classification and a trial embedding from its final hidden state.
 - **Session Level:** A separate GRU cell maintains a history vector, updated after each trial with the trial embedding and a binary failure indicator. This history vector is projected and used to initialize the window-level GRU's hidden state, conditioning current-trial processing on prior interaction history.
- **Localization:** Per-window logits were soft-maxed to obtain continuous pre-failure probabilities, summed across windows to predict the transition index, and supervised with a Huber regression loss.
- **Training:** Data was split at the participant level (75%/25%). Participants were shuffled, but trials within participants were processed sequentially to maintain temporal order. Loss combined window-level cross-entropy (with inverse-frequency weighting and label smoothing) and a Huber localization term. AdamW optimizer was used with cosine annealing.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Macro-averaged precision, recall, and F1 for binary classification at the window level. Mean and median absolute localization error (seconds), mean signed error, and F1 at temporal Intersection-over-Union (tIoU) thresholds (0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75) at the trial level.

**C. Task 2: Failure Classification**
- **Definition:** To classify the interaction into one of five condition types (comprehension, speech, timing, search failures, or success) using visual time-series features, considering the user's previous session experience.
- **Architecture:** A two-level, session-aware temporal model, also an HRNN variant.
 - **Cross-Session Level:** A bidirectional LSTM encodes frame-wise features within each trial, aggregated into a session embedding via a failure-aware temporal attention mechanism.
 - **Current Session Level:** A unidirectional GRU processes the sequence of trial embeddings chronologically to capture cross-interaction dependencies.
 - The final prediction is a multi-layer perceptron combining the trial embedding with its session-level context.
- **Training:** Participant-level data split (75%/25%) with sequential processing of trials within participants.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Standard multi-class precision, recall, and F1-score.

**D. Task 3: Recovery Strategy Prediction**
- **Definition:** To generate the top-k most appropriate recovery actions given the interaction transcript, failure label, and a predefined set of candidate recovery strategies. For successful interactions, an empty response is required.
- **Architecture:** Both base and QLoRA-fine-tuned Small Language Models (SLMs) were evaluated: Mistral-7B and Gemma-9B.
- **Fine-Tuning:** QLoRA (Quantized Low Rank Adaptation) was used for parameter-efficient fine-tuning on an NVIDIA A100 GPU (40GB) with 4-bit normal float (NF4) quantization. LoRA adapters were inserted into attention and MLP layers.
- **Training:** Random dataset split (60% train, 20% validation, 20% test). Prompts were tokenized, truncated, and padded. Training used paged AdamW optimizer with a causal language modeling objective. Only LoRA adapter parameters were updated. Early stopping was implemented.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Set-based Top-k metrics for k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5}: Hit@k (at least one gold strategy in top-k), Precision@k, Recall@k, and F1@k.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Task 1: Failure Detection**
The evaluation for failure detection was conducted at two levels: window-level binary classification (pre- vs. post-failure) and trial-level transition moment localization.

- **Window-Level Classification:**
 - The two-level Hierarchical Recurrent Neural Network (HRNN) with 3-second windows achieved the highest performance, with a macro F1-score of 0.80.
 - Its performance increased to 0.87 with a ±3s tolerance, and it attained an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.87.
 - This represents an improvement of 0.15 in F1-score compared to a standard GRU baseline.
 - Other models, including Random Forest (RF) and flat GRU/BiLSTM with history features, performed lower, with F1-scores ranging from 0.61 to 0.69.
- **Transition Moment Localization:**
 - The two-level HRNN successfully detected all 30 failure trials in the held-out participant set.
 - It achieved a median absolute error (MedAE) of 2.97 seconds and a mean Intersection-over-Union (mIoU) of 0.781.
 - The overall mean signed error was -0.51 seconds, indicating a slight tendency towards early prediction of the failure offset.
 - Performance varied slightly across failure types: Comprehension failures had the lowest MedAE (2.06 s) and highest mIoU (0.89), while Timing failures had the highest MedAE (4.38 s) and lowest mIoU (0.68).

**B. Task 2: Failure Classification**
This task focused on classifying the specific type of robot failure (comprehension, search, speech, timing, or success) using visual time-series features.

- The Hierarchical Recurrent Neural Network (HRNN) achieved the best overall performance with a macro F1-score of 0.44.
- This represents an improvement over the BiLSTM-GRU model, which had a macro F1 of 0.38, despite both models having the same number of parameters (1,012K).
- **Per-Class Performance (HRNN):**
 - The model showed strong performance on the Success class, achieving an F1-score of 0.93 (Precision 1.00, Recall 0.88).
 - Detection of Speech failures also improved relative to the BiLSTM-GRU, reaching an F1 of 0.35 (Precision 0.25, Recall 0.57).
 - However, Comprehension (F1=0.22) and Timing (F1=0.29) failures remained more challenging to distinguish. Search failures had an F1 of 0.40.
- These results are consistent with statistical analysis showing only a modest association between self-reported emotion and specific failure conditions (Cramér’s V = 0.386).

**C. Task 3: Recovery Preference Prediction**
This task evaluated the ability of models to predict user-preferred recovery strategies given interaction transcripts and failure context.

- **Base Model Comparison:**
 - Mistral-7B (base model) outperformed Gemma-9B (base model) across all Top-k metrics. For Mistral-7B, Hit@5 was 0.68 and F1@5 was 0.30.
 - Gemma-9B's base performance was near zero across all Top-k metrics, indicating its struggle to generate valid structured recovery strategy sets without task-specific adaptation, likely due to it not being instruction-tuned.
- **Effect of QLoRA Fine-Tuning:**
 - QLoRA fine-tuning consistently improved performance for both Mistral-7B and Gemma-9B.
 - For Mistral-7B, QLoRA fine-tuning resulted in a Hit@5 of 0.76 and an F1@5 of 0.32, showing an increase over the base model's 0.68 Hit@5 and 0.30 F1@5.
 - Gemma-9B, after QLoRA fine-tuning, achieved performance comparable to the QLoRA-tuned Mistral-7B (e.g., Hit@5 of 0.68 and F1@5 of 0.29), demonstrating the critical role of domain adaptation.
- **Trends Across k:**
 - Improvements were more pronounced at higher k values for F1, recall, and Hit metrics, suggesting that fine-tuned models were better at capturing a set of acceptable user-preferred strategies rather than just the single most likely action.
 - A plateau in performance was observed at k=4–5, indicating increased difficulty in accurately ranking a larger number of candidate strategies, possibly reflecting inherent ambiguity in user preferences.

Overall, the findings indicate that hierarchical recurrent modeling improves failure detection by leveraging cross-session history, that multi-class failure classification is feasible but challenging for certain types using visual cues alone, and that QLoRA-based instruction tuning is effective for enabling Small Language Models to predict user-preferred recovery strategies with improved accuracy.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

REPAIR-Bench represents a significant contribution to the field of Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), particularly in the context of robotic systems operating in critical environments such as healthcare. Its primary significance lies in providing the first end-to-end benchmark that integrates multimodal failure detection, fine-grained failure classification, and user-centered recovery prediction within a single framework.

The potential impacts of this work are multi-faceted:

- **Advancing Trustworthy HRI:** By enabling robots to not only detect failures but also understand their specific nature and respond with strategies aligned with user preferences, REPAIR-Bench directly contributes to building more transparent, adaptive, and trustworthy robot systems. This is particularly crucial in safety-critical medical settings where human confidence and efficient recovery are paramount.
- **Enabling Adaptive Robot Behavior:** The benchmark supports research into developing robots that can adapt their behaviors dynamically. Instead of relying on static, rule-based responses, future systems can use the insights from REPAIR-Bench to infer user states and preferences, leading to more contextually appropriate and effective recovery actions.
- **Standardized Evaluation for the HRI Community:** REPAIR-Bench offers a standardized framework and evaluation protocol, which is critical for systematic progress in HRI research. It allows researchers to compare different models and approaches consistently, fostering innovation and accelerating development in robot error management.
- **Highlighting the Value of Longitudinal Data:** By explicitly modeling cross-trial dependencies and longitudinal user adaptation, the benchmark encourages a deeper understanding of how repeated interactions and accumulated failures influence user perception and behavior. This moves beyond treating interactions as isolated events, reflecting a more realistic and complex view of long-term HRI.
- **Driving Research in Multimodal Fusion:** The reliance on multimodal signals (facial AUs, head pose, speech transcripts) for detection, classification, and recovery prediction emphasizes the importance of integrating diverse data streams. This can spur further research into novel multimodal fusion techniques to capture subtle human reactions and intentions more effectively.
- **Informing Design of Recovery Systems:** The results, particularly from the recovery prediction task, demonstrate that fine-tuned language models can recommend user-preferred recovery actions. This finding has direct implications for the design of robotic systems, suggesting that integrating such predictive capabilities can lead to interactions that better meet human expectations and reduce user frustration or mistrust following errors.
- **Specific Impact on Medical HRI:** Built on a dataset from a medical context, REPAIR-Bench is poised to significantly impact Medical HRI. It provides tools to investigate how robots can better support medical professionals during time-critical tasks, even when failures occur, by understanding and mitigating their negative effects on user experience and task efficacy.

While acknowledging limitations such as the relatively small dataset for recovery prediction and the use of predefined strategies, REPAIR-Bench establishes a foundational framework that can be expanded to incorporate richer multimodal signals and be applied to additional HRI domains, further validating its robustness and real-world applicability.
