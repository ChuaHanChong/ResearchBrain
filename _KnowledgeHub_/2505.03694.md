# Demonstrating ViSafe: Vision-enabled Safety for High-speed Detect and Avoid

> [!summary] Summary
> Carnegie Mellon University's ViSafe system demonstrates a vision-only Detect and Avoid (DAA) capability for high-speed small Uncrewed Aerial Systems (sUAS). The system achieves safe separation in real-world scenarios with closure rates up to 144 km/h by integrating learning-based vision with Control Barrier Functions for certified safety.

## Problem

- Integrating small Uncrewed Aerial Systems (sUAS) into shared airspace is challenging due to the need for assured safe separation from other airborne vehicles.
- Existing Detect and Avoid (DAA) solutions primarily rely on cooperative surveillance (e.g., transponders, ADS-B) and are unsuitable for sUAS due to SWaP-C constraints and inability to detect non-cooperative entities.
- Prior vision-based DAA approaches lacked a comprehensive, full-stack solution for collision avoidance that provides provably safe runtime guarantees with real-world, noisy perceptual inputs.

## Method

- **ViSafe** is a full-stack, vision-only DAA system combining a learning-based edge-AI visual detection module (an extension of ==AirTrack==) with a custom SWaP-C optimized hardware prototype.
- It employs multi-view fusion and ==decoupled Kalman filters== for robust 3D state estimation of intruders from noisy visual data.
- A ==Control Barrier Function (CBF)-based Quadratic Program (QP)== provides real-time, provably safe runtime guarantees by minimally modifying the nominal control input to ensure safe separation.

## Results

- ViSafe reduced the Probability of Near Mid Air Collision (P(NMAC)) and Risk Ratio across all tested encounter geometries in both simulations and real-world tests, achieving P(NMAC) of **0.0** in high-speed (**144 km/h** closure rate) head-on real-world scenarios.
- The system consistently increased the minimum separation distance (e.g., from **30.54m** nominal to **46.05m** ViSafe in real-world high-speed head-on encounters) and improved horizontal closure rates, demonstrating safe aerial separation.
- Digital twin simulations provided a conservative estimate of ViSafe's performance compared to real-world tests and proved effective for large-scale evaluation (**12,000 trajectories**) and assessing robustness to varying environmental conditions, though the system showed limitations when intruders were consistently below the horizon.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - High-fidelity digital twin simulations, particularly with hardware-in-the-loop, can provide a conservative and reliable proxy for real-world performance, enabling extensive pre-deployment testing for safety-critical systems.
> - Integrating noisy vision-based perceptual inputs with formal safety guarantees from Control Barrier Functions requires robust state estimation techniques and careful CBF formulation to account for sensor limitations.
> - Vision-only Detect and Avoid systems can achieve high-speed collision avoidance performance, including for non-cooperative targets, while adhering to the stringent Size, Weight, Power, and Cost (SWaP-C) constraints of small Uncrewed Aerial Systems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The integration of Uncrewed Aerial Systems (UASs) into the National Airspace System (NAS) is a critical objective for unlocking their full economic and societal potential. However, ensuring safe separation from other airborne vehicles, especially in high-density or non-segregated airspace, remains a significant challenge. This paper directly addresses this challenge by introducing ViSafe, a novel vision-only Detect and Avoid (DAA) system.

#### 1.1. Current DAA Solutions and Limitations

Existing DAA solutions, such as the Traffic Collision and Avoidance System (TCAS) and its modern variants like the ACAS family (e.g., ACAS Xa, Xu), primarily rely on cooperative surveillance mechanisms. These systems typically depend on active sensor modalities like transponders, radars, and Automatic Dependent Surveillance-Broadcast (ADS-B) to communicate aircraft positions and intentions. While highly effective for large, equipped aircraft, these systems are largely unsuitable for small UAS (sUAS) due to stringent Size, Weight, Power, and Cost (SWaP-C) constraints. Furthermore, they cannot detect non-cooperative aerial entities such as balloons or rogue drones that do not transmit identifying signals. These limitations necessitate strict line-of-sight (LOS) requirements for human operators, severely restricting the scalability and utility of sUAS operations beyond visual line of sight (BVLOS).

#### 1.2. Vision-Based Approaches

Cameras offer a lightweight and cost-effective alternative for enabling DAA capabilities in sUAS. The rise of data-driven methods, particularly deep learning, has shown promise in detecting small objects in high-resolution images, a crucial capability for tracking distant aircraft. However, the real-world deployment and robust integration of these vision-based detection systems with downstream provably safe collision avoidance methods have remained a significant research gap. Prior work in vision-based detection, including the authors' own AirTrack system, has focused on accurate detection and tracking but typically lacked a comprehensive, full-stack solution for collision avoidance with safety guarantees.

#### 1.3. Control Barrier Functions in Robotics

Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) have emerged as a powerful tool in control theory for formally certifying the safety of robotic systems by ensuring that a system remains within a defined "safe set." While CBFs have been successfully applied in various domains, including safe navigation and manipulation, their application to airborne collision avoidance has often assumed global availability of information (e.g., precise state estimates from active sensors) and proprioceptive information. Deploying CBFs in real-world scenarios with noisy, uncertain, and intermittently available perceptual inputs, such as those from vision systems, poses significant challenges that prior work had not fully addressed.

#### 1.4. ViSafe's Contribution to the Landscape

ViSafe bridges these gaps by providing a full-stack, vision-only DAA solution specifically tailored for resource-constrained sUAS operating at high speeds. It innovatively combines state-of-the-art learning-based edge AI for visual perception (extending AirTrack to multi-camera inputs) with a custom SWaP-C optimized hardware prototype. Crucially, it integrates perceptual input-focused CBFs to provide provably safe runtime guarantees *without* relying on global information or communication from the intruder. This work stands out by:
1. **Enabling non-cooperative DAA:** Unlike traditional systems, ViSafe handles non-communicating threats.
2. **Addressing SWaP-C:** Its vision-only approach and custom hardware meet the strict constraints of small UAS.
3. **Bridging theory and practice:** It tackles the complexities of integrating noisy, real-world vision data with formal safety guarantees offered by CBFs, a significant challenge in safe autonomy.
4. **Setting a new benchmark:** Through extensive real-world flight tests with unprecedented closure rates, it demonstrates practical feasibility and performance in high-speed scenarios.

In essence, ViSafe advances the state-of-the-art by moving beyond theoretical formulations of safe control and towards a practical, rigorously tested, and deployable solution for autonomous collision avoidance in the challenging context of small, fast, and vision-equipped aerial vehicles.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations behind the development of ViSafe are multifaceted, aiming to address critical needs for the widespread adoption and safe operation of Uncrewed Aerial Systems (UAS) in shared airspace:

- **Ensure Assured Safe-Separation:** The fundamental goal is to guarantee that the ownship (the controlled UAS) maintains a safe distance from other aerial entities (intruders) at all times, preventing mid-air collisions. This is paramount for achieving seamless, high-density operations in congested airspaces.
- **Enable Vision-Only Detect and Avoid (DAA) Capability:** Motivated by the limitations of traditional DAA systems, a key objective is to develop a system that relies solely on passive visual sensing. This approach is lightweight, cost-effective, and suitable for resource-constrained small UAS (sUAS) where active sensors like transponders or radar are impractical due to Size, Weight, Power, and Cost (SWaP-C) constraints.
- **Provide Provably Safe Runtime Guarantees:** Beyond mere detection and avoidance, a core objective is to offer formal, provable safety guarantees during operation. This is achieved by leveraging Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), which mathematically encode and enforce safety thresholds, ensuring the system remains within a safe set.
- **Support High-Speed Aerial Operations:** The system is specifically designed and validated for high-speed scenarios, demonstrating effectiveness even with high closure rates (up to 144 km/h), pushing the boundaries of real-time collision avoidance.
- **Address Non-Cooperative Intruder Detection:** A crucial motivation is the ability to detect and avoid non-cooperative aerial entities (e.g., other drones without transponders, balloons) that do not transmit their position or intent. This significantly expands the range of threats a DAA system can mitigate.
- **Bridge the Sim-to-Real Gap for Safety-Critical Systems:** The project aims to develop a robust testing methodology, utilizing both high-fidelity digital twins and extensive real-world flight tests, to validate the system's performance across diverse scenarios (agent types, closure rates, interaction geometries, environmental conditions) and ensure reliability in real-world deployment.
- **Overcome Challenges of Vision-Based Perception in Control:** Acknowledging that vision-based state estimation is inherently noisy and prone to inaccuracies, an objective is to integrate these perceptual inputs effectively into a safety-critical control framework. This involves robust multi-view fusion and careful consideration of sensor limitations in the CBF design.
- **Facilitate Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) Operations:** By providing an onboard, autonomous DAA capability, ViSafe seeks to enable regulatory approvals for BVLOS operations of sUAS, thereby significantly enhancing their utility and scalability.

In essence, the motivation is to create a truly autonomous, compact, and reliable DAA system that can safely navigate sUAS through complex, dynamic airspace, addressing the current regulatory and technological bottlenecks for broader UAS integration.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ViSafe presents a comprehensive, full-stack solution to the Detect and Avoid (DAA) problem, tightly integrating a learning-based edge-AI framework with custom hardware and control theory. The approach emphasizes real-time performance, SWaP-C constraints, and provable safety.

#### 3.1. ViSafe Framework Overview

The ViSafe framework operates as a real-time loop (Fig. 2) that processes sensor data, estimates intruder states, computes safe maneuvers, and commands the ownship.
1. **Visual Detection Module:** Multi-camera video streams are fed into a visual detection system (an extension of AirTrack) to identify potential intruders.
2. **Multi-View Fusion & Coordinate Frame Conversion:** Detected intruder positions from multiple cameras are fused, tracked, and converted into a 3D North-East-Down (NED) coordinate system relative to the ownship.
3. **Supervisory Safety Controller:** This module, leveraging Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), takes the estimated intruder state and the ownship's nominal control input to compute modifications that ensure safe separation.
4. **Motor Control:** The computed safe control output is converted into low-level drone commands and executed by the ownship's autopilot.

#### 3.2. Visual Detection Module

ViSafe extends **AirTrack**, a state-of-the-art deep learning model for aircraft detection and tracking, to a multi-camera setting. The module is optimized for edge inference on resource-constrained hardware. It processes two consecutive grayscale image frames and outputs a list of tracked objects with attributes like bounding box, center, and predicted distance. Its sequential pipeline includes:
- **Frame Alignment:** Uses optical flow to align successive frames, discarding ego-camera motion to better distinguish foreground objects.
- **Detection:** A fully-convolutional HR-Net-W32 network identifies object centers, bounding box sizes, offsets, and log-scale distance. It operates on full-resolution (1224x1024) aligned images.
- **Secondary Classifier:** A ResNet-18 binary classifier is used for false-positive rejection by analyzing cropped regions around detected objects.
- **Image-level Tracking:** An offset tracking vector-based approach associates new detections with existing tracks or initializes new ones, ensuring consistent tracking.
The system runs AirTrack on video streams from two cameras at 8 Hz on an NVIDIA Orin AGX compute system.

#### 3.3. Multi-View Fusion & Coordinate Frame Conversion

Once an intruder is detected by AirTrack, its image-level track is unprojected into a 3D camera coordinate frame using the predicted distance, bounding box center, and calibrated camera intrinsics. This 3D position is then transformed into the ownship's local North-East-Down (NED) coordinate frame, leveraging multi-camera and IMU extrinsic calibration.
To handle sensor noise and fuse multi-view data, two **Kalman Filters** are employed for each detected intruder: one for the noisier range (distance) estimates and another for the smoother angular (azimuth and elevation) estimates. This decoupling helps maintain robust tracking. The module continuously propagates the intruder's position and velocity to the safety controller at 24 Hz. It also computes the intruder's velocity components (North, East) and heading (yaw) relative to the ownship using ownship odometry and the intruder's range, range rate, azimuth, and azimuth rate.

#### 3.4. Supervisory Safety Controller (CBF-QP)

The supervisory safety controller is designed to provide certified, real-time, and performant safety by minimally modifying a nominal control input. It utilizes a **Control Barrier Function (CBF)**-based Quadratic Program (QP).
- **Safe Set Definition:** The safe set `C` is defined by `h(x) <= 0`, where `h(x)` is the CBF. A key innovation in ViSafe's CBF formulation is `h(x) = c + d^n_thresh - d^n - k * dot(d)`, which is chosen to avoid singularity issues encountered with simpler distance-based CBFs. Here, `d` is the distance between agents, `d_thresh` is the minimum safe distance, and `c, n, k` are tunable hyperparameters.
- **CBF Condition:** The CBF ensures forward invariance of the safe set by enforcing `dot(h(x)) <= -lambda * h(x)`, where `lambda` controls the rate of change of the barrier function.
- **QP Formulation:** The optimal control input `u` is found by solving a Quadratic Program that minimizes the deviation from the nominal control input (`u_nom`) while satisfying the CBF safety constraint and actuation constraints (`u_min <= u <= u_max`):
 `arg min_u ||u - u_nom||^2`
 `s.t. dot(h(x)) <= -lambda * h(x)`
 `u_min <= u <= u_max`
The derivatives (`dot(d)` and `ddot(d)`) are derived based on a simple unicycle model for the ownship and a constant velocity assumption for the intruder, ensuring the constraints remain affine in `u` for efficient QP solving. The computed safe control `u_safe` is then converted into low-level drone commands (velocity and yaw setpoints).

#### 3.5. Hardware Prototype (ViSafe Payload)

The ViSafe system is deployed on a custom-built, SWaP-C optimized payload weighing approximately 1.6 kg, including its 3D-printed casing. Key components include:
- **Cameras:** Six Sony IMX264 cameras providing a wide 220° horizontal and 48° vertical Field of View (FOV).
- **Compute:** NVIDIA AGX Orin Developer Kit, providing the necessary edge-AI processing power.
- **Camera Adaptor:** Leopard Imaging LI-JXAV-MIPI-ADPT-6CAM-FP for multi-camera input.
- **State Estimation:** 3DM-GQ7 GNS/INSS module with a dual-GPS antenna for precise ownship localization and heading.
The payload communicates with the ownship autopilot (PX4 in simulation, Ardupilot in real-world) via the MavLink protocol, issuing yaw turn rates for avoidance maneuvers.

#### 3.6. Testing and Evaluation

ViSafe undergoes extensive evaluation through:
- **Experiment Design:** Two-agent interaction scenarios (ownship vs. intruder) across three collision geometries (Head-on, Overtake, Lateral), with variations for intruder altitude (Above/Below horizon), leading to 6 distinct setups.
- **Reaction Time Profile:** Leverages ASTM F3442/F3442M standards and empirical performance of AirTrack to determine reliable detection ranges (e.g., 287m for hexarotor, 525m for VTOL), establishing critical reaction times (e.g., 14.35s for hexarotor, 13.12s for VTOL in head-on scenarios), highlighting the need for fast DAA.
- **Simulated Digital-Twin Setup:** A high-fidelity "digital twin" is created using Nvidia Isaac Sim (based on Omniverse) and ArcGIS CityEngine to simulate realistic environments, including diverse weather and lighting conditions. This enables large-scale hardware-in-the-loop (HITL) benchmarking (12,000 trajectories across 60 distinct scenarios) before real-world deployment.
- **Real-World Experiment Setup:** Conducted at two distinct FAA-approved test facilities (Nardo Flight Test Field and Leesburg, VA). Utilizes various airborne platforms: Aurelia X6 (ownship), Hexarotor (low-speed intruder), and a VTOL quad-plane (high-speed intruder).
- **Metrics:** Performance is assessed using: Probability of Near Mid Air Collision (P(NMAC)), Separation Minima (minimum distance), Horizontal Rate of Closure (HROC), and Risk Ratio (collision ratio vs. nominal baseline).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive testing campaign, encompassing both simulated digital twins and real-world flight tests, demonstrated ViSafe's effectiveness and robustness across diverse scenarios. The results consistently highlighted its superior performance compared to a nominal planner without an explicit avoidance strategy.

#### 4.1. High-Speed Collision Avoidance Capability

ViSafe significantly reduces collision risk across all tested encounter geometries (Head-on, Overtake, Lateral).
- **Reduced P(NMAC) and Risk Ratio:** In digital twin simulations, ViSafe drastically reduced the Probability of Near Mid Air Collision (P(NMAC)) and Risk Ratio, which measures collisions relative to the nominal planner. For instance, in the digital twin, P(NMAC) for E1 (Head-on) dropped from 1.0 (nominal) to 0.55. In real-world tests, these improvements were even more pronounced, with E4 (high-speed VTOL Head-on) achieving a P(NMAC) and Risk Ratio of 0.0 with ViSafe, meaning no collisions occurred.
- **Increased Separation Minima:** ViSafe consistently increased the minimum separation distance between agents. In digital twin E1, the separation minima increased from 19.9m (nominal) to 35.55m (ViSafe). Real-world results showed similar, often greater, improvements (e.g., E4 Head-on: 30.54m nominal vs. 46.05m ViSafe).
- **Improved Horizontal Rate of Closure (HROC):** ViSafe consistently resulted in higher (less negative or more positive) HROC values, indicating that agents were moving apart or approaching each other at a significantly reduced rate. For example, in real-world head-on scenarios, the average HROC improved from -19.25 m/s (nominal) to -13.83 m/s (ViSafe), demonstrating effective collision mitigation.
- **Benchmark Setting:** ViSafe successfully demonstrated safe aerial separation in real-world encounter scenarios with closure rates up to 144 km/h (E4), setting a new benchmark for vision-only autonomous collision avoidance.

#### 4.2. Robustness to Environmental Conditions

The digital twin experiments specifically evaluated ViSafe's robustness to varying weather and lighting conditions (e.g., no clouds, overcast, dusk, fog).
- Despite some minor increases in risk ratios under certain challenging weather conditions (attributed to detection and tracking failures in the vision system), the overall risk ratios across all trajectories remained considerably lower than the nominal values.
- ViSafe consistently achieved higher HROC values than the nominal planner across all simulated weather conditions (Fig. 6), indicating its ability to maintain safe and divergent trajectories even under adverse environmental factors.

#### 4.3. Digital-Twin Fidelity

A key finding was the strong alignment between the performance metrics observed in the digital-twin environment and those obtained during real-world testing.
- The digital twin provides a **conservative estimate** of ViSafe's performance, meaning the risk ratios in simulation were slightly higher than in real-world tests (e.g., simulated E1 P(NMAC) = 0.55 vs. real-world E1 P(NMAC) = 0.33). This is beneficial as it allows for rigorous testing and identification of edge cases in a safe, low-cost environment, ultimately leading to more robust real-world operations.
- The use of hardware-in-the-loop (HITL) simulation, where the actual ViSafe payload processed rendered images from the simulator, effectively minimized the sim-to-real gap, lending high credibility to the simulation results.
- The extensive scale of digital twin testing (12,000 trajectories) allowed for the capture of a wider range of edge cases and performance variations than would be feasible in real-world trials alone.

**Limitations Acknowledged in Findings:** While largely successful, the authors acknowledge that the current system struggled when the intruder was consistently **below the horizon**. This limitation is primarily attributed to the vision-based inference system (AirTrack) having difficulty establishing consistent tracks against rapidly changing backgrounds in such scenarios, a known bias from the training data distribution.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ViSafe represents a significant advancement in autonomous aerial navigation and collision avoidance, with profound implications for the future of Uncrewed Aerial Systems (UAS) and shared airspace management.

#### 5.1. Benchmarking and Advancing DAA

The most immediate significance of ViSafe lies in its **benchmark-setting performance** for vision-only airborne collision avoidance. By successfully demonstrating safe separation at unprecedented closure rates of up to 144 km/h in real-world flight tests, ViSafe pushes the boundaries of what is achievable with passive sensing. This establishes a new standard for high-speed DAA systems and provides concrete empirical evidence for the viability of vision-based solutions in safety-critical applications. This advancement is crucial for:
- **Integration into Shared Airspace:** It addresses a critical safety barrier to the widespread integration of sUAS into non-segregated airspace alongside manned aircraft and other drones.
- **Non-Cooperative Threat Mitigation:** Unlike traditional DAA systems that rely on transponders, ViSafe's vision-only approach enables detection and avoidance of non-cooperative targets, which include rogue drones, balloons, or other unequipped aircraft, significantly enhancing overall airspace safety.

#### 5.2. Addressing SWaP-C Constraints

ViSafe's design, adhering to strict Size, Weight, Power, and Cost (SWaP-C) constraints, provides a practical and deployable solution for resource-constrained sUAS. This is a crucial distinction from existing DAA solutions that are typically too heavy, power-intensive, or expensive for smaller platforms. By proving the efficacy of a lightweight, onboard, vision-only system, ViSafe opens up new operational possibilities for sUAS, including:
- **Beyond Visual Line of Sight (BVLOS) Operations:** Enabling robust DAA capabilities on sUAS is a key requirement for obtaining regulatory approval for BVLOS operations, which is essential for scaling applications in package delivery, infrastructure inspection, agriculture, and emergency response.
- **Increased Autonomy and Reliability:** The full-stack, edge-AI solution allows for real-time decision-making onboard the drone, reducing reliance on external ground control or communication infrastructure, leading to more autonomous and reliable operations.

#### 5.3. Future Directions and Challenges

The work also highlights critical challenges and outlines clear pathways for future research, underscoring its impact as a foundational study:
- **Bridging Theory and Practice of CBFs:** ViSafe demonstrates a successful real-world application of Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) with noisy perceptual inputs, an area where theoretical guarantees often conflict with practical deployment realities. The authors' "requirement refinement" approach, using empirical performance statistics of the vision system to account for uncertainty, offers a valuable blueprint for other safety-critical systems.
- **Robustness to Perception Failures:** While ViSafe uses Kalman filtering to mitigate vision inaccuracies, the authors explicitly identify the need for "Robust CBFs" to explicitly handle out-of-distribution inputs and perceptual false negatives, which is a promising area for continued research to further enhance safety.
- **Interaction-Aware Avoidance:** The current CBF design assumes a constant velocity and non-reactive intruder. Future work on adaptive control barrier functions for interaction-aware avoidance would significantly enhance the versatility and real-world applicability of ViSafe in more complex, multi-agent scenarios.
- **Improving Below-Horizon Performance:** Addressing the identified limitation of tracking objects below the horizon (e.g., through improved training data, optical flow, or temporal information) is critical for comprehensive airspace coverage.
- **Digital Twin as a Safety Tool:** The study validates the "digital twin" methodology as a reliable and conservative proxy for real-world performance evaluation, proving its utility for pre-deployment safety analysis and identifying edge cases. This reinforces the importance of high-fidelity simulation in certifying autonomous systems.

In conclusion, ViSafe's demonstration of a robust, vision-only, high-speed collision avoidance system represents a crucial step towards the safe and scalable integration of autonomous UAS into the global airspace. Its full-stack approach, rigorous testing, and clear identification of future research avenues solidify its position as a significant contribution to the field of robotic safety and autonomous navigation.
