# Quiet-STaR: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Think Before Speaking

> [!summary] Summary
> Quiet-STaR enables language models to self-supervise the learning of internal reasoning processes, or 'thoughts,' by optimizing for improved future token prediction in general text. This method leads to gains of up to 10.9% on zero-shot CommonsenseQA and 5.0% on GSM8K by allowing models to "think before speaking" internally.

## Problem

- Existing language model reasoning methods often require explicit prompting or rely on expensive, task-specific, human-annotated reasoning datasets.
- Lack of a generalizable and scalable mechanism for language models to acquire reasoning abilities directly from arbitrary, unstructured text.
- The computational inefficiency of generating intermediate thoughts sequentially for every token position during training.

## Method

- **Quiet-STaR** introduces a ==think, talk, learn== cycle where the language model generates internal multi-token rationales before predicting the next token.
- Employs a ==parallel sampling algorithm== using specialized attention masks and custom ==learnable meta-tokens== (`<|startofthought|>`, `<|endofthought|>`) for efficient simultaneous thought generation.
- Utilizes a ==learned mixing head== to blend predictions from the base model and the thought-augmented model, optimizing rationale generation via ==REINFORCE== with a ==non-myopic reward function==.

## Results

- Improved zero-shot accuracy on reasoning benchmarks: **10.9%** gain on CommonsenseQA (from **36.3% to 47.2%**) and **5.0%** gain on GSM8K (from **5.9% to 10.9%**).
- Improvements in reasoning capabilities consistently scaled with the length of the internal thoughts generated during training.
- Quiet-STaR models disproportionately improve prediction for difficult-to-predict tokens in natural text and enhance explicit Chain-of-Thought prompting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reasoning abilities can be implicitly learned by language models from general text data by optimizing for improved future token prediction.
> - Allowing models to generate multi-token internal 'thoughts' is more effective for acquiring reasoning skills than single-token pauses.
> - Internal reasoning learned through Quiet-STaR can enhance the quality and effectiveness of explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning during inference.
