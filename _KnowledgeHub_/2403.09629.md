# Quiet-STaR: Language Models Can Teach Themselves to Think Before Speaking

> [!summary] Summary
> Quiet-STaR enables language models to self-supervise the learning of internal reasoning processes, or 'thoughts,' by optimizing for improved future token prediction in general text. This method leads to gains of up to 10.9% on zero-shot CommonsenseQA and 5.0% on GSM8K by allowing models to "think before speaking" internally.

## Problem

- Existing language model reasoning methods often require explicit prompting or rely on expensive, task-specific, human-annotated reasoning datasets.
- Lack of a generalizable and scalable mechanism for language models to acquire reasoning abilities directly from arbitrary, unstructured text.
- The computational inefficiency of generating intermediate thoughts sequentially for every token position during training.

## Method

- **Quiet-STaR** introduces a ==think, talk, learn== cycle where the language model generates internal multi-token rationales before predicting the next token.
- Employs a ==parallel sampling algorithm== using specialized attention masks and custom ==learnable meta-tokens== (`<|startofthought|>`, `<|endofthought|>`) for efficient simultaneous thought generation.
- Utilizes a ==learned mixing head== to blend predictions from the base model and the thought-augmented model, optimizing rationale generation via ==REINFORCE== with a ==non-myopic reward function==.

## Results

- Improved zero-shot accuracy on reasoning benchmarks: **10.9%** gain on CommonsenseQA (from **3**6.3%** to 47.2%**) and **5.0%** gain on GSM8K (from **5.**9%** to 10.9%**).
- Improvements in reasoning capabilities consistently scaled with the length of the internal thoughts generated during training.
- Quiet-STaR models disproportionately improve prediction for difficult-to-predict tokens in natural text and enhance explicit Chain-of-Thought prompting.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reasoning abilities can be implicitly learned by language models from general text data by optimizing for improved future token prediction.
> - Allowing models to generate multi-token internal 'thoughts' is more effective for acquiring reasoning skills than single-token pauses.
> - Internal reasoning learned through Quiet-STaR can enhance the quality and effectiveness of explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning during inference.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

"Quiet-STaR" is situated within the rapidly evolving landscape of large language models (LLMs) and their ability to reason, generalize, and learn from data. It directly extends the line of research focused on enabling LLMs to perform complex reasoning tasks by generating intermediate steps or "thoughts."

**Building on Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and Self-Correction:** The paper acknowledges the significant impact of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting (Wei et al., 2022b; Kojima et al., 2022), scratchpads (Nye et al., 2021), and self-talk (Shwartz et al., 2020) in eliciting reasoning from LMs. These methods typically involve explicitly prompting the model to "think step by step" or generating visible reasoning traces. While effective, they often rely on specific task formats (e.g., question-answering) and may require carefully curated prompts or datasets. Quiet-STaR seeks to move beyond this explicit, task-specific approach.

**Advancing Self-Play and Self-Improvement:** A core predecessor to Quiet-STaR is "Self-Taught Reasoner" (STaR, Zelikman et al., 2022), which showed that LMs can iteratively improve their reasoning on question-answering datasets by sampling rationales and learning from those that lead to correct answers. Quiet-STaR generalizes this concept significantly. Whereas STaR was confined to specific QA tasks and relied on correctness feedback, Quiet-STaR aims for a more general form of reasoning learned from arbitrary, unstructured text by optimizing for improved future token prediction. This aligns with a broader trend in AI towards self-supervised learning and reinforcement learning from self-generated data, reminiscent of self-play in game AI (Silver et al., 2017).

**Distinction from Curated Reasoning Datasets:** Many efforts to train LMs for reasoning involve fine-tuning on datasets with human-annotated reasoning traces (Rajani et al., 2019; Wei et al., 2021a). The authors highlight the drawbacks of this approach: manual annotation is expensive, difficult to scale, can be off-policy (the reasoning might not be what the LM would naturally generate), and limited by human annotator capabilities. Quiet-STaR attempts to overcome these limitations by deriving reasoning signals implicitly from the language modeling objective itself, leveraging the vastness of internet-scale text.

**Novelty in Learning Internal Thoughts:** The paper carves out a distinct niche by enabling LMs to learn to generate "unstated rationales" or "implicit thinking" that helps them better predict *future text*. This is a significant departure from methods where reasoning is either explicitly prompted or provided as labeled data. The idea that "reasoning is implicit in almost all written text" forms the philosophical underpinning. By integrating "thinking" directly into the language modeling objective, Quiet-STaR proposes a scalable and general mechanism for acquiring reasoning abilities.

**Relationship with Meta-tokens:** The work also intersects with the use of "meta-tokens" or "function vectors" (Todd et al., 2023) in LMs, such as prompt-tuning (Lester et al., 2021) or context compression tokens (Mu et al., 2024). Quiet-STaR introduces custom learnable `<|startofthought|>` and `<|endofthought|>` tokens. This differentiates it from "pause tokens" (Goyal et al., 2023), which are single tokens acting as pauses, whereas Quiet-STaR's tokens initiate and delimit multi-token internal thoughts. The paper argues that multi-token rationales are more effective for reasoning than single-token pauses.

In essence, Quiet-STaR represents a move towards more autonomous and generalizable reasoning in LLMs, shifting from explicit task-specific reasoning or human-curated traces to implicitly learning reasoning from the fundamental task of language modeling itself. It suggests that the vast amount of unstructured text data contains sufficient implicit reasoning cues for models to learn sophisticated thought processes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of Quiet-STaR is to train Language Models (LMs) to learn general reasoning abilities by inferring and generating "unstated rationales" or "internal thoughts" within arbitrary text, which in turn helps them better predict future tokens. This approach is motivated by several observations and aims to address key limitations of existing reasoning methods in LMs:

**Motivation:**
1. **Implicit Reasoning in Text:** The authors posit that much of the meaning in text is "hidden between the lines" and that reasoning is implicit in almost all written content. For instance, the steps in a mathematical proof or the theory of mind in a conversation often involve unstated rationales.
2. **LM Benefits from Reasoning:** Prior work has consistently shown that LMs benefit significantly from reasoning, improving performance across various tasks from commonsense reasoning to programming and theorem proving (e.g., Chain-of-Thought prompting).
3. **Limitations of Existing Reasoning Paradigms:**
 - **Task-Specific:** Many successful reasoning methods (like STaR, Zelikman et al. 2022) have focused on specific tasks (e.g., question-answering) or predefined sets of tasks, often requiring carefully curated datasets or explicit prompting. This limits their generalizability and scalability.
 - **Reliance on Curated Data:** Training LMs on human-annotated reasoning traces is expensive, labor-intensive, difficult to scale, and can lead to models learning from "off-policy" data (reasoning that the LM wouldn't naturally generate). It also restricts the complexity of problems the model can learn to solve to what annotators are capable of.
4. **Leveraging Language Modeling:** Building on the idea that "language models are unsupervised multitask learners" (Radford et al., 2019), the authors propose that the task of language modeling itself, when augmented with internal thoughts, can be a powerful avenue for teaching general reasoning. If reasoning is implicit in all text, then the very act of predicting language should be able to teach it.

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Generalize STaR:** To extend the Self-Taught Reasoner (STaR) framework from learning reasoning on specific question-answering datasets to learning from diverse, unstructured internet text corpora. The goal is for the LM to learn to generate reasoning that helps it infer future text, thereby acquiring a broader range of reasoning skills.
2. **Improve Future Text Prediction:** The core objective is to optimize the LM such that the generation of intermediate thoughts (rationales) improves its ability to predict subsequent tokens in a sequence. The success criterion is not just generating "correct" thoughts, but generating thoughts that *help* the LM's primary task of language modeling.
3. **Address Computational Feasibility:** Develop efficient algorithms to allow for the generation and learning from internal thoughts at scale. This specifically targets the challenge of generating rationales after *every* token in parallel, which would be computationally prohibitive if done naively.
4. **Enable Self-Learning of Thought Usage:** Facilitate the LM's learning of *when* and *how* to generate and leverage internal thoughts. This involves introducing mechanisms for the LM to identify the start and end of a thought and to decide how much to incorporate thought-based predictions into its main output.
5. **Demonstrate Zero-Shot Reasoning Improvement:** Show that continued pretraining with Quiet-STaR on general web text leads to demonstrable improvements in zero-shot reasoning capabilities on established benchmarks, without any task-specific fine-tuning. This validates the generality of the learned reasoning.

In essence, Quiet-STaR seeks to unlock a more intrinsic and scalable path to reasoning for LMs by tightly integrating the "thinking" process with the fundamental language modeling objective, thereby allowing models to "teach themselves to think before speaking" in a highly generalizable manner.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Quiet-STaR introduces a novel method for training Language Models (LMs) to generate and learn from internal rationales by integrating a "think, talk, learn" cycle directly into the standard language modeling objective.

**Overall Framework (Think, Talk, Learn):**
The algorithm proceeds in three main steps during training for each sequence:
1. **Think (Parallel Rationale Generation):** The model generates internal thoughts (rationales) at multiple positions within the input text.
2. **Talk (Mixing Post-Rationale and Base Predictions):** The model produces its next-token prediction by blending the predictions made with and without the generated thoughts.
3. **Learn (Optimizing Rationale Generation):** The model updates its parameters to favor generating thoughts that lead to better predictions of future text, using a reinforcement learning signal.

**Detailed Breakdown of Methodology:**

1. **Parallel Rationale Generation (`think`):**
 - **Challenge:** Generating thoughts for every token position in a sequence would be computationally expensive if done sequentially.
 - **Solution: Parallel Sampling Algorithm:** The core innovation here is an attention mask strategy. Instead of separate forward passes, Quiet-STaR constructs a special attention mask that allows thought tokens to attend to themselves, preceding thought tokens within the same thought, and the preceding text, but *not* to tokens on other "counterfactual" paths. This allows the LM to generate continuations (thoughts) for *all* token positions in parallel within a single inference call. Each call generates one additional thought token for all text tokens simultaneously. This vastly improves computational efficiency.
 - **Meta-tokens:** Two learnable custom tokens, `<|startofthought|>` and `<|endofthought|>`, are introduced.
 - `<|startofthought|>`: Injected after each token `x_i` where a thought is to be generated, signaling the model to enter a "thinking mode."
 - `<|endofthought|>`: Injected after the thought sequence, signaling the model to conclude the thought and prepare for predicting the subsequent main text.
 - **Initialization:** These embeddings are initialized to the embedding of an em dash ("---"), a character often associated with pauses or internal thoughts, leveraging the LM's pre-existing knowledge.
 - **Optimization:** Their gradients are weighted higher during the update step to accelerate their learning.

2. **Mixing Post-Rationale and Base Predictions (`talk`):**
 - **Challenge:** Early in training, generated thoughts might be out-of-distribution and initially degrade the LM's performance.
 - **Solution: Mixing Head:** A shallow Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) acts as a "mixing head." This head takes as input a concatenation of the hidden state after the thought (`<|endofthought|>`) and the hidden state of the original text token (before the thought). It outputs a scalar weight. This weight determines the extent to which the next-token predicted logits from the LM *with* the thought are incorporated, compared to the logits from the base LM *without* the thought. This learned interpolation helps smooth the training process by allowing the model to gradually rely more on thoughts as they become more useful.

3. **Optimizing Rationale Generation (`learn`):**
 - **Reinforcement Learning (REINFORCE):** Quiet-STaR uses REINFORCE to provide a learning signal for generating better rationales.
 - **Reward Definition:** For each sampled rationale `T_j`, the reward `r_j` is defined as the difference between the log-likelihood of the true `n_true` next tokens *when using that rationale* (`logp_talk_j:j+n_true`) and the *average* log-likelihood across all sampled rationales for that token (`log_p_talk_j:j+n_true`).
 - `r_j = log p_talk(X_{j+1:j+n_true+1}) - log p_talk(X_{j+1:j+n_true+1})`
 - **Positive Reward Focus:** Only positive rewards (`r_j > 0`) contribute to the REINFORCE loss, meaning the model is only penalized for generating thoughts that actively *harm* prediction compared to the average, and rewarded for thoughts that *help*. This promotes stable training.
 - **Non-Myopic Loss / Teacher-Forcing:** The loss considers not just the immediate next token but `n_true` tokens ahead. Teacher forcing is applied by assuming the model selected the correct ground-truth token, allowing the computation of log-probabilities for future tokens given the thought. This encourages thoughts to be useful for a longer horizon of prediction.
 - **Simultaneous Updates:** The gradients from the REINFORCE loss update both the main LM parameters and the embeddings of the `<|startofthought|>` and `<|endofthought|>` tokens.
 - **Combined Objective:** The overall loss function includes both the REINFORCE term (to optimize rationale generation) and a standard negative log-likelihood (NLL) loss term (to ensure the LM continues to optimize its base language modeling ability and the mixing head).

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Base Model:** Mistral 7B (Jiang et al., 2023) is used as the foundational LM.
- **Training Data:** Primarily OpenWebMath (Paster et al., 2023), a dataset chosen for its anticipated higher density of reasoning-requiring tokens, and also evaluated on Colossal Clean Crawled Corpus (C4, Raffel et al., 2020) for broader applicability.
- **Evaluation:** Zero-shot direct question-answering accuracy on GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021) and CommonsenseQA (Talmor et al., 2018), without any task-specific fine-tuning. Perplexity improvement on difficult tokens in natural text is also measured.

This methodology represents a sophisticated integration of reinforcement learning and specialized architectural components (meta-tokens, mixing head) within the standard transformer framework to enable LMs to self-supervise the learning of internal reasoning processes.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Quiet-STaR demonstrates encouraging results, particularly in its ability to improve the zero-shot reasoning capabilities of language models by enabling them to learn implicit internal thoughts from general text data.

#### 4.1. Downstream Performance (Zero-Shot Reasoning)

The most significant finding is the improvement in zero-shot performance on challenging reasoning benchmarks, without any task-specific fine-tuning. This highlights the generalizability of the learned reasoning.
- **CommonsenseQA (Talmor et al., 2018):** Quiet-STaR improved accuracy from **36.3% to 47.2%**, an increase of **10.9%**.
- **GSM8K (Cobbe et al., 2021):** Accuracy increased from **5.9% to 10.9%**, a **5.0%** gain.
- **Scaling with Thought Length:** For both datasets, the improvements consistently increased with the number of tokens used in the model's internal rationales during Quiet-STaR training. This suggests that allowing for more extensive internal "thinking" directly translates to better reasoning performance.
- **Comparison to Baseline:** A baseline model trained on the same dataset without thought tokens showed no such improvements, confirming the efficacy of Quiet-STaR.
- **Training Data Impact:** Training on C4 (a more diverse web text corpus) also yielded improvements, albeit smaller (GSM8K: 5.9% → 8.1%; CommonsenseQA: 36.3% → 42.6%). This indicates that while specialized datasets like OpenWebMath (with higher reasoning density) might be more effective, the method is generally applicable.
- **Comparison to Pause Tokens:** Quiet-STaR significantly outperforms "pause tokens" (Goyal et al., 2023). While pause tokens showed minor gains on CommonsenseQA (26.9% to 28.8%), they harmed performance on GSM8K. This implies that Quiet-STaR's multi-token rationales are crucial for effective reasoning, distinguishing it from simpler single-token "pauses."

#### 4.2. Improvement Distribution and Qualitative Insights

- **Disproportionate Improvement on Difficult Tokens:** While the average improvement in next-token prediction for all tokens might be small, Quiet-STaR disproportionately helps in predicting *difficult-to-predict tokens*. This aligns with the intuition that easy tokens might not require extensive thought, but complex ones benefit greatly. This is visualized by a skewed distribution of log-probability changes, where a small fraction of hard tokens show substantial improvements.
- **Nature of Generated Rationales:** Although there's no explicit regularization for human interpretability, the generated thoughts are often partially understandable. Qualitatively, these rationales appear to help the model recall relevant information (e.g., theorems, next steps in a proof) that is useful for predicting subsequent text. Examples provided in the paper illustrate the model "thinking" about a chemical formula or the structure of a proof, leading to better predictions.

#### 4.3. Quiet-STaR and Chain-of-Thought (CoT)

- The paper explores the synergy between Quiet-STaR's internal rationales and explicit CoT prompting. When a Quiet-STaR trained Mistral model is used for zero-shot CoT (by adding "Let's think step by step."), its internal rationales improve the quality and coherence of the *external* chain of thought.
- **Enhanced CoT Performance:** On GSM8K, the majority-vote accuracy over 8 CoT samples (cot-maj@8) increased from 40.6% (baseline CoT) to 47.7% (Quiet-STaR + CoT). This indicates that the internally learned reasoning from Quiet-STaR can also manifest as improved external reasoning, which is a powerful combination.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Multiple Thoughts/Sequence & Multiple Tokens Ahead:** The study found that sampling multiple thoughts per sequence consistently outperformed a single-thought baseline. Similarly, predicting more than one token ahead (non-myopic loss) was beneficial, though the gains plateaued beyond two tokens ahead for some configurations. Qualitatively, more tokens ahead in supervision led to more coherent rationales.

In summary, the results strongly suggest that Quiet-STaR successfully teaches LMs a more general and scalable form of reasoning by embedding it within the fundamental language modeling task. The improvements are zero-shot, robust across different datasets (including general web text), scale with the depth of "thought," and enhance even explicit reasoning methods like CoT.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Quiet-STaR marks a significant advancement in the field of language model reasoning, offering a generalizable and scalable approach that addresses key limitations of prior methods. Its potential impact spans several areas:

#### 5.1. Towards Generalizable and Scalable Reasoning

- **Learning from Unstructured Text:** The most profound significance is demonstrating that LMs can learn to reason *generally* from diverse, unstructured web text, rather than being confined to curated reasoning tasks or datasets. This is a crucial step towards LMs that can reason across a multitude of domains and tasks encountered in natural language, truly embodying the "unsupervised multitask learner" paradigm.
- **Reduced Reliance on Human Annotation:** By allowing the LM to learn reasoning implicitly, Quiet-STaR reduces the need for expensive, labor-intensive, and potentially biased human-annotated reasoning traces. This opens pathways for more efficient and robust model development.
- **Foundation for Broader Reasoning Skills:** The method's ability to improve zero-shot performance on diverse reasoning tasks (math, commonsense) by training on general text suggests it teaches foundational reasoning skills applicable across various domains, paving the way for more adaptable and versatile LMs.

#### 5.2. Novel Training Mechanism

- **Implicit Thinking:** Quiet-STaR introduces a novel training paradigm where LMs learn to perform internal "thinking" without explicit prompting for reasoning steps during inference. This "quiet" internal process is optimized to improve the model's core language understanding and generation capabilities.
- **Efficient Parallel Sampling:** The proposed parallel sampling algorithm is a key methodological contribution, making the training procedure computationally feasible at scale. This innovation could inspire similar approaches for other complex self-supervised learning objectives in LMs.
- **Adaptive Thought Utilization:** The "mixing head" and non-myopic loss allow the model to learn not only *what* to think but also *how* to integrate those thoughts effectively, adapting its reliance on internal reasoning based on context and usefulness.

#### 5.3. Enhanced Model Capabilities

- **Improved Prediction for Difficult Tokens:** The finding that internal thoughts disproportionately aid in predicting difficult tokens suggests that Quiet-STaR helps LMs overcome ambiguities or complex dependencies in text that traditionally challenge them. This could lead to LMs with a deeper contextual understanding.
- **Synergy with Explicit Reasoning:** The demonstration that Quiet-STaR-trained models generate better external Chain-of-Thought reasoning (Net-of-Thought) highlights a complementary relationship. This indicates that internal learning can enhance external explainability and problem-solving, opening avenues for more robust hybrid reasoning systems.

#### 5.4. Future Directions and Practical Implications

- **Dynamic Compute Allocation:** The framework naturally lends itself to future work on dynamic inference. If the LM can predict when a thought will be useful (e.g., via the mixing head *before* generating the thought), it could selectively allocate compute for reasoning only when necessary, improving efficiency during deployment.
- **Deeper Understanding of LM Internal Processes:** While rationales are not explicitly regularized for interpretability, their partial comprehensibility offers a glimpse into the model's internal decision-making processes, contributing to the ongoing research on understanding and interpreting LLMs.
- **Stepping Stone to Human-like Cognition:** By learning to "think before speaking" and integrating reasoning into prediction, Quiet-STaR brings LMs a step closer to human-like cognitive processes, where implicit reasoning often precedes conscious expression.

**Limitations and Ethical Considerations:**
The paper acknowledges limitations such as not training from scratch or only applying to a 7B parameter model. The computational overhead during training is also noted, though framed as an advantage for leveraging additional compute. From an ethical standpoint, the authors wisely raise concerns about the faithfulness of generated rationales to the model's true internal processing and the risk of perpetuating harmful or biased reasoning patterns if the model finds them "useful" for prediction within biased datasets. These points underscore the ongoing need for research into interpretability, control, and safety in advanced AI systems.

Overall, Quiet-STaR represents a compelling contribution to the field, offering a promising new direction for imbuing language models with robust and generalizable reasoning abilities that are learned end-to-end from vast textual data.
