# Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning Enhances Activation Intensity and Diversity in the Internal Circuitry of LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University developed a framework to analyze how reinforcement learning (RL) fine-tuning alters the internal circuitry of large language models (LLMs). They found that online RL methods consistently increase activation intensity and diversity of internal pathways, while Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) shows weaker and inconsistent effects, a distinction supported by an intervention experiment establishing a causal link to performance.

## Problem

- Despite the empirical success of RL fine-tuning in enhancing LLM capabilities, the underlying internal mechanisms facilitating these improvements across diverse architectures remain underexplored.
- Existing mechanistic interpretability studies often do not correlate observed internal changes with the specific RL-based post-training methodologies used to obtain LLMs.
- It is challenging to transfer internal analysis strategies developed on simpler "toy problems" to the complex reasoning tasks where RL-induced improvements in LLMs are typically observed.

## Method

- The study adapted an efficient ==Edge Attribution Patching== (EAP) framework, conceptualizing LLMs as directed graphs of residual pathways to quantify the importance of internal information flow.
- A systematic procedure for filtering and truncating model-generated token sequences was developed, using self-entropy from partially truncated generations as the loss signal for internal analysis on mathematical problem-solving tasks.
- Three metrics	Activation Intensity, Information Complexity, and Distribution Kurtosis	were defined to quantify changes in overall engagement, heterogeneity, and concentration of internal pathway activations before and after RL fine-tuning.

## Results

- Online RL fine-tuning (PPO, GRPO) consistently increased "Activation Intensity" and "Information Complexity" while decreasing "Distribution Kurtosis" across multiple mathematical benchmarks, indicating broader and more diverse internal pathway engagement.
- DPO fine-tuning for Qwen2.5 showed inconsistent increases in activation intensity and weaker information complexity compared to online RL, though it also successfully reduced distribution kurtosis.
- An intervention experiment on Qwen2.5-**3B**-Instruct demonstrated that effective exploration (temperature **0.6**) during GRPO training causally led to significant internal circuit changes and a **51.2%** GSM8K accuracy, whereas noisy exploration (temperature 1.0) suppressed these internal evolutions and performance (**36.5%** accuracy).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Online RL fine-tuning systematically reshapes LLM internal circuitry by increasing both the redundancy (activation intensity) and flexibility (diversity in activation patterns) of information flow, which may explain its superior generalization ability.
> - A unified mechanistic view explains that online RL's on-policy sampling and dynamic gradient coefficients compel the network to activate and reinforce latent circuits, unlike DPO's reliance on a fixed support set.
> - Effective sampling in online RL causally drives the observed increases in internal activation intensity and diversity, establishing these internal changes as critical drivers, not just byproducts, of improved task performance.
