# SG-Former: Self-guided Transformer with Evolving Token Reallocation

> [!summary] Summary
> SG-Former introduces a hierarchical Vision Transformer that employs self-guided, evolving token reallocation to achieve efficient global self-attention with adaptive fine granularity. This architecture yields higher accuracy on ImageNet-1K classification, COCO object detection, and ADE20K semantic segmentation compared to prior Vision Transformers, frequently with reduced computational cost.

## Problem

- Vision Transformers incur quadratic computational and memory costs with input size, limiting their application to high-resolution images.
- Existing efficient Vision Transformers either sacrifice global context for local detail or lose fine-grained information for global context.
- Static token reduction methods fail to adaptively allocate computational resources based on the varying semantic importance of image regions.

## Method

- A hierarchical Transformer architecture is developed, featuring ==Self-guided Attention (SG-Attention)== and ==Hybrid-scale Attention== modules.
- SG-Attention dynamically reallocates Key and Value tokens based on a ==learned significance map==, assigning more tokens to salient regions for fine-grained analysis.
- Hybrid-scale Attention extracts multi-granularity information simultaneously and generates the significance map that guides ==evolving token reallocation== in SG-Attention.

## Results

- Achieved **84.7%** Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K (224x224) with SG-Former-B, surpassing Swin-B by **+1.3%** and CSWin-B by **+0.4%**.
- Demonstrated **51.3 mAP** and **45.2 mIoU** on COCO object detection and instance segmentation, outperforming Swin-B by **+2.8 mAP**.
- Attained **52.7 mIoU** on ADE20K semantic segmentation using UperNet, exceeding Swin-B by **+3 mIoU**, often with competitive or reduced parameter and FLOP counts.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Computational resources in Vision Transformers can be optimized by adaptively allocating more attention to semantically important image regions and less to less important ones.
> - A model's understanding of regional significance can be learned and dynamically refined, enabling self-guided and evolving token reallocation strategies.
> - Integrating hybrid-scale attention within a single layer effectively extracts both fine-grained local and coarse-grained global information while simultaneously generating a reliable significance map for adaptive processing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The development of Vision Transformers (ViTs) has been a significant breakthrough in computer vision, achieving state-of-the-art performance across various tasks by effectively modeling long-range dependencies, a limitation inherent in traditional convolutional neural networks (CNNs). However, the Achilles' heel of vanilla Transformers, especially ViTs, is their quadratic computational and memory cost with respect to the input sequence length (number of tokens). This quadratic complexity severely limits their application to high-resolution images or large feature maps, which are common in many dense prediction tasks like segmentation and detection.

The broader research landscape for ViTs is characterized by a strong push towards making them more efficient and scalable without sacrificing their performance advantages. Two primary strategies have emerged to address the quadratic cost:
1. **Local Fine-Grained Self-Attention:** Methods like Swin Transformer and CSWin Transformer restrict self-attention to local windows or regions. This reduces computation and preserves fine-grained local information but sacrifices the ability to model global context within a single attention layer.
2. **Global Coarse-Grained Self-Attention:** Approaches such as Pyramid Vision Transformer (PVT) and Focal Transformer aim to maintain a global receptive field by aggregating or reducing the number of tokens across the entire feature map. While preserving global context and reducing computation, these methods often lead to coarse-grained information processing, potentially losing fine details crucial for precise predictions.

SG-Former positions itself as a novel solution that seeks to reconcile these two seemingly contradictory goals. It aims to achieve **effective global self-attention with adaptive fine granularity**. Instead of rigidly predefining local windows or uniformly aggregating tokens, SG-Former proposes a dynamic, content-aware approach. It introduces the concept of **self-guided evolving token reallocation**, where the model itself learns to identify salient regions and allocates more computational resources (tokens) to them for fine-grained analysis, while allocating fewer tokens to less significant background regions for efficiency and global context. This adaptive strategy directly tackles the limitations of previous methods, which either prioritize local detail over global context or vice-versa, or employ static, input-agnostic token reduction schemes. By allowing the model to "guide itself" based on an evolving significance map, SG-Former represents a significant step towards more intelligent and resource-aware Vision Transformer architectures.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of the SG-Former paper is to develop a novel Vision Transformer architecture that can perform **efficient global self-attention with adaptive fine granularity**. This goal is motivated by the inherent limitations of existing Vision Transformer designs:

1. **High Computational Cost:** The quadratic complexity of self-attention in vanilla ViTs makes them computationally prohibitive for processing high-resolution images or large feature maps, which are essential for many computer vision tasks.
2. **Trade-off between Local and Global Information:** Current efficient Transformer designs often fall into one of two camps:
 - Those that achieve fine-grained attention by restricting it to local windows (e.g., Swin Transformer), thereby sacrificing the powerful long-range dependency modeling capability of global attention.
 - Those that maintain global receptive fields by uniformly aggregating tokens (e.g., PVT), leading to coarse-grained information loss and potential issues in salient regions.
3. **Lack of Adaptability:** Many existing token reduction strategies (e.g., fixed window sizes, uniform spatial reduction) are pre-defined and static, treating all image regions equally regardless of their semantic importance. This can lead to redundant computation in background areas and insufficient detail in crucial foreground objects.

To address these motivations, SG-Former proposes a key insight: **not all regions in an image contribute equally to understanding its content, and thus, not all regions require the same level of computational attention.** The motivation is to design a system that:
- Can dynamically identify salient (important) regions within an image.
- Allocate more tokens (and thus more fine-grained attention) to these salient regions.
- Allocate fewer tokens (for computational efficiency) to less important or background regions, while still maintaining a global receptive field.
- Allow this reallocation strategy to "evolve" during training, as the model's understanding of significance improves.

Essentially, the paper seeks to enable Transformers to intelligently focus their computational resources where they matter most, achieving a superior balance between efficiency, global context modeling, and fine-grained detail.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SG-Former introduces a novel hierarchical Transformer architecture that integrates two key components: **Self-guided Attention (SG-Attention)** and **Hybrid-scale Attention**. The overall pipeline follows a four-stage pyramid structure, similar to CNNs and other hierarchical Transformers, with patch embedding and downsampling layers between stages. Each of the first three stages consists of alternating Hybrid-scale Transformer blocks and Self-guided Transformer blocks.

#### 3.1. Self-Guided Attention (SG-Attention)

The core innovation for computational efficiency and adaptive granularity lies in SG-Attention. While Query (Q) tokens are kept at their original length, Key (K) and Value (V) tokens are reallocated and aggregated based on a dynamically estimated **significance map (S)**.
- **Importance Guided Aggregation Module (IAM):** This module takes the input feature map `X`, the significance map `S`, and an aggregation rate `r` to produce aggregated K and V tokens.
- **Significance-based Reallocation:** The significance map `S` (an `h x w` score map) is sorted, and the feature map `X` is divided into `n` sub-regions (`X_1..., X_n`) corresponding to varying levels of significance (`S_1..., S_n`).
- **Adaptive Aggregation Rates:** Each sub-region `X_i` is then aggregated using a specific rate `r_i`. Crucially, more important regions (higher significance) are assigned smaller `r_i` values (meaning fewer tokens are aggregated, preserving more fine-grained information), while less important regions (lower significance) are assigned larger `r_i` values (more tokens are aggregated, enhancing efficiency). The aggregation function `F(X, r)` is implemented using fully connected layers.
- **Self-Guidance and Evolution:** The term "self-guided" emphasizes that the significance map `S` is estimated directly by the Transformer itself. This means the token reallocation strategy is customized for each input image and evolves as the model learns to predict more accurate significance maps during training. This contrasts with static, human-defined strategies.

#### 3.2. Hybrid-scale Attention

The Hybrid-scale Attention module serves two critical purposes:
1. **Extracting Hybrid-scale Information:** It captures both fine-grained local and coarse-grained global information within a single self-attention layer without increasing computational cost beyond typical window attention. This is achieved by diversifying the receptive fields of different attention heads.
 - **Head Grouping and Scaling:** The total `H` attention heads are divided into `h` groups. Each group `j` is assigned a specific "scale" `s_j`.
 - **Token Merging:** For a given head `i` in group `j`, every `s_j x s_j` tokens in K and V are merged into one. This effectively enlarges the receptive field for that group by `s_j` times. Query window sizes are `s_j * M x s_j * M`, while Key/Value window sizes are `M x M`.
 - **Global Receptive Field:** In a special case, `s_j * M x s_j * M` can equal the full feature map size (`h x w`), allowing some heads to capture truly global information.
2. **Providing Significance for SG-Attention:** The significance map `S` used by SG-Attention is derived from the attention maps produced by the Hybrid-scale Attention. Specifically, `S_i` for each head `i` is calculated as the sum of all products of all tokens and the current token in its attention map, and the final `S` is the sum of `S_i` across all heads. This ensures that the significance map reflects the model's learned understanding of region importance across various scales.

#### 3.3. Transformer Block Design

SG-Former constructs its hierarchical architecture using two types of Transformer blocks:
- **Hybrid-scale Transformer Block:** Incorporates the Hybrid-scale Attention mechanism.
- **Self-guided Transformer Block:** Incorporates the Self-guided Attention mechanism.
These blocks alternate within the first three stages of the pyramid architecture. The final stage uses a vanilla Transformer block for simplicity, following common practices. The model also employs convolutional patch embedding and a locally-enhanced detail-specific feedforward layer, as seen in other recent efficient ViTs.

#### 3.4. Model Variants

Three model variants are proposed: SG-Former-S (Small), SG-Former-M (Medium), and SG-Former-B (Base), differing in channel dimensions, number of heads, and the number of blocks per stage, as well as the specific scales used in their hybrid-scale attention layers (Table 1).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SG-Former consistently demonstrates state-of-the-art performance across various computer vision tasks, outperforming existing Vision Transformers and even strong CNN baselines, often with lower computational costs and fewer parameters.

#### 4.1. ImageNet-1K Classification

- **Superior Accuracy:** SG-Former models achieve 83.2% (S-size), 84.1% (M-size), and 84.7% (B-size) Top-1 accuracy on ImageNet-1K at 224x224 resolution.
- **Outperforming Baselines:**
 - **Swin Transformer:** SG-Former-S/M/B improve upon Swin-T/S/B by +2.0%, +1.0%, and +1.3% respectively.
 - **CSWin Transformer (State-of-the-Art):** SG-Former-S/M/B surpass CSWin-T/S/B by +0.5%, +0.5%, and +0.4% under similar parameter counts.
 - **EfficientNet (CNN Baseline):** SG-Former is noted as the first Transformer backbone to outperform the strong CNN-based EfficientNet (e.g., SG-Former-B achieves +0.7% over EfficientNet-B6) despite EfficientNet's larger input resolutions and neural architecture search origin.
- **Efficiency:** SG-Former-S outperforms DeiT-B by +1.4% with only a quarter of its parameters and FLOPs, showcasing significant efficiency.
- **Higher Resolution (384x384):** When fine-tuned at 384x384 resolution, SG-Former-B achieves 85.8% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming Swin-B by +1.6% and CSWin-B by +0.4%.
- **Throughput:** SG-Former models demonstrate faster inference speeds (higher throughputs) compared to Swin and CSWin at similar performance levels (Figure 6).

#### 4.2. COCO Object Detection and Instance Segmentation

- **Mask R-CNN Pipeline:** Evaluated using Mask R-CNN with 1x and 3x training schedules.
- **Significant Improvements:**
 - **CNN Baselines (ResNeXt):** SG-Former significantly improves over ResNeXt by +6.4 mAP (1x schedule) and +6.9 mAP (3x schedule).
 - **Swin Transformer:** SG-Former-B achieves 51.3 mAP and 45.2 mIoU (3x schedule), outperforming Swin-B by +2.8 mAP and +1.8 mIoU.
 - **CSWin Transformer:** SG-Former-B shows improvements over CSWin-B by +0.5 mAP and +0.3 mIoU (3x schedule), reaching 51.3 mAP and 45.2 mIoU.

#### 4.3. ADE20K Semantic Segmentation

- **Semantic FPN and UperNet Frameworks:** Tested with two standard segmentation frameworks.
- **Leading Performance:**
 - **Semantic FPN:** SG-Former-B achieves 50.6 mIoU, outperforming Swin Transformer by +4.6 mIoU with 10% fewer parameters.
 - **UperNet:** SG-Former-B achieves 52.7 mIoU (MS testing), surpassing Swin-B by +3 mIoU and CSWin-B by +0.5 mIoU.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Single Scale vs. Hybrid Scale Self-Attention (Table 6):**
 - Hybrid-scale self-attention (Local-Global) achieves 82.6% Top-1 accuracy, outperforming Single Scale (Local) by +1.0% and Single Scale (Global) by +0.6%. This confirms the importance of integrating multiple granularities of information within one layer.
- **Significance Guidance (Table 7):**
 - Using the Hybrid-Scale significance map for token reallocation yields the best performance (83.2% Top-1 accuracy).
 - This is significantly better than a "Manual defined" uniform significance map (+0.9% improvement).
 - Hybrid-scale guidance also outperforms using only "Local" (+0.3%) or "Global" (+0.8%) scale significance maps, demonstrating that the combination of different scales provides the most effective guidance for adaptive token reallocation.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The SG-Former paper presents a highly significant advancement in the field of Vision Transformers, primarily by offering an elegant and effective solution to the long-standing challenge of balancing computational efficiency with performance, especially in dense prediction tasks. Its potential impact is multifaceted:

1. **Bridging the Efficiency-Accuracy Gap:** SG-Former successfully breaks the typical trade-off between efficient local attention (which sacrifices global context) and coarse-grained global attention (which sacrifices fine detail). By adaptively reallocating tokens based on learned significance, it achieves both a global receptive field and fine-grained information processing where it matters most, leading to superior performance across diverse tasks while maintaining or reducing computational costs. This makes powerful Transformer models more practical for real-world deployment.

2. **Adaptive Resource Allocation:** The core concept of "self-guided" and "evolving token reallocation" is a crucial methodological innovation. Instead of static, hand-engineered token reduction strategies, SG-Former allows the model to intelligently allocate computational resources dynamically for each input. This data-driven adaptability moves beyond pre-defined inductive biases and offers a more principled way to optimize computation. This concept could inspire future research in adaptive computation for other neural network architectures.

3. **Improved General-Purpose Backbone:** SG-Former demonstrates its strength as a versatile backbone for various computer vision applications, including image classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation. Its consistent outperformance of strong baselines like Swin Transformer and CSWin Transformer positions it as a new state-of-the-art general Vision Transformer backbone. This reliability across tasks suggests its utility in a wide range of computer vision systems.

4. **Foundation for Future Research:** The hybrid-scale attention mechanism, which simultaneously extracts multi-granularity information and generates reliable significance maps, provides a robust foundation for the self-guided attention. This synergistic design could lead to further exploration of dynamic attention mechanisms, multi-scale feature integration, and content-aware processing in other deep learning models. The concept of using internal attention mechanisms to generate "guidance" for subsequent layers or operations is a powerful paradigm.

5. **Practical Implications:** For industry and applied research, SG-Former offers models that are not only more accurate but also more efficient in terms of parameters and FLOPs, translating to faster training, quicker inference, and potentially lower hardware requirements. This makes advanced Vision Transformer capabilities accessible for applications with computational constraints.

In conclusion, SG-Former is a well-motivated and technically sound paper that significantly pushes the boundaries of efficient and effective Vision Transformer design. Its self-guided, adaptive approach to token reallocation and hybrid-scale attention offers a promising direction for developing the next generation of high-performance and practical deep learning models.
