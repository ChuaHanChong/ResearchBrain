# Self-Consistency of the Internal Reward Models Improves Self-Rewarding Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> The Self-Consistent Internal Rewards (SCIR) framework improves Large Language Model alignment by enforcing consistency between the model's generative and implicit reward mechanisms. This approach leads to more reliable preference judgments and demonstrates superior performance, including a 14% improvement in length-controlled win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 and higher MT-Bench scores.

## Problem

- Ensuring LLM outputs align with human preferences traditionally requires expensive, time-consuming human-annotated preference data.
- Self-Rewarding Language Models (SRLMs) mitigate this by using the model itself to generate preference judgments, but different internal reward models often produce inconsistent evaluations.
- This internal inconsistency leads to unreliable self-generated training data, hindering the overall alignment performance of SRLMs.

## Method

- The ==Self-Consistent Internal Rewards (SCIR)== framework enforces consistency between an LLM's generative and implicit internal reward models.
- It employs =="Consistency Training"== to penalize disagreements between these internal models and =="Dynamic Consistency Preference Optimization (DCPO)"== to select only consistently judged preference data for training.
- The iterative process refines the model by generating response pairs, evaluating them, and optimizing with a combination of ==consistency and preference optimization losses==.

## Results

- SCIR significantly increases the consistency rates between internal reward models compared to standard SRLM approaches, improving across training iterations.
- The framework improves the model's reward modeling accuracy on benchmarks like RewardBench and demonstrates superior overall performance on alignment benchmarks.
- Achieved a **14%** improvement in length-controlled win rate on AlpacaEval 2.0 and higher MT-Bench scores, consistently outperforming various baseline methods.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A fundamental limitation in Self-Rewarding Language Models (SRLMs) is the inconsistency between different internal reward mechanisms (e.g., explicit LLM-as-a-Judge and implicit DPO-derived rewards).
> - Improving the self-consistency of these internal reward models directly translates to more reliable self-generated preference data for training.
> - Explicitly encouraging consistency through training and dynamically selecting highly consistent preference pairs can significantly enhance LLM alignment with human preferences without reliance on new human annotations.
