# HOPE: Hand-Object Pressure Estimation from Monocular Videos

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Seoul National University developed HOPE, a framework for estimating per-vertex normal pressure and contact on the bare hand directly from monocular video. The method unifies heterogeneous pressure and contact supervision into a standard hand mesh representation, achieving an F1 score of 0.874 for frame-level contact and a MAE of 1.808 kPa for pressure on OpenTouch, while also improving generalization to bare-hand contact on ARCTIC.

## Problem

- Existing vision-based pressure methods are constrained to planar surfaces or instrumented objects, limiting interaction diversity and often producing 2D outputs.
- Tactile sensing using gloves introduces a visual domain gap for bare hands, obscures visual cues, and provides sensor-specific, limited-coverage data.
- Large-scale datasets for hand-object interaction typically provide binary contact information, lacking the metric pressure magnitude needed for full physical understanding.

## Method

- A unified pipeline maps heterogeneous pressure (from tactile gloves and planar sensors) and contact (from distance thresholds) data into a common ==MANO per-vertex space==.
- Introduced **VertexFormer**, a transformer-based neural network that processes video clips with ==interleaved spatial (image patches, hand vertices) and temporal attention== for contact and pressure prediction.
- Incorporates a ==contact-gated force prediction== mechanism to ensure pressure only manifests where contact is predicted, leveraging contact-only data as a structural prior.

## Results

- Achieved an F1 score of **0.874** for frame-level contact and a vertex-level MAE of **1.808 kPa** for pressure on the OpenTouch dataset, surpassing existing baselines.
- Demonstrated the lowest vertex-level force errors (MAE **0.449 kPa**, RMSE **2.314 kPa**) on planar hand-surface pressure using PressureVisionDB, comparable to specialized planar methods.
- Significantly improved bare-hand contact generalization, with the vertex F1 score on ARCTIC increasing from **0.069** to **0.498** when incorporating distance-based contact from HOI datasets.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing pressure and contact as per-vertex fields on a standard hand mesh (MANO) unifies output space, making it object-agnostic and universally applicable beyond sensor-specific constraints.
> - Combining diverse supervision, including metric pressure from gloved hands and structural contact from bare hands, enables robust pressure learning and generalization to real-world bare-hand interactions.
> - Temporal modeling via a video transformer is crucial for capturing the dynamic evolution of hand-object pressure and maintaining consistent predictions through transient occlusions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Understanding physical hand-object interaction is a critical area within computer vision and robotics, with implications for fields such as egocentric perception, augmented and virtual reality, robot learning, and assistive systems. Prior research has approached this problem from several angles, each with distinct methodologies and limitations.

**Vision-based Pressure Estimation:** Early methods have focused on inferring physical pressure from visual cues. Techniques like PressureVision and its successors demonstrated that RGB images contain discernible pressure information, such as skin deformation, color changes, and localized wrinkles. However, these methods typically operate under constrained sensing geometries, often relying on instrumented planar surfaces or specific calibration setups. The output of these systems is frequently tied to 2D planar maps, image-space representations, or fingertip-level labels, limiting their applicability to the diverse, unstructured nature of everyday hand-object interactions involving arbitrary 3D object surfaces and various contact modes (e.g., fingertip pressing, whole-hand grasping). EgoPressure moved towards a hand-centric representation using MANO models but still focused on bare-hand interaction with instrumented planar surfaces and lacked temporal modeling.

**Tactile Sensing and Touch Datasets:** Direct tactile sensing provides ground truth physical supervision that is challenging to obtain through vision alone. Datasets such as OpenTouch and PressureVisionDB utilize instrumented objects, sensing surfaces, or wearable tactile gloves to capture force, pressure, or contact data. While providing valuable metric information, these datasets present their own set of challenges. The annotations are often expressed in sensor-specific coordinate systems (e.g., taxel grids on a glove, 2D sensel images), making generalization across different hardware difficult. Furthermore, wearable tactile gloves cover the hand, obscuring visual cues relevant for bare-hand pressure estimation and creating a domain gap between gloved and bare-hand appearances. The spatial coverage of these sensors is also typically limited to specific regions, such as the palm and fingertips.

**Hand-Object Contact and Affordance Estimation:** A related line of research has focused on hand-object interaction through contact, grasp, and affordance. Large-scale datasets and pipelines provide annotations for hand-object pose, geometry, and contact, often derived from tracked geometry or proximity-based thresholds. Methods like HACO estimate dense vertex-level hand contact from RGB images. While these approaches provide crucial information about *where* contact occurs or *where* interaction is likely (affordances), they typically yield binary or proximity-based labels and do not provide metric pressure magnitude. LatentAct predicts 3D hand motion and MANO contact-map trajectories but focuses on contact location rather than pressure.

The presented work, HOPE, addresses the limitations of these existing paradigms by formulating pressure estimation as a hand-centric video prediction problem. It seeks to bridge the gap between constrained vision-based pressure estimation, sensor-specific tactile data, and contact-only interaction understanding. By lifting heterogeneous forms of supervision (tactile-glove pressure, planar-sensor pressure, and distance-based contact) into a unified MANO vertex space and employing temporal modeling, HOPE aims to provide a generalized solution for per-vertex contact and pressure estimation on the bare hand from monocular video, moving beyond fixed sensing surfaces and single-frame predictions.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to estimate physical pressure from monocular video observations, providing temporally evolving per-vertex normal pressure and contact directly on the hand mesh. This approach aims to yield a unified output space that is independent of specific object shapes or sensor layouts.

The motivation for this work stems from several challenges and limitations observed in prior research on understanding hand-object interaction:

1. **Limitations of Existing Pressure Estimation Methods:** Prior vision-based pressure estimation methods are largely constrained. They often rely on planar surfaces or instrumented objects, restricting the diversity of interactions and objects they can handle. These methods typically process single images, neglecting the temporal evolution of pressure during dynamic interactions like grasping, pressing, or tool use, where visually similar contact configurations can correspond to different pressure magnitudes over time. The output representations are also often specific to the sensor layout (e.g., 2D planar maps), making generalization difficult.

2. **Challenges with Tactile Sensing Datasets:** While tactile gloves (e.g., OpenTouch) provide valuable metric pressure annotations during manipulation, they introduce new limitations. The glove itself covers the hand, occluding visual cues such as skin deformation, color changes, and local wrinkles that are crucial for vision-based pressure inference on bare hands. Furthermore, the visual domain of gloved hands differs significantly from bare hands, creating a domain gap that hinders generalization to natural, bare-hand interactions commonly seen in everyday life. The pressure data collected is also sensor-specific, with annotations tied to the coordinate system and coverage of the underlying hardware.

3. **Incompleteness of Contact-Only Data:** Many large-scale hand-object interaction datasets provide extensive information on hand-object pose, geometry, and contact (often derived from proximity). However, these datasets typically provide binary contact labels (i.e., *where* contact occurs) without quantifying the magnitude of physical pressure applied. While contact is a necessary condition for pressure, it does not provide the full physical interaction information required for many downstream tasks, such as robot learning from human demonstrations or detailed egocentric perception.

To address these limitations, the authors pursue the following key objectives:

- **Unified Hand-Centric Formulation:** To overcome sensor-specific output spaces, the work aims to represent both pressure and contact as per-vertex fields directly on a standard hand mesh model (MANO). This unified representation is designed to be object-agnostic and universally applicable to different interaction scenarios.
- **Leveraging Heterogeneous Supervision:** The research seeks to combine complementary data sources: metric pressure from gloved-hand videos (e.g., OpenTouch), planar-sensor pressure data (e.g., PressureVisionDB), and contact-only annotations from bare-hand 3D hand-object interaction (HOI) datasets (e.g., DexYCB, ARCTIC). The motivation is to use metric pressure data where available and leverage abundant bare-hand contact data as a structural prior, thereby regularizing pressure learning and enabling generalization to bare-hand interactions.
- **Temporal Modeling for Pressure Estimation:** Recognizing that pressure is a dynamic process, the work aims to develop a model that processes video inputs rather than single frames. This is motivated by the need to capture the temporal evolution of hand-object contact, differentiate between various loading states (e.g., approach, contact establishment, loading, release), and propagate pressure cues through transient occlusions.
- **Enforcing Physical Priors:** A specific objective is to incorporate the physical principle that pressure vanishes in the absence of contact. This prior is designed to be enforced explicitly within the model architecture, allowing contact-only supervision to effectively guide pressure prediction.

By achieving these objectives, the research intends to advance the understanding of physical hand-object interaction beyond basic contact detection, enabling more robust and generalizable pressure estimation from monocular video.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, named HOPE, addresses the problem of estimating per-vertex contact and pressure on the hand surface from monocular RGB video clips. HOPE comprises two main components: a unified annotation pipeline and a VertexFormer model.

#### 3.1. Unified Pressure–Contact Annotation on MANO Vertex Space

This component addresses the heterogeneity and sensor-specificity of existing pressure and contact datasets. The core idea is to transform all forms of supervision into a common MANO vertex space (V=778 vertices).

- **Tactile-to-Vertex Mapping (OpenTouch):** The OpenTouch dataset provides pressure data from a 16x16 tactile taxel grid on a glove. A calibrated correspondence maps each taxel to the MANO vertices it covers. For each vertex, the pressure is calculated by averaging the pressures of all covering taxels. This piecewise-constant field is then smoothed using geodesic Gaussian filtering on the canonical MANO mesh, resulting in per-vertex pressures ($p_{OT} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^V$) localized to palmar vertices and their neighborhood.
- **Sensel-to-Vertex Projection (PressureVisionDB - PVDB):** PVDB provides dense pressure maps on a planar sensor synchronized with RGB video. Since PVDB lacks 3D hand annotations, a pre-trained hand reconstruction model (refined with multi-view fusion) is used to obtain a posed MANO mesh for each frame. Palmar vertices are projected onto the sensor pad plane using calibrated extrinsics, and the sensel pressure map is bilinearly sampled at these projected coordinates to yield per-vertex pressures ($p_{PV} \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge0}^V$).
- **Force-Derived Contact:** Both OpenTouch and PVDB report pressure in kPa. A binary contact label ($c_{t,v}$) is derived from these vertex-level pressures by applying a uniform threshold of 1 kPa ($c_{t,v} = 1[p_{t,v} > \tau_p]$). This process generates paired (pressure, contact) supervision for the force datasets.
- **Distance-Based Contact from HOI Data (DexYCB, ARCTIC):** To provide contact supervision for regions beyond sensor coverage and expose the model to bare-hand interactions, contact annotations are incorporated from existing HOI datasets. Following the protocol of HACO, a vertex is labeled as in contact if its distance to the ground-truth object surface is below a threshold of 10 mm ($\delta_c$). These datasets provide contact-only supervision ($c_{t,v}$ without $p_{t,v}$).

The final training set is a union of force samples (with both pressure and derived contact) from OpenTouch/PVDB and contact-only samples from HOI datasets, all consistently represented in the V-dimensional MANO vertex space.

#### 3.2. VertexFormer: A Vertex-Anchored Transformer

VertexFormer is a transformer-based neural network designed to predict contact and pressure. It processes input along two axes: image patches and hand vertices, integrating visual and pose information over time.

- **Patch Tokens:** Each input RGB frame is processed by a frozen DINOv3 backbone (ViT-B/16), which outputs a grid of patch features. These features are linearly projected to a dimension $d$ to form patch tokens ($F_t \in \mathbb{R}^{N_p \times d}$).
- **Vertex Tokens:** Simultaneously, per-frame MANO parameters (pose $\theta_t$, shape $\beta_t$, global orientation $\phi_t$, and translation $\tau_t$) are estimated using a frozen HaWoR model. For each vertex $v$, a $d$-dimensional vertex token ($z_{t,v}$) is constructed. This token combines a learned identity embedding ($e_v$, anchoring the token to a specific vertex), the posed hand-local vertex coordinate ($\tilde{V}_{t,v}$), the global orientation ($\phi_t$), and the global translation ($\tau_t$) through learned linear projections. This decomposition allows the model to reason about articulated shape, viewpoint, and position separately.
- **Interleaved Spatial and Temporal Attention:** The VertexFormer employs $L$ spatio-temporal blocks. Each block applies:
 - **Vertex Self-Attention (Vertex-SA):** Allows vertex tokens to exchange information across the hand topology, propagating contact signals (e.g., from visible to occluded regions).
 - **Vertex-to-Patch Cross-Attention (Vertex-Patch CA):** Enables each vertex token to attend to the full grid of patch tokens, enriching its representation with global visual context.
 - **Temporal Attention:** Shares information across the $T$ frames for each vertex identity, facilitating temporal smoothing of pressure and helping disambiguate contact during brief occlusions.
- **Contact-Gated Force Prediction:** After the transformer blocks, two MLP heads process the final vertex tokens:
 - **Contact Head:** Outputs a logit per vertex, passed through a sigmoid function to produce per-vertex contact probabilities ($\hat{c}_t$).
 - **Force Head:** Outputs non-negative pressure values per vertex ($\tilde{p}_t$).
 - **Gating Mechanism:** The final pressure prediction ($\hat{p}_t$) is obtained by element-wise multiplication of the contact probabilities with the raw pressure predictions ($\hat{p}_t = \hat{c}_t \odot \tilde{p}_t$). This mechanism enforces the physical prior that pressure can only exist where contact is present.

#### 3.3. Training Objective

The model is trained using a unified objective function tailored for heterogeneous supervision:

- **Contact Loss:** A Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss is used for contact prediction, computed over vertices with valid contact annotations ($\Omega_c^t$).
- **Pressure Loss:** An L1 loss is used for pressure prediction, computed over vertices with valid pressure annotations ($\Omega_p^t$).
- **Total Loss:** The overall loss is a weighted sum of the contact and pressure losses ($L = \lambda_c L_{contact} + \lambda_p L_{pressure}$). For contact-only HOI samples, the pressure loss term ($\lambda_p L_{pressure}$) is masked out, allowing the model to learn contact patterns from bare-hand interactions without requiring metric pressure labels.

The model is implemented with DINOv3 ViT-B/16 and HaWoR frozen. Input frames are resized to 224x224, with T=10 frames per clip. The transformer has L=2 blocks, hidden dimension $d=64$, and 4 attention heads. Training uses AdamW optimizer for 20 epochs.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of HOPE was conducted across three main settings: hand-object pressure estimation on OpenTouch, hand-surface pressure estimation on PressureVisionDB, and dense hand-object contact estimation on MOW, with additional ablations on dataset composition and input modalities.

#### 4.1. Quantitative Performance

- **Hand-object pressure on OpenTouch (Table 1):** HOPE demonstrates higher performance compared to baseline methods. For frame-level contact, HOPE achieves an F1 score of 0.874, surpassing PressureVision (0.610) and HACO (0.835). At the vertex level, HOPE's F1 score is 0.660, significantly higher than PressureVision (0.013) and HACO (0.361). In terms of force estimation, HOPE reports a vertex-level MAE of 1.808 kPa and an RMSE of 4.998 kPa, which are lower than PressureVision (MAE 1.93 kPa, RMSE 6.19 kPa). The baselines, particularly PressureVision, exhibit low vertex-level F1 scores due to their 2D planar output spaces not mapping effectively to MANO vertices. HACO shows higher recall but lower precision for gloved-hand interactions, indicating over-prediction.
- **Hand-surface pressure on PressureVisionDB (Table 2):** When applied to planar hand-surface pressure, HOPE is competitive with specialized planar methods. It achieves a frame-level contact F1 of 0.905, comparable to PressureVision (0.939). Crucially, HOPE achieves the lowest vertex-level force errors, with an MAE of 0.449 kPa and RMSE of 2.314 kPa, outperforming PressureVision (MAE 0.471 kPa, RMSE 3.726 kPa) and PressureVision++ (MAE 0.248 kPa, RMSE 3.025 kPa). Pixel-level contact IoU is lower for HOPE (0.328) compared to PressureVision (0.547), which the authors attribute to hand reconstruction errors when projecting MANO vertices back to the sensor plane.
- **Dense hand-object contact on MOW (Table 3):** For in-the-wild bare-hand contact estimation, HOPE achieves a precision of 0.530 and an F1 of 0.416. While its precision is higher than HACO (0.525), its recall (0.436) is lower than HACO (0.607), leading to a lower overall F1 score. This difference is discussed as a consequence of the supervision type: HOPE, trained primarily with force-derived contact, tends to predict more localized regions of high physical loading, whereas HACO, trained on distance-based contact, covers broader near-contact areas.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

- **Effect of Training Data Composition (Table 4):**
 - Training only on OpenTouch (OT) yields strong in-domain performance but poor generalization to PVDB and limited contact accuracy on bare-hand ARCTIC data.
 - Adding PVDB pressure supervision (OT + PV) significantly improves planar hand-surface pressure estimation (PVDB MAE drops from 0.822 to 0.265) without degrading OpenTouch performance.
 - Incorporating distance-based contact from HOI datasets (OT + PV + HOI) leads to a substantial improvement in bare-hand contact generalization on ARCTIC (vertex F1 increases from 0.069 to 0.498), with minimal impact on in-domain pressure metrics. This validates the approach of using contact-only HOI data as a structural prior.
- **Input Modality Ablation (Table 5):**
 - Variants using only pose, only RGB, or their combination show that visual features and hand pose provide complementary information, with RGB + Pose outperforming either alone.
 - Adding temporal context consistently improves performance across contact and force metrics.
 - The full model, integrating visual features, hand pose, and temporal context, achieves the best results for both contact and force estimation, indicating the value of each modality.
- **Robustness to Hand Reconstruction (Table 6):**
 - The model exhibits robustness to moderate perturbations in estimated MANO parameters at test time (e.g., global translation, orientation, joint pose, shape), with marginal drops in vertex-level contact F1 (maximum drop of 0.013 under 20° per-joint pose noise). This suggests tolerance to typical reconstruction errors.
 - Retraining the full pipeline with alternative hand reconstructors (WiLoR) or visual backbones (HaWoR's ViT-H features) yields comparable performance, indicating that the learned associations are not overly specific to the default components.

#### 4.3. Qualitative Results

Qualitative examples provided in Figures 3, 4, 5, and 6 visually support the quantitative findings. They illustrate HOPE's ability to predict physically meaningful and spatially localized pressure regions, generalize to bare-hand egocentric and in-the-wild videos, and effectively estimate contact on the MANO mesh. Figure 6, in particular, demonstrates the improved generalization to bare-hand interaction when augmented with HOI contact supervision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a advancement in the field of visual hand-object interaction understanding by offering a unified and generalizable framework for estimating physical pressure from monocular videos.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

- **Unified Hand-Centric Formulation:** By representing pressure and contact as per-vertex fields on the MANO hand mesh, HOPE establishes a standardized output space independent of specific sensor layouts or object shapes. This overcomes a major limitation of prior methods tied to constrained sensing geometries, allowing for broader applicability across diverse interaction scenarios.
- **Effective Integration of Heterogeneous Supervision:** The methodology of lifting tactile-glove pressure, planar-sensor pressure, and distance-based hand-object interaction (HOI) contact into a shared vertex space is a notable development. This approach effectively leverages the strengths of different data types—metric pressure from gloved-hand sensors and structural contact information from bare-hand HOI datasets—to train a more robust model. The use of contact-only HOI data as a structural prior is particularly significant for regularizing pressure learning in regions where metric pressure labels are unavailable and for enabling generalization to bare-hand interactions.
- **Temporal Modeling for Dynamic Interactions:** The introduction of a vertex-anchored video transformer with interleaved spatial and temporal attention addresses the dynamic nature of hand-object pressure. By reasoning over time, the model can capture the evolution of contact and pressure, disambiguate loading states, and maintain consistent predictions through short-term occlusions, leading to more physically plausible estimations than frame-wise approaches.
- **Generalization to Bare-Hand and In-the-Wild Videos:** Despite relying heavily on metric pressure supervision from gloved-hand videos, the framework demonstrates the ability to generalize to bare-hand egocentric and in-the-wild videos. This capability is crucial for applications in natural environments and represents a step towards models that can understand human manipulation in everyday contexts.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Understanding of Human Manipulation:** The ability to estimate dense, per-vertex pressure provides a more comprehensive understanding of human manipulation than contact or pose alone. This detailed physical interaction information can be valuable for cognitive science research and studying human motor control.
- **Advancements in Robotics:** For robot learning from human demonstrations, access to detailed pressure maps can enable robots to learn more nuanced and physically informed manipulation strategies, moving beyond simple grasp imitation to understanding how force is applied.
- **Augmented and Virtual Reality:** In AR/VR systems, realistic haptic feedback and interaction simulation require accurate models of physical contact and pressure. HOPE's output could contribute to more immersive and physically plausible virtual interactions.
- **Egocentric Perception and Assistive Systems:** In egocentric vision, understanding how users physically interact with objects is vital. HOPE can provide critical input for assistive systems that need to reason about fine-grained physical contact for tasks like rehabilitation, skill assessment, or object handling assistance.

While the work acknowledges limitations such as reliance on off-the-shelf hand reconstructors, the dominance of a single tactile-glove dataset for pressure supervision, and the focus on normal pressure, it establishes a foundational framework for vision-based hand-object pressure estimation, opening avenues for future research into broader interaction categories, more diverse pressure sensing, and the recovery of full contact wrenches.
