# RISE: Self-Improving Robot Policy with Compositional World Model

> [!summary] Summary
> RISE enables robots to autonomously improve their policies through imagination-based reinforcement learning, employing a compositional world model for dynamics prediction and progress value estimation. This framework achieved up to 95% success in complex real-world tasks like box closing and significantly improved performance on dynamic brick sorting and backpack packing compared to existing methods.

## Problem

- Current Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, relying on imitation learning, exhibit brittleness in contact-rich and dynamic real-world manipulation tasks due to limitations in expert data and exposure bias.
- Applying Reinforcement Learning (RL) directly in the physical world is constrained by safety risks, high hardware costs, slow interactions, and the need for frequent manual intervention.
- Prior world models for robotics often fail to reliably predict action-conditioned dynamics with high fidelity or provide dense, informative learning signals necessary for complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Method

- The **RISE** framework implements =="Reinforcement learning via Imagination"== by creating an online learning environment within an =="imaginary space"== using a learned ==world model==.
- A ==Compositional World Model== is developed, comprising a ==Controllable Dynamics Model== that predicts action-conditioned multi-view futures and a ==Progress Value Model== that estimates dense, failure-sensitive learning signals.
- Policies are first warmed up on real-world data and then iteratively refined within a ==self-improving loop==, generating imaginary rollouts, evaluating them with the world model, and updating based on derived advantages.

## Results

- RISE achieved superior success rates on complex real-world tasks, including **85%** for Dynamic Brick Sorting, **85%** for Backpack Packing, and **95%** for Box Closing, consistently outperforming state-of-the-art baselines.
- The framework demonstrated efficient learning, reaching high performance with substantially fewer training steps compared to alternative methods.
- Ablation studies confirmed that the compositional design of the dynamics and value models, task-centric batching, and the combined progress/TD learning objective are all critical for the system's robust performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Decomposing the world model into distinct dynamics prediction and value estimation components enables specialized architectures and training, yielding both controllable future predictions and informative learning signals.
> - Task-centric batching during dynamics model training significantly improves action adherence and fine-tuning efficiency for real-world manipulation tasks.
> - A combined loss for the value model, incorporating progress estimation and Temporal-Difference learning, provides robust and failure-sensitive advantages crucial for complex, long-horizon tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied artificial intelligence has progressed significantly with the development of foundation models, particularly Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. These models leverage large-scale pre-training on diverse data to achieve broad semantic understanding and instruction-following capabilities for generalist robot control. However, current VLA models exhibit brittleness in complex, contact-rich, and dynamic manipulation tasks. This limitation is largely attributed to their reliance on Imitation Learning (IL), which is constrained by the quality and coverage of expert demonstrations and susceptible to exposure bias, leading to compounding errors when robots deviate from demonstrated trajectories.

Reinforcement Learning (RL) offers an alternative approach to improving robot robustness by enabling agents to learn from their own successes and failures. While RL has shown promise in virtual simulators, its application in the physical world faces significant challenges. Real-world robot interactions are typically serial, time-consuming, expensive due to hardware costs, and require manual monitoring and resets. These constraints limit the scalability of on-policy RL and often confine real-world RL methods to offline data with potential distribution shifts from the current policy.

To address the gap between virtual simulation and the physical world, researchers have explored world models. These models learn from passive experience and then simulate future outcomes conditioned on different actions, potentially enabling "imagination-based" learning. However, constructing world models suitable for real-world robotics presents its own set of challenges: ensuring accurate and controllable prediction of future states over various actions, and generating informative learning signals (such as dense rewards or value estimates) for intermediate actions, rather than relying solely on sparse terminal success indicators. Existing generative world models often prioritize visual realism over action controllability and may have prohibitive inference costs, limiting their utility within an RL loop.

RISE positions itself within this landscape by developing a framework that leverages a learned world model to create an online learning environment for on-policy RL, thereby bypassing the physical-world limitations. It aims to overcome the brittleness of IL-based VLAs and the practical hurdles of real-world RL by enabling scalable self-improvement through imagination. The compositional design of its world model, which separates dynamics prediction from value estimation, is intended to allow for specialized architectures and training objectives, enhancing reliability and informativeness for policy improvement in complex manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the observed limitations of current robotic systems, particularly those based on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, when confronted with complex, contact-rich, and dynamic manipulation tasks in real-world environments. Despite advancements in high-level semantic competence, these VLA models often exhibit brittleness, where minor execution deviations can lead to task failures.

The core problem identified is a twofold limitation:
1. **Limitations of Imitation Learning (IL)**: VLA models primarily rely on IL, which is fundamentally constrained by the quality and diversity of expert demonstrations. IL struggles with exposure bias, meaning that if a robot deviates from the expert's demonstrated manifold, it often lacks the necessary recovery skills to correct its course, leading to cumulative errors.
2. **Challenges of Real-world Reinforcement Learning (RL)**: While RL offers a principled path to robustness by allowing agents to learn from their own trial and error, implementing on-policy RL in the physical world is severely bottlenecked. Physical interactions are serial, time-consuming, costly due to hardware requirements, entail safety risks, and necessitate labor-intensive manual monitoring and environment resets. These factors prevent the collection of sufficient on-policy data for effective policy improvement.

To bridge this gap, the key objectives of the RISE framework are:
- **To enable scalable and online self-improvement for robot policies**: This involves shifting the learning environment from costly physical interactions to an "imaginary space" powered by a learned world model.
- **To develop a Compositional World Model**: This model is central to providing an interactive environment with informative learning signals. Its design specifically aims to:
 - **Predict multi-view future observations accurately and controllably (Dynamics Model)**: The model must faithfully emulate future states based on proposed actions, exhibiting both visual plausibility and physical consistency to support reliable policy learning. This addresses the challenge of building world models that prioritize action controllability over general visual realism.
 - **Evaluate imagined outcomes with dense and failure-sensitive progress signals (Value Model)**: The model needs to provide informative rewards or value estimates for intermediate actions, rather than relying on sparse terminal rewards. This is crucial for guiding learning over long-horizon tasks and for detecting subtle failures in contact-rich manipulation.
- **To integrate these components into a closed-loop self-improving pipeline**: The goal is to continuously generate imaginary rollouts, estimate advantages, and update the robot's policy entirely within the imaginative space, minimizing the need for costly physical interaction.
- **To demonstrate significant performance improvements on challenging real-world tasks**: The framework aims to surpass prior imitation learning and reinforcement learning approaches in tasks requiring high dexterity, precision, and dynamic adaptation.

In summary, the motivation is to overcome the practical and inherent limitations of current robot learning paradigms by establishing a framework where robots can autonomously refine their skills through imagination, leading to more robust and adaptable manipulation capabilities in complex physical environments.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The RISE framework (Reinforcement learning via Imagination for SElf-improving robots) is designed as a holistic learning system that enables robots to self-improve their policies by interacting within a learned world model, rather than the physical environment. The methodology consists of three main stages: a Compositional World Model, a Policy Warm-up phase, and a Self-Improving Loop.

#### 3.1. Compositional World Model (CWM)

The core of RISE is its Compositional World Model, which disentangles the problem of environment modeling into dynamics prediction and value estimation. This allows for independent optimization of each component using architectures and training objectives tailored to their specific roles. The CWM provides an interactive environment that maps current states and policy actions to future dynamics and rewards.

#### 3.1.1. Controllable Dynamics Model

This component is responsible for predicting future multi-view observations.
- **Initialization and Architecture**: The dynamics model is initialized from the pre-trained Genie Envisioner (GE-base), which is a video diffusion model known for its balance between generation quality and inference speed. It leverages architectural advancements from LTX-Video. The model processes multi-view RGB observations (192 × 256) and is conditioned on future action chunks.
- **Action Controllability Enhancements**:
 - **Pre-training on Large-scale Action-labeled Datasets**: The model is further optimized on datasets such as Agibot World and Galaxea. This involves incorporating an additional lightweight action encoder to endow the model with precise action controllability.
 - **Robustness to Visual Artifacts**: Stronger noise is applied to context frames during training to improve generation robustness against motion blurs and visual artifacts that can occur in both recorded and synthesized data.
 - **Task-Centric Batching**: To mitigate instability and slow convergence during fine-tuning on heterogeneous action data, a task-centric batching strategy is employed. Each batch samples from a small fraction of tasks but covers a greater diversity of actions within those specific tasks. This prioritizes action diversity within a scene, enhancing action controllability.
- **Training Objective**: The model is trained using a Flow Matching objective, with timestep scheduling following a Logit-Normal distribution. Inference uses Euler discrete formulation with 50 denoising steps.

#### 3.1.2. Progress Value Model

This component evaluates the utility of imagined trajectories by assigning a scalar value to observations, which is then used to compute advantages.
- **Initialization and Architecture**: The value model (V) is parameterized from a pre-trained VLA policy (π_{0.5}). This leverages the policy backbone's robot-centric understanding and compatibility with multi-view inputs.
- **Training Objectives**: The value model is trained with a combined objective to ensure both density and failure sensitivity.
 - **Progress Estimate Loss (L_prog)**: This objective provides a coarse understanding of monotonic temporal structure by regressing the value to a normalized progress measure (t/T) within an episode.
 - **Temporal-Difference (TD) Learning Loss (L_TD)**: This augments the progress loss by training the value function to distinguish between successful and failed outcomes. It uses rewards (0 for intermediate steps, +1 for success, -1 for failure) and a temporal discount factor (γ=0.995).
 - **Combined Loss (L_V)**: L_prog + L_TD. The progress loss is applied for the initial 10k steps, and both losses are used for the remaining 40k steps.
- **Advantage Calculation**: The advantage A(o_t, a_t, ℓ) for a proposed action chunk is computed as the average cumulative improvement in value across the predicted future observations (ˆo_{t+k}) relative to the initial observation (o_t).

#### 3.2. Policy Warm-up on Real-world Experience

Before engaging the self-improving loop, the robot's policy undergoes a warm-up phase using offline-collected real-world data.
- **Purpose**: This phase anchors the policy to a physically plausible behavior distribution and equips it with advantage-conditioned capabilities, preventing erratic exploration in later stages.
- **Data**: The training data includes expert demonstrations, successful and failed policy rollouts, and human-intervened corrections (DAgger data).
- **Policy and Training**: The pre-trained π_{0.5} policy is fine-tuned. Advantage signals are labeled by the learned value model V for policy rollout data, while expert demonstrations and human corrections are directly assigned an optimal advantage (labeled as 1). The policy is trained to respond to these advantage signals, adopting an advantage-conditioned training scheme. This differs from prior work (e.g., RECAP) by discretizing advantages into 10 bins instead of binary and assigning highest advantage to human demos directly.

#### 3.3. Self-Improving Loop with World Model

After policy warm-up, an iterative self-improving loop refines the policy using the compositional world model as an interactive simulator. This loop alternates between a Rollout Stage and a Training Stage.

#### 3.3.1. Rollout Stage

- **Imaginary Rollout Generation**: An initial state is sampled from the warm-up offline dataset. The rollout policy (π_rollout), conditioned on an optimal advantage (1), proposes an action chunk (ˆa_t).
- **Dynamics Prediction**: The dynamics model synthesizes H future visual states based on the current observation and the proposed action.
- **Value Evaluation**: The value model then evaluates these imagined states to compute the "actual" advantage of the proposed action chunk. This advantage is discretized into one of N uniform bins.
- **State Coverage**: Imagined states can also serve as input for subsequent rollouts (up to two consecutive interactions) to broaden state coverage, while mitigating generative model error accumulation.
- **Policy Update**: The rollout policy parameters are updated via an Exponential Moving Average (EMA) of the behavior policy weights.

#### 3.3.2. Training Stage

- **Policy Optimization**: The on-policy rollout data (⟨o, ˆa, A⟩) generated from the CWM, along with a mix of offline labeled data, forms the batch samples for policy optimization.
- **Advantage-Conditioned Learning**: The VLA policy is trained to generate the proposed action (ˆa) when conditioned on the *evaluated* advantage (A). This allows the policy to learn from both high-advantage successes and low-advantage failures discovered in imagination.
- **Objective**: The policy is optimized under generic flow-matching criteria.

#### 3.4. Implementation Details

- **Computational Resources**: Dynamics model pre-training utilized 16 NVIDIA H100 GPUs for 7 days, with fine-tuning on 8 H100 GPUs for 3 days. The value model fine-tuning took 1 day on 8 GPUs. Policy warm-up and self-improving stages ran on 8 GPUs, with 10k steps for the self-improving loop.
- **Real-world Deployment**: An asynchronous control framework translates discrete, low-frequency VLA inference into continuous, high-frequency joint space commands. A Temporal Ensembling strategy with linear weighting smooths action chunk transitions, ensuring continuous robot motion without freezing during inference.
- **Zero Inference Overhead**: Crucially, both the dynamics and value models are used exclusively during the policy learning phase, imposing no computational overhead during real-world policy execution.

This comprehensive approach enables RISE to effectively perform on-policy reinforcement learning in an imaginary space, addressing the key limitations of physical-world RL and IL for complex robotic manipulation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of RISE was conducted across three challenging real-world tasks: Dynamic Brick Sorting, Backpack Packing, and Box Closing. The results consistently demonstrate the framework's superior performance compared to existing state-of-the-art imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL) baselines.

#### 4.1. Comparative Analysis

RISE was benchmarked against several baselines, all instantiated on the pre-trained π_{0.5} VLA model for a fair comparison:
- **π_{0.5}**: A state-of-the-art VLA fine-tuned on task demonstrations.
- **π_{0.5} + DAgger**: An interactive IL baseline using human corrections.
- **π_{0.5} + PPO**: A standard online RL baseline.
- **π_{0.5} + DSRL**: A sample-efficient RL method steering frozen VLAs.
- **RECAP**: An advantage-conditioned offline RL approach.

The quantitative results (Table I) indicate:
- **Dynamic Brick Sorting**: RISE achieved an 85.00% success rate and a score of 9.78, a significant increase compared to RECAP (50.00% success, 9.00 score) and π_{0.5} (35.00% success, 8.28 score). Online RL methods like PPO and DSRL showed performance degradation, with PPO achieving only 10.00% success.
- **Backpack Packing**: RISE attained an 85.00% success rate and a score of 9.50, outperforming RECAP by a substantial margin (40.00% success, 6.13 score).
- **Box Closing**: RISE demonstrated a 95.00% success rate and a score of 9.88, again significantly higher than RECAP (60.00% success, 8.13 score).

Across all tasks, RISE yielded absolute performance increases of more than +35% in dynamic brick sorting, +45% for backpack packing, and +35% for box closing relative to prior art. This indicates that training on massive imaginative rollouts effectively bootstraps RISE’s performance. Furthermore, extended training of baselines (RECAP, DSRL) did not achieve comparable performance, with RISE showing a +35% improvement in brick sorting with only 9k additional steps, attributed to the diverse samples provided by online world model interaction.

#### 4.2. Ablation Studies

Detailed ablation studies investigated the impact of various design choices within the RISE framework.

- **Offline Data Ratio**: Experiments on the mix of offline real-world data and online imagined data (Table II) showed that an optimal ratio of 0.6 for offline data yielded the best overall performance. A ratio too low (0.1) led to catastrophic forgetting (5% success), while an excessive ratio (0.9) caused over-regularization, hindering exploration. This highlights the importance of a balanced data mix to anchor learning while allowing for exploration.
- **Online Action and State Integration**:
 - A baseline without online signals achieved 35% success in "Sort Complete".
 - Introducing online actions (generated by the rollout policy) increased success to 40%, indicating benefits from expanded action space exploration.
 - Crucially, incorporating both online actions and online states (generated by the dynamics model) further raised the success rate to 70%. This suggests that dynamically generated online states provide a richer, virtually unbounded training distribution, overcoming the limitations of fixed offline datasets.
- **Compositional World Model Modules (Table IV)**:
 - **Dynamics Model**:
 - Removing visual pre-training resulted in a 32.15% drop in sorting accuracy and a 15% completion rate, underscoring the necessity of visual priors.
 - Ablating the task-centric batching strategy reduced completion by 30%, validating its role in filtering distractions and improving action controllability.
 - **Value Model**:
 - Removing progress regression lowered success by 20%, confirming the importance of dense learning signals.
 - Omitting TD learning led to a 35% decline, demonstrating its critical role in robust, failure-sensitive value estimation.
- **Dynamics Model Reliability**: Quantitative comparisons with Cosmos and Genie Envisioner (GE) (Table V) showed RISE's dynamics model to be superior in motion accuracy (lower End-Point Error, EPE) and perceptual quality (higher PSNR, SSIM; lower LPIPS, FVD) on both custom tasks and the Bridge dataset. The task-centric pre-training specifically improved motion awareness, as evidenced by the reduced EPE. Qualitatively (Fig. 6), RISE generated physically plausible and temporally coherent dynamics, in contrast to baselines which exhibited blurring, geometric distortion, and kinematic inconsistencies.
- **Advantage Conditioning**: Evaluation of the policy conditioned on different advantage bins (high, neutral, low) showed a clear performance drop from high to low advantage levels (Fig. 7), particularly in sorting tasks. This confirms that the learned advantages are informative and that the policy effectively captures diverse behaviors through advantage conditioning.

In summary, the main findings establish RISE as a robust and scalable framework that significantly advances real-world robot manipulation by effectively integrating a compositional world model for imagination-based policy improvement.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The RISE framework introduces a novel approach to robotic reinforcement learning, offering several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of embodied AI:

- **Scalable Self-Improvement**: RISE provides a principled framework for robotic reinforcement learning that enables autonomous self-improvement in a scalable and online manner. By shifting robotic interactions from the physical environment to an imaginative space, it overcomes the severe physical restrictions (hardware cost, safety risk, serial execution, manual resets) that typically bottleneck real-world RL. This paradigm shift could accelerate the development of more capable robots by reducing reliance on costly and time-consuming real-world data collection.
- **Enhanced Robustness and Precision**: The experimental results demonstrate that RISE yields substantial performance improvements across challenging real-world tasks requiring high dynamics, dexterity, and precision (dynamic brick sorting, backpack packing, box closing). This indicates that imagination-based learning can effectively bootstrap and refine robot policies, making them more robust to execution deviations and subtle failures in contact-rich environments. This directly addresses a key limitation of existing VLA models, which often struggle with acquiring robust manipulation skills.
- **Compositional World Model as an Online Learning Environment**: A core technical contribution is the Compositional World Model, which effectively disentangles dynamics prediction from value estimation. This modular design allows for the flexible integration of distinct, best-suited architectures and objectives for each component, resulting in a more reliable and informative simulation environment. The advancements in controllable dynamics and failure-sensitive value estimation are critical for generating stable learning signals for policy improvement, making world models an effective tool for on-policy RL in challenging real-world scenarios.
- **Foundation for Future Research**: RISE establishes world models as a viable and effective learning environment for complex real-world manipulation. This work serves as a reference for the community, opening new avenues for exploring scalable self-improving VLA models and deepening the understanding of how learned simulations can augment physical interaction in robot learning.

While offering substantial advancements, the paper also identifies limitations that indicate future research directions and potential impacts:
- **Bridging the Imagination-Realism Gap**: The effectiveness of RISE is constrained by the accuracy of the learned world model, which can still produce physically implausible transitions in novel or rare scenarios. Future work focusing on uncertainty-aware imagination and explicit physical constraints could further enhance the model's reliability, potentially leading to more generalized and safer robotic behaviors.
- **Optimizing Simulated-Real Data Balance**: The study suggests that a balanced mix of real-world and simulated data is crucial. Further research into understanding the principles and optimal ratios for integrating these data sources could lead to more efficient and robust learning procedures, maximizing the utility of both scarce physical data and abundant synthetic experience.
- **Shifting from Physical to Compute Cost**: RISE shifts the primary bottleneck from physical interaction to computational cost associated with training and running high-fidelity world models. Improvements in the efficiency of world models will be critical for broader applicability, especially in compute-constrained environments.

In conclusion, RISE represents a significant step towards autonomous, self-improving robotic intelligence. By demonstrating the feasibility and efficacy of on-policy reinforcement learning through imagination in complex real-world tasks, this work contributes to the vision of robots that can continuously learn, adapt, and perform dexterous manipulation with reduced reliance on human intervention and costly physical trials.
