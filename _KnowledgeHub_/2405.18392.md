# Scaling Laws and Compute-Optimal Training Beyond Fixed Training Durations

> [!summary] Summary
> Constant learning rates with a short cooldown or Stochastic Weight Averaging can match the performance of cosine schedules in large language model training while reducing the compute needed for scaling law experiments by a factor of two or more. This work provides flexible and efficient alternatives to the prevailing fixed-duration training paradigm for LLMs.

## Problem

- The standard cosine learning rate schedule necessitates knowing the full training duration in advance, making LLM training inflexible and costly for iterative experimentation or continual learning.
- Deriving scaling laws traditionally requires training numerous models from scratch for each target duration, leading to high computational expense (GPU hours and FLOPs).
- The rigidity of existing training recipes hinders the broader accessibility of LLM research and slows down the refinement of scaling laws for new architectures and data mixtures.

## Method

- Proposes a simpler learning rate strategy: a ==constant learning rate== for most of training, followed by a short, aggressive ==cooldown== phase.
- Investigates various functional forms for the cooldown, identifying a ==(1-sqrt) decay== as optimal, and determines efficient cooldown lengths (e.g., 5-20% of total steps).
- Explores ==Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)== and Schedule-Free Optimizers (SFO) as alternative methods to achieve robust performance without explicit long-term learning rate decay.

## Results

- The constant learning rate with a short cooldown strategy achieves perplexity values comparable to or better than a well-tuned cosine schedule across different training durations and model sizes (up to **8B parameters**).
- Stochastic Weight Averaging significantly improves the performance of models trained with a constant learning rate, closing much of the gap to explicit cooldowns, and enhances generalization for cosine-trained models.
- The proposed constant LR + cooldown method reduces the total compute and GPU hours for scaling law experiments by a factor of **two or more** compared to traditional cosine-based approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A constant learning rate combined with a brief cooldown phase can achieve performance equivalent to the cosine learning rate schedule for LLMs.
> - Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) provides strong intermediate models and improves generalization, offering a method to obtain robust checkpoints throughout training.
> - The (1-sqrt) functional form for the cooldown phase consistently outperforms standard linear decay and often surpasses untuned cosine schedules.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of Large Language Models (LLMs) has witnessed unprecedented growth, largely driven by the concept of "scale" – increasing model parameters, training data, and compute. This paradigm was notably formalized by **scaling laws**, first highlighted by Kaplan et al. (2020) and later refined by Hoffmann et al. (2022) with the "Chinchilla" paper. Chinchilla demonstrated that for optimal performance (lowest loss) at a given compute budget, models should be scaled simultaneously in parameters (N) and training tokens (D) in a roughly fixed ratio (e.g., D/N ≈ 20).

Crucially, the Chinchilla project and subsequent large model training (e.g., LLaMA, PaLM) heavily relied on the **cosine learning rate schedule** as a de-facto standard. This schedule involves a warm-up phase, followed by a gradual decay of the learning rate, typically to 10% of its peak value, over the entire training duration. A significant implication of the cosine schedule, as observed by Chinchilla, is that its "optimality" is tied to the pre-defined total training length; if training is stopped early or extended, the model performance might be suboptimal. This imposes a rigid constraint: to evaluate a model's scaling behavior or the impact of architectural/data changes across different training durations, one must train multiple models *from scratch* for each target duration. This process is inherently compute-intensive and costly.

This paper directly challenges the necessity of this rigid reliance on the cosine schedule. It argues that the prevailing research on training recipes and scaling laws has become "needlessly complex" and expensive due to this constraint. By proposing and validating alternative learning rate strategies that offer flexibility and allow for "reusable" training runs, the authors aim to make LLM research, particularly scaling studies, more accessible and efficient. This work contributes to a growing body of research exploring more efficient training methods, including alternative optimizers, adaptive learning rates, and the role of weight averaging, but specifically frames its contribution in the context of reducing the computational burden of scaling law derivation. Concurrent works by Porian et al. (2024) and Pearce & Song (2024) also explore discrepancies in scaling laws concerning learning rate, reinforcing the relevance of this paper's investigation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation behind this research stems from the high computational cost and inflexibility associated with training large language models and understanding their scaling properties, primarily due to the widespread adoption of the cosine learning rate schedule.

The **key objectives** of the paper are:

1. **Revisit and Question the Cosine Learning Rate Schedule:** To critically examine the long-standing prevalence of the cosine learning rate schedule in LLM training and its implications, particularly the requirement to know the full training duration in advance for optimal performance.
2. **Investigate Flexible Learning Rate Alternatives:** To propose and thoroughly evaluate a simpler, more flexible alternative: a constant learning rate followed by a short "cooldown" (or decay) phase. This includes exploring different functional forms and optimal lengths for this cooldown.
3. **Assess Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) and Schedule-Free Optimizers (SFO):** To explore whether these techniques can act as a replacement for explicit learning rate decay, offering robust performance at any point during training without separate cooldowns.
4. **Demonstrate Compute-Optimal Scaling Experiments:** To empirically show that the proposed constant LR + cooldown strategy, and potentially SWA, can significantly reduce the compute and GPU hours required for conducting scaling law experiments.
5. **Enable More Accessible and Flexible LLM Research:** Ultimately, to make scaling law derivation and continual pre-training more economically feasible and accessible to a wider range of researchers and practitioners, thereby fostering more frequent updates to scaling laws for evolving data mixtures and architectures.

The **motivation** is multifaceted:
- **Cost Reduction:** Training LLMs is immensely expensive in terms of time, energy, and compute. Any method that can achieve comparable performance with reduced resources is highly valuable.
- **Increased Flexibility:** The cosine schedule's dependency on a fixed training length hinders continual learning and iterative experimentation. A more flexible schedule would allow researchers to extend training or extract models at different stages without restarting.
- **Improved Scaling Law Derivation:** Current methods for deriving scaling laws involve many from-scratch training runs. Simplifying this process can accelerate fundamental research into how model performance scales with compute, parameters, and data.
- **Addressing Practical Challenges:** The paper explicitly acknowledges practical issues such as the difficulty of extrapolating loss curves beyond a cosine cycle and the challenges of rewarming learning rates for continued training.

By addressing these points, the authors aim to refine the fundamental "recipe" for LLM training, making it more adaptable and efficient for future advancements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a rigorous empirical methodology, conducting a multitude of training runs across various model sizes and training durations to systematically compare learning rate schedules.

**A. Experimental Setup:**

1. **Model Architecture:** The core model used is a decoder-only transformer, identical to architectures like LLaMA or Noam, featuring SwiGLU activations, RoPE embeddings, RMSNorm, and alternating attention/MLP blocks.
2. **Optimizer:** AdamW with standard beta parameters (β1=0.9, β2=0.95), weight decay of 0.1, and gradient clipping of 1.0.
3. **Warmup:** A short warm-up phase (300 steps for most runs, 1000-3000 for longer ones) is used across all schedules.
4. **Mixed Precision Training:** Bfloat16 for memory efficiency and throughput, with float32 parameters and bfloat16 activations.
5. **Infrastructure:** Experiments were primarily conducted on A100 GPUs (40GB/80GB) with 2 data-parallel GPUs per run, with some using 8 H100s. Larger 1B and 8B models utilized Hugging Face's `nanotron` library for parallelism on H100s and GH200s.
6. **Datasets:**
 - **SlimPajama (6B subset):** The primary dataset for most experiments, a cleaned and deduplicated corpus used for LLM pretraining.
 - **FineWeb:** Used for scaling up experiments with 1B and 8B parameter models.
 - **OpenWebText2:** Used for additional verification of findings.
7. **Evaluation Metrics:** Validation loss (perplexity) throughout training and full validation set perplexity at the end. For larger models (1B, 8B), downstream benchmarks (MMLU, ARC, PIQA, HellaSwag, etc.) are used, aggregated into a single score.

**B. Learning Rate Schedules and Techniques Investigated:**

1. **Cosine Learning Rate Schedule (Baseline):** The standard approach, decaying to 10% of the maximum LR. This serves as the benchmark against which alternatives are compared, particularly highlighting its fixed-duration optimality and inflexibility.
2. **Constant Learning Rate with Cooldown (Proposed Alternative):**
 - **Definition:** The learning rate is kept constant for the majority of training, with a short, aggressive decay phase (cooldown) at the end.
 - **Cooldown Forms:** Various functional forms for the decay are tested: linear, `1-sqrt` (their proposed best), cosine, mirror cosine, and `1-square`.
 - **Cooldown Length:** Ablations are performed to determine the optimal fraction of total steps dedicated to cooldown (e.g., 20%, 5%).
 - **LR Sensitivity:** Comparison of optimal LR values and sensitivity for cosine vs. constant+cooldown.
 - **Loss Landscape Analysis:** Investigation of the loss connectivity during the cooldown phase by linearly interpolating weights before and after cooldown.
3. **Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA):**
 - **Method:** Averaging model weights over fixed training windows (e.g., h=500 steps). The averaged model parameters are kept as an additional copy.
 - **Purpose:** To achieve improved generalization and simulate decayed learning rates without explicit decay.
 - **Comparison:** Applied to both constant LR and cosine schedules.
4. **Schedule-Free Optimizer (SFO):**
 - **Method:** An optimizer that uses an interpolation between standard averaging and Polyak-Ruppert averaging, theoretically not requiring a decreasing learning rate.
 - **Purpose:** To evaluate if a truly schedule-free approach can outperform cooldowns.
 - **Sensitivity:** Investigated sensitivity to momentum parameters (β1, β2).

**C. Scaling Law Experiment Design:**

- **Traditional (Cosine):** For each model size and each target token count (e.g., 10, 20, 30 tokens/parameter), a separate model is trained from scratch with a cosine schedule tuned to that specific length.
- **Proposed (Constant LR + Cooldown/SWA):** For each model size, only *one* sufficiently long training run is performed with a constant learning rate. To evaluate performance at different token counts, checkpoints from this single run are used:
 - **Cooldown:** A cooldown phase is initiated retrospectively from checkpoints at desired token counts.
 - **SWA:** The SWA model (which continuously averages weights) is evaluated at desired token counts.
- **Compute Savings Quantification:** FLOPs and GPU hours are measured for both traditional and proposed methods to demonstrate the reduction in computational cost.

This comprehensive approach allows the authors to not only demonstrate the viability of alternatives to the cosine schedule but also quantify their practical benefits in terms of computational efficiency and flexibility, particularly for the resource-intensive process of deriving scaling laws.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research paper presents several key findings that collectively challenge the reliance on the cosine learning rate schedule and offer more efficient alternatives for LLM training and scaling studies.

1. **Constant LR with Cooldown Matches Cosine Performance:**
 - The paper demonstrates that a constant learning rate followed by a short cooldown phase can achieve perplexity values almost perfectly matching those obtained with a well-tuned cosine schedule (Figure 3). This holds true across different training durations and exhibits slightly less sensitivity to learning rate variations.
 - **Optimal Cooldown Form:** A novel `(1-sqrt)` functional form for the cooldown phase consistently outperforms the standard linear decay, and both often surpass an untuned cosine schedule for longer training lengths (Figure 4).
 - **Optimal Cooldown Length:** The benefits of extended cooldown periods plateau around 20% of total training steps, suggesting that a short, dedicated cooldown is sufficient (Figure 5). For very long runs, even 5% cooldown steps can effectively match cosine performance (Figure 6), making the decay phase practically short.
 - **Cooldown Dynamics:** The sharp drop in loss during cooldown is consistent for both train and validation loss and correlates with the LR decay (Figure 15). Analysis of the loss landscape reveals that this transition corresponds to a smooth descent into a connected minimum (Figure 7).
 - **Downstream Task Performance:** These findings scale to larger models (1B and 8B parameters) and translate to comparable performance on various downstream benchmarks, with a noticeable uptick in aggregated scores correlating with the start of the cooldown (Figure 8, Figure 29).

2. **Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) Improves Generalization and Provides Intermediate Models:**
 - SWA significantly boosts performance when applied to a constant learning rate, closing a substantial portion of the gap to explicit cooldowns, though not fully matching them (Figure 10, left).
 - SWA also improves the generalization of models trained with a cosine schedule, with its intermediate checkpoints often aligning with the optimal loss trajectory of shorter cosine runs (Figure 10, right).
 - **Significance:** SWA offers a "for free" method to obtain stronger models along the training trajectory without additional training costs or separate decay phases, making it a valuable tool for extracting good models at any point in training.

3. **Schedule-Free Optimizer (SFO) Performance:**
 - While SFO provides strong performance and does not require an explicit schedule, it is sensitive to the choice of momentum parameters (β1, β2).
 - Despite good tuning, SFO generally performs slightly worse than the constant LR with cooldown strategy (Figure 11).

4. **Drastic Compute Savings for Scaling Law Research:**
 - This is arguably the most significant practical finding. The constant LR + cooldown schedule and SWA enable scaling experiments with *significantly reduced compute and GPU hours*.
 - Instead of training multiple models from scratch for different lengths (as required by cosine), researchers can train each model size only once for a long duration, then initiate cooldowns or use SWA from checkpoints.
 - **Quantified Savings:** In their experiments, this method reduced total FLOPs and GPU hours by approximately a factor of 2 or more (Figure 13a, Figure 24, Figure 25). The authors estimate that a large-scale project like Chinchilla could have saved more than half its compute (from 5.59 × 10^23 FLOPs to 2.36 × 10^23 FLOPs) using this approach (Figure 13b).
 - **Reliable Scaling:** The alternative schedules demonstrate reliable scaling behavior, with model performance (perplexity) aligning closely with cosine-trained models at equivalent compute levels (Figure 12).

In summary, the paper successfully demonstrates that the long-standing cosine learning rate schedule is not indispensable for achieving high-performance LLMs. Simpler, more flexible alternatives like constant LR with cooldown, or even SWA, can yield comparable results while offering profound benefits in terms of computational efficiency and experimental flexibility, particularly for the costly process of deriving scaling laws.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in this paper hold substantial significance and promise to exert a considerable impact on the future landscape of Large Language Model (LLM) research and development.

1. **Democratization and Accessibility of LLM Research:**
 - The most direct and impactful consequence is the drastic reduction in compute and GPU hours required for scaling experiments. By enabling researchers to perform these studies with "fewer but reusable training runs" (a factor of 2 or more savings), the barrier to entry for LLM research is significantly lowered. This can empower smaller academic labs and individual researchers to conduct empirical scaling studies that were previously exclusive to heavily resourced institutions.
 - This enhanced accessibility fosters a more diverse and vibrant research community, promoting innovation beyond the current compute-constrained environment.

2. **Accelerated Understanding of Scaling Laws:**
 - Scaling laws are foundational for efficient LLM development, guiding decisions on optimal resource allocation for model size and data. The ability to compute these laws more frequently and economically means that researchers can more quickly adapt to new insights regarding data quality, data mixtures (as highlighted by recent works from Bi et al., Goyal et al., Aghajanyan et al.), or novel architectures (e.g., Mamba, Griffin). This iterative refinement of scaling laws will lead to more efficient and effective LLM designs.

3. **Enhanced Flexibility for LLM Training and Deployment:**
 - **Continual Learning:** The proposed constant LR + cooldown schedule naturally supports continual pre-training. Researchers can train a model for an initial phase, save a checkpoint before the cooldown, and then seamlessly continue training with a high learning rate, avoiding the performance degradation and instability often associated with rewarming cosine schedules. This is critical for adapting models to new data or evolving tasks over time.
 - **Iterative Experimentation:** Developers can initiate cooldowns at any point during training to assess model performance, allowing for more agile and adaptive development workflows rather than committing to a fixed, pre-determined training duration.
 - **Post-Chinchilla Era Optimization:** In the current "post-Chinchilla" era, where models are often trained beyond compute-optimal points to reduce inference costs, the flexibility to extend training without retraining from scratch is invaluable.

4. **Refinement of Core LLM Training Best Practices:**
 - The paper directly challenges the long-standing status of the cosine learning rate schedule as the "de-facto standard." By demonstrating a simpler, equally effective alternative, it encourages the community to re-evaluate and optimize other fundamental components of the LLM training recipe. The identification of the `(1-sqrt)` cooldown form as superior to linear decay is a specific, actionable insight.

5. **Practical Tool for Model Development:**
 - The finding that Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) can provide robust, near-optimal models throughout the training trajectory, often "for free" as an additional checkpoint, is a highly practical contribution. It offers a straightforward way for practitioners to extract strong models at various training stages without the need for complex hyperparameter tuning or additional dedicated training phases.

While the authors acknowledge limitations, such as conducting experiments up to 8B parameters (though trends were consistent across scales) and potential brittleness at "modern scales" and "extremely long training," their work provides a robust empirical foundation. The demonstrated compute savings and increased flexibility offer a clear path towards more sustainable, efficient, and accessible research and development in the rapidly evolving field of large language models. This research lays the groundwork for more dynamic and responsive approaches to understanding and optimizing the training of future generations of AI models.
