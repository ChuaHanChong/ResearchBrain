# Scaling Laws and Compute-Optimal Training Beyond Fixed Training Durations

> [!summary] Summary
> Constant learning rates with a short cooldown or Stochastic Weight Averaging can match the performance of cosine schedules in large language model training while reducing the compute needed for scaling law experiments by a factor of two or more. This work provides flexible and efficient alternatives to the prevailing fixed-duration training paradigm for LLMs.

## Problem

- The standard cosine learning rate schedule necessitates knowing the full training duration in advance, making LLM training inflexible and costly for iterative experimentation or continual learning.
- Deriving scaling laws traditionally requires training numerous models from scratch for each target duration, leading to high computational expense (GPU hours and FLOPs).
- The rigidity of existing training recipes hinders the broader accessibility of LLM research and slows down the refinement of scaling laws for new architectures and data mixtures.

## Method

- Proposes a simpler learning rate strategy: a ==constant learning rate== for most of training, followed by a short, aggressive ==cooldown== phase.
- Investigates various functional forms for the cooldown, identifying a ==(1-sqrt) decay== as optimal, and determines efficient cooldown lengths (e.g., 5-20% of total steps).
- Explores ==Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA)== and Schedule-Free Optimizers (SFO) as alternative methods to achieve robust performance without explicit long-term learning rate decay.

## Results

- The constant learning rate with a short cooldown strategy achieves perplexity values comparable to or better than a well-tuned cosine schedule across different training durations and model sizes (up to **8B parameters**).
- Stochastic Weight Averaging significantly improves the performance of models trained with a constant learning rate, closing much of the gap to explicit cooldowns, and enhances generalization for cosine-trained models.
- The proposed constant LR + cooldown method reduces the total compute and GPU hours for scaling law experiments by a factor of **two or more** compared to traditional cosine-based approaches.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A constant learning rate combined with a brief cooldown phase can achieve performance equivalent to the cosine learning rate schedule for LLMs.
> - Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) provides strong intermediate models and improves generalization, offering a method to obtain robust checkpoints throughout training.
> - The (1-sqrt) functional form for the cooldown phase consistently outperforms standard linear decay and often surpasses untuned cosine schedules.
