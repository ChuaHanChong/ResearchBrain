# The Evolution of Tool Use in LLM Agents: From Single-Tool Call to Multi-Tool Orchestration

> [!summary] Summary
> This survey systematically reviews the evolution of Large Language Model agents from single-tool calls to complex multi-tool orchestration, unifying task formulations and analyzing advancements across six critical dimensions. It identifies key challenges and future research directions for developing reliable, scalable, and verifiable multi-tool agents for real-world applications.

## Problem

- Large Language Models (LLMs) are inherently limited by static parametric knowledge, hallucination, and inability to interact with external environments.
- The existing literature suffers from conceptual ambiguity, using various terms like "tool use" and "orchestration" interchangeably despite distinct capabilities.
- Research on LLM agents is fragmented, with critical aspects like planning, training, and safety often studied in isolation, hindering holistic system development.

## Method

- The authors establish a formal problem formulation for ==multi-tool orchestration== to provide a unified conceptual basis for research.
- They systematically organize the extensive literature into ==six core dimensions==, covering ==inference==, ==training==, ==safety==, ==efficiency==, ==completeness==, and ==benchmarking==.
- The survey identifies and summarizes representative applications, major challenges, and future research directions to guide the development of robust ==multi-tool agents==.

## Results

- Inference-time paradigms have advanced to include graph-structured execution, hierarchical planning, dynamic memory management, and agent self-improvement capabilities.
- Efficiency improvements are achieved through parallel execution, asynchronous decoupling, speculative reasoning, dynamic tool search, and adaptive model routing.
- Benchmarks now emphasize system-level evaluation, assessing topological complexity, temporal scale in long-horizon tasks, adaptation to dynamic environments, and mechanisms for state persistence and self-correction.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Advanced LLM agents require a shift from isolated tool invocation to complex multi-tool orchestration involving topology-aware planning and hierarchical delegation.
> - Training paradigms have moved towards sophisticated data synthesis, large-scale supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement learning to optimize multi-tool decision-making.
> - Safety in multi-tool systems necessitates robust defense mechanisms against parallel execution risks (e.g., race conditions) and long-horizon threats (e.g., state contamination, memory poisoning).
