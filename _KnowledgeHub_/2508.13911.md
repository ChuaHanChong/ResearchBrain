# PhysGM: Large Physical Gaussian Model for Feed-Forward 4D Synthesis

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysGM introduces a transformer-based feed-forward framework that generates physically-grounded 4D simulations and 3D Gaussian Splatting representations directly from a single image input. This approach achieves sub-minute 4D content creation while outperforming iterative optimization-based methods in physical plausibility and visual quality, supported by the new PhysAssets dataset for training.

## Problem

- Existing 4D content generation methods rely on time-consuming, per-scene optimization for 3DGS reconstruction, and integrating physics often requires manual property specification or unstable video-model-guided optimization, failing to resolve core efficiency bottlenecks.
- Prior approaches typically decouple appearance and physical property prediction, overlooking physical cues embedded in visual appearance and leading to suboptimal physical simulations.
- There is a lack of large-scale datasets that pair 3D assets with accurate physical property annotations and corresponding physically plausible simulation videos.

## Method

- Developed **PhysGM**, a transformer-based feed-forward framework that infers 3DGS representation and physical properties from a single image, integrating the ==Material Point Method (MPM)== with a one-to-one correspondence between material points and Gaussian primitives for coherent deformation.
- Implemented a two-stage training paradigm combining supervised pre-training with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) fine-tuning to leverage feedback from the non-differentiable physics simulator and rendering pipeline.
- Constructed PhysAssets, a large dataset of over 50,000 3D objects annotated with physical attributes and ground-truth simulation videos, to support training and evaluation.

## Results

- Achieved optimization-free 4D generation, producing physically-grounded simulations from a single image in under one minute, demonstrating significant speedup and generalization across diverse objects and material types.
- Outperformed optimization-based baselines (OmniPhysGS, DreamerPhysics) in physical plausibility and visual quality; DPO fine-tuning improved CLIP_sim from 0.2693 to 0.2748 and User Preference Rate from 30% to 42.8%.
- Demonstrated strong multi-view synthesis capabilities, achieving competitive 3D Gaussian reconstruction quality (e.g., LPIPS 0.039, SSIM 0.953 at 512 resolution) on the GSO dataset.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly predicting both 3DGS appearance and physical properties enables the model to learn physics-aware reconstructions, effectively leveraging implicit physical cues from visual data.
> - Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) can effectively refine generative models in complex, non-differentiable simulation environments, significantly improving perceptual realism and physical plausibility without requiring gradient computation through the simulator.
> - A feed-forward architecture can achieve competitive or superior 4D synthesis quality compared to iterative optimization-based methods, drastically reducing generation time to under one minute.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in "PhysGM" addresses challenges within the fields of 3D content generation, particularly 4D (dynamic 3D) scene synthesis, and physics-based simulation. Recent advancements in explicit 3D representations, such as 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS), have improved novel-view synthesis for static scenes. The research landscape is now shifting towards imbuing these representations with dynamic, physically plausible behaviors, which is crucial for applications in virtual reality, robotics, and autonomous systems.

Existing paradigms for 4D content generation generally fall into two categories. The first involves distilling knowledge from pre-trained 2D/video diffusion models into dynamic 3D representations, often utilizing techniques like Score Distillation Sampling (SDS). While these methods can generate temporally coherent animations, they are computationally intensive, prone to instability due to their iterative nature, and typically physics-agnostic, often producing visually appealing but physically implausible motions. The second paradigm focuses on training generative models directly on 3D data, capable of generating various representations from point clouds to implicit functions and 3D Gaussians. A primary limitation here is the scarcity of large-scale, dynamic 3D datasets. Both paradigms primarily focus on visual coherence rather than physical accuracy.

The integration of physics into 4D generation is an emerging area. Prior work, such as PhysGaussian, coupled 3DGS with Material Point Method (MPM) simulations, but required manual, per-scene parameter tuning. Subsequent efforts have attempted to automate this, often leveraging SDS to learn material properties or using Large Language Models (LLMs) to infer parameters. However, these approaches remain fundamentally limited by their reliance on pre-reconstructed 3D models and slow, per-scene optimization processes, which hinders their scalability and generalization for real-time or large-scale deployment.

PhysGM positions itself to overcome these limitations by proposing a feed-forward framework that unifies 3DGS reconstruction with physical property prediction and dynamics simulation. It seeks to bypass per-scene optimization entirely, learn a generalizable physical prior, and address the issue of ignoring physical cues embedded in visual appearance by jointly predicting appearance and physical properties. The introduction of the PhysAssets dataset also contributes to addressing the data scarcity bottleneck in this domain.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a feed-forward framework, named PhysGM, capable of generating physically-grounded 4D simulations directly from a single image input in an efficient, optimization-free manner. This objective is motivated by several identified limitations in existing physics-based 3D motion synthesis methods:

1. **Reliance on Time-Consuming Per-Scene Optimization for 3DGS Reconstruction:** Current approaches typically require first reconstructing a 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation from dense multi-view images, a process that involves computationally expensive and iterative per-scene optimization. This bottleneck makes current methods unsuitable for real-time or large-scale applications. PhysGM aims to eliminate this by inferring 3DGS directly in a single feed-forward pass.

2. **Inefficient and Unstable Physics Integration:**
 - **Manual Attribute Specification:** Traditional methods often necessitate manual specification of physical properties (e.g., stiffness, mass) through configuration files, which is labor-intensive, lacks scalability, and requires expert knowledge.
 - **Optimization-Heavy Guidance from Video Models:** More recent attempts to learn physical properties use techniques like Score Distillation Sampling (SDS) from video models, which involve gradient backpropagation through differentiable physics simulators. This process is computationally heavy, inefficient, and can be unstable, failing to resolve the core efficiency bottleneck. PhysGM aims for an optimization-free approach by directly predicting these properties.

3. **Suboptimal Performance Due to Disjoint Modules:** The common practice of naively concatenating pre-built 3DGS models with separate physics modules often overlooks the physical information implicitly embedded in the appearance of objects. This separation can lead to suboptimal performance in predicting realistic physical behaviors. PhysGM addresses this by jointly predicting both Gaussian representation and physical properties, enabling the model to learn a physics-aware reconstruction.

4. **Absence of Supporting Datasets:** A significant challenge in developing generative models for this task is the lack of large-scale datasets that pair 3D assets with accurate physical property annotations and corresponding physically plausible simulation videos. To overcome this, the authors motivate the creation and release of PhysAssets, a substantial benchmark to train and evaluate such models.

By addressing these limitations, PhysGM seeks to enable immediate simulation and high-fidelity 4D rendering from sparse inputs, providing a more efficient, scalable, and generalizable solution for dynamic 3D content creation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysGM is a transformer-based framework designed for the feed-forward synthesis of dynamic 4D scenes from image inputs. Its methodology involves a novel two-stage training paradigm and the development of a new dataset.

#### 3.1. 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) Fundamentals

The scene is represented as a collection of `N` anisotropic 3D Gaussians. Each Gaussian is parameterized by its position (μ), covariance matrix (Σ) defining shape and orientation, opacity (α), and Spherical Harmonics (SH) coefficients (c) for view-dependent color. The covariance matrix is derived from a rotation matrix (`R_mat`) and a diagonal scaling matrix (`S`). During rendering, these Gaussians are projected onto the 2D image plane and alpha-blended to compute pixel colors.

#### 3.2. Model Architecture

PhysGM employs a transformer-based architecture comprising three main components:

- **Multi-modality Tokenization:**
 - An image encoder (DINOv3 ViT-L/16) processes input images into high-dimensional features.
 - A dense representation encoder processes Plücker ray coordinates for each pixel, explicitly encoding camera geometry into camera tokens.
 - Image and camera tokens are concatenated for each view.
 - Three learnable global tokens are prepended to the token sequence to aggregate global scene information, specifically for physics prediction.
 - For single-image inference, an MVAdapter synthesizes three auxiliary views (rear, left, right) from the single frontal input image, providing comprehensive angular coverage.

- **Transformer Backbone:** The concatenated input tokens are fed into a 24-layer transformer backbone. This backbone learns contextualized representations and provides multi-scale features for subsequent prediction heads.

- **Prediction Heads:** Two specialized heads map the backbone’s output tokens:
 - **3DGS Prediction Head (DPT Head):** A Dense Prediction Transformer (DPT) head `f_gs` takes multi-scale features and upsamples them to output per-pixel maps corresponding to 3DGS parameters (mean, scale, opacity, rotation, SH coefficients) for each input view. Gaussians from all views are aggregated. During pre-training, this head is optimized using MSE, alpha, and LPIPS losses between rendered and ground-truth images.
 - **Physics Prediction Head (Physics Head):** This head utilizes the three global tokens to predict three physical attributes:
 - A classification head `f_material` determines the material class (categorized into `N_c` classes).
 - Two regression heads `f_phys` predict the mean (μ_θ) and log-variance (log σ²_θ) for Young's modulus (E) and Poisson's ratio (ν). This probabilistic formulation (a conditional Gaussian distribution) models uncertainty and allows for sampling diverse physical parameters.

#### 3.3. Physics-based Dynamics via Material Point Method (MPM)

PhysGM uses the Material Point Method (MPM) to simulate physics-based dynamics. MPM is a hybrid Lagrangian-Eulerian approach where objects are discretized into material points, each with physical state (mass, position, velocity, affine velocity matrix, and deformation gradient).

- **Simulation Steps:**
 1. **Particle-to-Grid (P2G) Transfer:** Particle properties (mass, momentum) are mapped to a background Eulerian grid using weighted summation. Internal forces (from deformation gradient and constitutive model) and external forces (gravity) are computed on the grid to update grid velocities.
 2. **Grid-to-Particle (G2P) Transfer:** Updated grid velocities are interpolated back to update particle states (velocity, deformation gradient). Particle positions are then updated.
- **Coupling with 3DGS:** A one-to-one correspondence is established between material points and Gaussian primitives. The updated particle position directly defines the Gaussian's mean (μ). The deformation gradient (`F_p`) is polar decomposed into a rotation matrix (`R_p`) and a stretch tensor (`S_p`). These components are then used to define the Gaussian’s rotation matrix (`R_mat`) and scaling vector (`s`), respectively, which construct the covariance matrix (Σ), ensuring geometric deformation mirrors the physical simulation.
- **Constitutive Models:** The predicted material class determines the constitutive model used in MPM:
 - **Neo-Hookean Model:** For soft materials like "jelly" (captures large, nonlinear deformations).
 - **Fixed Corotational (FCR) Model:** For stiff elastic solids like "metal" (suited for large rigid-body motions with small elastic deformations).
 - **Drucker-Prager Plasticity Model:** For frictional and cohesive materials like "sand," "snow," and "plasticine" (defines a yield criterion separating elastic from plastic deformation).

#### 3.4. Preference-based Fine-tuning with Direct Preference Optimization (DPO)

A second fine-tuning stage uses Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) to refine the pre-trained model. This allows for leveraging feedback from the non-differentiable physics simulator and rendering pipeline.

- **Preference Pair Creation:** For a given scene context, `K` candidate parameter vectors (physical properties θ and fixed appearance ψ) are sampled from the current policy. For each candidate, an MPM simulation is run, and the resulting 3D Gaussian sequence is rendered into a video clip (`V_k`). Each `V_k` is compared to a ground-truth video (`V_gt`) using a perceptual distance metric (`d`). The parameter set yielding the lowest distance is the "winner" (ϕ_w), and one with the highest distance is the "loser" (ϕ_l).
- **DPO Loss:** These dynamically generated preference pairs are used to fine-tune the model by minimizing the DPO loss. This objective directly increases the likelihood of generating "winner" parameters and decreases the likelihood of "loser" parameters, relative to a fixed reference policy, without requiring gradient computation through the complex simulator.

#### 3.5. PhysAssets Dataset

To support training and evaluation, the authors constructed PhysAssets, a dataset of over 50,000 3D objects aggregated from Objaverse, OmniObject3D, ABO, and HSSD.

- **Annotation Pipeline:** A Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) (Qwen3-VL) is used to annotate physical attributes. For each object, multi-view images are used with prompts to predict its material category, Young’s modulus (E), and Poisson’s ratio (ν). The continuous properties are mapped to discrete categories for MLLM prediction and then converted to numerical values.
- **Ground-Truth Videos:** Physically plausible ground-truth videos are generated using Framepack, conditioned on text instructions specifying the object’s physical properties.
- **Automated Preference Labeling:** For DPO training, preference labeling is automated by comparing simulated videos to ground-truth videos. This involves:
 - SAM-2 for instance segmentation and CoTracker-3 for trajectory extraction.
 - A three-stage trajectory alignment pipeline (spatial, landing frame, temporal) to account for viewpoint, scale, and temporal dynamics differences.
 - An L2-distance-based similarity metric to quantify fidelity and rank candidate simulations for DPO.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents quantitative and qualitative evaluations demonstrating the effectiveness and efficiency of PhysGM.

#### 4.1. Optimization-Free 4D Generation

PhysGM performs optimization-free 4D generation, capable of producing a 3D Gaussian representation with physical properties from a single input image in a single feed-forward pass. This allows for physics-based simulation in under one minute, representing a significant speedup compared to prior iterative methods. The model exhibits robustness and generalization across diverse objects, material types, and physical interactions, including complex deformations like stretching and twisting, multi-object dynamics, and processing of real-world data.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Comparisons with Baselines

PhysGM was quantitatively compared against two optimization-based baselines, OmniPhysGS and DreamerPhysics, which rely on Score Distillation Sampling (SDS). Evaluations were conducted across five different material types using CLIP_sim (semantic similarity, higher is better) and User Preference Rate (UPR, higher is better).

| Method | CLIP_sim (Avg) | UPR (Avg) |
|:------------------- |:------------- |:-------- |
| OmniPhysGS | 0.2091 | 10% |
| DreamPhysics | 0.2291 | 17.2% |
| PhysGM (w/o DPO) | 0.2693 | 30% |
| PhysGM (w/ DPO) | 0.2748 | 42.8% |

PhysGM (with DPO) achieved an average CLIP_sim of 0.2748 and UPR of 42.8%, outperforming OmniPhysGS (0.2091 CLIP_sim, 10% UPR) and DreamPhysics (0.2291 CLIP_sim, 17.2% UPR). This indicates that PhysGM produces physically plausible and perceptually realistic simulations at a higher quality than optimization-based techniques, despite its feed-forward nature.

#### 4.3. Multi-View Synthesis (MVS) Effectiveness

For 3D Gaussian reconstruction quality, PhysGM was compared against other Gaussian-based reconstruction methods on the GSO dataset using PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS metrics.

| Methods | Res. | PSNR ↑ | SSIM ↑ | LPIPS ↓ |
|:---------- |:--- |:----- |:----- |:------ |
| LGM | 256 | 21.44 | 0.832 | 0.122 |
| Our | 256 | 25.47 | 0.916 | 0.071 |
| GS-LRM | 512 | 30.52 | 0.952 | 0.050 |
| Ours | 512 | 28.95 | 0.953 | 0.039 |

PhysGM demonstrated strong performance in multi-view synthesis, achieving competitive or superior results (e.g., PSNR 28.95, SSIM 0.953, LPIPS 0.039 at 512 resolution) compared to methods like LGM and GS-LRM, while using a reduced amount of training data.

#### 4.4. Ablation Study on DPO Fine-tuning

An ablation study specifically evaluated the contribution of the Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) fine-tuning stage. Comparing PhysGM with DPO to a variant trained only with supervised pre-training ("w/o DPO") showed that the DPO stage significantly enhances performance.

| Methods | CLIP_sim | UPR |
|:----------------- |:------- |:--- |
| PhysGM (w/o DPO) | 0.2693 | 30% |
| PhysGM (w/ DPO) | 0.2748 | 42.8%|

The full PhysGM model achieved consistently higher CLIP_sim and UPR scores, indicating that DPO effectively refines the model to produce perceptually and physically superior simulations by leveraging feedback from the non-differentiable simulation pipeline. The DPO stage converts a statistically robust prior into a generator with improved perceptual quality.

#### 4.5. Efficiency and Quality-Time Trade-off

PhysGM requires no per-scene optimization during inference, relying on a single forward pass followed by MPM rollout. This enables end-to-end generation in under one minute, contrasting with competing approaches that take hours. This efficiency, combined with superior or comparable visual and physical fidelity, results in a more favorable quality-time trade-off.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of PhysGM represents a notable advancement in the field of 4D content generation and physics-based simulation, primarily due to its feed-forward, optimization-free approach.

**Key Significance:**

1. **Efficiency and Scalability:** By eliminating the reliance on time-consuming per-scene optimization, PhysGM enables rapid 4D synthesis, producing physically-grounded simulations in under a minute from a single image. This efficiency makes it suitable for scalable deployment in applications where real-time or near real-time generation is critical.
2. **Joint Appearance and Physics Prediction:** The framework's ability to jointly predict both 3D Gaussian appearance and physical properties addresses a limitation of prior work that decoupled these aspects. This joint learning allows the model to leverage physical cues embedded in visual data, leading to more accurate and plausible simulations.
3. **Novel Training Paradigm:** The two-stage training strategy, combining large-scale supervised pre-training with DPO-based refinement, is a significant methodological contribution. DPO's effectiveness in leveraging feedback from non-differentiable simulators to enhance perceptual realism and physical plausibility offers a pathway for improving generative models in complex simulation environments.
4. **Dataset Contribution:** The introduction of the PhysAssets Dataset, comprising over 50,000 3D assets annotated with physical properties and corresponding simulation videos, addresses a critical data scarcity issue in this research domain. This resource is poised to accelerate future research and development in generative 4D models.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** The ability to rapidly generate physically accurate 4D content can provide realistic training environments for embodied AI agents and robots, allowing them to learn interaction skills in diverse and dynamic virtual worlds.
- **Autonomous Systems:** For autonomous driving and other intelligent systems, understanding and predicting the physical behavior of objects in dynamic scenes is crucial. PhysGM could contribute to generating diverse physically simulated scenarios for testing and development.
- **Interactive Virtual Reality and Gaming:** The fast generation of physically plausible dynamic objects and scenes can enhance the realism and interactivity of virtual reality experiences and video games, enabling more immersive user engagement.
- **Content Creation:** It offers a more efficient tool for creators to generate dynamic 3D assets with realistic physical behaviors, reducing manual effort and computational costs.

**Limitations and Future Directions (as identified by the authors):**

- **Computational Cost of MPM:** The Material Point Method, while effective, remains computationally expensive, limiting its use in large-scale, real-time applications, especially for complex physics like fluid and fracture. Future work should focus on developing more efficient simulation frameworks.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Discrepancies between synthetic training data and physical reality, including simplified constitutive models, hinder robust real-world deployment. Bridging this gap is an important future research direction.
- **Data Dependency and Generalization:** The model's performance is tied to the PhysAssets dataset, which primarily consists of rigid objects. Generalization to highly deformable or articulated objects may be limited. Expanding the dataset and exploring domain adaptation techniques could address this.
- **Simplified Physics Representation:** PhysGM currently predicts a single, lumped vector of physical properties for an entire object, assuming uniform material composition. Extending the framework to predict spatially varying material properties would enable more complex and realistic simulations.
