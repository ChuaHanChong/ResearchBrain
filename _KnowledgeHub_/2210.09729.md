# HUMANISE: Language-conditioned Human Motion Generation in 3D Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> HUMANISE is a large-scale synthetic human-scene interaction dataset built by aligning AMASS motion capture with ScanNet indoor scans under collision and contact constraints, then auto-annotating each clip with templated referential language. It contains 19.6k motion sequences across 643 scenes and enables language-conditioned motion generation, tackled by a cVAE with scene-language self-attention and auxiliary grounding losses.

## Problem

- Existing human-scene interaction datasets are tiny: PROX covers 12 scenes with jittery fitted poses, GTA-IM only 49 synthetic scenes lacking body shape and movement diversity.
- Prior datasets lack action labels and complete 3D semantic segmentation, so models cannot learn instruction-aware motions grounded to specific objects referred to by language.
- Text-conditioned motion methods ignore scene context, while scene-conditioned methods generate uncontrollable motions or need explicit goal positions, since semantic constraints are absent.

## Method

- Motion alignment pipeline samples global translation and rotation for ==AMASS== clips using k-d-tree collision checks plus per-action contact constraints for sit, stand-up, walk, and lie-down.
- Compositional language templates borrowed from referential utterances emit action, target class, spatial relation, and anchor class, e.g. sit on the armchair near the desk.
- A ==cVAE== fuses ==Point Transformer== scene features and ==BERT== word features via self-attention, decodes ==SMPL-X== motion with a transformer, adding object-center regression and action-classification auxiliary losses.

## Results

- The dataset holds 19.6k clips over 643 scenes and 1.2M frames, with lower collision distance than PROX (**0.012** vs **0.024**) and higher rated quality (**4.14** vs **3.32**).
- Removing both auxiliary losses raises goal distance from **1.008** to **1.361** m and drops quality score from **2.57** to **1.97** and action score from **3.59** to **2.44**.
- Pretraining on HUMANISE then fine-tuning on PROX improves motion synthesis over PROX-only training: MPJPE **222.65** vs **242.50** mm, MPVPE **201.92** vs **222.13** mm.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Constraint-based retargeting of mocap into scanned scenes is a cheap route to large, semantically labelled interaction data without new capture hardware.
> - Auxiliary grounding and action-classification heads on the conditioning latent buy semantic correctness almost for free, barely changing reconstruction error.
> - Language grounding, not motion synthesis, is the bottleneck: giving ground-truth target position cuts goal distance to 0.207 m, and easy-to-ground large furniture inflates scores.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of human motion synthesis, particularly in the context of 3D scenes, has seen significant advancements. However, prior works often operate under severe limitations that hinder their practical applicability and generality. This paper addresses key gaps in the existing research landscape by tackling the challenge of generating diverse, scene-aware, and goal-oriented human motions in 3D environments, conditioned by natural language instructions.

**Challenges in Existing Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) Datasets:**
The paper identifies fundamental issues with previous HSI datasets, such as PROX [Hassan et al., 2019] and GTA-IM [Cao et al., 2020]. These datasets suffer from:
- **Limited Scale and Quality:** PROX, while valuable, contains only 12 indoor scenes with some jittering motions. GTA-IM, a synthetic dataset, has only 49 scenes and lacks diverse human shapes and motion styles.
- **Absence of Scene and Interaction Semantics:** Crucially, existing datasets often lack comprehensive 3D semantic segmentation annotations and, more significantly, human action labels or language descriptions. This semantic gap prevents models from learning to generate instruction-aware and generalizable motions.

**Limitations of Current Motion Generation Techniques:**
Existing human motion synthesis methods have explored various conditioning inputs:
- **Past Motion:** Generating future motion based on a sequence of past poses.
- **3D Scenes/Objects:** Synthesizing motions that physically interact with or navigate around scene geometry or specific objects.
- **Action Labels:** Generating motions corresponding to high-level action categories (e.g., "walking").
- **Language Descriptions (Motion Alone):** Some works generate motions from text, but typically ignore the crucial scene context.

The major limitation is the inability of these methods to jointly reason about a 3D scene, human motion, and natural language instructions. For instance, instructing a virtual agent to "sit on the armchair near the desk" requires understanding the "sit" action, identifying the specific "armchair" and its relation to the "desk" in a 3D environment, and then generating a physically plausible motion that culminates in the desired interaction. This holistic understanding is largely absent in previous approaches due to data limitations.

**Language Grounding in 3D Scenes:**
Another related but distinct area is language grounding in 3D scenes, which focuses on locating 3D objects based on natural language descriptions (e.g., ScanRefer [Chen et al., 2020]). While this addresses part of the problem, it typically does not extend to dynamic human-scene interactions or motion generation.

HUMANISE directly addresses these limitations by:
1. **Creating a large-scale, semantic-rich HSI dataset:** This dataset fills the critical data gap by providing high-quality human motions aligned with diverse 3D scenes and enriched with detailed language descriptions.
2. **Introducing a novel task:** Language-conditioned human motion generation in 3D scenes, which necessitates joint understanding across modalities.
3. **Developing a multi-modal generative model:** This model is specifically designed to leverage scene and language context to produce accurate and diverse human motions.

By integrating these aspects, HUMANISE pushes the boundaries of embodied AI, paving the way for more intelligent virtual agents and humanoid robots capable of comprehending and executing complex, semantically rich commands in realistic 3D environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core objectives and motivations of this research are clearly articulated in the abstract and introduction:

**Primary Objective 1: Address Data Limitations with a Novel Dataset.**
- **Motivation:** Existing Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) datasets are severely limited in scale, quality, and semantic annotations, particularly lacking explicit language descriptions of actions and interacting objects. This deficiency prevents models from learning to generate scene-aware and instruction-aware human motions.
- **Solution:** Propose **HUMANISE**, a large-scale and semantic-rich synthetic HSI dataset. It aims to provide high-quality motion sequences aligned with diverse 3D indoor scenes, automatically annotated with detailed language descriptions.

**Primary Objective 2: Define a New and Challenging Generation Task.**
- **Motivation:** Given the enriched data in HUMANISE, it becomes feasible and necessary to enable a more sophisticated motion generation task. Current methods cannot handle instructions that combine action type, specific objects, and spatial relationships within a 3D scene.
- **Solution:** Introduce the task of **language-conditioned human motion generation in 3D scenes**. This task requires models to generate diverse and physically plausible human motions that adhere to a specified action type and precisely interact with designated 3D objects, all dictated by a natural language description. The authors highlight this task's complexity, requiring multi-modal understanding (3D scene + language), language grounding (correct action at target location), and physically plausible generation.

**Primary Objective 3: Develop a Robust Generative Model for the New Task.**
- **Motivation:** The proposed task is inherently challenging, demanding capabilities beyond current motion synthesis models. A new model architecture is required that can jointly process and reason about 3D scene geometry, human motion dynamics, and the nuances of natural language instructions.
- **Solution:** Present a **novel scene-and-language conditioned generative model**. This model, built on a conditional variational auto-encoder (cVAE) framework, is designed to generate 3D human motions based on the desired action and specified interacting objects. It incorporates specialized modules for multi-modal feature extraction and fusion, along with auxiliary tasks to enhance object grounding and action-specific generation.

In summary, the authors are motivated by the fundamental lack of high-quality, semantically rich data for complex human-scene interactions and the corresponding need for advanced generative models that can understand and act upon natural language instructions in 3D environments. HUMANISE and the accompanying generative model are their proposed solutions to push the boundaries of embodied AI and enable more intelligent virtual characters and robots.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology of this paper consists of two main pillars: the creation of the HUMANISE dataset and the development of a novel generative model to leverage this dataset for the proposed task.

#### 3.1. The HUMANISE Dataset Creation

HUMANISE is a synthetic dataset created by aligning existing high-quality human motion sequences with scanned 3D indoor scenes and automatically annotating them with language descriptions.

**A. Data Sources:**
- **Human Motions:** AMASS [Mahmood et al., 2019] provides high-quality human motion capture data, and BABEL [Punnakkal et al., 2021] offers action labels for these motions.
- **3D Indoor Scenes:** ScanNet [Dai et al., 2017] provides richly annotated 3D reconstructions of indoor scenes.
- **Referential Descriptions:** Sr3D [Achlioptas et al., 2020] informs the structure for language descriptions, particularly for referring to specific objects.

**B. Motion Alignment Pipeline:**
The core idea is to automatically align captured human motion sequences with 3D scenes while ensuring physical plausibility.
1. **Action and Object Sampling:** For an action-specific motion (e.g., "sit"), a target interacting object (e.g., "armchair") and potential interaction points on its surface are sampled from the 3D scene based on predefined category labels.
2. **Transformation Parameter Sampling:** Valid global translation (T) and rotation (R) parameters are sampled for the human mesh using collision and contact constraints. Local poses from AMASS remain unchanged.
3. **Constraints for Plausibility:**
 - **Collision Constraint:** A 3D k-d tree representation of the scene allows for efficient collision checking. Any motion causing human vertices to penetrate scene objects or walls beyond a threshold is deemed invalid.
 - **Contact Constraint:** This is action-specific to ensure realistic interaction:
 - **Sit:** Hip-to-object proximity is enforced for the final pose on sittable objects.
 - **Stand-up:** Similar hip-to-object proximity is enforced for the starting pose.
 - **Walk:** Dense positions near the target object on the floor are sampled as potential endpoints. Feet-ground support and collision avoidance are enforced throughout the trajectory.
 - **Lie-down:** Contact with large flat surfaces (e.g., beds) is enforced for the final pose, ensuring the human body lies within the object's area.
 - **Diverse Affordance:** The pipeline aims to generate unique Human-Object Interactions (HOIs) not commonly found in daily activities or prior datasets (e.g., "stand up from the coffee table"), enriching the dataset's semantic variety.

**C. Language Description Generation:**
Language descriptions are automatically generated using a compositional template based on Sr3D: `<action><target-class>[<spatial-relation><anchor-class(es)>]`.
- **Action:** Taken from BABEL motion labels.
- **Target:** The interacting object.
- **Spatial Relation & Anchor:** Used to uniquely refer to a target object, defining relationships like proximity, support, or allocentric positioning relative to another object.

**D. Dataset Statistics:** HUMANISE contains 19.6k high-quality motion sequences in 643 indoor scenes, totaling 1.2M frames. It features four main actions: sit, stand up, lie down, and walk. It notably surpasses existing datasets in scene variety, clip/frame count, human pose quality, and uniquely offers rich semantic language descriptions.

#### 3.2. Language-Conditioned Generative Model

The proposed model is a conditional Variational Auto-Encoder (cVAE) designed to generate human motion sequences (represented by SMPL-X parameters: global translation `t`, global orientation `r`, and joint rotations `p`), conditioned on a 3D scene and a language description.

**A. Problem Definition and Notations:**
- **Scene (S):** RGB-colored point cloud (N points).
- **Language (L):** Tokenized word sequence.
- **Human Motion (Θ):** Sequence of SMPL-X parameters over time `Θ_1:T`. Body shape `β` is treated as an additional condition.

**B. Model Architecture (Illustrated in Fig. 3):**
1. **Condition Module:**
 - **Scene Encoder:** Point Transformer [Zhao et al., 2021] processes the 3D scene point cloud, producing point-level features. It is pre-trained on a semantic segmentation task on ScanNet.
 - **Language Encoder:** Pre-trained BERT [Kenton and Toutanova, 2019] extracts word-level embeddings from the language description.
 - **Feature Fusion:** Point-level and word-level features are concatenated and fed into a single-layer self-attention module. This allows cross-modal attention.
 - **Conditional Latent Embedding (`z_c`):** Updated features are processed by FC layers to produce `f_s` (scene feature) and `f_w` (language feature), which are concatenated and mapped to `z_c`.

2. **Motion Encoder:**
 - A bidirectional GRU processes the input motion sequence `Θ_1:T` to extract sequence-level features.
 - These features are concatenated with `z_c` and passed through an MLP to predict the parameters (`μ`, `Σ`) of a Gaussian distribution.
 - A latent vector `z` is sampled from this distribution using the reparameterization trick.

3. **Motion Decoder:**
 - A Transformer decoder generates the sequence of body parameters `Θ_1:T`.
 - It takes the concatenation of the sampled latent `z` and the conditional embedding `z_c` as input, queried by sinusoidal positional embeddings for sequence length `T`.
 - Outputs are mapped to SMPL-X body meshes using a differentiable function.

**C. Training Loss:**
The total training loss combines four components:
- **Reconstruction Loss (`L_rec`):** `L1` distance between predicted and ground-truth global translation (`t`), global orientation (`r`), and joint rotations (`p`). Also includes an `L_v` term for vertex positions.
- **KL Divergence Loss (`L_kl`):** Regularizes the learned latent distribution `ψ` to be close to a standard normal distribution `φ`, ensuring a structured latent space.
- **Auxiliary Losses (Crucial for `z_c`):** These tasks enhance the learned conditional embedding `z_c` by encouraging it to encode specific semantic information.
 - **Object Grounding Loss (`L_o`):** An FC layer on top of `z_c` regresses the target object's center (MSE loss). This helps `z_c` learn spatial grounding.
 - **Action Classification Loss (`L_a`):** Another FC layer followed by softmax maps `z_c` to action probabilities (cross-entropy loss). This helps `z_c` learn action comprehension.

**D. Implementation Details:**
The model is trained for 150 epochs using Adam. Specific hyperparameters for loss weights and latent dimensions are provided. Point Transformer is pre-trained on ScanNet for semantic segmentation, and BERT is pre-trained.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments benchmark the proposed task on HUMANISE, evaluating both reconstruction and generation capabilities, and comparing against ablative baselines and a downstream task.

**A. Quantitative and Qualitative Performance:**
- **Action-Specific Setting:** The model performs well for individual actions (e.g., sit, stand up, lie down, walk) in action-specific training. Qualitative results (Fig. 4) demonstrate visually appealing and semantically consistent human motions.
- **Goal Distance (Grounding):** The "goal dist." metric (shortest distance from human body to target object) is consistently low, especially for "lie down" actions (0.196m). The authors hypothesize this is because "lie down" interactions often involve large, flat objects like beds or tables, making grounding easier.
- **Diversity (APD):** Average Pairwise Distance (APD) indicates that the model generates diverse motions.
- **Perceptual Study:** Human evaluation shows reasonable "quality score" and "action score," indicating the motions are generally plausible and adhere to the language-specified actions.

**B. Impact of Auxiliary Tasks (Action-Agnostic Setting):**
- **Crucial for Grounding and Action Compliance:** Ablation studies in the action-agnostic setting (training on all actions) highlight the significant role of auxiliary losses:
 - Removing `L_o` (object grounding loss) leads to a higher `goal dist.` (1.383m vs. 1.008m for full model), confirming its role in locating the target 3D object.
 - Removing `L_a` (action classification loss) significantly degrades the `action score` (2.29 vs. 3.59 for full model), indicating its importance for generating motions with the correct action label.
 - Removing both auxiliary losses (`w/o aux. loss`) results in the worst performance across generation metrics (highest `goal dist.`, lowest quality/action scores), verifying their combined importance.
- **Qualitative Evidence:** Figure 5 visually demonstrates how the full model correctly grounds the interaction and performs the specified action, unlike models without auxiliary losses which struggle with action generation or object localization.

**C. Model Design Validations:**
- **Feature Fusion (Self-Attention):** The model with self-attention for cross-modal feature fusion (`Ours`) outperforms the direct concatenation baseline (`w/o self-att.`) in generation metrics for the 'walk' action, suggesting better conditional embedding learning.
- **Scene Encoder (Point Transformer):** The use of Point Transformer for scene encoding is validated, showing better grounding performance (lower `goal dist.`) compared to PointNet++ Encoder, likely due to its finer-grained scene understanding.

**D. Downstream Task Performance (Validation of HUMANISE Dataset):**
- **Improved Human Motion Synthesis:** The authors demonstrate the utility of HUMANISE by using it for pre-training in a traditional scene-conditioned human motion synthesis task (without language). A model pre-trained on HUMANISE and then fine-tuned on PROX significantly outperforms the same model trained solely on PROX across all reconstruction error metrics (translation, orientation, pose, MPJPE, MPVPE). This is a strong validation of HUMANISE's high quality and ability to alleviate the limitations of existing HSI datasets, indicating its potential to support broader applications in HSI research.

**E. Failure Cases:**
- **Object Grounding Errors:** The model sometimes struggles to correctly identify the target object, especially with complex referential descriptions, highlighting that 3D language grounding remains a challenging problem.
- **Physically Implausible HOI:** Incorrect Human-Object Interaction relations or collisions sometimes occur, suggesting a need for more explicit HOI modeling beyond point-level or scene-level features.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research makes several significant contributions that collectively advance the fields of computer vision, natural language processing, and embodied artificial intelligence.

**1. A Novel, Large-Scale, and Semantic-Rich Dataset (HUMANISE):**
- **Addressing Data Scarcity:** HUMANISE directly tackles the long-standing issue of limited scale, quality, and semantic annotations in existing Human-Scene Interaction (HSI) datasets. By aligning high-quality MoCap data with diverse 3D scenes and automatically generating rich language descriptions, HUMANISE provides an unprecedented resource for researchers.
- **Enabling New Research:** Its unique combination of precise 3D human motions, varied 3D scene contexts, and detailed language-based semantics unlocks new research avenues and allows for the training of more robust and intelligent models. The fact that it improves performance on a downstream HSI task (Table 3) further underscores its value and quality as a pre-training resource.

**2. Introduction of a New and Challenging Task:**
- **Language-Conditioned Human Motion Generation in 3D Scenes:** This is a crucial step towards truly intelligent virtual agents and robots. It demands multi-modal understanding, requiring models to simultaneously reason about geometric space, physical plausibility, and linguistic instructions. This task is inherently more complex and closer to real-world human-robot interaction scenarios than previous motion generation paradigms.
- **Driving Innovation:** By defining this complex task, the paper sets a new benchmark for future research, pushing the boundaries of what AI models can achieve in embodied perception and action.

**3. Development of an Effective Generative Model:**
- **Multi-modal Understanding:** The proposed cVAE-based generative model demonstrates a strong capability in jointly learning from 3D scene point clouds and natural language descriptions. The self-attention-based feature fusion module is a key component for effectively integrating these disparate modalities.
- **Improved Grounding and Action Fidelity:** The auxiliary tasks for object grounding and action classification are shown to be highly effective, significantly improving the model's ability to localize interactions and generate motions consistent with the specified actions. This highlights a powerful strategy for infusing semantic understanding into generative processes.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Embodied AI and Robotics:** The ability to generate complex, scene-aware human motions from language commands is foundational for developing more intelligent and intuitive virtual human agents, characters in games and simulations, and humanoid robots that can operate effectively in human environments based on high-level instructions.
- **Human-Computer Interaction (HCI):** This work could enable more natural and intuitive interfaces where users can instruct virtual entities using natural language, leading to more accessible and powerful applications.
- **Virtual Reality (VR) and Augmented Reality (AR):** The generation of realistic and interactive virtual characters can greatly enhance the immersion and utility of VR/AR experiences.
- **Digital Content Creation:** Animators and content creators could leverage such models to quickly prototype and generate complex character animations with realistic scene interactions, significantly streamlining workflows.

**Limitations and Future Directions:**
The authors are transparent about current limitations, which also point to promising future research:
- **Motion Duration:** The current motions are relatively short (action-specific). Future work needs to explore large-scale, long-duration HSI datasets for more complex, sustained activities.
- **Language Grounding Robustness:** Failures in object grounding reveal that this remains a critical, unsolved problem. More robust 3D language grounding modules are essential for achieving truly reliable language-conditioned generation.
- **Explicit HOI Modeling:** The current model's end-to-end nature might benefit from explicit 4D HOI (Human-Object Interaction) modeling and a deeper understanding of object affordances to prevent physically implausible interactions.

Overall, "HUMANISE" provides a crucial dataset and a robust baseline model that significantly advances the state-of-the-art in language-conditioned human motion generation within 3D scenes. It lays important groundwork for the development of highly intelligent, context-aware, and instruction-following AI systems.
