# Flow Policy Gradients for Robot Control

> [!summary] Summary
> This research presents FPO++, an enhanced flow matching policy gradient algorithm designed to improve training stability and performance for complex robot control. The framework enables robust learning from scratch, successful sim-to-real transfer for humanoids, and effective fine-tuning of manipulation policies by leveraging expressive generative models without requiring explicit action likelihood computations.

## Problem

- Traditional policy gradient methods are constrained to simpler action distributions due to their reliance on differentiable action likelihoods, limiting the representation of complex robot behaviors.
- Highly expressive generative policies (flow/diffusion models) could not be effectively integrated into online reinforcement learning due to the prohibitive computational cost and instability of calculating exact action likelihoods.
- Prior flow matching policy gradient implementations (vanilla FPO) proved unstable and prone to catastrophic failures when applied to challenging, high-dimensional robotics tasks.

## Method

- Introduces **FPO++**, an enhanced ==flow matching== ==policy gradient== algorithm that bypasses explicit likelihood computation by approximating policy gradient updates using differences in conditional flow matching (CFM) loss.
- Incorporates a 'per-sample ratio' mechanism, which computes and clips ratios for each individual Monte Carlo sample within an action, providing a finer-grained trust region for policy updates.
- Employs an 'asymmetric trust region' (ASPO) that combines PPO clipping for positive advantages with a Simple Policy Optimization (SPO) objective for negative advantages, stabilizing the variational gap and helping to preserve policy entropy.

## Results

- FPO++ dramatically improved training stability, consistently achieving higher and more stable episode returns across various quadruped and humanoid locomotion tasks compared to vanilla FPO.
- Successfully demonstrated the first reported sim-to-real transfer of flow policies trained purely with reinforcement learning for humanoid locomotion and motion tracking, exhibiting robust gaits on physical robots.
- Achieved higher success rates and faster convergence in fine-tuning pre-trained manipulation policies, showing greater robustness to initial policy quality than DPPO baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Effective policy gradient methods in continuous control do not necessarily require explicit computation of action likelihoods, broadening the design space for reinforcement learning algorithms.
> - Finer-grained control over ratio clipping (per-sample ratio) and an asymmetric trust region (ASPO) are critical for stabilizing flow policy gradient training in complex, high-dimensional robotics environments.
> - Expressive flow policies, when stabilized for online reinforcement learning, can learn more complex and correlated action distributions (e.g., trot gaits) that are challenging for simpler Gaussian policies.
