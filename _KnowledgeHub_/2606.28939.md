# ReGuide: From Test-Time Guidance to Self-Improving Diffusion Policies

> [!summary] Summary
> ReGuide, developed by researchers at Texas A&M University, introduces an iterative framework that transforms transient test-time guidance into a reusable source of on-policy recovery data for diffusion policies. This approach enables policies to self-improve by learning from their own guided corrective rollouts, leading to 1.2-7.7x higher success rates on various long-horizon manipulation tasks compared to base diffusion policies.

## Problem

- Behavior-cloned policies, especially expressive diffusion policies, are vulnerable to covariate shift, where small errors lead to out-of-distribution observations and compounding failures in long-horizon tasks.
- Existing data augmentation methods (e.g., DAgger) require continuous, expensive expert demonstrations or rely on strong assumptions for synthetic data generation.
- Prior test-time guidance methods discard successfully guided trajectories, preventing the policy itself from learning from these corrective experiences.

## Method

- **ReGuide** employs an iterative ==rollout-collect-train loop== where a policy generates guided rollouts, which are then used to update the policy, enabling self-improvement.
- ==Phase-Conditioned Guidance (PCG)== generates reliable corrective data by constructing phase-aware latent targets, applying guidance to estimated clean actions, and using a ==two-threshold gating== mechanism to activate guidance only in a 'drifted-but-recoverable' state.
- The policy is updated through fine-tuning (**ReGuide-FT**) or retraining from scratch (**ReGuide-FS**) on a dataset augmented with successful guided rollouts, making the corrective experiences reusable.

## Results

- All ReGuide variants consistently improved success rates, with ReGuide-FT achieving a **1.2-1.5x** success rate lift on Can, Square, and Transport, and a **7.7x** lift on Tool Hang after two iterations.
- The iterative self-improvement loop was demonstrated, with the second iteration of ReGuide-FT consistently outperforming the first across all **four** Robomimic tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the efficacy of the two-threshold guidance gate, clean-action guidance, and that improvements stemmed from the quality of guided recovery data rather than simply adding more data.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Test-time guidance can be effectively leveraged as a source of on-policy recovery data, enabling policies to learn from their own corrected mistakes without additional expert demonstrations.
> - Precise control over guidance application, through phase-aware targets and context-aware gating (two-threshold mechanism), is crucial for generating high-quality, actionable recovery data.
> - Differentiating guidance through estimated clean actions, rather than noisy intermediate steps, aligns guidance with the dynamics model's training distribution, improving its effectiveness.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research addresses a persistent challenge in imitation learning (IL): the vulnerability of behavior-cloned policies, particularly expressive diffusion policies, to covariate shift. Covariate shift occurs when a learned policy, upon making small errors, is queried on observations outside its training distribution, leading to compounding errors and task failure. This issue is particularly pronounced in long-horizon manipulation tasks where early deviations can render later subtasks unrecoverable.

The existing landscape for mitigating covariate shift in IL can be broadly categorized into two approaches:
- **Data Augmentation and Expansion**: Methods such as DAgger-style algorithms involve collecting expert corrections at states visited by the learner, thereby expanding the training distribution. Other approaches synthesize additional training examples using task structure, simulation, or local dynamics assumptions. While these methods aim to directly address the root cause of failures, they often necessitate continuous access to expert demonstrations, which can be expensive, or rely on specific assumptions about how perturbations preserve task correctness.
- **Test-Time Guidance**: More recent methods like DynaGuide, Latent Policy Barrier (LPB), PPGuide, and Generative Predictive Control (GPC) steer a frozen policy at inference time. These approaches typically use gradients from a learned dynamics model or other predictive signals to guide the policy back toward an "in-distribution" or "desirable" trajectory. A notable limitation of these methods is that the successfully guided trajectories are discarded after execution, meaning the policy itself does not learn from these corrective experiences.

ReGuide positions itself at the intersection of these two paradigms. It leverages the real-time corrective capabilities of test-time guidance but then integrates the resulting successful trajectories back into the policy's training data. This mechanism transforms guidance from a transient inference-time correction into a reusable source of on-policy recovery data for iterative self-improvement. By doing so, ReGuide aims to overcome the limitations of both data-augmentation (by reducing reliance on new expert data or strong assumptions) and test-time guidance (by enabling the policy to learn from its own corrected mistakes). The work builds upon the foundational Diffusion Policy for visuomotor control, extending its robustness through this self-improvement loop.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ReGuide is to enable behavior-cloned diffusion policies to self-improve iteratively by learning from their own guided corrective rollouts, thereby enhancing their robustness to covariate shift in long-horizon manipulation tasks without requiring additional expert demonstrations.

This overarching objective is driven by several motivations and necessitates addressing specific technical challenges:

1. **Addressing Covariate Shift in Diffusion Policies**: Imitation learning policies, including diffusion models, are trained on expert demonstrations. When deployed, small execution errors can push the robot into states not encountered in the training data, leading to a compounding of errors and task failure. The motivation is to mitigate this vulnerability, especially critical for complex, long-horizon tasks where early deviations can quickly lead to unrecoverable states.

2. **Leveraging Corrective Information**: The core motivation is the recognition that successful guided rollouts inherently contain valuable information. They reveal two key aspects: (a) where the learned policy tends to drift from the desired trajectory, and (b) which corrective actions effectively return the system to task completion. The objective is to harness this information by converting these trajectories into reusable training data.

3. **Overcoming Limitations of Existing Approaches**:
 - **Expert Data Cost**: DAgger-style methods require continuous expert supervision, which is expensive and often impractical in real-world robotics. ReGuide aims to remove this dependency.
 - **Data Augmentation Assumptions**: Synthetic data augmentation methods rely on assumptions about task structure, simulation fidelity, or local dynamics. ReGuide seeks a data generation mechanism that is less assumption-dependent, drawing corrective data directly from policy execution.
 - **Discarded Guided Trajectories**: Existing test-time guidance methods, while effective for immediate recovery, discard the corrected trajectories. ReGuide's motivation is to eliminate this inefficiency by treating these trajectories as a valuable source of training data.

4. **Enabling Reliable Guidance for Data Generation**: Translating the idea of using guided rollouts into practical training data introduces several technical requirements for the guidance mechanism itself:
 - **Phase-Specific Targets**: For long-horizon tasks, a single global target can be ambiguous or misleading, potentially pulling the policy towards an incorrect stage or collapsing valid behavioral modes. The objective is to construct phase-specific latent targets that respect the temporal structure and multimodality of demonstrations.
 - **Context-Aware Guidance Activation**: Guidance is only useful when the policy has drifted but the state is still recoverable. Applying guidance too close to the expert manifold can perturb correct actions, while applying it too far away can rely on unreliable dynamics model extrapolations. The motivation is to develop a gating mechanism that activates guidance only in the "drifted-but-recoverable" regime.
 - **Consistency with Dynamics Model Training**: Typical dynamics models are trained on "clean" action chunks, whereas standard diffusion guidance often differentiates through noisy denoising iterates. An objective is to align the guidance mechanism with the dynamics model's training distribution by applying gradients through estimated clean actions.

In summary, ReGuide's objectives center on creating an autonomous and effective self-improvement loop for diffusion policies, addressing the covariate shift problem by intelligently generating and integrating valuable corrective data from guided policy executions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ReGuide implements an iterative self-improving framework structured around a rollout–collect–train loop (Algorithm 1, Figure 1). It consists of three main components: Phase-Aware Target Construction, Phase-Conditioned Guidance (PCG) and Rollout Data Collection, and Iterative Self-Improvement Learning.

#### 3.1. Phase-Aware Target Construction

To provide effective guidance for long-horizon tasks with potentially multimodal behaviors, ReGuide develops a method for constructing localized, phase-conditioned target sets (Algorithm 3, Figure 2).
1. **Feature Augmentation**: For each state (o_t, a_t) in the demonstration data, visual (v_t) and proprioceptive (p_t) latent features are extracted using encoders shared with the diffusion policy. These are augmented with one-step temporal differences (∆v_t, ∆p_t) to create a combined feature vector [v_t, p_t, ∆v_t, ∆p_t]. Temporal differences help distinguish visually similar states belonging to different task phases.
2. **Clustering and Ordering**: The augmented features undergo dimensionality reduction via Principal Component Analysis (PCA). The reduced features are then clustered using K-Means (into `k` clusters). These clusters are ordered temporally based on their mean timestep within the demonstrations.
3. **Macro-Phase Definition**: The `k` ordered clusters are merged into `N_p` contiguous macro-phases, capturing broader stages of the task.
4. **Target Set Creation**: For each macro-phase `j`, `M` representative cluster centroids are selected to form the phase target set `P_j = {p_{j,1}, ..., p_{j,M}}`. This approach aims to capture multimodality within each phase.
5. **Distance Metric and Distribution**: A soft minimum function, `L(z, P_j) = -τ log (Σ_m exp(-|z-p_{j,m}|^2/τ))`, is defined to measure the distance of a predicted latent `z` to phase `j`. The empirical distribution `F_j` of these distances is stored for states belonging to each phase, used to calibrate guidance thresholds.

#### 3.2. Phase-Conditioned Guidance (PCG) and Rollout Data Collection

PCG is designed to generate reliable corrective rollouts by carefully controlling when and how guidance is applied (Algorithm 2).
1. **Clean-Action Guidance**: Unlike prior methods that differentiate with respect to the noisy denoising iterate (A_k_t), ReGuide computes guidance gradients with respect to the *estimated clean action* (`A_0_t`). This is achieved by first inferring `A_0_t` from `A_k_t` and the predicted noise `ϵ_θ` (Eq. 4). The guidance gradient `∇_A_0_t L(d_ϕ(z_t, A_0_t), P_j)` pushes this `A_0_t` towards the phase targets (Eq. 5). The guided `A_0_t` is then converted back into an adjusted noise prediction (`ϵ`) for the diffusion process (Eq. 6). This aligns the guidance with the dynamics model `d_ϕ`, which is trained on clean action chunks. The dynamics model (DINO-WM architecture) predicts future latent states `d_ϕ(z_t, A_t)` and shares its visual encoder `h` with the diffusion policy.
2. **Two-Threshold Gating**: Guidance is applied conditionally based on the phase-conditioned distance `ℓ = L(d_ϕ(z_t, A_0_t), P_j)`. From the empirical distance distribution `F_j`, lower (`ℓ_low`) and upper (`ℓ_high`) thresholds are defined (fixed percentiles). Guidance is activated only when `ℓ_low < ℓ < ℓ_high`.
 - The lower threshold prevents unnecessary intervention when the policy is already close to the expert manifold, avoiding perturbation of correct actions.
 - The upper threshold prevents reliance on dynamics gradients in extrapolation regions, where predictions might be unreliable due to significant deviation from the training data.
3. **Phase Progression**: During rollout, ReGuide tracks the current phase `j`. It advances to phase `j+1` only when the predicted latent is consistently closer to `P_{j+1}` than `P_j` over `K` consecutive steps, preventing premature switches from transient noise.
4. **Data Collection and Filtering**: After rollouts, only successful guided trajectories (determined by a trajectory-level success signal) are retained. These `{(O_t, A_g_t)}` pairs, where `A_g_t` are the guided actions, form the guided buffer `D_g_i` for the current iteration.

#### 3.3. Iterative Self-Improvement Learning

Successful guided rollouts are absorbed into the policy's training process through two complementary mechanisms:
1. **ReGuide-FT (Fine-Tuning)**: Fine-tunes the current policy checkpoint (`π_i`) on a rehearsal buffer comprising the existing dataset (`D_i`) and the newly collected guided rollouts (`D_g_i`). Minibatches are sampled from `D_i` and `D_g_i` with a fixed ratio `ρ`. This approach is computationally efficient and updates the policy incrementally.
2. **ReGuide-FS (From Scratch)**: Retrains a fresh diffusion policy (`π_i+1`) from scratch on the combined augmented dataset (`D_i ∪ D_g_i`). This is more computationally intensive but can lead to a different solution, particularly if the base policy (`π_i`) was a poor initialization.
3. **ReGuide-FS→FT (Composition)**: Combines the two, applying ReGuide-FT on a policy that was initially trained using ReGuide-FS. This leverages the stronger initialization from FS with the efficient refinement of FT.
The dynamics model (`d_ϕ`) is reused across iterations rather than being retrained, for computational efficiency. The iterative loop (`π_i` generates `D_g_i`, then `D_i+1` updates `π_i+1`) continues for a specified number of iterations.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

ReGuide was evaluated on four Robomimic manipulation tasks: Can, Square, Transport, and Tool Hang. To simulate a challenging, low-data regime, the base diffusion policies were trained on small subsets of demonstrations (15-80 demonstrations per task). The dynamics model was trained on rollouts from the corresponding base policy. Success rates were computed from 2,500 rollouts (50 initial seeds, 50 rollouts per seed) and reported as mean ± standard error.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate that ReGuide significantly improves the performance of base diffusion policies on various Robomimic tasks.

1. **Overall Performance Improvement**: All three ReGuide variants (ReGuide-FT, ReGuide-FS, ReGuide-FS→FT) consistently improved success rates over the base diffusion policy.
 - ReGuide-FT, fine-tuned from the base checkpoint, achieved a 1.2–1.5× success rate lift on Can, Square, and Transport in its first iteration, and a 7.7× lift on Tool Hang after two iterations.
 - ReGuide-FS, trained from scratch on augmented data, showed 1.3–1.5× improvements on Can, Square, and Transport, and a 5.3× lift on Tool Hang.
 - The composite method, ReGuide-FS→FT, achieved the highest success rates on Can (1.5×), Square (1.3×), and Transport (1.5×). On Tool Hang, the second iteration of ReGuide-FT slightly surpassed ReGuide-FS→FT (7.7× vs. 7.5×), attributed to the very weak initial base policy making iterative FT more effective than a single FS for refinement.

2. **Demonstration of Iterative Self-Improvement**: The second iteration of ReGuide-FT consistently outperformed the first iteration across all four tasks. For example, on Can, the success rate lift over the base policy increased from 1.2× to 1.3×, and on Tool Hang, it increased from 5.1× to 7.7×. This indicates that the updated policies can generate higher-quality recovery data, supporting the iterative rollout–collect–train loop. The paper notes diminishing returns in later iterations, potentially due to the fixed dynamics model becoming less aligned with the continually improving policy.

3. **Comparison to Latent Policy Barrier (LPB)**: In a test-time-only setting (without fine-tuning), ReGuide's Phase-Conditioned Guidance (PCG) component outperformed LPB on all evaluated tasks. While these test-time gains were modest, they indicate the effectiveness of PCG's design for generating cleaner data, which is its primary function within the full ReGuide framework.

4. **Ablation Studies Confirming Design Choices**:
 - **Number of Phase Targets (M)**: Experiments on Transport indicated a non-monotone relationship between the number of targets per phase and success rate. An intermediate number (e.g., M=50) yielded the best performance, supporting the need for sufficient targets to preserve multimodality without diluting the guidance signal.
 - **Two-Threshold Guidance Gate**: The full lower-and-upper threshold gating mechanism provided a significantly higher success rate improvement (+0.044) compared to a lower-only threshold (+0.014) or no guidance. This supports the hypothesis that guidance is most effective when applied in a "drifted-but-recoverable" regime, avoiding both unnecessary intervention and reliance on extrapolated dynamics gradients.
 - **Clean-Action Guidance**: Differentiating the guidance objective through the estimated clean action (`A_0_t`) outperformed differentiating through the noisy iterate (`A_k_t`) by approximately two standard errors on Transport. This result is consistent with the dynamics model being trained on clean action chunks, demonstrating the benefit of matching the guidance mechanism to the dynamics model's input distribution.
 - **Value of Guided Rollouts vs. Additional Data**: Ablations demonstrated that guided rollouts consistently outperformed an equivalent volume of unguided base-policy rollouts for both ReGuide-FT and ReGuide-FS variants. This finding indicates that ReGuide's improvements are attributable to the quality of the *guided recovery data*, rather than simply adding more data.
 - **ReGuide-FT vs. ReGuide-FS for Iteration**: When starting from an already strong ReGuide-FS checkpoint, further iterations of ReGuide-FT and ReGuide-FS achieved statistically comparable performance on Can. However, ReGuide-FT was found to be more computationally efficient for subsequent refinements, making it a practical choice for iterative improvement loops.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ReGuide presents a significant contribution to the field of robot learning, particularly in addressing the robustness and data efficiency challenges of imitation learning.

1. **Bridging Guidance and Self-Improvement**: The primary significance lies in its novel approach of transforming test-time guidance from a transient inference correction into a source of reusable, on-policy recovery data. This bridges a gap in existing methods, enabling policies to learn from their own corrected mistakes without requiring new expert demonstrations.
2. **Enhanced Robustness to Covariate Shift**: By explicitly training on states where the policy tends to drift and the corrective actions that lead to recovery, ReGuide directly improves the policy's robustness to covariate shift, a critical vulnerability in real-world robot deployment. The observed 1.3–7.7× improvements in success rates on various manipulation tasks demonstrate this enhanced robustness.
3. **Data Efficiency and Reduced Expert Reliance**: ReGuide reduces the reliance on costly, continuous expert data collection or complex, assumption-dependent synthetic data generation. It leverages a trajectory-level success signal, which is less demanding than per-state expert relabeling. This could lead to more scalable and autonomous robot learning systems.
4. **Refined Guidance Mechanisms**: The development of Phase-Conditioned Guidance (PCG), including phase-aware target construction, the two-threshold gating mechanism, and clean-action differentiation, represents a set of modular technical innovations that improve the reliability and effectiveness of test-time guidance itself. These components could be individually impactful for other guided diffusion applications.
5. **Iterative Learning Framework**: The iterative rollout–collect–train loop provides a practical framework for continuous policy improvement, allowing policies to progressively refine their behavior over time based on self-generated experience.
6. **Potential for Broader Impact**: The principles introduced by ReGuide could extend beyond diffusion policies and robotic manipulation, potentially influencing other domains where learned policies struggle with distribution shifts and where corrective feedback can be generated.

**Limitations and Future Work (as identified by the authors)**:
- The dynamics model is reused across iterations for efficiency; refreshing or uncertainty-calibrating it could improve later rounds.
- Rollout selection relies on trajectory-level success filtering and random sampling; more sophisticated diversity-aware or quality-aware selection could enhance data efficiency.
- Phase construction and gating thresholds are currently calibrated per task, suggesting future work on adaptive discovery and updates.
- Current evaluation uses fixed checkpoints and simulated tasks; future steps include broader training-seed studies and real-robot validation.

Despite these limitations, ReGuide offers a methodological advancement for training robust imitation learning policies that can learn from their own guided experiences, paving a path toward more capable and self-sufficient robotic agents.
