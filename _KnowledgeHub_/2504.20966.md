# Softpick: No Attention Sink, No Massive Activations with Rectified Softmax

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at MBZUAI developed Softpick, a rectified, non-sum-to-one normalization function for transformer attention, which eliminates attention sink and massive activations. This new function results in functionally sparse attention maps (over 95% sparsity) and substantially enhances quantization performance at lower bit precisions, outperforming softmax in 2-bit and 3-bit settings.

## Problem

- The standard softmax function in transformer attention leads to "attention sink," where irrelevant tokens (like BOS) receive disproportionately high attention scores.
- Softmax also contributes to "massive activations" in hidden states, presenting extreme outlier values that significantly hinder model quantization and low-precision training.
- Existing solutions for these issues often introduce complexity, additional parameters, or require custom optimizers, lacking a straightforward, universally applicable replacement for softmax.

## Method

- ==Softpick== is introduced as a ==rectified, non-sum-to-one attention normalization function==, defined by applying ReLU to (exp(x) - 1) in the numerator and normalizing by the sum of absolute values of (exp(x) - 1) in the denominator.
- The function's asymmetric design (ReLU in numerator, absolute value in denominator) allows for zero-valued attention scores and enables gradient flow for negative inputs, preventing 'dying heads'.
- Softpick is designed as a ==drop-in replacement for softmax==, maintaining compatibility with optimized kernels like FlashAttention-2, and requiring no extra parameters or specialized optimizers.

## Results

- Softpick successfully eliminated attention sink, achieving a **0%** sink rate across all tested models and thresholds, compared to significant rates (e.g., **68.28%**) for softmax models.
- Massive activations were entirely mitigated, with kurtosis in hidden states drastically reduced (e.g., from **33510** to **340** in 340M models) and activation magnitudes reduced by an order of magnitude.
- Attention maps generated by Softpick exhibited high functional sparsity (**99.34%** for 340M, **95.63%** for 1.8B models), allowing for direct opportunities for sparsity-based optimizations.
- Quantized Softpick models consistently outperformed softmax across various bit precisions and quantization techniques (HQQ, GPTQ, BNB), with particularly large gains at **2-bit** and **3-bit**, indicating enhanced robustness to quantization errors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The rectification (ReLU in numerator) allows for genuine sparsity in attention maps, producing many exact zero scores, while the absolute value in the denominator ensures stable gradient flow even for inputs that would otherwise result in zero attention.
> - Eliminating massive activations directly addresses a core challenge for efficient model quantization, enabling models to retain more accuracy at very low bit precisions (e.g., 2-bit).
> - The inherent sparsity in Softpick's attention mechanisms offers potential for significant computational savings through sparse matrix operations and improved model interpretability.
