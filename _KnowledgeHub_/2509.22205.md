# From Watch to Imagine: Steering Long-horizon Manipulation via Human Demonstration and Future Envisionment

> [!summary] Summary
> Super-Mimic is a hierarchical zero-shot manipulation framework: a Human Intent Translator parses unscripted human demonstration videos into language-grounded subtask plans, then a Future Dynamics Predictor generates imagined future videos per subtask, extracting 3D trajectories via depth and tracking to guide execution. It beats state-of-the-art zero-shot baselines by over 20% on long-horizon tasks.

## Problem

- Zero-shot foundation-model methods handle only simple, short-horizon tasks and struggle to decompose high-level commands into executable action sequences from static images alone.
- Text-only planners must infer complex spatial goals and multi-step procedures from abstract language, which is ambiguous and fails to convey fine-grained operational constraints.
- Prior static-observation methods (keypoint or pose regression) cannot capture dynamic object interactions and contact physics needed for robust subtask execution.

## Method

- **Human Intent Translator**: extracts keyframes from human video via ==wrist-stationarity detection==, then uses a VLM to build a transferable, language-grounded subtask plan, editable via text.
- **Future Dynamics Predictor**: conditions a ==video generation model== on current observation and subtask prompt to imagine a physically plausible future rollout for each subtask.
- ==Trajectory extraction pipeline==: Grounded SAM 2 tracking plus ==RDP simplification== and Video Depth Anything lift imagined video into a 3D waypoint trajectory for the Action Executor.

## Results

- Super-Mimic outperforms text-driven baselines ReKep and MOKA by over **20%**, reaching **50%** TSR on Meal Preparation versus MOKA's **20%** best case.
- On the language-ambiguous Irregular Traversal task, ReKep and MOKA achieve **0%** TSR even with detailed manual instructions, while Super-Mimic reaches **20%** TSR.
- Ablations show removing FDP drops Meal Preparation TSR from **50%** to **20%**, and removing HIT's keyframe parsing drops Irregular Traversal TSR from **20%** to **10%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Unscripted human video is a richer, less ambiguous high-level instruction signal than language for conveying spatial intent in long-horizon tasks.
> - Prospective video generation of task execution provides denser, more physically-grounded guidance for control than static keypoint or pose prediction from a single image.
> - Distilling raw demonstration into sparse, symbolic keyframes before planning matters: feeding raw video directly to a VLM degrades plan quality versus explicit key-action parsing.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotics is rapidly moving towards developing systems capable of generalized manipulation in unstructured, open-world environments. A central challenge in this pursuit is enabling robots to perform complex, "long-horizon" tasks without extensive, task-specific training data, often referred to as "zero-shot" learning.

Historically, robotic manipulation has relied on precise programming or extensive training datasets for specific tasks. The emergence of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs), pre-trained on vast internet-scale datasets, has offered a promising new pathway. These foundation models excel at perception, reasoning, and grounding language, leading to approaches where robots can interpret high-level textual commands.

However, existing zero-shot methods based on MLLMs often face significant limitations. They are typically constrained to simple, short-horizon tasks (e.g., picking up a single object) because they struggle to decompose complex high-level commands into actionable sequences from static visual input alone. Relying solely on textual descriptions for long-horizon tasks proves problematic due to language's abstract nature, the difficulty in exhaustively describing all constituent steps, and the unreliability of MLLMs in inferring complex action sequences from ambiguous visual scenes. Furthermore, many methods struggle to extract robust dynamic semantics from static observations, often regressing end-effector poses directly without fully capturing nuanced object interactions.

This paper positions "Super-Mimic" as a direct response to these limitations. It introduces a "Watch to Imagine" paradigm that leverages two richer modalities:
1. **Human Demonstration Videos:** Instead of relying on abstract language, Super-Mimic proposes using unscripted human demonstration videos as a direct and richer source of high-level instruction. These videos implicitly encode subtasks and manipulation skills, providing richer semantic cues than text. This builds on prior work that explores learning from human demonstrations but addresses the challenges of domain discrepancy and robustness across diverse environments.
2. **Future Envisionment via Video Generation:** To overcome the static nature of current visual understanding, Super-Mimic employs video generation models to "imagine" the future execution of subtasks. This provides dynamic, physically plausible video rollouts that explicitly model crucial object interactions and contact points, bridging the gap between high-level plans and low-level control. This is a novel application of general-purpose video generation models, differing from approaches that require specialized robot manipulation video generation models or trajectory translation modules.

By combining video-driven intent parsing with prospective dynamics modeling, Super-Mimic directly addresses the dual challenges of abstract high-level planning and lack of dynamic information for low-level control in open-world, long-horizon, zero-shot manipulation. It aims to achieve human-level proficiency by making robot learning more intuitive and robust, significantly advancing the state of the art in general-purpose robotic systems.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a hierarchical framework that enables **zero-shot generalization to complex, long-horizon robotic manipulation tasks** in unstructured, open-world environments. The authors aim to achieve this by overcoming the inherent limitations of current multimodal foundation model-based approaches, which typically struggle with both abstract high-level planning and physically-grounded low-level execution when relying solely on static visual input and textual instructions.

The key motivations driving this work are:

1. **Addressing the Abstraction Gap in Language-only Planning:** Current MLLM-based methods often fail to reliably decompose high-level textual commands into executable action sequences for long-horizon tasks. Language can be too abstract to convey fine-grained operational constraints, and manually scripting detailed instructions is laborious and often insufficient. The authors believe that **unscripted human demonstration videos** offer a more direct, intuitive, and information-rich source of instruction, implicitly encoding subtasks and manipulation skills.
2. **Bridging the Static-to-Dynamic Gap in Low-Level Control:** Prior zero-shot approaches struggle to extract robust dynamic semantics from static visual observations. Directly regressing end-effector poses from static images often fails to capture the nuances of object interactions, which are critical for robust physical execution in open-world scenarios. The motivation here is to provide **predictive foresight** by "imagining" future dynamics, thereby offering dense, physically-grounded spatio-temporal guidance for low-level control.
3. **Achieving Generalization and Flexibility:** The ultimate goal is to move beyond simple imitation to allow for **task modification** (e.g., changing destinations) and **skill transfer** (e.g., applying sorting skills to new objects) in a zero-shot manner, without requiring task-specific training data. This aims to build truly general-purpose robotic systems that can adapt to novel scenarios.

In essence, the paper is motivated by the vision of enabling robots to "Watch" human demonstrations to infer complex intent and then "Imagine" the physically plausible future steps to execute those intentions, thereby realizing robust and generalized long-horizon manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Super-Mimic is a hierarchical framework that operates on a "Watch to Imagine" paradigm, translating human demonstrations and text into high-level plans and using video generation for robust low-level execution. It comprises two main sequential modules: the Human Intent Translator (HIT) and the Future Dynamics Predictor (FDP), integrated with an Action Executor.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The problem is defined as achieving a complex goal (task T) from high-level instructions (human video V and optional language L) in a zero-shot, open-world setting. The system must produce a sequence of subtasks P and, for each subtask, generate a sub-plan (keyframe trajectory) k from the robot's current RGB-D observation O, providing spatio-temporal guidance for execution.

**B. System Overview**
The framework follows a planning-execution loop:
1. **Human Intent Translator (HIT):** Parses the human demonstration video and optional language command to produce a high-level, language-grounded subtask plan.
2. **Future Dynamics Predictor (FDP):** For each subtask, generates a physically plausible video rollout of its execution, from which a detailed 3D trajectory is extracted.
3. **Action Executor:** Grounds the imagined 3D trajectory into executable robot actions.
After a subtask is verified complete, the system updates its observation and repeats the loop for the next subtask. If verification fails, replanning is initiated.

**C. Human Intent Translator (HIT)**
The HIT module translates ambiguous human demonstrations into a structured, adaptable symbolic plan `P`.
- **Human Key-action Parsing:**
 - **Keyframe Extraction:** The raw human video `V` is processed to identify sparse, semantically critical keyframes `V_key`. This is done by tracking 2D wrist coordinates using a hand landmark detector (MediaPipe) and detecting significant interaction events based on wrist velocity (stationarity within a temporal window below a threshold). Temporally filtered keyframes are then selected.
 - **Demonstration Abstraction:** The `V_key` sequence is fed into a Vision-Language Model (VLM, specifically Qwen2.5-VL 72B) which interprets the visual sequence and abstracts the demonstrated actions into a structured, descriptive baseline plan `P_base`. This reduces computational load and focuses planning on task-relevant actions.
- **Unified Subtask Planning:**
 - This stage takes `P_base`, the robot's current RGB observation `O_rgb`, and an optional language command `L`.
 - If `L` is absent, the system simply mimics `P_base`, mapping actions and objects to the current scene.
 - If `L` is provided (e.g., "throw everything into the trash bin"), the VLM modifies `P_base` to satisfy the language-guided constraint. This allows for **task modification** (e.g., changing destinations) and **skill transfer** (adapting learned knowledge to new objects).
 - The output is the final plan `P = {τ_1, ..., τ_N}`, where each subtask `τ_n` is a tuple `(desc_n, obj_n, loc_n, guide_n, precond_n)` containing a natural language description, target object/destination identifiers, a textual prompt for FDP, and preconditions.

**D. Future Dynamics Predictor (FDP)**
The FDP module bridges the symbolic plan from HIT to physical execution by generating concrete, spatio-temporal guidance.
- **Future Video Generation:** For each subtask `τ_n`, its `guide_n` prompt and the robot's current `O_rgb_n` are fed into a video-generative model (Wan2.2-Lightning). This model forecasts the physical outcome by generating a short video sequence `V_future_n` of imagined future frames.
- **Trajectory Extraction:** The imagined video `V_future_n` is processed to extract a 3D keyframe trajectory `k_n`.
 - An object tracker (Grounded SAM 2) identifies and tracks the target object through `V_future_n`, yielding a dense 2D pixel trajectory.
 - The RDP algorithm refines this into a sparse set of 2D waypoints.
 - A video-based depth estimator (Video Depth Anything) generates temporally consistent depth maps. For each 2D waypoint, its depth is retrieved and projected to a 3D point `p_3D` using camera intrinsics.
 - These 3D points form the trajectory `k_n = {p_3D_1, ..., p_3D_M}`, providing spatio-temporal guidance.

**E. Action Executor**
The Action Executor translates `k_n` into safe, physical robot actions.
- **Grasp Planning and Trajectory Optimization:**
 - **Grasp Planning:** AnyGrasp proposes stable grasp candidates, and the MLLM selects the optimal one based on commonsense physical criteria.
 - **Trajectory Optimization:** The initial 3D waypoints `k_n` are refined to minimize a weighted sum of a smoothness cost (penalizing sharp turns) and a collision cost (penalizing proximity to obstacles queried from a KD-Tree). A gradient-based local solver is used for this.
- **Execution and Verification:** The robot executes the optimized path. A VLM then assesses the outcome against the subtask goal. If successful, the observation is updated, and the loop continues. If not, replanning is initiated via the HIT module.

The framework utilizes several off-the-shelf foundation models, allowing it to operate in a fully zero-shot manner without task-specific training data.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted aimed to validate Super-Mimic's effectiveness in complex zero-shot long-horizon tasks and to quantify the contributions of its "Watching" (HIT) and "Imagining" (FDP) modules.

**A. Experimental Setup**
- **Hardware:** A 7-DoF xArm7 robotic arm and an Orbbec Femto Bolt RGB-D camera.
- **Models Used:** Wan2.2-Lightning (video generation), MediaPipe (hand landmark detection), Grounded SAM 2 (object tracking), Video Depth Anything (video depth estimation), AnyGrasp (grasp planning), and Qwen2.5-VL 72B (VLM for all high-level planning and reasoning).
- **Metrics:** Task Success Rate (TSR, percentage of trials where all subtasks succeeded) and Subtask Success Rate (SSR, average percentage of successfully completed subtasks per trial) over N=20 trials.
- **Tasks:** Three long-horizon scenarios (5-8 subtasks each):
 - **Meal Preparation:** Sorting fruits and snack boxes.
 - **Tidy Up the Desk:** Sorting trash from useful items.
 - **Irregular Traversal:** Non-logical pick-and-place motions, challenging for language-based planners.
- **Baselines:** ReKep and MOKA, state-of-the-art text-driven methods, evaluated under "Short Simple Language" and "Long Detailed Language" conditions.

**B. Comparative Analysis**

1. **Text-Only Long-Horizon Planning Limitations:** Both ReKep and MOKA showed significantly higher performance when provided with "Long Detailed Language" (manually pre-decomposed subtasks) compared to a "Short Simple Language" command. For example, MOKA's TSR on "Tidy Up the Desk" increased from 10% to 20% with detailed instructions. This highlights the inherent difficulty of these baselines in autonomously decomposing complex tasks from abstract language.
2. **Superiority of Video Demonstrations:** Super-Mimic's video-guided approach ("Video" setting) significantly outperformed all baselines. On "Meal Preparation," Super-Mimic achieved a 50% TSR, more than double MOKA's best (20% with detailed instructions). This confirms that rich visual information from human videos allows the HIT module to ground task intent and spatial nuances with much higher fidelity than sparse language alone.
3. **Flexibility and Generalization:**
 - **Task Modification:** In the "Video + Constraints" setting, Super-Mimic maintained high success rates (e.g., 40% TSR on "Meal Preparation"), demonstrating its ability to flexibly refine demonstrated skills with new text commands.
 - **Skill Transfer:** The "Skill Generalization" setting yielded a 30% TSR on complex sorting tasks, validating Super-Mimic's capacity to abstract underlying task logic and apply it to novel scenes with entirely different objects.
4. **Robustness in Ambiguous Scenarios:** For the "Irregular Traversal" task, designed to be difficult to describe with language due to its non-logical nature, both ReKep and MOKA failed completely (0% TSR). In contrast, Super-Mimic achieved a 20% TSR by mimicking visual demonstrations and imagining trajectories, showcasing the unique robustness of its video-driven paradigm for tasks defying linguistic description. Even when given only "Short Simple Language," Super-Mimic achieved a 30% TSR on "Tidy Up the Desk," outperforming baselines even with detailed manual instructions.

**C. Ablation Studies**

1. **Contribution of the Future Dynamics Predictor (FDP) - "Imagining":**
 - **`w/o FDP` (static planner):** Replacing FDP with a static 2D waypoint planner (like MOKA) caused a drastic performance decline (e.g., TSR for "Meal Preparation" dropped from 50% to 20%). This confirms that static planning is insufficient for robust execution.
 - **`w/o Path Guidance` (only final destination):** Providing the planner with only the final destination from the imagined video (instead of the full trajectory) improved performance over the fully static planner but still underperformed the full pipeline. This indicates that the complete, dense trajectory provided by FDP, not just the endpoint, is crucial for robust execution.
2. **Contribution of the Human Intent Translator (HIT) - "Watching":**
 - **`w/o HIT` (text-only):** Measuring performance in a text-only setting (equivalent to "Short Simple Language") directly quantified the significant performance gain from using video demonstrations (e.g., TSR on "Tidy Up" jumped from 30% to 40%).
 - **`w/o HIT’s Parsing Step`:** Feeding the full, raw video directly to the VLM planner without HIT's key-action parsing resulted in performance degradation (e.g., TSR on "Irregular Traversal" dropped from 20% to 10%). This confirms that HIT's parsing is a critical step for distilling clear, actionable intent from noisy and redundant video signals.

**D. Failure Analysis**
The authors identified three primary failure modes:
1. **HIT Planning Failure:** The VLM misinterprets user intent, leading to incorrect subtask plans (e.g., placing an apple in a cup).
2. **FDP Prediction Failure:** The video generation model hallucinates physically implausible futures (e.g., deformed objects), resulting in unusable trajectories.
3. **Execution Failure:** Low-level physical interaction fails despite a correct plan, often due to perception inaccuracies or imprecise grasping (e.g., knocking over an object), which was identified as the most common failure.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Super-Mimic framework represents a significant advancement in the pursuit of general-purpose robotic manipulation, particularly for complex, long-horizon tasks in zero-shot, open-world settings. Its "Watch to Imagine" paradigm offers a compelling new direction for how robots can learn and adapt, moving beyond the limitations of purely language-driven or heavily pre-trained methods.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Novel Hierarchical Framework:** Super-Mimic introduces a unique two-stage hierarchical approach that effectively decouples high-level intent understanding from low-level dynamic execution. By using human demonstration videos for task planning (Watch) and video generation models for subtask execution guidance (Imagine), it addresses two fundamental challenges simultaneously.
2. **Enhanced Intent Parsing (HIT):** The Human Intent Translator demonstrates that human video demonstrations are a far richer and more intuitive source of information for complex task planning than abstract language. Its ability to parse unscripted human actions into transferable, language-guided robotic plans enables capabilities like task modification and skill transfer, which are crucial for adaptability. This makes robot learning more accessible and less dependent on explicit, laborious textual instructions.
3. **Robust Dynamics Prediction (FDP):** The Future Dynamics Predictor's use of generative video models to envision physically plausible future outcomes is a pivotal contribution. This module provides dense, dynamics-aware spatio-temporal guidance, effectively bridging the "symbol-to-physical" gap that often plagues methods relying on static scene understanding. This proactive envisioning of object interactions and contact points significantly enhances the robustness of low-level control, especially in multi-object manipulation scenarios.
4. **Zero-Shot Generalization:** A core strength of Super-Mimic is its zero-shot nature, operating without requiring task-specific training data. By leveraging off-the-shelf foundation models and general-purpose video generation, it significantly lowers the barrier to deployment in novel, unstructured environments, a critical step towards truly generalizable robots.
5. **Empirical Validation of a New Paradigm:** The extensive experiments rigorously demonstrate the superiority of Super-Mimic over state-of-the-art text-driven baselines, especially in challenging long-horizon and ambiguous tasks where language alone fails. The ablation studies clearly quantify the distinct advantages of both "Watching" and "Imagining," establishing this paradigm as a highly effective strategy.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Towards More Autonomous and Adaptable Robots:** Super-Mimic pushes the boundaries for robots that can understand human intent through observation and adapt to novel scenarios without reprogramming. This could lead to more versatile robots in manufacturing, logistics, healthcare, and domestic assistance.
- **Reduced Training Data Requirements:** The zero-shot capability promises to drastically reduce the need for extensive, costly data collection and domain-specific training, accelerating the development and deployment of new robotic applications.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** The work opens several promising avenues for future research, including improving the physical fidelity of video generation models, enhancing perception accuracy, and developing more flexible, non-linear replanning frameworks. These are fundamental challenges in embodied AI, and Super-Mimic provides a strong foundation and motivation for addressing them.
- **Intuitive Human-Robot Interaction:** By enabling robots to learn from human demonstrations, Super-Mimic promotes more intuitive and natural human-robot interaction, allowing users to teach robots new skills simply by showing them.

In conclusion, Super-Mimic marks a significant step towards developing intelligent robotic systems that can learn complex skills directly from human observation and intelligently envision their own actions, paving the way for more capable, adaptable, and general-purpose robots in our daily lives.
