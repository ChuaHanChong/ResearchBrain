# AnySafe: Adapting Latent Safety Filters at Runtime via Safety Constraint Parameterization in the Latent Space

> [!summary] Summary
> AnySafe enables latent safety filters to adapt to user-specified safety constraints at runtime by defining constraints through images within a world model's learned latent space. The system demonstrates adaptive behavior, successfully handling novel constraints and achieving high safety performance in both simulation and real-world robot tasks.

## Problem

- Existing safe control methods, including latent safety filters, typically rely on fixed, predefined safety constraints, which limits their adaptability in dynamic environments or with varying user requirements.
- Integrating flexible, user-defined safety specifications into complex, vision-based robotic tasks is challenging due to the need for extensive retraining or manual re-engineering of safety systems.
- The learned latent spaces of world models often do not naturally align with a human's semantic understanding of 'failure' or provide a structure for continuously parameterizing diverse safety constraints.

## Method

- AnySafe introduces a framework where latent safety filters are parameterized on image-based safety constraints within a world model's learned latent space.
- A 'failure projector' is used to align the world model's raw latent representations with a user's semantic understanding of 'similarity to failure', enabling effective constraint definition.
- Conformal prediction calibrates a statistical threshold for the failure set, allowing for principled, user-controlled conservativeness without requiring retraining for different safety levels.

## Results

- AnySafe demonstrates adaptive behavior in a Dubins' car simulation, constructing and enforcing diverse unsafe regions based on user-specified image constraints with performance comparable to specialized fixed-constraint filters.
- On a real-world Franka manipulator, the system successfully adapts to runtime-specified image constraints, intervening to prevent failures and maintaining safe distances from dynamically defined unsafe regions.
- The approach generalizes to novel, interpolated safety constraints not explicitly encountered during training, correctly identifying new unsafe sets where fixed-constraint baselines fail.
- Experiments confirm that varying the conformal calibration parameter (α) directly controls the robot's conservativeness in hardware, enabling users to adjust the margin from specified failure regions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning safety filters directly on user-provided constraint images in a learned latent space allows for flexible, runtime adaptation of safety boundaries.
> - A dedicated projection layer for 'failure-relevant' features is crucial to bridge the gap between abstract latent spaces and human-interpretable semantic notions of danger.
> - Conformal prediction provides a robust, distribution-free method for statistically calibrating safety thresholds, offering a tunable balance between safety and task performance.
