# AnySafe: Adapting Latent Safety Filters at Runtime via Safety Constraint Parameterization in the Latent Space

> [!summary] Summary
> AnySafe enables latent safety filters to adapt to user-specified safety constraints at runtime by defining constraints through images within a world model's learned latent space. The system demonstrates adaptive behavior, successfully handling novel constraints and achieving high safety performance in both simulation and real-world robot tasks.

## Problem

- Existing safe control methods, including latent safety filters, typically rely on fixed, predefined safety constraints, which limits their adaptability in dynamic environments or with varying user requirements.
- Integrating flexible, user-defined safety specifications into complex, vision-based robotic tasks is challenging due to the need for extensive retraining or manual re-engineering of safety systems.
- The learned latent spaces of world models often do not naturally align with a human's semantic understanding of 'failure' or provide a structure for continuously parameterizing diverse safety constraints.

## Method

- AnySafe introduces a framework where latent safety filters are parameterized on image-based safety constraints within a world model's learned latent space.
- A 'failure projector' is used to align the world model's raw latent representations with a user's semantic understanding of 'similarity to failure', enabling effective constraint definition.
- Conformal prediction calibrates a statistical threshold for the failure set, allowing for principled, user-controlled conservativeness without requiring retraining for different safety levels.

## Results

- AnySafe demonstrates adaptive behavior in a Dubins' car simulation, constructing and enforcing diverse unsafe regions based on user-specified image constraints with performance comparable to specialized fixed-constraint filters.
- On a real-world Franka manipulator, the system successfully adapts to runtime-specified image constraints, intervening to prevent failures and maintaining safe distances from dynamically defined unsafe regions.
- The approach generalizes to novel, interpolated safety constraints not explicitly encountered during training, correctly identifying new unsafe sets where fixed-constraint baselines fail.
- Experiments confirm that varying the conformal calibration parameter (α) directly controls the robot's conservativeness in hardware, enabling users to adjust the margin from specified failure regions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Conditioning safety filters directly on user-provided constraint images in a learned latent space allows for flexible, runtime adaptation of safety boundaries.
> - A dedicated projection layer for 'failure-relevant' features is crucial to bridge the gap between abstract latent spaces and human-interpretable semantic notions of danger.
> - Conformal prediction provides a robust, distribution-free method for statistically calibrating safety thresholds, offering a tunable balance between safety and task performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of several rapidly advancing fields: **robot control, world models, safe reinforcement learning (RL), and reachability analysis.**

**World Models and Latent Space Control:** Recent years have seen a surge in "world models" – neural networks that learn compact latent state representations and dynamics directly from high-dimensional sensor observations (e.g., images). These models offer a powerful paradigm for generalizing robot control to tasks that are difficult to simulate or model manually. They learn an internal "imagination" of the world, enabling planning and control in a lower-dimensional, abstract space.

**Foundational Safe Control Methods:** Concurrently, foundational control-theoretic methods, such as Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) reachability analysis and Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), provide rigorous guarantees for safety in autonomous systems. These methods compute "safety value functions" or "barrier functions" that delineate safe and unsafe regions in the state space, enabling controllers to proactively avoid undesirable states.

**Bridging the Gap: Latent Safety Filters:** Prior work, including some from the authors' group, has successfully applied these foundational safe control methods directly within the learned latent spaces of world models. These "latent safety filters" allow robots to anticipate and avoid future failures directly from high-dimensional sensor inputs, even for hard-to-model constraints like spilling deformable bags or toppling complex structures. This represents a significant step towards enabling provably safe control in complex, vision-based tasks without explicit hand-modeling of the environment.

**The Unaddressed Challenge: Adaptability:** The critical limitation of most existing safe control frameworks, including early latent safety filters, is the assumption that safety constraints are fixed and known *a priori* during deployment. In dynamic, real-world scenarios or with varying user requirements, this assumption is overly restrictive. A robot might need to adapt its notion of "unsafe" based on a new task or a user's instruction – for instance, avoiding one region in one scenario, then intentionally entering it while avoiding another region in a subsequent scenario.

**AnySafe's Contribution to the Landscape:** AnySafe directly addresses this gap. It aims to develop *adaptive* latent safety filters that can respond to *runtime, user-specified safety constraints*. It draws inspiration from "goal-conditioned reinforcement learning (GCRL)," which trains policies to achieve diverse goals by conditioning on goal representations. AnySafe applies a similar conditioning principle but to the *safety constraint* within the HJ reachability framework, using images to specify these constraints. While other works have explored observation-conditioned safety filters, AnySafe distinguishes itself by focusing on user-specified, arbitrary constraints *via images* within the *learned latent space* of a world model, providing a more flexible and generalizable solution. The authors argue that this adaptability, especially the ability to parameterize constraints continuously in latent space, is a key advancement beyond existing methods that rely on fixed constraints or a predefined, limited set of safety specifications.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central question driving this research is: **"How can latent safety filters adapt to safety constraints specified at test-time?"**

The paper articulates several key motivations and objectives to answer this question:

- **Addressing the Fixed Constraint Limitation:** The primary motivation stems from the inherent inflexibility of current safe control methods. While successful in ensuring safety for known constraints, their inability to adapt to changing environmental conditions or dynamic user preferences significantly hampers their applicability in real-world, human-centric robotic deployments. The authors explicitly state that assuming fixed constraints is "overly restrictive."
- **Enabling Runtime Adaptability:** The core objective is to design latent safety filters that can be adapted *at runtime* based on *user-specified* safety constraints. This means the robot should be able to understand and incorporate new safety rules on the fly, without needing extensive retraining or manual re-engineering of the safety system.
- **Leveraging World Models for Constraint Parameterization:** The authors aim to build upon the strengths of world models – their ability to learn rich, high-dimensional representations from raw sensor data. The challenge here is that latent spaces, unlike hand-designed state spaces, don't naturally offer structures for continuous parameterization of diverse safety constraints or dense measures of proximity to these constraints. This leads to the need for novel methods to define and manage these parameterized constraints within the latent space.
- **Semantic Alignment of Safety:** A crucial objective is to ensure that the robot's internal understanding of a "safety constraint" aligns with the end-user's semantic notion of failure. This is not trivial in a learned latent space where features might not directly correspond to human-interpretable concepts.
- **Maintaining Performance and Generalization:** The proposed solution must not sacrifice the safety performance or efficiency achieved by specialized, fixed-constraint filters. Furthermore, it should ideally generalize to constraints beyond those explicitly encountered during initial training, allowing for novel or interpolated safety specifications.
- **Controlling Conservativeness:** An additional objective is to provide a principled mechanism for users to control the level of conservativeness with which the robot avoids specified constraints. This allows for fine-tuning the balance between safety and task performance.

In essence, the paper aims to develop a robust, flexible, and user-friendly framework that allows robots to learn and dynamically adapt their safety specifications in complex, vision-based tasks, moving beyond static, predefined safety boundaries.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

AnySafe introduces a novel framework for constraint-parameterized latent safety filters, built upon three key ingredients: a latent similarity measure, conformal calibration, and a specialized training paradigm within the world model's imagination.

**A. Latent States, Dynamics, and World Model Foundation:**
AnySafe is founded on the concept of latent safety filters, which operate within the learned latent state representation (`z ∈ Z`) of a world model. A world model, pretrained on offline robot-environment interaction data, infers a low-dimensional latent state `z` from high-dimensional observations (`o`) and predicts its dynamics (`f_z`). Crucially, a **Failure Projector** `~E: Z → ~Z` is introduced. This is a 2-layer MLP trained atop the world model's latent space to map `z` into a "failure-relevant" latent representation `~z`. This projection is vital because raw world model embeddings are often not sufficiently aligned with an end-user's understanding of "similarity to failure." The failure projector is trained with a mean-square error (MSE) loss to align `sim(~z_i1, ~z_i2)` with ground-truth similarity scores based on robot positions (in simulation) or object pixel coordinates (in hardware).

**B. Defining Safety Constraints via Latent Similarity:**
Instead of a binary classifier for a fixed constraint, AnySafe proposes a dense **latent similarity measure** `~ℓ_z(z; z_c)` where `z_c` is an embedding of a constraint image provided by the user. Specifically, `~ℓ_z(z; z_c):= -sim(~z, ~z_c)`, where `sim` is cosine similarity between the projected latent states `~z` and `~z_c`. This provides a continuous signal of how close the robot is to a potential failure state represented by `z_c`. The safety constraint is then defined by a `δ`-sub-level set: `F_δ_zc:= {z: ~ℓ_z(z; z_c) <= δ}`.

**C. Calibrating the Latent Failure Set with Conformal Prediction:**
The choice of the threshold `δ` is critical as it defines the effective size and conservativeness of the failure set. AnySafe employs **Conformal Prediction (CP)** to calibrate `δ` in a principled, distribution-free statistical manner.
1. **Calibration Dataset:** A held-out calibration dataset `D_calib` is used, containing pairs of latent states with ground-truth labels (`y_j ∈ {0, 1}`) indicating semantic similarity (i.e., whether the two states are "similar" in terms of representing a failure).
2. **Recall Guarantee:** Class-conditioned CP is applied to guarantee the recall of failures at a user-specified confidence level `α ∈`. This ensures that `P(~ℓ_z(z_test; z'_test) <= δ | y_test = 1) >= 1 - α`.
3. **Threshold Determination:** `δ` is chosen as the `(1 - α)`-quantile of the nonconformity scores `{-sim(~z_j, ~z'_j)}` from the positive pairs in `D_calib`. This allows for explicit control over how conservatively the robot approaches the constraint. A smaller `α` (higher confidence) will generally lead to a larger `δ`, thus a larger effective failure set and more conservative behavior.

**D. Training the Constraint-Parameterized Latent Safety Filter:**
The safety filter is trained entirely within the "imagination" of the world model using a reinforcement learning-based solver (DDPG). The key innovation here is the *parameterization* of the safety value function `V_π(z; z_i)` and policy `π_π(z; z_i)` on a constraint representation `z_i`.
1. **Constraint Sampling:** During training, *any* observation from the world model's training dataset is treated as a potential test-time safety constraint. These observations are randomly sampled and encoded into `z_i` to condition the safety filter. This exposes the filter to a diverse range of possible constraints, enabling generalization.
2. **Parameterized Bellman Equation:** The training involves solving a constraint-parameterized fixed-point safety Bellman equation:
 `V_π(z_t; z_i) = (1 - γ) ~ℓ_z(~z, ~z_i) + γ min(~ℓ_z(~z, ~z_i), max_a_t E[V_π(z'_t+1; z_i)])`
 The safety value function `V_π(z_t; z_i)` measures how close the robot (starting from `~z_t`) will come to the failure representation `~z_i` in `~Z`, even with its best safety-preserving policy `π_π(z_t; z_i)`.
3. **Policy Derivation:** The safety-preserving policy `π_π(z; z_i)` is derived by maximizing this value function (e.g., `argmax_a E[Q(z_t, a_t; z_i)]`).

**E. Runtime Constraint-Parameterized Safety Filtering:**
At deployment, a user provides an image `o_c` representing the desired safety constraint. This image is encoded into `z_c = E(o_c)`. The robot executes a base task policy `π_task`. AnySafe then acts as a safety monitor:
1. It evaluates `V_π(f_z(z, π_task); z_c)`, predicting the safety value of the next state if `π_task` were executed.
2. If this predicted value is below the calibrated threshold `δ` (i.e., `V_π(next_state; z_c) <= δ`), indicating an inevitable entry into the unsafe set, the safety filter intervenes and overrides `π_task` with its safety-preserving policy `π_π(z; z_c)`.
3. Otherwise, if `V_π(next_state; z_c) > δ`, `π_task` is allowed to proceed.
Crucially, a theorem in the appendix demonstrates that applying the calibrated threshold `δ` directly to `V_π` (learned from the raw similarity measure) is equivalent to solving a threshold-dependent value function, thereby justifying post-hoc calibration without retraining for different `δ` values.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The authors present compelling evidence from both simulation and real-world hardware experiments to support AnySafe's effectiveness.

**A. Simulation Results (Dubins' Car Collision Avoidance):**
- **Adaptive Behavior:** AnySafe successfully adapts to diverse, user-specified safety constraints (e.g., different regions the car must avoid) by conditioning on different constraint images. It accurately constructs constraint-parameterized unsafe sets in the latent space, visualized across different heading slices (Fig. 3).
- **Comparable Performance to Specialized Filters:** Quantitatively, AnySafe achieves safety monitor performance (e.g., high recall and precision for unsafe states, high balanced accuracy, and safe rates during rollouts) comparable to "Latent-Fix" baselines, which are specialized safety filters trained *only* for a single, fixed constraint (Table I). This demonstrates that adaptability does not come at the cost of performance for a given constraint.
- **Necessity of the Failure Projector:** The "AnySafe (w.o. Proj)" variant (without the failure projector) performs significantly worse, with much lower accuracy and safe rates. This confirms the hypothesis that the raw latent space of a world model alone is not sufficiently aligned for detecting "failure-relevant" similarities, necessitating the `~E` projector.
- **Optimal Parameterization Strategy:** Ablation studies show that conditioning the safety filter directly on the original latent states (`Z × Z`) for both the current state and the constraint representation yields the best performance (Table II). This suggests the world model's latent space, even if requiring projection for similarity, retains enough information for the value function to generalize effectively.
- **Controlling Failure Set Size via Calibration:** The conformal calibration mechanism (using different `ϵ` thresholds to define "similarity" in calibration data) effectively controls the size of the failure and unsafe sets. A larger `ϵ` leads to a larger `δ` and thus a larger, more conservative unsafe set. This demonstrates a user's ability to adjust the robot's conservativeness while maintaining high accuracy and safety (Fig. 4, Table III).

**B. Hardware Results (Vision-Based Sweeping with Franka Manipulator):**
- **Real-world Adaptability:** On a Franka manipulator tasked with sweeping objects, AnySafe successfully adapts to runtime-specified safety constraints provided by user images. The safety filter intervenes, for example, by rotating the broom or stopping the teleoperator, only when objects approach the dynamically defined failure region (Fig. 5).
- **Quantitative Hardware Safety:** Compared to "LatentSafe" (a baseline using multiple fixed safety filters), AnySafe achieves higher success rates and consistently maintains a safe distance from the runtime failure region, keeping distances above the calibrated threshold (Fig. 6). This highlights AnySafe's superior adaptability to arbitrary constraints.
- **Generalization to Novel Constraints:** A single AnySafe model demonstrates generalization capabilities beyond the discrete constraints seen by specialized fixed filters. When presented with a constraint image representing a region *between* two distinct training regions (`o_2.5`), AnySafe correctly identifies the new unsafe set, whereas none of the fixed LatentSafe filters detect it (Fig. 7). This is a strong indicator of its ability to interpolate and generalize. Again, the raw DINOv2 features are insufficient without the failure projector.
- **Adaptive Conservativeness in Hardware:** Experiments confirm that varying the `α` parameter in conformal calibration allows for adaptive control over the level of conservativeness (Fig. 8). Smaller `α` values (higher confidence requirements for safety) result in larger margins from the failure region and more proactive filtering by the robot.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

**A. Significance:**
AnySafe represents a significant advancement in the field of safe robot control, particularly for complex, vision-based tasks. Its primary contribution is overcoming the critical limitation of fixed safety constraints, thereby enabling robots to operate more flexibly and robustly in dynamic, human-centric environments. The key innovations, including the learned latent similarity measure, principled conformal calibration, and constraint-parameterized training within world models, collectively provide a novel and effective framework. The work successfully bridges advanced deep learning (world models, representation learning) with rigorous control theory (Hamilton-Jacobi reachability), moving towards truly adaptive and provably safe autonomous systems. The demonstration on real hardware further validates its practical relevance and robustness.

**B. Potential Impact:**
The implications of AnySafe are substantial:
- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy and Adaptability:** Robots equipped with AnySafe can adapt their safety protocols on the fly, making them suitable for a broader range of tasks and environments where safety specifications might change dynamically (e.g., manufacturing lines, logistics, domestic assistance, disaster response). This reduces the need for extensive manual re-programming or retraining for every new scenario.
- **Improved Human-Robot Interaction and Trust:** Users can intuitively define complex safety constraints simply by providing an image, rather than requiring expertise in control theory or robot programming. This lowers the barrier to entry for customizing robot behavior and fosters greater trust, as users can directly specify what constitutes a "failure" for them.
- **Broader Applicability of Safe Control:** The ability to handle arbitrary, image-defined constraints in latent spaces expands the reach of formal safety guarantees to high-dimensional, vision-based tasks that were previously intractable with traditional, hand-modeled approaches. This can accelerate the deployment of safe learning-based robots in diverse real-world applications.
- **Foundational Research Directions:** AnySafe opens new avenues for research, particularly in areas like data-efficient metric learning (reducing reliance on dense labels for similarity measures) and developing techniques for generalizing to truly *unseen* or out-of-distribution safety constraints. It also highlights the importance of aligning learned representations with human semantic understanding.
- **Tunable Safety-Performance Trade-off:** The conformal calibration aspect offers a principled way for system designers or end-users to explicitly tune the level of conservativeness, striking an appropriate balance between safety and task performance based on application requirements.

**C. Limitations (as acknowledged by the authors):**
The authors judiciously identify two key limitations of the current work:
1. **Generalization to Unseen Constraints:** AnySafe currently assumes that runtime constraint images come from a distribution similar to those encountered during world model training. Generalizing to truly novel or out-of-distribution constraints remains an open challenge.
2. **Reliance on Dense Labels:** The training of the failure projector (for aligning latent space similarities with semantic failures) relies on dense labels. Future work could focus on more data-efficient metric learning techniques or self-supervised methods to reduce this dependency and improve the alignment of similarity with a user's notion of failure.

Despite these limitations, AnySafe marks a significant step towards developing flexible, user-adaptive, and robustly safe robotic systems.
