# Visual Sketchpad: Sketching as a Visual Chain of Thought for Multimodal Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> VisualSKETCHPAD equips multimodal language models with the ability to generate and utilize intermediate visual sketches as a dynamic reasoning aid. The University of Washington and Allen Institute for AI framework significantly improves performance across various math and computer vision benchmarks, setting new state-of-the-art results by enabling LMMs to create and observe their own visual 'thoughts'.

## Problem

- Current multimodal language models (LMMs) struggle with tasks requiring deep visuo-spatial understanding or symbolic grounding where intermediate visual aids are crucial, unlike humans who use sketching for problem-solving.
- Existing Chain-of-Thought (CoT) and tool-use paradigms for LMMs primarily rely on text-based intermediate steps, overlooking the powerful role of visual representation in many reasoning processes.
- Prior tool-use models often follow predefined plans and lack the flexibility for LMs to dynamically adjust their approach based on intermediate visual feedback, limiting their adaptiveness.

## Method

- The ==VisualSKETCHPAD== framework employs an iterative ==Thought-Action-Observation loop== where an LMM agent plans a visual action, generates Python code to execute it, and observes the resulting visual sketch.
- Visual sketches are generated dynamically through code generation, utilizing `matplotlib` for mathematical plotting and specialized, off-the-shelf vision models (e.g., ==Grounding-DINO==, ==SegmentAnything==) wrapped as Python functions.
- The framework provides utility functions for image manipulation like zooming and overlaying images, allowing the LMM to interactively process and refine visual information as part of its reasoning.

## Results

- SKETCHPAD improved GPT-4o's average accuracy on math tasks by **11.2%** (GPT-4 Turbo by **23.4%**), with significant gains on Graph Algorithms (e.g., **41.3%** on Max Flow) and Mathematical Functions (over **88%** accuracy).
- The framework achieved new state-of-the-art results on all seven evaluated computer vision tasks from V*Bench, MMVP, and BLINK, with GPT-4o showing average absolute gains of **14.3%** on V*Bench and **9.0%** on BLINK tasks.
- Human evaluations indicated high alignment, with LMs generating geometry plans similar to human-created ones **80%** of the time and **92.8%** of GPT-4o's vision plans being rated as valid by human subjects.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual sketches serve as an effective 'visual chain of thought', allowing multimodal language models to externalize and iterate on their reasoning in a visually interpretable manner.
> - Enabling dynamic, feedback-driven generation and observation of visual artifacts (sketches) significantly enhances LMMs' ability to solve complex visuo-spatial and mathematical problems.
> - Integrating off-the-shelf specialist vision models and plotting libraries as 'sketching tools' via code generation allows LMMs to dynamically leverage powerful perceptual capabilities within their reasoning loop.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of large language models (LLMs) has rapidly expanded, with multimodal variants (LMMs) now capable of processing both text and images. This paper addresses a critical limitation in current LMMs: their inability to leverage visual "sketching" as an intermediate step for reasoning, a capability fundamental to human problem-solving.

This work builds upon and extends several existing paradigms in AI:

- **Chain-of-Thought (CoT) Reasoning:** Introduced for LLMs, CoT enables models to decompose complex problems into intermediate, text-based reasoning steps. SKETCHPAD extends this by proposing a "visual chain of thought," where intermediate steps are not just textual but also visual artifacts (sketches). This addresses the inherently visual nature of many reasoning problems (e.g., geometry, spatial understanding) that text-only CoT cannot adequately capture.

- **Tool-Use Paradigms:** Recent work has augmented LLMs with "tools" – external APIs or models – that they can call to perform specific tasks (e.g., calculation, web search, or vision tasks). Frameworks like Visprog, ViperGPT, and VPD enable LMs to generate Python code to invoke specialized vision tools. SKETCHPAD aligns with this by using Python code generation to orchestrate visual tools. However, a key differentiation is that prior tool-use models often follow a predefined plan. SKETCHPAD, by contrast, allows the LM to *change its plan based on the intermediate visual artifacts generated*, fostering a more adaptive and robust reasoning process, similar to how a human might adjust their approach after drawing a diagram.

- **Visual Prompting:** Methods like Set-of-Mark (SoM) demonstrate that adding labeled segmentation masks to images can enhance GPT-4V's grounding ability. SKETCHPAD generalizes this concept by allowing the LM to *decide* what visual prompting to use and *when* to use it as part of an iterative reasoning process, rather than relying on static, pre-applied visual prompts. It integrates specialist vision models (like object detection and segmentation) not just for perception, but as dynamic sketching tools that augment the visual input for subsequent reasoning.

- **LLMs as Agents:** The broader trend of viewing LLMs as agents that can reason and act also provides context. While many LM agents primarily generate text during reasoning, SKETCHPAD equips the agent with the ability to *create and manipulate visual artifacts*, making it a truly multimodal agent that can plan and reason using both textual and visual information.

In essence, SKETCHPAD fills a crucial gap: while LMMs can *perceive* visual input and LLMs can *reason* textually, few frameworks effectively integrate *visual modification and observation* as a dynamic part of the reasoning process. By enabling LMs to "draw" and "see" their own sketches, SKETCHPAD offers a more human-like, interactive, and interpretable approach to complex multimodal problem-solving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of VisualSKETCHPAD is to **enhance the reasoning capabilities of multimodal language models by equipping them with the ability to generate and utilize intermediate visual sketches.**

The motivation for this capability stems from observing human problem-solving:
- **Human Analogy:** Humans instinctively use sketching to facilitate reasoning – drawing auxiliary lines in geometry, marking points on maps, or sketching diagrams to clarify complex ideas. This highlights the inherent visuo-spatial nature of many reasoning tasks that goes beyond pure linguistic description. Sketches directly convey spatial and abstract relationships that are cumbersome to express in text, amplifying ideas and offloading cognitive burden from working memory.
- **Limitations of Current LMMs:** Despite advancements, current LMMs often struggle with tasks requiring deep visuo-spatial understanding or symbolic grounding where intermediate visual aids would be beneficial. Benchmarks like Geometry3K, IsoBench, BLINK, and V*Bench expose these shortcomings, as they involve questions where humans would naturally draw or annotate diagrams. Existing CoT and tool-use paradigms primarily rely on text as intermediate steps, overlooking the powerful role of visual representation in reasoning.
- **Specialist Vision Models as Potential "Sketches":** The authors observe that many specialist computer vision models (e.g., object detection, segmentation, depth estimation) essentially "sketch" on natural images (e.g., drawing bounding boxes, masks, colormaps). The motivation is to integrate these powerful, pre-existing visual perception capabilities directly into the LMM's reasoning loop, allowing the model to decide when and how to apply them.
- **Improved Interpretability and Flexibility:** By externalizing the reasoning process through visual sketches, SKETCHPAD aims to make the LMM's decision-making more interpretable. Furthermore, allowing the LM to dynamically generate and observe these sketches provides greater flexibility and robustness compared to predefined tool-use pipelines, as the model can react to and correct errors in its visual "thoughts."

In summary, SKETCHPAD seeks to bridge the gap between powerful multimodal perception and robust complex reasoning by enabling LMs to engage in a dynamic, iterative process of visual ideation and analysis, mirroring human cognitive strategies.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VisualSKETCHPAD is a general framework that enables multimodal LMs to perform visual "chain-of-thought" reasoning without requiring finetuning or additional training of the base LMM. It operates on an iterative Thought-Action-Observation loop, where the LM dynamically plans, executes visual actions, and observes the resulting sketches to refine its reasoning.

#### 3.1. Framework Overview

The core of SKETCHPAD involves a multi-turn interaction between the LMM agent and an environment:
1. **Thought:** Given a multimodal query and the current context (previous thoughts, actions, and observations), the LMM generates a textual "thought" plan for its next action. This plan outlines what visual artifact it intends to create or what visual analysis it needs to perform.
2. **Action:** Based on the thought plan, the LMM synthesizes and executes a programmatic "action." This action is typically Python code that leverages various visual tools (e.g., plotting libraries, specialist vision models) to generate a visual sketch or manipulate an image.
3. **Observation:** The environment returns a new "observation," which is the visual output produced by the executed action (e.g., a modified diagram, a depth map, an image with bounding boxes). This new visual information, along with any textual output, updates the multimodal context for the next "Thought" step.

This iterative process continues until the LMM determines it has enough information to answer the query, at which point it generates a "Terminate" action and provides the final answer. Crucially, SKETCHPAD differs from prior work by allowing the LMM to manipulate *both* visual and textual content and to dynamically adjust its reasoning based on the intermediate visual feedback.

#### 3.2. Sketching via Code Generation

The mechanism for generating visual sketches is **code generation**. The LMM is prompted with a detailed description of available Python tools, including their function signatures and docstrings. The LMM then generates Python code blocks that call these tools. A special `display` function allows the LMM to visualize the generated sketch, which then becomes part of the "Observation" for the next turn. This approach leverages the LMM's strong code generation capabilities to orchestrate complex visual operations.

#### 3.3. Tools for Math Tasks

For mathematical reasoning tasks, SKETCHPAD integrates common Python plotting and graph manipulation libraries:
- **Geometry Problems:** The LMM receives geometry diagrams and their corresponding `matplotlib` code. It can then modify this code to draw auxiliary lines, define new points or angles, and visualize the updated diagram. This allows for visual reasoning based on geometric properties.
- **Mathematical Functions:** For tasks like classifying parity or identifying convexity, the LMM can plot the function using `matplotlib`. This visual representation aids in inspecting the function's properties, complementing analytical reasoning (which LMs are noted to struggle with).
- **Graph Algorithms:** Given an adjacency matrix, the LMM can use `networkx` to visualize the graph structure. This visual representation allows the LMM to directly reason about graph connectivity, maximum flow, or isomorphism.
- **Game Strategies (Chess):** For tasks involving chess, the LMM can use Python's `chess` library to draw the chessboard state from a Forsyth-Edwards Notation (FEN) string. This visual board enables the LM to analyze positions and formulate strategies.

#### 3.4. Tools for Computer Vision Tasks

For complex visual reasoning, SKETCHPAD leverages specialized, off-the-shelf vision models, wrapped as Python functions:
- **Detection:** Uses Grounding-DINO for open-vocabulary object detection. The LMM can prompt it to detect specific objects, and the tool returns an annotated image with bounding boxes and labels.
- **Segmentation:** Employs SegmentAnything and Semantic-SAM to generate colorful segmentation masks and number labels on objects. This helps the LMM reason about object relationships and visual grounding.
- **Depth Estimation:** Utilizes DepthAnything to produce a depth map (with a colormap) of the input image. This is crucial for spatial reasoning tasks (e.g., determining relative distances).
- **Visual Search via Sliding Window:** A custom module that mimics human visual search for small items. It runs a sliding window over the image and uses the detection model on cropped patches, returning relevant annotated patches. This is useful when objects are too small for direct detection.
- **Image Manipulation:** Includes utility functions like `zoom_in_image_by_bbox` (to crop and enlarge specific regions) and `overlay_images` (to combine images with transparency, e.g., overlaying a depth map on the original image), further enhancing the LMM's ability to manipulate and observe visual information.

By combining an iterative reasoning loop with dynamic code generation and a diverse set of visual tools, SKETCHPAD provides LMMs with a powerful "visual scratchpad" to simulate human-like visual reasoning.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SKETCHPAD significantly improves the performance of strong multimodal LMs (GPT-4 Turbo, GPT-4o) across a wide range of math and computer vision tasks, setting new state-of-the-art results.

#### 4.1. Performance on Math Tasks

Experiments were conducted on four categories of mathematical tasks from IsoBench and Geometry3K: geometry, mathematical functions (parity, convexity), graph algorithms (connectivity, max flow, isomorphism), and game strategies (chess winner identification).

- **Consistent Gains:** SKETCHPAD consistently improved the performance of baseline GPT-4o and GPT-4 Turbo models. GPT-4o with SKETCHPAD achieved an average gain of 11.2%, while GPT-4 Turbo saw an even larger average gain of 23.4%.
- **Notable Improvements:**
 - **Graph Algorithms:** Significant gains were observed, particularly for Maximum Flow (GPT-4o improved by 41.3% to 66.3%) and Connectivity (GPT-4o improved by 2.3% to 98.4%).
 - **Mathematical Functions:** SKETCHPAD helped GPT-4 Turbo achieve over 90% accuracy and GPT-4o over 88% accuracy on convexity and parity classification tasks, demonstrating its utility even for purely language-based inputs that are then visualized.
 - **Geometry:** GPT-4o improved by 4.2% to 66.7%.
 - **Game Strategies (Chess):** Modest but consistent gains (3-10%) were seen, indicating that visualizing the board aids strategic reasoning.

These results highlight that the ability to generate and observe visual representations of abstract mathematical concepts or game states empowers LMMs to reason more effectively.

#### 4.2. Performance on Computer Vision Tasks

The framework was evaluated on complex visual reasoning tasks from V*Bench, MMVP, and BLINK (relative depth, spatial reasoning, jigsaw, visual correspondence, semantic correspondence).

- **New State-of-the-Art:** GPT-4o with SKETCHPAD set new state-of-the-art results on all seven evaluated vision tasks.
- **Substantial Gains:**
 - **V*Bench:** SKETCHPAD yielded an 18.5% improvement for GPT-4 Turbo and a 14.3% improvement for GPT-4o, reaching 80.3%. This is particularly impressive as V*Bench focuses on small items, and SKETCHPAD's visual search tools proved highly effective.
 - **BLINK Tasks:** GPT-4o saw an average absolute accuracy gain of 9.0% across BLINK tasks (e.g., 12.1% on relative depth, 9.1% on spatial reasoning, 9.7% on semantic correspondence).
 - **Multi-Image Tasks:** Despite operating on single images, SKETCHPAD also showed significant improvements on multi-image tasks like jigsaw puzzles, visual correspondence, and semantic correspondence (e.g., GPT-4o improved visual correspondence by 7.5% to 80.8%).
- **Model Synergy:** The stronger base model (GPT-4o) benefited more from SKETCHPAD, suggesting that better inherent multimodal understanding allows for more effective utilization of the generated visual information.

#### 4.3. Analysis of Vision Specialist Usage

An analysis of how often each vision specialist tool was used revealed:
- **Task-Dependent Usage:** The LMs adapted their tool usage to the specific task. For V*Bench (small object search), detection, sliding window search, and zoom-in were predominantly used. For relative depth, depth estimation was primary. For spatial reasoning, detection and segmentation were common.
- **GPT-4o's Higher Engagement:** GPT-4o generally utilized vision specialists more frequently than GPT-4 Turbo, indicating a better ability to integrate and leverage these tools in its reasoning process. This difference in tool engagement might explain GPT-4o's higher overall performance.

#### 4.4. Comparison with Other Frameworks

SKETCHPAD was compared against visual prompting (SoM) and LLM tool-use frameworks (Visprog) on single-image tasks.
- **Consistent Outperformance:** SKETCHPAD was the only framework that consistently yielded improvements across all tasks.
- **Limitations of Baselines:** SoM, while boosting spatial reasoning (as originally reported), sometimes harmed performance on other tasks. Visprog often performed worse than the base LMs, potentially due to error propagation from specialist vision modules. This underscores SKETCHPAD's robustness, likely due to its iterative, feedback-driven approach allowing the LM to adapt to potential tool errors.

#### 4.5. Human Alignment & Open-Source Models

- **Human-like Plans:** A human study showed that LMs using SKETCHPAD generated plans remarkably similar to human-created plans. In geometry, LMs drew the same auxiliary lines as humans 80% of the time. For vision tasks, human subjects rated 92.8% of GPT-4o's plans as valid, with most "errors" stemming from specialist tool failures rather than the LM's planning. This alignment suggests that SKETCHPAD taps into effective, human-intuitive reasoning patterns.
- **Benefits for Open-Source Models:** Experiments with LLaVA-NEXT models showed that even open-source LMMs can benefit from visual artifacts generated by SKETCHPAD. Using "oracle Sketchpad" (visual artifacts from GPT-4o's last action), LLaVA-NEXT models showed consistent improvements on math tasks, demonstrating the generalizability of the visual reasoning approach.

### 5. Significance, Potential Impact, and Limitations

#### 5.1. Significance and Potential Impact

VisualSKETCHPAD represents a significant step forward in developing more capable and interpretable multimodal AI systems.
- **Enhanced Reasoning Capabilities:** By integrating visual sketching into the reasoning process, SKETCHPAD enables LMs to tackle complex problems that require deep visuo-spatial understanding, previously out of reach for even powerful LMMs. The substantial performance gains across diverse domains (math and vision) validate its effectiveness.
- **Human-like Intelligence:** The framework mimics a fundamental aspect of human cognition – using visual aids for thought and problem-solving. This moves LMs closer to a more generalized form of intelligence that can leverage complementary modalities in an integrated manner.
- **Improved Interpretability:** The visual sketches serve as an externalized "chain of thought," making the LM's reasoning process more transparent and interpretable. Researchers and users can visually follow the model's steps, understanding *how* it arrived at an answer, rather than just receiving a final textual output.
- **Flexible Tool Integration:** SKETCHPAD provides a robust and flexible way to combine the perceptual strengths of specialized vision models with the reasoning capabilities of LMMs. The dynamic, feedback-driven interaction allows the LM to adapt its plan based on tool outputs, overcoming limitations of rigid, pre-defined tool chains.
- **New Research Avenues:** The work opens doors for future research into training LMMs that natively generate visual outputs or are instruction-tuned specifically for this visual chain-of-thought paradigm. Its potential applications extend to fields like robotics (e.g., visual search in cluttered environments, depth-assisted navigation) and design, where visual interaction is paramount.

#### 5.2. Limitations

The paper acknowledges several limitations:
- **Computational Resources:** SKETCHPAD requires more computing resources compared to models that solely output language tokens. Each "Action" involving external tools (especially vision models) can be computationally intensive and adds latency. This could be a barrier for real-time applications or large-scale deployment.
- **Reliance on Off-the-Shelf LMs:** The current work focuses on using existing, off-the-shelf LMMs. Future research could explore training models specifically designed to integrate sketching, potentially leading to more efficient or deeply integrated visual reasoning.
- **Error Propagation from Specialists:** While the iterative nature of SKETCHPAD helps mitigate issues, the accuracy of the overall system is still dependent on the reliability of the underlying specialist vision models. Errors in detection, segmentation, or depth estimation can propagate and lead to incorrect reasoning steps.

Despite these limitations, VisualSKETCHPAD demonstrates a novel and highly effective approach to augmenting multimodal language models, pushing the boundaries of what these models can achieve in complex reasoning tasks.
