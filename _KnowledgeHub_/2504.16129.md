# MARFT: Multi-Agent Reinforcement Fine-Tuning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called Multi-Agent Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (MARFT) is introduced to optimize Large Language Model-based Multi-Agent Systems (LaMAS) using reinforcement learning techniques. It leverages a Flexible Markov Game formulation and achieves performance improvements on agentic tasks like mathematical problem-solving and coding, with MARFT-A increasing episodic return by approximately 18.45% on the MATH dataset and 14.75% in coding benchmarks.

## Problem

- LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems (LaMAS) lack principled methods for optimization and fine-tuning using foundational reinforcement learning (RL) techniques.
- Conventional Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) methods are not directly applicable to LaMAS due to their unique characteristics, such as asynchronous interactions, agent heterogeneity, and dynamic organizational structures.
- Existing LaMAS optimization approaches are primarily tuning-free or rely on ad-hoc parameter fine-tuning without strong theoretical grounding in RL.

## Method

- Introduces ==Multi-Agent Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (MARFT)== as a novel paradigm that reframes LaMAS optimization as a sequential decision-making process for effective reinforcement fine-tuning.
- Proposes the ==Flexible Markov Game (Flex-MG)== formulation, which includes a dynamic dependency function to model asynchronous and dependency-driven interactions inherent in LaMAS workflows.
- Develops two main instantiations, ==MARFT-A (action-level optimization)== and ==MARFT-T (token-level optimization)==, utilizing LoRA fine-tuning and a modified PPO objective with agent-by-agent updates.

## Results

- MARFT-A on a Duo system improved Episodic Return by approximately **8** percentage points (**~18.45%**) on mathematical problem-solving tasks (MATH) and achieved stable score increases of **~14.75%** in coding tasks.
- MARFT-A-tuned LaMAS demonstrated improved generalization, effectively enhancing performance in both in-domain and out-of-domain settings for mathematical problems.
- Multi-agent systems, particularly the Duo setup, significantly outperformed solo agents on complex tasks (e.g., **7.5** p.p. better on MATH500), while MARFT improved even initially sub-optimal multi-agent configurations.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - The Flexible Markov Game (Flex-MG) provides a suitable theoretical foundation for LaMAS by explicitly modeling dynamic inter-agent dependencies and asynchronous execution.
> - Optimizing agent policies at the action-level (MARFT-A) generally yields more stable and faster performance improvements compared to token-level optimization (MARFT-T) for end-task objectives.
> - Integrating explicit agent profiles into the joint observation space and using PPO's clipping mechanism helps preserve base LLM language capabilities while enhancing task-specific skills.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The work presented in this paper integrates several rapidly evolving fields within artificial intelligence: Large Language Models (LLMs), Reinforcement Learning (RL), Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT), and Multi-Agent Systems (MAS), particularly Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL).

LLMs have demonstrated capabilities in complex agentic tasks, requiring reasoning and interaction with dynamic environments. The deployment of LLMs as autonomous agents capable of decision-making and interaction has led to the emergence of LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems (LaMAS). These systems leverage the natural language understanding and generation capabilities of LLMs, often enhanced by retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and external tools, to address sophisticated problems, exemplified by frameworks such as Microsoft AutoGen and OpenAI Agents SDK.

Concurrently, RL has been recognized for its effectiveness in enhancing agent intelligence by learning optimal behaviors through trial-and-error. Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT) is a specialized application of RL that refines pretrained LLMs using high-quality interaction trajectories. This process aims to enhance reasoning and decision-making skills while preserving the LLM's original language capabilities, with algorithms like GRPO, DAPO, and VAPO demonstrating performance improvements in single-agent settings.

However, the direct application of conventional single-agent RFT to multi-agent LLM contexts remains underexplored. Traditional Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) methodologies, which extend RL to scenarios with multiple interacting agents, face significant challenges when applied to LaMAS due to unique characteristics such as asynchronous interactions, agent heterogeneity, and dynamic organizational structures. Existing MARL frameworks, like MAPPO, often assume agent homogeneity and synchronous actions, which do not align with the complexities of LaMAS. Some prior work, such as MAPoRL, has applied multi-agent PPO to LLMs, but without fully addressing the unique characteristics of LaMAS. Other parameter fine-tuning methods for LaMAS, like CORY and SiriuS, often lack a theoretical foundation in RL.

"MARFT: Multi-Agent Reinforcement Fine-Tuning" aims to bridge this gap by proposing a principled paradigm for optimizing LaMAS using foundational RL techniques, specifically adapted for the unique properties of LLM-based agents. It seeks to formalize and implement an LLM-based MARL framework that can unleash the collective intelligence of LaMAS in complex agentic tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives and motivations for this research are:

- **Addressing Challenges of Applying MARL to LaMAS:** The paper identifies that direct application of conventional MARL methodologies to LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems (LaMAS) introduces significant challenges. These challenges stem from the unique characteristics of LaMAS, including asynchronous agent interactions, the need for profile-aware agent design, heterogeneous architectural configurations, dynamic system organization, and large optimization spaces. Conventional MARL often assumes synchronous actions, homogeneous agents, and static environments, which do not reflect the realities of LaMAS.
- **Introducing a Novel Optimization Paradigm:** The central objective is to propose and formalize Multi-Agent Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (MARFT) as a novel paradigm for LLM-based MARL. This paradigm aims to enhance the intelligence of LaMAS by fine-tuning their policies through reinforcement learning, specifically tailored to their distinct operational characteristics.
- **Developing a Flexible Markov Game (Flex-MG) Formulation:** To better align with real-world LaMAS optimization, the paper aims to introduce a new Markov Game formulation, termed Flex-MG. This model is designed to explicitly capture the dynamic and dependent interactions among agents, which are crucial for complex agentic problem-solving.
- **Designing a Universal Algorithmic Framework:** The research seeks to develop a robust and scalable algorithmic framework for MARFT. This framework is intended to outline the conceptual foundations, highlight key distinctions from classical MARL, and provide practical implementation strategies, emphasizing modularity and adaptability for various LaMAS configurations.
- **Enabling Efficient and Stable LaMAS Optimization:** A core motivation is to allow for efficient and stable fine-tuning of LaMAS, even with limited high-quality interaction trajectories. This involves adapting RFT techniques, such as trust-region optimization and token-level adaptations, to the multi-agent context, while ensuring that the original language capabilities of the pretrained LLMs are preserved.
- **Promoting Collaborative Intelligence in LaMAS:** The work is motivated by the potential of LaMAS to solve complex, agentic tasks requiring multifaceted reasoning and collaboration, which individual LLMs may not achieve. MARFT aims to optimize both individual agent policies and collective system performance to foster effective coordination and adaptability in LaMAS.
- **Providing an Open-Source Implementation:** The authors aim to provide a complete, open-source implementation of the proposed framework to facilitate its adoption by the research community and encourage further advancements in MARFT.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The MARFT methodology integrates concepts from Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (RFT), LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems (LaMAS), and Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL), adapting them to address the complexities of LaMAS optimization.

#### 3.1. Flexible Markov Game (Flex-MG) Formulation

The paper introduces Flex-MG, denoted as $\langle V, N, S, A, T, R, \gamma, D \rangle$, to model LaMAS dynamics. Key components include:
- $V$: Vocabulary of the language model.
- $N = \{1, \ldots, n\}$: Set of agents with an organizational structure.
- $S$: State space.
- $A = \prod_{i=1}^{n} A_i$: Joint action space.
- $T: S \times A \to S'$: Transition function.
- $R: S \times A \to \mathbb{R}$: Reward function.
- $\gamma$: Discount factor.
- $D: A \times A \to \{0, 1\}$: A dynamic dependency function where $D(a_i, a_j) = 1$ indicates that agent $j$'s action is conditionally dependent on agent $i$'s action. This function can vary across timesteps, reflecting evolving coordination structures and problem decomposition, potentially governed by an orchestration agent.

#### 3.2. Categorizations of LaMAS in MARFT

LaMAS configurations are categorized along three dimensions:
- **Parameter sharing:** Agents can either share an identical base model (with full or LoRA fine-tuning on a single adapter) or use different base models/role-specific adapters. LoRA fine-tuning is highlighted for its efficiency and ability to mitigate parameter drift in shared models.
- **Execution synchronicity:** This describes how agents execute actions during inference.
 - *Synchronous:* Agents act concurrently, assuming independence of execution.
 - *Asynchronous:* Agents act independently, with execution triggered by task dependencies (common in real-world LaMAS).
 - *Hybrid:* Combines both synchronous and asynchronous elements.
- **Update simultaneity:** This refers to the training update scheme.
 - *Synchronous Update:* All agents are updated concurrently, which can introduce non-stationarity.
 - *Sequential (Agent-by-Agent) Update:* Agents are updated one after another, using the most current policies of others to ensure monotonic improvement and reduce non-stationarity.

#### 3.3. Differences and Resulting Challenges

The paper identifies several distinctions between traditional MARL and MARFT, which necessitate a specialized approach:
- **Execution Asynchronicity:** Unlike synchronous MARL, LaMAS actions are often executed asynchronously, with dependencies among agents (e.g., one agent's action relying on another's output).
- **Utilities:** LLMs are general-purpose language models, and RFT must enhance task-specific capabilities without degrading original language abilities. Value-based RL might lead to incomprehensible high-reward actions.
- **Characteristic Profiles:** LaMAS agents require explicit profiles (system prompts) to define roles and capabilities for orthogonal sub-tasks, augmenting the joint observation space.
- **Heterogeneity:** LaMAS can involve diverse LLMs with varying model structures, parameters, input/output formats, vocabularies, and access to different external tools, making training more complex.
- **System Organization:** LaMAS often operates in dynamic environments with flexible task decomposition and sequential dependencies, contrasting with static environments of traditional MARL.
- **Optimization Space:** The action space for LLMs (sequences of tokens) is vast (O($|V|^L$)), making value function convergence challenging. Token-level actions reduce complexity to O($|V| \times L$) but introduce reward sparsity.

#### 3.4. MARFT Instantiations

MARFT reframes the problem as a sequential decision-making process, leveraging the multi-agent advantage decomposition theorem.
- **Action-level MARFT:** Agents generate actions auto-regressively, where the "encoder" constructs local rollout states (using profiles), and the "decoder" combines these with predecessors' actions to generate the current agent's action. The optimization objective (Equation 6) is a PPO-like clipped surrogate objective, where each agent optimizes within a trust region conditioned on predecessor actions. This approach ensures monotonic improvement similar to HAPPO.
 - **Action Normalization:** To address variable action lengths in LLMs, joint action probabilities are normalized by token or word length.
 - **Agent-by-agent Update:** This sequential update mechanism, inspired by A2PO, updates one policy per training epoch, cycling through agents to promote stable convergence and monotonic improvement.
 - **Trajectory-level Optimization:** Adapting methods like GRPO and DAPO, MARFT can integrate trajectory-level optimization where rewards are aggregated at the end of a sequence of actions.

- **Token-level MARFT:** This variant treats each token generation as an individual action.
 - **Token-level Bellman Backups:** Introduces token-level value functions ($V^\pi$ and $Q^\pi$) and corresponding Bellman backups. While this drastically reduces optimization complexity from O($|V|^L$) to O($|V| \times L$), it creates extreme reward sparsity (reward only at the final token of the multi-agent system).
 - **Optimality Discrepancy:** The paper demonstrates that token-level optimization with an intra-action discount factor ($\gamma_w < 1$) can lead to conditionally inconsistent optimality compared to action-level optimization. Consistent optimality is achieved only when $\gamma_w = 1$, effectively removing the intra-action discounting bias (Appendix A).

#### 3.5. Implementation Details (Algorithm 3)

The base algorithm for MARFT alternates between an inference phase (collecting trajectories) and a training phase.
- **Policy and Critic Networks:** The agent policies ($\pi_\theta$) are fine-tuned using LoRA adapters on a frozen transformer base model. A centralized critic network ($V_\phi$) uses a frozen transformer module with a trainable MLP head to estimate values.
- **Trajectory Collection:** During rollouts, agents generate actions sequentially based on the current state and predecessor actions.
- **Optimization:**
 - **Value Function:** The critic network $V_\phi$ is updated by minimizing the squared Bellman error, with target values ($\hat{V}$) computed from accumulated rewards and Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE).
 - **Policy Function:** Agent policies are updated by maximizing the clipped PPO objective (Equation 6), utilizing estimated advantage values ($\hat{A}$ from GAE).
- **Token-level Adaptation in Implementation:** For token-level MARFT, all token logits and values are stored during rollouts, and token-level Bellman backups determine target values and TD errors, with GAE used for token-level advantage estimation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on mathematical (DeepScaler) and coding (DeepCoder) tasks, with evaluations on out-of-domain benchmarks, yielded several findings:

#### 4.1. MARFT Outperforms Independent PPO (MAPoRL)

- **Superior Performance:** Across various evaluation benchmarks (DeepCoder, LiveCodeBench, CodeForces for coding; AIME2024, MATH500, Minerva Math, DeepScaler, OlympiadBench for math), MARFT consistently achieved higher scores compared to Independent PPO (IPPO, also referred to as MAPoRL).
- **Scaling Agent Number:** The performance advantage of MARFT over IPPO became more pronounced as the number of agents increased. For Quad (4-agent) configurations, MARFT outperformed IPPO on all benchmarks by a relatively larger margin.
- **Credit Assignment:** This superiority is attributed to MARFT's centralized critic and its credit assignment mechanism across agents, which guides agents to take actions beneficial for the entire team's reward, unlike IPPO where agents optimize independently.

#### 4.2. MARFT Provides Monotonous Improvement

- **Convergence Speed and Final Reward:** Training dynamics, as observed in reward curves for DeepScaler and DeepCoder, showed that MARFT consistently converged faster and achieved higher final rewards than IPPO across Duo, Trio, and Quad configurations. This indicates more stable and effective collaborative optimization.
- **Stability:** IPPO exhibited instances of performance drops (e.g., a "Duo crash" around 550 training steps), whereas MARFT-tuned LaMAS demonstrated steady improvement throughout training.
- **Theoretical Alignment:** These observed dynamics are consistent with the theoretical monotonic-improvement guarantee offered by MARFT's approach, which leverages multi-agent advantage decomposition and trust-region learning.
- **Diminishing Returns with Homogeneous Scaling:** An observation was that simply increasing the number of agents (from Duo to Trio to Quad) did not lead to substantial performance improvements, suggesting diminishing returns. This is attributed to agents sharing identical base models and operating as a homogeneous ensemble, leading to redundant rather than complementary information. However, MARFT consistently improved the absolute capabilities of LaMAS at every scale, even when diversity was bottlenecked, by fundamentally enhancing how existing information channels are utilized through token-level optimization.

#### 4.3. Self-Evolution: Ablations on Role Specification

- **Anonymous vs. Pre-configured Roles:** An ablation study investigated the impact of explicit role descriptions (system prompts) versus anonymous agents. Surprisingly, anonymous settings consistently outperformed pre-configured ones across all comparison experiments in both parameter-sharing and non-parameter-sharing MARFT configurations.
- **Harmful Effect of Early Specification:** This phenomenon suggests that for general base models, intentionally specifying agent roles can hinder optimization during the early and middle stages of training. Agents may need to first adapt to their assigned roles, incurring an initial performance cost. This implies that allowing LaMAS to self-evolve its division of roles can be more effective.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The Multi-Agent Reinforcement Fine-Tuning (MARFT) paradigm presents several significant advancements and potential impacts:

- **Enhanced Capabilities for Complex Agentic Tasks:** MARFT empowers LLM-based Multi-Agent Systems (LaMAS) to tackle complex agentic tasks requiring autonomous decision-making, collaboration, and adaptive reasoning more effectively than standalone LLMs. By efficiently decomposing tasks and optimizing both individual and collective agent behaviors, MARFT can significantly improve performance in domains like logistics, collaborative robotics, and scientific research.
- **Scalability and Robust Generalization:** The framework offers a scalable approach to optimizing LaMAS, allowing for dynamic task decomposition and adaptation to varying agent populations and environmental complexities. This adaptability ensures stable learning performance and coordination capabilities, even when system organization changes, by reframing problems as sequential decision-making processes.
- **Improved Privacy and Federated Learning Alignment:** MARFT's design, where agents contribute intelligence through actions without necessarily sharing local data, aligns with privacy-preserving principles. This makes it suitable for scenarios where entities share agents but not sensitive information, drawing parallels with federated learning paradigms.
- **Integration with Blockchain Technology:** The decentralized nature and privacy-preserving aspects of MARFT make it potentially compatible with blockchain ecosystems. It could enable secure and efficient multi-agent collaboration in smart contract execution, decentralized autonomous organizations (DAOs), and decentralized finance (DeFi) platforms, enhancing transparency, security, and efficiency in these environments.
- **Roadmap for Future Research:** MARFT serves as a foundational roadmap for researchers. By bridging theoretical underpinnings with practical methodologies, it defines a new area of research at the intersection of RFT, LaMAS, and MARL, providing a framework for developing more resilient, adaptive, and human-aligned agentic systems. The open-source implementation further encourages community engagement and accelerated development.
- **Unleashing Swarm Intelligence:** By offering a principled approach to integrating LLMs as agents within dynamic environments, MARFT aims to unleash the "swarm intelligence" of LaMAS, enabling them to achieve collective goals that individual agents cannot. This is crucial for advancing towards generalizable artificial intelligence (AGI) capable of tackling complex open-world problems.
