# Dream.exe: Can Video Generation Models Dream Executable Robot Manipulation?

> [!summary] Summary
> Show Lab researchers at the National University of Singapore developed Dream.exe, a novel benchmark evaluating video generation models based on the physical executability of their generated robot manipulation trajectories in a physics simulator. The framework uncovered a negligible correlation between a video's visual quality and its physical task success, with general-purpose models achieving up to 21.4% binary success on simpler tasks but struggling significantly with complex, multi-stage manipulation.

## Problem

- Existing video generation benchmarks focus primarily on visual quality, temporal consistency, and text-video alignment, failing to assess if generated motions are physically executable by a robot.
- The 'world model hypothesis'—that video generation models implicitly learn physical causality—lacked direct, measurable validation through physical interaction and task success.
- Generated videos, often proposed as scalable behavioral priors for robot learning, lacked a robust evaluation framework to guarantee their physical executability and real-world task success.

## Method

- **Dream.exe** introduces a ==closed-loop evaluation pipeline== that converts generated robot manipulation videos into ==executable robot trajectories== and assesses their success in a ==MuJoCo== physics simulator.
- The benchmark utilizes a task suite of 101 manually curated episodes from ==RoboCasa365==, categorized into three levels of increasing physical and temporal complexity (atomic, multi-object, multi-stage).
- A five-step ==video-to-trajectory pipeline== extracts 3D end-effector and object trajectories from 2D video frames, infers gripper actions, and assembles them into an action stream for robot execution.

## Results

- General-purpose video generators achieved task-level binary success rates of up to **21.4%** on Level 2 multi-object manipulation tasks, demonstrating some physical knowledge acquisition from internet-scale data.
- All evaluated models showed significant performance degradation on Level 3 multi-stage tasks, with most general-purpose models achieving **zero** binary success rate, indicating limitations in long-horizon physical reasoning.
- Robot-specific models (e.g., CosmosPolicy) achieved higher trajectory executability (**E-SR up to 0.75**) but did not consistently outperform general generators in overall task-level success, suggesting potential generalization challenges for domain-specific models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - There is a low correlation (Pearson r ≈ -0.03) between the visual quality and physical plausibility of generated videos and their actual task-level execution success.
> - Current video generation models exhibit significant limitations in maintaining physical coherence over long task horizons, particularly on multi-stage tasks, often failing due to 'phantom grasps' or 'kinematic breakdowns'.
> - Depth estimation within the video-to-trajectory pipeline acts as a critical bottleneck, with task success rates notably improving when using ground-truth depth compared to learned depth.
