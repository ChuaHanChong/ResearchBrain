# Correct-by-Construction Behavior Tree Synthesis from Signal Temporal Logic Specifications with Application to Robotic Missions

> [!summary] Summary
> This work introduces a formal framework for synthesizing correct-by-construction Behavior Trees (BTs) directly from Signal Temporal Logic (STL) specifications, enabling autonomous robots to satisfy complex missions with quantitative timing constraints. The method guarantees forward invariance and monotonic progress, demonstrating high satisfaction rates in simulations and successful execution on a physical quadrotor even with disturbances.

## Problem

- Manually designed Behavior Trees (BTs) lack formal guarantees of correctness, especially for complex robotic missions with stringent requirements.
- Existing formal synthesis methods for BTs, typically based on Linear Temporal Logic (LTL), cannot express quantitative timing constraints essential for many practical robotic tasks.
- Other STL-based control approaches (e.g., MPC, CBF, RL, monolithic automata) often suffer from scalability issues for long-horizon tasks, lack modularity, are uninterpretable, or do not offer comprehensive formal guarantees.

## Method

- The approach models the robot and environment as a ==Timed Transition System (TTS)== abstracted into a finite ==zone graph==, augmented with memory states (flags and timers) to track STL satisfaction progress.
- A ==hierarchical fixed-point algorithm== computes ==winning sets== for individual and combined STL specifications (Safety, Reachability, Response, Recurrence, Persistence) over this augmented state space.
- A **correct-by-construction Behavior Tree (BT)** is automatically synthesized, with a root fallback node grouping subtrees for each specification type, incorporating ==action-selection logic== and ==safe-wait conditions== to ensure global specification satisfaction.

## Results

- Achieved high satisfaction rates (**S ≥ 0.96**) and consistently positive mean STL robustness across **30** diverse simulation scenarios, demonstrating robust handling of multiple concurrent specifications and timing constraints.
- Successfully completed a **192.0-second** gas monitoring and sealing mission with a physical quadrotor, satisfying **six** concurrent STL specifications (including safety, reachability, periodic patrol, and persistence) with positive quantitative robustness.
- The physical experiment showed dynamic adaptation to a physical perturbation during a persistence task, with the synthesized BT guiding the UAV to re-satisfy the temporal holding requirement within the given deadline.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - It is possible to formally synthesize modular and reactive Behavior Trees that guarantee satisfaction of quantitative timing constraints expressed in Signal Temporal Logic.
> - The framework ensures both forward invariance (safety) and monotonic progress (liveness) for concurrently active tasks, even when facing environmental non-determinism, by staying within a pre-computed winning set.
> - Decomposing complex STL specifications into hierarchical BT subtrees allows for interpretable, modular control while retaining formal guarantees over the entire mission.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Behavior Trees (BTs) have become a widely adopted architecture for robotic control due to their modularity, reactivity, hierarchical structure, and human readability, often favored over Finite State Machines (FSMs) for complex task execution. However, a primary challenge in BT design is that they are typically crafted manually by human experts, leading to a lack of formal guarantees regarding their correctness with respect to specified mission requirements.

The field of formal methods in robotics has sought to address this by developing automated verification and synthesis techniques for BTs. Previous work has explored the synthesis of correct-by-construction BTs from Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) specifications, which can guarantee task completion for certain specifications. A limitation of LTL, however, is its qualitative nature, meaning it cannot express quantitative timing constraints, such as deadlines or specific durations for tasks. Robotic missions frequently necessitate such quantitative temporal requirements (e.g., "reach a charging station within 10 minutes" or "monitor an area for at least 5 seconds").

Signal Temporal Logic (STL) extends LTL by incorporating real-valued time intervals, thereby enabling the expression of quantitative timing constraints. While STL offers enhanced expressiveness, synthesizing reactive controllers from STL specifications presents its own set of challenges. Existing approaches for STL-based control include:
- **Model Predictive Control (MPC):** Can encode STL as mixed-integer programs, but its receding horizon nature limits scalability for persistent tasks.
- **Control Barrier Function (CBF) methods:** Enforce STL through continuous-time invariance but typically lack discrete task-level modularity.
- **Reinforcement Learning (RL) with STL reward shaping:** Produces policies that are often uninterpretable "black-boxes" and lack formal guarantees.
- **Automata-based reactive synthesis:** Provides correct-by-construction guarantees through two-player games but results in a monolithic finite automaton, which can hinder per-task interpretability, incremental modification, and the modular benefits inherent to BTs.

This research aims to bridge the gap identified by these limitations. It addresses the need for a framework that simultaneously offers formal guarantees, allows for modular decomposition of multi-specification tasks, and supports reactive, long-horizon execution, particularly when quantitative timing constraints are involved. By synthesizing BTs from STL, the authors propose a method that combines the benefits of BTs (modularity, reactivity) with the formal rigor and quantitative expressiveness of STL, thereby advancing the state of the art in verifiable autonomous robotic control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this work arises from the increasing complexity of robotic missions in real-world scenarios. Modern robotic tasks frequently demand not only the completion of specific objectives but also adherence to precise quantitative temporal conditions. For instance, a robot might need to respond to an alarm within a strict deadline, maintain a presence in a zone for a minimum duration, or visit multiple locations periodically.
Current methods for designing robot behaviors, particularly through manually crafted Behavior Trees, lack the formal verification necessary to guarantee that these complex specifications, especially those involving timing, will be consistently met. This absence of formal guarantees can lead to unpredictable robot behavior, mission failure, or unsafe operations, particularly in critical applications.

While existing formal synthesis techniques for BTs based on Linear Temporal Logic (LTL) provide correctness guarantees, LTL's inherent qualitative nature prevents it from expressing the quantitative timing constraints essential for many practical robotic tasks. Other advanced control methods that handle STL, such as Model Predictive Control, Control Barrier Functions, or Reinforcement Learning, often fall short in terms of scalability for long-horizon tasks, modularity, interpretability, or providing strong formal guarantees. Monolithic automata-based synthesis, while offering guarantees, produces opaque controllers that counteract the modular and readable advantages of BTs.

Given these limitations, the key objectives of this research are:
1. **To develop a formal framework for synthesizing Behavior Trees** that are correct-by-construction, meaning their design inherently guarantees satisfaction of given specifications.
2. **To enable the use of Signal Temporal Logic (STL) specifications** within this synthesis framework, thereby allowing the expression and enforcement of quantitative timing constraints in robotic missions.
3. **To demonstrate the practical applicability and robustness** of the synthesized BTs through both numerical simulations and real-world physical experiments, validating their ability to manage multiple concurrent specifications and disturbances.
4. **To provide theoretical guarantees** regarding the correctness and computational complexity of the proposed synthesis approach.
This research aims to provide a robust, verifiable, and modular solution for designing autonomous robot controllers that can navigate complex environments while satisfying time-critical operational requirements.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework for synthesizing correct-by-construction Behavior Trees from STL specifications involves several sequential steps, integrating system modeling, formal logic, algorithmic computation, and BT construction.

**A. System Model and Abstraction:**
The robot and its operating environment are initially modeled as a **Timed Transition System (TTS)**. A TTS explicitly incorporates continuous time through clocks, along with discrete locations and actions. To manage the infinite continuous state space of a TTS, it is abstracted into a finite **zone graph ($G_Z$)**. Nodes in the zone graph consist of a discrete location and a clock zone (represented by a Difference Bound Matrix), while edges represent transitions between zones via actions or time progression.

**B. STL Specification:**
The robot's mission requirements are formalized using an STL fragment. The overall specification ($\phi$) is defined as a conjunction of five distinct STL formula types, each crucial for different aspects of robotic tasks:
- **Safety ($\phi_{\square,i}$):** Ensures a proposition holds for a specified duration (e.g., "avoid obstacles for 10 seconds").
- **Reachability ($\phi_{\diamond,i}$):** Guarantees a proposition is met within a time window (e.g., "reach the target within 30 seconds").
- **Response ($\phi_{\Rightarrow\diamond,i}$):** Specifies that if a condition occurs, another proposition must be met within a time window (e.g., "if an alarm sounds, reach a safe zone within 5 seconds").
- **Recurrence ($\phi_{\square\diamond,i}$):** Ensures periodic visits to a proposition while another condition holds (e.g., "while monitoring, visit Sensor N every 60 seconds").
- **Persistence ($\phi_{\diamond\square,i}$):** Requires maintaining a proposition for a duration within an overall time window, given a triggering condition (e.g., "if a leak alarm, maintain Region Valve for 10 seconds within 35 seconds").
These specifications involve controllable (robot actions) and uncontrollable (environment conditions) atomic propositions.

**C. Augmented State Space:**
To track both physical dynamics and satisfaction progress of STL specifications, each node `v` in the zone graph is augmented with a **memory state (M)**. This creates an **augmented state ($\xi = (v, M)$)**. The memory state `M` comprises:
- **Completion flags:** Boolean indicators for reachability and response tasks.
- **Mode flags:** Encode internal modes for recurrence (IDLE/ACTIVE) and persistence (IDLE/SEARCH/HOLD) tasks.
- **Timers:** Record elapsed time for various temporal constraints, which are saturated at their respective deadlines to maintain a finite state space.
Transitions in this augmented state space jointly update the zone graph node, advance timers (with time transitions), and modify logic flags based on satisfied atomic propositions.

**D. Winning Set Computation:**
The core of the synthesis is the computation of a "winning set" from which all specifications can be satisfied. This is achieved through:
1. **Value Function ($V$):** A backward Dijkstra algorithm is applied to the zone graph to compute, for each node `v` and proposition `p`, the minimum time-to-reach a state where `p` is true. This function serves as a heuristic for assessing timing feasibility.
2. **Requirement Function ($R$):** For each augmented state $\xi$, $R(\xi)$ is a boolean function that indicates whether the overall specification $\phi$ can still be satisfied. It is a conjunction of individual requirement functions $R'_{\kappa}(\xi, p_{\kappa,i})$ for each specific STL clause.
 - Each $R'_{\kappa}$ evaluates three dimensions: logical completion, timing feasibility (using $V$), and recursive invariance.
 - **Recursive Invariance** is enforced using a **Controllable Predecessor (CPre)** operator. $CPre(W)$ identifies states from which the robot can guarantee, through its action choice, that the system will transition to a state within the set $W$, even under environmental non-determinism.
3. **Hierarchical Fixed-Point Algorithm (Algorithm 2):** This algorithm iteratively computes the winning sets ($W_\kappa$) for each specification type (Safety, Reachability, Recurrence, Persistence, Response). The total winning set ($W_{total}$) is the intersection of these individual winning sets. The hierarchy ensures that earlier constraints (e.g., safety) are satisfied before later ones.

**E. Constraint Function:**
A **constraint function ($\rho(\xi, \Delta)$)** is defined to restrict the robot's actions at runtime. It has two components:
- **$\rho_{dead}(\xi, \Delta)$:** Checks if remaining in the current state for a waiting period $\Delta$ maintains the system within $W_{total}$ and satisfies location invariants.
- **$\rho_{env}(\xi)$:** Ensures that there is at least one action available that guarantees the system remains in $W_{total}$ after the transition, accounting for possible environmental influences.

**F. Behavior Tree Construction:**
The synthesized Behavior Tree (BT) is structured as a root fallback node that groups subtrees for each specification type. Each individual subtree incorporates:
- **Action-selection branch:** Selects actions that minimize the value function for relevant propositions, provided the resulting state remains within $W_{total}$ and satisfies $\rho_{env}$.
- **Safe-wait branch:** Allows the robot to wait for a duration $\Delta$ if $\rho_{dead}$ is true, when no immediate discrete action is optimal or necessary.
- **Type-specific logic:** Dedicated branches and decorator nodes (e.g., S→F, S→R, Inv) manage the completion flags, mode transitions, and timer updates specific to Reachability, Response, Recurrence, and Persistence tasks as defined by their requirement functions. For example, a Recurrence subtree uses internal modes (IDLE, ACTIVE) and checks for timeouts, while a Persistence subtree manages three modes (IDLE, SEARCH, HOLD) to ensure a proposition is maintained for a duration.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presented several findings, encompassing theoretical guarantees, numerical simulation results, and a physical quadrotor experiment.

**A. Theoretical Analysis:**
- **Forward Invariance (Lemma 1):** The execution of the synthesized Behavior Tree, coupled with the constraint function, guarantees that the system's state remains within the total winning set ($W_{total}$) at all times. This ensures that safety specifications are never violated.
- **Monotonic Progress and Timing Guarantees (Lemma 2):** Within $W_{total}$, the induced policy ensures that all active liveness tasks (Reachability, Response, Recurrence, Persistence) are completed within their specified timing constraints. The action selection, guided by the value function, forces a monotonic decrease in the "time-to-go" metric for active tasks. The framework also guarantees that satisfying one specification does not interfere with the feasibility of other concurrent tasks.
- **Correct-by-Construction Guarantee (Theorem 1):** Combining the proofs of forward invariance and monotonic progress, the authors formally established that the synthesized Behavior Tree (B) produces an induced policy ($\pi_B$) that satisfies the given STL specification ($\phi$).
- **Computational Complexity (Theorem 2):** The offline synthesis time complexity was bounded by $O(|\phi| \cdot |E| \cdot \log |V| + |\phi| \cdot |\Xi|^2 \cdot |A|)$, where $|V|$ and $|E|$ are the zone graph size, $|A|$ is the number of actions, $|\phi|$ is the number of specifications, and $|\Xi| = O(|V| \cdot 2^{|\phi|} \cdot U^{|\phi|})$ is the size of the augmented state space, with $U$ being the maximum saturated deadline.

**B. Simulation Results:**
The framework was evaluated across 30 distinct scenarios, categorized by grid scale, number of specifications, specification combinations, timing constraint tightness, and obstacle density.
- **Satisfaction and Robustness:** The simulations yielded a high satisfaction rate (S ≥ 0.96) and consistently positive mean STL robustness ($\bar{\rho} > 0$) across all categories. This indicates that the synthesized BTs reliably met the specifications with a positive margin.
- **Specification Handling:** Scenarios with varying specification counts and combinations achieved a perfect satisfaction rate (S=1.0), confirming the framework's ability to concurrently manage all five defined STL types.
- **Timing Constraint Impact:** The primary factor leading to a notable, albeit limited, degradation in performance was the tightness of timing constraints. When deadlines approached the theoretical shortest-path limit, the per-scenario satisfaction rate decreased to S=0.86.
- **Scalability:** Offline synthesis time primarily scaled with the workspace size, increasing from 1.78 seconds for a 10x10 grid to 25.02 seconds for a 35x35 grid. Variations in timing constraints or obstacle density had a negligible effect on synthesis time.

**C. Physical Experiment:**
A physical quadrotor experiment was conducted in a 4m x 8m indoor environment, simulating a gas monitoring and sealing mission. The quadrotor was tasked with simultaneously satisfying six complex STL specifications, including global safety, timed safety, initial reachability, periodic patrol, secondary response confirmation, and emergency persistence sealing.
- **Successful Execution:** The 192.0-second mission was successfully completed, with all six STL specifications demonstrably satisfied.
- **Quantitative Robustness:** Analysis of the UAV's trajectory showed that all specifications maintained strictly positive quantitative STL robustness, indicating that the robot operated with a safety margin against violating its requirements.
- **Disturbance Handling:** The system effectively managed a physical perturbation introduced during the execution of a persistence specification. The synthesized BT dynamically guided the UAV back to its target to fulfill the 10-second holding requirement within the 35-second deadline.
- **Onboard Deployment:** The offline synthesis was performed on the onboard computer, demonstrating the feasibility of deploying the framework in real-time robotic systems.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research contributes to the field of autonomous systems by addressing a notable limitation in Behavior Tree synthesis: the integration of quantitative timing constraints derived from formal specifications. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Enhanced Reliability and Formal Guarantees:** By providing a correct-by-construction framework, this work significantly enhances the reliability and predictability of robotic systems. It offers formal guarantees that mission-critical quantitative timing specifications will be met, which is a substantial improvement over manually designed or LTL-based BTs that cannot express such constraints. This addresses a core need for safety-critical applications where failure to meet deadlines can have severe consequences.

- **Bridging the Gap in Formal Methods for Robotics:** The research effectively combines the benefits of Behavior Trees (modularity, reactivity, human readability, hierarchical structure) with the expressive power of Signal Temporal Logic. This bridges a gap between control architectures that prioritize modularity and formal methods that provide strong correctness guarantees but often result in monolithic or less interpretable controllers.

- **Practical Applicability for Complex Missions:** The demonstration through both extensive simulations and a physical quadrotor experiment validates the practical deployability of the framework in complex, real-world robotic scenarios. The successful management of multiple, concurrent STL specifications, including handling unexpected disturbances, indicates its potential for missions requiring sophisticated temporal coordination.

- **Quantitative Performance Assessment:** The utilization of STL robustness provides a quantitative measure of performance and safety margins, offering more detailed insights than binary satisfaction. This metric can be crucial for system design, verification, and performance evaluation.

- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework lays a foundation for addressing more advanced challenges in autonomous robotics, as suggested by the authors' future work directions, such as online replanning in unknown environments and multi-robot coordination. The modular approach could simplify the extension to multi-agent systems.

- **Wider Adoption of Formal Methods:** By making formal methods more accessible and practical through the interpretable structure of Behavior Trees, this research could encourage wider adoption of rigorous verification and synthesis techniques in the robotics community, moving beyond empirical development.

In summary, this work provides a method for designing robust, verifiable, and adaptable autonomous agents capable of operating under strict temporal constraints, which is critical for the advancement of dependable robotic systems in diverse applications.
