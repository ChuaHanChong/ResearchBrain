# FasTac: A Curved Multispectral Vision-Based Tactile Sensor for High-Speed High-Precision 3D Shape and Force Perception

> [!summary] Summary
> FasTac introduces a curved multispectral vision-based tactile sensor capable of high-speed and high-precision perception of 3D shape and three-axis contact forces. Its edge deployment on an FPGA achieves low-latency, real-time feedback, supporting dynamic robotic manipulation tasks.

## Problem

- Existing tactile sensors struggle to achieve high spatial resolution and signal quality on curved surfaces, often providing insufficient 3D geometric information.
- Accurate three-axis force estimation on curved elastomers is challenging due to their spatially nonuniform mechanical responses and difficulty in decoupling normal and shear forces.
- High computational demands and data transmission latency in vision-based tactile systems limit real-time perception of rapid load variations, hindering dynamic robotic interactions.

## Method

- A compact, curved **FasTac** sensor integrates ==RGB-NIR illumination== and a single RGB-IR CMOS sensor for robust, high-resolution 3D shape reconstruction across the entire curved surface.
- The **HyperForce** method, an FEM-inspired ==dynamic convolution network==, models position-dependent stiffness for accurate three-axis force estimation on curved elastomers.
- The full image-to-normal-force estimation pipeline is deployed onto an ==FPGA edge platform== to achieve low-latency and high-speed operation suitable for dynamic robotic tasks.

## Results

- Achieved 3D depth reconstruction with a mean absolute error (MAE) of **0.0415mm** using RGB-NIR and a boundary prior, a **32.8%** reduction compared to RGB-only illumination.
- Demonstrated high three-axis force estimation accuracy with normalized mean absolute error (NMAE) values of **2.37%** for Fx, **2.41%** for Fy, and **2.74%** for Fz.
- The FPGA implementation yielded a processing latency of **1.09ms** per frame and an energy cost of **8.41mJ**/frame for normal force estimation, accurately capturing dynamic signals up to **100Hz**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Multispectral RGB-NIR illumination significantly improves surface normal and depth reconstruction accuracy on curved surfaces by providing more robust constraints compared to RGB-only methods.
> - Spatially adaptive, dynamic kernels within the HyperForce network are essential for accurately estimating three-axis forces on curved elastomers, effectively accounting for position-dependent stiffness.
> - Deploying the tactile pipeline on an FPGA dramatically reduces latency and energy consumption, enabling deterministic, high-bandwidth tactile feedback crucial for dynamic robotic interactions.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Tactile sensing is a critical component for enabling dexterous robotic manipulation, stable grasping, and dynamic contact interaction. In these applications, robots often engage with objects through curved fingertips, requiring the perception of fine contact geometry, accurate differentiation between normal and tangential loads, and the ability to capture transient signals rapidly.

Existing tactile sensors, categorized by their transduction mechanisms such as resistive, capacitive, and magnetic arrays, face several limitations. While effective for large-area pressure mapping, achieving high spatial resolution with these taxel-based arrays often compromises signal quality, increases wiring complexity, and introduces inter-taxel crosstalk. Furthermore, their discrete outputs typically provide insufficient geometric information for detailed 3D reconstruction. Although some conventional sensors can provide three-axis force sensing, decoupling normal and shear forces often necessitates specialized structures or complex calibration models. The integration of these capabilities into compact, curved fingertip designs introduces further complications, as local curvature affects baseline deformation, contact orientation, boundary constraints, and effective sensitivity, making both 3D reconstruction and three-axis force estimation challenging.

Vision-based tactile sensing has emerged as a promising alternative, leveraging optical imaging of elastomer deformation to infer geometry and force. Planar and near-planar vision-based sensors have demonstrated high-resolution 3D reconstruction and dense force estimation. Extending these designs to curved geometries, however, introduces specific technical hurdles. Uniform and sufficient illumination across a non-planar sensing surface becomes difficult, leading to issues like self-shadowing and non-uniform illumination that can impair 3D reconstruction methods such as photometric stereo. Force estimation on curved elastomers also presents a mechanically coupled inverse problem, where local curvature and boundary conditions alter the deformation-force relationship across the surface. Additionally, the computational demands of processing high-resolution tactile images and the associated data transmission to external processors can introduce significant latency, hindering the perception of rapid load variations necessary for dynamic interactions like vibrations or collisions.

This work introduces FasTac to address these specific challenges, building upon existing vision-based tactile sensing techniques while innovating in multispectral imaging, force estimation modeling, and hardware acceleration. Prior efforts have explored optimized illumination (e.g., GelSight360, RainbowSight) and multimodal imaging (e.g., GelSplitter3D) for curved-surface reconstruction, but often involve increased system volume or complex registration. For force estimation, both physics-based (e.g., FEM-based methods) and data-driven (e.g., neural networks in DenseTact 2.0, AllSight) approaches exist, yet often struggle with accurately modeling the spatially nonuniform mechanical response of curved elastomers. In terms of computational acceleration, some systems have employed optimized camera transfer, lightweight networks, or event cameras (e.g., MinSight, EveTac) or utilized FPGAs for pre-processing (e.g., Oballe-Peinado et al., Hundhausen et al.). However, a complete end-to-end vision-based tactile processing pipeline fully deployed on an edge FPGA platform for concurrent high-precision 3D shape and three-axis force perception at high speed remains an area of active development. FasTac aims to fill this gap by integrating these advanced capabilities into a compact, low-latency system.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a compact, curved vision-based tactile sensor, named FasTac, capable of high-speed and high-precision perception of 3D shape and three-axis forces. This overarching goal is driven by several specific motivations and aimed at overcoming limitations in existing tactile sensing technologies for dexterous robotic manipulation.

**Motivation for High-Precision 3D Shape Perception:**
Dexterous manipulation often involves interacting with objects that possess fine geometric features, such as small protrusions, pits, or edges. Robots require high spatial resolution to accurately perceive these subtle contact imprints. Existing curved vision-based tactile sensors often struggle to combine adequate and uniform illumination across the entire curved surface, leading to localized failures in 3D reconstruction methods like photometric stereo. Therefore, a key motivation is to ensure robust and accurate 3D surface normal and depth reconstruction, particularly in high-curvature regions where conventional illumination schemes might fail or introduce artifacts.

**Motivation for Reliable Three-Axis Force Estimation:**
For stable grasping and fine manipulation, robots need to distinguish between normal and tangential loads. Grasp stability, for instance, depends on the ability to resist tangential loads with available friction, not solely on the normal force. Vision-based force estimation on curved elastomers is complicated by the mechanically coupled nature of the problem, where local curvature, material thickness, and boundary conditions cause the deformation-force relationship to vary spatially across the surface. Existing data-driven models often do not explicitly encode this position-dependent stiffness. Thus, a significant objective is to develop a force estimation method that can accurately model and account for the spatially nonuniform mechanical response of curved elastomers to provide reliable three-axis force feedback.

**Motivation for High-Speed and Low-Latency Processing:**
Robotic tasks involving dynamic interactions, such as vibrations, slippage, or collisions, require tactile cues to be captured and processed rapidly. Traditional vision-based tactile systems often rely on external CPUs or GPUs, which introduce image transmission overhead, communication latency, and scheduling jitter, increasing the end-to-end latency of tactile feedback. This limitation can prevent the timely capture of transient tactile signals. A crucial objective is therefore to deploy the entire image-to-force estimation pipeline on an edge computing platform, specifically an FPGA, to minimize latency, reduce power consumption, and enable deterministic, real-time tactile feedback necessary for responsive control in compact multi-finger robotic systems.

In summary, the key objectives are:
1. To design and implement a compact curved tactile fingertip that integrates multispectral (RGB-NIR) illumination with a single image sensor for robust, high-resolution 3D shape reconstruction across the entire curved surface.
2. To develop a spatially adaptive force estimation method, HyperForce, that effectively models the position-dependent stiffness of curved elastomers to accurately estimate three-axis contact forces.
3. To accelerate the tactile perception pipeline by deploying the image-to-normal-force estimation process onto an FPGA, thereby achieving low-latency and high-speed operation suitable for dynamic robotic tasks.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FasTac sensor design and its perception pipeline integrate custom hardware with computational models optimized for curved surfaces and real-time processing.

#### 3.1. FasTac Sensor Design

The sensor prioritizes compactness for dexterous manipulation. It comprises three main modules:
- **Light-Guiding Fingertip Skeleton:** This acrylic skeleton houses the camera module and channels light.
- **Multispectral LED FPC:** A Flexible Printed Circuit (FPC) fitted to the skeleton’s dorsal curvature contains LED arrays in red, green, blue, and near-infrared (NIR) wavelengths, secured by a rear cover. LEDs are placed at four corners with perpendicular illumination directions (red/green vs. blue/NIR) to enhance angular diversity and spatial distinctiveness of illumination vectors. The translucent skeleton diffuses these point sources into a soft, uniform light field, reducing specular highlights.
- **Sensing Interface:** A gel skin, made from platinum-catalyzed silicone rubber (Smooth-On Solaris) mixed with Slacker softener (1:1:3 ratio), provides compliance for sensitive deformation capture. A precision-machined optical-grade acrylic mold ensures a smooth surface. A specialized reflective paint (800-mesh silver powder, silicone solvent, thinner) is applied to the elastomer surface for optimal optical properties, followed by laser marking and black silicone ink application to create a marker-dot array for tangential displacement tracking.
- **Imaging Module:** A custom miniature camera module, controlled by an onboard FPGA, uses an OmniVision OV2736 RGB-IR CMOS sensor. This sensor utilizes a unique 4x4 RGB-IR Color Filter Array (CFA) to capture visible and NIR information simultaneously in a single exposure. This single-sensor design provides intrinsic pixel-level alignment, avoiding registration errors common in multi-camera or beam-splitting systems, and reduces overall volume.

#### 3.2. FasTac 3D Shape Reconstruction Pipeline

The geometric perception relies on multispectral photometric stereo and boundary-prior depth reconstruction.
- **Spectral Demultiplexing and Pre-processing:** The RAW10 data from the OV2736 sensor is unpacked from the MIPI CSI-2 stream. A 4x4 RGB-IR CFA pattern is decoded, followed by dead-pixel correction (DPC) and black-level correction (BLC). The NIR channel is sampled from odd rows/columns, interpolated, and then used for IR crosstalk subtraction from visible pixels. The corrected RGB-IR CFA is rearranged into a standard BGGR Bayer image, demosaiced to form an RGB image, and the NIR image is mapped to 8-bit grayscale. This process yields synchronized, zero-parallax RGB and NIR images.
- **Four-Source Photometric Stereo:** The system uses RGB-NIR as four independent spectral channels. For a Lambertian surface, intensity $I$ is related to albedo $\rho$, illumination direction $l$, and surface normal $n$ by $I = \rho l^T n$. By constructing an over-determined system $I = \rho Ln$ from $L = [l_R, l_G, l_B, l_{NIR}]^T$, the sensor ensures robust surface normal estimation even if one channel is occluded in a curved geometry.
- **Position-Aware MLP for Surface Normal Estimation:** Due to the compact and curved nature of the sensor, illumination vectors are position-dependent ($L(p)$). To account for this, a lightweight pixel-wise Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) takes concatenated normalized coordinates ($p_{u,v}$) and four-channel spectral intensity ($I_{u,v}$) as input. This MLP, with a 6-dimensional input vector, estimates the unit-normalized surface normal $\hat{n}_{u,v}$, optimized using an L1 loss.
- **Fast Poisson Depth Reconstruction with Boundary Prior:** Estimated surface normals are integrated into a depth map. A Dirichlet-constrained fast Poisson solver is employed, where a boundary prior ($D_{prior}$) derived from the sensor's CAD model is embedded into the source term. This method uses the standard type-I discrete sine transform (DST-I) to efficiently solve for depth while avoiding drift caused by the zero-boundary assumption common in standard Poisson solvers.

#### 3.3. FEM-inspired Dynamic Convolution for Three-Axis Force Estimation (HyperForce)

- **3D Displacement Field Extraction:** The 3D displacement field $u(p) = [d_x(p), d_y(p), d_z(p)]^T$ is crucial.
 - **Tangential Displacement:** Sparse marker displacements ($d_k$) are extracted from the elastomer surface using thresholding and blob detection. A radial basis function (RBF) interpolator with an inverse multiquadric (IMQ) kernel is then used to reconstruct the dense tangential displacement field ($u_{tan}(p) = [d_x(p), d_y(p)]^T$).
 - **Normal Displacement:** Normal displacement ($d_z(p)$) is calculated as the difference between the background depth ($D_0(p)$) and the current reconstructed depth ($D_t(p)$).
- **FEM-inspired Dynamic Convolution Network:** HyperForce addresses the spatially nonuniform effective stiffness of curved elastomers.
 - It approximates the Finite Element Method (FEM) operator by treating each pixel as a node and assuming local force response $f(p)$ is a convolution-like operation over a local displacement neighborhood $u(p+q)$, using a position-dependent stiffness-like coupling weight $G_{p,q}$.
 - Unlike a fixed convolutional neural network (CNN) kernel, HyperForce uses a lightweight hypernetwork (implemented with 1x1 convolutional layers) to generate position-aware dynamic kernels $w_{\alpha}(p)$ for each force component $\alpha \in \{x, y, z\}$. These kernels are generated from the normalized 3D coordinate encoding $c(p)$.
 - The pixel-wise force component $\hat{f}_{\alpha}(p)$ is computed by the dot product of the dynamic kernel $w_{\alpha}(p)$ and the vectorized $K \times K$ local displacement patch $v(p)$.
 - The resultant three-axis force $\hat{F}$ is obtained by summing the pixel-wise force components over the valid tactile region $\Omega$. The network is trained using global three-axis force supervision with an L1 loss.

#### 3.4. FPGA-Based Edge Deployment of the Image-to-F$_z$ Estimation Pipeline

To meet dynamic feedback requirements, the normal-force ($F_z$) branch of FasTac's pipeline is deployed on a Xilinx Zynq UltraScale+ MPSoC.
- **Architecture:** A heterogeneous architecture with a Processing System (PS) for orchestration (e.g., image synchronization, control, readout) and Programmable Logic (PL) for latency-critical streaming datapath (computation modules with internal FIFOs).
- **Preprocessing:** Input images (originally 1920x1080) are cropped and downsampled to 128x128 to fit FPGA resources and increase camera frame rate.
- **Surface Normal Estimation Module:** Implements the lightweight MLP using fully unrolled 16x16 matrix-vector multiplication (MVM) tiles, ReLU, and requantization. It processes pixels as a continuous stream for minimal latency.
- **Depth Reconstruction Module:** Converts surface normals to gradients and solves the boundary-prior Poisson problem over a 126x126 inner region. It includes a right-hand side (RHS) generation module that computes divergence and injects the Dirichlet boundary prior. An orthonormal DST-I Poisson solver performs separable 2-D DST transforms, utilizing ping-pong buffers and partitioning into strips to optimize memory movement and throughput.
- **Normal-Force Estimation Module:** Focuses on $F_z$ estimation. It quantizes the normal displacement, then uses a patch generator to emit a $K \times K$ displacement vector and a kernel reader to retrieve a coordinate-indexed, position-aware kernel vector from Read-Only Memory (ROM). Convolution and full-frame accumulation are performed using these quantized values, followed by scaling to output the force estimate in Newtons. The module employs a sliding window approach with row buffers to avoid full-frame caching and enable streaming processing.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of FasTac involved assessing its performance in 3D geometry reconstruction, three-axis force sensing, and the benefits of FPGA-based edge deployment.

#### 4.1. 3D Geometry Reconstruction Evaluation

Experiments with a spherical indenter (radius 2.5mm) and real-world objects validated FasTac's geometric perception:
- **Gradient Error:** RGB-NIR illumination reduced the mean absolute error (MAE) of surface gradients ($G_x$ and $G_y$) by 40% and 22% respectively, compared to RGB-only illumination. The total gradient MAE decreased from 0.0098 mm/pixel to 0.0068 mm/pixel (Table II). This indicates that the additional NIR channel provides valuable constraints for surface normal estimation on curved surfaces.
- **Depth Reconstruction Accuracy:** With RGB-NIR illumination and the boundary prior, FasTac achieved a depth MAE of 0.0415 mm. This represents a 32.8% reduction compared to RGB-only illumination with the depth prior (0.0618 mm MAE). Crucially, without the boundary prior, both illumination schemes resulted in a significantly higher MAE of approximately 0.27 mm, demonstrating the essential role of the boundary-prior constraint for stable curved-surface reconstruction (Table III).
- **Real-Object Reconstruction:** Qualitative results on a strawberry surface, a fingerprint, and an LED array showed that RGB-NIR reconstruction produced more complete surface pits, better preserved ridge-valley continuity, and resolved miniature LED pads more clearly than RGB-only reconstruction (Fig. 7).

#### 4.2. Three-Axis Force Sensing Evaluation

HyperForce's performance was evaluated against a six-axis ATI force sensor:
- **Force Estimation Accuracy:** The predicted three-axis contact forces demonstrated strong linear agreement with the ground truth (Fig. 8). The MAE values were 0.0235 N for $F_x$, 0.0246 N for $F_y$, and 0.0545 N for $F_z$. These corresponded to normalized mean absolute error (NMAE) values of 2.37%, 2.41%, and 2.74% for $F_x$, $F_y$, and $F_z$, respectively. Normalized root-mean-square error (NRMSE) values were 3.15%, 3.33%, and 3.72%, with $R^2$ values of 0.9921, 0.9873, and 0.9690.
- **Ablation Study of HyperForce:** The dynamic kernel, which accounts for position-dependent stiffness, consistently outperformed a fixed convolutional neural network (CNN) kernel for the same input (Table IV). For the full $d_x + d_y + d_z$ displacement input, the $F_z$ MAE decreased from 0.4905 N with a fixed kernel to 0.0545 N with a dynamic kernel. Furthermore, using full 3D displacement ($d_x + d_y + d_z$) as input yielded the best overall accuracy under the dynamic kernel setting, indicating the complementary nature of normal indentation and tangential marker displacement cues.
- **Friction-Aware Grasping:** A friction-coefficient-based grasping task with a dexterous hand demonstrated the real-time closed-loop utility of FasTac. When external disturbances were applied, the sensor successfully detected increases in the friction coefficient ($\mu$) beyond a safety margin ($\mu_s + d$). This triggered a feedback mechanism that tightened the grasp (increased normal force $F_n$ by adjusting inter-finger angles), returning $\mu$ to the stable region and preventing slip. In contrast, without feedback, repeated disturbances led to slip (Fig. 9).

#### 4.3. Edge Deployment and High-Speed Tactile Processing Evaluation

The performance of the image-to-$F_z$ pipeline was compared across CPU, GPU, and FPGA implementations:
- **Latency and Energy Efficiency:** The FPGA implementation completed the full pipeline in 1.09 ms per frame with an energy cost of 8.41 mJ/frame. This represents a significant improvement over the CPU (6.82 ms, 238.05 mJ/frame) and GPU (3.26 ms, 33.60 mJ/frame) while maintaining comparable force accuracy ($F_z$ MAE of 0.0679 N for FPGA vs. 0.0669 N for CPU and 0.0667 N for GPU) (Table V).
- **Dynamic Performance (Vibration Measurement):** Under controlled vibration inputs, the FPGA demonstrated superior performance at higher frequencies (Fig. 10).
 - At 50 Hz, all platforms (CPU, GPU, FPGA) accurately recovered the excitation frequency and periodicity.
 - At 70 Hz, the CPU exhibited aliasing, producing an incorrect frequency, whereas the GPU and FPGA retained the correct response.
 - At 100 Hz, only the FPGA accurately preserved both the spectral peak and temporal periodicity of the normal-force response; both the CPU and GPU showed significant aliasing or degraded responses.
These results demonstrate the FPGA's capability for deterministic high-bandwidth tactile processing, crucial for sensing dynamic contact.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

FasTac represents an advancement in vision-based tactile sensing by integrating high-precision 3D shape reconstruction, three-axis force estimation, and high-speed processing into a compact, curved sensor. This work addresses several limitations of existing tactile technologies, particularly for dexterous robotic applications.

The development of a compact, single-image-sensor RGB-NIR imaging system, combined with boundary-prior fast Poisson depth reconstruction, offers a robust method for perceiving fine geometric features on curved surfaces. The ability to achieve a depth MAE of 0.0415 mm on a curved surface, significantly reducing error compared to conventional methods, enhances the capability of robots to discern subtle surface details during manipulation. This is particularly impactful for tasks requiring delicate object handling or interaction with complex textures.

The introduction of HyperForce, an FEM-inspired dynamic convolution network, provides a solution to the challenge of spatially nonuniform mechanical responses in curved elastomers. By explicitly modeling position-dependent stiffness, HyperForce achieves accurate three-axis force estimation with NMAE values of 2.74% for normal forces and 2.39% for shear forces. This precision in force perception, validated through experiments like friction-aware grasping, enables more stable and adaptive robotic manipulation, where the distinction between normal and tangential loads is critical for preventing slip and maintaining control during external disturbances.

Furthermore, the successful deployment of the complete image-to-normal-force pipeline on an FPGA edge platform signifies a key step towards practical integration of advanced tactile sensing into robotic systems. Achieving a processing latency of 1.09 ms with low energy consumption (8.41 mJ/frame) for $F_z$ estimation, while maintaining accuracy, directly addresses the need for low-latency, deterministic feedback in real-time robotic control. The FPGA's ability to accurately capture dynamic signals at frequencies up to 100 Hz, where CPU and GPU implementations exhibit aliasing, highlights its potential for sensing rapid contact events, such as vibrations or collisions.

The implications of FasTac's capabilities are substantial for various domains:
- **Robotic Manipulation:** Enables more advanced and robust dexterous manipulation, allowing robots to perform tasks requiring fine geometric perception, stable grasping in dynamic environments, and rapid response to contact changes. This could include tasks like assembling delicate components, manipulating deformable objects, or interacting with unpredictable environments.
- **Prosthetics and Haptics:** The compact size and high-performance tactile feedback could enhance the realism and functionality of prosthetic hands, providing users with more intuitive and sensitive touch perception.
- **Edge Computing in Robotics:** The demonstration of a full vision-based tactile pipeline on an FPGA paves the way for integrating sophisticated sensing and processing capabilities directly into robot end-effectors, reducing reliance on bulky external computation and minimizing data transfer bottlenecks. This facilitates the development of more autonomous, compact, and energy-efficient robotic systems.

In summary, FasTac contributes a comprehensive solution that integrates compact multispectral hardware design, advanced 3D reconstruction and force estimation algorithms, and real-time FPGA acceleration. This combination represents an important step toward equipping robots with tactile sensing capabilities that closely mimic human perception in terms of detail, sensitivity, and speed, thereby expanding the range and dexterity of robotic applications.
