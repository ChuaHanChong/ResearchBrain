# Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards Implicitly Incentivizes Correct Reasoning in Base LLMs

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Microsoft Research Asia and collaborating universities demonstrate that Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) fundamentally enhances Large Language Model reasoning capabilities, extending their problem-solving boundaries beyond improved sampling efficiency. The work introduces CoT-Pass@K to rigorously evaluate reasoning and provides a theoretical framework explaining how answer-based rewards implicitly incentivize correct intermediate thought processes.

## Problem

- The ongoing debate regarding whether Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) genuinely improves Large Language Model (LLM) intrinsic reasoning or merely boosts sampling efficiency for existing reasoning paths.
- Limitations of standard evaluation metrics like Pass@K, which can be inflated by LLMs producing correct final answers through flawed intermediate reasoning, especially in mathematical tasks.
- A lack of a comprehensive theoretical framework explaining how RLVR implicitly incentivizes correct reasoning, particularly when rewards are solely based on final answer correctness.

## Method

- Introduced ==CoT-Pass@K==, a refined evaluation metric that verifies both the final answer and the correctness of intermediate Chain-of-Thought (CoT) steps, leveraging an ==LLM-as-a-CoT-Judge== paradigm for scalable verification.
- Developed a theoretical framework demonstrating that RLVR, under a =='Logic Prior' assumption== (correct CoTs more likely to yield correct answers), implicitly incentivizes the generation of correct CoTs, even with binary answer-based rewards.
- Conducted empirical analyses of RLVR training dynamics and evaluated the quality of ==RLVR-generated CoTs== through supervised fine-tuning experiments, using generalization performance as a proxy for CoT quality.

## Results

- RLVR models (e.g., DAPO-Qwen-32B) showed a consistent and significant performance gap over base LLMs on math benchmarks (AIME 2024/2025) across large sampling numbers (up to K=**1024**) when measured by CoT-Pass@K.
- The theoretical framework proved that the GRPO policy gradient positively biases correct CoTs and negatively biases incorrect ones, leading to a monotonic increase in the probability of generating correct CoTs.
- Supervised fine-tuning on CoTs generated by the final RLVR model (DAPO CoTs) nearly replicated the Pass@1 performance of the RLVR-tuned model, demonstrating a fundamental enhancement in the quality of reasoning paths.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - RLVR fundamentally extends the reasoning capabilities of LLMs beyond mere sampling efficiency, as evidenced by consistent performance gaps over base models on challenging benchmarks when evaluated with CoT-Pass@K.
> - The 'Logic Prior' assumption, combined with the GRPO algorithm, explains how even binary answer-level rewards can implicitly and monotonically increase the probability of generating correct intermediate reasoning steps.
> - RLVR produces high-quality reasoning CoTs that can be used to significantly improve other models via supervised fine-tuning, nearly replicating the performance of computationally expensive RLVR-tuned models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper is strategically positioned within a burgeoning and intensely debated area of Large Language Model (LLM) research: the efficacy of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) in enhancing complex reasoning. The context is set by the "successful replication of long chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning" by models like DeepSeek-R1, employing algorithms such as Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which has ignited significant interest in RLVR as a pathway to "unlimited intelligence" through learning from free exploration.

However, a critical concern has emerged, questioning whether RLVR genuinely improves an LLM's intrinsic *reasoning abilities* or merely boosts its *sampling efficiency*—making it more likely to produce a correct answer it already "knew" how to generate, perhaps through lucky guesses or by selecting from an existing repertoire of reasoning paths. This concern is substantiated by observations, notably from Shao et al. (2024) and a systematic study by Yue et al. (2025), indicating that for "moderately large K," the standard Pass@K metric (which measures the probability of at least one correct answer out of K attempts) for a base (pre-RLVR) model can eventually match or even surpass its RLVR-tuned counterpart. This led to the "adventurous hypothesis" that all correct reasoning paths are already present in the base model, and RLVR's primary effect is to improve the sampling probability of these existing paths, potentially at the cost of overall reasoning capacity.

This paper directly confronts and aims to resolve this debate. It acknowledges the conflicting empirical findings in the literature—some studies supporting the "sampling efficiency only" view (e.g., Zhu et al., 2025; Zhang etal., 2025) while others report contradictory findings, such as the emergence of new reasoning patterns (Liu et al., 2025a) or persistent Pass@K improvements in specific domains like competitive coding (Chen et al., 2025b). The authors present their work as a systematic investigation to "reconcile these contradictory findings," offering a "systematic explanation" to demonstrate that RLVR *can* fundamentally enhance LLM reasoning abilities, extending their "reasoning capability boundary." By introducing a refined evaluation metric, CoT-Pass@K, and developing a theoretical framework, the paper contributes a new perspective that emphasizes the correctness of intermediate reasoning steps, moving beyond the sole focus on final answer correctness. This directly challenges the prevailing "sampling efficiency only" hypothesis and advocates for a deeper understanding of RLVR's mechanistic impact on LLM internal processes.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to systematically investigate and clarify the true impact of Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) on the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs). This overarching goal is driven by several interconnected motivations:

1. **Resolve the RLVR Debate:** The primary motivation stems from the ongoing academic debate and conflicting empirical evidence surrounding RLVR. While RLVR holds the promise of enabling LLMs to learn through experience and achieve "unlimited intelligence," concerns persist about whether it genuinely enhances reasoning or merely improves the sampling efficiency of already existing reasoning paths within the base model. The authors aim to provide a definitive, systematic answer to this fundamental question.
2. **Address Limitations of Existing Metrics:** A key motivation is the recognition that standard evaluation metrics like Pass@K can be unreliable, particularly in mathematical reasoning tasks. LLMs might produce a correct final answer through flawed reasoning or lucky guesses, leading to an inflated assessment of their true reasoning capacity. This paper seeks to overcome this limitation by proposing a more robust metric.
3. **Unveil Extended Reasoning Boundaries:** The authors are motivated to demonstrate that RLVR can indeed extend the "reasoning boundary" of LLMs, enabling them to solve problems that were beyond the intrinsic capabilities of their base models, rather than just sampling existing solutions more efficiently.
4. **Develop a Theoretical Understanding:** There is a lack of a comprehensive theoretical framework explaining *why* RLVR works in the context of LLMs, especially when rewards are purely based on answer correctness. The paper aims to fill this gap by formalizing the incentive mechanism of RLVR, particularly GRPO, to show how it can encourage correct reasoning.
5. **Analyze Training Dynamics:** To gain deeper insights into RLVR's effects, the authors are motivated to investigate its training dynamics. This involves understanding *when* and *how* improved reasoning abilities emerge during the RL process and whether these improvements generalize to unseen tasks.
6. **Evaluate CoT Quality:** The paper seeks to assess whether RLVR fundamentally improves the *quality* of the generated Chains of Thought (CoTs) from a learning perspective, by examining if these RLVR-generated CoTs can be used to train new models with enhanced generalization performance through supervised fine-tuning.

In essence, the paper aims to move beyond superficial observations of answer correctness to provide a more profound, nuanced, and theoretically grounded understanding of RLVR's capacity to cultivate genuine, verifiable reasoning abilities in LLMs.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors employ a multi-faceted approach combining empirical evaluation, theoretical analysis, and training dynamics investigation to systematically address their research questions.

#### 3.1. Re-evaluating Reasoning Capabilities with CoT-Pass@K

The core of their empirical methodology lies in revisiting Pass@K experiments, but with a critical refinement: the introduction of **CoT-Pass@K**.
- **Problem with Standard Pass@K:** The paper highlights that for mathematical tasks, a base LLM might produce a correct final answer through flawed intermediate reasoning ("spurious guesses"). Standard Pass@K, which only verifies the final answer, fails to capture this distinction.
- **CoT-Pass@K Definition:** CoT-Pass@K evaluates success only when *both* the final answer *and* the intermediate reasoning Chain-of-Thought (CoT) are correct.
- **LLM-as-a-CoT-Judge Paradigm:** To practically verify CoT correctness at scale, the authors leverage a specialized LLM, DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B, as a "powerful yet lightweight verifier." This verifier is prompted to analyze the solution step-by-step for computational accuracy, logical consistency, conceptual understanding, and overall validity.
- **Multi-Verification Strategy:** To mitigate potential errors in the LLM verifier, a multi-verification approach is implemented. For each CoT, three independent verification attempts are conducted. The results are aggregated using three strategies: "all-correct" (all verifications pass), "majority-correct" (most verifications pass), and "any-correct" (at least one verification passes), providing a robust assessment. Manual inspection of conflicting cases further ensures reliability.
- **Benchmarking:** Evaluations are performed on prominent math reasoning benchmarks (AIME 2024, AIME 2025, MATH-500, AMC23, Minerva) using Qwen2.5-32B as the base LLM and DAPO-Qwen-32B as the post-RLVR model. For code reasoning, where execution-based verification makes Pass@K more reliable, they extend experiments on LiveCodeBench (v1-v6), comparing DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B (distilled base) with AceReason-Nemotron-7B and Skywork-OR1-7B (post-RLVR models).

#### 3.2. Theoretical Framework for RLVR's Incentive Mechanism

The paper develops a formal theoretical framework to explain how RLVR, specifically the GRPO algorithm, incentivizes correct reasoning.
- **Problem Setup:** The framework formally distinguishes between CoT correctness ($I_{CoT}$) and answer correctness ($I_{Ans}$), and defines a binary, verifiable reward ($R(y_i) = I_{Ans}(a_i)$) based solely on the final answer.
- **Logic Prior Assumption:** A critical assumption is introduced: "compared with incorrect CoTs, correct CoTs have higher probabilities to induce correct answers." This means $P(I_{Ans}(a_i) = 1 | I_{CoT}(c_i) = 1) = \alpha > P(I_{Ans}(a_i) = 1 | I_{CoT}(c_i) = 0) = \beta$. This assumption leverages the inherent knowledge and logic priors established during pre-training.
- **Theorem 1:** The authors prove that, under this Logic Prior assumption, the expected GRPO advantage for correct CoTs is positive, and for incorrect CoTs is negative. Consequently, the GRPO policy gradient increases the probability of generating correct CoTs ($p_c$) monotonically.

#### 3.3. Analysis of RLVR Training Dynamics

To understand the emergence of improved reasoning, the authors reproduce a GRPO-style training using the open-source DAPO recipe (Yu et al., 2025) on the Qwen2.5-32B model with 17k mathematical problems.
- **Key Indicators:** They track per-prompt metrics: `P(CA)` (probability of correct answer) and `P(CC|CA)` (probability of correct CoT given a correct answer).
- **Optimization Effects:** They analyze how these indicators evolve over training steps for both easy and hard training questions.
- **Generalization Behaviors:** The generalization of reasoning improvements is assessed by evaluating Pass@K and CoT-Pass@K on unseen AIME test sets at different training checkpoints.

#### 3.4. Evaluation of CoT Quality from a Learning Perspective

The quality of CoTs generated at different RLVR stages is assessed by a learning-based approach.
- **Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** Multiple SFT procedures are performed, starting from the same base LLM (Qwen2.5-32B), but fine-tuned on CoT data generated by different models (base model, models at various RLVR checkpoints, and the final post-RLVR model).
- **Generalization as Proxy for Quality:** The generalization performance (Pass@1 and CoT-Pass@K) of these post-SFT models on test sets (AIME 2024, 2025) serves as a proxy for the quality of the underlying CoT training data. Better generalization implies higher quality CoTs. This method helps to confirm whether RLVR leads to fundamentally better reasoning steps.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings that collectively support the paper's central hypothesis: RLVR implicitly incentivizes and fundamentally enhances correct reasoning in base LLMs.

#### 4.1. Extended Reasoning Capability Boundary After RLVR

- **Math Reasoning (CoT-Pass@K):** When evaluated with the novel CoT-Pass@K metric, RLVR models (DAPO-Qwen-32B) demonstrate a **consistent and significant performance gap** over the base LLM (Qwen2.5-32B) on challenging math benchmarks like AIME 2024 and AIME 2025, even at large sampling numbers (K up to 1024). This directly refutes the hypothesis that RLVR merely improves sampling efficiency without extending reasoning capacity for math problems. The discrepancy with standard Pass@K results (where the base LLM often catches up) is attributed to the base LLM producing incorrect CoTs that coincidentally lead to correct answers, especially for hard questions with simple numerical answers. AIME 2025, being newer and thus less susceptible to data contamination, shows the most pronounced gap.
- **Code Reasoning (Pass@K):** For competitive coding tasks, where answer verification via execution is highly reliable, RLVR models (AceReason-Nemotron-7B and Skywork-OR1-7B) show **clear and persistent Pass@K improvements** over their distilled base counterparts (DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B) across various LiveCodeBench versions. This confirms that RLVR can extend reasoning boundaries in coding, particularly for medium and hard problems, beyond mere sampling efficiency.

#### 4.2. Theoretical Understanding: RLVR Implicitly Incentivizes Correct Reasoning

- **Theorem 1 Proof:** The theoretical framework demonstrates that under the "Logic Prior" assumption (where correct CoTs are more likely to lead to correct answers than incorrect ones), the GRPO algorithm's policy gradient *implicitly incentivizes the generation of correct CoTs*. Specifically, the expected advantage for correct CoTs is positive, while for incorrect CoTs it is negative, leading to a monotonic increase in the probability of generating correct CoTs ($p_c$) over training. This holds true even when the reward signal is solely based on binary answer correctness.
- **Mechanism Insight:** The driving factor is the positive gap ($\alpha - \beta$) between the probability of a correct CoT yielding a correct answer and an incorrect CoT yielding a correct answer. This highlights that LLMs' pre-trained knowledge and logic priors are crucial for RLVR's effectiveness.

#### 4.3. Training Dynamics of RLVR

- **Early Incentivization and Generalization:** Analysis of DAPO training dynamics reveals that RLVR begins to incentivize correct reasoning (`P(CC|CA)` improvements) from the early stages of training. These improvements in reasoning quality generalize well to unseen test questions from the very beginning of the optimization process, as evidenced by consistent gains in CoT-Pass@K on AIME 2024.
- **Optimization Effects:** For fully optimized training questions, while the probability of correct answers (`P(CA)`) approaches 1.0, a non-negligible portion of CoTs still remain imperfect (`P(CC|CA)` around 0.7). This indicates that while answers become consistently correct, some unexpected reasoning behaviors might still be learned and reinforced, highlighting a limitation of purely answer-based rewards.

#### 4.4. Quality of Reasoning CoTs Enhanced by RLVR

- **SFT Replication:** Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) experiments using CoTs generated at different RLVR stages demonstrate a significant improvement in CoT quality. SFT on CoT data from the final post-RLVR model (DAPO CoTs) can nearly **replicate the Pass@1 performance** of the computationally expensive RLVR-tuned model.
- **Fundamental Improvement:** The generalization performance of models trained on RLVR-generated CoTs consistently improves as RLVR training progresses, even for "incorrect" CoTs (i.e., those initially identified with errors by the LLM-judge but still leading to correct answers). This suggests that RLVR fundamentally optimizes the model's reasoning abilities, producing CoTs that are more accurate, coherent, and reliable, which cannot be directly sampled from base LLMs.

In summary, the paper provides compelling empirical and theoretical evidence that RLVR, when properly evaluated, extends the reasoning capabilities of LLMs beyond mere sampling efficiency, fundamentally improving the quality of their internal thought processes.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research carries substantial significance and holds several potential impacts on the field of Large Language Model development and our understanding of AI reasoning:

1. **Resolution of a Fundamental Debate:** The most immediate impact is the strong evidence and theoretical framework provided to settle the critical debate regarding RLVR's true effectiveness. By demonstrating that RLVR *can* fundamentally extend LLM reasoning capabilities and is not merely a sampling efficiency booster, the paper validates RLVR as a powerful paradigm for advancing LLM intelligence. This clarifies a contentious point in the community and offers a renewed mandate for investing in RLVR research.
2. **Paradigm Shift in Evaluation:** The introduction of `CoT-Pass@K` and the "LLM-as-a-CoT-Judge" approach is a significant methodological contribution. It highlights the inadequacy of relying solely on final answer correctness for complex reasoning tasks, especially in mathematics. This pushes for a more rigorous, process-oriented evaluation of LLMs, urging the community to develop and adopt metrics that verify intermediate reasoning steps. This could lead to a new generation of benchmarks and evaluation tools that better reflect true reasoning abilities.
3. **Deeper Mechanistic Understanding of RLVR:** The theoretical framework, particularly Theorem 1 and the "Logic Prior" assumption, provides crucial insights into *how* RLVR implicitly incentivizes correct reasoning. This moves beyond empirical observations to a mechanistic understanding, explaining why answer-level rewards can still shape internal reasoning processes. This understanding can guide the design of more efficient, targeted, and robust RLVR algorithms in the future.
4. **Implications for LLM Development and Alignment:**
 - **Enhanced Reasoning:** The confirmed ability of RLVR to extend reasoning boundaries suggests a pathway to more robust and less "guessing"-prone LLMs. This is crucial for applications requiring high-stakes, verifiable reasoning, such as scientific discovery, complex problem-solving, and safety-critical systems.
 - **Self-Correction and Learning from Experience:** The findings reinforce the vision of LLMs that learn through interaction and self-correction, as articulated by major AI labs. RLVR's capacity to refine reasoning paths aligns LLMs more closely with human cognitive processes of trial-and-error learning.
 - **Efficient Model Training:** The demonstration that SFT on RLVR-generated CoTs can nearly replicate the performance of an RLVR-tuned model is highly impactful. This suggests a potential for significantly reducing the computational cost of training advanced reasoning models, as generating high-quality CoT data through RLVR and then using SFT might be more efficient than continuous, expensive RL training.
5. **Future Research Directions:** The paper implicitly outlines several important avenues for future research:
 - **Live and Challenging Benchmarks:** The need for continually evolving, contamination-free benchmarks that genuinely test extended reasoning capabilities is emphasized.
 - **Lightweight and Reliable CoT Verifiers:** The reliance on an LLM-as-a-judge, while practical, also points to the need for more robust, possibly smaller, and more reliable verifiers for reasoning correctness.
 - **Novel RLVR Algorithms:** With a clearer understanding of how RLVR incentivizes reasoning, new algorithms can be designed to more directly and efficiently promote correct reasoning paths, mitigating observed limitations like the persistence of "imperfect CoTs" despite correct answers.
6. **Broader AI Impact:** By solidifying RLVR's role in cultivating genuine reasoning, this work contributes to the broader goal of Artificial General Intelligence (AGI). It suggests that scalable learning from experience, powered by verifiable feedback, is a cornerstone for LLMs to transcend mere pattern matching and achieve more profound, adaptable intelligence.

In conclusion, this research not only resolves a critical debate but also fundamentally reshapes our understanding of RLVR's potential, providing both empirical validation and theoretical grounding for its role in developing LLMs that are not just intelligent, but also capable of truly correct and verifiable reasoning.
