# Exploratory Memory-Augmented LLM Agent via Hybrid On- and Off-Policy Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Microsoft Research and KAIST developed EMPO², a hybrid reinforcement learning framework that enhances exploration in LLM agents by combining self-generated memory with on- and off-policy optimization. This approach led to a 128.6% performance gain over GRPO on ScienceWorld and improved adaptability to out-of-distribution tasks by an average of 136%, internalizing memory-derived insights into the model's parameters for robust inference without external memory.

## Problem

- Large Language Model (LLM) agents tend to exploit existing prior knowledge, leading to erroneous behaviors and poor performance in environments requiring systematic exploration of novel states.
- Current memory-augmented methods for LLM agents often exhibit performance saturation as they fail to capture the diversity needed for continuous improvement and generalization.
- Online reinforcement learning algorithms used with LLMs, such as GRPO, suffer from insufficient exploration, frequently converging to suboptimal solutions.

## Method

- Developed EMPO², a ==hybrid reinforcement learning== framework that integrates self-generated external memory with a dual-mode policy optimization strategy.
- The agent continuously generates and stores natural-language "self-guidance tips" based on past experiences, used to augment decision-making during rollouts.
- Implemented a two-phase update mechanism (on-policy and off-policy), where off-policy updates distill the benefits of memory-augmented exploration into the LLM's core parameters without requiring memory at inference.

## Results

- EMPO² significantly outperformed baselines, achieving a **128.6%** improvement over GRPO on ScienceWorld with an average return of 75.9, and a **76.9%** success rate on WebShop.
- The framework exhibited enhanced generalizability, adapting to out-of-distribution tasks with an average improvement of **136%** over GRPO in early adaptation steps.
- Ablation studies demonstrated that the integration of all three learning modes and the use of intrinsic rewards were critical for stable and superior learning performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A hybrid approach unifying parametric policy updates with non-parametric memory-augmented exploration leads to superior performance and adaptability for LLM agents.
> - Reward-guided knowledge distillation, performed via off-policy updates, effectively internalizes insights gained from memory-augmented exploration into the LLM's parameters.
> - Stochastic modes for both rollouts and updates, coupled with intrinsic rewards for state novelty, are essential for driving continuous exploration and robust learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated capabilities in reasoning, planning, and interacting with environments, positioning them as agents in various applications such as interactive decision-making, tool use, and embodied AI. The integration of LLMs with reinforcement learning (RL) allows these agents to adapt their behaviors based on experience and feedback, moving beyond static prompting or supervised fine-tuning (SFT). This paradigm represents an advancement in creating more dynamic and autonomous LLM-based systems.

However, a recognized limitation in current LLM-based agents is their tendency to exploit existing prior knowledge rather than engaging in systematic exploration. While RL frameworks inherently seek to balance exploration and exploitation, many LLM agent systems predominantly rely on their pretrained knowledge. This characteristic can hinder their performance in environments that necessitate the discovery of novel states or the active acquisition of new information, particularly when pre-existing knowledge is insufficient or misleading. Prior research has attempted to address this by incorporating external memory modules into LLMs, enabling the models to recall past experiences and correct previous failures. Approaches like Reflexion and REMEMBERER facilitate improvements in decision-making without direct parameter updates. However, these methods often exhibit performance saturation, as the collection of experiences with static parameters may not capture the diversity required for continuous improvement and long-term generalization.

This work contributes to the landscape by proposing a hybrid RL framework that aims to overcome the exploration bottleneck. It seeks to combine the benefits of external memory for enhancing exploration with parametric policy optimization. The objective is to enable LLM agents to both leverage past experiences for guidance and internalize this learned behavior into their model parameters, thereby fostering more robust and generalizable intelligence that is less reliant on external memory during inference. The paper positions its approach as a bridge between parameter-level optimization and memory-augmented reasoning, aiming for autonomous growth through the agent's self-improvement rather than extensive reliance on external data or large-scale closed-source models. It builds upon and extends online RL methods like Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) by integrating non-parametric memory updates into both on- and off-policy learning.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address the exploration bottleneck in Large Language Model (LLM) agents trained with reinforcement learning (RL). The authors identify that while LLMs possess rich prior knowledge, this knowledge often does not align with the specific rules or dynamics of novel environments. Consequently, agents relying solely on pretrained knowledge frequently exhibit erroneous behaviors and struggle in settings that demand the discovery of new states or active information acquisition through systematic exploration. Existing methods, including those that employ external memory, tend to saturate quickly because they do not adequately facilitate the broad, continuous exploration necessary for sustained improvement and generalization.

The core motivations and specific objectives for developing the Exploratory Memory-Augmented On- and Off-Policy Optimization (EMPO²) framework are as follows:

- **Enhance Exploration Capability**: The main motivation is to enable LLM agents to move beyond mere exploitation of prior knowledge and engage in more effective exploration. This involves encouraging the agent to take atypical or counterintuitive actions to discover novel states and acquire new information, which is crucial for adaptation in complex, unfamiliar environments.
- **Bridge Parametric and Non-Parametric Learning**: The research aims to unify parametric policy updates (via RL) with non-parametric memory updates (through interaction and self-reflection). The motivation is that non-parametric updates can complement and enhance the efficiency of parametric learning, providing a mechanism for short-term adaptation and guiding the long-term learning of the model's parameters.
- **Internalize Learned Knowledge**: A critical objective is to ensure that the benefits gained from memory-augmented exploration are not solely dependent on the external memory module. Instead, the framework seeks to internalize this acquired knowledge directly into the model's parameters, thereby reducing dependence on external memory during inference and promoting more generalizable intelligence. This is intended to allow the agent to perform robustly even in the absence of external memory.
- **Improve Learning Efficiency and Robustness**: By combining on-policy and off-policy updates, the authors aim to improve both the efficiency and stability of LLM agent training. On-policy updates are expected to contribute to stable learning, while off-policy updates are designed to facilitate the distillation of exploratory behaviors learned with memory into the core policy.
- **Overcome Limitations of Prior RL Methods for LLMs**: The paper highlights that current online RL algorithms, such as GRPO, struggle with insufficient exploration, leading to premature convergence to suboptimal solutions. For instance, in a ScienceWorld task, an agent might repeatedly fail to locate a required object because its policy does not effectively explore the environment, leading to stagnant performance. The motivation is to provide a mechanism that allows agents to analyze failure causes and broaden their action space for discovering successful strategies, thereby enabling continuous improvement.
- **Reduce Reliance on External Support**: Many prior studies align agents with environments using warm-start SFT, large closed-source models (like GPT-4), or human engineering. This work aims to reduce reliance on such external resources and emphasize autonomous growth through the agent’s own exploration and self-improvement, enhancing its ability to generalize to unseen scenarios without extensive external support.

In summary, the motivation is to develop a robust and adaptive LLM agent that can efficiently explore complex environments, internalize new knowledge, and generalize effectively to novel tasks without being perpetually reliant on external memory or extensive human-provided data.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed framework, Exploratory Memory-Augmented On- and Off-Policy Optimization (EMPO²), is a hybrid reinforcement learning algorithm designed to enhance exploration in LLM agents by combining parametric and non-parametric updates. The methodology integrates self-generated memory with a dual-mode policy optimization strategy.

#### 3.1. Advancing Exploration with Self-Generated Memory

A central tenet of EMPO² is the continuous maintenance and utilization of an external memory module, which provides continuity across rollouts.
- **Self-Generated Memory and Tips**: The policy `π_θ` itself is leveraged to review past rollouts and generate "self-guidance tips." These tips are natural-language reflections that summarize experiences, identify failures, and suggest alternative strategies. They are stored in a memory buffer `M = {tip_1, tip_2...}`. The process involves the policy taking the final state `s_t`, task `u`, and a specific tip-generation prompt as input to produce a tip (`tip_i ~ π_θ(s_t, u, tip-generation prompt)`). Unlike methods focused purely on iterative verbal guidance for higher rewards (e.g., Reflexion), EMPO²'s tips are designed to lead to enhanced exploration that is ultimately consolidated through parametric updates.

#### 3.2. Parameterize Non-Parametric Updates via Hybrid Policy Optimization

To ensure that the knowledge gained through memory-augmented exploration is internalized into the model's parameters, EMPO² introduces two distinct modes for both the rollout and update phases, leading to three hybrid learning configurations.

- **Rollout Modes**: During the rollout phase, the agent stochastically selects one of two modes at each step:
 1. **Prompting Without Memory (Probability `p`)**: The policy `π_θ` generates actions conditioned only on the current state `s_t` and the task `u` (`a_{t+1} ~ π_θ(· | s_t, u)`).
 2. **Memory-Augmented Prompting (Probability `1-p`)**: A retrieval operator `Retr(o_t; M)` selects the most relevant tips from `M` based on the current state `s_t` (e.g., via similarity search in embedding space). The policy then conditions its action on `s_t`, `u`, and the retrieved tips (`a_{t+1} ~ π_θ(· | s_t, u, tips_t)`). The number of retrieved tips is limited to 10.

- **Update Modes**: Trajectories generated under "Prompting Without Memory" are used for standard updates. For trajectories generated via "Memory-Augmented Prompting," one of two update modes is chosen randomly during the update phase:
 1. **On-Policy Updates (Probability `1-q`)**: The update uses the same prompt as in the rollout. The importance sampling ratio `ρ_θ(a_t)` is calculated as `π_θ(a_t | s_t, u, tips_t) / π_θ_old(a_t | s_t, u, tips_t)`, where both policies are conditioned on the retrieved tips.
 2. **Off-Policy Updates (Probability `q`)**: In this mode, the log-probabilities of actions (`ℓ_tips_t = log π_θ(a_t | s_t, u, tips_t)`) stored from the rollout are replaced with log-probabilities assigned by the *same policy* but conditioned *only* on the state and task (`ℓ_no-tips_t = log π_θ(a_t | s_t, u)`). This effectively performs a reward-guided knowledge distillation. High-advantage actions from memory-augmented rollouts are reinforced in the base policy (without tips), while low-advantage ones are suppressed. The importance sampling ratio `ρ_θ(a_t)` becomes `π_θ(a_t | s_t, u) / π_θ_old(a_t | s_t, u, tips_t)`. This mechanism allows the policy to internalize the benefits of tip conditioning without requiring tips at inference time.

- **Stabilizing Off-Policy Training**: To mitigate the instability often associated with off-policy training, a masking mechanism is introduced. This mechanism suppresses the advantage term for tokens whose probability under `π_θ` falls below a threshold `δ`, thereby preventing gradient magnitudes from diverging due to unbounded likelihood ratios. The GRPO loss function is modified to incorporate this mask.

- **Intrinsic Rewards for Exploration**: To further encourage exploration, an intrinsic reward `r_intrinsic = 1/n` is used, where `n` is the number of similar past states to the current novel state. A memory list stores distinct states, and a reward is assigned if a new state's cosine similarity with existing entries falls below a threshold. This mechanism promotes exploration of novel states even in the absence of extrinsic rewards and helps maintain policy entropy.

**Experimental Setup:**
- **Environments**: ScienceWorld (a text-based benchmark for science experiments requiring long-term planning and exploration) and WebShop (an HTML-based online shopping environment for multi-step navigation and purchasing).
- **Base Model**: Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct.
- **Baselines**: Compared against Naive prompting, Reflexion (non-parametric RL), Retrospex (offline RL), GRPO (online RL), and GiGPO (online RL, for WebShop).
- **Evaluation**: EMPO²'s performance is evaluated using the trained model *without memory* at test time, emphasizing internalized knowledge.
- **Training Details**: EMPO² implementation is based on the verl library, extending GRPO for multi-step interactions and incorporating memory and off-policy loss. Specific hyperparameters for learning rates, clipping, group sizes, and episode lengths are provided for each environment.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted on ScienceWorld and WebShop environments demonstrate that EMPO² generally achieves substantial improvements over established baselines, particularly in exploration and adaptability.

#### 4.1. ScienceWorld Results

- **Superior Performance**: EMPO² significantly outperformed all baselines in ScienceWorld tasks. It achieved an average return of 75.9, marking a 128.6% improvement over GRPO (33.2 average return).
- **Learning Dynamics**: Unlike GRPO, which often converged prematurely to suboptimal solutions (e.g., in the `power-component` task), EMPO² demonstrated continuous improvement, successfully solving tasks that GRPO struggled with (Figure 1a).
- **Task Success**: Among tasks that initially yielded negative rewards with the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct baseline, EMPO² enabled seven of them to reach the maximum score of 100, indicating effective problem-solving through enhanced exploration.
- **Comparison with Other RL Paradigms**: While offline RL (Retrospex) showed considerable gains over naive and non-parametric RL (Reflexion), it occasionally underperformed Reflexion in specific tasks. Online RL with GRPO generally performed comparably to offline RL but did not underperform non-parametric methods, suggesting better generalization to unseen task variants. EMPO² consistently surpassed these, showcasing higher learning performance.

#### 4.2. WebShop Results

- **Strongest Performance**: On the WebShop environment, EMPO² again demonstrated the strongest performance among all baselines. It achieved an average score of 88.3 and a success rate of 76.9%, surpassing GiGPO (86.2 score, 75.2% success), which itself is an improvement over GRPO. This indicates improved exploration leads to superior performance in web-based tasks.

#### 4.3. Adaptation in New Tasks (Out-of-Distribution - OOD)

- **Enhanced Generalizability**: EMPO² exhibited superior adaptability to novel situations compared to GRPO. When a model trained on one task was introduced to a new, unseen task, EMPO² (with memory enabled) adapted rapidly, showing an average improvement of 136% across three different OOD scenarios within 10 steps (Figure 8).
- **Robustness vs. Variability**: GRPO, by contrast, showed greater variability in OOD adaptation; while it achieved gains in some cases, it also failed to adapt in others, with performance sometimes dropping below the base model. This suggests that EMPO²'s acquired ability to explore through memory is generalizable to novel domains, even without parameter updates during testing.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies

- **Role of Mode Combinations**: Ablation studies confirmed the importance of integrating all three learning modes (on-policy without memory, on-policy with memory, and off-policy learning). Removing either off-policy learning or on-policy learning with memory resulted in suboptimal learning (Figure 9), indicating their complementary roles in providing stable learning and internalizing memory-guided reasoning.
- **Mode Selection Probability (`p` and `q`)**:
 - `p` (memory rollout probability): A low `p` (e.g., 0.1) caused performance degradation, essentially collapsing EMPO² to GRPO, thereby highlighting the necessity of memory in rollouts. A value of `p=0.25` provided stable convergence.
 - `q` (off-policy update probability): Extreme values of `q` (e.g., 0.3 or 0.95) resulted in suboptimal performance. While `q=0.85` showed faster early exploration than the default `q=2/3`, the framework demonstrated robustness across a reasonable range of hyperparameters, with default settings prioritizing overall robustness.
- **Role of Intrinsic Reward**: Varying the scale of the intrinsic reward primarily influenced the speed and stability of learning dynamics. However, completely removing the intrinsic reward caused learning to plateau at a lower level, underscoring its necessity in encouraging exploration and preventing policy collapse. EMPO² demonstrated robustness to the specific mechanism and scale of the intrinsic reward.

#### 4.5. Computational Cost Analysis

- **Memory Overhead**: The memory mechanism introduced an additional computational overhead of approximately 19% (50.4 seconds) per training iteration during the rollout phase, primarily from tip generation and storage (Figure 12).
- **Time-Performance Efficiency**: Despite the memory overhead, time-performance curves (Figure 13) showed that EMPO² achieved significantly higher efficiency than GRPO in terms of the time required to reach a specific performance level. The model's longer response length with tips, while increasing rollout time, was associated with enhanced reasoning and exploration.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents EMPO² as a framework that addresses a critical challenge in training Large Language Model (LLM) agents with reinforcement learning: the exploration bottleneck. The findings indicate several areas of significance and potential impact.

**Addressing a Core Limitation**: By proposing a hybrid approach that integrates non-parametric memory updates with both on- and off-policy optimization, EMPO² directly confronts the tendency of LLM agents to rely solely on prior knowledge rather than engaging in systematic exploration. This capability is crucial for LLMs to function effectively as agents in complex, open-ended environments where novel states and unknown dynamics are prevalent.

**Enhanced Learning Efficiency and Robustness**: The framework demonstrates a substantial improvement in training efficiency, evidenced by the 128.6% gain over GRPO on ScienceWorld and 11.3% on WebShop. This suggests that EMPO² can enable LLM agents to learn more effectively from interaction, achieving higher performance levels within a comparable number of training steps. The mechanism of reward-guided knowledge distillation, which internalizes memory-derived insights into the model's parameters, contributes to this efficiency and the robustness of the trained policy without requiring memory at test time.

**Improved Generalizability and Adaptability**: A notable finding is EMPO²'s superior adaptability to new, out-of-distribution tasks. The ability to leverage memory for rapid, few-shot adaptation to novel scenarios, even without further parameter updates, indicates that the framework fosters a more generalizable form of intelligence. This is a key step towards developing agents that are not rigidly tied to their training distributions but can learn and perform in unfamiliar contexts.

**Novel Hybrid Learning Paradigm**: EMPO² introduces a principled way to combine the strengths of parametric (policy optimization) and non-parametric (memory-based reflection) learning. This hybrid approach allows for the benefits of explicit reasoning and reflection to guide exploration, while simultaneously ensuring that these benefits are absorbed into the model’s core capabilities, leading to more inherent and transferable skills. The ablation studies confirm the complementary roles of the different modes, highlighting the value of their integrated design.

**Reduced Reliance on Extensive External Resources**: By enabling autonomous exploration and self-improvement, EMPO² moves towards reducing the reliance on vast amounts of golden trajectories, human engineering, or large closed-source models often used in previous LLM agent research. This could make the development and deployment of adaptive LLM agents more scalable and accessible.

**Future Research Directions**: The work also opens several avenues for future research.
- **Advanced Memory Mechanisms**: The current memory retrieval system is similarity-based. Exploring more sophisticated retrieval and organization mechanisms could further enhance performance.
- **Broader Model and Domain Applicability**: Extending EMPO² to a wider range of LLM architectures, sizes, and new domains (e.g., mathematics, coding, multi-hop question answering, multimodal RL) would provide deeper insights into its generality and scalability.
- **Alternative Off-Policy Techniques**: Investigating other off-policy techniques beyond importance sampling could lead to even more stable and efficient hybrid optimization strategies.

In summary, EMPO² represents a significant step towards developing more exploratory, adaptive, and generalizable LLM-based agents. Its ability to effectively combine and internalize knowledge from both direct experience and self-generated reflection has implications for building autonomous AI systems that can navigate and learn in complex, real-world environments.
