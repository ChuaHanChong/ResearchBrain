# LAWM-3D: Learning 3D-Aware Latent Actions from Human Videos for Generalizable Robot World Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LAWM-3D learns 3D-aware latent action representations from unlabeled multi-view human videos to enhance robot world models' generalization for embodied intelligence. The method demonstrates superior performance on out-of-distribution benchmarks and achieves improved action fidelity and spatial consistency in video generation.

## Problem

- Traditional world models require costly action annotations and struggle with diverse action spaces across robotic platforms.
- Existing Latent Action Models (LAMs) primarily operate on 2D pixel inputs, limiting their ability to capture explicit 3D motion and interaction semantics.
- Naive integration of multi-view data into LAMs is ineffective due to future-frame appearance leakage, inter-camera discrepancies, and task-irrelevant pixel information.

## Method

- **LAWM-3D** employs a two-stage training paradigm: large-scale human video pretraining followed by robot fine-tuning.
- It introduces a ==3D-aware Latent Action Model (LAM)== that uses a multi-view variational autoencoder with random view masking and pixel-agnostic learning via non-injective depth prediction.
- ==Geometric representation alignment== explicitly enforces cross-view consistent 3D structure learning by aligning intermediate encoder features with a pretrained 3D foundation model (VGGT).

## Results

- LAWM-3D achieved higher **PSNR** and **SSIM** scores for both RGB and depth prediction on Ego-Exo4D and Assembly101 datasets, outperforming established LAMs.
- The method demonstrated superior performance in action fidelity and 3D Accuracy on the WorldArena benchmark, surpassing existing world models in video generation quality.
- LAWM-3D consistently outperformed DreamDojo across all three out-of-distribution benchmarks, exhibiting stronger generalization with higher **PSNR**/**SSIM** and lower **LPIPS** scores.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicit geometric alignment and depth-based pixel-agnostic learning are crucial for training 3D-aware latent actions from heterogeneous multi-view human videos.
> - Naive multi-view integration does not inherently lead to 3D understanding in latent action models; specific architectural and loss designs are necessary.
> - Pretraining on diverse human video data, especially in combination with robot data, provides a better foundation for learning generalizable temporal representations for world models.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

World models provide a mechanism for agents to perform forward rollouts and planning without direct real-world interaction. However, their deployment in open-world embodied intelligence is often hindered by the substantial cost of action annotations and the inherent heterogeneity of action spaces across various robotic platforms. Recent advancements have introduced Latent Action Models (LAMs), which mitigate these issues by learning action representations from unlabeled human videos in a self-supervised manner.

Most existing LAMs primarily operate on single-view inputs and process information within a 2D pixel space. This 2D focus limits their ability to capture explicit 3D motion and interaction semantics, potentially affecting the performance of downstream world models in tasks requiring spatial understanding and physical consistency. While incorporating multi-view data is a logical step toward improving 3D understanding, the paper identifies that a naive integration strategy is insufficient. This is attributed to issues such as task-irrelevant information in pixel space, future-frame appearance leakage leading to shortcut learning, and the absence of explicit cross-view geometric constraints.

Within the broader landscape of 3D-aware world models, prior work has explored various techniques, including geometric constraints, 3D representations, panoramic representations, and alignment with pretrained 3D foundation models. However, existing LAMs that incorporate geometric awareness often do so under single-view settings (e.g., UniLACT) or rely on synthetic or strictly aligned multi-view data (e.g., MVP-LAM). LAWM-3D positions itself by addressing the challenge of learning 3D-aware latent actions from real-world human multi-view videos with inherent viewpoint heterogeneity, aiming to overcome the limitations of 2D-centric models and the inadequacies of naive multi-view integration.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method, termed LAWM-3D, for learning 3D-aware latent action representations from unlabeled human videos. This is intended to enhance the performance and generalization capabilities of robot world models in open-world embodied intelligence tasks.

The motivation for this work stems from several identified limitations in current world modeling paradigms:
- **High Cost and Scalability of Action Annotations:** Traditional world models often necessitate large-scale datasets with explicit action labels, which are expensive to collect and challenging to scale for diverse, open-world scenarios. This bottleneck restricts their applicability.
- **Heterogeneity of Robotic Platforms:** The diverse action spaces of different robotic platforms and the limited variety in current datasets impede the transferability of learned models to novel environments and tasks.
- **Limitations of Existing Latent Action Models (LAMs):** While LAMs have reduced reliance on explicit action annotations by learning representations from unlabeled videos, most operate on single-view inputs and primarily model 2D appearance changes. This limits their capacity to reason about true 3D motion and interactions, consequently impacting prediction and planning performance.
- **Ineffectiveness of Naive Multi-View Integration:** The authors empirically observe that simply incorporating multi-view videos into LAM training does not inherently endow learned latent actions with 3D-aware perception. This ineffectiveness is attributed to three main challenges:
 - **Future-frame Appearance Leakage:** When LAMs are trained with adjacent-frame objectives, the encoder can exploit future-frame RGB information, leading latent actions to degenerate into compressed appearance representations rather than capturing motion semantics.
 - **Inter-camera Appearance Discrepancies and Viewpoint Variations:** Multi-view observations are 2D projections, and discrepancies in appearance or viewpoint across cameras make it difficult for models to learn consistent 3D representations without explicit geometric guidance.
 - **Task-irrelevant Information in Pixel Space:** Performing latent action extraction directly in pixel space can cause the model to prioritize pixel reconstruction over the extraction of motion semantics, due to substantial task-irrelevant visual information.

To address these issues, the research proposes LAWM-3D with three coupled design principles aimed at overcoming the identified limitations and facilitating the learning of truly 3D-aware latent actions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

LAWM-3D is structured around a two-stage training paradigm: large-scale human video pretraining followed by robot fine-tuning. The overall framework comprises a latent action autoencoder and a conditional world model.

The training pipeline involves three stages:
1. **Latent Action Model (LAM) Training:** The LAM is trained on large-scale multi-view and monocular video data to extract view-invariant action representations.
2. **World Model Pretraining:** The world model is pretrained on human videos using the pseudo-action labels generated by the LAM to learn the dynamics mapping from actions to future observations.
3. **Robot Fine-tuning:** The pretrained world model is fine-tuned on real robot interaction data to align the learned latent actions with actual control signals, thereby enabling downstream control and planning tasks.

**3D-Aware Latent Action Model (LAM):**
The LAM is formulated as a multi-view variational autoencoder designed to learn view-invariant latent action representations.
- **Input and Architecture:** The model takes a set of synchronized observations from multiple camera views, F_t = {f^v_t}^V_{v=1}, as input, encompassing ego and exo views. Input frames are patched, tokenized, concatenated with action tokens, and augmented with view embeddings and rotary positional encodings. The encoder employs a spatio-temporal Transformer, and the decoder is a spatial Transformer. The objective follows a β-VAE formulation for future frame reconstruction.
- **Joint Training with Single and Multi-View Data:** A random view masking strategy is applied during training, ensuring at least one view is retained. For decoding, a random view from the retained subset is selected for reconstruction. This compels the encoder to infer a consistent latent action representation despite incomplete observations, enhancing robustness to viewpoint variations and enabling joint training on mixed multi-view and monocular datasets.
- **Pixel-Agnostic Learning via Depth Map:** To prevent shortcut learning from future-frame RGB information and bias the model toward motion cues, a non-injective depth prediction mechanism is introduced. The decoder, conditioned on the current frame and its depth map, jointly predicts {f_{t+1}, d_{t+1}}. Depth provides view-consistent geometric cues that are less sensitive to appearance variations, encouraging focus on geometrically meaningful dynamic regions.
- **Geometric Representation Alignment:** To explicitly enforce learning of cross-view consistent 3D structures, a geometry alignment constraint is introduced. This involves aligning intermediate spatial-semantic features of the encoder with those from a pretrained 3D foundation model (VGGT). A projection head maps encoder features to the VGGT feature space, and alignment is achieved through a cosine similarity loss (L_Angular) for directional alignment and an L2 loss (L_Scale) on normalized features for magnitude regression. The overall LAM loss is a weighted sum of the reconstruction loss and these alignment losses.

**World Model:**
- **Backbone and Pretraining:** Cosmos-Predict2.5 serves as the world model backbone. During pretraining, latent action representations extracted by the LAM from human videos are projected via a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) into the world model's timestep embedding space. These projected action embeddings are then added to the timestep embeddings and injected into the adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN) modules within each DiT block.
- **Post-training:** For fine-tuning on robot data, the action space is reformulated into a relative action space using pose increments. The first layer of the action MLP is reinitialized and fine-tuned alongside other pretrained parameters. In addition to the standard Cosmos training objective, a temporal consistency loss from DreamDojo is incorporated to enhance temporal coherence.

**Implementation Details:**
The LAM encoder and decoder each consist of 24 layers. Alignment with VGGT features occurs at layers 6 and 11. The latent action dimensionality is 32. Specific hyperparameters for β-VAE, angular and scale losses, and optimizers (AdamW) are detailed. Both LAM and world models are pretrained on H20 GPUs with specified batch sizes and iterations. The training datasets include Ego-Exo4D, Assembly101 (multi-view), EgoDex (single-view), and robot datasets like AgiBot-World, RT-1, Language-Table, and DROID for world model pretraining. A random view masking strategy is employed, and temporal downsampling is applied to vary motion intervals.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments conducted to evaluate LAWM-3D compared its performance against various baseline methods across multiple dimensions, including latent action representation quality, world model generation, generalization ability, and specific component contributions.

**Comparison of Latent Action Models (LAMs):**
- Quantitative evaluations on Ego-Exo4D and Assembly101 datasets showed that LAWM-3D consistently surpassed established LAMs such as LAPA, UniLACT, CoMo, DreamDojo, and a depth-augmented baseline (DreamDojo-D). LAWM-3D achieved higher PSNR and SSIM scores for both RGB and depth prediction.
- Visual comparisons indicated that LAWM-3D generated more accurate and spatially consistent depth predictions, suggesting an improved 3D scene understanding.
- Additional analyses in the appendix, including cross-view transferability and viewpoint invariance tests, mutual information estimation with ground-truth actions, and linear probing for action recoverability, further supported the robustness, effectiveness, and 3D awareness of the learned latent action representations. Mean Pooling was identified as the most effective strategy for aggregating multi-view action information.

**Video Quality Evaluation for World Models:**
- Evaluations on the WorldArena benchmark, comparing LAWM-3D against methods such as GigaWorld-0, Genie Envisioner, RoboMaster, Cosmos 2.5, DreamDojo, WoW, and IRASim, demonstrated LAWM-3D's superior performance.
- The method achieved competitive visual quality and significantly outperformed existing approaches in action fidelity. It exhibited particularly strong performance in metrics related to Motion Quality and 3D Accuracy across 16 dimensions of video generation quality. This indicates that 3D-aware latent action pretraining contributes to the world model's capabilities in spatial structure understanding and dynamic motion modeling.
- Qualitative visualizations of robotic manipulation tasks further illustrated the improved generation quality.

**Generalization Evaluation:**
- LAWM-3D was evaluated on three out-of-distribution (OOD) benchmarks (In-lab Eval, EgoDex Eval, DreamDojo-HV Eval) after post-training on a robot dataset.
- The method consistently outperformed DreamDojo across all OOD benchmarks, achieving higher PSNR and SSIM, and lower LPIPS scores. This indicates stronger robustness in unseen environments and complex interactions.
- Qualitative results showed improved action following and physical consistency, such as consistent object handover and stable block placement, attributed to the enhanced capture of spatial relationships, object states, and interaction dynamics by the 3D-aware latent action modeling.
- Long-horizon real-world rollout analysis demonstrated that LAWM-3D maintained higher visual quality and stronger temporal consistency over extended predictions compared to the baseline, exhibiting better action following and 3D understanding.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Component Analysis:**
 - Introducing multi-view data alone did not improve performance, and sometimes caused degradation, suggesting that naive integration is insufficient.
 - Geometric representation alignment consistently improved all metrics, even in a single-view setting, indicating its role in enhancing structural latent action representations.
 - Adding non-injective depth decoding further improved performance by providing additional geometric constraints.
 - The combination of multi-view data with geometric alignment yielded stronger results, demonstrating synergy.
 - The full LAWM-3D model achieved the best performance across all metrics, confirming the complementary nature of its components.
- **Dataset Ablation:**
 - All pretrained models surpassed the no-pretraining baseline.
 - Human-video pretraining provided greater performance gains than robot-only pretraining, suggesting that diverse human interaction dynamics aid in learning generalizable temporal representations.
 - Joint human-and-robot pretraining achieved the best overall performance and accelerated convergence during post-training, indicating strong complementarity between data sources.
- **Geometric Alignment Layer Analysis:** Aligning intermediate encoder layers (h =) with VGGT features yielded the best performance, indicating an optimal trade-off between capturing low-level appearance and high-level task-specific features.
- **Alignment Loss Weight Sensitivity:** The model demonstrated robustness to a range of angular and scale loss weights, with a moderate angular weight (0.5) and a small scale weight (0.05) proving effective and stable.
- **3D Foundation Model Comparison:** VGGT was identified as the most effective 3D foundation model for alignment, outperforming DINOv2, Depth-Anything-V2, and DUSt3R, due to its ability to jointly model geometry and multi-view correspondence.
- **World Model-based Policy Evaluation and Selection (Appendix):** The learned world model provided a reliable proxy for evaluating candidate policies (Spearman rank correlation coefficient ρ = 0.952) and enabled effective online policy selection, particularly for high-variance policy groups, improving success rates by combining multiple static checkpoints into an adaptive controller.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

LAWM-3D addresses several limitations inherent in current world models and latent action models (LAMs), particularly their reliance on 2D pixel space and the substantial cost of action annotations. By effectively learning 3D-aware, view-invariant latent action representations from unlabeled human videos, this work contributes to the advancement of embodied AI.

The primary significance lies in demonstrating that explicit 3D-awareness can be integrated into latent action learning, which substantially improves the capabilities of world models. The model's enhanced understanding of 3D spatial structures, object states, and interaction dynamics leads to more physically plausible, temporally consistent, and stable predictions, even over long horizons. This is critical for robust planning and control in complex real-world environments.

The method's ability to significantly improve generalization to unseen environments and interaction patterns, as evidenced by its performance on OOD benchmarks, suggests a path toward more adaptable and broadly applicable robot learning systems. By leveraging large-scale human video data, LAWM-3D reduces the dependency on expensive and scarce robot-specific interaction data, potentially accelerating the development and deployment of generalist robots.

Furthermore, the framework's utility in downstream tasks, such as reliable offline policy evaluation and adaptive online policy selection, demonstrates its practical impact on robot control. By enabling accurate assessment of candidate policies without physical execution and dynamic selection of actions based on imagined future states, LAWM-3D can contribute to more efficient and safer robot learning and deployment. The findings indicate that the proposed approach has the potential to foster the creation of more intelligent and autonomous embodied agents that can reason about and interact with the 3D world in a more sophisticated manner.
