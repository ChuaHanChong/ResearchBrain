# Beyond Markovian: Reflective Exploration via Bayes-Adaptive RL for LLM Reasoning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Northwestern University and Google DeepMind developed Bayes-Adaptive RL for LLM Reasoning (BARL), a framework that uses Bayesian reinforcement learning to enable principled reflective exploration in large language models. BARL achieved higher pass@1 accuracies on mathematical reasoning benchmarks while reducing token usage by up to 1.63x compared to progress baselines and 2x against GRPO.

## Problem

- Conventional Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies for Large Language Models (LLMs) are Markovian, which inherently prevents principled reflective exploration or adaptation based on past reasoning attempts.
- The observed self-reflection behavior in RL-trained LLMs lacked a formal theoretical explanation for its emergence, consistency, or benefits within existing frameworks.
- Standard RL approaches confine exploration to the training phase, failing to incentivize adaptive, information-gathering behaviors during test time.

## Method

- Introduced a ==Bayesian Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework== that models ==epistemic uncertainty== about the environment to formally ground reflective exploration in LLMs.
- Developed ==Bayes-Adaptive RL for LLM Reasoning (BARL)==, an algorithm that optimizes for Bayes-expected return by maintaining a posterior distribution over candidate MDP hypotheses.
- BARL provides explicit step-level guidance for LLMs to adapt strategies, using observed rewards to update beliefs and signal when to switch approaches through ==consistency-based penalties==.

## Results

- BARL consistently surpassed conventional RL baselines (GRPO and progress-reward) with higher pass@1 accuracies across various LLMs and challenging mathematical reasoning tasks.
- The method showed substantial token efficiency improvements, using up to **1.63x** fewer tokens than progress baselines and **2x** fewer than GRPO for superior performance.
- BARL exhibited improved generalization capabilities on synthetic problems, adaptively discovering optimal strategies, unlike conventional RL which merely memorized training solutions.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Optimal policies derived from conventional Reinforcement Learning are Markovian, preventing principled reflective exploration and often leading to memorized solutions that generalize poorly.
> - Bayesian Reinforcement Learning naturally yields uncertainty-adaptive policies that are inherently incentivized to perform information-gathering actions, thereby inducing genuine reflective exploration.
> - Effective self-reflection in LLMs is tied to the quality and efficiency of token usage for exploration, rather than a mere high frequency of reflection-related phrases.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This paper addresses a crucial and emerging area at the intersection of Large Language Models (LLMs) and Reinforcement Learning (RL), specifically focusing on the mechanism and benefits of "reflective exploration" or self-reflection in LLM reasoning.

**Evolution of LLM Reasoning:** The landscape of LLM reasoning has rapidly evolved. Initial progress was driven by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, enabling LLMs to break down complex problems into intermediate steps. This was further enhanced by supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on human-annotated data or linearized search traces. More recently, Reinforcement Learning (RL) has gained prominence in refining LLM reasoning abilities, particularly through optimizing for verifiable outcomes. RL-trained models have shown emergent behaviors like self-reflection, where models revisit previous steps to correct errors, suggesting a path for scaling test-time performance by generating longer, more deliberate CoTs.

**The Gap in Understanding Reflection:** Despite these compelling observations, the *underlying mechanisms* behind why and under what conditions self-reflection emerges or proves beneficial through conventional RL training remained largely unclear. Previous work, including, even suggests that reflections do not consistently emerge and show weak correlation with performance, indicating a gap between observed phenomena and principled understanding. Some related work, like, explores reflective exploration from a meta-RL perspective or using progress rewards to reduce regret, but the current paper explicitly differentiates itself by grounding reflective reasoning in *environment rewards* and optimizing a Bayesian objective directly during RL fine-tuning, rather than relying solely on internal CoT states or external algorithmic scaffolding.

**Limitations of Conventional RL:** The paper highlights a fundamental limitation of conventional RL in this context: standard RL algorithms yield Markovian optimal policies. A Markovian policy depends only on the current state, not the full history. This means it has no inherent incentive to revisit an identical state and behave differently based on additional context or past outcomes. Exploration in conventional RL is typically confined to the training phase (e.g., epsilon-greedy exploration) to learn the optimal policy through trial-and-error. Once deployed, these policies operate in a purely exploitative, fixed manner, offering no principled mechanism for adaptive exploration or self-reflection at test time.

**Introduction of Bayesian RL:** This work positions itself by introducing Bayesian RL as a principled framework to address these limitations. Bayesian RL inherently deals with uncertainty about the environment (the MDP). By maintaining a posterior distribution over possible MDPs and optimizing for expected return under this uncertainty, Bayesian RL policies are *uncertainty-adaptive*. They are incentivized to perform information-gathering actions that reduce this uncertainty, which naturally leads to behaviors like revisiting states and switching strategies based on new information—precisely what "reflective exploration" entails. Previous work has brought Bayesian principles to LLMs, for example, using Posterior Sampling for RL (PSRL) or active query selection for reward models, but this paper focuses on *optimizing a Bayesian objective directly within the LLM's policy fine-tuning*.

In essence, this paper provides a much-needed theoretical and algorithmic bridge to explain and effectively harness emergent reflective behaviors in LLMs, moving "beyond Markovian" policies to genuinely adaptive ones.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation for this research stems from the observed yet unexplained phenomenon of self-reflection in RL-trained Large Language Models (LLMs). While LLMs have showcased impressive reasoning abilities, often enhanced by Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting and Reinforcement Learning (RL) fine-tuning, the emergent behavior of "self-reflection"—revisiting prior states to correct mistakes or refine strategies—lacks a foundational understanding within conventional RL paradigms.

The paper identifies several key challenges and ambiguities:
1. **Lack of Principled Explanation:** Conventional RL policies are Markovian, meaning their actions depend solely on the current state, not the entire history or accumulated knowledge. This fundamental characteristic offers no inherent mechanism or incentive for an LLM to "reflect" on past attempts, gather additional context, and alter its behavior upon revisiting a seemingly identical state. The "why" and "when" of self-reflection remain opaque under this framework.
2. **Confined Exploration:** In conventional RL, exploration is primarily a training-time phenomenon, employed through trial-and-error to discover an optimal policy. Once the policy is trained and deployed, it typically operates in a purely exploitative manner, with no built-in incentives for further exploration or adaptive behavior in uncertain or novel situations. This implies that any observed test-time self-reflection might be an unprincipled artifact rather than a beneficial, goal-directed behavior.
3. **Unclear Benefits:** It is not definitively established whether self-reflection consistently emerges, or if it is always beneficial for LLM reasoning. Empirical findings in prior work have been mixed, with some suggesting a weak correlation between reflection frequency and performance.

To address these critical gaps, the key objectives of this research are:
- **Provide a Principled Basis:** To formally ground the concept of "reflective exploration" within a robust theoretical framework, explaining *why* and *when* LLMs should engage in such behaviors. This involves defining reflective exploration precisely, linking it to uncertainty and information gathering.
- **Recast within Bayesian RL:** To leverage the Bayesian Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework, which inherently models and reduces epistemic uncertainty about the environment (Markov Decision Process, MDP). The hypothesis is that this framework will naturally incentivize uncertainty-adaptive policies that perform information-gathering actions, thus leading to self-reflection.
- **Develop a Practical Algorithm:** To translate this theoretical foundation into a practical and efficient algorithm, named Bayes-Adaptive RL for LLM Reasoning (BARL). BARL aims to provide step-level guidance to LLMs on *how* to stitch and switch strategies based on observed outcomes, explicitly encouraging self-reflection.
- **Demonstrate Empirical Superiority:** To empirically validate the proposed BARL algorithm on both synthetic tasks (designed to highlight generalization benefits) and challenging real-world mathematical reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating its superiority over conventional RL approaches in terms of performance and efficiency.

In essence, the paper seeks to demystify and harness the power of LLM self-reflection by embedding it within a mathematically sound and practically implementable Bayesian RL paradigm, thereby improving LLM reasoning capabilities and generalization.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a novel approach to induce and formalize reflective exploration in LLMs by grounding it in a Bayesian Reinforcement Learning (RL) framework.

#### 3.1. Problem Formulation

The problem is set within a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP) where the initial state is the prompt, and actions are steps of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) reasoning. A crucial distinction is made between the training and deployment phases. During training, the agent interacts with the true MDP (M*) and collects data (D). However, this data does not fully specify M*, leading to *epistemic uncertainty*. This uncertainty is modeled as a posterior distribution over MDPs, `p(M|D)`. The objective shifts from maximizing return in a known MDP (conventional RL) to maximizing the *expected return* under this posterior distribution during deployment: `J(π) = E_M~p(M|D)[J_M(π)]`. This "Bayes-expected return" objective is central to the framework.

#### 3.2. Reward Structure

The authors employ a combination of outcome-level and progress rewards. The outcome reward is a sparse 0/1 signal indicating correctness. The progress reward is defined as the increase in the model's probability of outputting the correct answer `y*s0` after generating a reasoning step `a_t` at state `s_t`. This is computationally efficient as it avoids multiple branched rollouts at each step and reuses KV cache.

#### 3.3. Formalizing Reflective Exploration

The paper formally defines reflective exploration through the lens of **uncertainty-adaptive policies**.
- **Belief:** The core concept is the belief `b(h_t)(M):= p(M | h_t, D)`, representing the posterior over MDPs given the observed history `h_t` and training data `D`.
- **Uncertainty-Adaptive Policy:** A policy `π` is uncertainty-adaptive if its action distribution depends not just on the current state `s(h_t)` but also on the belief `b(h_t)`.
- **Strictly Uncertainty-Adaptive Policy:** If, for two different histories `h_t` and `h'_t` leading to the *same state* `s(h_t) = s(h'_t)`, the policy prescribes *different action distributions* because their beliefs `b(h_t)` and `b(h'_t)` differ, then it is strictly uncertainty-adaptive.
- **Reflective Exploration:** This behavior is defined as returning to the same underlying latent state `φ(s(h_t1)) = φ(s(h_t2))` but taking different actions `π(· | h_t1) ≠ π(· | h_t2)` due to updated learning from past rewards.

#### 3.4. The Necessity of Bayesian RL (Theoretical Contributions)

The paper provides key theoretical results that justify the Bayesian RL approach:
- **Theorem 4.1 (RL Admits Markovian Optimal Policy):** Conventional RL objectives admit optimal policies that are Markovian (not strictly uncertainty-adaptive). Such policies do not induce reflective exploration because they have no incentive to enrich identical states with additional context. Exploration in conventional RL is confined to training; at deployment, it's pure exploitation.
- **Theorem 4.2 (Bayesian RL Admits Adaptive Optimal Policy):** The Bayesian RL objective (3.1) leads to optimal policies that are uncertainty-adaptive, naturally inducing reflective exploration. This is because the policy needs to update its belief about the MDP from cumulative observations, incentivizing information gathering.
- **Theorem 4.3 (Gap Between Markovian and Adaptive Policies):** There exist scenarios where an optimal strictly uncertainty-adaptive policy can achieve arbitrarily higher Bayes-expected return than any optimal Markovian policy. A didactic example using a binary decision tree illustrates how belief updates can lead to guaranteed reward, whereas a Markovian policy offers only a probabilistic chance.

#### 3.5. BARL Algorithm: Bayes-Adaptive RL for LLM Reasoning

To optimize the Bayesian RL objective, the authors derive a policy gradient (Eq 5.1) that replaces the conventional state-action value with a posterior-weighted value. The practical implementation of BARL involves:
1. **Hypothesis Generation:** For each prompt, the LLM performs online rollouts to generate a set of `|M|` candidate answers, each forming an "MDP hypothesis" `M_i`. The ground-truth MDP `M_0` is also included.
2. **Belief Update & Value Calculation:** At each timestep `t`, the algorithm computes the posterior-weighted state-action value (Eq 5.4). This involves:
 - `Q_π_θ_Mi(h_t, a_t)`: The value function for each hypothesis `M_i`.
 - `π_θ(y_Mi_s0 | s_t + </think>)`: Model's state-conditional belief in the plausibility of `M_i` (how likely the LLM thinks `M_i`'s answer is correct).
 - `∏ exp(-β |r_t' - r_Mi(s_t', a_t')|)`: A product term that measures the consistency between the *actual observed rewards* `r_t'` and the *predicted rewards under hypothesis M_i*. This term acts as a crucial "reflective signal": large discrepancies downweight hypotheses that are inconsistent with observations, signaling when to "switch strategies" by eliminating less plausible hypotheses.
3. **Policy Update:** The policy `π_θ` is then updated using the derived gradient, encouraging actions that maximize this posterior-weighted value.

BARL effectively stitches together plausible strategies and provides explicit guidance on when and how to reflectively explore by maintaining and updating beliefs over MDP hypotheses based on observed rewards.

#### 3.6. Didactic Example

A synthetic task is used to demonstrate BARL's capabilities. The agent must repeat a prompt token three times to get a reward. Training uses tokens 0 or 1, while evaluation uses token 2 (unseen during training). Conventional RL memorizes 000 or 111 and fails on 222. BARL, by treating potential correct triplets as hypotheses, learns to eliminate hypotheses (e.g., if 000 doesn't yield a reward, it's not the correct strategy) and thus adaptively discovers the 222 strategy for the evaluation task.

#### 3.7. Experimental Setup

BARL is evaluated on mathematical reasoning tasks using Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama-8B models. Training is on the Big-Math dataset, and evaluation on GSM8K, MATH, CollegeMath, and OlympiadBench. It is compared against GRPO (a strong outcome-reward baseline) and a progress-reward baseline (a variant of MRT).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The empirical evaluation of BARL on both synthetic and real-world mathematical reasoning tasks yielded several significant findings:

#### 4.1. Synthetic Task (Didactic Example)

- **Generalization through Reflection:** On the synthetic task (repeating a specific token three times), conventional RL (REINFORCE) quickly memorized the training solutions (e.g., repeating '0' or '1' three times) but entirely failed to generalize to an unseen prompt token (e.g., '2').
- **BARL's Adaptive Strategy:** In contrast, BARL significantly increased evaluation accuracies. It demonstrated the ability to "switch strategies" by eliminating hypotheses inconsistent with observed (zero) rewards, eventually discovering the ground-truth MDP for optimal behavior. The accuracy and convergence rate further improved when BARL was given prior knowledge about the structure of rewarding patterns, emphasizing the importance of informed candidate sets that balance diversity and plausibility.

#### 4.2. Mathematical Reasoning Tasks (Qwen2.5-Math and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Llama models)

- **Superior Performance:** Across various LLMs (Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, R1-Distill-Llama-8B) and challenging math benchmarks (GSM8K, MATH, CollegeMath, OlympiadBench, AIME, AMC), BARL consistently achieved higher pass@1 accuracies compared to conventional RL algorithms (GRPO and the progress-reward baseline). The gains were particularly notable on benchmarks demanding effective exploration. For instance, BARL achieved an average accuracy of 53.3% for Qwen-1.5B, surpassing GRPO (51.0%) and Progress (51.4%). Similar improvements were seen for Qwen-7B (BARL 59.4% vs GRPO 57.1% vs Progress 57.9%) and Llama-8B (BARL 52.7% vs GRPO 52.1% vs Progress 51.0%).
- **Enhanced Token Efficiency:** BARL demonstrated significantly greater token efficiency. It achieved higher accuracies using substantially fewer average tokens. On Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, BARL required up to 1.63x fewer tokens than the progress baseline, 2x fewer than GRPO, and over 10x fewer than the base model, while achieving superior performance. This suggests that BARL's thinking process is more targeted and effective.
- **Effective vs. Frequent Reflections:** An ablation study on reflective reasoning behaviors revealed that while the base LLM often exhibited a higher *frequency* of keyword-detected self-reflections, BARL's reflections were more *effective*. This indicates that base models might acquire superficial stylistic reflection patterns during pre-training without genuinely productive exploration. There was no strong correlation observed between overall performance and the mere frequency of reflections. Instead, the *quality* and *efficiency* of exploration tokens were the determining factors.
- **Higher Bayesian Values of CoTs:** Analyzing the effectiveness of CoTs by calculating average Bayesian state-action values at each timestep, BARL consistently showed higher values than GRPO and base models. This metric inherently captures both exploitation (expected returns) and exploration (value of information gained through belief updates). This finding directly supports the claim that BARL's actions lead to more effective exploration and exploitation, gathering informative contexts to reduce MDP uncertainty.
- **Markovian RL Optimality and Memorization:** An ablation with a length-controlled GRPO (LC-GRPO) showed that it rapidly learned to skip CoT generation and directly emit final answers during training, achieving high accuracy by merely memorizing solutions. However, this policy generalized poorly during evaluation. This finding provides empirical support for Theorem 4.1, illustrating that conventional Markovian RL can achieve optimality by memorizing solutions without developing reflective reasoning or generalization capabilities.

In summary, the results strongly affirm that BARL's Bayesian RL framework successfully induces principled, effective, and token-efficient reflective exploration, leading to improved generalization and superior performance on complex reasoning tasks compared to conventional RL approaches.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in understanding and enhancing the reasoning capabilities of Large Language Models, particularly concerning the emergent behavior of self-reflection. Its impact can be discussed across theoretical, algorithmic, and practical dimensions.

#### 5.1. Theoretical Significance

- **Principled Foundation for Reflection:** The most profound contribution is providing a rigorous theoretical framework—Bayesian Reinforcement Learning—to explain *why* and *when* LLMs should engage in reflective exploration. This moves beyond treating self-reflection as an ad-hoc emergent property or stylistic pattern, grounding it in the fundamental need to reduce epistemic uncertainty about the environment.
- **Addressing Markovian Limitations:** By formally demonstrating that conventional RL's Markovian policies are inherently incapable of principled reflective exploration, the paper highlights a critical limitation in existing LLM fine-tuning paradigms. This theoretical clarity is essential for guiding future research.
- **Bridging Theory and Practice:** The work successfully bridges advanced RL theory (Bayes-Adaptive RL, epistemic POMDPs) with practical LLM development, offering a concrete formulation for how beliefs, uncertainty, and information gathering can be integrated into LLM policies.

#### 5.2. Algorithmic Advancements

- **Introduction of BARL:** The proposed Bayes-Adaptive RL for LLM Reasoning (BARL) algorithm provides a practical, computationally efficient mechanism for LLMs to perform reflective exploration. Its method of maintaining a posterior over MDP hypotheses and using consistency-based penalties to signal strategy switching is novel and effective.
- **Efficient Exploration:** BARL's ability to achieve superior performance with significantly fewer tokens indicates a more efficient and targeted exploration strategy. This is a crucial advancement for managing the computational costs associated with LLM reasoning, where longer CoTs can be prohibitively expensive.
- **Guidance on Adaptive Policies:** BARL offers explicit guidance on *how* LLMs should adapt on-the-fly, revising plausible strategies based on observed outcomes, rather than simply generating varied responses.

#### 5.3. Practical Impact and Future Directions

- **More Robust and Generalizable LLMs:** By enabling LLMs to learn and utilize reflective exploration, BARL enhances their ability to generalize to novel problems and handle uncertainty more effectively. This is vital for deploying LLMs in real-world scenarios where unseen situations or subtle variations from training data are common.
- **Improved Mathematical and Complex Reasoning:** The demonstrated gains on challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks suggest that BARL can significantly improve LLMs' performance in domains requiring iterative problem-solving, error correction, and strategic thinking.
- **Foundation for Autonomous LLM Agents:** The framework of uncertainty-adaptive policies and belief updates is highly relevant for developing more autonomous and intelligent LLM agents. Such agents could dynamically adjust their reasoning depth and strategies based on their confidence and the complexity of the task, leading to more robust and human-like problem-solving.
- **Reduced Inference Costs:** The improved token efficiency offered by BARL translates directly into lower computational costs for LLM inference, making high-quality reasoning more accessible and scalable.
- **Inspiration for Future Research:** This work opens several avenues for future exploration, including:
 - Developing more sophisticated and computationally efficient methods for generating and maintaining MDP hypotheses (candidate sets).
 - Exploring different forms of reward functions and belief update mechanisms within the Bayesian RL framework.
 - Applying BARL to a broader range of reasoning tasks and agentic behaviors, beyond mathematical problems.
 - Investigating the interplay between BARL's principled reflective exploration and other advanced LLM techniques like tree-of-thought or self-correction.

In conclusion, "Beyond Markovian" provides a foundational understanding and an effective algorithmic solution for integrating principled reflective exploration into LLMs. This has the potential to elevate LLM reasoning capabilities, making them more adaptive, efficient, and robust across complex problem domains.
