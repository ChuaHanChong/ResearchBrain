# JAMB: Joint Action-Motion Diffusion for Bimanual Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> JAMB is a diffusion policy for bimanual manipulation that denoises robot action chunks and future 3D point tracks together inside one Diffusion Transformer with full self-attention, grounding visual, state, track and action tokens in a shared world-space and trajectory-time frame via 4D rotary positional encoding. Across 16 RoboTwin 2.0 tasks it reaches 83.4% success, 23.9 points over the best baseline, and 85.6% on three real robot tasks.

## Problem

- Most diffusion policies only reconstruct demonstrated actions from current observations, never linking action generation to the resulting object motion or shared scene change.
- Future-prediction variants use pixels, video latents or dense 3D geometry as auxiliary supervision or fixed conditioning, spending capacity on appearance and static task-irrelevant regions.
- Existing track-and-action methods keep tracks in 2D image space, missing metric depth and 3D displacement needed to reason about relative geometry between two arms.

## Method

- **Joint action-motion diffusion**: a DiT denoises noisy bimanual ==action chunks== and patch-aligned ==3D track displacements== in one full-self-attention sequence, so both hypotheses refine each other at every step.
- **4D rotary positional encoding** grounds fused DINOv2 visual-track tokens, arm-state tokens and action tokens in shared world position plus trajectory time; action anchors interpolate from current end-effector positions as noise drops.
- **Motion-weighted track loss** applies a sigmoid weight on final metric displacement to downweight near-static background queries, added to ==action MSE==; real supervision comes from CoTracker3 tracks lifted by FoundationStereo depth.

## Results

- **85.2%** on 8 Sync-bimanual and **81.6%** on 8 Seq-coordinate RoboTwin tasks, **83.4%** overall, **23.9 points** above strongest baseline DICP; real-world **85.6%** versus **64.4%** GAP and **35.6%** DP3.
- Ablation over eight tasks: action-only **61.4%**, track regression **73.9%**, track-conditioned action diffusion **76.3%**, full joint denoising **81.7%**; removing 4D RoPE drops success to **74.1%**.
- Joint denoising cuts track error to **7.6/10.0 mm** ADE/FDE from **9.9/12.5**; easy-to-hard transfer hits **17.9%** versus **4.3%** GAP, beating 2D tracks **9.3%** and endpoint geometry **10.3%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Coupling future prediction as a co-diffused variable beats auxiliary supervision or fixed conditioning: mutual refinement improves action success and prediction accuracy together.
> - Sparse full-horizon 3D motion tracks make a better intermediate representation than dense endpoint geometry or 2D tracks, especially under visual distribution shift.
> - Shared spatiotemporal positional grounding across modalities is load-bearing, not cosmetic; noisy action anchors need schedule-dependent interpolation to be usable early in denoising.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

JAMB addresses a practical problem in two-arm manipulation: one arm can move a shared object and change what the other arm can do. A policy that predicts only commands, or only a final scene, has less explicit information about how the shared scene should move during the task. JAMB lets the arms' action plan and 3D motion predictions develop together, so each can inform the other while both are being denoised. Its 3D tracks describe where sampled scene points move over a future horizon, rather than only what the endpoint looks like.

In simulation, the approach performs strongly. Across RoboTwin 2.0's 16 Sync-bimanual and Seq-coordinate tasks, JAMB's mean task success rate is 83.4%. The abstract reports a 23.9-percentage-point advantage over the strongest baseline. Success rate is the percentage of rollouts that complete a task, averaged over tasks and evaluation seeds. Each simulation task uses 100 demonstrations, three seeds, and 50 rollouts per seed.

### 2. Letting the plan and the motion evolve together

A 3D point track is the future displacement of a sampled scene point from its current position over the prediction horizon. The model starts with one query per visual patch. It lifts the patch center into 3D using depth and camera geometry and predicts that point's future track.

A shared Transformer receives four inputs: the current RGB patch features, arm proprioception, noisy bimanual action chunks, and noisy tracks. Full self-attention lets action and track hypotheses refine each other at every denoising step. Only the actions are executed. The tracks are internal predictions, not future observations supplied at test time.

Positions enter attention through 4D RoPE, a rotary positional encoding that represents each grounded token's world-space position (x, y, z) and physical trajectory-time index. Visual and track tokens are anchored at current patch positions, and arm-state tokens at current end-effector positions. Action-token anchors move from current end-effector positions toward positions implied by the noisy action as denoising proceeds.

### 3. Testing the coupling itself

A controlled ablation on eight RoboTwin tasks (four Sync-bimanual and four Seq-coordinate) tests whether joint refinement matters beyond merely predicting the future. Each task uses three seeds and 50 rollouts per seed. The variants share the visual encoder, action representation, training data, and DiT capacity whenever possible.

The variants differ in how tracks enter the model:

- **Track regression** adds future-motion supervision but does not maintain an evolving track diffusion state.
- **Track-conditioned action diffusion** predicts tracks first and holds them fixed during action denoising.
- **The full model** jointly denoises both.

Average task success rates are 61.4% for action-only, 73.9% for track regression, 76.3% for fixed-track conditioning, and 81.7% for joint denoising. This supports the authors' interpretation that coupling adds value in this setting, but it does not prove coupling is the only cause of the full model's gains. Separately, removing 4D RoPE reduces average success from 81.7% to 74.1% in the same ablation suite.

### 4. Contribution and limits

Representing the path of shared-scene motion alongside the action plan may provide useful coordination information beyond an endpoint scene description. The results are evidence for this approach in the evaluated tasks. They do not show that 3D tracks are universally necessary or that track accuracy alone causes higher task success. The method uses a single camera view and a fixed grid of track queries, and real-world training also relies on point tracking and stereo-depth estimation.
