# Taming Preference Mode Collapse via Directional Decoupling Alignment in Diffusion Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Alibaba Group introduced Directional Decoupling Alignment (D²-Align) to address "Preference Mode Collapse" (PMC), a diversity degradation in text-to-image diffusion models aligned with human preferences. The method, combined with the new DivGenBench for evaluation, successfully enhances both human preference alignment and generative diversity, outperforming baselines in quality metrics and showing significant improvements in diversity scores.

## Problem

- Text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models aligned with Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) often suffer from "Preference Mode Collapse" (PMC), where generative diversity degrades despite high reward scores.
- Existing RLHF methods struggle with a trade-off, either achieving high preference scores at the cost of diversity or vice versa.
- There is a lack of standardized, computationally efficient quantitative metrics for evaluating generative diversity in T2I alignment, and current reward hacking mitigations do not address inherent directional biases in reward models.

## Method

- Introduces ==Directional Decoupling== Alignment (D²-Align), a two-stage framework that learns a continuous directional vector to correct the reward signal, counteracting intrinsic biases of the reward model.
- The first stage freezes the generator and optimizes a learnable vector within the reward model's embedding space to minimize a 'guided reward' loss.
- The second stage then optimizes the generator using this learned, corrected reward signal to achieve genuine human preference alignment while preserving generative diversity, supported by the new DivGenBench for diversity evaluation.

## Results

- D²-Align achieved superior performance in human preference alignment and semantic consistency, scoring highest across multiple quality metrics (e.g., Aesthetic **6.671**, ImageReward **1.762**) compared to baselines.
- The method effectively mitigated PMC, demonstrating the highest generative diversity scores across all DivGenBench metrics (lowest Identity Divergence Score, highest Artistic Style Coverage, Spatial Dispersion Index, and Photographic Variance Score).
- Human evaluations confirmed D²-Align's perceptual superiority in image quality and its ability to preserve diverse identities, styles, layouts, and tones, securing the highest preference rates in user studies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preference Mode Collapse (PMC) is a distinct form of reward hacking in T2I models, causing severe diversity degradation due to over-optimization on reward model biases.
> - By directionally correcting the reward signal in a continuous embedding space, it is possible to achieve both high human preference alignment and generative diversity simultaneously.
> - Quantitative assessment of generative diversity requires a specialized benchmark (DivGenBench) with dimension-customized metrics for aspects like identity, style, layout, and tonal variations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative models, particularly text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models, has made substantial advancements in creating high-fidelity and diverse visual content. A key direction in enhancing these models is aligning their outputs with human preferences, often achieved through Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF). This approach typically involves training models to maximize a predefined reward score, which reflects human aesthetic judgments or prompt adherence.

However, a recurring challenge in RLHF for generative models is "reward hacking." While prior research has made progress in mitigating the most overt forms of reward hacking, such as outright quality degradation (e.g., Flow-GRPO, DanceGRPO), a less explored but significant issue persists: the tendency of models to converge on a narrow set of high-scoring outputs. This phenomenon, which the authors term "Preference Mode Collapse (PMC)," results in a severe degradation of generative diversity, even when individual outputs score highly on automated reward metrics. Existing methods often struggle with a trade-off between achieving high preference scores and maintaining output diversity. Current evaluation benchmarks largely prioritize fidelity and semantic consistency, lacking comprehensive quantitative metrics specifically designed to assess diversity in the context of reward optimization. Furthermore, methods to counteract reward hacking have largely focused on modulating the magnitude of reward signals, rather than addressing the inherent directional biases within the reward models themselves.

This work addresses these gaps by introducing and quantifying PMC as a specific form of reward hacking that impacts diversity. It proposes a novel framework that aims to break the observed trade-off, enabling models to achieve both high human preference alignment and generative diversity. By developing a dedicated benchmark for diversity evaluation and a methodological approach that corrects reward signals directionally, the research contributes to a more robust and human-aligned development of T2I generative models.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to identify, quantify, and mitigate "Preference Mode Collapse (PMC)" in text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models that are aligned with human preference using Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF).

The motivation for pursuing this objective stems from several critical observations and challenges in the current landscape of T2I generation and RLHF:

- **Prevalence of Preference Mode Collapse (PMC):** Existing RLHF methods, while successful in achieving high scores on automated reward metrics, frequently lead to a severe loss of generative diversity. Models tend to converge on narrow, high-scoring patterns, producing homogeneous outputs (e.g., monolithic styles, pervasive overexposure) across varied prompts. This phenomenon, termed PMC, represents a specific form of reward hacking from a diversity perspective.
- **Neglect of Generative Diversity:** Current approaches for aligning T2I models primarily prioritize image quality and fidelity, often overlooking or inadequately addressing the crucial aspect of output diversity. Generative diversity is fundamental for creative content generation, data augmentation, and enhancing downstream task performance, making its preservation a critical requirement for robust T2I systems.
- **Lack of Standardized Diversity Metrics:** A significant challenge contributing to the oversight of diversity is the difficulty in quantitatively measuring it in T2I alignment. Unlike fidelity, generative diversity lacks standardized and computationally efficient quantitative metrics suitable for direct use as reward signals or comprehensive evaluation, hindering explicit regularization and assessment.
- **Limitations of Existing Reward Hacking Mitigation Strategies:** Current methods designed to counteract reward hacking are often empirical, hyperparameter-sensitive, and primarily modulate the magnitude of the reward signal. They tend to temper the severity of reward hacking rather than fundamentally altering the model’s optimization direction. The researchers hypothesize that the inherent biases of reward models are a primary cause of PMC, driving the T2I model to overfit these specific preferences and thereby "collapse" the generative distribution.
- **Breaking the Fidelity-Diversity Trade-off:** The research is motivated by the observed trade-off where models either achieve high quality with low diversity or vice-versa. The objective is to develop a framework that can simultaneously achieve superior alignment with human preference (high quality) and preserve generative diversity, thus breaking this trade-off.

To address these motivations, the research sets out to:
1. **Introduce and quantify PMC:** Define PMC and provide empirical evidence of its occurrence in current RLHF methods.
2. **Develop DivGenBench:** Propose a novel benchmark specifically designed to quantitatively measure the extent of PMC and provide a standardized tool for assessing generative diversity.
3. **Propose Directional Decoupling Alignment (D²-Align):** Introduce a new optimization framework that mitigates PMC by directionally correcting the reward signal, aiming to achieve a more genuine alignment with human preference by preventing over-optimization into narrow modes.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

This research introduces a framework called Directional Decoupling Alignment (D²-Align) to mitigate Preference Mode Collapse (PMC) in diffusion reinforcement learning. The methodology involves analyzing PMC, proposing a two-stage alignment framework, and establishing a new benchmark for diversity evaluation.

#### 3.1. Preference Mode Collapse (PMC) Analysis

The authors identify PMC as a form of reward hacking where T2I models, despite achieving high reward scores, produce homogeneous outputs, leading to a decline in generative diversity. This phenomenon is attributed to two factors:
1. **Evaluation Difficulty:** Unlike image quality, generative diversity is challenging to quantify, with a lack of standardized, computationally efficient metrics suitable for direct reward signals or regularization.
2. **Reward Model Biases:** Reward models possess intrinsic preferences. The optimization process can cause the T2I model to overfit these biases, leading the generative distribution to collapse towards a monolithic style favored by the reward model.

To address these issues, the work proposes D²-Align to mitigate reward model biases and introduces DivGenBench to quantitatively measure PMC.

#### 3.2. Directional Decoupling Alignment (D²-Align) Framework

D²-Align is a two-stage framework designed to decouple reward signal correction from generator alignment. It aims to directionally correct the reward signal to counteract intrinsic biases of the reward model.

**Stage 1: Learning Directional Correction**
- **Objective:** To learn a directional vector (`b_v`) within the reward model's continuous embedding space that corrects the reward signal, making it more aligned with true human aesthetic judgment.
- **Process:**
 1. The generator (G_theta) is frozen.
 2. HPS-v2.1 is used as the reward model, which evaluates cosine similarity between image and text embeddings.
 3. A learnable vector `b_v` (∈ R^d) is introduced.
 4. Perturbed text embeddings (`e+` and `e-`) are created by adding and subtracting `b_v` from the original text embedding (`e_text = Φ_text(c)`), followed by normalization.
 5. A "guided text embedding" (`~e_text`) is constructed by extrapolating from `e-` towards `e+`, controlled by a guidance scale `ω` (>1).
 6. A "guided reward" (`R_guided`) is computed using the image embedding (`e_img`) and `~e_text`.
 7. To ensure a stable reward signal, a ground-truth noising strategy is employed: a clean image `x_0` is noised to `x_t` with known noise `ϵ_gt`, and then a high-fidelity estimate `^x_0` is reconstructed via one-step denoising for reward evaluation.
 8. The vector `b_v` is optimized by minimizing the loss L_stage1(`b_v`) = E[-R_guided]. This empirically-driven correction is found to be more effective than manually-selected discrete vocabulary perturbations.

**Stage 2: Guided Alignment**
- **Objective:** To optimize the generator (G_theta) using the corrected reward signal, steering it away from mode collapse and towards genuine human preference.
- **Process:**
 1. The generator (G_theta) is unfrozen, and the learned directional vector `b*_v` from Stage 1 is frozen.
 2. The generator's parameters `θ` are optimized by minimizing the loss L_stage2(`θ`) = E[-R_guided(`x_0`, `c`; `b*_v`)].
 3. This process leverages the learned directional correction to provide a more faithful reward signal, preventing the generator from over-optimizing for superficial patterns and encouraging it to produce more diverse and high-fidelity outputs.

#### 3.3. DivGenBench Benchmark

To quantitatively measure PMC and assess "Generative Breadth," the authors introduce DivGenBench.
- **Motivation:** Existing benchmarks are deemed insufficient as they primarily focus on fidelity, measure variance from ambiguous prompts, or lack comprehensive diversity dimensions.
- **Design:** DivGenBench features a "keyword-driven" prompt design with a hierarchical structure covering four key dimensions:
 - **ID (Identity):** Probes high-level semantics related to human attributes (age, ethnicity, gender, physical features). Prompts are constructed from templates augmented with attributes from CelebA and other sources.
 - **Style:** Evaluates mid-level aesthetics, pairing art styles from WikiArt with base prompts.
 - **Layout:** Tests structural control and spatial diversity, combining object classes from COCO with specific counts.
 - **Tonal:** Assesses low-level physical properties, augmenting base prompts with fine-grained keywords for saturation, contrast, and brightness.
- **Scale:** Comprises 3,200 prompts, with 800 prompts per dimension.
- **Metrics:** A suite of dimension-customized metrics is employed:
 - **Identity Divergence Score (IDS):** Measures the average pairwise cosine similarity of ArcFace embeddings for generated faces. Lower IDS indicates greater diversity.
 - **Artistic Style Coverage (ASC):** Quantifies the retrievable style diversity relative to a ground-truth dataset using CSD features. Higher ASC indicates better coverage.
 - **Spatial Dispersion Index (SDI):** Evaluates the diversity of object layouts using bounding boxes (Grounding DINO) and IoU matching to calculate average pairwise layout similarity. Higher SDI indicates greater diversity.
 - **Photographic Variance Score (PVS):** Quantifies the spread of tonal values (saturation, brightness, contrast) in generated images. Higher PVS indicates greater tonal control and diversity.

#### 3.4. Experimental Setup

- **Base Model:** FLUX.1.Dev.
- **Reward Models:** HPS-v2.1 and a combined HPS-v2.1 + CLIP Score.
- **Baselines:** DanceGRPO, Flow-GRPO, SRPO.
- **Evaluation:** Comprehensive assessment of quality (Aesthetic predictors, CLIP Score, semantic fidelity metrics like DeQA, GenEval) and diversity (DivGenBench metrics), complemented by human evaluations.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of D²-Align involved extensive qualitative and quantitative experiments, including human evaluations, comparing it against the base model (FLUX.1.Dev) and several state-of-the-art RL-based alignment baselines.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Evaluation

- **HPDv2 Benchmark:** D²-Align consistently demonstrated superior performance in fidelity and text-to-image alignment. Baselines such as DanceGRPO, FlowGRPO, and SRPO exhibited "concept forgetting," failing to accurately render well-known subjects (e.g., "Big Chungus," "Iron Man") or to synthesize all attributes in complex prompts (e.g., "Indian goddess... nighttime anime style"). D²-Align successfully generated images that met these requirements.
- **DivGenBench Benchmark:** The qualitative examples on DivGenBench highlighted severe Preference Mode Collapse (PMC) in baseline methods. DanceGRPO and Flow-GRPO produced largely homogeneous faces despite prompts requiring ID diversity and failed to render requested tonal diversities (e.g., "low key," "black and white" styles), often defaulting to a uniform aesthetic. In contrast, D²-Align successfully preserved diverse identities, styles, layouts, and tones while maintaining high fidelity and detail.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Evaluation

- **Human Preference Alignment and Semantic Consistency (Quality):**
 - As shown in Table 1, D²-Align achieved superior scores across multiple quality metrics, including Aesthetic, ImageReward, Pick Score, Q-Align, CLIP, DeQA, and GenEval. This indicates that the method successfully aligns with human preference, maintains high fidelity, and exhibits robust text-to-image alignment and semantic consistency.
 - For instance, under the HPS-v2.1 + CLIP reward configuration, D²-Align achieved the highest scores in Aesthetic (6.671), ImageReward (1.762), Pick Score (0.246), Q-Align (4.970), CLIP (0.328), DeQA (4.498), and GenEval (0.660), surpassing all baselines.
- **Generative Diversity (DivGenBench):**
 - Table 2 demonstrated D²-Align's effectiveness in mitigating PMC. It consistently attained the highest scores across all DivGenBench metrics: the lowest Identity Divergence Score (IDS ↓, indicating higher diversity) and the highest Artistic Style Coverage (ASC ↑), Spatial Dispersion Index (SDI ↑), and Photographic Variance Score (PVS ↑).
 - Notably, methods like DanceGRPO and FlowGRPO, despite achieving high HPS-v2.1 scores, exhibited significant drops in diversity, with higher IDS and lower ASC, SDI, and PVS. This observation further validates the existence of PMC and underscores the necessity of the proposed approach.
- **Human Evaluation (User Studies):**
 - **HPDv2 User Study (Quality):** D²-Align achieved the highest preference rates across all quality dimensions: Detail Preservation (61.7%), Color Consistency (29.0%), Image-Text Alignment (52.2%), and Overall Preference (48.2%). This confirmed that D²-Align produces perceptually superior images recognized by human evaluators.
 - **DivGenBench User Study (Diversity):** This study directly assessed diversity. It confirmed PMC in baselines, with DanceGRPO and Flow-GRPO often performing worse than the unaligned FLUX model in dimensions like Tonal and Style diversity. D²-Align consistently secured the highest preference rates across Identity (35.2%), Style (37.3%), Layout, and Tonal (33.7%) diversity, demonstrating its ability to break the trade-off.

#### 4.3. Ablation Study

- **Convergence of Learned Vector (`b_v`):** The corrective effect of `b_v` was observed to become robust and yield significant performance improvements after approximately 2000 training steps.
- **Guidance Scale (`ω`):** An empirical setting of `ω = 1.5` was found to yield superior results on both HPS-v2.1 and Pickscore metrics.
- **Effectiveness of Learned Vector (`b_v`):** The learned continuous vector `b_v` consistently outperformed a baseline using manually selected discrete words (e.g., "realistic") for reward correction across all evaluated metrics. It also provided comprehensive improvement compared to using an uncorrected reward signal.
- **Generalizability of Learned Vector (`b_v`):** When integrated as a plug-and-play component into other PMC-susceptible methods (e.g., DanceGRPO), the learned `b_v` consistently mitigated mode collapse. For DanceGRPO, it led to a 4.7% improvement in Aesthetic Score, a 6.1% gain in CLIP score, a 20.1% reduction in IDS, and a 57.7% expansion in ASC, demonstrating its effectiveness in fostering a better balance between fidelity and diversity without requiring complex re-tuning of the external framework.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents several significant contributions to the field of generative AI, particularly in the domain of text-to-image synthesis and reinforcement learning from human feedback.

Firstly, the formal introduction and quantification of **Preference Mode Collapse (PMC)** addresses a critical, yet previously under-recognized, form of reward hacking. By explicitly defining PMC as the severe degradation of generative diversity due to over-optimization on reward models, the authors highlight a fundamental limitation of current RLHF approaches. This conceptual clarity encourages a more nuanced understanding of alignment challenges beyond mere quality degradation.

Secondly, the development of **Directional Decoupling Alignment (D²-Align)** offers a methodological advancement for mitigating PMC. By directionally correcting the reward signal within the continuous embedding space, the framework moves beyond empirical or magnitude-based adjustments to reward functions. This principled approach to de-biasing reward models represents a significant step towards achieving a more genuine alignment with human preferences, fostering both high quality and diversity simultaneously, thereby breaking a commonly observed trade-off. The demonstrated generalizability of the learned directional vector (`b_v`) suggests its potential as a plug-and-play component for enhancing the robustness of other RL-based generative models.

Thirdly, the introduction of **DivGenBench** fills a crucial gap in the evaluation of generative models. As a novel, keyword-driven benchmark with dimension-customized metrics (IDS, ASC, SDI, PVS), DivGenBench provides a standardized and quantitative tool for measuring generative diversity. This advancement allows researchers to systematically assess and compare models' "Generative Breadth," moving beyond qualitative observations or fidelity-centric metrics. The benchmark itself is a valuable resource for future research aimed at improving diversity in T2I models.

The potential impact of this work is multifaceted:
- **Enhanced Generative Creativity:** By enabling T2I models to produce diverse and high-quality outputs, D²-Align could significantly empower creative industries, artists, and designers, allowing for a wider range of expressive content generation from textual prompts.
- **More Robust AI Systems:** Mitigating PMC leads to more robust and reliable generative AI systems that are less prone to generating repetitive or uninspired content, even when optimized for human preference. This could improve user satisfaction and trust in AI-generated media.
- **Improved Understanding of RLHF:** This research contributes to a deeper understanding of the complexities of RLHF, particularly the subtle ways in which reward models can introduce biases that affect generative output. This could inform the design of future reward models and alignment algorithms.
- **New Avenues for Research:** DivGenBench and the D²-Align framework open new avenues for research into diversity-aware generative modeling, evaluation methodologies, and sophisticated techniques for debiasing and steering complex neural networks.

In conclusion, this paper addresses a fundamental challenge in the alignment of T2I diffusion models, providing both a rigorous analysis of the problem and a novel, effective solution. Its contributions are likely to influence both the practical application and theoretical development of generative AI.
