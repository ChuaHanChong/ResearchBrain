# Taming Preference Mode Collapse via Directional Decoupling Alignment in Diffusion Reinforcement Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Tsinghua University and Alibaba Group introduced Directional Decoupling Alignment (D²-Align) to address "Preference Mode Collapse" (PMC), a diversity degradation in text-to-image diffusion models aligned with human preferences. The method, combined with the new DivGenBench for evaluation, successfully enhances both human preference alignment and generative diversity, outperforming baselines in quality metrics and showing significant improvements in diversity scores.

## Problem

- Text-to-image (T2I) diffusion models aligned with Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF) often suffer from "Preference Mode Collapse" (PMC), where generative diversity degrades despite high reward scores.
- Existing RLHF methods struggle with a trade-off, either achieving high preference scores at the cost of diversity or vice versa.
- There is a lack of standardized, computationally efficient quantitative metrics for evaluating generative diversity in T2I alignment, and current reward hacking mitigations do not address inherent directional biases in reward models.

## Method

- Introduces ==Directional Decoupling== Alignment (D²-Align), a two-stage framework that learns a continuous directional vector to correct the reward signal, counteracting intrinsic biases of the reward model.
- The first stage freezes the generator and optimizes a learnable vector within the reward model's embedding space to minimize a 'guided reward' loss.
- The second stage then optimizes the generator using this learned, corrected reward signal to achieve genuine human preference alignment while preserving generative diversity, supported by the new DivGenBench for diversity evaluation.

## Results

- D²-Align achieved superior performance in human preference alignment and semantic consistency, scoring highest across multiple quality metrics (e.g., Aesthetic **6.671**, ImageReward **1.762**) compared to baselines.
- The method effectively mitigated PMC, demonstrating the highest generative diversity scores across all DivGenBench metrics (lowest Identity Divergence Score, highest Artistic Style Coverage, Spatial Dispersion Index, and Photographic Variance Score).
- Human evaluations confirmed D²-Align's perceptual superiority in image quality and its ability to preserve diverse identities, styles, layouts, and tones, securing the highest preference rates in user studies.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Preference Mode Collapse (PMC) is a distinct form of reward hacking in T2I models, causing severe diversity degradation due to over-optimization on reward model biases.
> - By directionally correcting the reward signal in a continuous embedding space, it is possible to achieve both high human preference alignment and generative diversity simultaneously.
> - Quantitative assessment of generative diversity requires a specialized benchmark (DivGenBench) with dimension-customized metrics for aspects like identity, style, layout, and tonal variations.
