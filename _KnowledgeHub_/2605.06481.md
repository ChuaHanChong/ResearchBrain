# OA-WAM: Object-Addressable World Action Model for Robust Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> OA-WAM (Object-Addressable World Action Model) enhances robot manipulation robustness by architecturally decoupling object identity from dynamic visual content through object-slot tokenization and a novel object-addressable attention mechanism. This approach achieves higher success rates on geometric perturbation benchmarks like LIBERO-Plus (+4.8% Geo Avg over π0.5) while maintaining strong in-distribution performance.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation systems, including Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies and World Action Models (WAMs), exhibit reduced robustness when faced with scene perturbations.
- Existing WAMs represent the world holistically, leading to target object identity becoming entangled with its surrounding visual context.
- This entanglement makes action decoders vulnerable to scene changes, resulting in object misidentification and unreliable action selection under varied conditions.

## Method

- **OA-WAM** decomposes each frame into structured ==per-slot state tokens==, where each slot contains a frozen, episode-unique ==Identity Address== and time-varying Content.
- Implements ==Object-Addressable Attention== by constraining the key projection in transformer self-attention to read only the Identity Address for slot-typed positions.
- A ==per-layer Address-Stream Reset== mechanism overwrites the identity subvector with its cached value after each transformer block, preventing corruption from content information.

## Results

- OA-WAM achieved **97.8%** average success on LIBERO and **79.3%** on SimplerEnv WidowX, outperforming previous SOTA baselines on in-distribution tasks.
- Established a new SOTA on LIBERO-Plus geometric robustness axes, with **84.3% Geometric Average** (**+4.8 pp** over π0.5) and **80.5%** on Camera perturbations.
- Ablation studies and causal slot intervention demonstrated that the object-addressable design directly routes target selection through the explicit address subspace, achieving a **swap-binding cosine of 0.87** vs. ≤ 0.09 for holistic baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust robot manipulation requires architecturally enforcing object addressability, explicitly decoupling stable object identity from dynamic visual content.
> - The proposed address-only key projection and per-layer address-stream reset create a stable, queryable interface for objects, ensuring consistent target selection despite scene perturbations.
> - The robustness gains on out-of-distribution geometric tasks stem from the specific inductive bias of the OA constraint, rather than a general increase in model capacity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robot manipulation systems commonly rely on Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, which have advanced significantly by scaling up vision-language backbones, robot data, and action decoders. World Action Models (WAMs) further extend these policies by integrating the prediction of future world states alongside robot actions, thereby providing additional temporal and causal supervision. However, a limitation of many existing VLA policies and WAMs is their reduced robustness when faced with scene perturbations. Benchmarks designed to test robustness demonstrate that performance often degrades under modest alterations to object layout, camera viewpoint, robot initial state, background, lighting, or sensor noise. This behavior suggests that current models tend to bind object selection to specific training layouts and visual contexts rather than to the language-named objects themselves.

The core issue identified by the authors is that most WAMs represent the predicted world holistically—as full-frame observations, image/video token streams, or global latents. Such representations, while capable of capturing scene evolution, do not provide the action decoder with a stable, queryable interface for identifying and acting upon specific target objects. When a scene undergoes changes, the target object's identity can become entangled with its surrounding context within these holistic tokens, leading to inaccurate action selection.

While object-centric approaches have attempted to address this by using slot-based dynamics models or grounding object representations, many still depend on discovery mechanisms like Slot-Attention, which can be sensitive to factors such as fixed slot counts, occlusion, and cluttered environments. Furthermore, even when stable slots are obtained, each slot typically encodes a mixture of identity, appearance, pose, and local context, which may still allow for object misidentification under significant perturbations. This research positions itself by proposing an architectural solution to decouple object identity from transient visual content to enhance robustness in robot manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this work is to develop a robust robot manipulation system by addressing the identified "lack of object addressability" in existing VLA and WAM architectures. The authors contend that robot manipulation tasks inherently operate at an object level, where instructions like "put the red mug on the green tray" necessitate the robot's ability to identify specific, language-named objects, reason about their relationships, and generate reliable actions, even under novel scene conditions.

The motivation stems from the observation that despite high performance on standard manipulation benchmarks, current VLA and WAM policies often fail in robustness tests. This failure is attributed to the holistic nature of their world representations, where target object identity is inextricably linked with its visual context. This entanglement renders the action decoder vulnerable to scene shifts, leading to incorrect target object selection.

To overcome this, the paper proposes to architecturally enforce object addressability. The key motivation is to provide a stable, object-level interface that allows the action decoder to consistently identify and act upon a specific target object, regardless of changes to its appearance or surrounding environment. This design aims to separate the decision of "which object to act on" from the transient information of "what that object currently is," thereby fostering more robust robot manipulation performance under diverse scene perturbations.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed model, **OA-WAM (Object-Addressable World Action Model)**, introduces an architecture that structurally decouples object identity from dynamic content information to enhance robustness.

**Object-Slot Tokenization and Unified Sequence:**
Each frame is decomposed into $N+1$ per-slot state tokens (one for the robot and $N$ for objects). Each slot token $s_t^k$ is structured as a 320-dimensional vector, comprising:
- **Identity Address ($addr_k$)**: A 32-dimensional vector unique to each object within an episode. It is computed once at the beginning of an episode ($t=0$) from the language label and the initial DINOv3 feature for that object and remains frozen thereafter.
- **Time-Varying Content ($cnt_t^k$)**: A 256-dimensional vector representing the object's current state, recomputed at each frame using features derived from SAM3 masks and DINOv3.
- **Temporal Positional Encoding ($\pi_t$)**: A 16-dimensional sinusoidal embedding of the frame index.
- **Role Positional Encoding ($\rho_k$)**: A 16-dimensional learned lookup based on object roles (robot, object, padding).

The system processes six parallel token streams, fed into a Chameleon-7B-style multimodal autoregressive transformer trunk:
1. **BPE Text Tokens**: From the language instruction.
2. **Qwen3-VL Noun Phrases**: Used as prompts for SAM3 to identify objects, but not directly included in the trunk's input sequence.
3. **Chameleon VQ-GAN Image Codes**: Discrete visual tokens.
4. **SAM3 + DINOv3 + Pose Object Slots**: These are the core slot tokens, projected into the trunk's hidden dimension by a learnable slot adapter ($f_\phi$).
5. **Discretized Proprioception**: 7-dimensional end-effector state, uniformly discretized.
6. **Discretized Past Actions**: Uniformly discretized past actions.

These tokens are assembled into a block-causal sequence, where tokens from a given frame group can attend to all prior tokens but not future ones. The sequence terminates with a learnable `[ACT_Q]` query token.

**Object-Addressable Attention (OA Constraint):**
The central architectural innovation is how OA-WAM enforces object addressability at every transformer layer:
1. **Address-Only Key Projection**: For slot-typed positions, the key projection `K^(ℓ)_k` in the self-attention mechanism is constrained to read only the `addr_k` subvector. This is achieved by applying a `mask_<=32` that zeros out all dimensions beyond the first 32 on the residual stream before applying the weight matrix $W_K$. This method reuses the pretrained $W_K$ without introducing new parameters.
2. **Per-Layer Address-Stream Reset**: After each transformer block, a forward hook overwrites the first 32 coordinates of the output hidden state $x^(ℓ+1)_k$ at slot positions with the cached, frozen $addr_k$. This prevents the `addr` subvector from being corrupted by content information or residual updates as it propagates through the network.

These two mechanisms ensure that the routing decision for cross-slot attention at every layer depends solely on the invariant object identity address, decoupling "which object to act on" from its dynamic appearance or surrounding context.

**Prediction Heads:**
OA-WAM includes three prediction heads that read from the final hidden states of the transformer trunk:
1. **World Head ($h_\psi$)**: Composed of two parallel MLPs (content and pose branches), it regresses the next-frame per-slot content ($\hat{c}_{t+1}^k$) and pose ($\hat{p}_{t+1}^k$). It is supervised by an MSE loss ($L_{world}$), excluding the robot slot.
2. **Action Head ($h_\xi$)**: A flow-matching MLP that processes the `[ACT_Q]` hidden state to predict a 16-step continuous action chunk. Training employs a conditional flow-matching objective ($L_{act}$), while inference uses a 4-step forward Euler integration.
3. **Auxiliary Image-VQ Head**: This head reuses the trunk's pretrained `lm_head` to predict next-frame VQ tokens, supervised by a weighted cross-entropy loss ($L_{vq}$). It introduces no new parameters.

**Training Objective and Strategy:**
The total training loss combines the objectives from the three heads with two auxiliary regularizers:
- `L_{compose}`: Encourages invariance of action and attention outputs to random permutations and insertions of distractor objects.
- `L_{role}`: Aligns the action head's soft slot assignments with language-extracted target/reference labels during early training.

The training proceeds in three stages:
1. **Stage 0**: Pretraining of the full 7B transformer trunk from a Chameleon-7B base on a mixed corpus, with the OA constraint linearly annealed and then enforced. This stage establishes the slot-aware trunk.
2. **Stage I**: Alignment of the slot adapter and world head, with the trunk frozen.
3. **Stage II**: Fine-tuning of the full system on standard LIBERO demonstrations. This involves adding LoRA modules to the transformer trunk, integrating the action head, and continuing updates to the slot adapter and world head.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

OA-WAM's performance was evaluated across three simulation benchmarks: LIBERO (in-distribution), SimplerEnv WidowX (sim-to-real visual matching), and LIBERO-Plus (robustness).

**Standard Benchmarks (LIBERO and SimplerEnv):**
- On the LIBERO benchmark, OA-WAM achieved an average success rate of 97.8%, exceeding the previously reported state-of-the-art (VLA-JEPA, 97.2%).
- For SimplerEnv WidowX (Visual Matching), OA-WAM obtained an average success rate of 79.3%, surpassing the best prior baseline (CoWVLA, 76.0%).
These results indicate that the slot-aware decomposition and architectural constraints introduced by OA-WAM do not compromise performance on in-distribution tasks.

**Robustness Benchmark (LIBERO-Plus):**
- OA-WAM established a new state-of-the-art on the "geometric axes" of LIBERO-Plus—Camera, Robot initial state, and Layout. These axes involve geometric rearrangement of the scene while preserving target object identity, which directly aligns with the model's hypothesis.
 - It achieved a Geometric Average (Geo Avg) of 84.3%, representing a +4.8% improvement over $\pi_{0.5}$.
 - Specific scores include 80.5% on Camera perturbations (+4.7% over Cosmos-Policy) and 89.6% on Robot initial state (within 0.1% of X-VLA).
- The model demonstrated competitive performance on appearance- and language-related perturbation axes.
- A notable reduction in performance was observed on the "Sensor Noise" axis (-17.1%), which the authors attribute to failures in the upstream slot-extraction pipeline (e.g., photometric distortion corrupting per-object content) rather than limitations in the policy stage itself.

**Ablation Studies (A1: Object-Addressability Isolation):**
The ablation study, comparing full OA-WAM (V0) against variants with the address-only key mask (V1) or both the mask and address-reset hook (V2) disabled, showed the following:
- Comparing V0 (full OA-WAM) to V2 (slot-based perception without OA constraints), OA-WAM exhibited a +20.0% increase in LP camera success and a +7.7% increase in LP average success. Crucially, the in-distribution LIBERO score only marginally changed by +2.4%, indicating that the robustness gains are attributable to the OOD-specific inductive bias of the OA constraint rather than a general capacity increase from the perception stack.
- Disabling the address-only key mask alone (V0 to V1) resulted in significant drops of -13.3% on LP camera and -18.2% on LP robot, while the in-distribution LIBERO score only decreased by -1.5%. This highlights the disproportionate impact of the key mask on out-of-distribution geometric robustness.
- Further removing the per-layer address-reset hook (V1 to V2) further reduced the "swap-binding" metric from 0.19 to 0.06, supporting its role in maintaining address isolation.

**Causal Slot Intervention (A2: Swap Binding):**
This diagnostic test involved swapping the identity address of the language-specified target object with another object in the scene during inference, while keeping all other inputs fixed. The metric measured was the cosine alignment between the resulting end-effector residual trajectory and the displacement direction towards the *swapped* target.
- OA-WAM achieved a swap-binding cosine of 0.87. In contrast, eight holistic VLA/WAM baselines consistently scored $\le 0.09$.
- This result provides direct behavioral evidence that OA-WAM effectively routes target selection through the explicit address subspace. The study further confirmed that both the architectural trunk constraint and the addressable readout mechanism are necessary for this binding.

Additional ablations (detailed in Appendix I) indicated that the world-prediction head contributes specifically to geometric-axis robustness, and the distractor-consistency loss is genuinely effective in maintaining performance under object permutation and insertion.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The development of OA-WAM represents a contribution to the field of robot manipulation by demonstrating a verifiable architectural approach to enhance robustness under scene perturbations. By explicitly formulating and addressing the "lack of object addressability," the research provides an alternative to existing holistic world representations in VLA and WAMs.

The significance of this work lies in its capacity to decouple the stable identity of an object from its dynamic visual content. This enables more consistent and reliable target object selection, which is critical for robots operating in real-world environments characterized by variability in object placement, lighting, and camera views. The observed performance improvements on geometric robustness benchmarks, without sacrificing in-distribution accuracy, suggest that the object-addressable design offers a path toward policies that are structurally robust. The causal slot-intervention test provides mechanistic evidence for how the model achieves this robustness, which is not commonly observed in existing holistic models. This explicit architectural commitment to addressable routing offers a methodological blueprint for future robust robot manipulation systems.

**Potential Impact:**
- **Enhanced Reliability**: Robots could operate more reliably in varied real-world scenarios, adapting to unexpected changes in object positions or viewing angles without needing re-training for every new configuration.
- **Improved Generalization**: The ability to bind actions to object identity rather than visual context could improve generalization to novel scenes and instances of objects.
- **Architectural Guidance**: The methodology, particularly the architectural enforcement of object addressability, provides a verifiable design principle for future VLA and WAM development.

**Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Simulator-Only Validation**: The current validation is restricted to simulated environments, and real-robot deployment remains unproven.
- **Perception Stack Fragility**: The frozen tokenizer (SAM3, DINOv3) exhibits limitations when processing small, reflective, transparent, occluded, or motion-blurred objects. This accounts for the reduced performance on the "Sensor Noise" axis, indicating that some robustness challenges stem from upstream perception rather than the policy itself.
- **Distractor Coupling Assumption**: The distractor-consistency loss assumes weak coupling between target and distractor objects, which might not always hold in complex, cluttered scenes.
- **Inference Latency**: The perception stack introduces a notable latency (~95 ms/frame), which is higher than the trunk+head processing (~5.6 ms/chunk), potentially posing a bottleneck for applications requiring high-frequency real-time control.
