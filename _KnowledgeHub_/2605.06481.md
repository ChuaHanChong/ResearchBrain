# OA-WAM: Object-Addressable World Action Model for Robust Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> OA-WAM (Object-Addressable World Action Model) enhances robot manipulation robustness by architecturally decoupling object identity from dynamic visual content through object-slot tokenization and a novel object-addressable attention mechanism. This approach achieves higher success rates on geometric perturbation benchmarks like LIBERO-Plus (+4.8% Geo Avg over \u03c00.5) while maintaining strong in-distribution performance.

## Problem

- Robot manipulation systems, including Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies and World Action Models (WAMs), exhibit reduced robustness when faced with scene perturbations.
- Existing WAMs represent the world holistically, leading to target object identity becoming entangled with its surrounding visual context.
- This entanglement makes action decoders vulnerable to scene changes, resulting in object misidentification and unreliable action selection under varied conditions.

## Method

- **OA-WAM** decomposes each frame into structured ==per-slot state tokens==, where each slot contains a frozen, episode-unique ==Identity Address== and time-varying Content.
- Implements ==Object-Addressable Attention== by constraining the key projection in transformer self-attention to read only the Identity Address for slot-typed positions.
- A ==per-layer Address-Stream Reset== mechanism overwrites the identity subvector with its cached value after each transformer block, preventing corruption from content information.

## Results

- OA-WAM achieved **97.8%** average success on LIBERO and **79.3%** on SimplerEnv WidowX, outperforming previous SOTA baselines on in-distribution tasks.
- Established a new SOTA on LIBERO-Plus geometric robustness axes, with **84.3% Geometric Average** (**+4.8 pp** over \u03c00.5) and **80.5%** on Camera perturbations.
- Ablation studies and causal slot intervention demonstrated that the object-addressable design directly routes target selection through the explicit address subspace, achieving a **swap-binding cosine of 0.87** vs. \u2264 0.09 for holistic baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Robust robot manipulation requires architecturally enforcing object addressability, explicitly decoupling stable object identity from dynamic visual content.
> - The proposed address-only key projection and per-layer address-stream reset create a stable, queryable interface for objects, ensuring consistent target selection despite scene perturbations.
> - The robustness gains on out-of-distribution geometric tasks stem from the specific inductive bias of the OA constraint, rather than a general increase in model capacity.
