# GDPO: Group reward-Decoupled Normalization Policy Optimization for Multi-reward RL Optimization

> [!summary] Summary
> GDPO (Group reward-Decoupled Normalization Policy Optimization) improves multi-reward reinforcement learning for large language model alignment by addressing "reward signal collapse" inherent in existing GRPO methods. It achieves this by decoupling the normalization of individual reward components, leading to more stable training and enhanced performance across various tasks, such as increasing mathematical reasoning accuracy by up to 6.3% on AIME.

## Problem

- Aligning Large Language Models (LLMs) with diverse human preferences (e.g., safety, coherence, efficiency) requires multi-reward reinforcement learning.
- Commonly used Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) algorithms suffer from "reward signal collapse" when applied to a sum of multiple rewards, meaning distinct reward combinations can yield identical advantage values.
- This reward signal collapse diminishes the resolution of the training signal, leading to suboptimal convergence, reduced training stability, and potential early training failure.

## Method

- GDPO ==decouples the normalization== of individual reward components, performing ==group-wise normalization== for each reward separately to preserve fine-grained distinctions.
- The individually normalized advantages are then summed and subjected to an additional batch-wise normalization to ensure numerical stability for policy updates.
- To manage priorities among rewards, GDPO explores reward weighting and advocates for ==reward function conditioning==, where an easier reward is granted only if a harder objective is met.

## Results

- GDPO consistently preserves a substantially larger number of distinct advantage groups compared to GRPO, demonstrating a more expressive training signal.
- Across tool-calling, mathematical reasoning, and coding reasoning tasks, GDPO achieves higher reward scores and exhibits greater training stability, preventing training collapses observed with GRPO.
- GDPO improves mathematical reasoning accuracy by up to **6.3%** on AIME for DeepSeek-R1-1.5B models while simultaneously reducing the length-exceeding ratio by up to **80%**.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Summing individual rewards before group-wise normalization in algorithms like GRPO causes a loss of information (reward signal collapse), leading to a less effective training signal.
> - Decoupling the normalization process for each reward component preserves a significantly richer and more expressive advantage signal, crucial for accurate multi-objective optimization.
> - Simply weighting rewards is often insufficient for guiding model behavior when objectives differ greatly in difficulty; instead, conditioning an easier reward on a harder one is more effective for prioritized learning.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the critical intersection of large language model (LLM) alignment, reinforcement learning (RL), and multi-objective optimization. As LLMs become increasingly sophisticated, the demands placed upon them extend beyond mere accuracy to encompass a diverse array of human preferences, including safety, efficiency, coherence, style, and adherence to specific formats. Aligning LLMs with these multifaceted preferences is a grand challenge in AI, typically addressed through reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) or AI feedback (RLAIF) pipelines.

The paper specifically targets a widely adopted family of RL algorithms known as **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** and its variants (e.g., DAPO, Reinforce++-Baseline). GRPO is popular due to its efficiency and simplicity, often used for single-objective reward optimization, typically accuracy. However, as LLM alignment evolves to incorporate *multiple* rewards—each representing a distinct human preference—the current practice has often defaulted to directly applying GRPO to the sum of these rewards, without a thorough examination of its suitability.

This paper identifies a critical, yet overlooked, limitation in this common practice: **reward signal collapse**. When GRPO normalizes a combined (summed) multi-reward signal, it can inadvertently cause distinct combinations of individual rewards to yield identical advantage values. This loss of information effectively diminishes the resolution of the training signal, leading to suboptimal convergence, reduced training stability, and, in some cases, early training failure.

Therefore, this work makes a methodological contribution to the RLHF/RLAIF landscape by proposing **GDPO (Group reward-Decoupled Normalization Policy Optimization)**. GDPO is presented as a direct algorithmic improvement over GRPO, specifically designed to mitigate the reward signal collapse issue in multi-reward settings. It seeks to make the underlying RL optimization process more robust and effective for complex LLM alignment tasks.

The "Related Work" section explicitly connects GDPO to:
- **GRPO Variants:** Positioning GDPO as a new variant alongside GSPO, DAPO, GFPO, and DLER, but specifically addressing the multi-reward challenge, rather than just stability, efficiency, or length control.
- **Multi-Reward Reinforcement Learning:** Placing the work within the broader context of methods for modeling diverse human preferences (Safe RLHF, RLPHF, ALARM) and integrating multiple rewards for complex tasks like reasoning (DeepSeek V3.2, O1-Pruner, L1). Unlike many of these, which focus on *designing* new reward functions or *balancing* conflicting objectives, GDPO focuses on improving the *underlying optimization algorithm* to better *process* existing or new multi-reward signals.

In essence, while others focus on *what* to reward, GDPO focuses on *how* to optimize with multiple rewards more effectively, filling a crucial gap in the RLHF pipeline.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central motivation for this research stems from the increasing complexity of aligning modern large language models with human preferences. Users now expect LLMs to exhibit a wide range of desirable behaviors beyond mere factual correctness, including efficiency, safety, logical coherence, format adherence, and more. To achieve this, reinforcement learning pipelines for LLMs have begun incorporating multiple reward signals, with each signal designed to capture a specific preference.

However, the authors identify a critical, yet often unexamined, assumption in current multi-reward RL practices for LLMs: the direct application of **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)**. While GRPO and its variants have become popular due to their efficiency and simplicity, their suitability for optimizing heterogeneous combinations of multi-reward signals has not been thoroughly investigated.

The **key problem** identified is that directly applying GRPO to normalize the sum of distinct rollout reward combinations leads to "reward signal collapse." This phenomenon means that different combinations of individual rewards can be mapped to identical advantage values. This collapse effectively:
1. **Reduces the resolution of the training signal:** Important distinctions between policy outcomes are lost.
2. **Leads to inaccurate policy updates:** The model receives less precise feedback, hindering effective learning.
3. **Causes suboptimal reward convergence:** The model struggles to reach the best possible performance across objectives.
4. **Can result in early training failure:** In some cases, the training process becomes unstable and collapses.

The **primary objective** of this paper is to resolve this reward signal collapse issue. To achieve this, the authors propose a new policy optimization method, **Group reward-Decoupled Normalization Policy Optimization (GDPO)**, with the following aims:
- To decouple the normalization of individual rewards, thereby preserving their relative differences more faithfully.
- To enable more accurate multi-reward optimization, preventing the loss of fine-grained information.
- To substantially improve training stability and convergence in multi-reward RL settings.
- To demonstrate the effectiveness and generalizability of GDPO across a variety of LLM tasks and reward configurations.

Furthermore, a secondary objective is to provide a systematic overview and empirical analysis of how to effectively incorporate priority variations among multiple rewards, exploring both reward weighting and reward function conditioning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of GDPO's methodology lies in a fundamental alteration to how advantage values are computed in multi-reward reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO).

**GRPO's Problematic Approach (Baseline):**
In standard GRPO, for a given question-answer pair $(q_i, o_j)$ and a group of $G$ responses, with $n$ objectives, the process is:
1. **Summation:** All $n$ individual reward components for a response ($r^{(i,j)}_1, \dots, r^{(i,j)}_n$) are first summed to form a single aggregated reward: $r^{(i,j)}_{sum} = r^{(i,j)}_1 + \dots + r^{(i,j)}_n$.
2. **Group-wise Normalization:** This aggregated sum reward is then normalized across the group of $G$ responses (i.e., with respect to the mean and standard deviation of $r^{(i,1)}_{sum}, \dots, r^{(i,G)}_{sum}$) to compute the group-relative advantage $A^{(i,j)}_{sum}$.
As demonstrated in Figure 2, this summation-then-normalization approach can lead to "reward signal collapse," where distinct combinations of underlying individual rewards result in identical normalized advantage values, losing crucial information.

**GDPO's Proposed Solution: Decoupled Normalization:**
GDPO addresses this by decoupling the normalization process:
1. **Individual Reward Normalization:** Instead of summing first, GDPO performs group-wise normalization for *each individual reward component separately*. For each of the $n$ rewards, and for each response $j$ within a group $G$, an individual normalized advantage $A^{(i,j)}_k$ is computed:
 $A^{(i,j)}_k = \frac{r^{(i,j)}_k - \text{mean}\{r^{(i,1)}_k, \dots, r^{(i,G)}_k\}}{\text{std}\{r^{(i,1)}_k, \dots, r^{(i,G)}_k\}}$ for each $k \in \{1, \dots, n\}$.
 This step ensures that the fine-grained differences and relative performance for each specific reward objective are preserved within its own normalized scale.
2. **Aggregated Normalized Advantages:** These individually normalized advantage values are then summed to obtain an overall advantage for the policy update:
 $A^{(i,j)}_{sum} = A^{(i,j)}_1 + \dots + A^{(i,j)}_n$.
 This sum now combines the *decoupled* and *preserved* signals from each reward dimension.
3. **Batch-wise Advantage Normalization (for Stability):** Finally, to ensure numerical stability and prevent the magnitude of the final advantage from growing with an increasing number of rewards, GDPO applies an additional *batch-wise* normalization step to $A^{(i,j)}_{sum}$. This normalizes the advantages across all responses in the entire training batch, ensuring a stable range for policy updates. The paper empirically shows that removing this batch-wise normalization can occasionally lead to convergence failures (Appendix A).

**Policy Optimization:**
The resulting $\hat{A}^{(i,j)}_{sum}$ is then used in a policy optimization objective similar to PPO or standard GRPO (Eq. 3), guiding the model's policy updates. The KL-divergence loss term is omitted for clarity in the formulation but is typically included in practice.

**Incorporating Priority Variation:**
GDPO also provides a systematic approach for incorporating varying priorities among objectives:
- **Reward Weighting:** For objectives with different importances, GDPO allows assigning different weights ($w_k$) to each *individually normalized advantage* before summation: $A^{(i,j)}_{sum} = w_1A^{(i,j)}_1 + \dots + w_nA^{(i,j)}_n$. However, the authors empirically demonstrate that simply adjusting weights may not be sufficient when objectives differ significantly in difficulty, as the model might still prioritize easier tasks.
- **Reward Function Conditioning:** To address the difficulty disparity, GDPO suggests modifying reward functions. An "easier" reward ($r_k$) can be conditioned on a "harder" reward ($r_l$), meaning $r_k$ is only awarded if $r_l$ meets a certain threshold (e.g., $r_l=1$). This forces the model to prioritize the more challenging objective first. For example, a length reward could only be granted if the response is also correct. This strategy is shown to be more effective in guiding model behavior towards desired priorities.

By decoupling the normalization, GDPO aims to provide a richer, more accurate, and stable training signal, overcoming the limitations of GRPO in complex multi-reward scenarios.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research provides compelling evidence for the efficacy of GDPO over GRPO in multi-reward reinforcement learning across various tasks and model scales.

#### 4.1. Reward Signal Collapse and Resolution

- **GRPO's Limitation:** The paper clearly demonstrates GRPO's propensity for "reward signal collapse." In a simplified two-binary-reward, two-rollout example (Figure 2), GRPO maps six distinct reward combinations into only two unique advantage groups. This over-compresses the signal, losing crucial information (e.g., (0,1) and (0,2) yielding identical advantages).
- **GDPO's Improvement:** GDPO, by decoupling individual reward normalization, retains significantly more distinct advantage groups. In the same example, it yields three distinct groups, accurately reflecting the stronger learning signal from (0,2) compared to (0,1).
- **Scalability of Advantage Granularity:** Quantitative analysis in Figure 3 shows that as the number of rollouts or rewards increases, GDPO consistently preserves a substantially larger number of distinct advantage groups compared to GRPO and GRPO "without standard deviation (std) normalization." This confirms that GDPO provides a more expressive and precise training signal.

#### 4.2. Improved Training Stability and Convergence

- **Consistent Convergence:** GDPO consistently converges to higher reward scores and exhibits more stable training curves than GRPO across all evaluated tasks (Figure 1b, Figure 4, Figure 5).
- **Addressing Training Collapse:** In the math reasoning task (Figure 5), GRPO's correctness reward score began to decline after approximately 400 steps, indicating a partial training collapse. In contrast, GDPO continued to improve its correctness reward score throughout training, while also demonstrating better control over response length.
- **Importance of Batch-wise Normalization:** An ablation study (Appendix A) highlights the importance of GDPO's batch-wise advantage normalization step for overall training stability, showing that runs without it occasionally fail to converge.
- **GRPO w/o std Limitations:** While removing the standard deviation normalization from GRPO (GRPO w/o std) slightly increased advantage diversity, it often led to training instability. For instance, in tool-calling, GRPO w/o std improved correctness but completely failed to converge on the format reward, resulting in 0% format correctness (Table 2).

#### 4.3. Task-Specific Performance Improvements

- **Tool Calling:**
 - GDPO-trained Qwen2.5-Instruct-1.5B/3B models consistently outperform GRPO in both tool-calling accuracy and format correctness (Table 1).
 - For 1.5B, GDPO achieved roughly 2.7% higher overall average accuracy and over 4% better correct format ratio. Similar gains were observed for the 3B model.

- **Mathematical Reasoning (Accuracy + Length Constraint):**
 - GDPO-trained DeepSeek-R1 and Qwen3 models demonstrate superior performance on challenging math benchmarks (AIME, MATH, AMC, Minerva, Olympiad) (Table 3).
 - GDPO achieves substantial accuracy improvements (e.g., up to 6.3% on AIME for DeepSeek-R1-1.5B) while significantly reducing the length-exceeding ratio (up to 80% reduction on AIME). This highlights GDPO's ability to achieve better accuracy-efficiency trade-offs.

- **Coding Reasoning (Pass Rate + Conditioned Length + Bug Ratio):**
 - In a three-reward setting, GDPO (GDPO$_{3-obj}$) maintains similar pass rates to GRPO (GRPO$_{3-obj}$) but markedly reduces both the length-exceeding ratio and the bug ratio across multiple coding benchmarks (Apps, Codecontests, Codeforces, Taco) (Table 5). This demonstrates GDPO's effectiveness as the number of reward signals increases, achieving a more favorable balance across all objectives.

#### 4.4. Impact Analysis of Different Reward Priority Configurations

- **Limitations of Reward Weighting:** Simply adjusting reward weights (e.g., reducing the weight of an easier length reward) proved unreliable for inducing desired prioritization when objectives differed substantially in difficulty. The model often continued to maximize the easier objective (Figure 6, Table 8).
- **Effectiveness of Reward Conditioning:** Conditioning an easier reward (e.g., length) on a more challenging one (e.g., correctness) proved highly effective. For example, conditioning the length reward on correctness ($\tilde{\mathcal{R}}_{length}$) prevented the model from aggressively maximizing length at the expense of correctness, leading to more consistent accuracy improvements and better control over length violations (Figure 7, Table 4, Table 9).
- **Fine-grained Control:** Once the difficulty disparity was mitigated by reward conditioning, subsequent adjustments to reward weights for the conditioned reward led to more predictable and faithful reflections of fine-grained preference adjustments (Figure 6, Table 9).

In summary, the main findings unequivocally demonstrate that GDPO is a more robust, stable, and effective policy optimization method for multi-reward reinforcement learning compared to GRPO, consistently yielding superior performance across diverse tasks and objective configurations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Group reward-Decoupled Normalization Policy Optimization (GDPO) carries significant implications for the field of reinforcement learning, particularly in the context of large language model (LLM) alignment.

### 6. Fundamental Algorithmic Advancement in RLHF/RLAIF

The most significant contribution of this work is the identification and principled resolution of the "reward signal collapse" problem inherent in applying GRPO to multi-reward settings. This issue, previously overlooked despite GRPO's widespread adoption, represents a fundamental limitation in how LLMs are optimized for complex human preferences. GDPO offers a simple yet profoundly effective algorithmic modification that ensures a higher-resolution and more faithful training signal. This insight could influence the design of future RL algorithms for multi-objective optimization.

### 7. Improved LLM Alignment with Diverse Preferences

By enabling more accurate and stable multi-reward optimization, GDPO facilitates the creation of LLMs that are better aligned with nuanced and diverse human preferences. This is crucial for real-world applications where models must balance conflicting objectives like factual correctness, helpfulness, safety, conciseness, and stylistic requirements. GDPO allows model developers to incorporate a richer set of feedback signals without compromising the quality of the training process, leading to more well-behaved and versatile LLMs.

### 8. Enhanced Training Stability and Efficiency

The consistent demonstration of improved training stability and convergence across various tasks is a major practical benefit. Preventing issues like training collapse or suboptimal convergence translates directly into more reliable and efficient development cycles for advanced LLMs. The added batch-wise normalization for stability underscores a practical design choice that makes GDPO robust for large-scale training.

### 9. Generalizability Across Tasks and Model Scales

The evaluation across three distinct and challenging tasks—tool calling, mathematical reasoning, and coding reasoning—and with different numbers of reward objectives (two and three) highlights GDPO's generalizability. This broad applicability suggests that GDPO can be a foundational improvement for a wide array of LLM-powered agents and applications that require multi-objective optimization.

### 10. Practical Guidance for Multi-Reward System Design

Beyond the core algorithm, the paper provides valuable empirical insights into effectively managing priority variations in multi-reward settings. The finding that simply adjusting reward weights is often insufficient when objectives differ greatly in difficulty, and that reward conditioning is a more robust strategy, offers critical practical guidance for practitioners designing complex reward functions. This helps address common pitfalls in multi-objective RL.

### 11. Potential for New Industry Standard

Given its clear advantages over GRPO, GDPO has the potential to become a new standard or a strongly preferred baseline for multi-reward RL optimization in LLM pipelines. Its integration with existing popular frameworks (HF-TRL, verl, Nemo-RL) indicates that it is readily adoptable by the broader research and development community.

### 12. Reinforcing NVIDIA's Research Leadership

The paper, originating largely from NVIDIA Research, reinforces the company's role as a key contributor to fundamental AI algorithm development, not just hardware. This deepens NVIDIA's ecosystem by providing advanced software solutions that optimize the performance and utility of LLMs, benefiting their users and platform.

In conclusion, GDPO represents a significant step forward in the methodology of LLM alignment. By providing a more precise and stable optimization mechanism for multi-reward signals, it empowers developers to build more capable, reliable, and human-aligned AI agents, paving the way for more sophisticated and trustworthy AI applications.
