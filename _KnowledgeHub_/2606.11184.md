# TacForeSight: Force-Guided Tactile World Model for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TacForeSight introduces a force-guided tactile world model that predicts future tactile states based on global force signals, enabling robots to anticipate and proactively manage contact-rich manipulation. The system achieves an average completion score of 79.0% across five manipulation tasks and 86.7% under perturbations on a real robot, surpassing reactive baselines.

## Problem

- Existing imitation learning approaches for contact-rich manipulation are largely reactive, processing current sensor readings without anticipating future contact states.
- The asymmetric spatio-temporal relationship between global wrist force/torque and local fingertip tactile sensing, where force precedes tactile changes, is often unmodeled.
- Generating high-dimensional sensory predictions (e.g., raw tactile images) for real-time robotic control is computationally intensive and limits anticipatory capabilities.

## Method

- A force-conditioned ==tactile world model== (TacForceWM) predicts short-horizon tactile evolution in a compact latent space, leveraging high-frequency wrist force/torque signals as leading indicators.
- A predictive tactile-conditioned policy uses the forecasted tactile latents as anticipatory contact priors for action sequence generation via a conditional flow-matching framework.
- The policy integrates current-to-future tactile interaction through cross-attention and adaptively fuses visuo-tactile features using a tactile-guided gating mechanism.

## Results

- TacForeSight achieved an average task completion score of **79.0%** across five nominal contact-rich manipulation tasks and **86.7%** under in-process perturbations, outperforming all baselines.
- The tactile world model, when conditioned on wrist wrench, demonstrated superior prediction quality with the lowest MSE of **0.017**, highest cosine similarity (**0.992**), and lowest symmetric KL divergence (**0.009**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that all proposed components, including force conditioning, predicted tactile features, current-future tactile interaction, and adaptive visuo-tactile fusion, contribute significantly to the framework's robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Global wrist force/torque signals provide a high-frequency, anticipatory cue (approximately 200 ms lead) for future local fingertip tactile deformations, enabling proactive contact reasoning.
> - Predicting tactile dynamics in a compact latent space significantly reduces computational overhead, allowing for efficient, real-time anticipatory control at 20 Hz.
> - Explicitly modeling current-to-future tactile interaction and dynamically balancing visual and tactile modalities through an adaptive gate are crucial for robust contact regulation and recovery from perturbations.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Contact-rich manipulation is a significant challenge in robotics, requiring robots to manage continuous physical interactions and dynamic contact transitions. Recent advancements in imitation learning have sought to improve contact-aware control by integrating multimodal feedback, such as visual, tactile, and force/torque (F/T) sensing.

Current approaches in this domain typically fall into several categories:
- **Direct Feature Fusion:** Many methods combine force and tactile signals as separate modalities into a joint representation or integrate them with visual and language inputs for visuomotor policies. Examples include extensions of visuomotor policies with tactile and force, or vision-language-action (VLA) models incorporating force-aware modules.
- **Reactive Control:** Some systems utilize force or tactile feedback primarily for reactive closed-loop control, adjusting actions in response to observed contact changes.
- **World Models for State Prediction:** The broader field of robotic manipulation has seen the emergence of world models that predict future states, primarily focusing on visual or video prediction to support planning or policy conditioning. Some works also predict future force or tactile observations, though generating high-dimensional sensory data can be computationally intensive for real-time applications.

Despite these developments, existing methods often treat force and tactile sensing reactively or fuse them without explicitly modeling their asymmetric spatio-temporal relationship. Wrist F/T sensors provide high-frequency, global cues about external load changes, which typically precede fine-grained local deformations captured by optical tactile sensors. This predictive, coarse-to-fine dependency between global force and local tactile responses has largely been underexplored. Previous works, while improving contact awareness, do not explicitly leverage this leading indicator relationship to anticipate future contact states proactively. TacForeSight aims to bridge this gap by explicitly modeling the force-to-tactile predictive process, moving beyond reactive feedback to enable anticipatory contact reasoning and control.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enhance a robot's ability to continuously perceive and regulate evolving physical interactions during contact-rich manipulation tasks, particularly under dynamic contact transitions and complex surface geometries. This enhancement is sought by enabling proactive rather than purely reactive control.

The motivation for TacForeSight stems from several observations and limitations in existing robotic manipulation systems:
- **Reactive Nature of Current Methods:** Many contemporary imitation learning approaches that integrate tactile and force sensing operate reactively. They process current sensor readings to inform immediate control decisions but do not explicitly anticipate future contact states. This reactive paradigm can limit effectiveness in tasks requiring precise, temporally coordinated interactions, where subtle changes can rapidly lead to failure (e.g., slippage, misalignment, contact loss).
- **Asymmetric Spatio-Temporal Roles of Force and Tactile Sensing:** The authors identify a crucial but often unmodeled relationship between global wrist force/torque and local fingertip tactile sensing. Wrist F/T signals provide high-frequency, global indications of external load variations, which empirically precede the manifestation of local deformation patterns on the tactile sensors. For instance, in tasks like bulb insertion, wrist wrench changes become evident before tactile responses at the fingertip. This suggests a coarse-to-fine temporal dependency where global force can serve as a leading indicator of upcoming local tactile states.
- **Inspiration from Human Sensorimotor Control:** Human manipulation relies on anticipatory mechanisms where load-related cues are used to predict object interaction states, guiding subsequent local contact adjustments. This biological inspiration motivates the development of a predictive framework for robotic systems.
- **Computational Efficiency for Real-Time Control:** Generating high-dimensional sensory predictions (e.g., raw tactile images or video frames) for real-time control is computationally expensive. The authors aim for a lightweight, efficient prediction mechanism that can operate at high frequencies suitable for manipulation control.

Based on these motivations, the key objectives of TacForeSight are:
1. **Develop a force-conditioned tactile prediction framework:** This framework, TacForeSight, explicitly models the predictive relationship from global wrist force/torque to short-horizon future tactile representations.
2. **Introduce TacForceWM:** A tactile world model designed to predict short-horizon tactile latent dynamics, conditioned on high-frequency wrist force/torque signals and dual-finger tactile observations, within a compact latent space.
3. **Design a Predictive Tactile-Conditioned Policy:** This policy leverages the predicted tactile latents as anticipatory contact priors for action sequence prediction. It incorporates a current-to-future tactile interaction module via cross-attention and adaptively fuses visuo-tactile features through a tactile-guided gating mechanism.
4. **Enable proactive contact reasoning with real-time inference:** By forecasting within a compact latent space, the system aims to achieve efficient, real-time inference (e.g., 20 Hz) for high-frequency manipulation control.
5. **Achieve superior robustness:** The framework aims to demonstrate improved performance in contact establishment, maintenance, and recovery from dynamic disturbances compared to existing reactive baselines.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

TacForeSight operates as a cascaded predictive framework, comprising two main stages: a pretrained force-conditioned tactile world model (TacForceWM) and a predictive tactile-conditioned policy.

**A. Force-conditioned Tactile World Model (TacForceWM)**
This stage is responsible for modeling and predicting short-horizon tactile evolution in a latent space, conditioned on wrist force/torque signals.

1. **Tactile Tokenizer:**
 - **Function:** Converts dense dual-finger tactile fields into compact frame-level tokens.
 - **Input:** Tactile observations $X_t^s \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 3}$ for each finger $s \in \{L, R\}$, representing 3D marker displacement.
 - **Components:**
 - A shared spatial encoder ($\Phi_{sp}$) composed of residual convolutional blocks and hierarchical downsampling extracts local tactile deformation features $F_t^s$.
 - Feature maps are augmented with learnable spatial positional embeddings ($E_{pos}$) and finger-specific identity embeddings ($E_{id}^s$) to create $\tilde{F}_t^s$.
 - Flattened patch-level tactile tokens are concatenated with a learnable `[CLS]` token.
 - A Transformer processes the concatenated tokens, enabling self-attention within and across fingers.
 - **Output:** A frame-level tactile latent $z_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_z}$ from the `[CLS]` token, representing the joint in-hand interaction state.

2. **Force Encoder:**
 - **Function:** Extracts temporally consistent conditions from high-rate wrist force/torque signals.
 - **Input:** A six-axis F/T sequence $w_{t-nH:t} \in \mathbb{R}^{nH \times 6}$, sampled at a higher rate than tactile observations.
 - **Components:**
 - Projects raw F/T vectors into a latent feature space.
 - Applies dilated causal 1D convolutional blocks to capture multi-scale temporal variations.
 - Includes a causal temporal downsampling layer to align high-rate force features with the tactile latent sequence.
 - **Output:** A tactile-aligned condition sequence $c_{t-H:t}$.

3. **Latent Dynamics Predictor:**
 - **Function:** Forecasts a future tactile latent chunk by conditioning spatial tactile prediction on temporal force features.
 - **Input:** A tactile latent chunk $z_{t-H:t}^{tac}$ and its aligned force condition $c_{t-H:t}^{tac}$.
 - **Mechanism:** A force-conditioned latent Transformer backbone ($T_{\psi}$) estimates a future tactile latent chunk $\hat{z}_{t-H+\Delta:t+\Delta}^{tac}$ with a temporal offset $\Delta$.
 - **Force Conditioning:** Force conditions are injected through adaptive layer normalization (AdaLN), modulating intermediate features based on the temporal wrench context.
 - **Formulation:** Chunk-based forecasting is used to obtain temporally coherent dynamics, rather than frame-wise one-step prediction.

4. **Training Objectives:**
 - **Prediction Objective ($L_{pred}$):** Uses Mean Squared Error (MSE) to supervise both absolute future tactile latents and their first-order temporal dynamics (temporal difference) to capture evolution and prevent over-smoothing.
 - **Latent Regularization Objective ($L_{sig}$):** Incorporates the Sketched Isotropic Gaussian Regularizer (SIGReg), inspired by LeJEPA, to regularize the tactile latent distribution towards an isotropic Gaussian structure and prevent representation collapse.
 - **Overall World Model Objective ($L_{WM}$):** $L_{WM} = L_{pred} + \lambda_{sig}L_{sig}$.

**B. Predictive Tactile-conditioned Policy**
This stage leverages the predicted tactile dynamics from TacForceWM to generate contact-aware action sequences using a conditional flow-matching framework.

1. **Multimodal Feature Extraction:**
 - **Visual Context:** A frozen DINOv2-small backbone encodes the current RGB observation into a visual feature $h_t^{img}$.
 - **Proprioceptive History:** Recent proprioceptive history is flattened and encoded by an MLP into a state feature $h_t^s$.
 - **Tactile Latent Dynamics:** The tactile tokenizer encodes recent tactile observations into current tactile latents $z_{t-H:t}^{tac}$. The pretrained world model then predicts future tactile latents $\hat{Z}_t^{tac}$ using $z_{t-H:t}^{tac}$ and aligned wrist F/T features $c_{t-H:t}$.

2. **Current-Future Tactile Interaction:**
 - **Function:** Models the interaction between current and predicted tactile latents to augment the immediate contact state with anticipatory dynamics.
 - **Mechanism:** Cross-attention is employed. Current tactile latents (with temporal embeddings) serve as queries, while predicted future latents (with temporal embeddings) serve as keys and values.
 - **Output:** An enhanced tactile sequence $H_t^{tac}$, which is then averaged over the temporal dimension to obtain a compact future-aware tactile representation $h_t^{tac}$.

3. **Adaptive Visuo-Tactile Fusion:**
 - **Function:** Dynamically regulates the contribution of visual context and predictive tactile dynamics at the feature-channel granularity.
 - **Mechanism:** A tactile-guided gate $\alpha$ is generated by an MLP with sigmoid activation, conditioned on $h_t^{tac}$.
 - **Fusion:** Visual and tactile features are projected into a shared space and adaptively fused: $h_{vt}^t = (1 - \alpha) \odot h_{img}^t + \alpha \odot h_{tac}^t$. This allows the policy to balance modalities based on interaction dynamics.

4. **Flow-matching Action Head:**
 - **Function:** Predicts an action chunk (sequence) conditioned on the fused multimodal features.
 - **Condition:** The fused visuo-tactile feature $h_{vt}^t$ and proprioceptive feature $h_t^s$ are concatenated to form the global policy condition $y_t$.
 - **Mechanism:** A lightweight conditional flow matching framework is adopted. It defines a linear interpolation path between a Gaussian noise action chunk $A_t^{(0)}$ and an expert action chunk $A_t^{(1)}$. A temporal U-Net $v_{\theta}$ is trained to predict the conditional velocity field along this trajectory.
 - **Inference:** Starting with $A_0^t \sim N(0, I)$, the learned ordinary differential equation is integrated from $\tau=0$ to $\tau=1$ to obtain the denoised predicted action chunk $\hat{A}_t$.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

**A. Experimental Setup**
- **Robotic Platform:** A 7-DoF UFactory xArm7 with a Robotiq 2F-85 gripper, Intel RealSense D435 camera, UFactory 6-axis F/T sensor, and two Xense tactile sensors (35x20 3D displacement map).
- **Data Rates:** Image and tactile at 30 Hz, wrist wrench at 120 Hz.
- **Training:** TacForceWM (11.8M params) trained on 2,700 force-tactile episodes for 150k steps. The flow-matching policy (68.9M params) uses frozen pretrained tactile encoder and predictor. Training conducted on 8 NVIDIA A100 GPUs.
- **Baselines:**
 - **DP:** Visuomotor imitation policy (RGB + proprioception).
 - **DP+Tactile+Force:** Multimodal extension of DP (RGB + proprioception + tactile + wrist wrench).
 - **KineDex:** Tactile-aware visuomotor policy (action + tactile representation prediction from visual + tactile).
 - **FoAR:** Force-aware policy (modulates force representations using a future contact predictor; RGB encoder for fair comparison).
 - **RDP:** Reactive visual-tactile policy (tactile + force for online action refinement).

**B. Benchmark Tasks**
1. **Contact-Rich Manipulation Tasks (5 tasks):**
 - Vase Wiping (Wiping): Stable sliding contact.
 - Card Swiping (Swiping): Stable sliding contact.
 - Tube Adjustment & Insertion (Adjustment): Sequential alignment and constrained contact.
 - Bulb Insertion & Locking (Locking): Sequential alignment and constrained contact.
 - Wire Insertion (Insertion): Precise contact control for flexible wire.
2. **In-Process Perturbation Settings (3 tasks):**
 - Height perturbation during wiping (Wiping-P).
 - Angle perturbation during swiping (Swiping-P).
 - Pose perturbation during tube adjustment (Adjustment-P).
 - Policies trained with both nominal and recovery demonstrations under perturbations.
3. **Evaluation Metrics:** Average task completion score over 20 independent trials. Wiping/Swiping: ratio of completed length. Adjustment/Locking: 50% for first stage, 100% for both. Wire Insertion: 100% for full insertion. Perturbation tasks: success only if policy recovers and completes.

**C. Real-Robot Task Performance**
- **Overall Superiority:** TacForeSight achieved an average completion score of 79.0% across the five nominal contact-rich manipulation tasks, outperforming all baselines. Its performance was 100% on Wiping, 85% on Swiping, 70% on Adjustment, 80% on Locking, and 60% on Insertion.
- **Robustness to Perturbations:** The method demonstrated an average score of 86.7% under in-process perturbations, with 90% on Wiping-P, 85% on Swiping-P, and 85% on Adjustment-P. This significantly surpassed all baselines, which showed considerable performance degradation under disturbances (e.g., RDP 65% on Swiping-P, 0% on Adjustment-P; DP+Tactile+Force 35% on Wiping-P, 0% on Swiping-P).
- **Qualitative Observations:** The qualitative execution sequences illustrated successful re-establishment of stable contact, correction of end-effector motion, and task continuation after height, angle, and pose disturbances.

**D. Predictive Tactile Representation Analysis**
- **Temporal Anticipation:** Visualization of tactile latents (Fig. 4a) revealed that predicted tactile latents exhibited contact-related variations approximately 200 ms before similar changes became evident in current tactile latents. Latent difference responses were prominent around key contact-stage transitions, indicating that the world model provides anticipatory contact cues.
- **Representation Discriminability:** t-SNE visualization (Fig. 4b) of aggregated tactile latents for unseen interactions (pressing, twisting, sliding) showed distinct, well-separated clusters. This suggests that the tactile encoder learns contact-discriminative representations capable of generalizing to different interaction patterns beyond the training distribution.

**E. Ablation Studies**
1. **World Model Conditioning (Table II):**
 - Comparison of conditioning modalities (no external, RGB image, robot state, wrist wrench) for tactile world model prediction quality.
 - **Finding:** Wrist wrench conditioning achieved the best performance across all metrics (MSE, cosine similarity, symmetric KL divergence). It reduced MSE from 0.027 (no condition) to 0.017, increased cosine similarity from 0.954 to 0.992, and reduced KL_sym from 0.014 to 0.009.
 - **Conclusion:** Wrist wrench provides the most informative physical condition for forecasting tactile contact evolution due to its direct capture of physical interaction changes.

2. **Policy Ablation (Table III):**
 - Investigation of the roles of force conditioning, predicted tactile features, current-future tactile interaction, and adaptive visuo-tactile fusion.
 - **Parallel Fusion:** Performed poorly under perturbations (25% on Wiping-P, 10% on Swiping-P), indicating that simple concatenation of force and tactile is insufficient.
 - **w/o Predicted Tactile:** Significant performance drop on Wiping-P (15%) and Swiping-P (15%), highlighting the importance of future tactile priors for contact maintenance and recovery.
 - **w/o Force Condition:** Reduced performance (70% on Wiping, 50% on Wiping-P, 75% on Swiping-P), confirming the necessity of wrist-wrench conditioning for effective tactile prediction.
 - **w/o Cross-attention:** Resulted in 0% success on Swiping-P, underscoring the critical role of explicitly modeling current-future tactile interaction.
 - **w/o Adaptive Gate:** Showed reduced Wiping-P performance (65%) and increased recovery time (2.56 s to 4.06 s), suggesting that adaptive visuo-tactile fusion improves contact regulation during perturbation recovery. Visualization (Fig. 5) showed that the tactile-guided gate responses varied across contact stages, dynamically modulating tactile information.
 - **Conclusion:** All proposed components contribute to the robustness and performance of TacForeSight, especially under dynamic disturbances.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

TacForeSight represents a shift from reactive to proactive paradigms in contact-rich robotic manipulation. By explicitly modeling the asymmetric temporal relationship where global force signals precede and predict local tactile responses, the framework enables anticipatory contact reasoning.

The significance and potential impact of this work include:
- **Enhanced Robustness in Manipulation:** The demonstrated superior performance in diverse contact-rich tasks and, particularly, under dynamic perturbations, suggests that TacForeSight can lead to more robust and reliable robotic systems capable of operating in unstructured and dynamic environments. This is crucial for applications requiring high precision and compliance, such as assembly, delicate object handling, and medical procedures.
- **Efficient Predictive Control:** The use of a compact latent space for tactile prediction ensures that the framework remains lightweight and computationally efficient, capable of real-time inference at high frequencies (20 Hz). This addresses a major challenge in implementing predictive world models for real-world robotic control, avoiding the computational burden of high-dimensional sensory prediction.
- **Improved Understanding of Multimodal Integration:** The research provides insights into effectively integrating heterogeneous sensory modalities (global force, local tactile, vision). By leveraging the inherent temporal lead of force over tactile, it offers a principled way to combine these sensors to extract richer, anticipatory information about physical interactions.
- **Foundation for Future World Models:** This work contributes to the development of tactile-specific world models, demonstrating that latent-space tactile prediction is an effective mechanism for integrating physical reasoning into robotic control. This could inspire further research into multimodal predictive models that integrate force, tactile, and visual cues more holistically and proactively.
- **Applicability in Real-World Scenarios:** The evaluation on a real robotic platform across various tasks and perturbation settings validates the practical utility of TacForeSight, suggesting its potential for deployment in industrial automation, service robotics, and other domains where intricate physical interactions are common.

Overall, TacForeSight advances the field of contact-rich manipulation by demonstrating the benefits of force-conditioned tactile foresight, offering a mechanism for robots to not just react to, but anticipate, complex physical interactions.
