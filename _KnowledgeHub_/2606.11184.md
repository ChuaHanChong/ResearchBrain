# TacForeSight: Force-Guided Tactile World Model for Contact-Rich Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> TacForeSight introduces a force-guided tactile world model that predicts future tactile states based on global force signals, enabling robots to anticipate and proactively manage contact-rich manipulation. The system achieves an average completion score of 79.0% across five manipulation tasks and 86.7% under perturbations on a real robot, surpassing reactive baselines.

## Problem

- Existing imitation learning approaches for contact-rich manipulation are largely reactive, processing current sensor readings without anticipating future contact states.
- The asymmetric spatio-temporal relationship between global wrist force/torque and local fingertip tactile sensing, where force precedes tactile changes, is often unmodeled.
- Generating high-dimensional sensory predictions (e.g., raw tactile images) for real-time robotic control is computationally intensive and limits anticipatory capabilities.

## Method

- A force-conditioned ==tactile world model== (TacForceWM) predicts short-horizon tactile evolution in a compact latent space, leveraging high-frequency wrist force/torque signals as leading indicators.
- A predictive tactile-conditioned policy uses the forecasted tactile latents as anticipatory contact priors for action sequence generation via a conditional flow-matching framework.
- The policy integrates current-to-future tactile interaction through cross-attention and adaptively fuses visuo-tactile features using a tactile-guided gating mechanism.

## Results

- TacForeSight achieved an average task completion score of **79.0%** across five nominal contact-rich manipulation tasks and **86.7%** under in-process perturbations, outperforming all baselines.
- The tactile world model, when conditioned on wrist wrench, demonstrated superior prediction quality with the lowest MSE of **0.017**, highest cosine similarity (**0.992**), and lowest symmetric KL divergence (**0.009**).
- Ablation studies confirmed that all proposed components, including force conditioning, predicted tactile features, current-future tactile interaction, and adaptive visuo-tactile fusion, contribute significantly to the framework's robustness.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Global wrist force/torque signals provide a high-frequency, anticipatory cue (approximately 200 ms lead) for future local fingertip tactile deformations, enabling proactive contact reasoning.
> - Predicting tactile dynamics in a compact latent space significantly reduces computational overhead, allowing for efficient, real-time anticipatory control at 20 Hz.
> - Explicitly modeling current-to-future tactile interaction and dynamically balancing visual and tactile modalities through an adaptive gate are crucial for robust contact regulation and recovery from perturbations.
