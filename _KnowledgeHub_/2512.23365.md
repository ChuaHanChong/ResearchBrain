# SpatialMosaic: A Multiview VLM Dataset for Partial Visibility

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from Seoul National University and collaborators introduce SpatialMosaic, a multi-view dataset and benchmark comprising 3 million QA pairs, specifically designed to train and evaluate multimodal large language models (MLLMs) on 3D spatial reasoning tasks under partial visibility, occlusion, and low-overlap conditions. Their proposed SpatialMosaicVLM, a hybrid VLM integrating 3D geometric encoders, achieves 81.8% average accuracy on the new benchmark and demonstrates strong zero-shot generalization to another temporal spatial reasoning benchmark, outperforming larger proprietary and open-source models.

## Problem

- Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) lack robust 3D spatial reasoning capabilities when processing multi-view images, particularly under real-world conditions of partial visibility, occlusion, and low-overlap.
- Existing approaches often depend on costly explicit 3D representations or struggle to consistently integrate fragmented visual information across sparse viewpoints.
- Current MLLMs do not achieve human-like implicit 3D scene reconstruction or reliable reasoning about unseen objects from imperfect, limited observations.

## Method

- A scalable multi-view data generation pipeline was developed, leveraging ScanNet++ to automatically construct ==3 million geometrically grounded== spatial reasoning question-answer pairs.
- The SpatialMosaic dataset was introduced, a large-scale instruction-tuning dataset specifically designed to train MLLMs on tasks involving ==partial visibility, occlusion, and low-overlap conditions== from multi-view inputs.
- ==SpatialMosaicVLM==, a hybrid framework, was proposed, which integrates ==3D reconstruction models as explicit geometry encoders== to provide geometry-aware representations and improve cross-view alignment for robust spatial reasoning.

## Results

- SpatialMosaicVLM achieved an average accuracy of **81.8%** on the SpatialMosaic-Bench, significantly outperforming existing MLLM baselines (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B at **47.8%**).
- The model demonstrated strong zero-shot generalization to VSTI-Bench, securing the top rank with **46.8%** average accuracy and surpassing larger proprietary models like GPT-4o and Gemini-1.5 Flash.
- Diagnostic analysis confirmed that performance degrades with increased occlusion and reduced view overlap, but SpatialMosaicVLM consistently maintained superior robustness across all difficulty levels and task types compared to baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly addressing scenarios of partial visibility, occlusion, and low-overlap through tailored data generation is crucial for advancing MLLMs' 3D spatial reasoning capabilities.
> - Integrating 3D reconstruction models as geometry encoders within a VLM architecture provides explicit geometric priors, which are essential for achieving robust cross-view alignment and consistent object understanding.
> - Training on a large-scale, diagnostically challenging dataset fosters a more generalized spatial intelligence, enabling strong zero-shot performance on diverse, unseen spatial reasoning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) has demonstrated increasing capabilities in 3D scene understanding and spatial reasoning by processing multi-view images, often bypassing the need for explicit 3D reconstructions. Early MLLMs primarily extended large language models (LLMs) with visual encoders for tasks such as classification, segmentation, and captioning on single-image inputs. More recent works have aimed to incorporate 3D signals, like depth maps or point clouds, or leverage multi-view consistency to enhance spatial understanding for tasks such as depth estimation, metric distance prediction, and holistic spatial reasoning.

However, a recurring limitation in existing approaches is their reliance on either pre-built 3D representations or readily available 3D reconstruction modules. Such dependencies restrict their applicability in real-world environments where pre-constructed 3D maps are often unavailable. While some studies have explored deriving 3D spatial reasoning directly from multi-view images to mitigate these dependencies, they frequently fall short in addressing complex, realistic conditions. These conditions include situations where available views are sparse, share limited visual overlap, or are not provided as temporally adjacent video frames—scenarios where human perception is notably robust.

Existing multi-view spatial datasets often focus on video inputs with sequential frames or do not explicitly account for challenges arising from partial visibility, object occlusion, and varying image overlap. Consequently, MLLMs frequently struggle to integrate fragmented visual cues from such imperfect observations. This paper positions itself as an effort to systematically investigate these under-explored scenarios and to bridge the gap between current MLLM capabilities and human-level robustness in interpreting sparse, fragmented multi-view observations. It aims to provide a comprehensive resource for training and evaluating MLLMs under these challenging, real-world constraints.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to address critical limitations in current MLLMs regarding multi-view spatial reasoning, particularly under conditions frequently encountered in real-world environments but inadequately represented in existing datasets. The authors identify three specific and challenging scenarios:
1. **Partial visibility**: An object of interest is visible in only a subset of the available camera views, rather than being observable from all viewpoints.
2. **Occlusion**: Within a single view, an object is partially obscured by other instances in the scene or truncated by the camera's field of view.
3. **Low-overlap**: The available multi-views exhibit minimal shared content, offering limited redundant information for robust spatial inference.

The motivation for focusing on these conditions stems from several observations:
- **Real-world prevalence**: These scenarios are pervasive in real-world settings, such as navigating complex indoor spaces or dynamic outdoor environments with moving obstacles.
- **Human capability vs. MLLM limitations**: Humans are adept at integrating fragmented observations across different views to implicitly reconstruct coherent 3D scenes and reason about objects that are not fully visible. Current MLLMs, however, often rely on complete visual information or strong cross-view consistency, struggling when cues are partial or sparse.
- **Deficiencies in existing datasets**: Prior multi-view spatial datasets primarily focus on either indoor or outdoor layouts, or rely on sequential video frames. They do not explicitly emphasize conditions requiring reasoning from non-sequential, low-overlap, and partially visible observations, which restricts comprehensive evaluation and training.

To address these motivations, the authors set forth the following key objectives:
- **Develop a scalable data generation pipeline**: Construct a novel multi-view data generation and annotation pipeline specifically tailored to create realistic spatial reasoning Question-Answering (QA) pairs under partial visibility, occlusion, and low-overlap conditions.
- **Introduce SpatialMosaic dataset**: Generate a comprehensive instruction-tuning dataset, named SpatialMosaic, containing 2 million QA pairs that capture these challenging real-world scenarios across both indoor and outdoor environments.
- **Establish SpatialMosaic-Bench benchmark**: Create a challenging benchmark, SpatialMosaic-Bench, for evaluating multi-view spatial reasoning. This benchmark comprises 1 million QA pairs across 11 tasks, including both multiple-choice and numerical-answer formats, designed to probe model robustness in diverse and complex scenarios.
- **Provide a practical baseline**: Integrate geometry encoders into a Vision-Language Model (VLM) framework to establish a practical baseline for multi-view settings, aiming to improve cross-view consistency and spatial grounding.
- **Demonstrate effectiveness**: Through extensive experiments, validate that the SpatialMosaic dataset and its data generation pipeline effectively enhance spatial reasoning capabilities in MLLMs under demanding multi-view conditions.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The research introduces SpatialMosaic, a multi-view VLM dataset and benchmark generated through a three-stage data generation pipeline, building upon existing 3D datasets.

**Data Sources:**
The dataset leverages ScanNet++ for indoor scenes and the Waymo Open Dataset (WOD) for outdoor scenes. These datasets provide 3D point clouds and multi-view images with calibrated camera parameters, which are essential for generating geometrically grounded annotations.

**Data Generation Pipeline:** The pipeline consists of three main stages:
1. **Data Preparation (Section 3.1):**
 - **Object Occlusion Ratio ($r_{n,obj}$):** Quantifies how much an object instance ($n$) is occluded by other objects from a specific camera viewpoint. This is calculated by rendering the full scene depth (D) and the depth of only the instance ($D_n$). Points where $D < D_n$ are considered occluded. The ratio is the number of occluded points relative to the total visible and occluded points.
 - **Field-of-View (FoV) Occlusion Ratio ($r_{n,FoV}$):** Measures the proportion of an instance that is truncated by the image boundaries. This is determined by rendering the instance in an "extended" field of view (double the original resolution) and comparing the visible points within the original FoV to those visible only in the extended region.

2. **QA Generation and Relations (Section 3.2):**
 - **Sparse Multi-view Sampling:** To ensure challenging scenarios, image pairs are sampled with limited visual overlap. An overlap ratio is calculated as the Intersection-over-Union (IoU) of visible 3D points between two images. Only image pairs with an overlap ratio below a predefined threshold (set to $\tau = 0.3$ based on empirical analysis) are retained, promoting integrative reasoning across diverse and non-redundant viewpoints.
 - **Instance Filtering:** Instances are filtered to ensure they are suitable for generating complex QA pairs. Criteria include: (1) the target instance must *not* be visible in all images of a selected combination (ensuring partial visibility), and (2) the target instance must have an occlusion ratio below 0.9 in at least one image (ensuring it is observable). Task-specific filters are also applied, such as requiring the source instance to be visible while the target is not in a query image. These constraints aim to increase the spatial complexity and reduce reliance on trivial single-view cues.
 - **Compute Geometric Quantities:** Various geometric quantities are computed from the derived annotations, including 2D and 3D bounding boxes, visibility profiles, and camera parameters. For relation-based tasks, 3D oriented bounding boxes of objects are transformed into the camera coordinate system of the query view to determine precise positional relationships (e.g., "to the left," "above," "closer").

3. **QA Template and Output (Section 3.3):**
 - **Task and Object Selection:** Based on filtered instances and computed geometric quantities, appropriate source and target objects are selected for each task, ensuring compliance with visibility and occlusion constraints.
 - **QA Template Generation:** The paper defines 11 task categories, utilizing templates with placeholders that are populated by concrete object labels and spatial information. Tasks are categorized into:
 - **Multiple-Choice Answer (MCA) Tasks:** Object Count, Best-View Selection, Object Existence (Left/Right, Above/Below, Farther/Closer), Object Attribute (Left/Right, Above/Below, Farther/Closer), and Spatial Relation (Left/Right, Above/Below, Farther/Closer).
 - **Numerical Answer (NA) Tasks:** Object Count (numerical), Camera-Object Distance, Object-Object Distance, Object Size, and Object Localization.
 - For MCA tasks, distractors are automatically generated from incorrect spatial configurations. For NA tasks, ground-truth answers are directly derived from geometric computations.

**Human Verification and Bias Analysis (Section 3.4):**
A human verification study was conducted on a subset of 3,000 QAs (100 per task category, balanced across indoor/outdoor) to assess reliability. Annotators checked question clarity, answer correctness, object grounding, visibility-condition validity, and inferability from images. Problematic cases were filtered, resulting in a 90.9% acceptance rate. Bias analysis included randomizing correct answer positions, ensuring uniform QA generation across object categories, and relying on explicit geometric criteria for answers rather than viewpoint-specific patterns.

**SpatialMosaic-Bench (Section 3.5):**
The benchmark consists of 1 million QA pairs with 2-5 frames per instance. Evaluation protocols vary by task type (accuracy for MCA, tolerance for NA). Diagnostic labels for Visibility Scenario (consistently visible vs. partially occluded) and GT Scenario (all target instances captured vs. subset) enable fine-grained performance analysis.

**Practical Baseline for Multi-view Reasoning (Section 5, Appendix I):**
A baseline model is proposed, integrating a 3D reconstruction model (VGGT) into a VLM framework.
- **Visual Encoder:** A pretrained CLIP ViT extracts patch-level visual tokens from each view. These tokens are concatenated across all views.
- **Geometric Encoder:** VGGT processes multi-view images to produce geometry-aware spatial features and camera tokens. These are concatenated to form geometric tokens.
- **Cross-attention Fusion:** Visual tokens and geometric tokens are fused via cross-attention to obtain geometry-aware visual tokens. These fused tokens are then projected and concatenated with question tokens before being fed into a large language model backbone for answer generation.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents several key findings from analyses and experiments conducted on SpatialMosaic and other benchmarks.

**Analysis of Challenging Conditions (Section 4, Appendix A):**
The analysis confirms that the proposed conditions (partial visibility, low-overlap, and occlusion) introduce significant difficulty for current VLMs.
- **Partial Visibility:** Tasks requiring reasoning about objects that are *never co-visible* in a single frame are substantially harder than when source and target objects are jointly visible (Figure 12). This demonstrates that partial visibility prevents reliance on single-view shortcuts and necessitates cross-view integration.
- **Low-Overlap:** As the overlap ratio between frames decreases, the ability of models to establish cross-view correspondence diminishes. Attention mechanisms struggle to align with target objects under low overlap, often diffusing to context or unrelated regions (Figures 5, 13). Quantitatively, VQA accuracy decreases with lower overlap ratios (Figures 4a, 14). Moreover, tasks requiring *multi-hop aggregation* (connecting non-overlapping query and target-visible views via intermediate views) exhibit the lowest performance, indicating increased difficulty when direct query-reference alignment is not possible (Figure 15).
- **Occlusion:** Increased occlusion severely degrades object grounding and spatial reasoning. Under high occlusion, models' attention shifts away from the target object toward visible context or distractors (Figures 5, 16). Both VQA accuracy (Figures 4b, 17) and grounding mIoU (Figure 18) decline as occlusion ratios increase, indicating that obscured visual cues weaken target identification and subsequent inference.
- **Object Scale and Duplicate Instances (Appendix A.1):** Additional analyses revealed that smaller object scales (fewer visible pixels) lead to diffuse attention and lower grounding quality (Figures 7, 8). While larger objects generally improve accuracy, high occlusion can still hinder reasoning (Figure 9). Furthermore, the presence of multiple instances of the same object category (duplicates) makes cross-view correspondence ambiguous, causing attention to spread among candidates (Figure 10) and decreasing object counting accuracy and grounding mIoU (Figure 11).

**Evaluation on SpatialMosaic-Indoor (Section 6.1, Table 2):**
The SpatialMosaic-Bench presents a challenging evaluation for existing MLLMs under partial visibility, occlusion, and minimal overlap.
- The model fine-tuned on SpatialMosaic (`LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B + SpatialMosaic`) achieved an average performance of 67.6%, significantly outperforming all other open-sourced VLMs (e.g., Qwen3-VL-8B at 48.4%, VILA-1.5-40B at 43.1%).
- Human performance on a reduced subset (SpatialMosaic-tiny) scored 25.5% for numerical tasks and 54.5% for multiple-choice tasks, providing context for the benchmark's inherent difficulty.

**Evaluation on VSI-Bench (Section 6.2, Table 3):**
The proposed practical baseline, `LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B + VGGT`, demonstrated strong performance on VSI-Bench, a benchmark for conventional spatial understanding.
- This model achieved the highest overall performance with an average of 59.6%, surpassing larger proprietary models (GPT-4o, Gemini-1.5 Pro) and 72B-scale open-source models (e.g., LLaVA-NeXT-Video-72B at 40.9%) by a substantial margin (18.7% over the best 72B model).
- This indicates that the integration of geometry encoders (VGGT) enhances spatial reasoning not only in challenging multi-view settings but also in more standard spatial tasks.

**Evaluation on SpatialMosaic-Outdoor (Section 6.3, Table 4):**
The fine-tuned models were evaluated in a zero-shot setting on SpatialMosaic-Outdoor (Waymo dataset) to assess out-of-domain generalization.
- The `LLaVA-NeXT-Video-7B + VGGT + SpatialMosaic` model achieved the highest average performance of 57.7%.
- Despite the domain shift from indoor training data, the fine-tuned models maintained strong performance, suggesting that the learned spatial representations and the geometry encoder facilitate robust out-of-domain spatial reasoning and mitigate layout-specific biases.

**Ablation Studies (Section 6.4, Table 5, Appendix B Table 6):**
Ablation studies confirmed the contribution of the geometry encoder.
- Removing the geometric features consistently degraded performance across both SpatialMosaic-Bench and VSI-Bench.
- The performance drop was particularly pronounced in tasks requiring explicit spatial reasoning, such as object attribute understanding, spatial relations, and directional reasoning, reinforcing the importance of geometric cues for robust multi-view spatial understanding in both indoor and outdoor environments.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work addresses a recognized gap in the capabilities of current MLLMs to perform robust spatial reasoning under the complex visual conditions prevalent in real-world environments. By systematically defining and operationalizing partial visibility, occlusion, and low-overlap scenarios, the authors provide a framework for advancing multi-view understanding.

The introduction of **SpatialMosaic** and **SpatialMosaic-Bench** represents a significant contribution to the field of vision-language models. This comprehensive dataset, with its 2 million QA pairs spanning diverse indoor and outdoor scenes, offers a scalable and challenging resource for training and rigorously evaluating MLLMs. Its explicit focus on geometrically grounded, multi-view tasks under difficult conditions is intended to guide future model development toward more resilient and generalizable spatial intelligence. The diagnostic capabilities embedded within the benchmark allow for fine-grained analysis of model failures, which could inform targeted improvements in areas like cross-view correspondence, instance grounding, and multi-hop evidence aggregation.

The proposed practical baseline, which integrates geometry encoders (VGGT) into a VLM framework, demonstrates that incorporating explicit geometric cues can substantially enhance multi-view spatial reasoning. The experimental results, showing this baseline outperforming much larger and more complex models on both novel and existing benchmarks, highlight the effectiveness of geometry-aware architectures. This finding suggests a promising direction for developing more efficient and performant MLLMs without necessarily relying on increased parameter counts.

The demonstrated ability of models fine-tuned on SpatialMosaic to generalize to out-of-domain outdoor scenes in a zero-shot manner underscores the potential for learning transferable spatial representations. This capability is critical for deploying MLLMs in varied real-world applications, from robotics and autonomous navigation to augmented reality, where scene layouts and conditions can differ significantly.

Overall, this research is significant for its potential to:
- **Elevate MLLM robustness**: By training and evaluating models under realistic, imperfect visual conditions, the work aims to bridge the gap between current MLLM performance and human-level spatial reasoning in complex 3D environments.
- **Foster new architectural designs**: The success of the geometry-infused baseline may encourage further exploration of hybrid architectures that integrate explicit geometric understanding with neural network capabilities.
- **Expand real-world applicability**: Improved spatial reasoning under partial visibility and occlusion is crucial for applications requiring agents to interpret dynamic and cluttered environments.

While acknowledging limitations such as the focus on a limited set of source datasets and the challenge of fully capturing open-ended real-world complexities, this work lays foundational groundwork for developing MLLMs capable of more coherent 3D scene understanding from fragmented visual inputs, thereby enhancing their scalability and applicability in real-world intelligent systems.
