# DaViNCi: A Dataset Towards Outdoor Vision-and-Language Navigation with Continuous Actions and Dynamic Elements

> [!summary] Summary
> DaViNCi is the first outdoor vision-and-language navigation dataset that is both continuous and dynamic. Built in CARLA across six towns, it tracks autonomous-vehicle trajectories into 6,933 path-instruction pairs, renders first-person views online, and spawns random vehicles and pedestrians. Instructions come from a VLM pipeline with turn-event priors. A reinforcement-learning baseline, COVL-RL, navigates via continuous actions, and success rates drop over 10% versus prior datasets.

## Problem

- Most vision-and-language navigation datasets are indoor, where visual and linguistic elements are sparser, so they do not transfer directly to outdoor driving scenes.
- Existing outdoor datasets constrain agents to fixed discrete topological graphs, ignoring real vehicle mobility and hiding traffic regulations that govern legal maneuvers.
- Prior outdoor data is statically pre-collected, so imagery at a location never changes over time, mismatching rapidly changing streets and blocking sim-to-real transfer.

## Method

- Dataset construction tracks ==CARLA== autonomous vehicles at one-second intervals across six towns, filters collisions and traffic violations, yielding 6,933 continuous ground-truth paths spanning intersections, roundabouts, and lane changes.
- A ==two-stage instruction pipeline== extracts ==turn events== from trajectories as priors, then prompts a VLM on first-person driving video for landmark-grounded instructions, refined by manual fine-tuning.
- **COVL-RL**, a context-aware online vision-language RL baseline, conditions a ==CLIP-Transformer-LSTM== policy on instruction, live image, and past vision-action history over a seven-way action space including lane-change and stop.

## Results

- On the discretized benchmark, FLAME+Mem4Nav reaches **31.3** task completion on town01 versus **48.5** on Touchdown and **60.4** on Map2Seq, a drop exceeding **10** points.
- In the continuous dynamic setting, COVL-RL scores **5.8-18.2%** success and **28.0-48.0%** path completion, while random policies achieve **0.0-0.3%** success and about **1-3%** completion.
- Ablations: raising dynamic elements from none to **30** vehicles plus **30** pedestrians cuts town01 success from **17.9%** to **13.2%**, while **3m/4m/5m** action granularity changes results only marginally.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Discretizing a navigation benchmark hides difficulty; the same routes become substantially harder once agents must emit continuous actions under live rendering.
> - Dynamic traffic hurts mainly by occluding the agent's field of view, so robustness to partial visual defects matters more than finer control resolution.
> - Action granularity trades information density against episode length, producing a non-linear, largely flat effect rather than a monotonic accuracy gain.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) is a research area focused on enabling intelligent agents to navigate environments by comprehending natural language instructions and utilizing visual observations. Initially, VLN research concentrated on indoor environments, as evidenced by datasets such as R2R, RR, CVDN, REVERIE, SOON, and VLN-CE. These datasets have facilitated the development of numerous methods aimed at advancing agents' capabilities in multimodal tasks.

The field has subsequently expanded to include outdoor environments, with datasets like TouchDown and Talk2Nav being constructed using real-world imagery such as Google Street View. However, existing outdoor VLN datasets possess characteristics that limit their applicability to real-world vehicle navigation. These limitations primarily include:

1. **Fixed Discrete Topological Graphs:** Most outdoor VLN datasets constrain an agent's movement to a predefined network of discrete waypoints and accessible edges. This approach does not accurately reflect the continuous nature of vehicle movement in physical environments and may obscure specific traffic regulations.
2. **Absence of Dynamic Elements:** The visual information in existing outdoor datasets is typically static, meaning environmental elements at any given location remain unchanged over time. This fixed representation is not compatible with the constantly changing and unpredictable nature of real-world outdoor environments, which feature dynamic elements like other vehicles, pedestrians, and varying traffic conditions.

These limitations collectively hinder the "sim-to-real" transferability of VLN agents, making methods developed on such datasets less practical for deployment in autonomous vehicles operating in complex, dynamic outdoor settings.

The DaViNCi dataset positions itself within this landscape by addressing these specific shortcomings. It aims to bridge the gap between simplified outdoor VLN datasets and the complexities of real-world navigation by simultaneously incorporating continuous action spaces and dynamic environmental elements. This work seeks to advance the frontier of outdoor VLN research by providing a more realistic and challenging benchmark, thereby fostering the development of more robust and adaptable navigation agents for autonomous vehicles. It introduces a paradigm shift from discrete, static representations to continuous, dynamic representations in outdoor VLN.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to propose and establish DaViNCi (Dynamic Vision-and-Language Navigation in Continuous Environment), a novel dataset designed to advance Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) capabilities towards more realistic outdoor environments for autonomous vehicles.

The motivation behind creating DaViNCi stems from several critical limitations observed in existing VLN datasets, particularly those focusing on outdoor settings:

1. **Bridging the Reality Gap:** Current outdoor VLN datasets, despite expanding the scope beyond indoor environments, largely fail to accurately represent the complexity and variability of real-world outdoor scenarios. This discrepancy impedes the practical deployment and "sim-to-real" transfer of VLN agents from simulated or static environments to actual autonomous vehicles. The researchers are motivated by the need for autonomous vehicles to comprehend verbal instructions and navigate autonomously in unfamiliar destinations, a task made formidable by the gap in relevant, realistic datasets.

2. **Addressing Discrete Action Spaces:** A significant limitation of existing outdoor VLN datasets is their reliance on fixed discrete topological graphs. Agents navigate by moving between predefined, discrete waypoints. This model oversimplifies real-world vehicle mobility, which involves continuous actions (e.g., precise steering, acceleration, braking) and nuanced adherence to traffic regulations. The authors contend that this discrete representation prevents agents from learning fine-grained control and decision-making necessary for continuous driving.

3. **Incorporating Dynamic Environmental Elements:** Existing outdoor VLN datasets typically feature static environments where visual information at any given location remains constant. In contrast, real-world outdoor environments are inherently dynamic, characterized by the unpredictable movement of other vehicles, pedestrians, and changing light conditions. The absence of such dynamic elements in datasets prevents VLN agents from developing the crucial ability to perceive, react to, and navigate amidst evolving circumstances. This gap renders current VLN methods impractical for real-world scenarios where an agent must continuously adapt to its surroundings.

4. **Creating a Challenging Benchmark:** The researchers are motivated to provide a more challenging benchmark for VLN models. By introducing continuous actions and dynamic elements, DaViNCi aims to expose the limitations of current VLN methods and stimulate the development of more sophisticated algorithms capable of handling increased complexity, uncertainty, and the need for real-time adaptation. The goal is to move beyond environments where visual and linguistic elements are sparser, common in indoor settings, to the rich and complex sensory input of outdoor environments.

In summary, the key objective is to address these deficiencies by providing a dataset where agents operate with continuous actions in dynamic, complex outdoor environments, thereby fostering the development of VLN agents that are better equipped for real-world application in autonomous driving.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The DaViNCi dataset was constructed using the CARLA simulator, a platform designed for autonomous driving research. The methodology focuses on generating continuous trajectories, natural language instructions, and incorporating dynamic environmental elements.

#### 3.1. Construction Method

##### 3.1.1. Scenario Selection

Six distinct outdoor maps from the CARLA simulator were chosen: `town01`, `town02`, `town03`, `town04`, `town05`, and `town10`. These maps encompass a range of outdoor driving scenarios, from rural areas (`town01`, `town02`) with narrow horizons and fewer characteristic elements, to simple town structures (`town03`) with roundabouts and varied terrain, to highways (`town04`), and complex urban areas (`town05`, `town10`) rich in landmarks like buildings, traffic signs, and billboards. The scenarios were designed to include interactions with traffic lights, signs, other vehicles, pedestrians, cyclists, negotiations at signalized/non-signalized intersections, roundabouts, and lane changes, referencing the CARLA AD Leaderboard. This diversity aims to provide rich visual features for localization and challenge path planning and memory capabilities.

##### 3.1.2. Data Collection and Annotation

- **Path Generation:** Ground-truth paths were generated by tracking autonomous vehicles in CARLA. Initially, the environment was devoid of other dynamic elements. A random autonomous vehicle was initialized to navigate freely. Its position information was collected at one-second intervals, connecting these waypoints to form continuous trajectories. This process yielded 6,933 paths, distributed across the selected towns to ensure comprehensive map coverage.

- **Instruction Generation and Alignment:** First-person driving videos recorded during path generation served as source material. A two-stage framework was employed, leveraging a Visual Language Model (VLM):
 1. Turn events were accurately extracted from trajectory data, providing prior constraints.
 2. Multi-modal visual analysis was integrated to generate structured navigation instructions.
 Manual fine-tuning was then applied to refine the VLM-generated instructions, ensuring they were aligned with driving logic and included relevant landmark details, resulting in natural language navigation sequences.

- **Visual Information Acquisition:** Unlike previous datasets that rely on pre-collected panoramic images at fixed waypoints, DaViNCi emphasizes dynamic environments. The system provides first-person perspective RGB images (1280x720 resolution) in real-time whenever the agent requests visual information during navigation. This real-time acquisition allows for variable visual information at the same location, fulfilling the dynamic requirements of the dataset.

##### 3.1.3. Dynamic Elements of Environment

After establishing the static path-instruction pairs, dynamic elements were introduced into the simulated world. At the beginning of each experimental trial, other autonomous vehicles and pedestrians were spawned randomly. These entities operate based on programmatic rules, ensuring that the agent encounters varying environmental information even when traversing identical paths. This feature is intended to challenge an agent's ability to adapt to changing conditions.

##### 3.1.4. Quality Control

To maintain consistency and quality, the same Large Language Model (LLM) and prompts were used for instruction generation. Preliminary screening was conducted to identify and remove navigation text descriptions with logical inconsistencies or unreasonable content. Path instances resulting from collisions or traffic violations were also eliminated, leading to 6,933 high-quality path-instruction pairs.

#### 3.2. Experimentation Approach

The research involved two main experimental setups: discrete and continuous environments.

##### 3.2.1. Discrete Environmental Experiments

- **Discretization Adaptation:** A subset of DaViNCi maps (`town01`, `town02`, `town03`, `town10`) was discretized into fixed topological graph structures, emulating the format of datasets like Touchdown. Different granularities were applied: 5 meters for `town01`, `town02`, `town10` (finer detail), and 12 meters for `town03` (coarser, similar to previous outdoor VLN datasets). Continuous driving paths were converted into discrete point sequences.
- **Experiment Setup:** Trajectories from these four maps were randomly split into training (approximately 80%), validation (10%), and test (10%) sets, ensuring similar distributions of path lengths and instruction complexities.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Three standard metrics were used: Trajectory Completion (TC), Shortest Path Distance (SPD), and normalized Dynamic Time Warping (nDTW).
- **Implementation:** Various existing VLN models (GA, Rconcat, VLN-trans, ORAR, VELMA, FLAME, with and without Mem4Nav) were trained using an optimizer with a learning rate of 1.5x10^-3 and a batch size of 16 on four NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs.

##### 3.2.2. Continuous Environmental Experiments (COVL-RL)

- **COVL-RL Framework:** A Reinforcement Learning (RL)-based framework, COVL-RL (Context-Aware Online Vision-Language Reinforcement Learning), was developed to enable agents to dynamically respond to environmental changes and learn continuously from interactive experience in the continuous environment.
- **Architecture:** The framework involves:
 1. **Feature Extraction:** Global textual instructions are processed using CLIP, current visual observations (first-person RGB images) are processed by CLIP, and historical trajectory context (past visual inputs and actions) is processed by a Transformer and an LSTM.
 2. **Navigation Loop:** The agent infers the next action based on integrated features. Actions are continuous and include `sharpleft`, `left`, `straight`, `right`, `sharpright`, `lanechange`, and `stop`. Available actions are dynamically constrained by real-time road conditions.
 3. **Policy Update:** The agent's policy is updated based on rewards and loss from simulator interactions.
- **Data Composition:** The agent receives visual (V_t) and textual (I) inputs. The agent's state includes spatial coordinates (x,y,z) and orientation angles (yaw, pitch).
- **Experiment Setup:** The `town10` map was designated as the training environment (6:1:1 split for training, validation, test), while the remaining five maps served as unseen test scenes to assess generalization.
- **Evaluation Metrics:** Adapted metrics for continuous environments: Average Navigation Error (ANE, mean Euclidean distance to target), Success Rate (SR, percentage of instances within a 10-meter radius of the endpoint), and Average Path Completion (APC, proportion of the reference path traversed).
- **Implementation:** A learning rate of 3x10^-4 was used. Actions were executed with a step size of 4 meters per timestep. The default dynamic environment included 30 dynamic pedestrians and 30 vehicles. Experiments also investigated the impact of different granularities (3m, 4m, 5m per action) and varying numbers of dynamic elements (None, 10 vehicles/10 pedestrians, 30 vehicles/30 pedestrians).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The study presented several findings regarding the characteristics of the DaViNCi dataset and the performance of VLN models on both discrete and continuous versions of the environment.

#### 4.1. Data Statistics

- **Path Length:** The dataset comprises 6,933 trajectories across six maps, with path lengths ranging from approximately 200m to 800m. The distribution of path lengths varies per map, with general concentrations around specific values, indicating routes of different difficulty and type. Longer-distance navigation is generally considered more challenging.
- **Text Length:** Instruction text lengths in DaViNCi are generally longer than those in the Touchdown dataset. Directional terms (e.g., "turn left," "turn right," "straight") appear with high frequency, contributing to action guidance. Landmark-related terms are also prevalent, indicating their role in aiding agent localization. The longer text length and complex vocabulary aim to challenge text-vision alignment.

#### 4.2. Discrete Environmental Experiments

- **Performance Comparison:** Existing VLN models, when evaluated on the discretized DaViNCi dataset, generally exhibited lower performance metrics (TC, SPD, nDTW) compared to their performance on previous datasets like Touchdown and Map2Seq. Specifically, the success rate on DaViNCi decreased by more than 10% compared to previous datasets. This outcome indicates that DaViNCi introduces new challenges for current VLN methods.
- **Granularity Impact (Discrete):** The `town03` map, which utilized a coarser granularity (12 meters), achieved comparatively superior experimental results. This was attributed to a reduction in the complexity of information the agent had to process due to the larger step size.
- **Path Length Correlation:** Performance on the other three maps (`town01`, `town02`, `town10`), which used a finer 5-meter granularity, showed a slight decrease, with a positive correlation between Trajectory Completion (TC) variation and path length. This further underscored the challenging nature of long-distance navigation within the VLN task.

#### 4.3. Continuous Environmental Experiments (COVL-RL)

- **Baseline Performance:** The proposed COVL-RL baseline method demonstrated a capacity to complete tasks on certain trajectories, achieving a considerable path completion rate, in contrast to random navigation approaches which yielded virtually no successful cases and extremely poor path completion.
- **Challenge of Continuous Dynamic Environment:** The success rates observed in the continuous dynamic environment experiments were lower compared to those in the discrete environment experiments. This reflects the increased challenges posed by the continuous nature of actions and the dynamic elements in the environment for VLN tasks.
- **Generalization to Unseen Environments:** Performance on unseen maps (those not used in training) was generally inferior to that on the seen environment (`town10`). This indicates a challenge for the model's generalization capabilities when encountering novel environmental configurations and visual cues.

- **Impact of Granularity (Continuous Actions):** Experiments conducted with the agent executing actions at different granularities (3m, 4m, 5m per action) showed that variations in granularity did not lead to significant changes in overall experimental performance (ANE, SR, APC). The researchers posited that this could be due to a trade-off: finer granularity provides more environmental information but increases the number of action steps required, while coarser granularity reduces steps but might lead to a loss of detail. This trade-off resulted in a non-linear impact on task performance.

- **Impact of Dynamic Elements:** An increase in the number of dynamic elements (vehicles and pedestrians) in the environment directly corresponded to a progressive decline in the agent's task completion performance. This finding confirms that dynamic elements genuinely affect the agent's ability to process environmental information and pose new challenges for VLN tasks. Case analysis suggested that the challenge partly stems from the obstruction of the agent's partial field of view by these dynamic elements, requiring the agent to adapt to such visual limitations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The DaViNCi dataset introduces several advancements with potential impacts on the field of Vision-and-Language Navigation (VLN) and embodied AI:

1. **Paradigm Shift in Outdoor VLN:** DaViNCi represents a significant shift from the established paradigm of discrete, static environments to continuous, dynamic representations in outdoor VLN datasets. This fundamental change is critical for bridging the fidelity gap between simulated environments and the complexities of the real world.

2. **Enhanced Realism for Autonomous Navigation:** By incorporating continuous action spaces and dynamic elements (such as other vehicles and pedestrians) that interact with the agent's field of view, DaViNCi more accurately simulates the challenges faced by autonomous vehicles in urban and rural settings. This enhanced realism provides a more relevant testing ground for developing navigation agents.

3. **Increased Challenge and Robustness Development:** The experimental results demonstrate that DaViNCi is considerably more challenging than previous outdoor VLN datasets, evidenced by a notable decrease in success rates for existing models. This increased difficulty necessitates the development of more robust, adaptive, and intelligent VLN agents capable of handling unpredictable situations, continuous control, and dynamic visual information.

4. **Advancement of Sim-to-Real Transfer:** A primary motivation for DaViNCi is to improve the "sim-to-real" transferability of VLN agents. By creating a simulated environment that closely mirrors real-world outdoor conditions, the dataset facilitates the training of agents that are more likely to perform effectively when deployed in physical autonomous vehicles, moving VLN research closer to practical application.

5. **Catalyst for New Research Directions:** DaViNCi is expected to stimulate new research avenues in several areas:
 - **Continuous Control:** Encouraging the development of VLN models that can output and execute continuous actions, moving beyond discrete waypoints.
 - **Dynamic Scene Understanding:** Fostering algorithms capable of perceiving, tracking, and predicting the behavior of dynamic agents in complex environments.
 - **Robust Multimodal Fusion:** Driving innovations in integrating visual and linguistic information under conditions of partial occlusion or rapidly changing scenes.
 - **Generalization Capabilities:** The findings regarding unseen environments highlight the need for models with better generalization abilities to novel scenarios.

In summary, DaViNCi provides a timely and necessary resource for the VLN community, pushing the boundaries of what VLN agents can accomplish in environments that better reflect the challenges of real-world autonomous navigation.
