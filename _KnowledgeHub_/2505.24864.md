# ProRL: Prolonged Reinforcement Learning Expands Reasoning Boundaries in Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> NVIDIA researchers developed Prolonged Reinforcement Learning (ProRL) to expand large language models' reasoning capabilities beyond their initial pre-training. This method, featuring KL divergence control and reference policy resetting on diverse tasks, enables significant performance gains, including average pass@1 improvements of up to 54.8% on logic puzzles, and the discovery of novel solution pathways.

## Problem

- The community debates whether reinforcement learning genuinely expands a large language model's intrinsic reasoning capabilities or merely amplifies pre-existing knowledge.
- Prior research, often focused on specialized domains like mathematics, suggested RL's impact was limited to refining existing solutions rather than discovering new ones.
- Many existing RL experiments for LLMs utilized insufficient training steps, potentially hindering the full exploration of novel reasoning strategies.

## Method

- Develop ==Prolonged Reinforcement Learning (ProRL)==, an extended and stable RL training methodology for LLMs based on ==Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)==.
- Integrate key enhancements like a ==KL divergence penalty== with periodic ==reference policy resetting== to prevent policy drift and enable continuous exploration over long training durations.
- Utilize a large and diverse training dataset spanning five distinct reasoning domains to promote generalization and the discovery of new strategies.

## Results

- The ProRL-trained **1.5B** parameter model significantly outperforms its base model and even larger **7B** models across diverse reasoning benchmarks, with average pass@1 improvements up to **54.8%** on logic puzzles and **+14.7%** on math.
- Prolonged RL training enabled the discovery of novel solution pathways, achieving **100%** success on tasks where the base model had **0%** pass@k, and demonstrated continued performance scaling with increased computational budget (over **2,000** training steps).
- The model exhibits strong generalization capabilities, showing significant improvements on out-of-distribution tasks and maintaining higher accuracy on unseen, more difficult problem instances.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Reinforcement learning, when prolonged and carefully managed, can genuinely expand LLM reasoning capabilities and uncover novel solution pathways, refuting prior skepticism about its limitations.
> - Extended training duration, coupled with diverse task exposure, is crucial for fostering exploration, consolidating new strategies, and achieving significant performance gains.
> - The most substantial reasoning improvements from RL are observed in tasks where the base model initially performs poorly, indicating effective boundary expansion for challenging problems.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

#### 1.1. Executive Summary

This report analyzes "ProRL: Prolonged Reinforcement Learning Expands Reasoning Boundaries in Large Language Models," a research paper that challenges prevailing assumptions about the limitations of reinforcement learning (RL) in enhancing the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). The authors, a team from NVIDIA, introduce a novel training methodology called Prolonged Reinforcement Learning (ProRL) that incorporates KL divergence control, reference policy resetting, and training on a diverse suite of tasks. Their findings demonstrate that extended RL training can indeed uncover novel reasoning strategies previously inaccessible to base models, significantly outperforming them across various benchmarks, including tasks where base models completely fail. This work not only provides new insights into the conditions under which RL meaningfully expands reasoning boundaries but also establishes a foundation for future long-horizon RL research in LLMs, reaffirming RL's potential for developing more capable and generalizable AI systems.

#### 1.2. Research Landscape

The development of sophisticated reasoning capabilities in Large Language Models (LLMs) is a central and actively debated area in AI research. Recent breakthroughs, such as OpenAI-O1 and DeepSeek-R1, have showcased the potential of scaling test-time computation and leveraging reinforcement learning (RL) to induce complex reasoning behaviors, particularly in domains like mathematics and code generation. RL is seen as instrumental due to its ability to optimize against verifiable objective rewards, mitigating issues like reward hacking often associated with learned reward models.

However, a fundamental controversy has emerged within the community: Does RL genuinely *expand* an LLM's intrinsic reasoning capabilities, or does it merely *amplify* the sampling efficiency of solutions already latent within the base model's pre-existing distribution? Prior studies [13–15] have leaned towards the latter, suggesting that RL-trained models do not acquire new reasoning skills, and sometimes even show a decline in reasoning breadth (e.g., measured by pass@k metrics at higher 'k' values). These works have attributed RL's impact to a narrowing of the output distribution, making the model more confident in a subset of solutions it already "knows," rather than exploring novel solution pathways.

This paper directly confronts these skeptical viewpoints. The authors posit that the negative conclusions of previous research may be artifacts of methodological limitations, specifically:
1. **Over-reliance on specialized, potentially overtrained domains:** Many studies focus heavily on mathematics, where models might already be saturated during pre-training and supervised fine-tuning, leaving little room for RL to discover genuinely *new* strategies.
2. **Premature termination of RL training:** Existing research often involves limited RL training steps (e.g., hundreds), which the authors argue is insufficient for models to fully explore and develop novel reasoning capabilities.

"ProRL: Prolonged Reinforcement Learning" directly addresses these gaps. By extending RL training significantly and applying it across a diverse suite of tasks, the authors aim to provide empirical evidence that RL can indeed unlock novel reasoning strategies. This work attempts to shift the prevailing narrative by demonstrating that, under appropriate conditions (long training and diverse tasks), RL is not just an optimizer of existing knowledge but a catalyst for genuine capability expansion, akin to how RL has enabled superhuman performance and novel strategies in other domains like Go (AlphaGo) and Atari games. It contributes to the ongoing discussion about the true mechanisms of learning in LLMs and the optimal ways to integrate various training paradigms (pre-training, SFT, RL).

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to directly address and provide compelling evidence for the contentious question: **Does reinforcement learning truly expand a language model’s reasoning capabilities, or does it merely amplify high-reward outputs already latent in the base model’s distribution?**

The authors are motivated by the observation that despite the success of RL in state-of-the-art reasoning LLMs (like OpenAI-O1 and DeepSeek-R1), a segment of the research community remains unconvinced about RL's fundamental ability to imbue new reasoning skills. They specifically pinpoint two limitations in prior work that led to this skepticism:
1. **Narrow Domain Focus:** Previous studies often concentrate on highly specialized domains (e.g., mathematics) where base models might already be near their performance ceiling due to extensive pre-training and fine-tuning. This limited scope might restrict the observable impact of RL on *new* reasoning exploration.
2. **Insufficient Training Duration:** Many RL experiments terminate prematurely, after only a few hundred training steps, which the authors hypothesize is too short for LLMs to fully explore and consolidate novel reasoning strategies.

Against this backdrop, the key objectives of the "ProRL" work are to:
- **Develop a novel methodology for prolonged and stable RL training:** This involves introducing techniques to mitigate common RL training challenges like entropy collapse and instability over extended periods.
- **Demonstrate that prolonged RL training leads to performance improvements that reliably scale with increased compute:** This aims to challenge the notion that RL gains plateau quickly or even diminish with extended training.
- **Uncover and quantify the emergence of novel reasoning strategies:** This involves showing that the RL-trained model can produce solutions that are genuinely new and inaccessible to the base model, even with extensive sampling.
- **Show strong generalization to diverse and out-of-distribution (OOD) tasks:** This addresses the concern that RL might only specialize models for in-distribution tasks rather than enhancing general reasoning.
- **Provide open-source models and resources:** To support further research and reproducibility in the community.

In essence, the motivation is to leverage NVIDIA's deep expertise and computational resources to settle a critical debate in LLM research, thereby solidifying the role of RL as a powerful technique for genuine capability expansion rather than just fine-tuning.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this research revolves around **Prolonged Reinforcement Learning (ProRL)**, a novel training methodology designed to enable stable and extended RL training for LLMs, thereby fostering deeper exploration of reasoning strategies.

#### 3.1. Core RL Algorithm

The authors adopt **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** as their foundational RL algorithm. GRPO is a policy optimization method that, unlike Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), foregoes a separate value model (critic) and instead estimates advantages based on group scores. This approach simplifies the RL setup by directly optimizing the policy based on aggregated rewards from a batch of trajectories.

#### 3.2. ProRL Enhancements

To enable prolonged and stable training, ProRL integrates several critical enhancements:

- **Mitigating Entropy Collapse:** A key challenge in extended policy optimization is "entropy collapse," where the model's output distribution becomes overly peaked, limiting exploration and leading to stagnation.
 - **High Rollout Temperature:** While not a complete solution, they employ a high sampling temperature (1.2) during rollouts to encourage initial exploration.
 - **Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO) components:** They adopt elements from DAPO:
 - **Decoupled Clipping:** The PPO objective's clipping bounds ($\epsilon_{low}$ and $\epsilon_{high}$) are treated as separate hyperparameters, with $\epsilon_{high}$ set higher (0.4 vs. $\epsilon_{low}=0.2$). This "clip-higher" strategy promotes uplifting probabilities of previously unlikely tokens, encouraging broader exploration and helping to retain entropy.
 - **Dynamic Sampling:** Prompts for which the model consistently succeeds or fails (accuracy 1 or 0) are filtered out. This focuses training on "intermediate difficulty" examples, which provide the most informative learning signals and help maintain output diversity.
 - **KL Regularization and Reference Policy Reset:** This is a crucial ProRL innovation.
 - **KL Divergence Penalty:** A KL divergence penalty is explicitly incorporated into the GRPO objective ($L_{KL-RL}(\theta) = L_{GRPO}(\theta) - \beta D_{KL}(\pi_\theta || \pi_{ref})$). This penalty prevents the online policy ($\pi_\theta$) from drifting too far from a stable reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$), thereby stabilizing learning and mitigating overfitting, while also helping to maintain entropy. The authors argue this is beneficial even when starting from a strong, fine-tuned base model, unlike some prior works that suggest removing it.
 - **Reference Policy Reset:** To counter the KL term's potential to dominate the loss and diminish policy updates over time, the reference policy ($\pi_{ref}$) is periodically hard-reset to a more recent snapshot of the online policy ($\pi_\theta$), and optimizer states are reinitialized. This allows the model to continue exploring and improving while retaining the benefits of KL regularization, preventing premature convergence.

#### 3.3. Training Data and Setup

- **Base Model:** The training starts from `DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B`, a relatively small (1.5B parameters) but capable reasoning-focused checkpoint.
- **Diverse Training Dataset:** A key aspect is the use of a large and diverse verifiable training dataset comprising 136K problems across five distinct task domains:
 - **Math:** 40K problems from DeepScaleR dataset (binary reward).
 - **Code:** 24K problems from Eurus-2-RL dataset (continuous reward based on test case pass rate).
 - **STEM:** 25K filtered problems from SCP-116K dataset (binary reward, GPT-4o judged alignment with ground truth).
 - **Logical Puzzles:** 37K synthetic samples from Reasoning Gym (continuous reward).
 - **Instruction Following:** 10K synthetically generated samples from Llama-Nemotron (continuous reward).
 This broad coverage is hypothesized to be crucial for generalization and enabling the discovery of new reasoning patterns.
- **Training Infrastructure:** Training was conducted on 4 NVIDIA-H100-80GB nodes, accumulating approximately 16k GPU hours.
- **Hyperparameters:** Key settings include `n=16` responses sampled per prompt, context window limit of 8096 tokens (increased to 16k in final stages), sampling temperature of 1.2, batch size of 256, mini-batch size of 64, and AdamW optimizer with a constant learning rate of $2 \times 10^{-6}$.
- **Training Dynamics Management:** A blended validation set was used for monitoring. Reference model and optimizer resets were performed when validation performance stagnated or degraded. Reward shaping was introduced to penalize improper response termination.

This comprehensive approach aims to create a robust and stable environment for prolonged RL, allowing the LLM to explore and solidify new reasoning capabilities across a wide range of tasks.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents compelling evidence that ProRL successfully expands the reasoning boundaries of LLMs, challenging the previous skeptical viewpoints.

#### 4.1. Performance Benchmarks

- **Overall Superiority:** The `Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B` model, trained with ProRL, consistently and significantly outperforms its base model, `DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-1.5B`, across all evaluated domains.
 - Average pass@1 improvements: +14.7% on math, +13.9% on coding, +54.8% on logic puzzles, +25.1% on STEM reasoning, and +18.1% on instruction-following tasks.
- **Scaling with Compute:** The model demonstrates continued performance improvements even after an unprecedented 2,000+ training steps (up to 16k GPU hours), suggesting that RL training scales effectively with increased computational budget, contrary to previous observations of early saturation.
- **Competitive with Larger Models:** Notably, the 1.5B parameter ProRL model achieves comparable or even superior performance to the much larger `DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B` across multiple domains.
- **Generalist vs. Specialized Models:** The ProRL generalist model even surpasses domain-specialized 1.5B baselines like `DeepScaleR-1.5B` (math, +4.6% pass@1) and `DeepCoder-1.5B` (code, +6.5% pass@1), highlighting its strong generalization capabilities.

#### 4.2. Novelty and Capability Expansion

- **Discovery of Novel Solution Pathways:** A crucial finding is that ProRL enables the discovery of "genuinely new solution pathways entirely absent in base models." The authors identify numerous tasks where the base model produces zero correct solutions (0% pass@k regardless of sampling), while the ProRL-trained model achieves 100% pass rates. This directly refutes the "mere amplification" argument.
- **Creativity Index:** Quantifying the novelty of reasoning trajectories using the Creativity Index, the study shows that prolonged RL training leads to trajectories with higher novelty (Figure 1, Middle), indicating the emergence of new, less overlapped reasoning patterns compared to pretraining data.
- **Correlation with Base Model Performance:** A strong negative correlation is observed between the base model's initial performance (pass@128) and the extent of improvement from RL. This means RL is most effective in expanding reasoning boundaries for tasks where the base model initially struggles. Conversely, for tasks where the base model already performs well, RL tends to narrow the reasoning boundary, sharpening the output distribution at the expense of exploration (Diminish trend in Pass@k analysis).

#### 4.3. Generalization to OOD and Difficulty

- **Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Tasks:** The ProRL model demonstrates significant improvements on OOD tasks from Reasoning Gym (e.g., `boxnet`), which were not seen during training. For `boxnet`, the base model had 0% pass@k, while ProRL achieved close to 100% pass@k at higher 'k' values, showcasing a clear expansion in reasoning boundary for unseen challenges.
- **Increased Task Difficulty:** The model generalizes well to increased task difficulty. On the `graph_color` task, where training only included graphs of size 10, the ProRL model maintains significantly higher accuracy across larger (unseen) graph sizes compared to both base and intermediate models, indicating improved robustness to complexity.

#### 4.4. Pass@1 Distribution Evolution

- **Significant Distribution Shifts:** Unlike some previous works that observed declining pass@k, the ProRL approach shows continuous improvement in both pass@1 and pass@16. Analysis of pass@1 distributions (Figures 7, 14-17) reveals significant rightward shifts, particularly for challenging tasks like `codeforces` and `family_relationships`. This indicates a successful transition from low or zero accuracy to peaking at perfect accuracy for a majority of prompts, confirming that extended RL training drives substantial gains in expected pass@1, overcoming any negative effects from increased variance.
- **Three Regimes of Pass@k Evolution:** The authors categorize pass@k trends into:
 - **Diminish:** For tasks where the base model is already strong, RL might narrow the diversity, though pass@1 might still improve.
 - **Plateau:** Gains in reasoning breadth quickly saturate early in training.
 - **Sustained:** For complex tasks (e.g., coding), ProRL shows continued improvements in reasoning capacity with prolonged training, requiring extensive exploration.

In summary, the results provide strong empirical backing for the claim that prolonged and carefully managed RL training can genuinely expand LLMs' reasoning capabilities, leading to more robust, generalizable, and even novel problem-solving skills.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The findings presented in "ProRL: Prolonged Reinforcement Learning Expands Reasoning Boundaries in Large Language Models" hold significant implications for the broader AI community and the trajectory of LLM development.

**Challenging Previous Assumptions:** The most immediate and profound impact is the direct refutation of recent claims that reinforcement learning merely optimizes existing model behaviors or that its benefits quickly plateau. By meticulously demonstrating how prolonged, stable RL training, combined with diverse datasets, leads to the discovery of genuinely novel reasoning strategies and significant performance gains, this research shifts the narrative. It provides a strong counter-argument to the skepticism surrounding RL's capacity to fundamentally enhance LLM intelligence, affirming its role as a tool for true capability expansion.

**Reaffirming RL's Value:** This work unequivocally reaffirms the critical value of reinforcement learning as a pathway toward more capable and generalizable AI systems. It aligns LLM development more closely with the successes of RL in other domains (e.g., AlphaGo), where agents developed superhuman strategies previously unknown to humans. This suggests that LLMs, too, can, through sustained exploration, develop new knowledge and reasoning patterns that transcend their initial pre-training.

**Guidance for Future RL Research:** The methodology introduced, ProRL, provides a robust recipe for conducting long-horizon RL training. Key techniques like KL divergence control and periodic reference policy resetting are shown to be crucial for maintaining training stability and preventing entropy collapse over extended periods. This offers valuable practical guidance for researchers and practitioners attempting to implement RL for LLMs, encouraging them to invest in longer training durations and more sophisticated stability mechanisms. The emphasis on diverse training datasets also underscores the importance of breadth in problem-solving exposure for fostering generalizable reasoning.

**Democratization of Advanced AI Capabilities:** By achieving state-of-the-art reasoning performance with a relatively small 1.5B parameter model (Nemotron-Research-Reasoning-Qwen-1.5B), the paper opens doors for wider adoption and deployment of advanced AI. Smaller, highly capable models require fewer computational resources for both training and inference, reducing energy consumption and operational costs. This can democratize access to sophisticated AI, making it available to organizations and researchers with limited budgets, as well as enabling deployment in edge devices or privacy-sensitive on-premises environments (e.g., healthcare, finance).

**Potential for Broader Societal Benefit:** The enhanced reasoning capabilities demonstrated by ProRL could lead to significant advancements in real-world applications. This includes:
- **Scientific Discovery:** A model that can explore and internalize abstract reasoning patterns could accelerate scientific research by generating novel hypotheses or solving complex problems in fields like material science or drug discovery.
- **Advanced Problem Solving:** Improved general reasoning can enhance AI performance in critical domains like complex engineering design, logistics optimization, or even climate modeling.
- **AI Assistants:** More capable and efficient reasoning models can power smarter AI assistants, educational tools, or specialized expert systems, boosting human productivity and access to information.

**Ethical Considerations and Future Directions:** The paper acknowledges limitations, such as the high computational cost still required for *prolonged* training, and scalability concerns to much larger models. It also thoughtfully addresses societal impacts, emphasizing both the benefits (democratization, efficiency) and the ethical challenges (resource inequality, potential for misuse of enhanced capabilities). This highlights the need for ongoing research into value alignment and the development of dynamic evaluation benchmarks to monitor emergent behaviors and risks as AI systems become more capable.

In conclusion, "ProRL" represents a significant step forward in understanding and leveraging reinforcement learning for LLMs. It not only provides strong empirical evidence for RL's capacity to expand reasoning boundaries but also sets a new benchmark for what can be achieved with efficient, prolonged training, paving the way for the next generation of highly intelligent and broadly applicable AI systems.
