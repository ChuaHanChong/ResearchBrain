# Aligning Transformers with Continuous Feedback via Energy Rank Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University introduced Energy Rank Alignment (ERA), a gradient-based algorithm that directly aligns autoregressive transformers to explicit, continuous reward functions for tasks in molecular and protein design. ERA demonstrates superior sample efficiency and diversity compared to existing methods in molecular docking, while also robustly guiding protein language models for directed evolution.

## Problem

- Efficiently searching combinatorially vast chemical and biological spaces to discover compounds or sequences with specific, desired properties using generative models.
- Limitations of current generative model alignment techniques (RLHF, DPO) when dealing with explicit, continuous reward functions common in scientific discovery, often requiring extensive preference data or leading to mode collapse.
- Generating diverse and chemically/biologically valid outputs while optimizing for quantifiable metrics remains a challenge for generative models.

## Method

- Proposed **Energy Rank Alignment** (ERA), a gradient-based method for directly optimizing autoregressive transformer policies using explicit, continuous reward functions.
- ERA formulates an objective function based on a free energy functional, integrating a negative reward (energy), an entropic term for exploration, and a Kullback-Leibler regularization term to stay close to a reference policy.
- A practical loss function, L_ERA, is derived as a KL divergence between an entropy-regularized target preference distribution (incorporating reward and reference policy) and the policy's parametric preference distribution.

## Results

- ERA successfully aligned molecular generators to produce desired distributions for various chemical properties (e.g., QED, LogP, Ring Count) and effectively managed multi-objective optimization.
- In molecular docking tasks, ERA achieved comparable or superior mean docking scores to state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating significantly higher diversity and sample efficiency for top-scoring molecules.
- The algorithm successfully guided the ESM3-**1.4B** protein language model to generate TrpB protein mutants with substantially increased EVmutation scores, indicating applicability to biomolecule engineering.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ERA's theoretical foundation demonstrates that its minimizer asymptotically converges to an ideal Gibbs-Boltzmann distribution, where the reward function acts as an energy, offering interpretable control over generated output entropy and diversity.
> - The algorithm provides independent, tunable control over the reward's influence (via inverse temperature "beta") and regularization towards the reference policy (via "gamma"), allowing fine-grained balancing of exploitation and exploration.
> - Unlike DPO, ERA's direct utilization of explicit reward functions contributes to greater stability and diversity of generated samples, mitigating issues like mode collapse particularly in scenarios with continuous feedback.
