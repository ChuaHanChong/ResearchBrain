# Aligning Transformers with Continuous Feedback via Energy Rank Alignment

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at Stanford University introduced Energy Rank Alignment (ERA), a gradient-based algorithm that directly aligns autoregressive transformers to explicit, continuous reward functions for tasks in molecular and protein design. ERA demonstrates superior sample efficiency and diversity compared to existing methods in molecular docking, while also robustly guiding protein language models for directed evolution.

## Problem

- Efficiently searching combinatorially vast chemical and biological spaces to discover compounds or sequences with specific, desired properties using generative models.
- Limitations of current generative model alignment techniques (RLHF, DPO) when dealing with explicit, continuous reward functions common in scientific discovery, often requiring extensive preference data or leading to mode collapse.
- Generating diverse and chemically/biologically valid outputs while optimizing for quantifiable metrics remains a challenge for generative models.

## Method

- Proposed **Energy Rank Alignment** (ERA), a gradient-based method for directly optimizing autoregressive transformer policies using explicit, continuous reward functions.
- ERA formulates an objective function based on a free energy functional, integrating a negative reward (energy), an entropic term for exploration, and a Kullback-Leibler regularization term to stay close to a reference policy.
- A practical loss function, L_ERA, is derived as a KL divergence between an entropy-regularized target preference distribution (incorporating reward and reference policy) and the policy's parametric preference distribution.

## Results

- ERA successfully aligned molecular generators to produce desired distributions for various chemical properties (e.g., QED, LogP, Ring Count) and effectively managed multi-objective optimization.
- In molecular docking tasks, ERA achieved comparable or superior mean docking scores to state-of-the-art methods, demonstrating significantly higher diversity and sample efficiency for top-scoring molecules.
- The algorithm successfully guided the ESM3-**1.4B** protein language model to generate TrpB protein mutants with substantially increased EVmutation scores, indicating applicability to biomolecule engineering.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - ERA's theoretical foundation demonstrates that its minimizer asymptotically converges to an ideal Gibbs-Boltzmann distribution, where the reward function acts as an energy, offering interpretable control over generated output entropy and diversity.
> - The algorithm provides independent, tunable control over the reward's influence (via inverse temperature "beta") and regularization towards the reference policy (via "gamma"), allowing fine-grained balancing of exploitation and exploration.
> - Unlike DPO, ERA's direct utilization of explicit reward functions contributes to greater stability and diversity of generated samples, mitigating issues like mode collapse particularly in scenarios with continuous feedback.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research sits at the nexus of two rapidly evolving fields: generative artificial intelligence and computational chemistry/materials science.

**Generative Models in Chemistry and Biology:** The paper addresses the formidable challenge of searching through combinatorially vast chemical spaces to discover molecules with desired properties. Traditional methods for molecular design are often slow, expensive, and limited in scope. The advent of large, autoregressive models (like transformers) trained on databases of chemical compounds (e.g., SMILES strings) or protein sequences has revolutionized this area, offering powerful tools for generating novel structures. These "chemical language models" and "protein language models" can predict properties and reactions, but guiding their generation towards specific, user-defined criteria remains a significant hurdle.

**The "Alignment" Problem in Large Language Models (LLMs):** The authors draw a direct parallel between the molecular search problem and the "alignment" problem in LLMs. In the general LLM context, alignment refers to the process of fine-tuning a pre-trained model to produce outputs that conform to human preferences, instructions, or ethical guidelines, rather than simply reflecting the statistical distribution of its training data. Current state-of-the-art alignment techniques, such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), are known to be expensive and data-intensive, requiring large datasets of human preference rankings. More recent advancements like Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) simplify this by implicitly modeling the reward function, but still fundamentally rely on pairwise preference data and can exhibit limitations such as mode collapse or difficulty handling continuous reward signals.

**Bridging the Gap:** The present work specifically targets a gap where many chemical and biological tasks offer readily evaluable, often continuous, reward functions (e.g., predicted drug-likeness, docking scores, evolutionary fitness) that are not easily translated into the pairwise human preferences assumed by DPO or the complex reward model training of RLHF. Existing methods struggle with this scenario, leading to a need for a more direct and robust alignment strategy that can leverage explicit reward functions. The paper positions Energy Rank Alignment (ERA) as a solution that directly addresses these limitations, offering a gradient-based objective that avoids the complexities and data requirements of previous methods while being uniquely suited for tasks with explicit, quantifiable rewards.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the inherent difficulty of searching vast chemical and biological spaces and the limitations of current generative models and alignment techniques in effectively navigating these spaces to produce desirable outputs.

The **overarching objective** is to introduce a novel, robust, and scalable algorithm that can align autoregressive transformer policies to generate molecules and protein sequences with externally specified, easily evaluable properties.

More **specific objectives** include:
- **Leveraging Explicit Rewards:** To develop an alignment algorithm that directly utilizes an explicit, easily evaluable reward function, which is common in many scientific domains but not well-suited for existing preference optimization methods like DPO.
- **Gradient-Based Policy Optimization:** To formulate a gradient-based objective that allows for direct optimization of the policy, bypassing the complexities of reinforcement learning frameworks (e.g., actor-critic setups in PPO).
- **Promoting Sample Diversity:** To design the objective such that the aligned policy samples fluctuations around maximal reward values, thereby promoting diversity in generated outputs rather than merely mode-seeking or greedily converging to a single optimum. This is crucial in discovery tasks where novel and diverse candidates are valued.
- **Theoretical Soundness:** To provide a theoretical framework demonstrating that the algorithm's minimizer converges to an ideal Gibbs-Boltzmann distribution, where the reward function acts as an energy, offering clear interpretability and control over sampling behavior (e.g., via tuneable entropy).
- **Scalability and Efficiency:** To develop an algorithm that is highly scalable, does not require a separate reward model training step (as in RLHF), and performs well even when the number of preference observations per pairing is small.
- **Broad Applicability:** To demonstrate the method's effectiveness across diverse applications, from small molecule generation with various chemical properties to the directed evolution of protein sequences.

The motivation arises from the recognized challenges with RLHF (labor-intensive data acquisition, computational cost, difficulty in practice) and DPO (reliance on implicit rewards from preference data, potential for mode collapse, unsuitability for continuous signals). ERA aims to overcome these by offering a more direct, controlled, and interpretable approach specifically designed for domains with quantifiable feedback.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The core of this work is the introduction of **Energy Rank Alignment (ERA)**, an algorithm designed for direct, gradient-based optimization of autoregressive policies using explicit reward functions.

**Theoretical Framework:**
1. **Objective Function:** ERA begins by defining a free energy functional (Eq. 2) for a policy $\pi$. This functional incorporates three key terms:
 - An *energy term* $U(x,y)$, where $U$ is the negative reward function.
 - An *entropic term* $log \pi(y|x)$ which encourages exploration.
 - A *Kullback-Leibler (KL) regularization term* $log(\pi_{ref}(y|x))$ that keeps the optimized policy $\pi$ close to a pre-trained reference policy $\pi_{ref}$.
2. **Minimizer Properties:** The authors show theoretically that the policy $\pi^*$ that minimizes this objective converges asymptotically to a Gibbs-Boltzmann distribution (Eq. 5). This distribution is proportional to $exp(- \frac{\beta}{1+\gamma} U(x,y) + \frac{\gamma}{1+\gamma} log \pi_{ref}(y|x))$. This formulation reveals that:
 - $\beta$ (inverse temperature) directly controls the magnitude of the reward's influence and the entropy of the target distribution. Higher $\beta$ leads to sharper distributions concentrated around high rewards; lower $\beta$ increases fluctuations and diversity.
 - $\gamma$ controls the strength of regularization towards the reference policy. As $\gamma \rightarrow 0$, ERA recovers an ideal Gibbs-Boltzmann distribution $exp(-\beta U)$, allowing full exploration guided by the reward. As $\gamma \rightarrow \infty$, the policy is dominated by $\pi_{ref}$.
3. **Loss Function Derivation:** Instead of directly minimizing the functional (Eq. 2) via complex RL setups, ERA derives a practical, gradient-based loss function, $L_{ERA}$ (Eq. 9). This loss is formulated as a Kullback-Leibler divergence between a *target entropy-regularized preference distribution* ($p_{\gamma}$) and the *parametric preference distribution* of the current policy ($p_{\theta}$). Both $p_{\gamma}$ and $p_{\theta}$ are defined using a pairwise Boltzmann ranking within the Bradley-Terry model. The target preference $p_{\gamma}$ explicitly incorporates the reward $U$ and the reference policy $\pi_{ref}$, allowing direct computation.
4. **Comparison to PPO and DPO:** The paper provides a theoretical analysis comparing ERA to PPO and DPO. It highlights that ERA offers more direct control over the entropy of the target distribution (via $\beta$) and the regularization strength (via $\gamma$) independently. Unlike PPO, ERA can recover the ideal Boltzmann distribution when $\gamma \rightarrow 0$. Unlike DPO, ERA uses an explicit reward function, preventing mode collapse in cases of sparse preference data by assigning non-zero probabilities, and offering more stable gradients.

**Experimental Approach:**
The methodology is demonstrated across diverse generative tasks for molecules and proteins.

1. **Molecular Generator Pretraining:** A decoder-only transformer model is pretrained on SMILES strings from the ChEMBL database for next-token prediction. This model serves as the initial "unaligned" or "reference" policy ($\pi_{ref}$).
2. **Reward Functions:** For each task, an explicit, computable energy (negative reward) function $U$ is defined. These include:
 - **RDKit Oracles:** Properties like Quantitative Estimate of Drug-Likeness (QED), Wildman-Crippen LogP (hydrophobicity), Ring Count, and Molar Refractivity (polarizability) are calculated using RDKit. Harmonic potentials or negative logarithms are used to define $U$. Tanimoto similarity is used for prompted generation.
 - **Protein-Ligand Docking Oracles:** Computational oracles predict docking scores for kinases JNK3 and GSK3β.
3. **Alignment Procedure:**
 - **Data Generation:** Pairs of molecules ($y, y'$) are sampled from the reference policy $\pi_{ref}$ (or a fine-tuned version for prompted tasks). Their corresponding energies $U(x,y)$ and $U(x,y')$ are computed.
 - **Optimization:** The $L_{ERA}$ loss is minimized using an Adam optimizer to fine-tune the trainable policy $\pi_{\theta}$, initialized with $\pi_{ref}$'s weights.
 - **Unprompted vs. Prompted:** Experiments are conducted for both unprompted generation (only a start-of-sequence token) and prompted generation (conditioning on a "prompt molecule" $x$).
 - **Multi-property Alignment:** The energy function is constructed as a weighted sum of multiple property energies, with property-specific $\beta$ values to control trade-offs.
4. **Protein Language Model Alignment:** The state-of-the-art ESM3-1.4B protein language model is used as $\pi_{ref}$. ERA is applied to generate mutated protein sequences (TrpB) by masking specific sites and optimizing for the EVmutation score (an oracle for evolutionary fitness).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments demonstrate the robust performance and theoretical advantages of Energy Rank Alignment (ERA) across various molecular and protein design tasks.

1. **Robust Single-Property Alignment:** ERA successfully aligns the molecular generator to produce molecules with desired distributions for properties like Molar Refractivity, Ring Count, LogP, and QED. It can shift distributions to target both higher and lower mean values, confirming its ability to guide generation effectively (Figure 2).
2. **Controllable Entropy and Regularization:** The inverse-temperature parameter $\beta$ effectively controls the spread of the generated distribution around the target property value. Higher $\beta$ leads to outputs more concentrated near the optimal reward (less diversity), while lower $\beta$ promotes greater diversity. The regularization parameter $\gamma$ significantly influences the trade-off between targeting rewards and maintaining chemical validity or proximity to the reference policy. For unprompted tasks, even with $\gamma=0$, ERA maintains good sample diversity and chemical validity (Table S6, Figure S8).
3. **Effective Multi-Property Alignment:** ERA successfully handles multi-objective optimization. By weighting different property energies with individual $\beta$ values, the model can balance multiple desired properties (e.g., high QED and LogP), demonstrating clear trade-offs (Figure 3, Table S7, Figure S9). This is critical for real-world design challenges.
4. **Successful Prompted Molecular Generation:** For lead optimization tasks, ERA effectively aligns the model to generate molecules that are chemically similar to a prompt molecule while also exhibiting desired properties (e.g., high QED or LogP). This often requires non-zero $\gamma$ to maintain sample validity and ensure generated molecules remain chemically plausible and similar to the prompt (Figure S10, S11, Table S8).
5. **Superior Performance in Docking Tasks:** When designing compounds for high predicted docking scores against GSK3β and JNK3 kinases, ERA performs comparably to or better than state-of-the-art methods (MolRL-MGPT, GFlowNet, GraphGA, JT-VAE, REINVENT) in terms of mean docking scores. Crucially, ERA achieves significantly *higher diversity* (lower Internal Diversity (IntDiv) scores) among top-scoring molecules and *higher sample efficiency* (Top-10 AUC), meaning it finds high-performing and diverse candidates with fewer oracle calls (Table 1, Table S9, Figure S12, S13). This is a major advantage for expensive real-world experiments.
6. **Applicability to Protein Directed Evolution:** ERA successfully aligns the ESM3-1.4B protein language model to generate protein mutants of TrpB with significantly increased EVmutation scores (Figure 5). This demonstrates the algorithm's versatility beyond small molecules and its potential for guiding large biomolecule engineering efforts.
7. **Comparison to DPO:** Ablation studies and direct comparisons show that ERA exhibits greater stability in maintaining chemical validity over long training durations compared to DPO, which can experience precipitous drops in validity due to mode collapse (Figure S6). ERA’s explicit reward model and direct control over entropy and regularization help prevent these issues, leading to more robust and diverse generations, especially when dealing with explicit rewards rather than purely implicit preferences.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of Energy Rank Alignment (ERA) represents a significant advancement in the field of generative model alignment, particularly for scientific applications.

**Novelty and Theoretical Rigor:** ERA offers a novel approach to policy optimization by directly leveraging explicit reward functions within a gradient-based framework. Its strong theoretical foundation, demonstrating convergence to an ideal Gibbs-Boltzmann distribution with independently tuneable entropy and regularization parameters, distinguishes it from existing methods like PPO and DPO. This theoretical clarity provides better control and interpretability over the alignment process.

**Practical Advantages:**
- **Efficiency and Scalability:** ERA bypasses the complexities and computational overhead of reinforcement learning (no separate reward model training as in RLHF, no actor-critic setup as in PPO). It performs well even with limited preference observations, making it suitable for scenarios where data might be sparse or expensive to generate. The low inference costs further enhance its practical utility.
- **Enhanced Diversity and Robustness:** By explicitly promoting sampling fluctuations around optimal reward values and offering direct control over entropy, ERA effectively mitigates the mode collapse issues observed in other methods, particularly DPO. This leads to the generation of more diverse and chemically valid outputs, which is paramount in discovery science where novelty is highly valued.
- **Direct Control over Trade-offs:** The independent tuning of $\beta$ (reward influence/entropy) and $\gamma$ (reference policy regularization) allows researchers to precisely control the balance between exploiting high-reward regions and exploring diverse parts of the chemical or sequence space, as well as maintaining generated output validity.

**Broad Impact Across Scientific Domains:**
- **Drug Discovery and Materials Science:** ERA provides a powerful tool for accelerating the inverse design problem in these fields. Its ability to efficiently generate novel molecules with desired properties, balance multiple objectives, and improve sample efficiency for expensive evaluations (like docking or experimental assays) could dramatically shorten discovery cycles for new drugs, catalysts, and functional materials.
- **Protein Engineering and Directed Evolution:** The successful application to protein language models for directed evolution campaigns is highly impactful. Guiding protein mutagenesis to enhance desired functions (e.g., enzyme activity, stability) with computational oracles can reduce the cost and time of experimental efforts, leading to faster development of novel biomolecules for therapeutics, industrial processes, and fundamental research.
- **General Scientific AI:** The methodology of translating explicit reward functions into a robust alignment objective is generalizable beyond chemistry and biology. Any scientific domain that utilizes generative models and has quantifiable objective functions could potentially benefit from ERA.

**Future Directions and Limitations:** While highly promising, the authors acknowledge limitations such as the reliance on an explicit reward model (which can still be challenging to design for very complex tasks) and the current lack of explicit optimization for synthetic accessibility in molecular generation (though this could be integrated by including a synthesizability oracle in the reward function). Nevertheless, ERA marks a significant step forward in making generative AI models more controllable, efficient, and impactful for scientific discovery.
