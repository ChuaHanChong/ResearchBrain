# QuantVLA: Scale-Calibrated Post-Training Quantization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> QuantVLA, a training-free post-training quantization framework, effectively quantizes Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, including their sensitive Diffusion Transformer (DiT) action heads. This approach reduces memory footprint by up to 70% while maintaining or slightly improving task success rates on state-of-the-art VLA policies like OpenPI π0.5 and GR00T N1.5.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models demand high computational and memory resources, impeding their real-time deployment on robotic platforms with limited resources.
- Existing VLA efficiency frameworks often struggle to effectively optimize the diffusion-based action head, which is a significant computational bottleneck, due to its inherent sensitivity.
- Direct application of standard post-training quantization (PTQ) methods leads to performance degradation in VLA systems, particularly within the DiT action head, due to quantization-induced scale drifts.

## Method

- **QuantVLA** employs a training-free, ==selective quantization== layout that integerizes the language backbone and only the MLP blocks of the DiT action head, leaving attention projection layers in floating point.
- It introduces ==Attention Temperature Matching== (ATM), a lightweight per-head scalar calibration, to correct attention logits scale mismatch and align attention distributions with the full-precision teacher model.
- The framework incorporates Output Head Balancing (OHB), a lightweight per-layer scalar calibration, to mitigate post-projection energy drift at the residual interface, restoring residual injection gain without added runtime overhead.

## Results

- QuantVLA achieved substantial memory reductions, realizing **70%** relative savings for OpenPI π0.5 (from **4.27 GB** to **1.28 GB**) and **55%** for GR00T N1.5 (from **2.02 GB** to **0.91 GB**) at W4A8 precision.
- The framework maintained or slightly improved task success rates, achieving **97.6%** for OpenPI π0.5 (compared to **97.1%** FP16) and **88.0%** for GR00T N1.5 (compared to **86.5%** FP16) on LIBERO benchmarks.
- QuantVLA demonstrated robustness, maintaining a **95.3%** average success rate on OpenPI π0.5 even at aggressive W4A4 precision and showing consistent performance across varying denoising steps and extended manipulation benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Quantization-induced scale drifts in VLA models primarily affect the effective attention-logits temperature and residual-stream energy within the sensitive DiT action head, leading to instability.
> - Selectively keeping attention projection layers in full precision while quantizing other components is crucial for maintaining performance stability in VLA models with DiT action heads.
> - Lightweight, training-free scalar calibrations (ATM and OHB) can effectively counteract quantization-induced scale shifts in attention and residual paths, enabling stable low-bit inference without new operators.
