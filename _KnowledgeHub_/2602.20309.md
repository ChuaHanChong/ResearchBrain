# QuantVLA: Scale-Calibrated Post-Training Quantization for Vision-Language-Action Models

> [!summary] Summary
> QuantVLA, a training-free post-training quantization framework, effectively quantizes Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, including their sensitive Diffusion Transformer (DiT) action heads. This approach reduces memory footprint by up to 70% while maintaining or slightly improving task success rates on state-of-the-art VLA policies like OpenPI π0.5 and GR00T N1.5.

## Problem

- Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models demand high computational and memory resources, impeding their real-time deployment on robotic platforms with limited resources.
- Existing VLA efficiency frameworks often struggle to effectively optimize the diffusion-based action head, which is a significant computational bottleneck, due to its inherent sensitivity.
- Direct application of standard post-training quantization (PTQ) methods leads to performance degradation in VLA systems, particularly within the DiT action head, due to quantization-induced scale drifts.

## Method

- **QuantVLA** employs a training-free, ==selective quantization== layout that integerizes the language backbone and only the MLP blocks of the DiT action head, leaving attention projection layers in floating point.
- It introduces ==Attention Temperature Matching== (ATM), a lightweight per-head scalar calibration, to correct attention logits scale mismatch and align attention distributions with the full-precision teacher model.
- The framework incorporates Output Head Balancing (OHB), a lightweight per-layer scalar calibration, to mitigate post-projection energy drift at the residual interface, restoring residual injection gain without added runtime overhead.

## Results

- QuantVLA achieved substantial memory reductions, realizing **70%** relative savings for OpenPI π0.5 (from **4.27 GB** to **1.28 GB**) and **55%** for GR00T N1.5 (from **2.02 GB** to **0.91 GB**) at W4A8 precision.
- The framework maintained or slightly improved task success rates, achieving **97.6%** for OpenPI π0.5 (compared to **97.1%** FP16) and **88.0%** for GR00T N1.5 (compared to **86.5%** FP16) on LIBERO benchmarks.
- QuantVLA demonstrated robustness, maintaining a **95.3%** average success rate on OpenPI π0.5 even at aggressive W4A4 precision and showing consistent performance across varying denoising steps and extended manipulation benchmarks.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Quantization-induced scale drifts in VLA models primarily affect the effective attention-logits temperature and residual-stream energy within the sensitive DiT action head, leading to instability.
> - Selectively keeping attention projection layers in full precision while quantizing other components is crucial for maintaining performance stability in VLA models with DiT action heads.
> - Lightweight, training-free scalar calibrations (ATM and OHB) can effectively counteract quantization-induced scale shifts in attention and residual paths, enabling stable low-bit inference without new operators.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models represent an emerging paradigm in embodied artificial intelligence, unifying perception, language understanding, and robotic control within a single policy. These models, exemplified by systems such as π0.5, OpenVLA, and GR00T N1.5, leverage advancements in large language models (LLMs), vision-language models (VLMs), and Diffusion Transformer (DiT) architectures to enable robots to interpret natural language instructions and execute complex actions based on visual observations.

Despite their performance capabilities, VLA models face substantial practical deployment challenges due to their rapidly increasing computational and memory demands. This bottleneck is exacerbated as models scale to longer operational horizons and integrate larger backbones. Existing efforts to improve VLA efficiency primarily fall into two categories: designing inherently more efficient VLA model architectures (e.g., TinyVLA, SmolVLA, FLOWER) or developing efficiency frameworks that optimize inference around existing policies (e.g., EfficientVLA, VLA-Cache, MoLe-VLA). However, these approaches often focus on the visual encoder or language front-end, frequently leaving the diffusion-based policy head, which is a significant contributor to computational overhead and is tightly coupled to upstream reasoning, in full precision. Modifying or optimizing this component without degrading stability has been challenging.

Post-training quantization (PTQ) offers a method to reduce memory usage and accelerate inference without requiring additional training. While PTQ techniques like SmoothQuant and DuQuant have been developed for large language or vision-language models, their direct application to VLA systems, particularly those with DiT action heads, has been limited. VLA pipelines introduce heterogeneous activation and precision behaviors due to tight multimodal–diffusion coupling and scale drift across modalities and along the diffusion rollout. These factors can distort critical internal mechanisms like effective attention-logits temperature and residual-stream energy, making stable low-bit control difficult.

QuantVLA addresses this specific gap by introducing a PTQ framework explicitly designed for VLA models, with a focus on successfully quantizing the sensitive DiT action head alongside the language backbone. This work aims to enable significant memory savings and computational efficiency for VLA models without retraining, providing a pathway for their deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a training-free post-training quantization (PTQ) framework, named QuantVLA, that effectively reduces the computational and memory demands of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, enabling their practical deployment on robotic platforms with limited resources.

This objective is motivated by several key challenges inherent in current VLA systems:

1. **High Computational and Memory Demands:** Modern VLA models integrate large language backbones and Diffusion Transformer (DiT) action heads, leading to rapidly increasing compute and memory requirements. As these models expand in size and operate over longer temporal horizons, their deployment on embedded or mobile robotic hardware becomes increasingly constrained by these resource limitations.
2. **Unaddressed Efficiency Bottleneck in Action Heads:** While existing VLA efficiency frameworks have explored architectural redesigns, pruning, routing, or caching mechanisms, many primarily target the vision encoder or language front-end. The diffusion-based action head, despite being a major contributor to computational and memory overhead and being tightly coupled with upstream reasoning, often remains in full precision due to its sensitivity. This represents an unexploited opportunity for efficiency gains.
3. **Fragility of DiT Action Heads to Quantization:** Direct application of existing PTQ methods, typically designed for unimodal or loosely coupled language/vision-language models, to VLA systems proves challenging. The tightly coupled multimodal reasoning and diffusion-based action generation in VLA pipelines introduces scale drift across modalities and along the diffusion rollout. This quantization-induced scale mismatch can distort crucial internal dynamics, specifically altering the effective attention-logits temperature and the residual-stream energy within the DiT action head, leading to significant performance degradation.
4. **Need for a Training-Free and Architecture-Preserving Solution:** The high cost and complexity of retraining large VLA models necessitate a PTQ approach that can be applied to pre-trained models without additional training. Furthermore, maintaining the original model architecture and operator schedule is desirable to ensure compatibility with existing hardware and software stacks and to avoid introducing new sources of computational overhead during inference.

QuantVLA aims to overcome these motivations by systematically analyzing quantization sensitivity in VLA models with DiT action heads, identifying specific failure modes, and subsequently developing a scale-calibrated PTQ framework that mitigates these issues. The framework seeks to achieve substantial memory savings and maintain or even exceed the performance of full-precision baselines, thereby providing a practical pathway towards scalable low-bit embodied intelligence.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

QuantVLA is presented as a training-free post-training quantization (PTQ) framework specifically developed for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models utilizing a Diffusion Transformer (DiT) as their action head. The methodology is structured around identifying and mitigating specific quantization-induced scale drifts within the VLA pipeline.

#### 3.1. Preliminaries on Diffusion-based VLA Models

The paper focuses on VLA systems where the action head is a DiT. These systems process a history of RGB frames via a vision encoder and natural language instructions via a language backbone. These multimodal inputs are projected into a shared transformer space, forming a task-conditioned representation ($F_{VL}$). The DiT policy head, conditioned on $F_{VL}$, robot proprioception, and a diffusion timestep, iteratively refines an action latent. Flow matching is used as the learning objective to train the DiT.

#### 3.2. Post-Training Quantization Setup and Emergent DiT Sensitivity

The framework adopts DuQuant's reparameterization for linear layers, which uses per-channel smoothing, block-orthogonal rotations, and zigzag channel permutations to make activations and weights more amenable to low-bit quantization.

However, deploying this to tightly coupled VLA stacks exposes specific sensitivities. Quantizing the upstream language backbone perturbs intermediate representations that condition the DiT action policy, leading to input drift. Furthermore, the DiT head requires precise action token generation, making it highly susceptible to small rounding and scale mismatches.

The paper provides a first-order error propagation analysis to explain how quantization introduces two systematic drifts:
1. **Attention Logits Scale Change:** Quantization in the Query (Q) and Key (K) paths, even when attention weights remain in floating point, perturbs the pre-softmax logits ($L$). This changes the effective temperature of the softmax function ($T_{eff} = \frac{\sqrt{d}}{s_q s_k}$, where $s_q, s_k$ are dequantization scales for Q, K), shifting attention entropy away from the teacher distribution. This temperature bias propagates across layers.
2. **Residual Stream Energy Drift:** After multi-head concatenation and the output projection, the amplitude of the attention output exhibits a systematic change. This modifies the residual injection gain and the operating point of layer normalization, driven primarily by the product of dequantization scales ($s_v s_o$) from the Value (V) path and output projection. In deep DiT stacks, these two drifts accumulate, degrading stability and performance.

#### 3.3. QuantVLA Framework

QuantVLA integrates a selective quantization layout with two calibration mechanisms, Attention Temperature Matching (ATM) and Output Head Balancing (OHB), to address the identified sensitivities. The framework is training-free and preserves the original architecture and operator schedule.

1. **Selective Quantization Layout:**
 - All linear layers in the language backbone are integerized.
 - In the DiT action head, only the MLP blocks are integerized.
 - Crucially, the attention projection layers (Query $W_q$, Key $W_k$, Value $W_v$, and Output $W_o$) are kept in floating point.
 - Rationale: This selective approach avoids compounding errors at the most fragile interfaces, specifically those that directly determine the stability of the softmax distribution and residual injection.

2. **Attention Temperature Matching (ATM):**
 - **Purpose:** To correct the attention logits scale mismatch and align the attention distribution with the full-precision teacher model.
 - **Mechanism:** A lightweight per-head scalar, $\alpha$, is estimated from a small unlabeled calibration buffer.
 - **Calibration:** $\alpha_{raw}$ is calculated as the ratio of the standard deviations of the teacher's logits ($Std(L_T)$) and the quantized model's logits ($Std(L_Q)$) (Eq. 9). This raw scalar is then clipped to a safe range (e.g., $\pm 0.4$) and a neutrality band ($\epsilon = 0.03$) is applied to ignore negligible differences (Eqs. 10-11).
 - **Integration:** The calibrated $\alpha$ is folded into the dequantization scales during inference (effectively scaling $L_Q = L_T / \alpha$, Eq. 12), introducing no new operators or runtime overhead.

3. **Output Head Balancing (OHB):**
 - **Purpose:** To mitigate post-projection energy drift at the residual interface, restoring the residual injection gain and the operating point of layer normalization.
 - **Mechanism:** A lightweight per-layer scalar, $\beta(l)$, is estimated for each layer $l$ from the same unlabeled calibration buffer.
 - **Calibration:** $\beta_{raw}(l)$ is calculated as the ratio of the Root Mean Square (RMS) energy of the teacher's output head activation ($RMS(Z_{T,l})$) and the quantized model's output head activation ($RMS(Z_{Q,l})$) (Eq. 14). Similar to ATM, $\beta$ is clipped and a neutrality band is applied (Eqs. 15-16).
 - **Integration:** The calibrated $\beta$ is also folded into the dequantization scales (scaling $Z_Q = Z_l / \beta(l)$, Eq. 17), maintaining the original operator schedule and integer GEMMs without additional runtime computation.

These calibration mechanisms are instantiated specifically at the language-to-action interface, where quantized language features condition the DiT head and induce the strongest scale drift. The parameters for DuQuant (W4A8, block size 64, channel permutation, row rotation), and calibration (activation percentile 99.9, 32 batches, smoothing coefficient 0.15) are detailed in the appendix.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of QuantVLA was conducted on two state-of-the-art VLA policies, OpenPI π0.5 and GR00T N1.5, using the LIBERO simulator, which includes Spatial, Object, Goal, and Long-horizon task suites. The reported metric is task success rate.

#### 4.1. Empirical Validation of the Selective Quantization Layout

An ablation study (Table 1) was performed to compare different layer selection schemes for quantization (W4A8) without ATM and OHB calibration.
- Quantizing only the LLM resulted in minor performance drops (e.g., π0.5: 97.1% FP16 vs. 96.5% LLM W4A8).
- Quantizing only the DiT action head led to significant degradation (e.g., π0.5: 71.6% DiT W4A8).
- Quantizing both LLM and the entire DiT resulted in the largest performance drops (e.g., π0.5: 76.3% LLM+DiT W4A8; GR00T N1.5: 70.0% LLM+DiT W4A8), particularly affecting long-horizon tasks.
- **Key finding:** Quantizing the LLM alongside only the MLP blocks of the DiT (leaving attention projections in floating point) consistently maintained performance closest to the baseline (e.g., π0.5: 95.4% LLM+DiT(MLP) W4A8; GR00T N1.5: 82.5% LLM+DiT(MLP) W4A8) while retaining memory benefits. This supports the theoretical analysis regarding the sensitivity of attention projections and residual interfaces.

#### 4.2. Effect of ATM and OHB Calibration

Empirical validation (Figure 3) demonstrated the effectiveness of ATM and OHB in restoring logits statistics and output energy on GR00T N1.5.
- **ATM (Left Panel):** Quantization without ATM caused a divergence in the standard deviation of logits compared to the teacher model. ATM significantly reduced this mismatch, bringing the logits standard deviation of each attention block closer to the teacher, indicating correction of temperature shifts.
- **OHB (Right Panel):** Quantization without OHB resulted in a drift in the Root Mean Square (RMS) of the attention output after the output projection. OHB successfully aligned the attention output RMS with the teacher, mitigating residual-stream energy drift and stabilizing the downstream residual path.
- **Key finding:** Both ATM and OHB effectively correct the identified scale drifts across attention blocks, especially in deeper layers, narrowing the gap to the full-precision teacher model.

#### 4.3. QuantVLA Results in LIBERO Simulation

The main results (Table 2) compared QuantVLA against DuQuant and FP16 baselines.
- **DuQuant Performance:** Applying DuQuant to both the LLM and DiT resulted in significant accuracy drops (e.g., π0.5: 76.3% average success rate; GR00T N1.5: 70.0% average success rate). This suggests existing PTQ methods designed for unimodal settings do not effectively transfer to tightly coupled VLA systems.
- **QuantVLA Performance:**
 - **OpenPI π0.5 (W4A8):** QuantVLA achieved an average success rate of 97.6%, matching or slightly exceeding the FP16 baseline (97.1%). This was accomplished with a substantial memory reduction from 4.27 GB to 1.28 GB (70% relative savings).
 - **GR00T N1.5 (W4A8):** QuantVLA achieved an average success rate of 88.0%, surpassing the FP16 baseline (86.5%). Memory usage was reduced from 2.02 GB to 0.91 GB (55% relative savings).
- **Key finding:** QuantVLA is the first PTQ framework to effectively quantize VLA models, including the DiT action head, while maintaining or exceeding full-precision performance and achieving significant memory savings.

#### 4.4. Efficiency of QuantVLA

QuantVLA consistently demonstrated substantial memory reductions (Figure 4), achieving 70% relative memory savings on quantized components for OpenPI π0.5 and 55% for GR00T N1.5. These savings are critical for deployment under tight memory budgets and for processing longer temporal contexts.

#### 4.5. Robustness and Generalization Analysis

- **Different Quantization Precisions (Table 3):** On OpenPI π0.5, QuantVLA maintained strong performance even at lower bit widths, achieving a 95.3% average success rate at W4A4 precision, demonstrating stable behavior under aggressive quantization.
- **Different Denoising Steps (Table 4):** On GR00T N1.5, QuantVLA consistently matched or exceeded the baseline across different denoising steps (e.g., 88.0% average success at 8 steps, 88.5% at 16 steps), confirming robustness to varied inference settings.
- **Comparison with SmoothQuant (Appendix E):** QuantVLA at W4A8 achieved comparable or slightly better results than SmoothQuant at W8A8, particularly in long-horizon tasks, indicating a more favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off.
- **Extended Benchmark (Appendix F):** On the Pick-and-Can manipulation benchmark, QuantVLA (W4A8) significantly narrowed the performance gap to FP16 compared to SmoothQuant (W4A8), demonstrating better task preservation under aggressive quantization in manipulation scenarios.
- **Applicability Beyond DiT-Based Models (Appendix G):** The framework's ability to match OpenVLA's FP16 performance at W8A16, even though OpenVLA uses a non-DiT action head, suggests broader applicability beyond strictly DiT-based VLA models, potentially leveraging the selective quantization layout.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The introduction of QuantVLA represents a notable advancement in the field of embodied artificial intelligence, addressing critical challenges related to the practical deployment of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models.

The significance of this work lies in several key contributions:
- **Pioneering PTQ for VLA Models:** QuantVLA is presented as the first post-training quantization (PTQ) framework specifically designed for VLA systems, and notably, the first to successfully quantize the Diffusion Transformer (DiT) action head. This opens up a previously unaddressed area for efficiency improvements in these complex multimodal models.
- **Addressing Deployment Bottlenecks:** By achieving substantial memory savings (approximately 70% relative memory savings on quantized components) and maintaining or exceeding full-precision task success rates, QuantVLA provides a practical solution to the compute, memory, and power constraints inherent in deploying large VLA models on robotic platforms and other edge devices.
- **Training-Free and Architecture-Preserving:** The framework operates without requiring additional training and preserves the original model architecture and operator schedule. This makes QuantVLA highly adaptable, as it can be applied to existing pre-trained VLA models as a post-deployment step, significantly reducing the overhead associated with model optimization.
- **Robustness Through Scale Calibration:** The detailed analysis of quantization sensitivity in VLA models, coupled with the introduction of Attention Temperature Matching (ATM) and Output Head Balancing (OHB), effectively mitigates critical scale drifts in attention logits and residual stream energy. This rigorous approach ensures stable low-bit inference even under aggressive quantization.
- **Enhanced Performance and Scalability:** The ability to quantize VLA models to low bit widths (e.g., W4A8, W4A4) while maintaining or improving task accuracy enables several advancements. It allows for the processing of longer temporal contexts, extends input horizons, or facilitates the execution of multiple control policies in parallel within the same hardware budget, thereby enhancing the overall scalability and capabilities of VLA applications.

The potential impact of QuantVLA extends to various areas:
- **Embodied AI Research and Development:** It provides a robust and efficient foundation for future research in VLA models, encouraging the development of even larger and more capable models without being immediately hindered by deployment constraints.
- **Robotics Applications:** The memory and computational efficiency gains directly translate to more reliable and accessible robotic systems. Robots can operate with reduced power consumption, extended battery life, and on less expensive hardware, making advanced embodied intelligence more feasible for real-world scenarios.
- **Edge AI and Mobile Devices:** The principles and techniques demonstrated by QuantVLA could be extended to other complex multimodal models deployed on resource-constrained edge devices, fostering wider adoption of advanced AI capabilities.
- **Long-Horizon Task Generation:** The framework's robustness in long-horizon tasks, where errors typically accumulate, suggests its utility in enabling more stable and reliable sequential action generation in real-world robotic operations.

Overall, QuantVLA offers a concrete pathway toward making advanced VLA models practical and widely deployable, laying foundational groundwork for future advances in low-power, robust, and scalable embodied intelligence.
