# LLMDet: Learning Strong Open-Vocabulary Object Detectors under the Supervision of Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LLMDet, from Sun Yat-sen University and Alibaba Group, leverages Large Language Models to provide rich, detailed image-level and region-level captions, improving open-vocabulary object detection performance, particularly for rare categories. The approach also demonstrates mutual benefits, enhancing large multi-modal models when integrated as a vision foundation.

## Problem

- Traditional object detectors are limited to a predefined set of categories, lacking the flexibility to detect novel objects.
- Existing open-vocabulary object detection methods primarily rely on short, coarse-grained captions that describe objects in isolation, omitting rich contextual scene information.
- These methods struggle to capture object attributes, spatial relationships, and actions, limiting their comprehensive scene understanding.

## Method

- LLMDet introduces a novel approach using a Large Language Model to generate both ==region-level short phrases== and ==detailed image-level long captions== for richer supervision.
- A ==co-training framework== is employed where the open-vocabulary detector and the LLM collaboratively learn from these comprehensive descriptions.
- The ==GroundingCap-1M dataset==, comprising 1.12 million images with detailed LLM-generated captions and bounding box annotations, was created to facilitate this training.

## Results

- LLMDet achieves significant improvements on the LVIS dataset, including up to **14.3%** AP and **17.0%** APr for rare categories over the MM-GDINO baseline.
- The model demonstrates consistent performance gains on ODinW and COCO-O datasets, indicating robustness to domain shifts.
- When integrated as a vision foundation, LLMDet enhances the performance of large multi-modal models on tasks like GQA, POPE, and MME.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Detailed image-level captions provide a more comprehensive understanding of scenes, capturing object attributes, relationships, and contextual information.
> - Leveraging LLMs to generate rich, multi-grained captions offers a powerful supervision signal for open-vocabulary object detection.
> - A synergistic relationship exists where LLM supervision enhances object detection, and the improved detector can in turn enhance large multi-modal models (LMMs).
