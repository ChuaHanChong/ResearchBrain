# LLMDet: Learning Strong Open-Vocabulary Object Detectors under the Supervision of Large Language Models

> [!summary] Summary
> LLMDet, from Sun Yat-sen University and Alibaba Group, leverages Large Language Models to provide rich, detailed image-level and region-level captions, improving open-vocabulary object detection performance, particularly for rare categories. The approach also demonstrates mutual benefits, enhancing large multi-modal models when integrated as a vision foundation.

## Problem

- Traditional object detectors are limited to a predefined set of categories, lacking the flexibility to detect novel objects.
- Existing open-vocabulary object detection methods primarily rely on short, coarse-grained captions that describe objects in isolation, omitting rich contextual scene information.
- These methods struggle to capture object attributes, spatial relationships, and actions, limiting their comprehensive scene understanding.

## Method

- LLMDet introduces a novel approach using a Large Language Model to generate both ==region-level short phrases== and ==detailed image-level long captions== for richer supervision.
- A ==co-training framework== is employed where the open-vocabulary detector and the LLM collaboratively learn from these comprehensive descriptions.
- The ==GroundingCap-1M dataset==, comprising 1.12 million images with detailed LLM-generated captions and bounding box annotations, was created to facilitate this training.

## Results

- LLMDet achieves significant improvements on the LVIS dataset, including up to **14.3%** AP and **17.0%** APr for rare categories over the MM-GDINO baseline.
- The model demonstrates consistent performance gains on ODinW and COCO-O datasets, indicating robustness to domain shifts.
- When integrated as a vision foundation, LLMDet enhances the performance of large multi-modal models on tasks like GQA, POPE, and MME.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Detailed image-level captions provide a more comprehensive understanding of scenes, capturing object attributes, relationships, and contextual information.
> - Leveraging LLMs to generate rich, multi-grained captions offers a powerful supervision signal for open-vocabulary object detection.
> - A synergistic relationship exists where LLM supervision enhances object detection, and the improved detector can in turn enhance large multi-modal models (LMMs).

## Detailed Report

### 1. Introduction

Object detection has been a fundamental task in computer vision for decades, but traditional object detectors are limited to identifying objects within a predefined set of categories. Open-vocabulary object detection (OVD) represents a significant advancement, enabling systems to detect objects described by arbitrary text prompts, even for object categories not seen during training.

Recent approaches to OVD have shown promising results by leveraging vision-language pre-training, region-word contrastive learning, and integration with foundation models like CLIP. However, these methods primarily rely on short, coarse-grained captions that describe individual objects in isolation, missing the rich context provided by detailed image descriptions.

LLMDet, proposed by researchers from Sun Yat-sen University and Alibaba Group, takes a novel approach by harnessing the language understanding capabilities of Large Language Models (LLMs) to supervise open-vocabulary object detection. The key insight is that detailed image-level captions provide a more comprehensive understanding of scenes, capturing not just object identities but also their attributes, relationships, and contextual information.

### 2. Key Innovations of LLMDet

LLMDet represents a paradigm shift in open-vocabulary object detection through several key innovations:

1. **LLM Supervision**: Rather than relying solely on short object descriptions, LLMDet leverages an LLM to generate both region-level short phrases and image-level long captions. This dual approach provides richer supervision signals for the detector.

2. **Joint Learning Framework**: LLMDet introduces a co-training framework where an OVD detector and an LLM work together. The detector identifies objects in images, while the LLM generates detailed descriptions of those objects and the overall scene.

3. **Two-way Benefits**: The research shows that not only does LLM supervision improve object detection performance, but the improved detector can also enhance large multi-modal models (LMMs), creating a virtuous cycle of improvement.

4. **Training Efficiency**: The authors demonstrate that LLMDet achieves state-of-the-art performance with less data than competing methods, thanks to the rich supervision provided by the LLM.

The fundamental approach differs from existing methods in its focus on comprehensive scene understanding rather than isolated object recognition. By incorporating detailed contextual information from image-level captions, LLMDet can better understand relationships between objects and their surrounding environment, leading to more accurate detection.

### 3. The GroundingCap-1M Dataset

A critical contribution of this work is the creation of the GroundingCap-1M dataset. This dataset was specifically designed to support the training of open-vocabulary detectors with rich, detailed captions.

GroundingCap-1M contains 1.12 million images, each with:
- Short grounding text (phrases describing objects)
- Annotated bounding boxes
- Detailed image-level captions

The dataset was constructed by augmenting existing datasets (COCO, V3Det, GoldG, and LCS) with detailed captions generated using Qwen2-VL-72b, a state-of-the-art large vision-language model. This approach allowed the researchers to create a large-scale dataset of paired images and detailed captions without the prohibitive cost of manual annotation.

The captions in GroundingCap-1M go far beyond simple object labels, including information about:
- Object attributes (color, texture, size)
- Spatial relationships between objects
- Actions and states of objects
- Background context and scene setting

This rich captioning provides a more comprehensive supervision signal for the detector, allowing it to learn more nuanced visual representations and improve its ability to recognize objects in diverse contexts.

### 4. Model Architecture and Training Process

LLMDet employs a sophisticated architecture that combines a DETR-based open-vocabulary detector with a large language model.

The training process occurs in two distinct steps:

1. **Projector Alignment**: In this initial step, a projector is trained to map the detector's visual features to the LLM's input space. This step uses image captioning as a task to ensure that the detector's features can be effectively processed by the LLM. During this stage, both the detector and the LLM are kept frozen to preserve their pre-trained capabilities.

2. **End-to-End Finetuning**: Once the projector is aligned, the entire system (detector, projector, and LLM) is finetuned using the GroundingCap-1M dataset. This step combines multiple loss functions:
 - Grounding loss for word-region alignment and box regression
 - Image-level caption generation loss
 - Region-level caption generation loss

A key aspect of the LLMDet design is that during inference, the LLM component is discarded. This ensures that the inference cost remains identical to the baseline model, making LLMDet practical for real-world applications despite the added complexity during training.

The implementation uses the MMDetection framework with automatic mixed-precision and gradient checkpointing to manage the computational demands of training such a complex system. The vision backbone of the pre-trained MM-GDINO detector is kept frozen during training to preserve its feature extraction capabilities.

### 5. Performance and Results

LLMDet demonstrates impressive performance improvements across multiple benchmarks for zero-shot object detection.

Key results include:

1. **LVIS Dataset Performance**: On the challenging LVIS dataset, LLMDet achieves significant improvements over the baseline MM-GDINO:
 - 3.3%/3.8%/14.3% AP improvement with Swin-T/B/L backbones
 - 3.1%/3.3%/17.0% APr improvement for rare categories

 This substantial gain for rare categories demonstrates LLMDet's ability to learn more fine-grained visual representations through the detailed caption supervision.

2. **ODinW Performance**: On the Objects365 Dataset in the Wild (ODinW), which tests generalization to new domains, LLMDet shows consistent improvements, indicating its robustness to domain shifts.

3. **COCO-O Performance**: LLMDet also outperforms baselines on COCO-O, further confirming its ability to handle domain shifts effectively.

4. **Enhanced Multi-Modal Performance**: When LLMDet is used as a vision foundation model to build a large multi-modal model (LMM), it improves performance on tasks like GQA, POPE, and MME. This demonstrates mutual benefits between improved object detection and enhanced multi-modal capabilities.

The authors also conducted extensive ablation studies to validate individual components' contributions. These studies confirmed that:
- Both region-level and image-level captions contribute to performance improvements
- The quality of captions matters, with more detailed and accurate captions leading to better results
- The approach is data-efficient, achieving state-of-the-art performance with less training data

### 6. Applications and Impact

The improvements achieved by LLMDet have significant implications for various real-world applications:

1. **Robotics and Embodied AI**: Enhanced object detection capabilities can improve robots' ability to perceive and interact with their environment, particularly for identifying rare or previously unseen objects.

2. **Visual Search and Retrieval**: Better open-vocabulary detection enables more accurate and flexible visual search systems that can find objects based on natural language descriptions.

3. **Accessibility Technologies**: Improved scene understanding can enhance technologies that describe visual content to visually impaired users, providing more detailed and accurate descriptions.

4. **Autonomous Vehicles**: More robust object detection, especially for rare categories and under domain shifts, can improve the safety and reliability of perception systems in self-driving cars.

5. **Augmented and Mixed Reality**: Enhanced object recognition enables more seamless integration of virtual content with the real world in AR/MR applications.

Beyond specific applications, LLMDet represents an important step towards more general visual understanding systems that can flexibly recognize and describe a wide range of objects and scenes without requiring exhaustive category-specific training data.

The mutual benefit demonstrated between object detection and multi-modal capabilities also points to a promising direction for creating more powerful and versatile AI systems through the synergistic combination of specialized components.

### 7. Conclusion

LLMDet introduces a paradigm shift in open-vocabulary object detection by leveraging the semantic understanding capabilities of large language models. By incorporating detailed image-level captions alongside traditional region-level descriptions, LLMDet achieves significant improvements in detection performance, particularly for rare object categories and under domain shifts.

The research makes several key contributions:

1. A novel approach that uses LLMs to supervise open-vocabulary object detectors
2. The GroundingCap-1M dataset with 1.12 million images paired with detailed captions
3. A two-step training process that efficiently integrates detector and LLM capabilities
4. Empirical evidence showing state-of-the-art performance across multiple benchmarks
5. Demonstration of mutual benefits between improved object detection and enhanced multi-modal capabilities

The work opens up numerous avenues for future research, including exploring larger language models for even richer supervision, extending the approach to video understanding, and investigating how the improved detector can enhance other vision tasks beyond multi-modal applications.

As vision systems continue to evolve towards more general and flexible understanding capabilities, approaches like LLMDet that effectively combine the strengths of specialized components represent a promising direction for creating AI systems with more human-like perception and understanding abilities.
