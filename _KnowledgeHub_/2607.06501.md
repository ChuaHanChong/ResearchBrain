# Hypothesis-driven Model Expansion under Uncertainty for Open-World Robot Planning

> [!summary] Summary
> A framework called HUME allows service robots to expand their knowledge and adapt to unknown environments by generating, verifying, and updating object-centric hypotheses through an iterative process integrating symbolic planners with foundation models. This approach achieved a 0.93 success rate and 0.86 Success-weighted Path Length in real-world mobile manipulation tasks, improving robustness over methods without uncertainty-aware model expansion.

## Problem

- Traditional automated planning systems require complete and accurate world models, making them brittle and non-generalizable in open-world, uncertain human environments.
- Large Language Models (LLMs) struggle with long-horizon plan coherence, constraint satisfaction, "hallucinations," and active information acquisition when used directly for embodied robot planning.
- Existing hybrid approaches often treat LLM-acquired knowledge as static or update it only passively upon execution failure, leading to plans based on unverified or incorrect assumptions.

## Method

- The **Hypothesis-driven Uncertainty-aware Model Expansion (HUME)** framework treats missing environmental knowledge as a set of ==object-centric hypotheses== with unknown truth values.
- It augments the planning problem with explicit ==verification actions== for each hypothesis, enabling a classical planner to proactively seek information to validate or refute assumptions.
- Foundation models (LLMs, VLMs) are utilized to generate plausible hypotheses based on task instructions and current state, and to verify them against sensory observations during execution.

## Results

- Uncertainty-aware model expansion significantly improved task success rates and efficiency (SPL) for both symbolic and LLM-based planners in simulation (Block Processing World, AI2-THOR) and real-world experiments.
- The PDDL planner with uncertainty-aware model expansion (PDDL-H-V) achieved a **0.93** success rate and **0.86** SPL in real-world mobile manipulation tasks, outperforming LLM-based planners.
- Real-world demonstrations showed robots successfully adapting by verifying object attributes (e.g., finding a zero-sugar drink) and action effects (e.g., microwave button functionality), revising their models when initial hypotheses were refuted.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Explicitly representing model expansion as an uncertain and actively verifiable process is crucial for robots to operate robustly and adaptively in open-world settings.
> - Integrating the structured reasoning capabilities of symbolic planners with the flexible knowledge generation of LLMs can overcome limitations of both, leading to more reliable and autonomous knowledge acquisition.
> - Maintaining a 'history context' of past hypotheses and verification outcomes helps the LLM generate consistent and non-redundant model expansions over time.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How this work fits into the broader research landscape

Autonomous robots operating in real-world human environments, such as homes, face challenges due to inherent uncertainty and incomplete knowledge of objects, their attributes, and action effects. This scenario represents an "open-world" setting, contrasting with "closed-world" assumptions prevalent in classical automated planning. Traditional planning approaches, which rely on predefined state representations and transition models, struggle in such environments where critical information is often missing or underspecified.

Recent advancements in Large Language Models (LLMs) have introduced capabilities for zero-shot decision-making and plan generation, leveraging extensive commonsense knowledge. However, these LLM-based planners frequently encounter difficulties with long-horizon coherence, constraint satisfaction, and active information acquisition, particularly when operating with partial or potentially hallucinatory knowledge in open-world contexts. The integration of LLMs with classical planning frameworks has emerged as a strategy to combine the symbolic structure of traditional AI with the generalization abilities of LLMs. Previous work in this hybrid domain has explored using foundation models to construct or amend symbolic representations, such as generating planning domains, inferring missing preconditions or effects, or building probabilistic world models. However, these methods often treat the acquired knowledge as static or update it only passively upon execution failure, overlooking the inherent uncertainty in dynamically expanded world models.

This paper addresses the gap between structured reasoning and unstructured open-world knowledge by explicitly integrating uncertainty-aware model expansion. It differentiates itself from prior efforts by treating the model expansion itself as an uncertain and actively verifiable process. This perspective aligns with Bayes-adaptive planning, where an agent reasons over model uncertainty and selects actions that jointly achieve task goals and reduce model uncertainty. The work builds upon existing research that uses LLMs for model acquisition by introducing an active verification mechanism within the planning loop, thereby enabling robots to proactively seek information to validate or refute hypotheses about their environment model. This contrasts with approaches that rely solely on passive inference or replanning after detected failures, positioning the presented framework as a method for more robust and adaptive open-world operation.

### 2. Key objectives and motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop an open-world planning framework that enables service robots to operate effectively in unknown environments with incomplete knowledge. This involves addressing the fundamental challenge of autonomous knowledge expansion in human environments where traditional closed-world planning approaches are insufficient.

The motivation for this work stems from several limitations observed in current robotic planning systems:
- **Limitations of Traditional Planning:** Classical automated planning systems, exemplified by PDDL (Planning Domain Definition Language), require complete and accurate knowledge of the environment, including object locations, attributes, and action effects. This reliance on pre-programmed and manually designed knowledge renders them brittle and non-generalizable in dynamic, open-world settings like human households, where information is often underspecified or entirely missing.
- **Challenges with Large Language Models (LLMs):** While LLMs possess extensive commonsense knowledge and can generate plausible plans, their application in embodied robotics is hindered by issues such as "hallucinations," where they generate knowledge not grounded in facts, and difficulties in enforcing logical constraints and ensuring long-horizon plan coherence. They also typically lack mechanisms for active information acquisition under uncertainty.
- **Deficiencies in Existing Hybrid Approaches:** Prior research has attempted to bridge LLMs with symbolic planning by using LLMs to generate or fix symbolic representations. However, these methods often treat the expanded knowledge as certain or update it only reactively after an execution failure. This passive approach can lead to "silent failures" where plans are executed based on incorrect assumptions without explicit verification, increasing risks and reducing reliability.

To address these motivations, the paper proposes the Hypothesis-driven Uncertainty-aware Model Expansion (HUME) framework. The key insight is to explicitly treat model expansion as an uncertain and actively verifiable process. This involves:
- **Maintaining Uncertainty-Aware Knowledge:** Explicitly representing unknown or uncertain aspects of the environment (e.g., object locations, attributes, action effects) as object-centric hypotheses.
- **Integrating Hypothesis Verification into Planning:** Formulating the planning problem such that actions are selected not only to achieve task goals but also to actively reduce model uncertainty by verifying these hypotheses.
- **Leveraging Foundation Models for Hypothesis Management:** Utilizing foundation models (e.g., LLMs, VLMs) to generate initial plausible hypotheses and to ground and update these hypotheses based on new sensory observations and execution feedback.

The ultimate goal is to enable robots to autonomously expand their world knowledge and adapt their behavior through an iterative process of hypothesis generation, planning, execution, and refinement, thereby enhancing their robustness and practical deployability in real-world, open-ended scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and approach

The proposed framework, Hypothesis-driven Uncertainty-aware Model Expansion (HUME), operates as an iterative closed-loop system encompassing model expansion, planning, and execution. This section details the components and processes that constitute HUME.

**A. Problem Formulation**
The system considers planning under an incomplete world model $M = \langle S, P, A, T \rangle$, where $S$ is the symbolic state space, $P$ is the predicate set, $A$ is the action space, and $T$ is a deterministic transition function. The true environment is governed by an unknown ground-truth model $M_{gt}$. Tasks are specified by natural language instructions $L$, translating to a goal set $G \subseteq S_{gt}$. The core challenge is to plan and act when $M$ is insufficient to reach $G$.

Model incompleteness is addressed by introducing a set of factorized hypotheses $H = \{h_1, \ldots, h_n\}$. Each hypothesis $h_i$ encodes an object-centric fact (e.g., object existence, attribute, action effect) with an unknown truth value $v(h_i) \in \{0, 1\}$. The robot maintains a belief $b(H) = p(v_1, \ldots, v_n | \tau)$ over these hypotheses. A verification action $\phi(h_i) \rightarrow A_{i}^{verify}$ is generated for each hypothesis, augmenting the action space to $A' = A \cup \{A_{i}^{verify}\}$. Decision-making is framed as a Bayes-adaptive MDP with state $(s_0, b(H))$, where actions transition the state according to an expected model $M^* = M \circ H$, and hypotheses are updated based on observations. The objective is to find a plan $\pi$ that maximizes expected cumulative reward by jointly considering task execution and hypothesis verification.

**B. Model Initialization**
HUME begins with an initial symbolic model $M$, which serves as the robot’s prior knowledge. This model is composed of:
- **Environment Knowledge:** Represented as a scene graph depicting rooms, locations, and objects, along with their relationships.
- **Domain Knowledge:** Specified in PDDL format, defining the preconditions and effects of known actions.

This initial model is acknowledged to be incomplete, lacking crucial information such as object locations, specific attributes, or context-dependent action effects. It can be manually specified or extracted from datasets offline.

**C. Model Expansion as Hypotheses**
Missing knowledge is represented as a set of object-centric hypotheses, $H$. Each hypothesis $h \in H$ is a tentative extension to the world model, with an initially unknown truth value. Hypotheses are structured with:
- `id`: A unique identifier (e.g., h1).
- `type`: Categorized as `object_existence`, `object_attribute`, or `action_effect`.
- `description`: A natural language statement of the hypothesized fact.
- `content`: PDDL facts or action effects to be added to the model if the hypothesis is true. This content can be an API call to modify the PDDL problem file.
- `condition`: An optional dependency on another hypothesis (e.g., an attribute hypothesis depending on object existence).
- `verification_condition`: PDDL-like preconditions (e.g., `(At ?r fridge2) (IsOpen fridge2)`) that must be met for the hypothesis to be reliably tested. If `None`, the hypothesis is assumed true without explicit verification.

**D. Task-Oriented Hypothesis Generation**
Hypotheses are generated by an LLM-based generator, $\Phi_{LLM}$, in a structured manner. Given a natural language instruction $L$, the system first parses it into a formal goal specification $G$ (a conjunction of logical predicates). By comparing $G$ with the initial model $M$ and current scene graph $s$, the robot identifies `missing_objects` $O_{miss}$ and `missing_predicates` $P_{miss}$. The LLM then categorizes $P_{miss}$ into `attribute_predicates` and `effect_predicates`.

The LLM is prompted with the current state $s$, goal $G$, categorized missing predicates, and a `history_context` $C_{hist}$. The `history_context` is crucial for maintaining consistency and avoiding redundant hypothesis generation by tracking past executions, generated hypotheses, and their verification outcomes. This process produces a set of plausible hypotheses $H$.

**E. Planning Under Hypothesis Uncertainty**
The planning problem is augmented to $\Pi = \langle S^+, P^+, A^+, T^+, s_0, G \rangle$, where $S^+ = S \times B$ includes a belief space $B$ over hypothesis assignments. Initially, all hypotheses are `Unknown`.
To address hypothesis uncertainty, a set of verification actions $A_{verify}$ is introduced. For each hypothesis $h$, an abstract action $a_{h}^{verify}$ is generated to observe its truth value.
To integrate with classical deterministic planners (e.g., Fast Downward, ENHSP), an "all-outcomes determinization" strategy is applied to verification actions. Each $a_{h}^{verify}$ is determinized into two actions: $a_{h+}^{verify}$ (hypothesis verified as true) and $a_{h-}^{verify}$ (hypothesis refuted). In this framework, $a_{h-}^{verify}$ is explicitly excluded from the planning graph to encourage an optimistic strategy, where the planner prioritizes paths assuming hypotheses will be true if verified. Negative outcomes are handled by replanning.

Verification actions are defined in PDDL with specific preconditions (`(At ?r ?l)`, `(¬True ?h)`, `(Related ?h ?o)`, $\phi_{cond}$, $\phi_{verify}$) and effects (`(True ?h)`). To bias the planner towards resolving uncertainty early, actions that rely on `Unknown` hypotheses incur a high penalty cost. This encourages the planner to schedule `verify` actions before manipulation actions dependent on uncertain facts.

**F. Hypothesis Verification and Model Update**
During execution:
- **Standard Actions:** Executed using the robot's low-level skill library.
- **Verification Actions:** When an $a \in A_{verify}$ is prescribed, a dedicated verification routine is invoked. This occurs only when the `verification_condition` (as predicted by the LLM and reasoned by the planner) is met.
 - **Object Existence:** Verified by comparing hypothesized objects against a newly observed scene graph from object detectors.
 - **Object Attributes/Action Effects:** Verified by querying a Vision-Language Model (VLM) with current/recent images and the hypothesis description to perform question answering.
- **Model Update:** If a hypothesis is confirmed, its facts are incorporated into the world model. If refuted, it is marked `False` in $C_{hist}$, triggering hypothesis regeneration (conditioned on new context) and a replanning cycle.

**G. Necessary Assumptions**
The framework relies on several assumptions:
1. **Task Scoping:** Focuses on underspecified symbolic world modeling, assuming reliable execution of atomic skills under full observability.
2. **Goal Requirements:** Task goals must be expressible in structured logic, define the abstraction of missing knowledge, and be verifiable using robot sensing at known configurations. Missing concepts and predicates are encoded in task instructions.
3. **Language Model Capabilities:** The LLM is assumed to generate correct hypotheses within bounded attempts, propose verification conditions, and evaluate hypotheses from raw sensory observations.

### 4. Main findings and results

The experiments evaluated HUME in both simulation and real-world environments, comparing six approaches categorized by their representation (explicit model expansion, uncertainty awareness) and inference method (formal symbolic planners, LLMs). Performance was primarily measured by Success Rate and Success-weighted Path Length (SPL).

**A. Simulation Results**
1. **Block Processing World:** This environment extended the classic Blocks World, requiring the robot to discover unknown effects of block processors.
 - **Impact of Model Expansion:** Approaches utilizing model expansion (LLM-H-NV, PDDL-H-NV, LLM-H-V, PDDL-H-V) demonstrated substantial performance gains over those without (LLM-NH-NV, PDDL-NH-NV). Without model expansion, planners consistently failed, indicating that static initial models are insufficient for open-world tasks.
 - **Impact of Uncertainty Awareness:** Incorporating uncertainty awareness (LLM-H-V, PDDL-H-V) further improved performance in both success rate and SPL compared to deterministic expansion (LLM-H-NV, PDDL-H-NV). This highlights the importance of explicitly reasoning about hypothesis validity rather than assuming certainty.
 - **Planner Comparison:** The formal PDDL planner (PDDL-H-V) achieved a higher success rate (0.87) and SPL (0.77) than the LLM planner (LLM-H-V: 0.69 success, 0.53 SPL) under uncertain model expansion. This suggests that explicit hypothesis representation and structured planning allow the PDDL planner to restore solvability more reliably.
 - **Scalability:** As task complexity (number of blocks and regions) increased, uncertainty-aware expansion maintained more robust performance, and the performance gap between LLM and formal planners narrowed, indicating that proper hypothesis handling became a dominant factor.

2. **Mobile Manipulation in Unknown Environments (AI2-THOR):** This setting involved a mobile manipulator in household-like environments with unknown object locations and attributes.
 - **Similar Trends:** The results mirrored the Block Processing World, with both planners benefiting from model expansion and uncertainty-aware reasoning. The PDDL planner with uncertain model expansion (PDDL-H-V) achieved the highest success rate (0.82) and SPL (0.58).
 - **LLM Limitations:** The relative improvement for the LLM-based planner was smaller in AI2-THOR compared to BlockWorld, potentially due to the increased complexity of verification and planning constraints in more realistic environments, which LLMs found harder to enforce.

**B. Real-World Experiments**
Experiments were conducted on a Fetch mobile manipulator in a household environment with five complex, long-horizon tasks (T1-T5) involving object attributes, locations, and action effects.
- **Confirmation of Simulation Trends:** Real-world results corroborated simulation findings: uncertainty-aware model expansion significantly improved task performance for both LLM-based and formal planners. PDDL-H-V achieved a success rate of 0.93 and SPL of 0.86, outperforming other variants.
- **Behavioral Illustration (T1: Deliver a zero-sugar drink):** The robot first hypothesized a drink in the refrigerator, but verification after picking it up revealed it was not zero-sugar. This triggered hypothesis regeneration and replanning. The robot then explored the kitchen island, where it successfully verified both the existence and attribute of a zero-sugar drink, which it then delivered.
- **Behavioral Illustration (T5: Throw away a blue-floral bowl and turn off the living room light):** The robot hypothesized bowl location, attribute, and light switch effects. It searched the kitchen island, then the kitchen counter, rejecting an initial bowl hypothesis before confirming the correct one and discarding it. For the light, it triggered one switch, observed no effect, updated its hypothesis, and then triggered a second switch, which successfully turned off the light. These instances demonstrated the framework's ability to adapt and correct its world model through active verification.

**C. Appliance Operation**
The framework was applied to an Autolife S2 humanoid robot operating a microwave to defrost food for 40 seconds, where button effects were initially unknown.
- **Numeric Planning:** Using the ENHSP numeric planner, the robot hypothesized a 10-second time increase from the `increase` button in time-defrost mode.
- **Hypothesis Revision:** Verification revealed a 5-second increase, prompting the robot to revise its hypothesis. The updated, confirmed hypothesis was then used for subsequent planning and execution to reach the 40-second goal, demonstrating generalizability to numeric action effects.

In summary, the results consistently indicate that explicitly representing and proactively verifying uncertain model expansions is crucial for effective robot operation in open-world settings. This mechanism enhances robustness and success rates for both symbolic and LLM-based planners, with formal planners showing an advantage in constraint enforcement and reliable solvability restoration.

### 5. Significance and potential impact

The research presented in this paper introduces a hypothesis-driven framework for open-world robot planning, HUME, which demonstrates a method for integrating structured symbolic reasoning with the unstructured knowledge from foundation models. This approach offers several significant contributions and potential impacts on the field of robotics and artificial intelligence:

**A. Bridging Symbolic AI and Foundation Models:**
The core significance lies in providing a principled mechanism to bridge the gap between classical planning systems—which offer logical rigor and constraint consistency but are brittle under incomplete models—and large language models (LLMs)—which are flexible and knowledge-rich but prone to hallucination and difficulty in enforcing strict logical constraints. HUME leverages LLMs not as direct planners, but as generators of uncertain model expansions whose validity is then systematically tested through a planning and execution loop. This shifts the burden of uncertainty management to the planning system, which is better equipped to reason about dependencies and action feasibility.

**B. Enabling Autonomous Knowledge Expansion:**
HUME allows robots to autonomously expand their world models in an iterative and adaptive manner. By generating, reasoning over, and verifying object-centric hypotheses (e.g., object locations, attributes, action effects), the robot can incrementally acquire missing knowledge relevant to a task. This contrasts with static models or passive updates, promoting a more active and informed learning process in dynamic environments. This capability is critical for robots operating in novel or underspecified environments without extensive pre-programming.

**C. Enhanced Robustness and Task Success in Open-World Settings:**
The experimental results consistently demonstrate that uncertainty-aware model expansion significantly improves task success rates and efficiency (SPL) for both symbolic (PDDL) and LLM-based planners. The framework enables robots to proactively explore and gather information to validate assumptions, reducing the risk of silent failures caused by incorrect or unverified knowledge. This leads to more robust and reliable robot behavior in complex, open-world tasks, as exemplified by the real-world mobile manipulation and appliance operation scenarios.

**D. Advancing Practical Deployment of Service Robots:**
The ability of HUME to handle model incompleteness and uncertainty makes it relevant for the practical deployment of service robots in real human environments. Household robots, for instance, frequently encounter objects in unknown locations, novel attributes, or unmodeled interactions with appliances. By allowing robots to adapt their understanding of the world on-the-fly, the framework moves towards more capable and general-purpose autonomous agents.

**E. Insights into AI Integration:**
The work offers three key insights into integrating different AI paradigms:
- **Injecting Compositional Structure:** It demonstrates how LLM-generated unstructured knowledge can be translated into object-centric hypotheses that impose a compositional structure, enabling logical reasoning by the planner.
- **Automated Domain and Problem Specification:** Hypothesis generation can be viewed as an automated, task-driven process for specifying and refining planning domains and problems incrementally.
- **Bayes-Adaptive Planning Perspective:** The framework aligns conceptually with Bayes-adaptive planning, treating model uncertainty as a fundamental aspect to be reasoned about and traded off against task progress.

**Limitations and Future Work:**
Despite its contributions, the paper identifies several limitations that represent important avenues for future research:
- **Uncertainty Representation and Planning Algorithms:** The current ternary belief system (true, false, unknown) and optimistic determinization simplify planning but limit graded confidence or risk reasoning. Future work aims to integrate richer probabilistic representations and advanced POMDP solvers.
- **Generalizing Verification Conditions:** The reliance on LLMs to propose sufficient verification conditions might be limited for complex geometric, long-horizon, or perception-intensive interactions. Integrating geometry and perception-aware reasoning with language-predicted conditions is a future direction.
- **Hypothesis Generation Complexity:** Current hypotheses are object-centric facts. Scaling hypothesis generation to more complex knowledge, such as abstract mechanisms or long-range causal dependencies, remains a challenge.
- **Source of Abstraction:** The framework currently requires task instructions to define missing knowledge abstraction. Exploring failure reasoning modules as an additional source of abstraction is suggested.
- **Open-Ended Exploration and Impossibility Reasoning:** The framework assumes task achievability and bounds exploration. Incorporating explicit impossibility reasoning or belief-space pruning could enhance its capabilities.

In conclusion, HUME provides a foundation for developing more intelligent and adaptive robots that can operate reliably in complex and unknown real-world environments by systematically managing uncertainty in their world models.
