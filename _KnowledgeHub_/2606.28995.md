# HJ-SafeDMP: Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability-Guided Dynamic Movement Primitives for Provably Safe Robot Motion

> [!summary] Summary
> HJ-SafeDMP integrates Dynamic Movement Primitives with learned Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability-based safety value functions for provably safe robot motion, eliminating online optimization. The framework demonstrated a 0.0% collision rate and achieved execution speeds of 1.2e-4 seconds per control step, orders of magnitude faster than optimization-based safety methods, while maintaining robust trajectory adherence.

## Problem

- Existing robot control methods struggle to simultaneously provide both computational efficiency and formal, provable safety guarantees.
- Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs) offer efficient and robust motion generation but lack inherent mechanisms for formal collision avoidance, relying on heuristic methods that can be unpredictable.
- Control-theoretic safety methods like Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) and classical Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability provide strong guarantees but often incur high computational costs or suffer from scalability limitations for real-time, high-dimensional systems.

## Method

- A two-stage **HJ-SafeDMP** framework is proposed, combining ==Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs)== with a learned ==Control Barrier Value Function (CBVF)== derived from ==Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability==.
- A CBVF is learned offline from diverse demonstration and exploratory data using a model-free, ==finite-difference Hamilton-Jacobi recursion== with ==expectile regression== to account for out-of-distribution actions.
- A ==closed-form safety modulation law== is developed, utilizing the gradient of the learned CBVF, to adjust DMP trajectories in real-time, thereby replacing computationally expensive online optimization procedures.

## Results

- Achieved an execution speed of **1.2e-4 seconds** per control step, demonstrating orders of magnitude faster performance compared to optimization-based safety baselines (e.g., **0.3207 s** for NODE-CLF-CBF).
- Maintained a **0.0%** collision rate across **100** trials with both static and dynamic obstacles, matching the formal safety guarantees of other methods while outperforming heuristic approaches.
- Exhibited faster obstacle avoidance convergence (**0.287 s**) and robust trajectory recovery from impulse perturbations (**0.031 s** convergence time) compared to existing safe motion generation baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Offline learning of a Control Barrier Value Function (CBVF) from diverse state-action data, employing expectile regression, enables approximating worst-case safety margins without real-time interaction.
> - Replacing computationally expensive online quadratic programming (QP) with a closed-form safety modulation derived from the CBVF gradient allows for high-frequency control while maintaining formal safety guarantees.
> - The integration of conformal prediction calibration for the learned CBVF boundary provides finite-sample probabilistic safety coverage, enhancing the reliability of empirical safety certificates.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Robotic systems operating in environments that involve human interaction or critical safety requirements must exhibit both robustness to unexpected events and formal guarantees against collisions. Current approaches to robot control often address these properties separately, leading to compromises in performance or safety.

Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs) represent a class of methods for learning and generalizing robot motions from demonstrations. They are recognized for their inherent stability, adaptability to temporal and spatial scaling, and efficiency in generating trajectories from limited data. These characteristics make DMPs suitable for applications such as human-robot interaction. However, DMPs inherently lack mechanisms for formally guaranteeing safety. They are frequently augmented with reactive strategies, such as Artificial Potential Fields (APFs), which can suffer from issues like local minima and oscillations, and do not provide formal safety certificates.

Conversely, control-theoretic methods, including Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) and Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) Reachability analysis, offer frameworks for rigorous safety guarantees. CBFs ensure forward invariance of safe sets by formulating safety as a constraint within an optimization problem, typically a Quadratic Program (QP). While effective, solving a QP at each control step introduces computational overhead, limiting applicability in high-frequency control. Moreover, the analytical construction of valid CBFs for complex, high-dimensional systems is challenging. HJ Reachability analysis provides a foundational approach for computing worst-case safety margins and forward-invariant safe sets by solving Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) partial differential equations. Traditional grid-based HJ methods, however, face scalability limitations due to the "curse of dimensionality," making them impractical for real-time robotic systems.

Recent advancements have focused on learning-based approximations of HJ-inspired value functions, such as Control Barrier Value Functions (CBVFs), from offline data. These methods utilize techniques like model-free Bellman recursions, expectile regression to manage out-of-distribution actions, and conformal prediction for probabilistic safety coverage, thereby mitigating the dimensionality issues of classical HJ methods and avoiding online interactions.

This paper addresses the gap between the computational efficiency and robustness provided by DMPs and the formal safety guarantees offered by HJ Reachability. It aims to integrate these distinct paradigms to achieve simultaneously robust, provably safe, and computationally efficient robot motion without relying on online optimization, which is a common bottleneck in CBF-based approaches.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary motivation for this research stems from the challenge of deploying robots reliably in safety-critical, human-centric environments. Such deployments require robots to not only maintain stable, goal-directed behavior under disturbances (robustness) but also to formally guarantee collision avoidance with people, objects, and environmental boundaries (safety). The efficient computation of control actions that satisfy both properties is a central problem in robotic control.

The limitations of existing approaches highlight the need for a novel framework:
- DMPs, while robust and computationally efficient for learning and generalizing motions, do not inherently provide safety guarantees. Their reliance on heuristic avoidance methods can lead to unpredictable behavior and a lack of formal certification.
- Formal safety methods like CBFs, though providing strong guarantees, incur significant computational costs due to online quadratic programming, hindering real-time, high-frequency control. Constructing CBFs for complex robotic systems also presents a non-trivial challenge.
- HJ Reachability analysis offers a principled approach to safety, but its classical implementations are computationally intensive for high-dimensional systems. While learning-based approximations exist, their integration into frameworks that also leverage the benefits of DMPs has been less explored, particularly in a manner that preserves computational efficiency.

Against this background, the key objectives of the HJ-SafeDMP framework are:
1. To propose a novel framework, HJ-SafeDMP, that integrates Dynamic Movement Primitives with learned Hamilton-Jacobi Reachability-based safety value functions. This integration aims to enable provably safe motion generation without requiring online optimization procedures, thereby preserving computational efficiency.
2. To derive and utilize a model-free, finite-difference CBVF recursion based on HJ Reachability theory. This recursion is intended to facilitate the learning of safety certificates entirely from offline demonstration data, employing expectile regression to manage challenges associated with out-of-distribution actions in the offline learning context.
3. To develop a closed-form safety modulation law. This law is designed to filter the outputs of DMPs by using the gradient of the learned CBVF. The goal is to replace the computational overhead typically associated with CBF-QP (Quadratic Program) approaches with a single gradient evaluation at each control step.
4. To experimentally validate that HJ-SafeDMP achieves formal safety guarantees with a significantly faster execution speed compared to optimization-based baselines. Furthermore, the evaluation seeks to demonstrate that the framework maintains the inherent robustness and adaptability characteristic of DMPs, particularly in contexts like human-robot interaction.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

HJ-SafeDMP is structured as a two-stage framework comprising an offline learning phase for a safety value function and an online execution phase for real-time safety-guided motion generation.

**A. Stage 1: Learning the Control Barrier Value Function (CBVF) from Offline Data**

1. **Data Collection:** An offline dataset, denoted as $D = \{(x_t, u_t, x_{t+1})\}$, is collected. This dataset consists of state-action-next state transitions derived from kinesthetic demonstrations and exploratory robot trajectories within the workspace. Each transition includes the end-effector state $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$, the control input $u_t$, and the subsequent state $x_{t+1}$.
2. **Safety Specification:** For each state $x_t$, a safety specification $\ell(x_t)$ is computed. This function quantifies the signed distance to obstacles, where $\ell(x) < 0$ indicates that the state $x$ is within an obstacle and thus unsafe. The formulation used is $\ell(x) = \min_i (\|x - o_i\| - r_i)$, where $o_i$ and $r_i$ are the center and radius of the $i$-th obstacle, respectively.
3. **Finite-Difference CBVF Recursion:** To learn the CBVF from offline data, the continuous-time HJB variational inequality is approximated using a finite-difference barrier recursion. The discounted recursion target is defined as $B_D^{Target} = (1 - \gamma) \ell(x) + \gamma \min \{\ell(x), B_{\psi}(x')\}$, where $x'$ is the observed successor state, $\gamma \in (0, 1]$ is a discount factor, and $B_{\psi}$ is a neural network parameterizing the CBVF. This recursive formulation enables the propagation of worst-case safety information backward in time, marking a state as unsafe if any future state along a possible trajectory could enter the failure set.
4. **Expectile-Based Learning Objective:** In the offline learning context, directly maximizing over all possible actions, as in classical HJ formulations, is impractical due to the limited scope of the collected dataset. To address this, expectile regression is employed. The learning objective $L_B(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{(x,u,x') \sim D} [L_{\tau}(B_D^{Target}(x) - B_{\theta}(x))]$ minimizes a $\tau$-expectile loss. For $\tau > 0.5$, this loss formulation assigns greater weight to positive residuals, causing the learned CBVF $B_{\theta}$ to approximate the upper envelope of safety values achievable with the actions present in the dataset. This results in a CBVF that reflects the best possible safety performance supported by the observed data, rather than an average behavior policy.
5. **Conformal Prediction Calibration:** Given that the learned CBVF is an approximation from finite data, its zero-level set (the nominal safety boundary) may be miscalibrated. To provide finite-sample probabilistic safety coverage, a conformal prediction calibration step is applied. This involves computing rollout safety values $V_{\pi_c}(x_i)$ for a set of calibration states $N_s$. A correction margin $\delta^*$ is then determined, such that the probability of a true unsafe state being within the calibrated safe set $S_{\delta^*} = \{x : B_{\theta}(x) \ge \delta^*\}$ is bounded, providing a specified confidence level $(1 - \beta_s)$ that the actual safety violation rate is below a threshold $\epsilon_s$.

**B. Stage 2: HJ-SafeDMP — Closed-Form Safety Modulation**

1. **DMP as Nominal Motion Plan:** The fundamental motion is generated by a Dynamic Movement Primitive (DMP), which is learned from a single kinesthetic demonstration. The DMP dynamics, typically represented as a second-order system with a learned forcing term, provide a reference trajectory $x_{DMP}(t)$ and velocity $\dot{x}_{DMP}(t)$, characterized by inherent robustness to perturbations and stable convergence to a goal.
2. **CBVF-Based Safety Modulation:** Instead of employing an online Quadratic Program (QP) for safety enforcement, the gradient of the learned CBVF $B_{\theta}(x)$ is utilized to construct a closed-form safety modulation term, $f_{HJ}(x)$, which is added to the nominal DMP dynamics:
 $\tau^2 \ddot{x} = \alpha_z (\beta_z (g - x) - \tau \dot{x}) + f(z) + f_{HJ}(x, \dot{x}, O)$.
 The safety modulation term is defined as:
 $f_{HJ}(x) = \begin{cases} k_s \frac{1}{\xi(B_{\theta}(x))} \cdot \nabla_x B_{\theta}(x) & \text{if } B_{\theta}(x) < B_{thresh} \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$
 where $\xi(b) = \max(b - \delta^*, \epsilon_{min})$, $k_s > 0$ is a safety gain, $B_{thresh}$ is an activation threshold, $\delta^*$ is the conformal calibration margin, and $\epsilon_{min}$ is a small positive constant. This term functions as a repulsion force: as the robot state approaches the calibrated unsafe region ($B_{\theta}(x) \to \delta^*$), $1/\xi(B_{\theta}(x))$ increases significantly, generating a strong corrective force in the direction that increases safety (normal to the barrier surface). When the robot is well within the safe region, the modulation term is inactive.
3. **Dynamic Obstacle Handling:** For dynamic obstacles with time-varying positions $o_c(t)$ and velocities $\dot{o}_c(t)$, the safety specification $\ell(x, t)$ is updated online. The learned CBVF $B_{\theta}(x)$ can then be re-evaluated at each control step through a single neural network forward pass, ensuring adaptability to changing environments with minimal computational cost.
4. **Adaptive Temporal Scaling:** To manage deviations caused by safety modulation, the DMP's temporal scaling factor $\tau$ is adaptively adjusted. An error term $e$ between the actual robot position $x_{actual}$ and the DMP's reference $x_{DMP}$ is tracked. The temporal factor $\tau$ increases when deviations are large, slowing down the DMP execution to allow more time for safety-guided recovery, and decreases when deviations are small, ensuring smooth progress.
5. **Safety Guarantee:** A forward invariance theorem states that if the initial state $x(0)$ is within the calibrated safe set ($B_{\theta}(x(0)) \ge \delta^*$) and the safety gain $k_s$ is sufficiently large, then the system's trajectory will remain within this safe set for all subsequent times. This is due to the modulation term ensuring that the time derivative of $B_{\theta}$ becomes positive whenever the state approaches the boundary, effectively pushing the state back into the safe region.

The complete procedure integrates these steps, executing the learned DMP modulated by the CBVF gradient in real-time.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The HJ-SafeDMP framework was evaluated using a 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda manipulator in a simulated cubic workspace. Comparisons were made against three baselines: NODE–CLF–CBF (a learning-based dynamics method with CBFs via online QP), DMP–APF (standard DMPs with Artificial Potential Fields), and SafeDMPs (DMPs with Spatio-Temporal Tubes).

**A. Experimental Setup Details:**
- **Robot & Environment:** 7-DOF Franka Emika Panda in a 1.1x1.1x1.1 m workspace. Control frequency: 200 Hz ($\Delta t = 0.005$ s). Obstacles modeled as spheres.
- **Demonstration Data:** LASA handwriting dataset (2D demonstrations lifted to 3D), augmented with random rotations and temporal resampling. DMPs were encoded with 25 basis functions.
- **CBVF Training:** The CBVF dataset combined demonstrations with exploratory rollouts, totaling 75,000 transitions to ensure diverse state-action coverage. The CBVF was parameterized by a 2-hidden-layer MLP (256 neurons per layer, ReLU activations). Training used Adam optimizer (learning rate $3 \times 10^{-4}$), expectile parameter $\tau=0.9$, and discount factor $\gamma=0.99$. Conformal calibration involved $N_s=500$ states, with $\epsilon_s=0.05$ and $\beta_s=0.01$.

**B. Key Quantitative and Qualitative Results:**

1. **Nominal Performance and Computational Efficiency:**
 - HJ-SafeDMP demonstrated an average execution time of $1.2 \times 10^{-4}$ seconds per control step. This is comparable to SafeDMPs ($1.03 \times 10^{-4}$ s) and DMP–APF ($6.7 \times 10^{-4}$ s), indicating highly efficient real-time operation.
 - Crucially, HJ-SafeDMP achieved orders of magnitude faster execution than the optimization-based NODE–CLF–CBF ($0.3207$ s per step). This substantial speedup is attributed to replacing online QP solves with a single neural network forward pass and gradient computation.
 - The Mean Absolute Error (MAE) from the nominal demonstration for HJ-SafeDMP was $0.0098$ m, indicating close adherence to the desired trajectory in the absence of obstacles, similar to SafeDMPs ($0.0113$ m).

2. **Robustness to Perturbations:**
 - Under impulse perturbations, HJ-SafeDMP exhibited smooth and bounded recovery, maintaining the trajectory within the safe set. Its MAE under perturbation was $0.0187$ m, and convergence time after perturbation was $0.031$ s, which is robust and fast.
 - In contrast, NODE–CLF–CBF showed overshoot and slower convergence. DMP–APF displayed sharp deviations and a risk of oscillation, characteristic of potential field methods. The proactive safety awareness provided by the learned CBVF in HJ-SafeDMP was observed to facilitate smoother avoidance compared to reactive baselines.

3. **Safety from Static and Dynamic Obstacles:**
 - HJ-SafeDMP achieved a $0.0\%$ collision rate across 100 trials with both static and dynamic obstacles. This matches the formal safety guarantees provided by NODE–CLF–CBF and SafeDMPs.
 - DMP–APF, lacking formal guarantees, suffered a $14.0\%$ collision rate due to susceptibility to local minima.
 - HJ-SafeDMP demonstrated a significantly faster convergence time for obstacle avoidance ($0.287$ s) compared to SafeDMPs ($0.633$ s) and NODE–CLF–CBF ($0.411$ s). This faster convergence is attributed to the learned CBVF's ability to encode global safety information derived from HJ Bellman recursions, allowing for more proactive and efficient path deformation.
 - Qualitatively, the CBVF-based modulation in HJ-SafeDMP produced smoother trajectory deviations compared to the jerky corrections observed with CBF-QP methods and more predictable paths than APF-based approaches.

4. **Ablation Studies:**
 - **Effect of Expectile Parameter $\tau$**: Varying $\tau$ in the expectile loss revealed that $\tau=0.9$ yielded the optimal safety rate (98.3%). Lower values (e.g., $\tau=0.5$) resulted in an overly conservative barrier and a smaller safe set. Higher values (e.g., $\tau=0.99$) led to a slight degradation in safety, suggesting overfitting to potentially optimistic transitions.
 - **Conformal Calibration**: Without conformal calibration ($\delta^*=0$), the collision rate in dynamic obstacle trials increased to $2\%$, indicating miscalibration of the learned safety boundary. With calibration ($\delta^*=-0.04$), the collision rate was reduced to $0\%$, confirming the importance of this step for robust probabilistic safety coverage.

These results indicate that HJ-SafeDMP successfully integrates the robustness and adaptability of DMPs with the formal safety guarantees of HJ Reachability, achieving superior computational efficiency and obstacle avoidance performance compared to existing methods.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The HJ-SafeDMP framework presents a notable contribution to the field of robotic control by addressing a persistent challenge: achieving simultaneous provable safety, robustness, and computational efficiency for robot motion generation.

**Significance:**
- **Resolution of Trade-offs:** The primary significance lies in its ability to resolve the long-standing trade-off between the computational efficiency and inherent robustness of Dynamic Movement Primitives (DMPs) and the formal safety guarantees offered by Hamilton-Jacobi (HJ) Reachability analysis. By integrating these paradigms, the framework enables robotic systems to operate with both agility and verifiable safety.
- **Computationally Efficient Formal Safety:** A key achievement is the elimination of online optimization (e.g., Quadratic Program solves) typically required by Control Barrier Function (CBF) based methods. The proposed closed-form safety modulation, which utilizes the gradient of a learned Control Barrier Value Function (CBVF), significantly reduces computational overhead, making formal safety guarantees feasible for high-frequency control applications in real-time.
- **Offline Learning for Safety Certificates:** The methodology demonstrates a practical approach to learning complex safety certificates (CBVFs) entirely from offline demonstration data. This leverages advancements in model-free finite-difference HJ recursions and expectile regression, which effectively handle challenges like out-of-distribution actions and uncertainty in offline learning environments.
- **Probabilistic Safety Guarantees:** The incorporation of conformal prediction calibration provides finite-sample probabilistic safety coverage. This step is important for bridging the gap between empirically learned neural safety certificates and the reliability demands of real-world robot deployment, offering quantified confidence in safety.
- **Improved Obstacle Avoidance Performance:** Compared to existing closed-form safety methods like SafeDMPs which rely on hand-designed Spatio-Temporal Tubes, HJ-SafeDMP exhibits faster obstacle avoidance convergence. This improvement is attributed to the learned CBVF's capacity to encode global safety information, allowing for more proactive and informed trajectory deformations.

**Potential Impact:**
The HJ-SafeDMP framework has the potential to enhance the safe deployment of robots in a variety of safety-critical and human-centric environments. This includes, but is not limited to:
- **Human-Robot Interaction (HRI):** Robots can interact more safely and predictably with humans in collaborative tasks, manufacturing, and service robotics, where real-time adaptability and guaranteed collision avoidance are paramount.
- **Autonomous Navigation:** The framework could contribute to safer autonomous driving and aerial robotics, where rapid, collision-free path planning in dynamic, uncertain environments is essential.
- **Medical Robotics:** Increased safety guarantees could facilitate the wider adoption of robots in surgical procedures or rehabilitation, where precision and verifiable safety are critical.
- **Industrial Automation:** Robots in factories could operate more efficiently and safely alongside human workers, enabling more flexible and adaptable production lines.

**Limitations:**
The authors acknowledge several limitations:
- **Empirical vs. Absolute Guarantees:** The learned CBVF relies on a neural network approximation. Consequently, the safety guarantees are empirical, subject to function approximation errors, and are not absolute formal verifications.
- **Known Safety Specification:** The framework assumes that the safety specification $\ell(x)$ is known and that obstacle geometries can be accurately described by signed distance functions.
- **Data Requirements:** The effectiveness of CBVF training depends on the availability of a sufficiently diverse offline dataset that covers both safe and potentially unsafe regions of the state space.

**Future Work:**
Future research directions outlined by the authors aim to address these limitations and expand the framework's capabilities:
- **Strengthening Guarantees:** Integration of formal verification methods, such as Lipschitz-based certification and advanced conformal prediction refinements, is planned to enhance the rigor of safety guarantees.
- **Extended Applicability:** Expanding the framework to include whole-body safety for complex humanoid robots and integrating with high-level task planners for multi-step manipulation are identified as key extensions.
- **Robustness to Uncertainty:** Future work will explore robust extensions to handle adversarial disturbances and distributional shifts between training and deployment conditions, building on robust HJ Reachability and uncertainty-aware offline learning.
- **Data Efficiency and Generalization:** The authors intend to investigate the integration with model-based offline reinforcement learning (RL) and constrained RL techniques to improve data efficiency and generalization capabilities.
