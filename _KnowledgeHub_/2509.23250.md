# Training Vision-Language Process Reward Models for Test-Time Scaling in Multimodal Reasoning: Key Insights and Lessons Learned

> [!summary] Summary
> This research details Vision-Language Process Reward Models (VL-PRMs) trained using a hybrid data synthesis framework, integrating strong VLM judgments and structured reasoning traces. The approach leads to consistent performance enhancements for policy VLMs across various multimodal reasoning tasks during test-time scaling, notably revealing latent reasoning capacities in larger models.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Process Reward Models (VL-PRMs) relied heavily on Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for data generation, yielding noisy supervision and limited generalization across diverse tasks.
- VL-PRMs lacked effective mechanisms to detect early-stage perception errors, a crucial bottleneck in multimodal reasoning that can propagate through subsequent steps.
- There was a need for systematic evaluation of test-time scaling strategies for VL-PRMs and broader assessment of their effectiveness beyond math-specific benchmarks.

## Method

- Developed ==VL-PRM300K==, a hybrid data synthesis framework combining MCTS with strong VLM (o4-mini) judgments to create **1.32 million** accurate step-level labels for multimodal reasoning traces.
- Trained QWEN-VL-PRM-3B and QWEN-VL-PRM-7B with ==structured reasoning==, explicitly separating and supervising ==perception steps== to enhance early error detection.
- Systematically investigated three test-time scaling strategies: ==Guided Greedy Search==, One-shot Search, and Step-Score Aggregation, to integrate VL-PRM guidance into policy VLM inference.

## Results

- VL-PRMs consistently boosted policy VLM performance, achieving **7-20%** gains on abstract, algorithmic, and mathematical reasoning tasks (e.g., PuzzleVQA, AlgoPuzzleVQA, MathVista, MathVision).
- QWEN-VL-PRM-3B demonstrated a **16%** gain in macro-F1 score for step-level error detection on VisualProcessBench, even outperforming the larger QWEN-VL-PRM-7B by **5%**.
- The models successfully unlocked latent reasoning capabilities in larger policy VLMs (e.g., Qwen-2.5-VL-32B, Gemma3-27B), yielding disproportionately substantial gains under test-time scaling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - One-shot search, where VL-PRMs evaluate entire solutions (acting as Outcome Reward Models), proved to be the most effective test-time scaling strategy, often outperforming step-by-step guidance.
> - Smaller VL-PRMs, specifically QWEN-VL-PRM-3B, achieved competitive or even superior step-level error detection performance compared to larger models or general VLMs.
> - Explicit perception-focused supervision during VL-PRM training is critical for detecting visual grounding errors, leading to substantial improvements in test-time scaling performance.
