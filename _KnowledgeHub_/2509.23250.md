# Training Vision-Language Process Reward Models for Test-Time Scaling in Multimodal Reasoning: Key Insights and Lessons Learned

> [!summary] Summary
> This research details Vision-Language Process Reward Models (VL-PRMs) trained using a hybrid data synthesis framework, integrating strong VLM judgments and structured reasoning traces. The approach leads to consistent performance enhancements for policy VLMs across various multimodal reasoning tasks during test-time scaling, notably revealing latent reasoning capacities in larger models.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language Process Reward Models (VL-PRMs) relied heavily on Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for data generation, yielding noisy supervision and limited generalization across diverse tasks.
- VL-PRMs lacked effective mechanisms to detect early-stage perception errors, a crucial bottleneck in multimodal reasoning that can propagate through subsequent steps.
- There was a need for systematic evaluation of test-time scaling strategies for VL-PRMs and broader assessment of their effectiveness beyond math-specific benchmarks.

## Method

- Developed ==VL-PRM300K==, a hybrid data synthesis framework combining MCTS with strong VLM (o4-mini) judgments to create **1.32 million** accurate step-level labels for multimodal reasoning traces.
- Trained QWEN-VL-PRM-3B and QWEN-VL-PRM-7B with ==structured reasoning==, explicitly separating and supervising ==perception steps== to enhance early error detection.
- Systematically investigated three test-time scaling strategies: ==Guided Greedy Search==, One-shot Search, and Step-Score Aggregation, to integrate VL-PRM guidance into policy VLM inference.

## Results

- VL-PRMs consistently boosted policy VLM performance, achieving **7-20%** gains on abstract, algorithmic, and mathematical reasoning tasks (e.g., PuzzleVQA, AlgoPuzzleVQA, MathVista, MathVision).
- QWEN-VL-PRM-3B demonstrated a **16%** gain in macro-F1 score for step-level error detection on VisualProcessBench, even outperforming the larger QWEN-VL-PRM-7B by **5%**.
- The models successfully unlocked latent reasoning capabilities in larger policy VLMs (e.g., Qwen-2.5-VL-32B, Gemma3-27B), yielding disproportionately substantial gains under test-time scaling.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - One-shot search, where VL-PRMs evaluate entire solutions (acting as Outcome Reward Models), proved to be the most effective test-time scaling strategy, often outperforming step-by-step guidance.
> - Smaller VL-PRMs, specifically QWEN-VL-PRM-3B, achieved competitive or even superior step-level error detection performance compared to larger models or general VLMs.
> - Explicit perception-focused supervision during VL-PRM training is critical for detecting visual grounding errors, leading to substantial improvements in test-time scaling performance.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This research is situated at the intersection of large language models (LLMs), multimodal AI, and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), specifically focusing on enhancing the reliability and reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The paper builds upon the significant advancements brought by Chain-of-of-Thought (CoT) prompting, which has substantially improved LLM performance by enabling step-by-step reasoning. However, a persistent challenge in LLMs, particularly in complex multi-step reasoning, is their vulnerability to hallucinations and logical inconsistencies.

Process Reward Models (PRMs) emerged as a solution to this, providing step-level supervision rather than just evaluating the final answer. While PRMs have been extensively studied and proven effective in text-based domains, especially for mathematical reasoning, their extension to the multimodal domain (VL-PRMs) remains nascent. Existing VL-PRM efforts, notably by Wang et al. (2025) (VisualPRM), primarily rely on Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) for dataset construction. However, MCTS-based scoring can introduce noisy supervision signals and limit generalization across diverse tasks, often focusing heavily on math-specific benchmarks.

This work directly addresses these limitations and expands the design space of VL-PRMs. It differentiates itself from prior work by:
- **Diversifying data construction:** Moving beyond sole reliance on MCTS by integrating judgments from powerful VLMs (o4-mini) for more accurate step-level labeling.
- **Broadening task coverage:** Evaluating VL-PRMs on a wider array of multimodal reasoning tasks, including abstract and algorithmic reasoning, rather than being confined to math-heavy domains. This contrasts with VisualPRM, which primarily trained and evaluated on advanced math datasets.
- **Introducing perception-focused supervision:** Explicitly addressing a known bottleneck in multimodal reasoning—perception errors—by designing PRMs to detect and penalize these early-stage mistakes.
- **Systematically exploring test-time scaling (TTS) strategies:** Providing a comprehensive comparison of different inference-time guidance mechanisms for VL-PRMs, an area where previous works (e.g., GM-PRM, ThinkPRM) had less comprehensive evaluations.

By tackling these open questions, the paper aims to contribute to a more robust and generalized framework for process supervision in multimodal AI, ultimately making VLMs more reliable and effective across a broader spectrum of complex reasoning tasks. It pushes the frontier by demonstrating how meticulously constructed datasets and targeted supervision can unlock latent reasoning capabilities in powerful VLM backbones.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The overarching objective of this research is to elucidate and expand the design space of Vision-Language Process Reward Models (VL-PRMs) to enhance the reliability and reasoning capabilities of Vision-Language Models (VLMs) during test-time scaling (TTS). The authors are motivated by several key limitations and unanswered questions in the current landscape of PRM research, especially concerning its application to multimodal reasoning:

1. **Improving Dataset Construction Beyond MCTS:** Existing VL-PRMs primarily rely on Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) to generate step-level supervision data. While useful, MCTS-derived scores can be noisy and may not capture nuanced correctness. A core motivation is to investigate whether a hybrid data synthesis framework, combining MCTS with judgments from a strong VLM (e.g., o4-mini), can produce more accurate and reliable step-level labels for training VL-PRMs.

2. **Assessing the Impact of VL-PRM Size:** The efficiency and scalability of PRMs are crucial. The authors aim to determine if smaller VL-PRMs can achieve comparable or even superior performance in detecting process errors compared to larger, more computationally expensive models. This addresses the practical question of whether scaling up PRM size is always necessary for effective supervision.

3. **Evaluating Diverse Test-Time Scaling Strategies:** While PRMs can detect errors, their utility in guiding VLM inference needs to be thoroughly explored. The paper seeks to systematically evaluate multiple test-time scaling strategies (Guided Greedy Search, One-shot Search, Step-Score Aggregation) to understand how VL-PRMs can be most effectively leveraged during inference to guide VLMs toward more accurate solutions.

4. **Enhancing Generalization Across Reasoning Tasks:** Prior VL-PRM research has often focused on math-heavy datasets. The authors are motivated to test the generalizability of VL-PRMs across a wider range of reasoning domains, including expert subject knowledge (MMMU), abstract reasoning (PuzzleVQA), algorithmic reasoning (AlgoPuzzleVQA), and diverse mathematical problem-solving (MathVista, MathVision). This includes investigating whether training on general VQA and abstract reasoning tasks, without advanced math datasets, can still improve performance on mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

5. **Incorporating Structured Reasoning, Especially Perception:** Recognizing that perception is a critical bottleneck in multimodal reasoning, the paper aims to explore whether designing VL-PRMs with explicit perception-focused supervision can improve test-time scaling performance. The motivation here is to enable PRMs to detect and penalize errors at the visual grounding stage, preventing error propagation.

In summary, the motivation stems from a desire to make VL-PRMs more robust, efficient, and broadly applicable by innovating in data generation, training methodologies, and inference strategies, thereby addressing key gaps in the current research landscape of multimodal process supervision.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a comprehensive methodology to address their objectives, spanning data construction, VL-PRM training, and test-time scaling strategies.

#### 3.1. Dataset Construction (VL-PRM300K)

- **Hybrid Data Synthesis Framework:** Unlike previous work that relied solely on MCTS for scoring reasoning steps, this paper introduces a hybrid approach. It combines MCTS-based data generation with direct judgments from a strong external VLM, **o4-mini**, to label the correctness of each reasoning step. For a given image, question, and a partial reasoning prefix, the VLM judge emits a binary label (correct/incorrect) and a rationale. If a step is labeled incorrect, all subsequent steps are discarded.
- **Structured Reasoning Traces:** The dataset, named **VL-PRM300K**, is built with structured traces that explicitly separate "perception" steps from higher-level "reasoning" steps. This design is motivated by the observation that perception errors can significantly impact downstream reasoning.
- **Diverse Data Sources:** VL-PRM300K comprises approximately 300,000 (Image, Question, Solution) pairs and 1.32 million step-level samples. The data is sampled from six diverse VQA datasets:
 - **Document and Chart Understanding:** DVQA
 - **OCR:** InfoVQA
 - **General Commonsense:** VQAv2
 - **Grade-school Science:** AI2D
 - **Grade-school Math Reasoning:** CLEVR-Math (elementary level)
 - **Abstract Reasoning:** RAVEN (constituting the majority, 199K samples).
 Notably, advanced math datasets (unlike those used in VisualPRM) were deliberately excluded from the training data to test generalization.
- **Balanced Variant:** A balanced version of VL-PRM300K was also constructed to investigate the effect of label imbalance, ensuring equal proportions of correct and incorrect samples.
- **PERCEPTIONPROCESSBENCH:** To specifically evaluate perception error detection, a synthetic dataset was created from PuzzleVQA. It includes 1,000 positive (correct) and 1,000 negative (subtly altered, plausible error) perception steps, using GPT-4.1 to generate variations.

#### 3.2. Training VL-PRMs

- **Model Backbones:** Two Vision-Language Process Reward Models, **QWEN-VL-PRM-3B** and **QWEN-VL-PRM-7B**, were fine-tuned from existing **Qwen-2.5-VL-3B** and **Qwen-2.5-VL-7B** Instruct models, respectively.
- **Training Objective:** The VL-PRMs are trained to predict a binary judgment ("+" or "–") for each step, minimizing cross-entropy loss over these label tokens. The input consists of the image, question, and the current reasoning step (including prior context).
- **Vision Encoder Tuning:** Experiments showed that freezing the vision encoder layers while tuning other parameters consistently resulted in better performance.
- **Image Resizing:** Dynamic image resizing, as recommended by Qwen-2.5-VL, was applied during training for optimized performance.

#### 3.3. Test-Time Scaling (TTS) Strategies

The authors investigate three distinct strategies for integrating VL-PRMs into the inference process of policy VLMs (Qwen-2.5-VL and Gemma3 families):

1. **Guided Greedy Search:** At each decoding step, the policy VLM generates *N* candidate next steps. The VL-PRM scores each candidate, and the highest-scoring step is chosen to continue generation.
2. **One-shot Search (Outcome Reward Model-like):** The policy VLM generates *N* complete candidate solutions. The VL-PRM then scores each full solution in a single pass (P_theta(+ | I, q, S^(j))) and selects the highest-scoring one. This resembles the behavior of an Outcome Reward Model (ORM).
3. **Step-Score Aggregation:** The policy VLM generates *N* complete solutions, each with multiple steps. The VL-PRM assigns a probability of correctness to each individual step within a solution. These step-level probabilities are then aggregated (e.g., by averaging) to compute an overall score for the entire solution, and the solution with the highest aggregated score is selected.

The policy models used for TTS were instructed to perform structured reasoning, first describing the image (perception) and then engaging in inductive and deductive reasoning.

#### 3.4. Evaluation

- **Policy Models:** Qwen-2.5-VL-3B, Qwen-2.5-VL-7B, Qwen-2.5-VL-32B, Gemma3-12B, Gemma3-27B, and MiniCPM-V-2 6.
- **Evaluation Datasets:**
 - **Expert Knowledge:** MMMU
 - **Abstract Reasoning:** PuzzleVQA
 - **Algorithmic Reasoning:** AlgoPuzzleVQA
 - **Mathematical Problem-Solving:** MathVista, MathVision
 - **Step-level Correctness:** VisualProcessBench (macro-F1 score)
 - **Perception Error Detection:** PERCEPTIONPROCESSBENCH (F1 score)

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments yielded several key insights into the design and effectiveness of VL-PRMs for multimodal reasoning:

#### 4.1. VL-PRM Improves Performance Under Test-Time Scaling

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** Both QWEN-VL-PRM-3B and QWEN-VL-PRM-7B consistently enhanced the performance of all evaluated policy VLMs across diverse benchmarks.
- **Task-Specific Impact:** Significant improvements (7-20% gains) were observed on datasets requiring abstract, algorithmic, or mathematical reasoning (PuzzleVQA, AlgoPuzzleVQA, MathVista, MathVision). Gains on expert-knowledge–oriented MMMU were more modest (~2.5%).
- **Generalization to Math:** Despite VL-PRM300K containing only elementary math and predominantly abstract reasoning data, the models showed moderate improvements (3-5%) on advanced math datasets (MathVista, MathVision). This suggests that training on general VQA and abstract reasoning tasks strengthens logical error detection, benefiting mathematical reasoning.
- **Uncovering Latent Reasoning Abilities:** Larger policy models (Qwen-2.5-VL-32B, Gemma3-12B, Gemma3-27B) exhibited disproportionately substantial gains under TTS, even when their baseline performance was comparable to smaller models. This indicates that VL-PRMs can unlock hidden reasoning capacities in powerful VLMs that are not apparent under standard greedy decoding.

#### 4.2. Performance on VisualProcessBench (Step-Level Error Detection)

- **Effectiveness of PRM Training:** VL-PRMs significantly outperformed their base VLM counterparts (Qwen-2.5-VL-3B/7B) in detecting step-level correctness, as measured by macro-F1 score.
- **Smaller PRMs Can Be Highly Effective:** QWEN-VL-PRM-3B achieved a substantial 16% gain in macro-F1 score and performed comparably to GPT-4o, even outperforming the larger QWEN-VL-PRM-7B by 5%. This is a crucial finding, suggesting that smaller PRMs can be highly competitive for error detection.

#### 4.3. Results on PERCEPTIONPROCESSBENCH (Perception Error Detection)

- **Importance of Perception-Focused Supervision:** Training VL-PRMs with explicit perception steps significantly improved their ability to detect perception errors (14-20% gain over baselines). Without this supervision, VL-PRMs largely failed to identify perception errors, often classifying them as correct. This highlights the critical role of structured reasoning and targeted supervision for early-stage error detection.

#### 4.4. Analyses and Ablations

- **Test-Time Scaling Strategies:**
 - **One-shot Search (acting as ORM) was the most effective strategy**, consistently outperforming Step-Score Aggregation and Guided Greedy Search. It showed marked improvements (4-9% over greedy for PuzzleVQA/AlgoPuzzleVQA).
 - **Guided Greedy Search performed the worst**, sometimes even reducing performance below the baseline, likely due to error propagation from local optimal step selection.
 - The finding that VL-PRMs used as Outcome Reward Models (ORMs) for full solution evaluation (One-shot Search) outperformed process-step selection is a key insight, contrasting with some prior work (e.g., VisualPRM) where explicit ORMs often underperformed their PRMs.
- **Role of Judge Labels:** Using o4-mini as a judge for step correctness in VL-PRM300K construction significantly improved VL-PRM performance compared to relying solely on MCTS-derived MC-scores. The agreement between the two labeling schemes for incorrect samples was found to be only ~20%, underscoring the divergence and the value of VLM-based judgments.
- **Impact of Dataset Size/Balance:** Training on a balanced subset of VL-PRM300K showed inconsistent (marginal or slight decrease) improvements compared to the full, imbalanced dataset, suggesting the benefit of a larger, more natural distribution.
- **Perception Guidance Enhances VL-PRMs:** Ablation studies confirmed that excluding perception guidance during training led to consistent drops in performance, particularly for smaller policy models.
- **VL-PRMs vs. VLMs as Judges:** VL-PRMs acted as more effective critics for TTS than using general VLMs (e.g., Qwen-2.5-VL) as judges. Attempting greedy search TTS with VLMs as judges actually degraded performance below baseline.
- **Majority Voting:** PRM-based TTS generally outperformed majority voting, especially for smaller policy models; performance became comparable for larger models (e.g., Gemma 27B).
- **Limited TTS Benefits for MiniCPM-V:** Some models, like MiniCPM-V-2 6, showed limited TTS benefits (averaging 2% improvement), highlighting challenges in generalizing TTS effectiveness across all VLM architectures.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research marks a significant step forward in making multimodal reasoning more reliable and interpretable by rigorously exploring the design space of Vision-Language Process Reward Models (VL-PRMs). Its potential impact is multi-faceted:

1. **Enhanced VLM Reliability and Trustworthiness:** By providing step-level supervision and test-time guidance, VL-PRMs can significantly reduce hallucinations and logical inconsistencies in VLMs. This is crucial for deploying VLMs in high-stakes applications where accuracy and explainability are paramount, such as medical diagnosis, scientific discovery, or complex engineering tasks.

2. **Unlocking Latent VLM Capabilities:** The discovery that VL-PRMs can uncover and leverage hidden reasoning abilities in larger VLM backbones is profound. It suggests that state-of-the-art VLMs possess more sophisticated reasoning potential than observed through standard greedy decoding, and PRMs act as a key to unlocking this potential, leading to more intelligent and robust AI systems.

3. **Improved Data Efficiency and Quality for PRM Training:** The introduction of a hybrid data synthesis framework, combining MCTS with strong VLM judges, offers a more effective and accurate method for generating process supervision data. This approach can lead to higher quality PRMs with less reliance on potentially noisy MCTS-only scores, making data collection more efficient and targeted. The public release of VL-PRM300K further aids the community in this regard.

4. **Practical Guidance for Test-Time Scaling:** The comprehensive evaluation of various test-time scaling strategies provides clear guidance for practitioners and researchers. The finding that one-shot scoring (acting as an Outcome Reward Model) is often superior to step-by-step guidance is a critical insight for designing efficient and effective inference mechanisms, potentially simplifying deployment. Moreover, demonstrating that smaller VL-PRMs can achieve high performance in error detection opens avenues for developing lightweight, yet powerful, reward models.

5. **Addressing Perception as a Core Bottleneck:** The emphasis on perception-focused supervision directly tackles a known weakness in multimodal reasoning. By enabling VL-PRMs to explicitly detect visual grounding errors, the research contributes to building VLMs that are not only logically sound but also visually accurate, ensuring that reasoning is built upon a correct understanding of the visual input.

6. **Broader Generalization of Multimodal Reasoning:** Demonstrating that VL-PRMs trained on general VQA and abstract reasoning tasks can effectively generalize to complex mathematical problems is a testament to their robust logical reasoning capabilities. This suggests that the developed techniques foster more generalized reasoning abilities in VLMs, rather than just task-specific improvements.

In conclusion, this work provides critical insights into how to train and deploy more effective VL-PRMs, offering a pathway toward more reliable, transparent, and powerful multimodal AI systems. The authors' commitment to open science, by releasing models and datasets, ensures that these contributions will serve as a foundation for future research and innovation in the field.
