# ClutterDexGrasp: A Sim-to-Real System for General Dexterous Grasping in Cluttered Scenes

> [!summary] Summary
> Peking University and Princeton researchers present ClutterDexGrasp, a system that achieves zero-shot sim-to-real transfer for dexterous target-oriented grasping in highly cluttered environments. It demonstrates an 83.9% real-world success rate on unseen objects and layouts, utilizing human-like strategies like gentle clutter clearance.

## Problem

- Existing robotic grasping methods often fail in cluttered environments due to reliance on single-object focus or open-loop planning.
- Closed-loop reinforcement learning for dexterous hands struggles with complex interactions, occlusions, and high-dimensional state/action spaces in cluttered scenes.
- Collecting sufficient real-world demonstration data for imitation learning in diverse, cluttered scenarios is prohibitively expensive and time-consuming.
- Bridging the "sim-to-real gap" for highly interactive, dexterous manipulation tasks in clutter remains a challenge, affecting transferability from simulation to physical robots.

## Method

- A two-stage teacher-student framework, trained entirely in simulation, uses a teacher policy to learn robust grasping with privileged information and then distills this knowledge into a student policy operating on partial point clouds.
- A novel Geometry and Spatial (GS) representation efficiently encodes object local geometry and spatial relationships with the hand, enabling collision-aware grasping.
- Multi-stage curriculum learning, including clutter-density and interaction safety curricula, guides the teacher policy to learn general, strategic, and safe behaviors, such as gentle clutter clearance.
- A 3D ==Diffusion Policy== (DP3) serves as the student's backbone, leveraging synthetic robot point cloud augmentation and system identification for effective zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

## Results

- The system achieved an **83.9%** overall success rate on unseen objects and layouts during real-world zero-shot transfer, consistently reproducing human-like clutter clearance and grasp strategies.
- Simulation experiments showed robust generalization, with the policy maintaining high success rates (e.g., **81.6%** for unseen objects in ultra-dense clutter) even in significantly more cluttered scenes not encountered during training.
- The interaction safety curriculum reduced average maximum contact forces by **46.3%** while preserving high grasping performance, leading to safer and gentler robot behaviors.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Complex dexterous grasping in dense clutter, including obstacle manipulation, can be effectively learned through a structured curriculum in simulation without explicit programming of interaction strategies.
> - A compact, hand-centric geometry and spatial representation, combined with a reward function penalizing non-target proximity, is crucial for learning collision-aware and safe grasping in cluttered scenes.
> - Zero-shot transfer of high-dimensional dexterous manipulation policies from simulation to the real world is achievable for cluttered environments by meticulously addressing both perception and dynamics gaps through techniques like point cloud augmentation and system identification.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of robotic manipulation, particularly dexterous grasping, has seen substantial progress. However, deploying robots with human-like dexterity in unstructured, cluttered environments remains a significant challenge. This paper positions itself at the forefront of addressing this critical gap.

**Existing Limitations:**
- **Single-Object Focus:** Many successful methods for robotic grasping are designed for single objects or controlled environments, where object geometries and poses are relatively predictable. These methods often involve generating grasp poses offline and then executing them, lacking the adaptability required for dynamic and cluttered scenes.
- **Open-Loop vs. Closed-Loop:** Open-loop methods, which pre-plan grasps, are inherently brittle to unexpected changes or interactions in cluttered scenes. While closed-loop approaches, often leveraging Reinforcement Learning (RL), have achieved high success rates for single-object tabletop grasping, they struggle with the complexity of cluttered scenes due to intricate interactions, occlusions, and the difficulty of compact state representation.
- **Data Scarcity for Real-World Learning:** Imitation Learning (IL) methods, while powerful, demand extensive and costly human-collected real-world demonstrations to generalize across diverse cluttered scenarios. Vision-language-action models, a recent promising direction, also face similar data requirements.
- **Sim-to-Real Gap:** Training policies purely in simulation offers scalability but faces the formidable "sim-to-real gap," where policies trained in virtual environments fail to transfer effectively to physical robots due due to discrepancies in dynamics, sensing, and physical parameters. Previous sim-to-real RL work for dexterous manipulation often focuses on simpler, single-object tasks.

**ClutterDexGrasp's Contribution to the Landscape:**
This work directly tackles these limitations by proposing a *zero-shot sim-to-real closed-loop system for target-oriented dexterous grasping in cluttered scenes*. It explicitly aims to achieve generalization to *diverse objects and layouts* without requiring any real-world demonstrations. By combining a sophisticated sim-to-real pipeline with a robust learning framework, ClutterDexGrasp attempts to bridge the gap between simulation scalability and real-world applicability for complex manipulation tasks. The paper positions itself as the *first* to achieve this specific combination of capabilities, particularly emphasizing dexterous grasping in *dense clutter* with *zero-shot transfer*. This represents a significant step towards truly autonomous and adaptable robotic manipulation in human-centric environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of ClutterDexGrasp is to develop a generalized, closed-loop policy for dexterous target-oriented grasping in highly cluttered environments, capable of zero-shot transfer from simulation to the real world. This overarching goal is broken down into several specific aims and motivations:

**Key Objectives:**
1. **Generalizable Closed-Loop Grasping:** To enable a dexterous hand to robustly grasp a designated target object amidst varying levels of clutter, handling diverse object geometries and complex interactions.
2. **Simulation-Only Training:** To train the entire grasping policy exclusively in a simulation environment, thereby avoiding the prohibitive cost and scalability issues associated with collecting extensive real-world demonstration data.
3. **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** To ensure that the policy trained in simulation can be directly deployed on a physical robot without any further fine-tuning or adaptation in the real world.
4. **Addressing RL Instability:** To overcome the inherent challenges of training Reinforcement Learning policies in high-dimensional observation and action spaces, particularly when dealing with the high degrees of freedom (DoF) of dexterous hands and the variability of cluttered scenes.
5. **Bridging the Reality Gap:** To specifically address the sim-to-real gap concerning observation space discrepancies (e.g., partial point clouds in reality vs. privileged information in sim), policy safety during physical interaction, and the differences in physical dynamics between simulation and reality.

**Motivation:**
The motivation for ClutterDexGrasp stems from the recognized importance of human-level dexterous manipulation for a wide range of real-world applications (e.g., logistics, manufacturing, service robotics) and the current limitations of robotic systems in achieving this. Humans effortlessly navigate and manipulate objects in cluttered environments, a capability that robots currently lack. Existing solutions are insufficient for complex cluttered scenes because they either:
- Are open-loop and not reactive to dynamic changes.
- Are closed-loop but limited to simpler, single-object scenarios.
- Require vast amounts of expensive real-world data.
- Struggle with the complexities of sim-to-real transfer for highly interactive tasks.

By developing a sim-to-real system for general dexterous grasping in clutter, the authors aim to advance the state-of-the-art in robotics, making autonomous manipulation more robust, versatile, and practically deployable, ultimately reducing the dependency on real-world data collection and engineering complex behaviors manually.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ClutterDexGrasp employs a sophisticated two-stage teacher-student framework, entirely trained in simulation, designed to achieve general, dynamic, and safe dexterous grasping in cluttered scenes with zero-shot real-world transfer.

**Overall Framework:**
The system comprises:
1. **Teacher Policy Learning:** A teacher policy is trained in a physics simulation using privileged state information and advanced curriculum learning strategies to master dexterous grasping in cluttered environments.
2. **Student Policy Distillation:** The knowledge from the successful teacher policy is distilled into a student policy that operates on realistic, partial point cloud observations, enabling real-world deployment.

#### 3.1. Teacher Policy Learning in Simulation

The teacher policy is trained using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO), leveraging a three-stage curriculum and a novel scene representation.
- **Geometry and Spatial (GS) Representation:**
 - **Cluttered Scene Representation:** For each finger link of the dexterous hand, the system computes 3D distance vectors to the nearest sampled points on both the target object (d_pos) and non-target objects (d_neg). This compact representation efficiently encodes both the local geometry of objects and their spatial relationships with the hand and surrounding clutter. It allows the teacher to learn collision-aware grasping without relying on computationally expensive point cloud rendering.
 - **Dense Reward Function:** The reward function (Eq. 3) is designed to encourage desired behaviors: `r = (c1 * r_grasp + c2 * r_pos) * (1 - r_neg)`. `r_grasp` provides a base grasping reward, `r_pos` encourages proximity to the target, and `r_neg` imposes a penalty for risky closeness to non-target objects, promoting collision avoidance.
- **Two-Stage Clutter-Density Curriculum:** To address the difficulty of learning complex grasping strategies from scratch in cluttered scenes, the learning process is decomposed:
 1. **Stage 1: General Grasping:** The policy first learns robust, generalizable grasping skills in single-object scenarios. This establishes fundamental manipulation capabilities.
 2. **Stage 2: Strategic Grasping:** The pre-trained policy from Stage 1 is then fine-tuned in cluttered environments. This stage focuses on learning contact-rich, strategic behaviors necessary for clutter, such as gently nudging obstacle objects out of the way before grasping the target.
- **Interaction Safety Curriculum (Stage 3):** To ensure safe and robust real-world deployment, especially during contact-rich interactions:
 1. The policy is fine-tuned by progressively tightening a *force threshold* (`f`). When the policy's success rate (`w`) surpasses a predefined threshold (`w_bar`), `f` is reduced.
 2. Exceeding this force threshold incurs a sparse penalty (`r_force`) in the reward function (Eq. 4) and leads to early episode termination.
 3. Collisions between the hand and the table are handled by terminating episodes if fingertips penetrate the table surface too deeply, preventing dangerous behaviors during contact.
- **Coarse-to-Fine Data-Efficient Learning:** To manage the high DoFs of dexterous hands, the grasping process is modeled as a coarse-to-fine trajectory. Hand DoFs are initially frozen during approach, and the full action space is enabled only when the hand is sufficiently close to the object, improving learning efficiency.

#### 3.2. Student Point-Cloud Policy Distillation

The student policy is designed for real-world deployment, operating on realistic sensor data (partial point clouds).
- **Multi-Level Clutter Density Demonstration:** The teacher policy generates a diverse dataset of successful demonstrations across *varying* clutter densities (sparse to dense). This dataset is crucial for the student to learn adaptive behaviors, such as direct grasping in sparse scenes versus obstacle clearance in dense scenes.
- **3D Diffusion Policy (DP3):** The student policy uses DP3 as its backbone, which is well-suited for processing point cloud observations and modeling complex, high-dimensional action distributions in dexterous manipulation. It's trained using offline imitation learning on the collected teacher demonstrations.
- **Point Cloud Alignment:** To bridge the sim-to-real perception gap and handle occlusions, the observed point cloud is augmented with *synthetic, dense robot point clouds* (of both the arm and hand). This augmentation provides critical spatial relationships for accurate grasping and collision avoidance, aligning the visual input between simulation and reality.
- **System Identification (SI):** To minimize the dynamics gap, system identification is performed to calibrate the physical parameters of the robotic arm and hand. This involves iteratively comparing and optimizing simulated trajectories against real-world counterparts under identical commands until behaviors closely match.
- **Robot Setup:** The real-world system consists of a RealMan RM75-6F 7-DoF robotic arm equipped with an AgiBot 6-DoF dexterous hand. The student policy controls both the arm's relative joint changes (7D) and the hand's absolute actuated joint positions (6D).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

ClutterDexGrasp demonstrates strong performance in both simulation and the real world, achieving its objectives of robust, generalizable, and safe dexterous grasping in cluttered scenes with zero-shot sim-to-real transfer.

#### 4.1. Simulation Experiments

- **Generalization Across Diverse Clutter Density and Novel Scenes:**
 - The teacher policy achieved a high success rate of **91.9%** on seen objects and layouts. Crucially, it generalized effectively to seen objects with **unseen layouts** and even **unseen objects with unseen layouts**, showing comparable performance across sparse, dense, and ultra-dense clutter levels (e.g., 81.6% for unseen objects in ultra-dense clutter).
 - The student policy, despite operating on partial point cloud observations, showed a remarkably small average success rate drop of less than 5% compared to the teacher across all test conditions, validating the effectiveness of the distillation framework.
 - Both policies successfully generalized to much harder **ultra-dense clutter scenes** (16-25 objects) that were not encountered during training, highlighting their robustness.
- **Human-like Clutter Scene Grasping Strategy:** The policies learned emergent, human-like strategies without explicit programming:
 - **Clutter Clearance via Gentle Interaction:** When a target object was deeply buried, the robot gently nudged overlying objects instead of forceful pushing, applying low contact forces.
 - **Clutter-aware Grasp:** Once obstacles were partially cleared, the hand approached the target from the side, demonstrating an adaptive, collision-minimized strategy.
- **Effectiveness of Density Curriculum:** Directly training a policy in cluttered scenes without the two-stage clutter-density curriculum resulted in **0% success**, indicating complete failure to learn. In contrast, initializing from the general grasp policy (Stage 1) and fine-tuning in clutter (Stage 2) achieved an **87.0% success rate**, proving the curriculum's critical role.
- **Effectiveness of Safety Curriculum:** The safety curriculum significantly reduced the average maximum contact forces (from 80.6 to 43.2 units), indicating safer, gentler interactions. This was achieved with only a minor performance drop (from 88.9% to 87.0% success rate), demonstrating its ability to temper aggressive behaviors while preserving effective grasping. Ablation studies showed that removing the geometry-spatial representation or the negative distance penalty led to unsafe, high-force interactions and direct, collision-prone grasps.

#### 4.2. Real-World Experiments

- **Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Transfer:** The system achieved an impressive **83.9% overall success rate** on unseen layouts with unseen objects over 167 grasping attempts, without any real-world training or fine-tuning. This performance is comparable to simulation results, confirming the efficacy of the transfer pipeline. No cluttered scene was prematurely terminated due to consecutive failures.
- **Robust Generalization:** The policy generalized robustly to a wide range of object geometries, sizes, and materials, including challenging irregular shapes.
- **Efficiency:** Most successful grasps were completed within 30 seconds, even in dense clutter, and the cumulative success curve reached 80% within 40 seconds, underscoring the system's efficiency.
- **Reproduced Strategies:** The student policy in the real world reproduced the human-like, contact-aware strategies observed in simulation.
- **Safety:** Despite the complexity, no safety-related human intervention was required during real-world experiments, thanks to the robust safety curriculum and practical deployment adjustments (e.g., torque-based success detection, end-effector force threshold).

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ClutterDexGrasp represents a substantial advancement in the field of robotic manipulation, particularly for dexterous grasping in complex, unstructured environments. Its significance and potential impact can be summarized as follows:

- **Pioneering Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real for Cluttered Dexterous Grasping:** The most significant contribution is the development of the *first zero-shot sim-to-real closed-loop system for target-oriented dexterous grasping in cluttered scenes*. This addresses a long-standing challenge by demonstrating that complex, contact-rich manipulation behaviors in highly variable environments can be learned entirely in simulation and directly transferred to the real world without any real-world data collection or fine-tuning.
- **Breakthrough in Generalization and Robustness:** The system's ability to generalize robustly across diverse objects (seen and unseen), unseen layouts, and even to significantly higher clutter densities than encountered during training, showcases a new level of adaptability for dexterous robotic systems. This moves beyond narrowly defined tasks to more versatile capabilities.
- **Emergent Human-like Intelligence:** The automatic emergence of sophisticated, human-like strategies – such as gentle clutter clearance and clutter-aware grasping – without explicit programming, heuristics, or mode switching, highlights the power of the proposed learning framework. This suggests a path toward more intelligent and intuitive robotic behaviors.
- **Scalability and Cost Reduction:** By eliminating the need for real-world demonstrations, ClutterDexGrasp drastically reduces the cost, time, and logistical challenges associated with data collection for complex manipulation tasks. This makes it a highly scalable approach for developing and deploying advanced robotic skills.
- **Novel Methodology Integration:** The work successfully integrates several advanced techniques:
 - A novel **geometry- and spatially-embedded scene representation** that is compact yet informative for collision-aware dexterous grasping in clutter.
 - An effective **clutter-density curriculum learning** strategy that enables stable RL training for complex behaviors.
 - A crucial **interaction-aware safety curriculum** that promotes safe, deployable behaviors by minimizing excessive forces and collisions.
 - Leveraging **3D Diffusion Policies (DP3)** for robust imitation learning from privileged teacher data to partial point cloud observations.
 - Meticulous **sim-to-real transfer techniques** including point cloud augmentation and system identification.
- **Enabling Future Robotic Applications:** The capabilities demonstrated by ClutterDexGrasp are fundamental to unlocking numerous real-world applications where dexterous manipulation in cluttered environments is essential. These include automated warehouses, manufacturing assembly, domestic assistance for the elderly or disabled, and even hazardous material handling.
- **Guidance for Future Research:** While highly impactful, the paper also acknowledges limitations, such as challenges with tiny objects due to perception gaps and occlusions. This opens avenues for future research into enhanced perception strategies (e.g., multi-view fusion, active vision) to further improve robustness in extreme scenarios.

In conclusion, ClutterDexGrasp marks a significant milestone in robotic manipulation, bridging the long-standing gap between scalable simulation training and robust real-world deployment for complex dexterous grasping tasks in cluttered scenes. Its innovative methodology and demonstrated success pave the way for a new generation of adaptable and intelligent robotic systems.
