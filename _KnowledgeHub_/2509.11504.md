# FR-Net: Learning Robust Quadrupedal Fall Recovery on Challenging Terrains through Mass-Contact Prediction

> [!summary] Summary
> The University of Hong Kong developed FR-Net, a reinforcement learning framework enabling quadrupedal robots to recover from falls on challenging terrains by predicting mass distribution and contact states from proprioception. It achieved higher success rates in simulation on complex terrains (e.g., 69.5% on rough terrains at maximum difficulty) and demonstrated robust, controlled recovery on a Go2 robot across diverse real-world obstacles like steep stairs and suspended beams.

## Problem

- Legged robots struggle with fall recovery on complex, unpredictable terrains (e.g., steep slopes, gaps) due to compromised balance and stability.
- Existing model-based methods are limited by simplified dynamics, while many learning-based approaches fail on highly irregular terrains due to incomplete perception or reliance on vulnerable external sensors.
- Autonomous, robust, and generalizable fall recovery is critical for robot operational efficiency, safety, and sustained task completion in hazardous environments.

## Method

- **FR-Net**, a learning-based framework, integrates a Mass-Contact Predictor (MCP) Network with an asymmetric actor-critic ==reinforcement learning== paradigm.
- The MCP infers robot mass distribution and body component collision probabilities from a history of proprioceptive observations, providing internal state awareness without external sensors during deployment.
- A meticulously designed reward function, including a stability control reward, encourages safe, upright recovery motions and prevents dangerous rolling, especially on steep inclines.

## Results

- FR-Net achieved superior success rates in simulation across challenging terrains, outperforming baselines like Vanilla PPO by **21.8%** on rough terrains and **25.3%** on beams at maximum difficulty levels.
- The framework demonstrated robust sim-to-real transfer, enabling a Unitree Go2 robot to perform controlled, safe recoveries on real-world steep stairs (**38.5**° incline), challenging gaps, and suspended beams.
- The Mass-Contact Predictor (MCP) network showed improved accuracy in mass distribution estimation during dynamic robot motion and effectively predicted contact probabilities of various body segments without external sensing.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Inferring critical internal state information (mass distribution, contact points) from limited proprioceptive data significantly enhances fall recovery capabilities on complex terrains without relying on vulnerable external sensors.
> - Precisely designed reward components, such as penalizing horizontal forces at knee contacts, are crucial for achieving safe and controlled recovery behaviors, particularly on steep or irregular surfaces, by preventing dangerous tumbling.
> - An asymmetric actor-critic architecture combined with a learned predictor for privileged information enables robust policy learning that generalizes effectively across diverse environments and fall scenarios.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The locomotion of legged robots in complex and unpredictable terrains, such as steep slopes, cantilever beams, and gaps, presents significant challenges. These environments often lead to falls due to intricate and uneven surfaces that compromise balance and stability. Consequently, the capacity for autonomous fall recovery is critical for enhancing operational efficiency, reducing downtime, preventing robot damage, and enabling sustained task completion in mission-critical applications like search and rescue or hazardous environment exploration.

Current methodologies for autonomous fall recovery can be broadly classified into model-based and learning-based approaches. Model-based methods typically rely on simplified dynamic models and predefined contact points, which can limit their effectiveness in scenarios involving non-smooth dynamics or uncertain contact interactions prevalent in complex terrains. Learning-based methods, particularly those leveraging Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL), have demonstrated progress in recovery tasks on flat or moderately uneven surfaces. However, these approaches often struggle with highly irregular and unpredictable terrains, where accurately perceiving and interpreting complex environmental features is challenging. While multi-sensor systems (e.g., cameras, LiDAR) can improve environmental awareness, they introduce additional hardware that may be vulnerable during falls. Alternative methods using proprioceptive information and interaction forces for terrain estimation also face limitations in accurately interpreting complex geometries.

This research addresses a notable gap in the development of robust fall recovery strategies for highly challenging terrains. It aims to develop a framework that can adapt to a wide array of environmental conditions and diverse fall scenarios, from simple tumbles to complex multi-contact situations, without requiring explicit terrain data during deployment. By focusing on a learning-based framework that integrates internal state prediction with proprioceptive feedback, the work seeks to overcome the limitations of relying on external sensors or oversimplified models, thereby contributing to more flexible and resilient solutions for autonomous fall recovery in complex environments.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a learning-based framework, named FR-Net, that enables quadrupedal robots to robustly recover from arbitrary fall poses across diverse and challenging environments. This overarching goal is driven by several key motivations:

1. **Addressing Limitations of Current Fall Recovery Methods:** Existing model-based methods are often ineffective on complex terrains due to their reliance on simplified models and predefined contact points, which fail to capture non-smooth dynamics and uncertain interactions during falls. Similarly, many learning-based approaches, while effective on flat or moderately uneven terrains, struggle to generate robust recovery strategies for highly irregular and unpredictable environments, often due to incomplete terrain perception or over-reliance on external sensing that can be compromised during a fall.
2. **Enhancing Robot Autonomy and Safety:** The ability for a robot to autonomously recover from a fall is of significant practical importance. It directly contributes to the robot's operational efficiency by minimizing downtime and prevents further damage by mitigating uncontrolled slips or tumbles. In critical applications like search and rescue or exploration in hazardous zones, autonomous recovery capability is crucial for sustained mission execution without human intervention, thereby conserving resources and ensuring mission success.
3. **Facilitating Generalizable Recovery Strategies:** Developing recovery strategies that generalize across different robot morphologies and a wide range of challenging terrains (e.g., steep stairs, cantilever beams, gaps) is a major motivation. Such generalization is crucial for deploying robots in unknown or varied real-world conditions.
4. **Leveraging Internal State Information for Robustness:** The paper identifies that explicit terrain perception and understanding of the robot's internal state (like mass distribution and contact points) are vital for effective recovery, especially on complex terrains. The motivation is to infer this critical information from limited, robust sensory inputs (proprioception) rather than relying on external sensors that might be vulnerable during a fall or provide incomplete information.

To achieve these objectives, the research outlines three specific contributions:

- **Development of a novel Mass-Contact Predictor (MCP) network architecture:** This network aims to effectively estimate the robot's mass distribution and contact states from limited sensory inputs (proprioception history), thereby enabling the generation of robust recovery strategies adaptable to diverse terrains without explicit terrain data during deployment.
- **Design of a carefully crafted reward function:** This reward function is engineered to ensure safe recovery motions, particularly on steep terrains, preventing dangerous rolling behaviors that are common shortcomings of existing methods. The focus is on promoting stable posture, controlled contact management, and robust stability.
- **Comprehensive validation of generalization capabilities:** The framework is intended to be validated through cross-platform testing in simulation (Go2, Spot, Lite3, etc.) and extensive real-world experiments on the Go2 robot across numerous challenging scenarios to demonstrate its superior and generalizable recovery performance.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The FR-Net framework is a learning-based system designed to enable quadrupedal robots to perform fall recovery in complex scenarios. It integrates three sub-networks: a Mass-Contact Predictor (MCP) Network, an Actor Network, and a Critic Network, all operating within an asymmetric actor-critic reinforcement learning paradigm.

#### 3.1. Asymmetric Actor-Critic Network

The fall recovery problem is formulated as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) because certain privileged information (e.g., center of mass relative to support polygon on slopes, accurate terrain perception for foot placement on gaps) is crucial but not directly observable by the robot's policy during deployment. The asymmetric actor-critic structure facilitates learning by allowing the critic to utilize privileged information during training, while the actor relies on an augmented, deployable observation space.

- **Actor Network:** The actor network, $\pi_{\theta}(a_t|p_t)$, takes an augmented state $p_t$ as input and outputs control actions $a_t$. The augmented state $p_t \in \mathbb{R}^{75}$ concatenates the proprioceptive observation $o_t \in \mathbb{R}^{42}$ with outputs from the Mass-Contact Predictor Network: $\hat{m}_t$ (predicted mass distribution across base, hip, thigh, calf, $\in \mathbb{R}^4$), $\hat{c}_t$ (predicted collision probabilities of body components, $\in \mathbb{R}^{13}$), and $\hat{z}_t$ (a latent representation, $\in \mathbb{R}^{16}$). The proprioceptive observation $o_t$ includes body angular velocity, projected gravity vector, joint angles, joint velocities, and the previous action. Actions $a_t \in \mathbb{R}^{12}$ represent desired increments to initial joint angles, which are tracked by a joint-level PD controller.
- **Critic Network:** The critic network, $V_{\psi}(s_t)$, estimates the state-value function using a privileged observation $s_t$. This privileged observation $s_t$ includes the proprioceptive observation $o_t$, a height map scan $h_t \in \mathbb{R}^{187}$, ground truth masses $m_t \in \mathbb{R}^4$, PD control gains $k_{PD} \in \mathbb{R}^{24}$, center of body mass $p_{com} \in \mathbb{R}^2$, ground truth contact states $c_t \in \mathbb{R}^{13}$, contact forces of each foot $c_f \in \mathbb{R}^4$, and the friction coefficient $\mu \in \mathbb{R}^1$. The critic's access to this complete state information during training aids in learning an accurate value function, which in turn guides the actor's policy learning.

#### 3.2. Mass-Contact Predictor (MCP) Network

The MCP network infers critical environmental and robot state information from proprioceptive data. It aims to estimate the robot's mass distribution and the contact probabilities of its body components, inspired by the idea of inferring terrain from contact interactions and real-time mass distribution estimation enhancing adaptability.

- **Predictor Network Architecture:** The MCP comprises an Encoder $E_{\phi}$ and a Decoder $D_{\theta}$. The Encoder processes a history of $H=5$ consecutive proprioceptive observation frames, $o_H^t$, to output the predicted mass distribution $\hat{m}_t$, predicted collision probabilities $\hat{c}_t$, and a latent vector $\hat{z}_t$. The Decoder then uses $\hat{m}_t$, $\hat{c}_t$, and $\hat{z}_t$ to predict the subsequent proprioceptive observation $\hat{o}_{t+1}$.
- **Loss Function:** The MCP is trained with a total loss $L_{\varphi}$ composed of an Estimation Loss and a Variational Autoencoder (VAE) Loss:
 - **Estimation Loss ($L_{est}^{\varphi}$):** This term includes a Mean Squared Error (MSE) for mass estimation ($MSE(\hat{m}_t, m)$) against ground truth masses $m$, and a Binary Cross-Entropy (BCE) loss for collision estimation against ground truth contact information ($c_t \log \hat{c}_t + (1-c_t) \log(1-\hat{c}_t)$). Hyperparameters $\lambda_m$ and $\lambda_c$ balance these terms.
 - **VAE Loss ($L_{VAE}^{\varphi}$):** This term consists of a Reconstruction Loss ($MSE(\hat{o}_{t+1}, o_{t+1})$) for the accuracy of predicted observations, and a Kullback-Leibler (KL) Divergence Loss ($D_{KL}(q(z_t|o_H^t) \| p(z_t))$) to regularize the latent space distribution towards a standard normal distribution. Hyperparameters $\lambda_{rec}$ and $\lambda_{KL}$ balance these terms.

#### 3.3. Reward Function Design

The reward functions are meticulously designed to encourage safe and effective recovery behaviors on extremely complex terrains. They are categorized as follows:

- **Orientation Posture:** Encourages stable posture and upright recovery. It includes:
 - A penalty for excessive base tilt ($g_{xy}^2$).
 - A Gaussian-based reward for upright orientation, maximizing when the robot is upright and penalizing when the ventral surface faces upward.
 - A postural convergence term, active near upright orientation, encouraging the robot to assume a predefined standing posture.
- **Contact Management:** Promotes appropriate contact with the environment:
 - A discrete reward for stable foot contact (summing binary indicators for all four feet).
 - A penalty for undesired body contact (excluding feet) to prevent collisions and unstable configurations.
- **Stability Reward:** Ensures safe and controlled recovery, particularly on challenging surfaces:
 - **Safety force regulation:** Penalizes horizontal forces at knee contacts ($\sum ||f_{xy,i}||^2$). This is crucial for preventing dangerous rolling moments on stairs and slopes, distinguishing between stable horizontal contacts (primarily vertical forces, allowing controlled sideways rolling) and unstable vertical contacts (high horizontal forces, causing pitch instability and COM rise).
 - **Body position bias:** A clipped Euclidean distance reward penalizing significant displacement from the initial horizontal position during recovery.
- **Motion Constraints:** Standard kinematic penalties to ensure smooth and feasible robot movements, including limits on joint positions, angular velocities, accelerations, action smoothing, and joint torques.

#### 3.4. Curriculum Learning

To facilitate the progressive learning of recovery skills, curriculum learning is adopted. The difficulty of various terrain types (obstacle placement, sizes, gaps) is gradually increased. This approach improves robustness and helps prevent overfitting to specific terrain configurations.

#### 3.5. Implementation Details

- **Training:** The controller is trained in NVIDIA Isaac Gym using the PPO algorithm. Training involves 4,096 parallel domain-randomized quadrupedal robots, starting from random supine positions. Episodes last 350 timesteps. Domain randomization is applied to robot morphology (trunk, hip, thigh, calf masses and dimensions) and control parameters (PD gains, motor strength, COM shift, payload, motor friction) to enhance policy robustness. Training is performed on an Intel Core i7-13700K, 32 GB RAM, and NVIDIA RTX 4070 Ti GPU.
- **Deployment:** The trained policy is deployed on a Unitree Go2 robot, utilizing its onboard NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano. The controller operates at 50Hz, processing joint states and IMU data. Minimal adaptation for deployment includes sensor data preprocessing and action scaling, often with adjusted PD parameters (higher $k_d$, lower $k_p$) to achieve smoother motions.

#### 3.6. Compared Methods

The study compares FR-Net against several baselines and ablation studies:
- **FR-Net (Ours):** Full framework.
- **Ablations:** FR-Net without mass distribution estimation, without collision estimator, and without the autoencoder model.
- **Baselines:** DreamRiser (learning terrain imagination via autoencoder), RMA (Rapid Motor Adaptation, an online adaptation approach with a teacher-student framework), and Vanilla PPO (trained with only proprioception).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research presents findings from extensive simulations and real-world experiments, evaluating the robustness, safety, and generalizability of the FR-Net framework.

#### 4.1. Analysis of Success Rate in Simulation

The robustness of FR-Net and comparison methods was assessed across ten increasing difficulty levels of various terrains. Success was defined as achieving a stable upright pose within five seconds, with positional displacement under 1 meter.

- **Overall Performance:** While all methods achieved high success rates (>90%) at lower difficulty levels (1-3), FR-Net consistently demonstrated superior performance as terrain complexity increased.
- **Performance on Challenging Terrains:** At maximum difficulty (level 10), FR-Net attained the highest success rates on Rough Terrains (69.5%) and Beams (64.1%), outperforming Vanilla PPO by 21.8% and 25.3%, respectively. On Stairs and Slopes, FR-Net maintained success rates $\ge$ 58%, whereas alternative methods fell below 45%. For other demanding terrains like Discrete Stones and Gaps, FR-Net surpassed DreamRiser by 8-12% and Vanilla PPO by 13-25%.
- **Ablation Studies:** The ablation studies confirmed the importance of the MCP components. Removing state estimation (FR-Net w/o Est) reduced Beam performance by 12.7%. Omitting mass consideration (FR-Net w/o Mass) lowered Gaps success from 46.8% to 35.5%. These results underscore the contribution of explicit mass-contact prediction to robust recovery.

#### 4.2. Effects of Stability Reward in Simulation

The effectiveness of the stability control reward component was specifically investigated on challenging inclined terrains.

- **Controlled Recovery on Steep Terrains:** On 40° stairs, the robot with FR-Net incorporating stability rewards maintained lateral displacement within 0.8 m. Similarly, on 40° slopes, displacement was limited to 1.2 m. This controlled behavior effectively prevented dangerous tumbling.
- **Comparison without Stability Reward:** Visual comparisons (Fig. 8) showed that without these stability rewards, the robot exhibited unstable behaviors and dangerous rolling motions, particularly on steep stairs and slopes, highlighting the reward function's role in enhancing safety.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments on Unitree Go2 Robot

Extensive real-world validation was conducted on the Unitree Go2 robot across eleven diverse and challenging scenarios, including natural terrains (grass field, small stones) and structured obstacles (stairs, air beams, hazardous gaps).

- **Robust Recovery:** FR-Net demonstrated robust recovery capabilities across all tested scenarios, effectively bridging the reality gap from simulation to real-world deployment. Action scaling and PD parameter adjustments were used to mitigate aggressive motions learned in RL.
- **Stairs (38.5° incline):** A detailed comparison (Fig. 9) on stairs showed that FR-Net with the Stability Control reward achieved controlled recovery, preventing the robot from tumbling downwards. In contrast, the baseline without this reward exhibited uncontrolled, dangerous rolling motions down the stairs. This provided empirical validation of the reward design's efficacy in maintaining safer recovery behaviors.
- **Challenging Gaps:** The robot successfully recovered even when two legs stepped into a gap (Fig. 10). Although the method cannot precisely predict hidden contact geometry, it executed effective recovery motions using proprioceptive feedback, adjusting limb positioning to leverage available support surfaces through reactive adaptation.
- **Suspended Beams:** On suspended beams (Fig. 11), the robot performed a controlled recovery from an unstable initial position. It demonstrated precise weight redistribution, using the front right leg for stabilization while intentionally keeping one foot off-ground. This strategic planning prevented mechanical wedging and achieved a stable final pose with only partial support, highlighting capability in confined scenarios.

#### 4.4. Analysis of MCP Output

The performance of the Mass-Contact Predictor was analyzed during robot recovery (Fig. 12).

- **Mass Estimation Accuracy:** During static phases (before dynamic motion), mass estimation accuracy was observed to be limited due to minimal joint forces. However, during dynamic motion, when joint loads increased, the prediction accuracy for mass distribution improved significantly.
- **Contact Probability Predictions:** The MCP demonstrated its ability to anticipate physical interactions through contact probability predictions for different leg segments during recovery, all without relying on external sensing.

#### 4.5. Validation on Humanoid Robot

The framework was also tested on the humanoid robot Unitree G1 in simulation. By combining the proposed reward functions with humanoid-specific rewards, the robot successfully recovered on irregular terrains, indicating the adaptability of FR-Net's core methods to different robot platforms.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents FR-Net, a robust learning-based framework for quadrupedal fall recovery, demonstrating several significant contributions and potential impacts.

#### 5.1. Significance

- **Robustness on Challenging Terrains:** FR-Net significantly advances the capability of quadrupedal robots to recover from falls on highly complex and unpredictable terrains, an area where traditional and many existing learning-based methods fall short. This includes steep stairs, cantilever beams, and gaps, which pose substantial challenges due to uncertain interactions and incomplete terrain perception.
- **Innovation in State Estimation:** The introduction of the Mass-Contact Predictor (MCP) network is a key innovation. By effectively estimating mass distribution and contact states from limited proprioceptive sensory inputs, FR-Net provides crucial internal state awareness without relying on vulnerable external sensors or explicit terrain maps during deployment. This contributes to the robustness and practicality of the system.
- **Safety-Oriented Reward Design:** The carefully designed reward functions, particularly the stability control reward, address critical safety aspects of fall recovery. By penalizing hazardous motions like uncontrolled rolling on steep inclines and promoting stable contact management, the framework guides the policy towards safer and more controlled recovery behaviors.
- **Demonstrated Generalizability:** The comprehensive validation across multiple quadrupedal platforms in simulation (Go2, Spot, Lite3) and extensive real-world experiments on the Go2 robot across numerous diverse scenarios highlights the framework's strong generalization capabilities. The successful bridging of the simulation-to-reality gap further underscores its practical utility.
- **Adaptability to Other Platforms:** The successful preliminary validation on a humanoid robot (Unitree G1) in simulation suggests that the core principles and methodologies of FR-Net, particularly the reward function design and learning approach, could be adaptable to other legged robot morphologies, extending its potential impact.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Enhanced Robot Autonomy:** The ability of robots to autonomously recover from falls reduces the need for human intervention, thereby increasing operational uptime and efficiency in various applications. This is particularly valuable in environments that are dangerous or inaccessible to humans, such as disaster zones, industrial inspection sites, or extraterrestrial exploration.
- **Increased Robot Durability and Mission Success:** By preventing uncontrolled tumbles and minimizing damage during falls, FR-Net contributes to the longevity and reliability of legged robots. This directly impacts mission success in critical applications where robot failure can have severe consequences.
- **Advancement in Robot Intelligence:** The framework's ability to infer complex environmental and internal states from limited sensory input and adapt its behavior represents a step towards more intelligent and resilient robotic systems capable of operating in unstructured real-world environments.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** This work identifies limitations, such as the current estimation of only general body and joint collisions rather than precise contact locations. This points to clear directions for future research, such as the integration of additional tactile sensors, to further refine terrain interaction understanding.
- **Broader Application Potential:** Beyond fall recovery, the concept of a Mass-Contact Predictor could potentially be applied to other challenging locomotion tasks requiring an understanding of robot-environment interaction and internal state, such as agile manipulation or complex terrain traversal.

In summary, FR-Net represents a significant advancement in robust quadrupedal fall recovery, offering a promising direction for enhancing the autonomy, safety, and generalizability of legged robots in challenging real-world environments.
