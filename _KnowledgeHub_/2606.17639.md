# ERQA-Plus: A Diagnostic Benchmark for Reasoning in Embodied AI

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers from A*STAR and Nanyang Technological University, Singapore, introduced ERQA-Plus, a diagnostic benchmark for embodied AI featuring a fine-grained reasoning taxonomy and a multi-stage QA generation pipeline. This benchmark revealed that large vision-language models struggle with complex temporal reasoning and path planning, while robotics-specific training in models like RoboBrain2.5-8B can provide efficient inductive biases that yield superior performance in these challenging areas.

## Problem

- Existing VQA and EQA benchmarks often allow models to achieve high scores through shortcut learning or linguistic biases, rather than deep, grounded reasoning.
- There is limited control over the specific reasoning processes being evaluated in current benchmarks, hindering diagnostic analysis of model capabilities.
- Generalist embodied agents lack fine-grained diagnostic frameworks to identify specific weaknesses and measure progress in distinct reasoning skills.

## Method

- Developed **ERQA-Plus**, a diagnostic benchmark structured around a taxonomy of ==five interdependent reasoning categories== for generalist embodied agents.
- Implemented a multi-stage, iterative QA generation and validation pipeline using Generator (GPT-4o), Judge (Gemini 3 Pro), and Reviser (GPT-4o) agents to ensure visual grounding and minimize linguistic bias.
- Focused on creating multiple-choice and open-ended questions that require deep visual justification and target specific reasoning subtypes like spatial, functional, and procedural understanding.

## Results

- Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking achieved the highest overall performance with **83.4%** MCQ accuracy, demonstrating the benefit of scale and chain-of-thought reasoning.
- RoboBrain2.5-8B exhibited superior performance in Temporal Reasoning (**73.3%** MCQ) and Path Planning, surpassing Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking by **26.6** percentage points in Temporal Reasoning MCQ.
- LEO, an embodied generalist agent, showed notably low performance (**4.1%** MCQ), largely attributed to the limitations introduced by approximating 3D inputs from static RGB images.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Temporal Reasoning and Path Planning are the most challenging reasoning subtypes for current vision-language and embodied models, with many scoring below chance.
> - Domain-aligned training, as seen in RoboBrain2.5-8B, can provide efficient inductive biases, allowing it to significantly outperform larger general VLMs on hard reasoning tasks like Temporal Reasoning.
> - Current LLM-based judges show limited agreement with human evaluators in QA quality assessment, indicating their insufficiency as standalone dataset validators for complex reasoning tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Embodied AI systems aim to integrate perception, reasoning, and action to enable effective operation in dynamic, real-world environments. Recent developments in vision-language models (VLMs) and embodied agents have demonstrated advancements in visual understanding and instruction following. However, a persistent challenge within this domain involves accurately evaluating whether these systems perform genuine grounded reasoning or merely rely on superficial visual or linguistic pattern matching.

Existing benchmarks for Visual Question Answering (VQA) and Embodied Question Answering (EQA) have limitations. Many VQA datasets are susceptible to shortcut learning, linguistic priors, and weak grounding requirements, allowing models to achieve high performance without engaging in deep visual reasoning. EQA benchmarks, while useful for testing navigation and task completion, often provide insufficient control over the specific reasoning processes being evaluated. This restricts the ability to disentangle different reasoning skills and conduct fine-grained diagnostics of model capabilities. This gap is particularly relevant for generalist embodied agents, which require the coordination of multiple reasoning forms, including perception, action understanding, spatial awareness, and commonsense inference, within a unified decision-making framework. Without structured and controlled evaluation, identifying failure modes, measuring progress, and guiding the development of more robust embodied intelligence remains challenging.

ERQA-Plus addresses this gap by introducing a diagnostic benchmark specifically designed to systematically evaluate reasoning in embodied AI. Unlike previous datasets, it emphasizes controlled reasoning dependencies, strong visual grounding, and rigorous validation to provide a more discriminative and diagnostic assessment of embodied intelligence.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a diagnostic benchmark capable of systematically evaluating and understanding the reasoning capabilities of embodied AI systems. The authors aim to move beyond simple accuracy metrics to provide a fine-grained analysis of which forms of embodied reasoning agents can and cannot reliably perform.

The key motivations for introducing ERQA-Plus include:

- **Addressing Limitations of Existing Benchmarks:** Current VQA and EQA benchmarks are often criticized for enabling shortcut learning and linguistic biases, where models can perform well without deep visual grounding. ERQA-Plus seeks to overcome these limitations by designing questions that necessitate genuine visual and embodied reasoning.
- **Enabling Fine-grained Diagnostic Analysis:** Generalist embodied agents require a diverse set of reasoning skills, including perceptual, action-centric, social, navigation-environmental, and contextual commonsense reasoning. Existing benchmarks often lack the granularity to disentangle these interdependent skills. ERQA-Plus aims to provide a structured taxonomy for evaluation, allowing for a more detailed diagnosis of strengths and weaknesses.
- **Ensuring Strong Visual Grounding and Reduced Linguistic Bias:** The benchmark is motivated by the need for questions that are unambiguously grounded in visual observations, minimizing reliance on linguistic priors. This ensures that models are tested on their ability to interpret and reason about the visual scene rather than pattern matching in language.
- **Promoting Robust and Interpretable Embodied Intelligence:** By offering a clear framework to identify specific reasoning failures, ERQA-Plus intends to guide the development of more robust, reliable, and interpretable embodied AI systems. The authors believe that understanding *how* and *why* models succeed or fail is crucial for future progress.
- **Providing a Quality-Controlled Evaluation Framework:** The motivation extends to establishing a robust and reliable dataset creation process, incorporating automated and human validation to ensure the high quality and diagnostic value of the generated question-answer pairs.

In summary, the motivation behind ERQA-Plus is to bridge the gap between observed performance and true grounded reasoning in embodied AI, offering a tool for deeper analysis and targeted improvement of agent capabilities.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

ERQA-Plus is a diagnostic benchmark comprising 1,766 question-answer (QA) instances grounded in 711 robot-centric images. The images are sourced from the original ERQA dataset (630 images) and additional images curated from COIN and ScanNet (81 images). The dataset is structured according to a hierarchical taxonomy of reasoning types, generated through a multi-stage pipeline, and evaluated using both multiple-choice and open-ended formats.

#### 3.1. Reasoning Taxonomy for Generalist Embodied Agents

The benchmark organizes reasoning into five interdependent categories, reflecting the cognitive architecture required for navigation, manipulation, and human collaboration:

1. **Perceptual Reasoning**: Focuses on recognizing entities and their spatial and temporal relationships.
 - *Subtypes*: Object reasoning (identifying objects and attributes), Spatial reasoning (geometric structure, relative positioning, occlusion), Temporal reasoning (event sequences, motion dynamics).
2. **Action-centric Reasoning**: Addresses understanding and generating behavior.
 - *Subtypes*: Action recognition (identifying manipulations), Intention reasoning (inferring goals, predicting actions), Procedural reasoning (modeling multi-step tasks, action sequences).
3. **Social and Interaction Reasoning**: Supports collaboration by interpreting human mental states and communicative cues.
 - *Subtype*: Attention and role understanding (inferring gaze, task roles).
4. **Navigation and Environmental Reasoning**: Focuses on understanding scene layouts and acting within them.
 - *Subtypes*: Layout understanding (scene structure, object-environment relationships), Path planning (feasible trajectories, obstacle avoidance).
5. **Contextual and Commonsense Reasoning**: Leverages prior knowledge to interpret scenes and anticipate outcomes.
 - *Subtypes*: Functional context (inferring object/space use), Plausible event prediction (anticipating future states), World knowledge grounding (applying commonsense priors).

This taxonomy emphasizes interdependency, with perceptual reasoning underpinning higher-level categories, and contextual reasoning providing global priors across all stages. The dataset's distribution shows a dominance of spatial and object-centric reasoning, with higher-order skills deliberately included despite their lower frequency.

#### 3.2. Multi-stage QA Generation and Validation Pipeline

The dataset is constructed using a multi-stage pipeline involving three primary components: a Generator, a Judge agent, and a Reviser agent, applied iteratively.

- **Generator Agent**: Using GPT-4o, the Generator takes one or more robot-centric images and produces 2-5 multiple-choice questions aligned with the reasoning taxonomy.
 - *Design Principles*: Prioritizes grounded visual reasoning over linguistic shortcuts, aligns each question with a specific reasoning subtype, maintains causal coherence in question sequencing, ensures relevance to embodied robotic tasks, and constructs answer choices with balanced, plausible distractors.
 - *Visual Grounding Constraints*: Questions are formulated to withhold explicit visual cues from the text, compelling models to reason directly from visual observations. Answer options are designed to be mutually exclusive and visually confusable under shallow reasoning, with a single correct answer supported by a concise visual justification.

- **Judge Agent**: Gemini 3 Pro serves as the Judge, automatically evaluating the quality of each generated QA instance.
 - *Role and Objective*: Assesses if the QA is meaningful, visually grounded, logically and spatially sound, unambiguous, and correctly labeled. It outputs a structured JSON object containing a numerical score (0-10), a justification, and detected issues.
 - *Evaluation Dimensions*: Assesses question quality (grounding, visual perception accuracy, spatial relationships, reasoning depth), option quality (mutual exclusivity, visual distinguishability, single correct option), ground-truth validity, spatial reasoning correctness (distance, occlusion, viewpoint), and overall usefulness for embodied reasoning benchmarking.

- **Reviser Agent**: GPT-4o also functions as the Reviser, refining candidate QA pairs based on structured feedback from the Judge agent. This iterative correction module aims to improve clarity, grounding, and reasoning depth, while reducing ambiguity and eliminating weak distractors. The pipeline loops between the Judge and Reviser for generated questions until quality standards are met.

#### 3.3. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Metrics

The authors benchmarked a range of vision-language models (VLMs) and embodied models:
- **VLMs**: LLaVA-NeXT-8B, Prismatic-7B, MiniCPM-V-4.5-8B, Qwen3-VL-8B, and Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking (a larger-scale reasoning model).
- **Embodied Models**: RoboRefer-8B, RoboBrain2.5-8B, and LEO.

Experiments were conducted under two settings:
1. **Multiple-Choice Question (MCQ) Answering**: Models were instructed to output only the option letter.
2. **Open-ended Question Answering**: Models provided direct answers (max 20 words) without explanation.

For LEO, approximate 3D representations were constructed from RGB images using depth estimation (DepthAnything v3), object proposal (GroundingDINO), and segmentation (SAM2), due to the dataset's lack of direct 3D point cloud inputs. LEO processes a single image per inference step.

**Evaluation Metrics**:
- **MCQ Accuracy**: Standard accuracy (proportion of correctly selected options).
- **Open-ended Semantic Similarity**: Cosine similarity over Sentence-BERT (SBERT) embeddings between model predictions and ground-truth answers, capturing semantic alignment.

#### 3.4. Ablation Studies

A balanced subset of 300 QA pairs was used for ablation studies:

- **Generator Comparison**: Compared GPT-4o and Qwen3-VL-32B as generators. GPT-4o demonstrated faster convergence and consistently higher judge scores, suggesting superior initial QA quality and efficiency in the revision process.
- **Judge Comparison**: Assessed Gemini 3 Pro and Qwen3-VL-32B as judges against human annotations. Gemini 3 Pro showed stronger correlation (Pearson 0.42) and exact-matching accuracy (0.68) with human judgment compared to Qwen3-VL-32B (Pearson 0.18, accuracy 0.53). Neither model achieved strong agreement with human evaluators, indicating limitations for standalone LLM-based judges.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments on ERQA-Plus provided insights into the performance of various vision-language and embodied models across 12 fine-grained reasoning subtypes and 5 high-level reasoning categories.

- **Overall Performance Hierarchy**: Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking achieved the highest overall performance with 83.4% MCQ accuracy and a 61.4% SBERT score in open-ended evaluation. This was attributed to its larger scale and explicit chain-of-thought reasoning capabilities. The performance metrics showed consistency between MCQ accuracy and open-ended SBERT scores across all models.

- **Performance of 8B-scale Models**: Among models around the 8 billion parameter scale, Qwen3-VL-8B and MiniCPM-V-4.5-8B established strong general-VLM baselines, achieving over 79% MCQ accuracy and approximately 56% in open-ended SBERT scores. RoboBrain2.5-8B performed comparably with 80.8% MCQ accuracy and 55.7% open-ended SBERT score, indicating that its robotics-oriented training did not compromise its general reasoning breadth. In contrast, RoboRefer-8B lagged significantly (69.6% MCQ, 47.7% open-ended), suggesting that domain-specific training does not uniformly confer generalization benefits across all reasoning tasks.

- **Persistent Weaknesses in Hard Subtypes**: Temporal Reasoning and Path Planning emerged as the most discriminative reasoning subtypes, with several models scoring below chance in MCQ. Notably, even the top-performing Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking achieved only 46.7% on Temporal Reasoning, its lowest subtype score. This indicates that increasing model scale alone does not resolve challenges in multi-image temporal understanding.

- **Impact of Robotics-specific Training**: RoboBrain2.5-8B demonstrated a significant advantage on certain challenging subtypes. It achieved 73.3% MCQ accuracy on Temporal Reasoning, surpassing Qwen3-VL-32B-Thinking by 26.6 percentage points and the next-best 8B model by 20.0 percentage points. It also led open-ended evaluation on this subtype with 70.3%. Similarly, on Path Planning, RoboBrain2.5-8B led all models with 86.7% MCQ accuracy. These results suggest that domain-aligned training can compensate for substantial capacity differences, offering an efficient inductive bias for specific embodied reasoning tasks.

- **Cross-modal Asymmetries and Bottlenecks**: The evaluation revealed distinctions between recognition and generation competencies. Social and Interaction Reasoning ranked highly under MCQ but became the hardest category in open-ended evaluation (best SBERT score of 50.4%). Conversely, Perceptual Reasoning exhibited an inverse pattern. Action-Centric Reasoning remained a consistent bottleneck for all architectures in open-ended generation, with most 8B models scoring below 52%, highlighting procedural and intentional reasoning as areas requiring further research in embodied VQA.

- **Ablation Study Insights**:
 - **Generator Performance**: Ablation studies on the QA creation pipeline showed that GPT-4o as a generator led to faster convergence and higher average quality scores for QA pairs compared to Qwen3-VL-32B, indicating its effectiveness in producing high-quality initial questions.
 - **Judge Reliability**: The comparison of LLM-based judges against human annotations indicated that Gemini 3 Pro showed stronger alignment with human judgment (higher Pearson correlation and exact-matching accuracy) than Qwen3-VL-32B. However, neither LLM-based judge achieved strong overall agreement with human evaluators, suggesting that current automated judges are not sufficient as standalone evaluators and may require calibration or human-in-the-loop strategies.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

ERQA-Plus contributes to the field of embodied AI by providing a structured and quality-controlled diagnostic benchmark designed to evaluate reasoning capabilities with granularity. Its significance and potential impact are multifaceted:

- **Fine-Grained Diagnostic Tool**: The benchmark offers a framework for assessing not only whether embodied agents provide correct answers, but also *which* specific forms of embodied reasoning they can reliably perform and where they exhibit weaknesses. This moves beyond aggregated accuracy metrics to provide a more detailed understanding of model competence.
- **Guidance for Model Development**: By systematically identifying failure modes in spatial reasoning, procedural understanding, event prediction, and intention inference, ERQA-Plus can guide researchers in developing more robust, interpretable, and cognitively grounded embodied intelligence. It highlights specific areas where current models, including large-scale VLMs, remain inconsistent.
- **Rigorous Evaluation of Embodied Models**: The benchmark facilitates the comparative evaluation of both general-purpose vision-language models and specialized embodied agents. The results underscore that domain-aligned training, as demonstrated by RoboBrain2.5-8B's performance on Temporal Reasoning and Path Planning, can provide efficient inductive biases, sometimes surpassing the performance of larger, generalist models in specific complex reasoning tasks.
- **Improved Safety and Reliability**: By pinpointing reasoning failures in a controlled environment, ERQA-Plus can contribute to the development of safer and more reliable embodied agents prior to their deployment in real-world settings. Understanding these limitations is critical for mitigating risks.
- **Promotion of Grounded Reasoning**: The benchmark implicitly encourages the development of models that perform true grounded reasoning from visual observations, rather than relying on linguistic shortcuts or superficial pattern matching. It reinforces the idea that strong performance on general VQA or language tasks does not automatically translate to robust embodied reasoning.
- **Reusable Data Generation Pipeline**: The multi-stage question generation and validation pipeline, integrating automated generation, judging, and revision, can serve as a methodology for creating other high-quality, visually grounded QA datasets. The ablation studies on generator and judge agents also provide insights into the capabilities and limitations of using large language models in data curation.

The authors acknowledge limitations such as the dataset's uneven coverage of reasoning skills, reliance on static RGB images (rather than closed-loop control), potential biases from the model-assisted generation pipeline, and the suboptimal agreement of automated judges with human evaluators. These limitations suggest avenues for future work, including expanding to richer modalities, balancing category coverage, and incorporating interactive, multilingual settings. Overall, ERQA-Plus provides a foundation for more interpretable evaluation and the targeted advancement of embodied AI.
