# SG-I2V: Self-Guided Trajectory Control in Image-to-Video Generation

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Toronto and Vector Institute developed SG-I2V, a zero-shot, self-guided framework for controllable image-to-video generation from pre-trained diffusion models. It enables precise control over object and camera movements via bounding box trajectories, achieving an FID of 28.87 and maintaining the original high resolution of the base model, outperforming existing zero-shot methods and remaining competitive with supervised approaches.

## Problem

- Existing image-to-video (I2V) generation models lack fine-grained control over specific motions, leading to tedious trial-and-error for desired outputs.
- Supervised control methods are computationally expensive, require large, labor-intensive annotated datasets, and often operate at lower resolutions than base models.
- Zero-shot text-to-video (T2V) control techniques cannot be directly applied to animate existing images or for precise, part-level control not easily expressible via text.

## Method

- A self-guided, zero-shot framework built on Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) that optimizes the noisy latent space to follow user-defined bounding box trajectories.
- A modified spatial self-==attention mechanism== is introduced to enforce strong cross-frame semantic alignment by anchoring subsequent frames' features to the first frame's key and value tokens.
- A frequency-based post-processing step combines the low-frequency component of the optimized latent with the high-frequency component of the original noisy latent, preserving visual quality while retaining motion control.

## Results

- SG-I2V achieves competitive visual quality (FID of 28.87) compared to supervised methods like DragNUWA (30.73) and DragAnything (30.81), despite being zero-shot and operating at the original SVD resolution (576x1024) which is higher than baselines (320x576).
- The framework effectively controls diverse rigid and non-rigid motions while maintaining scene element identity and blending seamlessly, demonstrating a unified approach for both object and camera motion.
- SG-I2V significantly outperforms adapted zero-shot baselines in motion fidelity (ObjMC 14.43 vs. 31.49-36.43 for others) and visual quality (lower FID/FVD), confirming the necessity of its specific feature alignment strategy.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Feature maps from upsampling blocks in pre-trained video diffusion models exhibit weak semantic correspondence across frames at early denoising steps, making naive image-based control insufficient.
> - Modifying the spatial self-attention mechanism by anchoring key and value tokens to the first frame's features effectively enforces strong cross-frame semantic alignment in video diffusion models.
> - High-frequency components of the latent space are crucial for visual quality and noise distribution, while low-frequency components primarily encode motion; separating these during optimization prevents degradation.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of generative AI, particularly in visual media, has witnessed explosive growth, with diffusion models leading the charge in creating high-quality images and increasingly, videos. While early image-to-video (I2V) generation models have achieved impressive photorealistic quality, a persistent challenge remains: fine-grained control over specific elements within the generated video, such as the motion of objects or the camera. This lack of control often relegates the generation process to a tedious trial-and-error approach, requiring multiple regeneration attempts with different random seeds or prompts.

Existing approaches to introduce control typically fall into two main categories:
1. **Supervised Fine-tuning:** Methods like DragNUWA and DragAnything fine-tune pre-trained video generators by adding "motion adapters" that learn to follow conditioning signals (e.g., bounding boxes, point trajectories). While effective, these methods are computationally expensive and demand large datasets with labor-intensive annotations of object motion or camera poses. Furthermore, due to the high training costs, they often operate at lower resolutions than the original base models.
2. **Zero-shot Text-to-Video (T2V) Control:** More recent techniques (e.g., FreeTraj, Trailblazer) aim for zero-shot control by modulating internal model mechanisms, such as cross-attention maps, based on text prompts. However, these methods are primarily designed for text-to-video generation and cannot be directly applied to animate existing images. They also struggle with associating desired edits with text tokens, limiting their precision for fine-grained or part-level control.

SG-I2V positions itself innovatively at the intersection of these challenges. It addresses the critical gap of **zero-shot, controllable image-to-video generation**. Unlike supervised methods, it avoids expensive fine-tuning and the need for annotated datasets, making it highly resource-efficient and accessible. Crucially, it extends the concept of zero-shot control beyond text-based conditioning, enabling direct manipulation of video elements from an input image.

The work also builds upon insights from spatial control in *image* diffusion models, where feature maps are manipulated to guide image layout. However, SG-I2V critically analyzes and identifies fundamental differences in feature alignment within *video* diffusion models, particularly the Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) model, revealing that naive application of image-based techniques is insufficient. This discovery leads to their novel approach of modifying self-attention mechanisms to achieve cross-frame semantic alignment, a unique contribution to the understanding and control of video diffusion models. By offering a self-guided approach that leverages the inherent knowledge within a pre-trained I2V model, SG-I2V represents a significant step towards more intuitive, flexible, and resource-efficient video content creation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a novel framework, named **SG-I2V (Self-Guided Trajectory Control in Image-to-Video Generation)**, that enables **controllable image-to-video generation** in a **zero-shot** manner. This means achieving precise manipulation of video elements without requiring any further training or fine-tuning of the underlying pre-trained diffusion model, nor relying on external annotated datasets or knowledge.

The motivation for SG-I2V stems from several critical limitations in the current state of I2V generation:

- **Lack of Fine-Grained Control:** While modern video diffusion models excel at producing high-quality, photorealistic videos, their outputs are largely unpredictable regarding specific motions. Users often resort to tedious trial-and-error, regenerating videos with different random seeds or text prompts, to achieve a desired outcome. This process is inefficient and frustrating.
- **High Costs of Supervised Control Methods:** Existing methods that offer fine-grained control typically rely on supervised fine-tuning. This incurs significant computational expense for training and necessitates the costly and labor-intensive procurement of large datasets with detailed motion annotations (e.g., bounding boxes or point trajectories). These costs limit the widespread applicability and accessibility of such methods, often forcing them to operate at lower resolutions.
- **Inapplicability of Text-to-Video Control to I2V:** While zero-shot control techniques have emerged for text-to-video generation, they are inherently tied to text prompts. They cannot be directly used to animate existing real-world images or to control motions that are not easily expressible or associable with specific text tokens (e.g., part-level movements).
- **Understanding and Leveraging Model Knowledge:** The authors are motivated to explore whether the complex knowledge embedded within a pre-trained video diffusion model can be "unlocked" and leveraged for control without external guidance. This involves a deep analysis of the internal feature representations of these models, particularly how semantic information is encoded across spatial and temporal dimensions.

In essence, SG-I2V aims to provide an intuitive and efficient solution for users to animate any input image with precise control over object and camera movements, using a simple interface of bounding box trajectories, thereby democratizing controllable video synthesis.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

SG-I2V is a self-guided, zero-shot framework for controllable image-to-video generation built upon a pre-trained image-to-video diffusion model, specifically Stable Video Diffusion (SVD). The core idea is to manipulate the latent space during the denoising process by optimizing it to adhere to user-specified bounding box trajectories. This approach is divided into three main steps: feature map analysis and alignment, latent optimization for trajectory control, and a high-frequency-preserving post-processing step.

#### 3.1. Stable Video Diffusion (SVD) as Base Model

The framework utilizes the image-to-video variant of SVD. SVD operates in a latent space, where a variational autoencoder encodes a video into a lower-dimensional latent representation. A 3D U-Net then iteratively denoises a noisy latent, conditioned on an input image, to generate the video frames. The U-Net consists of downsampling and upsampling paths, incorporating residual blocks, spatial, and temporal attention layers.

#### 3.2. Extracting and Aligning Semantic Video Layout (Crucial Insight)

The authors first conduct a novel analysis of feature maps within SVD to understand how semantic information is encoded. Prior work in image diffusion models showed that upsampling block outputs contain semantically aligned features useful for layout control. However, their analysis of SVD reveals that features from upsampling blocks show *weak semantic correspondence across frames* at early denoising steps, which are crucial for determining the overall video structure.

To overcome this, SG-I2V proposes a **modified spatial self-attention mechanism** for feature alignment. Recognizing that spatial self-attention operates per frame, and temporal attention only aligns pixels at the same position across frames, they modify the spatial self-attention within the U-Net. For each frame *n*, they replace the standard Key (K*n*) and Value (V*n*) tokens with the Key (K1) and Value (V1) tokens from the *first frame*. This operation, `SoftMax(Q_n · SG(K_1)^T / sqrt(D)) · SG(V_1)`, ensures that all subsequent frames' features are "anchored" to the semantic content of the first frame, thereby enforcing strong cross-frame semantic alignment. A stop gradient (SG) is applied to K1 and V1 to stabilize the optimization. This modification is only applied during the loss computation, not during the standard denoising process. Features are extracted from the self-attention layers within the middle stage of the U-Net, which are found to be most effective.

#### 3.3. Trajectory Control with Latent Optimization

Given the semantically aligned feature maps, the next step is to guide the video generation process to follow user-defined bounding box trajectories. For a given input image, the user specifies a set of bounding boxes with their desired center point coordinates for each frame. The method then optimizes the noisy latent `z_t` (the input to the denoising network) at specific denoising timesteps.

The optimization objective is to minimize the squared difference between the aligned feature maps within the moving bounding boxes across frames:
`min || G_b ⊙ (~F_n(z_t)[B_b,n] - SG(~F_1(z_t)[B_b,1])) ||^2`
where `~F_n(z_t)[B_b,n]` represents the cropped feature map for bounding box `b` in frame `n`, and `G_b` is a Gaussian heatmap applied within the bounding box to focus optimization on the center of the region. The optimization is applied selectively during early denoising timesteps (t ∈) as these steps primarily determine the coarse structure and motion. The AdamW optimizer is used for this process. This formulation allows for both object motion control (by bounding foreground objects) and camera motion control (by bounding static background regions and specifying an opposite trajectory).

#### 3.4. High-Frequency Preserved Post-Processing

A direct optimization of the latent `z_t` can lead to visual quality degradation, such as oversmoothing or artifacts, because it can push the latent out of the diffusion model's expected sampling distribution. To mitigate this, inspired by FreeInit, SG-I2V employs a frequency-based post-processing step. It recognizes that motion information is primarily encoded in the low-frequency components of the noisy latent, while high-frequency components contain fine details and noise crucial for visual quality.

After optimization, the new latent `~z_t` is formed by taking the low-frequency component of the *optimized latent* `z*_t` and combining it with the high-frequency component of the *original noisy latent* `z_t`. This is achieved using 2D Fast Fourier Transforms (FFT) and Inverse FFT (IFFT) with a Butterworth low-pass filter (Hγ) at a specific cut-off frequency (γ = 0.5). This ensures that the desired motion control is retained while preserving the natural noise distribution and visual fidelity expected by the diffusion model.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

SG-I2V demonstrates compelling performance in controllable image-to-video generation, substantiated by both qualitative visualizations and quantitative metrics.

#### 4.1. Qualitative Results

- **Versatile Control:** SG-I2V effectively controls a wide range of motions for diverse scene elements. This includes rigid motions (e.g., a train moving), non-rigid deformations (e.g., human hair flowing), and even non-physical elements (e.g., smoke, fire).
- **Natural Motion and Identity Preservation:** The moved scene elements naturally adapt to their new positions within the video, seamlessly blending with the background while maintaining their original identity and appearance.
- **Unified Object and Camera Motion Control:** The framework's general formulation allows for both foreground object motion control and camera motion control (by specifying trajectories for static background regions in the opposite direction of the desired camera movement).
- **Handling Different Granularities:** Unlike some supervised methods trained only on entity-level control, SG-I2V can handle varying granularities of control, from entire objects to smaller parts (e.g., closing a cat's mouth).
- **Superiority over Baselines' Failure Cases:** Qualitative comparisons highlight SG-I2V's ability to produce more natural movements compared to supervised baselines like DragNUWA, which tends to distort objects rather than move them.

#### 4.2. Quantitative Assessment

The method was evaluated on the VIPSeg dataset, using standard metrics for video quality (Frechet Inception Distance - FID, Frechet Video Distance - FVD) and motion fidelity (ObjMC, measuring average distance between generated and target trajectories).

- **Comparison with Supervised Baselines (DragNUWA v1.5, DragAnything, Image Conductor):**
 - SG-I2V achieves competitive visual quality (lower FID and FVD values) compared to supervised methods, despite being a zero-shot approach. Its FID of 28.87 is even better than DragNUWA (30.73) and DragAnything (30.81), and significantly outperforms Image Conductor (48.81).
 - While its motion fidelity (ObjMC of 14.43) is slightly higher (worse) than DragNUWA (10.84) and DragAnything (11.64), this small gap is offset by a crucial advantage: SG-I2V operates at the **original high resolution of SVD (576x1024)**. In contrast, supervised baselines are fine-tuned and generate videos at a lower resolution (320x576) due to the prohibitive computational cost of high-resolution fine-tuning. This demonstrates SG-I2V's superior resource efficiency and practical applicability at higher resolutions.
- **Comparison with Adapted Zero-Shot Baselines (FreeTraj†, MOFT†, DragDiffusion†):**
 - SG-I2V significantly **outperforms all adapted zero-shot baselines** across all metrics. For instance, its ObjMC (14.43) is vastly superior to FreeTraj† (36.43), MOFT† (33.58), and DragDiffusion† (31.49). Similarly, its FID and FVD are substantially lower (better).
 - This finding highlights that zero-shot techniques effective in text-to-video models do not readily transfer to the image-to-video domain without the specific adaptations proposed by SG-I2V, particularly the feature alignment strategy.

#### 4.3. Ablation Studies

The comprehensive ablation studies confirm the importance of SG-I2V's design choices:
- **Feature Map Selection:** The proposed modified self-attention feature maps are critical. Optimizing features from original self/temporal attention layers or upsampling blocks leads to significantly worse motion fidelity (higher ObjMC) and visual quality, validating the need for explicit cross-frame semantic alignment.
- **U-Net Layer:** Feature maps extracted from the middle-resolution layers (M2, M3) of the U-Net's upsampling path are most effective for trajectory guidance, consistent with their semantic richness. Joint optimization across M2 and M3 yields the best results.
- **High-Frequency Preservation:** The post-processing step is crucial for maintaining visual quality. Without it (equivalent to γ=1 cutoff frequency), FID and FVD drastically increase due to oversmoothing and artifacts. It effectively preserves desired motion signals while eliminating undesirable noise disruptions.
- **Optimization Timesteps:** Optimizing during early to middle denoising timesteps (e.g., t=30-45) provides the best balance between motion fidelity and visual quality. Too early, and semantic information is insufficient; too late, and optimization introduces severe artifacts. Continuous optimization over a range of timesteps is superior to single-timestep optimization.

In summary, SG-I2V achieves state-of-the-art zero-shot control in I2V generation, providing a practical and high-quality solution that bridges the performance gap with supervised methods while offering superior resolution and efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

SG-I2V represents a significant advancement in the field of controllable generative AI, particularly for video synthesis. Its contributions have several key implications and potential impacts:

1. **Democratization of Controllable Video Generation:** By offering a zero-shot, self-guided approach, SG-I2V eliminates the need for computationally expensive fine-tuning and the procurement of large, labor-intensive annotated datasets. This significantly lowers the barrier to entry for precise video editing and creation, making advanced motion control accessible to a much broader audience, including artists, content creators, and researchers with limited computational resources.

2. **Enhanced User Experience and Efficiency:** The framework transforms the often-tedious process of trial-and-error in I2V generation into an intuitive and direct manipulation workflow. Users can specify desired object or camera movements via simple bounding box trajectories, leading to more efficient content creation and rapid prototyping of video ideas.

3. **Deeper Understanding of Video Diffusion Models:** The authors' detailed analysis of feature alignment within pre-trained image-to-video diffusion models, particularly the discovery of weak cross-frame semantic correspondence in upsampling blocks and the effectiveness of modified self-attention for alignment, provides crucial insights into the internal workings of these complex models. This understanding can inspire future architectural designs and control mechanisms, leading to even more robust and controllable video generators.

4. **Foundation for Future Research:** SG-I2V's success in leveraging inherent model knowledge opens new avenues for research in self-supervised and zero-shot control for complex generative tasks. It establishes a strong baseline for developing more sophisticated control strategies that are both effective and resource-efficient. The identified limitations, such as handling very large motions, complex physical interactions, or robustly maintaining in-distribution latents during aggressive optimization, also lay out clear directions for future work.

5. **Broad Applications:** The ability to precisely control motions in videos generated from existing images has immense potential across various domains:
 - **Creative Industries:** Artists and animators can easily bring static images to life with custom movements, enhancing visual storytelling for film, animation, and digital art.
 - **Marketing and Advertising:** Businesses can create dynamic and engaging video content from product images or static advertisements, tailored to specific marketing campaigns without extensive production costs.
 - **Interactive Media:** It could serve as a component in interactive tools for video editing, virtual reality, and augmented reality applications.
 - **Scientific Visualization:** Researchers might use it to animate static data visualizations, highlighting dynamic processes.

6. **Ethical Considerations:** The authors responsibly acknowledge the ethical implications of advancing photorealistic video generation. As the quality of synthesized videos continues to improve, the potential for misuse (e.g., creating deceptive "deepfakes" or spreading misinformation) becomes a serious concern. Their statement advocating for safe and responsible use highlights the importance of ethical guidelines alongside technological progress.

In conclusion, SG-I2V represents a significant stride towards practical and intuitive control over generative video models. By prioritizing efficiency and leveraging internal model knowledge, it offers a powerful tool for content creation and a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanics of video diffusion.
