# Decompose and Recompose: Reasoning New Skills from Existing Abilities for Cross-Task Robotic Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> The 'Decompose and Recompose' framework enables robots to perform new manipulation tasks by reasoning about and recomposing existing skills from interpretable atomic skill–action pairs. This approach achieved an overall success rate of 26.4% on the AGNOSTOS simulation benchmark and 36% in real-world experiments.

## Problem

- Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models struggle with zero-shot cross-task generalization, showing performance degradation on novel tasks requiring skill transfer.
- Current in-context learning (ICL) methods in robotics often rely on low-level continuous action sequences, causing large language models (LLMs) to perform superficial trajectory imitation rather than deeper skill reasoning.
- Robotic manipulation lacks explicit, composable skill representations, hindering the ability to effectively combine fundamental actions for unseen, complex tasks.

## Method

- A training-free 'Decompose and Recompose' framework is proposed, converting raw demonstrations into interpretable atomic skill–action pairs through VLM-based annotation.
- A dual-library demonstration retrieval system combines dynamic (task-adaptive visual and plan similarity) and coverage-aware static libraries to select relevant and comprehensive examples for in-context learning.
- Skill-Augmented In-Context Learning (SA-ICL) leverages an LLM (Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct) prompted with skill-action alignment to guide compositional reasoning for generating executable low-level actions.

## Results

- Achieved an average success rate of **26.4%** across 23 unseen tasks on the AGNOSTOS simulation benchmark, outperforming baselines such as "0 (**17.5%**) and X-ICM (**23.5%**).
- Demonstrated efficacy in real-world settings with a UFACTORY xArm6 robotic arm, achieving an average success rate of **36%** across five diverse physical manipulation tasks.
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical contributions of the dynamic demonstrations library, coverage-aware static library, and especially the skill-augmented in-context learning component to overall performance.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Providing LLMs with explicit, interpretable atomic skill-action pairs is crucial for activating compositional reasoning capabilities, significantly enhancing zero-shot cross-task generalization beyond trajectory imitation.
> - A synergistic dual-library demonstration retrieval approach, balancing task-specific relevance with comprehensive skill coverage, is essential for robust and effective in-context learning.
> - The quality of both the visual encoder for static scene semantics and the LLM backbone for reasoning directly impacts the framework's ability to generalize across diverse manipulation tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The research presented in this paper addresses a fundamental challenge in robotic manipulation: cross-task generalization in open-world environments. This domain requires robots to transfer learned manipulation knowledge from a set of seen tasks to entirely novel tasks, including those involving new objects, different goals, or unprecedented combinations of actions, without requiring specific parameter updates.

The field has seen significant advancements with the emergence of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, such as RT-2, OpenVLA, and $\pi^0$. These models have demonstrated progress in handling visual variations within known tasks. However, as highlighted by benchmarks like AGNOSTOS, these VLA models often exhibit degradation when confronted with zero-shot cross-task generalization scenarios, indicating that large-scale pre-training alone does not sufficiently equip them for transferring skills across diverse, unseen tasks. The limitation lies in their capacity to understand and apply transferable manipulation knowledge.

Concurrently, in-context learning (ICL) approaches, primarily driven by large language models (LLMs), have shown promise for generalizing to new tasks from a few demonstrations without parameter updates. In robotics, works like RoboPrompt, KAT, and Instant Policy have applied ICL for control. However, existing ICL methods for cross-task settings, such as X-ICM, typically provide only low-level continuous action sequences as context to LLMs. This approach often leads to LLMs performing superficial trajectory imitation based on direct alignment of numerical sequences, rather than engaging in deeper skill reasoning. Such low-level representations are insufficient to convey causal or procedural information (e.g., what skill is performed, why, and how it links to subsequent steps), which limits their generalization capability to tasks requiring novel skill compositions.

This work positions itself to bridge this gap by proposing an approach that leverages the strengths of LLMs for reasoning while addressing the limitations of purely numerical action contexts. By introducing atomic skill–action pairs as an intermediate, interpretable representation, the paper aims to elevate cross-task transfer from mere trajectory shape similarity to a more abstract and composable understanding of skills. This focus on explicit skill semantics is intended to activate the compositional reasoning capabilities of LLMs, thereby enhancing zero-shot generalization across novel manipulation tasks.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to enable robots to achieve zero-shot cross-task generalization in manipulation, allowing them to perform entirely new tasks that involve novel objects, goals, and action compositions, without requiring any parameter updates or specific training for those new tasks.

The motivation for this objective stems from several limitations observed in current robotic manipulation systems:

1. **Lack of Transferable Skill Knowledge:** While existing VLA models have improved visual robustness within known tasks, they struggle to extract and transfer generalizable manipulation knowledge to unseen tasks. The problem is not merely about adapting to visual perturbations but about understanding and composing fundamental manipulation skills in novel contexts.
2. **Superficial Trajectory Imitation in In-Context Learning:** Current in-context learning approaches in robotics, particularly for cross-task scenarios, typically provide low-level numerical action sequences as demonstrations. This often leads LLMs to degenerate into mimicking trajectory patterns rather than comprehending the underlying manipulation skills. Such an approach fails to capture the causal and procedural information inherent in manipulation, making it difficult to adapt to tasks requiring novel compositions of known skills.
3. **Absence of Composable Skill Representations:** The reliance on continuous action sequences as context neglects the hierarchical and compositional nature of robotic tasks. Without explicit representations of atomic skills (e.g., "grasp," "move," "release") and their logical sequencing, LLMs cannot effectively reason about how to combine these primitives to achieve new, complex goals.
4. **Need for Explicit Compositional Reasoning:** To truly generalize across tasks, a robot system needs to move beyond direct pattern matching. It requires the ability to decompose a task into its constituent skills and then intelligently recompose these skills in a new order or combination to address an unseen task. Current methods do not explicitly elicit this compositional reasoning capability.
5. **Desire for a Training-Free Generalization Pipeline:** Developing a system that can generalize without specific training on new task distributions is crucial for deploying general-purpose robots in dynamic, open-world environments. This necessitates leveraging pre-trained models and sophisticated demonstration retrieval strategies.

To address these motivations, the key objectives of the proposed "Decompose and Recompose" framework are:

- **To construct atomic skill label–action aligned data:** This involves transforming raw demonstrations into interpretable intermediate representations that explicitly link high-level semantic skills (e.g., "Grasp[object]") with their corresponding low-level continuous action sequences.
- **To develop a dual-library demonstration retrieval system:** This system aims to provide the LLM with demonstrations that are both highly relevant to the target unseen task (task-adaptive) and comprehensively cover all necessary atomic skill patterns (skill-comprehensive). This is achieved by combining a dynamic retrieval library (based on visual-semantic and plan similarity) with a coverage-aware static library (for supplementing missing skill patterns).
- **To enable skill-augmented in-context learning:** The framework aims to feed these carefully selected, skill-augmented demonstrations to an LLM, prompting it to perform compositional reasoning over the interpretable skill structures for skill composition and execution ordering, while ultimately generating executable low-level action sequences for the robot.

By achieving these objectives, the research seeks to provide a more robust and generalizable approach to zero-shot cross-task robotic manipulation.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed "Decompose and Recompose" framework is a training-free pipeline designed for zero-shot cross-task robotic manipulation, leveraging pre-trained visual encoders and large language models. The methodology focuses on providing LLMs with compositional and interpretable intermediate representations—atomic skill labels aligned with low-level actions—rather than raw numerical action sequences. The approach comprises three main components: Atomic Skill Collection, Dual-Library Demonstration Retrieval, and Skill-Augmented In-Context Learning.

**Problem Formulation:**
The system operates on a library of seen demonstrations $D_{seen}$ from seen tasks $T_{seen}$. Each demonstration $d \in D_{seen}$ includes a task instruction, a sequence of keyframe observations, and corresponding 7-DoF actions (end-effector position via 3D voxel indices, rotation via Euler angles discretized into bins, and binary gripper state). The goal is, for an unseen task $\tau_{unseen} \notin T_{seen}$ with instruction $\ell_q$ and initial observation $o_q^0$, to generate an action sequence that successfully accomplishes the task.

#### 3.1. Atomic Skill Collection

This component converts opaque numerical action sequences from seen demonstrations into interpretable skill–action pairs, which are denoted as $\{(s_k, a_k)\}_{k=1}^K$.

- **Keyframe Extraction:** Demonstrations are segmented into coherent atomic actions. Keyframes are identified based on changes in gripper state (open/closed), motion stopping events (joint velocities below a threshold), and episode termination.
- **VLM-based Annotation:** For each action segment between keyframes, a Vision-Language Model (VLM) is used to annotate it with an atomic skill label, typically in the format `Verb[obj]` or `Verb[obj1, obj2]`. The VLM takes as input the start and end keyframe images, gripper state transition, object names from segmentation masks, and the task instruction.
- **Gripper-Constrained Labeling:** To ensure physical consistency, hard constraints are applied based on gripper state changes: a transition from open to closed forces the label to `Grasp[·]`, and from closed to open forces it to `Release[·]`.
- **Post-processing:** Rule-based post-processing is applied to refine labels, such as enforcing argument order for relational verbs (e.g., `Close`, `Insert`, `Place`) and verifying object types for `Grasp/Release`. If the gripper remains open during a relational action, the label is downgraded to `Move`.

#### 3.2. Dual-Library Demonstration Retrieval

This component selects demonstrations that are both relevant to the query task and comprehensively cover necessary skills. It employs a synergistic dual-library system.

- **4.2.1. Dynamic Demonstrations Library:**
 This library retrieves task-adaptive demonstrations by combining visual similarity with plan-based skill matching.
 - **Visual Similarity:** A pre-trained DINOv3 visual encoder extracts a normalized global feature $f_q$ from the query observation $o_q$. Cosine similarity $s_{vis}^i = f_q \cdot f_i$ is computed with features from each seen demonstration $d_i$.
 - **Plan-based Similarity:** A planning agent $\pi_{plan}$ generates a predicted atomic skill sequence $\hat{S}$ for the unseen task, based on the instruction and scene state. Jaccard similarity $s_{plan}^i$ is then calculated between this predicted plan and each demonstration's skill labels $S_i$, considering both verb sets and bigram chains of verbs.
 - **Fusion and Selection:** The visual and plan-based similarities are fused using a weighted sum $s_i = \alpha \cdot \tilde{s}_{vis}^i + (1 - \alpha) \cdot s_{plan}^i$, where $\tilde{s}_{vis}^i$ is min-max normalized. The top-$k_{sim}$ demonstrations are selected to form $D_{dyn}$.

- **4.2.2. Coverage-aware Static Demonstrations Library:**
 This library ensures comprehensive skill coverage by supplementing any critical skill patterns potentially missed by dynamic retrieval.
 - **Token Representation:** Each demonstration is represented by object-agnostic tokens, comprising verb tokens (e.g., `[Push]`) and verb chains (e.g., `[Move->grasp]`), abstracting away object identities.
 - **IDF-based Token Weighting:** Inverse Document Frequency (IDF) is used to assign weights $w_t$ to each token, prioritizing rare but important skills.
 - **Selection Score:** A greedy selection algorithm is employed, using a score that balances the coverage gain (sum of weights of newly covered tokens) against the demonstration's length: $Score(d) = \frac{\sum_{t \in T(d)\setminus C} w_t}{1 + \gamma \cdot |S_d|}$.
 - **Inference Completion:** During inference, the system calculates the coverage gap $G$ (tokens required by the predicted plan but not covered by $D_{dyn}$). It then greedily selects up to $k_{cov}$ demonstrations from the static library $L_{cov}$ to fill these missing tokens, resulting in the final demonstration set $D = D_{dyn} \cup D_{cov}$.

#### 3.3. Skill-Augmented In-Context Learning (SA-ICL)

The retrieved demonstrations are formatted for the LLM.

- **Prompt Structure:** Each demonstration is presented as: `[instruction_i, observation_i, plan_i] -> [(skill_1, action_1)..., (skill_K, action_K)]`. The skill labels (e.g., `Grasp[cup]`) serve as interpretable intermediate representations that explain the action semantics and execution order.
- **LLM Output:** Crucially, for the query task, while the instruction, initial observation, and predicted skill plan are provided, the LLM is prompted to output *only* the executable low-level action sequence, without the skill labels. This ensures direct compatibility with the robot controller while implicitly leveraging the skill-level reasoning guided by the demonstrations.
- **Action Discretization/Reconstruction:** Low-level continuous actions are discretized into 7-tuple integer bins (3 for position, 3 for rotation, 1 for gripper state) for LLM input, and then reconstructed back into continuous end-effector pose and gripper commands for robot execution.
- **System Prompting:** The system prompt explicitly guides the LLM that skill annotations in demonstrations are for reasoning, but the final output should be raw action sequences.

This multi-faceted approach aims to elevate the LLM's capability from superficial trajectory imitation to deep compositional skill reasoning for robust cross-task generalization. The planning agent and LLM used in the reasoning phase is Qwen-2.5-7B-Instruct, with Qwen2.5-VL used for offline atomic skill collection.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The evaluation of the proposed "Decompose and Recompose" method was conducted on the AGNOSTOS benchmark in simulation and in real-world environments.

#### 4.1. AGNOSTOS Benchmark (Simulation)

Experiments were performed on 23 unseen tasks from the AGNOSTOS benchmark, categorized into Level-1 and Level-2 difficulty tiers. The method was compared against various Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, including Foundation VLA Models, Human-Video Pretrained VLA Models, In-Domain Trained VLA Models, and other In-Context Learning (ICL) approaches. Each task involved 75 episodes (25 rollouts across 3 seeds).

- **Overall Performance:** The "Decompose and Recompose" method achieved the highest overall success rates across both Level-1 and Level-2 tasks. The average success rate was 32.5% for Level-1, 18.5% for Level-2, and 26.4% across all tasks. This surpasses other baselines, including $\pi^0$ (21.7% for Level-1, 12.0% for Level-2, 17.5% overall) and X-ICM (28.6% for Level-1, 16.9% for Level-2, 23.5% overall).
- **Task-Specific Success:** The method achieved success rates exceeding 60% on four distinct tasks (Microwave, Seat, LampOff, and USB), which was more than any other individual baseline (which achieved this threshold on at most three tasks). This indicates a broader generalization capability across diverse unseen scenarios.
- **Mechanism Validation:** The results suggest that providing LLMs with atomic skill-action alignment pairs effectively preserves executable low-level actions while offering compositional and reasoned intermediate representations. This structure appears to stimulate the model’s reasoning capabilities for skill composition and execution ordering, enhancing zero-shot cross-task generalization. Visualizations demonstrate the method's reasoning process across various unseen tasks.

#### 4.2. Ablation Analysis

A series of ablation studies were performed to validate the contribution of each core component and the impact of architectural choices.

- **Component Contribution (Table 2):**
 - **Baseline (random demonstration selection):** Achieved 21.6% overall success.
 - **+ Dynamic Demonstrations Library (DDL):** Incorporating task-adaptive retrieval (visual-semantic and skill-sequence matching) improved performance to 23.3%. This validates the importance of relevant demonstration selection.
 - **+ Coverage-aware Demonstrations Library (CDL):** Further adding the static library to supplement missing skill patterns increased performance to 24.9%. This indicates the value of ensuring comprehensive skill coverage.
 - **+ Skill-Augmented In-Context Learning (SA-ICL):** With explicit skill–action alignment annotations, the method reached its best performance of 26.4%. This confirms that structured skill representations are crucial for eliciting compositional reasoning beyond trajectory imitation.

- **Impact of Visual Encoders (Table 3, upper portion):**
 - Replacing DINOv3 (26.4% overall) with DINOv2 (25.7% overall) or CLIP (24.8% overall) resulted in reduced performance. This highlights that the capability of the visual encoder to extract static scene semantic representations directly influences the method’s zero-shot cross-task generalization.

- **Impact of LLM Backbones (Table 3, lower portion):**
 - Using different LLM backbones (Qwen2.5-7B: 26.4%; InternLM3-8B: 24.1%; Llama3.0-8B: 22.4%; Ministral-8B: 20.2%) showed that backbone capability significantly affects generalization. Stronger LLMs demonstrated better cross-task generalization.

- **Number of Demonstrations (Table 5, upper portion):**
 - The model achieved 0% success with zero demonstrations, confirming their necessity. Performance consistently improved as the number of demonstrations increased from 5 (19.8% overall) to 20 (26.4% overall), indicating that richer demonstration sets provide more comprehensive skill–action patterns for reasoning.

- **Number of Coverage-aware Demonstrations (Table 5, lower portion):**
 - Relying solely on dynamic retrieval (0 coverage-aware demos) yielded 24.9%. Performance improved by adding 1 (25.5%), 2 (26.0%), and 3 (26.4%) coverage-aware demonstrations. A slight decrease with 4 demonstrations (26.1%) suggests an optimal balance exists between coverage completeness and avoiding noise from excessive static examples.

#### 4.3. Real-World Experiments

The method was tested on a UFACTORY xArm6 robotic arm performing five physical manipulation tasks.

- **Performance:** The method achieved an average success rate of 36% across the five tasks, with individual task success rates ranging from 20% to 70% (e.g., "Put a bunch of grapes on a plate" at 70%, "Stack cups" at 30%).
- **Efficacy:** These results demonstrate the method's capability for zero-shot cross-task generalization in real-world settings. Successful cases involved generating appropriate skill sequences and executing precise actions in diverse scenarios, such as grasping and placing objects correctly for stacking.
- **Failure Analysis:** Observed failure cases highlighted challenges such as inaccurate 6-DoF pose understanding (leading to tilted grasps), difficulty with slender objects (e.g., spoon handle), and imprecise 3D spatial reasoning (e.g., releasing an object over the edge instead of the center of a target). These indicate areas for future improvement, such as explicit 3D spatial reasoning and object affordance understanding.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The "Decompose and Recompose" framework represents a notable contribution to the field of robotic manipulation, particularly in addressing the challenge of cross-task generalization.

- **Advancing Cross-Task Generalization:** By moving beyond superficial trajectory imitation, this work enables a deeper level of skill reasoning. This is crucial for equipping robots to handle the inherent variability of open-world environments, where tasks are rarely identical to those previously encountered. The demonstrated ability to generalize to entirely unseen tasks without parameter updates signifies progress towards more versatile and autonomous robotic systems.
- **Introduction of Interpretable Skill Representations:** The construction and utilization of atomic skill–action pairs as intermediate representations provide LLMs with a structured and semantically rich understanding of manipulation sequences. This interpretability not only facilitates compositional reasoning but also offers a potential avenue for improved human oversight and debugging of robotic policies. The explicit alignment of high-level skills with low-level actions bridges the gap between abstract task semantics and concrete robot controls.
- **Robust and Comprehensive Demonstration Selection:** The dual-library retrieval strategy, which combines task-adaptive dynamic retrieval with coverage-aware static complementation, offers a robust mechanism for selecting high-quality in-context demonstrations. This ensures that the LLM is provided with examples that are both relevant to the immediate task and collectively cover a comprehensive range of necessary manipulation skills, thereby mitigating the risk of critical skill patterns being overlooked.
- **Leveraging Pre-trained Models for Training-Free Generalization:** The framework's ability to achieve zero-shot cross-task generalization using pre-trained visual encoders and large language models, without requiring specific training on new task distributions, is significant. This approach reduces the data and computational burden typically associated with training new policies for every novel task, making robot deployment more efficient and scalable.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose Robots:** This research contributes to the long-term goal of developing general-purpose robots capable of performing a wide array of manipulation tasks. By enabling robots to reason about and recompose existing skills to form new ones, the framework moves closer to robots that can adapt to novel situations, objects, and goal specifications, which is a key requirement for pervasive robotic assistance.
- **Guiding Future Research:** The real-world experiments, while demonstrating efficacy, also highlight remaining challenges such as robust 6-DoF pose estimation, secure grasping of varied objects, and precise 3D spatial reasoning. These identified limitations provide clear directions for future research to further enhance the reliability and capabilities of cross-task robotic manipulation systems.
