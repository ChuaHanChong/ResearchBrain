# Actions as Language: Fine-Tuning VLMs into VLAs Without Catastrophic Forgetting

> [!summary] Summary
> VLM2VLA is a framework from Princeton University that fine- tunes Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by recasting low-level robot actions as natural language descriptions. This method preserves the VLM's original multimodal understanding and world knowledge, enabling zero-shot generalization to novel robotic tasks without catastrophic forgetting.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) suffer from catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuned on narrow robot teleoperation data, losing their general-purpose world knowledge.
- Existing solutions like co-training are computationally expensive, require careful data mixing, and don't fully resolve the distribution mismatch between VLM pretraining and robotics data.
- Robots fine-tuned with current methods struggle to generalize to novel objects, linguistic variations, or open-world semantic concepts outside their specific training domain.

## Method

- The framework represents low-level robot actions as ==natural language descriptions==, transforming robot control into a standard text generation task within the VLM's native language domain.
- It employs a three-stage hierarchical action representation (high-level subtask, mid-level motion plan, low-level action chunk) all expressed in natural language.
- Parameter-efficient Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is used exclusively for fine-tuning a base VLM (Gemma-3-12B-IT) on automatically curated natural language action data, without requiring co-training with large VLM datasets.

## Results

- VLM2VLA retains over **85%** of the base VLM's performance across various VQA benchmarks, demonstrating successful preservation of multimodal reasoning capabilities.
- It achieves superior zero-shot generalization in robotic manipulation tasks requiring multilingual instruction following and open-world semantic reasoning, outperforming tokenization-based VLAs like OpenVLA and ECoT.
- The framework delivers competitive in-distribution robotic manipulation performance comparable to models trained on much larger datasets, while being significantly more computationally efficient by avoiding co-training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing robot actions as natural language resolves the distribution mismatch between internet-scale VLM pretraining and robotics fine-tuning data, enabling effective transfer learning.
> - Parameter-efficient fine-tuning with LoRA, when applied to language-aligned action data, successfully prevents catastrophic forgetting of the VLM's foundational multimodal understanding and world knowledge.
> - The hierarchical, language-based action reasoning enhances robotic generalization, allowing the VLM to leverage its preserved semantic understanding for out-of-distribution tasks.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of embodied AI, particularly in robotics, has seen significant advancements with the integration of Vision-Language Models (VLMs). The prevailing paradigm involves fine-tuning these internet-scale pretrained VLMs on robot demonstration data to create Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, which can map natural language commands and visual observations to robot actions. This approach holds immense promise for developing generalist robot policies capable of sophisticated perception, semantic understanding, and commonsense reasoning.

However, a critical and widespread challenge in this landscape is **catastrophic forgetting**. When VLMs are fine-tuned on the relatively narrow and domain-specific robot teleoperation data, they often lose a substantial portion of the rich, general-purpose world knowledge acquired during their internet-scale pretraining. This loss manifests as a diminished ability to generalize to novel objects, handle linguistic variations, reason about concepts outside the training data, or be robust to distractions.

Existing strategies to mitigate catastrophic forgetting include:
- **Co-training**: Mixing robot data with non-robotic VLM-scale datasets during fine-tuning. While effective, this is computationally expensive, requires careful tuning of data mixture ratios, and doesn't fundamentally resolve the distribution mismatch between the datasets.
- **Architectural modifications**: Augmenting VLMs with separate, lightweight action heads (e.g., using diffusion models) or discretizing continuous actions into a VLM's least likely tokens. These can introduce new, randomly initialized parameters that corrupt pretrained representations or create a mismatch in the VLM's latent space.
- **Advanced training schemes**: More complex methods like Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures, gradient shielding, or sequential training procedures.

This paper, VLM2VLA, positions itself as a novel and more efficient solution to this fundamental problem. It challenges the necessity of expensive co-training or complex architectural modifications. Instead, it proposes a **data-centric approach** that resolves the distribution mismatch *at the data level* by representing low-level actions as natural language. By aligning the fine-tuning data with the VLM's existing representational space, VLM2VLA enables parameter-efficient methods like Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) to adapt the VLM for robotic control effectively while minimally perturbing its pretrained weights. This places VLM2VLA as a significant contribution to developing generalist robot policies that are both capable and computationally efficient.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a method for fine-tuning Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for robotic control while effectively **preserving the VLM's foundational reasoning and multimodal understanding capabilities**, thereby averting catastrophic forgetting.

The motivations driving this objective are multifaceted:
- **Addressing Catastrophic Forgetting**: Current VLA training paradigms often lead to catastrophic forgetting, where the VLM's rich, general-purpose world knowledge is lost when adapted for specific robotic tasks. This severely limits a robot's ability to generalize to new scenarios, understand varied instructions, or interact with novel objects.
- **Improving Generalization to Novel Scenarios**: To be truly useful, robots need to operate in diverse, unstructured environments. This requires policies that can generalize beyond their immediate training data, interpreting new instructions, handling unseen objects, and reasoning about open-world semantics. Catastrophic forgetting directly undermines this goal.
- **Reducing Training Complexity and Cost**: Existing solutions like co-training, while mitigating forgetting, are computationally expensive and require significant effort to tune hyper-parameters and curate complex data mixtures. The authors are motivated to find a simpler, more efficient training paradigm that bypasses these hurdles.
- **Leveraging Foundational Models Effectively**: VLMs represent a vast repository of world knowledge. The motivation is to fully capitalize on this knowledge for robotics, ensuring that the transition to action generation does not compromise the VLM's core strengths in perception, language understanding, and commonsense reasoning.
- **Enabling Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning**: The authors observe that parameter-efficient methods like LoRA are excellent at preventing catastrophic forgetting, but their efficacy hinges on the fine-tuning data being close to the model’s pretrained representations. This observation directly motivates their approach to resolve the distributional mismatch at the data level.

In essence, the paper aims to provide a more robust, efficient, and generalizable pathway for creating robot policies by ensuring that the fine-tuning process for robotic control augments, rather than diminishes, the powerful capabilities of the underlying VLM.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

VLM2VLA introduces a data-centric paradigm and training methodology predicated on the principle of aligning robot data with the VLM's existing representational space to enable effective LoRA fine-tuning and prevent catastrophic forgetting.

**Core Concept: Actions as Language**
The central tenet is to represent low-level robot actions not as discrete tokens or continuous vectors, but as natural language descriptions. This transforms robot control into a standard supervised fine-tuning task within the VLM's native language domain, minimizing the distribution shift during training.

**Hierarchical Action Representation**
VLM2VLA frames action prediction as a three-stage hierarchical reasoning process, where each stage is expressed in natural language:

1. **High-Level Subtask Prediction (l_i)**: Given an observation ($\bar{o}_i$) and a main language instruction (L), the model first generates a natural language description of the immediate subtask needed to progress towards the main goal.
2. **Mid-Level Motion Planning (m_i)**: Conditioned on the current subtask ($l_i$) and observation ($\bar{o}_i$), the model generates a spatially informative motion plan. This plan describes directional end-effector movements (e.g., "move primarily downward and slightly right") leveraging the VLM's latent spatial reasoning.
3. **Low-Level Action Generation ($\bar{a}_i$)**: Conditioned on the subtask, motion plan, and observation, the policy generates a variable-length "action-chunk." This chunk is a list of lists, where each inner list contains text representations of individual commands for each degree of freedom (e.g., `[[-0.001, 0.001, -0.019, 1.0]...]`). The continuous action values are treated as standard text strings, allowing the VLM to use its intrinsic understanding of numerical magnitudes.

**Inference-Time Procedure:**
During inference, the model follows this hierarchical decomposition. All subtasks are generated at once given the initial observation and remain fixed. The system operates in a closed-loop with an external **verifier** (Gemini 2.5 Pro). After each action-generation cycle, the verifier assesses if the current subtask was successfully completed based on pre- and post-action observations and the current/next subtasks. This dictates whether the policy retries the current subtask or proceeds to the next.

**Data Curation Pipeline (VLM2VLA pipeline):**
To implement "Actions as Language," existing robot datasets (D_rob), specifically a subset of the Bridgev2 dataset of human teleoperated trajectories, are re-labeled into a natural language annotated dataset (D_lan).
- **Automated Annotation with Gemini 2.5**: A large multimodal model (Gemini 2.5) is used to automatically annotate trajectories. Gemini is provided with the full robot trajectory (RGB observations, raw actions), the main-task instruction, and the robot's coordinate system/sign convention.
- **Decomposition and Annotation**: Gemini decomposes each trajectory into a sequence of steps, generating natural language for subtasks ($l_i$), motion plans ($m_i$), and directly copying the corresponding raw action chunks ($\bar{a}_i$) which are then represented as text strings.
- **Action Chunking**: A post-processing step ensures that actions along a single dimension have sufficient magnitude, as the VLM initially provided negligible action predictions without this.
- **Auxiliary Data**: Additional data for positive/negative subtask completion examples and directional movement prediction (e.g., "move left") are generated to further aid policy training, although the verifier training part was not successfully implemented in this work.

**Training Details:**
- **Base Model**: Gemma-3-12B-IT, a prominent VLM.
- **Fine-tuning Method**: Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is exclusively used. LoRA layers are applied to all linear modules of the VLM (q_proj, k_proj, v_proj, o_proj, up_proj, down_proj, gate_proj) with rank (r)=16 and alpha ($\alpha$)=32.
- **Loss Function**: Cross-entropy loss, framing the problem as a standard text generation task.
- **No Co-training**: Crucially, VLM2VLA does not use co-training with internet-scale VLM datasets.
- **Computational Resources**: Training was conducted on a single node with 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs for one epoch, totaling approximately 300 GPU hours. The effective global batch size was kept small (8).

This methodology effectively transforms the VLM fine-tuning challenge from an architectural or data-mixing problem into a data representation problem, leveraging the VLM's inherent language capabilities for action grounding.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The extensive empirical validation of VLM2VLA, comprising Visual Question Answering (VQA) studies and over 800 real-world robotics experiments, demonstrates its effectiveness in preserving VLM capabilities and enabling generalization.

**Q1 – Multimodal Understanding: Preservation of VLM Capabilities**
- **Strong VQA Performance**: VLM2VLA successfully preserves the base VLM's multimodal reasoning ability, experiencing only minor losses (retaining over 85% of performance) across a wide range of VQA benchmarks (e.g., MMMU, MMStar, MME, OCRBench, TextVQA). This directly addresses the problem of catastrophic forgetting.
- **Comparison with Baselines**: In stark contrast, state-of-the-art tokenization-based VLAs like OpenVLA and ECoT suffer severe catastrophic forgetting, exhibiting significantly degraded VQA performance compared to their original VLM backbones (Prismatic VLM).
- **Competitiveness with Co-trained Models**: VLM2VLA (12B parameters) outperforms MolmoAct (7B), a co-trained VLA, across all reported VQA benchmarks, suggesting that its data-centric approach can be competitive with computationally more intensive co-training methods. The significantly lower performance of $\pi_{0.5}$, another co-trained VLA, further underscores that co-training is not a guaranteed solution.
- **LoRA's Role**: The ablation study with VLM2VLA-AT (actions as tokens) showed comparable VQA scores to VLM2VLA, indicating that LoRA is primarily responsible for mitigating catastrophic forgetting in multimodal understanding.

**Q2 – Robotic Manipulation: In-Distribution Performance**
- **Competitive Action Prediction**: VLM2VLA achieves competitive in-distribution manipulation performance. On simpler tasks like "Pick Up the Carrot" and "Put the Carrot On the Yellow Plate," it performs comparably to OpenVLA, which benefits from being fine-tuned on the much larger Open-X-Embodiment dataset.
- **Robustness in Complex Tasks**: As task complexity increases, VLM2VLA's hierarchical reasoning provides an advantage. For the multi-step "Put the Eggplant In the Pan, Then Lift the Fish" task, VLM2VLA not only generates correct hierarchical reasoning but also executes these plans more frequently than OpenVLA (which often fails on the second subtask) and ECoT (which plans but executes less accurately).

**Q3 – Reasoning Generalization: Out-of-Distribution Performance**
- **Zero-Shot Multilingual Instruction Following**: In the "Pick Up the Carrot - T" task, where instructions were given in Spanish, Mandarin, or Hindi, VLM2VLA significantly outperforms OpenVLA and ECoT. It successfully translates non-English instructions and identifies the correct objects, demonstrating a direct application of its retained multilingual capabilities. ECoT showed some success but was suspected to rely on heuristics rather than genuine understanding.
- **Open-World Semantic Reasoning**: For the "Pick Up the Item Above Ash Ketchum" task, which requires recognizing a pop-culture figure and performing spatial reasoning, VLM2VLA was the only model to achieve a meaningful success rate. This finding strongly validates that the preserved foundational world knowledge enables zero-shot generalization to tasks requiring semantic concepts unseen during robotic training.

**Ablation Study: Importance of "Actions as Language"**
- **VLM2VLA vs. VLM2VLA-AT**: While VLM2VLA-AT (actions as tokens) shows comparable VQA performance, its robotic manipulation performance, especially in OOD scenarios, is consistently worse than VLM2VLA. This demonstrates a crucial disconnect: simply mitigating catastrophic forgetting through LoRA is not sufficient. The "actions as language" representation is essential for effectively grounding the VLM's latent world knowledge into actionable robot control, translating preserved general intelligence into superior generalization in robotic tasks.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The VLM2VLA framework represents a significant advancement in the field of embodied AI and robotic learning, offering a novel and efficient pathway towards building truly generalist robot policies.

**Overcoming Catastrophic Forgetting**: The primary significance of VLM2VLA lies in its elegant solution to catastrophic forgetting. By reframing low-level actions as natural language, it resolves the fundamental distribution mismatch between internet-scale pretraining data and robotics fine-tuning data. This enables parameter-efficient methods like LoRA to effectively adapt VLMs for control without sacrificing their vast world knowledge, a persistent bottleneck in VLA development.

**Enhanced Generalization and Robustness**: The preserved multimodal understanding and semantic reasoning capabilities directly translate to superior generalization. VLM2VLA demonstrates remarkable zero-shot generalization to novel tasks that demand open-world semantic reasoning (e.g., recognizing "Ash Ketchum") and multilingual instruction following. This ability to interpret and execute commands in unseen linguistic contexts and interact with previously unencountered semantic concepts is crucial for deploying robots in dynamic, real-world environments.

**Efficiency and Accessibility**: Unlike methods that rely on expensive co-training or complex architectural modifications, VLM2VLA utilizes a simple, data-centric approach combined with LoRA. This significantly reduces computational costs and training complexity, making the development of capable VLA models more accessible to researchers and practitioners with limited resources. The model-agnostic nature of representing "actions as language" further enhances its practical applicability across different VLM backbones.

**A Step Towards Truly Generalist Policies**: By closing the gap between the high-level reasoning of foundation models and the low-level control of robots, VLM2VLA offers a promising blueprint for developing generalist agents. Robots trained with this paradigm are better equipped to understand diverse human commands, adapt to novel situations, and reason about their actions in a human-interpretable manner.

**Potential Impact**:
- **Broader Adoption of VLAs**: The reduced complexity and cost could accelerate the adoption and development of VLA models across academic and industrial robotics.
- **More Capable and Adaptable Robots**: Robots could be deployed in more varied settings, requiring less re-training for new tasks or environments, leading to more flexible automation.
- **Human-Robot Interaction**: The inherent language-based reasoning could facilitate more intuitive and natural human-robot interaction, as robots would possess a deeper semantic understanding of instructions.

**Limitations and Future Directions**:
The paper candidly acknowledges several limitations, which also define clear avenues for future research:
- **Inference Latency**: Autoregressive action generation is inherently slow. Future work will need to explore faster decoding techniques to achieve real-time performance critical for many robotic applications.
- **Dexterous Tasks**: The current focus on translational end-effector control limits applicability to dexterous manipulation tasks requiring nuanced rotational control. Future work involves extending language annotations to cover finer-grained movements and full 6-DoF control.
- **Cross-Embodiment Learning**: The current policy is tied to a specific robot embodiment. However, the "actions as language" concept holds promise for cross-embodiment learning, where language could serve as a universal medium for describing actions across different robot platforms.
- **Verifier Dependence**: The reliance on an external verifier (Gemini) adds latency and complexity. Integrating the verifier function into the base VLM or eliminating it through improved intrinsic reasoning is a desirable future goal.
- **Larger-Scale Training**: Scaling the training pipeline with larger robotics datasets is hypothesized to unlock even greater zero-shot generalization capabilities.

Overall, VLM2VLA presents a compelling and practical approach to a foundational challenge in embodied AI, paving the way for more intelligent, adaptable, and generalist robotic systems.
