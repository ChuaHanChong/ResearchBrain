# Actions as Language: Fine-Tuning VLMs into VLAs Without Catastrophic Forgetting

> [!summary] Summary
> VLM2VLA is a framework from Princeton University that fine- tunes Vision-Language Models (VLMs) into Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models by recasting low-level robot actions as natural language descriptions. This method preserves the VLM's original multimodal understanding and world knowledge, enabling zero-shot generalization to novel robotic tasks without catastrophic forgetting.

## Problem

- Vision-Language Models (VLMs) suffer from catastrophic forgetting when fine-tuned on narrow robot teleoperation data, losing their general-purpose world knowledge.
- Existing solutions like co-training are computationally expensive, require careful data mixing, and don't fully resolve the distribution mismatch between VLM pretraining and robotics data.
- Robots fine-tuned with current methods struggle to generalize to novel objects, linguistic variations, or open-world semantic concepts outside their specific training domain.

## Method

- The framework represents low-level robot actions as ==natural language descriptions==, transforming robot control into a standard text generation task within the VLM's native language domain.
- It employs a three-stage hierarchical action representation (high-level subtask, mid-level motion plan, low-level action chunk) all expressed in natural language.
- Parameter-efficient Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) is used exclusively for fine-tuning a base VLM (Gemma-3-12B-IT) on automatically curated natural language action data, without requiring co-training with large VLM datasets.

## Results

- VLM2VLA retains over **85%** of the base VLM's performance across various VQA benchmarks, demonstrating successful preservation of multimodal reasoning capabilities.
- It achieves superior zero-shot generalization in robotic manipulation tasks requiring multilingual instruction following and open-world semantic reasoning, outperforming tokenization-based VLAs like OpenVLA and ECoT.
- The framework delivers competitive in-distribution robotic manipulation performance comparable to models trained on much larger datasets, while being significantly more computationally efficient by avoiding co-training.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Representing robot actions as natural language resolves the distribution mismatch between internet-scale VLM pretraining and robotics fine-tuning data, enabling effective transfer learning.
> - Parameter-efficient fine-tuning with LoRA, when applied to language-aligned action data, successfully prevents catastrophic forgetting of the VLM's foundational multimodal understanding and world knowledge.
> - The hierarchical, language-based action reasoning enhances robotic generalization, allowing the VLM to leverage its preserved semantic understanding for out-of-distribution tasks.
