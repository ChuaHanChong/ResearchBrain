# Vid2Sim: Generalizable, Video-based Reconstruction of Appearance, Geometry and Physics for Mesh-free Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Vid2Sim reconstructs appearance, geometry, and physical properties from multi-view videos for mesh-free simulation, offering a generalizable and efficient two-stage framework. The system achieves high-fidelity dynamic reconstruction and significantly reduces optimization time, enabling robust future state prediction.

## Problem

- Existing physics-aware dynamic 3D reconstruction methods lack generalizability and incur high computational costs due to heavy per-scene optimization.
- Prior approaches, especially those using Material Point Methods (MPM) or simple mass-spring models, struggle with limitations in modeling complex deformable object dynamics and can suffer from instability.
- The challenge of generating simulation-ready assets with accurate physical properties from video observations remains computationally intensive and lacks efficiency.

## Method

- **Vid2Sim** employs a two-stage pipeline: a feed-forward network provides generalizable initial estimates of physical properties (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio) and Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) weights using pre-trained models (LGM, **VideoMAE**).
- Scene-specific refinement uses 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== for geometry/appearance and a mesh-free, reduced-order simulation based on LBS with an implicit Euler solver for dynamics.
- A novel Neural Jacobian module is introduced to predict deformation gradients, significantly accelerating the differentiable simulation and refinement process without explicit Jacobian computation.

## Results

- Vid2Sim achieves superior dynamic reconstruction quality on synthetic data, outperforming state-of-the-art methods with an average PSNR of 30.17 compared to PAC-NeRF's 22.06.
- The framework significantly reduces total optimization time, completing scene-specific refinement in approximately 15 minutes per scene, a substantial improvement over baselines that take 54-120 minutes.
- It demonstrates effective future state prediction, maintaining higher accuracy (average PSNR of 25.07 for Vid2Sim vs. 20.11 for PAC-NeRF) for unobserved frames, indicating a more accurate underlying physical model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining generalizable feed-forward initial prediction with scene-specific refinement effectively overcomes the limitations of per-scene optimization, leading to higher efficiency and robustness.
> - A mesh-free, reduced-order simulation based on LBS and an implicit Euler solver provides a more stable, accurate, and expressive method for modeling complex deformations compared to grid-based or simpler models.
> - The Neural Jacobian module drastically speeds up differentiable simulation by predicting Jacobian values, making the entire optimization process more computationally efficient.
