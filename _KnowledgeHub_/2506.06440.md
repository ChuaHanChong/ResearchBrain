# Vid2Sim: Generalizable, Video-based Reconstruction of Appearance, Geometry and Physics for Mesh-free Simulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Vid2Sim reconstructs appearance, geometry, and physical properties from multi-view videos for mesh-free simulation, offering a generalizable and efficient two-stage framework. The system achieves high-fidelity dynamic reconstruction and significantly reduces optimization time, enabling robust future state prediction.

## Problem

- Existing physics-aware dynamic 3D reconstruction methods lack generalizability and incur high computational costs due to heavy per-scene optimization.
- Prior approaches, especially those using Material Point Methods (MPM) or simple mass-spring models, struggle with limitations in modeling complex deformable object dynamics and can suffer from instability.
- The challenge of generating simulation-ready assets with accurate physical properties from video observations remains computationally intensive and lacks efficiency.

## Method

- **Vid2Sim** employs a two-stage pipeline: a feed-forward network provides generalizable initial estimates of physical properties (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio) and Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) weights using pre-trained models (LGM, **VideoMAE**).
- Scene-specific refinement uses 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== for geometry/appearance and a mesh-free, reduced-order simulation based on LBS with an implicit Euler solver for dynamics.
- A novel Neural Jacobian module is introduced to predict deformation gradients, significantly accelerating the differentiable simulation and refinement process without explicit Jacobian computation.

## Results

- Vid2Sim achieves superior dynamic reconstruction quality on synthetic data, outperforming state-of-the-art methods with an average PSNR of 30.17 compared to PAC-NeRF's 22.06.
- The framework significantly reduces total optimization time, completing scene-specific refinement in approximately 15 minutes per scene, a substantial improvement over baselines that take 54-120 minutes.
- It demonstrates effective future state prediction, maintaining higher accuracy (average PSNR of 25.07 for Vid2Sim vs. 20.11 for PAC-NeRF) for unobserved frames, indicating a more accurate underlying physical model.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining generalizable feed-forward initial prediction with scene-specific refinement effectively overcomes the limitations of per-scene optimization, leading to higher efficiency and robustness.
> - A mesh-free, reduced-order simulation based on LBS and an implicit Euler solver provides a more stable, accurate, and expressive method for modeling complex deformations compared to grid-based or simpler models.
> - The Neural Jacobian module drastically speeds up differentiable simulation by predicting Jacobian values, making the entire optimization process more computationally efficient.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

This work is positioned within the challenging and evolving field of **system identification** in computer vision and graphics, specifically focusing on **physics-aware dynamic 3D reconstruction** from video observations. The goal is to reconstruct not only the visual appearance and geometry of deformable objects but also their underlying physical properties, enabling realistic simulation.

Historically, system identification relied on known object shapes, limiting practicality. Recent advancements have leveraged neural representations like Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian Splatting in conjunction with differentiable simulators (e.g., Material Point Methods or MPM) to create unified frameworks for joint learning of geometry, appearance, and physical parameters. Papers like PAC-NeRF, GIC, and Spring-Gaus represent this wave of research.

However, the authors identify two significant limitations in these existing physics-aware dynamic 3D reconstruction methods:
1. **Lack of Generalizability and High Computational Cost:** Most prior methods rely on "heavy per-scene optimization" for identifying physical parameters. This process is computationally expensive, often requires extensive hyperparameter tuning for each scene, and therefore limits their practical applicability and generalizability to diverse scenarios.
2. **Limitations in Modeling Complex Deformations:** Existing approaches, particularly those using MPM, are constrained by their grid-based representation and often depend on symplectic time integration, which can lead to oscillations, instability, and limited expressiveness for complex, physics-driven deformations. While mass-spring models (like in Spring-Gaus) offer efficiency, they are typically limited to modeling elastic dynamics.

Vid2Sim addresses these gaps by proposing a novel, generalizable, and efficient framework. It moves beyond per-scene optimization by incorporating a **feed-forward neural network trained with physical world knowledge** to provide a strong initial estimate. Crucially, it adopts a **mesh-free, reduced-order simulation** technique based on Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) with an implicit Euler solver, inspired by Simplicits, which offers superior computational efficiency and adaptability to complex deformations compared to traditional MPM or simpler mass-spring models. By combining a generalized initial prediction with a lightweight, scene-specific refinement, Vid2Sim aims to achieve state-of-the-art performance in both accuracy and speed, pushing the boundaries of what is possible in vision-based physical simulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of Vid2Sim are to:

1. **Achieve high-fidelity reconstruction:** Reconstruct textured shapes, dynamic geometry, and physical properties (e.g., Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio) from multi-view videos with high accuracy.
2. **Ensure generalizability:** Develop a framework that can generalize across different scenes and objects without requiring extensive per-scene tuning or costly optimization pipelines.
3. **Enhance computational efficiency:** Significantly reduce the time required for system identification and subsequent simulation compared to existing methods.
4. **Enable mesh-free simulation:** Utilize a mesh-free, reduced-order simulation approach that is highly efficient, flexible, and capable of modeling complex deformations.
5. **Produce simulation-ready outputs:** Generate reconstructed assets (geometry, appearance, and physical properties) that are directly usable for high-quality and visually appealing physics-based animations.

The motivation stems directly from the limitations of current physics-aware dynamic 3D reconstruction techniques. The authors seek to overcome the prohibitively high computational cost and lack of generalizability associated with heavy per-scene optimization in existing methods, and to improve the accuracy and stability of physics-driven deformation modeling by moving away from constrained simulation paradigms like grid-based MPM to more flexible and efficient reduced-order approaches.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

Vid2Sim employs a two-stage pipeline to achieve its objectives, integrating advanced neural networks for initial prediction with an efficient optimization framework.

**Stage I: Feed-forward Physical System Identification**
This stage aims to provide a generalizable and efficient initial estimation of the physical system's configuration.

- **Geometry and Appearance Reconstruction:** The first multi-view frames of the input videos are processed by a pre-trained Large Multi-view Gaussian Model (LGM). This model leverages generalizable knowledge from large-scale 3D datasets to efficiently reconstruct the object's geometry and appearance into 3D Gaussians, which are then normalized into a canonical space.
- **Physical Property Estimation:** A feed-forward predictor, built upon a pre-trained Video Vision Transformer backbone (VideoMAE), takes a single front-view video as input. It utilizes several small Multilayer Perceptrons (MLPs) as regression heads to estimate two key elastic material parameters: Young's modulus (E) and Poisson's ratio (ν).
- **Linear Blend Skinning (LBS) Weight Prediction:** To enable the mesh-free, reduced-order simulation, the network also needs to predict the LBS weights, W$_{θ;j}$(X$_i$), which define how points deform under the influence of control handles. Instead of directly predicting these weights (which are implicitly modeled by an MLP in the simulation framework), Vid2Sim introduces a **HyperNetwork** approach. This HyperNetwork, also a small MLP, predicts the weights and biases of the *final linear layer* of the LBS network, while keeping other layers fixed. This design enhances generalizability and robustness.

The output of Stage I is a simulation-ready prediction, encompassing 3D Gaussians for appearance and geometry, along with initial estimates for physical properties (E, ν) and LBS network parameters.

**Stage II: Scene-specific Refinement**
This stage refines the general estimations from Stage I to precisely match the input multi-view videos, optimizing for accuracy and efficiency.

- **3D Gaussian Refinement:** The 3D Gaussians (geometry and appearance) are refined using standard 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) training techniques to improve alignment with video observations.
- **Mesh-Free, Reduced-Order Simulation:** The core simulation relies on a method inspired by Simplicits. It uses a set of handles (full affine transformations) to deform object points via **Linear Blend Skinning (LBS)**. The dynamics of these handles are governed by an **implicit Euler solver**, solving an incremental potential equation that includes inertia and potential energy terms. This approach is computationally efficient as it operates on a reduced dimension (few handles compared to many points) and avoids meshes.
- **Neural Jacobian Module:** A key innovation for efficiency during refinement is the **Neural Jacobian module**. Instead of explicitly computing the Jacobian of the deformation gradient with respect to transformations (which becomes computationally expensive, especially when LBS weights are also optimized), a neural network is trained to predict this Jacobian, J$_{θ}$(X). This module is trained in a data-free manner to minimize the difference between its output and a deformation gradient estimated via finite differences, significantly accelerating the refinement process.
- **Joint Optimization:** The physical parameters (E, ν), the LBS estimation model, and the corresponding Neural Jacobian are jointly optimized. This optimization is supervised by a **rendering loss**, which minimizes the L2 difference between the predicted rendered video sequence from the simulation and the ground truth multi-view video observations. To further improve efficiency, the rendering loss is computed over small, randomly sampled sequences of frames.

The framework primarily focuses on Neo-Hookean elastic materials but is designed to be extensible to various physical phenomena and material types.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

Vid2Sim demonstrates superior performance across various metrics and tasks, highlighting its accuracy, efficiency, and generalizability.

- **Superior Dynamic Reconstruction Quality:**
 - **Quantitative Results (Synthetic Dataset):** Vid2Sim significantly outperforms state-of-the-art methods like PAC-NeRF, Spring-Gaus, and GIC across all metrics (PSNR, SSIM, FoVVDP) for dynamic reconstruction on a diverse synthetic dataset of 12 objects (e.g., average PSNR of 30.17 for Vid2Sim vs. 22.06 for PAC-NeRF). The improvement is substantial, with Vid2Sim achieving much higher scores.
 - **Qualitative Results (Synthetic Dataset):** Visual comparisons show Vid2Sim producing higher-quality, less blurry textures and more realistic physical dynamics compared to baselines. Existing methods often struggle with high-dimensional appearance optimization and introduce oscillations due to symplectic solvers.
 - **Real-world Dataset Evaluation:** Vid2Sim also shows enhanced capability on real-world objects (orange, bird, cup) from sparse viewpoints, particularly in future state prediction, where it maintains better accuracy than Spring-Gaus.

- **Exceptional Efficiency:**
 - **Total Training Time:** Vid2Sim significantly reduces the total optimization time, completing the scene-specific refinement in approximately **15 minutes** (on an NVIDIA-RTX-4090 GPU) compared to 54-120 minutes for baselines like Spring-Gaus, PAC-NeRF, and GIC.
 - **Per Iteration Time:** The per-iteration optimization time for Vid2Sim is drastically lower (1.44s with Neural Jacobian vs. 8.08s for Spring-Gaus), further emphasizing its efficiency.
 - **Neural Jacobian Impact:** The Neural Jacobian module plays a crucial role in this speedup, especially with more cubature points and handles, making precomputation faster.

- **Effective Future State Prediction:** Vid2Sim maintains better accuracy in predicting future states (8 frames after 16 observed frames) compared to all baselines (average PSNR of 25.07 for Vid2Sim vs. 20.11 for PAC-NeRF), indicating a more accurate underlying physical model.

- **Comprehensive Ablation Studies:**
 - The ablation studies confirm the importance of each component. While Stage I's physical property prediction is effective for simulation, the refinement of 3D Gaussians (geometry and appearance) is crucial for overall dynamic reconstruction quality.
 - Using LGM initialization for 3D Gaussians (Stage I+refine GS) is better than training from scratch (Stage I+fit GS).
 - The full model, including LBS optimization, achieves the best results.
 - A reliable initialization from the feed-forward Stage I is crucial for convergence; purely optimizing with random physics initialization performs worse.

- **Accurate Physical Parameter Estimation:** Vid2Sim demonstrates superior accuracy in estimating physical parameters (log(E) and ν), achieving lower Mean Absolute Error (MAE) compared to PAC-NeRF and GIC across most test cases.

- **Generalization Capability (Supplementary Material):**
 - **Different Materials:** The framework can be generalized to materials with various constitutive models beyond Neo-Hookean elasticity, including Plasticine (using StVK and von Mises return mapping) and Sand (using StVK and Drucker-Prager yield criteria), demonstrating its versatility.
 - **Complex Boundary Conditions:** The reconstructed assets and physical properties from Vid2Sim can be seamlessly integrated into simulations with complex boundary conditions (e.g., object sliding on a moving floor, blocks dropping on balls), producing high-quality animations.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

Vid2Sim represents a significant advancement in the field of physics-aware dynamic 3D reconstruction and simulation. Its key contributions and potential impacts are:

- **Breaking the Generalizability Barrier:** By introducing a generalizable feed-forward model to provide initial estimates, Vid2Sim largely mitigates the common problem of costly per-scene optimization that plagues previous methods. This is a crucial step towards making physics-aware reconstruction practical for real-world applications with diverse objects and scenes.
- **Unprecedented Efficiency:** The drastic reduction in reconstruction and simulation time (from hours to minutes) makes complex physics-based system identification more accessible and usable for rapid prototyping, interactive applications, and large-scale data processing. The Neural Jacobian module is a particularly clever innovation for accelerating differentiable simulation.
- **Robust and Accurate Physics Modeling:** The adoption of a mesh-free, reduced-order simulation based on LBS and an implicit Euler solver provides a more stable, accurate, and expressive way to model complex deformations compared to grid-based or simpler mass-spring approaches. This leads to higher fidelity in both dynamic reconstruction and future state prediction.
- **Enabling New Applications:** The ability to efficiently reconstruct simulation-ready assets, complete with appearance, geometry, and physical properties, opens doors for numerous applications:
 - **Robotics:** More realistic simulations for robot manipulation tasks involving deformable objects.
 - **Augmented/Virtual Reality (AR/VR):** Creating highly realistic and interactive virtual objects that behave physically correctly.
 - **Content Creation:** Accelerating the creation of physically accurate animations and digital twins for games, movies, and industrial design.
 - **Scientific Research:** Providing a robust tool for studying material properties from visual observations.
- **Foundation for Future Research:** The framework's modularity (separation of general prediction and scene-specific refinement) and extensibility to different materials and boundary conditions lay a strong foundation for future research into even more complex physical phenomena (e.g., fluids, fracturing) and further integration of large-scale visual models. The identified limitation of reconstructing complex materials like fluids and the suggestion to merge Stage I branches into a unified feed-forward network are clear paths for continued innovation.

In conclusion, Vid2Sim addresses critical challenges in dynamic 3D reconstruction by offering a highly efficient, accurate, and generalizable solution, marking a substantial step forward in the quest to bridge the gap between visual perception and physical simulation.
