# Emergency Stopping for Liquid-manipulating Robots

> [!summary] Summary
> This research introduces a real-time emergency stopping system for robots manipulating open liquid containers, utilizing a Model Predictive Control framework to compute time-optimal, spill-free trajectories. The system achieved a 0.87-second stop with a maximum slosh of 5.3 degrees in physical robot experiments, effectively preventing spills within predefined limits while maintaining reactive control.

## Problem

- Existing liquid manipulation methods primarily focus on planned, slosh-free trajectories and lack real-time reactive capabilities for unexpected emergency stops.
- Uncoordinated emergency stops by liquid-carrying robots can cause severe spills, leading to potential safety hazards (e.g., injury from hot liquids, chemical contamination) or equipment damage.
- Current emergency stopping systems for robots typically consider empty manipulators or are limited to simpler 1D motions, inadequately addressing the complex 6D motions and 2 DoF liquid slosh dynamics of a multi-DoF robot.

## Method

- Formulates the emergency stopping problem as an optimal control problem, continuously solved within a ==Model Predictive Control== (MPC) framework to generate real-time, spill-free trajectories.
- Employs a computationally efficient, linearized spherical pendulum model to account for liquid slosh dynamics, specifically retaining bilinear coupling terms for improved accuracy under vertical accelerations.
- Integrates the linearized liquid model with robot kinematics into a Quadratic Program (QP) that minimizes stopping time (cumulative Cartesian velocity) while adhering to no-spill constraints on liquid tilt and robot kinematic limits.

## Results

- The proposed method achieved a stopping time of **0.87** seconds with a maximum measured slosh of **5.3** degrees in real-world robot experiments, successfully keeping the liquid within a specified 5-degree limit.
- Compared to a baseline without slosh modeling, which stopped in **0.73** seconds but caused a significant **22.3**-degree slosh, the system effectively mitigated spills while maintaining controlled deceleration.
- Simulation and real-world robustness tests to modeling errors (misspecified rod length) demonstrated a moderate sim-to-real gap, with real-world errors leading to higher slosh violations (e.g., **8.1** degrees vs. **5.5** degrees for **+50%** error).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Combining a carefully linearized spherical pendulum model with an MPC-based QP formulation allows for efficient, real-time optimization of emergency stopping trajectories that prevent liquid spillage.
> - The method serves as a 'plug-and-play' safety layer, enabling robust emergency reactions for liquid-manipulating robots without requiring extensive redesign of existing motion planning systems.
> - Prioritizing spill avoidance through appropriately weighted slack variables in the objective function is crucial for ensuring liquid containment during rapid deceleration, even at the cost of slightly longer stopping times.
