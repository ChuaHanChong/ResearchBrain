# ACT-Bench: Towards Action Controllable World Models for Autonomous Driving

> [!summary] Summary
> ACT-BENCH is open evaluation framework for action fidelity in driving world models: pairs nuScenes context video with trajectory instructions, uses learned ACT-ESTIMATOR to measure instruction-execution match and trajectory error. Baseline model TERRA (GAIA-1-style, frame-level trajectory conditioning) beats Vista, 44.11% vs 30.72% instruction match, both still far from reliable.

## Problem

- Prior action-fidelity evaluation for driving world models (GenAD, Vista) relies on closed-source visual-odometry estimators, blocking reproducibility and open comparison.
- Open-access baseline world models are scarce: GAIA-1 shows strong action fidelity design but only a technical report, no released model or weights.
- Existing metrics (FID, FVD, CLIPSIM, downstream detection) assess visual realism or task performance but not whether generated scenes actually obey given action instructions.

## Method

- **ACT-BENCH** dataset: 2,286 nuScenes front-camera video-trajectory pairs across nine driving-maneuver categories, filtered by speed-deviation and object-interaction criteria.
- **ACT-ESTIMATOR**: ==I3D==-backbone, ==self-attention==, dual-head model jointly trained with ==cross-entropy== plus ==smooth-L1 loss== to classify high-level action and autoregressively predict 2D trajectory via ==GRU with cross-attention==.
- **TERRA**: ==GAIA-1==-style ==autoregressive transformer== world model trained on OpenDV-YouTube, nuScenes and CoVLA, supporting per-frame trajectory conditioning unlike Vista's per-round conditioning.

## Results

- ACT-ESTIMATOR reaches **94.03%** high-level action classification accuracy and outperforms DROID-SLAM baseline, average ADE **0.81** versus **7.52**, FDE **1.59** versus **13.75**.
- Instruction-execution consistency: Vista scores **30.72%** match rate, TERRA scores **44.11%**, both leaving large adherence gaps especially on curving actions.
- Trajectory alignment: TERRA achieves lower average ADE (**3.98** versus **4.50**) and FDE (**8.21** versus **8.66**) than Vista across most action categories.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Visual realism metrics like FID/FVD say nothing about whether a world model actually follows conditioning instructions; action fidelity needs its own dedicated benchmark.
> - Conditioning frequency matters: injecting trajectory instructions at every frame instead of once per generation round measurably improves action adherence.
> - Even improved models show causal misalignment, where ego-vehicle instructions leak into other agents' behavior, exposing a physical-consistency failure mode beyond fidelity scores.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The field of autonomous driving has seen rapid advancements, with world models emerging as powerful "neural simulators" to address critical challenges. These models aim to generate synthetic environments and scenarios, supplementing scarce and often dangerous real-world data, and enabling rigorous closed-loop evaluations of autonomous systems. Historically, early world models for autonomous driving were explored as policy models, but the complexity of modeling shifted focus towards their use as data generators and policy evaluators.

However, the authors identify a significant gap: while existing world models are often evaluated on visual realism (e.g., FID, FVD) or performance in downstream tasks (e.g., 3D object detection, planning), there has been limited focus on their **fidelity to specific action instructions**. This "action fidelity" is crucial for generating targeted simulation scenes, especially for safety-critical situations or for testing specific driving maneuvers. Previous attempts to address action fidelity, such as Vista and GenAD, either rely on closed-source evaluation mechanisms or lack publicly accessible baseline models, hindering reproducibility and broader research efforts. GAIA-1, another notable world model, only has a technical report with limited public resources.

"ACT-BENCH" directly tackles this reproducibility and evaluation gap. It positions itself as a foundational contribution by providing:
1. **An open-access, standardized evaluation framework** specifically designed to quantify action fidelity.
2. **A large-scale, open dataset** annotated with high-level actions and corresponding trajectories, built upon the widely-used nuScenes dataset.
3. **An open-source baseline world model (TERRA)** that follows a similar philosophy to state-of-the-art models like GAIA-1 but is publicly accessible and trained on extensive trajectory-annotated data.
4. **A transparent and reproducible evaluation mechanism (ACT-ESTIMATOR)** for automatically assessing action execution.

By establishing these open components, Turing Inc. aims to "foster innovation, support standardized evaluations, and promote reproducible studies in driving world models," thereby accelerating the development of more reliable and controllable autonomous driving systems. This work shifts the focus from purely visual quality or general task performance to the crucial aspect of precise, instruction-conditioned scene generation, which is vital for robust simulation and testing in autonomous driving.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objectives of this research are clearly articulated and motivated by the identified gaps in the existing literature:

1. **To develop an open-access evaluation framework, ACT-BENCH, for quantifying action fidelity in driving world models.** The motivation stems from the critical need for world models to generate scenes that faithfully adhere to specific action instructions, a capability largely overlooked or insufficiently evaluated in prior work. Current evaluations focus heavily on visual realism or downstream task performance, leaving a crucial gap in assessing how accurately a model executes a given driving command.
2. **To construct a large-scale, annotated dataset for action fidelity evaluation.** The authors recognize that current datasets or evaluation methods for action fidelity are often closed-source, limiting reproducibility and community-wide progress. Thus, a key objective is to provide a public dataset, based on nuScenes, enriched with high-level action and trajectory annotations to enable systematic and reproducible assessment.
3. **To introduce a systematic evaluator model, ACT-ESTIMATOR, for objective scoring.** Manually evaluating action fidelity is impractical. The motivation is to create an automated, reliable tool that can estimate actions and trajectories from generated videos, allowing for quantitative comparison against ground truth instructions, thereby overcoming the limitations of closed-source evaluation mechanisms used by prior works like Vista and GenAD.
4. **To propose TERRA, an open-access baseline world model designed for flexible trajectory control.** Recognizing the scarcity of public, high-fidelity driving world models (e.g., GAIA-1's limited accessibility), the authors aim to provide a comparable, open-source model that can serve as a reference point for future research and allow researchers to build upon it. The motivation is to enhance action fidelity by training on multiple large-scale trajectory-annotated datasets.
5. **To empirically demonstrate the current state of action controllability in driving world models.** By benchmarking existing models (like Vista) and their own baseline (TERRA) using the proposed framework, the authors aim to reveal the strengths and weaknesses of current approaches in adhering to action instructions, thereby highlighting areas for significant improvement and guiding future research directions.

In essence, the overarching motivation is to fill the void in open, standardized, and reproducible evaluation for action-controllable driving world models, thereby accelerating research towards more reliable and adaptable autonomous driving systems capable of generating targeted, safety-critical scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The authors propose a comprehensive methodology comprising two main pillars: the ACT-BENCH evaluation suite and the TERRA baseline world model.

#### 3.1. ACT-BENCH: Action Controllability Test Benchmark

ACT-BENCH is designed as an open-access framework to systematically evaluate action fidelity. It consists of three key components:

- **Benchmark Dataset:**
 - **Source:** A subset of the validation split from the nuScenes dataset, focusing on CAM_FRONT camera footage.
 - **Structure:** Short video segments (10-frame context) are paired with one or more trajectory templates.
 - **Annotation:** High-level actions (e.g., "curving to right," "stopping," "accelerating") and corresponding future trajectory data are annotated. Nine distinct driving maneuver categories are defined, covering a broad spectrum of actions with varying curvature and speed.
 - **Scale:** The dataset consists of 2,286 video-trajectory pairs after rigorous filtering (removing pairs with inconsistent initial speeds or potentially occluded interactions).
 - **Frame-level Trajectories:** Trajectory templates are prepared for each frame in the sequence to support world models requiring per-frame action inputs, leveraging data from the CoVLA dataset for diverse non-linear actions.

- **Benchmark Metrics:**
 - **Instruction-Execution Consistency (IEC):** Quantifies the agreement between the intended high-level action (`a_ins`) provided as input and the estimated action (`a_est`) derived from the generated scenes. It is a simple match rate (indicator function sum / total samples).
 - **Trajectory Alignment (TA):** Measures the spatial closeness between the intended trajectory (`τ_ins`) and the estimated trajectory (`τ_est`) from the generated video. Common metrics like Average Displacement Error (ADE) and Final Displacement Error (FDE) are used to quantify this. Lower values indicate better alignment.

- **ACT-ESTIMATOR:**
 - **Purpose:** An automated motion estimator to objectively score the alignment of generated scenes with specified instructions by classifying high-level actions and predicting vehicle trajectories from video frames.
 - **Training Data:** Constructed independently from the ACT-BENCH dataset, also from nuScenes. It includes four-second frame sequences with corresponding ego-pose-derived trajectory data and rule-based high-level action labels (11 classes, 9 used for classification due to imbalance).
 - **Architecture:** A multi-task framework with an I3D CNN backbone for spatiotemporal feature extraction. These features are flattened and passed through self-attention layers.
 - **Classification Head:** An MLP predicts high-level action classes (e.g., "curving to left").
 - **Trajectory Prediction Head:** A GRU-based unit with cross-attention autoregressively predicts 2D trajectory coordinates (x, y) for each point in the path.
 - **Optimization:** Jointly optimized using a weighted sum of cross-entropy loss (for classification) and smooth L1 loss (for trajectory).

#### 3.2. TERRA: A Baseline World Model

TERRA is introduced as an open-access world model for flexible trajectory control, serving as a reference point for the benchmark.

- **Architecture:** Follows the design philosophy of GAIA-1 (autoregressive Transformer-based world model) but omits text conditioning and simplifies the video decoder to maintain open accessibility.
 - **Image Tokenization:** Uses a CNN-based encoder with Lookup-Free Quantization (specifically, pre-trained weights of Open-MAGVIT2) to convert image frames into sequences of discrete latent tokens.
 - **Action-Conditioning:** Accepts future vehicle trajectories (sequence of 3D points: x, y, time) as input at each time step, enabling precise control. Trajectories are processed through an Action Embedder.
 - **Interleaved Inputs:** During training and inference, discrete image tokens and trajectory embeddings are interleaved as input to the Transformer.
 - **Learnable Positional Embedding:** Employs decomposed temporal and spatial positional encodings.
 - **Training Objective:** Trained on a next-token prediction task, with loss computed only for image token sequences.
- **Training Data:** Trained on three large-scale, open datasets: OpenDV-YouTube, nuScenes, and CoVLA (a dataset specifically rich in trajectory annotations). This expanded dataset is intended to enhance action fidelity compared to models trained on fewer datasets (e.g., Vista).
- **Video Refiner:** To improve temporal consistency and resolution of decoded videos, a latent diffusion model (LDM) based Video Refiner is employed, fine-tuned from Stable Video Diffusion (SVD). It upscales coarse predictions and ensures smoother video output.

The comprehensive approach of ACT-BENCH, paired with the open-source TERRA model and its detailed training, aims to provide a robust and reproducible framework for advancing action-controllable world models in autonomous driving.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The research yields several key findings across the validation of the ACT-ESTIMATOR and the comparative evaluation of TERRA against Vista.

#### 4.1. ACT-ESTIMATOR Validation

The ACT-ESTIMATOR, crucial for the benchmark's objectivity, demonstrated strong performance:
- **High-level Action Classification:** Achieved an overall accuracy exceeding 94% on a held-out validation dataset. The confusion matrix (Figure 4) showed robust performance across all nine included action classes, indicating its reliability in correctly identifying intended actions from video sequences.
- **Vehicle Trajectory Estimation:** The model significantly outperformed DROID-SLAM (a well-established SLAM approach adapted for monocular input) on both Average Displacement Error (ADE) and Final Displacement Error (FDE) metrics. For instance, the average ADE for ACT-ESTIMATOR was 0.81 compared to DROID-SLAM's 7.52, and average FDE was 1.59 compared to 13.75 (Table 3). This indicates superior accuracy in reconstructing vehicle trajectories, making the ACT-ESTIMATOR well-suited for precise trajectory alignment evaluations. Qualitative examples further showed DROID-SLAM tending to overestimate trajectory length, while ACT-ESTIMATOR aligned closely with ground truth (Figure 5).

#### 4.2. Action Controllability Exploration (Vista vs. TERRA)

The primary evaluation focused on comparing Vista (a state-of-the-art model) and TERRA using the ACT-BENCH framework.

- **Instruction-Execution Consistency (IEC):**
 - Vista achieved a match rate of 30.72%, while TERRA achieved 44.11% (Figure 6).
 - This indicates that while TERRA showed improved consistency, both models still have significant room for improvement in faithfully executing conditioned high-level actions. The authors suggest that TERRA's ability to accept instruction injection at every frame (unlike Vista's per-round conditioning) might contribute to its better performance.
- **Trajectory Alignment (TA):**
 - TERRA generally outperformed Vista across most high-level action classes on both ADE and FDE metrics (Table 4). For example, TERRA's average ADE was 3.98 compared to Vista's 4.50, and average FDE was 8.21 compared to Vista's 8.66.
 - Qualitative trajectory scatter plots (Figure 7) visually confirmed TERRA's superior ability to follow curving actions, producing trajectories closer to the intended curvature compared to Vista, which often generated straighter paths. Despite some instances of TERRA showing slightly higher ADE for specific curving actions, the overall visual adherence to the instruction was stronger. TERRA's frame-level conditioning was highlighted as enabling more responsive and accurate directional adherence, although maintaining consistent travel distance remained a challenge.

#### 4.3. Noteworthy Findings (Qualitative Observations)

Beyond quantitative metrics, the analysis revealed important qualitative observations:

- **Abrupt Motion Changes:** Vista, due to its round-based action conditioning, exhibited abrupt and unnatural motion changes at generation round transitions (Figure 8). This suggests a lack of temporal consistency when instructions are updated intermittently.
- **Causal Misalignment:** Both Vista and TERRA showed instances of "Causal Misalignment." This phenomenon occurs when ego-vehicle actions inadvertently influence other agents in the scene in an unnatural way. For example, when the ego-vehicle was instructed to stop, leading or oncoming cars also unexpectedly stopped (Figure 9 for TERRA, Figure 14 for Vista examples). This deviation from real-world physics poses a significant challenge for using world models as reliable simulators.
- **Crash Scene Generation:** TERRA's high level of controllability enabled the deliberate generation of crash scenes (Figure 10). This capability is significant as it demonstrates the potential of world models to produce difficult-to-collect, safety-critical scenarios that are invaluable for training and testing autonomous driving systems.

In summary, the findings validate the ACT-BENCH framework, establish TERRA as an improved baseline for action fidelity, and critically assess the limitations of current world models, pointing toward future research directions.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

The ACT-BENCH research paper makes several significant contributions with the potential for substantial impact on the field of autonomous driving and world model development:

1. **Standardization and Reproducibility:** The most critical impact is the introduction of an **open-access, standardized evaluation framework**. By providing a public dataset (ACT-BENCH dataset), transparent metrics (IEC, TA), and an accessible evaluator (ACT-ESTIMATOR), the authors address a major bottleneck in the field. This will enable researchers worldwide to benchmark their world models against a common standard, foster fair comparisons, and significantly improve the reproducibility of research results, which is often a challenge in rapidly evolving AI domains.
2. **Focus on Action Fidelity:** The work shifts the research focus towards the crucial aspect of **action fidelity**, moving beyond mere visual realism. For autonomous driving, generating scenes that precisely adhere to specific driving instructions is paramount for effective simulation, especially for testing safety-critical maneuvers, corner cases, and policy evaluation. This emphasis can drive the development of more robust and controllable world models.
3. **Open-Source Baseline Model:** The release of **TERRA** as an open-access baseline world model is a valuable contribution. Given the limited public access to state-of-the-art models like GAIA-1, TERRA provides a concrete starting point for researchers, democratizing access to powerful tools and accelerating the development of new techniques for trajectory-conditioned video generation. This fosters collaborative efforts and broader discussions within the research community.
4. **Identification of Key Challenges:** The empirical evaluation, particularly the comparison between Vista and TERRA, highlights that even state-of-the-art models exhibit considerable room for improvement in action fidelity. The findings on **abrupt motion changes** in multi-round generation and, more critically, **causal misalignment** (where ego-vehicle actions unnaturally affect other agents) reveal deep-seated challenges in modeling realistic physical interactions within world models. Identifying these limitations is crucial for guiding future research towards more physically consistent and trustworthy simulations.
5. **Potential for Safety-Critical Scenario Generation:** The demonstration that TERRA can deliberately generate **crash scenes** underscores the immense potential of action-controllable world models. These models can create "hard-to-collect" scenarios that are rare, dangerous, or expensive to capture in the real world. This capability is invaluable for:
 - **Data Augmentation:** Supplementing real-world datasets with diverse and targeted challenging scenarios.
 - **Policy Testing:** Rigorously evaluating autonomous driving policies in a safe, simulated environment, including edge cases and hazardous situations.
 - **Safety Assurance:** Identifying potential weaknesses and improving the robustness of autonomous systems before real-world deployment.

In conclusion, "ACT-BENCH" is a foundational work that aims to build a more transparent, reproducible, and effective research ecosystem for action-controllable world models in autonomous driving. By providing essential open-source tools and benchmarking critical capabilities, it lays the groundwork for developing the next generation of neural simulators that can truly accelerate the path towards safer and more reliable autonomous vehicles.
