# DeltaWAM: Delta World Action Models for Bimanual Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> DeltaWAM is a world-action model that splits visual modeling into a dense anchor stream encoding a reference observation, a sparse delta stream of single-token inter-frame changes, and an action stream, trained by flow matching. Streaming Delta Memory incrementally updates cached anchor context with observed deltas. On RoboTwin average success rises from 81.3% to 85.4% clean and 75.8% to 83.9% randomized, cutting training FLOPs 17.78-23.77%.

## Problem

- Existing world-action models supervise dense future-frame prediction, repeatedly modeling backgrounds and static geometry that stay unchanged, wasting training compute on redundant spatial tokens.
- Dense frame objectives couple action-conditioned dynamics to nuisance appearance factors like lighting, texture and background, hurting policy robustness when visual conditions are randomized.
- Action-only models still push every complete observation through a heavy video expert each control cycle, dominating latency once action denoising uses few steps.

## Method

- Three token streams: a dense anchor network initialized from a pretrained video backbone, a frozen delta encoder emitting one token per transition, and an action stream, coordinated by ==structured attention masks==.
- Three sharing variants, **Anchor-Shared**, **Separated** and **Action-Shared**, with **Dual-Stream Residual Experts** adding stream-private low-rank updates inside the shared delta-action backbone; a joint delta-action ==flow-matching== loss weights both branches.
- **Streaming Delta Memory** caches layer-wise anchor keys and values and appends only new delta entries, trained with a ==causal context mask==, plus periodic anchor refresh and asynchronous update during execution.

## Results

- Across ten RoboTwin 2.0 tasks, DeltaWAM with SDM averages **85.4%** clean and **83.9%** randomized versus Fast-WAM's **81.3%** and **75.8%**; the supplemental panel rises from **76.4/72.6%** to **88.0/81.2%**.
- Training FLOPs drop **17.78-23.77%** across the three variants; SDM cuts visual-context KV computation **71.97%** and one-step latency and FLOPs by **36.57%** and **31.55%** at comparable success.
- Ablations show the dense anchor is essential, **32.6%** clean without it versus **80.0%** with LoRA adaptation; real-world bimanual trials reach **41.67%** success and **72.14%** progress, beating both baselines.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Adjacent observations are largely redundant, so supervising sparse change instead of full frames cuts compute and reduces sensitivity to nuisance appearance, though clean-setting accuracy can dip.
> - Let prediction streams share the pretrained video backbone rather than run independently; inherited visual and motion priors matter more than dedicated per-stream capacity.
> - Compression can eat its own savings: the delta encoder's cost offsets much of the video-expert reduction, so lightweight delta encoders are the next efficiency lever.

## Detailed Report

### 1. Overview

Bimanual manipulation requires a robot to use a full view of its workspace, yet most of a manipulation scene stays still between observations. A robot needs the scene's stable context as well as what changed. Dense video models still process the whole scene repeatedly during training. Even after [[2603.16666|Fast-WAM]] removed future-video generation, the full observation still passes through a heavy visual model each control cycle.

DeltaWAM keeps one dense visual reference of the scene, predicts compact changes between observations, and predicts actions alongside those changes. On RoboTwin 2.0, DeltaWAM with Streaming Delta Memory (SDM) reports higher aggregate success than Fast-WAM, especially under visual randomization. There is one clean-setting exception: adding SDM lowers clean success on one five-task set.

### 2. Keep the scene; predict the change

A world-action model learns both how the visual world evolves and what actions to take. Dense future frames repeatedly represent backgrounds and other unchanged content. Fast-WAM removed future-video generation at test time, but it still processes the complete current observation through its video expert to provide action context.

DeltaWAM instead uses three token streams:

- a dense anchor for the reference scene,
- sparse deltas for visual transitions,
- an action stream for executable control chunks.

Each transition contributes one delta token in the encoders used here. The model learns future deltas and actions jointly.

The authors argue that focusing on changes should reduce wasted computation and sensitivity to appearance variation. The measurements support lower computation and improved results in the reported settings. They do not directly establish that reduced sensitivity to nuisance appearance is the sole cause of better robustness.

Figure 2 is a schematic. Panel (a) contrasts Anchor-Shared, Separated, and Action-Shared designs, in which anchor, delta, and action tokens feed differently shared visual/action networks. Panel (b) expands Anchor-Shared: the anchor and delta use the shared visual backbone, while the Action DiT has stream-specific processing and interacts through shared attention. Panel (c) shows structured attention masks for anchor, delta, and action.

### 3. Update context without rerunning the full view

At deployment, SDM begins with one anchor observation. The heavy video expert processes that anchor to create cached, layer-wise key/value context. For each new observation, SDM encodes the transition from the previous observation and processes that compact delta. It then appends the delta's new key/value entries while retaining the earlier entries. The anchor is refreshed periodically as the scene evolves.

### 4. What the tests show, and where they stop

**Across the first ten RoboTwin 2.0 tasks**, DeltaWAM + SDM averages **85.4% clean success and 83.9% randomized success**. Fast-WAM averages **81.3% and 75.8%**.

- These figures are averages of task success rates: five tasks per panel, with separate models trained on the two five-task sets.
- Methods evaluated in this work use 100 episodes per task and setting, with official task-completion criteria.
- The randomized result is the clearer gain.

**SDM ablation.** On the initial five-task set alone, adding SDM raises randomized mean success from **82.6% to 86.6%** but lowers clean mean success from **86.4% to 82.8%**. This comparison uses the same DeltaWAM reference without SDM, and it supports a conditional robustness benefit rather than a universal performance gain.

Table III: ablations on RoboTwin 2.0, reporting clean and randomized mean success rate (%). In the final "Streaming Delta Memory" rows, "Without SDM" scores 86.4 clean and 82.6 randomized, versus 82.8 and 86.6 "With SDM."

**Efficiency in the one-step regime.** In the separately evaluated **one-step action-denoising** regime, DeltaWAM + SDM compares with Fast-WAM as follows:

- Latency falls from **121.02 to 76.76 ms** (36.57%).
- Inference computation falls from **1.351 to 0.925 TFLOPs/action chunk** (31.55%).
- Mean success is similar but not identical: **71.6/69.6% versus 72.8/69.0%** clean/randomized.

Inference FLOPs are amortized over a four-replan cache-refresh cycle. The delta encoder adds **0.457 TFLOPs/action chunk**, which limits the net inference savings. These measurements show fewer FLOPs and lower latency in that regime only, not a general speedup under every inference setting.

### 5. A reference, then change

DeltaWAM keeps a detailed scene reference and makes both predicted evolution and incremental updates compact. The reported results show higher randomized-setting success and one-step efficiency gains. They do not show that deltas eliminate appearance sensitivity or computation overhead, and SDM lowers clean success in the initial five-task panel.
