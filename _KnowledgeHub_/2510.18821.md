# Search Self-play: Pushing the Frontier of Agent Capability without Supervision

> [!summary] Summary
> A self-evolving reinforcement learning framework, Search Self-play (SSP), enables deep search agents to autonomously improve without extensive human supervision by having a single LLM generate and solve its own tasks. This approach led to a 26.4 point average performance increase for Qwen2.5-7B-Base and demonstrated effective continual training for existing agents across diverse benchmarks.

## Problem

- Scarcity of high-quality supervised training data for LLM agents, as human annotation of complex trajectories is labor-intensive and tool-set specific.
- Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) relies on a large volume of pre-existing, verified ground-truth question-answer pairs, limiting its scalability.
- Existing query-synthesis methods face limitations in scalability due to rigorous validation requirements and lack adaptive control over task difficulty during training.

## Method

- Introduces **Search Self-play** (SSP), a ==self-evolving== reinforcement learning framework where a single LLM policy alternates between a question proposer and a problem solver.
- Implements a RAG verification mechanism where the solver validates generated questions using only the proposer's search results (plus noisy documents), ensuring task quality without human intervention.
- Employs a min-max adversarial game formulation, updating the solver using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) and the proposer using REINFORCE, to drive co-evolution of capabilities.

## Results

- SSP consistently improved performance across seven diverse question-answering benchmarks, showing an average 26.4-point gain for Qwen2.5-**7B**-Base and 8.0 points for Qwen2.5-**7B**-Instruct.
- The framework demonstrated model-agnosticism by enhancing capabilities of LLaMA-3.1-**8B** and Qwen3-**8B**, and effectively served as a continual training strategy for specialized agents like ZeroSearch-**7B** (2.3-point average improvement).
- Ablation studies confirmed the critical role of co-evolution and RAG verification; the solver learned more complex multi-step searches, and the proposer generated progressively difficult, high-quality questions, indicating an adaptive curriculum.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Co-evolution of the proposer and solver within a self-play framework is critical for sustained agent improvement and creates an adaptive learning curriculum for deep search agents.
> - A robust RAG-based verification mechanism, augmented with noisy documents, is essential to filter invalid and 'hackable' questions, ensuring the quality and generalizability of autonomously generated training data.
> - Careful design of the proposer's reward scheme and the choice of RL algorithms are crucial for stable training and avoiding catastrophic failure in the co-evolutionary process.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

The rapid advancement of large language models (LLMs) has led to their application in diverse scenarios, including the development of AI agents capable of interacting with complex tools and executing multi-step decision-making processes. Deep search agents, which leverage LLMs with search engines, represent a significant category within this domain. A primary challenge in training these LLM agents is the scarcity of high-quality supervised training data, as human annotation of agentic trajectories can be labor-intensive and tool-set specific.

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has emerged as a prominent technique to address this data scarcity by focusing on the correctness of final predictions rather than requiring detailed intermediate step annotations. However, RLVR still depends on a substantial volume of well-crafted task queries paired with accurate ground-truth answers for reward computation. This dependency limits its scalability. Recent efforts have explored query-synthesis methods to generate training data, but these approaches often face limitations in scalability due to the need for rigorous validation of each synthesized question-answer pair. Furthermore, such offline synthesis schemes may not adaptively control task difficulty, which can hinder effective RL training.

Self-play methods, exemplified by AlphaGo Zero, have demonstrated effectiveness in improving agent capabilities through adversarial games against themselves, requiring no external supervision once the game rules and outcome computations are defined. While self-play has been applied to LLMs for tasks such as safety, alignment, and reasoning, its application to the training of LLM agents, particularly deep search agents that interact with external environments, has remained largely unexplored. This research addresses the annotation scarcity in agentic RL training by introducing a self-play paradigm specifically designed for deep search agents, aiming to overcome the limitations of supervised data collection, RLVR's data dependency, and the inflexibility of existing query-synthesis methods.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The central objective of this research is to develop a scalable and self-sustaining method for training deep search agents without extensive human supervision. This is pursued by exploring a self-play training paradigm to enable LLMs to autonomously improve their agent capabilities.

The key motivations driving this work are:

- **Mitigating Annotation Scarcity in Agentic RL:** Traditional RLVR for LLM agents, while less data-intensive than full supervised learning, still requires a large quantity of verified ground-truth question-answer pairs. Generating these pairs necessitates significant human effort, which creates a bottleneck for scaling agent training. The research aims to reduce or eliminate this human annotation dependency.
- **Addressing Limitations of Query-Synthesis Methods:** Existing task synthesis approaches, designed to generate agentic tasks, often suffer from two main drawbacks. First, their scalability is limited by the need for rigorous validation of each generated question-answer pair to ensure correctness and logical consistency, which can be computationally expensive. Second, these offline methods lack the ability to dynamically adjust task difficulty during RL training, potentially leading to suboptimal learning progress. The researchers seek a method that can dynamically generate high-quality, verifiable tasks with adaptive difficulty.
- **Leveraging Self-Play for Agentic Scenarios:** Inspired by the success of self-play in domains like game AI, the authors hypothesize that a similar approach could be effective for LLM agents. However, existing self-play methods for LLMs often rely on the model's internal knowledge and are not directly applicable to agentic scenarios that involve interaction with external tools, such as search engines. The objective is to adapt the self-play paradigm to enable LLM agents to learn through interaction with external environments.
- **Co-evolution of Task Generation and Problem Solving:** The research is motivated by the idea that an LLM can simultaneously act as a task generator (proposer) and a problem solver. By having the same LLM engage in both roles within a competitive yet cooperative framework, the intent is for both capabilities—generating challenging and verifiable tasks, and effectively solving them—to co-evolve and mutually enhance each other. This dynamic is intended to create a robust and adaptive learning curriculum.
- **Ensuring Verifiability and Quality of Generated Tasks:** A critical motivation is to ensure that the autonomously generated questions are correct and solvable. Without a robust verification mechanism, the proposer could generate nonsensical or unanswerable questions, degrading the training process. The research aims to integrate a self-verification process that uses the proposer's own search trajectory to validate the generated questions, thereby maintaining reward accuracy without human intervention.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method, Search Self-play (SSP), is designed as a self-evolving reinforcement learning framework for deep search agents. It operates as a two-player game where a single LLM policy alternately assumes the roles of a question proposer and a problem solver.

**Agent Modeling:**
The LLM is modeled as an auto-regressive next-token prediction policy, $\pi_\theta$, with $\theta$ representing the model parameters. A search agent's exploration is conceptualized as a token-level Markov Decision Process (S, A, T, r), where the state space S is the natural language sequence, the action space A is the vocabulary, and transitions involve token generation or tool observation. A trajectory $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ consists of LLM outputs and observations from search tools.

**Search Self-play Design:**
The SSP game involves two roles, proposer and solver, differentiated by distinct system prompts ($\boldsymbol{x}_{\text{propose}}$ and $\boldsymbol{x}_{\text{solve}}$) provided to the same LLM policy $\pi_\theta$.
1. **Question Proposer:** Given a ground-truth answer $\boldsymbol{a}^*$, the proposer policy $u(\cdot|\boldsymbol{a}^*)$ generates a challenging deep search question $\boldsymbol{q}$ using multi-turn search engine interactions. The proposer's goal is to create questions that are progressively difficult for the solver but remain verifiable and correct.
2. **Problem Solver:** For a generated question $\boldsymbol{q}$, the solver policy $v(\cdot|\boldsymbol{q})$ attempts to find the correct answer through multi-turn reasoning and search tool usage, mirroring an ordinary deep search agent. The solver's objective is to maximize its answer correctness.

**Adversarial and Cooperative Dynamics:**
The relationship between the proposer and solver is designed with both adversarial and cooperative elements. Adversarially, the proposer aims to challenge the solver by creating difficult questions, while the solver strives to correctly answer them. Cooperatively, a verification mechanism ensures the quality of generated questions.

**RAG Verification Mechanism:**
To prevent the proposer from generating incorrect or unanswerable questions, a crucial RAG verification step is introduced. The search results from the proposer's trajectory ($\boldsymbol{O}_T$) are collected and presented to the solver as RAG documents. The solver then attempts to answer the generated question $\boldsymbol{q}$ using only these provided documents, without performing new searches. If the solver can correctly answer the question under this RAG setup, the proposed question is deemed valid. This mechanism enforces cooperation by requiring the proposer to generate questions that are not only challenging but also genuinely solvable with the evidence it gathered. To further enhance robustness and prevent the proposer from "hacking" the verification by generating questions trivially solvable with its exact search results but ill-posed for general search, irrelevant documents from other trajectories are mixed into the RAG materials during verification.

**Search Self-play Modeling and Optimization:**
The primary objective of SSP is formulated as a min-max adversarial game:
$\min_u \max_v \mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{a}^* \sim \mathcal{D}, \boldsymbol{\tau} \sim u(\cdot|\boldsymbol{a}^*), \boldsymbol{\rho} \sim v(\cdot|\boldsymbol{q}=\text{Q}(\boldsymbol{\tau}))} [r(\text{A}(\boldsymbol{\rho}), \boldsymbol{a}^*)]$, subject to the RAG verification constraint $\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma} \sim v(\cdot|\boldsymbol{q}=\text{Q}(\boldsymbol{\tau}), \boldsymbol{O}_T=\text{O}(\boldsymbol{\tau}))} [r(\text{A}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}), \boldsymbol{a}^*)] = 1$.
In practice, training jointly on cooperation and competition is complex. The paper adopts rejection sampling for the cooperative objective, filtering out invalid questions to ensure a batch of high-quality tasks for optimizing the adversarial objective.

**Implementation Details:**
- **Filtering Strategies:**
 - **Rule-based filtering:** Ensures the proposer's output adheres to a correct format (e.g., `<question></question>` tags), is non-empty, involves search tool invocation, is not excessively short, and does not contain the original ground-truth answer.
 - **RAG verification:** Applied after rule-based filtering. The solver, using a dedicated RAG solver prompt and the proposer's collected search results (augmented with 4 noisy documents), validates the question.
- **Reward Calculation:**
 - **Solver Reward ($r_{\text{solve}}$):** A binary reward (1 for correct, 0 for incorrect) determined by an LLM-as-a-judge function, comparing the solver's predicted answer to the ground-truth.
 - **Proposer Reward ($r_{\text{propose}}$):** Calculated as $1 - \bar{r}_{\text{solve}}$, where $\bar{r}_{\text{solve}}$ is the average success rate of the solver over $n$ trajectories for a given question. This incentivizes the proposer to generate questions that are difficult for the solver.
- **Optimization Algorithms:**
 - The solver's policy is updated using Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which leverages the average reward of a group of trajectories as a baseline to reduce variance.
 - The proposer's policy is updated using the REINFORCE algorithm, aiming to increase the log-probability of trajectories that result in high proposer rewards (i.e., low solver success rates).
- **Training Details:** A pre-defined answer set $\mathcal{D}$ (50,000 ground-truth answers from public training sets) is used. Tool calls are limited to 10 rounds. During training, the content within `<information></information>` tags is masked to exclude loss computation, promoting stable learning. A Replay Buffer (Periodic Reset) strategy is employed for batch sampling, where the buffer is cleared every 10 steps to balance data reuse and novelty.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Search Self-play (SSP) across various benchmarks, models, and training setups yielded several findings:

- **Consistent Performance Gains:** SSP consistently demonstrated performance improvements across seven diverse question-answering benchmarks, including NQ, TriviaQA, PopQA, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, Musique, and Bamboogle. These gains were observed across both general (GeneralQA) and multi-hop reasoning (Multi-HopQA) tasks.

- **Impact on From-Scratch Training:** When applied to models trained from scratch, SSP delivered substantial improvements. For Qwen2.5-7B-Base, the average performance gain was 26.4 points, with a particularly high gain of 40.4 points on TriviaQA. Instruction-tuned models, such as Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, also benefited, showing an average improvement of 8.0 points.

- **Generalization Across Model Architectures:** SSP proved to be model-agnostic, enhancing the capabilities of models from different architectural families, including LLaMA-3.1-8B and Qwen3-8B, with average improvements of 9.6 and 3.8 points, respectively.

- **Effectiveness in Continual Training:** SSP served as an effective continual training strategy for existing search-specialized agents like ZeroSearch-7B, Search-R1-7B, and R-Search-7B. These models, already extensively trained, exhibited further performance improvements on average by 2.3, 1.8, and 1.8 points, respectively.

- **Scalability to Larger Models:** The performance benefits of SSP extended to larger models. When applied to Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct, it continued to yield improvements (average 2.1 and 3.4 points, respectively), with Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct + SSP achieving competitive results on five of the seven benchmarks.

- **Importance of Co-evolution (Self-play vs. Fixed-Opponent Training):** Ablation studies confirmed that the co-evolution of the proposer and solver is critical. The full SSP framework significantly outperformed variants where only the solver (Solver-Only) or only the proposer (Proposer-Only) was trained. Solver-Only models quickly overfit to static tasks, leading to performance plateaus or declines on held-out evaluations. Proposer-Only models showed limited gains, as the fixed solver couldn't adapt to increasingly complex tasks. The dynamic interaction in SSP, where the proposer generates more challenging tasks as the solver improves, created an adaptive curriculum that sustained learning.

- **Role of RAG Verification:** Removing the RAG verification mechanism resulted in a significant performance decay, particularly on GeneralQA benchmarks. This finding confirms the importance of RAG verification in filtering invalid questions and ensuring the quality of the training data, preventing the solver from learning from noisy or incorrect tasks.

- **Impact of Noisy Documents in RAG Verification:** Injecting a moderate number of noisy documents (four documents found optimal) into the RAG verification context improved performance. This strategy discourages the proposer from generating "hacking" questions that are trivially verifiable with a limited, specific document set but lack robustness for general deep search scenarios. Excessive noise, however, degraded accuracy.

- **Training Dynamics Insights:** Analysis of training dynamics showed that the solver learned to perform more complex multi-step searches (increased search tool calls) and generated more comprehensive answers (increased response length). Concurrently, the proposer demonstrated an increase in search tool utilization and an improved question validation success rate, indicating enhanced question quality. The difficulty of generated questions progressively increased over training, exhibiting an adaptive curriculum, while maintaining a balanced topic distribution.

- **Sensitivity to Proposer Reward Design:** An ablation demonstrated that a punitive reward for proposer format errors (e.g., -0.1) led to catastrophic training failure, causing the proposer's policy entropy to rise and its valid question generation rate to collapse. This highlights the necessity of a carefully calibrated reward scheme for stable co-evolution.

- **RL Algorithms Comparison:** While training both proposer and solver with GRPO achieved slightly higher accuracy, it incurred a substantial computational cost (approximately 6 times slower). The default configuration, using REINFORCE for the proposer and GRPO for the solver (RF-GRPO), demonstrated the best balance between effectiveness and efficiency.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research introduces Search Self-play (SSP), a novel approach that significantly advances the capability of deep search agents by enabling self-supervised and scalable training. The primary significance of SSP lies in its ability to circumvent the fundamental bottlenecks associated with human annotation and static data generation in the development of LLM agents.

The potential impacts are multifaceted:

- **Enhanced Scalability and Data Efficiency:** SSP removes the reliance on extensive human-annotated ground-truth data for agentic RL training. By allowing the LLM to autonomously generate and solve its own tasks, it offers a pathway to train agents more efficiently and at a larger scale, without requiring continuous human intervention for data curation or task validation. This is particularly impactful for complex agentic scenarios where data annotation is prohibitively expensive or difficult.
- **Adaptive Curriculum Learning:** The co-evolutionary dynamic between the proposer and solver in SSP inherently creates an adaptive learning curriculum. As the solver's capabilities improve, the proposer learns to generate increasingly challenging and sophisticated tasks. This dynamic prevents overfitting, promotes continuous learning, and ensures that the agent is always challenged at its frontier of capability, fostering robust skill development.
- **Improved Agent Capabilities:** The experimental results demonstrate consistent and substantial performance improvements across a wide range of question-answering benchmarks, diverse model architectures, and various training paradigms (from-scratch and continual learning). This indicates that SSP effectively enhances an agent's abilities in multi-turn reasoning, effective tool use, and answer generation.
- **Foundation for Self-Sustaining RL Systems:** SSP represents a step towards building truly self-sustaining reinforcement learning systems for LLM agents. By allowing agents to autonomously generate their own training data and learning signals, it reduces external dependencies and could accelerate the development cycle for advanced AI agents.
- **Potential for Generalization to Other Agentic Domains:** While demonstrated specifically for deep search agents, the core principles of the SSP framework—a self-play game with distinct adversarial and cooperative roles, coupled with a robust task verification mechanism—may be adaptable to other types of LLM agents interacting with different tool sets or environments. This could include GUI agents, coding agents, or agents in virtual environments, expanding the applicability of self-play in agent training.

In conclusion, SSP offers a scalable, data-efficient, and effective paradigm for training LLM agents, pushing the frontier of agent capability without requiring external human supervision, and potentially paving the way for more autonomous and robust AI agent development.
