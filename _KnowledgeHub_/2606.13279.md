# See Selectively, Act Adaptively: Dual-Level Structural Decomposition for Bimanual Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Dongguk University researchers developed a Dual-Level Structural Decomposition Framework that enhances bimanual robot manipulation by dynamically selecting relevant visual inputs and adapting action generation to varying interaction modes. This framework improved task success rates in complex simulated and real-world environments by up to +37% and +50% respectively, addressing challenges posed by dynamic visual relevance and alternating arm coordination.

## Problem

- Bimanual robot manipulation is challenging due to dynamic changes in task-relevant visual information (perceptual heterogeneity).
- Robotic arms exhibit varying spatial and temporal dependencies, alternating between independent and coordinated movements (interaction heterogeneity).
- Existing monolithic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies often fail to explicitly model these intertwined heterogeneities, limiting robustness and generalization.

## Method

- A Dual-Level Structural Decomposition Framework enhances a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model with two specialized modules.
- A View-Selective Visual Router (VSR) dynamically adjusts wrist-view contributions based on task context to handle perceptual heterogeneity.
- An Interaction-Aware Action Mixture-of-Experts (IAMoE) separates action generation into coordinated and arm-wise pathways to manage interaction heterogeneity.

## Results

- The full framework achieved absolute success rates up to +**37%** higher in simulation and +**50%** higher in real-world "hard" settings compared to monolithic baselines.
- The View-Selective Visual Router (VSR) improved success rates by up to +**37%** on tasks with high perceptual heterogeneity (e.g., S2 Blocks Ranking Size).
- The Interaction-Aware Action Mixture-of-Experts (IAMoE) yielded up to +**30%** absolute success rate gains on tasks with dominant interaction heterogeneity (e.g., S4 Place Bread Skillet).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly modeling dynamic view relevance and bimanual interaction structures through explicit decomposition is crucial for robust policy learning.
> - Decoupling complex bimanual learning signals through specialized modules reduces interference and leads to more stable policy execution.
> - Parameter-matched baselines confirmed that performance gains stem from the structural decomposition itself, not just increased model capacity.
