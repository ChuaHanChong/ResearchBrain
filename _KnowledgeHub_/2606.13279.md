# See Selectively, Act Adaptively: Dual-Level Structural Decomposition for Bimanual Robot Manipulation

> [!summary] Summary
> Dongguk University researchers developed a Dual-Level Structural Decomposition Framework that enhances bimanual robot manipulation by dynamically selecting relevant visual inputs and adapting action generation to varying interaction modes. This framework improved task success rates in complex simulated and real-world environments by up to +37% and +50% respectively, addressing challenges posed by dynamic visual relevance and alternating arm coordination.

## Problem

- Bimanual robot manipulation is challenging due to dynamic changes in task-relevant visual information (perceptual heterogeneity).
- Robotic arms exhibit varying spatial and temporal dependencies, alternating between independent and coordinated movements (interaction heterogeneity).
- Existing monolithic Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies often fail to explicitly model these intertwined heterogeneities, limiting robustness and generalization.

## Method

- A Dual-Level Structural Decomposition Framework enhances a Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model with two specialized modules.
- A View-Selective Visual Router (VSR) dynamically adjusts wrist-view contributions based on task context to handle perceptual heterogeneity.
- An Interaction-Aware Action Mixture-of-Experts (IAMoE) separates action generation into coordinated and arm-wise pathways to manage interaction heterogeneity.

## Results

- The full framework achieved absolute success rates up to +**37%** higher in simulation and +**50%** higher in real-world "hard" settings compared to monolithic baselines.
- The View-Selective Visual Router (VSR) improved success rates by up to +**37%** on tasks with high perceptual heterogeneity (e.g., S2 Blocks Ranking Size).
- The Interaction-Aware Action Mixture-of-Experts (IAMoE) yielded up to +**30%** absolute success rate gains on tasks with dominant interaction heterogeneity (e.g., S4 Place Bread Skillet).

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Jointly modeling dynamic view relevance and bimanual interaction structures through explicit decomposition is crucial for robust policy learning.
> - Decoupling complex bimanual learning signals through specialized modules reduces interference and leads to more stable policy execution.
> - Parameter-matched baselines confirmed that performance gains stem from the structural decomposition itself, not just increased model capacity.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Bimanual robotic manipulation, which involves using two robotic arms to perform both independent and coordinated movements, is considered a critical capability for general-purpose robots operating in human-centered environments. Many complex real-world tasks that are inefficient or challenging for a single robotic arm can be executed more stably and flexibly with two arms, examples include organizing multiple objects, transferring items, or stabilizing an object while the other arm manipulates it.

Recent advancements in imitation learning and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have extended visuomotor policy learning from human demonstrations to bimanual settings. However, bimanual manipulation introduces complexities beyond merely increasing the degrees of freedom. The task-relevant visual information often changes dynamically across task stages, and the spatial and temporal dependencies between the arms vary significantly, alternating between independent and coordinated modes. These complexities are characterized in the paper as "perceptual heterogeneity" and "interaction heterogeneity."

Existing monolithic VLA policies typically process diverse visual inputs and interaction patterns through a single shared representation and action generation pathway. This approach often fails to explicitly account for the dynamic relevance of multi-camera inputs (perceptual heterogeneity) and the distinct action structures required for independent versus coordinated arm movements (interaction heterogeneity). Such monolithic approaches can introduce task-irrelevant visual cues, weaken critical information, entangle mode-specific learning signals, and ultimately degrade policy robustness and generalization.

Previous research has made progress in several related areas:
- **Bimanual Policy Learning and Generalist Policies:** Efforts have focused on joint policy learning and leveraging unimanual priors for generalization, with VLA models extending to bimanual settings. However, these often prioritize generalization without explicitly addressing the structural complexity inherent in bimanual tasks.
- **Selective Perception for Multi-View Manipulation:** Methods for multi-view manipulation have explored selecting or reweighting task-relevant views to aid action generation. In bimanual contexts, however, the relevance of different camera views is closely linked to the interaction mode of the arms, a coupling that prior work typically does not directly address.
- **Structured Interaction Modeling for Bimanual Manipulation:** Some studies have modeled inter-arm dependencies through arm-wise policy decomposition or by assigning predefined roles (e.g., acting and stabilizing). These static decompositions are less suitable for tasks where independent and coordinated motions dynamically alternate throughout the task.

This work aims to address the limitations of prior approaches by proposing a framework that jointly accounts for dynamically evolving view relevance and interaction structure, thereby offering a more robust approach to bimanual manipulation.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop a robust VLA framework for bimanual robotic manipulation by explicitly addressing two forms of heterogeneity: perceptual and interaction. The motivation stems from the limitations of existing monolithic VLA policies in handling these challenges effectively.

**Detailed Objectives and Motivations:**

1. **Addressing Perceptual Heterogeneity:**
 - **Motivation:** In bimanual tasks, the relevance of visual information, particularly from wrist-mounted cameras, changes significantly with the task stage and context. For instance, when one arm performs a grasp, its wrist view is crucial, while the other arm's wrist view might be irrelevant or even distracting. Monolithic policies that aggregate visual features without explicitly accounting for this time-varying relevance can dilute critical visual cues and introduce noise, thereby reducing the policy's robustness and generalization capabilities.
 - **Objective:** To dynamically adjust the contributions of multi-camera inputs, specifically wrist-mounted views, to emphasize task-relevant visual cues and suppress irrelevant information. This aims to enable the robot to "see selectively" based on the immediate task context.

2. **Addressing Interaction Heterogeneity:**
 - **Motivation:** Bimanual tasks intrinsically involve different action structures. Some stages require independent movement of each arm toward separate goals, while others necessitate coordinated movement toward a shared objective. Existing monolithic policies learn both independent and coordinated actions within a single, shared action generation pathway. This can lead to interference between mode-specific learning signals, biasing the policy towards a particular interaction mode or diminishing overall task reliability.
 - **Objective:** To explicitly decompose the action generation process into distinct pathways that can adapt to varying bimanual interaction modes (independent vs. coordinated). This aims to reduce interference and enable the robot to "act adaptively" according to the required interaction structure.

3. **Jointly Mitigating Intertwined Heterogeneities:**
 - **Motivation:** Perceptual and interaction heterogeneities are often intertwined. For example, when one arm stabilizes an object for the other to manipulate, the arms' actions become interdependent, and visual cues from the manipulating arm's wrist become particularly critical. Monolithic approaches struggle to model this dynamic coupling.
 - **Objective:** To propose a Dual-Level Structural Decomposition Framework that models "what to see" (perceptual level) and "how to act" (action level) as separate yet integrated components. The goal is to demonstrate that jointly considering selective visual processing and explicit decomposition of bimanual interaction structures provides a more effective inductive bias for learning robust policies in complex bimanual tasks.

In summary, the core motivation is to move beyond monolithic policy learning by introducing structural inductive biases that reflect the dynamic nature of visual relevance and bimanual interaction patterns, thereby enhancing the robustness and reliability of robot manipulation in diverse, complex scenarios.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The proposed method introduces a Dual-Level Structural Decomposition Framework that augments a pre-trained Vision-Language-Action (VLA) model, $\pi_{0.5}$, with two specialized modules: a View-Selective Visual Router (VSR) and an Interaction-Aware Action Mixture-of-Experts (IAMoE). The framework addresses the challenges of perceptual and interaction heterogeneity by modulating visual contributions at the input stage and decomposing action generation according to the bimanual interaction mode.

#### 3.1. Overall Framework

The framework operates on observations $O_t = (\{I_v^t\}_{v \in V}, \ell, s_t)$, where $V = \{E, L, R\}$ denotes external, left, and right camera views; $I_v^t$ is the image from view $v$; $\ell$ is the language instruction; and $s_t$ is the robot state. The model predicts an H-step action chunk $A_t$, comprising joint control commands and gripper states for both arms.

The VSR module is positioned at the input stage of the pre-trained VLM to dynamically adjust the contributions of wrist-view tokens. The IAMoE module extends the base model's action expert, enabling selection among different action generation pathways based on the interaction mode.

#### 3.2. View-Selective Visual Router (VSR)

The VSR module is designed to model and dynamically adjust wrist-view relevance based on the task stage and the role of each arm.

- **Input Modulation:** The external view ($F_E^t$) provides global context and its contributions are not modulated. VSR focuses solely on reweighting the contributions of the left ($F_L^t$) and right ($F_R^t$) wrist visual tokens.
- **Relevance Weight Prediction:** VSR predicts relevance weights $w_t = [w_L^t, w_R^t] \in^2$. These weights are derived from a "routing context" $z_{vis}^t$, which is constructed using the external visual tokens ($F_E^t$) and language-state tokens ($C_t$). Notably, wrist visual tokens are explicitly excluded from the routing context $z_{vis}^t$ to prevent the router from learning shortcut patterns specific to the training environment (Appendix A).
- **Weight Application:** The predicted weights reweight the wrist visual tokens as $F_L^{'t} = w_L^t F_L^t$ and $F_R^{'t} = w_R^t F_R^t$.
- **Shared Representation:** The reweighted wrist tokens, along with the unmodulated external visual tokens ($F_E^t$) and language-state tokens ($C_t$), are then fed into the pre-trained VLM to form a shared conditioning representation $G_t$.
- **Implementation Details (Appendix A):** An MLP followed by an element-wise sigmoid predicts the weights, allowing independent modulation of each wrist view. Attention pooling summarizes external visual tokens, and mean pooling summarizes language-state tokens for $z_{vis}^t$.

#### 3.3. Interaction-Aware Action Mixture-of-Experts (IAMoE)

The IAMoE module is responsible for generating actions by separately modeling coordinated and arm-wise actions. It operates within the conditional flow matching framework used by the base policy to predict the target velocity field $u_\tau$.

- **Expert Architecture:** IAMoE consists of three LoRA-based experts, all conditioned on the shared representation $G_t$:
 - **Coordinated Expert:** This expert operates on the full action space, producing $\hat{u}_{coord}^\tau$.
 - **Left Arm-Wise Expert:** This expert is restricted to the left arm's action subspace through an action mask $M_L$, producing $\hat{u}_L^\tau$.
 - **Right Arm-Wise Expert:** Similarly, this expert is restricted to the right arm's action subspace via action mask $M_R$, producing $\hat{u}_R^\tau$.
 - The combined independent expert output is defined as $\hat{u}_{ind}^\tau = \hat{u}_L^\tau + \hat{u}_R^\tau$.
- **Action Router:** An Action Router predicts a hard one-hot interaction mode $m_t = [m_{coord}^t, m_{ind}^t]$ (either coordinated or independent). The router's decision is based on a routing context $z_{act}^t$, also constructed from external visual tokens ($F_E^t$) and language-state tokens ($C_t$).
- **Final Action Selection:** The final velocity prediction $\hat{u}_\tau$ is determined by the selected mode: $\hat{u}_\tau = m_{coord}^t \hat{u}_{coord}^\tau + m_{ind}^t \hat{u}_{ind}^\tau$.
- **Training and Inference:** During training, a straight-through Gumbel-Softmax is used for hard routing with soft gradient passing. During inference, $m_t$ is determined by arg max and held fixed for the entire action chunk.
- **Implementation Details (Appendix A):** All three experts share a common action backbone, each equipped with a separate LoRA adapter for mode-specific specialization. Expert-specific view-level attention masks are applied: the coordinated expert accesses all views, while arm-wise experts only access the external view and their corresponding wrist view.

#### 3.4. Training Objective and Label Generation

The total training objective $L_{total}$ combines several loss terms:
- **Main Action Objective ($L_{main}$):** Standard conditional flow matching loss for the final velocity prediction $\hat{u}_\tau$.
- **Router Supervision Losses ($L_{vis}$, $L_{act}$):**
 - $L_{vis}$ is a binary cross-entropy loss for VSR, supervised by $y_{vis}^t \in \{0,1\}^2$ (indicating active left/right wrist views).
 - $L_{act}$ is a cross-entropy loss for the Action Router, supervised by $y_{act}^t \in \{,\}$ (indicating coordinated or independent interaction mode).
- **Auxiliary Expert Loss ($L_{aux}$):** An L2 loss ensures all experts receive sufficient learning signals by comparing each expert's output to the ground truth masked action.
- **Total Loss:** $L_{total} = L_{main} + \lambda_{vis}L_{vis} + \lambda_{act}L_{act} + \lambda_{aux}L_{aux}$, where $\lambda$ values are loss weights, and $\lambda_{aux}$ is gradually decreased during training.

**Semi-Automatic Label Generation (Appendix B):**
To supervise the routers, task-specific wrist-view relevance ($y_{vis}^t$) and interaction-mode ($y_{act}^t$) labels are generated using a semi-automatic procedure.
- **Manual Annotation:** Approximately 10% of demonstration episodes are manually annotated at the frame level.
- **KNN-Based Propagation:** Proprioceptive features (gripper state/velocity, end-effector position/velocity) from neighboring timesteps are used to train task-specific KNN classifiers for label propagation to the remaining unlabeled episodes.
- **Refinement:** A two-pass classification strategy with temporal smoothing and boundary-aware reclassification is applied to mitigate prediction noise and improve accuracy near transition boundaries. The procedure achieved high frame-level accuracy (0.9977 for wrist-view relevance, 0.9988 for interaction mode) against a held-out manual evaluation set.

#### 3.5. Experimental Setup

- **Simulation Benchmark:** Six bimanual tasks from RoboTwin 2.0 were used, categorized by the type of heterogeneity they predominantly exhibit:
 - Perceptual heterogeneity: S1 (Stack Bowls Three), S2 (Blocks Ranking Size)
 - Interaction heterogeneity: S3 (Lift Pot), S4 (Place Bread Skillet)
 - Both heterogeneities: S5 (Handover Block), S6 (Put Bottles Dustbin)
 - Evaluations were conducted under both "easy" and "hard" settings (varied initial object positions, increased visual ambiguity).
- **Real-World Tasks:** Three long-horizon tasks, each with six stages, were designed to induce both forms of heterogeneity: R1 (Pack and Place), R2 (Handover and Pour), R3 (Rotate and Place). Evaluations were conducted under "easy" (training environment) and "hard" (unseen environments, distractors) conditions.
- **Baselines and Ablations:**
 - **Baseline:** Monolithic $\pi_{0.5}$.
 - **w/o IAMoE:** Full model with only VSR enabled.
 - **w/o VSR:** Full model with only IAMoE enabled.
 - **Ours:** The full proposed model with both VSR and IAMoE.
 - **LoRA 2$\times$ (Appendix C):** A parameter-matched baseline to ensure gains are not due to increased capacity.
- **Training and Evaluation Protocol:** All models were trained separately per task using LoRA fine-tuning for 30,000 steps with a batch size of 16. Training data consisted of 50 demonstrations per task in simulation and 40 in real-world. Evaluation involved 100 rollouts per task in simulation and 10 trials per task in real-world (easy setting; hard setting for Baseline and Ours).

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experiments were designed to evaluate the proposed framework's ability to address perceptual and interaction heterogeneity in bimanual manipulation across various simulated and real-world tasks.

#### 4.1. Contribution of Individual Modules (Q1)

**Effect of VSR:**
- Comparison of "w/o IAMoE" (VSR only) against "Baseline" revealed VSR's contribution to mitigating perceptual heterogeneity.
- On tasks with pronounced perceptual heterogeneity (S1, S2), "w/o IAMoE" consistently outperformed "Baseline" across both easy and hard settings. Specifically, on S2 (Blocks Ranking Size), absolute success rates improved by +23% in the easy setting and +37% in the hard setting. This indicates that explicitly modulating wrist-view contributions based on task context enhances the policy's ability to handle dynamic visual relevance.
- Real-world failure analysis showed that "w/o VSR" (IAMoE only) frequently exhibited errors in arm-role assignment and target-position inference, which is consistent with insufficient attention to task-relevant visual cues.

**Effect of IAMoE:**
- Comparison of "w/o VSR" (IAMoE only) against "Baseline" demonstrated IAMoE's role in addressing interaction heterogeneity.
- On tasks with pronounced interaction heterogeneity (S3, S4), "w/o VSR" achieved absolute success-rate gains of +28% and +30% respectively under the hard setting. This suggests that the separate modeling of coordinated and arm-wise actions supports more stable action generation when interaction modes vary.
- An additional observation on S1 indicated that arm-wise experts might contribute to capturing left-right asymmetric manipulation patterns.
- Real-world failure analysis revealed that "w/o IAMoE" (VSR only) often failed during coordinated manipulation stages requiring stable inter-arm alignment (e.g., box transport in R1, handover in R2, axis alignment in R3).

#### 4.2. Benefit of Dual-Level Decomposition (Q2)

When both forms of heterogeneity were present, the full "Ours" architecture, combining both VSR and IAMoE, outperformed both the monolithic "Baseline" and the single-module variants.
- **Simulation Results:** On complex bimanual tasks (S5: Handover Block, S6: Put Bottles Dustbin), "Ours" achieved higher success rates than "Baseline," "w/o IAMoE," and "w/o VSR."
 - In the easy setting, "Ours" improved absolute success rates over the single-module variants by 13.4% (vs. w/o IAMoE) and 6.7% (vs. w/o VSR).
 - These margins widened in the hard setting to 17.3% and 13.2%, respectively.
- **Real-World Results:** "Ours" achieved higher final success rates across R1–R3 compared to both single-module variants. Stage-wise analysis indicated that the integration of both modules improved overall success and reduced failures at intermediate stages, particularly during stage transitions. For example, on R1, "Ours" maintained a 100% success rate through Stage 5, addressing the distinct failure patterns observed in individual module variants.
- These findings suggest that complex bimanual manipulation requires joint modeling of dynamically changing view relevance and interaction structure, as the two modules provide complementary benefits.

#### 4.3. Robustness of the Proposed Routing-Based Structure (Q3)

The study evaluated the robustness of the router-based architecture under the hard setting, which introduced environmental variations and visual ambiguity.
- **Simulation Robustness:** "Ours" achieved the highest average success rate in both easy and hard settings. Notably, under the hard setting, "Ours" improved over "Baseline" by +35.7% (absolute success rate). This indicates that explicit routing helps mitigate ambiguity when environmental changes increase the burden on a monolithic policy to implicitly resolve visual cues and interaction patterns.
- **Real-World Robustness:** In the real-world hard evaluation, "Ours" improved absolute success rates over "Baseline" by +30% on R1, +40% on R2, and +50% on R3. Despite unseen environmental variations, "Ours" maintained a 60% success rate on R2 and R3. These results suggest that explicitly selecting relevant wrist views and interaction modes enhances robustness in real-world bimanual manipulation under environmental changes.

#### 4.4. Ablation Studies (Appendix C)

Additional ablation studies further supported the design choices:
- **Parameter-matched comparison:** The "LoRA 2$\times$" baseline, which had more parameters than "Ours" but lacked the structural decomposition, did not improve over the "Baseline." This confirmed that the performance gains of "Ours" are attributable to its structural decomposition rather than merely increased parameter capacity.
- **VSR design analysis:** Including wrist visual tokens in the VSR routing context led to less stable routing behavior and degraded performance under hard settings (e.g., S2 Hard). Removing router supervision or using reversed labels significantly reduced performance, underscoring the importance of explicit and correct view-relevance supervision.
- **IAMoE design analysis:** Similar to VSR, removing router supervision or using reversed interaction-mode labels for IAMoE resulted in substantial performance degradation, particularly in hard settings. This suggests that the IAMoE structure is effective only when guided by meaningful interaction-mode routing through correct supervision.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This research presents a framework that contributes to the field of robotic manipulation by systematically addressing fundamental challenges in bimanual control.

**Key Contributions and Significance:**

1. **Dual-Level Structural Decomposition:** The introduction of a Dual-Level Structural Decomposition Framework that models "what to see" (perceptual level) and "how to act" (action level) as separate components is a notable architectural advancement. This explicit decomposition provides a principled way to incorporate inductive biases specific to bimanual tasks.
2. **Mitigation of Heterogeneities:** The VSR and IAMoE modules demonstrate effectiveness in mitigating perceptual heterogeneity (dynamic view relevance) and interaction heterogeneity (alternating independent/coordinated arm motions), respectively. The study shows that jointly addressing these intertwined challenges yields complementary performance gains.
3. **Enhanced Robustness and Generalization:** The framework significantly improves task success rates in both simulated and real-world environments, particularly under "hard" settings involving environmental variations and unseen objects. This enhanced robustness suggests that the model learns generalizable principles of visual selection and interaction structure rather than merely exploiting easy-setting correlations. This is crucial for deploying robots in complex, unstructured real-world scenarios.
4. **Improved Policy Learning:** By decoupling complex bimanual learning signals, the proposed approach allows for more specialized learning within each module, reducing interference and leading to more stable and reliable policy execution.

**Potential Impact:**

- **Advancement in Bimanual Robotics:** The framework provides a robust foundation for developing more capable bimanual robotic systems, which are essential for many dexterous manipulation tasks beyond the capabilities of single-arm robots. This could accelerate the deployment of robots in diverse applications, including industrial assembly, domestic assistance, and hazardous environment operations.
- **Foundation for General-Purpose Robots:** As general-purpose robots become more prevalent, their ability to perform complex bimanual tasks with varying visual and interaction demands will be critical. This work offers a step towards policies that can adapt more effectively to the intricacies of human-centered environments.
- **Future Research Directions:** The paper identifies several limitations that suggest avenues for future research, such as developing VLM-based scene understanding for automatic generation of routing labels, exploring more granular or hierarchical representations of interaction modes (e.g., continuous coordination scores), and validating robustness to broader visual distribution shifts or longer-horizon tasks. Addressing these limitations could lead to even more sophisticated and autonomous bimanual manipulation capabilities.

Overall, this research provides a comprehensive approach to modeling the complexities of bimanual manipulation, demonstrating that explicit structural decomposition at both the perceptual and action levels is a powerful strategy for achieving robust and adaptive robot control.
