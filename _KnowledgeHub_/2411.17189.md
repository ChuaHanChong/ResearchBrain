# PhysMotion: Physics-Grounded Dynamics From a Single Image

> [!summary] Summary
> PhysMotion introduces a framework that generates physically plausible 3D dynamics from a single input image by integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting, a differentiable Material Point Method simulator, and diffusion models. It produced realistic object motions across various material types, quantitatively outperforming baselines in physical commonsense and semantic adherence scores.

## Problem

- Traditional video generation and editing are labor-intensive, requiring specialized expertise and significant time.
- Data-driven generative AI models often produce videos that lack physical plausibility and struggle with precise controllability over complex dynamics.
- Existing physics-based simulation methods require extensive 3D inputs (e.g., multi-view images, explicit 3D models), limiting their accessibility and ease of use from common data formats.

## Method

- Reconstructs a refined 3D ==Gaussian Splatting== (3DGS) representation of the foreground object from a single image using multi-view supervision and a hard-depth loss.
- Generates coarse, physics-grounded object dynamics by simulating the 3DGS within a differentiable ==Material Point Method== (==MPM==) simulator, guided by user-specified forces or torques.
- Enhances these coarse dynamics into high-fidelity, temporally consistent videos using diffusion models with **ControlNet** guidance, key-frame attention, and feature propagation for visual refinement.

## Results

- Generated realistic dynamics for a diverse set of material properties, including rigid body, elastic deformation, viscoplastic flow, granular particles, and fracture effects, from single images.
- Achieved superior average normalized z-scores on the VideoPhy benchmark for Physical Commonsense (0.5142) and Semantic Adherence (0.7230), surpassing all tested baselines.
- Demonstrated consistent qualitative superiority and higher user preference in studies evaluating temporal consistency, physics plausibility, and overall visual quality compared to state-of-the-art image-to-video models.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - A synergistic integration of differentiable physics simulation (MPM) with 3D geometry representation (3DGS) and generative AI (diffusion models) can achieve both physical realism and high visual quality from minimal input.
> - Refining single-image-derived 3DGS geometry with hard-depth loss and selective color supervision is crucial for accurate 3D reconstruction and mitigating artifacts.
> - Blending foreground object dynamics with a static background in image space, rather than latent space, is critical for maintaining temporal consistency and preserving high-frequency details during diffusion-based video enhancement.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

PhysMotion operates at the confluence of several rapidly advancing fields: video generation, 3D reconstruction, physics-based simulation, and generative AI (particularly diffusion models). The broader research landscape is characterized by a strong drive to automate and enhance content creation workflows for industries such as film, gaming, and virtual reality.

Traditionally, high-quality video generation and editing have been labor-intensive processes demanding significant expertise and time using commercial software. Recent advancements in deep learning, especially generative models like diffusion models, have significantly democratized and automated image and video synthesis. However, these data-driven generative models face inherent limitations:
- **Lack of Physical Plausibility:** They often produce videos that violate fundamental physical laws, leading to unrealistic or "uncanny" motions. This is due to their reliance on learning patterns from data rather than understanding underlying physical principles.
- **Limited Controllability and Detail:** While text prompts or sparse conditions (e.g., motion trajectories) can guide generation, they often struggle to capture intricate object layouts, detailed material properties, or complex, precise motion features required for high-fidelity, controllable dynamics.

A parallel line of research has focused on physics-based simulation to generate realistic dynamics. Methods like PhysGaussian, VR-GS, PhysGen, PhysDreamer, PAC-NeRF, and GIC integrate simulators (MPM, XPBD, rigid body) with 3D representations (NeRF, 3D Gaussian Splatting) to ensure physical plausibility. However, these simulation-based approaches also have their own set of limitations:
- **Input Requirements:** Many require multi-view images, videos, or explicit 3D object models as input, which are not always readily available, thus limiting their generalizability and ease of use.
- **Dimensionality/Detail:** Some approaches, like PhysGen, are constrained to 2D dynamics, while others, like Phy124, might produce coarse video outputs lacking intricate visual details.

PhysMotion positions itself as a pioneering solution that addresses the shortcomings of both paradigms. It aims to bridge the gap between the high visual fidelity achievable with generative AI and the physical consistency guaranteed by physics simulations. Critically, it does so by accepting only a *single image* as input, a much more accessible and common data format compared to multi-view data or pre-existing 3D models. By integrating 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) for geometry representation with a differentiable MPM simulator for dynamics and then refining the output with advanced diffusion models, PhysMotion carves out a unique niche. It seeks to provide the "first single image-to-video framework with 3D geometry awareness and physics-grounded dynamics," significantly advancing the state-of-the-art in controllable, realistic video generation from minimal input.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The core motivation behind PhysMotion stems from the recognized challenges in creating realistic and controllable video content efficiently. The paper highlights a dual problem: while traditional content creation is expensive and specialized, modern generative AI, despite its impressive visual synthesis capabilities, often fails to adhere to physical laws, producing inconsistent or implausible motions. Physics-based simulators offer plausibility but typically demand complex 3D inputs, limiting their practical applicability.

Given these challenges, the key objectives of PhysMotion are:

- **To develop a novel framework for high-quality, physically plausible video generation from a single image input.** This is the overarching goal, aiming to combine the best aspects of generative AI (visual quality, detail) with physics simulation (realism, consistency) while making the input requirement extremely user-friendly.
- **To leverage principled physics-based simulations (specifically continuum mechanics models via MPM) as prior knowledge to guide intermediate 3D representations.** This ensures that the generated motions are inherently physically consistent, overcoming a major limitation of purely data-driven generative models.
- **To reconstruct an effective 3D representation (3D Gaussian Splatting) from a single image and use it as the foundation for 3D physics simulations.** This objective addresses the input limitation of many existing physics-based methods by working with a sparse input.
- **To enhance the geometry, appearance, and spatiotemporal consistency of the coarse physics simulation output using diffusion models with cross-frame attention.** This aims to refine the physically plausible but potentially low-detail simulation into a visually compelling video that retains the intricate details and quality of the input image.
- **To enable controllable video generation through input conditions like applied force and torque.** This provides users with intuitive controls to dictate the type of physical interaction and motion.
- **To conduct comprehensive qualitative and quantitative evaluations to validate the efficacy of the method** across various material properties and interaction scenarios.

In essence, the motivation is to create a more accessible, robust, and physically intelligent video generation tool that can produce high-fidelity 3D dynamics from a single image, pushing the boundaries of what is possible with current generative models.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

PhysMotion employs a two-stage pipeline to achieve its objectives: **Geometry-Aware Reconstruction** followed by **Generative Video Enhancement**.

**Stage 1: Geometry-Aware Reconstruction**
1. **Input Processing:** Given a single input image ($I_0$), the system first uses a segmentation model (SAM) to delineate the foreground object ($I_0^F$) from the background ($I_0^B$).
2. **Coarse 3DGS Initialization:** A pre-trained model like LGM is used to generate an initial, coarse 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) representation of the foreground object. This lifts the 2D image into a rough 3D structure.
3. **Geometry-Aware Optimization:** To refine the coarse 3DGS, a multi-view image generation model (MVDream) creates four synthetic multi-view images from the foreground. These, along with the original foreground image, are used for optimization.
 - **Hard-Depth Loss ($L_{hard}$):** Inspired by DNGaussian, a hard-depth loss is introduced. This loss leverages monocular depth estimates (e.g., from MiDaS) from the generated multi-view images and the input image to regularize the 3DGS geometry. It ensures that the rendered depth from the 3DGS aligns accurately with the estimated ground truth depth, freezing all parameters except Gaussian centers during this optimization to focus on geometric information.
 - **Color Supervision ($L_{color}$):** Color consistency is enforced using an L1 and D-SSIM loss, but crucially, this is applied *only* on the input view ($I_0^F$). The authors observe that using generated multi-view images for color supervision can lead to poor spatial consistency due to inaccurate camera poses.
4. **Physics-Grounded Dynamics Generation:** The optimized 3DGS representation is then fed into a differentiable Material Point Method (MPM) simulator. MPM, a continuum mechanics-based method, is chosen for its robustness in handling complex material behaviors (rigid body motion, elastic deformation, viscoplastic flow, fracture, collisions).
 - The 3DGS Gaussians are augmented to be time-dependent, allowing their positions ($x_k$) and covariance matrices ($h_k$) to evolve according to the MPM simulation.
 - Users can specify input conditions, such as applied forces or torques, to initiate specific dynamics.
 - The MPM simulator time-steps the 3DGS, generating a sequence of coarse object dynamics frames ($ \{I_j\}_{j=1}^N $).

**Stage 2: Generative Video Enhancement**
This stage refines the coarse physics-driven dynamics into high-fidelity, temporally consistent videos.
1. **Inversion Stage:**
 - **Image Space Blending:** The coarse object dynamics frames ($I_j$) are blended with the static background ($I_0^B$) in *image space* using the alpha values from the optimized 3DGS. This is a critical design choice, as blending in latent space (as in some prior works like Image Sculpting) was found to cause temporal inconsistencies and loss of high-frequency details at object edges.
 - **DDIM+ Inversion:** A pre-trained image diffusion model (Stable Diffusion) is fine-tuned using LoRA based on the segmented object and generated multi-view images. DDIM+ inversion is then applied to the blended images to obtain noisy latents ($ \{\tilde{I}_j\}_{j=1}^N $).
 - **ControlNet Guidance:** ControlNet is used with two control signals: depth maps (rendered from the 3DGS) and Canny edges (extracted from the coarse dynamics frames). This helps maintain geometric consistency between the foreground and background within each frame during the diffusion process.
 - **Textural Priors:** GPT-4o is employed to extract textural priors, further augmenting the inversion process to enhance texture and appearance consistency with the input image.
2. **Sampling Stage:**
 - **Coarse and Enhanced Sampling:** Both coarse and enhanced sampling processes are performed simultaneously within the diffusion model. Feature and attention injections are applied from the coarse sampling stage to the enhanced sampling stage to guide the refinement.
 - **Key-Frame Enhancement:** A subset of key-frames ($ \{I_{j_k}\}_{k=1}^K $, with the first frame always being a key-frame) is selected. During enhanced sampling, self-attention features (queries, keys, values) for these key-frames are concatenated and shared, enabling joint enhancement and enforcing consistency among key-frames.
 - **Non-Key-Frame Propagation:** For non-key-frames, enhanced features from neighboring key-frames are propagated using Nearest-Neighbor correspondences derived from the coarse blended video (inspired by TokenFlow). This ensures temporal consistency across the entire video, even for frames that are not explicitly enhanced as key-frames.
 - The output is the final enhanced video ($ \{I_j^*\}_{j=1}^N $) with high fidelity and temporal consistency.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

PhysMotion demonstrates significant advancements in generating physically plausible and visually compelling 3D dynamics from a single image, as evidenced by both qualitative and quantitative evaluations.

**Qualitative Evaluation:**
- **Versatility:** The method showcases exceptional versatility, generating realistic dynamics for a wide variety of material properties including rigid body motion, elastic deformation, viscoplastic flow, granular particles, and even fracture effects. Examples include a diamond falling, a tennis ball bouncing, bread tearing, and various objects colliding.
- **Visual Quality and Consistency:** PhysMotion produces visually compelling and temporally consistent videos. It effectively preserves the intricate geometry and texture details of the input image throughout the dynamic sequence.
- **Superiority over Baselines:** When compared qualitatively against state-of-the-art image-to-video generation models (MotionI2V, DragAnything, I2VGen-XL, CogVideoX-5B, DynamiCrafter), PhysMotion consistently outperforms them. Baselines often exhibit issues such as:
 - Shifting textures without true geometric motion (MotionI2V).
 - Misinterpreting camera-object relationships, leading to camera movement instead of object motion (DragAnything).
 - Producing inconsistent motion, unintended object deformations, or object disappearances (I2VGen-XL, CogVideoX-5B, DynamiCrafter).
 PhysMotion's physics-guided approach avoids these common pitfalls, resulting in motions that are both visually impressive and physically believable.

**Quantitative Evaluation:**
- **Metrics:** The evaluation uses three sets of metrics:
 - **Physical Commonsense (PC) and Semantic Adherence (SA):** Measured by VideoPhy, a dedicated tool for evaluating physical realism and semantic alignment in video generation. Scores are normalized via z-score for fair comparison across different scenes.
 - **User Study:** A 2AFC (two-alternative forced choice) method assessed Temporal Consistency (TC), Physics Plausibility (PP), and Overall Preference (UP) from 20 users over approximately 1,000 judgments per baseline.
- **Performance:**
 - **VideoPhy Scores:** PhysMotion achieved the highest average normalized z-scores for both PC (0.5142) and SA (0.7230), significantly outperforming all baselines. This indicates superior performance in generating physically accurate dynamics and aligning with semantic descriptions of motion.
 - **User Study:** PhysMotion consistently outperformed all baselines across every user study criterion (TC, PP, and overall preference). While specific percentages for PhysMotion were not provided in the abstract/introduction, the statement "our proposed method consistently outperforms all baselines across every evaluation criterion" implies significantly higher user preference compared to baselines which scored in the 76-94% range.

**Ablation Studies:**
- **Geometry-Aware Reconstruction:** The ablation showed that refining the initial coarse 3DGS with hard-depth loss and color supervision is crucial. Without refinement, LGM-generated 3DGS appears blurry with texture errors. Color supervision improves texture but can introduce "floaters" (geometric artifacts). The hard-depth loss effectively mitigates these floaters and preserves correct object shape and front-view alignment, validating the necessity of this stage.
- **Image Blending Stage:** Blending foreground and background in *image space* (as done in PhysMotion) is critical for temporal consistency, especially at object edges. Blending in latent space (as in some prior works) leads to poor results, loss of high-frequency details, and temporal inconsistencies due to inaccurate masking.
- **Video Enhancement Pipeline:** The full enhancement pipeline is essential for achieving high visual quality. The coarse dynamics from the MPM simulator alone often suffer from blurriness and artifacts, particularly when internal particles are exposed during deformation (e.g., tearing bread). The video enhancement module effectively adds realistic textures and details to these blurred or internal sections, which purely physics-based 3DGS methods struggle with.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

PhysMotion represents a significant leap forward in the field of physically intelligent video generation, with broad potential impacts across multiple domains.

**Novelty and Technical Advancement:**
- **Single-Image Input for 3D Physics:** The most significant contribution is enabling 3D geometry-aware and physics-grounded dynamics from just a *single image*. This drastically lowers the input barrier compared to methods requiring multi-view data or pre-existing 3D models, making advanced dynamics generation accessible to a wider audience.
- **Synergistic Integration:** PhysMotion expertly integrates the strengths of differentiable physics simulators (MPM for physical consistency) with state-of-the-art generative AI (3D Gaussian Splatting for 3D representation and diffusion models for high-fidelity visual detail and temporal consistency). This synergistic approach addresses the inherent weaknesses of each individual component.
- **Enhanced Realism and Controllability:** By grounding the dynamics in continuum mechanics and refining visuals with diffusion models, the framework achieves a level of physical realism and visual quality that surpasses previous data-driven and many simulation-only methods. The ability to control dynamics via applied forces and torques adds a crucial layer of user interaction.

**Potential Impact and Applications:**
- **Content Creation Industries (Film, Games, VR/AR):** PhysMotion can revolutionize traditional workflows in these industries. Animators and designers can generate complex, physically accurate animations (e.g., destruction, fluid dynamics, soft body deformations) from simple image inputs, drastically reducing the labor, time, and expertise traditionally required. This could accelerate prototyping, asset creation, and special effects production.
- **Democratization of Advanced Graphics:** By making sophisticated physics-driven animation accessible from a single image, PhysMotion can empower non-experts and hobbyists to create high-quality dynamic content, fostering creativity and expanding the community of digital artists.
- **Design and Engineering:** Rapid visualization of material behavior under various stresses and forces could aid product design, material science research, and engineering simulations by providing quick, realistic previews of interactions.
- **Education and Training:** Creating interactive and physically accurate simulations for educational purposes could become much simpler, enhancing learning experiences in physics, engineering, and other sciences.
- **Future AI Research:** This work contributes to the broader goal of building more physically intelligent AI systems. By demonstrating how to inject fundamental physical laws into generative models, PhysMotion paves the way for AI that not only generates visually coherent content but also understands and respects the underlying physics of the real world. This could lead to advancements in embodied AI, robotics, and common-sense reasoning.

**Acknowledged Limitations and Future Work:**
The authors acknowledge that despite its strengths, PhysMotion may introduce slight color distortions due to the image diffusion models, affecting overall color consistency. This points to a future research direction: maintaining color fidelity during video enhancement. Another exciting avenue for future work is the integration of video diffusion models directly with physics-based simulators, which could potentially offer even greater spatiotemporal consistency and higher quality dynamic generation.

In conclusion, PhysMotion marks a significant step towards creating intelligent tools for realistic and controllable digital content, blending the best of physics simulation and generative AI to deliver physically plausible, high-fidelity dynamics from an unprecedentedly minimal input.
