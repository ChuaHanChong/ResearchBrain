# Temporal-Difference Variational Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Oxford developed Temporal-Difference Variational Continual Learning (TD-VCL), a new family of objectives that mitigate compounding approximation errors in standard Variational Continual Learning by integrating regularization from multiple past posterior estimates. This approach consistently enhanced performance across continual learning benchmarks, reducing catastrophic forgetting and establishing a theoretical link to Temporal-Difference methods.

## Problem

- Machine learning models often suffer from Catastrophic Forgetting, where learning new tasks severely degrades performance on previously acquired knowledge.
- Standard Variational Continual Learning (VCL) relies solely on the immediately preceding posterior estimate for regularization, which leads to compounding approximation errors as tasks are learned sequentially.
- These compounding errors undermine memory stability and prevent models from reliably retaining knowledge from a sequence of tasks.

## Method

- Introduced ==n-Step KL Regularization==, which incorporates 'n' previous posterior estimates into the regularization term, distributing the influence of individual approximation errors.
- Proposed Temporal-Difference VCL (TD(λ)-VCL), a generalized objective using a geometrically decaying factor 'λ' to weight past posterior estimates, offering fine-grained control over historical influence.
- Formalized a theoretical connection between these multi-step VCL objectives and Temporal-Difference methods from Reinforcement Learning, representing **TD-VCL** as a discounted sum of n-step TD targets.

## Results

- TD-VCL objectives consistently outperformed standard VCL and non-variational baselines across various challenging benchmarks (PermutedMNIST-Hard, SplitMNIST-Hard, CIFAR100-10, TinyImageNet-10), demonstrating superior resilience to Catastrophic Forgetting.
- Per-task analysis showed TD-VCL methods maintained higher accuracy on early tasks, e.g., 80-**85%** on PermutedMNIST-Hard Task 1 after observing all tasks, compared to 50-**60%** for VCL baselines.
- The TD objective successfully enhanced the performance of existing Bayesian CL methods like UCL and UCB when integrated, confirming its robustness and general applicability across different VCL-based algorithms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Compounding approximation errors in VCL arise from relying on a single, potentially inaccurate, previous posterior estimate, leading to exacerbated catastrophic forgetting.
> - Integrating regularization from multiple past posterior estimates can dilute individual approximation errors and leverage more accurate historical information to stabilize continual learning.
> - The inherent structure of multi-step VCL objectives shares a principled connection with Temporal-Difference methods, providing a unified theoretical foundation for sequential Bayesian learning and suggesting a natural emergence of replay mechanisms.
