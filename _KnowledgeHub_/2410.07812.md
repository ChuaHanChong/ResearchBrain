# Temporal-Difference Variational Continual Learning

> [!summary] Summary
> Researchers at the University of Oxford developed Temporal-Difference Variational Continual Learning (TD-VCL), a new family of objectives that mitigate compounding approximation errors in standard Variational Continual Learning by integrating regularization from multiple past posterior estimates. This approach consistently enhanced performance across continual learning benchmarks, reducing catastrophic forgetting and establishing a theoretical link to Temporal-Difference methods.

## Problem

- Machine learning models often suffer from Catastrophic Forgetting, where learning new tasks severely degrades performance on previously acquired knowledge.
- Standard Variational Continual Learning (VCL) relies solely on the immediately preceding posterior estimate for regularization, which leads to compounding approximation errors as tasks are learned sequentially.
- These compounding errors undermine memory stability and prevent models from reliably retaining knowledge from a sequence of tasks.

## Method

- Introduced ==n-Step KL Regularization==, which incorporates 'n' previous posterior estimates into the regularization term, distributing the influence of individual approximation errors.
- Proposed Temporal-Difference VCL (TD(λ)-VCL), a generalized objective using a geometrically decaying factor 'λ' to weight past posterior estimates, offering fine-grained control over historical influence.
- Formalized a theoretical connection between these multi-step VCL objectives and Temporal-Difference methods from Reinforcement Learning, representing **TD-VCL** as a discounted sum of n-step TD targets.

## Results

- TD-VCL objectives consistently outperformed standard VCL and non-variational baselines across various challenging benchmarks (PermutedMNIST-Hard, SplitMNIST-Hard, CIFAR100-10, TinyImageNet-10), demonstrating superior resilience to Catastrophic Forgetting.
- Per-task analysis showed TD-VCL methods maintained higher accuracy on early tasks, e.g., 80-**85%** on PermutedMNIST-Hard Task 1 after observing all tasks, compared to 50-**60%** for VCL baselines.
- The TD objective successfully enhanced the performance of existing Bayesian CL methods like UCL and UCB when integrated, confirming its robustness and general applicability across different VCL-based algorithms.

## Takeaways

> [!tip] Key Insights
> - Compounding approximation errors in VCL arise from relying on a single, potentially inaccurate, previous posterior estimate, leading to exacerbated catastrophic forgetting.
> - Integrating regularization from multiple past posterior estimates can dilute individual approximation errors and leverage more accurate historical information to stabilize continual learning.
> - The inherent structure of multi-step VCL objectives shares a principled connection with Temporal-Difference methods, providing a unified theoretical foundation for sequential Bayesian learning and suggesting a natural emergence of replay mechanisms.

## Detailed Report

### 1. How This Work Fits into the Broader Research Landscape

Machine learning models operating in real-world, dynamic environments are often exposed to sequential data streams and shifts in data-generating distributions. A fundamental requirement for such models is Continual Learning (CL), which necessitates two primary capabilities: plasticity and memory stability. Plasticity refers to the ability to effectively learn new tasks or adapt to new data, while memory stability denotes the capacity to retain knowledge acquired from previously learned tasks. The challenge of balancing these two properties is central to CL research, as models frequently exhibit Catastrophic Forgetting—a phenomenon where learning new information leads to a significant degradation of performance on prior tasks. This issue undermines the reliability of continuously deployed machine learning systems.

Within the extensive literature addressing CL, this work specifically focuses on Bayesian Continual Learning (BCL) methods. BCL provides a principled framework for online and low-data learning scenarios, offering a self-consistent approach to knowledge acquisition. A key advantage of Bayesian models is their ability to quantify uncertainty in predictions, which is relevant for safety-critical applications and for guiding data selection strategies. The paper primarily investigates Variational Continual Learning (VCL), a prominent BCL approach that leverages a recursive relationship between successive posterior distributions. Standard VCL formulates a variational optimization objective that regularizes the updated posterior distribution to remain close to the most recently estimated posterior. However, the authors identify a limitation in this approach: relying solely on a single previous posterior estimate can lead to compounding approximation errors as new tasks are learned sequentially, potentially hindering the model's ability to maintain memory stability.

This research aims to advance the field of Bayesian Continual Learning by proposing novel learning objectives that mitigate the issue of compounding approximation errors in VCL. It seeks to integrate regularization effects from multiple previous posterior estimations, thereby providing a more robust mechanism for balancing plasticity and memory stability. The work also establishes a theoretical connection between these new CL objectives and Temporal-Difference (TD) methods, a well-established concept in Reinforcement Learning and Neuroscience, suggesting a deeper underlying relationship across these disciplines.

### 2. Key Objectives and Motivation

The primary objective of this research is to develop more effective learning objectives for Variational Continual Learning (VCL) that address the problem of Catastrophic Forgetting, particularly by mitigating the impact of compounding approximation errors inherent in existing VCL approaches.

The motivation for this work stems from a critical observation regarding the conventional VCL objective. In standard VCL, the posterior distribution over model parameters for the current task is recursively updated by minimizing a Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence to a target distribution. This target distribution is constructed using the likelihood of the current task's data and the posterior approximation from the *immediately preceding* task. While this recursive formulation aims to balance learning new tasks (plasticity) with retaining old knowledge (memory stability) by keeping the new posterior close to the previous one, the authors argue that this reliance on a single previous estimate is problematic.

Specifically, the authors contend that any approximation error in a given posterior estimate will propagate to the subsequent update. Over successive recursive updates, these individual errors can accumulate and compound. If a particular estimation is substantially inaccurate, its error will be entirely carried forward, potentially leading to a significant divergence of the variational posterior from the true posterior distribution. This compounding error can severely degrade the model's performance on previously learned tasks, exacerbating Catastrophic Forgetting.

To counter this, the paper's key motivation is to design new learning objectives that can integrate information from *multiple* previous posterior estimations. By incorporating a sequence of past approximations rather than just the latest one, the proposed objectives aim to:
1. **Dilute individual errors**: Distributing the regularization effect across several past estimates can lessen the impact of a particularly poor approximation.
2. **Leverage corrective influence**: More accurate past posterior estimates can exert a corrective influence on the learning process, guiding the current posterior toward a more robust approximation.
3. **Provide broader context**: Utilizing a wider historical context in the learning target can reduce the overall effect of compounding errors over time, leading to more stable and accurate continual learning.

Furthermore, a significant motivation is to reveal and formalize a connection between these multi-step regularization objectives and Temporal-Difference methods, a well-known concept from Reinforcement Learning and Neuroscience. This connection is intended to provide a principled theoretical foundation for the proposed objectives, extending the understanding of Continual Learning mechanisms.

### 3. Methodology and Approach

The methodology proposed in this paper extends the Variational Continual Learning (VCL) framework by introducing new learning objectives designed to mitigate compounding approximation errors. The core of the approach involves incorporating information from multiple past posterior estimates into the regularization term.

**Preliminaries in Variational Continual Learning (VCL):**
In Bayesian CL, the objective is to approximate the true posterior distribution `p(θ | D_1:T)` given a prior `p(θ)` and a sequence of tasks `D_1:T`. The tasks are assumed to be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) over time. A recursive property of posteriors states `p(θ | D_1:T) ∝ p(θ | D_1:T-1)p(D_T | θ)`. VCL approximates this intractable posterior using online variational inference, where a variational distribution `q_t(θ)` is learned at each timestep `t` by minimizing the KL divergence:
`q_t(θ) = arg min_{q∈Q} D_KL(q(θ) || (1/Z_t)q_{t-1}(θ)p(D_t | θ))`
This objective is equivalent to maximizing the variational lower bound (ELBO):
`L_t^VCL(θ) = E_{θ∼q_t(θ)}[log p(D_t | θ)] − D_KL(q_t(θ) || q_{t-1}(θ))`
The first term promotes plasticity (learning the new task `D_t`), and the second term encourages memory stability by penalizing deviations from the previous posterior `q_{t-1}(θ)`.

**Critique and Proposed Solution:**
The paper argues that relying solely on `q_{t-1}(θ)` for regularization allows approximation errors to propagate and compound. To address this, two new objectives are proposed:

1. **n-Step KL Regularization (Proposition 4.1):**
 This objective reformulates the standard VCL KL minimization to consider `n` previous posterior estimates. The proposed objective is:
 `q_t(θ) = arg max_{q∈Q} E_{θ∼q_t(θ)}[ sum_{i=0}^{n-1} ((n − i)/n) log p(D_{t−i} | θ) ] − sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (1/n) D_KL(q_t(θ) || q_{t−i−1}(θ))`
 Here, `n` is a hyperparameter defining how far back in time the learning target incorporates previous estimates. Setting `n=1` recovers the vanilla VCL objective. The objective explicitly weights the likelihood of past data, suggesting that a replay mechanism for previous tasks naturally emerges from this formulation. By distributing the regularization across multiple `D_KL` terms, individual errors from any single past estimate have a reduced impact.

2. **Temporal-Difference VCL (TD(λ)-VCL) (Proposition 4.2):**
 This objective generalizes the n-Step KL regularization by introducing a geometrically decaying weighting factor `λ`. The objective is:
 `q_t(θ) = arg max_{q∈Q} E_{θ∼q_t(θ)}[ sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (λ^i (1 − λ^{n−i}) / (1 − λ^n)) log p(D_{t−i} | θ) ] − sum_{i=0}^{n-1} (λ^i (1 − λ) / (1 − λ^n)) D_KL(q_t(θ) || q_{t−i−1}(θ))`
 The hyperparameter `λ ∈ [0, 1)` controls the influence of older estimates, with more recent estimates receiving higher weight. This provides more granular control over the learning target's dependence on past information.

**Connection to Temporal-Difference (TD) Methods (Proposition 4.4):**
The paper formalizes a connection between these objectives and Temporal-Difference (TD) methods from Reinforcement Learning (RL).
- **n-Step Temporal-Difference target for VCL (Definition 4.3):**
 `TD_t(n) = E_{θ∼q_t(θ)}[ sum_{i=0}^{n-1} log p(D_{t−i} | θ) ] − D_KL(q_t(θ) || q_{t−n}(θ))`
 This definition reveals that the TD(λ)-VCL objective can be expressed as a discounted sum of these n-step TD targets:
 `q_t(θ) = arg max_{q∈Q} ( (1 − λ) / (1 − λ^n) ) * sum_{k=0}^{n-1} λ^k TD_t(k + 1))`
 This formulation is analogous to λ-returns in RL, where a blend of multi-step updates is used. The TD(λ)-VCL objective thus forms a spectrum of CL algorithms, spanning from vanilla VCL (when `λ=0`) to n-Step KL Regularization (when `λ→1`). This blending mechanism allows for balancing the bias (from bootstrapping) and variance (from Monte Carlo estimation) in the learning objective.

**Implementation Details:**
- A Gaussian mean-field approximate posterior and a Gaussian prior `N(0, σ^2 I)` are used.
- Distributions are parameterized as deep neural networks.
- The KL term is computed analytically, while expected log-likelihoods are estimated using Monte Carlo approximations with the reparametrization trick for gradient computation.
- Likelihood-tempering is applied to mitigate variational over-pruning.
- Test-time evaluation involves computing the posterior predictive distribution through Monte Carlo sampling from the approximated posterior.

### 4. Main Findings and Results

The experimental evaluation of Temporal-Difference VCL (TD-VCL) methods focused on their ability to mitigate Catastrophic Forgetting across various challenging Continual Learning (CL) benchmarks.

**Benchmarks and Baselines:**
The evaluation included three novel "Hard" benchmarks (PermutedMNIST-Hard, SplitMNIST-Hard, SplitNotMNIST-Hard) designed with stricter replay memory limitations and single-head classifiers to intensify the Catastrophic Forgetting challenge. Additionally, two established, challenging CL benchmarks (CIFAR100-10 and TinyImageNet-10) were used. Comparison methods included non-variational baselines (Online MLE, Batch MLE) and prominent variational CL methods (VCL, VCL CoreSet, UCL, UCB), with the latter two also evaluated in their TD-enhanced variants (TD-UCL, TD-UCB).

**Effectiveness in Mitigating Catastrophic Forgetting (Tables 1 & 2):**
Across all benchmarks, the proposed TD-VCL objectives (n-Step TD-VCL and TD(λ)-VCL) consistently demonstrated superior performance compared to standard VCL and non-variational baselines. This improvement was particularly evident as the number of observed tasks increased, indicating a greater resilience to Catastrophic Forgetting.
- **PermutedMNIST-Hard**: TD-VCL methods maintained approximately 90% average accuracy after all tasks, significantly outperforming VCL (approximately 78%) and non-variational approaches which suffered drastic performance drops.
- **SplitMNIST-Hard and SplitNotMNIST-Hard**: TD-VCL objectives surpassed baselines, with the performance difference becoming more pronounced with an increasing number of tasks, especially in the harder SplitNotMNIST-Hard setup where modest architectures face higher approximation errors.
- **CIFAR100-10 and TinyImageNet-10 (using Bayesian CNNs)**: In these more complex and larger-scale benchmarks, TD-VCL objectives also achieved superior performance, particularly at later timesteps where baselines showed greater forgetting. This indicates the benefits of leveraging multiple posterior estimates even with high approximation errors.

**Per-Task Performance (Figure 3 & Appendix K):**
Analysis of per-task accuracy over time revealed that baselines, especially non-variational methods, exhibited a stronger Catastrophic Forgetting effect on earlier tasks. In contrast, TD-VCL objectives demonstrated more robust performance. For example, on Task 1 of PermutedMNIST-Hard, TD-VCL methods maintained 80-85% accuracy after observing all tasks, while VCL baselines dropped to 50-60%, and MLE-based methods to 20%. This suggests that TD-VCL does not merely "compensate" for forgetting on early tasks with higher accuracy on later tasks but genuinely preserves knowledge.

**Compatibility with Other Bayesian CL Methods (Table 3):**
The study incorporated the TD objective into existing Bayesian CL methods (UCL and UCB). The results showed that the TD-enhanced versions (TD-UCL and TD-UCB) consistently improved upon their respective base methods, particularly at later timesteps. This finding suggests that the TD objectives are robust and generally applicable across different Bayesian CL algorithms, indicating their potential to enhance various VCL-based approaches.

**Hyperparameter Robustness (Appendix L):**
An ablation study on the PermutedMNIST-Hard benchmark investigated the sensitivity of TD-VCL to its hyperparameters: `n` (number of steps), `λ` (decay factor), and `β` (likelihood-tempering parameter).
- **`β` sensitivity**: Similar to VCL, TD-VCL objectives were sensitive to `β`. High values caused variational over-pruning (preventing new task learning), while low values led to insufficient knowledge retention (Catastrophic Forgetting). Optimal `β` values balanced this trade-off.
- **`n` effect**: Performance generally improved by increasing `n` up to a certain point (e.g., `n=5`), after which the benefits saturated or slightly diminished. This suggests an optimal range for the number of past posterior estimates to incorporate.
- **`λ` robustness**: TD-VCL objectives exhibited robustness to the choice of `λ`, with its effect becoming more pronounced as the number of observed tasks increased. This implies that the choice of `λ` can be adjusted based on the perceived quality and relevance of recent versus older posterior estimates.

**StreamingPermutedMNIST-Hard (Appendix N):**
An additional experiment on a "StreamingPermutedMNIST-Hard" benchmark, where task boundaries were obscured, demonstrated that TD-VCL methods do not inherently require explicit knowledge of task boundaries. Their performance was comparable to or even slightly improved over the task-boundary-aware setting, further supporting the theoretical self-consistency of the Bayesian framework under sequential updates.

### 5. Significance and Potential Impact

This work introduces a novel family of variational objectives for Continual Learning, termed Temporal-Difference Variational Continual Learning (TD-VCL), offering several contributions to the field.

### 6. Significance

1. **Mitigation of Compounding Approximation Errors**: The central significance lies in the proposed solution to a recognized limitation of standard VCL: the compounding of approximation errors when relying solely on the most recent posterior estimate. By integrating regularization from multiple past posterior estimates, TD-VCL provides a more robust and stable learning process, which is critical for maintaining memory stability in sequential learning settings.
2. **Enhanced Catastrophic Forgetting Alleviation**: The consistent outperformance of TD-VCL objectives against strong VCL baselines and non-variational methods across a range of challenging benchmarks (including newly proposed "Hard" versions) empirically supports its effectiveness in alleviating Catastrophic Forgetting. This addresses a fundamental problem in continual learning and enhances the reliability of models learning in dynamic environments.
3. **Novel Theoretical Connections**: The work establishes an insightful and principled theoretical link between variational continual learning objectives and Temporal-Difference methods, a prominent learning mechanism in Reinforcement Learning and Neuroscience. This connection broadens the theoretical understanding of CL mechanisms and suggests a potential for cross-pollination of ideas between these previously distinct research areas. The representation of TD-VCL as a discounted sum of n-step Temporal-Difference targets provides a unified perspective on different forms of multi-step learning.
4. **Natural Replay Mechanism**: The n-Step KL regularization objective inherently includes terms that re-estimate old task likelihoods, implying that a form of replay naturally emerges from the learning objective. This is significant because replay mechanisms are often employed heuristically in CL, and their principled emergence from a Bayesian objective provides a stronger theoretical grounding.

**Potential Impact:**
1. **Improved Robustness and Reliability of AI Systems**: By effectively combating Catastrophic Forgetting, TD-VCL can contribute to the development of more robust and reliable machine learning models for real-world applications that require continuous adaptation and learning, such as autonomous systems, personalized assistants, and long-term predictive analytics.
2. **Advancement in Bayesian Machine Learning**: The work pushes the boundaries of Bayesian Continual Learning, offering a more theoretically sound and empirically validated approach to sequential variational inference. This can lead to further innovations in uncertainty-aware and data-efficient learning systems, which are crucial for safety-critical applications where quantifying model uncertainty is paramount.
3. **Interdisciplinary Research Opportunities**: The established connection between CL and TD methods may inspire new research at the intersection of these fields, potentially leading to novel algorithms for tasks in both Continual Learning and Reinforcement Learning, or even drawing further insights from cognitive neuroscience.
4. **Foundation for Future Work**: The limitations identified, such as hyperparameter tuning and memory requirements, open avenues for future research into adaptive hyperparameter selection and memory-efficient Bayesian architectures (e.g., using low-rank approximations or distributed memory systems), further refining the practical applicability of TD-VCL. The exploration of formal mathematical connections with Markov Decision/Reward Processes could also deepen theoretical understanding and lead to more generalized continual learning frameworks.
